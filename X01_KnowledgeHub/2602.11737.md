# Mask What Matters: Mitigating Object Hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models with Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich, Amazon, and MBZUAI developed Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD), an inference-time method that uses DINO Vision Transformers to create object-aligned auxiliary views for Visual Contrastive Decoding. This approach reduced object hallucination in MLLMs, achieving up to a 1.5% accuracy gain on the POPE benchmark and improving average scores on the MME hallucination subset for models like LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate responses referring to objects not present or incorrectly grounded in images, a phenomenon known as object hallucination.
- Existing Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) methods often rely on generic, heuristic image-level perturbations that do not precisely target object extents.
- Some VCD approaches depend on prompt- and model-specific cross-modal signals, potentially introducing computational overhead, complexity, or circularity.

## Method

- The work introduces ==Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD)==, enhancing standard VCD with a novel approach for generating auxiliary views.
- Pre-trained ==DINO Vision Transformers== are leveraged to extract object-centric saliency maps from input images, exploiting their inherent ability to identify semantic regions.
- Auxiliary views are created by ==masking out the most salient object regions== based on a quantile threshold of these saliency maps, with the masked areas filled by a neutral background.

## Results

- OA-VCD consistently outperformed baseline methods on the POPE benchmark, achieving up to **1.5%** higher Accuracy and **1.6%** higher F1 score compared to AGLA on LLaVA-v1.5 (7B).
- The method led to improved average scores on the MME hallucination subset, showing notable gains in "Existence" and "Color" categories for both LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the method's robustness to different choices of neutral background (e.g., blur, black, mean color) and masking area ratio (γ\gammaγ).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-aligned auxiliary views, derived from self-supervised ViT attention, provide a more semantically targeted and stronger contrast signal for VCD compared to generic perturbations.
> - The method is prompt-agnostic and model-agnostic, enabling broad applicability across various MLLMs without requiring model-specific fine-tuning or internal states.
> - Generating these targeted auxiliary views requires only a single cacheable forward pass through a DINO ViT, ensuring computational efficiency during inference.
