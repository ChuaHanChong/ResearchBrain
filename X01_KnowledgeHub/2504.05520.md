# Efficient Reinforcement Finetuning via Adaptive Curriculum Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at USC and the University of Maryland developed ADARFT, a method that uses adaptive curriculum learning to improve the efficiency and accuracy of Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT) for Large Language Models (LLMs) in mathematical reasoning tasks. The system dynamically adjusts problem difficulty based on the model's performance, leading to up to 2x faster training convergence and consistent final accuracy improvements across various models and data distributions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Finetuning (RFT) for LLMs is sample-inefficient and computationally expensive, requiring extensive resources and training time.
- Existing RFT methods struggle with non-uniform or imbalanced data distributions, leading to suboptimal learning when problems are too easy or too hard.
- Current curriculum learning approaches for RFT are either manually staged and lack adaptivity or incur significant online rollout overhead for data filtering.

## Method

- ==ADARFT== employs a ==dynamic curriculum learning== strategy that adjusts training problem difficulty based on the model's recent reward signals, sampling problems closest to a 'target difficulty'.
- It theoretically justifies setting the ==target success rate (beta=0.5)==, maximizing the learning signal by focusing on problems where the model experiences a productive struggle.
- Difficulty scores are estimated using rollout-based empirical methods, pre-existing labels (AoPS), or lightweight LLM-judged assessments, making it flexible and generalizable as a 'plug-and-play' extension for various RFT algorithms.

## Results

- ADARFT reduced training steps by up to **2x** (e.g., Qwen 2.5 Math 1.5B in `skew-difficult` setting, requiring **71.7%** fewer steps than standard PPO to reach comparable performance).
- It consistently achieved higher final accuracy, with gains up to **3.91 percentage points** (e.g., Qwen 2.5 Math 1.5B in `uniform` setting, from **37.20%** to **41.11%**).
- The approach maintained effectiveness even when using lightweight LLM-judged difficulty scores, demonstrating practicality without requiring extensive rollouts for difficulty estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on problems where the model has approximately a 50% chance of success (target reward β=0.5) maximizes the learning signal, leading to the strongest policy updates and highest accuracy.
> - A dynamically adjusted curriculum, as opposed to static or online filtering methods, significantly improves both training efficiency and final model performance, especially in imbalanced data scenarios.
> - ADARFT's lightweight and algorithm-agnostic design allows it to seamlessly integrate with and enhance existing RFT algorithms (e.g., PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO) without specific modifications.
