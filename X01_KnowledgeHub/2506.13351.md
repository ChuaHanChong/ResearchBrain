# Direct Reasoning Optimization: Constrained RL with Token-Level Dense Reward and Rubric-Gated Constraints for Open-ended Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft and UCLA developed Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO), an RL-based framework that allows large language models to self-refine their reasoning for open-ended tasks by using a novel internal Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3). This method leverages the model's self-certainty about a reference outcome given its Chain-of-Thought, leading to superior reasoning quality on tasks like paragraph revision and comparable performance to explicit verifiers on structured QA.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles with open-ended, long-form reasoning tasks due to the lack of objective, scalable reward signals.
- Training separate reward models is computationally expensive and requires extensive human preference data, while "LLM-as-a-judge" introduces biases and prompt sensitivity.
- Traditional metrics like ROUGE fail to capture logical coherence or reasoning quality in open-ended generations.

## Method

- ==Direct Reasoning Optimization (DRO)==, an RL-based fine-tuning framework, is introduced to enable self-reward and self-refinement for LLMs on open-ended tasks.
- A novel internal reward signal, ==Reasoning Reflection Reward (R3)==, quantifies the consistency between a model's Chain-of-Thought and a reference outcome by emphasizing =="reasoning-reflective tokens."==
- R3 addresses log-probability aggregation issues by weighting crucial tokens and subtracting a ==masked-CoT baseline==, while a dynamic data filtering mechanism optimizes training efficiency.

## Results

- On the open-ended ParaRev dataset, DRO-R3 achieved higher win rates against GPT-4o than all baselines, including ROUGE-rewarded and aggregate-certainty models, as evaluated by GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet.
- For the structured FinQA task, DRO-R3 demonstrated performance comparable to an ideal correctness-based reward, falling just **0.9%** short on Pass@1 and surpassing it for Pass@k (k 2).
- The dynamic data filtering mechanism reduced training time by approximately **45%** while maintaining similar final reward scores and ensuring smoother convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively reward and refine their own reasoning for open-ended tasks using internal self-certainty signals, eliminating reliance on external feedback.
> - Not all tokens contribute equally to reflecting reasoning quality; emphasizing "reasoning-reflective tokens" via variance weighting provides a more precise reward signal than naive log-probability aggregation.
> - Dynamically filtering training data based on model difficulty and reasoning variation significantly improves training efficiency and focuses learning on impactful samples.
