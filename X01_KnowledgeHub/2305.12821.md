# FurnitureBench: Reproducible Real-World Benchmark for Long-Horizon Complex Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FurnitureBench introduces a real-world benchmark for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation, focusing on furniture assembly with 3D-printable models, a standardized software stack, and a large teleoperated dataset. Initial evaluations reveal that current state-of-the-art algorithms struggle significantly, failing to complete any full assembly tasks.

## Problem

- The lack of standardized, reproducible, and sufficiently complex real-world benchmarks for robotic manipulation research.
- Existing real-world benchmarks often focus on simple, short-horizon tasks, creating a 'sim-to-real' gap for complex, multi-step operations.
- Difficulties in reproducing robotics research due to variations in hardware, environments, and software configurations.

## Method

- Developed ==FurnitureBench==, a reproducible real-world benchmark for furniture assembly using a Franka Emika Panda robot and 3D-printable furniture models.
- Provided a Dockerized robot control software stack, a systematic task initialization tool, and comprehensive setup guides to ensure ease of use and reproducibility across labs.
- Released a large ==teleoperated demonstration dataset== (200+ hours, 5000+ demonstrations) and a high-fidelity simulator (==FurnitureSim==) to support offline learning and sim-to-real transfer research.

## Results

- Reproducibility analysis showed that new environments set up by different participants achieved **75-93%** relative performance compared to the original setup.
- Neither Behavioral Cloning (BC) nor Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) policies successfully completed any full furniture assembly tasks across all eight models or randomness levels.
- The 'inserting' skill proved to be the most challenging (**0-20%** success rate), and 'screwing' for round parts also showed very low success (**0-10%**), highlighting critical bottlenecks for existing algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Furniture assembly serves as an effective long-horizon, complex manipulation challenge, requiring diverse dexterous skills like precise insertion and robust screwing under varying conditions.
> - Reproducibility in real-world robotics can be achieved through standardized hardware, 3D-printed components, and a robust software stack, with independent setups showing high performance consistency.
> - Current state-of-the-art offline reinforcement learning and imitation learning algorithms are significantly limited and unable to solve full, real-world long-horizon assembly tasks.
