# Few-Shot Object Detection with Attention-RPN and Multi-Relation Detector

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Tencent developed a few-shot object detection network capable of detecting objects from unseen categories without requiring further training or fine-tuning. The system demonstrated 31.3% AP50 on MS COCO minival for novel categories and introduced a new diverse FSOD dataset for training and evaluation.

## Problem

- Traditional object detection models heavily rely on large, labor-intensive datasets, making them impractical for rare or rapidly evolving object categories.
- Applying few-shot classification methods directly to object detection is challenging, as it requires both accurate classification and precise localization.
- Standard Region Proposal Networks (RPNs) often generate irrelevant or low-quality proposals for novel objects due to a lack of category-specific knowledge.

## Method

- A deep attentioned few-shot detection network was developed, integrating an ==Attention-RPN== and a ==Multi-Relation Detector== within a modified Faster R-CNN framework.
- The Attention-RPN leverages support image features via ==depth-wise cross-correlation== to guide proposal generation, focusing on regions similar to the target object.
- A Multi-Relation Detector combines Global, Local, and Patch-Relation heads to robustly measure similarity between query proposals and support objects for fine-grained classification.
- A ==2-way contrastive training== strategy was employed, using triplets of query, positive support, and negative support images to teach the model to differentiate between similar and dissimilar categories.

## Results

- The model achieved **31.3%** AP50 and **16.1%** AP75 on the MS COCO minival dataset (20 novel categories with 10 supports), outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods that required fine-tuning.
- On the ImageNet Detection dataset (50-way 5-shot), the model achieved **44.1%** AP50 and **31.0%** AP75 after fine-tuning, establishing new performance benchmarks.
- Ablation studies showed the Attention-RPN improved AP50/AP75 by **2.0%/2.1%** and delivered higher proposal quality (**0.9130** recall) compared to a regular RPN.
- Training on the newly introduced FSOD dataset, with **800** diverse categories, consistently yielded better performance than training on datasets with fewer categories but more samples, demonstrating the importance of category diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating support information directly into the RPN effectively improves the quality of generated proposals for novel categories by filtering out irrelevant background boxes.
> - Combining multiple complementary attention mechanisms in the detector (global, local, patch) provides a more robust and discriminative similarity matching for few-shot learning.
> - A contrastive training strategy that explicitly teaches the model to distinguish between target and non-target novel categories is crucial for achieving strong generalization without fine-tuning.
> - Category diversity in the training dataset is more critical for few-shot object detection performance than the sheer number of samples per category.
