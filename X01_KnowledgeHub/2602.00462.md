# LatentLens: Revealing Highly Interpretable Visual Tokens in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila and affiliated universities introduce LATENTLENS, a training-free method to interpret visual tokens within vision-language models (VLMs) by comparing them to contextualized text representations from an LLM. This approach reveals that visual tokens are consistently interpretable across all layers and align with the semantic richness found in the LLM's intermediate processing layers.

## Problem

- Understanding how large language models (LLMs) integrate visual information when adapted into vision-language models (VLMs) remains a challenge.
- Previous training-free interpretability methods (e.g., EmbeddingLens, LogitLens) often found visual tokens to be largely uninterpretable, particularly in early layers.
- Existing methods were limited by relying on the LLM's fixed vocabulary and static input/output embedding matrices, potentially failing to capture nuanced, contextualized semantics.

## Method

- Developed ==LATENTLENS==, a training-free interpretability technique that compares a VLM's visual token representations to a corpus of contextualized text representations generated by the same LLM across multiple intermediate layers.
- Utilized an ==LLM-based judge==, validated against human annotations, to quantitatively assess the interpretability of visual token descriptions across various VLM architectures.
- Constructed a large reference corpus of ==2.99 million Visual Genome captions==, processed by the LLM, to provide rich, sentence-level descriptions for visual tokens.

## Results

- LATENTLENS showed an average of **72%** of visual tokens were interpretable, outperforming EmbeddingLens (approx. **30%**) and LogitLens (approx. **23%**) across models and layers.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed LATENTLENS provides semantically richer, full-word or sentence-level interpretations that accurately describe visual content, unlike the subword or unrenderable outputs from baselines.
- The method successfully generalized to an off-the-shelf VLM (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct), maintaining high interpretability (**49-70%**) and exhibiting a similar Mid-Layer Leap pattern.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual tokens are highly interpretable across all LLM layers, with LATENTLENS revealing an average of 72% interpretability, significantly higher than baseline methods.
> - A "Mid-Layer Leap" phenomenon demonstrates that visual tokens, even from early layers, align most strongly with contextualized text representations from the LLM's intermediate layers (e.g., layers 8-16).
> - Visual tokens undergo less internal transformation within the LLM compared to text tokens, suggesting they arrive in a pre-contextualized state compatible with the LLM's semantic processing.
