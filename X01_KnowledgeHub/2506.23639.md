# Unified Multimodal Understanding via Byte-Pair Visual Encoding

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework unifies multimodal understanding by applying a byte-pair encoding (BPE) strategy to visual tokens, which are derived from quantized images and incorporate spatial consistency. This approach achieves performance competitive with leading continuous representation MLLMs on diverse vision-language tasks and contributes to reduced visual hallucinations.

## Problem

- Conventional Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) using continuous visual representations suffer from a "modality gap" with discrete text tokens and an "information bottleneck" leading to visual hallucinations.
- Existing discrete visual tokenization methods often quantize images into fixed-size tokens without fully capturing semantic structure or incorporating crucial spatial relationships.
- Prior work on visual BPE was primarily frequency-based, overlooking spatial consistency, and lacked effective training procedures for practical multimodal models.

## Method

- Transforms images into discrete BPE visual tokens using a pre-trained ==VQ-GAN== for initial quantization, followed by a ==BPE== process that adapts text-based BPE for visual data.
- Introduces a priority-guided encoding scheme for visual BPE that considers both co-occurrence frequency and a novel measure of spatial consistency to form semantically meaningful tokens.
- Employs a ==multi-stage training pipeline== with ==curriculum-based data composition== (progressing from foundation to instruction data) and progressive parameter unfreezing to optimize learning with hierarchical BPE tokens.

## Results

- Proposed models (Being-VL-0.5 and Being-VL-0.5

) achieve performance comparable to leading continuous embedding-based MLLMs (e.g., Being-VL-0.5
+
reaches **80.6 on VQAv2** and **72.1 on MMBench**).
- The approach significantly outperforms previous discrete token methods (Being-VL-0) across benchmarks and shows reduced visual hallucination on POPE (**84.3** for Being-VL-0.5).
- Ablation studies demonstrate that both curriculum-based data composition and progressive parameter unfreezing are essential, with the multi-stage strategy outperforming single-stage training by **over 12%** on perception and reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying byte-pair encoding (BPE) to visual data, guided by both frequency and spatial consistency, helps bridge the representation gap between visual and textual modalities.
> - A multi-stage training strategy, combining curriculum-based data composition and progressive parameter unfreezing, is crucial for effectively training models with hierarchical BPE visual tokens.
> - BPE tokenization enables a more unified multimodal knowledge space by balancing the activation magnitudes of visual and text token embeddings.
