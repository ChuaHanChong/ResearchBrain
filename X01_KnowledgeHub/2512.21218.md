# Latent Implicit Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Implicit Visual Reasoning (LIVR) enhances Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by enabling them to implicitly learn and utilize visual abstractions through dedicated latent tokens and a visual bottleneck mechanism. This approach consistently outperforms direct supervised fine-tuning by an average of 3.43% to 6.24% and surpasses methods relying on explicit visual supervision across various perception-heavy tasks, eliminating the need for costly intermediate annotations.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are predominantly text-centric, limiting their ability to perform complex, sophisticated visual reasoning tasks.
- Existing methods to enhance LMM visual reasoning rely on explicit supervision for intermediate visual steps, leading to high annotation costs, human biases, and poor generalization.
- LMMs often struggle to form rich, spatially structured visual abstractions akin to human 'mental imagery' without explicit verbalization.

## Method

- Augment LMMs with learnable, randomly initialized =="latent tokens"== that are appended to the input prompt, creating an additional visual compute space.
- Implement a novel ==visual bottlenecking mechanism== using an attention mask, forcing all visual information to flow exclusively through these latent tokens to reach the answer.
- Utilize a ==two-stage training process==: initially optimize latent tokens with the visual bottleneck, then fine-tune the model to jointly integrate original visual inputs with the enriched latent representations.

## Results

- LIVR consistently improved performance over Direct Supervised Fine-Tuning across three LMM backbones and nine perception-heavy tasks, achieving average gains ranging from **3.43%** to **6.24%**.
- The method demonstrated significant improvements on tasks requiring complex visual abstractions, such as Jigsaw (**+12%** on Qwen2.5-VL) and Functional Correspondence (**+27.40%** on LLaVA-OneVision).
- LIVR significantly outperformed Mirage, an approach using explicit visual supervision, achieving **68.00%** accuracy on Jigsaw versus Mirage's **48.60%** and **66.00%** versus **46.00%** on Visual Spatial Planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Multimodal Models can be trained to autonomously discover and utilize complex visual abstractions implicitly, without explicit human supervision for intermediate steps.
> - A dedicated visual bottleneck mechanism effectively compels latent tokens to encode task-relevant visual information by restricting direct attention to visual inputs.
> - Latent tokens provide a flexible, task-agnostic mechanism for LMMs to perform sophisticated visual reasoning, outperforming methods reliant on predefined visual intermediates.
