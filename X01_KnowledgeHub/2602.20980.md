# CrystaL: Spontaneous Emergence of Visual Latents in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> CrystaL is a self-supervised, single-stage framework for Multimodal Large Language Models that cultivates robust latent reasoning by enforcing consistency across dual processing paths, leading to the spontaneous emergence of task-relevant visual semantics. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on perception-intensive benchmarks with a significant reduction in training data.

## Problem

- Existing latent Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods for MLLMs suffer from insufficient supervision signals for intermediate latent states, hindering genuine visual reasoning.
- Prior approaches often depend on external modules, auxiliary human annotations, or complex multi-stage training, increasing complexity and cost.
- The reliance on indirect supervision can lead to MLLMs performing superficial reasoning or struggling with fine-grained visual information in complex perception tasks.

## Method

- A single-stage, self-supervised framework employs a ==dual-path architecture== to process high-fidelity and stochastically corrupted visual inputs concurrently.
- ==Visual latent tokens== from the intact path are copied to the corrupted path, creating an ==information bottleneck== that compels these latents to encode essential visual semantics.
- Alignment objectives, including predictive distribution consistency and mechanistic consistency, enforce similarity between the two paths' outputs and internal reasoning patterns.

## Results

- CrystaL achieved a state-of-the-art average score of **75.4%** across diverse perception-intensive benchmarks, surpassing strong baselines.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced performance in high-resolution perception, improving scores on HRBench 4K by **4.8%** and HRBench 8K by **6.2%** over Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- It exhibited high data efficiency, achieving top accuracy with only **16,000** training samples, a more than **6x** reduction compared to some previous methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised consistency training can compel MLLMs to spontaneously distill task-relevant visual semantics into their internal latent representations.
> - Stochastic image corruption acts as an effective information bottleneck, forcing the model to rely on visual latent tokens and ensuring their functional indispensability.
> - Aligning both the output predictive distributions and the internal attention mechanisms provides strong supervisory signals for effective latent visual reasoning.
