# In value-based deep reinforcement learning, a pruned network is a good network

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind, Mila, and Université de Montréal demonstrate that gradual magnitude pruning dramatically enhances deep reinforcement learning agent performance, achieving up to 60% improvement on Atari, and enables robust scaling of network architectures by improving learning stability and plasticity.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning networks often struggle with training instabilities, parameter under-utilization, and diminishing returns or degradation as network size increases.
- Existing deep RL networks commonly suffer from pathologies like dormant neurons, loss of plasticity, and a tendency to overfit early experiences.
- Scaling neural network parameters in deep RL does not reliably lead to performance gains, contrasting with 'scaling laws' observed in supervised learning.

## Method

- The authors systematically applied ==gradual magnitude pruning (GMP)==, a technique that progressively removes low-magnitude parameters during training, to various deep RL agents.
- They investigated GMP across diverse value-based agents (==DQN, Rainbow, IQN==), offline RL settings (CQL), and actor-critic methods (SAC, PPO), primarily using an Impala-based ResNet architecture.
- Experiments were conducted on the Arcade Learning Environment, Atari 100k, and MuJoCo, varying ==network widths, sparsity levels==, and other hyperparameters to assess generality and scalability.

## Results

- Gradual magnitude pruning achieved up to a **60%** performance improvement for DQN and **50%** for Rainbow over dense baselines on Atari, consistently using only **5%** of the original network parameters.
- Pruned networks exhibited monotonically increasing performance with increased network width, effectively mitigating the performance degradation typically observed in larger dense RL models.
- The technique consistently outperformed alternative regularization methods, plasticity injection techniques, and other sparse training approaches like RigL across various RL algorithms and regimes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradual magnitude pruning acts as a potent regularization mechanism, reducing Q-value variance, average parameter norms, and the fraction of dormant neurons within the network.
> - Pruning enhances network plasticity by increasing the effective rank of network parameters and significantly reducing gradient correlation and interference during training.
> - The benefits of pruning extend beyond simple compression, actively shaping learning dynamics to enable robust performance scaling with larger initial network architectures.
