# Reinforcing Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models with Interwoven Thinking and Visual Drawing

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces VILASR, a Vision-Language Model capable of spatial reasoning by generating and interpreting visual drawing operations directly within its reasoning process. The model, developed by researchers at Institute of Automation, CAS, Ant Group, CUHK MMLab, and Nanjing University, consistently outperforms existing methods by an average of 18.4% across various spatial reasoning benchmarks, including maze navigation and video analysis.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) exhibit limited spatial reasoning capabilities, often relying on text-centric processing that loses fine-grained visual details.
- Existing approaches that integrate external visual tools for LVLMs suffer from black-box limitations, offering fixed perception capabilities and leading to fragmented reasoning.
- LVLMs lack intrinsic visual manipulation abilities, hindering their capacity for iterative refinement and dynamic interaction with visual information during reasoning.

## Method

- A novel =="drawing to reason in space" paradigm== enables LVLMs to decompose complex spatial tasks into interleaved steps of natural language reasoning and visual drawing operations (bounding boxes and auxiliary lines).
- The model utilizes two fundamental drawing operations: ==`T_box` for localizing objects with bounding boxes== and ==`T_line` for visualizing spatial relationships and trajectories with auxiliary lines==.
- A ==three-stage training framework== progressively builds capabilities: cold-start training with synthetic data, reflective rejection sampling to cultivate self-correction, and reinforcement learning (GRPO) to optimize for accuracy and efficient visual thinking using a rule-based reward function.

## Results

- VILASR consistently outperforms existing methods across **five diverse spatial reasoning benchmarks**, achieving an average improvement of **18.4%** over strong baselines.
- The model achieves **98.2%** on MAZE navigation and state-of-the-art results on video and multi-view spatial reasoning benchmarks like VSI-Bench (**45.4%**), SPAR-Bench (**37.6%**), and MMSI-Bench (**30.2%**).
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of each training stage: cold-start for foundational drawing, reflective rejection sampling for self-correction (reducing reflection patterns by **96.5%** without it), and reinforcement learning for efficiency and precise numerical reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct visual manipulation via elementary drawing operations allows Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to reason about fine-grained spatial details and dynamic changes more effectively than text-centric methods.
> - A progressive three-stage training framework, incorporating supervised fine-tuning, reflective rejection sampling, and reinforcement learning, is essential for cultivating robust, self-correcting, and efficient spatial reasoning.
> - Empowering LVLMs with an intrinsic visual workspace enables them to express and analyze spatial relationships more naturally, drawing parallels to human cognitive processes.
