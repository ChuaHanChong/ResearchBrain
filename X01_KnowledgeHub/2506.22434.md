# MiCo: Multi-image Contrast for Reinforcement Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> MiCo (Multi-image Contrast) introduces a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to enhance their Chain-of-Thought reasoning across multiple images without relying on human-annotated reasoning steps. The framework leverages inherent visual constraints from video frames and image editing datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance on multi-image reasoning benchmarks like VLM2-Bench and generalizing effectively to a range of other vision tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle to robustly link fine-grained visual cues and perform complex logical reasoning when presented with multiple related images.
- Existing methods for improving VLM reasoning, especially those using reinforcement learning, heavily depend on prohibitively expensive and time-consuming human-annotated question-answer pairs with ground-truth reasoning steps.
- Current VLMs often lack the "meta-cognitive" ability to effectively compare and connect visual information across different views, leading to failures or hallucinations in multi-image tasks.

## Method

- ==MiCo== develops a ==self-supervised framework== that constructs challenging visual comparison tasks by selecting visually similar but meaningfully different image pairs from video frames and image editing datasets.
- It formulates training triplets using augmented views of these image pairs, implicitly supervising the VLM to identify subtle differences and similarities.
- An ==Augmented Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization (GRPO) strategy== is employed, where reasoning trajectories sampled from weakly augmented inputs are used to optimize the VLM's policy for stronger augmentations, improving generalization.

## Results

- MiCo-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VLM2-Bench benchmark with an average accuracy of **61.06%**, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by **+12.93%** and surpassing larger models like GPT-4o in multi-image comparison.
- The framework demonstrated strong performance on other multi-image benchmarks (MuirBench, BLINK) in correspondence and spatial relation tasks, and showed steady improvements across general vision benchmarks (MMStar, MMMU, HallusionBench, MathVista).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical roles of CoT reinforcement learning, diverse data sources, the Augmented GRPO strategy, and varied prompt templates in achieving robust and generalized multi-image reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning, leveraging inherent visual constraints, can effectively instill complex multi-image Chain-of-Thought reasoning capabilities in VLMs without the need for explicit human labels.
> - The Augmented GRPO strategy successfully transfers high-quality CoT reasoning behaviors learned from simpler visual conditions (weak augmentations) to more challenging ones (strong augmentations), significantly improving model generalization.
> - Training VLMs on visual comparison skills through contrastive learning not only enhances multi-image reasoning but also generalizes to a broader range of general vision tasks, indicating the acquisition of a fundamental "meta-cognitive" visual skill.
