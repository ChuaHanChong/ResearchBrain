# Program of Thoughts Prompting: Disentangling Computation from Reasoning for Numerical Reasoning Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Program of Thoughts (PoT) prompting enables Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance their numerical reasoning capabilities by generating executable programs (e.g., Python code) for computation, which are then processed by an external interpreter. This method consistently improves performance on mathematical word problems and financial question-answering tasks, yielding gains of approximately 8% on MWP datasets and 15% on financial datasets in few-shot settings compared to Chain-of-Thought.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are prone to arithmetic errors and struggle with complex mathematical expressions when performing calculations directly within their natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT).
- CoT prompting is inefficient and error-prone for numerical problems involving repetitive calculations or complex iterations.
- The inherent computational weaknesses of LLMs limit their reliability and accuracy in quantitative reasoning tasks, even when their high-level reasoning is conceptually sound.

## Method

- The method prompts Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate ==formal programs (e.g., Python code)== that encapsulate the reasoning steps for numerical problems.
- An ==external program interpreter== (e.g., Python 3.8 with SymPy) executes the generated code to perform all calculations and obtain the final answer.
- This approach allows LLMs to leverage their strengths in language understanding while ==offloading precise computation to a deterministic system==.

## Results

- PoT consistently outperformed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, showing an average accuracy gain of approximately **8%** on Math Word Problem (MWP) datasets and **15%** on financial datasets in few-shot settings.
- Zero-shot PoT often surpassed the performance of few-shot CoT, highlighting the method's strong generalization capabilities without needing task-specific examples.
- When combined with Self-Consistency (PoT+SC), the method achieved the best-known results on all evaluated MWP datasets (pre-GPT-4), and `gpt-3.5-turbo` emerged as a top-performing LLM backend for PoT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling reasoning (handled by the LLM) from precise computation (delegated to an external interpreter) is critical for achieving robust numerical problem-solving with LLMs.
> - Prompting LLMs to generate executable code, rather than natural language rationales, enables them to leverage external, exact computational tools effectively.
> - Using semantically meaningful variable names and encouraging multi-step program generation significantly aids LLMs in structuring their reasoning for complex numerical problems.
