# Evolving Language Models without Labels: Majority Drives Selection, Novelty Promotes Variation

> [!summary] Summary
> EVOL-RL introduces an evolutionary, label-free reinforcement learning framework for large language models, mitigating the prevalent 'entropy collapse' by balancing majority-driven selection with novelty-promoting variation. The Tencent AI Lab and University of Notre Dame collaboration achieves over 20 percentage points improvement in pass@16 accuracy on challenging benchmarks and robust out-of-domain generalization.

## Problem

- Existing label-free LLM self-improvement methods, like Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL), suffer from 'entropy collapse' due to over-reliance on self-confirmation signals, suppressing solution diversity.
- This leads to degraded `pass@n` performance, shorter reasoning chains, and a loss of exploratory capability, hindering long-term autonomous learning.
- The challenge is to enable LLMs to continuously self-improve without ground-truth labels while maintaining robust diversity and avoiding premature convergence.

## Method

- EVOL-RL proposes a framework based on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), guided by a novel ==three-part reward function== inspired by biological evolution.
- The reward function incorporates validity checks, ==majority-based selection== for correctness, and a ==semantically-aware novelty incentive== for reasoning paths, with separate normalization for majority and minority groups.
- Supporting mechanisms include ==asymmetric clipping== in GRPO and a ==token-level entropy regularizer== to further promote diversity and preserve beneficial 'mutation' signals.

## Results

- EVOL-RL consistently and substantially improves `pass@16` performance over TTRL, with gains often exceeding **20** percentage points on challenging benchmarks like AIME24, and enhances `pass@1` accuracy.
- The method demonstrates strong cross-difficulty robustness and out-of-domain generalization, transferring learned reasoning skills effectively across mathematical and non-mathematical tasks (e.g., GPQA).
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the novelty reward and exploration mechanisms in fostering long-term performance and maintaining diversity, even providing synergistic benefits to supervised RLVR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Label-free self-improvement requires explicitly balancing 'selection' (reinforcing majority consensus) with 'variation' (promoting novel reasoning paths) to prevent degenerative feedback loops.
> - The novelty reward creates an inherently unstable state for uniform solution populations, continuously driving the policy away from homogeneity and promoting diverse reasoning.
> - Entropy collapse observed in majority-only methods is not permanent; EVOL-RL demonstrates a distinct 'evolving point' after which policy entropy and reasoning length recover, leading to sustained performance gains.
