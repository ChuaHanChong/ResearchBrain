# A Spatially-Aware Multiple Instance Learning Framework for Digital Pathology

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Eindhoven University of Technology introduce Global ABMIL (GABMIL), an extension of Attention-Based Deep Multiple Instance Learning, which enhances whole slide image classification by integrating spatial information into patch features using computationally efficient MLP-based modules. The framework demonstrates improved accuracy for tumor subtyping while maintaining ABMIL's efficiency, often matching or surpassing the performance of more complex Transformer-based models.

## Problem

- Traditional Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) methods for Whole Slide Image (WSI) analysis often overlook the spatial relationships between tissue patches, missing crucial diagnostic context pathologists rely on.
- Existing spatially-aware MIL approaches, such as Transformer-based models, incur high computational costs due to their self-attention mechanisms, limiting their practical applicability to gigapixel WSIs.
- It was unclear if an MLP-based MIL framework could effectively integrate spatial context to improve performance without a complete architectural redesign or prohibitive computational overhead.

## Method

- The proposed ==Global ABMIL (GABMIL)== framework enhances the standard ABMIL by incorporating a ==Spatial Information Mixing Module (SIMM)== to enrich patch feature embeddings with spatial context.
- SIMM explicitly reconfigures 1D patch features into their original 2D spatial grid and applies ==MLP-based BLOCK and GRID attention modules== to capture both local and global spatial dependencies.
- Spatially contextualized patch features are then fed into the conventional ABMIL attention mechanism for weighted aggregation and subsequent slide-level classification.

## Results

- GABMIL consistently improved classification performance across TCGA Breast Invasive Carcinoma and Lung Cancer datasets, achieving up to a **2 percentage point** increase in AUC and a **7 percentage point** increase in AUPRC on BRCA over baseline ABMIL.
- The framework maintained computational efficiency, demonstrating similar FLOPs (**approximately 94M**) to ABMIL, which is substantially lower than TransMIL (**614M** FLOPs), while achieving comparable or superior accuracy.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed that the SIMM effectively refines slide-level feature maps by integrating spatial information, leading to more discriminative representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling spatial interactions among patches is vital for enhancing the accuracy of weakly supervised WSI classification in digital pathology.
> - Computationally efficient MLP-based mechanisms, inspired by architectures like MaxViT and MLP-Mixer, can effectively capture these spatial dependencies without relying on the higher computational burden of Transformer's self-attention.
> - The BLOCK attention module, which captures local spatial relationships within defined windows, proved particularly effective and robust in refining patch representations across different datasets and feature types.
