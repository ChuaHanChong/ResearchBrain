# RLPR: Extrapolating RLVR to General Domains without Verifiers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and NUS developed RLPR, a verifier-free reinforcement learning framework that enhances Large Language Model reasoning across general domains by using an intrinsic probability-based reward. This method achieved an average 24.9% improvement on general-domain benchmarks and consistently outperformed existing RLVR and concurrent verifier-free approaches by removing the need for external verification.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs heavily rely on domain-specific external verifiers.
- Handcrafting rule-based verifiers is laborious, complex, and leads to brittle systems, while training LLM-based verifiers introduces high annotation costs and complexity.
- Applying RLVR to general, open-ended natural language reasoning is difficult due to the inability to devise general-purpose verifiers, limiting scalability.

## Method

- ==RLPR== introduces a =='Probability-based Reward' (PR)== that uses the mean of an LLM's per-token decoding probabilities for a ground-truth reference answer as a reward signal.
- A =='Reward Debiasing'== mechanism is employed to calculate the reward as the improvement in probability gained by generating reasoning, by subtracting a base score obtained without intermediate reasoning.
- A =='Standard Deviation Filtering'== approach dynamically filters out training prompts with low reward standard deviation, focusing learning on more informative samples and stabilizing training.

## Results

- RLPR achieved an average **24.9%** improvement on four general-domain benchmarks using Qwen2.5-7B, demonstrating its ability to extend RL to broader reasoning tasks.
- The framework consistently outperformed traditional RLVR baselines and specialized model-based verifiers, for example, surpassing General Reasoner-7B by **1.6 average points** across seven benchmarks.
- Quantitative analysis showed the Probability-based Reward (PR) achieved high discrimination (e.g., **0.95** ROC-AUC on math data), significantly outperforming rule-based verifiers (**0.61** on general data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LLM's intrinsic probability of generating a correct free-form answer can serve as an internal evaluation of reasoning reward.
> - The mean of per-token probabilities for a reference answer is a more robust reward signal than naive sequence likelihood, which is highly sensitive to minor variations.
> - Debiasing the reward by subtracting a base score (probability without reasoning) isolates the contribution of the generated reasoning process.
