# SHIELD: Suppressing Hallucinations In LVLM Encoders via Bias and Vulnerability Defense

> [!summary] Summary
> SHIELD introduces a training-free framework that effectively mitigates object hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by targeting specific issues within their visual encoders, including statistical bias, inherent bias, and vulnerability. The method achieves substantial reductions in hallucination metrics, such as an 18% improvement over previous training-free approaches on CHAIR benchmarks, while also enhancing general perception capabilities across various LVLM architectures.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate object hallucinations, producing plausible but factually inaccurate descriptions of objects, which compromises their reliability and safety in critical applications.
- Current hallucination mitigation strategies primarily focus on the Large Language Model (LLM) component of LVLMs or require computationally expensive retraining/fine-tuning.
- The direct contributions of the visual encoder—the component responsible for processing image information—to the problem of object hallucination in LVLMs remained largely unaddressed.

## Method

- ==SHIELD== proposes a training-free framework that intervenes on visual tokens extracted by the encoder and adjusts the decoding process to suppress hallucinations.
- It implements ==Token Re-weighting== to mitigate statistical bias by redistributing attention across object-relevant visual tokens, leveraging a similarity matrix derived from a naive image caption.
- The framework incorporates ==Token Subtraction== to reduce inherent bias by removing noise-derived erroneous features, and utilizes ==Contrastive Decoding== with adversarial attacks and an adaptive plausibility constraint to address visual encoder vulnerability and suppress hallucination-prone tokens.

## Results

- SHIELD significantly outperformed existing training-free methods, achieving an **18%** improvement over the second-best method (OPERA) in reducing sentence-level hallucinations on CHAIR benchmarks for LLaVA-1.5.
- Consistent and substantial reductions in object, attribute, and relational hallucinations were observed across multiple LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) and various benchmarks, including GPT4o-aided LLaVA-Bench and POPE.
- The framework also improved general perception capabilities, showing gains in subtasks like OCR and Posters on the MME benchmark, indicating that addressing visual encoder issues enhances overall visual understanding beyond just hallucination suppression.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object hallucinations in LVLMs are directly linked to specific issues within their visual encoders: statistical bias (overemphasis on frequent patterns), inherent bias (generating dominant objects regardless of input), and vulnerability (sensitivity to minor input perturbations).
> - Targeting these visual encoder-specific biases and vulnerabilities at the feature processing level can effectively reduce hallucinations without requiring modifications to the core model parameters.
> - Interventions like re-weighting visual tokens based on textual context and subtracting bias-induced features, combined with contrastive decoding, improve factual consistency and overall visual understanding in LVLMs.
