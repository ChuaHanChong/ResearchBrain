# Transformers in Vision: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive overview of Transformer models in computer vision, systematically organizing the rapidly expanding literature by architectural design and task applications. It synthesizes findings on how these models, originating from NLP, have been successfully adapted to achieve competitive performance across various vision tasks, including classification, detection, and segmentation.

## Problem

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), while dominant, often struggle with capturing long-range dependencies and global context in visual data.
- The rapidly growing body of research on applying Transformer models (successful in NLP) to computer vision lacked a dedicated, comprehensive, and structured review.
- A clear resource was needed to understand the motivations, architectural adaptations, and performance of Transformers across diverse vision tasks.

## Method

- Introduces foundational Transformer concepts, including ==self-attention== and supervised/self-supervised pre-training mechanisms.
- Develops a comprehensive ==taxonomy== to categorize vision Transformer models based on their architectural designs (e.g., uniform-scale, multi-scale, hybrid, self-supervised).
- Systematically reviews the application of these models across a wide array of computer vision tasks, highlighting task-specific adaptations and performance.

## Results

- Transformer models demonstrate strengths in vision including enhanced long-range dependency modeling, parallel processing capabilities, and scalability to large datasets.
- Many successful Vision Transformers integrate with CNNs, combining CNN's inductive biases for local features with Transformer's global context modeling.
- Transformer-based approaches have shown competitive performance in diverse tasks such as object detection (e.g., DETR), image generation (e.g., DALL·E), multi-modal tasks (e.g., CLIP), and 3D analysis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers successfully transitioned from NLP to computer vision, achieving competitive performance across high-level and low-level tasks due to their ability to model long-range dependencies and inherent parallelism.
> - Architectural adaptations like patch-based inputs, hierarchical designs, and hybrid CNN-Transformer models have been crucial for integrating Transformers into vision and addressing their computational complexity.
> - Large-scale pre-training, especially self-supervised learning, is fundamental for Vision Transformers to learn robust and generalizable representations, often leading to emergent properties.
