# Beyond the Trade-off: Self-Supervised Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Models' Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Ant Group developed a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework to enhance large language models' instruction following without external model dependency. The approach improves instruction adherence by up to 10 points on benchmarks like IFEval, while maintaining or improving core reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Large language models often exhibit a trade-off between complex reasoning abilities and consistent adherence to intricate, multi-faceted instructions, which hinders their real-world utility.
- Existing methods for enhancing instruction following, such as distillation or reinforcement learning, typically rely on stronger external models for supervision, creating limitations in scalability, cost, and accessibility.
- Applying reinforcement learning to complex instruction following tasks is challenged by sparse reward signals and the difficulty in effectively modeling rewards for diverse, especially semantic 'soft', constraints.

## Method

- A ==self-supervised reinforcement learning framework== is proposed that leverages the reasoning model's own internal signals to improve instruction following, thereby eliminating the reliance on external models or human annotations.
- An ==incremental constraint curriculum== is employed to decompose complex multi-constraint instructions into progressive sub-instructions, generating dense and effective reward signals during training.
- A ==dual reward modeling approach== combines rule-based rewards for hard, verifiable constraints with a self-supervised, binary classification reward model (trained on automatically generated data) for nuanced soft constraints.

## Results

- Instruction following capabilities were significantly improved, with models like Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-IF achieving **83.9** on IFEval compared to **73.9** for the base model, and R1-Distill-Qwen-7B-IF scoring **71.7** versus **61.7**.
- The framework maintained or improved core reasoning performance on general capability benchmarks such as GPQA, Diamond, BBEH, FOLIO, and MMLU-Pro, successfully resolving the identified trade-off.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficiency and effectiveness of the self-supervised reward model (Kendall Tau **61.2** with human judgments, inference speed **0.3s/group**) and validated the necessity of the incremental constraint curriculum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal model signals, when appropriately structured and utilized via self-supervision, can effectively enhance instruction following capabilities without external model dependency or sacrificing core reasoning performance.
> - An incremental constraint curriculum is crucial for overcoming sparse reward problems in reinforcement learning for complex tasks, enabling more efficient and stable policy optimization.
> - Integrating instruction-following reasoning data during a 'cold-start' training phase can instill systematic thinking patterns, leading to more robust and consistently compliant responses from the outset.
