# Mixture-of-Recursions: Learning Dynamic Recursive Depths for Adaptive Token-Level Computation

> [!summary] Summary
> Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR) introduces a unified framework for language models that combines parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching. It achieves strong performance, outperforming vanilla Transformers with nearly 50% fewer parameters and reducing training FLOPs by 25%, while increasing inference throughput by up to 2.06x.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) face high computational and memory costs during training and deployment, hindering accessibility and sustainability.
- Existing approaches to efficiency, such as parameter sharing and adaptive computation, often operate in isolation or introduce practical hurdles like complex training procedures.
- Traditional Recursive Transformers use fixed recursion depths for all tokens, ignoring varying computational needs, while KV caches consume vast GPU memory during inference.

## Method

- ==Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR)== integrates ==dynamic, token-level computation== within a parameter-shared architecture, leveraging the =="Middle-Cycle" strategy== for efficient weight reuse.
- Lightweight routers, either ==Expert-choice== (selecting top-k tokens for continued processing) or ==Token-choice== (assigning fixed recursion depth per token), enable adaptive computational allocation.
- Two KV caching strategies, Recursion-wise (selective storage) and Recursive KV Sharing (single computation and reuse), optimize memory usage during inference.

## Results

- MoR models achieve lower validation NLL (**2.7511**) and higher average few-shot accuracy (**43.1%**) compared to Vanilla Transformers (NLL **2.7824**, accuracy **42.3%**), utilizing nearly **50%** fewer parameters under equivalent training FLOPs.
- Processing the same amount of data (**20B** tokens), MoR reduces training FLOPs by **25%**, leading to a **19%** reduction in training time and **25%** less peak memory usage.
- Inference throughput is boosted by up to **2.06x** over vanilla baselines when MoR is combined with continuous depth-wise batching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unification of parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching within a single framework establishes a new Pareto frontier for LLM efficiency.
> - Dynamic, token-level routing allows models to adaptively allocate computational resources, enabling deeper "thinking" on complex tokens while optimizing overall compute.
> - MoR exhibits compute-optimal scaling behavior that prioritizes increases in model capacity (parameter count) over extending training length under isoFLOPs constraints.
