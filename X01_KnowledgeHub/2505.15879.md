# GRIT: Teaching MLLMs to Think with Images

> [!summary] Summary
> GRIT enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) from UC Santa Cruz and eBay to generate visually grounded reasoning chains by interleaving natural language with explicit bounding box coordinates. This method teaches MLLMs to "think with images" using a lightweight reinforcement learning approach on a dataset of only 20 image-question-answer triplets, achieving strong performance on various visual reasoning tasks without dense human annotations.

## Problem

- Existing open-source visual reasoning models primarily generate reasoning content using pure natural language, often lacking explicit integration of visual information, making their reasoning opaque and difficult to verify.
- MLLMs face technical challenges in directly generating visual elements like bounding box coordinates within a textual reasoning chain and maintaining context across sequences interleaving numerous visual elements.
- Obtaining human-annotated data that explicitly incorporates visual evidence in each reasoning step is exceptionally scarce and expensive, hindering the training of truly visually grounded models.

## Method

- Introduces a ==grounded reasoning paradigm== where MLLMs generate reasoning chains that seamlessly interleave natural language text with explicit bounding box coordinates, acting as pointers to specific image regions.
- Employs ==GRPO-GR==, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, to train MLLMs to adhere to this paradigm, utilizing a ==composite reward system== that incentivizes correct answers and proper formatting of interleaved text and bounding boxes.
- Achieves high ==data efficiency== by training models with as few as 20 unique image-query-answer triplets from existing datasets, eliminating the need for expensive human annotations of intermediate reasoning steps or bounding box labels.

## Results

- GRIT-trained models consistently outperformed all baselines in both GPT-as-judge answer accuracy and grounding IoU across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain visual reasoning datasets (e.g., VSR, TallyQA, GQA, MathVista-mini, MME), despite training on only **20** image-query-answer triplets.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated GRIT models exhibit flexible reasoning strategies, dynamically grounding critical image regions when necessary and correctly avoiding groundings for non-existent entities.
- GRIT models achieved significantly higher Vision-Language Reasoning Cross-Modal Correlation, indicating strong semantic alignment between generated text and image regions, and analysis showed generated bounding boxes actively increased visual attention in subsequent reasoning steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO-GR, effectively teaches Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to unify visual grounding and textual reasoning into a single generative process without requiring dense, step-by-step annotations.
> - The act of generating bounding boxes actively enhances the model's attention to relevant visual input in subsequent reasoning segments, leading to more visually consistent and coherent thought processes.
> - Interleaved visual-textual reasoning, where models 'think with images' by dynamically mixing bounding boxes and natural language, can be achieved in an open-source MLLM via lightweight RL, democratizing a capability previously seen in proprietary systems.
