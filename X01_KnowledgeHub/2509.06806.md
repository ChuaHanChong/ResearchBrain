# MachineLearningLM: Scaling Many-shot In-context Learning via Continued Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> A continued pretraining framework, MACHINELEARNINGLM, enhances a general-purpose LLM with robust many-shot in-context learning capabilities for tabular data, achieving monotonic accuracy scaling and performance comparable to Random Forest. The approach maintains the LLM's general knowledge while outperforming vanilla LLMs by 12-16% in high-shot tabular tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with effective many-shot in-context learning (ICL) for numerical tabular data, showing quick accuracy plateaus and sensitivity to example order.
- Specialized tabular models (e.g., TabPFN) excel at tabular ICL but lack the broad world knowledge, general reasoning, and multimodal perception inherent in LLMs.
- A gap exists in creating a unified model that combines the robust numerical modeling of specialized methods with the versatility and general intelligence of LLMs without performance degradation.

## Method

- A ==continued pretraining framework== (MACHINELEARNINGLM) is applied to a general-purpose LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) using LoRA, framing the task as conditional sequence modeling.
- Millions of diverse, synthetic binary/multiclass tabular prediction tasks are generated from ==Structural Causal Models (SCMs)== for pretraining, ensuring out-of-distribution generalization.
- ==Token-efficient prompting strategies==, including tabular encoding, compact integer representation, and sequence-level batch prediction, maximize the number of in-context examples within the context window.

## Results

- MACHINELEARNINGLM exhibits a striking monotonic many-shot scaling law, with accuracy increasing consistently from **8** to **1,024** in-context demonstrations, outperforming vanilla LLMs (e.g., GPT-5-mini by ~**12%**, o3-mini by ~**16%**) in high-shot settings.
- The model achieves accuracy levels comparable to Random Forest (within **2%** relative) and surpasses instance-based learners (kNN by >**4%** relative) on diverse tabular tasks, without task-specific fine-tuning.
- Continued pretraining successfully preserves the LLM's general knowledge and reasoning abilities, demonstrating comparable performance on the MMLU benchmark (e.g., **73.2%** 0-shot, **75.4%** 50-shot).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse synthetic tasks derived from SCMs effectively instills tree-inductive biases and robust numerical modeling capabilities into an LLM.
> - Token-efficient encoding and batch prediction significantly overcome LLM context length limitations, allowing an order of magnitude more demonstrations and improved throughput.
> - A Random Forest mimic warm-start stabilizes training, and confidence-aware, order-robust self-consistency at inference time improves prediction accuracy and mitigates position bias.
