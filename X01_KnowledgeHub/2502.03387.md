# LIMO: Less is More for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The LIMO (Less is More for Reasoning) research from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and SII-GAIR develops a supervised fine-tuning method for large language models, demonstrating that advanced mathematical reasoning can be achieved with a minimal dataset of 800 carefully curated examples. This approach yields an average performance of 78.1% across various benchmarks, outperforming models like OpenAI-o1-preview (61.1%) and QwQ-32B-Preview (66.9%) that were fine-tuned on significantly larger datasets.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption that complex reasoning in large language models necessitates vast quantities of training data.
- High computational costs and resource demands associated with collecting and training on massive datasets.
- Limited generalization and potential memorization in models trained on large, uncurated reasoning datasets.

## Method

- A ==data-efficient supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== approach, named LIMO, which utilizes a minimal dataset.
- A highly curated dataset of **800** mathematical reasoning problems, constructed through ==multi-stage filtering== for question difficulty and detailed ==rule-based scoring== for reasoning chain quality.
- The training process enables the base model to integrate ==meta-reasoning tasks== into cohesive chains, leveraging its pre-trained knowledge.

## Results

- LIMO achieved an average performance of **78.1%** across all benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art LLMs like OpenAI-o1-preview (**61.1%**) and QwQ-32B-Preview (**66.9%**).
- The model exhibited strong out-of-distribution generalization, successfully tackling diverse mathematical and multilingual benchmarks, including novel Chinese-language exams.
- Ablation studies confirmed that high-quality reasoning chains and training on challenging problems significantly enhance performance, with diminishing returns observed for data beyond **800** samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning in LLMs can emerge from minimal, high-quality data if foundation models possess comprehensive pre-trained domain knowledge.
> - The quality of reasoning chains and the difficulty of training problems are critical factors in fostering robust and generalized reasoning abilities.
> - A rich pre-trained knowledge base in the foundation model is essential for efficient elicitation of reasoning capabilities through fine-tuning.
