# Revisiting Reinforcement Learning for LLM Reasoning from A Cross-Domain Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces GURU, a multi-domain reinforcement learning corpus with 92K verifiable examples across six distinct reasoning domains, enabling the training of GURU-7B and GURU-32B models. These models achieve state-of-the-art general reasoning capabilities among open RL-trained LLMs, demonstrating how multi-domain RL can lead to robust, generalized reasoning abilities.

## Problem

- Current reinforcement learning (RL) for large language model (LLM) reasoning predominantly focuses on mathematics and programming code, limiting the generalizability of understanding how RL improves reasoning and hindering the development of versatile models.
- A significant bottleneck exists due to the scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and verifiable datasets suitable for RL training across a broad spectrum of reasoning domains beyond math and code.
- Existing RL-trained LLMs tend to be highly specialized, often exhibiting performance degradation when applied to diverse reasoning tasks outside their specific training distribution.

## Method

- Introduced ==GURU==, a ==multi-domain reinforcement learning corpus== comprising **92K** verifiable examples across six diverse reasoning domains: Math, Code, Science, Logic, Simulation, and Tabular.
- Developed a meticulous ==five-stage data curation pipeline== for GURU, including data sourcing from multiple datasets, conservative deduplication, domain-tailored binary reward design, heuristic filtering, and a crucial difficulty filtering stage.
- Performed large-scale RL training experiments using the ==GRPO algorithm== on single-domain and mixed-domain subsets of GURU, leveraging Qwen2.5-7B-Base and Qwen2.5-32B-Base models, and systematically evaluated them on comprehensive benchmark suites.

## Results

- GURU-7B and GURU-32B models establish new state-of-the-art performance among open models RL-trained with publicly available data, achieving average scores of **43.29%** (**+9.0%** over best baselines) and **54.24%** (**+6.7%** over best baselines) respectively across a 17-task evaluation suite.
- Unlike domain-specific baselines, GURU models demonstrate superior and well-balanced performance across all evaluated domains (Math, Code, Science, Logic, Simulation, Tabular) and unseen generalization tasks.
- Pass@k analysis indicates task-dependent patterns where RL expands the reasoning boundary on complex, less pretraining-exposed tasks like the synthetic Zebra Puzzle, providing evidence of genuine skill acquisition, while on tasks like AIME (Math), it aligns with knowledge elicitation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL's impact on LLM reasoning is highly domain-dependent: it primarily elicits existing knowledge in domains frequently encountered during pretraining (Math, Code, Science) and facilitates genuine skill acquisition in less-exposed domains (Logic, Simulation, Tabular).
> - Training on a uniformly mixed, diverse dataset is remarkably effective, enabling models to achieve performance comparable to, and often surpassing, those trained exclusively on single-domain data, indicating enhanced general reasoning with minimal apparent interference.
> - Training on difficulty-filtered (harder) in-domain data consistently improves in-domain performance but can lead to degradation on easier cross-domain tasks, suggesting a risk of negative transfer if not carefully managed for beneficial cross-domain transfer.
