# GELLO: A General, Low-Cost, and Intuitive Teleoperation Framework for Robot Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed GELLO, a low-cost, intuitive teleoperation framework for robot manipulators that uses a scaled, 3D-printed kinematically equivalent controller. This system enables efficient collection of high-quality human demonstration data, achieving superior task success rates and faster completion times compared to other low-cost teleoperation methods.

## Problem

- Collecting large, high-quality human demonstration data is a bottleneck for training robust, data-driven robot learning policies, especially in imitation learning.
- Existing high-fidelity teleoperation systems are prohibitively expensive, limiting accessibility and scalability for widespread data collection.
- Low-cost teleoperation devices often abstract robot kinematics, leading to unintuitive control, increased operator errors (e.g., self-collisions), and lower quality demonstration data.

## Method

- **GELLO** introduces a low-cost teleoperation framework utilizing a scaled, 3D-printed, ==kinematically isomorphic== replica of the target robot arm as a controller.
- It employs inexpensive ==backdrivable servo motors== primarily for their integrated ==high-resolution encoder== functionality, alongside ==passive joint regularizers== to manage arm posture and provide subtle tactile feedback.
- The entire system, including hardware designs and software stack, is open-sourced with a bill of materials under $300 per device, ensuring ease of build and replication for various robot manipulators.

## Results

- GELLO achieved an average **92%** task success rate across **five** bi-manual manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming 3D mouses (**63%**) and VR controllers (**72%**).
- Human operators using GELLO completed tasks faster and experienced significantly fewer failures, including a notable reduction in self-collisions, compared to other low-cost teleoperation devices.
- The framework was successfully implemented and demonstrated on **three** different robot arms (UR5, xArm7, Franka Panda), enabling complex, contact-rich, and bi-manual manipulation tasks with a total bill of materials under **$300** per device.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A scaled, kinematically isomorphic controller built from economical components can provide highly intuitive and precise robot teleoperation, significantly reducing operator errors like self-collisions.
> - Inexpensive servo motors can effectively function as high-resolution encoders, offering a cost-effective alternative to expensive haptic devices or full robot-to-robot teleoperation.
> - Simple passive joint regularization, using components like springs, can enhance user experience by providing natural damping and maintaining arm posture without requiring complex active force feedback systems.
