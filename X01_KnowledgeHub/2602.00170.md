# The Blessing of Dimensionality in LLM Fine-tuning: A Variance-Curvature Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> This research analyzes why Evolution Strategies (ES) effectively fine-tune large language models (LLMs) with small populations and why training reward often peaks and then declines. It attributes these phenomena to a "blessing of dimensionality," where LLM fine-tuning landscapes possess low-dimensional, heterogeneous curvature dominated by a few "stiff" directions that govern early learning, while fixed noise accumulating along numerous "flat" directions causes the observed reward degradation. The study demonstrates that the population size needed for ES to find improving perturbations does not increase with model scale, providing a new understanding of high-dimensional optimization.

## Problem

- Evolution Strategies (ES) effectively fine-tune billion-parameter LLMs with surprisingly small populations, contradicting classical "curse of dimensionality" intuitions.
- Training reward consistently exhibits a "rise-then-decay" pattern during stochastic LLM fine-tuning under fixed hyperparameters.
- There is a lack of a unified theoretical framework to explain both the scalability of ES and the non-monotonic training dynamics in LLM fine-tuning.

## Method

- Propose a =="geometry–variance perspective"== where LLM fine-tuning landscapes are low-dimensional in curvature, characterized by a few =='stiff' and many 'flat' directions==.
- Empirically probe LLM fine-tuning landscapes using ==Evolution Strategies (ES)== to measure the accessibility of reward-improving perturbations across various model scales.
- Develop a ==minimal quadratic stochastic-ascent model== to analytically demonstrate how heterogeneous curvature interacting with fixed stochasticity leads to the observed rise-then-decay behavior.

## Results

- Training reward consistently exhibits a "rise-then-decay" pattern in both ES and GRPO fine-tuning of LLMs, which is modulated by algorithmic stochasticity (e.g., population size).
- Empirical "best-of-N" analysis shows that the population size required to find an improving perturbation (N ≈ **30-40**) does not systematically increase as LLM parameter count scales from **0.5B** to **7B**, challenging the curse-of-dimensionality.
- A minimal quadratic stochastic-ascent model successfully reproduces the rise-then-decay behavior, demonstrating that heterogeneous curvature interacting with fixed noise is sufficient to cause this phenomenon.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM fine-tuning landscapes benefit from a "blessing of dimensionality," meaning improvement is primarily governed by a small, stable set of 'curvature-active' (stiff) directions that do not scale with total parameter count.
> - The surprising scalability of small-population ES for high-dimensional LLMs is due to 'degeneracy,' where many random perturbations can effectively contribute to progress within this low-dimensional, curvature-active subspace.
> - The 'rise-then-decay' training reward stems from heterogeneous time scales: rapid progress along stiff directions quickly saturates, while slower, persistent stochastic drift along numerous 'flat' directions leads to variance accumulation and performance degradation.
