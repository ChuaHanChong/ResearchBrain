# Analyzing Vision Transformers for Image Classification in Class Embedding Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Goethe University Frankfurt developed a framework for Vision Transformers (ViTs) that projects internal representations onto a learned class embedding space, revealing how class-specific information is built across layers. This method provides an efficient and task-relevant tool for mechanistic interpretability and explainability, offering insights into the distinct contributions of self-attention and MLP layers.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks, particularly Vision Transformers, often operate as 'black boxes', making it challenging to mechanistically understand why they make specific predictions.
- Existing interpretability methods for ViTs, such as linear probing, suffer from limitations in computational efficiency and direct relevance to the model's actual decision-making process.
- The internal representational dynamics of ViTs are less understood compared to traditional CNNs or NLP transformers, hindering trust, debugging, and targeted improvements.

## Method

- The framework projects hidden states of image and `[CLS]` tokens, as well as parameter matrices (e.g., MLP `W_out`), onto the learned ==class embedding space== to quantify ==class identifiability==.
- It utilizes a ==gradient-based== method to generate fine-grained, block- and attention-head-specific feature importance maps, identifying image regions critical for class representation.
- The approach draws on NLP interpretability by treating transformer layers as ==key-value memory systems==, analyzing how self-attention and MLP layers contribute to categorical updates via their value vectors.

## Results

- Class identifiability scores consistently increased across ViT blocks for both image and `[CLS]` tokens in all **7** tested ViT variants.
- MLP layers promoted stronger categorical updates, with their value vectors manually inspected to cluster semantically similar concepts like 'racket, tennis ball, ping-pong ball'.
- The gradient-based explainability tool generates global relevancy maps comparable in quality to established methods while being significantly more time-efficient, requiring only a **single forward pass** over validation images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image tokens progressively encode class-specific information across ViT layers, not solely relying on the `[CLS]` token, with distinct patterns for class-labeled versus context-labeled regions.
> - Self-attention layers provide weaker, more compositional, and broadly distributed categorical updates, primarily in earlier blocks, while MLP layers yield stronger, more direct, and semantically clustered updates, peaking in penultimate blocks.
> - The proposed method offers a more task-relevant and efficient measure of feature importance compared to linear probing, which often fails to predict actual token relevance to the network's classification decision.
