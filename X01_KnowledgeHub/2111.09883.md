# Swin Transformer V2: Scaling Up Capacity and Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> Swin Transformer V2, developed by Microsoft Research Asia, successfully scales vision models to 3 billion parameters and high resolutions by addressing training instability, resolution transfer, and data efficiency. This enabled new state-of-the-art results across various computer vision tasks while significantly reducing the dependency on massive labeled datasets.

## Problem

- Training large vision models (billions of parameters) often leads to instability and divergence, with activation values varying by orders of magnitude across layers.
- Existing methods struggle to effectively transfer models pre-trained on low-resolution images to high-resolution downstream tasks due to a significant 'resolution gap'.
- Scaling vision models traditionally requires proprietary, super-huge labeled datasets (e.g., 3 billion images), limiting accessibility and increasing training costs.

## Method

- Introduced ==Residual Post-Normalization== and ==Scaled Cosine Attention== to stabilize training by controlling activation amplitudes and attention map values in deeper, larger models.
- Developed ==Log-Spaced Continuous Position Bias (Log-CPB)== using a meta-network and log-transformed coordinates to enable seamless and effective transfer across varying input and window resolutions.
- Employed ==self-supervised pre-training with SimMIM== and leveraged GPU memory-saving techniques like ZeRO optimizer, activation checkpointing, and sequential self-attention computation for efficient large model training.

## Results

- Successfully trained a **3-billion-parameter** Swin Transformer V2 (SwinV2-G) capable of processing images up to **1,536×1,536** resolution, representing the largest dense vision model reported.
- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance across diverse vision tasks: **84.0%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-V2, **63.1/54.4** box/mask AP on COCO test-dev, **59.9** mIoU on ADE20K, and **86.8%** top-1 accuracy on Kinetics-400.
- Utilized SimMIM for self-supervised pre-training, enabling SwinV2-G to achieve SOTA results with **40x** less labeled data and training time compared to previous billion-level vision models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision models can be successfully scaled to billions of parameters, akin to large language models, by addressing architectural stability and resolution transfer challenges.
> - Self-supervised pre-training (SimMIM) significantly reduces the reliance on vast labeled datasets for training multi-billion parameter vision models, making them more accessible.
> - Novel techniques like residual post-normalization, scaled cosine attention, and log-spaced continuous position bias are crucial for stable training and effective resolution transfer in large vision Transformers.
