# ViPER: Empowering the Self-Evolution of Visual Perception Abilities in Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ViPER, a self-evolutionary framework, iteratively enhances Vision-Language Models' fine-grained visual perception by autonomously generating high-quality training data and employing a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy. This approach enables average performance gains of 1.6-1.7% on comprehensive multimodal benchmarks and reduces hallucination rates in the resulting ViPER-trained models.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) possess limited capacity for fine-grained visual perception, hindering their utility in real-world applications.
- Acquiring vast amounts of precisely annotated data for fine-grained perception is costly and time-consuming, while existing data scaling methods yield diminishing returns.
- Current fine-tuning paradigms (SFT, RFT) often compromise VLM general capabilities or fail to enhance intrinsic visual understanding, frequently focusing on superficial textual reasoning or tool manipulation.

## Method

- ==ViPER== introduces a self-evolutionary, closed-loop framework that progressively refines VLM perceptual abilities through ==self-critiquing and self-prediction==.
- It employs a two-stage task formulation: =='Caption Self-Refining'== for holistic image reasoning and =='Visual-Operation Predicting'== for fine-grained understanding.
- The framework integrates a ==data synthesis module== using a VLM and a diffusion model to autonomously generate perception-intensive training data (Viper10K) for both image-level and instance-level reconstruction, alongside a tailored two-stage reinforcement learning strategy.

## Results

- ViPER-trained models (Qwen-Viper-3B/7B) achieved average performance gains of **1.7%** and **1.6%** respectively across seven comprehensive multimodal benchmarks.
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in fine-grained perception tasks, with gains of **4.4%** for Qwen-Viper-3B and **6.0%** for Qwen-Viper-7B.
- The models exhibited reduced hallucination rates, enhanced generalizability across domains (e.g., **3.6%** gain in Science & Technology for 7B), and validated the efficacy of the two-stage reinforcement learning strategy over mixed training approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive coarse-to-fine learning strategy, moving from holistic scene understanding to instance-level perception, effectively enhances VLM visual abilities.
> - The reciprocal relationship between visual generation and self-critique profoundly improves VLM visual understanding, enabling a self-bootstrapping paradigm without relying on external annotations.
> - The self-synthesized training data effectively eliminates the dependency on expensive cold-start data and large-scale external data distillation for VLM enhancement.
