# CALVIN: A Benchmark for Language-Conditioned Policy Learning for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> CALVIN introduces an open-source simulated benchmark for developing language-conditioned robot policies capable of executing complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks using continuous control. The benchmark includes diverse environments, multimodal sensor data, and natural language instructions, with a baseline model achieving 53.9% success on single tasks but only 0.08% on long-horizon sequences, highlighting current limitations.

## Problem

- Enabling general-purpose robots to understand and act on natural language instructions for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Lack of standardized benchmarks for language-conditioned continuous visuomotor control, hindering reproducible research and cross-comparison of methods.
- Difficulty for robot policies to generalize acquired skills and language understanding to novel environments and unseen instructions in a zero-shot manner.

## Method

- **CALVIN** provides an open-source simulated benchmark featuring a ==7-DOF robot arm== interacting in four distinct, varied environments with common manipulation objects and ==continuous control==.
- It includes a ==multimodal observation space== (RGB-D, tactile, proprioception) and a dataset of approximately 24 hours of unstructured ==teleoperated play data==, with 1% annotated by over 400 crowd-sourced natural language instructions for 34 tasks.
- The **CALVIN Challenge** defines evaluation protocols for ==multi-task language control== and ==long-horizon tasks==, including demanding ==zero-shot generalization== settings to unseen environments.

## Results

- The MCIL baseline achieved a **53.9%** success rate on individual manipulation tasks (MTLC) when trained and tested in the same environment using static camera RGB-D.
- Long-horizon performance was notably low, with the MCIL baseline achieving only a **0.08%** success rate for chains of five language instructions (LH-MTLC) even within the same environment.
- Generalization to unseen environments (zero-shot) severely impacted performance, dropping single-task success to **38.6%** and effectively **0%** for instruction chains of two or more, indicating poor abstract concept generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The design of CALVIN with diverse tasks, multimodal inputs, and zero-shot generalization capabilities reveals the significant challenges for current robot learning methods in bridging language and continuous control.
> - Unstructured "play" data combined with sparse language annotations can be used to create a realistic and scalable dataset for language-conditioned policy learning.
> - Establishing a common evaluation framework is critical for advancing research in general-purpose, language-driven robot manipulation, as it highlights current limitations and encourages innovation.
