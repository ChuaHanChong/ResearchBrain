# What Does Flow Matching Bring To TD Learning?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University explain the empirical success of flow-matching critics in Temporal Difference (TD) learning, attributing their benefits to Test-Time Recovery and Plastic Feature Learning rather than distributional modeling. This approach significantly enhances performance and stability in high update-to-data online reinforcement learning, yielding up to 2x higher final performance and 5x better sample efficiency.

## Problem

- The underlying mechanisms driving the observed empirical gains and robustness of flow-matching critics in RL remained largely unexplained.
- Prevailing hypotheses attributed flow-matching success to implicit modeling of return distributions, similar to distributional RL, requiring rigorous testing.
- Standard TD learning algorithms suffer from pathologies like plasticity loss and sensitivity to non-stationary targets, which existing methods often address with explicit regularization.

## Method

- The ==`floq` architecture==, which uses ==flow matching== solely for expected Q-value parameterization, was utilized to disentangle its benefits from distributional RL.
- A rigorous combination of theoretical analysis and empirical validation was applied to formalize and test two proposed mechanisms: ==Test-Time Recovery (TTR)== and ==Plastic Feature Learning==.
- Controlled experiments, including injecting staleness, adding noise, and freezing features, were designed to validate these mechanisms and compare flow-matching critics against monolithic baselines.

## Results

- Flow-matching critics demonstrate approximately **2x** higher final performance and **5x** better sample efficiency in high update-to-data online RL on OGBench tasks compared to monolithic baselines.
- They exhibit strong Test-Time Recovery, maintaining comparable performance even when **25-50%** of initial integration steps use stale parameters, and show significantly higher robustness to noisy TD targets.
- The learned features in flow-matching critics display improved plasticity, allowing adaptation to non-stationary TD targets through feature reweighting, while monolithic critics suffer severe performance collapse when features are frozen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The success of flow-matching critics is not due to their ability to model return distributions; an expected-value flow-matching critic performs comparably to or better than distributional variants.
> - Test-Time Recovery (TTR), enabled by iterative integration, allows flow-matching critics to correct imperfect intermediate estimates and dampen errors, with error effects decaying polynomially with integration steps.
> - Dense velocity supervision during training induces Plastic Feature Learning, allowing flow-matching critics to adapt to non-stationary TD targets by reweighting existing features rather than modifying them directly.
