# PostAlign: Multimodal Grounding as a Corrective Lens for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The PostAlign framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) visual understanding and reduces hallucinations by incorporating multimodal grounding as a corrective lens. This approach employs explicit visual and textual grounding mechanisms, enabling MLLMs to anchor responses more faithfully to visual evidence.

## Problem

- MLLMs frequently over-rely on spurious correlations and linguistic priors, leading to generated content inconsistent with actual visual information.
- A direct consequence of linguistic biases is multimodal hallucination, where MLLMs generate objects, attributes, or actions not present in the input image.
- Current MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained visual understanding and complex reasoning, tending to prioritize text-driven generalizations over specific visual evidence.

## Method

- Introduced ==MMGrounded-PostAlign==, a post-multimodal alignment framework that integrates an MLLM backbone, a visual grounding encoder, and a multi-task decoder.
- Developed a ==negative rejection mechanism== where the MLLM predicts a special token for non-existent objects, preventing visual hallucinations by explicitly signaling absence.
- Implemented a ==selective reasoning mechanism==, training the MLLM to dynamically assess query complexity and generate textual rationales only for complex queries, optimizing efficiency and accuracy.

## Results

- The negative rejection mechanism provided the most significant improvement in mitigating hallucinations on the HaloQuest benchmark, particularly in anti-concept scenarios.
- MMGrounded-PostAlign outperformed bounding-box-as-language token methods on the POPE benchmark, achieving superior reduction of object and attribute hallucinations.
- The framework preserved or improved the general reasoning abilities of the underlying MLLM backbone on datasets like VQAv2, MME, and MMBench (e.g., MMBench score of **1514.3** for 7B model vs. baseline **1504.6**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Linguistic priors can progressively override visual information during MLLM decoding, leading to hallucinations that persist even when visual evidence contradicts them.
> - Explicit visual grounding, particularly the negative rejection mechanism, significantly mitigates hallucinations by enforcing stronger multimodal alignment between text and image.
> - The framework allows MLLMs to retain or enhance general reasoning capabilities, suggesting a balanced approach that avoids the degradation seen when over-biasing models towards specific visual tokens.
