# PaperBanana: Automating Academic Illustration for AI Scientists

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and Google Cloud AI Research developed PaperBanana, an agentic framework that automates the generation of academic illustrations, including methodology diagrams and statistical plots. It significantly outperformed existing baselines on a new benchmark, achieving an overall score of 60.2 and demonstrating human-competitive results for statistical plots.

## Problem

- The manual creation of high-quality academic illustrations, such as methodology diagrams and statistical plots, is time-consuming and a bottleneck in the research workflow.
- Existing automated diagram generation methods, whether code-based or general-purpose image generation, struggle to meet the specific stylistic rigor and factual accuracy required for scientific publications.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks has hindered rigorous evaluation and comparison of systems designed for automated academic illustration.

## Method

- A ==reference-driven agentic framework called PaperBanana== was developed, orchestrating five specialized AI agents (Retriever, Planner, Stylist, Visualizer, Critic) powered by state-of-the-art VLMs and image generation models.
- The framework employs an ==iterative Visualizer-Critic loop== for refinement, ensuring content fidelity and adherence to academic aesthetic standards.
- A new benchmark, ==PaperBananaBench==, was introduced, featuring 292 methodology diagrams from NeurIPS 2025, alongside a VLM-as-a-Judge evaluation protocol, to standardize assessment.

## Results

- PaperBanana achieved an overall score of **60.2** on the PaperBananaBench, outperforming vanilla (**43.2**) and few-shot baselines by **17.0%** and demonstrating specific gains in faithfulness (**+2.8%**), conciseness (**+37.2%**), readability (**+12.9%**), and aesthetics (**+6.6%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the Critic agent in improving faithfulness and overall quality through iterative refinement, and highlighted that any relevant structural/stylistic reference is more important than content-specific retrieval.
- The framework successfully generated statistical plots by converting descriptions to Python Matplotlib code, achieving human-competitive results in conciseness, readability, and aesthetics while maintaining strong faithfulness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agentic framework mimicking human workflow, with specialized roles for content planning, style optimization, and iterative self-critique, is effective for complex generative tasks like academic illustration.
> - While style-optimizing agents can enhance aesthetics and conciseness, a critical feedback loop (Critic agent) is essential to maintain factual faithfulness.
> - For statistical plots, a hybrid visualization strategy leveraging code generation for numerical precision and image generation for visual presentation offers the most balanced approach.
