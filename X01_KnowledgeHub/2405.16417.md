# CRoFT: Robust Fine-Tuning with Concurrent Optimization for OOD Generalization and Open-Set OOD Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced CRoFT, a fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language Pre-trained Models that concurrently optimizes for out-of-distribution generalization and open-set OOD detection. This approach, which theoretically links OOD detection to generalization via Hessian matrices, achieved improved performance in both areas, including a 25.3% reduction in FPR95 for OOD detection in one setting and a 4.3% boost in OOD generalization accuracy.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) for specific tasks often degrades their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) data and detect novel, unseen classes.
- Real-world AI applications encounter both covariate shifts (requiring OOD generalization for known classes) and semantic shifts (requiring open-set OOD detection for unknown classes), challenges typically addressed independently.
- Current fine-tuning strategies for VL-PTMs lack a unified approach to simultaneously enhance robustness to distribution shifts and reliably identify novel categories.

## Method

- A lightweight ==adapter-tuning== strategy is applied to Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) to efficiently adapt them to downstream tasks.
- An ==Energy Distribution Reshaping (EDR) loss== is introduced to optimize open-set Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection by reshaping energy distributions, making unknown classes more distinguishable.
- ==Adversarial learning== is employed to generate worst-case covariate-shifted OOD features, which are then used to minimize a novel ==Hessian-based OOD generalization bound==.

## Results

- The framework achieved state-of-the-art performance in both Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization and open-set OOD detection across multiple benchmarks.
- CRoFT reduced the False Positive Rate at 95% True Positive Rate (FPR95) by up to **25.3%** for OOD detection and improved OOD generalization accuracy by **4.3%** compared to baselines in various settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the EDR loss and adversarial loss, and visualizations showed improved separation of feature and energy distributions for different data types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A theoretical connection exists between open-set Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection and OOD generalization, demonstrating that optimizing for one can benefit the other through domain-consistent Hessians.
> - Minimizing the gradient magnitude of energy scores on in-distribution data effectively reshapes energy distributions, improving the discriminability of open-set OOD data.
> - Adversarial generation of worst-case covariate-shifted features during training helps the model learn more robust adapters for improved generalization to various data styles.
