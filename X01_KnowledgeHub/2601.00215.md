# From Sight to Insight: Improving Visual Reasoning Capabilities of Multimodal Models via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers identified that visual perception, not algorithmic reasoning, primarily limits multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on visual puzzles. They enhanced MLLMs' visual reasoning through a reinforcement learning framework using specially designed reward functions, successfully inducing multi-step, visually-grounded thought processes without reliance on costly human annotations.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently underperform on visual reasoning tasks, such as solving visual puzzles, that demand precise visual perception and complex multi-step reasoning.
- MLLMs often exhibit 'weakly grounded' reasoning, failing to consistently interpret visual information and bypassing visual details in favor of abstract reasoning or direct answers.
- Existing RL-based approaches for multimodal reasoning often require dense intermediate supervision like executable tool outputs or human-annotated reasoning traces, which are expensive to obtain for complex visual tasks.

## Method

- Empirically determined that ==visual perception== is the primary bottleneck for MLLMs in solving visual puzzles by comparing performance on multimodal versus text-only inputs.
- Utilized ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to fine-tune the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B MLLM using synthetically generated, diverse visual puzzle datasets.
- Designed and evaluated ==six distinct reward functions== within a reinforcement learning framework to incentivize structured, multi-step, and explicitly visually-grounded reasoning without requiring costly human-annotated reasoning traces.

## Results

- MLLMs (Claude-3.5/3.7) showed substantial average performance gains of **23.6 to 26.7 percentage points** when visual information was converted to textual descriptions, confirming visual perception as the primary bottleneck.
- The 'Mixture' reward function achieved the highest average accuracy (**15.56%**) on the `Diverse-8k` dataset and successfully encouraged the MLLM to generate longer, structured, and visually-grounded reasoning trajectories across various categories.
- RL training on a diverse dataset significantly improved generalization across both in-domain and out-of-domain visual puzzle categories compared to the no-RL baseline, albeit with overall accuracy remaining modest on highly complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual perception is the fundamental bottleneck limiting MLLM performance on complex visual puzzles, as converting images to text significantly boosts their reasoning accuracy.
> - Reward-driven reinforcement learning, especially with 'Mixture' and 'Continuous' reward functions, can effectively induce longer, structured, and visually-grounded reasoning chains ('think with image') in MLLMs.
> - It is possible to foster emergent, multi-step reasoning capabilities in MLLMs purely through careful reward design, reducing dependency on expensive, dense intermediate supervision for complex multimodal tasks.
