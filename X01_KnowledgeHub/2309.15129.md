# Evaluating Cognitive Maps and Planning in Large Language Models with CogEval

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research introduced CogEval, a cognitive science-inspired protocol, to systematically evaluate large language models' (LLMs) cognitive mapping and planning abilities. The study found that current LLMs, including GPT-4, display "apparent competence" in simpler tasks but consistently fail on complex planning problems requiring the inference of latent relational structures, exhibiting common issues like hallucinating invalid paths and generating suboptimal routes.

## Problem

- Claims about emergent cognitive abilities in large language models (LLMs) often lack rigorous, controlled evaluation and may be susceptible to data contamination.
- Existing AI evaluation methods can conflate linguistic fluency with deeper, functional cognitive capacities like planning or causal inference.
- There is a need for unbiased assessment of whether LLMs can genuinely grasp and utilize latent relational structures for planning, rather than simply retrieving memorized patterns.

## Method

- Introduced ==CogEval==, a ==cognitive science-inspired protocol== for systematic evaluation of LLM cognitive abilities, adhering to practices like latent construct operationalization and using non-linguistic human experiment paradigms.
- Systematically evaluated eight state-of-the-art LLMs across diverse ==graph structures==, domains, and planning task conditions (e.g., reward revaluation, shortcuts) to assess cognitive maps and planning.
- Investigated the effect of ==Chain of Thought instructions== on GPT-4's performance and conducted supplementary experiments on systematic graph explorations.

## Results

- LLM version, graph structure, task condition, and domain all significantly contributed to performance variations (all p-values **< 0.001**) in planning tasks.
- LLMs, including GPT-4, showed apparent competence in simpler tasks but significantly failed on tasks requiring genuine understanding of latent relational structures, particularly in community graphs.
- Chain of Thought instructions resulted in only marginal and inconsistent performance improvements, and common failure modes included hallucinating invalid edges, getting trapped in loops, and generating suboptimally long routes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs' planning capabilities are highly sensitive to graph structure, task condition, and domain, but the temperature parameter had no significant effect on overall performance in broad tasks.
> - Apparent competence in simpler planning tasks stems from "route memorization" of explicitly provided information rather than a genuine understanding of latent relational structures.
> - CogEval provides a robust, cognitive science-inspired framework for assessing LLMs' cognitive capacities, challenging claims of emergent intelligence by demonstrating systematic failure modes under controlled conditions.
