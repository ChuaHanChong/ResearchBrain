# Caption This, Reason That: VLMs Caught in the Middle

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from McGill University, Mila, and Microsoft Research developed the Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM) and Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) datasets to assess Vision-Language Models (VLMs) from a cognitive science perspective. The study determined that the main limitation in VLM reasoning, particularly for spatial tasks, is the integration of visual information into the reasoning process, which can be improved by an 18.31% average increase in location-based task accuracy using vision-text decoupling through self-captioning.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate persistent weaknesses in specific visual decision-making and reasoning tasks, such as spatial reasoning and multi-object understanding.
- Existing VLM benchmarks primarily assess high-level task performance and often fail to diagnose the root causes of failures by neglecting low-level cognitive abilities like Perception, Attention, and Memory.
- The underlying mechanisms of VLM struggles with complex visual reasoning are not well-understood, hindering the development of more robust and human-like artificial intelligence.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Perception-Attention-Memory (PAM) dataset== to systematically evaluate VLMs on isolated cognitive abilities, including fine-grained perception, selective attention, and information maintenance in memory.
- Developed the ==Composite Visual Reasoning (CVR) dataset==, consisting of procedurally generated, complex tasks that combine various cognitive abilities, to assess integrated reasoning.
- Implemented a ==vision-text decoupling paradigm==, using ground-truth or self-generated captions, to diagnose whether VLM reasoning bottlenecks stem from visual encoding or the integration of visual information into the language model component.

## Results

- GPT-4o demonstrated the highest performance across PAM and CVR tasks, often nearing human levels on category-based tasks, but showed notable gaps in spatial attention and memory tasks.
- Vision-text decoupling via self-captioning led to substantial performance gains, with an average **18.31%** improvement on location-based tasks, by effectively providing accurate visual information in a textual format to the LLM component.
- Targeted LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved core cognitive abilities, especially for location-based PAM tasks, but these improvements translated to only modest gains on out-of-distribution benchmarks like MMBench and VQAv2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs, including top-performing proprietary models like GPT-4o, exhibit a pervasive and significant weakness in integrating spatial object information into their reasoning processes.
> - The primary bottleneck for VLM spatial reasoning lies in inadequate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training or visual-to-textual integration, as models can often describe visual information accurately but fail to leverage it during direct visual reasoning.
> - Self-captioning significantly mitigates spatial reasoning deficits, indicating that providing textual representations of visual content can unlock the underlying language model's reasoning capabilities.
