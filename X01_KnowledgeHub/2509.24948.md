# RehearseVLA: Simulated Post-Training for VLAs with Physically-Consistent World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A simulated post-training framework, RehearseVLA, enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by employing a physically-consistent world model and a VLM-guided instant reflector. The approach yields higher success rates for VLA policies in data-scarce conditions and transfers effectively to real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- VLA models, reliant on imitation learning, struggle with data scarcity, requiring costly and often unsafe large-scale real-world demonstrations.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) for VLA post-training is hindered by high real-world interaction costs, non-resettable environments, and the "sim-to-real" gap in traditional simulators.
- Current VLA approaches lack robust task completion detection, leading to redundant actions that can disrupt achieved goal states and reduce overall task success.

## Method

- Proposes **RehearseVLA**, a framework for VLA post-training that uses a low-cost, ==world model-based virtual simulator== to replace real-world interactions for reinforcement learning.
- Develops a ==physically-consistent world simulator== that generates ==action-conditioned future observations== by injecting ==geometry-aware features== (==VGGT==, ==CLIP==) into a ==U-Net diffusion network==.
- Implements a ==VLM-guided instant reflector==, a ==semantics-aware reward mechanism== that provides continuous reward signals and dynamically terminates actions upon task success.

## Results

- Achieved an average **79.6%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark with only **five** expert demonstrations per task, surpassing supervised fine-tuning baselines like OpenVLA-OFT (**74.85%**).
- Demonstrated effective transfer to real-world tasks, improving success rates (e.g., from **20%** for baseline to **30%** for RehearseVLA on "clean table").
- The VLM-guided instant reflector's dynamic termination mechanism improved average success rates (**74.9%** vs. **63.05%** for OpenVLA-OFT without termination) by preventing redundant post-success actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training the world simulator with a diverse dataset, including autonomously explored successful and failed trajectories, is crucial for improving its fidelity and the robustness of VLA training.
> - Integrating geometry-aware features (VGGT and CLIP) into the world model's generation process is essential for synthesizing physically plausible and temporally consistent visual observations.
> - A continuous, trainable reward head in the instant reflector, rather than sparse binary rewards or VLM-based classification, provides more reliable advantage estimates, improving RL sample efficiency and stability.
