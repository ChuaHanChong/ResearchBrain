# Tactile-VLA: Unlocking Vision-Language-Action Model's Physical Knowledge for Tactile Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> TACTILE-VLA integrates tactile sensing into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling robots to interpret force-related language and apply common-sense grasp forces for contact-rich tasks. This framework demonstrates generalization to novel objects and environments, along with adaptive reasoning for physical failure recovery.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on visual feedback, limiting their ability to ground decisions in fine-grained physical interactions, especially in contact-rich scenarios.
- VLAs struggle with tasks requiring precise force control, such as grasping fragile objects or delicate insertion, often failing due to insufficient or ambiguous visual information.
- Existing tactile sensing efforts in robotics often lack the language-driven generalization and world knowledge offered by modern VLA models.

## Method

- A ==multi-modal transformer== architecture fuses vision, language, and ==tactile inputs== into a deeply integrated representation, leveraging a pre-trained VLM backbone.
- A ==force-aware action expert== generates augmented action vectors specifying both target position and target contact force, coupled with a ==hybrid position-force controller== for adaptive force regulation.
- A ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== reasoning module, finetuned on failure events, enables the model to diagnose physical interaction problems from tactile feedback and autonomously generate corrective instructions.

## Results

- **TACTILE-VLA** achieved superior success rates in tactile-relevant instruction following, such as **90%** in the Charger task compared to **40-25%** for baselines, and successfully generalized force adverbs to unseen contexts (e.g., 'harder' applied **2.94N** vs 'hard' **2.57N**).
- The model demonstrated strong common-sense generalization in tabletop grasping, inferring and applying appropriate forces for various out-of-domain objects (e.g., **90%** success for OOD fragile paper box without damage).
- **TACTILE-VLA-CoT** achieved **80%** zero-shot success in the challenging blackboard wiping task by autonomously adapting applied force from an initial **3.5N** to **6.7N** after diagnosing insufficient force, while baselines largely failed (**0-15%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) implicitly retain a latent, semantic understanding of physical interaction that can be activated and leveraged through tactile sensing with minimal demonstrations.
> - Directly incorporating force targets into the action space and training with a Conditional Flow Matching objective enables the model to effectively map linguistic nuances to corresponding physical force magnitudes.
> - Tactile-involved Chain-of-Thought reasoning allows for robust adaptation to novel, physically distinct scenarios by diagnosing failures and adjusting force-based strategies autonomously.
