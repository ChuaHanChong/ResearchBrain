# SPARQ: Synthetic Problem Generation for Reasoning via Quality-Diversity Algorithms

> [!summary] Summary
> SPARQ introduces an algorithm for generating high-quality and diverse synthetic math problems by leveraging Quality-Diversity methods and a model-centric "solve-rate" to filter problem quality. The approach improved a Gemma-2-9b model's math reasoning accuracy by 9% and enabled successful recursive self-improvement where the model generated its own training data.

## Problem

- A scarcity of high-quality and diverse training data for large language models (LLMs) performing complex reasoning tasks, particularly in domains like mathematics.
- Existing synthetic data generation methods often rely on large "teacher" models, generate only solutions for fixed problems, or lack robust mechanisms to filter out illogical or invalid generated problems.
- The absence of a reliable ground truth signal makes it challenging to assess the quality and correctness of newly generated problem statements for LLM training.

## Method

- Introduces ==SPARQ==, an iterative pipeline that generates and filters synthetic math problem-solution pairs using a single student model for quality assessment.
- Defines a novel =="solve-rate" metric==, representing the student model's average success at solving a problem, which serves as an internal proxy for problem difficulty and quality to filter out trivial, impossible, or potentially invalid problems.
- Integrates ==Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms== to explicitly promote the generation of problems diverse in their required skill-sets, alongside quality optimization, within the iterative generation process.

## Results

- SPARQ improved the Gemma-2-9b model's pass@1 accuracy on the MATH test set by **9%** (from **38%** to **47%**) when using the static uniform generation method.
- Training on the synthetic data also enhanced out-of-distribution generalization, boosting AIME pass@n accuracy from **20%** to **25%** (with K=96 inference samples).
- Successfully demonstrated recursive self-improvement, with a Gemma-2-9b model showing a **20%** relative performance increase over its baseline when generating and learning from its own problems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "solve-rate" metric, while effective for filtering, is a noisy proxy for true logical validity, especially for harder problems, yet LLM fine-tuning proved robust to this moderate level of noise in training data.
> - Post-generation filtering strategies, specifically those that jointly optimize for quality and diversity, can achieve strong performance comparable to larger datasets with fewer samples.
> - Data quality primarily correlates with improvements in in-distribution performance, while data diversity is crucial for enhancing out-of-distribution generalization.
