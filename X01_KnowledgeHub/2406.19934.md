# From the Least to the Most: Building a Plug-and-Play Visual Reasoner via Data Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a method for generating high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data using a 'least-to-most' synthesis approach, primarily leveraging open-source models. The synthesized data then trains a plug-and-play visual reasoner, which consistently enhances the reasoning capabilities of existing Vision-Language Models across diverse visual question answering benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step visual reasoning due to the scarcity of training data that explicitly outlines sequential reasoning paths (similar to Chain-of-Thought for LLMs).
- VLMs lack the ability to dynamically manipulate or focus on specific visual regions and objects during intermediate reasoning steps, hindering complex visual deduction.
- Existing methods for enhancing VLM reasoning often rely on expensive, proprietary Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for top-down data generation, leading to issues with cost, reproducibility, and data quality.

## Method

- Introduces a =='Least-to-Most Visual Reasoning' paradigm== where a visual reasoner decomposes complex visual questions into simpler sub-questions and iteratively invokes specialized tools (Grounding, Highlight, OCR, Answer).
- Proposes a =='Least-to-Most Synthesis' pipeline==, a bottom-up data generation method that automatically constructs multi-step visual reasoning data using fine-tuned open-source LLMs.
- Constructs the large-scale ==VIREO dataset== (50K examples, with a 500K version released) and fine-tunes an ==LLaVA-1.5-7B model== on it to serve as a general-purpose, plug-and-play visual reasoner.

## Results

- The Reasoner consistently improved performance for four representative VLMs (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, LLaVA) across various VQA benchmarks, with absolute gains up to **38.96%** on TallyQA-C.
- A human study confirmed the high quality of the synthesized VIREO dataset, with all **200** sampled examples rated as correct across sub-question fidelity, tool argument accuracy, and main question coherence.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the removal of any specialized tool (OCR, Highlight, Grounding) negatively impacted performance, affirming the necessity of multi-tool integration for robust visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A bottom-up, open-source-reliant data synthesis approach can effectively generate high-quality, multi-step visual reasoning data (VIREO dataset), validated by human evaluation.
> - Complex visual reasoning tasks benefit significantly from the synergistic application of modular, specialized tools (Grounding, Highlight, OCR) integrated into a reasoning framework.
> - A fine-tuned visual reasoner can act as a 'plug-and-play' module, universally enhancing the reasoning capabilities of diverse existing Vision-Language Models without requiring their re-training.
