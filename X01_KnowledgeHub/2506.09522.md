# Revisit What You See: Disclose Language Prior in Vision Tokens for LVLM Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> ReVisiT, from Yonsei University, proposes a decoding strategy that leverages intrinsic object-level semantics within Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) vision tokens to reduce visual hallucinations. The method enhances visual grounding and general VQA performance across models, achieving this with negligible computational overhead compared to greedy decoding.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from "visual hallucination," generating text descriptions of objects or attributes not present in the input image.
- Existing methods to mitigate hallucinations often incur significant computational overhead, requiring multiple forward passes or complex interventions during decoding.
- The intrinsic semantic information within LVLM "vision tokens"—visual embeddings projected into the text embedding space—is often latent and under-leveraged by conventional decoding processes.

## Method

- ReVisiT employs a ==training-free, model-agnostic decoding strategy== that dynamically refines output logits at each generation step.
- It constructs an ==adaptive, context-aware vocabulary subset== and projects relevant vision tokens from specific decoder layers over this subset.
- The method combines the vanilla output probability distribution with the selected vision token's projected distribution (chosen by minimizing ==Jensen-Shannon Divergence==) to guide text generation, amplifying visually grounded tokens.

## Results

- ReVisiT significantly reduced hallucination rates across benchmarks (e.g., HallusionBench qAcc improved from **11.55%** to **20.22%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B) and consistently improved general VQA performance (e.g., VQAv2 accuracy for LLaVA-1.5-7B from **69.47%** to **70.40%**).
- The method maintains inference efficiency comparable to greedy decoding, with only a marginal runtime increase (**+1.9%** for LLaVA-1.5-7B), substantially outperforming other intra-alignment techniques.
- It demonstrated broad applicability and scalability, improving performance across various LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL) and model sizes (**7B to 32B**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even at hallucinated steps, ground-truth visual information often remains present among the LVLM's high-probability output candidates.
> - Vision tokens intrinsically encode meaningful object-level semantics, but their interpretability becomes explicit only when projected onto a semantically coherent, constrained vocabulary subset.
> - The presence of vision tokens significantly contributes to visual grounding, with their removal leading to a reversal in probability mass distribution between ground-truth and hallucinated objects.
