# TreeRL: LLM Reinforcement Learning with On-Policy Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> TreeRL, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University and Caltech, introduces an on-policy reinforcement learning framework that integrates tree search to enhance large language model reasoning. It achieves superior performance over traditional ChainRL on challenging math and code benchmarks by leveraging an efficient entropy-guided tree search (EPTree) and deriving fine-grained process supervision from the generated reasoning trees.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning for LLMs (ChainRL) relies on sparse, delayed rewards from final outcomes, making it difficult to learn from intermediate reasoning steps.
- Existing tree search applications for LLMs are often used for inference-time enhancement or offline data generation, leading to potential distribution mismatch and reward hacking during online training.
- Inefficient exploration of the vast reasoning space by independent chain sampling and the computational inefficiency of classical MCTS for modern LLM inference engines.

## Method

- Introduces ==EPTree (Entropy-Guided Tree Search)==, an efficient and parallelizable tree search algorithm that iteratively expands branches from high-uncertainty (high-entropy) tokens, optimizing for diversity and PassRate under a given inference budget.
- Develops an ==on-policy process supervision== mechanism that derives fine-grained reward signals for each reasoning step by calculating global and local advantages based on the success rate of descendant nodes in the EPTree.
- Integrates EPTree and its derived process rewards directly into the LLM's on-policy reinforcement learning loop, updating the policy using a policy gradient method while ==re-weighting rewards for non-leaf steps==.

## Results

- EPTree consistently achieved a **3%** higher PassRate compared to independent multi-chain sampling under the same inference cost, demonstrating superior efficiency and diversity in generation.
- TreeRL outperformed traditional ChainRL across all evaluated math and code reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MATH500, AIME2024, LiveCodeBench), showing consistent performance gains after approximately **100 training steps**.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of entropy-guided forking and the combined global and local advantage signals for process supervision, both of which were crucial for optimal RL training performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Entropy-guided forking (EPTree) efficiently generates more diverse and high-quality responses than traditional sampling methods, leading to improved search PassRate.
> - Deriving on-policy process supervision signals, using a combination of global and local advantages re-weighted by subtree size, provides effective and stable feedback for LLM reinforcement learning.
> - Directly integrating tree search into the on-policy RL loop mitigates issues of distribution mismatch and reward hacking often seen with offline reward models or static data generation.
