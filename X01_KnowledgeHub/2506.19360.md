# SoK: Can Synthetic Images Replace Real Data? A Survey of Utility and Privacy of Synthetic Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Chung et al. present a Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) that empirically evaluates the utility and privacy of various synthetic image generation models and mitigation strategies. Their analysis establishes that synthetic data can offer a favorable utility-privacy tradeoff, often outperforming DP-SGD on real data for downstream tasks, with diffusion models generally demonstrating superior performance.

## Problem

- A lack of comprehensive, unified analysis and empirical benchmarking for synthetic image data regarding its utility and privacy. Existing literature focused more on tabular data or had limited scope.
- Inconsistencies and fragmentation in prior empirical studies on privacy-preserving image generation, making it difficult to compare different generative models and privacy attacks under unified conditions.
- Absence of a systematic framework to categorize and analyze methods, attacks, and mitigations across different stages of privacy-preserving data synthesis for image data.

## Method

- Introduced a novel =="generation-sampling-classification pipeline"== to systematically analyze information release stages and evaluate methods, attacks, and mitigations.
- Conducted a broad empirical evaluation across diverse generative models (GANs, VAEs, Diffusion Models, Data Condensation) and privacy mitigation strategies (DP, adversarial training).
- Employed three distinct, model-agnostic ==Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs)== to measure privacy and standardized classification performance metrics (F1-score, Accuracy, AUC) for utility across three varied datasets.

## Results

- Diffusion-based models (e.g., SMP-LoRA, LoRA, TI) consistently achieved more favorable utility-privacy tradeoffs compared to GANs and VAEs across both image-based and classifier-based attacks.
- Classifiers trained on high-quality synthetic data, especially from advanced diffusion models, showed superior utility for a given privacy level compared to classifiers trained directly on real data using DP-SGD.
- Several synthetic data generation methods (e.g., TI, SMP-LoRA) demonstrated a better capacity to maintain or improve performance on minority groups, potentially mitigating the disparate impact sometimes observed with direct DP applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The optimal strategy for privacy-preserving synthetic image generation (e.g., sharing synthetic images vs. classifiers) is highly dependent on dataset characteristics, specific utility requirements, and privacy goals.
> - Synthetic data can provide superior utility-privacy tradeoffs compared to directly applying differentially private stochastic gradient descent (DP-SGD) to real data for classifier training.
> - Diffusion models generally offer more favorable utility-privacy tradeoffs, and implicit privacy gains can be achieved through careful hyperparameter tuning or specific generative process designs like multi-instance synthesis.
