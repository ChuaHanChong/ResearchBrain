The explicit-coupling thesis: six clusters, one bet

the reduced direction Of ~77 directions across the umbrella and six mechanism/capability docs, six clusters reduce to one thesis: when two physically coupled subsystems are controlled together, their joint value doesn't factor — the cross-term is a low-dimensional, structured quantity to predict, not data to collect. Three coupling anchors carry it, three mechanism roles compose it. The target is maximal idea-boundedness × humanoid-distinguishing weight — what a solo team betting ideas, not capital, should hunt for.

Monolithic generalist VLAs - GR00T N1.5 (~41%) and pi0.5 (44%) - mark the only channel with a published number: arm↔leg out-of-distribution margin. HEX's implicit MoE-coupled policy already widens that to ~62% OOD (79.8% on the same seven tasks in-distribution), but on the field's scene-generalization axis, not the disturbance/coupling-OOD axis this program targets: precedent, not this program's own claim. The bet is the next increment - an explicit predicted term must add further margin over implicit, pre-registered to count only at ≥5pp (δ₁A), concentrated where the cross-term is largest. Co-equal across all three channels in that bet, not in mechanism depth:

  • A is deepest (own reactive-observer + analytic-residual baselines).
  • C rides A's shared wrench head.
  • B is a thinner factoring-only claim with no reactive-observer comparator.

Honest deliverable: the crossover boundary, not a declared winner.

    Where the gain must concentrate
    De-circularizing the test: each target would otherwise be defined by the very model the policy also learns, which makes the ablation circular. Fix: define each target from a deliberately perturbed model, then report the explicit-over-implicit margin as a function of model error. Those margin-vs-error curves are themselves the verify-harness deliverable.
    Null result is a result: if explicit is roughly equal to implicit on a cluster (no OOD margin widening, no gain concentration where that cluster's cross-term is largest), that cluster's contribution is void, learned three ways at once. All three falsifiers cheaply proving themselves wrong up front is what makes them good problems, not just attractive ones.

    Wrong physics model —
    • A wrong URDF or contact model poisons the coupling heads; explicit collapses to implicit and that channel's bet is void.
    • Exactly why Sim2Real·B (ground) is core, not extension.
    • If system-ID can't be learned cleanly on a channel, the fallback is that channel's implicit baseline — still produces the definitive explicit-vs-implicit ablation, which Embodied-AI·B (verify) makes publishable either way.
    Engine floor error, not just a wrong model — GAUGE:
    • Physics engines given ground-truth-calibrated parameters still show order-of-magnitude errors on dynamic contact/deformation.
    • Video world models hit near-perfect trajectory fit while inferring free-fall acceleration under 1% of gravity.
    Even a channel that clears the grounding gate can still forward-simulate wrong. Sharpens the H8 pre-gate — GAUGE's worst regime (dynamic contact) is exactly H8's target.
