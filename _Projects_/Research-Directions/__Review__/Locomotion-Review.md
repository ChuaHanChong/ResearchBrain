## Locomotion — doc-level synthesis

### Capsule

Locomotion is a two-cluster agenda: **A** (humanoid whole-body skill acquisition — perceptive terrain, extreme agile skills, fall-recovery, embodiment-cost regulation, sample-efficient learning) and **B** (quadruped state-inference and mapless navigation — proprioceptive context inference, world-model dreaming, mapless-vs-mapped). It is a coherent, internally well-organized body of bets: nearly every direction sits on an *uncontested mechanism floor* (perception beats blindness, adaptive tolerance works, proprioceptive inference deploys, mapless beats fragile SLAM) and then makes a *narrow second-order bet* about the marginal lever (fusion super-additivity, rejection-vs-scale, periodic-prior penalty, conditional-head necessity, flow-vs-Gaussian, backbone Pareto, dreaming-vs-reconstruction, memory-vs-reactive). The doc is honest about its whitespace and consistently identifies the exact unrun experiment. But the value is fragile and concentrated: the strongest contributions are the **benchmarks and diagnostics** the directions back into, not the headline method claims, several of which the reviewers judge likely to fire their own falsifiers. Two directions (A1, A4) earn A- on framing quality; the rest cluster at B+ because the marginal lever is contested by a freshly-found 2026 paper in every single case. Overall this is a mature, well-grounded but late-to-a-crowded-party agenda whose durable value lives in measurement, not method.

### Cross-direction pattern

**The mechanism is settled, the marginal lever is contested, and the durable asset is the benchmark — every single direction.** In all eight, the first-order claim is uncontested ("perception beats blind," "tolerance works," "proprioception deploys," "mapless beats SLAM off-road," "embodiment costs are real," "off-policy beats PPO") and the *bet* is a narrow second-order interaction (does fusing/rejecting/conditioning/dreaming/swapping-backbone add anything over the cheaper alternative already shown to work). In all eight, the reviewers route the researcher away from the method headline and toward **owning a named benchmark/diagnostic** as the moat: a compute-normalized sparse-foothold suite (A1), a difficulty-stratified extreme-tail suite (A2), a named arbitrary-config getting-up suite (A3), a thermal-acoustic-force cost suite (A4), the stratified payload×terrain protocol (B1), the dreaming-vs-reconstruction diagnostic (B2), the representational-budget design rule (B3). The shared bottleneck is therefore not technical but **epistemic-market**: the mechanism cells are filling within ~6-12 months by industry-compute labs, so the academically-defensible contribution is the measuring stick, not the policy.

### Sharpest intra-doc contradiction

**A5 (Skeptic) directly contradicts Cluster A's own organizing thesis.** Cluster A's spine is "the reference/constraint *structure* the policy tracks is the lever" (A1 generated references, A2 rejection+tolerance, A3 curriculum scaffold). A5 then bets that an *expressive flow policy* is the lever for multimodal-contact skills — and the A5 Skeptic perspective demolishes this *using Cluster A's own thesis*: Multi-Gait selective-AMP (2604.19102) and CoRe-MoE deploy 5 gaits including jump on a real humanoid with a plain **Gaussian** policy precisely by *structuring the reference/reward* so each mode is locally unimodal. So A5's "Gaussian under-fits multimodal contact" headline is refuted by the structural-conditioning lever that the rest of Cluster A champions: if A's thesis is right (structure is the lever), A5's flow bet is decorative. The two cannot both be the durable contribution. A secondary, sharper-on-evidence contradiction sits inside B: **B1's "attention/long-context backbone sits highest" vs TAR (2503.20839)**, which holds both payload and terrain axes in one zero-shot deployment with a *contrastive-recurrent* (not attention) winner — the card's headline prediction and its strongest threat are the same paper.

### Recurring blind spots

- **Safety / hardware-survival at the failure boundary.** Pervasive. A1 (gaze-generation deadlock: swing toward unobserved geometry), A2 (disturbance-rejection DURING the skill, not reference MPJPE), A3 (the unrecoverable-state boundary and mis-triggered recovery), A4 (graceful degradation when the user dials a jointly-infeasible cost preference), B1 (the off-diagonal heavy-payload-on-stairs cell is a hardware-damaging RISK surface, not a Pareto point), B2 (adapt-time safety: 5 real trajectories collected by a partially-adapted policy whose imagined errors become torque commands). Every direction optimizes a success-rate metric that silently assumes the robot survives.
- **Sim-quantity vs hardware-reality.** A3, A4, B1, B2 all anchor their decisive deltas on sim numbers whose sign can invert on hardware (a periodic prior that lowers sim SR may raise real-world survivability; a sim-Pareto backbone winner that misses the 10ms control budget is a non-result; thermal trade-offs may sit below the cross-environment noise floor of dBA/temperature metrics).
- **The deploy-relevant cost axis is the wrong one.** A1 (scan-FLOPs vs perception latency), A4 (heat lives on hours, noise on per-step — incommensurable inside one RL episode), A5 (training time vs onboard inference latency). Multiple cards measure something the robot does not feel.
- **Un-vaulted load-bearing papers.** A recurring grounding flaw: the threat or evidence that decides the verdict is a paper absent from KnowledgeHub and uncited in the card — T-GMP (A1), OmniXtreme headline (A2), PTDL/Lu (A3), Multi-Gait 2604.19102 (A5), Biased Dreams (B2), LRN 2504.13149 (B3). The verdicts repeatedly hinge on papers nobody in the vault has read.

### Commit

- **A4 (Joint Embodiment-Cost Conditioning)** — A-, highest mean confidence (7.6); premise rock-solid, and even after dropping the false "necessity" headline a real defensible core survives (the FORCE axis nobody has + the measured thermal-vs-acoustic curve + the amortization thesis).
- **A1 (Perceptive Gaze×Generation Fusion)** — A-, still-whitespace, both pillars proven on a real G1, fusion genuinely unbuilt; commit *conditional* on running the matched-compute 2×2 with the H1 null pre-registered and the deploy-latency re-baseline.
- **B1 (Proprioceptive Context-Inference Pareto)** — mechanism floor uncontested, the stratified-protocol-as-moat reframe is durable; commit to the *protocol + 80%-payload stratum*, not the backbone verdict.
- **B3 (Mapless graded curve)** — phenomenon hardware-proven across embodiments; commit to the three-way (map / reactive-mapless / recurrent-mapless) graded curve as a representational-budget design law.

### Reshape

- **A5 (Flow / sample-efficient locomotion)** — lowest mean confidence (6.4, low 4); the flow half is contradicted by Cluster A's own structure-is-the-lever thesis (Multi-Gait Gaussian humanoid) and the whole verdict rests on an un-vaulted paper. Reshape: demote flow to a conditioning-matched sub-study, lead with the fast-iteration (H4) half.
- **A2 (Rejection + adaptive tolerance)** — the rejection-necessity half is being actively bypassed (OmniXtreme tracks-everything 91% real; the originating lab itself swapped rejection for relaxation in KungfuBot2). Reshape into a *diagnostic* ("when does rejection help") + the feasibility-certificate frontier; keep only the tolerance half as safe core. Also: the 96.36% headline is a flips-subset cherry-pick (true overall 91.08%).
- **A3 (Periodic-prior penalty in recovery)** — qualitative whitespace already closed by PTDL/Lu gating the prior off per-phase; the H4 ablation is foregone and H1's "parity within 5pp" may self-refute (HUMANUP single-stage fails to converge vs HoST 100%). Reshape around the named getting-up benchmark + regime-selection mechanism.
- **B2 (World-model dreaming few-shot)** — the "epistemic bound keeps imagination honest" premise is contradicted by Biased Dreams (latent uncertainty attractor-corrupted on the exact RSSM/Dreamer family); reconstruct-then-retrain may win held-out via a parameterized twin's unlimited offline terrain. Reshape into the latent-uncertainty-validity diagnostic first.

### Doc grade

**B+** — A coherent, honest, well-grounded agenda with two A- directions and a consistent escape-hatch (own the benchmark) that gives it real durability. Held below A- by the recurring pattern that every method headline is contested by a fresh 2026 paper, by the systematic safety/hardware-survival blind spot, and by the grounding flaw that load-bearing threats sit un-vaulted in over half the directions.

### A (Cluster review)

Topic: the COHERENCE + central shared-bottleneck thesis of Cluster A "Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills" — does grouping A1 (perceptive terrain), A2 (agile-skill imitation), A3 (fall recovery), A4 (embodiment-cost constraints), A5 (off-policy/flow training) under the shared bottleneck *"high-DoF whole-body balance under partial observation, where flat RL fails and the reference/constraint structure is the lever"* carve the problem at the right joint, and is that shared bet sound?

The cluster's own framing (Cluster-Overview row): *"High-DoF whole-body balance under partial observation, where flat RL fails and the reference/constraint structure is the lever"*, with synergy claim *"A1's perceptive terrain policy needs the dynamic skills A2 makes feasible; A3's fall-recovery is the boundary case A1/A2 must survive when balance is lost; A4 grounds all three in real GRF/thermal limits; A5 supplies the off-policy/flow training substrate every other A-direction trains on."* Cluster intro: *"The humanoid's legs ... plus the dynamic agile skills ... that make a humanoid more than a slow walker. Where flat RL fails and the reference/constraint structure is the lever."*

#### Prompt 1 — Five Perspectives

**1. PRACTITIONER (builds/deploys G1-class policies daily).**
- *Core position*: The cluster's real shared bottleneck is not "balance under partial observation" in the abstract — it is that every one of these sub-skills is validated on the *same single robot* (Unitree G1) in *sim-dominant* evaluation, and the deployment realities that actually gate publication-to-product (perception latency, motor thermals, sim2real gait gap) are siloed into A4/A5 instead of being the spine. The grouping is engineering-coherent (you do build all five on one stack) but the "reference/constraint structure is the lever" thesis is a sim-side abstraction that under-weights the messy parts.
- *Strongest supporting evidence*: A1's own DPL note (2510.07152) reports ~20 ms perception latency and 3.25 cm reconstruction MAE — real numbers that decide whether the gaze/generation fusion even runs onboard; A4's Thermal-Aware Residual (2605.27046) shows 70%→<10% overheating, i.e. a *standard* perceptive policy overheats 70% of the time — which means A1/A2's headline parkour SRs (G1 WBC-Gen+Track 0.962 box-climb; PHP 1.25 m wall in 3.63 s) are not deployable for sustained operation without A4's machinery. The synergy row admits this ("A4 grounds all three in real GRF/thermal limits") but ranks A4 as a *supporting* direction rather than a gate.
- *The one thing only this perspective says*: A1's fusion bet (gaze + online generation) adds a *second* learned perceptual stage on top of an already latency-bound diffusion generator (G1 WBC-Gen+Track runs a 0.5 s receding-horizon diffusion reference) — nobody in the card reports the *combined* onboard inference budget. The fusion could be a paper that never closes the real-time loop on hardware. Demand a wall-clock latency budget before committing.

**2. ACADEMIC (knows the peer-reviewed literature).**
- *Core position*: The cluster is bibliographically *over-determined*: each direction is anchored on 2025–2026 arXiv preprints, several from the same labs (Shanghai AI Lab on A3 HoST/HUMANUP; HIT on A1 "Now You See That" and T-GMP), and the "flat RL fails" premise rests almost entirely on one benchmark (HumanoidBench 2403.10506) cited across all five directions. The shared-bottleneck thesis is real but the *evidence base is narrow and incestuous* — a reviewer will note the field's own survey (2501.02116, Humanoid Locomotion and Manipulation: Current Progress and Challenges, DeepMind/CMU/Stanford) already frames perception+control+learning as one problem.
- *Strongest supporting evidence*: The card's strongest academic move is that it correctly distinguishes *settled* from *open* in each direction (A1: perceptive-beats-blind is settled — PRIOR-Loco 100% traversal, Deep WB Parkour 100% OOD; A5: off-policy-beats-PPO is settled — Parallel Q-Learning 2023, SAC Legged Locomotion "closes the PPO gap entirely"). That is honest literature-reading. The contradiction it under-plays: T-GMP (2606.06944, alphaxiv-confirmed) *already* does terrain-conditioned generative motion priors via a CVAE decoder conditioned on the live height map — a generation-alone near-miss the card does not cite, narrowing A1's "no perceptive policy generates online" claim.
- *The one thing only this perspective says*: "Reference/constraint structure is the lever" is not a novel framing — it is the standard mimic/AMP paradigm restated. The cluster's actual academic contribution is *negative-space mapping* (which combination cells are unfilled), not a new principle; frame the publications as "we filled cell X," not "we discovered the lever."

**3. SKEPTIC (thinks the bet is wrong or already solved).**
- *Core position*: The shared bottleneck is a post-hoc umbrella stretched to cover five only-loosely-related bets. A4 (regulate heat/noise/force) and A5 (flow vs Gaussian, fast iteration) are *not* about "balance under partial observation" at all — A4 is multi-objective cost regulation, A5 is RL-optimizer plumbing. The card even concedes the connective tissue is mechanical: "A5 supplies the training substrate every other A-direction trains on" is true of *every* RL paper ever and is not a shared *bottleneck*.
- *Strongest supporting evidence*: A5's own thesis admits "off-policy-beats-PPO is settled background" and "the contrarian premium is gone" — so A5's live bet (flow beats Gaussian on multimodal contact) is a *training-method* question with zero dependence on partial observation or terrain. A4's Olaf (2512.16705) already co-regulates heat+noise in one real-hardware policy, and A4's thesis retreats to a *very* narrow wedge (the heat∧noise∧**force** triple under a *threshold-conditioned* head with a mapped cross-cost Pareto) — a wedge so specific it reads as gap-hunting, not bottleneck-driven. The "lever" that unifies A1/A2/A3 (reference structure) is genuinely different from the "lever" in A4 (cost head) and A5 (off-policy replay).
- *The one thing only this perspective says*: The real coherent cluster is A1+A2+A3 (the reference-structure trio: generate-against-terrain / project-onto-feasible / scaffold-the-sparse-reward — all three are "what reference do you track and how do you make it feasible"). A4 and A5 are *cross-cutting substrates* that belong in every cluster, not members of this one. The honest move is to demote A4/A5 to cross-cutting themes and tighten the bottleneck statement to the reference trio.

**4. ECONOMIST (follows money/incentives).**
- *Core position*: This cluster is shaped by the Unitree G1 becoming the field's cheap standard platform — the entire cluster is "what can you publish on a G1 this year," and the incentive is *benchmark-and-platform-chasing*, not problem-importance. Every headline result is a single-platform SR on a hand-built obstacle course because there is no saturated shared benchmark (the card itself repeatedly notes "no named suite," "course stays hand-built").
- *Strongest supporting evidence*: A1 alone cites ~30 papers, nearly all 2026 preprints on G1, racing the same "highest box / widest gap" leaderboard (TAGA 120 cm gap "+50% over prior max"; APEX 0.8 m platform "114% of leg length"). This is a classic pre-saturation land-grab: cheap hardware + no canonical benchmark = a flood of single-number-SOTA papers, which is exactly the regime where citation incentives reward *novel cell* claims over *robust* ones. A4/A5 exist because they are *fundable adjacent niches* (thermal safety sells to industry; fast-iteration sells the consumer-GPU 4090 narrative — Humanoid Loco 15min, FlashNav 15–25 s).
- *The one thing only this perspective says*: The window is closing. Once a canonical perceptive-humanoid benchmark lands (GenTe 2504.09997 and RDT-Bench are early candidates the card cites), the single-SR land-grab saturates like LIBERO/RoboTwin did, and the A1 fusion bet's value collapses to "+2 pp on a solved benchmark." Commit *now* and publish before the benchmark freezes, or the novelty evaporates.

**5. HISTORIAN (has seen prior waves).**
- *Core position*: This is the quadruped perceptive-locomotion wave of 2020–2022 (ANYmal, Lee et al., Cheetah parkour, DTC) replayed on bipeds with diffusion swapped in for the gait library — the cluster is at the exact stage quadrupeds were when "blind-vs-perceptive" had just been settled and the field turned to "what perceptual interface." The reference/constraint-structure thesis is the AMP/DeepMimic lineage (2018–2021) re-applied; "flat RL fails, structure is the lever" is the same lesson DeepMimic taught with mocap references.
- *Strongest supporting evidence*: A1 explicitly recapitulates the quadruped arc — LocoTransformer (2107.03996, the depth+proprio quadruped precedent), Perceptive Internal Model (2411.14386, perception-in-the-estimator) and AME-2 (2601.08485, attention-over-elevation-map on ANYmal-D) are the quadruped-era interfaces now ported to G1. The fixed-scan-vs-learned-attention debate A1 stages is the *same* debate the quadruped community had about height-scan vs learned terrain encoders. A5's flow-vs-Gaussian echoes the pixel-vs-latent / Gaussian-vs-mixture cycles, and A5 itself dates the off-policy-vs-PPO debate to 2023 (Parallel Q-Learning).
- *The one thing only this perspective says*: In the quadruped wave, the "what to look at" interface question (A1's bet) did *not* produce a durable winner — the field converged on "just use the full elevation map + a decent encoder, scale compute" because compute got cheap faster than clever-attention paid off. The A1 gaze-saves-65% efficiency win (TAGA) is the same efficiency argument that lost to brute-force scan in quadrupeds. Bet on the *capability* frontier (what skills become feasible), not the *efficiency* frontier (cheaper perception), because history says efficiency gets bulldozed by compute.

### A1 (Direction review)

Topic: A perceptive humanoid-locomotion policy that *fuses* online terrain-conditioned reference generation (G1 WBC-Gen+Track 2604.17335-style diffusion) with a *learned active-gaze* module (TAGA 2606.05880-style where-to-look) — the bet being that the fused policy beats either alone, concentrating its win on sparse-foothold tasks at fixed compute and exhibiting an interior-optimal reference horizon under perturbation.

The verbatim bet (First-principles): "A policy that *fuses* online reference generation ([[2604.17335|G1 WBC-Gen+Track]]-style) with learned active gaze ([[2606.05880|TAGA]]-style) beats both gaze-alone and generation-alone, and concentrates its win where the height-scan is sparse: on stepping-stone / wide-gap tasks (≥70 cm spacing) the fused policy holds ≥0.95 SR at ≤40% of the full-scan compute, while on dense rough terrain it ties full-scan within 2 pp; and there is an *interior-optimal reference horizon* — SR is non-monotone in horizon under ~0.5 m perturbation (PHP-class)."

#### Prompt 1 — Five Perspectives

**1. PRACTITIONER (builds/deploys G1 policies).**
- *Core position*: The two pillars work on real hardware *separately* and the fusion is buildable, but the headline claim ("≤40% of full-scan compute") is the wrong axis to optimize — on a real G1 the bottleneck is depth/LiDAR-to-height-map latency and elevation-map noise, not height-scan FLOPs, which are already cheap. TAGA's 65.2% figure is *training* cost, not deploy compute, so the bet's compute-budget framing risks measuring something the robot doesn't feel.
- *Strongest evidence*: TAGA (2606.05880) explicitly reports "65.2% lower **training** cost" (KH note, Results), not inference. DPL (2510.07152) measures the real cost — "~20 ms perception latency" for depth→height-map reconstruction — which is the latency that actually gates a 3.41 m/s vault. RPL (2602.03002) ships at "50 Hz onboard" with multi-view depth, showing the deployable interface is coverage + render speed, not scan sparsity.
- *Only-this-perspective*: Re-baseline the compute claim on *wall-clock inference latency and elevation-map-noise robustness*, not training FLOPs or scan-FLOPs; otherwise H1/H3's win may be real in sim and invisible on the robot. And budget for the integration tax: a diffusion generator at 0.5 s receding horizon (2604.17335) plus an active-gaze ROI predictor plus a PPO tracker is three networks in the 50 Hz loop — the generator's own latency may eat the gaze savings.

**2. ACADEMIC (knows the peer-reviewed literature).**
- *Core position*: The card's central empirical pillars are real and correctly cited, but two framing claims are softer than stated: "perceptive-beats-blind is settled" is true, yet "no perceptive policy decides where to look" is *already* contradicted by a cluster of learned-attention papers the card itself lists (AME-2, GLAD, FocusNav), so the genuine whitespace is narrower — it is *generation × learned-gaze in one policy*, not "learned gaze" at all.
- *Strongest evidence*: GLAD (2606.00637) already "couples selective attention to compute reduction" via state-conditioned sparsification (D_attn 0.137 m, 1.00-day training vs AME 1.36); FocusNav (2601.12790) already does *variable-horizon* gaze via Stability-Aware Selective Gating (87.02% vs Gallant 50.32%) — i.e. H3's "truncate distal terrain" and even part of H2's "horizon adapts under instability" are partially demonstrated. AME-2 (2601.08485) gives learned attention with uncertainty (95.2% vs 51.2% AME-1).
- *Only-this-perspective*: The contribution must be framed as the *interaction effect* (does learned gaze help *more* when the reference is generated than when it is fixed?), because each marginal axis is occupied. The clean, publishable result is the 2×2 (gaze {fixed, learned} × reference {none, generated}) showing super-additivity — anything less is incremental.

**3. SKEPTIC (thinks it is wrong or already solved).**
- *Core position*: The "unclaimed cell" is an artifact of slicing the design space finely enough that any new combination looks empty; the *mechanism* the bet relies on (focus perception where footholds are sparse + synthesize a reference for the obstacle) is already jointly approximated by existing systems, and the super-additivity hypothesis (H1) has a strong null: a *good full-scan generation policy already implicitly learns to ignore irrelevant scan regions* inside its encoder, so learned gaze adds nothing it doesn't already get for free.
- *Strongest evidence*: T-GMP (2606.06944, uncited, confirmed via alphaxiv get_paper_content) is terrain-conditioned *generative* (CVAE) motion priors fused into RL with a foothold penalty — it deepens the generation pillar beyond the card's diffusion row, learning a *terrain-conditioned motion manifold* from only ~29.6 min of data, and still wins traversal SR with a *fixed local height patch and no gaze*. GLAD's state-conditioned sparsification is exactly "the encoder learns to drop irrelevant scan regions." So H1's falsifier ("generation-alone at full scan matches the fused policy") is a live, plausible outcome, not a strawman.
- *Only-this-perspective*: Pre-register H1's falsifier as the *primary* result and power the experiment to detect a *null*, because the most likely honest finding is "fusion ties generation-alone within noise on the metrics that matter." If you can't show super-additivity, you have a systems paper, not a science result.

**4. ECONOMIST (follows money/incentives).**
- *Core position*: This direction rides a 2026 humanoid-locomotion publication wave dominated by Unitree G1 demos (TAGA, GLAD, FocusNav, PHP, G1 WBC-Gen+Track, T-GMP all on G1/Kuavo), where the incentive is "new SOTA box-height / gap-distance on a recognizable platform," which rewards a fusion-demo paper but punishes the careful null-result the Skeptic wants. The benchmark substrate (BeamDojo, GenTe, RDT-Bench) is fragmenting, so there is no saturated leaderboard to beat — which is both the opening and the risk.
- *Strongest evidence*: The card's own targets are an arms race of single numbers (TAGA 120 cm gap "+50% over prior max"; PHP 1.25 m wall; G1 WBC 0.962 box-climb), and the related-work table lists ~30 papers in <12 months, most G1, most claiming a new max — a classic crowded-demo market. No single accepted benchmark: H1 uses BeamDojo (2502.10363), H3 uses GenTe (2504.09997), Now-You-See-That uses RDT-Bench — fragmentation means a fusion claim is hard to adjudicate against priors.
- *Only-this-perspective*: The cheap, defensible win is *the benchmark*, not the policy: a standardized sparse-foothold-vs-dense suite with a *compute-normalized* SR axis would let the fusion claim be judged and would be cited regardless of whether fusion wins — de-risking the bet against the saturation it is exposed to. **5. HISTORIAN (has seen prior waves).**
- *Core position*: This is the legged-locomotion replay of two old cycles at once — (a) the planner-generated-reference vs end-to-end-reactive swing (DCM/LIPM footstep planners → learned references → end-to-end depth policies → now *generated* references again), and (b) the passive-full-perception vs active-foveated-perception cycle that ran in active vision and SLAM decades ago. #### Prompt 3 — Synthesis Briefing

**CEO summary.** A4 bets that the three embodiment costs that kill real legged-robot deployments — motor heat, foot-impact noise, and ground-reaction force — can be regulated *jointly* by one threshold-conditioned cost-head that sweeps a Pareto front, and that a genuine thermal-vs-acoustic trade-off (quieter ⇒ hotter) exists and *requires* such a head to map. The premise is rock-solid and every cited axis is individually demonstrated, but the contribution rests almost entirely on two things the literature has nearly cornered: the conditional-Pareto *mechanism* is a saturating 2026 subfield (QuietPaw, PCHC, AMOR, plus generic MORL machinery), and Olaf already co-regulates heat+noise in one real policy. The defensible, publishable core is narrow but real: the *force* axis in the joint head (nobody has it) and the *measured* thermal-vs-acoustic cross-cost curve (nobody has mapped it). The danger is not that it's wrong — it's that the wedge is thin, the clock is short, and the headline ("conditioning is *necessary*") is likely refutable by a fixed-weight weight-sweep.

**Key findings, ranked by reliability.**
1. *Embodiment costs are distinct, deployment-fatal, individually-regulable levers.* (Supported by all five; challenged by none.) Most reliable — it is the uncontested premise, evidenced by Thermal-Aware Residual 70%→<10%, QuietWalk 7.17 dBA, QuietPaw cost-violation 0.107, IMF 35% peak-power.
2. *The conditional-Pareto-sweep mechanism is mature and saturating, so novelty must come from the physical triple, not the head.* (Academic, Skeptic, Historian; Practitioner neutral.) High reliability — QuietPaw/PCHC/AMOR plus alphaxiv-surfaced 2602.07764, 2605.08946 confirm a crowded mechanism layer.
3. *Heat+noise joint co-regulation in one real policy is already shipped (Olaf), making the wedge force-axis + cross-cost-measurement.* (Skeptic strongest; Academic, Card.) High reliability — Olaf's 77.3 °C/1 hr + −13.5 dB on hardware is directly in scope; the card concedes only the fixed-weight/separate-eval/no-GRF gaps remain.
4. *H5's strong form ("only a conditional head can map the front") is likely false; the head's real advantage is amortization, not capability.* (Skeptic; Historian aligned.) Medium-high — grounded in AMOR's post-training weight-sweep tracing the front; this directly threatens the headline and is cheap to test.
5. *The central H5 measurement may be infeasible at claimed fidelity because heat and noise live on incommensurable timescales.* (Practitioner alone; partially Economist via measurement-rig cost.) Medium — plausible and serious, but contingent on rollout/thermal-sim choices; mitigable with a validated thermal model.

**Hidden cross-perspective connection.** The Practitioner's timescale objection and the Skeptic's amortization objection are the same coin: heat is *slow*, so observing the thermal-vs-acoustic trade-off needs many long rollouts regardless of architecture — which is *exactly* when a conditional head (no per-operating-point retraining) earns its keep. The conditional head's true value proposition isn't "maps a front fixed weights can't" (false), it's "maps the *slow-cost* front at 1× training instead of N× retrainings" — reframing H4/H5 around amortization-under-slow-costs turns the Skeptic's and Practitioner's attacks into the paper's actual thesis.

**Actionable insight for the researcher — DE-RISK, then commit.** Do not commit to the headline as written ("conditional head is *necessary* to map the front") — the Skeptic refutes it. *Concrete next experiment (kills the project in ~2 weeks of sim if it's dead)*: in simulation only, on a single Unitree platform, train (a) the threshold-conditioned joint head over heat+noise+GRF and (b) an Olaf-style fixed-weight composite *swept* over ~8 weight vectors, and plot both thermal-vs-acoustic fronts. Decision gate: if (a) and (b) trace the *same* front, pivot the headline from "necessity" to "amortization: 1× vs N× training to obtain the deployment cost-front" AND make the *measured curve* (where it bends, force-axis included) the contribution; if the front is *flat*, DROP (H5's falsifier fired, the cross-cost coupling doesn't exist); if (a) holds both bounds at ≤5% SR loss where (b) cannot, COMMIT hard and race to publish. In parallel, own the gap nobody fills: define the thermal-acoustic-force cost *benchmark* (per-surface, hour-scale) and characterize the *feasibility boundary* of the threshold space (the blind spot) — that benchmark + the GRF axis is the durable, defensible artifact even if the head loses.

**Frontier question.** Is there a *universal* shape to the embodiment-cost Pareto manifold across legged platforms — i.e., does the quieter-⇒-hotter trade-off (and its force coupling) bend at a predictable, hardware-explainable point — such that one could *predict* a robot's deployable cost-envelope from its actuator/thermal/contact parameters without retraining a policy per platform? If that manifold transfers, embodiment-cost regulation becomes a calibration problem, not a per-robot RL problem.

Finding #2 (matched-supervision contrast partially run). The load-bearing premise is that TERT's 60%-vs-0% is a *matched-supervision backbone swap*. #### Prompt 1 — Five Perspectives

**1. PRACTITIONER (builds/deploys policies daily).**
- *Position*: The bet is operationally backwards-loaded. The hard cost in few-shot real adaptation is not the algorithm but collecting *clean, comparable* 5-trajectory budgets across multiple held-out terrains on real hardware without bricking the robot — and a latent dreamer adds an онboard-unfriendly RSSM ensemble + fine-tuning loop on top.
- *Evidence*: DayDreamer (2206.14176) needed 1 hr real to walk and 10 min to push-recover from scratch with *no* sim — the practitioner's existence proof that the real bottleneck is wall-clock and falls, not multiplier theory. LoopSR (2409.17992) and Simulator-Adaptation (2604.11090) both deliver at <5 min real, proprioception-only, no mocap — the deploy-friendliest baselines.

Budget-matching on *input real data* understates the dreamer's deployment friction.

**2. ACADEMIC (knows the peer-reviewed literature).**

NEUBAY (2512.04341) independently reports explicit uncertainty penalties *degrade* performance vs adaptive truncation — a second crack in the "penalty bounds it" story.
- *Only-this-perspective*: H2 as written ("penalty vs truncation vs conformal-filtering") presumes the *signal* (latent epistemic uncertainty) is informative. Biased Dreams says the signal itself may be corrupt in exactly the RSSM/Dreamer family DreamTIP is built on. The honest H2 must first *validate* that latent uncertainty tracks held-out physical error before sweeping how to penalize it.

**3. SKEPTIC (thinks it's wrong or already solved).**

LoopSR already reports 100% Stair-Up and *generalizes* because a rebuilt sim can be re-randomized over held-out geometry — the very mechanism the card assumes dreaming wins on. Biased Dreams supplies the ignored evidence: the bound the card leans on may not exist for latent models.
- *Only-this-perspective*: The falsifier may already be half-met. If reconstruct-then-retrain can regenerate held-out terrain B in sim for free, the dreamer's claimed "generalizes across the latent" advantage has to overcome an opponent with *unlimited* synthetic held-out data. The bet quietly assumes the reconstructed sim "overfits the rebuilt geometry" — but a digital twin is parameterized, not geometry-frozen.

**4. ECONOMIST (follows money/incentives).**
- *Position*: The "dreaming" narrative is favored because it is publishable and compute-light relative to foundation-WM scaling, and it rides the Dreamer brand — but the incentive structure pushes toward sim-heavy benchmark wins, not the expensive real A→B held-out study the bet actually requires.

- *Position*: This is the pixel-vs-latent and model-based-RL cycle replaying on legged hardware. ##### 4. Missing perspective (a 6th angle that would change conclusions)

**The Statistician / experiment-design referee.** Every claim in this briefing — the multiplier, the bound, the held-out margin, the attractor replication — turns on detecting a *difference* between arms (DR-blind / reconstruct / dream) on a binary or near-binary success metric, from ≤5 real trajectories, on a handful of terrains.
