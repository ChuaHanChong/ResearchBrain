# Promising Research Directions: World Action Models

> [!abstract] Overview
> A World Action Model imagines a future *and* commits to an action in one model, so its central tension is that the imagined state has to be represented *somewhere* — and every substrate choice trades fidelity against the latency and OOD-robustness a policy actually needs. These **eight directions across three clusters** organize the field's open bets around that tension: the *substrate and encoding* of the imagined state (Cluster A — what the latent is, how dense it is, what modalities it spans), the *training-time grounding* that keeps imagination honest enough to act on (Cluster B — contact discreteness, self-evolution, calibration, and data synthesis), and whether the WAM's *action space itself* is complete (Cluster C — a third action category for information-seeking, distinct from task-progress).
> The non-consensus thesis the doc collectively bets: a WAM's imagination is not one fixed thing to be optimized — train density is independent of deploy density (A1), the encoder objective outweighs the latent-vs-pixel question (A3), contact physics needs *discrete* structure no smooth latent reaches (B1), the imagination's most durable product is a *training corpus*, not an in-episode rollout (B4), and some ambiguities are not solved by imagining or verifying harder — only a real action taken to collect new evidence closes them (C1). The substrate is task-conditional, the imagination is a verifiable surface, and now, sometimes, a probe.

## Methodology

**Scope.** This doc reads ~22 pure-WAM and adjacent surveys plus ~274 WAM-method and benchmark papers from `_KnowledgeHub_/` (the Survey Landscape below names the surveys/benchmarks; the cards cite the methods), cross-checked against [[../../../General/12_Benchmarks-and-Surveys|12_Benchmarks-and-Surveys]] and the deep-dives [[../../../Embodied-AI/06_WAM|06_WAM]], [[../../../Embodied-AI/07_Latent-World-Models|07_Latent-World-Models]], [[../../../Embodied-AI/16_Self-Evolving-VLA-WAM|16_Self-Evolving-VLA-WAM]], [[../../../Embodied-AI/08_Physics-Aware-Embodied-AI|08_Physics-Aware-Embodied-AI]], and [[../../../Embodied-AI/15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer|15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer]]. It owns the WAM-internal *substrate* and its *training-time grounding*: the latent / architecture / encoding choices for the imagined state (Cluster A) and the losses and data strategies that keep imagination aligned with physics (Cluster B). Five model-spanning directions the umbrella covers (joint WAM–policy co-evolution, physics-consistency verification, joint causal-consistency evaluation, real-time deployment, cross-embodiment transfer) and the model-agnostic 3D/4D geometric substrate are cross-referenced, not re-clustered — see Cross-References.

> - **Imagination violates physics, and visual fidelity does not predict executability** (6-way): [[2503.21765|Physics-Cognition-Survey]], [[2510.04978|Physical-AI-Survey]], [[2501.10928|Generative-Physical-AI-Survey]], and [[2601.15533|Actionable-Simulators]] all diagnose dynamical hallucinations and converge on verifiable / neuro-symbolic physics, [[2604.19092|RoboWM-Bench]] makes it empirical — visual plausibility ≠ executability, measured — and [[2608.09298|WorldSimProbe]] decomposes the same gap into named, separately-failing stages (grounding collapses under appearance-induced false contact, shake dynamics near-zero while tap peaks) — the mandate for B1's discrete contact structure and B3's train-time calibration.
> - **The WAM's most underexploited output is training data, not a rollout** (3-way): [[2605.12090|WAM-Survey]] (names "data-ecosystem mixing" as open), [[2604.15395|Foundation-Models-in-Robotics-Survey]] (names dataset/challenge mapping as the integration gap), [[2601.15533|Actionable-Simulators]] (names structured, decision-oriented simulation that *produces* usable data) — three surveys point at the same underexploited product, the mandate for B4's data engine.
> - **Self-evolution and runtime verification are unbuilt as an integrated loop** (4-way): [[2604.22748|Agentic-World-Modeling-Survey]] (its L3 Evolver is "emerging not mature"), [[2602.04411|Self-evolving-Embodied-AI]] (multi-timescale closed-loop co-evolution unbuilt), [[2508.07407|Self-Evolving-AI-Agents-Survey]] (self-improvement without forgetting unresolved), [[2604.23775|VLA-Safety-Survey]] (imagination as an adversarial/jailbreak surface that must be verified) — four surveys name the pieces, none names the integrated detect→imagine→recover→verify loop, the mandate for B2.

## Formal Framing

**The world-action joint objective.** A WAM is defined — per [[2605.12090|WAM-Survey]] — as an embodied foundation model that predicts a *joint* distribution over the next observation and the action, not the action alone:

> "WAMs are defined as embodied foundation models that integrate predictive state modeling with action generation, moving beyond merely predicting actions to predicting a joint distribution over future states and actions." — [[2605.12090|WAM-Survey]]

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{WAM}} = \mathbb{E}_{(o,l,o',a) \sim \mathcal{D}} \big[ -\log p(o', a \mid o, l) \big]$$

The family splits by what each model predicts:

| Family | Distribution | Predicts |
|---|---|---|
| **VLA** | $p(a \mid o, l)$ | Action only; no dynamics |
| **WM** | $p(o' \mid o, a)$ | Dynamics only; no action |
| **WAM** | $p(o', a \mid o, l)$ | Both; the unifying frontier |

WAMs further split into **Cascaded** (predict the state, then derive the action by inverse dynamics) vs **Joint** (one end-to-end model). The joint-vs-cascaded *optimization* question lives in the umbrella; this doc keeps the WAM-internal fact that whatever the optimizer, the imagined state $o'$ has to be *represented* — and that representation choice is what Cluster A attacks.

**The spatial-representation axis.** [[2510.16732|World-Models-for-Embodied-AI-Survey]] gives the canonical axis the substrate choice moves along:

> "The world models are categorized along three axes: Functionality (Decision-Coupled vs General-Purpose), Temporal Modeling (Sequential Simulation vs Global Difference Prediction), and Spatial Representation (Global Latent Vector, Token Feature Sequence, Spatial Latent Grid, Decomposed Rendering Representation)." — [[2510.16732|World-Models-for-Embodied-AI-Survey]]

Reading this axis as a *design space* rather than an evolution is A1's move: a model can be trained at the rendering end (dense 3D supervision) yet deployed at the latent-vector end (cheap rollout). Two quantities make this measurable — **train density** $\rho_{\text{train}}$ (the supervision signal's richness) and **deploy density** $\rho_{\text{deploy}}$ (the rollout representation's cost) — and the doc's first claim is that they are independent.

**The capability hierarchy.** [[2604.22748|Agentic-World-Modeling-Survey]] gives the canonical levels Cluster B's grounding directions target:

> "We introduce three capability levels: L1 Predictor, which learns one-step local transition operators; L2 Simulator, which composes them into multi-step, action-conditioned rollouts that respect domain laws; and L3 Evolver, which autonomously revises its own model when predictions fail against new evidence." — [[2604.22748|Agentic-World-Modeling-Survey]]

The physical-law L3 Evolver is the target for B2's self-evolution loop; the survey's decision-centric metrics — **ASR** (Action Success Rate) and **COD** (Counterfactual Outcome Deviation) — anchor the joint causal-consistency evaluation that lives in the umbrella. The recurring quantity across B2/B3 is the **imagined-vs-real success-rate correlation** $\rho$ (Pearson): a WAM whose imagined outcomes track real ones ($\rho$ high) can train a policy on dreams; a WAM whose imagination diverges cannot.

**The identifiability criterion.** [[2605.26379|LeJEPA-World-Model]] supplies the formal test for *when* a learned latent is a world model:

> "[[2511.08544|LeJEPA]] achieves linear identifiability — recovering true latent variables up to an orthogonal transformation — if and only if the underlying latent variables follow an isotropic Gaussian distribution." — [[2605.26379|LeJEPA-World-Model]]

This gives A1's substrate and A3's encoding a membership test: a latent is identifiable iff isotropic-Gaussian, at which point latent-space planning matches an oracle controller ($R^2 > 0.999$ to 1024 dims). A3's sharpest question is whether the encodings that *win control* are exactly the ones that *pass this test*.

## Cluster A — WAM Substrate & Encoding

*The imagined state has to live somewhere — a latent vector, a token grid, a rendered 3D scene, or a wrench trajectory. The three directions attack the same representation question from different axes: how dense the substrate is at train vs deploy (A1), which modality it spans (A2), and what its latent is trained to encode once dense-vs-sparse is fixed (A3).*

### A1 — Hybrid Latent+Pixel WAM Architectures

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Substrate & Encoding |
| **Thesis** | Train a WAM on dense supervision but roll out on a cheap latent at deployment — and ask which *form* of dense signal buys the OOD. The reason it must work: train density and deploy density are independent, the way a human rehearses in full detail but acts on a compressed prediction. The drop-the-dense-head mechanism is now established ([[2503.00200\|UVA]]); the field assumes the dense head's form is interchangeable and so never runs the density sweep. [[2606.19531\|ImageWAM]] widens the question — its 83.1% [[2510.13626\|LIBERO-Plus]] from a single *edited image* (not a video, not 3DGS) shows the dense signal need not even be temporal — so the sweep is over {3DGS, 2D-video, single-edited-image} forms. The bet is in First-principles below — a 3DGS head buys OOD a matched 2D-video head does not. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2510.16732\|World-Models-for-Embodied-AI-Survey]] (survey), [[2511.02097\|WM-Manipulation-Survey]] (survey), [[2604.19092\|RoboWM-Bench]] (benchmark), [[2605.20752\|GaussianDream]] (method), [[2604.16484\|DexWorldModel]] (method), [[2604.25859\|PFD]] (method), [[2606.17046\|GAM]] (method) |
| **Key targets** | Match [[2605.20752\|GaussianDream]]'s 98.4% [[2306.03310\|LIBERO]] / 34.4→50% real at lower deploy cost than a pixel WAM (its own 531 ms/chunk reference); keep pure-latent OOD retention on [[2510.13626\|LIBERO-Plus]] from dense co-training; latent ~10 ms vs pixel ~150 ms forward latency; [[2604.25859\|PFD]] 98.10%; [[2608.02365\|Faster-WAM]] is now the sharpest joint latency+OOD reference at 66.5 ms + 75.0% [[2510.13626\|LIBERO-Plus]] |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a WAM must imagine a future to plan, but a pixel/video substrate is robust-yet-slow ([[../../../Embodied-AI/06_WAM#6. Efficient & Action-Centered WAMs|06_WAM §6]] finds VideoGen 4.8× slower but most robust) while a pure latent is fast-yet-opaque, and single-paradigm WAMs are stuck on that trade-off.
- **Today's answers**: the drop-the-dense-head-at-deploy *mechanism* is already shown — [[2503.00200|UVA]] co-trains a dense video head with a latent action head in one backbone, drops the video head at inference, decodes action-only at action-policy speed (0.23 s vs 0.22 s Diffusion Policy), and gains **+40%** real-world multi-task OOD (0.90 Libero10); [[2504.02792|UWM]] confirms the same joint-train/deploy-light recipe (action-free-video co-training lifts OOD 0.76→0.84). [[2605.20752|GaussianDream]] supervises a renderable 3D-Gaussian future at train time then drops the auxiliary heads at inference (98.4% [[2306.03310|LIBERO]], 34.4→50% real, 531 ms/chunk); [[2604.16484|DexWorldModel]] uses semantic [[2508.10104|DINOv3]] latents as prediction targets (94% [[2504.13059|RoboTwin]], zero-shot sim-to-real). The generic recipe works — but each is a *single point* on the train-density/deploy-density plane, and none isolates the [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]] OOD margin a renderable-3DGS dense head buys over a matched 2D-video dense head at the same in-dist SR. [[2608.04996|DreamWAM]] now runs the closest published approximation of that sweep *inside one backbone*, a four-way internal ablation across appearance/motion/geometry/semantics (LIBERO-Plus 69.16%→75.47%), though internal to one architecture rather than a controlled cross-form swap. [[2607.04927|DSWAM]] now supplies the missing *matched-condition real-robot* WAM-vs-VLA comparison this cluster's density argument has lacked — its WAM executor (dense video-prediction training, action-only deploy) beats a strong VLA baseline (DeMaVLA) head-to-head under identical platform/data/protocol (96.3% vs 92.5% real deformable-manipulation SR, −34 s completion time, 73.8 ms TensorRT latency), though the drop-the-video-head mechanism itself restates UVA's recipe rather than adding a new dense-form point.
- **The opening**: [[2606.05254|Flash-WAM]]'s modality-aware distillation cuts a two-stage WAM 8.1 s → 348 ms (23×) while keeping 81.41% of the 91.25% teacher's [[2504.13059|RoboTwin]] SR — direct proof that deploy density can be cut hard without dropping the WM, so the hybrid recipe has measured headroom.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Train density and deploy density are independent quantities. A model can learn from pixel/3DGS-dense signal yet act on latent-dense signal, because the supervision that shapes the weights and the representation that carries the rollout are decoupled — the dense branch is a *teacher* the deploy path need not retain. [[2605.20752|GaussianDream]] demonstrates the decoupling directly: its renderable-3D heads exist only at train time and are discarded at inference with no SR loss.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not the bare drop-head mechanism — [[2503.00200|UVA]] already owns "one backbone, dense head dropped at deploy, latent-only rollout, +40% OOD" and [[2504.02792|UWM]] confirms it, so that is settled. The live assumption is that the *form* of the dropped dense signal is interchangeable — that a 2D-video head and a renderable-3DGS head buy the same OOD, so nobody runs the density sweep. [[2605.20752|GaussianDream]] and [[2606.03188|GeoSem-WAM]] (geometry + semantic supervision on latent tokens, branches dropped at test; 98.55% [[2306.03310|LIBERO]], +6.6 pp real) hint a 3D-structured head differs, but neither isolates *how much* OOD the 3D structure buys over a 2D-video head at matched in-dist SR. A *third* pole now pressures the framing from the opposite side: [[2606.19531|ImageWAM]] argues the dense signal need not be a *video* at all — a single instruction-edited endpoint frame reaches 83.1% [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]] at −75% latency — so the density sweep should range over {3DGS, 2D-video, single-edited-image} dense forms, not 3DGS-vs-video alone, and "the dropped head must be a *video*" is itself an assumption ImageWAM breaks. A *prior* worth flagging cuts the other way: [[2603.01549|Pri4R]] ablates the dropped-head *form* on a neighbouring axis and finds a *dense* depth-map head (+8.3) *underperforms* a *sparse* metric-3D-point-track head (+13.2) — but those are RoboCasa SR deltas, not [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]] OOD-retention, and Pri4R itself shows 3D > 2D, so it argues "geometry, maybe sparse" not "video wins." It is a prior on the experiment's shape (add a sparse-3D arm), not evidence the renderable form loses on OOD.
- **The bet**: A renderable-3DGS dense head dropped at deploy beats a *matched-capacity 2D-video* dense head (the [[2503.00200|UVA]] form) on [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]] OOD retention by ≥5 pp *at matched in-distribution SR* (both held to ≥97.2%, the [[2602.10098|VLA-JEPA]] pure-latent reference), while keeping the latent-only deploy path under 2× pure-latent latency and far below the 4.8× pixel-WAM cost ([[2603.22078|WAM-vs-VLA-Robustness]]) — a controlled density sweep, not a single operating point. The 3D-structure × OOD margin, isolated against UVA's 2D-video head, is the only non-consensus quantity left. Interpretability (the dense branch stays inspectable) is a qualitative aside, not part of the falsifiable claim.

**Related research papers.**

Fifty-five systems that put the imagined state at a different point on the *train-density × deploy-density* plane — the axis the direction turns on. The discriminator is whether dense supervision is *retained* or *dropped* at deploy, and what the deploy representation is:

| System | Train substrate | Deploy substrate | Key result | What's missing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[2503.00200\|UVA]] | dense video head + latent action head (one backbone) | latent action, video head dropped | 0.90 Libero10, +40% real OOD, 0.23 s/step | the canonical drop-dense-head prior — but a 2D-*video* head, no 3DGS structure, no [[2510.13626\|LIBERO-Plus]] OOD curve |
| [[2504.02792\|UWM]] | unified video+action diffusion, action-free co-train | latent action | OOD 0.76→0.84 from video co-train, +20% DROID | confirms joint-train/deploy-light, but video-density only — the 2D-video baseline A1's 3DGS head must beat |

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (a hybrid backbone keeps GaussianDream-class real SR + pure-latent OOD at <2× latent latency), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 — A 3DGS dense head beats a matched 2D-video dense head on OOD at matched in-dist SR.**
   - *Prediction*: co-training [[2602.10098|VLA-JEPA]]'s latent backbone with a [[2605.20752|GaussianDream]]-style renderable-3DGS auxiliary head (dropped at inference) holds 97.2% [[2306.03310|LIBERO]] *and* lifts [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]] OOD ≥5 pp above the *same backbone with a matched-capacity [[2503.00200|UVA]]-style 2D-video head*, both deployed latent-only.
   - *Test*: four-arm A/B — no dense head / 2D-video head / 3DGS head / single-edited-image head, matched capacity; deploy latent-only; report in-dist SR + OOD retention + forward latency.
   - *Row*: GaussianDream (3DGS) vs UVA (2D-video head) vs [[2606.19531|ImageWAM]] (single-edited-image, 83.1% [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]]) vs VLA-JEPA (pure latent); add a *sparse metric-3D-track* arm ([[2603.01549|Pri4R]] form) so the sweep is multi-form, not 3DGS-vs-video alone.

2. **H2 — The deploy-density frontier is below 2× pure-latent latency.**

   - *Prediction*: [[2605.15153|Pelican-Unified]]'s shared z anchors a hybrid where imagination decodes to pixel/3DGS for inspection and action decodes to latent for speed, with no SR cost vs decoding both from the latent.

### A2 — Tactile/Force-Integrated WAM Imagination

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Substrate & Encoding |
| **Thesis** | Make a WAM forecast a *future 6-DoF wrench* (force + torque) as a rolled-forward output the policy acts on inside imagination — not a present-time force token consumed as input. The reason it must work: in contact, force is the generative *cause* and what you see is the *effect*, so the causal law lives in the future-wrench rollout, not in a consumed reading. **Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: current WAMs imagine visual and proprioceptive futures but rarely tactile/force futures, even though force is the dominant signal in contact-rich manipulation — [[2605.12090|WAM-Survey]] names the modality gap and [[2511.02097|WM-Manipulation-Survey]]'s 13 capabilities rank Multimodal Perception first, Physics Awareness third.
- **Today's answers**: two stronger claims than the card once held are now in. [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]] *predicts* a sensorless force token from vision+proprioception and feeds it to the policy at deploy — and beats the with-real-sensor baseline (61.1% vs 38.9%, vision-only floor 23.3%), so the "sensorless predicted force substitutes for a missing sensor" sub-claim is already exceeded. [[2604.13015|HTD]] (Touch Dreaming) goes further toward A2: it forecasts *future* per-joint tactile/force as a WM head and acts on it (+30.0 pp SR over ACT on five contact tasks). The rest still consume force as input — [[2603.15169|ForceVLA2]] (66%, prompt), [[2601.20321|TaF-VLA]] (60.3% cross-sensor), [[2603.15257|HapticVLA]] (distilled away) — and [[2512.23864|DreamTacVLA]] / [[2606.08737|Dream-Tac]] (83.3% over Cosmos-Policy's 51.7%) predict future tactile *images*, and [[2606.26663|Tactile-WAM]] now forecasts joint visuo-tactile *features* inside the rollout (44.7% sim / 51% real, +38.9 / +33 pp over an RGB-only WAM). Three papers now go further and predict a *structured future 6-DoF wrench*: [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] (85% full loop), [[2606.30988|MuSe]] (11.5/15 vase-wiping, routed into a fixed admittance controller), and [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]] (12/30→28/30, consumed as RL context) — but all three keep the sensor at deploy, and none has the policy's own action-selection reason directly over the imagined rollout. What survives as A2's target is the *sensorless* + *policy-reasons-over-the-rollout* combination, not the bare future-6-DoF-wrench claim.
- **The opening**: [[2603.17851|DexViTac]] proves tactile is *modelable*: kinematics-grounded tactile pretraining resolves the semantic ambiguity of raw touch (85.8% contact-rich SR), and the pretraining ablation (pipetting 83.3%→43.3%) shows the tactile representation is load-bearing — so a WAM head that *imagines* that representation has a tractable target, and [[2512.23864|DreamTacVLA]] now realizes exactly that head on tactile *images*, leaving the wrench (6-DoF force+torque) channel A2 targets still open.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: In contact, force is the cause and the observed motion is the effect — the object accelerates *because* of an applied wrench. A WM that rolls forward only the effect can never fully pin down the contact dynamics, because the causal variable is missing from the imagined future. The data precondition is now amply met: [[2603.17851|DexViTac]]'s ablation (83.3→43.3% without tactile pretraining) shows the tactile latent carries causal information vision lacks, [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]] shows that information is even *predictable* from vision+proprioception alone (sensorless 61.1% > with-sensor 38.9%), and [[2607.01684|TacImag]] closes the sensorless case further (its imagined TacFF force-field, 57.5% sim, *matches a physical tactile sensor* at 58.2%), so a wrench *rollout* head has a tractable, sensor-free target, not just a sensed one.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "force is never predicted" — [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]] predicts a *present-time* sensorless force token (and beats a real sensor), [[2604.13015|HTD]] forecasts *future* per-joint force, and [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] / [[2606.30988|MuSe]] / [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]] already forecast a *structured future 6-DoF wrench*, so the bare predict-force claim — and even the future-6-DoF-wrench claim — is taken. The live assumption is twofold: that the forecast is handed to a fixed control law ([[2606.08555|FAWAM]]'s residual corrector, [[2606.30988|MuSe]]'s admittance-controller target) or an outer refiner ([[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]]'s RL context) rather than reasoned over directly by the policy's own action-selection, and that a sensor stays present at deploy. Nobody breaks *both* — H1 attacks the sensor-presence half, H3 attacks the fixed-control-law-vs-policy-reasoning half, and no cited paper does either while holding future tense and 6-DoF structure constant.
- **The bet**: A WAM head that forecasts a *future 6-DoF wrench* (force+torque) as a rolled-forward WM output the policy acts on in imagination beats [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]]'s present-time force-*token* baseline (61.1%) by ≥5 pp on contact-rich SR with *no* force sensor at deploy, and beats a vision-only WAM by ≥50% of the [[2603.17851|DexViTac]] tactile drop (83.3%→43.3% pipetting, i.e. recovering the floor toward ~63%), approaching the with-tactile 85.8% ceiling. The three distinctions that survive the prior art — *future* tense, *6-DoF wrench* structure, and a *policy that reasons over a multi-step imagined rollout* rather than a fixed control law merely consuming the forecast as its target — are the load-bearing novelty; no single paper holds all three. [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] and [[2606.30988|MuSe]] both reach future-tense + 6-DoF + a controller-coupled use of the forecast, but neither has the policy's own action-selection reason over the rollout — that finer distinction is what H3 tests directly. Falsifiable: if the rolled-forward wrench ties FD-VLA's present-time token at matched SR, the tense+rollout role adds nothing over a consumed force token.

**Hypotheses & tests.** H1 is the headline claim — does either lever, architecture or training-signal, close the sensorless-to-sensored gap. H3 tests a *different* attribute of the FP bet entirely: whether the policy reasoning directly over the imagined rollout is itself load-bearing (over a fixed control law or outer-loop context), deliberately held sensor-status-independent (its foils, MuSe and TORL-VLA, are both sensored), so it neither confirms nor depends on H1's result. H4 is not a fourth independent claim; it *stratifies* H1's headline number by task regime rather than adding a new mechanism — and, per the falsifier discussion below, the closest available evidence currently argues against the naive "sensorless degrades in light-touch" prediction, so H4 is run as an open, contested question, not a directional confirmation. H2 and H5 are peripheral sub-questions (cross-sensor transfer; an inverted-Force-Prompting forecast) orthogonal to H1's number. Each item below is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (imagined wrench recovers ≥50% of the no-tactile drop at deploy), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 — A *sensorless* future-wrench forecast closes most of the gap to the *sensored* future-wrench ceiling, on either an architecture lever or a training-signal lever.**
   - *Prediction*: two independent routes to a sensorless wrench forecast, tested head-to-head, not conflated: **(a) architecture lever** — a 6-DoF wrench head that *rolls forward* (predicts $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{t+1..t+k}$ inside the WM, policy acts on the rollout) with *no* F/T sensor at any point, trained on [[2604.20444|VTouch++]]'s 36M synchronized frames and [[2506.14754|Sparsh-X]]'s 1M contacts; **(b) training-signal lever** — the same rolled-forward head, but supervised against *measured* wrench only at train time (sensor present in training, dropped at deploy). Each is predicted to beat [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]]'s present-time sensorless force-token 61.1% by ≥5 pp on contact-rich SR and to recover ≥50% of the gap up to [[2606.08555|FAWAM]]'s *sensored* future-wrench loop (85% full / 73.75% FEAM-alone base) — the live wedge is closing the sensored-FAWAM gap *without* a sensor at deploy, not beating FD-VLA alone, and the FP bet does not commit to which lever gets there.
   - *Test*: head-to-head on [[2505.18472|ManiFeel]]'s shared sim-to-real-validated suite (TacFF force-field / TacRGB tactile-image / vision-only) — condition (a) sensorless architecture-only rollout, condition (b) train-time-wrench-auxiliary vs an equal-capacity proprioception-only auxiliary (both sensorless at deploy), both scored against FD-VLA's present-time token and the sensored FAWAM ceiling on the same contact-task SR metric, so a reader can see whether either lever alone suffices or the two compose.
   - *Row*: FD-VLA (sensorless present-time token, baseline) vs [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] (sensored future-wrench, ceiling) vs DexViTac (perceives, 85.8% with-tactile ceiling); shared substrate ManiFeel. [[2607.28391|TacWAM]]'s AGT-gated train-time-only wrench-regularizer (75.0% avg, +37.5 pp over VT-WAM, ablation shows direct deploy-time future-tactile access *hurts*) is the sharpest existing instance of condition (b), though on a wrench-*regularized* tactile latent rather than a rolled-forward 6-DoF wrench. [[2505.13982|AdapTac]]'s Future Force Prediction ablation (Flip 50→90%) substantiates that a future-force auxiliary is measurably load-bearing, short of a full wrench-rollout head. [[2606.30988|MuSe]] is a *second* sensored future-wrench datapoint, but it isolates the coupling-tightness attribute (controller-target vs. policy-reasons-over-the-rollout), not the sensorless one — H3 tests that half.
   - *Falsifier*: neither lever closes the [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] gap sensorlessly → tense + rollout adds nothing sensorlessly, and A2 collapses to FD-VLA + sensored FAWAM. **Status**: both levers remain untested against each other and against the FAWAM ceiling; the coupling-tightness attribute (which lever (a) and (b) both share by construction, since both are architecture/training choices orthogonal to how the forecast is consumed) is tested separately and directly in H3.
2. **H2 — A tactile latent transfers across sensors as an imagination target.**
   - *Prediction*: using [[2601.20321|TaF-VLA]]'s VQ-VAE force latent (or [[2603.17851|DexViTac]]'s kinematics-grounded latent) as the WAM's *imagination target* and decoding per-sensor on demand beats TaF-VLA's 60.3% policy-side cross-sensor transfer.
   - *Test*: evaluate on [[2410.24090|Sparsh]]'s cross-sensor labeled protocol (DIGIT / GelSight / ReSkin force-estimation + slip detection, 95.1% avg improvement over end-to-end baselines) — train the WAM to imagine the shared force latent, decode to a held-out sensor, report cross-sensor SR; the same labeled force/slip ground truth backs H1's wrench-prediction accuracy.
   - *Row*: TaF-VLA (policy reads it); cross-sensor substrate Sparsh.
   - *Falsifier*: imagination-target transfer ≤ 60.3% → the latent is no better predicted than consumed.
3. **H3 — The policy's own action-selection reasoning over the imagined wrench rollout beats routing the same forecast through a fixed control law or consuming it as outer-loop context, independent of sensor status.**
   - *Prediction*: holding future-tense and 6-DoF-wrench-structure fixed — both [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]] and [[2606.30988|MuSe]] already forecast a *sensored* future 6-DoF wrench, so sensor status is not the variable here — three distinct couplings between forecast and executed action rank by *tightness*, and each rung up beats the one below at matched wrench-prediction accuracy: (a) [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]]'s consumed-as-context coupling (the forecast is read by an outer RL refiner alongside other inputs, no dedicated causal role) is weakest; (b) [[2606.30988|MuSe]]'s controller-target coupling (the forecast trajectory is routed into a fixed 6D admittance-control law that reacts to it) is stronger — MuSe's own ablation already shows this beats mere conditioning (disabling the controller-target use drops vase-wiping SR 11.5/15→8/15); (c) A2's own policy-reasons-over-the-rollout coupling — the WM's imagined wrench rollout is an input the *policy's own action-selection* reasons over directly, rather than being handed off to a separate fixed controller — is predicted strongest, because a fixed control law cannot adapt its response to what the rollout implies about upcoming task structure the way a learned policy conditioning on the same rollout can.
   - *Test*: three-arm A/B/C on a shared contact-rich task set — (A) [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]]'s consumed-as-context design, (B) [[2606.30988|MuSe]]'s controller-target design, (C) A2's own policy-reasons-over-the-rollout design — holding wrench-prediction accuracy matched across arms so the comparison isolates the coupling type, not forecast quality.
   - *Row*: [[2606.30988|MuSe]] (controller-target coupling, ablation-verified 11.5/15→8/15) vs [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]] (consumed-as-context, 12/30→28/30 via RL refinement); both are sensored, existing published foils for the third, unbuilt arm A2 proposes.
   - *Falsifier*: [[2606.30988|MuSe]]'s controller-target coupling matches or beats A2's policy-conditioned design at equal wrench accuracy → routing the forecast through a well-tuned fixed control law is as good as folding it into the policy's own decision process, and A2's rollout-role claim narrows to "the forecast must be causally coupled to the action, controller-style" rather than requiring the policy to reason over it directly; *or* all three arms tie → the coupling type is not itself load-bearing, and A2's rollout-role attribute collapses to "predicted at all," leaving sensorlessness (H1) as the wedge's only surviving axis.
4. **H4 (open) — Whether H1's sensorless gap-closing is uniform across contact-richness regimes, or concentrated in high-force tasks, is contested, not settled.**
   - *Prediction*: the Noise-floor risk's mechanical intuition (subtle slip/microvibration is not visually observable, so imagined force should plateau below measured) predicts H1's gap-closing is *stronger* on high-force, precise-alignment tasks (peg/USB insertion, gear assembly, [[2606.09337|TORL-VLA]]'s latch/coffee-cup insertion — where the load-bearing force is large and correlates with visible motion) than on light-touch, fragile-object tasks ([[2603.15257|HapticVLA]]'s jar/waffles/egg, [[2603.17851|DexViTac]]'s pipetting). But the closest available evidence cuts *against* that lean: [[2603.15257|HapticVLA]]'s sensor-free distilled student reaches **86.7%** on exactly this light-touch/fragile regime, *exceeding* its own sensored teacher (75% / 81.7%) — a token-distillation mechanism, not A2's future-wrench rollout, but the sharpest published datapoint on whether sensorless can match sensored in a fragile regime, and it says yes. (To be tested, not asserted:) the stratified experiment is run to *decide* between the noise-floor intuition and HapticVLA's counter-evidence, not to confirm the intuition.
   - *Test*: stratify H1's ManiFeel head-to-head (plus DexViTac's pipetting task) by task category — force-regulation/precise-alignment vs light-touch/fragile — and report the sensorless-to-sensored gap-closing fraction separately per stratum, not as one pooled number; treat HapticVLA's distillation-based result as a pre-registered counter-arm the wrench-forecast result must be compared against, not dismissed by mechanism difference alone. **Confound**: ManiFeel's own 13 tasks (insertion/screwing/exploration, per its KH note) carry no fragile-object category, so the light-touch stratum must be sourced from HapticVLA's and DexViTac's setups — different robots, grippers, and policies than ManiFeel's Franka+GelSight harness. This is a regime-*and*-setup confound, not a controlled within-suite comparison; mitigate by adding a matched fragile/delicate task inside the ManiFeel harness itself (e.g. a low-force object-handling task) before trusting the cross-suite comparison as more than suggestive.
   - *Row*: [[2505.18472|ManiFeel]] (TacFF +26 pp peg insertion — high-force stratum) vs [[2603.15257|HapticVLA]] (86.7% SR sensor-free on jar/waffles/egg, *beating* its own 75–81.7% sensored teacher — the counter-evidence, light-touch stratum) vs [[2603.17851|DexViTac]] (pipetting 83.3%→43.3% pretraining-ablation drop, both conditions sensored — the light-touch stratum's evidence that force *is* hard to learn well here, orthogonal to the sensorless question).
   - *Falsifier*: either extreme settles it — the gap-closing fraction is uniform across strata → the noise-floor intuition was unfounded and HapticVLA's result generalizes, so A2's claim can be reported as regime-independent; *or* the fraction collapses specifically in the light-touch stratum for a future-wrench-rollout mechanism (unlike HapticVLA's token distillation) → the noise-floor intuition holds for A2's specific architecture even though it does not hold for distillation, and the claim must carry the stratified caveat.
5. **H5 — Force-conditioned video prediction run backward yields a usable force forecast.**
   - *Prediction*: inverting [[2505.19386|Force-Prompting]] (predict force *from* frames, then condition the next step on the predicted force) gives a force forecast accurate enough to improve next-step manipulation over an unconditioned baseline.
   - *Test*: train the inverse predictor; condition rollout on predicted force; compare next-step SR vs unconditioned.
   - *Row*: Force Prompting (force→video).
   - *Falsifier*: predicted-force conditioning ties unconditioned → the inverse forecast is too noisy to act on.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Noise floor — but the closest evidence currently disagrees** — subtle slip and microvibration are not in vision, so imagined force may plateau below measured, but [[2603.15257|HapticVLA]]'s sensor-free student beats its own sensored teacher (86.7% vs 75–81.7%) on exactly the fragile regime this risk worries about. → H4 runs the stratified test as an open question, not a confirmation; report the gap-closing fraction per regime and let the result — not the intuition — decide whether the claim needs the stratified caveat.
> - **Cross-sensor brittleness** — 60.3% zero-shot ([[2601.20321|TaF-VLA]]) is not deployment-ready. → Use [[2603.17851|DexViTac]]'s kinematics grounding to stabilize the cross-sensor latent; H2 measures whether imagination-as-target beats policy-as-consumer.
> - **Force-prediction is now crowded — the wedge is tense+structure+role+sensorless, not "predict force at all"** — [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]] predicts present force (and beats a sensor), [[2604.13015|HTD]] forecasts future joint-force, [[2512.23864|DreamTacVLA]] / [[2606.08737|Dream-Tac]] predict future tactile images, [[2606.12406|FACTR-2]] estimates a present 6-DoF wrench, and [[2606.08555|FAWAM]] closes a *sensored* future-6-DoF-wrench loop (85%). → Do not claim "first to predict force" or "first to a future wrench loop"; claim only the surviving intersection (future + 6-DoF + the policy reasoning directly over the rollout, not just a controller-target coupling + *sensorless* at deploy), anchor H1 against FAWAM as the sensored ceiling and FD-VLA as the sensorless baseline, test the rollout-vs-controller distinction directly via H3, and treat that distinction + no-sensor-at-deploy — now decomposed across H1 and H3 rather than bundled into one claim — as the load-bearing novelty.
> - **Imagined wrench is confidently wrong exactly at contact make/break** — the rolled-forward wrench is most uncertain at the slip/impact boundary, and a silently-wrong forecast drives a force overshoot or a collision the policy never flags. → Carry an uncertainty/abstention signal on the imagined wrench (cross-ref B3 calibration); measure the confidently-wrong-at-contact failure rate, not just mean contact-task SR, and let the policy fall back when the wrench forecast's confidence collapses at make/break.
> - **Sensor cost decides whether the sensorless half (H1) is worth solving** — if the target arms already carry joint-torque or cheap wrist F/T sensing, FAWAM's sensored residual loop is the dominant design and H1's sensorless wedge solves a problem few deployers have; if the target is low-cost grippers with no F/T, sensorless is the whole game. → Price the installed sensor base of the target embodiment explicitly; scope H1's claim to the no-F/T regime and report it against the sensored ceiling, not as a universal win. This concern is scoped to H1 specifically — H3's rollout-vs-controller finding, if it holds, benefits sensored deployers too ([[2606.30988|MuSe]]'s own ablation is sensored), so the card's value no longer hinges entirely on the sensor-cost question.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: A1 decides *how dense* the imagined state is at train vs deploy; A3 decides *what the latent encodes* once you commit to one — and the second is the under-examined axis, because [[2604.02029|Latent-Space-Survey]] names evaluability and controllability of the latent as open and [[2511.02097|WM-Manipulation-Survey]] ranks structured task-relevant representation above raw capacity.
- **Today's answers**: the semantic-vs-static side is largely settled. [[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]] holds the action-conditioned LDM fixed and swaps only the latent's objective (reconstruction-aligned SD3-VAE / VA-VAE vs semantic-aligned V-JEPA 2.1 / Web-DINO / SigLIP 2), finding semantic latents lift closed-loop SR +9.8 pp and OOD +13.6 pp; and [[2602.11832|JEPA-VLA]] already reproduced the *cross-backbone* swap A3 demanded — DINOv2 vs SigLIP vs V-JEPA 2 in a fixed non-LDM VLA, video-predictive wins (+7.4% [[2306.03310|LIBERO]], +6.7% LIBERO-plus, +15.2% LIBERO-Long). But JEPA-VLA's own swap has *no reconstruction/VAE arm* — [[2607.08436|EgoWAM]] now partially pre-empts this on a *different* (HPT, human-video) backbone, finding semantic ≫ recon (up to 4× OOD), so the recon-vs-semantic margin is measured on a second backbone, just not yet on JEPA-VLA's own non-LDM VLA backbone.
- **The opening**: the *bottleneck-type* axis is genuinely open and *contested* — [[2605.15725|DiLA]] finds a *continuous* bottleneck beats discrete VQ + VAE on generation quality and stability, yet [[2603.05438|CompACT]] finds a *discrete* FSQ-over-DINOv3 latent wins action-conditioned planning (40× planning speedup, 3× lower action-prediction error at 16 tokens). Two papers disagree on continuous-vs-discrete *for control* — a clean controlled lever nobody has settled.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A policy consumes dynamics, not pixels. A latent trained to reconstruct appearance spends capacity on detail the controller discards; a latent trained to predict future semantics keeps exactly the action-relevant structure. What the encoder is *told to preserve* fixes a ceiling on downstream control that no amount of downstream architecture can lift. [[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]]'s controlled swap demonstrates it: the LDM is held fixed and only the encoder objective changes, yet closed-loop SR swings +9.8 pp. [[2607.27017|POKEWORLD]] supplies the mechanistic *why*: in a controlled factorial diagnostic, a physical quantity (contact stiffness) is acquired by the latent only when it is a *prediction target*, not merely a *fused input* (R² 0.40–0.57 vs ≈−0.02 chance-level), so the encoder objective, not the sensor stream, causally gates what survives.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "semantic beats static, reproduced cross-backbone" — [[2602.11832|JEPA-VLA]] already ran that swap (video-predictive > DINOv2/SigLIP, +6.7% OOD) on a non-LDM backbone, so the recon-vs-semantic *direction* is consensus. The live, *contested* assumption is that the bottleneck *type* settles in favor of continuous: [[2605.15725|DiLA]] bets continuous > VQ/VAE, but [[2603.05438|CompACT]] bets discrete-FSQ wins *control* — and no one has run a recon/VQ/continuous three-arm at matched dim *scored on closed-loop SR* to break the tie.
- **The bet**: The *deliverable* is the matched-capacity, matched-deploy-latency three-arm sweep (continuous / discrete-FSQ / VQ) scored on closed-loop SR — *not* a predicted single winner. Whether the [[2605.15725|DiLA]]↔[[2603.05438|CompACT]] disagreement resolves as a global ≥5 pp winner or as a *regime-contingent contingency map* is genuinely open, and the control-grounded evidence already splits by task: [[2503.00653|DC-MPC]] finds discrete > continuous on return (contact / return-style regimes), [[2505.04999|CLAM]] finds continuous > discrete on fine-manipulation SR. The **null is pre-registered**: the live expectation is a regime split, not a universal victor, so the contingency map (which encoding wins which task-family) is the publishable result whether or not any single arm wins outright. The separable, near-settled half — the recon-vs-semantic margin ([[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]]'s +9.8 pp closed-loop / +13.6 pp OOD) reproduced with a *reconstruction/VAE arm* on a second non-LDM backbone ([[2602.11832|JEPA-VLA]] omitted it) — is the publishable floor (H1). Falsifiable framing: a global ≥5 pp winner *across all regimes* is the bet's *least*-expected outcome; the contingency map is the deliverable, and a flat three-way tie within a regime is itself a reportable finding (bottleneck type is a free choice *there*).

A1's H5 now carries *two* independent orthogonality confirmations ([[2608.05903|Robust-WAM]] + [[2606.30988|MuSe]]), not one — see A1 for detail.
6. **H6 — Uncertainty-aware encoding wins specifically under distractors.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2601.14354|VJEPA-Probabilistic]]'s variational bottleneck beats deterministic semantic encoders *specifically* on distractor-heavy OOD (where it holds R²>0.84 vs ~0.50), and ties them on clean tasks — so the win is the discard-nuisance mechanism, not capacity.
   - *Test*: compare variational vs deterministic semantic on a real manipulation distractor suite — [[2402.08191|THE-COLOSSEUM]]'s 14-axis perturbations (distractor count + object color + lighting are its top-3 most-damaging, the exact nuisance the variational bottleneck must discard) — and on [[2409.18330|DMC-VB]]'s static+dynamic-distractor control tasks (paired state/pixel observations isolate the representation gap), versus matched clean tasks.
   - *Row*: VJEPA-Probabilistic (uncertainty-aware); distractor substrate THE COLOSSEUM (manipulation) + DMC-VB (representation-for-control).
   - *Falsifier*: the variational encoder wins equally on clean tasks → the gain is capacity, not nuisance-discarding.
7. **H7 — The control-winning encoding also knows when its imagined latent is wrong.**
   - *Prediction*: the encoding that wins closed-loop SR in a regime also carries the best-calibrated uncertainty on its own imagined latent under drift — measuring abstention/uncertainty when the latent rollout is confidently wrong separates a truly control-relevant encoding from one that merely scores high mean SR while failing silently in the OOD tail.
   - *Test*: on each arm of the H2 sweep, add an uncertainty/abstention readout on the imagined latent; under [[2402.08191|THE-COLOSSEUM]] perturbations stratify outcomes into correct / abstained / confidently-wrong and report the confidently-wrong rate at matched aggregate SR, alongside the closed-loop SR rank.
   - *Row*: VJEPA-Probabilistic (uncertainty-aware) / VLA-JEPA (winning encoding); calibration cross-ref B3.
   - *Falsifier*: the SR-winning encoding shows no better calibration than the losers → control-relevance and calibration-under-drift are independent, and the contingency map must be read on SR alone.

> - **Encoding gain is dataset-specific** — the +9.8 / +13.6 pp swing may not transfer off [[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]]'s Bridge-V2 setup. → H1 reproduces on a second backbone + dataset before claiming the lever is general; report per-dataset deltas.
> - **Semantic latents destabilize diffusion training** — high-dimensional SSL/VL latents historically break diffusion. → Reuse [[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]]'s wide-head DiT + S-VAE compression recipe rather than feeding raw high-dim latents; H2 reports stability as a first-class metric.
> - **Encoding quality ≠ controllability** — a latent that recovers actions well may still be hard to plan in. → Pair the IDM-recoverability diagnostic with closed-loop SR and [[2605.26379|LeJEPA-World-Model]]'s identifiability test (H4), never action-recovery alone.

## Cluster B — WAM Training-Time Grounding

*A WAM that imagines freely will imagine physically impossible futures, and a policy trained on those futures inherits the impossibility. The four directions install a training-time signal that keeps imagination honest: discrete contact structure (B1), a self-evolution loop that verifies its own dreams (B2), forward-inverse calibration before runtime (B3), and a physics-validation filter on synthesized data (B4).*

### B1 — Contact-Aware (Discrete-Mode) WAM for Fine Manipulation

| | |

The reason it must work: contact physics jumps sharply, and at assembly precision the make/break/slip labels are too sparse for a reward-driven gate to discover. The "discrete beats smooth" argument is now consensus ([[2512.08411\|PRISM-WM]]) — but only proven for locomotion with an implicit gate. **Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: latent WAMs handle free-space trajectories but fail at insertion/assembly, because contact physics is locally non-smooth (make/break, slip, normal-force singularities) — three deep-dives converge that latent WAMs miss sub-millimeter contact ([[../../../Embodied-AI/07_Latent-World-Models#6. Open Problems|07_Latent-World-Models §6]]), verifiable physics scales poorly to clutter ([[../../../Embodied-AI/08_Physics-Aware-Embodied-AI#8. Open Problems|08_Physics-Aware-Embodied-AI §8]]), and learned sims blur on contact ([[../../../Embodied-AI/15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer#7. Open Problems|15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer §7]]).
- **Today's answers**: the *discrete-mode WM beats a smooth one* argument is no longer B1's alone — [[2512.08411|PRISM-WM]] states it near-verbatim ("monolithic nets over-smooth distinct dynamic modes — sticking vs sliding, flight vs stance") and builds a MoE-gated discrete-physical-mode latent that beats vanilla MoE, but proves it on *locomotion* (DMControl / Humanoid-Bench / DiffRL), with an *implicit* learned gate, no fine manipulation; [[2503.01842|DHAL]] learns discrete contact-mode automata (3 modes) but as a legged-locomotion *policy* (100% indoor / 60% uneven). The contact gains in *manipulation* stay policy-side — [[2602.23253|SPARR]] 95–100% [[2407.08028|AutoMate]], [[2603.15956|ExpertGen]] 90.5% — and the closest WM substrate, [[2604.16484|DexWorldModel]], keeps contact *continuous*.
- **The opening**: [[2606.05645|Discrete-WAM]] proves a discrete shared latent trains for joint world + policy (90.4 EPDMS NAVSIM-v2) but in AV with scene/maneuver tokens; [[2604.27367|DOT-Sim]]'s differentiable MPM tactile sim now manufactures the make/break/slip *labels* a smooth WAM cannot self-generate (96.55% tumor detection zero-shot, 0.896 mm) — so an *explicit, tactile-supervised* 5-mode taxonomy for sub-mm assembly is the trainable residue PRISM-WM's implicit locomotion gate leaves untouched.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Contact physics jumps sharply — friction-cone boundaries, normal-force singularities, and slip-stick are abrupt, discrete state changes in the physics itself, not the model. A smooth latent can only represent a hard step by internally splitting into discrete pieces, and approximating it gets exponentially more expensive right at the boundary. [[2604.27367|DOT-Sim]]'s differentiable contact sim demonstrates that make/break/slip are *categorically* distinct regimes with distinct governing equations, and [[2607.05966|iKCE]] shows the failure this induces empirically: a smooth WM's imagination is statistically *flat across a friction sweep* (0.1–1.7) exactly where real behaviour changes regime, i.e. it never represents the friction-dependent contact transition an explicit contact mode encodes.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not the bare "discrete beats smooth" structural-jump argument — [[2512.08411|PRISM-WM]], [[2503.01842|DHAL]], and the switching-dynamics literature already hold it, so among hybrid-system researchers it is consensus. The live assumption is that the discrete structure can stay *implicit and self-learned* (PRISM-WM's MoE gate discovers modes from reward; DHAL's automata emerge from skating). The bet is the opposite: in *sub-millimeter manipulation* the modes must be an *explicit make/break/slip taxonomy supervised by tactile contact ground truth* — capacity and self-learned gates both cap out before assembly precision, where the labels are too sparse to discover. [[2604.16484|DexWorldModel]]'s continuous causal latent caps out; [[2606.05645|Discrete-WAM]]'s discreteness is scene-level, not contact-level.
- **The bet**: An *explicit, tactile-supervised* contact mode $c_t \in \{\text{no-contact, making, in-contact, breaking, slipping}\}$ — its taxonomy distilled from [[2604.27367|DOT-Sim]] contact ground truth, not discovered by a reward-driven gate — hits >90.5% [[2407.08028|AutoMate]] (the contact-naive ceiling) and sub-millimeter assembly that (a) purely smooth WAMs cannot reach at any scale *and* (b) [[2512.08411|PRISM-WM]]'s *implicit* MoE-gated mode latent cannot match without the tactile-supervised taxonomy. Falsifiable: if either a scaled smooth latent *or* an implicit self-gated discrete latent matches the tactile-supervised taxonomy on sub-millimeter insertion, the explicit-supervision claim is wrong.

1. **H1 — An *explicit tactile-supervised* contact-mode latent beats a smooth latent, an *implicit* mode gate, *and* a reactive measured-wrench baseline on sub-mm insertion.**

3. **H3 — Contact-mode-conditional physics losses beat a single global physics loss.**

   - *Test*: ablate mode-gated vs global physics loss, stratifying contact-onset frequency by [[2502.05086|REASSEMBLE]]'s hierarchical temporal action segmentation (approach/align/insert/release = make/break-style contact-state boundaries over 4,551 NIST-board demos with 6-axis force-torque) rather than a hand-defined split — and bound the *number* of mode-gated loss terms by [[2211.16657|Task-Driven-Hybrid-Model-Reduction]]'s finding that a *small task-relevant subset* (~7 active of thousands, <5% task loss) carries the contact dynamics, so the mode-gating need not enumerate every contact mode.

   - *Row*: DOT-Sim + [[2403.08716|DIFFTACTILE]] (differentiable-sim ground truth) / REASSEMBLE (real contact-state labels).
   - *Falsifier*: insertion SR is flat in mode-classification accuracy → the discrete modes are not the operative variable.
5. **H5 — Discrete contact modes transfer sim-to-real on AutoMate / NIST.**

   - *Falsifier*: the discrete-mode advantage vanishes on real → the modes overfit the simulator.
6. **H6 — Contact-event time prediction is a useful auxiliary head.**
   - *Prediction*: an auxiliary regression head predicting contact-onset time $\hat t_{\text{contact}}$ (supervised by the simulator) improves pre-contact deceleration and raises insertion SR over the discrete-mode WAM without it.
   - *Test*: add the $\hat t_{\text{contact}}$ head; compare insertion SR + impact force at contact on [[2603.15257|HapticVLA]]'s fragile force-regulated tasks (jar/waffles/egg, safety-aware reward; its +45 pp absolute egg-manipulation gain is the impact-force-sensitive regime where pre-contact deceleration pays off).

> - **Contact-mode supervision requires a simulator** — real make/break/slip labels are unavailable. → Distill from [[2604.27367|DOT-Sim]] / [[2511.04665|Real-to-Sim-GS]] twins where contact ground truth exists (H4); H5 then tests sim-to-real retention.

### B2 — WAM-Driven Self-Evolution & Recovery

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | B — Training-Time Grounding |
| **Thesis** | Close a loop where the WAM *actively* generates failures, a ρ stop-gate detects WM-gaming, and a separate verifier screens bad dreams — not just RL inside imagination. **Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: [[2604.22748|Agentic-World-Modeling-Survey]] defines L1 Predictor / L2 Simulator / L3 Evolver and calls physical L3 Evolver the gap ("emerging not mature") — an agent that revises itself when predictions fail. [[2606.12690|EWAM]] now integrates detection + routing + correction/rollback + experience-filtered retention end-to-end, so full-loop integration alone is no longer the open question — what's still missing is an *active* (not passive/online-encountered) failure-finder and an imagined-vs-real $\rho$-specific stop-gate, versus EWAM's admission-filter gate.
- **Today's answers**: the *imagined-RL-improves-SR* half of the loop is now crowded 2026 consensus, not an opening — [[2602.11075|RISE]] runs RL inside a learned WM with a failure-sensitive progress-value model and offline-data mixing against forgetting (85–95% real); [[2602.13977|WoVR]] (+29.3 pp LIBERO, 91.7% real sim-to-real), [[2602.12063|VLAW]] (+39.2 pp, mean 0.46→0.868), and [[2602.06508|World-VLA-Loop]] (+24.0% LIBERO, real 13.3→50.0%) all confirm RL-in-imagination lifts SR. The other stages exist singly — [[2412.02818|RoboMD]] (adversary on a *real* robot), [[2510.09459|FIPER]] (detection only), [[2401.16650|WMAR]] (+0.071 vs 0.665 forgetting), [[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]] (truncates unreliable imaginations, +6.83 pp). None has a *dedicated failure-finder*, an imagined-vs-real *ρ stop gate*, or a *separate verifier* in a detect→recover framing.
- **The opening**: [[2603.08403|SPIRAL]]'s imagine→verify→GRPO cycle (58.72% EgoPlan-Bench) proves the spine works, [[2603.25685|Persistent-Robot-World-Models]] supplies a ready ρ anchor (imagined-vs-real Pearson 0.822), and [[2606.00267|StressDream]] now realizes the *active* failure-finder half — VLM-steering a video WM toward rare high-impact failures and reweighting demos lifts real SR 39%→71% — so the three pieces RISE leaves open (active failure-generation, ρ-as-operative-stop, Pre-VLA-class verifier) are the seam B2 fills, with StressDream covering the active-generation piece but not the ρ-gate or separate verifier, and "imagined RL improves SR" already taken.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: How well an agent prepares is bounded by what it can imagine. A recovery policy trains on the failures it sees, so an agent only learns to recover from failures it can *generate* for itself — making a self-improvement loop capped by how widely the WM can imagine failure, not by how much real interaction it logs. [[2603.13528|Counterfactual-Failure-Synthesis]]'s Dream2Fix demonstrates the supply side: perturbing actions in a generative WM yields 120K counterfactual-failure + recovery pairs that lift real recovery to 46%.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "self-evolution needs real exploration" — [[2602.11075|RISE]], [[2602.13977|WoVR]], and [[2602.12063|VLAW]] already drive real improvement from imagined RL, so that is now consensus, not a contrarian bet. The live assumption is that *passive* failure discovery (RISE finds failures via low advantages) and an *ungated, unverified* loop suffice. B2 bets the opposite: reachable recovery competence is bounded by what the WM is *actively driven to imagine*, the loop needs ρ as an *operative stop condition* (not just a diagnostic), and hallucinated rollouts need a *separate* verifier — none of which RISE/WoVR build.
- **The bet**: Against [[2602.11075|RISE]]/[[2602.13977|WoVR]] as imagined-RL baselines, adding the three missing pieces — an *active* failure-finder (vs RISE's passive low-advantage discovery), an imagined-vs-real ρ > 0.7 (Pearson) *stop gate* anchored to [[2603.25685|Persistent-Robot-World-Models]]'s 0.822, and a [[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]]-class separate verifier at ≥0.83 F1 — yields higher per-cycle real-SR gain at equal imagined-rollout budget, *without* forgetting ([[2401.16650|WMAR]]-style, +0.071 vs 0.665). Falsifiable: if the active finder + ρ-gate + verifier tie a plain RISE-style passive loop at matched budget, the three additions are unnecessary and B2 collapses to imagined RL.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (a WAM-driven loop drives real improvement at high imagined-vs-real $\rho$), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 — An *active* WAM failure-finder beats RISE's *passive* failure discovery on real recovery.**
   - *Prediction*: recasting [[2412.02818|RoboMD]] as a WAM adversary (finder proposes initial states, WAM rolls forward, policy judged on imagined outcomes) covers more failure modes and yields higher real recovery than [[2602.11075|RISE]]'s passive low-advantage discovery at equal imagined-rollout budget, at least matching [[2603.13528|Counterfactual-Failure-Synthesis]]'s offline 46% real recovery.

2. **H2 — Per-cycle SR rises only while imagined-vs-real $\rho$ stays > 0.7.**

4. **H4 — GRPO over joint (action, imagination) beats RL on action alone.**

5. **H5 — Phase-aware replay holds forgetting near WMAR levels across cycles.**

> - **Misevolution drift** — self-reward biases amplify across cycles ([[2509.26354|Misevolution]] names the risk class). → Red-team after each cycle ([[2604.05498|JailWAM]] / [[2506.07468|SELF-REDTEAM]] probes, H6); keep a novelty bonus against entropy collapse.
> - **Reward hacking on imagined SR** — the model games the WM, not reality. → Periodic real-robot validation + [[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]]'s rollout truncation; the $\rho$ > 0.7 gate (H2) is the stop condition that catches it.
> - **WAM drifts from real dynamics** — imagination diverges over cycles, so old recoveries become invalid. → Outer-loop WAM updates ([[2603.04029|Self-Adapting-RL]]) and the $\rho$ stop condition; validate against the joint causal-binding metric in the umbrella, not imagined SR alone.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: [[2604.22748|Agentic-World-Modeling-Survey]]'s L3 Evolver "revises its own model when predictions fail" — but the usual tool for knowing *when* a prediction failed is uncertainty estimation, which [[2604.01985|WAV]] shows "often fails in under-explored data regions where new information is most needed," exactly where calibration matters, and [[2310.06253|Objective-Mismatch-MBRL-Survey]] generalizes it: low predictive WM loss does not imply high downstream return.
- **Today's answers**: the field verifies at *runtime* — [[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]] truncates unreliable imaginations (+6.83 pp [[2306.03310|LIBERO]]), [[2510.09459|FIPER]] / [[2506.09937|SAFE]] detect failure post-hoc — patching a dream after it is generated rather than shaping it during training. But [[2606.27326|MMBench2]] now shows imagination failure is *predictable before it happens* (hallucination predictors ρ≈−0.80 with rollout error) and *preventable at pretrain* (coverage-aware training +0.88 dB), the first direct evidence for B3's train-time-dominates bet rather than the runtime patch, and [[2607.05966|iKCE]] sharpens *why* a runtime patch is too late: it diagnoses that imagined rollouts are *kinematic, not dynamic* (DreamerV3's imagination stays statistically flat across a friction sweep of 0.1–1.7 where the real policy's reward collapses at μ=0.20), so the miscalibration is a structural property of the imagination, not a compounding-error tail a late filter can catch. [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] now confirms the same failure at real-world scale across 22 task families spanning rigid/textile/deformable regimes: video WMs hit trajectory R²=0.99 while recovering free-fall acceleration <1% of gravity and badly-wrong pendulum periods, high visual/trajectory fidelity coexisting with wrong physical constants, not a rare pathology.
- **The opening**: the forward-inverse asymmetry as a train-time lever is now multiply-instantiated — [[2604.01985|WAV]] turns it into a self-improving cycle (2× sample-eff, +22% reward, no extra labels), [[2602.06130|SWIRL]] runs the identical FWM↔IDM reciprocal cycle on action-free state-only sequences (+26.4% dynamics prediction), and [[2604.16391|DeFI]] decouples forward/inverse pretraining on action-free video (81.3% real Franka, 60% of CALVIN data) — and [[2602.02762|LAPO+]] formally proves *why*: the IDM is a lower-complexity hypothesis class, recoverable in low-data regimes where BC fails. The mechanism is real and owned; what no one has done is the *train-time-vs-runtime* head-to-head, or train a WM to *maximize* imagined-vs-real ρ directly.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Making a prediction and checking one are not equally hard. Video without action labels is plentiful, so judging whether an imagined future *looks* plausible is cheap; and the action-relevant part of a state is low-dimensional, so judging whether a future is *reachable* needs little labeled data. A checker that exploits this gap is cheaper than the generator it checks — now multiply-proven: [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s sparse inverse is more OOD-robust than a dense one, [[2602.06130|SWIRL]]'s reciprocal FWM↔IDM cycle needs no labels, and [[2602.02762|LAPO+]] formally shows the IDM is a lower-complexity hypothesis class.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "the asymmetry is exploitable" — [[2604.01985|WAV]], [[2602.06130|SWIRL]], and [[2604.16391|DeFI]] collectively own that, and [[2602.02762|LAPO+]] proves the IDM is a lower-complexity class. The live, *unclaimed* assumption is that *when* you calibrate (train vs runtime) is a free choice, and that imagined-vs-real ρ is only a diagnostic to *gate* on, never a *trainable objective*. The runtime-verification line ([[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]], [[2510.09459|FIPER]]) implicitly bets calibration-time doesn't matter; B3 bets train-time calibration strictly dominates and ρ is directly maximizable.
- **The bet**: On a *latent robot WAM*, train-time forward-inverse calibration beats a matched runtime-only verifier on both ≥2× WM sample-efficiency and +22% downstream reward at equal labels ([[2604.01985|WAV]]'s margins as the target) — the head-to-head no paper has run; *and* training the WM to maximize imagined-vs-real ρ as an objective yields higher final ρ than gating on it (against [[2606.05773|PiL-World]]'s r=0.94 / [[2603.25685|Persistent-Robot-World-Models]]'s 0.822 references). Falsifiable: if a runtime-only verifier matches train-time calibration on sample-efficiency and reward, *or* if ρ is no higher trained-for than gated-on, the train-time/ρ-objective wedge is empty and B3 reduces to WAV+SWIRL+DeFI.

   - *Prediction*: adapting [[2504.16680|RWM-U]]'s [[2005.13239|MOPO]] penalty to a latent-consistency reward on A1's hybrid backbone stabilizes the latent-rollout objective (lower divergence over horizon) vs an ungated rollout.
   - *Test*: add the uncertainty penalty to A1's latent rollout; measure rollout divergence vs horizon.
   - *Row*: RWM-U (train-time uncertainty) / ELVIS (UCB-gated).
   - *Falsifier*: the penalty leaves rollout divergence unchanged → uncertainty gating does not stabilize the latent.
3. **H3 — Training to maximize imagined-vs-real $\rho$ beats gating on it.**
   - *Prediction*: treating the B2 $\rho$ > 0.7 gate as B3's *objective* (train the WM to maximize imagined-vs-real SR correlation directly, against [[2606.05773|PiL-World]]'s r=0.94 reference) yields higher final $\rho$ than using $\rho$ only as a stop condition — [[2606.17906|WAM-RL]]'s dense reconstruction reward (similarity between imagined and executed observations, LIBERO-Object 68→82%) is the first instance of training *toward* ρ rather than measuring it, so the H3 A/B is now anchored on a real method, not a thought experiment.
   - *Test*: A/B $\rho$-as-objective ([[2606.17906|WAM-RL]]-style reconstruction reward) vs $\rho$-as-stop-condition on [[2605.03941|iWorld-Bench]]'s action-controllability + memory protocol (nine metrics, 0.8053 Spearman alignment with human preference — the named suite for the controllability/$\rho$ axis); report final $\rho$ and downstream SR. **First gate**: [[2606.18610|SC3-Eval]]'s ρ=0.984 in-dist / 0.929 closed-loop is the headline imagined-vs-real correlation but is **single-source** (one evaluator, one paper's 7-policy setup), so before any train-on-ρ claim the ρ instrument must clear *independent held-out validation* — reproduce the ρ-vs-real-SR correlation on a held-out policy set / second evaluator, and treat the single-source 0.984 as a reference to be re-established, not a fixed anchor. [[2607.07196|Admissibility Ladder]] gives this gate a formal shape: its VV&A/SOTIF ladder shows visual fidelity (L0) does not predict action-robustness (L1), a Vista-vs-Epona ranking reversal, so the ρ instrument must clear an *action-robustness* rung, not just a generation-quality one, before its verdict is trusted. [[2608.11174|VIScore]] supplies the concrete validation *template* this gate demands — disjoint checkpoint pools, cluster-bootstrap resampling, leave-one-task-out calibration — though on toy domains, not manipulation SR, so it is a protocol to port, not itself the second evaluator.
   - *Row*: WAM-RL (ρ-as-objective) vs PiL-World ($\rho$-calibration) / Video WM Policy Eval (measures $\rho$) / SC3-Eval (single-source ρ=0.984, validate-before-use) / [[2606.23296|IOI]] (kinematics-physics-decoupled evaluator, ρ=0.9992 — the tightest reference, same single-source caveat); controllability substrate iWorld-Bench.
   - *Falsifier*: optimizing $\rho$ directly does not raise final $\rho$ over gating → $\rho$ is not directly trainable; *or* the ρ instrument fails independent held-out validation → the 0.984 anchor was setup-specific and the ρ-objective rests on an un-validated metric.
4. **H4 — Verifier disagreement is a useful active-data signal.**
   - *Prediction*: using [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s plausibility/reachability discrepancy to choose which real-robot interactions to collect next (closing the loop with B2's failure-finder) reaches target SR with fewer real interactions than uniform collection.
   - *Test*: discrepancy-driven vs uniform real-data collection; report real-interaction budget to fixed SR.
   - *Row*: WAV (train-time asymmetry).
   - *Falsifier*: discrepancy-driven collection ties uniform → the disagreement signal carries no information about what to collect.
5. **H5 (open) — Which reward form calibrates a WM best — IDM-frame vs pixel-MSE — is contested, not settled.**
   - *Prediction*: B3's first-principles lean toward [[2509.23958|RLIR]]'s inverse-dynamics frame-level reward as the calibration lever, but [[2606.17906|WAM-RL]]'s *own* ablation reports that *pixel-level MSE reconstruction* is the most effective reward signal — an open contradiction, since the two results sit on different setups (RLIR frame-level recoverability-vs-GT; WAM-RL pixel-reconstruction ρ-reward) and neither settles it. (To be tested, not asserted:) at matched budget across AR and diffusion WMs, *one of* {IDM-frame reward, pixel-MSE reward, [[2510.00406|VLA-RFT]] trajectory-level verified reward, [[2505.13934|RLVR-World]] rule-verified reward, [[2608.04964|WorldCycle]]'s reversible-cycle-consistency reward, human-preference} calibrates best — and the experiment is run to *decide* between RLIR's IDM bet and WAM-RL's pixel-MSE finding, with pixel-MSE pre-registered as a first-class arm rather than a foil.
   - *Test*: post-train the same WM with IDM-frame vs pixel-MSE vs verified-trajectory vs reversible-cycle-consistency vs preference reward; report action-classification accuracy + downstream calibration; pre-register that pixel-MSE may win (per WAM-RL).
   - *Row*: RLIR (frame-level IDM-reward) vs WAM-RL (pixel-MSE reconstruction reward, the contradicting result) vs VLA-RFT (trajectory-level verified) / RLVR-World (rule-verified) / WorldCycle (reversible-cycle-consistency, needs no learned inverse model or ground truth at all — the annotation-free arm).
   - *Falsifier*: pixel-MSE (or preference / generic verified / cycle-consistency) matches or beats the IDM signal → the inverse signal is *not* the calibration lever and WAM-RL's pixel-best finding stands, closing this open question against the IDM bet.
6. **H6 — Sparse-inverse OOD robustness holds in contact-rich regimes.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s sparse-inverse OOD robustness holds on contact-rich tasks when paired with B1's discrete contact modes — i.e., the low-dimensional action features remain recoverable across make/break transitions — and [[2604.17887|StableIDM]] bounds *where* that holds, since manipulator truncation (an inverse OOD failure it lifts 18.6→30.7% Acc) is exactly the regime where the sparse inverse breaks.
   - *Test*: evaluate sparse-vs-dense inverse (and [[2604.17887|StableIDM]]'s truncation-robust refinement) on contact-onset-heavy tasks with B1's contact-mode gating, scoring execution success at the high-complexity end of [[2606.04811|Dream.exe]]'s three physical-complexity levels and on [[2604.19092|RoboWM-Bench]]'s rigid/articulated/deformable contact splits.
   - *Row*: WAV (train-time asymmetry) / StableIDM (inverse-OOD robustness) / cross-ref B1; contact-complexity substrate Dream.exe + RoboWM-Bench.
   - *Falsifier*: the sparse inverse drops contact transients → calibration must go dense in contact, bounding the claim.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Sparse inverse model misses subtle dynamics** — low-dimensional action features may drop contact transients. → Bound the claim to where action-relevant features are recoverable; pair with B1's discrete contact modes for contact-rich regimes (H6).
> - **Uncertainty gating too conservative — or, per [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]], unreliably optimistic instead** — penalizing all high-uncertainty states kills exploration ([[2504.16680|RWM-U]]'s penalty coefficient is a critical hyperparameter), but [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] shows the opposite failure can also hold: ensemble-disagreement uncertainty computed *within closed-loop imagination* converges to in-distribution levels even as real compounding error keeps growing, so the gate can look calm exactly when it should fire. → Tune the penalty on a held-out real-robot calibration set, not in simulation alone — Biased Dreams is precisely why that check must use real rollouts, not further imagination; H2 reports the exploration cost.
> - **Calibration ≠ correctness** — a WM can be well-calibrated about being wrong. → Validate against B2's imagined-vs-real $\rho$ (H3) AND the joint causal-binding metric in the umbrella, not calibration alone.
> - **The IDM-reward bet is internally contradicted by [[2606.17906|WAM-RL]]** — H5 leans toward [[2509.23958|RLIR]]'s inverse-dynamics reward, but WAM-RL's own ablation finds *pixel-MSE reconstruction* the most effective reward, so "IDM-reward wins calibration" is an **open question, not a settled claim**. → Run H5 as a decision experiment with pixel-MSE pre-registered as a first-class arm; do not assert IDM-reward dominance in the card until the head-to-head settles it.
> - **The ρ=0.984 anchor is single-source** — [[2606.18610|SC3-Eval]]'s correlation comes from one evaluator on one 7-policy setup. → Make independent held-out validation of the ρ instrument the *first* gate (H3) before training toward it; treat 0.984 as a reference to re-establish, not a fixed constant.
> - **Adversarial drift between imagination and action is a distinct threat class** — [[2607.15207|BadWAM]] shows an attacker can hold the imagined future plausible while steering the executed action, so a calibration signal anchored only on imagination plausibility misses it entirely. → Anchor calibration on the forward-inverse/reachability check (not visual plausibility alone), and treat BadWAM's transferable perturbations as a standing red-team target for any deployed calibration stack.

### B4 — WAM-as-Data-Engine

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | B — Training-Time Grounding |
| **Thesis** | Ask *what makes imagined data executable rather than merely plausible* — and whether that property lives in the filter (transferable) or the generator (bound). The reason it must work: a video can look right and be kinematically impossible, so the filter decides what the policy learns; a kinematic-physics predicate is intrinsic to the data and should carry over even when the filter was validated on non-generated data and is now applied to a generative engine's rollouts, where success-replay or a learned judge may not. The field treats the engine as the contribution and the filter as plumbing, and treats the bridging/scoring machinery around the filter — which IDM recovers a trajectory, which embodiment the score is calibrated for, which sub-component of the score does the work — as a nuisance to average away rather than a set of testable confounds. The bet is in First-principles below — the filter *type* is load-bearing and transferable, and that transfer claim survives being probed on each of those confounds in turn. |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: the field knows a WAM data engine helps, and single-generator filter-ablations now isolate which *property* of the data is load-bearing — [[2604.11351|WM-DAgger]]'s filter-ablation drops SR 96.7→46.7%, [[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]]'s physics-critic-alone cuts collisions 7.8→1.9% — but nobody has tested whether a filter's load-bearingness, demonstrated on non-generated data, *transfers when the filter is applied to an actual generative engine's rollouts*. [[2605.12090|WAM-Survey]] names "data-ecosystem mixing" and [[2604.15395|Foundation-Models-in-Robotics-Survey]] names dataset/challenge mapping as the underexploited output, but neither asks this transfer question.
- **Today's answers**: that a WAM data engine *beats real collection* is now settled by five-plus papers, so it is not the open question. [[2606.02577|RoboDream]] (Gen-Mix 62.5% vs 36.3% real-only, 2.2× faster) and its own lab's [[2512.11797|AnchorDream]] (+36% sim 22.5→30.7%, real 28.0→60.0%) ship anchored video-diffusion engines; [[2604.03552|CRAFT]] already *pairs* a generator with a load-bearing validation filter (89.3% cross-embodiment vs 55–69% target-collected, Canny-conditioning 10.3→21.6%); [[2606.01027|τ0-WM]] triples zero-shot SR (0.55 vs 0.14). The *unsettled* mechanism question: which *type* of filter makes imagination executable, and does a filter's load-bearingness carry over when applied to a generator?
- **The opening**: the filters disagree on *type*. [[2606.04708|VISTA]] uses *analytic kinematic-physics-feasibility* scoring on human-teleoperated UMI trajectories — a score-controlled comparison of high-score vs low-score subsets, 0.65 vs 0.00 (stapler placement, RealMan) — [[2604.03552|CRAFT]] uses sim-success replay on its own generated video, and [[2605.27491|GE-Sim-2.0]] uses a *learned VLM World-Judge* (+15% real) on its own generated video. Whether a kinematic-physics filter validated on non-generated data *transfers* when applied to a generative engine's rollouts — or is bound to the teleop-replay substrate it was built on — is the clean, unanswered mechanism lever.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: What separates *executable* imagined data from merely *plausible-looking* imagined data is a property of the filter, not the generator — a video can look right and be kinematically impossible, so the filter is what decides what the policy learns. If that property is intrinsic to the data (a kinematic-physics predicate), the same filter should carry over from non-generated to generated sources; if it is success-replay or a learned judge, it may be bound to the substrate it was built on. Which it is teaches *what makes imagination executable*. [[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s score-controlled comparison — 0.65 high-score vs 0.00 low-score UMI subsets (stapler placement, RealMan) — isolates the filter as load-bearing *on human-teleoperated data*. VISTA has no generative engine of its own, so whether that load-bearingness survives the jump to an actual generator's rollouts is untested, not something VISTA's own number already shows.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That the *engine* is the contribution and the filter is interchangeable plumbing — held by the data-engine line ([[2606.02577|RoboDream]], [[2512.11797|AnchorDream]], [[2505.12705|DreamGen]]) where validation is sim-success-replay or generation-time anchoring, not an ablated downstream filter. B4 bets the *filter type* is load-bearing and *transferable*: a kinematic-physics-feasibility filter ([[2606.04708|VISTA]]) does real work [[2604.03552|CRAFT]]'s success-replay and [[2605.27491|GE-Sim-2.0]]'s learned VLM-judge cannot — VISTA showed this on human-teleoperated data, not on any generator's output, so the bet is that the same load-bearingness carries over the first time the filter is applied to a generative engine's rollouts.
- **The bet**: [[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s kinematic-physics-feasibility filter — whose load-bearingness VISTA demonstrates on human-teleoperated data with a score-controlled 0.65-vs-0.00 comparison (high-score vs low-score UMI subsets, stapler placement, RealMan) — reproduces a ≥15 pp downstream-SR gap when applied, via an IDM that converts [[2606.02577|RoboDream]]'s generated video into scoreable trajectories, to RoboDream's compositional-engine rollouts, whereas a success-replay filter ([[2604.03552|CRAFT]]) does not transfer as cleanly. This is a *new*, untested extension of VISTA's result — VISTA's own number never touched a generative engine's output. The engine-beats-collection claim (≥25 pp SR, [[2606.02577|RoboDream]] +26.2 pp; ≥2× cheaper, 2.2×) is the *settled* backdrop, not the bet. Falsifiable: if the kinematic-physics filter's gap vanishes on RoboDream's rollouts, or the IDM's own reconstruction error is large enough to account for the observed gap, the filter's load-bearingness is teleop-data-specific and B4's transfer claim is wrong.

**Hypotheses & tests.** B4's bet is specifically about filter *transfer*: whether a filter validated on non-generated data ([[2606.04708|VISTA]] on human-teleoperated UMI trajectories) carries its load-bearingness when applied to a generative engine's rollouts. H1 and H2 are the two transfer claims: H1 tests transfer within one generator's compositional-video output, H2 tests transfer across a structurally different generator architecture class (a 3DGS-based physics twin). H3, H4, and H5 are not a third and fourth transfer claim — they are three *validity conditions* H1/H2's numbers must clear before "the filter transferred" is a safe reading of the gap: each has its own independent variable, its own run, and its own falsifier, but all three interrogate what a measured gap actually means, so they are coupled to H1/H2 rather than freestanding. The couplings are specific, not generic: H2 removes the IDM-bridging confound *by construction* (DREMA's physics engine needs no IDM), while H3 removes the same confound *by measurement* (sweeping IDMs on H1's RoboDream substrate) — complementary routes to the same question, not redundant ones. H4's practical necessity is contingent on H5's outcome: if H5 finds the embodiment-*dependent* sub-scores (self-collision, execution-fidelity) carry the load-bearing signal, H4's per-embodiment re-scoring is required; if H5 finds the embodiment-*agnostic* continuity score alone reproduces the gap, H4's re-scoring requirement dissolves. This coupling is the point, not a flaw to paper over: H3/H4/H5 are how the card checks whether H1/H2's headline number means what it appears to mean. The broader "a WAM data engine beats real-only" claim is the settled backdrop (see the Related table: [[2606.02577|RoboDream]], [[2512.11797|AnchorDream]], [[2606.01027|τ0-WM]], [[2511.19861|GigaWorld-0]]), and properties like mix ratio, cross-embodiment zero-shot demo synthesis, rectifier co-training, prop-free collection, and OOD-diversity breadth remain visible as Related-table evidence for that broader "data engine" picture, but they are not sub-hypotheses of this card's headline and are not tested here.
1. **H1 — A kinematic-physics filter validated on non-generated data transfers its load-bearingness when applied to a generative engine's rollouts; success-replay does not transfer as cleanly.**
   - *Prediction*: applying [[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s continuity / self-collision / execution-fidelity scoring — via an IDM that recovers a scoreable trajectory from generated video — to [[2606.02577|RoboDream]]'s compositional-generator rollouts reproduces a ≥15 pp gap analogous to VISTA's score-controlled 0.65-vs-0.00 result on human-teleoperated data (stapler placement, RealMan), this time on a generative engine's output the filter was never validated against, whereas [[2604.03552|CRAFT]]'s success-replay filter (built for its own engine) shows a smaller gap when applied to RoboDream. The filter-type roster the sweep ranges over is now concrete: analytic-kinematic ([[2606.04708|VISTA]]), success-replay ([[2604.03552|CRAFT]]), VLM-judge ([[2605.27491|GE-Sim-2.0]]), DINOv2-embedding-consistency ([[2604.11351|WM-DAgger]], whose filter-ablation drops SR 96.7→46.7%), two-critic physics-vs-semantic ([[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]], whose physics-critic-alone cuts collisions 7.8→1.9% and lifts SR to 39.1% vs 13.2%), and constrained-optimization IK ([[2509.19454|ROPA]]).
   - *Test*: the filter-transfer ablation — {[[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s filter, validated on human-teleop UMI data} vs {[[2604.03552|CRAFT]]'s filter, validated on CRAFT's own engine}, both applied to RoboDream's rollouts — because the "success-replay does not transfer" claim is currently *asserted, not shown* (CRAFT itself reports 89.3% cross-*embodiment*, a different invariance than cross-*generator*), so success-replay's transfer must be *measured under this swap*, not assumed to fail. Applying VISTA's filter to RoboDream's video output first requires an IDM to recover a scoreable trajectory from generated pixels — this is a *precondition of the test*, not a detail to hold constant: the IDM's own reconstruction error is a confound the test must measure and report (see H3), not a variable to assume away. Score downstream SR on a *fixed* common suite ([[2406.02523|RoboCasa]]'s 100 tasks) so the gap is comparable. [[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]]'s and [[2604.11351|WM-DAgger]]'s in-paper filter ablations are the single-source precedents this test extends.
   - *Row*: [[2606.04708|VISTA]] (kinematic-physics, validated on human-teleop data) vs [[2604.03552|CRAFT]] (success-replay, validated on its own engine), both applied to [[2606.02577|RoboDream]] (compositional engine) via an IDM bridging step, with [[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]] / [[2604.11351|WM-DAgger]] as ablated-filter precedents; fixed eval suite [[2406.02523|RoboCasa]].
   - *Falsifier*: the kinematic filter's gap vanishes when applied to RoboDream's rollouts → its load-bearingness is specific to teleop-replay data and does not survive the jump to generated output; **or** success-replay transfers *equally* (its measured gap on RoboDream matches VISTA's) → the *differential* claim collapses to "all ground-truth-anchored filters transfer," a weaker headline; **or** the IDM's own error rate is large enough to account for the observed gap in either direction → the test is measuring IDM quality, not filter transfer (H3 is the dedicated test for this), and H1 must be rerun with a higher-fidelity IDM before the result can be trusted. **Power floor**: the ≥15 pp differential is a point estimate, and RoboCasa-100 SR over a handful of seeds carries CIs that can swallow 15 pp — compute the n (tasks × seeds × rollouts) needed to *detect* a 15 pp differential at p<0.05 *first*; if that n is infeasible, the differential narrative is dead on power grounds regardless of truth.
2. **H2 — The filter-transfer result generalizes past RoboDream's compositional-video-diffusion architecture to a structurally different generator class (a 3DGS-based physics twin).**
   - *Prediction*: applying [[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s continuity / self-collision / execution-fidelity scoring to [[2412.14957|DREMA]]'s Gaussian-Splatting-plus-PyBullet rollouts reproduces a downstream-SR gap in the same direction as H1's RoboDream result — evidence the filter's load-bearingness is a property of kinematic-physics validation itself, not an artifact of compositional-video-diffusion engines or the IDM-bridging step H1 needs. Unlike RoboDream's pixel-only output, DREMA's physics engine already yields per-object positions and orientations, so VISTA's filter scores that trajectory state directly — no IDM reconstruction, and no IDM-error confound.
   - *Test*: repeat H1's score-controlled high-vs-low ablation with DREMA substituted for RoboDream as the target generator, on the same fixed suite ([[2406.02523|RoboCasa]]); add DREMA's own native filter — its goal-position-proximity check, which discards a transformed demo if final object positions miss the expected goal beyond a threshold — as a second baseline, parallel to how H1 pits VISTA against CRAFT's success-replay.
   - *Row*: [[2412.14957|DREMA]] (digital-twin generator, 3DGS + PyBullet physics) as the second-generator target; [[2606.04708|VISTA]] (kinematic-physics filter) as before; DREMA's own goal-position-proximity check as the native-filter baseline.
   - *Falsifier*: the gap vanishes or reverses on DREMA's rollouts, or VISTA's filter adds nothing beyond DREMA's own native goal-position-proximity check → the load-bearingness is either bound to compositional-video-diffusion artifacts (narrowing B4's claim to "transfers within video-diffusion engines only") or redundant with generator-native physics verification, and B4's cross-architecture-class transfer claim is wrong.
3. **H3 — The measured filter-transfer gap is a property of the filter, not an artifact of which IDM bridges pixels to a scoreable trajectory.**
   - *Prediction*: repeating H1's VISTA-on-RoboDream ablation with three structurally different IDMs as the bridging step — [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s sparse inverse, [[2605.12167|MoLA]]'s mixture-of-IDMs (semantic/depth/flow), and [[2604.17887|StableIDM]]'s truncation-robust refinement — yields a filter-transfer gap that stays within a narrow band across all three bridges, i.e. the ≥15 pp differential (or its absence) does not flip sign or collapse depending on which IDM recovers the trajectory.
   - *Test*: hold VISTA's filter and RoboDream's rollouts fixed; swap only the bridging IDM across the three candidates; report the filter-transfer gap (high-score-filtered SR minus low-score-filtered SR) for each IDM alongside each IDM's own reconstruction/action-recovery accuracy on a held-out set, so gap-vs-IDM-quality can be regressed directly instead of averaged away as H1's Risks note it currently is.
   - *Row*: [[2604.01985|WAV]] (sparse inverse) / [[2605.12167|MoLA]] (mixture-of-IDMs, 92.7% LIBERO-Plus) / [[2604.17887|StableIDM]] (truncation-robust, AgiBot truncated 30.7% Acc) as the three IDM-bridge candidates, swapped into H1's VISTA-on-RoboDream ablation.
   - *Falsifier*: the gap varies systematically with IDM choice — e.g. tracks each IDM's own accuracy rather than staying stable — → H1's headline number is measuring IDM quality, not filter transfer, and the transfer claim must be reported as IDM-conditional rather than a single number.
4. **H4 — Filter load-bearingness requires re-scoring for the generative engine's target embodiment; it does not transfer embodiment-blind.**
   - *Prediction*: VISTA's own data already shows the surprise this hypothesis turns on — the *same* trajectories that score 0.00 and fail on RealMan score high and succeed 0.80 overall SR / 1.00 post-grasp SR when re-scored for R1Pro; a subset that looks unusable for one robot body is a *high-quality* subset for a differently-shaped one. That is not a small calibration detail, it means "load-bearing" is a claim about a (filter, body) pair, not the filter alone. So: applying VISTA's filter calibrated on RealMan to a generative engine whose target embodiment differs (RoboDream's Franka rollouts, or a cross-embodiment engine like [[2512.02729|RoboWheel]] / [[2606.17385|EgoInfinity]]) without re-scoring should reproduce the same reversal — the filter-transfer gap only holds when the filter is re-scored for the *target* embodiment, and collapses or reverses if a RealMan-calibrated score is applied embodiment-blind to a different target.
   - *Test*: score the same generated rollouts under VISTA's filter twice — once using RealMan-calibrated scoring parameters, once re-scored for the generator's actual target embodiment — and compare the resulting downstream-SR gap between the two scoring regimes on RoboDream (H1's substrate) and on a cross-embodiment engine.
   - *Row*: [[2606.04708|VISTA]] (RealMan / R1Pro / ACone embodiment-conditioned re-scoring, 0.80 SR / 1.00 PSR on R1Pro vs failure elsewhere) as the mechanism; [[2512.02729|RoboWheel]] (cross-embodiment physics-feasibility filter, 47.5% vs 35.0% teleop) / [[2606.17385|EgoInfinity]] (SE(3)-equivariant cross-embodiment IK retargeting, 0.821 G1 / 0.774 Robonaut2) as the cross-embodiment generator targets.
   - *Falsifier*: the embodiment-blind score produces the same downstream-SR gap as the re-scored version → the filter's load-bearingness is embodiment-invariant and H4's re-scoring requirement is unnecessary overhead, simplifying B4's transfer protocol.
5. **H5 — The transfer-relevant signal lives in the physics/executability sub-scores, not the smoothness sub-score.**
   - *Prediction*: VISTA's aggregate filter is a weighted product of three components — trajectory continuity ($s_{tc}$, embodiment-agnostic smoothness), self-collision risk ($s_{sr}$, embodiment-dependent), and execution fidelity ($s_{ef}$, embodiment-dependent) — and decomposing it on RoboDream's rollouts (H1's substrate) shows the collision+fidelity subset ($s_{sr} \times s_{ef}$) reproduces most of the full filter's downstream-SR gap, mirroring [[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]]'s finding that its physics-critic-alone (collisions 7.8→1.9%, downstream SR 39.1% vs 13.2%) carries the load-bearing signal in a two-critic filter, while continuity alone ($s_{tc}$) captures substantially less of the gap.
   - *Test*: run H1's score-controlled ablation three ways on the same RoboDream rollouts — full VISTA score, continuity-only ($s_{tc}$), collision+fidelity-only ($s_{sr} \times s_{ef}$) — and compare each subset's downstream-SR gap against the full filter's.
   - *Row*: [[2606.04708|VISTA]] (three-component score $s_{tc}$/$s_{sr}$/$s_{ef}$) as the decomposition target; [[2602.10116|SAGE-Scene]] (physics-critic-alone ablation) as the precedent that a filter's sub-components carry unequal load.
   - *Falsifier*: continuity-only ($s_{tc}$) reproduces the full filter's gap as well as collision+fidelity does → the cheap, embodiment-agnostic smoothness score is sufficient on its own, and H4's costlier per-embodiment re-scoring is unnecessary for most of the transfer benefit.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Synthesized data looks plausible but is not executable** — physically infeasible demos teach the wrong dynamics. → Make a physics-validation filter mandatory, not optional (H1); report the score-controlled (high-score vs low-score) downstream SR gap as the first ablation.
> - **The IDM bridging step is unproven, not a controlled variable** — [[2606.04708|VISTA]]'s filter scores kinematic trajectories, not pixels; applying it to a generative engine's rollouts requires an IDM to first recover a trajectory from generated video, and that IDM's own error could be large enough to explain H1's hunted-for ≥15 pp differential in either direction. → H3 is the dedicated test: sweep three structurally different IDMs and regress gap-vs-IDM-quality directly; if the gap tracks IDM accuracy rather than staying stable, treat the transfer claim as untested with current tooling, not confirmed or refuted.
> - **The "success-replay does not transfer" foil is asserted, not shown** — the differential claim's weakest leg: [[2604.03552|CRAFT]] reports 89.3% cross-*embodiment*, which is a different invariance than cross-*generator*, so success-replay's transfer failure is assumed. → Make H1's ablation measure success-replay's transfer directly; if it transfers equally to the kinematic filter, demote the headline from "physics transfers, success-replay doesn't" to "ground-truth-anchored filters transfer."
> - **The 15 pp differential may be below detectable power** — RoboCasa-100 SR CIs over few seeds can swallow a 15 pp inter-filter gap. → Run the power calc (n needed to detect 15 pp at p<0.05) before the ablation; if infeasible, the differential cannot be the headline regardless of whether it is true.
> - **DREMA's built-in goal-position-proximity check confounds H2** — DREMA already discards physically-invalid demos before VISTA's filter ever sees them, so a null result could mean "VISTA's filter is redundant with any physics check," not "VISTA's filter doesn't transfer." → Run H2 on DREMA's *unfiltered* raw rollouts (bypass DREMA's own τ-threshold discard) as the primary test surface; use DREMA's native filter only as a second baseline, not the input VISTA scores.
> - **Applying a RealMan-calibrated score embodiment-blind silently inflates or deflates the gap** — VISTA's own Table 5 shows the *same* trajectories score and succeed differently on RealMan/R1Pro/ACone, so H1/H2's numbers are only meaningful for the embodiment the filter was scored for. → Make H4's re-scoring check a precondition before reporting any cross-generator gap, not an afterthought; state which embodiment each reported number is calibrated for.
> - **A single dominant sub-score could be mistaken for the whole filter's contribution** — if H5 finds collision+fidelity alone reproduces most of the gap, continuing to report VISTA's full three-term product as "the filter" overstates what is actually load-bearing and misleads future adopters into recomputing an unnecessary component. → Report H5's per-component decomposition alongside the aggregate gap, not just the aggregate.

## Cluster C — WAM Action-Space Completeness

*Every direction in A and B treats the imagined state as something to represent better (A) or verify harder (B) — neither cluster asks whether the WAM's own action space is complete. C1 asks what happens when the field's calibration machinery itself flags an ambiguity no amount of training-time verification can resolve, and only a real action taken for its information value alone can close.*

### C1 — Calibration-Triggered Epistemic Probing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | C — Action-Space Completeness |
| **Thesis** | Give a WAM a third action category — a brief, targeted probe taken purely to collapse an ambiguity the WAM's own calibration signal flags as irresolvable from imagination alone — then return to the task. The reason it must work: some physical facts (what's under an occluding object, today's coefficient of friction, a sub-millimeter peg-hole offset) are not recoverable from the image no matter how well the WAM is trained or how hard its rollout is verified; only a real touch or wiggle produces the missing evidence. Every WAM in this doc's ~265-method-paper corpus is trained on demonstrations where every recorded action serves the labeled goal, so this category does not exist in the field's action space today. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2606.26025\|ICWM]] (method), [[2604.01985\|WAV]] (method), [[2509.00178\|Poke and Strike]] (method), [[2410.18964\|DISaM]] (method) |
| **Key targets** | ≥15pp real-task SR gain on a high-ambiguity stratum (occluded content / ambiguous friction / sub-mm peg-hole offset) vs a matched calibrate-only WAM, at ≤2 extra real timesteps, with no SR change on a low-ambiguity control stratum (the card's central bet); match [[2509.00178\|Poke and Strike]]'s real-hardware exploration-then-execution reliability (8/9 Striking, 8/10 Edge Pushing) using a single calibration-triggered probe instead of Poke and Strike's multi-step learned exploration policy; beat [[2601.08325\|ActiveVLA]]'s 91.8% RLBench / 65.9% COLOSSEUM always-on active-perception ceiling with probes firing on <20% of episodes |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: every WAM in this doc's corpus is trained on demonstration data — human teleop, scripted planners, RL rollouts scored on task return — where every recorded action serves the labeled goal, so behavior-cloning-style tooling cannot represent an epistemic-action *category*: a human teleoperator's disambiguating micro-actions fold indistinguishably into the demonstration. A2's entire 37-system sensed-vs-predicted-force table and B3's train-vs-runtime calibration axis both silently assume the action space is exhaustively task-progressing — neither considers a third option. Notably, none of this doc's own ~22-survey Survey Landscape names this gap — not because it is a niche concern, but because the surveys taxonomize *within* the demonstration-imitation paradigm (substrate form, encoding objective, calibration timing) and never step outside it to ask whether the action space itself is complete; the absence is the finding, not an oversight in coverage. This is why C1 carries no survey or benchmark anchor: none exists, because the gap has never been named as a gap, only worked around.
- **Today's answers**: [[2606.26025|ICWM]]'s Active Probing Phase gets closest inside a WAM-adjacent backbone — but its own ablation of four probing strategies finds "no single strategy dominating," resolves a *fixed* system configuration (camera viewpoint, gripper offset) constant across the whole deployment rather than a per-episode hidden variable, and never fires again once the task starts (+13.0% [[2306.03310|LIBERO]] OOD, +71% real UR5e). [[2509.00178|Poke and Strike]] proves an explore-then-execute switch works on real hardware (KUKA iiwa 8/9 Striking, 8/10 Edge Pushing) and [[2410.18964|DISaM]] formalizes the information-seeking/information-receiving split with a genuine uncertainty-triggered switch (beats sparse-reward baselines on 5/5 sim+real tasks) — but neither is built on a generative WAM or triggered by a WAM's own imagined-rollout calibration signal; both are bespoke RL architectures evaluated off this doc's own LIBERO/RoboTwin suite. [[2606.00267|StressDream]] shows "active" failure-finding *inside* a WAM's imagination (recall 54%→94%, real fine-tuning SR 39%→71%) but never leaves the imagination — a false friend, not a counter-example.
- **The opening**: [[2607.11822|VoNI]] proves the exact trigger *logic* C1 needs already works once formalized correctly — a certificate-aware Value-of-Information criterion fires diagnostic probes 69% faster and with 85% fewer probing actions than entropy-based triggering on an abrupt noise-jump, and 41% lower tracking error with 59% fewer probes on a gradual drift — just not on a robot, and not on a WAM's imagined rollout. B3's own calibration machinery already computes exactly the signal a probe-trigger needs (imagined-vs-real $\rho$ = 0.984–0.9992 across [[2606.18610|SC3-Eval]] / [[2606.23296|IOI]]); nobody has wired it to a real action. [[2604.01985|WAV]] itself considered and discarded an info-max-exploration design as "unreliable in under-explored data regions," building the purely statistical asymmetry signal C1 now proposes to reuse as a trigger instead of a replacement.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Some physical ambiguities are informationally unresolvable from a static observation, or even from a well-calibrated rollout, no matter how much compute the WAM spends imagining — only new evidence collapses them, and the cheapest source of new evidence is a real action taken for that purpose alone. [[2607.11822|VoNI]] gives the general shape formally (a value-of-information criterion that fires a probe only when a formal signal — there, a reliability certificate gap — is threatened, not on generic entropy), and [[1911.10601|Scaling-Active-Inference]]'s expected-free-energy decomposition splits pragmatic value from epistemic value as two terms in one objective, a decades-old result establishing that "act for information" is a well-posed goal distinct from "act for reward" — a result the generative-WAM lineage has never operationalized inside its own imagination-plus-calibration loop.
- **Assumption being challenged**: not that epistemic actions are impossible to formalize — [[2607.11822|VoNI]]'s certificate-triggered probing and [[1911.10601|Scaling-Active-Inference]]'s EFE objective already do, and [[2410.18964|DISaM]] and [[2509.00178|Poke and Strike]] already run the general recipe (learn to explore, then switch to execution) on real robot hardware. A real-robot *manipulation* instance of classical deep active inference (arXiv:2512.01924) goes further still — it explicitly "switches between goal-directed and exploratory actions in uncertain settings" via expected-free-energy minimization — but it is not a counter-example to this direction's novelty: its world model is a recurrent state-space model (RSSM-family; the paper's own dual-timescale slow/fast design distinguishes it from a standard Dreamer/PlaNet RSSM), not this doc's transformer/diffusion generative-WAM substrate; its explore/exploit balance is a continuously-blended, manually-tuned precision scalar rather than a discrete threshold-triggered probe-then-resume on a specific hidden variable; and it is evaluated on a custom real-robot setup (CALVIN appears only as an appendix check), never this doc's own LIBERO/RoboTwin suite. The assumption every WAM in this doc's corpus makes — B3's own hypothesis structure treats "verify imagination" as choosing *when* to compute on data already collected (train-time vs. runtime), never as an option to go collect new data; A2's 37-system table assumes force is either sensed continuously or predicted from vision, with no third "touch on demand" row; [[2604.01985|WAV]] explicitly discards information-seeking exploration as a *design*, building a purely statistical checker instead — is that a WAM's action space is exhaustively task-progressing. Nobody has coupled a real disambiguating action to the WAM's *own* calibration signal on a *per-episode* hidden variable; [[2606.26025|ICWM]] gets closest and still misses on both counts (random, and fixed pre-task).
- **The bet**: a WAM that, when its own B3-style calibration signal ($\rho$, or forward-inverse divergence) exceeds threshold on a task-relevant hidden variable, inserts one targeted disambiguating action before resuming the task, beats a matched WAM that only calibrates/filters/retries by ≥15pp real-task SR on a high-ambiguity stratum (occluded content, ambiguous friction, sub-mm peg-hole offset), at a cost of ≤2 extra real timesteps, with no SR change on the low-ambiguity stratum where the probe never fires. Both numbers are thresholds this direction *sets*, not margins it inherits — no cited system runs this exact calibration-triggered-probe-vs-calibrate-only comparison, so unlike A2's or B1's targets (each pinned to a specific paper's ceiling), ≥15pp and ≤2 timesteps are fresh, unprecedented thresholds stated plainly as such, in the spirit of B4's "new, untested extension" framing. This bet is not uniformly as strong across ambiguity types: it rests on the calibration signal correctly exceeding threshold in the first place, which [[2607.05966|iKCE]] and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] show holds more plausibly for occlusion-type (missing-information) ambiguity than for friction/regime-transition-type (confidently-wrong) ambiguity, where a single model's own consistency signal can stay flat exactly when it should fire; H6 below tests this directly rather than assuming it away.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (a calibration-triggered probe beats calibrate-only and runtime-filter-only WAMs on genuinely irreducible ambiguity), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 (lead) — A calibration-triggered single probe beats a matched no-probe WAM on high-ambiguity strata, at bounded cost.**
   - *Prediction*: when a WAV-style forward-inverse divergence signal on a task-relevant hidden variable exceeds threshold, inserting one targeted disambiguating action before resuming the task beats a matched WAM that only calibrates/filters/retries by ≥15pp SR on a high-ambiguity stratum, at ≤2 extra real timesteps, with no SR change on a matched low-ambiguity control stratum.
   - *Test*: construct three high-ambiguity strata (occluded content, an [[2607.05966|iKCE]]-style friction sweep, a sub-mm peg-hole offset per [[2407.08028|AutoMate]]) plus a matched low-ambiguity control; compare a calibration-triggered-probe WAM against a calibrate-only WAM ([[2604.01985|WAV]]-style) and a runtime-filter-only WAM ([[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]]-style); report SR delta and extra-timestep cost.
   - *Row*: WAV (calibrate-only foil) / ICWM (fixed pre-task probe foil, never mid-rollout) / the occluded-content stratum's sharpest existing foil is an occlusion-oriented LIBERO extension that answers the same ambiguity with a *synthesized complementary view* rather than a real action (arXiv:2606.10862, "without requiring additional cameras at deployment time") — exactly the imagine-harder answer H1's bet is set against.
   - *Falsifier*: the probe-augmented WAM ties the calibrate-only WAM on the high-ambiguity stratum → the ambiguity was resolvable from calibration/verification alone, and the epistemic-action category adds nothing. On the friction stratum specifically, a tie is ambiguous between two readings — the ambiguity was resolvable without probing, or the calibration trigger simply never fired because, per [[2607.05966|iKCE]] and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]], its own signal stays flat across exactly this kind of regime shift; H6 below isolates which.
2. **H2 — A2's sensed-vs-predicted-force binary is a false dichotomy; touch-on-demand is a genuine third option.**
   - *Prediction*: on a task-relevant contact property (e.g., unknown object stiffness or friction before grasp), keeping a force sensor disabled except during a calibration-triggered brief touch-probe (touch-on-demand) matches [[2606.08555|FAWAM]]'s sensored-continuous ceiling within a small margin while using the sensor on a fraction of timesteps [[2602.02142|FD-VLA]]'s vision-only prediction cannot reach.
   - *Test*: three-arm A/B on A2's [[2505.18472|ManiFeel]]-class contact-rich tasks — continuous-sensored (upper bound) / vision-only-predicted (lower bound) / calibration-triggered touch-on-demand (new arm); report SR and sensor duty-cycle.
   - *Row*: FAWAM (sensored ceiling) / FD-VLA (sensorless-predicted floor) / [[2603.17851|DexViTac]] (recovers ≥50% of the 83.3%→43.3% tactile-drop, A2's own key target, as the shared success bar).
   - *Falsifier*: touch-on-demand's SR is statistically indistinguishable from vision-only-predicted at matched sensor duty-cycle → the third option collapses into the predict-from-vision pole and A2's binary stands.
3. **H3 — Act-time calibration is a genuine third pole beyond B3's train-time-vs-runtime axis.**
   - *Prediction*: for tasks where a B3-style calibration signal stays high even after both train-time calibration *and* runtime filtering/retrying — i.e. the WAM has exhausted what it can do with existing evidence — only a real disambiguating action measurably lowers the divergence; train-time-only and runtime-filter-only calibration cannot close this residual gap.
   - *Test*: pre-register the candidate stratum on an *independent, measurable* property (occlusion severity / friction unknown-ness / offset magnitude — the same three strata H1 defines), not on whether train-time/runtime calibration already plateau there; then, as a reported finding rather than an entry condition, measure whether train-time-only and runtime-filter-only calibration in fact plateau on that stratum; compare final $\rho$ and downstream SR of act-time-probe-augmented vs. train-time-only vs. runtime-filter-only calibration. On the friction stratum, this plateau measurement is not a given: [[2607.05966|iKCE]] and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] show a single calibration signal can stay flat rather than elevated across a regime-transition boundary, so friction may fail to clear H3's implicit entry condition (a signal that reads as high in the first place) in a different way than occlusion or sub-mm offset would; if so, H3's residual-gap claim is best evaluated on the occlusion and offset strata, with the friction stratum's trigger-detection question left to H6.
   - *Row*: WAV (train-time) / Pre-VLA (runtime filter) / [[2606.18610|SC3-Eval]] and [[2606.23296|IOI]] ($\rho$-measurement instruments for the residual-divergence stratum).
   - *Falsifier*: on the pre-registered stratum, train-time or runtime calibration alone (a) fails to plateau (never exhausts what it can do) — in which case H3 has no residual gap to test, and is inconclusive rather than confirmed — or (b) plateaus but still closes the residual gap without any real action → either result means the "irreducible ambiguity" premise is false and act-time calibration is unnecessary.
4. **H4 — B2's imagination-only "active" failure-finding needs a real-world epistemic-action twin.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2606.00267|StressDream]]-style imagined active search (steering initial noise toward high-impact failures *within* imagination) does not close the real-world forward-inverse divergence gap on genuinely ambiguous states, because the search never touches reality; a real disambiguating probe does.
   - *Test*: on the same ambiguous-state set, compare StressDream's imagined-failure-steering followed by *more imagination* vs. followed by a real disambiguating probe; measure real downstream SR.
   - *Row*: StressDream (imagination-only active search) / B2's [[2602.11075|RISE]]-style imagined-RL loop as the reference imagined-only baseline.
   - *Falsifier*: the imagined-only steering closes as much of the real SR gap as the real-probe intervention → imagination substitutes for action and the epistemic-action category is unnecessary.
5. **H5 — The mechanism generalizes across ambiguity modalities, not tied to one sense.**
   - *Prediction*: the same threshold-trigger-then-probe-then-resume mechanism, instantiated as a *physical* viewpoint-shift probe for occlusion ([[2503.12609|VISO-Grasp]]-style camera motion — not [[2601.08325|ActiveVLA]]'s virtual re-render from the existing point cloud, which produces no new evidence and would collapse H5's occlusion arm into the same imagine-harder pole H1 sets itself against), a wiggle probe for friction ([[2607.05966|iKCE]]-style), and a touch probe for sub-mm offset (B1's contact-mode taxonomy), each independently clears the ≥15pp bar on its respective high-ambiguity stratum.
   - *Test*: run H1's protocol separately per modality; for the viewpoint modality, run on RLBench/COLOSSEUM (ActiveVLA's own suite) so the physical-probe's SR is comparable to ActiveVLA's always-on 91.8%/65.9% ceiling at matched task set — the comparison target, not the probe's own mechanism; report whether all three modalities clear the bar or the gain is modality-specific.
   - *Row*: VISO-Grasp (viewpoint modality, physical probe mechanism) / ActiveVLA (viewpoint modality, ceiling comparator only) / cross-ref B1 (contact-mode taxonomy, offset modality).
   - *Falsifier*: only one modality clears the bar → the mechanism is not a general epistemic-action category but a modality-specific fix, undermining the structural claim.
6. **H6 — Trigger choice is failure-type dependent: the calibration signal should match an ensemble-uncertainty trigger on occlusion, but miss what it catches on friction/regime-transition.** Distinct from H1: H1 compares a probe-augmented WAM against a no-probe (calibrate-only) WAM; H6 holds the probe-vs-no-probe question settled and instead ablates *which signal fires the trigger*, stratified by failure type, with the IS/IR policies themselves held fixed — a component-level swap, not a system-level comparison. This hypothesis was originally framed as a single "equal-or-higher" bet across all strata; [[2607.05966|iKCE]] and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] (both cited elsewhere in this doc) show that framing cannot hold uniformly — a single model's own consistency signal is a defensible proxy for genuinely-missing information (occlusion), but stays flat exactly where a real system crosses a physical regime boundary it was never trained to extrapolate past (friction), so the comparison has to run per failure type, not once.
   - *Prediction*: on the occlusion (missing-information) stratum, replacing [[2410.18964|DISaM]]'s ensemble-uncertainty (KL-divergence across encoder-sampled contexts fed through the IR policy) trigger with a WAM's own calibration signal ($\rho$ or forward-inverse divergence) yields comparable firing rate and SR — a single model's uncertainty about content it never observed is a reasonable stand-in for genuine ambiguity. On the friction (regime-transition) stratum, the calibration-signal trigger fires on fewer than half of the friction-regime transitions the ensemble-uncertainty trigger catches at a matched false-positive rate on the low-ambiguity control stratum (H1's control) — because [[2607.05966|iKCE]]'s own diagnostic shows a single imagining model's forward consistency stays statistically flat across its 13-magnitude friction sweep (0.1–1.7) where the real policy's reward collapses at μ=0.20, and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] confirms the same confidently-fluent-but-wrong pattern at real-world scale (trajectory R²=0.99 alongside recovered free-fall acceleration under 1% of gravity). DISaM's intrinsic reward (the IR policy's predicted-vs-ground-truth action discrepancy) stays untouched by this swap in either stratum — it trains the IS policy offline and is not itself the runtime trigger.
   - *Test*: for each stratum, swap only the runtime trigger mechanism in DISaM's dual-policy architecture — calibration-signal vs. ensemble-uncertainty — holding the IS/IR policies and their training fixed. For the friction stratum, source the regime-transition trials from [[2607.05966|iKCE]]'s own conditioning-perturbation protocol (13 friction magnitudes, 0.1–1.7, real-reward collapse at μ=0.20); calibrate both triggers' false-positive rate on H1's low-ambiguity control stratum; then compare, at matched FPR, the fraction of friction-regime transitions each trigger catches. For the occlusion stratum, compare firing rate and downstream SR directly. **Constraint on the comparison arm**: the ensemble-uncertainty trigger must be computed against real interaction, exactly as DISaM's own IR policy does it — never inside a further closed-loop imagined rollout. [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] shows a signal from the same ensemble-disagreement-uncertainty family (there, disagreement across a dynamics-model ensemble; here, disagreement across DISaM's context-encoder ensemble — related mechanisms, not the identical computation) converges to in-distribution levels *inside* closed-loop imagination even as real compounding error keeps growing; an imagined-rollout ensemble-uncertainty arm would risk inheriting the same attractor-bias blindness the calibration-signal trigger is being tested against, collapsing the comparison into a test of two equally-blind signals rather than a real contrast.
   - *Row*: DISaM (ensemble-uncertainty trigger, the comparison arm, computed on real interaction) / [[2607.05966|iKCE]] (friction-sweep protocol and μ=0.20 regime boundary supplying the regime-transition stratum, and the mechanism for why a single-model signal misses it) / [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] (real-world-scale confirmation that the same fluent-but-wrong pattern holds beyond the DMC toy setting) / [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] (the reason the comparison arm must stay real-rollout-computed, not imagined).
   - *Falsifier*: on the friction stratum, the calibration-signal trigger matches or beats the ensemble-uncertainty trigger at matched FPR → the flat-signal-at-regime-transition concern does not transfer to trigger-detection, and a single calibration signal suffices even for regime-transition ambiguity. Conversely, if the calibration-signal trigger also underperforms the ensemble on the occlusion stratum → the calibration signal carries no stratified advantage at all, just a uniform disadvantage, and C1's bet that the WAM's own signal is sufficient for at least some ambiguity types fails outright.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **The falsifier could go either way** — if train-time-plus-runtime calibration already closes the ambiguity gap on the chosen strata, the whole category collapses into B3 and C1 was never load-bearing. → Pre-register the high-ambiguity strata (occlusion / friction / sub-mm offset) against existing benchmark splits *before* running H1's A/B, so the strata are not chosen post-hoc to guarantee a positive result.
> - **Episode-length benchmarks penalize the probe by construction** — every fixed-length suite this doc cites ([[2306.03310|LIBERO]], [[2510.13626|LIBERO-Plus]], [[2504.13059|RoboTwin]], CALVIN, [[2505.18472|ManiFeel]], [[2606.13877|ContactWorld]]) scores 1–2 extra disambiguating steps as strictly worse, so C1's own bet can look like a regression on the suites this doc otherwise trusts. → Report SR at matched *total* budget (task steps plus probe steps), not fixed episode length, and report epistemic-action cost as its own axis, separate from task-progress cost.
> - **A resolved-looking probe may not have resolved anything** — recovering whether the ambiguity actually collapsed (not just that a probe happened) needs its own verification step, mirroring B4's own IDM-bridging-step fix (H3 there regresses gap-vs-bridge-quality rather than averaging it away). → Measure the probe's own information yield (did the post-probe calibration signal actually drop?) as a first-class metric, not just downstream SR.
> - **An unconstrained probe can make things worse** — touching the wrong object, or a wiggle that perturbs an already-stable grasp, wastes the ≤2-step budget and can actively harm the task. → Bound the probe to a whitelisted, task-agnostic-but-safe action set (per B1's discrete contact-mode taxonomy for what "safe touch" means) rather than letting the policy invent an arbitrary disambiguating action.
> - **The calibration signal inherits every blind spot of the imagination it's computed from** — because $\rho$ / forward-inverse divergence is itself a product of the same imagining WAM, it cannot be a uniformly reliable trigger: [[2607.05966|iKCE]] shows this exact class of signal stays statistically flat across a friction-regime boundary where the real policy's reward collapses (μ=0.20), and [[2608.05948|GAUGE]] confirms the same smoothly-confidently-wrong pattern at real-world scale (trajectory R²=0.99 alongside recovered free-fall acceleration under 1% of gravity) — the calibration signal cannot detect the specific class of failure where the model is fluent and wrong. → Do not treat C1's three ambiguity strata as interchangeable evidence for one claim: the calibration signal is a plausible trigger for occlusion-type (missing-information) ambiguity, but its reliability on friction/regime-transition-type (confidently-wrong) ambiguity is not assumed — H6 tests directly whether an ensemble-uncertainty trigger recovers what it misses there.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

> [!tip] Latent Prediction Is the Dominant Substrate — and Now Has a Formal Membership Test
> A1, A2, A3, and B2 all assume "dense signal at training, latent at deployment" with JEPA / DiT-on-latent backbones, but the field has lacked a test for *when* a learned latent is actually a world model. [[2605.26379|LeJEPA-World-Model]] supplies it (identifiable iff isotropic-Gaussian, then latent planning matches an oracle), and [[2605.25313|UWM-JEPA]] extends the substrate to belief space. So A1's hybrid latents, A2's tactile-imagination latent, A3's encoding-quality choice, and B2's self-evolution rollouts answer to one membership test instead of convention — and A3 makes the sharpest use of it, asking whether the semantic / continuous latents that win control are exactly the ones that pass the isotropic-Gaussian test (its H4).

> [!tip] Verifiable Predicates over Imagined State Turn Diagnosis into Action
> B1, B2, and B3 each make the recurring "statistical correlation ≠ causal understanding" diagnosis enforceable on the *imagination itself*, not just the pixels: B1 makes contact a discrete verifiable transition ($c_t \in$ {no-contact, making, in-contact, breaking, slipping}), B2 makes recovery contingent on a verified imagined rollout, and B3 makes the forward-inverse asymmetry a train-time calibration signal. [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s asymmetry and [[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]]'s rollout truncation are the shared mechanism — and A2's imagined wrench is one more channel the same predicate machinery must score before the policy trusts it.

> [!tip] Calibrated Imagination Is the Training-Time Twin of Runtime Verification
> B3, B2, and A2 form a trust stack at three different times. B3 calibrates imagination at *training* time (forward-inverse asymmetry, [[2604.01985|WAV]] 2× sample-eff; epistemic gating, [[2504.16680|RWM-U]] 0.91 real-robot reward); B2 verifies and recovers at *runtime* ([[2605.22446|Pre-VLA]] truncates unreliable dreams); A2's imagined-vs-measured wrench loss is a train-time forecast the same machinery can score. The coupling is quantitative: B3's calibration raises the imagined-vs-real $\rho$ that B2 uses as its stop condition, so investing in B3 shrinks B2's recovery work, and A2's force imagination is one more channel calibration must keep honest.

> [!tip] Train Density and Deploy Density Are Two Independent Knobs the Field Treats as One
> A1, A3, and B4 all separate a quantity the field bundles. A1 separates *train density* from *deploy density* — dense pixel/3DGS supervision yet a cheap latent rollout, [[2605.20752|GaussianDream]]'s heads dropped at inference. A3 separates the *encoder objective* from the latent-vs-pixel question — semantic / continuous over reconstruction, [[2605.06388|Semantic-LDM-WM]]'s +9.8 pp swing at fixed architecture. B4 separates the *output's durability* from its in-episode use — the same imagination that rolls out once becomes a reusable training corpus, [[2606.02577|RoboDream]]'s Gen-Mix 62.5% vs 36.3%. The common error each corrects is treating one WAM design decision as a single binary when it is two orthogonal axes.

> [!tip] Efficiency Is a Deployment Prerequisite That Couples to Every Direction Here
> No direction owns efficiency, yet A1 and B2 both need real-time budgets to be feasible at all — the 3–5 Hz AR ceiling and 4.8× WAM latency cost are the anchors ([[2604.16484|DexWorldModel]]'s O(1) memory + async inference shows the levers are co-designable; full real-time co-design lives in the umbrella). A1's train-dense/deploy-light hybrid is itself an efficiency move, and B2's evolution cycle is infeasible if each imagined rollout is too slow to iterate, so a method that ignores the latency budget cannot be deployed whatever its SR. [[2606.05254|Flash-WAM]]'s 23× distillation and [[2605.28816|Gamma-World]]'s linear-scaling streaming are the levers A1 and B4 both draw on.

> [!tip] A Real Action Is the Fourth Trust Mechanism, Beside Substrate, Calibration, and Verification
> A2, B2, B3, and C1 all confront the same fact from different angles: some ambiguity in the imagined state cannot be resolved by computing on data already collected. B3's calibration signal ($\rho$, forward-inverse divergence) tells a WAM *that* it is uncertain; B2's separate verifier and StressDream's imagination-only active search look for failure *inside* what the WAM already knows; A2's imagined wrench forecasts a force channel the WAM cannot yet directly sense. C1 is the missing fourth leg — it wires B3's own signal to a real, targeted action instead of another round of computing, closing exactly the ambiguity the other three can only flag or search around. [[2604.01985|WAV]]'s asymmetry signal is the shared substrate all four already compute; C1's bet is that nobody has connected it to the one intervention guaranteed to produce new evidence — a real touch.
