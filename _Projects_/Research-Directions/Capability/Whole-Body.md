# Promising Research Directions: Whole-Body Coordination — Loco-Manipulation, Mobile Manipulation, Force-Adaptive Control

> [!abstract] Overview
> A humanoid that manipulates *while it moves* faces one structural fact: **the arm and the legs are mechanically coupled** — an arm reach is a reaction torque on the base, so whole-body competence does not factor into "an arm controller plus a leg controller." The cross-term is where part-wise stacks fail under load and out of distribution.
> These **13 directions across 4 clusters** organize the bets around that coupling: make it an *explicit predicted quantity* (A), coordinate it with a *moving base* (B), hold it under *external force* (C), and get the *demonstration data* to learn it (D).
> The editorial bet: **the coupling is structure to *predict*, not data to *collect*** — the directions that model it win on a fixed data budget, where more teleop does not.

## Methodology

**Scope.** This doc reads ~16 humanoid / loco-manipulation / mobile-manipulation / teleoperation surveys and benchmarks (the Survey Landscape below) plus ~40 whole-body method papers from `_KnowledgeHub_/`, cross-checked against [[07_Robotics-and-Embodied-AI|07_Robotics-and-Embodied-AI]] and the `Embodied-AI/` deep-dives [[02_Dataset-Benchmark-Environment|02_Dataset-Benchmark-Environment]], [[09_Contact-Rich-and-Whole-Body-Control|09_Contact-Rich-and-Whole-Body-Control]], and [[14_Sim-to-Real-Transfer|14_Sim-to-Real-Transfer]]. It owns the *coupling* between manipulation and locomotion — whole-body loco-manipulation, mobile manipulation (moving base + arm together), force-adaptive control under external load, and whole-body teleoperation / retargeting; a direction is admitted only if both base/leg motion and manipulation are essential. Pure locomotion goes to [[Locomotion|Locomotion]]; fixed-base or arm-only manipulation goes to [[Manipulation|Manipulation]]; world-model and sim-to-real substrates are cross-referenced ([[WAM|WAM]], [[Sim2Real|Sim2Real]]), not re-clustered.

## Formal Framing

**The whole-body coupled-dynamics object.** A whole-body system carries a configuration $q = (q_{\text{base}}, q_{\text{leg}}, q_{\text{arm}}, q_{\text{hand}})$ on a single floating base. Its dynamics couple every subsystem through the shared inertia matrix and the external-wrench Jacobian:

$$\ddot q = M(q)^{-1}\!\left(\tau - C(q,\dot q)\,\dot q - g(q) + J_{\text{ext}}^{\top} F_{\text{ext}}\right)$$

The coupling is structural: $M(q)$ is **not block-diagonal** across (base, leg, arm). An arm acceleration induces a base/leg reaction torque (the off-diagonal $M_{\text{arm,leg}}$ term), and an external hand wrench $F_{\text{ext}}$ propagates through $J_{\text{ext}}^{\top}$ to *every* joint, including the legs that keep the centre-of-mass over the support polygon. So a part-wise policy that factors $\pi = (\pi_{\text{leg}}, \pi_{\text{arm}})$ with independent value $V(a_{\text{leg}}) + V(a_{\text{arm}})$ throws away the load-bearing cross-term — the central reframing of Cluster A.

Three coupling formalisms organize this doc:

| Object | Formalism | Cluster |
|---|---|---|
| **Arm-as-disturbance** | An arm command $a_{\text{arm}}$ injects a reaction $\delta_{\text{base}} = M_{\text{base,arm}}\,\ddot q_{\text{arm}}$ the leg policy must reject *anticipatorily*; the whole-body value is non-separable across subsystems | A |
| **Base-as-manipulation-DoF** | The mobile-manipulation action is a joint $a = (a_{\text{base}}, a_{\text{arm}}) \sim \pi(a \mid o, l)$ where base velocity is a *manipulation* degree of freedom — repositioning extends the reachable workspace mid-task, not before it | B |
| **External wrench under load** | A payload / contact force $F_{\text{ext}}$ at the hand sets a whole-body equilibrium constraint $J_{\text{ext}}^{\top} F_{\text{ext}} + g(q) \in$ feasible-torque $\times$ support-polygon; the legs compensate for what the arms feel | C |

**The mobile-manipulation joint action.** The mobile-manipulation action is a single joint distribution over the whole body, $p(a_{\text{base}}, a_{\text{torso}}, a_{\text{arm}} \mid o)$, and the order in which it is factored is *not* arbitrary. Autoregressive conditioning — drawing the base action first, then the torso conditioned on it, then the arm conditioned on both — is the factoring

$$p(a_{\text{base}}, a_{\text{torso}}, a_{\text{arm}} \mid o) = p(a_{\text{base}} \mid o)\,p(a_{\text{torso}} \mid a_{\text{base}}, o)\,p(a_{\text{arm}} \mid a_{\text{base}}, a_{\text{torso}}, o)$$

so that each downstream link absorbs the displacement its upstream link just committed, rather than the independent factorization $\prod_i p(a_i \mid o)$ that lets the end-effector drift accumulate across links. This is the formal content of "base is a manipulation DoF" — the base action is the *condition* the arm action is drawn under, not a separate navigate-then-grasp phase. Cluster B builds on this.

**The feasibility-preserving composition.** A whole-body action assembled as a per-joint convex combination of a fixed set of $K$ individually-feasible primitives,

$$a(q) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} w_k(q)\,\pi_k^{\text{prim}}(q), \qquad w_k(q) \ge 0,\ \textstyle\sum_k w_k(q) = 1,$$

lies in the convex hull of $\{\pi_k^{\text{prim}}\}$ by construction. If each primitive respects the torque / contact / balance constraints, any simplex-weighted blend of them does too — feasibility is then a property of the *combination rule*, not of a per-task reward, and it is the simplex constraint ($w \ge 0$, $\sum_k w_k = 1$) that supplies it. This is the inverse of a monolith, which must rediscover the feasible set from scratch on every new task. Cluster A builds on this.

## Cluster A — Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation

*Coupled arm–leg dynamics on a single floating base — where an arm reach is a balance disturbance, the whole-body value is non-separable, and flat RL fails on the coupled high-DoF action space. The four directions attack the same non-separability from different layers: modelling the coupling term itself (A1), composing feasible primitives so the action never leaves the feasible set (A2), closing the precision loop in the world frame with the base as an active transport DoF (A3), and emitting joint loco+manip commands through one latent interface (A4).*

### A1 — Coupled-Dynamics Whole-Body Action Models

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation |
| **Thesis** | Build a whole-body policy that predicts the base/leg reaction an arm reach induces, instead of bolting an arm policy onto a leg policy. The reason it must work: the inertia matrix $M(q)$ is non-block-diagonal, so an arm acceleration *is* a leg-balance disturbance, not a separate sub-problem. The field assumes whole-body competence factors into an arm controller plus a balance controller. The bet is in First-principles below — make the coupling term an *explicit* predicted quantity and beat the implicit-coupling state of the art on the same fixed data. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark), [[2603.20147\|AGILE]] (method), [[2602.06341\|HiWET]] (method), [[2604.07993\|HEX]] (method), [[2505.06776\|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] (method) |
| **Key targets** | [[2604.07993\|HEX]] 79.8% ID (7 tasks) / 61.8% OOD (8 scene-variation) vs GR00T N1.5 70.2% / 41.0% and π0.5 OOD 44.3%, 100% initial + 53.3% Place-Box on long-horizon box-convey; [[2505.06776\|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] 0.37 vs 0.60 upper-body tracking error under large force (~2× over best baseline), 100 N cart-pull + 1.2 kg/hand payload; [[2602.06341\|HiWET]] 12.4 mm world-frame end-effector error compensating base disturbance |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a whole-body policy must account for the base/leg reaction an arm reach induces, but most stacks control the parts separately and let that coupling term vanish.
- **Today's answers**: [[2604.07993|HEX]] learns the coupling *implicitly* in a predictive MoE (79.8 ID / 61.8 OOD vs part-wise GR00T N1.5 at 70.2 / 41.0); [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] lets it *emerge* from a shared observation between two RL agents (0.37 vs 0.60 upper-body tracking error under load). The closest *explicit* precedent is [[2509.21231|SEEC]], which combines an analytic dynamics term with a learned residual under an L2 torque-matching loss — but it predicts the *base→arm* end-effector-stabilization direction and explicitly assumes the *reverse* arm→base back-coupling is "negligible because arms are dynamically light," the very premise A1 must overturn. Each works in its lane — none makes the *arm→base reaction* an explicit predicted quantity.
- **The opening**: HEX's own ablation: removing the coupling component (the UPP) is the single largest drop (Pouring 11/12 → 6/12) while its 12M-frame pretraining adds only second-order gain, so the coupling is the lever and no one predicts the arm→base reaction explicitly yet — and [[2509.21231|SEEC]]'s "arms-are-light" assumption is exactly what fails in the fast/loaded-reach regime where the reaction is largest (H2's stratification).

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: The inertia matrix $M(q)$ is non-block-diagonal — the arm, legs, and base are mechanically linked, so by the equations of motion an arm acceleration *is* a reaction torque on the base and legs. This holds no matter the training data. So the value of a whole-body action cannot be split into an arm part plus a leg part. The evidence that this structure is load-bearing is [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s ablation: with the controller and inputs held identical, removing the coupling component (UPP) costs the largest single drop (Pouring 11/12 → 6/12) while the 12M-frame pretraining is second-order — the coupling, not the data, carries whole-body performance.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That a humanoid policy factors into an arm policy plus a leg/balance policy — the bet of every part-wise stack — *and* the sharper sub-assumption [[2509.21231|SEEC]] makes explicit: that the arm→base back-coupling is negligible "because arms are dynamically light," so only the base→arm direction needs an explicit term. [[2604.07993|HEX]] names the factoring failure ("uncoordinated and unstable behaviors") and beats GR00T N1.5 (70.2 / 41.0), ACT, and π0.5 (OOD 44.3) under a *matched* controller + inputs; the 41.0→61.8 OOD gap is the cost of discarding the cross-body link, unrecoverable by more per-part data. The coupling-aware systems leave the term *implicit* ([[2604.07993|HEX]]'s learned MoE), *emergent* ([[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]]'s shared observation), or *one-directional* ([[2509.21231|SEEC]]'s base→arm-only explicit term); nobody predicts the arm→base reaction directly.
- **The bet**: An *explicit* predicted base-reaction $\hat\delta_{\text{base}}=\hat M_{\text{base,arm}}\ddot q_{\text{arm}}$ (an analytic term + learned residual, the same recipe [[2509.21231|SEEC]] uses for the *opposite* direction) fed as an auxiliary-loss target beats [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s implicit MoE *on the same fixed dataset* — widening the 41.0→61.8 OOD margin by ≥ +3 pp (into the mid-60s), with the gain concentrated on the top arm-acceleration quartile exactly where [[2509.21231|SEEC]]'s "arms-are-light" assumption is false, and matching the scale of [[2503.05652|BRS]]'s 53%-from-decoding swing without new data. Falsifiable: if explicit ≤ 61.8% at matched latency — or if the arm→base term is genuinely negligible as [[2509.21231|SEEC]] claims — the coupling is better left to the MoE.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (explicit coupling beats implicit on fixed data), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 — Explicit base-reaction beats the implicit MoE on HEX's fixed data.**
   - *Prediction*: adding $\hat\delta_{\text{base}} = \hat M_{\text{base,arm}}\ddot q_{\text{arm}}$ as an auxiliary-loss target to [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s exact backbone + same dataset lifts OOD past 61.8% (target ≥ +3 pp, into the mid-60s).
   - *Test*: re-run HEX's UPP ablation with the explicit term swapped in for the implicit MoE at matched parameter count; the within-paper 6/12 → 11/12 Pouring swing is the control.
   - *Row*: HEX (implicit).
   - *Falsifier*: explicit ≤ 61.8% at matched latency → the MoE already captures it.
2. **H2 — The explicit-term gain concentrates on fast arm motions, where SEEC's "arms-are-light" assumption is false.**
   - *Prediction*: stratifying OOD tasks by arm-acceleration magnitude, the explicit-over-implicit margin is largest in the top acceleration quartile (where $\ddot q_{\text{arm}}$, hence the reaction, is largest) and near-zero in the bottom quartile — directly measuring where [[2509.21231|SEEC]]'s negligible-back-coupling premise breaks.
   - *Test*: bin HEX's 8 OOD scene-variation tasks by peak arm acceleration; report margin per bin, and report the measured arm→base reaction magnitude vs SEEC's "negligible" threshold in the top bin.
   - *Row*: HEX (implicit) / SEEC (explicit base→arm, arms-light premise).
   - *Falsifier*: a flat margin across bins, or the top-quartile reaction is genuinely negligible as SEEC claims → the gain isn't the reaction term.
3. **H3 — A shared *predictor* carries more coupling than a shared *observation*.**

> - **Coupling model needs an accurate inertia model** — predicting $M_{\text{base,arm}}$ depends on the URDF/mass distribution. → Treat the coupling predictor as a learned residual over a nominal analytic model; report sim-vs-real coupling-prediction error, and lean on [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s learned MoE where analytic terms are uncertain.

### A2 — Skill-Blending vs Monolithic Whole-Body Control

| | |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: the reflexive recipe for a new whole-body task is "tune the reward until the monolith does it," but a high-DoF monolith must rediscover feasibility from scratch every time, and [[2506.09366|SkillBlender]] shows task-specific reward engineering "leads to reward hacking and unnatural behaviors."
- **Today's answers**: four systems all confine the action to a feasible set, but at *different points in the pipeline*: [[2506.09366|SkillBlender]] blends frozen primitives *during* control (convex hull); [[2604.00202|DreamControl-v2]] (0.925 vs 0.101 RL) and [[2606.03536|Bionic Whole-Body Control]] (96.0% hardware) bake feasibility into a prior *before* control; [[2606.06139|MotionDisco]] discovers it from a *planner* with no demos; [[2602.13850|Humanoid Hanoi]] composes by *sequencing* skills through a shared adaptive controller (49% complete-task vs baseline adaptation). Meanwhile the wave of strong reuse-vs-monolith systems goes the *opposite* way — [[2506.12779|Experts-to-Generalist]] (66.84% generalist) and [[2606.06493|HANDOFF]] (0.31 m³ workspace vs FALCON's 0.06) *distill* experts into one monolith. So the reuse-vs-monolith field is split: blend-and-keep-frozen vs distill-into-one, and no one has run online-blend against upstream-prior against the distilled monolith on the same skills. (The downstream-RL-reference half — feasible reference → better RL — is now near-consensus across these systems; the live novelty is the pipeline-location head-to-head.)
- **The opening**: SkillBlender's softmax ablation is the legible mechanism: drop the simplex constraint and the feasibility metrics collapse, so the convex-combination rule, not a per-task reward, is what keeps the blend executable.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A softmax-weighted blend of frozen, individually-feasible primitives is a weighted average that never leaves the set the primitives span — so any mix of feasible skills stays feasible, for free, with no per-task feasibility reward. Feasibility is a property of the *combination rule*, recoverable from convexity alone. The direct evidence is [[2506.09366|SkillBlender]]'s softmax-removal ablation: drop the simplex constraint and the feasibility metrics collapse.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That whole-body competence needs task-specific reward engineering on a monolith — the implicit bet of the standard RL recipe and the framing [[2403.10506|HumanoidBench]] documents (flat RL fails, hierarchy helps). [[2506.09366|SkillBlender]]'s reward-hacking diagnosis relocates the binding constraint to *feasibility-preserving composition*: a monolith without the feasible substrate rediscovers feasibility and hacks the reward to do it. [[2604.00202|DreamControl-v2]] and [[2606.03536|Bionic Whole-Body Control]] show the same constraint is satisfiable *upstream* of control, not just during it.
- **The bet**: The load-bearing claim is the *pipeline-location head-to-head* no one has run: online convex-blend ([[2506.09366|SkillBlender]]) vs upstream feasible prior ([[2604.00202|DreamControl-v2]]) vs a distilled-experts monolith ([[2506.12779|Experts-to-Generalist]] 66.84% / [[2606.06493|HANDOFF]] 0.31 m³) on *one* shared 8-task / 3-embodiment skill set with *zero* per-task reward tuning. Prediction: the feasibility-by-construction blend matches SkillBlender's outperformance of vanilla-RL + hierarchical baselines while the monolith reward-hacks, and the upstream prior used as a downstream-RL reference lifts the valid-trajectory rate toward DreamControl-v2's 68% (vs 8%) and RL success toward 0.925 (vs 0.101) *more* than blend-during-control. Falsifiable: if a reward-tuned monolith (RL or distilled) matches the blend without hacking the feasibility metrics, or if pipeline location makes no difference, composition buys nothing.

### A3 — World-Frame End-Effector Tracking with the Base as an Active DoF

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation |
| **Thesis** | Formulate loco-manipulation as world-frame end-effector tracking where the base is an *actively-commanded* transport DoF, not a robot-centric controller that drifts. The reason it must work: a task target lives in the world frame, but a robot-centric policy folds base motion into cumulative drift, so long-horizon precision needs the loop closed in world coordinates and the base used to extend reach. The field assumes body-centric control suffices for task-space precision. The bet is in First-principles below — world-frame closure plus active base transport holds millimetre precision where a matched body-centric policy drifts with distance. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2602.06341\|HiWET]] (method), [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark), [[2603.20147\|AGILE]] (benchmark), [[2603.03279\|ULTRA]] (method), [[2503.05652\|BRS]] (benchmark) |
| **Key targets** | [[2602.06341\|HiWET]] 12.4 mm sim / 12–15 mm real world-frame end-effector RMSE, Kinematic-Manifold-Prior halves hand error (25.2 mm w/o), active base + waist modulation via a tunable α to trade reachability against balance; [[2603.03279\|ULTRA]] 73% dense-reference tracking + 50–90% sparse-goal real G1, RL finetuning +200% (tripling) OOD position-only; [[2503.05652\|BRS]] 88% sub-task via autoregressive base→torso→arm decoding as the drift-mitigation precedent |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: when a humanoid places a hand precisely while walking, the target is in the *world frame*, but most controllers are *robot-centric*, which [[2602.06341|HiWET]] shows "leads to cumulative world-frame drift and high-frequency oscillations" as the base walks. Error accumulates with distance, not capacity.
- **Today's answers**: world-frame closure through base motion is now emerging consensus *on quadrupeds* — [[2407.10353|UMI-on-Legs]] makes the task-frame the interface and is robust to random base transport (80% zero-shot cup rearrangement); [[2409.16048|WB-EE Pose Tracking]] expands the workspace "by moving the body" (2.03 cm flat / 2.64 cm stairs on ALMA); [[2507.08656|Multi-Critic Twist Tracking]] holds a world-frame EE pose while walking; [[2412.03012|Omni WBLM]] tracks 6-DoF EE on a wheeled-quad (0.022 m error). On *bipedal humanoids*, [[2602.06341|HiWET]]'s tunable α makes the base-as-DoF idea operational (full HiWET 12.4 mm, removing its feasibility prior doubles error to 25.2 mm) and [[2602.16705|HERO (Humanoid EE Control)]] reaches 2.44 cm via learned residual FK + odometry; [[2603.03279|ULTRA]] shows the command interface need not fix granularity (73% dense / 50–90% sparse, +200% OOD). The frame distinction is real, but mostly proven on quadrupeds.
- **The opening**: no one has isolated the *frame* as the lever *on a bipedal humanoid with a walking gait*: a world-frame vs body-centric controller, error tracked against locomotion distance (H1), with a *learned continuous α-schedule* (H2) rather than a tuned constant or discrete trigger — exactly the package the quadruped precedents leave open.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A manipulation target lives in the world frame; a robot-centric controller folds the floating base's motion into the end-effector estimate, so error accumulates with locomotion distance. World-frame closure is the only formulation where precision is decoupled from how far the base has walked — and the mobile base is the DoF that brings out-of-static-reach targets into reach. [[2602.06341|HiWET]] measures this directly: the world-frame formulation holds 12.4 mm, and its KMP-removal ablation doubles hand error to 25.2 mm.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That body-centric whole-body control suffices for task-space precision, and that base motion is a disturbance to reject — and the narrower convention the quadruped precedents leave intact: that the reach-vs-balance trade is a *fixed/tuned* parameter ([[2602.06341|HiWET]]'s constant α) or a *discrete* go/no-go reach-extender ([[2409.16048|WB-EE Pose Tracking]], [[2605.21133|Spatial Brain Cerebellum]]), never a *learned continuous schedule over the task*. [[2602.06341|HiWET]] beats body-centric baselines, and the drift it measures is a *frame* problem, not a capacity problem — scaling the body-centric policy cannot fix it. [[2503.05652|BRS]] confirms the same drift from the *factoring* side (autoregressive base→torso→arm vs naive joint, 13×/21× over DP3/RGB-DP), and HiWET's α shows the base is better *scheduled* than suppressed — but as a tuned constant, not learned.
- **The bet**: On a *bipedal humanoid with a walking gait*, a world-frame Commander/Tracker hierarchy with a *learned continuous α-schedule* holds [[2602.06341|HiWET]]'s 12.4 mm sim / 12–15 mm real error over long horizons, where (i) a matched body-centric controller's error grows monotonically with locomotion distance while the world-frame one stays flat (H1, the falsifier no quadruped precedent runs), and (ii) the learned α reaches more out-of-static-range targets than HiWET's tuned-constant α and [[2605.21133|Spatial Brain Cerebellum]]'s discrete trigger (H2). Falsifiable: if a body-centric baseline stays flat with distance, or a tuned-constant α ties the learned schedule, the frame/schedule is not the lever and closure buys nothing.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: most policies are implicitly *manipulation* models, "confined to limited workspaces" because they "lack integrated humanoid locomotion," as [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]] puts it; bolting a velocity controller underneath causes "decision-execution misalignment," where high-level intent and the controller disagree.
- **Today's answers**: "unified beats decoupled" is now the consensus headline. [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]] splits the *action* latent by visual dynamics (separate manip/loco VQ-VAE models, 78.0% beating same-LMO baselines that top out at 56.7%); [[2506.13751|LeVERB]] makes the *command* latent a kinematically-grounded "verb" (58.5%, 7.8× over naive hierarchical); [[2512.20188|Fast-Slow WB VLA]] gives the *unified-action-token* face (an RVQ-VAE whole-body action tokenizer, 90% scoop at 32.3 Hz, ~3× synchronous baselines, tokenizer-ablation 83.3%→0%); [[2603.05410|PhysiFlow]] gives the *semantic-latent* face at whole-body scale (74.9% on 9 tasks, +9.9 pp over LeVERB). The reasoning↔control interface being a *learned latent* is no longer the contribution.
- **The opening**: the still-non-consensus part is that the *visual statistics force the architecture* — that manipulation video (near-static camera) and locomotion video (gait-moving camera) need *separate* latent models, not one shared latent. WholeBodyVLA asserts it; [[2512.20188|Fast-Slow WB VLA]] uses *one unified* whole-body action token and [[2606.09215|MotionWAM]] *one shared* motion latent — the opposite bet — and nobody has run the two-LAM-vs-one-LAM ablation with the visual-dynamics split as the variable (H1).

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Manipulation and locomotion have different visual dynamics (local-contact, near-static camera vs global-terrain, moving camera), so a single shared latent model underfits one — yet the next whole-body action is a *joint* over both, and predicting them through separate stacks re-introduces decision-execution misalignment. The right structure is distinct latent models with a joint prediction interface. [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]] supplies the evidence: separate VQ-VAE latents beat a shared model, +38.7% from action-free video pretraining and +24.0% from the loco-oriented RL policy, with same-LMO baselines (GR00T 42.0%, OpenVLA-OFT 56.7%) trailing its 78.0%.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "unified beats decoupled" (now consensus, [[2512.20188|Fast-Slow WB VLA]] / [[2603.05410|PhysiFlow]] both unify) but the sharper claim that *visual dynamics force two distinct latent models* rather than one — the bet [[2606.09215|MotionWAM]]'s single shared motion latent and [[2512.20188|Fast-Slow WB VLA]]'s single unified action token both deny. [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]]'s misalignment diagnosis and its +24.0% LMO-over-velocity-controller result mark the boundary against decoupling; the open question is whether the *split* by visual statistics, not the unification itself, earns the win.
- **The bet**: The load-bearing test is the *two-LAM-vs-one-LAM* ablation no one external has run: [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]]'s two visual-dynamics-split VQ-VAE latents beat a single shared latent — using [[2606.09215|MotionWAM]]'s *unified* whole-body motion latent as the negative control — at matched capacity, with the separate-over-shared margin scaling with camera motion (largest on loco-heavy splits, near-zero on manip-heavy). Prediction: ≥78.0% unified-split vs ≤56.7% same-LMO decoupled on Bag/Box/Cart, gain traced to +38.7% action-free pretraining and +24.0% loco-RL, reaching [[2506.13751|LeVERB]]'s 58.5% (7.8× over naive hierarchical). Falsifiable: if a single shared latent ([[2606.09215|MotionWAM]]-style) matches the split, or the margin is flat across camera-motion bins, the visual-dynamics-split is not the lever even though unification is.

## Cluster B — Mobile Manipulation (Nav↔Manip Coupling)

*Jointly controlling a moving base and an arm, where the base velocity is itself a manipulation degree of freedom — repositioning extends the reachable workspace mid-task, not before it. The cluster reads the moving base as one coupling that surfaces in three places: the order base and arm actions are factored, what the moving camera keeps in view, and what scene state persists once the base turns away.*

### B1 — Joint Base-Arm Action vs Sequential Decomposition

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: the reflexive pipeline drives to a pose and *then* runs a fixed-base manipulation policy, so it freezes the base before the arm acts — but a mobile manipulator grasps *while* repositioning, because the base velocity changes the reachable workspace mid-grasp; committing the base pose up front throws that lever away.
- **Today's answers**: the base-first conditioning question is now *settled* — three systems independently confirm the structure. [[2503.05652|BRS]]'s WB-VIMA draws base→torso→arm *autoregressively* (88% sub-task / 58% entire-task, 13×/21× over DP3/RGB-DP, "essential for mitigating error propagation"); [[2507.01961|AC-DiT]] runs the directional ablation directly — base→arm (49.5) beats arm→base (47.3) — establishing the conditioning *direction* as load-bearing; and [[2602.23024|InCoM]] runs the head-to-head this card once called open, benchmarking *and beating* both WB-VIMA and AC-DiT on shared ManiSkill-HAB (83.8% vs 55.6% AC-DiT, DCFM-removal ↓7.9) while arguing unidirectional base→arm is *insufficient* and replacing it with explicit bidirectional coordination. [[2401.02117|Mobile ALOHA]]'s flat 16-D concatenation is the baseline all three beat. The "go base-first" claim — and even the naive "go bidirectional" refinement — is now taken.
- **The opening**: what *no one* has done is stratify the coordination-structure margin by *in-task base motion* — whether the unidirectional/bidirectional/flat gap concentrates on reach-extension tasks where the base must move mid-grasp (H2/H3), and when bidirectional ([[2602.23024|InCoM]]) actually beats unidirectional ([[2507.01961|AC-DiT]]). BRS's own ablation shows the factoring *order* carries the win; the surviving question is *which* structure, *when*.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: The base velocity is a manipulation degree of freedom — it moves the reachable workspace continuously during the task. By the joint's structure the right arm action is *conditional* on the base action chosen for that instant: $p(a_{\text{base}},a_{\text{torso}},a_{\text{arm}}\mid o)=p(a_{\text{base}}\mid o)\,p(a_{\text{torso}}\mid a_{\text{base}},o)\,p(a_{\text{arm}}\mid a_{\text{base}},a_{\text{torso}},o)$, so each downstream link absorbs the displacement its upstream link committed. This holds regardless of the data. [[2503.05652|BRS]] demonstrates it is load-bearing: swapping its autoregressive decoding for a naive joint head collapses the 13×/21× margin over DP3/RGB-DP.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "factor base-first vs flat concatenation" (settled — [[2507.01961|AC-DiT]]'s directional ablation and [[2602.23024|InCoM]]'s head-to-head both ran it) but the *unaddressed* sub-question: *which* coordination structure (unidirectional base→arm / bidirectional / flat) wins, *and when*, as a function of in-task base motion. [[2305.04866|Causal WBMM]] established the canonical version of the assumption years earlier — that whole-body mobile-manip action dependencies are causal, not flat (93.6% vs 74.3% vanilla PPO) — so the cross-conditioning insight is old; the live frontier is its conditional structure.
- **The bet**: The coordination-structure margin (unidirectional [[2507.01961|AC-DiT]] / bidirectional [[2602.23024|InCoM]] / flat [[2401.02117|Mobile ALOHA]]) is *concentrated on reach-extension tasks where the base moves mid-grasp* and near-zero on fixed-base reaches, and bidirectional beats unidirectional *only* when the arm action feeds back to constrain the base — isolating *when* each structure matters rather than asserting one dominates. Prediction: the structure gap tracks in-task base-travel demand, holding [[2503.05652|BRS]]'s 88%/58% and 13×/21× on reach-extension tasks and matching [[2602.23024|InCoM]]'s 83.8% ManiSkill-HAB, while collapsing to ≤ flat-concat on fixed-base tasks. Falsifiable: if the structure margin is flat across in-task base motion — or bidirectional never beats unidirectional — the conditioning structure is not the conditional lever and any single factoring suffices.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: most mobile-manipulation stacks read a fixed forward camera, but a moving base continuously changes what is visible, so the target, the contact, and the next subgoal can leave the frame exactly when they matter — and an unobserved state cannot be acted on, no matter how good the policy.
- **Today's answers**: "active gaze beats fixed camera" is now consensus, contested on two fronts. *Planners* couple gaze to base motion at runtime: [[2605.02487|Visibility-Aware Mobile Grasping]] jointly optimizes whole-body trajectory *and* gaze on a real moving-base Fetch (68.8% / 58.0%, +18.0% over decoupled nav-then-manip, collision 6.3% vs 17.5%); [[2310.00433|Active-Perceptive Motion Gen]] jointly plans base + gaze (92.6% sim, 80% real TIAGo++). *Learned* active-gaze policies abound but mostly fixed-base — [[2602.04600|Act-Sense-Act]] learns active perception on a wheeled humanoid (83.3% SR vs OpenVLA-OFT 12.7%), while [[2510.01607|ActiveUMI]] (70%, +44% over fixed head), [[2506.15666|Vision-in-Action]] (+45%), [[2602.22461|EgoAVFlow]] (up to 4× over human-viewpoint imitation), [[2511.00153|EgoMI]] and [[2410.18964|DISaM]] all learn where-to-look but on a static base. [[2603.03243|HoMMI]]'s "3D look-at point" head and [[2411.04999|DynaMem]]'s exploration-driven re-observation (70% dynamic vs 30% static) close the loop on a moving base — but none learns gaze *conditioned on the base trajectory B1 chooses*.
- **The opening**: the surviving lever is the *learned look-at coupled to the chosen base trajectory* plus the attribution none of these run — gaze-vs-exploration on in-view-vs-out-of-view relocations (H3) and the during-vs-between manipulation-stability decomposition (H5). [[2306.11565|HomeRobot]]'s 5–15%→0.4–0.6% collapse (GT → real DETIC) is the headroom an active-gaze policy reaches that a passive camera cannot.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A mobile base changes the camera's viewpoint continuously, so what is observable is a function of the base + head action — a controllable quantity, not a given. To keep the target and contact in view the policy must *act* (gaze, head, base orientation), and an unobserved state is unrecoverable from the current frame by any policy capacity. [[2603.03243|HoMMI]]'s "3D look-at point" makes this operational — and it represents gaze as a *relaxed* head action precisely because directly copying human 6-DoF head poses yields kinematically infeasible robot motion, so observability must be an explicitly-controlled DoF.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "active gaze beats passive" (now consensus, instantiated fixed-base by [[2510.01607|ActiveUMI]] / [[2506.15666|Vision-in-Action]] / [[2602.22461|EgoAVFlow]] and as a moving-base planner by [[2605.02487|Visibility-Aware Mobile Grasping]]) but the un-run conjunction: a *learned* look-at policy *conditioned on the base trajectory the action policy chooses*, with the perception-coupling slice isolated from the action-coupling slice. The planners ([[2605.02487|Visibility-Aware Mobile Grasping]], [[2310.00433|Active-Perceptive Motion Gen]]) couple gaze to base but as receding-horizon search with a see-for-safety objective, not a learned demo policy, and never decompose gaze-vs-exploration or during-vs-between stability.
- **The bet**: A *learned* active-gaze policy that predicts a look-at point *conditioned on B1's chosen base trajectory* recovers the perception slice of [[2306.11565|HomeRobot]]'s 5–15%→0.4–0.6% collapse with the gain largest exactly when the base is moving (near-zero stationary), matches [[2605.02487|Visibility-Aware Mobile Grasping]]'s +18.0%-over-decoupled while beating its planner on demo tasks, and recovers dynamic-object relocations to [[2411.04999|DynaMem]]'s 70% (vs 30% static, locate-failure 53.3%→6.7%) *for in-view relocations where gaze beats re-driving* (H3). Falsifiable: if a fixed forward camera matches learned gaze on moving-base tasks, or independently-predicted gaze matches base-coupled gaze, observability is not coupled to the base action and the planner suffices.

Falsifiable: if a static-scene-memory policy holds on relocated objects, or a memoryless long-window policy matches in-policy memory on multi-room tasks, the dynamic/in-policy/multi-room combination is not the lever.

## Cluster C — Force-Adaptive Coordination Under Load

*Whole-body control under external wrench, payload, and contact force — where a force at the hand propagates through the kinematic chain to the support polygon, the legs compensate for what the arms feel, and the standard task reward silently omits the force. The two directions split the load problem along its two irreducible halves: making the policy perform under a force it must model and anticipate (C1), and certifying that it cannot fall or collide when the load drifts out of distribution (C2).*

### C1 — Force-Adaptive Whole-Body Control Under Unknown Wrench

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | C — Force-Adaptive Coordination Under Load |
| **Thesis** | Build a whole-body policy that *anticipates* the unknown external hand wrench and the base/leg reaction it induces, instead of a stiff tracker that treats force as a disturbance to reject. The reason it must work: an external wrench at the hand propagates through the kinematic chain to every joint, including the support legs, so force adaptation is a whole-body equilibrium problem, not an arm problem. The field assumes whole-body controllers can be force-agnostic. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2505.06776\|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] (method), [[2606.03297\|SplitAdapter]] (method), [[2502.10894\|UAN]] (method), [[2512.01061\|Sim-to-Real Door]] (method), [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark) |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a humanoid opening a heavy door or carrying a variable payload meets "significant, dynamic, and multi-directional 3D end-effector forces," but the standard stack either solves the upper body with a kinematic IK that compensates force late, or trains one monolith that never models force at all — the wrench is left as an unmodelled disturbance.
- **Today's answers**: the humanoid force-adaptive cluster is *reactive* — [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] gets implicit compensation from a torque-limit-aware 3D force curriculum (0.37 under large force, ~2× over a 0.60 monolith, 100 N cart); [[2511.20275|HAFO]] follows upper-body angles under 10–50 N hand disturbances; [[2510.26280|Thor]] earns 167.7 N pull via a force-augmented reward; [[2603.14308|Load-Aware Loco-Manipulation]] estimates load from history for a 5 kg carry; [[2606.03297|SplitAdapter]] factorizes the load context from history (26/27 vs 16/27 under 6 kg). All *estimate-then-compensate from the past*. The one paper that *anticipates* — [[2201.03871|ALMA (Wrench-Prediction)]] — predicts the wrench and feeds it forward to the legs 0–0.8 s ahead (208% over reactive, leans against a 150 N push), but only for the *self-induced known* wrench from the robot's own arm plan, on a *quadruped*, with a *model-based* predictor.
- **The opening**: ALMA proves anticipatory feedforward beats reactive — but explicitly *not* for unknown external environmental forces (cart/door/payload), and not on a humanoid support polygon. The unexplored delta is estimating an *unknown external* wrench first, then *learning-forward-modeling* its whole-body reaction on a *humanoid* — exactly where FALCON's "kinematically limited force compensation" diagnosis and ALMA's self-induced-only scope leave the gap.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A force at the hand — an external wrench $F_{\text{ext}}$ — does not stay at the hand. It propagates through the kinematic chain via $J_{\text{ext}}^{\top}$ to every joint, including the support legs that keep the centre of mass over the feet. So force adaptation is a whole-body balance problem: the legs must compensate for what the arms feel, while every joint torque stays within limits and the centre of mass stays over the support polygon. It is not a local arm-stiffness problem, regardless of the training data. [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] demonstrates the structure is load-bearing: its torque-limit-aware force curriculum — applying only *physically feasible* forces that respect joint limits via inverse dynamics — is what earns the 0.60→0.37 tracking gain and the stable exploration a naive (limit-blind) force schedule cannot.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "anticipation beats reactive for legged force" (taken — [[2201.03871|ALMA (Wrench-Prediction)]] proved it, 208% over reactive) but the narrower scope ALMA explicitly excludes: that anticipation only works for the *self-induced known* wrench, with a *model-based* predictor, on a quadruped. The reactive humanoid trackers ([[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]], [[2511.20275|HAFO]], [[2510.26280|Thor]], [[2603.14308|Load-Aware Loco-Manipulation]]) all leave the *unknown external* wrench un-anticipated, and [[2606.03297|SplitAdapter]] sharpens it from the adaptation side — a unified latent conflating load with dynamics mismatch loses robustness under heavy load, so the load needs a dedicated representation that history estimation provides only *after* the load lands.
- **The bet**: A humanoid policy that estimates an *unknown external* wrench then *learns to forward-model* its whole-body reaction (a learned residual through $J_{\text{ext}}^{\top}$ feeding pre-emptive leg compensation) holds balance under a *step-applied* load with a measurable advantage in the first ~100 ms after onset where [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]]'s reactive compensation lags — the sudden-onset CoM-excursion advantage at matched force is the headline metric, not FALCON's borrowed 0.37 — while sustaining [[2502.10894|UAN]]-class loads (113 N cart, 8 kg lift), and extends [[2201.03871|ALMA (Wrench-Prediction)]]'s anticipatory mechanism from self-induced-known to unknown-external on a tight support polygon. Falsifiable: if anticipation matches reactive at the same step load on a humanoid, or the wrench is too noisy to forward-model, force is adequately handled reactively.

### C2 — Safety-Bounded Whole-Body Control Under Load

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | C — Force-Adaptive Coordination Under Load |
| **Thesis** | Wrap whole-body loco-manipulation in a *certified* safety layer — a barrier or learned-manifold projection that provably keeps balance and collision-freeness, or a predictive monitor that fires before a fall — instead of hoping a learned policy stays safe when load and geometry drift out of distribution. The reason it must work: a learned policy optimizes an expectation and carries no per-step guarantee under OOD wrench/geometry, while balance and collision are hard physical boundaries a violation cannot undo. The field assumes domain randomization and reward shaping yield safe-enough behavior. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2604.07457\|CMP]] (method), [[2605.25546\|ISSf-CBF WBC]] (method), [[2602.01515\|RAPT]] (method), [[2310.12567\|Safety-Gymnasium]] (benchmark), [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark) |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a policy that works in-distribution gives no guarantee when geometry, payload, or sensor noise drifts OOD — and under load the failure modes are *physical*: a fall, a self-collision, a workspace-boundary violation, each unrecoverable. The standard answer is domain randomization plus reward shaping, which buys robustness *in expectation* but certifies nothing.
- **Today's answers**: the "certified barrier over a black-box learned policy on a real humanoid" mechanism is no longer novel in itself — [[2505.11494|SHIELD-Humanoid]] already ships a probabilistic certified barrier over a learned RL policy on a real Unitree G1, and [[2510.14959|CBF-RL]] internalizes the CBF into the policy (99.0% vs 38.7% filter-only) — but *both for locomotion / obstacle-avoidance, with no payload or external-wrench term*. The on-axis under-load systems split into certified and reactive: [[2604.07457|CMP]] lifts unshielded 4.7%→46.9% sim / 86.7% extreme-OOD real via learned manifold projection (2.99 ms); [[2605.25546|ISSf-CBF WBC]] stays collision-free under 20% mass mismatch (vs ~50% standard CBF) on a real TOCABI; [[2603.02443|Safe WBLM]] adds a wrench-aware certified governor but *on a quadruped* with no humanoid survival numbers; [[2602.01515|RAPT]] flags OOD (AUROC 0.92) and fires a predefined stop. The robustness-by-training / detect-then-stop baselines ([[2503.00923|HWC]], [[2603.22703|Safe-Stoppability Monitor]], [[2511.18509|SafeFall]]) are the falsifier targets. No one compares certified vs reward-shaping *under load*.
- **The opening**: CMP's reduction makes a certified check O(1) (2.99 ms) — but the surviving novelty is not the barrier-over-learned-policy mechanism (taken by SHIELD); it is the *payload/wrench-aware barrier* (H2) and the *certified-vs-reward head-to-head under load* (H1), neither of which any humanoid safety paper runs.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Balance — the centre of mass inside the support polygon — and collision-freeness are *hard constraints* with no soft trade-off: a violation is a fall or a crash, not a small loss. A learned policy optimizes an expectation over training conditions and carries no per-step guarantee once load or geometry drifts OOD; a barrier or projection filter enforces the constraint set as a hard boundary, projecting any unsafe command to the closest feasible one. [[2604.07457|CMP]] measures the necessity directly: unshielded whole-body tracking survives only 4.7% of OOD-geometry tasks, and its key insight — separating "out-of-*training*-distribution" from "out-of-*safe*-distribution" so a never-mastered but in-distribution command is also caught — is what a reward penalty cannot express.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "a certified barrier over a learned policy is novel" ([[2505.11494|SHIELD-Humanoid]] / [[2510.14959|CBF-RL]] already do it on a real humanoid) but the under-load gap they leave: that a *kinematics-only* barrier (no payload/wrench term) and *robustness-by-training* ([[2503.00923|HWC]]) suffice when the load drifts OOD. The DR + reward-shaping policies CMP and ISSf-CBF beat bet on this; [[2604.07457|CMP]]'s 4.7% OOD-geometry survival and [[2605.25546|ISSf-CBF WBC]]'s ~50% baseline collision under mass mismatch mark the boundary — a soft reward cannot *guarantee* a hard constraint under shift, and a wrench-blind barrier has no support-polygon term once the payload pulls the CoM. [[2602.01515|RAPT]] adds the silent-failure evidence: sim-robust policies "execute confidently in OOD states," exactly what reward shaping leaves uncaught.
- **The bet**: A *wrench-aware* certified layer — a barrier whose safe set carries the external-payload equilibrium term — holds collision-free single-leg balance under an unknown payload where [[2505.11494|SHIELD-Humanoid]]'s and [[2605.25546|ISSf-CBF WBC]]'s kinematics-only barriers (tuned without a force term) violate the support polygon, and in a *certified-vs-reward head-to-head under load* lifts OOD whole-body survival to [[2604.07457|CMP]]'s 86.7% extreme / 93.3% moderate real (at 2.99 ms) where a reward-penalty-only policy of equal capacity ([[2503.00923|HWC]]-class robustness-by-training) cannot. Falsifiable: if a kinematics-only barrier holds balance under load, or reward shaping matches the certified layer on OOD survival and collision under mass mismatch, the wrench term and the guarantee buy nothing over robustness-by-training.

## Cluster D — Whole-Body Teleoperation & Human-Motion Retargeting

*The whole-body data wall — demonstrations of coupled loco-manipulation are scarce, embodiment-mismatched, and per-platform. The four directions remove four different bricks from that wall, each a distinct verb on a distinct object: retarget the human motion that exists so its contact survives the morphology gap (D1), capture new coupled demos with a human in the per-demo loop (D2), transfer a trained policy onto a new body without retraining (D3), and synthesize coupled demos with the human removed from the per-demo loop entirely (D4). The sharpest line is D2 vs D4 — whether a person performs each demonstration (capture) or a generator authors it (synthesis).*

### D1 — Interaction-Preserving Whole-Body Retargeting

| | |
|---|---|

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: loco-manipulation references come from human motion, but a human's loco-manipulation is defined by its *interactions* — hand on object, foot on floor, body against scene — and a retargeter that minimizes joint-space pose error can be joint-accurate yet contact-broken: the hand floats off the object, the foot skates, the reference penetrates the ground, and the policy inherits the violation.
- **Today's answers**: interaction-preservation is now *consensus*. [[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]] preserves the interaction via an interaction-mesh Laplacian objective (penetration 0.00–0.01, zero foot-skating, 82.20–94.73% downstream RL); [[2606.03476|Human2Humanoid]] adds a *morphology-invariant end-effector consistency loss* (88.5% SR, 0.05 cm penetration); [[2603.22201|NMR]] adds feasibility-by-distribution (−54% self-collisions). And [[2602.15733|MeshMimic]] *extends* the OmniRetarget recipe past flat ground to large-scale terrain (−25.5% world MPJPE, −18.7% Chamfer vs VideoMimic), with [[2604.18557|SynAgent]] (multi-agent contact, 45% vs 7.26% InterMimic), [[2601.09518|Human-Humanoid Interaction]] (PAIR contact-F1 0.841) and [[2602.06827|DynaRetarget]] (dynamically-feasible retarget, 97.09% downstream vs 79.41% raw) as PARTIAL follow-ons on the same mechanism. The interaction-preservation half is owned; what no one isolates is *which* contact relationship is load-bearing, or whether contact-fidelity *predicts* trainability monotonically rather than correlating.
- **The opening**: OmniRetarget's recipe ties reference quality to trainability — but the surviving, unclaimed scientific core is the *quantitative* one: the cross-method penetration/foot-skating → downstream-SR regression as a *designed-against predictor* (slope + R²) and the controlled isolation of *which* contact relationship matters at *fixed* joint accuracy, neither of which OmniRetarget, MeshMimic, or the follow-ons measure.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: The information in a loco-manipulation reference is the contact graph — which surfaces touch (hand–object, foot–floor, body–scene) and with what relative geometry — not the absolute joint angles, which are a morphology-specific realization. A pose-error-minimizing retarget optimizes the wrong quantity: it can match joints while breaking the contact that defines the task. [[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]] demonstrates the structure is load-bearing — its interaction-mesh objective preserves contact where Laplacian-blind baselines foot-skate and penetrate, and that contact fidelity is what enables the 82.20–94.73% downstream RL from a minimal reward set.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "retargeting is pose matching" (interaction-preservation is now consensus — [[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]], [[2606.03476|Human2Humanoid]], [[2602.15733|MeshMimic]] all preserve it) but the next-order claim none of them tests: that contact-fidelity merely *correlates* with trainability rather than *predicting* it monotonically, and that *all* contact relationships are equally load-bearing. A joint-accurate-but-contact-broken reference trains worse than a joint-loose-but-contact-true one — but *which* contact term, and *how monotone* the relationship, is unmeasured.
- **The bet**: Across retargeting methods ([[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]], [[2606.03476|Human2Humanoid]], [[2603.22201|NMR]], [[2602.06827|DynaRetarget]], [[2603.03279|ULTRA]]), the penetration/foot-skating → downstream-RL-SR regression is *monotone with a measurable slope and R²* on a shared task suite — so contact-fidelity is a *designed-against quantitative predictor*, not a correlate — and the per-contact-term ablation (object-only vs object+scene at *fixed* joint accuracy) shows a measurable SR delta isolating the load-bearing relationship. Falsifiable: if SR is flat or non-monotone in contact violation, or a pose-matching retarget of equal joint accuracy reaches the same downstream SR, the contact graph is not the load-bearing quantity and the metric is decorative.

> - **The human–humanoid embodiment gap can break alignment** — different proportions and DoF limit transfer. → [[2602.10106|EgoHumanoid]]'s alignment pipeline + co-training bridges it; H2 reports the in-domain vs generalization split by alignment quality (couples to D1's morphology-range limit).

> - **Capture is bounded by human hours** — every demo costs a person, so capture cannot reach synthesis-scale corpora. → This is the clean line to D4: report demos-per-human-hour for capture vs demos-per-seed for synthesis, so the boundary (who authors the demo) is a measured quantity, not an assertion.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: whole-body-tracking policies are trained per-platform — a new humanoid (LimX Oli, LimX Luna, G1, H1) restarts training from scratch, paying the full large-scale data + compute bill each time, even though the *coordination* it learns is largely the same.
- **Today's answers**: the space has densely filled, splitting into *cheap transfer* and *generalist scale*. Cheap transfer: [[2605.23733|Any2Any]] PEFTs the dynamics-sensitive modules across 4 humanoids at ~1% compute; [[2606.01851|PHASOR]] makes motion's *periodicity* the invariant (90.3% R@1, only method beating raw-kinematics teleop at 64.75 mm); [[2604.19734|UniT]] / [[2511.07820|SONIC]] use shared token spaces; [[2601.15419|Unified Latent Cross-Embodiment]] integrates a new robot in ~15 min (sub-cm reach); [[2602.13764|MOTIF]] does 1-shot/5-shot transfer (67.5% real). But [[2512.00971|H-Zero]] executes the transfer-not-from-scratch thesis (81% on unseen robots, ~30 min few-shot) *only on locomotion*. Generalist scale (the opposite of cheap transfer): [[2602.05791|XHugWBC]] (100% survival on 19 sim + 7 real, ~85% of specialists, one policy) and [[2602.02960|Embodiment-Aware Distillation]] (EAGLE, zero-shot across 5 humanoids) train *one* generalist; [[2505.05753|Embodiment Scaling Laws]] (817 embodiments) and [[2509.02815|Multi-Embodiment Loco]] (50 robots) show scale buys generalization. Two camps, never run head-to-head on *whole-body loco-manipulation*.
- **The opening**: the two front-line gaps the literature leaves are (i) *whole-body loco-MANIPULATION* — H-Zero and the scaling-laws line stop at locomotion, exactly where D3's novelty lives — and (ii) the *transfer-vs-generalist-scale head-to-head* (cheap localized PEFT vs a one-time-trained XHugWBC/EAGLE generalist), now the thickest tension in the field. Any2Any's ablation localizes the lever (PEFT into the dynamics-sensitive pathway), so the structure is a specific pathway, not a coincidence of scale.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Whole-body coordination decomposes into a morphology-invariant structure (a phase-clocked balance schedule + end-effector goals any humanoid shares) and a body-specific joint realization. The structure is the transferable invariant; the joint angles are the per-body projection. A representation anchored to the structure (phase, shared tokens, end-effector) transfers where a joint-space policy cannot. [[2606.01851|PHASOR]] demonstrates it directly — the phase manifold is "intrinsic to the behavior rather than to the body that produces it," and anchoring multiple robots to a shared human-pretrained manifold yields cross-embodiment retrieval a joint-space encoder cannot.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "per-platform retraining is avoidable" (consensus on *locomotion* — [[2512.00971|H-Zero]], [[2602.05791|XHugWBC]], [[2505.05753|Embodiment Scaling Laws]] all show it) but two sharper claims: that the result holds for *whole-body loco-MANIPULATION* (where H-Zero and the scaling line stop at locomotion), and that *cheap localized transfer* beats *one-time generalist scale* below a crossover — the live tension between [[2605.23733|Any2Any]]-style PEFT and [[2602.05791|XHugWBC]]/[[2602.02960|Embodiment-Aware Distillation]] generalists that no one has resolved on manipulation-coupled tasks.
- **The bet**: On *whole-body loco-MANIPULATION* (not locomotion), a cross-embodiment adapter transfers a pretrained policy at [[2605.23733|Any2Any]]'s ~1% of from-scratch compute/data across 4 humanoids via a [[2606.01851|PHASOR]]-class phase-anchored representation (90.3% R@1, 1.62 mm next-frame), and — the promoted front-line falsifier — *beats a one-time-trained generalist* ([[2602.05791|XHugWBC]] ~85% of specialists / [[2602.02960|Embodiment-Aware Distillation]]) on cost below a measurable crossover, with the transferable structure *localized* to the dynamics-sensitive pathway. Falsifiable: if joint-space transfer at equal budget matches the phase-anchored representation, or a one-time generalist beats cheap transfer at equal cost on loco-manipulation, neither the structure nor the cheap-transfer regime is the lever.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | D — Whole-Body Teleoperation & Human-Motion Retargeting |
| **Thesis** | *Synthesize* coupled loco-manipulation demonstrations with no human in the per-demo loop — amplifying a small seed through whole-body planning, or generating demos from 3D assets and video priors — instead of hand-collecting each demo by teleoperation. **Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: the whole-body data wall has two exits, and D2 takes the first — *capture* coupled demos faster — but every captured demo still costs a human performing the motion, so capture cannot reach the corpus sizes VLA training wants. The second exit removes the human from the per-demo loop entirely: *synthesize* the coupled trajectory.
- **Today's answers**: the seed-amplification route is now *multiply-instantiated*, not a single-anchor frontier. [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] adapts one teleoperated seed via whole-body planning (1,000 demos, 0.89 PSR vs 0.33 manipulation-only DexMimicGen+); [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]] runs the *same* seed → object-centric whole-body replay through a balance-enforcing controller → thousands of demos → sim-to-real G1 (5/5 LiftBox, published Oct 2025, *earlier* than HumanoidMimicGen); [[2510.18316|MoMaGen]] (ICLR'26) does it for mobile manip, training a policy from *one* source demo (60% real Pick-Cup after FT). From-scratch: [[2606.05160|GRAIL]] composes 3D assets + video priors into 4D HOI with no teleoperation (81.4% SR, synthetic-only real G1 90%/84%/80%). Feasibility-enforced generation: [[2509.20322|VisualMimic]] and [[2504.16843|Latent Diffusion LocoMan]] generate physically-plausible coupled trajectories; [[2410.18907|SkillMimicGen]] is the mimicgen replay precedent (65% vs 14% valid-demo generation). The "whole-body feasibility is the lever" claim is answered yes (DemoHLM + HumanoidMimicGen both confirm); the per-demo human is removable.
- **The opening**: with the seed route confirmed across three independent groups, the surviving lever is *not* "is whole-body feasibility the lever" but the un-measured ones: the *from-scratch-vs-seed diversity ceiling* (does a fixed seed cap synthesized variety — [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]] / HumanoidMimicGen both need a seed, GRAIL needs none) and the *feasibility→trainability monotone curve* (does lower synthesized penetration train to higher SR), which no prior paper plots.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A loco-manipulation demonstration is just a physically-feasible coupled trajectory — the same body dynamics ($M(q)$) and contact constraints hold whether a human or a planner authored it. Feasibility (dynamic stability, contact consistency, no penetration) is a *property of the trajectory*, checkable and enforceable, not a property of who made it. So a generator that enforces whole-body feasibility produces training-valid demos — and arbitrarily more than a human can teleoperate. [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] demonstrates the structure is load-bearing: the manipulation-only generator's 0.33 (vs 0.89) shows feasibility must be enforced *as whole-body dynamics*, not just contact, to make valid coupled demos.
- **Assumption being challenged**: Not "the human collection rate caps coupled-demo quantity" (the seed route refutes it — [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] 1,000-from-one-seed, [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]] thousands-from-one-seed, [[2606.05160|GRAIL]] 20,000-from-assets) but the next-order question the crowd left un-measured: whether a *fixed seed* caps synthesized diversity (the from-scratch-vs-seed ceiling) and whether *enforced contact-fidelity predicts* synthetic-demo trainability monotonically. The whole-body-feasibility-is-the-lever assumption is already settled yes; the diversity ceiling and the feasibility→trainability curve are not.
- **The bet**: (i) From-scratch generation ([[2606.05160|GRAIL]] 81.4% SR, synthetic-only 90% real) breaks the *diversity ceiling* a single-seed route ([[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] 0.89 PSR, [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]]) hits — sweeping seed/asset breadth, generalization rises then plateaus for the seed route while from-scratch keeps climbing; and (ii) enforced synthesized contact-fidelity → downstream-SR is *monotone* ([[2606.05160|GRAIL]]'s 0.90% penetration / 88.9% tracking), so feasibility-enforcement during generation is a quantitative trainability predictor, with co-training lifting real SR 0.51→0.71 (+20%). Falsifiable: if the seed route's diversity does not plateau below from-scratch, or downstream SR is flat in synthesized feasibility, neither the ceiling nor the feasibility-curve is the lever.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (enforced-feasibility whole-body synthesis supplies coupled demos at scale, no per-demo human), with the experiment and the Related-table row it lands on.
1. **H1 — From-scratch generation breaks the seed's diversity ceiling that DemoHLM and HumanoidMimicGen share.**
   - *Prediction*: sweeping seed/asset breadth, the seed-amplification route ([[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] / [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]]) generalization rises then *plateaus* at a measurable diversity ceiling tied to the single seed, while from-scratch generation ([[2606.05160|GRAIL]], asset-driven) keeps climbing past it on held-out object/scene variation — the comparison neither seed paper runs. (The whole-body-vs-manipulation-only lever, 0.33→0.89, is already settled by HumanoidMimicGen + DemoHLM, so it is the control, not the test.)
   - *Test*: match generation budget; sweep seed count (seed route) vs asset-pool breadth (from-scratch); plot generalization SR vs diversity for both on [[2505.12705|DreamGen]]'s DreamGen Bench GR1-humanoid generalization splits (22 novel behaviors 43.2% vs 11.2%, 10 unseen-env behaviors 28.5% vs 0%) as the held-out behavior/environment yardstick, locate the seed-route plateau.
   - *Row*: HumanoidMimicGen / DemoHLM (seed-amplification) vs GRAIL / DreamGen (from-scratch generation + generalization splits).
   - *Falsifier*: the seed route's diversity does not plateau below from-scratch → the seed is not the diversity bottleneck and from-scratch generation buys no extra breadth.
2. **H2 — Synthetic-only suffices for some tasks; others need a real-data anchor.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2606.05160|GRAIL]]'s synthetic-only transfer (90% stair-climb) holds for dynamics-dominated tasks, while contact-precision tasks need [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]]-style real+synthetic co-training (0.51→0.71) — the synthetic-only/co-trained boundary is set by contact-precision demand.
   - *Test*: synthetic-only vs co-trained on dynamics-heavy vs contact-precision tasks; report the SR gap per task property.
   - *Row*: GRAIL (synthetic-only) vs HumanoidMimicGen (co-trained) / RoboMIND 2.0 (real anchor).
   - *Falsifier*: synthetic-only matches co-trained on contact-precision tasks → no real anchor is ever needed.
3. **H3 — Synthesized diversity has a feasibility ceiling per seed, shared across seed-amplification systems.**
   - *Prediction*: sweeping [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]]'s randomization breadth (the ablation that drops 0.89→0.49 without motion noise, 0.51 without init randomization) and [[2510.11258|DemoHLM]]'s object-pose randomization, generalization rises then plateaus, and pushing diversity past a measurable point breaks feasibility — a fixed seed supports only bounded synthesized variety regardless of the seed-amplification system.
   - *Test*: sweep motion-noise + init/object-pose randomization magnitude on both seed systems; report generalization SR + feasibility-violation rate vs breadth.
   - *Row*: HumanoidMimicGen / DemoHLM (seed-amplification).
   - *Falsifier*: diversity scales without a feasibility ceiling → the seed is not the diversity bottleneck and synthesis is unbounded.
4. **H4 — Enforced contact-fidelity predicts synthetic-demo trainability.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2606.05160|GRAIL]]'s contact/depth-aligned optimization (0.90% penetration, 88.9% sim tracking) traces a monotone synthesized-feasibility → downstream-SR curve — synthetic demos with lower penetration train to higher SR, so enforced contact-fidelity is what makes them trainable (couples to D1's interaction-preservation and A2's feasibility-first imitation).
   - *Test*: vary generation-time contact-enforcement strength; regress downstream SR on synthesized penetration/foot-skating, calibrating against [[2505.12705|DreamGen]]'s DreamGen Bench (Instruction-Following + Physics-Alignment scoring with >90% Pearson correlation to downstream SR) as the standardized generation-feasibility→trainability diagnostic (it scores generated-video plausibility, narrowing not closing the whole-body-trajectory gap).
   - *Row*: GRAIL (asset+video-prior generation) / DreamGen (generation-quality benchmark).
   - *Falsifier*: downstream SR is flat in synthesized feasibility → contact-enforcement during generation is decorative.
5. **H5 — A robust tracker beats clean generation for noisy generated video.**
   - *Prediction*: for *generated* (not captured) human video, [[2512.05094|GenMimic]]'s robustness-from-the-tracker (symmetry regularization + keypoint-weighted rewards) recovers more SR than trying to clean the generated video upstream — the noise is better absorbed by the policy than removed from the data (86.77% vs GMT 4.29% / TWIST 2.69%).
   - *Test*: robust-tracker-on-noisy-video vs cleaned-video-standard-tracker on GenMimicBench; report SR per generation-noise level.
   - *Row*: GenMimic (generated-video→retarget) vs ExoActor (generated-video, no explicit robustness).
   - *Falsifier*: cleaning the video upstream matches the robust tracker → robustness should live in the data pipeline, not the policy.
6. **H6 — A planner-discovered demo is recyclable into a reusable library, not just per-task.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2606.06139|MotionDisco]]'s demonstration-free contact-plan search, when distilled into a reusable feasible-demo library, transfers to *unseen* long-horizon coupled tasks without re-running the per-task search — closing its named "per-task search, no reusable library" gap.
   - *Test*: distill MotionDisco plans into a demo library; evaluate trained-policy SR on held-out tasks vs re-searching per task.
   - *Row*: MotionDisco (planner-search, demo-free).
   - *Falsifier*: distilled plans do not transfer → planner synthesis is irreducibly per-task and cannot build a corpus.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **A feasibility-enforcing generator is itself hard to build** — synthesis replaces collection cost with generator-engineering cost. → [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]] reuses a learned locomotion controller + classical IK/planning; H1/H3 report demos-per-seed and seed-collection cost, so the amortization (one generator, many demos) is the measured win.
> - **Synthetic demos can carry a sim-to-real gap the policy inherits** — generated feasibility need not equal real feasibility. → [[2606.05160|GRAIL]]'s contact/depth alignment + [[2601.02078|Genie Sim 3.0]]'s R²=0.94 bound it; H2/H4 report synthetic-only vs co-trained real SR, treating the gap as a measured quantity.
> - **Generation diversity is capped by the seed / asset pool** — a narrow seed or asset set limits coverage. → H3 sweeps randomization breadth; [[2606.05160|GRAIL]] widens it with video-prior assets; report generalization vs seed/asset breadth, not single-task SR.
> - **Generated video is plausible but not dynamically feasible** — a video model can render physically impossible motion. → [[2512.05094|GenMimic]]'s physics-aware RL tracker absorbs the noise (H5); never train directly on generated video — pass it through 4D-retarget + a feasibility-enforcing tracker.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

> [!tip] The Coupling Is the Object — Part-Wise Factoring Discards the Load-Bearing Term
> A1, B1, and C1 are three faces of one fact: a humanoid is not an arm bolted to legs. A1 makes the arm–leg inertial coupling ($M(q)$ non-block-diagonal) a predicted quantity; B1 makes the base velocity a manipulation DoF the arm action is conditioned on (autoregressive base→torso→arm); C1 makes the external wrench a whole-body equilibrium term the legs compensate. All three beat part-wise / sequential baselines by modeling the cross-subsystem coupling the factored approach discards — [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s 41.0%→61.8% OOD, [[2503.05652|BRS]]'s 13×/21× over DP3/RGB-DP, [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]]'s 0.60→0.37 — so the coupling, not either subsystem, is the load-bearing term. A4's unified-latent policy is the interface that commands all three couplings jointly.

> [!tip] The Action Target Must Be Feasible Before the Policy Can Use It
> A2, D1, and A3 converge on feasibility-of-the-reference as the binding constraint, inverting the "collect more demonstrations" reflex. A2 composes behavior from frozen *feasible* primitives (softmax convex-hull confinement avoids reward hacking); D1 retargets human motion by preserving *interaction* contact so the reference is realizable; A3 closes the loop in the world frame so the tracked target is drift-free. The evidence is a feasibility→trainability chain: [[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]]'s zero foot-skating → 82–94% downstream RL, [[2604.00202|DreamControl-v2]]'s 68%-vs-8% valid trajectories → 0.925-vs-0.101 RL, [[2606.03476|Human2Humanoid]]'s 0.05 cm penetration → 88.5% SR — fix the target manifold and small data suffices, the same inversion [[Locomotion|Locomotion]]'s motion-imitation direction reaches for pure locomotion.

> [!tip] Structured Decomposition Beats Monolithic Whole-Body Scaling
> A2, A3, B1, and C1 all reject monolithic end-to-end whole-body policies for structured decomposition, because the coupled high-DoF action space is where flat RL fails ([[2403.10506|HumanoidBench]]). A2 blends frozen primitives; A3 splits a world-frame Commander from a whole-body Tracker; B1 factors autoregressively base→torso→arm; C1 decomposes lower/upper agents with shared proprioception. Each reports the decomposition as the source of the win — [[2506.09366|SkillBlender]] avoids reward hacking, [[2602.06341|HiWET]]'s Commander/Tracker hits 12.4 mm, [[2503.05652|BRS]]'s autoregressive factoring mitigates drift, [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]]'s decomposition converges faster than monolithic — the field is converging on structure over scale for the coupled action space.

> [!tip] The Whole-Body Data Wall Is Crossed by Human Video, Synthesis, and Cross-Embodiment Amortization
> A4, B3, D2, D3, and D4 all confront the scarcity of *coupled* loco-manip data, and converge on three levers: human video, automatic synthesis, and cross-body transfer. A4 pretrains on action-free human egocentric video (+38.7%); D2 captures simultaneous loco+manip via whole-body teleop or robot-free egocentric demos (+51 pp generalization); D4 *synthesizes* coupled demos through whole-body planning or from 3D assets + video priors, removing the per-demo human; D3 amortizes a pretrained policy across humanoids at ~1% compute; B3's memory lets one traversal cover a workspace larger than the view. The shared insight — [[2602.10106|EgoHumanoid]]'s +51 pp, [[2512.11047|WholeBodyVLA]]'s +38.7%, [[2605.20373|SUGAR]]'s 32.7%→76.0%, [[2605.27724|HumanoidMimicGen]]'s 0.89 vs 0.33, [[2605.23733|Any2Any]]'s ~1% — is that the coupling must be *in the demonstrations*, whether captured (D2), synthesized with whole-body feasibility enforced (D4), or *transferred from a body that has it* (D3), never assembled from decoupled data, the same human-video substrate the [[Embodied-AI|Embodied-AI]] umbrella's egocentric-pretraining and morphology-invariance directions develop.

> [!tip] Whole-Body Deployment Is Bounded by Hard Physical Constraints the Task Reward Omits
> C2, A3, and B2 surface deployment limits that live *off* the task-success surface. C2 enforces balance + collision as hard constraints via certified projection (the task reward only softly discourages violation); A3's Kinematic Manifold Prior keeps the action kinematically feasible; B2 treats observability as an action because a moving base loses sight of the target. The evidence — [[2604.07457|CMP]]'s 4.7%→46.9% OOD survival from projection, [[2605.25546|ISSf-CBF WBC]]'s collision-free-under-20%-mass-mismatch vs ~50% baseline, [[2306.11565|HomeRobot]]'s 5–15%→0.4–0.6% perception collapse — shows deployability is bounded by constraints (balance, collision, observability) the task reward silently ignores, the same embodiment-cost insight [[Locomotion|Locomotion]]'s force/thermal direction raises for pure locomotion.

> [!tip] Data Is the Breadth Substrate, Explicit Coupling Is the Build — on a Fixed Budget the Idea Beats the Data
> On a *fixed* data budget, the architectural idea moves whole-body capability more than data volume, and the sharpest evidence is internal to this doc's anchors. [[2604.07993|HEX]]'s ablation: removing the coupling component (UPP) costs **−5/12** on Pouring (the largest single-component drop), while its 12M-frame pretraining adds only **+1/12** at convergence — "pretraining mainly improves optimization efficiency rather than the final converged performance." [[2503.05652|BRS]] reports up to a **53%** drop from removing autoregressive decoding *on identical JoyLo data*. [[2505.06776|FALCON (Loco-Manipulation)]] gets its **~2×** tracking gain from dual-agent decomposition with *zero* demonstration data. Two head-to-head tests put the idea above the data: [[2603.12263|Psi0]] beats a competitor trained on **>10×** more data by **+40%** via a *data recipe*, and [[2511.05275|TwinVLA]] beats proprietary bimanual data with a cheap coordination term and *zero* bimanual pretraining. What data uniquely buys is generalization *breadth* — [[2602.10106|EgoHumanoid]]'s **+51 pp** (82% vs 31%) from adding robot-free human demos to a fixed policy is real and architecture-on-fixed-data cannot manufacture it — but breadth is a *substrate*, not the contribution. The reorder: **A1's thin explicit coupling is the build, A4 the interface that grounds into it, D2/D1 the breadth substrate the method consumes** — the scarce, idea-bound contribution is making the coupling *explicit*, not collecting more demos. ([[2511.05275|TwinVLA]] proves the coupling is thin: a cheap explicit term beats a coupling-is-everything model trained on far more data.)
