# Promising Research Directions: Locomotion — Bipedal & Quadruped

> [!abstract] Overview
> A legged robot that moves through the real world faces one structural fact: **it must act on what it cannot directly sense.** The privileged physical state that makes a gait work — terrain friction, ground height ahead of the swing foot, payload, contact, model error — is available in simulation and absent on hardware, so a deployable policy has to recover or bound it from proprioception, exteroception, or a learned model.
> These **10 directions across 2 clusters** organize the bets around that recovery problem and the agile skills it unlocks: the humanoid's legs and dynamic skills under partial observation (A), and the quadruped's real-world adaptation when the unobserved state shifts (B).
> The non-consensus bet: across both clusters, the lever is the *mechanism that extracts more from each step* — feasible references over more demonstrations, anticipatory perception over reactive recovery, off-policy reuse and world-model imagination over PPO rollout-discard — not more data, more scale, or more domain randomization.

## Methodology

**Scope.** Corpus: ~13 humanoid/quadruped/legged-locomotion surveys, benchmarks, and simulators plus ~99 locomotion-method papers from `_KnowledgeHub_/` (of which ~25 are the core per-direction anchors), cross-checked against [[../../../General/11_Robotics-and-Embodied-AI|11_Robotics-and-Embodied-AI]] and the `Embodied-AI/` deep-dives [[../../../Embodied-AI/02_Dataset-Benchmark-Environment|02_Dataset-Benchmark-Environment]] and [[../../../Embodied-AI/15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer|15_Sim-to-Real-Transfer]]. **Subsystem boundary**: locomotion only — commands that move the body via legs/wheels (gait and velocity tracking, terrain traversal, balance and push-recovery, agile skills via motion imitation, fall-recovery, proprioceptive-vs-perceptive locomotion, mapless mobility-to-goal). Loco-manipulation coupling, mobile manipulation (arm + base), and arm/hand manipulation belong to the sibling [[Manipulation|Manipulation]] and [[Whole-Body|Whole-Body]] docs; if a paper manipulates an *object*, it is out of scope here. A **Wheeled Mobility & Navigation cluster was considered and dropped** — the vault's wheeled/mapless papers are platform-agnostic locomotion-to-goal control, not a distinct wheeled frontier, leaving fewer than two distinct non-VLN wheeled directions; the strongest ([[2506.05997|SRU]]) folds into B3. VLN goal-*reasoning* and the world-model substrate are cross-referenced to the umbrella and WAM docs, not re-clustered.

> [!tip] Convergence patterns
> - **The privileged-state gap, not the policy, is the deployment bottleneck** (4-way): [[2107.04034|RMA]] (the deployable policy must *infer* the privileged extrinsics — friction, payload, contact — from proprioception, carrying **12 kg** zero-fine-tune), [[2408.14472|DWL]] (a *denoising* world model estimates the true state + terrain from noisy proprioception for zero-shot snow/stairs), [[2504.16680|RWM-U]] (an epistemic-uncertainty penalty *bounds* reliance on the model exactly where the unobserved state is unknown), [[2403.10506|HumanoidBench]] (flat RL "generally fails" because high-DoF exploration is the wall) — four suites name the same wall under different words: the hard part is recovering or bounding the unobserved physical state, not generating the gait, the empirical mandate for A1/B1/B2's three recovery routes.
> - **Real-world adaptation is bottlenecked by interaction budget, not policy class** (3-way): [[2206.14176|DayDreamer]] (DRL needs *millions* of interactions impractical on hardware — the named motivation for learning skills in hours not weeks), [[2107.04034|RMA]] (online adaptation *without* real-world fine-tuning, since on-robot trials are the cost), [[2504.16680|RWM-U]] (offline MBRL on sim+real beats *online* model-free at **0.91** on ANYmal D precisely by not collecting more real rollouts) — three suites name the same wall: the binding constraint is how much real-world interaction a deployable policy demands, the empirical mandate for B2 and A5's sample-efficiency bets.
> - **Cross-terrain generalization is unsolved — flat and TCN-style methods collapse on hard terrain** (3-way): [[2212.07740|TERT]] (TCN-style adaptation fails on stairs, **0%** for the RMA baseline, motivating a terrain-representation architecture), [[2408.14472|DWL]] (robust traversal of uneven terrain from *noisy proprioception alone* under partial observability is unsolved), [[2403.10506|HumanoidBench]] (flat RL fails most whole-body tasks because the high-DoF action space is the exploration bottleneck) — three suites name the same wall: existing policies generalize poorly across terrains and need structure (terrain representation, denoising, hierarchy), the empirical mandate for A1's perceptive and B1's proprioceptive terrain bets.
> - **No standardized whole-body protocol localizes *where* a gait fails** (3-way): [[2603.20147|AGILE]] (no standardized I/O contract, late environment-bug discovery, and missing motion-quality diagnostics), [[2502.08844|MuJoCo-Playground]] (a unified GPU sim, but the cross-legged/humanoid evaluation protocol is still per-platform), [[2403.13358|QUARD-Auto]] (a 99-sub-task generalist suite that measures skill breadth but not the embodiment cost — heat, noise, force — a gait pays) — three suites name the same missing layer: a shared protocol that reports *how* a gait succeeds (jerk, force, temperature, energy), not only *whether*, which the Benchmark Gaps section enumerates per direction.

## Formal Framing

**The locomotion control object.** A locomotion policy maps an observation $o$ — proprioception $q$ (joint angles, IMU, contact), optionally exteroception $e$ (depth / height-scan) — and a command $c$ (target velocity $v^*$, heading, gait, or a reference motion clip) to a joint action $a$:

$$\pi: (q, e, c) \mapsto a, \qquad a = \tau \text{ or } q^{\text{des}}$$

Locomotion differs from manipulation in what the action regulates: not an object–effector **contact-state**, but the body's **base trajectory and balance** — the centre-of-mass path, the foot-placement sequence, and dynamic stability against gravity and disturbance. The body is moved, not an external object via a grasp.

**The privileged-state / proprioception split.** Every legged method faces a partially-observed physical state. Let $z$ be the **privileged context** — terrain friction $\mu$, ground height $h(\cdot)$, payload $m$, contact forces, actuator state — available in sim but not on hardware. The deployable policy must act on $o$ alone:

| Regime | Policy input | Privileged $z$ | Exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Privileged (sim oracle)** | $(q, e, z)$ | observed | [[2107.04034\|RMA]] base policy (trains with extrinsics) |
| **Proprioceptive (blind, deployable)** | $q$ only | inferred from history $\hat{z}(q_{t-H:t})$ | [[2107.04034\|RMA]] adaptation module, [[2408.14472\|DWL]] denoising WM |
| **Perceptive (deployable + exteroception)** | $(q, e)$ | partially observed via $e$ | [[2604.17335\|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]], [[2602.15827\|PHP]] (depth) |

The central question is **how the unobserved $z$ is recovered or bounded**. Three operators answer it: an *inference* operator regresses $\hat z = f(q_{t-H:t})$ from proprioceptive history; a *perception* operator reads the anticipatory part of $z$ that lives in exteroception, $\hat z = g(e)$; and a *bounding* operator $u_{\text{epi}}(s,a)$ estimates how unreliable the learned dynamics are at $(s,a)$ and discounts the reward, $r \leftarrow r - \beta\, u_{\text{epi}}$, so the policy avoids acting where $z$ is effectively unknown. A1's perceptive bet, B1's proprioceptive bet, and B2's dreaming bet are three instantiations of this same recovery problem.

**The reference as a feasibility-constrained command.** When the command $c$ is a reference clip $\xi_{1:T}$, the tracking objective $\min_a \sum_t \lVert q_t - \xi_t \rVert$ is only well-posed if every $\xi_t$ lies on the robot's *dynamically-feasible manifold* $\mathcal{F}$ — the set of configurations and transitions the robot's torque, contact-timing, and balance limits actually permit. Raw human mocap violates $\mathcal{F}$ (a human backflip exceeds the robot's torque margins), so the imitation target must first be projected, $\xi \mapsto \Pi_{\mathcal F}(\xi)$, before the policy can track it. There are two places to apply $\Pi_{\mathcal F}$: *offline*, projecting a fixed pre-recorded clip once before any rollout (A2's regime), or *online*, generating a fresh reference each control window against the terrain seen at runtime and filtering it with an RL tracker (A1's regime). This reframing — feasibility of the *target*, not quantity of *data* — is the inverse of the "collect more demonstrations" reflex, and it is what A1 and A2 build on at opposite ends of the timeline.

**Cross-morphology portability.** A quadruped (12 DoF, ANYmal/Go2) and a humanoid (≥23 DoF, G1/H1) share *gait structure* — a phase-clocked foot-contact schedule and a velocity-tracking objective — even though their joint-space realization differs. This makes the locomotion control object more morphology-portable than the manipulation grasp object: [[2504.16680|RWM-U]] and [[2501.10100|RWM]] run the *same* world-model + MBRL pipeline across ANYmal D and Unitree G1 unchanged. The lever B2 and Cluster A both rest on is that the gait's *phase* is already a low-dimensional cross-morphology invariant, where a grasp needs a function-aligned action space to bridge hands.

## Cluster A — Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills

*The humanoid's legs — whole-body balance and locomotion under partial observation, plus the dynamic agile skills (terrain traversal, parkour, dance, fall-recovery) that make a humanoid more than a slow walker. Where flat RL fails and the reference/constraint structure is the lever.*

### A1 — Perceptive Terrain Traversal & Vertical Mobility

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills |
| **Thesis** | Generate the gait reference *online* against the terrain seen at runtime *and* learn where to look — fuse online reference synthesis with an active-gaze module in one perceptive policy, not a fixed gait library nor a full passive height-scan. The reason it must work: feasible foot-placement for a 75 cm box depends on the *perceived* local geometry ahead of the swing foot, but a fixed-horizon scan of the whole terrain wastes capacity where it doesn't matter. The field now agrees perception beats blindness on obstacle terrain; what it has not settled is *what to look at, over what horizon*. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark), [[2603.20147\|AGILE]] (benchmark), [[2408.14472\|DWL]] (method), [[2604.17335\|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] (method), [[2602.15827\|PHP]] (method), [[2606.05880\|TAGA]] (method), [[2506.09588\|Attention-Map-Encoding]] (method), [[2601.08485\|AME-2]] (method), [[2602.06382\|Now-You-See-That]] (method) |
| **Key targets** | [[2604.17335\|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] 80 cm box-climb SR 0.962 (Tracker+Gen) vs 0.230 (Tracker-Only), 75 cm box + stairs + hurdles real; [[2606.05880\|TAGA]] 120 cm gap on a real G1 (+50% over prior perceptive max) at 65.2% lower training cost; [[2602.15827\|PHP]] 1.25 m wall (96% height) in 3.63 s + cat-vault 3.41 m/s + ~0.5 m perturbation recovery; [[2408.14472\|DWL]] zero-shot snowy-incline/stairs from proprioception alone |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a humanoid clearing a 75 cm box must commit its swing trajectory *before* the foot touches, so the policy needs the local geometry ahead of it — but the cheap perceptive answer is a fixed full height-scan over a fixed horizon, which neither prioritizes the sparse footholds that matter nor adapts its reference to disturbance, and [[2403.10506|HumanoidBench]] sets the wall that flat RL "generally fails" on whole-body locomotion.
- **Today's answers**: the perceptive-beats-blind battle is now settled — [[2603.18979|PRIOR-Loco]] hits 100% traversal on Pyramid Stairs / Inverted Stairs / Boxes with a parametric gait generator + self-supervised depth→height-map terrain reconstruction, [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] closes the 0.230→0.962 box-climb gap with an online diffusion-generated reference, and [[2601.07701|Deep-WB-Parkour]] reaches 100% over a 1.2 m OOD start range by folding depth into a whole-body tracking policy. All anticipate; what none does is decide *where* to look — they pay for a full terrain scan over a fixed horizon.
- **The opening**: [[2606.05880|TAGA]] shows the perceptive ceiling is still rising on the *attention* axis — an *emergent* active-gaze module that learns where to look matches full-height-scan performance at 65.2% lower training cost and clears a 120 cm gap (+50% over the prior perceptive max). The unclaimed cell is the combination: online reference *generation* fused with *learned gaze*, which no perceptive parkour policy has yet built.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: On obstacle terrain, feasible foot-placement depends on the *local geometry ahead of the swing foot*, but that geometry is *sparse* — a few load-bearing footholds carry the placement decision, while most of the height-scan is irrelevant. A fixed full scan over a fixed horizon spends equal capacity everywhere and equal staleness under disturbance. [[2606.05880|TAGA]] demonstrates the first half: a learned gaze matches the full scan at 65.2% lower cost, proving most of the scan is wasted; [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s 0.962 vs 0.230 ablation proves the anticipatory half — the signal must be perceived, not felt.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That the two perceptive levers this literature has separately proven — an *online-generated reference* and *learned where-to-look* — are substitutes, not complements: pick the lever that beats the fixed-full-scan baseline, not both at once. That gaze-alone beats a fixed full scan is [[2606.05880|TAGA]]'s own already-published result (65.2% cheaper, 120 cm gap) — settled background, not this direction's risk. What every cited row actually holds by construction is the substitutes framing: [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] closes 0.230→0.962 with *generation alone* at a fixed full passive scan (no gaze); [[2606.05880|TAGA]] closes its cost gap with *gaze alone* at no online reference generation; [[2506.09588|Attention-Map-Encoding]], [[2606.00637|GLAD]], and [[2601.08485|AME-2]] all sparsify attention *within* the passive scan but synthesize no reference. No cited system does both. This direction bets the opposite of "pick one lever": that camera-gaze and reference-generation are complements whose combination is super-additive on sparse-foothold terrain, not merely additive — a claim no perceptive-parkour paper has tested because none has built the fused system.
- **The bet**: A policy that *fuses* online reference generation ([[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]-style) with learned **camera-gaze** ([[2606.05880|TAGA]]-style — sensor *pointing* under limited field-of-view, distinct from the map-attention that [[2606.00637|GLAD]]/[[2601.08485|AME-2]] apply *within* a full passive elevation map) beats both gaze-alone and generation-alone, with its win concentrated where the height-scan is sparse: on stepping-stone / wide-gap tasks (≥70 cm spacing) the fused policy holds ≥0.95 SR at ≤40% of the full-scan *training* compute, while on dense rough terrain it ties full-scan within 2 pp; and there is an *interior-optimal reference horizon* — SR is non-monotone in horizon under ~0.5 m perturbation ([[2602.15827|PHP]]-class). The ≤40% figure is a *training/scan-FLOP* axis and is **re-baselined on wall-clock onboard inference latency and elevation-map-noise robustness** — the deploy-relevant cost the robot feels ([[2510.07152|DPL]]'s ~20 ms perception latency, [[2503.00692|Perceptive-Humanoid-Terrain]]'s 74.3% at 200% noise) — because scan-FLOPs are already cheap and a three-net stack (diffusion generator at 0.5 s horizon + gaze ROI predictor + PPO tracker) may eat the gaze savings in latency. The H1 null — a strong full-scan generation encoder already implicitly foveating — is live regardless of any unverified paper: the generation-alone arm can be run with the in-KH [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] (diffusion) or [[2603.18979|PRIOR-Loco]] (parametric) at full passive scan, so the null is honestly powered from the cited corpus alone (an additional CVAE near-miss the table does not cite — T-GMP, 2606.06944, not yet in KH, to verify — would reinforce it), and it must be **pre-registered**. Falsifiable: if generation-alone (G1 WBC-Gen+Track or T-GMP at full passive scan) matches the fused camera-gaze+generation policy on sparse-foothold SR-at-fixed-compute *and at matched inference latency*, learned camera-gaze adds nothing over a fixed scan; if SR rises monotonically with horizon, there is no staleness penalty.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (fused gaze+generation beats either alone, with its win concentrated where the height-scan is sparse and at an interior-optimal horizon).
1. **H1 — Camera-gaze × generation is super-additive on sparse-foothold tasks at matched compute (interaction term pre-registered; the null is a live, publishable outcome).**
   - *Prediction*: a policy fusing [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s online generation with [[2606.05880|TAGA]]'s learned *camera-gaze* (sensor pointing under limited FoV, **not** map-attention within a full passive scan) holds ≥0.95 SR on ≥70 cm stepping-stones at ≤40% of the full-scan *training* compute, beating both generation-alone and gaze-alone — but the gaze×generation *interaction term* is the pre-registered primary result, and a null (a strong full-scan generation encoder already implicitly foveates) is an expected, publishable outcome, not a strawman.
   - *Test*: matched-compute **2×2** — gaze {fixed full passive elevation-map scan, learned camera-gaze} × reference {none, online-generated} — on the [[2502.10363|BeamDojo]] sparse-foothold suite (stepping-stones/beams/gaps; BeamDojo itself hits 91.67% SR on hard Stepping Stones, 4/5 Stepping Stones + 5/5 Gaps real G1, 7.79% foothold error), reporting SR and FLOPs per stratum **and onboard wall-clock inference latency** (not just training/scan-FLOPs), with the generation-alone arm run on the in-KH [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] / [[2603.18979|PRIOR-Loco]] full-passive-scan generators (optionally a T-GMP-class CVAE generator, 2606.06944, not yet in KH, to verify) so the null is honestly powered.
   - *Row*: G1 WBC-Gen+Track (generation) + TAGA (camera-gaze) vs PRIOR-Loco (full-scan generation).
   - *Falsifier*: the gaze×generation interaction term is null — generation-alone at full passive scan matches the fused policy on sparse-foothold SR-at-fixed-compute *and at matched inference latency* → learned camera-gaze adds nothing over a fixed scan, and the contribution pivots to the compute-normalized sparse-vs-dense benchmark.
2. **H2 — There is an optimal reference horizon under perturbation.**
   - *Prediction*: sweeping [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s 0.5 s generation horizon against [[2602.15827|PHP]]'s ~0.5 m perturbation, anticipation improves with horizon up to a point, then stale references hurt under disturbance — a non-monotone curve with an interior optimum.
   - *Test*: vary horizon length, measure SR under fixed perturbation magnitude.
   - *Row*: G1 WBC-Gen+Track (0.5 s receding) / PHP (chained).
   - *Falsifier*: SR rises monotonically with horizon → no staleness penalty, longer is always better.
3. **H3 — The fused policy avoids the gaze-generation deadlock that naively composing the two modules risks.**
   - *Prediction*: bolting [[2606.05880|TAGA]]'s frozen gaze module onto [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s frozen generator (a naive, independently-trained composition) produces a measurable deadlock failure mode — the gaze looks away from a region the generator then swings the foot into — because neither module was trained to anticipate the other's output; a policy that trains gaze and generation *jointly* (the fused system H1 tests) has a lower deadlock-failure rate than the naive composition at matched sparse-foothold SR.
   - *Test*: construct an adversarial sparse-foothold course where the independently-optimal gaze target and the generator's next-reference region diverge; compare fall/deadlock rate between (a) frozen-TAGA-gaze + frozen-G1-WBC-Gen+Track-generator glued via a scan-mask handoff and (b) the jointly-trained fused policy from H1, at matched SR on non-adversarial terrain.
   - *Row*: TAGA (frozen gaze) + G1 WBC-Gen+Track (frozen generation), naive composition, vs the H1 jointly-trained fused policy.
   - *Falsifier*: the naive frozen-module composition matches the jointly-trained fused policy's deadlock rate → gaze and generation compose freely with no interaction cost, and joint training buys nothing beyond what H1's SR number already shows.
4. **H4 — A generated reference library chains into a multi-obstacle course no fixed graph covers.**
   - *Prediction*: composing [[2602.15827|PHP]]'s motion-matching skill graph with [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s online generation clears a multi-obstacle course that PHP's fixed skill graph alone fails, because the generator synthesizes transitions for obstacle combinations absent from the graph — and [[2608.02653|LightLP]]'s reward-only conditional-AMP transitions (98% chaining SR with no generated or fixed-graph reference at all) is a third arm this hypothesis must also beat, since it already chains skills without either mechanism.
   - *Test*: build a course mixing seen skills in unseen order; compare chained-generation vs fixed-graph vs reward-only-transition SR.
   - *Row*: PHP (fixed skill chain) + G1 WBC-Gen+Track (online generated) + LightLP (reward-only autonomous transition, no reference).
   - *Falsifier*: the fixed graph matches chained generation → composition over a fixed library suffices; if LightLP's reward-only transitions already match chained-generation SR, an explicit reference (fixed or generated) is unnecessary for chaining at all.
5. **H5 — Blind robustness survives as a fallback under perception dropout.**
   - *Prediction*: when exteroception degrades (occlusion, darkness), a perceptive policy with a [[2408.14472|DWL]]-style proprioceptive safety mode underneath falls back gracefully on flat/rough terrain rather than catastrophically, where a perception-only policy fails.
   - *Test*: inject depth dropout mid-traverse on flat terrain; compare fall rate with vs without the proprioceptive fallback.
   - *Row*: DWL (blind floor) under G1 WBC-Gen+Track (perceptive).
   - *Falsifier*: the fallback doesn't recover flat-terrain stability under dropout → perception failure is unavoidably catastrophic.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Perception failure is catastrophic, not graceful** — a depth dropout mid-vault can be fatal where a blind policy would have stumbled and recovered. → H5 is the go/no-go; require a [[2408.14472|DWL]]-class proprioceptive safety mode underneath the perceptive policy (couples to A3's recovery layer) and report fall rate under induced dropout.
> - **Generated references can be infeasible** — the generator may propose what the tracker cannot execute. → [[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]]'s RL fine-tuning filters them; report the tracker's reject/clamp rate, not just headline SR.
> - **Parkour-class skills risk hardware damage** — 1.25 m walls and 3.41 m/s vaults stress real humanoids. → Bound aggressive-skill claims to validated platforms; report contact-force and motor-temperature (couples to A4).
> - **The fused stack may never close the real-time loop** — a 0.5 s-horizon diffusion generator + a learned camera-gaze ROI predictor + a PPO tracker is three networks competing for the same 50 Hz control budget, and the ≤40%-scan-FLOP win is a *training* number, not deploy latency. → Gate every headline SR on a *composed* onboard deployment-latency budget (generator + gaze + tracker wall-clock against the control period) plus A4's thermal budget, and probe the gaze-generation deadlock (the gaze looking away from a region the generator then references a swing into) as a co-primary go/no-go alongside H1.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a human backflip breaks the robot's torque limits, contact timing, and balance margins, so a policy asked to imitate raw mocap optimizes toward a target it physically cannot reach — yet the reflexive recipe for a new agile skill is "imitate more human motion."
- **Today's answers**: feasibility-projection is now a mature toolkit run *at scale* — [[2511.09484|SPIDER]] projects 2.4 M frames across 9 embodiments by constrained optimization (100% task SR, +18% from virtual contact guidance), [[2409.20514|Opt2Skill]] generates torque- and contact-force-feasible references by trajectory optimization (2.0 cm hand / 5.23 cm foot tracking), and [[2603.09956|Kinodynamic-Retargeting]] recovers heel-toe contact and GRF magnitudes — so projection-onto-$\mathcal F$ and data-scale are *complements*, not opposites. What none of them does is *reject* the residue that stays untrackable on extreme agile-bipedal skills, or schedule tracking tolerance to learn what a fixed tolerance cannot.
- **The opening**: [[2510.14454|Adaptive-Motion-Tracking]] is the direct proof the tolerance schedule is the lever — adaptive phase/tracking adapters beat fixed-tolerance baselines on 7 agile tasks on a real G1; the same mechanism is doing the work inside [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]'s own headline number — its adaptive tracking factor, not the filtering alone, is part of how it hits 53.25 mm where fixed-tolerance deployable baselines (OmniH2O, ExBody2) exceed 233 mm. [[2605.10063|EFGCL]] extends the same principle further: a force-guided curriculum *grows* the feasible manifold to reach backflips a PPO baseline cannot learn under *any* fixed tolerance at all. Clip-level rejection is a separate, secondary diagnostic (H1) rather than this direction's bet — and the field's own evidence explains why: [[2602.23843|OmniXtreme]] reaches **91.08% overall** real-world success across 24 high-dynamic motions with *no* accept/reject gate at all, and the originating lab itself swapped rejection for *relaxed* tracking in [[2509.16638|KungfuBot2]]. The rejection question that survives is narrower — *when*, if ever, an explicit clip-level gate adds anything beyond adaptive tolerance and actuation-aware scale (H1's diagnostic), not whether rejection wins outright. A distinct, complementary lever leaves the reference-*source* itself variable: [[2608.01410|GenTrack]]'s online bidirectional co-training — a lagged tracker scoring a text-to-motion generator via FlowGRPO, the generator then re-supplying the tracker's training pool each round — cuts generated-motion executability error by 20.7% and lifts zero-shot SR up to 5.0 pp with no per-clip filter, tolerance schedule, or data-scale, extending [[2506.12769|RL-from-Physical-Feedback]]'s one-way generate-then-feedback into a continuously co-evolving loop.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Asking a policy to track a reference only makes sense if that reference lies on what the robot can physically do — its torque, contact-timing, and balance limits. Raw human mocap does not, so the first operation is the projection $\xi \mapsto \Pi_{\mathcal F}(\xi)$ onto the dynamically-feasible manifold; only then is the imitation loss well-posed. This is prior to any question of how much data you have. [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]] demonstrates it: *filtering* untrackable motions (not adding data) delivers the 233→53 mm cut.
- **Assumption being challenged**: *Not* that feasibility beats scale — [[2511.09484|SPIDER]] (2.4 M frames, 9 embodiments) and [[2509.15443|Implicit-Kinodynamic-Retargeting]] (5000 fps, dynamics-aware fine-tuning) prove projection scales, so they are complements. The challenged assumption is narrower: that a *fixed* tolerance on the tracking loss is enough once the reference is projected. SPIDER, Opt2Skill, and KDMR project and track against a fixed tolerance; none schedules it. [[2510.14454|Adaptive-Motion-Tracking]]'s win over fixed-tolerance baselines on 7 agile tasks, and the adaptive tracking factor inside [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]'s own 233→53 mm result, bet the opposite — that a *scheduled* tolerance, not a fixed one, is the lever on extreme bipedal skills, beyond what projecting-then-tracking at scale delivers.
- **The bet**: On extreme agile-bipedal skills (flips, martial-arts), an *adaptive tracking-tolerance schedule* ([[2510.14454|Adaptive-Motion-Tracking]]/[[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]-style) beats a matched fixed-tolerance project-everything pipeline ([[2511.09484|SPIDER]]/[[2409.20514|Opt2Skill]]-style) at equal compute on the high-difficulty tail — cutting tracking error to [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]'s 53.25 mm (vs >233 mm OmniH2O/ExBody2) and lifting downstream RL to [[2605.06593|ReActor]]'s 97.45% (G1) at zero penetration. Falsifiable: if an adaptive-tolerance policy ties a fixed-tolerance project-everything pipeline at equal compute on the high-difficulty tail, the tolerance schedule is not the lever; if a *reference-free* policy ([[2607.12114|GaitSpan]]/[[2607.24036|WARL]]-style, no mocap clip to track-tolerance-schedule at all) matches it on the extreme-skill tail, the whole projection/tolerance axis is sidestepped, not won. (Clip-level *rejection* is a separate, secondary question — see H1.)

### A3 — Autonomous Fall Recovery as Non-Periodic Whole-Body Control

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills |
| **Thesis** | Treat getting-up as its own non-periodic, rich-contact, sparse-reward control problem, not a degenerate gait. The reason it must work: fall-recovery has no phase clock and no nominal contact schedule, so the periodicity and foot-contact priors that make locomotion learnable actively mislead it. That a *learned, curriculum-shaped* recovery beats a scripted routine is now consensus; what is unsettled is *which* structural prior makes the sparse-reward skill discoverable — and whether a locomotion phase-clock prior actively hurts. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2502.12152\|HUMANUP]] (method), [[2502.08378\|HoST]] (method), [[2606.12814\|Stubborn]] (method), [[2105.08328\|Blind Bipedal Stair Climbing]] (method), [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark), [[2603.20147\|AGILE]] (benchmark) |
| **Key targets** | [[2502.12152\|HUMANUP]] 78.3% getting-up (supine) + 98.3% roll-over real on 6 terrains, ~6 s vs manufacturer 11 s, 20,000 randomized postures, single-stage training fails to converge; [[2502.08378\|HoST]] 100% standing on real G1 across flat/platform/wall/slope via multi-critic + vertical-force curriculum, multi-critic ablation **0%** without it, robust to 12 kg payload; [[2603.08619\|Classical-Balance-RL]] 93.4% recovery (sim), 10/10 zero-shot H1-2, balance-structure ablation fails to lift off |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a humanoid that cannot stand up after a fall is not autonomous — it needs a human — yet getting-up has no gait cycle, an arbitrary post-fall configuration, and a single binary reward at the end, so the locomotion playbook's inductive biases work *against* it.
- **Today's answers**: learned recovery is now multiply-instantiated, and crucially *via different structural priors* — [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]] separates a Discovery Policy from a Deployable Policy over 20,000 postures (78.3% supine / 98.3% roll-over, 6 terrains, ~6 s vs 11 s), while [[2502.08378|HoST]] reaches 100% standing on the same real G1 with **no** discover/refine split — a multi-critic architecture (0% without it) plus a vertical-force curriculum. Two decompositions reach parity, so the lever is *curriculum-shaped exploration generally*, not HUMANUP's specific discovery stage.
- **The opening**: the structural-prior question is sharpened, not closed — [[2603.08619|Classical-Balance-RL]] runs A3's own ablation (remove the balance-metric structure → the policy fails to lift off, 93.4% with it) and [[2602.16511|VIGOR]] beats HoST/FIRM by up to 5× on safe recovery, so what each prior *buys* is now measurable. The one untested claim is the cleanest: that a *periodic / phase-clock* prior — the field's clock-based RL default (e.g. [[2105.08328|Blind Bipedal Stair Climbing]]) — actively *lowers* recovery SR, which no recovery paper has injected and measured.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Fall-recovery has no phase clock and no nominal contact schedule — the initial state is an arbitrary post-fall configuration and the contact set is unknown. The phase-clocked, foot-contact-prior structure that makes locomotion well-shaped is *absent*; imposing it biases the policy away from the contact-rich ground transitions recovery needs. Two independent results show the sparse-reward landscape needs *some* structural scaffold: [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]] (single-stage fails to converge → discover/refine split) and [[2502.08378|HoST]] (no single critic suffices → 0% without multi-critic). What kind of scaffold is open; that a *gait-like* prior is the wrong one is the sharp claim.
- **Assumption being challenged**: *Not* that learned curriculum-shaped recovery beats a script — [[2502.08378|HoST]], [[2602.16511|VIGOR]] (89.5% stand-up / 90.5% recovery, beats HoST/FIRM up to 5×), [[2502.20061|HiFAR]] (100% supine/prone on Booster T1), and [[2410.08655|FRASA]] all demonstrate it, so that is consensus. The challenged assumption is finer, and it has a specific holder: the clock-based-reward RL lineage most locomotion controllers in this doc's own Related tables inherit from. [[2105.08328|Blind Bipedal Stair Climbing]] (Siekmann, Fern, Hurst et al., Oregon State/Agility Robotics) carries a cyclic phase-clock input over *unmodified* from prior flat-ground RL into blind stair traversal, and reports the resulting policy exhibits "error-correcting behavior" recovering from missteps on real stairs, logs, and curbs (80% ascent / 100% descent SR, 10 outdoor trials) — the clock is treated as a robustness-neutral-or-positive default, never ablated in isolation from the terrain-randomization change that paper actually tests. The same lab's own later work, [[2404.19173|Single-Contact++-RL]] (already an H4 anchor below), directly tested that default: comparing a clock-free "Single Contact RL" against a "Clock Based RL" controller on standing disturbance rejection, the clock-free policy *generally outperformed* the clock-based one, and the refined clock-free "Single Contact++ RL" reached **perfect** rejection up to **258 N @ 500 ms** — undercutting the neutral-or-positive assumption specifically on the *standing push-recovery* task, but leaving the harder, fully non-periodic *ground-up getting-up* regime this direction targets untested. Closing that gap is what H4 does.
- **The bet**: Injecting a phase-clock / periodic foot-contact prior into a prior-free recovery policy *lowers* arbitrary-config getting-up SR by a measurable margin (≥10 pp) versus the prior-free [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]] / [[2502.08378|HoST]] formulations; and HUMANUP's two-stage discover/refine and HoST's single-stage multi-critic reach *parity* (within 5 pp) on a matched posture/terrain distribution — confirming the lever is curriculum-shaped exploration, not a specific decomposition, and that gait structure actively hurts. Falsifiable: if the periodic prior is neutral-or-helpful, locomotion structure transfers to recovery; if multi-critic and two-stage differ by >5 pp at matched data, one decomposition *is* the lever after all.

1. **H1 — Two-stage discovery and multi-critic reach parity — the lever is curriculum-shaped exploration, not one decomposition.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]]'s discover/refine split and [[2502.08378|HoST]]'s single-stage multi-critic + vertical-force curriculum reach arbitrary-config getting-up SR within 5 pp on a matched posture/terrain distribution, while an unstructured single-critic single-stage baseline fails to converge — confirming *some* curriculum-shaped scaffold is load-bearing but no specific decomposition is uniquely so.
   - *Test*: train discover/refine, multi-critic, and unstructured-single-stage on identical posture/terrain distributions; report convergence + SR; separately test [[2606.12814|Stubborn]]'s emergent recovery on arbitrary supine configs.
   - *Row*: HUMANUP (two-stage discovery) vs HoST (multi-critic) vs Stubborn (emergent).
   - *Falsifier*: the two decompositions differ by >5 pp at matched data → one decomposition *is* the lever, contra the parity claim.
2. **H2 — Posture coverage sets arbitrary-config robustness.**
   - *Prediction*: sweeping the number of randomized lying postures toward [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]]'s 20,000, success on held-out post-fall configurations rises sub-linearly to a coverage threshold beyond which more postures add little.
   - *Test*: vary posture count; report held-out-config SR per count.
   - *Row*: HUMANUP (two-stage discovery).
   - *Falsifier*: SR scales linearly with postures with no saturation → coverage is unbounded and the 20,000 figure is arbitrary.
3. **H3 — Terrain-conditioned recovery beats terrain-blind on extreme surfaces.**
   - *Prediction*: conditioning the Deployable Policy on perceived terrain (slope, compliance) beats [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]]'s terrain-blind policy specifically on snow/mud, where ground compliance changes the feasible push-off, and ties on rigid concrete.
   - *Test*: compare terrain-conditioned vs blind recovery, stratified by surface compliance.
   - *Row*: HUMANUP (two-stage discovery).
   - *Falsifier*: terrain conditioning doesn't help on compliant surfaces → recovery is compliance-invariant.
4. **H4 — A periodic prior actively hurts non-periodic recovery.**
   - *Prediction*: injecting a phase-clock / foot-contact prior (the locomotion inductive bias) into the recovery policy *lowers* SR versus the prior-free [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]] formulation, because it biases toward gait-like transitions the ground-up motion doesn't use.

   - *Falsifier*: the periodic prior is neutral or helps → locomotion structure transfers to recovery.
5. **H5 — A unified locomotion+recovery stack reaches end-to-end autonomy.**
   - *Prediction*: wiring fall-recovery as the fallback when A1's perceptive policy loses balance (perception dropout, push beyond recovery margin) yields a stack that completes a multi-obstacle course *including* falls without human intervention, where locomotion-only fails on the first fall.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Recovery motions stress hardware** — flailing limbs and ground impacts risk motor/joint damage. → [[2502.12152|HUMANUP]]'s strong regularization lowers arm-motor temperature; report contact-force and temperature, treat smoothness as a first-class objective (couples to A4).
> - **Discovery may find unsafe trajectories** — weak regularization can produce violent motions infeasible for hardware. → The two-stage design refines discovery into a deployable policy; report the discovery→deployment safety-margin gap.
> - **Real falls exceed simulation coverage** — 20,000 postures may miss adversarial real falls. → H2's coverage curve bounds the claim; report failure modes by initial-configuration class, not a single average.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: simulation rewards task success — reach the velocity, climb the box — and silently omits the physical cost the real robot pays: motors overheat, gaits are loud, contact forces spike, and these are deployment-fatal, not cosmetic.
- **Today's answers**: each cost axis is now solved in isolation, two papers couple a *pair*, and one even co-regulates heat **and** noise jointly — [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] drops overheating 70%→<10% (650 m + 3 kg), [[2603.01631|Thermal-Aware-Locomotion]] independently quadruples thermal-safe runtime (7→27 min) on the same heat axis, [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]] cuts noise 7.17 dBA via a GRF predictor (R²≈0.99), [[2510.09543|IMF-Reward]] genuinely couples energy + impact (35% peak-power, 18–32% torque cut), and Disney's [[2512.16705|Olaf]] trains *one* PPO policy with both a thermal-CBF reward (~100 °C in 40 s baseline → 77.3 °C over 1 hr) **and** a foot-impact-noise reward (−13.5 dB) on real hardware. So heat+noise *co-regulation in one policy* is no longer open — but Olaf uses *fixed* reward weights (no conditional head), reports thermal (Fig 6) and noise (Fig 7) *separately* with no cross-cost Pareto and no reward-term ablations, and regulates no GRF.
- **The opening**: [[2503.05035|QuietPaw]] already supplies the instrument this direction needs, not an unclaimed step — a *single conditional cost-head* (CNCP) that takes a desired cost threshold ε and produces a whole Pareto front (hypervolume 10.416×10⁻²) on a real Go2, so a joint sweep is affordable, not something to invent. What's still unmeasured is what that instrument reveals once it is pointed at all three costs at once, which Olaf's fixed-composite policy never does: feed it the heat ∧ noise ∧ **force** triple (Olaf has no GRF), and read off the joint surface — does pushing noise down provably raise peak motor temperature, *and* does pushing peak GRF down measurably move at least one of the other two, under one regulated policy?

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A real robot's gait is bounded by hard embodiment limits — motor-temperature ceilings, actuator force limits, and (around people) noise budgets — that exist *off* the sim reward surface. A policy optimizing only task success saturates these limits because nothing penalizes them; the cost is invisible until the hardware fails or the gait is unacceptable. [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s 70% overheating under standard policies is the direct evidence: the embodiment cost is load-bearing for deployment, not a second-order concern.
- **Assumption being challenged**: [[2512.16705|Olaf]]'s own design choice is the contested position this direction bets against. Disney's team built *one* policy with a thermal-CBF reward (T_max = 80 °C) **and** a foot-impact-noise reward, gave each a *fixed*, separately-tuned weight, and evaluated the two objectives in *separate* figures — no conditional head, no sweep, no cross-cost axis. That bet paid off on real hardware: neck-pitch actuator held at 77.3 °C over a 1-hr run (vs ~100 °C in 40 s for the untuned baseline), footstep noise down 13.5 dB over 5 min, on a deployed bipedal character robot. Olaf is the strongest existing evidence that fixed, separately-tuned weights suffice for two-cost humanoid locomotion — and exactly why a swept alternative has to out-argue a working baseline, not an absent one. This direction bets that Olaf's fixed-weight recipe caps what's reachable: that a genuine coupling between heat and noise exists and is left unexploited by tuning each axis in isolation, and that adding force (which Olaf omits entirely) only sharpens the case for measuring the joint surface rather than three separate specialists. Two adjacent claims stay settled, not reopened — that embodiment cost is a distinct lever (QuietWalk, Thermal-Aware-Residual, [[2503.05035|QuietPaw]], [[2510.09543|IMF-Reward]] all show it), and that a conditional head can sweep a Pareto front at all ([[2503.05035|QuietPaw]] on Go2, [[2603.24047|PCHC]] on G1 already run that exact mechanism on real hardware). What's contested is narrower: whether Olaf's fixed-weight, unswept recipe is the ceiling for multi-cost locomotion, or whether it's leaving a swept three-way front on the table.
- **The bet**: A single threshold-conditioned cost-head ([[2503.05035|QuietPaw]]-style CNCP) fed heat + acoustic + predicted GRF (at [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s R²≈0.99) traces a genuine three-way cost front that no single- or two-cost policy has measured: pushing noise down by ≥3 dBA provably raises peak motor temperature by a measurable margin (the thermal-vs-acoustic curve — the moat), and pushing peak GRF down by a comparable margin measurably moves at least one of heat or noise (the force-coupling extension nobody has tested) — both read off *one* swept policy that holds overheating below [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s <10% and noise within +1 dBA of [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s quiet-policy mean at ≤5% task-SR loss. That training this sweep once is cheaper than retraining a fixed-weight specialist per operating point is a real efficiency the mechanism inherits from QuietPaw/PCHC — not the finding this direction is betting on. Falsifiable: if either pairwise front is flat (noise-vs-temperature, or force-vs-{heat, noise}), that axis is decoupled from the others and the joint surface collapses to independent sub-problems; if a swept fixed-weight composite ([[2512.16705|Olaf]]-style, weight-swept à la [[2505.23708|AMOR]]) reaches the same three-way front at comparable total cost, the conditional head buys convenience, not a measurement a cheaper baseline couldn't already surface.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (the thermal-acoustic-force cost surface is real, jointly measurable, and unmeasured by any prior single- or two-cost policy). The predict-then-regulate, residual-vs-monolith, and cross-footwear-generalization questions are *already answered* by [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s and [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s own internal ablations (folded into the Related table above), so they are not re-tested here — what remains is the genuinely novel content: the force axis, the cross-cost surface, and whether the cost-prediction mechanism itself survives a platform change (not just a footwear change).
1. **H1 — The thermal-acoustic-force cost front is real and measurable, jointly (the direction's primary claim).**
   - *Prediction*: pushing noise down ≥3 dBA provably raises peak motor temperature by a measurable margin (a downward-sloping thermal-vs-acoustic front), and pushing peak GRF down by a comparable margin measurably moves at least one of heat or noise — a genuine *three-way* cross-cost coupling that two single-cost policies (each blind to the others' objectives) never surface and [[2512.16705|Olaf]]'s separately-evaluated heat+noise composite never sweeps. [[2510.09543|IMF-Reward]]'s energy+impact coupling is the only prior cross-cost-sweep precedent and it omits both heat and noise.
   - *Test*: sweep the noise and force thresholds under a fixed heat budget (and vice versa) on a single conditional cost-head; report the resulting thermal×acoustic×force surface. *First* run a measurement-repeatability pre-test confirming the trade-off signal exceeds the cross-surface/cross-ambient noise floor of dBA/temperature/force metrics, and specify the thermal substrate (hour-scale hardware rollouts, or a named motor-thermal surrogate with RMSE vs ground truth); compare against IMF's energy+impact coupling for a cross-cost baseline.
   - *Row*: IMF Reward (two-cost coupling) / QuietPaw (single conditional head) vs Olaf (fixed-weight composite, single operating point, no sweep).
   - *Falsifier*: any pairwise front is flat, or the trade-off margin sits below its own measurement noise floor → that axis is decoupled (or unmeasurable) and the cross-cost coupling the direction rests on does not hold there.
2. **H2 — A single head carries the force axis alongside heat and noise without breaking either bound; training it once is cheaper than N specialist retrainings (a secondary, not primary, result).**
   - *Prediction*: extending [[2503.05035|QuietPaw]]'s single conditional head (CNCP) to take heat + acoustic + force thresholds holds overheating <10% **and** noise within +1 dBA of [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s mean at ≤5% task-SR loss across the *whole* operating range from **one** training run — the FORCE axis ([[2503.05035|QuietPaw]]/[[2510.09543|IMF-Reward]]/[[2512.16705|Olaf]] all omit it) integrates without degrading the other two bounds.
   - *Test*: train the three-cost conditional head; separately train an Olaf-style fixed-weight heat+noise+GRF composite *swept* over ~8 weight vectors; compare (a) whether both bounds hold at ≤5% SR loss and (b) total training cost for matched front coverage.
   - *Row*: QuietPaw (single conditional head) + Thermal-Aware Residual (thermal) vs Olaf (fixed-weight composite, swept).
   - *Falsifier*: adding the force threshold breaks either the heat or noise bound at matched SR loss → the three costs are not jointly regulable in one head; or the swept composite matches the conditional head on the full three-way front at comparable total cost → the head's only advantage is convenience, not capability.
3. **H3 — Off the front, the head degrades gracefully instead of violating a hard limit.**
   - *Prediction*: some heat + acoustic + force threshold combinations sit outside the achievable three-way front mapped in H1 (e.g. max-quiet + max-cool + max-force-limited at once is not jointly reachable). When such an infeasible combination is dialed in, the conditional head returns the nearest feasible point (or breaches the least-binding threshold by a bounded margin) rather than an unsafe gait — no true hard-limit breach (real motor-thermal ceiling, not the soft training bound) and no fall.
   - *Test*: use H1's mapped front to identify the infeasible region; dial in threshold combinations just outside it; measure which threshold is violated, by how much, and whether the gait stays within hard safety limits.
   - *Row*: QuietPaw (single conditional head) — the CNCP mechanism whose behavior outside its trained feasible region nobody has characterized.
   - *Falsifier*: an infeasible combination causes a true hard-limit breach or destabilizes the gait → the head is unsafe outside its trained region and needs an explicit infeasibility detector before deployment.
4. **H4 — A data-driven acoustic/thermal cost predictor needs PACE-style physics-grounding to survive a platform change; it doesn't travel for free (independent of H1–H3 — this tests the predictor mechanism itself, not the joint front).**
   - *Prediction*: [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s data-fit GRF-acoustic predictor (RMSE 14.49/14.00 N, R²=0.9887/0.9899, fit on its own platform) and [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s data-fit thermal residual, deployed with zero re-fitting on a structurally different platform (different mass, DoF, actuator type), degrade well past their same-platform error — unlike [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s *physics-grounded* PMSM energy model, which travels across ANYMAL/TYTAN/MINIMAL on only ~20 s of per-platform CMA-ES calibration (32% CoT cut on ANYMAL D, CoT 0.97 on TYTAN).
   - *Test*: fit QuietWalk's GRF-acoustic predictor and Thermal-Aware-Residual's thermal residual on platform X; deploy each with zero re-fitting on platform Y; compare cross-platform error (RMSE/R² for GRF-acoustic, MAE for thermal) against each predictor's own same-platform error and against PACE's ~20-s-recalibration error budget.
   - *Row*: [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]] (physics-grounded, cross-platform) vs [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]] (data-driven, held-out-footwear-only) / [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] (data-driven, single-platform).
   - *Falsifier*: cross-platform error stays within a small margin of same-platform error → the data-driven predictors already generalize without PACE-style physics-grounding, and H1/H2's joint head can deploy across robots unmodified; if cross-platform error blows past a usable threshold (R² collapses, or RMSE/MAE multiplies), the predictors are platform-locked and H1/H2's front needs per-platform re-calibration — or a physics-grounded redesign — before it generalizes beyond the platform it was measured on.
5. **H5 — A hard-constraint (Lagrangian/CBF) formulation cannot match the conditional head's front coverage without paying N× the training cost.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2602.06445|ECO]]-style PPO-Lagrangian and [[2508.07611|End-to-End]]-style CBF-CMDP formulations each enforce one fixed cost ceiling ε per training run (a single Lagrange-multiplier target or CBF bound), so covering N distinct points on the thermal-acoustic-force front costs N separate constrained-RL runs — the same N× retraining cost the conditional head (H2) is built to avoid — and, because the constraint is a hard inequality rather than a graded threshold input, the resulting gait should also leave less task-SR headroom than the conditional head's own operating point at a matched cost bound.
   - *Test*: extend ECO's Lagrangian-energy and End-to-End's CBF-CMDP formulations to the heat+acoustic+force triple; sweep each to N operating points via N separate training runs (one fixed ε per run); compare total training cost and task-SR at matched cost bounds against H2's single-run conditional-head sweep.
   - *Row*: ECO (PPO-Lagrangian, hard-constraint) + End-to-End (CBF-CMDP) vs QuietPaw-style conditional head (H2).
   - *Falsifier*: a single Lagrangian/CBF run with an online-tuned multiplier reaches comparable three-way front coverage to the conditional head at comparable total training cost → the hard-constraint family is not N×-costlier after all, and H2's advantage narrows to interface convenience, not training-cost amortization.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Cost-regulation can degrade task performance** — a quiet/cool gait may be slower or less agile. → [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] keeps performance via a residual; report the cost-vs-task Pareto front (H1), not a single number.
> - **GRF/thermal models are platform-specific** — R²≈0.99 on one robot may not transfer to a structurally different one. → H4 is the direct test: [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s own held-out-footwear ablation only probes footwear, not platform; treat cost predictors as per-platform-calibrated until H4 re-validates cross-platform, and report the transfer gap against [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s physics-grounded precedent.
> - **Acoustic and thermal metrics are measurement-fragile** — dBA depends on surface/room, peak motor temperature on ambient/duty-cycle/sensor placement. → the three-way front (H1) could be smaller than its own measurement noise floor; the pre-registered repeatability pre-test in H1's *Test* is the gate, and [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]] reports noise per surface (not a single average) as the precedent to follow.
> - **The thermal/latency axis is a gate on the A1+A2+A3 spine, not just a wing** — parkour (A1) and recovery (A3) saturate motors, and three stacked cost estimators plus the base policy compete for the same onboard compute and thermal budget. → Carry the thermal budget as a *composed deployment-latency-plus-heat gate* on every A1/A2/A3 headline SR (the spine is one deployable system or three incompatible artifacts); the acoustic/GRF/force Pareto stays the separate cross-cutting substrate bet.
> - **A dialed-in preference can be jointly infeasible** — a user (or an automated controller) can request max-quiet + max-cool + max-force-limited at once, a point that may not exist on the front at all. → H3 is the go/no-go: confirm the head degrades to the nearest feasible point or a bounded soft-threshold breach, never a true hard-limit violation; do not deploy the head without an explicit infeasibility detector if H3's falsifier fires.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: off-policy-beats-PPO-on-wall-clock is now established — that's background, not the open question here. The live question is whether a *flow* policy's richer action distribution actually earns its cost on multimodal-contact skills, or whether structured conditioning already does the job a plain Gaussian needs.
- **Today's answers (settled background)**: the off-policy win is multiply-proven — [[2307.12983|Parallel-Q-Learning]] (ICML'23) already beat PPO/DDPG/SAC on wall-clock on 5/6 Isaac Gym tasks, [[2605.24975|SAC-Legged-Locomotion]] *closes the PPO gap entirely* across 7 quadruped/humanoid platforms, and [[2505.22642|FastTD3]] solves HumanoidBench <3 hrs beating PPO/SAC/SimbaV2/TDMPC2/DreamerV3. The contrarian premium on "off-policy beats PPO" is gone — this direction's bet builds on top of that settled result, it does not restate it.
- **The opening**: the genuinely open edge is the flow axis, not the iteration axis. [[2602.01156|PolicyFlow]] shows a flow policy prevents mode collapse where Gaussian baselines fail and [[2505.22094|ReinFlow]] shows flow-RL with 82.63% wall-time savings over diffusion-RL — but the "Gaussian under-fits multimodal contact" premise is *threatened*: the in-KH [[2606.04718|CoRe-MoE]] handles multi-terrain gait selection with a plain Gaussian mixture-of-experts, and an unverified report (Multi-Gait / Selective-AMP, 2604.19102, not yet in KH, to verify) claims five distinct gaits including a jump on a real 12-DoF humanoid with a **Gaussian-PPO** policy by switching the adversarial motion prior on/off per gait — so each mode may be *locally* unimodal and a Gaussian could suffice; even setting the unverified paper aside, CoRe-MoE alone shows expressive *conditioning* already reaches multi-gait. So the honest test is *expressive policy vs expressive conditioning*, run at matched conditioning — a clean null (conditioning-matched Gaussian ties flow) is a publishable outcome, not a failure. A separate, lower-stakes opportunity sits beside it: [[2512.01996|Humanoid-Loco-15min]]'s 15-minute loop makes a reward/curriculum search affordable that PPO's hours-long run cannot, and nobody has run the N-fast-vs-one-slow locomotion comparison — but that lever builds on the off-policy-beats-PPO result above and is background motivation for this direction, not its bet (not part of the numbered hypothesis set below).

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A policy's action distribution must be able to represent every mode the optimal control law actually needs. Locomotion's contact structure is multimodal — which foot plants, which gait transition — and a unimodal Gaussian head collapses that structure to its mean regardless of how much data or compute trains it. This is a representational limit, not a sample-efficiency one: no amount of off-policy reuse fixes a policy that cannot express the right distribution shape in the first place. [[2602.01156|PolicyFlow]] demonstrates the failure mode directly — Gaussian-PPO exhibits limited, single-mode goal coverage that its flow head with a Brownian regularizer resolves into near-complete, balanced coverage.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That a unimodal Gaussian action head is sufficient for legged control — an assumption the entire off-policy locomotion literature holds by construction: [[2505.22642|FastTD3]], [[2605.24975|SAC-Legged-Locomotion]], and [[2307.12983|Parallel-Q-Learning]] all beat PPO on wall-clock with a plain Gaussian head, none questioning whether the head itself under-fits. (Off-policy's wall-clock win over PPO is separately settled by those same papers — that is background here, not what's challenged.) [[2602.01156|PolicyFlow]]'s anti-mode-collapse result and [[2602.02481|FPO++]]'s sim-to-real flow gait bet the opposite — that multimodal contact needs an expressive head. But the premise is itself threatened: [[2606.04718|CoRe-MoE]] already handles multi-terrain gait selection with a plain Gaussian mixture-of-experts, showing structured *conditioning* may make each mode locally unimodal, so a Gaussian could suffice with the right conditioning — the live question is expressive policy vs expressive conditioning.
- **The bet** *(qualitative — no legged-control flow-vs-Gaussian numeric threshold exists in the cited corpus; the falsifier below is threshold-free by design)*: A flow policy ([[2602.02481|FPO++]]/[[2602.01156|PolicyFlow]]-style) beats a Gaussian off-policy learner ([[2605.24975|SAC-Legged-Locomotion]]-style) given *matched mode/phase conditioning* on gait quality and sim-to-real SR specifically on multimodal-contact skills (agile transitions, motion tracking), and ties on smooth walking where a single mode already suffices — the honest comparison is expressive *policy* vs expressive *conditioning*, since the latter (per-gait AMP à la Multi-Gait 2604.19102 not yet in KH, MoE à la [[2606.04718|CoRe-MoE]]) already handles multi-gait humanoids, so a clean null (conditioning-matched Gaussian ties flow) is a publishable, commit-worthy outcome. Falsifiable: if flow ties a conditioning-matched Gaussian on multimodal-contact tasks at matched compute, the richer distribution adds nothing structure does not — the contribution then routes entirely to the background off-policy-efficiency result, which was never this direction's claim.

### A6 — Regime-Contingent MPC-Learning Coupling for Legged Control

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills |
| **Thesis** | The MPC+learning consensus has crystallized into ~4 distinct coupling types, but every paper validates *its own* coupling against pure-RL or pure-MPC at its own training-compute budget, never against the *other* couplings at *matched onboard latency* — so the field reads a headline margin as an architectural win when part of it may be a compute-budget artifact. Run the head-to-head: hold the platform, task, and *latency budget* fixed; vary only the coupling type across the same standardized MPC-vs-RL legged harness; and ask which margins survive matched-latency re-scoring. The contrarian commit: the field's most-cited coupling number shrinks toward parity with a cheaper coupling once latency is normalized on a well-modelled regime, and only re-emerges as real in a narrower, specific regime most readers of that paper would not predict from its headline number alone. The bet is in First-principles below. |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: every hybrid-control paper validates *its own* coupling against the same two baselines — pure end-to-end RL and pure model-based MPC — and reports a win, but no paper holds platform, task, and compute fixed and swaps *only the coupling type* against the *other* hybrids, so the field has four "we beat the baselines" results and zero "coupling X beats coupling Y here, Y beats X there." [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]] already shows the two endpoints split the axes (RL wins energy + x-disturbance, MPC wins lateral stability + y-disturbance, neither dominates), which is exactly the structural pattern that makes a coupling-level bake-off worth running rather than assuming one architecture is best.
- **Today's answers**: the four couplings are mature and each beats the endpoints on its chosen axis — [[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]] offloads contact scheduling to a high-level RL agent so the low-level MPC stays tractable (4–10 M steps, zero-shot Centauro), [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]] runs 100 Hz MPC *inside* the RL loop and learns torque residuals over it (+78% velocity range), [[2601.12169|Legged-Neural-MPC]] and [[2603.14333|Physics-Embedded-DDPC]] make the dynamics model itself learned-and-differentiable (2–50× lower cost; 4× faster LNN inference), and [[2510.20706|Gait-Adapt-MPC-RL]] puts a learned Dreamer model *inside* MPPI's sampler (36.48% lower CoT). [[2604.08036|PriPG-RL]] is the teacher-coupling endpoint — MPC as an anytime-feasible privileged planner distilled into a deployed reactive RL policy (100% SR where vanilla SAC/PPO fail). All five win against pure-RL/pure-MPC; none is compared against the *other four*. Two newer systems corroborate the compute-confound argument from *outside* this taxonomy at the *training-time* layer specifically, without settling A6's own *onboard-latency* question: [[2606.05687|MPC-Guided]]'s own training-time ablation finds a short horizon (N=10, the shortest tested) and an intermediate update rate (10 Hz, not the fastest 50 Hz) train best — but its MPC module shapes the reward only during training and is entirely absent at deployment, so the "latency" it engineers around (πnMPC's batched-solver throughput) is training-time solver cost, not the onboard inference latency this direction's bake-off matches; and [[2509.18466|RL-augmented]] separately builds its own GPU-batched CusADi solver as a preliminary >20×-faster accelerator over the CPU-based solve that already produces its headline 86%/74% numbers — both treat *training-time* solver compute as load-bearing enough to engineer around, which is suggestive but distinct from whether either coupling's headline margin survives matched-*onboard*-latency re-scoring, which is exactly the open question this direction poses.
- **The opening**: the unclaimed result is the coupling-by-regime matrix — which of the four couplings wins on contact-rich vs contact-sparse terrain, full-state vs partially-observed, and short vs long horizon, with pure-RL and pure-MPC as the floor in every cell. The sharpest instance is already sitting in the corpus: [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]]'s own headline **+78%** trackable-velocity-range margin is reported by running a 100 Hz GPU-parallel MPC *inside* the RL loop against a baseline that never spends the same onboard-latency budget — nobody has re-scored that number at matched latency to see how much survives. [[2510.15626|Online-Learning-MPC]]'s sublinear-dynamic-regret guarantee hints the broader answer is regime-contingent too (its residual-learning coupling shines precisely under payload/friction shift, an observability regime), and the corpus already runs this exact "compare-across-types-nobody-benchmarked-together" move for cross-embodiment invariants and world-model encoders — A6 ports it to MPC↔learning coupling.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: An MPC controller and a learned policy each have a structural strength the other lacks — MPC enforces a dynamics model and hard constraints (friction cone, joint limits, contact timing) by construction, so it is reliable exactly where the model is right and the horizon is short; a learned policy compiles experience into a reactive map, so it is fast and robust exactly where the model is wrong or the state is unobserved. These strengths are *regime-indexed*: which one matters depends on contact-richness (how discrete the dynamics are), observability (whether the privileged state $z$ is recoverable), and horizon (how far the model stays accurate). A coupling is just a *choice of where the learned component enters the MPC stack* — high-level scheduler, low-level residual, the dynamics surrogate, the in-loop sampler, or an offline teacher — and there is no a-priori reason one entry point is optimal across all regimes.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That a coupling's reported margin over the endpoint baselines is an *architectural* result, comparable across couplings, because each paper matches training compute or wall-clock to its own chosen baseline. It is not comparable, and the sharpest example is already in this table: [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]] runs a 100 Hz GPU-parallel MPC *inside* the RL control loop and reports its headline **+78%** trackable-velocity-range margin against a lighter, MPC-out-of-the-loop pure-RL baseline — the paper never re-scores its endpoint at its own onboard-latency budget, so readers (and the four other coupling papers, which each make the identical move against their own chosen baseline: [[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]] (RL scheduler), [[2601.12169|Legged-Neural-MPC]] (differentiable surrogate, with [[2510.15626|Online-Learning-MPC]] and [[2603.14333|Physics-Embedded-DDPC]] as siblings), [[2510.20706|Gait-Adapt-MPC-RL]] (in-loop sampler), [[2604.08036|PriPG-RL]] (MPC teacher)) treat the reported margin as a fact about the architecture rather than a fact about spending extra inference-time compute. [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]]'s own finding (the endpoints split the axes, neither dominates) is direct evidence that integration benefits are regime-split, not monotone — which already implies margins are not universal, but does not by itself say whether any *specific* published margin is real or a latency artifact. A6 bets the opposite of "my coupling is best because it beat the baselines": that once every coupling is re-scored at *matched onboard latency* — not matched training compute — the field's most-cited number partially dissolves on a well-modelled regime, and the residual coupling's real, latency-fair advantage survives only in a narrower regime (contact-rich or actively disturbed terrain) that the paper's own headline framing does not name.
- **The bet**: On [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]]'s standardized Go-class legged harness *extended with the four coupling arms* (RL-scheduler / RL-residual / differentiable-surrogate / in-loop-sampler) plus the two endpoints (pure-PPO, pure-MPPI) and the MPC-teacher distillation arm, evaluated across a 3-axis regime grid (contact-richness × observability × horizon) at *matched onboard inference latency, not matched training compute*: [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]]'s own reported margin (**+78%** trackable velocity range) shrinks to **within 2 pp** SR parity with the cheapest coupling (RL-scheduler or MPC-teacher) on a well-modelled, contact-sparse cell, **AND** the same residual coupling's margin re-emerges at **≥10 pp** specifically on contact-rich / actively-disturbed cells — a *regime-flip*, not a uniform "no coupling wins everywhere," and a specific, falsifiable prediction that contradicts what a reader of Residual-MPC's paper alone would expect. More broadly, no single coupling wins by more than 5 pp across *all* regime cells. Falsifiable three ways: if the +78% margin survives matched-latency normalization everywhere (not just contact-rich cells), the number was architectural all along and this direction's central claim is wrong; if the margin never re-emerges anywhere even under contact-rich/disturbed regimes, the residual architecture adds nothing beyond its compute cost, not just its headline framing; and if one coupling wins every cell by >5 pp on the broader grid, coupling is not regime-contingent and a universal best exists. The win, if A6 is right, is the *coupling-selection map* (which entry point to use given measured contact-richness/observability/horizon, and which headline margins survive matched-latency scrutiny), not a new coupling.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (the winning coupling is regime-contingent, and at least one regime makes a pure method tie the best hybrid).
1. **H1 — No single coupling wins by >5 pp across all regime cells (the regime-contingency core).**
   - *Prediction*: across the 3-axis regime grid (contact-richness × observability × horizon), the best coupling *changes cell to cell* — the per-cell winner among {RL-scheduler, RL-residual, differentiable-surrogate, in-loop-sampler, MPC-teacher} is not the same coupling everywhere, and no coupling's mean SR margin over the second-best exceeds 5 pp in *every* cell.
   - *Test*: extend [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]]'s Go-class harness with the five coupling arms + two endpoints; run the full regime grid at matched wall-clock; report a coupling × regime SR matrix with per-cell winners.
   - *Row*: RL-Augmented (scheduler) / Residual MPC (residual) / Legged Neural-MPC (surrogate) / Gait-Adapt MPC-RL (sampler) / PriPG-RL (teacher), all on the MPC-vs-RL Legged harness.
   - *Falsifier*: one coupling wins every cell by >5 pp → coupling is not regime-contingent, a universal best exists, and the contrarian commit is refuted (publishable either way).
2. **H2 — In at least one wide regime, a pure method ties the best hybrid (integration is not always justified).**
   - *Prediction*: on a wide, well-modelled regime (contact-sparse, full-state, short-horizon — flat/mild terrain with clean proprioception), pure-MPC or pure-RL from [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]] ties the best hybrid within 2 pp SR, so the integration overhead (extra nets, in-loop MPC latency) buys nothing there.
   - *Test*: isolate the contact-sparse × full-state × short-horizon cell; compare the best hybrid's SR to the better endpoint at matched onboard inference latency.
   - *Row*: MPC-vs-RL Legged (pure endpoints) vs the best-in-cell hybrid.
   - *Falsifier*: every hybrid beats both endpoints by >2 pp in every cell → integration is always worth it, and the "ties a pure method somewhere" half is wrong.
3. **H3 — Observability shift selects the online-adaptive-surrogate and teacher couplings.**
   - *Prediction*: under payload/friction shift and partial observability (the unobserved $z$ regime), [[2510.15626|Online-Learning-MPC]]'s online residual surrogate (sublinear-regret) and [[2604.08036|PriPG-RL]]'s MPC-teacher-distilled reactive policy win the cell, while the differentiable-*offline*-surrogate and pure-MPC degrade fastest because their model is fixed.
   - *Test*: sweep payload (0→12g-equivalent) × friction on the harness; rank couplings by SR-retention per observability level.
   - *Row*: Online-Learning MPC (online surrogate) + PriPG-RL (teacher) vs Legged Neural-MPC (offline surrogate) + MPC-vs-RL Legged (pure-MPC).
   - *Falsifier*: the offline surrogate or pure-MPC retains SR as well as the online/teacher couplings under shift → observability does not select the adaptive couplings.
4. **H4 — Horizon length selects the in-loop-sampler vs RL-scheduler couplings.**
   - *Prediction*: as the planning horizon lengthens, [[2510.20706|Gait-Adapt-MPC-RL]]'s in-loop learned-model sampler degrades (compounding model error over the horizon) while [[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]]'s RL-scheduler-over-short-MPC holds, so the per-cell winner flips with horizon — a coupling × horizon non-monotone, not a fixed ranking.
   - *Test*: sweep MPC/MPPI horizon length at fixed control rate; measure SR and long-horizon prediction error per coupling.
   - *Row*: Gait-Adapt MPC-RL (in-loop sampler) vs RL-Augmented (RL-scheduler-over-short-MPC).
   - *Falsifier*: one of the two wins at every horizon → horizon does not select the coupling, the ranking is horizon-invariant.
5. **H5 — Matched-latency normalization erases the residual coupling's headline edge on cheap regimes, and it re-emerges only on contact-rich/disturbed ones - and that re-emergence is a tail-*quality* effect (an optimality-gap spike under Residual-MPC's fixed iteration budget, not a solve-time spike) the mean hides (the direction's primary, headline claim, restated as a falsifiable test).**
   - *Prediction*: [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]]'s +78%-velocity win is partly an *extra-compute* win (100 Hz MPC inside the RL loop); when SR is re-baselined on matched onboard inference latency against the cheaper couplings (RL-scheduler, MPC-teacher) on a well-modelled regime, its margin shrinks below 2 pp, while its edge survives only on contact-rich/disturbed cells where the residual earns its latency. Neither anchor paper reports latency variance - [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]] and [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]] both give only point/mean solve-time - and Residual-MPC's own solver design rules out a time-based tail: it runs OSQP's ADMM for a hard-fixed 25 iterations every control step, on every parallel environment, by explicit real-time-iteration design ("strictly limiting the number of subproblems solved, directly trading optimality for responsiveness"), so wall-clock solve time is close to deterministic by construction and a p99/mean *time* ratio near 1.0 is the expected, uninteresting result - not evidence the coupling's edge is architectural. What the fixed budget cannot fix is solution *quality*: 25 ADMM iterations converges well when the warm-start is close (contact-sparse, undisturbed cells) and leaves a larger unconverged primal-dual residual when a sudden disturbance or contact-schedule change moves the true optimum far from the prior step's warm-start - and Residual-MPC's own analysis already shows the mechanism this predicts: the residual torque ratio spikes and *opposes* the MPC output specifically "near contact switches." So the tail claim moves from time to quality: Residual-MPC's 99th-percentile-to-mean ratio of the QP's primal-dual residual (optimality gap) at the fixed 25-iteration cutoff should exceed **2×** specifically in the contact-rich/actively-disturbed cells, against **<1.2×** for the RL-scheduler ([[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]]) and pure-RL ([[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]]) arms, whose forward pass has no analogous convergence gap by construction.
   - *Test*: report every coupling's SR at a *fixed* onboard wall-clock budget (not training compute); compare Residual MPC's per-cell margin under matched vs unmatched latency. Because Residual-MPC's fixed-iteration solver makes wall-clock time close to constant by design, log the QP's per-step primal-dual residual (optimality gap) at the 25-iteration cutoff - not solve time - as the tail-effect signal, reporting its p50/p95/p99 distribution per regime cell; separately confirm solve time itself stays flat across cells as a sanity check on the fixed-budget premise, so a mean-normalized SR ranking and a tail-normalized (p99 optimality-gap) ranking can be compared cell by cell.
   - *Row*: Residual MPC (residual, in-loop MPC) vs RL-Augmented (cheap scheduler) + PriPG-RL (deploy-time MPC-free).
   - *Falsifier*: the residual coupling keeps its margin at matched latency across all cells → its edge is architectural, not a compute artifact, and latency normalization does not change the map. Separately, on the tail-quality mechanism specifically: if the QP's optimality-gap p99/mean ratio does not exceed **2×** in contact-rich/disturbed cells and instead stays **<1.2×**, flat like the RL arms' (which have no comparable convergence gap) → the fixed-budget solver is not degrading unevenly across regimes, the proposed mechanism for *why* the residual coupling's edge re-emerges specifically there does not hold, and any observed re-emergence needs a different explanation.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Re-implementing four couplings faithfully is a large engineering surface, and a weak arm fakes regime-contingency** — an under-tuned coupling will lose cells for implementation reasons, not architectural ones, manufacturing a false "regime-contingent" result. → Reproduce each coupling against its *own* paper's reported number on its *own* platform first (RL-Augmented's CoT ~0.12, Residual MPC's +78%, Legged Neural-MPC's 5/5) as an arm-validity gate *before* the cross-coupling grid; report each arm's reproduction gap.
> - **The endpoints and couplings do not share a platform** — [[2510.12717|Residual-MPC]] is MIT Humanoid, [[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]] is Centauro, the surrogate rows are Go1/Go2, the harness is Go1 — so a naive merge confounds coupling with platform. → Port every arm onto [[2501.16590|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]]'s single Go-class platform and metric contract; treat any result that requires a different platform as out-of-scope, not as evidence.
> - **Matched compute is ambiguous between training and inference** — a coupling can win on training-sample efficiency yet lose on onboard latency (in-loop MPC eats the control budget). → Fix *both* a training wall-clock budget and an onboard inference-latency budget (couples to A4's thermal/latency budget and A5's inference-latency discipline); report SR under each separately, never a single conflated "compute."
> - **The contrarian bet can be unfalsifiable if the grid is too coarse** — too few regime cells and "no coupling wins by >5 pp everywhere" is trivially true or trivially false. → Pre-register the 3-axis grid resolution and the 5 pp / 2 pp thresholds before running; a coupling that wins by >5 pp on a *pre-registered* full grid is the clean refutation, and that outcome must be as publishable as the contrarian one.

### A7 — Persistent Actuator-Health State Across Episode Resets (Cross-Episode Wear-Leveling)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | A — Bipedal Locomotion & Dynamic Skills |
| **Thesis** | Every RL/MPC training loop in this doc's corpus treats episode reset as a physical reset of the robot's body - peak torque, thermal load, and contact-force spikes are discarded, and episode N+1 starts from an idealized, undamaged actuator. Real hardware does not reset: winding fatigue, bearing wear, and thermal cycling accumulate across the robot's service life regardless of where the simulator draws the episode boundary. The bet is in First-principles below. |
| **Anchor papers** | [[2604.23702\|QuietWalk]] (method), [[2605.27046\|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] (method), [[2603.01631\|Thermal-Aware-Locomotion]] (method), [[2606.25965\|Fault-Tolerant-MoE]] (method), [[2312.17606\|Control-Strategy-for]] (method), [[2409.17992\|LoopSR]] (method), [[2403.10506\|HumanoidBench]] (benchmark), [[2603.20147\|AGILE]] (benchmark) |
| **Key targets** | [[2604.23702\|QuietWalk]] 7.17 dBA mean noise / R²=0.9887-0.9899 GRF predictor (a single-rollout peak, not a cycle count); [[2605.27046\|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] overheating 70%→<10%, <50 °C over 650 m (a per-deployment bound); [[2603.01631\|Thermal-Aware-Locomotion]] 7→27 min thermal-safe runtime, 4× baseline (a single-session ceiling); [[2606.25965\|Fault-Tolerant-MoE]] a 32×32×32 MoE matches a 128×128×128 monolith post-fault (reactive, not proactive) - no existing number in this corpus for cumulative damage or cycles-to-failure; that metric is this direction's own gap, not a target it inherits |

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: every simulator backing this doc's corpus - IsaacGym, IsaacLab, MuJoCo Playground, Genesis - implements the episode boundary as a full physical reset by default, and every cited RL/MPC training loop inherits that default without arguing for it; it comes from the MDP formalism, not from any paper's own design choice. Whatever a policy did to its joints in episode N is discarded at reset, so episode N+1 starts undamaged.
- **Today's answers**: [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]], [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]], and [[2603.01631|Thermal-Aware-Locomotion]] all instrument real embodiment cost - GRF, footstep noise, motor temperature - and QuietWalk's own Problem section names "accelerated hardware wear" as its motivation. But every one of these costs is charged *within* one deployment: Thermal-Aware-Residual's number is peak temperature over one 650 m walk (<50 °C), not cumulative thermal-cycling stress across a service life; QuietWalk's dBA reduction is per-rollout. [[2606.25965|Fault-Tolerant-MoE]] goes further and routes around an already-failed actuator, but only *after* a fault is diagnosed - reactive compensation, not proactive redistribution that slows the failure from happening. None of these carries a damage state across episode or mission boundaries.
- **The opening**: two mature, decades-old fields have never been bridged. Health-aware control / prognostics-and-health-management (PHM) already has the mechanism this direction needs - the PHM Society's *RUL-aware RRT\** (Degradation-Balanced Motion Planning for Robotic Manipulators, PHM Society European Conference; no arXiv ID) redistributes motion away from a weak joint via adaptive joint-weighting, keeping degradation balanced across the arm. But it is a classical sampling-based planner on a fixed-base industrial arm, evaluated once per trajectory, and never confronts an episode-reset MDP because there isn't one. A direct search for "health-aware" or "RUL-aware" combined with legged/quadruped/humanoid gait control returns nothing. The unclaimed cell: fold a persistent, cross-episode damage state into a legged RL policy the way RUL-aware RRT* folds one into a planner.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A robot's actuators do not know when an episode ends. Winding fatigue, bearing wear, and thermal cycling are cumulative physical processes running on the robot's own clock, indifferent to the simulator's episode boundary. This doc's own Formal Framing already names *actuator state* as one component of the privileged context $z$ (alongside terrain friction $\mu$, ground height $h$, and payload $m$) - every RMA/HIM/TERT-style adaptation module already regresses friction, payload, and terrain from proprioceptive history, and nothing in that formalism stops $z$ from also carrying a damage scalar the same history could regress. The gap is not conceptual; it is that every cited instantiation of $z$ restricts it to externally-caused, episode-scoped quantities, never a policy-caused, cross-episode one.
- **Assumption being challenged**: that the episode-reset-as-physical-reset default is harmless because embodiment cost is already handled - the position implicit in every cited A4 method and in every simulator (IsaacGym/IsaacLab/MuJoCo Playground/Genesis) that ships this default unquestioned. Two papers get partway to challenging it themselves, and neither closes it. [[2312.17606|Control-Strategy-for]] trains against actuator degradation, but the degradation is a *fixed*, externally-imposed scenario chosen before training starts - a scripted joint failure, not a state the policy's own actions accumulate or could have slowed. SATA (arXiv 2502.12674, not yet in `_KnowledgeHub_`) comes closer still: it carries a torque-integral fatigue state $\zeta_t = (\zeta_{t-1} + |\tau| \cdot dt) \cdot \beta$ that explicitly discourages "over-reliance on two-/three-legged motion patterns" during exploration - a real accumulating, load-redistributing state. But the paper documents this as an exploration regularizer for training stability, not a hardware-wear model; it explicitly randomizes the fatigue level at each reset ("varying levels of motor fatigue already applied... random initialization enhances the generalization capability") rather than carrying it over from the prior rollout - the opposite of persistence; it grounds the decay in a bio-inspired heuristic rather than fatigue physics (rainflow-counted cycling or Miner's-rule accumulation); and it reports no cross-deployment persistence protocol and no cycles-to-failure number. So load-redistribution-from-an-accumulating-state is not entirely unclaimed - SATA gets partway there - but a physically-grounded, deployment-persistent damage state that a wear-leveling policy is trained against is.
- **The bet**: A policy carrying a persistent, cross-episode actuator-health accumulator - grounded in fatigue physics (rainflow-counted thermal-cycling amplitude and count, Miner's-rule-style accumulation, the same machinery Prolonging Tool Life (arXiv 2507.17275, not yet in `_KnowledgeHub_`; held-tool scope, [[Manipulation|Manipulation]]) validates against FEA for a held tool) rather than SATA's bio-inspired decay - and trained to redistribute load away from the most-damaged joint, extends simulated cycles-to-failure by ≥15% over a matched policy trained only on today's within-episode costs ([[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]/[[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]-style), at ≤3% task-SR cost. That bet is conditional on a prior, decisive check: whether today's within-episode peak metrics already correlate with cumulative damage. If they do (Pearson ρ≥0.5 between a peak metric and the rainflow-counted damage accumulator), this direction collapses into A4, re-tuned with a longer-horizon reward - not a new axis. There is a concrete physical reason the correlation could be weak instead: winding fatigue tracks thermal-cycling *amplitude and count*, not peak temperature, and a GRF-minimizing gait can raise step frequency, trading fewer high-impact cycles for more low-impact ones - a gait that is cooler and quieter today by [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]/[[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s own metrics could plausibly accumulate *more* cumulative damage, not less. Nobody in this corpus has run that correlation; it is currently unknown, not settled either way. Falsifiable: if ρ≥0.5, the peak-metric proxies already track cumulative damage well enough and no persistent state is needed; if the wear-leveling variant's cycles-to-failure gain is <5% or its task-SR cost exceeds 8%, the persistent-state machinery is not worth the engineering cost of plumbing it through every simulator's episode reset.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (a persistent, physically-grounded damage state enables proactive wear-leveling that today's within-episode cost proxies cannot, and this is only worth building if those proxies do not already track cumulative damage).
1. **H1 — Within-episode cost proxies do not predict cumulative damage, and a single cyclic accumulator is the wrong instrument for insulation-dominated wear (the decisive falsifier, run first).**
   - *Prediction*: peak temperature ([[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s minimized quantity) and peak GRF ([[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s minimized quantity) correlate weakly with a rainflow-counted, Miner's-rule-style cyclic damage accumulator (Pearson ρ<0.5), because minimizing peak GRF can raise step frequency - trading fewer high-impact cycles for more low-impact ones - and minimizing peak temperature does not bound cycling amplitude or count. But rainflow/Miner's is the right tool only for bearing wear and mechanical fatigue - genuinely cyclic-amplitude processes - not for winding-insulation life, the failure mode that dominates near thermal ceiling: insulation aging follows Arrhenius chemical kinetics, a sustained temperature-and-duration integral (the "10 °C rise halves insulation life" rule), not a cycle count. So H1 regresses peak metrics against *two* damage accumulators, not one: the existing cyclic accumulator (rainflow/Miner's, bearings/mechanical fatigue) and a sustained-exposure accumulator $\int \exp(-E_a / k T(t))\,dt$ (the Arrhenius reaction-rate integral, windings/insulation). The two should disagree on gait ranking: an A4-style thermal-residual gait tuned to minimize discrete high-amplitude cycles ([[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]) is predicted to accumulate a *higher* Arrhenius-integrated dose than an unregulated baseline with shorter, higher-amplitude spikes - sustained moderate-high exposure integrates more reacted fraction than brief peaks do whenever the regulated gait's time-weighted dwell at moderate-high temperature is long enough to outweigh the baseline's brief spike (the expected case for a policy that paces itself to stay under a thermal ceiling, per the same Montsinger ~10 °C-halves-life scaling; a sufficiently long or extreme spike could still dominate instead, which is exactly what the test below checks rather than assumes) - even though the cyclic accumulator scores the regulated gait as safer.
   - *Test*: run QuietWalk's and Thermal-Aware-Residual's trained policies over a long, multi-episode continuous duty cycle (or a simulated service-life horizon); compute the within-episode peak metrics, the rainflow-counted cyclic damage accumulator (the Miner's-rule machinery Prolonging Tool Life validates against FEA for a held tool), AND the Arrhenius sustained-exposure integral over the same temperature trace; regress peak metrics against both accumulators, and separately rank the Thermal-Aware-Residual gait against an unregulated baseline gait under each accumulator.
   - *Row*: QuietWalk / Thermal-Aware-Residual (peak-metric rows) vs. the rainflow-counted cyclic accumulator AND the Arrhenius sustained-exposure accumulator this hypothesis defines.
   - *Falsifier*: two-part. (1) ρ≥0.5 against the cyclic accumulator → within-episode peak-metric optimization already tracks cyclic damage well enough; this direction collapses into A4, re-tuned with a longer-horizon reward, not a new axis - the honest kill condition, unchanged. (2) with only two gaits being ranked, "agree vs. disagree" reduces to a sign test: compute the dose difference (Thermal-Aware-Residual minus the unregulated baseline) under each accumulator separately. If both differences exceed 2σ of their own repeated-seed noise floor and carry the *same* sign → the accumulators agree, the single-accumulator simplification is fine as-is, and A7 does not need a second damage model. If both differences exceed their own 2σ floor but carry *opposite* signs (the regulated gait scores safer under one accumulator and riskier under the other) → the accumulators disagree, and A7's current single-accumulator falsifier chain has a real blind spot for insulation-dominated failure modes. If either difference falls inside its own 2σ floor, the run is underpowered to call agreement or disagreement either way - add seeds before this clause can fire.
2. **H2 — A wear-leveling policy with persistent damage state extends cycles-to-failure over a matched within-episode-cost baseline.**
   - *Prediction*: conditional on H1's falsifier not firing, a policy carrying the H1 damage accumulator across episode resets, trained to redistribute torque/gait timing away from the most-damaged joint, extends simulated cycles-to-failure by ≥15% over a matched policy trained on QuietWalk/Thermal-Aware-Residual-style within-episode costs alone, at ≤3% task-SR cost.
   - *Test*: train both variants on an identical task/terrain distribution; run each to a simulated-failure threshold (the damage accumulator crossing a calibrated per-joint limit) across N seeds; compare cycles/hours-to-failure and task-SR.
   - *Row*: QuietWalk + Thermal-Aware-Residual (within-episode baseline) vs. the wear-leveling variant; RUL-aware RRT*'s adaptive joint-weighting is the non-learned analogue this row's mechanism borrows from.
   - *Falsifier*: cycles-to-failure gain <5% or task-SR cost >8% → wear-leveling's win is too small to justify plumbing a persistent damage channel through every simulator's episode-reset machinery.
3. **H3 — Proactive wear-leveling beats reactive post-fault compensation on cumulative cycles-to-failure.**
   - *Prediction*: a policy conditioned on the continuous H1 damage accumulator (proactive, redistributing load before failure) reaches higher cumulative cycles-to-failure than [[2606.25965|Fault-Tolerant-MoE]]'s one-hot fault-conditioned expert switch (reactive, engaging only after a fault is already diagnosed), at matched nominal-condition task performance.
   - *Test*: extend Fault-Tolerant-MoE's fault-detection module from a binary fault/no-fault signal to the continuous damage accumulator; compare proactive-conditioning against its native reactive one-hot switch on simulated fatigue-to-failure trials.
   - *Row*: [[2606.25965|Fault-Tolerant-MoE]] (reactive) vs. the proactive-conditioning variant this hypothesis defines.
   - *Falsifier*: reactive switching matches proactive wear-leveling on cumulative cycles-to-failure → damage is well-approximated as a step function (nominal→faulty), and smooth proactive redistribution buys nothing over waiting for the fault.
4. **H4 — GRF-minimizing gaits raise contact-cycle count, the specific mechanism that could make H1 fail.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]]'s GRF-minimizing policy, measured over a long rollout, shows a higher mean step frequency than its own unregulated baseline at matched forward velocity - trading fewer high-impact cycles for more low-impact ones - so QuietWalk's own quiet gait accumulates more total ground-contact cycles per km than the baseline it improves on.
   - *Test*: measure step frequency and total ground-contact-cycle count per km for QuietWalk's trained policy against its own reported unregulated baseline, at matched velocity.
   - *Row*: [[2604.23702|QuietWalk]] (GRF-minimized) vs. its own within-paper baseline.
   - *Falsifier*: step frequency and cycle count are unchanged or lower under GRF minimization → the proposed peak-reduction-trades-for-cycle-count-increase mechanism does not hold for QuietWalk specifically, weakening (not disproving) this direction's physical argument for why peak metrics might mislead.
5. **H5 — A persistent damage scalar composes with the existing privileged-context ($z$) formalism without degrading context inference.**
   - *Prediction*: adding a persistent actuator-health scalar to $z$ - alongside [[2107.04034|RMA]]'s friction/payload/terrain - and inferring it via the same proprioceptive-history regression B1's RMA/HIM/TERT-style backbones already use for the other components of $z$, degrades neither the existing context-inference accuracy nor task SR, because damage accumulates on a much slower timescale than the terrain/payload signals those backbones already separate (the same multi-timescale argument B1's H2 makes for payload vs. discontinuous terrain).
   - *Test*: add the damage scalar as a second regression target to an RMA/HIM-style adaptation module; retrain; compare context-inference error and task-SR against the unmodified baseline.
   - *Row*: [[2107.04034|RMA]] (B1's baseline $z$-inference architecture, cross-referenced) vs. the damage-augmented variant.
   - *Falsifier*: adding the damage-state target measurably degrades existing context-inference accuracy or task SR → a persistent health channel is not a "free" addition to the privileged-context formalism, and needs its own dedicated architecture rather than riding along in $z$.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **The correlation might already hold, collapsing this into A4** — H1's own falsifier is this direction's biggest existential risk: if ρ≥0.5, wear-leveling has nothing to add over a longer-horizon within-episode reward. → Run H1 first, cheaply, as a pre-registered gate before investing in H2-H5's training runs; publish the ρ number either way, since a null result here is itself informative for A4.
> - **Every simulator in this doc's corpus needs new infrastructure** — IsaacGym, IsaacLab, MuJoCo Playground, and Genesis all implement episode reset as a full state reset by default; plumbing a persistent, policy-influenced damage channel through that reset is a new realism axis alongside domain randomization, not a reward-function change. → Start with a lightweight surrogate accumulator (rainflow-counted torque/temperature history, updated outside the physics step) rather than a full actuator-FEA model; validate the surrogate's own fidelity before trusting downstream SR numbers.
> - **The damage surrogate's own ground truth is unverified** — without real hardware run to actual failure (expensive, slow, rare), H1-H3 answer a question about a modeled damage quantity, not the real one; a surrogate that doesn't track real winding fatigue validates the wrong physics. → Anchor the surrogate to established fatigue-accumulation machinery (Miner's rule / rainflow counting - the approach Prolonging Tool Life validates against FEA) and report the surrogate's own validation error, not only downstream cycles-to-failure.
> - **Re-scoring existing directions against a service-life cost they currently pay for free** — A2's extreme skills and A1's parkour-class terrain traversal are scored purely on task SR today; once charged against a persistent damage state, some headline numbers could look systematically worse, creating resistance to adopting the new axis. → Report service-life cost as an additional axis alongside existing SR, not a replacement metric, so current results stay comparable while the new axis accumulates evidence.
> - **Measurement fragility compounds over a service-life horizon** — A4 already flags thermal/acoustic/GRF sensing as measurement-fragile within one rollout; a cumulative accumulator built on noisy per-step readings compounds that noise over a full service-life horizon rather than one rollout. → Report the accumulator's own noise floor (via repeated-seed variance) before trusting any single cycles-to-failure number; inherit A4's repeatability pre-test discipline rather than reinventing it.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: every method that filters or retargets a mocap clip onto what the robot can physically do — checked directly against each paper's own methodology — reads a single torque-limit number from the URDF or vendor spec once and treats it as fixed for the lifetime of training and deployment. But a motor's electrical dissipation scales with its winding resistance ($P_{el} \propto \tau^2 R$, the exact relation [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s own PACE energy model uses), and that resistance rises as the winding heats. For a fixed thermal safety margin, this means the *sustained* torque a joint can deliver — not its instantaneous peak, its continuous-duty capacity — shrinks as the actuator's own temperature climbs. The torque limit every cited pipeline projects against is a snapshot at one operating point, not the physical envelope. This is a different axis from A7, not a restatement of it: A7 asks whether the *reward signal* sees cross-episode accumulated damage; A8 asks whether the *imitation-target definition* — $\mathcal F$ itself, which clips are even attempted — accounts for real-time-varying thermal capability within a single session. A7's damage state persists *across* episode resets; A8's temperature-derated bound applies *within* one, before the reset ever happens.
- **Today's answers**: every A2 anchor — [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]] (physics-based filtering against fixed joint/torque/kinematic limits), [[2511.09484|SPIDER]] (constrained-optimization projection at scale, 2.4 M frames / 9 embodiments), [[2409.20514|Opt2Skill]] (whole-body trajectory optimization with explicit joint-and-torque-limit constraints, the cleanest formal statement of $\Pi_{\mathcal F}$), [[2509.15443|Implicit-Kinodynamic-Retargeting]] (dynamics-aware fine-tuning), and [[2605.06593|ReActor]] (bilevel RL retargeting correcting penetration) — computes this projection once against a static bound; none re-identifies the torque limit itself as a function of thermal state. Two papers get close and stop short: [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s PACE framework re-identifies a robot's armature inertia, viscous damping, Coulomb friction, and joint bias from ~20 s of real data, but these are dynamics-*fidelity* parameters calibrated once, offline — never the torque-limit boundary itself, and never re-derated online as temperature changes. [[2604.24916|asRoBallet]] builds a friction-aware simulator for the same reason, better sim-to-real contact matching, without ever touching a torque ceiling. [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] might look like the missing piece, but a direct read of its methodology shows otherwise: its nominal policy carries zero thermal terms by design, and its residual layer only reward-penalizes the motor-temperature *change rate* at runtime — real-time control-layer compensation around a reference the offline projection already fixed, never a redefinition of $\Pi_{\mathcal F}$ or the torque bound it enforces. It is the strongest available data anchor for what the operating envelope actually looks like, not a paper that already claims this direction's bet.
- **The opening**: the two ingredients this direction needs exist, unconnected. [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s PACE supplies the correct functional form — electrical dissipation scales with resistance, and resistance rises with temperature, so a fixed thermal budget buys less sustained torque the hotter the motor already is. [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] supplies the real-hardware scale of the problem: a validated whole-body thermal model, a 60 °C safety ceiling, and measured behavior at 30/50/58 °C initial motor temperature, showing an unregulated policy overheats 70% of the time at the high end. Nobody has combined the two — built a temperature-derated torque bound from PACE's dissipation relation, scaled to Thermal-Aware-Residual's measured envelope, and used it to re-score [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]'s own room-temperature rejection filter. A targeted search for thermal-aware trajectory optimization or temperature-conditioned torque derating applied to humanoid motion imitation returns nothing in this corpus or beyond it — the same "direct search returns nothing" result A7 reports for health-aware legged control.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: a motor's achievable *sustained* torque is bounded by how much heat it can still dissipate before crossing a thermal safety ceiling, and that headroom shrinks continuously as the motor's own winding temperature rises — an electrical fact independent of any control policy, gait, or training regime. [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s own PMSM energy model formalizes the mechanism directly: electrical dissipation $P_{el} \propto \tau^2 R$, so a hotter winding (higher $R$) dissipates more heat for the same torque draw. [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s own measured 70%→<10% overheating swing between an unregulated and a thermally-managed policy is direct real-hardware evidence that this headroom moves by a large margin within the operating range these robots actually reach — its highest tested condition, 58 °C, sits only 2 °C below its own 60 °C reward ceiling, leaving almost no sustained-duty margin left at all.
- **Assumption being challenged**: every A2 anchor method — [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]], [[2511.09484|SPIDER]], [[2409.20514|Opt2Skill]], [[2509.15443|Implicit-Kinodynamic-Retargeting]], [[2605.06593|ReActor]] — computes $\Pi_{\mathcal F}$ once, offline, from a fixed torque bound and treats it as settled for the lifetime of training and deployment. This is inherited from the URDF's own convention (a static kinematic-and-dynamic description), never argued for in any of the five papers checked directly. [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s PACE and [[2604.24916|asRoBallet]] each prove the field already re-calibrates *some* physical parameters against real data — inertia/damping/friction, tribological contact terms — but neither extends that practice to the torque-limit boundary, and both calibrate once, offline, rather than re-deriving the bound online as thermal state changes.
- **The bet**: a temperature-derated torque bound — its functional form taken from [[2509.06342|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s $P_{el} \propto \tau^2 R$ dissipation relation, its operating scale anchored to [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s measured 30/50/58 °C envelope and 60 °C safety ceiling — is this direction's own construction, built from those two ingredients, not an existing curve either paper already reports. Re-projecting [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]'s extreme-skill corpus against it newly rejects at least **10%** of the clips KungfuBot's own room-temperature filter currently accepts, once actuator temperature nears **58 °C**. A policy retrained against this temperature-conditioned manifold holds tracking error near KungfuBot's own **53.25 mm** benchmark through a sustained multi-minute duty cycle at that temperature, while the original static-bound-trained policy shows either measurably worse tracking error or a torque-saturation fault at the same thermal state. Falsifiable: if the additional rejection rate stays under 5 percentage points at 58 °C, or the static-bound-trained policy matches the temperature-conditioned policy's tracking error with no saturation fault, thermal derating is a corner case the static bound already absorbs, not a load-bearing axis.

   - *Row*: Thermal-Aware-Residual (A1-measured envelope) vs. KungfuBot (G1 platform) — the cross-platform-transfer question A4's own H4 raises for cost predictors, now asked of the derating bound.
   - *Falsifier*: the A1-derived bound transfers to G1 within a small margin of the G1-specific bound → a single generic envelope suffices across platforms, and per-platform re-measurement is unneeded engineering overhead.
5. **H5 — Temperature-conditioning $\mathcal F$ composes with A2's adaptive tracking-tolerance schedule rather than competing with it.**
   - *Prediction*: temperature-conditioning *which* clips are attempted (this direction) and [[2506.12851|KungfuBot]]/[[2510.14454|Adaptive-Motion-Tracking]]'s adaptive tolerance schedule for *how tightly* an attempted clip is tracked operate on different axes and compose — a policy with both rejects more clips near 58 °C (H1) while tracking the surviving clips at least as well as the tolerance-schedule-only baseline.
   - *Test*: train four variants (static-$\mathcal F$/fixed-tolerance, static-$\mathcal F$/adaptive-tolerance [KungfuBot], temperature-$\mathcal F$/fixed-tolerance, temperature-$\mathcal F$/adaptive-tolerance); compare tracking error and rejection ratio across the tested thermal range.
   - *Row*: KungfuBot (static-$\mathcal F$ + adaptive tolerance) vs. the temperature-$\mathcal F$ + adaptive-tolerance variant this hypothesis defines.
   - *Falsifier*: adding temperature-conditioning degrades tracking error on the surviving clips relative to KungfuBot's static-$\mathcal F$ baseline → the two axes interact adversely and don't simply compose.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: a quadruped's deployable policy must act on proprioception alone — joint angles, IMU, contact — because the privileged context sim provides (friction $\mu$, payload $m$, ground compliance) is unavailable on hardware, and the field's two escape routes (a camera, or on-robot fine-tuning) both add cost.
- **Today's answers**: proprioceptive context inference is now mature and *backbone-diverse* — [[2107.04034|RMA]] regresses extrinsics with a TCN (12 kg, zero fine-tuning), [[2301.10602|DreamWaQ]] infers a latent context via a Beta-VAE estimator (95.23% survival, 430 m real outdoor), [[2312.11460|HIM]] learns a contrastive internal-model embedding (100% real stairs, 8 kg), and [[2507.07825|LoadAdapt]] adds an explicit Load-Characteristics Estimator (zero-shot 4/6 kg on uneven terrain). The mechanism is settled; what each backbone *trades* is not.
- **The opening**: [[2212.07740|TERT]]'s 60%-vs-0% stair result with the *same* privileged supervision but a Transformer instead of RMA's TCN proves backbone choice — not signal availability — sets discontinuous-terrain context, while [[2507.07825|LoadAdapt]] caps near 8 kg without running the stair contrast. [[2503.20839|TAR]] now exercises *both* axes in one model — 12 kg payload *and* 150 N pushes *and* discontinuous steps, zero-fine-tune on real Go2 — so the conjunction is no longer un-held. But TAR reaches it as a single zero-shot deployment *demo* with a single composite OOD metric, and its backbone comparison swaps only {contrastive-recurrent, MLP, TCN} (no Transformer/Transformer-XL), reporting the contrastive backbone "consistently outperformed all variants" — not a *measurable payload-vs-terrain trade-off curve*. The open cell narrows to the *controlled backbone-swap at identical supervision* that maps the payload×terrain Pareto TAR's single-metric demo does not.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: The privileged context — friction, payload, terrain — cannot be read directly off the hardware, but it shapes how the robot's joints and IMU respond, so it leaves a recoverable fingerprint in the recent proprioceptive history. A supervised module can regress that history into a context estimate, making the privileged state inferable without ever sensing it directly. [[2107.04034|RMA]] demonstrates it: an adaptation module reading proprioceptive history at 10 Hz sustains a 12 kg payload across four terrains, zero fine-tuning. But payload and discontinuous terrain live on different timescales — payload is a near-static, slowly-varying scalar set once per episode, while a stair edge is a fast, step-to-step transient — and a single fixed-size recurrent/hidden-state backbone (TCN, contrastive-recurrent) must compress *both* signals into the same vector, so tracking one can crowd out the other exactly when both must be inferred *jointly*. This is not merely hypothetical: [[2212.07740|TERT]]'s Transformer already beats [[2107.04034|RMA]]'s TCN on the terrain axis alone (60% vs 0% stairs) under *identical* privileged supervision — an attention backbone keeps the window indexable rather than collapsing it into one state, so it can allocate separate capacity to a slow and a fast signal instead of forcing them to share one.
- **Assumption being challenged**: *Not* that proprioceptive inference works at all — [[2301.10602|DreamWaQ]], [[2312.11460|HIM]], and [[2507.07825|LoadAdapt]] settled that, none needing vision or fine-tuning; *nor* that the payload-and-terrain conjunction is unreachable — [[2503.20839|TAR]] already holds 12 kg *and* discontinuous steps zero-fine-tune in one model. The challenged assumption is narrower, and it has a specific holder: that TAR's own within-corpus finding — its contrastive-recurrent backbone "consistently outperformed all variants" (MLP, TCN) — generalizes to the *joint, multi-timescale* stratum this direction targets. It should not, on the paper's own design: TAR's backbone comparison never included an attention/Transformer arm, and its analysis of what each factor contributes (t-SNE over friction/force/mass) is run *in isolation*, one factor at a time — never at the simultaneous 80%-payload-and-stairs stratum where a single recurrent hidden state has to represent a slow and a fast signal at once. [[2212.07740|TERT]]'s Transformer-over-TCN win on the terrain axis alone (60% vs 0%, identical supervision to RMA) is the one piece of in-corpus evidence that *does* isolate a backbone-only swap, and it favors attention over a weaker recurrent-style backbone, not the reverse. So the two pieces of within-corpus evidence do not actually conflict: TAR shows contrastive-recurrence beats weaker backbones (MLP, TCN) on the conjunction under isolated-factor analysis; TERT shows attention beats TCN specifically on the terrain axis under a joint-supervision swap. Neither has run attention against contrastive-recurrence at the joint stratum — that comparison is what H2 must supply, and the first-principles argument above, not TAR's isolated-factor result, is what predicts which way it goes.
- **The bet**: Under a controlled backbone swap (TCN / contrastive-IMC / Transformer / Transformer-XL) at *identical* privileged supervision and on a *stratified* payload(0→80% body weight) × discontinuous-terrain grid — not a single composite OOD metric, a zero-shot demo like [[2503.20839|TAR]]'s, or an isolated-factor sweep like TAR's t-SNE analysis — there is a measurable payload-vs-terrain *trade-off curve*: a backbone tuned for [[2107.04034|RMA]]'s 12 kg payload pays on [[2312.11460|HIM]]/[[2212.07740|TERT]]'s stairs (≥60% where TCN scores 0%) and vice versa, and specifically *at the joint 80%-payload/stairs stratum* — where a single fixed-size hidden state must carry both a slow and a fast signal at once — an attention/long-context backbone with allocated capacity beats the contrastive-recurrent backbone by ≥5 pp joint SR, extending [[2212.07740|TERT]]'s terrain-only Transformer-over-TCN win (60% vs 0%) to the harder joint condition rather than contradicting [[2503.20839|TAR]]'s isolated-factor result. TAR's contrastive-recurrent backbone holds *both* axes in one zero-shot demo and beats MLP/TCN on it, but never ran the swap against Transformer/Transformer-XL nor isolated the joint-stratum interference the first-principles argument predicts — so its "consistently outperformed" finding is evidence against MLP/TCN, not evidence against attention. Falsifiable: if a stratified controlled swap finds contrastive-recurrence matches or beats attention even at the joint 80%-payload/stairs stratum, the multi-timescale-interference argument is wrong and TAR's backbone generalizes as-is — attention buys nothing over contrastive-recurrence anywhere; if an exteroceptive policy beats proprioceptive inference on *dynamics* context (friction/payload, not geometry), proprioception alone is insufficient.

### B2 — World-Model Dreaming for Few-Shot Real-World Adaptation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cluster** | B — Quadruped Locomotion & Real-World Adaptation |
| **Thesis** | Before committing to dreaming-as-core, *first establish that the latent uncertainty is valid* — then adapt a sim-pretrained *learned latent* world model with a handful of real trajectories *by dreaming* rather than reconstructing a digital-twin sim and retraining in it. The reason it must work: a learned dynamics model lets the policy *imagine* action consequences, so each real interaction updates a model that generates thousands of synthetic ones, and an epistemic bound is *supposed* to keep the imagination honest. But that "epistemic bound keeps imagination honest" premise cannot be assumed: an epistemic estimate calibrated in sim can decorrelate from true held-out error under sim-to-real dynamics drift, so the calibration must be *measured under drift* before it is trusted — a check that is good practice on the RSSM/Dreamer family regardless, and Berger et al.'s [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] (RLC 2026) gives it teeth: on DMC Suite tasks (off-legged, OOD-initial-state shift rather than sim-to-real specifically), RSSM ensemble-disagreement uncertainty reconverges to in-distribution levels via "attractor" rollouts even as true rollout error keeps growing — an architecture-level finding on the RSSM/Dreamer family [[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] and [[2502.11377|PrivilegedDreamer]] are directly built on ([[2504.16680|RWM-U]] instead descends from a different, GRU-autoregressive backbone, so whether its own epistemic penalty shares this failure mode is untested, not established — a reason H6 checks the specific bound in use rather than assuming architecture-wide transfer). Anti-DR few-shot adaptation is consensus; what is unsettled is whether latent *dreaming* beats *reconstructed-sim retraining* at a matched real-data budget, and whether the bound is even valid. **Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: deep RL needs millions of interactions — impractical on hardware — and the field's two answers (exhaustive domain randomization, extensive on-robot RL) are both inefficient ways to close the sim-to-real dynamics gap.
- **Today's answers**: anti-DR few-shot adaptation is now a crowded field with *two distinct mechanisms* — the *reconstruct-then-retrain* line ([[2409.17992|LoopSR]] rebuilds a digital twin from <5 min real data and retrains the policy: 100% Stair-Up vs 70%; [[2604.11090|Simulator-Adaptation-Loco]] identifies sim parameters via Wasserstein matching, cutting drift 80% from <5 min data) and the *latent-dreaming* line ([[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] adapts a task-invariant latent: 100% vs 10% on a 52 cm climb from ~5 trajectories; [[2206.14176|DayDreamer]] walks in 1 hr). Both beat DR at a tiny budget — but nobody has run them head-to-head.
- **The opening**: the surviving wedge is *which* mechanism wins at a matched budget, plus a bound none of them carries — [[2504.16680|RWM-U]] supplies the missing epistemic penalty (0.91 on ANYmal D, beats online model-free from offline data), and [[2502.11377|PrivilegedDreamer]] shows latent dreaming with hidden-parameter estimation adapts in a few steps (+41% off-legged). LoopSR and Simulator Adaptation both *lack* an epistemic bound, so a bounded dreamer is the distinct bet — *but only if the bound is valid*: a sim-calibrated uncertainty estimate can decorrelate from true error once sim-to-real drift sets in, which threatens the "the bound keeps imagination honest" premise and makes a *calibration-under-drift* check the prerequisite, not a footnote. Berger et al.'s [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] (RLC 2026) is real, published evidence for exactly this failure mode on the RSSM/Dreamer family [[2604.02911|DreamTIP]]/[[2502.11377|PrivilegedDreamer]] are directly built on (RWM-U instead descends from a different, GRU-autoregressive backbone, so whether its own penalty shares the failure mode stays untested): on DMC Suite (Cartpole/Cheetah/Hopper/Walker) plus MuJoCo HalfCheetah, RSSM ensemble-disagreement uncertainty on OOD-initial-state rollouts "rapidly converges to ID levels" while true proprioceptive rollout error keeps growing — an "attractor behavior" pulling latent predictions toward well-supported regions regardless of accuracy. The paper is off-legged and tests OOD-initial-state shift rather than sim-to-real drift specifically, reports the effect qualitatively (no correlation coefficient) and proposes no fix — so it motivates H6's instrument rather than settling it for the legged, sim-to-real setting. Should the bound prove invalid, reconstruct-then-retrain may win held-out terrain via a parameterized twin's unlimited offline terrain.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: A learned dynamics model is a *multiplier* on real data — each real transition updates the model and, through imagination, generates many synthetic transitions for policy optimization. Sample efficiency is governed by model accuracy per real interaction, not raw interaction count; a good model makes 5 trajectories worth thousands. [[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] demonstrates the multiplier: ~5 real trajectories yield 100% on a 52 cm climb where a non-dreaming baseline gets 10%.
- **Assumption being challenged**: *Not* that few-shot adaptation beats DR — [[2409.17992|LoopSR]] and [[2604.11090|Simulator-Adaptation-Loco]] settled that via reconstruction, on the same No-Free-Lunch anti-DR framing. The challenged assumption is that *reconstructing a digital-twin sim and retraining* is the way to spend those few trajectories. Reconstruct-then-retrain (LoopSR) needs a faithful sim rebuild and carries no uncertainty estimate; the bet is that *latent dreaming* extracts more per trajectory and that an epistemic bound — which LoopSR and Simulator Adaptation both lack — is what *should* make the tiny budget safe, conditional on that bound being *calibrated under drift* (H6) rather than decorrelating from true held-out error once drift sets in — the failure mode Berger et al.'s [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] (RLC 2026) documents on the RSSM/Dreamer family that [[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] and [[2502.11377|PrivilegedDreamer]] are directly built on (RWM-U instead descends from a different, GRU-autoregressive backbone, so whether its own bound shares the failure mode stays untested): ensemble-disagreement uncertainty reconverges to in-distribution levels via attractor rollouts even as true error keeps growing, though only on off-legged DMC tasks under OOD-initial-state shift, qualitatively, with no fix proposed — real evidence that motivates the check without settling it for this direction's legged, sim-to-real setting.
- **The bet**: *Instrument the bound first (H6):* once the epistemic estimate is shown calibrated under drift, a *bounded latent dreamer* ([[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] + [[2504.16680|RWM-U]]'s epistemic penalty, 0.91 ANYmal D) at a matched ≤5-trajectory / 15–30-min real budget reaches DreamTIP's 100%-vs-10% on a 52 cm climb *and beats reconstruct-then-retrain* ([[2409.17992|LoopSR]]'s 100% Stair-Up via digital-twin) on held-out terrain by a measurable margin, because dreaming generalizes across the latent where a reconstructed sim overfits the rebuilt geometry. Falsifiable: if the bound fails its calibration-under-drift check (H6), dreaming-as-core is premature and reconstruct-then-retrain is the safer default; if reconstruct-then-retrain (LoopSR/Simulator-Adaptation) matches the bounded dreamer at the matched budget on held-out terrain, latent dreaming adds no multiplier over rebuilding the sim; if a DR-blind policy matches both, neither adapts.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (a bounded latent dreamer beats reconstruct-then-retrain at a matched few-trajectory budget, especially on held-out terrain).
1. **H1 — Latent dreaming beats reconstruct-then-retrain at a matched real-data budget.**

   - *Test*: three-arm comparison at fixed real-data budget — DR-blind / reconstruct-then-retrain / latent-dreaming — adapt on terrain A, evaluate held-out terrain B.

2. **H2 — Bounding the imagination matters; penalty vs adaptive-truncation is the open design choice.**

   - *Falsifier*: SR is flat across $\beta$ *and* across the truncation quantile → neither bound is doing work; or penalty and truncation are interchangeable → the design choice is inert.
3. **H3 — The task-invariant latent transfers across terrains, not just tasks.**

   - *Row*: DreamTIP (transfer latent) + RMA (B1 context inference).
   - *Falsifier*: the latent fails to transfer across terrains → it's task-invariant but terrain-specific.
4. **H4 — The dynamics model is morphology-portable across legged embodiments.**

5. **H5 — Few-shot dreaming overfits the test terrain without held-out validation.**

6. **H6 — The latent uncertainty estimate is calibrated under drift — the prerequisite before dreaming-as-core (instrument first).**
   - *Prediction*: on the dynamics-model backbone each dreamer actually uses — the RSSM/Dreamer architecture [[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] is built on, and separately the different, GRU-autoregressive backbone RWM-U descends from (whether RWM-U's penalty shares the RSSM finding at all is exactly what this test must establish, not assume) — the epistemic-uncertainty estimate stays *calibrated* under sim-to-real dynamics drift — predicted uncertainty tracks measured held-out long-horizon model error monotonically — rather than decorrelating from it as drift grows, the "attractor" mode Berger et al.'s [[2604.25416|Biased Dreams]] (RLC 2026) documents on the same RSSM family under off-legged, OOD-initial-state shift (qualitatively, no correlation coefficient, no proposed fix) — so the check stands on its own for the legged, sim-to-real setting regardless of how directly that evidence transfers; if it does not hold here, the [[2504.16680|RWM-U]]-style bound is regulating noise and dreaming-as-core is premature.

> - **Dreaming needs a good simulator for pretraining** — garbage-in poisons the model. → [[2603.15759|SimDist]] stresses diverse large-scale pretraining; cross-ref [[WAM|WAM]] for substrate quality and [[Sim2Real|Sim2Real]] for the sim side.

**Why it matters.**
- **The gap**: long-range goal-reaching is the locomotion-to-goal problem — distinct from VLN goal *reasoning* and from manipulation — and the classical map-build → plan → track pipeline is brittle on unstructured terrain (drift, dynamic obstacles, no GPS) because the explicit metric map is the weakest link, yet that brittleness is *asserted*, not measured against a graded map-friendliness axis.
- **Today's answers**: [[2506.05997|SRU]] takes the memory axis — a Spatially-enhanced Recurrent Unit gives an end-to-end RL policy a spatial state that survives hundreds of steps, lifting mapless SR 23.5% over LSTM/GRU and transferring zero-shot 100+ m on a real B2W; [[2604.26504|HiPAN]] takes the traversal axis (94.7% SR / 83.6 SPL in Complex-2, Go1 depth); and the SRU authors' own follow-on [[2603.13888|Path-Conditioned-Local-Planner]] adds long-range path guidance (SPL 0.82, +7.02%, robust to degraded paths) — the recurrent-memory core none of these displaces.
- **The opening**: two narrowing results sharpen the bet rather than take it — [[2512.02851|SwarmDiffusion]] *jointly* infers traversability + generates a trajectory in one diffusion model (80–100% SR, 0.09 s, adapts to new embodiments from a few hundred samples), going beyond [[2605.28442|COTRATE]]'s perception-only substrate, while [[2405.01792|Wheeled-Legged-NavLoco]]'s kilometer-scale *map-using* hierarchical planner is the strong incumbent the mapless claim must beat *where the map should be at its best*.

**First-principles framing.**
- **First principle**: Long-range locomotion-to-goal needs a spatial state that persists across hundreds of control steps (where have I been, where is the goal relative to me) *and* a coupling between that state and the gait. An explicit metric map is one lossy realization — brittle under drift and dynamics — and the map-build → plan → track factoring severs the perception-gait coupling the policy could exploit. [[2506.05997|SRU]] demonstrates the alternative: a learned recurrent spatial state lifts mapless SR 23.5% over LSTM/GRU and transfers zero-shot 100+ m.
- **Assumption being challenged**: That goal-reaching factors cleanly into map-build → plan → track — held strongest by map-using stacks like [[2405.01792|Wheeled-Legged-NavLoco]] (kilometer-scale urban nav, 0 collisions). [[2506.05997|SRU]]'s 23.5% mapless gain and [[2604.26504|HiPAN]]'s 94.7% dead-end SR bet the opposite — *learned* spatial memory beats the factored pipeline where the map breaks first. The open question the incumbents force is *where exactly* it breaks: the mapless advantage should be a function of map-friendliness, not a constant.
- **The bet**: The mapless-memory advantage is *graded by map-friendliness* — the [[2506.05997|SRU]]-style mapless-minus-mapped SR gap is near-zero on feature-rich static courses (where [[2405.01792|Wheeled-Legged-NavLoco]]'s map→plan→track is at its best) and *widens monotonically* to ≥20 pp as feature density drops, dynamics rise, and drift accumulates; with [[2512.02851|SwarmDiffusion]]-style joint traversability+trajectory and [[2605.28442|COTRATE]] cross-platform perception holding the advantage across embodiments. Falsifiable: if the mapless-minus-mapped gap is *constant or shrinks* as terrain becomes less map-friendly, the explicit metric map is not the weakest link and learned memory buys nothing the map doesn't.

**Hypotheses & tests.** Each item is a falsifiable sub-hypothesis of the FP bet (the mapless-memory advantage is graded by map-friendliness, and joint traversability+memory beats the map → plan → track stack where the map breaks).
1. **H1 — A three-way graded curve (map / reactive-mapless / recurrent-mapless) as a function of map-friendliness — the representational-budget design law.**
   - *Prediction*: across three representations — explicit map→plan→track ([[2405.01792|Wheeled-Legged-NavLoco]]), reactive-mapless ([[2107.03996|LocoTransformer]]-style, no persistent spatial state), and recurrent-mapless ([[2506.05997|SRU]]-style learned spatial memory) — LocoTransformer stands in for the un-vaulted LRN (2504.13149), the named long-range-memoryless dissociation row — SR orders differently *by map-friendliness*: the map wins on feature-rich static courses, reactive-mapless ties cheaply on short-horizon open courses, and recurrent-mapless pulls ahead *monotonically* (to ≥20 pp over the map) as feature density drops, dynamics rise, and drift accumulates — a graded curve crossing over at predictable points, not a single backbone winning everywhere.
   - *Test*: grade courses by map-friendliness (feature density, dynamics, drift); report all three representations' SR per grade and the crossover points.
   - *Row*: SRU (recurrent-mapless) vs LocoTransformer (reactive-mapless) vs Wheeled-Legged NavLoco (map → plan → track).
   - *Falsifier*: the three curves don't separate by map-friendliness (one representation dominates at every grade, or the recurrent-minus-map gap is constant) → there is no representational-budget law and the map isn't the weakest link where it breaks.
2. **H2 — Recurrent structure governs spatial retention over horizon.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2506.05997|SRU]]'s spatial-memory structure beats LSTM/GRU more as goal distance grows, because the spatially-enhanced state retains relative-goal information over hundreds of steps where vanilla recurrence forgets — and the test must hold even against a modern selective-SSM memory ([[2508.11849|LocoMamba]]'s Δ-gated linear recurrence), not just LSTM/GRU.
   - *Test*: ablate memory structure against goal distance on the [[2012.03912|MultiON]] benchmark — the one named suite isolating explicit-map-memory vs implicit-recurrence with controlled task complexity (1-ON → 3-ON ordered goals: OracleMap SR 94%→48% as horizon grows, OracleMap 48% vs NoMap-RNN 10% on 3-ON); report SR vs distance per memory type, adding a [[2508.11849|LocoMamba]]-style SSM memory as the modern linear-recurrence comparator, and treating MultiON as the map-memory-vs-implicit abstraction substrate (photorealistic indoor object-nav with perfect localization, so it backs the memory-horizon axis only, not the legged-terrain coupling).
   - *Row*: SRU (recurrent memory) vs Blind Nav Agents (generic LSTM, emergent map to 1,000 steps) vs LocoMamba (selective-SSM memory); abstraction substrate [[2012.03912|MultiON]].
   - *Falsifier*: SRU ties a generic LSTM ([[2301.13261|Blind-Nav-Agents]]) or a [[2508.11849|LocoMamba]]-style SSM at long distances → the explicit spatial enhancement isn't the retention lever, the recurrence alone suffices.
3. **H3 — Posture-conditioned locomotion's traversable-space gain generalizes beyond HiPAN's own training distribution.**
   - *Prediction*: [[2604.26504|HiPAN]]'s own ablation already confirms posture adaptation raises SPL in-distribution (Room: 74.1 → 88.8 SPL vs. the w/o-Pos.-Adjustment variant); the open question is whether that SPL delta survives on confined courses HiPAN never trained on, or on a different quadruped platform — a genuine design-law claim needs the gap to persist out-of-distribution, not hold only on HiPAN's own procedurally-generated test set.
   - *Test*: evaluate HiPAN's posture-adaptive vs w/o-Pos.-Adjustment variant on held-out confined-space generators or a second platform; report the SPL delta against the in-distribution 14.7-point baseline (88.8 − 74.1).
   - *Row*: HiPAN (hierarchical posture).
   - *Falsifier*: the SPL delta collapses out-of-distribution or on a new platform → posture adaptation's benefit is HiPAN-specific curriculum overfitting, not a general design law.
4. **H4 — Joint traversability+trajectory beats a perception-only substrate for cross-embodiment goal-reaching.**
   - *Prediction*: a [[2512.02851|SwarmDiffusion]]-style head that *jointly* infers traversability and generates the trajectory improves cross-embodiment goal-reaching over a perception-only substrate ([[2605.28442|COTRATE]]) plugged into the mapless policy, with zero-shot transfer (Spot→Husky) sometimes beating per-platform continual learning, because joint inference couples where-is-traversable to where-to-go.
   - *Test*: compare SwarmDiffusion-style joint head vs COTRATE-perception-then-plan inside the mapless policy; report cross-embodiment SR + path effort.
   - *Row*: SwarmDiffusion (joint traversability+trajectory) vs COTRATE (perception-only substrate) + SRU (mapless memory).
   - *Falsifier*: the joint head underperforms perception-then-plan → coupling traversability to trajectory adds nothing over a separate perception layer.
5. **H5 — SPL exposes mapless looping the SR number hides, and path conditioning narrows the gap.**
   - *Prediction*: mapless policies without a map risk revisiting dead-ends, so [[2604.26504|HiPAN]]'s SPL drops below SR more on loop-prone courses, exposing inefficiency the SR number alone hides — and adding [[2603.13888|Path-Conditioned-Local-Planner]]-style global-path conditioning narrows the SPL–SR gap (its SPL 0.82 vs baseline holds even under degraded paths).
   - *Test*: report SPL alongside SR on loop-prone vs open courses, with and without path conditioning.
   - *Row*: Path-Conditioned Local Planner (path-guided) / HiPAN (hierarchical posture) / SRU (mapless memory).
   - *Falsifier*: SPL tracks SR on loop-prone courses → mapless policies don't loop and the SPL caveat is moot.

> [!warning] Risks
> - **Mapless policies can loop or get stuck** — without a map, the policy may revisit dead-ends. → [[2604.26504|HiPAN]]'s SPL catches this; report SPL alongside SR, not SR alone (H5).
> - **Overlap with the umbrella's VLN direction** — high-level goal *reasoning* is VLN territory. → This direction is scoped to low-level mapless *control + traversability*; language-instruction goal-reasoning is cross-referenced to [[Embodied-AI|Embodied-AI]], not duplicated.
> - **Traversability self-supervision needs experience** — [[2605.28442|COTRATE]] learns from robot rollouts. → It transfers cross-platform (Spot→Husky) zero-shot in places; report where transfer holds vs needs continual learning (H4).

## Cross-Cutting Themes

> [!tip] The Privileged-State Gap Is the Bottleneck — Inference, Perception, and Dreaming Are the Three Answers
> A1, B1, and B2 face the same problem the surveys name under different words: the policy must act without the privileged physical state (terrain $\mu/h$, payload, contact, model error) that simulation provides. They answer at three points of the recovery operator: B1 *infers* the context from proprioceptive history ([[2107.04034|RMA]]'s 10 Hz adaptation module, 12 kg payload), A1 *perceives* the anticipatory geometry exteroception exposes ([[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] 0.962 vs 0.230 box-climb), and B2 *dreams* — a world model imagines consequences so 5 real trajectories suffice ([[2604.02911|DreamTIP]] 100% vs 10%). [[2408.14472|DWL]]'s denoising world model and [[2504.16680|RWM-U]]'s epistemic penalty share the move: recover or bound the unobserved state, don't pretend it's observed.

> [!tip] Real-World Adaptation in Minutes, Not Millions — Sample Efficiency Through the Right Substrate
> A5, A6, and B2 converge on the same lever for deployable locomotion: sample efficiency through the right substrate, not more compute or more real data. A5 shows off-policy/flow RL beats PPO on wall-clock ([[2505.22642|FastTD3]] <3 hrs, [[2512.01996|Humanoid-Loco-15min]] 15 min); B2 shows world-model pretraining + tiny real adaptation ([[2206.14176|DayDreamer]] 1 hr, [[2603.15759|SimDist]] 15–30 min) displaces both exhaustive domain randomization and extensive on-robot RL. A6 adds the *coupling* dimension to the same lever — an MPC prior is itself a sample-efficiency substrate ([[2603.10878|RL-Augmented]] 4–10 M steps, an order of magnitude over blind end-to-end RL; [[2604.08036|PriPG-RL]] learns where vanilla SAC/PPO fail) — and asks *which* coupling makes the substrate most efficient in which regime, with A5's pure-RL as its endpoint. The shared move is biological: the brain learns from few interactions because it has a model — favor the mechanism (off-policy reuse, world-model imagination, a model-based prior in the loop) that extracts more from each step over the one that discards rollouts or randomizes blindly.

> [!tip] The Imitation Target, Not the Imitation Data, Is the Lever for Dynamic Skills
> A1 and A2 share one discipline — make the reference feasible, don't collect more demonstrations — but apply it at opposite ends of the timeline, and that split is why they stay separate. **A2 fixes a clip offline**: a one-time projection of pre-recorded mocap onto the feasible manifold *before* any rollout ([[2506.12851|KungfuBot]] 53.25 mm vs >233 mm, [[2605.06593|ReActor]] 97.45% downstream RL). **A1 generates a fresh reference online** against the terrain seen at runtime — a new reference each control window, filtered by an RL tracker ([[2604.17335|G1-WBC-Gen+Track]] 0.962 box-climb). A2 asks "is *this clip* trackable?"; A1 asks "what does *this terrain ahead* demand?" [[2605.10063|EFGCL]]'s force-guided curriculum even *expands* A2's feasible manifold to reach backflips. Same lever, different *when* — complementary directions, not two phrasings of one idea.

> [!tip] Deployability Is Bounded by Embodiment Costs the Task Reward Ignores
> A3, A4, A7, A8, and B1 all hit real limits the standard task reward omits. A4 makes GRF, acoustic, and thermal cost first-class predicted-and-regulated quantities ([[2604.23702|QuietWalk]] R²≈0.99, 7.17 dBA; [[2605.27046|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] 70%→<10% overheating); A3 treats fall-recovery as the non-periodic embodiment-stress boundary case ([[2502.12152|HUMANUP]] lower motor temperature, ~6 s recovery); B1 sustains locomotion under real payload ([[2107.04034|RMA]] 12 kg). The insight: task success is necessary but not sufficient — a gait that overheats, deafens, or cannot recover from a fall is undeployable regardless of its tracking reward. A7 and A8 push the same insight along two axes A4 leaves closed within one episode: A7 asks whether A4's within-episode cost proxies predict cumulative damage across a service life, and A8 asks whether A2's feasibility target itself — which clips are even attempted — should be conditioned on the same thermal state A4 regulates, rather than fixed at room temperature before training starts.

> [!tip] The Locomotion Control Object Is More Morphology-Portable Than the Manipulation Grasp Object
> B2, A5, A1, and A2 share one representational bet: the locomotion control object (phase-clocked gait + velocity-tracking) transfers across embodiments more readily than the manipulation grasp object does. B2's [[2501.10100|RWM]]/[[2504.16680|RWM-U]] run the *same* world-model + MBRL pipeline across ANYmal D (quadruped) and Unitree G1 (humanoid); A5's off-policy/flow methods ([[2505.22642|FastTD3]], [[2602.02481|FPO++]]) span both unchanged; A1's perception-fusion precedent ([[2107.03996|LocoTransformer]], quadruped) transfers to bipedal terrain. A2 supplies the direct evidence that the gait's *phase* is the invariant: [[2606.01851|PHASOR]] anchors a universal action representation on motion phase for cross-embodiment transfer (90.3% R@1 Human→Robot retrieval), and [[2606.03476|Human2Humanoid]] bridges morphology gaps with a morphology-invariant end-effector loss. This is the locomotion counterpart to the morphology-invariance direction in [[Embodied-AI|Embodied-AI]] — but where manipulation needs a function-aligned action space to bridge hands, locomotion's gait structure is *already* a low-dimensional cross-morphology invariant.

| Coupling × regime matrix — the four MPC↔learning couplings + two endpoints head-to-head on one platform at matched compute | A6 | [[2501.16590\|MPC-vs-RL-Legged]] is the only standardized Go-class harness that benchmarks the two *endpoints* (pure-MPC predictive-sampling vs pure-PPO) on shared metrics (CoT, disturbance N, lateral variance) and already shows neither dominates — but it has no *coupling* arms, so the contact-richness × observability × horizon regime grid (H1) and the matched-latency normalization (H5) have no existing substrate; every coupling reports against pure-RL/pure-MPC on its *own* platform ([[2510.12717\|Residual-MPC]] MIT Humanoid +78%, [[2603.10878\|RL-Augmented]] Centauro CoT ~0.12, surrogate rows [[2601.12169\|Legged-Neural-MPC]]/[[2603.14333\|Physics-Embedded-DDPC]] Go1/Go2), so no suite reports a coupling × regime SR matrix — the unfilled gap is exactly the extended harness with five coupling arms + the pre-registered 5 pp / 2 pp thresholds. [[2408.00342\|MuJoCo-MPC-HumanoidBench]] supplies the pure-MPC endpoint number on HumanoidBench (beats DreamerV3/TD-MPC2/SAC/PPO on Stand/Walk/Push), but it benchmarks only the MPC endpoint, not the couplings |
| Cumulative-damage ground truth (cycles-to-failure) and whether within-episode cost proxies predict it | A7 | No legged-RL benchmark reports cycles-to-failure or any cumulative-damage metric — every embodiment-cost number in this corpus is a single-deployment quantity ([[2604.23702\|QuietWalk]]'s 7.17 dBA, [[2605.27046\|Thermal-Aware-Residual]]'s <50 °C peak, [[2603.01631\|Thermal-Aware-Locomotion]]'s 7→27 min session ceiling); the closest existing machinery sits entirely outside embodied-AI — the PHM Society's RUL-aware RRT* (adaptive joint-weighting against a Remaining-Useful-Life estimate, a classical planner on a fixed-base arm, no arXiv ID) and Prolonging Tool Life's FEA + Miner's-rule RUL estimator (arXiv 2507.17275, held-tool not actuator, [[Manipulation\|Manipulation]] scope) — neither closes the gap for a legged RL policy with an episode-reset MDP. The gap is widening, not closing, as deployments lengthen: [[2502.16230\|Humanoid-Locomotion-World-Model]]'s ~20-hour cumulative real-world humanoid deployment across one week (3.2 km, unassisted) — the longest cumulative real-hardware duty cycle in this doc's corpus — reports zero actuator-health, thermal, or wear instrumentation over that run, confirming that even the field's longest sustained deployment to date still treats cumulative actuator state as unmeasured |
| Sustained-torque-derated feasibility rejection rate, and derating-curve transfer across platforms | A8 | No suite re-scores a motion-imitation feasibility filter against a temperature-derated torque bound — every rejection number in this corpus is measured at one implicit room-temperature operating point ([[2506.12851\|KungfuBot]]'s 4-of-10 filtering-validation rejections, [[2409.20514\|Opt2Skill]]'s fixed joint-and-torque-limit TO constraints); the closest existing machinery is split across two unconnected papers — [[2509.06342\|Towards-bridging-the-gap]]'s PACE supplies the $P_{el} \propto \tau^2 R$ dissipation relation (the functional form) and [[2605.27046\|Thermal-Aware-Residual]] supplies the measured 30/50/58 °C operating envelope against a 60 °C ceiling (the real-hardware scale) — but neither paper, nor any other in this corpus, builds the derating bound from the two and applies it to a rejection filter; a targeted search for thermal-aware trajectory optimization or temperature-conditioned torque derating in humanoid motion imitation returns nothing |
| Proprioceptive context-inference architecture matching exteroception across friction × payload × terrain | B1 | [[2602.00678\|RoboGauge]] (proprioceptive quadruped, 10 levels × 7 tasks, terrain × difficulty × domain-randomization sim-to-real metrics — graded but no explicit 80%-body-weight payload sweep) + [[2305.14654\|Barkour]] (standardized quadruped agility/terrain course, time-scored — no payload or context-inference axis) + [[2308.14636\|Linear-Impactor]] (calibrated disturbance-rejection protocol — disturbance bandwidth, not payload × terrain conjunction); residual hole: no suite reports the full payload(0→80%) × discontinuous-terrain JOINT surface under a controlled backbone swap ([[2107.04034\|RMA]] 12 kg but TCN fails stairs, [[2212.07740\|TERT]] 60% stairs but no payload-range study). H3 (adaptation-rate vs disturbance-bandwidth) now has a model-based comparator: [[2606.16542\|ADAPT-Locomotion]]'s analytical observer recovers residual force/torque online from proprioception under calibrated 60 N pull / 4 kg loads, giving the bandwidth sweep an analytic baseline to test learned-inference rate against — but no suite yet runs the observer-vs-learned-regressor rate sweep on one platform, so the bandwidth-matched optimum remains unmeasured |
| World-model dreaming for few-shot (~5 traj) real adaptation with calibrated epistemic bounding | B2 | No shared/named few-shot real-world legged adaptation benchmark exists (field-acknowledged open problem) — adapted-terrain SR, held-out-terrain SR, and real-data-budget all rest on ad-hoc per-paper protocols ([[2604.02911\|DreamTIP]] 5-traj, 100% vs 10%, no uncertainty bound; [[2409.17992\|LoopSR]]'s 5-terrain set), which is why H1's latent-dreaming-vs-reconstruct-then-retrain falsifier has no common yardstick; the one real benchmark backing a Hypothesis is D4RL (standardized offline-MBRL suite — halfcheetah/hopper/walker2d + RWM-U's Velocity-ANYmal-D/Velocity-G1 tasks) behind H2's epistemic-penalty calibration via [[2504.16680\|RWM-U]] / [[2005.13239\|MOPO]] (D4RL is currently unnamed in the doc and not in KH — ingest candidate). The uncertainty-bound axis (H2) now carries two distinct epistemic formulations — RWM-U's MOPO-PPO reward penalty vs [[2602.14351\|WIMLE]]'s ensemble-IMLE confidence weighting and [[2604.08958\|WOMBET]]'s planning-with-uncertainty-penalty + dual-criterion filtering — yet none has been tested on real legged few-shot terrain transfer, so the benchmark gap is a head-to-head of epistemic-bound *type* (reward-penalty vs synthetic-weighting vs plan-filtering) at the matched ≤5-trajectory real budget |
| End-to-end mapless locomotion-to-goal with learned spatial memory + cross-platform traversability | B3 | [[2012.03912\|MultiON]] (benchmark — explicit-map vs implicit-memory in goal-reaching, OracleMap 48% vs NoMap-RNN 10% on 3-ON, but indoor object-nav with perfect localization: backs the map-memory-vs-implicit axis only) — NO standardized legged-locomotion-to-goal-over-terrain suite exists that stratifies courses by map-friendliness (feature density / dynamics / drift), so H1's graded curve and H3/H4's confined-geometry + cross-platform terrain axes have no named benchmark ([[2506.05997\|SRU]] is single-platform mapless memory, [[2604.26504\|HiPAN]] posture-adaptive quadruped with no cross-platform). [[2509.20739\|Semantic-Object-Exploration]] supplies the first real-legged (Go1) anchor for B3's SLAM-brittleness premise — its Problem section measures geometry-centric SLAM as unstable under legged dynamic motion, ground impacts, and unstable viewpoints — so the FP bet's "the explicit metric map is the weakest link" is no longer only asserted on the legged platform, though its topological-memory map-friendliness gap is still un-swept against the NavLoco metric-map incumbent |
