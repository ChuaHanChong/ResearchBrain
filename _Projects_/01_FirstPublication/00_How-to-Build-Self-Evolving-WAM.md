# How to Build a Self-Evolving WAM

> [!abstract] One-Line Summary
> 6-step blueprint for adding self-evolution to a trained [[2602.15922|DreamZero]] or [[2602.10098|VLA-JEPA]], organized as 4 parallel tracks + coevolution + auto-curriculum.

## Starting Point: Pick Your Base WAM

> [!tip] Model-First Wins
> For embodied AI, ==start with a trained world model and add self-evolution== (not the other way around). A world model already has a robust latent space for generating synthetic future states — the challenge shifts to data quality within the model's own imagination.

| | [[2602.15922|DreamZero]] | [[2602.10098|VLA-JEPA]] |
|---|---|---|
| **Architecture** | 14B autoregressive diffusion transformer (DiT) | Latent-space predictive encoder (JEPA) |
| **How it models dynamics** | Flow-matching: jointly predicts video + continuous actions as next-token prediction | Leakage-free state prediction: predicts latent future representations, not raw pixels |
| **Self-evolution signal** | Denoising / flow-matching loss spike on novel physics | JEPA alignment loss between target and student encoders |
| **Strength** | Internet-scale visual dynamics, zero-shot generalization | Noise-invariant (ignores lighting/camera changes), cleaner latent space |
| **Weakness** | Inference speed, massive compute for continuous fine-tuning | World model quality bounded by latent abstraction capacity |

## The Core Insight: Decouple Imaginer from Actor

> [!warning] Critical Architectural Boundary
> ==A self-evolving agent is not the same as a self-evolving world action model.== The world model's job is to predict and imagine future states; the agent's job is to execute actions. The agent itself does not need the capacity to imagine the future — it only needs to figure out how to act based on the world model's predictions.

**Coevolution does not mean merging their functions.** It means mathematically linking their loss functions so that an upgrade in the Imaginer immediately forces an upgrade in the Actor, and vice versa.

## Step 1 — Evolve the World Model (Track 1: The Imaginer)

**Goal**: Continuously improve physics understanding without human labels.

**For DreamZero**: Track the denoising/flow-matching loss. When the robot encounters a new physical interaction, the loss spikes. Use this spike as an intrinsic learning signal to continuously fine-tune the DiT backbone, allowing it to learn new physical dynamics (like how a novel material bends or breaks) without human labels.

**For VLA-JEPA**: Continuously train the target encoder against the student pathway using the JEPA alignment loss. As the robot experiences new environments, the world model updates its latent abstractions to account for new physics while ignoring visual noise (new lighting, camera angles).

### Papers to Borrow From

| Paper | What to Borrow | Why |
|-------|---------------|-----|
| [[2506.09985\|V-JEPA 2]] (Meta, 2025) | Action-free pretraining → action-conditioned fine-tuning transition protocol | Learn general physics from massive unlabeled datasets before conditioning on agent actions |
| OccSora (ICLR 2025) | 4D occupancy generation — predict volumetric occupancy over time | Natively ingrains spatial depth and object permanence, drastically reducing physical hallucinations |
| PhysGen (ECCV 2024) | Physics-grounded loss functions with rigid-body constraints | Penalizes the model heavily if generated future frames show solid objects clipping through each other |
| DriveDreamer-2 (AAAI 2025) | LLM as active "scenario injector" | Use a lightweight LLM to generate diverse, out-of-distribution textual conditions that force the world model to imagine edge-case physics |
| Genesis (ICML 2024) | Tie generative world model to a deterministic universal physics engine | Grounds the latent space in hard physical laws before allowing the model to freely imagine |

## Step 2 — Evolve the Agent (Track 2: The Actor)

**Goal**: Evolve the policy network via population-based methods operating inside latent dreams.

**Mechanism**:
1. **Isolate the action head** — In DreamZero, isolate continuous action decoders. In VLA-JEPA, isolate the action-head fine-tuned on top of the JEPA backbone.
2. **Create a population** — Clone into ~50 variants with slight weight mutations.
3. **Simulate via imagination** — Let all 50 agents operate inside dreams generated by the World Model.
4. **Select & mutate** — Score by task success + curiosity reward. Discard worst, clone/mutate best.

> [!important] Why Evolution Over Gradients?
> Gradient descent is great for the Dynamics Model, but evolution is often better for discovering entirely novel behaviors in the Actor. It can find unconventional action strategies that standard gradient descent might get stuck trying to optimize.

### Papers to Borrow From

| Paper | What to Borrow | Why |
|-------|---------------|-----|
| DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023) | Actor-critic design operating entirely in latent space | The actor takes compressed state from world model and outputs action — lightweight, reactive policy |
| Generalized PBT with Pairwise Learning (IEEE, Oct 2024) | Pairwise comparisons for guided gradient updates to underperforming agents | Better than kill-and-clone: identifies specific performance differentials between agents |
| EvoRL via Hierarchical Parallelism (2024) | GPU parallelization strategies for 50+ mutating agents querying the world model | Prevents GPU bottlenecking with serialized inference queues (Python + Redis) |
| CBET for World Models (OpenReview 2025) | Transfer learned behaviors across agents within world model architectures | Proven to work specifically with DreamerV3 and IMPALA in sparse-reward environments |
| NIDL via STDP (2024) | Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity for energy-efficient evolutionary updates | Leaves bulk compute for the massive DreamZero/JEPA backbone |

## Step 3 — Wire the Curiosity-Driven Feedback Loop (Track 3)

**Goal**: Create an autonomous mathematical drive that pushes the Agent to seek the unknown, so the World Model can learn from what it finds.

**Core mechanism** (from Plan2Explore): Train a lightweight ensemble of $k$ dynamics networks alongside the main world model. The Agent asks the world model to imagine a future rollout. If the ensemble's predictions for that future state highly disagree, the world model is uncertain. The intrinsic reward is:

$$R_{\text{intrinsic}} = \text{Var}\left(\hat{s}_{t+1}^{(1)}, \ldots, \hat{s}_{t+1}^{(k)}\right)$$

### The Self-Evolving Loop

```
1. World Model calculates prediction error (how badly it predicted the future)
2. Error → intrinsic reward → given to Agent
3. Agent evolves to maximize this reward (actively seeks edge cases & novel physics)
4. World Model observes novel states, trains on them, reduces error to zero
5. Reward disappears → Agent must find the next unknown frontier
6. → GOTO 1
```

### Papers to Borrow From

| Paper | What to Borrow | Why |
|-------|---------------|-----|
| Plan2Explore (Sekar et al., 2020) | Expected future novelty via ensemble disagreement | Plans toward uncertainty *before* reaching the state, not just after |
| Empowerment (arXiv, March 2025) | Mutual information $I(A; S')$ between actions and outcomes | Correlates highest with effective, human-like open-world exploration |
| SENSEI (NeurIPS 2024) | Foundation model provides semantic guidance to curiosity | Solves the "noisy TV" problem — agent explores meaningful physics, not visual static |
| Causal Curiosity (NeurIPS 2024) | Reward for discovering causal links between specific actions and environmental changes | Goes beyond prediction error to understand *why* things happen |
| RKHS Skill Disentanglement (NeurIPS 2024) | Force agent population to explore disentangled skills via Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space | Ensures each evolutionary branch learns a fundamentally distinct physical behavior |
| A Single Goal is All You Need (NeurIPS 2024) | Autonomous, shifting single-goal generation | Focuses evolutionary pressure instead of diffuse "explore everything" |

## Step 4 — Stabilize with Continual Learning (Track 4)

**Goal**: Prevent catastrophic forgetting as both components continuously update.

**Core mechanism**: Implement a **Latent Experience Replay Buffer**. As the system self-evolves, store highly compressed latent trajectories of past successful behaviors. During training updates, interleave small batches of historical data to anchor memory.

> [!danger] The Synthetic Data Trap
> Because the agent trains entirely inside the "dreams" of the world model, it is essentially relying on ==synthetic images for representation learning==. If the diffusion backbone begins to hallucinate during continuous training, it introduces severe data quality issues — leakage, systemic biases, and visual artifacts. If the agent trains on broken simulations, it will learn to ==exploit generative artifacts (reward hacking)== rather than learning real-world dynamics, causing **policy collapse**. The replay buffer must validate synthetic rollouts against real-world ground-truth segments.

### Papers to Borrow From

| Paper | What to Borrow | Why |
|-------|---------------|-----|
| Synthetic Data Contamination in Online CL (NeurIPS 2024) | Filtration techniques for leakage, biases, and generative artifacts | Prevents hallucinated dynamics from entering the replay buffer |
| Strong Experience Replay (SER, March 2026) | Mimic future experiences on current training data | Forces updated model to maintain strict prediction consistency with the old model |
| Relational Experience Replay (RER, March 2026) | Bi-level learning: adaptively tune sample importance across tasks | Mathematically optimizes the stability–plasticity trade-off |
| Experience Replay with Random Reshuffling (March 2026) | Random Reshuffling algorithms within buffer | Drastically improves convergence over standard uniform sampling |
| Task-Aware Prompt Gradient Projection (ICCV 2025) | Project new gradients orthogonally to weights critical for past tasks | Physically prevents the network from overwriting established physical laws |

**Also consider**: Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) — identifies critical neural weights via Fisher information matrix and applies a mathematical "spring" to protect them during new learning.

## Step 5 — Coevolve Imaginer + Actor (Adversarial Symbiosis)

**Goal**: Mathematically link the loss functions of Imaginer and Actor so upgrades in one force upgrades in the other — without merging their distinct roles.

**The Framework**:
1. The Imaginer predicts future states based on reality.
2. The Imaginer alters parameters in its latent simulation to create synthetic edge-case scenarios ==just slightly beyond the Actor's current skill level==.
3. The Actor evolves to solve these synthetic edge-cases.
4. The Actor's new behaviors generate novel physical interactions in the real world, forcing the Imaginer to update.

> [!quote] The Arms Race
> The Imaginer generates increasingly complex, high-quality synthetic physics, and the Actor evolves increasingly sophisticated policies to navigate them.

### 5 Concepts to Borrow

| Concept | Source | Implementation |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Unsupervised Environment Design** | ACCEL (2024) | Imaginer acts as "Teacher" — mutates dream starting conditions to maximize the Actor's "Regret" (gap between actual and theoretical max performance). Keeps Actor in optimal learning zone. |
| **Active Inference / Expected Free Energy** | Scaling Active Inference (NeurIPS 2024/2025) | Unify Imaginer's prediction loss and Actor's reward loss under Friston's framework. Both minimize surprise: Imaginer minimizes *actual* surprise, Actor minimizes *expected future* surprise. Tightly couples gradients without blurring roles. |
| **Value-Aware Generative Rollouts** | Value-Equivalent Models (2024/2025) | Inject Actor's gradient back into Imaginer. Imaginer learns to focus rendering capacity only on physical features that impact the Actor's current decisions. As Actor learns to care about new objects, Imaginer auto-evolves to render them with higher fidelity. |
| **Adversarial Data Validation** | Adversarial Validation of Synthetic Data (ICLR 2025) | A secondary discriminator polices data quality within the closed loop. Penalizes the Imaginer if it generates physically impossible shortcuts, preventing simulation degradation and policy collapse. |
| **Differentiable Trust Region Rollouts** | Model-Based RL with Differentiable Simulators (OpenReview 2025) | Since DreamZero and VLA-JEPA are fully differentiable, backpropagate the Actor's reward loss directly through the Imaginer's generated dream into the Actor's weights. Exponentially faster than population-based neuroevolution alone. |

## Step 6 — Auto-Curriculum (Environment Generation)

**Goal**: If the model is in a static environment, it will eventually learn everything and stop evolving. The system must generate its own increasingly difficult environments.

**Mechanism**: Introduce a secondary adversarial network whose sole job is to generate new environments/parameters that are ==just barely outside of the current Actor's ability to solve them==. This creates an arms race: the environment generator makes things harder, and the self-evolving model adapts.

### Papers to Borrow From

| Paper | What to Borrow |
|-------|---------------|
| POET (Wang et al., 2019) | Generates a continuously expanding, increasingly difficult set of environments while simultaneously evolving the agents to solve them — passing skills back and forth between different environments |
| AI-Generating Algorithms (Clune, 2019) | Architectural blueprint arguing AGI will be *discovered* by algorithms combining meta-learning, evolving architectures, and open-ended environment generation |
| DriveDreamer-2 (AAAI 2025) | LLM as scenario injector — generate diverse edge-case conditions for world model imagination |

> [!success] The Loop in One Sentence
> The Agent evolves to find the unknown → the World Model observes the unknown and updates its physics engine → the Agent evolves new actions to exploit the updated physics → **Repeat**.

## Safety: Misevolution Risks

> [!danger] Misevolution
> As agents become capable of autonomous evolution, they present novel risks that are often overlooked by traditional safety research. Misevolution is the phenomenon where an agent's self-evolution deviates in unintended or harmful ways.

| Pathway | Emergent Risk | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| **Model** | Degradation of safety alignment | Agent prioritizes task completion over ethical constraints |
| **Memory** | Biased knowledge accumulation | Over-refunding or unfair treatment based on historical patterns |
| **Tool** | Injection of vulnerabilities | Data leakage through insecurely generated code (>76% of cases) |
| **Workflow** | Cross-domain tool misuse | Privacy issues from inappropriate data sharing between domains |

**Key paper**: Misevolution: On the Risks of Self-Evolving Agents (2025)

## Supplementary Self-Evolving Methods

Beyond the 4-track architecture, these additional mechanisms can drive self-evolution:

| Method | How It Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Hindsight Experience Replay** | Failed attempts are relabeled — "I failed to reach Goal A but I learned the exact actions to reach State B." Extracts successful learning data from failures. |
| **AI-Assisted Self-Correction** | A larger VLM observes the action model's execution and provides automated scoring or natural language corrections to update the policy. |
| **Asymmetric Self-Play** | Two internal agents interact: a "setter" creates tasks at the edge of the "solver's" capabilities, generating an automated curriculum that evolves as the solver improves. |

See also: [[2602.12063\|VLAW]] (co-evolutionary loop), [[2510.16079\|EVOLVER]], [[2203.14465\|STaR]], [[2403.09629\|Quiet-STaR]]
