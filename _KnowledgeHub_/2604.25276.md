# OmniVTG: A Large-Scale Dataset and Training Paradigm for Open-World Video Temporal Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and Huawei introduced OmniVTG, a large-scale, semantically diverse dataset, and a Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought training paradigm for open-world video temporal grounding. This approach achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across public benchmarks and demonstrated enhanced robustness for rare concepts, obtaining 63.3% R1@0.5 on the OmniVTG Test Set.

## Problem

- Existing Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) datasets often lack sufficient scale, semantic diversity, and domain coverage, limiting models' ability to generalize to open-world scenarios, particularly for rare concepts.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with robust temporal grounding in open-world settings, exhibiting a performance gap between common and rare concepts despite strong general video understanding abilities.
- Manual annotation for VTG datasets is resource-intensive, while automated methods often rely on ASR, which can lead to imprecise alignment between spoken queries and visual content.

## Method

- A large-scale, semantically diverse dataset named **OmniVTG** was constructed using a ==Semantic Coverage== Iterative Expansion pipeline, leveraging MLLMs (Gemini-2.5 Pro) to generate dense, timestamped captions with target rare words.
- A three-stage Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm was developed, comprising Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for foundational skills, CoT Finetuning to teach a 'predict then correct' reasoning path, and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for further refinement.
- The CoT finetuning stage explicitly trains the MLLM to leverage its internal video understanding by first predicting a coarse segment (e.g., a visually similar enclosing event) and then refining it to the precise target segment.

## Results

- OmniVTG provides 2124.1 hours of video (**4.3x** ActivityNet Captions) and 359,221 queries, achieving **95%** coverage of target rare vocabulary, with automated annotations validated by **93.82%** having >**0.5** IoU with manual corrections.
- The proposed model achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across four public VTG benchmarks, including **54.5%** R1@**0.3** on TVGBench, outperforming baselines like Time-R1 (**41.8%**).
- On the OmniVTG Test Set, the model reached **63.3%** R1@**0.5** and maintained performance on the OmniVTG Rare subset (**62.4%** R1@**0.5**), demonstrating enhanced robustness and a reduced performance gap for rare concepts compared to other MLLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit stronger capabilities in general video understanding tasks (e.g., query-clip matching, dense captioning) than in direct temporal grounding, especially showing less performance disparity for rare concepts in understanding tasks.
> - High-quality, large-scale VTG dataset creation can be significantly improved by reformulating the annotation task into dense, timestamped caption generation, which MLLMs perform more precisely than direct grounding.
> - An explicit 'predict then correct' reasoning path, integrated into MLLM training, can leverage the model's video understanding to self-reflect, verify, and refine its temporal localization predictions, enhancing robustness for rare concepts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) is a task within the field of multimodal artificial intelligence that involves localizing specific video segments corresponding to natural language text queries. This capability is relevant for applications such as video search, content summarization, and automated video analysis.

The broader research landscape for VTG has seen advancements through the development of deep learning architectures and larger datasets. Early approaches to VTG primarily relied on task-specific models that extracted video and text features, followed by cross-modal fusion and temporal localization modules. These methods often required extensive training on domain-specific datasets, which limited their ability to generalize to unseen data or open-world scenarios.

More recently, the emergence of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has introduced new possibilities for VTG. MLLMs, with their enhanced multimodal comprehension and reasoning abilities, have been adapted for VTG by explicitly encoding timestamps as text or by incorporating specialized temporal encoders and decoders. Training paradigms for these MLLMs have often involved multi-stage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and, in some cases, Reinforcement Learning (RL) to optimize temporal reasoning. For instance, some methods have explored Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to guide models in generating intermediate reasoning steps before providing an answer.

However, despite these developments, challenges persist, particularly in open-world settings. Existing VTG datasets often suffer from limitations in scale, semantic diversity, and domain coverage. Many are restricted to specific domains (e.g., indoor activities, vlogs) or possess a narrow vocabulary, leading to a significant performance gap when models encounter rare or abstract concepts in real-world videos. Manual annotation is resource-intensive, while automated methods using sources like Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) can lead to mismatches between speech and visual content. On the model front, while MLLMs show promise, current CoT reasoning processes sometimes lack explicit self-reflection and correction mechanisms, resulting in suboptimal performance for challenging, open-world temporal grounding tasks involving rare concepts.

This research addresses these identified gaps by introducing a large-scale, semantically diverse dataset (OmniVTG) specifically designed for open-world scenarios and by proposing a novel training paradigm (Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought) that aims to enhance MLLMs' ability to handle diverse and rare concepts by leveraging their internal video understanding capabilities for self-reflection and correction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to improve Video Temporal Grounding (VTG) performance in open-world settings, specifically by addressing the limitations of existing datasets and enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

The motivations for these objectives are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Dataset Limitations for Open-World VTG:** Current public VTG datasets are characterized by two main shortcomings that hinder open-world performance.
 - **Limited Semantic Coverage:** Datasets frequently focus on common activities or specific domains, resulting in a narrow vocabulary. This restricts models' exposure to the broad spectrum of events and concepts found in real-world videos, particularly rare, abstract, or domain-specific phenomena. The paper highlights that the performance of existing VTG MLLMs is significantly lower when processing queries containing rare concepts (Figure 1a), indicating a deficiency in semantic diversity within training data. For example, Figure 2c illustrates that existing datasets offer substantially less vocabulary coverage compared to the proposed OmniVTG.
 - **Limited Scale and Quality:** Datasets relying on manual annotation are expensive and laborious to scale, leading to smaller data volumes. Conversely, automated pipelines often depend on ASR, which may not ensure precise alignment between spoken queries and visual content. These issues collectively limit the ability of models to generalize across diverse real-world scenarios.

- **Enhancing MLLM Reasoning for Robust Grounding:** While Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on existing or even larger datasets can improve overall VTG performance, a persistent performance gap between common and rare concepts remains (Figure 1b). This suggests that MLLMs require a more sophisticated reasoning mechanism to effectively handle unfamiliar or infrequent concepts.
 - **Leveraging MLLM's Internal Capabilities:** A key observation motivating the proposed methodology is that modern MLLMs exhibit significantly stronger capabilities in general video understanding tasks (e.g., query-clip matching, event status classification) than in direct temporal grounding. Furthermore, this robust understanding ability shows a much smaller performance disparity between rare and common concepts (Figure 1b). The paper also notes that MLLMs demonstrate higher accuracy in generating timestamps during dense captioning compared to direct grounding (Figure 1c). These insights suggest an opportunity to leverage MLLMs' inherent understanding and descriptive abilities to improve their localization performance.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives are:
1. **Develop a large-scale, semantic-rich open-world dataset (OmniVTG):** This dataset is intended to expand semantic coverage, specifically targeting underrepresented rare concepts, and to provide high-quality, timestamped annotations at scale.
2. **Propose a Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm:** This paradigm aims to enhance the grounding capabilities of MLLMs by teaching them to leverage their video understanding abilities to reflect on, verify, and refine their initial temporal grounding predictions, thereby improving robustness, especially for rare concepts.

By addressing both data and model limitations, the work aims to bridge the performance gap in open-world VTG.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces OmniVTG, a large-scale dataset, and a Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm.

#### 3.1. OmniVTG Dataset Construction

The OmniVTG dataset is constructed using a Semantic Coverage Iterative Expansion pipeline, designed to achieve both massive scale and diverse concept coverage. This pipeline consists of three main stages:

- **Target Words Identification:**
 - The process begins by utilizing the vocabulary from the BERT tokenizer, chosen for its broad linguistic representation.
 - This vocabulary is then cleaned using a spell checker to ensure relevance and prevent the inclusion of overly obscure terms.
 - The cleaned vocabulary is compared against the vocabulary present in existing mainstream VTG datasets (e.g., ActivityNet Captions, Charades-STA, QVHighlights) to identify underrepresented "rare" concepts.

- **Interactive Targeted Video Collection:**
 - For identified target rare words, a large language model (LLM), specifically Gemini-2.5 Pro, is prompted to generate more specific, event-centric search keywords. This step addresses the inefficiency of directly searching for generic or abstract words, ensuring that retrieved videos are more likely to contain distinct, locatable events relevant to the target concept (e.g., "meticulous" translated to "watchmaker meticulous assembly movement").
 - These LLM-generated keywords are then used to collect relevant videos from online video platforms (e.g., YouTube, Bilibili).
 - The pipeline is iterative: after a batch of data collection, the semantic coverage of the new data is re-examined, and any still-uncovered vocabulary is used to initiate the next iteration of collection.

- **Automated Annotation:**
 - Leveraging the observation that MLLMs excel at generating precise timestamps for dense captioning more than direct temporal grounding, the task is reformulated. Instead of directly asking the MLLM to localize a query, it is prompted to generate multiple dense, timestamped captions describing events within the video.
 - Crucially, the MLLM (Gemini-2.5 Pro) is explicitly encouraged to use the target rare words identified earlier in its descriptions, ensuring the dataset's semantic richness.
 - **Test Set Annotation:** To ensure high-quality evaluation, a subset of 10,871 videos is randomly selected from the automatically annotated data for manual validation and correction by human annotators. This manually verified subset forms the official OmniVTG test set.

#### 3.2. Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Training Paradigm

The training paradigm is designed to enhance MLLMs' temporal grounding by leveraging their understanding capabilities for self-correction. It comprises a three-stage pipeline:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - This stage aims to establish foundational grounding abilities and the specific understanding skills necessary for the subsequent self-correction process.
 - The MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is fine-tuned on OmniVTG data using a multi-task approach encompassing:
 - **Temporal Grounding:** Predicting start and end timestamps for a given text query.
 - **Event Captioning:** Generating textual descriptions for specified time intervals.
 - **Query-Clip Matching:** Classifying if a query matches a given video interval (match, partial match, mismatch).
 - **Event Status Classification:** Predicting the status of an event (Not Started, In Progress, Ended) at a specific timestamp.

- **Stage 2: Self-Correction CoT Finetuning:**
 - This stage explicitly teaches the MLLM a "predict then correct" reasoning path.
 - Data from OmniVTG is reformatted into a CoT template. For a target event B (from $t_{B_s}$ to $t_{B_e}$), a negative event A (from $t_{A_s}$ to $t_{A_e}$) is selected from the same video's annotations. Event A is chosen to be visually similar to B and to temporally enclose B (i.e., $t_{A_s} \le t_{B_s}$ and $t_{B_e} \le t_{A_e}$).
 - The model is prompted to first predict a coarse segment (e.g., event A) and then refine it to the precise target segment (event B). The CoT format instructs the model to state: "I find that A from $t_{A_s}$ to $t_{A_e}$. Zooming in further, the event B occurs from $t_{B_s}$ to $t_{B_e}$." This process trains the model to utilize its video understanding abilities for verification and correction of its own localization predictions.

- **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - The final stage further refines the model's complex reasoning abilities, particularly on challenging examples.
 - Following the setup of Time-R1, challenging examples (average IoU 0.3) are sampled from the training data.
 - The GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) algorithm is used for policy optimization.
 - A composite reward function, $r(o) = r_{tIoU}(o) + r_{format}(o)$, guides the learning. $r_{tIoU}$ penalizes deviations from ground-truth timestamps based on IoU and relative time differences, while $r_{format}$ encourages adherence to the required reasoning template format. This stage strengthens the "time-aware reasoning" structure, promoting the model to explore and reinforce the content it should first predict in the "predict-then-correct" reasoning path.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

The experiments are conducted using the Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM as the base model. LoRA (rank=8, $\alpha=8$) with a learning rate of 2e-4 is used for SFT and CoT finetuning. For the RL stage, full-parameter fine-tuning of the LLM is performed with a learning rate of 1e-6. The vision encoder remains frozen across all training stages.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents findings related to the OmniVTG dataset's characteristics, the proposed model's performance on public benchmarks, its effectiveness in open-world scenarios with rare concepts, and ablation studies validating the methodological design.

#### 4.1. OmniVTG Dataset Analysis

- **Scale:** OmniVTG comprises 2124.1 hours of video from 46,176 distinct videos, along with 359,221 text queries and corresponding timestamp annotations. This represents a significant increase in scale compared to prior datasets, being more than 4.3 times larger than ActivityNet Captions and 5.0 times larger than QVHighlights in terms of video duration.
- **Query Semantic Diversity:** The dataset exhibits a substantial increase in vocabulary diversity, covering a broader range of unique nouns, verbs, and adjectives. It achieves 95% coverage of target rare vocabulary identified through the Semantic Coverage Iterative Expansion process, in contrast to existing public datasets like ActivityNet Captions, which only reached 48%. The video domain diversity is also broad, categorized into various topics, indicating its suitability for open-world research.
- **Annotation Quality:** Evaluation of the manually-verified test set revealed that 93.82% of the automated labeled timestamps had an Intersection-over-Union (IoU) greater than 0.5 with the corrected manual annotations, demonstrating the high quality of the automated annotation process.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Performance on Public Benchmarks

- The model trained on OmniVTG achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across four established public benchmarks: Charades-STA, ActivityNet Captions, QVHighlights, and TVGBench (Table 2).
- Compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, the OmniVTG-trained model showed substantial improvements (e.g., from 13.6% R1@0.5 on ActivityNet to 39.8%).
- It consistently outperformed other MLLMs designed for VTG, including strong baselines like Time-R1 and UniTime, particularly on the TVGBench which features diverse data sources and query types. For example, on TVGBench, the model achieved 54.5% R1@0.3, surpassing Time-R1 (41.8%) and TRACE (37.0%), indicating enhanced generalization capabilities.

#### 4.3. Open-World Temporal Grounding Performance

- On the OmniVTG Test Set, the proposed model achieved an R1@0.5 of 63.3%, outperforming Time-R1 (33.9%) and UniTime (37.1%) significantly (Table 3).
- Crucially, when evaluated on the OmniVTG Rare subset (containing queries with rare concepts), the model maintained performance close to that on the full set (62.4% R1@0.5 vs. 63.3%). In contrast, Time-R1's performance dropped from 33.9% to 29.4% on the rare subset.
- This trend was corroborated on the ActivityNet Rare subset, where the model's performance remained stable (39.5% R1@0.5 vs. 39.8% on full set), while Time-R1 showed a decline.
- These results suggest that the proposed method is not only more accurate overall but also more robust to rare concepts, effectively narrowing the performance gap between common and rare concepts in open-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of Training Stages (Table 4, Part 1):**
 - The multi-task SFT stage provided the most significant performance boost, improving the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B's R1@0.5 from 34.5% to 62.3% on OmniVTG (Full) and from 13.6% to 25.6% on ActivityNet.
 - Adding the Self-Correction CoT finetuning on top of SFT further improved performance, notably on the OmniVTG Rare subset (from 59.6% to 61.3%) and ActivityNet (from 25.6% to 32.5%), supporting its role in improving generalization.
 - The full model (SFT + CoT + RL) achieved the best performance (63.3% R1@0.5 on OmniVTG Full), demonstrating the complementary nature and essential contribution of all three stages.
 - Directly applying RL after SFT (SFT+RL) resulted in lower performance compared to SFT+CoT+RL, particularly on rare sets, indicating that the explicit self-correction CoT provides a superior reasoning path.
- **Impact of SFT Data Scale (Table 4, Part 2):** Performance scaled consistently with increasing amounts of OmniVTG SFT data, confirming the effectiveness of the large-scale dataset.
- **Comparison of Reasoning Strategy (Table 4, Part 3):** Both rule-based and content-aware self-correction CoT strategies outperformed the baseline without reasoning. The content-aware reflection, which uses semantically similar video content for initial predictions, performed best (63.3% R1@0.5 on OmniVTG Full), suggesting its efficacy in guiding the correction process.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

- A qualitative example (Figure 4) demonstrated the self-correction mechanism. In a query involving a "strapless" wedding dress, the model initially predicted an incorrect segment containing a dress with straps, similar to Time-R1. However, during its self-correction phase, the proposed model identified and refined its prediction to the correct segment showing a strapless dress, while Time-R1 returned the initial incorrect answer. This illustrates the explicit self-reflection and refinement process learned by the model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents two primary contributions: the OmniVTG dataset and the Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm, both of which have notable significance and potential impact on the field of video temporal grounding and broader multimodal AI.

#### 5.1. Dataset Contribution

The introduction of OmniVTG addresses a critical limitation in the existing landscape of VTG datasets—namely, insufficient scale, limited semantic diversity, and poor open-world applicability.
- **Enabling Open-World Research:** By significantly expanding the vocabulary coverage, especially for rare concepts, and encompassing a wide range of video domains, OmniVTG provides a more realistic and challenging benchmark for open-world VTG. This allows researchers to develop and evaluate models that can generalize beyond common, domain-specific events, moving closer to real-world deployment scenarios.
- **Automated and Scalable Annotation:** The novel Semantic Coverage Iterative Expansion pipeline, leveraging MLLMs for dense captioning, offers a scalable and efficient method for generating high-quality timestamped annotations. This approach mitigates the cost and labor intensity of purely manual annotation while ensuring higher precision than traditional ASR-based methods.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** OmniVTG provides a robust foundation for developing and testing next-generation MLLMs capable of nuanced video understanding and precise temporal localization, especially for complex and rare events.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contribution

The Self-Correction Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm represents an advancement in how MLLMs are trained for temporal grounding.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By explicitly training MLLMs to "predict then correct" through a three-stage pipeline (SFT, CoT Finetuning, RL), the method instills a more robust and explicit reasoning mechanism. This directly leverages the MLLM's stronger video understanding abilities to refine its less robust direct grounding predictions.
- **Improved Robustness to Rare Concepts:** The demonstrated ability of the model to maintain performance on rare concepts, where other methods falter, is a significant step towards practical, real-world VTG systems. This addresses a major bottleneck in existing models, which often exhibit a performance degradation when encountering less frequent or abstract linguistic terms.
- **Generalization to Unseen Data:** The superior zero-shot performance on diverse public benchmarks indicates that the training on OmniVTG combined with the self-correction paradigm fosters strong generalization capabilities, allowing the model to perform well even on datasets with different distributions.

#### 5.3. Broader Implications

- **Advancement in Multimodal AI:** The work contributes to the broader development of multimodal AI by showcasing an effective strategy for integrating and leveraging different internal capabilities of MLLMs (e.g., dense captioning vs. direct grounding, understanding vs. localization) to achieve more sophisticated task performance.
- **Real-world Applications:** The enhanced accuracy and robustness of temporal grounding, particularly for diverse and rare concepts, can lead to more effective real-world applications. These include improved video search engines that can accurately locate specific, complex events described by user queries, more precise content analysis for media production, and advanced tools for video surveillance or autonomous systems requiring fine-grained event detection. The ability to handle "open-world" scenarios means these systems can operate more reliably outside of tightly controlled environments.

In summary, this research provides both a substantial dataset and an innovative training methodology that collectively push the boundaries of open-world video temporal grounding, addressing key challenges and paving the way for more capable and generalizable multimodal AI systems.
