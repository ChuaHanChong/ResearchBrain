# AeroAct: Action-Centered World-Action Models for Language-Conditioned Quadrotor Flight

> [!summary] Summary
> AEROACT is an action-centered World-Action Model (WAM) developed at the Beijing Institute of Technology for language-conditioned quadrotor flight, generating smooth local trajectory chunks. It utilizes future visual prediction during training while decoupling it from inference, which enabled robust closed-loop flight in simulation with 100% success and 0% collisions, culminating in the first reported real-world flight of a WAM-based quadrotor.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language navigation (VLN) methods are ill-suited for quadrotors due to their reliance on discrete actions and inability to handle continuous, dynamics-constrained aerial flight.
- Learning-based models often lack explicit modeling of the visual consequences of actions, which is crucial for aerial robots where ego-motion rapidly changes the visual stream and actions can lead to instability.
- A scarcity of multimodal, tightly synchronized datasets for training World-Action Models (WAMs) for quadrotors, combined with the computational expense and latency of video-centric WAMs during inference, hinders real-world deployment.

## Method

- Develops **AeroAct**, an action-centered ==World-Action Model (WAM)== based on a ==video diffusion Transformer==, predicting smooth, ==fifth-order polynomial trajectory segments==, and decoupling visual prediction from inference for efficiency.
- Utilizes a hybrid data generation pipeline, combining high-fidelity simulation and diverse ==3D Gaussian Splatting== environments with a low-cost real-world handheld data collection method.
- Incorporates an ==inference-time self-guidance== procedure to ensure temporal consistency and smoothness across overlapping predicted trajectory chunks for stable quadrotor control.

## Results

- Achieved **100%** view success in target tracking and **100%** success with **0%** collisions in language-conditioned object search tasks in simulation by utilizing extended temporal context (**nine** reference frames).
- Maintained strong performance after finetuning with real-world data, demonstrating **100%** success rates and **0%** collisions in simulated tracking and searching tasks.
- Successfully demonstrated the first reported real-world quadrotor flight using a World-Action Model (WAM) policy, enabling the physical robot to navigate toward a language-specified object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual consequence prediction during training from its omission during inference allows World-Action Models to leverage dense visual supervision for robust action distribution shaping while maintaining real-time efficiency and avoiding error accumulation in deployment.
> - A hybrid data pipeline, combining diverse simulated environments (Isaac Lab, 3DGS) with low-cost real-world data collection, is crucial for addressing data scarcity in complex aerial robotics and bridging the sim-to-real gap.
> - Incorporating extended temporal visual context and inference-time self-guidance for trajectory continuity significantly improves quadrotor control robustness, leading to higher success rates and fewer collisions in language-conditioned tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in language-conditioned quadrotor flight, a specialized area within robotics that extends beyond traditional vision-language navigation (VLN). Existing VLN methods for ground robots often utilize discrete actions or high-level waypoints, which are not directly applicable to the continuous, dynamics-constrained, and safety-critical nature of quadrotor flight. Learning-based agile flight techniques, while successful in reactive control for tasks like racing or obstacle avoidance, typically rely on task-specific rewards or geometric goals, rather than open-vocabulary language instructions.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have improved semantic generalization by integrating language with action outputs. However, these models frequently map observations and language directly to actions without explicitly modeling the visual consequences of those actions. This gap is significant in aerial robotics, where ego-motion rapidly alters the first-person visual stream, and inappropriate actions can lead to instability or collision.

The paper positions itself within the emerging field of World-Action Models (WAMs). WAMs build upon advancements in world models (e.g., Dreamer, Genie), which learn predictive dynamics, by jointly training visual consequence prediction and action generation. This joint training allows dense video supervision to regularize the learned action distribution, providing a more robust foundation for control and planning. While WAMs have been explored for tabletop manipulation tasks, their application to complex aerial navigation, characterized by rapidly changing egocentric views, dynamic constraints, and safety requirements, presents distinct challenges that AeroAct aims to address. Specifically, AeroAct focuses on making WAMs practical for real-world quadrotor deployment, emphasizing smooth, dynamically feasible actions and efficient inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and demonstrate an action-centered World-Action Model (WAM), named AeroAct, for language-conditioned quadrotor flight that can operate effectively in real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and aims to address specific limitations in current approaches:

1. **Bridging Semantic Grounding with Quadrotor Dynamics:** A key motivation is the need for aerial robots to execute natural-language commands while maintaining safe and dynamically stable flight through cluttered 3D environments. Existing methods often fail to adequately connect high-level semantic goals, derived from language, with the low-level, continuous control references required for quadrotor dynamics. AeroAct aims to provide a policy that outputs local, smooth, and dynamically executable trajectory segments directly, rather than discrete actions or instantaneous velocity commands, which offer limited supervision regarding their visual and dynamical consequences.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Aerial WAMs:** Training WAMs requires extensive multimodal datasets comprising tightly synchronized egocentric video, language instructions, proprioceptive states, and dynamically feasible flight actions. Such data is significantly more challenging and risky to collect for quadrotors compared to ground or manipulation robots due to the speed, safety-critical nature, and underactuated dynamics of aerial platforms. A core objective is to develop a scalable and efficient data generation pipeline to overcome this bottleneck.

3. **Enhancing Inference Efficiency and Robustness for Real-time Flight:** Video-centric WAMs can be computationally expensive at inference time, as autoregressive future-video generation increases latency. Furthermore, small errors in visual prediction can accumulate and negatively impact downstream action decoding, which is particularly problematic for aerial robots requiring smooth, stable trajectory references. AeroAct aims to design a WAM that leverages dense visual consequence supervision during training but performs action decoding efficiently during deployment without generating future video, thereby reducing latency and avoiding error propagation.

4. **Ensuring Temporal Consistency and Smoothness in Quadrotor Trajectories:** Independent sampling of action chunks in diffusion-based models can lead to discontinuities in successive control commands, causing jitter or instability. An objective is to develop mechanisms, such as inference-time self-guidance, to ensure temporal consistency and smoothness across overlapping predicted trajectory chunks, critical for stable quadrotor operation.

5. **Demonstrating Real-World Deployability:** Ultimately, the research is motivated by the goal of demonstrating that WAM-based policies can be successfully executed on a physical quadrotor. This involves tackling the practical challenges of integrating the learned policy with a real-world aerial platform, including hardware limitations and computational requirements.

By addressing these motivations, AeroAct aims to advance the capabilities of language-conditioned aerial robots by offering a more robust, efficient, and dynamically informed approach to control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AeroAct employs an action-centered World-Action Model (WAM) framework, adapting a pre-trained video diffusion Transformer for language-conditioned quadrotor flight. The methodology focuses on novel architectures, data generation, and control strategies to achieve smooth, dynamically feasible aerial navigation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as sequential prediction from first-person sensing. At each time step *t*, the quadrotor observes a history of egocentric RGB images ($O_h^t$), a proprioceptive state ($s_t$), and a natural language instruction ($l$). The policy predicts a *chunk* of local, fifth-order trajectory parameters ($a_{t:t+p-1}$) of length *p*. This action chunk represents a dynamically executable local trajectory segment rather than discrete commands. The model $g_\Theta$ is unified for both action prediction and visual consequence modeling, meaning it predicts both the action chunk and latent representations ($c_t$) for future visual observations ($o_{t+\Delta}, \dots, o_{t+K\Delta}$). During the current implementation, a temporal stride $\Delta = 3$ is used, with each action chunk containing $K=8$ future visual frames and 24 low-level action steps.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

AeroAct leverages a 1.3B-parameter Wan video diffusion Transformer (Wan2.1-1.3B) as its backbone.
The architecture comprises several key components:
- **Input Encoders:** At training time, a clip of visual observations is encoded into spatiotemporal latent tokens using a frozen video VAE. The proprioceptive state ($s_t$) and action chunk ($a_{t:t+p-1}$) are projected by MLP encoders into state tokens ($T_s$) and action tokens ($T_a$). The language instruction ($l$) is encoded by a pre-trained text encoder into language tokens ($T_l$).
- **Transformer Backbone:** Non-language tokens ($T_s, T_o, T_a, T_f$) are concatenated into a single sequence, with language tokens ($T_l$) provided as cross-attention context. A 3D RoPE positional encoding is applied to the visual tokens.
- **Blockwise Causal Masking:** A critical design choice is the implementation of a blockwise causal mask. This mask ensures that during training:
 - State and reference visual tokens ($T_s, T_o$) cannot attend to future predicted tokens ($T_a, T_f$).
 - Action tokens ($T_a$) can attend only to state, reference, and other action tokens.
 - Future visual tokens ($T_f$) can attend to all tokens.
 This masking prevents action prediction from "leaking" information from future frames and maintains the action-consequence ordering.
- **Inference-time Efficiency:** During deployment, the model omits the future visual tokens ($T_f$) and decodes only the action stream. This design significantly reduces computational cost (by 37.8% in experiments) and prevents error accumulation that could arise from autoregressive generation of future visual frames, which is vital for real-time aerial control.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained with a combined loss function: $L(\Theta) = L_{act} + \lambda_{vis}L_{vis}$. $L_{act}$ is a diffusion or flow-matching loss for action prediction, while $L_{vis}$ is a similar loss for future-frame latent prediction. The visual term acts as dense consequence supervision during training, shaping the action distribution, but is not used during closed-loop deployment.

#### 3.3. Data Generation

To address the challenge of data scarcity for aerial WAMs, AeroAct develops a hybrid data pipeline that combines simulation and real-world data sources:
- **DiffAero-based Simulation:** The core of the simulation pipeline is DiffAero, which provides fast quadrotor dynamics and flight controllers. This common interface is shared by two rendering branches:
 - **Isaac Lab Branch:** Offers realistic lighting, materials, shadows, and camera effects, providing high visual fidelity. Scene diversity is limited by asset availability.
 - **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Branch:** Renders RGB and depth from Gaussian-splat scenes, enabling easier scaling of scene and object diversity with lower asset-engineering costs. This complements Isaac Lab by providing a wider variety of environments.
- **Simulation Scenarios:** Three types of demonstrations are collected:
 - **Isaac tracking:** An expert controller tracks a moving target in random corridors, training motion anticipation and visual servoing.
 - **Isaac reaching:** The quadrotor approaches a language-specified object among distractors, linking language grounding to goal-directed flight.
 - **3DGS reaching:** Scenes and objects are sampled and merged, with the quadrotor approaching a target object from randomized initial states.
 These scenarios collectively train collision-aware local trajectory generation, language grounding in egocentric views, and visual consequence forecasting.
- **Real-World Data Collection:** A low-cost handheld device is introduced for efficient real-world data collection. It consists of a fisheye camera, an Intel RealSense T265 stereo camera for odometry estimation, and a lightweight computer. The device's motion is converted into simulated quadrotor poses, which are used to crop fisheye images into quadrotor-consistent first-person views. This method facilitates low-cost fine-tuning with real-world egocentric trajectory data without extensive autonomous flight. The collected dataset comprises 858 trajectories (approximately 3 hours of data).

#### 3.4. Smooth Trajectory Generation with Self-Guidance

AeroAct addresses the issue of temporal inconsistencies between independently sampled action chunks (common in diffusion models) by introducing an inference-time self-guidance procedure. When consecutive action chunks overlap in time (due to replanning before the previous chunk expires), the prefix velocity of the newly sampled chunk is constrained by the corresponding suffix of the previously executed chunk. This ensures temporal continuity and reduces discontinuities in the reference trajectory, which is crucial for stable quadrotor control. The action space itself parameterizes local fifth-order polynomial trajectory segments, providing a smooth and dynamically feasible control interface. Each action chunk specifies endpoint position, velocity, and acceleration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both closed-loop simulation experiments and real-world quadrotor flights, evaluating the effectiveness of AeroAct's design choices.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

**Impact of Temporal Context:**
- **Target Tracking:** When AeroAct was configured with only one reference frame (minimal temporal context), it exhibited limited performance in closed-loop tracking, achieving a 20.0% view success rate (target in view for at least 80% of the trajectory) and a high collision rate of 90.0% at 1 Hz inference frequency. This indicated that a single egocentric image was insufficient for robust relative motion estimation and safe trajectory generation.
- **Enhanced Performance with Extended Context:** Increasing the temporal context to nine reference frames significantly improved performance. In tracking, it achieved a 100.0% view success rate and a 0.0% collision rate across all tested inference frequencies (1, 2, and 5 Hz). The average final distance to the target also substantially decreased, for instance, from 7.902 m with one frame to 3.436 m with nine frames at 1 Hz.
- **Language-Conditioned Object Search:** Similar improvements were observed in the language-conditioned object search task. With one reference frame, success rates were around 75.0% to 90.0% with collision rates of 10.0% to 20.0%. With nine reference frames, AeroAct consistently achieved 100.0% success and 0.0% collisions across all frequencies. The average final distance to the target also improved from around 2.616 m (1 Hz, 1 frame) to 1.982 m (1 Hz, 9 frames).
- **Generalization to Unseen Objects:** The benefits of extended temporal context also translated to scenarios involving unseen target objects in the searching task. Success rates increased from 75.0% (1 frame) to 100.0% (9 frames), and the average final distance to the target decreased from 3.819 m to 1.983 m.

**Effect of Real-World Finetuning (AeroAct-FT):**
- The model finetuned with real-world handheld data (AeroAct-FT) maintained strong performance in simulation. For both tracking and searching tasks with nine reference frames, it achieved 100.0% view success in tracking (with minor variation for 1 Hz) and 100.0% success in searching, consistently exhibiting 0.0% collisions. This indicates that integrating real-world data for adaptation did not substantially degrade the learned closed-loop policy's performance in simulation.

#### 4.2. Real-World Flight

- **Platform and Setup:** Real-world experiments were conducted using a modified OddityRC 35Pro quadrotor equipped with an Intel RealSense D435i camera and a Radxa ROCK 5C onboard computer. A motion-capture system was used for state measurement and safety. Inference was performed offboard on a remote workstation (NVIDIA RTX 5090 Laptop GPU) due to the computational demands, with a latency of approximately 0.8 seconds.
- **First WAM-based Quadrotor Flight:** AeroAct successfully demonstrated the first known real-world quadrotor flight using a WAM-based policy. In an example experiment, given the instruction "fly to the yellow foam mat," the model generated feasible flight commands and guided the quadrotor toward the specified target object within a laboratory environment.
- **Execution of Learned Policy:** The demonstration illustrated the closed-loop execution of the learned WAM policy on a physical aerial robot, connecting visual-language task specification with predictive action generation and actual quadrotor control. This finding validates the practical applicability of the proposed action-centered WAM design for embodied aerial intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AeroAct research contributes to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence by advancing language-conditioned aerial navigation, particularly through the novel application and design of World-Action Models (WAMs) for quadrotors.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **First Real-World WAM for Aerial Flight:** AeroAct represents, to the best of the authors' knowledge, the first instantiation and demonstration of a WAM for real-world aerial flight on a physical quadrotor. This is a significant step beyond simulation, indicating the feasibility of using complex generative models for safety-critical, dynamics-constrained robotic tasks.
2. **Action-Centered WAM Design for Quadrotors:** The introduction of an action-centered WAM tailored for quadrotors is a notable advancement. By outputting smooth local trajectory chunks instead of discrete actions or instantaneous velocities, AeroAct provides a control interface more compatible with quadrotor dynamics. The use of future egocentric visual prediction as training-time consequence supervision, while decoupling it from inference-time action generation, offers a method to harness the benefits of generative video models without incurring their computational overhead or error accumulation during deployment. This design choice contributes to both robustness and efficiency.
3. **Hybrid Data Generation Pipeline:** The scalable data-generation pipeline, integrating DiffAero dynamics with Isaac Lab and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering, is a practical solution to the data scarcity problem inherent in training advanced aerial robotics policies. The complementary nature of Isaac Lab (visual fidelity) and 3DGS (scene diversity) allows for the creation of diverse, high-quality multimodal data. The development of a low-cost handheld device for real-world data collection further enhances the ability to gather valuable fine-tuning data, bridging the sim-to-real gap efficiently.
4. **Temporal Consistency through Self-Guidance:** The formulation of a smooth chunked action space, combined with the inference-time self-guidance procedure, addresses the challenge of temporal inconsistency across overlapping action chunks generated by diffusion models. This mechanism is crucial for ensuring stable and smooth flight trajectories, which are essential for practical quadrotor control.
5. **Improved Performance with Temporal Context:** The empirical findings demonstrate that temporal visual context significantly enhances performance in both target tracking and object search tasks, leading to higher success rates and reduced collisions in simulation. This highlights the importance of incorporating historical observations for robust navigation in dynamic environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancing Embodied AI:** AeroAct pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by enabling aerial robots to understand and execute complex, natural-language commands in physical environments. This capability is critical for a wide range of applications, from search and rescue to inspection and delivery.
- **Bridging Simulation and Reality:** The hybrid data collection strategy and successful real-world deployment demonstrate effective strategies for transferring policies learned primarily in simulation to real physical systems, a long-standing challenge in robotics.
- **Foundation for Future Aerial Systems:** This work lays a foundation for future aerial intelligence, suggesting that WAMs can be a viable paradigm for developing highly capable, autonomous quadrotors. Future work focusing on onboard efficiency, robustness in more aggressive flight conditions, uncertainty-aware actions, and long-horizon multi-stage instruction following could further expand the impact of this approach.
- **Beyond Aerial Robotics:** The action-centered WAM design, inference-time efficiency techniques, and hybrid data generation strategies could potentially inspire similar advancements in other domains of robotics where complex dynamics, real-time constraints, and high-fidelity sensing are critical.

While current limitations include short indoor trajectories and reliance on offboard inference, the successful real-world demonstration of a WAM-based policy for language-conditioned quadrotor flight marks a significant step toward more intelligent and autonomous aerial robots.
