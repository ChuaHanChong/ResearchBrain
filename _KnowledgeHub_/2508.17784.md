# Proximal Supervised Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Proximal Supervised Fine-Tuning (PSFT) introduces a PPO-inspired clipped objective to stabilize large language model fine-tuning, preventing entropy collapse and catastrophic forgetting. This approach yields a more robust and generalized base model, serving as a superior "cold start" for subsequent reinforcement learning from human feedback or direct preference optimization, which ultimately leads to enhanced performance across various tasks.

## Problem

- Standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often leads to "catastrophic forgetting" or "capability erosion," resulting in poor generalization to out-of-domain tasks.
- SFT can cause "entropy collapse," making the model overly confident and reducing its exploration capacity, which is detrimental for subsequent reinforcement learning (RL) stages.
- SFT-trained models provide a suboptimal "cold start" for RL-based alignment, limiting the effectiveness and stability of advanced post-training techniques like RLHF or DPO.

## Method

- ==Proximal Supervised Fine-Tuning (PSFT)== adapts the ==clipped surrogate objective== from Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to the SFT setting, drawing inspiration from trust-region methods.
- The method frames SFT as a special case of ==policy gradient== with constant positive advantages, enabling the application of stability-enhancing principles from RL.
- PSFT regularizes SFT updates by limiting how much the probability of an action under the new policy can deviate from its probability under a previous policy, preventing drastic changes.

## Results

- PSFT consistently maintains smoother entropy curves and matches or slightly surpasses standard SFT on in-domain math reasoning tasks while significantly improving out-of-domain generalization and instruction-following capabilities.
- Models initialized with PSFT achieve higher entropy during subsequent RL training, leading to superior final performance on both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks compared to SFT-initialized models.
- PSFT reduces the **"alignment tax"** and enables more effective Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), resulting in better human alignment on benchmarks like AlpacaEval2 and MT-Bench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning can be theoretically understood as a policy gradient method where expert actions are treated with a constant positive advantage.
> - Applying a PPO-like clipped surrogate objective to SFT effectively prevents entropy collapse and preserves generalization capabilities by constraining policy updates.
> - A more robust and entropy-preserving SFT output provides a significantly better initial policy, leading to greater exploration and superior final performance in subsequent RL-based alignment stages.
