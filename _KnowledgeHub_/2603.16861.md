# MolmoB0T: Large-Scale Simulation Enables Zero-Shot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the Allen Institute for AI introduces MolmoBot, a system that achieves robust zero-shot real-world robotic manipulation using policies trained exclusively on large-scale, procedurally generated simulation data. The MolmoBot (F=2) policy attained a 79.2% success rate on real-world tabletop pick-and-place tasks with a Franka FR3, outperforming prior state-of-the-art systems trained on extensive real-world data.

## Problem

- Existing 'robotics foundation models' from industrial labs often rely on proprietary real-world data and undisclosed methods, hindering open research and reproducibility.
- The prevailing assumption in robotics is that simulation alone is insufficient for robust manipulation policies, necessitating real-world data or fine-tuning to bridge the 'sim-to-real gap'.
- There is a need for generalist manipulation policies that can perform diverse tasks across various robotic platforms without task-specific real-world training.

## Method

- Developed **MolmoBot-Engine**, an open-source, scalable ==procedural data generation== pipeline leveraging 232,000 indoor environments and 48,000 manipulable objects in ==MuJoCo==, generating 1.8 million expert trajectories.
- Trained multiple policy architectures, including a novel ==multi-frame vision-language model== (**MolmoBot**) and an architectural replication of a state-of-the-art model (**MolmoBot-Pi0**), exclusively on the synthetic MolmoBot-Data.
- Employed extensive ==domain randomization== for visual and physical parameters, diverse object sampling, and language instructions with ==referring expressions== to enhance ==sim-to-real transfer== without real-world fine-tuning or photorealistic rendering.

## Results

- MolmoBot (F=2) achieved a **79.2%** success rate on real-world tabletop pick-and-place tasks (Franka FR3), significantly outperforming baselines like π0.5-DROID (**39.2%**) trained on real-world data.
- MolmoBot-Pi0, trained from scratch on MolmoBot-Data using the π0 architecture, achieved **46.7%** real-world success with the Franka FR3, demonstrating the substantial impact of the synthetic data itself.
- The MolmoBot Door Specialist policy achieved **77.7%** success in simulation on door opening, and showed partial real-world success with a Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1, grasping the handle in **4 out of 9** trials and fully opening the door in **2 out of 9** trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, procedurally generated simulation data, when sufficiently diverse, can enable robust zero-shot transfer to real-world manipulation tasks, challenging the traditional view of the 'sim-to-real gap'.
> - Open-sourcing the MolmoBot-Engine and MolmoBot-Data democratizes access to tools and knowledge for building robotics foundation models, fostering community-wide study.
> - For real-world transfer, an absolute joint position action representation substantially outperformed delta action representations, despite similar simulation performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in robot learning: the sim-to-real gap. A prevailing view in the field suggests that simulation alone is insufficient for robust real-world robot manipulation, necessitating real-world data collection or task-specific fine-tuning for effective transfer. This assumption has contributed to the concentration of generalist robot learning model development in well-resourced industrial labs, which possess the means for extensive real-world data collection. Recent efforts by entities such as NVIDIA (GR00T), Physical Intelligence (π0), and Google DeepMind (Gemini Robotics) exemplify this trend, often with partial disclosure of the underlying data mixtures, collection processes, and training methodologies. This limits the broader academic community's ability to replicate and advance research in this area.

The presented research challenges this conventional assumption by proposing that sufficiently large-scale and diverse simulated synthetic training data can enable effective zero-shot transfer to physical environments for both static and mobile manipulation. This approach draws inspiration from prior work, SPOC, which demonstrated that scaled simulation data could facilitate zero-shot transfer for navigation tasks. The paper distinguishes itself from existing vision-language-action (VLA) models, such as RT-1, RT-2, and π0, which primarily rely on large-scale real-world robot demonstration datasets. By focusing exclusively on simulation-generated trajectories, this work aims to provide an open-source, scalable alternative for training generalist robotic foundation models, making the process more accessible to the research community. It also extends existing work on large-scale synthetic datasets and procedural generation by specifically focusing on diverse articulated and mobile manipulation scenarios, which remain underexplored in large-scale imitation learning frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to demonstrate that large-scale and diverse simulated synthetic training data can enable effective zero-shot transfer of manipulation policies to the real world, without requiring any real-world data collection or task-specific fine-tuning. This directly challenges the widely held assumption that real-world data is indispensable for bridging the sim-to-real gap in robot learning.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Challenging the Sim-to-Real Gap Assumption:** The authors seek to empirically test the hypothesis that extensive scaling of simulated data, encompassing diversity in environments, robot embodiments, articulated assets, and tasks, can circumvent the need for real-world adaptation.
- **Democratizing Robotics Foundation Model Development:** By open-sourcing the data generation pipeline and the generated dataset, the project aims to make the knowledge and tools for building robotics foundation models more broadly accessible. This addresses the current landscape where much of the critical information for training such systems is concentrated within a few industrial labs.
- **Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** Real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and logistically complex. Developing a simulation-only training paradigm offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative for generating vast quantities of diverse training data.
- **Extending Zero-Shot Transfer to Manipulation:** Building on the success of SPOC in navigation, the researchers aimed to investigate if the same principle—scaled simulated data for imitation learning—could be successfully applied to the more complex domain of manipulation.
- **Supporting Generalist Robot Policies:** The goal is to produce robust manipulation policies that can generalize broadly to various unseen objects, environments, and tasks, including both rigid and articulated object manipulation, as well as mobile manipulation.

To achieve these objectives, the research introduces and releases three core components:
1. **MolmoBot-Engine:** A fully open-source pipeline for procedural data generation.
2. **MolmoBot-Data:** A large-scale dataset of 1.7 million expert trajectories for manipulation tasks.
3. **MolmoBot (and variants):** Multiple policy classes trained on this synthetic data for evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around generating a large-scale, diverse dataset purely in simulation and training various robot policies on this data to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

#### 3.1. MolmoBot-Engine: Scalable Manipulation Data Engine

MolmoBot-Engine is an open-source pipeline for procedural data generation. Its core insight is that diversity in objects, configurations, and viewpoints is more critical than photorealistic rendering for manipulation policies. Data is generated in MuJoCo using procedurally generated MolmoSpaces environments with extensive domain randomization.
- **MolmoSpaces Environments and Assets:** Leverages a collection of 200k+ procedurally generated indoor environments from MolmoSpaces, featuring varied layouts, furniture, and static objects. Task-relevant rigid objects (from iTHOR and Objaverse) and articulated assets are sampled and placed.
- **Domain Randomization:** Applied extensively across environment, action, and camera parameters, as well as during model training via image augmentation. Environment randomization includes lighting (1-N lights, positions, intensities, colors, shadows), textures (procedural and real-world maps), dynamics (friction, mass, joint damping), and 6-DoF pose randomization for manipulable assets.

#### 3.2. Robot Configuration

Data is generated for two robot platforms:
- **Franka FR3:** A 7-DoF arm with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, fixed on a pedestal. Data generation and evaluation are at 15 Hz, matching DROID configuration.
- **Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1:** A mobile manipulator with a 3-DoF holonomic base, 6-DoF torso, 2-DoF head, and two 7-DoF arms with parallel-jaw grippers.
- **Initial Joint-Configuration Randomization:** Joint positions are perturbed from nominal home configurations, with larger perturbations for distal joints.
- **Action Noise Injection:** Action-proportional noise is injected during data collection to promote robustness, applied in TCP space for arms and directly to (x,y,θ) for the RB-Y1 base.
- **Gripper Handling:** Fixed durations for gripper commands and settle periods simulate real-world conditions.

#### 3.3. Sensor Configuration

- **FR3 Camera System:** Five viewpoints: a wrist camera, a fixed shoulder camera, and three randomized exocentric cameras (two ZED2 analogues, one GoPro analogue). Rendered at 624x352.
- **RB-Y1 Camera System:** Three cameras matching the real robot: a head-mounted GoPro analogue (cropped to 768x576) and left/right wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D405 analogues (1024x576). Fisheye warping is applied to head camera per-frame during training.
- **Proprioception and Additional Sensors:** Records joint positions and velocities, TCP poses, robot base pose, action labels (absolute/relative joint positions, end-effector twist/pose), task state (object poses, grasp state, policy phase, retry counts), and camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters. Depth images are recorded but not used in training for this work.

#### 3.4. Task Definitions

- **Rigid Object Manipulation:**
 - **Pick:** Grasp and lift a target object by at least 1 cm.
 - **Pick-and-place:** Transport an object to a specified receptacle.
 - **Pick-and-place-next-to:** Place an object adjacent to a reference object on the same surface.
 - **Pick-and-place-color:** Place an object on a receptacle identified by color.
- **Articulated Object Manipulation (RB-Y1 only):**
 - **Open:** Open an articulated object (cabinet, drawer, oven, dishwasher) to at least 67% joint range.
 - **Open-door:** Open a hinged door to at least 67% hinge joint range, with instruction conditioned on robot pose ("push" or "pull").
- **Language Instructions:** Natural-language instructions with referential expressions procedurally sampled at episode initialization, using CLIP-based similarity to ensure diversity and unambiguous context.

#### 3.5. Expert Planners

Scripted demonstrators generate expert trajectories by iteratively sampling grasps, verifying feasibility, and executing motion for each task phase.
- **Grasp Sampling and Filtering:** Pre-computed 6-DoF grasp candidates are loaded, transformed, ranked (by TCP proximity, rotation similarity, vertical alignment, COM distance), and filtered for gripper-scene collision and kinematic reachability.
- **Phase-Based Trajectory Generation:** Tasks are decomposed into fixed sequences of phases (e.g., Pregrasp → Grasp → Lift → Preplace → Place → Post-place → Stow for pick-and-place).
- **Retry Behavior:** Demonstrators reset and retry if mistakes occur (max 3 retries), imbuing policies with recovery capabilities.
- **Motion Planning for RB-Y1 with CuRobo:** Uses GPU-accelerated collision-aware trajectory optimization for 23-DOF whole-body kinematics, planning in batches, and re-planning when necessary.

#### 3.6. Dataset Statistics

MolmoBot-Data comprises 1.7 million episodes (295.2M frames), 11.4k+ unique target object assets, 9.4k+ receptacle assets, and 94.2k+ environments. This totals 5,704 hours of robot experience. It represents a 2.6x data throughput compared to real-world collection at equivalent scale, generated in approximately 6,500 GPU-hours using 100 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

#### 3.7. Models and Training

Three policy classes are trained on MolmoBot-Data:
- **MolmoBot (VLM-based manipulation policy):**
 - Built on Molmo2-4B, a vision-language model.
 - **Vision Encoder:** Frozen SigLIP2 encodes RGB observations; supports up to 3 frames per view.
 - **LLM:** Processes visual tokens and tokenized language instructions, optionally conditioned on 2D point coordinates for spatial grounding.
 - **Action Head:** DiT-based flow matching iteratively denoises action chunks (H=16 actions) conditioned on a continuous timestep embedding. Layerwise coupled to the VLM backbone, cross-attending to LLM hidden states and incorporating robot-state features.
 - **Action Representation:** Trained with either absolute joint positions or joint position deltas in joint space. RB-Y1 policies additionally predict base velocity commands.
 - **Training:** Behavior cloning objective, batch size 1024, 200k steps (static) / 100k steps (mobile). Upsamples steps with retry grasping, successful picks, and task completion. Parallel denoising over T=8 timesteps per example.
- **MolmoBot-Pi0:**
 - Replicates the architecture of π0 (Paligemma 3B VLM with flow-matching action expert), trained entirely on MolmoBot-Data from initial Paligemma weights.
 - Used for controlled comparison to isolate the effect of the dataset.
- **MolmoBot-SPOC (Lightweight transformer policy):**
 - Inspired by SPOC, a transformer-based architecture for navigation.
 - **Encoding:** SigLIP2-Base image encoder and SigLIP text encoder. Robot's current joint state projected into token dimension. Optional point-based goal encodings (sinusoidal positional encoder). Does not use trajectory history, optionally uses initial-timestep images.
 - **Action Representation:** Continuous action values tokenized using quantile binning (256 bins), predicting a categorical distribution over bins for each action dimension.
 - **Parallel Action Decoding:** Non-causal parallel decoder predicts an entire chunk of D x T action tokens (T=16) in a single forward pass using learnable query embeddings and sinusoidal positional encodings.

#### 3.8. Implementation Details

- **Data Mixing:** Specific ratios for Franka FR3 tasks (Pick, Pick-and-place Fixed/Random Height, PnP Next-To, PnP Color) and RB-Y1 tasks (Open, Door-open, Pick, Pick-and-place) to ensure coverage and robustness.
- **Data Augmentation:** ColorJitter, GaussianBlur, RandomPosterize, RandomSharpness, RandomGrayscale, and prompt randomization.
- **DROID Input/Output:** Franka FR3 policies use wrist and one randomized exocentric camera, current joint angles, and output absolute joint position commands.
- **RB-Y1 Input/Output:** RB-Y1 policies use head and both wrist cameras, current joint angles. MolmoBot-SPOC Articulated uses repeated first frame and normalized image point. Output delta joint position commands for torso/arm and linear/angular offsets for mobile base.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents results from real-world zero-shot transfer evaluations and extensive simulation evaluations, alongside ablations on data and model parameters.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Real-World Transfer

- **Baselines:** For DROID evaluations, comparisons were made against π0-DROID and π0.5-DROID, which are state-of-the-art open-weights manipulation policies trained on 10k+ hours of real-world data. For RB-Y1, MolmoBot policies were noted as among the first generalizable policies for this platform.
- **Static Manipulation (Franka FR3):**
 - MolmoBot policies (MolmoBot and MolmoBot-Img) demonstrated strong zero-shot sim2real transfer, significantly outperforming π0.5-DROID. On tabletop pick-and-place, MolmoBot achieved a 79.2% success rate across four real-world settings, compared to π0.5's 39.2%.
 - MolmoBot-Pi0, using the identical architecture to π0 but trained on MolmoBot-Data, achieved a 46.7% success rate, outperforming π0 (31.3%) and being competitive with π0.5, suggesting that data diversity from simulation significantly contributes to performance.
- **Mobile Manipulation (RB-Y1):**
 - MolmoBot Door Specialist policy was evaluated on door opening across three distinct real-world environments. 4 out of 9 trials achieved handle grasp success, and 2 out of 9 achieved full door opening. Failures often attributed to underrepresentation of specific handle configurations in training data or hardware faults. In cases where faults occurred during the opening phase, the policy had often committed to a successful trajectory.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluation

- **Setup:** Policies were evaluated on held-out procedural MolmoSpaces houses with unseen assets, using various tasks (e.g., Pick MSProc, Pick Classic, Pick, Pick Random-Cam, Pick&Place, PnP Next-To, PnP Color). Oracle success rate (task completion at any timestep) and success at end were reported.
- **Results (Franka FR3):**
 - MolmoBot policies consistently outperformed all baselines (π0.5, StereoVLA, LAP-VLA, X-VLA) across tasks. MolmoBot (F=2) achieved 93.5% success on "Pick MSProc" compared to 48.0% for the best baseline (π0.5-Finetune).
 - On more challenging distributions like "Pick Random-Cam," MolmoBot variants achieved 40-66% success, while π0.5 variants achieved 8-30%. Other VLA baselines performed below 7% on most tasks.
 - MolmoBot-Pi0 achieved 41.5% average success across simulation tasks, substantially higher than π0.5 zero-shot (10.0%), reinforcing the impact of MolmoBot-Data.
 - In a restricted camera setup (fixed-shoulder camera), MolmoBot-Img achieved 86.6% real-world success, outperforming π0.5 (63.3%). MolmoBot-SPOC achieved 36.6%, a notable result given its smaller size.
- **Results (RB-Y1):**
 - MolmoBot Multitask outperformed MolmoBot-SPOC across all shared tasks (e.g., 44.8% vs. 10.5% for Pick), attributed to MolmoBot's richer VLM backbone and multitask training benefits.
- **External Benchmarks (SIMPLER, LIBERO):** While MolmoBot showed some superiority on adapted versions of SIMPLER-DROID and LIBERO-DROID, overall performance remained low. This was attributed to these benchmarks measuring familiarity with specific assets and scene configurations (out-of-distribution for zero-shot policies) rather than general manipulation capability.

#### 4.3. Data Ablations

- **Scaling Number of Demonstrations:** Performance (for pick task, MolmoBot-Img) in both simulation and real-world improved monotonically with an increased number of training demonstrations (e.g., from 10k to 50k trajectories).
- **Scaling Environment Diversity:** Increasing the number of unique MolmoSpaces house environments (from 5k to 50k houses) with a fixed number of trajectories (50k) had negligible effect on pick task performance in both simulation and real. This suggests that for pick tasks, local interaction data is more critical than background environment diversity.
- **Scaling Object Diversity:** Performance improved in simulation as the number of unique object classes increased (from 5 to 100). However, real-world performance on DROID did not show a clear trend, possibly due to the limited and semantically common objects used in real-world evaluations.

#### 4.4. Model Ablations

- **Timesteps Sampled During Training (T):** For MolmoBot-Img, sampling multiple denoising timesteps T per example in parallel improved simulation performance as T increased, peaking at T=8. Real-world performance was less monotonic, peaking at T=4.
- **Action Representation:** For Franka FR3 policies, training MolmoBot-Img with absolute joint position representation significantly outperformed delta joint position representation in real-world evaluations, while simulation results were comparable. This suggests absolute joint policies may transfer better to the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries significant implications for the field of robot learning, particularly regarding the development of generalist manipulation agents.

- **Re-evaluation of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The central finding that large-scale, diverse simulated data can enable effective zero-shot transfer to the real world directly challenges a long-standing assumption in robotics. This suggests that the barrier to general-purpose manipulation may be surmountable through scaled simulation rather than an irreducible reliance on expensive real-world data. This paradigm shift could accelerate research and development by reducing the costs and logistical complexities associated with real-robot experiments.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By open-sourcing MolmoBot-Engine (the data generation pipeline) and MolmoBot-Data (the large-scale dataset), the authors aim to make the tools and insights required for building robotics foundation models widely accessible. This initiative has the potential to decentralize the development of advanced robotic capabilities, moving away from a concentration in a few industrial labs and fostering broader community participation and innovation.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The demonstration of generating 1.7 million expert trajectories (5,704 hours of robot experience) in approximately 6,500 GPU-hours highlights the scalability and efficiency advantages of simulation-based data generation. This allows for rapid iteration on data composition and task design, and quick adoption of new robotic platforms, which is impractical with real-world data collection methods.
- **Advancement in Manipulation Capabilities:** The policies, particularly MolmoBot, demonstrate robust performance on both static (Franka FR3 tabletop pick-and-place at 79.2% success rate) and mobile (Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1 door opening) manipulation tasks in the real world, generalizing to unseen objects and environments without fine-tuning. This represents a substantial step towards more capable and autonomous robots.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The open-source nature of MolmoBot-Engine and MolmoBot-Data provides a foundation for future work. Researchers can leverage this framework to extend to new robots, tasks, and object categories, as well as explore more challenging manipulation scenarios like contact-rich tasks, deformable objects, or tasks requiring accurate fluid/granular dynamics, potentially coupled with advances in physics-based and generative world models.
- **Insights into Data Properties:** The data ablations provide valuable insights into the properties of training data that influence policy performance. While increased demonstration count and object diversity generally improved simulation performance, the surprising finding that increasing environment diversity had little effect for local pick tasks suggests that generalist policies for specific task types may benefit more from task-relevant interaction data quantity than broad environmental variations. The benefit of absolute action representation over delta actions for real-world transfer is also a notable practical insight.

In conclusion, this work challenges a fundamental assumption in robot learning, offering a scalable and open-source pathway to developing robust, general-purpose manipulation policies through large-scale simulation. Its impact extends beyond technical achievement, aiming to foster a more inclusive and collaborative research environment in robotics.
