# Hierarchical Denoising For Multi-Step Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Peking University and The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology developed Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning (HDR), a framework that enables robust multi-step visual reasoning in video generation models while maintaining low-latency streaming by integrating hierarchical latent spaces. HDR achieved a 76.2% relative gain in success rate on a multi-step reasoning benchmark and demonstrated comparable streaming latency to prior autoregressive methods, outperforming both streaming and full-attention baselines.

## Problem

- Streaming autoregressive diffusion models offer low-latency generation but lack revision capabilities, leading to poor multi-step reasoning for complex visual tasks.
- Bidirectional diffusion models excel at multi-step reasoning through global information flow and revision, but incur high computational costs and are unsuitable for low-latency streaming applications.
- A fundamental tension exists between achieving reliable multi-step visual reasoning and maintaining efficient, low-latency streaming generation in video models.

## Method

- Introduced **Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning (HDR)**, a unified framework that integrates a ==tree-structured hierarchy of latent tokens== into the ==streaming autoregressive diffusion== process.
- Employed a ==layer-wise flow-matching objective== and a ==hierarchy-matched denoising schedule==, allowing coarse layers to maintain high-level, partially stochastic plans and fine layers to instantiate concrete visual states.
- Implemented a ==Sparse Hierarchical Attention Pattern (SHAP)== that enables efficient cross-level information flow by allowing each token to attend to its parent, immediate predecessor, and neighboring parent-level tokens within an autoregressive order.

## Results

- HDR achieved a **76.2%** relative gain in overall success on a multi-step visual reasoning benchmark (from **34.22%** to **60.29%**) and an **89.56** average progress score, surpassing both streaming and full-attention baselines.
- Maintained low streaming latency at **0.70 seconds per latent**, comparable to efficient streaming baselines (**0.72s**) and **54.2 times** faster than bidirectional diffusion models.
- Demonstrated robustness to reduced denoising budgets and limited training data (retaining significantly more performance than baselines under adverse conditions), and transferred successfully to physical robot interaction, setting a new state-of-the-art for no-pretraining world action models on RoboDojo.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining hierarchical latent spaces with differentiated denoising strengths effectively bridges the gap between the strong reasoning of bidirectional models and the efficiency of autoregressive models.
> - Maintaining high-level plans in partially noisy coarse layers allows for flexible revision and consistent multi-step reasoning without requiring dense, full-sequence updates.
> - A carefully designed sparse hierarchical attention pattern efficiently supports cross-level information sharing and planning within an autoregressive generation pipeline.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the evolving field of video generation models, which are transitioning from systems focused on realistic video synthesis to generalist visual foundation models capable of complex visual reasoning. Prior research has indicated that large video generation models can perform tasks such as maze navigation and physical reasoning by generating visual trajectories. Furthermore, analyses of diffusion-based video reasoning suggest that the ability to reason is linked to the iterative refinement of intermediate denoising states, where models can maintain and update candidate solutions over multiple steps.

However, existing video generation paradigms face challenges in simultaneously supporting reliable multi-step reasoning and low-latency streaming generation. Streaming autoregressive diffusion models, exemplified by approaches like CausVid and CausalForcing, are efficient due to their left-to-right generation process, conditioning each step only on past context. This efficiency, however, limits their capacity for revision, making them less suitable for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning where early decisions might need global correction. Conversely, bidirectional diffusion models, which jointly denoise a fixed video sequence, allow for global information flow and revision across time. This capability often comes at the cost of high inference computational expense and poor compatibility with low-latency streaming applications, as they require dense full-sequence updates at every denoising step.

This paper addresses this specific tension, aiming to develop a framework that can integrate the reasoning capabilities typically found in computationally intensive bidirectional models with the deployment efficiency characteristic of streaming autoregressive methods. It seeks to contribute to the development of video models that can maintain logical consistency over extended reasoning trajectories without sacrificing real-time or low-latency generation requirements, particularly relevant for applications like closed-loop robot interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework that can enable multi-step visual reasoning in video generation models while simultaneously supporting low-latency streaming output. The authors identify a fundamental tension between the current paradigms of video generation:

- **Streaming Autoregressive Diffusion Models:** These models are designed for efficiency by generating content sequentially from left to right, conditioning each new frame or latent chunk solely on previously generated content and initial conditions. This "left-to-right commitment" approach facilitates low-latency streaming and efficient reuse of key-value (KV) caches. However, this inherent irreversibility means that once a decision is made and a frame is generated, subsequent predictions are bound by that history. If an early decision is incorrect, the model lacks a mechanism to revise it, leading to a degradation in logical consistency and performance in complex multi-step reasoning tasks that require long-range planning and potential backtracking.
- **Bidirectional Diffusion Models:** In contrast, these models denoise an entire fixed-length video sequence jointly across all temporal positions at each step. This global information flow allows for comprehensive revision and refinement of the entire sequence, making them more capable of maintaining uncertain hypotheses and correcting errors over multiple denoising steps, which is beneficial for reasoning tasks. However, this global interaction necessitates dense, full-sequence updates repeatedly, resulting in high inference costs and making them unsuitable for low-latency streaming or real-time applications.

The motivation for proposing Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning (HDR) stems directly from this identified dilemma. The researchers aim to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. They seek to design a system that can recover the strong reasoning abilities observed in bidirectional models, particularly their capacity for global planning and revision, but within the low-latency and efficient streaming architecture of autoregressive diffusion models. The core idea is to introduce a structured intermediate processing layer that allows for high-level planning and hypothesis refinement before committing to fine-grained, frame-level visual outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **HDR (Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning)**, a unified framework designed to integrate hierarchical latents into the streaming autoregressive diffusion process.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Latent Representation

HDR represents a video using a tree-structured hierarchy of latent tokens. The hierarchy consists of `L` levels, where `V_ℓ` is the set of `N_ℓ` latent tokens at level `ℓ`. The coarsest level is `ℓ=1`, and the finest is `ℓ=L`. Coarse-level tokens are intended to summarize global temporal structure and represent high-level plans, while fine-level tokens encode local visual details and instantiate the final video dynamics. Each non-root token `v_ℓ,i` has a parent token `π(ℓ, i)` in the preceding coarser level, which corresponds to the broader temporal segment that the current token refines.

#### 3.2. Layer-wise Flow-Matching Objective

During training, each clean hierarchy token `v_0_ℓ,i` is corrupted by sampling a noise token `ϵ` and constructing an interpolated token `v_t_ℓ,i = (1 - t)v_0_ℓ,i + tϵ`, where `t` is a continuous time step. The HDR network is trained to predict the target velocity field `u_t_ℓ,i = ϵ - v_0_ℓ,i` from the interpolated token `v_t_ℓ,i`, the time step `t`, the hierarchical context `h_ℓ,i`, and a conditioning signal `c` (e.g., first frame, text). The training objective `L_HDR` is a sum of velocity regression losses across all hierarchy levels and tokens, with `λ_ℓ` coefficients balancing the contribution of different levels. This objective encourages each level to learn a velocity field appropriate to its temporal abstraction.

#### 3.3. Hierarchy-Matched Denoising Schedule

A key aspect of HDR is matching denoising strength to the hierarchy level. Unlike approaches that assign a uniform inference budget to every layer, HDR uses a level-dependent sampling budget `K_ℓ`, where `K_1 < K_2 < ... < K_L`. This implies that the residual noise level decreases from coarse to fine layers (`ρ_1 > ρ_2 > ... > ρ_L`). Coarse layers are intentionally stopped at higher noise levels, allowing their predictions to remain partially stochastic and preserve multiple possible global plans. Finer layers receive stronger denoising and lower residual noise to progressively instantiate these hypotheses into concrete visual states. The specific `K_ℓ` values are derived using an entropy-matched rule, which considers the effective temporal support (˜N_ℓ) of each layer and a tunable parameter `β`.

#### 3.4. Sparse Hierarchical Attention Pattern (SHAP)

HDR implements its hierarchical reasoning through SHAP, a structured attention mask applied over flattened tree tokens. All tree tokens are flattened into a coarse-to-fine autoregressive order, meaning all tokens in `V_1` are generated first, followed by `V_2`, and so on. For any given token `v_ℓ,i`, SHAP defines a sparse context for attention. This context includes:
- The immediately preceding token at the same hierarchy level (`(ℓ, i-1)`).
- The first-frame condition (`x_ref`) if it is the first token at its level.
- Its parent token in the coarser level (`π(ℓ, i)`).
- Neighboring parent-level tokens (`left(π(ℓ, i))`, `right(π(ℓ, i))`), which provide boundary information from adjacent coarse segments.

This mask is sparse, ensuring each token attends only to a fixed, small number of targets, independent of video length. It is also autoregressive under the flattened order, as all valid context tokens are either already generated or the initial condition. SHAP facilitates cross-level KV-cache sharing; once a token is generated, its key-value state is written into a global cache, allowing coarse-level information to be reused by lower levels.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

HDR is built upon the Wan2.2-5B-TI2V architecture. For experiments involving 125 frames, six hierarchy levels with sizes are used, coupled with the entropy-matched denoising schedule for the respective layers. The model is trained on an 18,000-video reasoning dataset (3,000 synthetic videos per task) and conditioned on the first frame. Training uses a flow-matching objective, initialized from a pretrained Wan2.2-5B-TI2V checkpoint, for 100,000 steps. Classifier-free guidance with a scale of 3.0 is applied during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings regarding HDR's performance across multi-step visual reasoning, efficiency, robustness, and transferability.

#### 4.1. Multi-Step Reasoning Performance

On a custom-built, level-stratified multi-step video reasoning benchmark comprising six tasks (maze navigation, Tower of Hanoi, one-line drawing, sliding puzzle, Sokoban, and water pouring), HDR demonstrated improved task completion and intermediate logical consistency. Compared to the streaming autoregressive diffusion baseline (CausalForcing), HDR achieved a 76.2% relative gain in overall success, increasing the score from 34.22 to 60.29. The overall average progress score also improved from 76.00 to 89.56, indicating more consistent intermediate reasoning trajectories. The model achieved the highest overall success and average progress, outperforming both streaming and full-attention baselines like bidirectional diffusion.

#### 4.2. Streaming Efficiency

HDR maintained low-latency streaming capabilities. Its average latency during streaming was 0.70 seconds per latent, which is comparable to CausalForcing (0.72s) and significantly faster (54.2 times) than bidirectional diffusion (37.92s). This suggests that HDR improves reasoning without substantially increasing the streaming latency.

#### 4.3. Mechanism Analysis: Hierarchical Layer Importance

An ablation study on the number of active hierarchical layers revealed that increasing the number of layers from a single-layer setup (equivalent to CausalForcing) to the full six-layer HDR hierarchy consistently improved performance. This indicates that each hierarchical level contributes to the coarse-to-fine reasoning process, and deeper hierarchies lead to enhanced reasoning abilities.

#### 4.4. Robustness to Reduced Budgets and Data

HDR exhibited robustness under reduced denoising budgets and limited training data.
- **Denoising-step reduction:** With only one denoising step, HDR maintained a success rate of 34.72, retaining 57.6% of its full-step performance. This contrasts with bidirectional diffusion and CausalForcing, which retained 29.6% and 32.9% of their respective full-step performance under the same condition.
- **Data reduction:** When trained with only 2% of the full dataset, HDR retained 82.9% of its full-data success score and 97.2% of its full-data average progress. In comparison, bidirectional diffusion retained 52.0% of its success and 89.5% of its average progress under the same data reduction. This suggests that the hierarchical structure provides an inductive bias for learning transferable task rules.

#### 4.5. Physical World Modeling: Robot Interaction

To assess real-world transferability, HDR was evaluated in a physical-world robot maze experiment. After pretraining on virtual maze videos and fine-tuning on a small dataset of 50 real-world robot maze videos, HDR maintained strong success rates across mazes of varying difficulty, including out-of-distribution (OOD) layouts. This indicates its potential for transferring hierarchical reasoning to physical interaction tasks.

#### 4.6. World Action Modeling on RoboDojo

HDR was adapted into HDR-WAM for embodied world-action modeling. Without robot-domain or embodied-interaction pretraining, HDR-WAM achieved an overall score of 5.47 and an average success rate of 3.00% on the RoboDojo simulation benchmark. This performance surpassed other non-pretraining World Action Model baselines, setting a new state of the art in this category. Notably, HDR-WAM showed strong gains in the Long-Horizon (9.85/4.75%) and Memory (6.65/4.67%) dimensions, suggesting its capacity to maintain coherent task structure over extended interactions.

#### 4.7. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Closed-Source Models

Off-the-shelf closed-source models (Wan2.6, Veo) showed limited success on the reasoning benchmarks when evaluated without fine-tuning. HDR, which was explicitly trained for the multi-step reasoning tasks, achieved substantially higher scores, indicating the benefit of its specialized architecture and training approach for these complex tasks.

#### 4.8. Analysis of Residual Failure Modes

Despite its improvements, HDR exhibits residual failure modes, primarily characterized by late-stage state-consistency drift. While coarse layers generally encode the correct multi-step structure, and finer layers refine this intent, errors sometimes appear near the end of the rollout where exact geometry, object identity, or terminal constraints are violated (e.g., disappearing walls in maze or Sokoban, incorrect color mixing in water pouring). These failures suggest that the model sometimes struggles to preserve exact state consistency until the final frames.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of generative video modeling by addressing a central challenge: simultaneously achieving robust multi-step reasoning and efficient, low-latency streaming generation. The introduction of HDR (Hierarchical Denoising for Visual Reasoning) offers a novel architectural paradigm that integrates hierarchical latent spaces, a hierarchy-matched denoising schedule, and a sparse hierarchical attention pattern (SHAP) to reconcile these often conflicting objectives.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

- **Bridging Efficiency and Reasoning:** HDR demonstrates that it is possible to recover substantial reasoning capabilities, previously mostly found in computationally expensive bidirectional diffusion models, within a framework that retains the deployment efficiency of streaming autoregressive models. This challenges the notion that global reasoning necessarily requires dense all-to-all temporal computation, proposing structured multi-scale latent planning as a viable and effective alternative.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Performance:** The improvements in multi-step reasoning accuracy and consistency across a diverse set of benchmarks indicate HDR's capacity to handle complex logical trajectories, a critical step towards more human-like visual intelligence in generative models.
- **Robustness and Data Efficiency:** HDR's demonstrated robustness to reduced denoising steps and limited training data suggests that its hierarchical structure provides a valuable inductive bias. This can enable models to learn transferable reasoning rules more effectively and efficiently, potentially reducing the reliance on massive datasets for specific reasoning tasks.
- **Real-World Application Potential:** The successful transfer of HDR's reasoning ability to physical robot interaction in maze navigation tasks highlights its potential for applications in physical world modeling and robot control. This is further reinforced by HDR-WAM's performance on the RoboDojo benchmark, establishing a new state of the art for no-pretraining world action models, especially for tasks requiring long-horizon planning and memory.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified residual failure modes—primarily late-stage state-consistency drift—point towards specific avenues for future research, such as developing more constraint-aware refinement mechanisms or lightweight verification during the final stages of generation to ensure exact adherence to terminal conditions.

Overall, this work presents a meaningful advancement in the capabilities of visual foundation models, offering a framework that balances computational pragmatism with the increasing demand for sophisticated reasoning in AI systems.
