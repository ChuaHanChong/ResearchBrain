# Wavelet Predictive Representations for Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The WISDOM framework, developed by researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology, Microsoft Research AI Frontiers, and University of the Sunshine Coast, introduces wavelet-domain predictive task representations to enhance reinforcement learning in non-stationary environments. It achieves superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across Meta-World, Type-1 Diabetes, and MuJoCo benchmarks, demonstrating improved adaptability and robustness to noise.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning agents struggle to adapt effectively in real-world non-stationary environments where dynamics, objectives, or rewards change over time.
- Existing Non-Stationary RL (NSRL) methods often assume regular task evolution or struggle with sudden, irregular changes, leading to suboptimal performance and accumulated prediction errors in dynamic settings.
- Many current NSRL approaches require prior knowledge or increase model parameters, limiting their applicability and efficiency.

## Method

- The ==Wavelet-Induced== task repreSentation preDiction for nOn-stationary reinforceMent learning (**WISDOM**) framework is introduced, employing wavelet-domain predictive task representations.
- A Wavelet Representation Network (Yϕ) applies Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to task representation sequences, capturing both global trends and local variations of task evolution.
- The Yϕ network is optimized with a novel wavelet Temporal Difference (TD) loss, theoretically proven to converge, and an auto-regressive (AR) loss to reinforce temporal dependencies, with the predicted wavelet representations then conditioning a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) policy.

## Results

- WISDOM consistently achieved superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance on Meta-World, Type-1 Diabetes, and MuJoCo benchmarks compared to existing baselines.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced adaptability in complex environments characterized by non-stationary and stochastically evolving tasks.
- It exhibited robust performance under noisy conditions, maintaining the highest success rate and rapid convergence when Gaussian noise was injected into states.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Wavelet analysis can effectively decompose non-stationary task evolution into multi-scale features, allowing for simultaneous capture of both slow global trends and rapid local variations.
> - A novel wavelet Temporal Difference (TD) update operator provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for robustly tracking and predicting MDP evolution, stabilizing learning.
> - Wavelet-domain task representations preserve information relevant to policy performance, enabling policies conditioned on these representations to improve by focusing on essential non-stationary characteristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities in stationary environments. However, real-world applications frequently involve non-stationary dynamics, where environmental factors, task objectives, or reward structures change over time. This challenge is addressed by Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning (NSRL), a field focused on developing agents that can adapt quickly to sequences of distinct Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).

Existing NSRL methods often derive from Meta-RL, which aims to improve sample efficiency and policy generality by identifying shared structures across tasks. While Meta-RL facilitates rapid adaptation, it typically operates under an inherent stationarity assumption and may not adequately account for temporal correlations between evolving tasks. Current NSRL approaches have explored modeling task evolution through various means, including Gaussian mixture distributions, distance metric losses, first-order Markov chains, recurrent neural networks, and Gaussian Processes. These methods typically exhibit limitations, such as:
- Suboptimal performance in highly dynamic settings due to accumulated prediction errors when task changes are rapid or complex.
- Limited applicability to tasks with smoothly evolving patterns, struggling with sudden or irregular changes.
- Requirements for prior knowledge (e.g., non-stationary kernel functions in Gaussian Processes) or an increase in model parameters.
- Difficulties in handling tasks with time-varying periods or frequencies, often relying on assumptions of regular, fixed-period patterns.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing wavelet analysis, a technique traditionally used in time series modeling for its ability to decompose signals into different frequency components, thereby capturing both global trends and fine-grained local variations. By applying wavelet analysis to task representations, the research aims to provide a more robust and adaptive mechanism for handling the diverse and dynamic nature of non-stationary environments, moving beyond methods that struggle with irregular task evolution or rely on implicit temporal modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the adaptability and performance of reinforcement learning agents in non-stationary environments. This is pursued by developing a novel approach, Wavelet-Induced task repreSentation preDiction for nOn-stationary reinforceMent learning (WISDOM), which leverages wavelet-domain predictive task representations.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:
- **Addressing limitations of existing NSRL methods:** Current approaches often fall short in highly dynamic real-world settings where factors like weather or traffic patterns are constantly changing. These methods tend to focus on tasks with regularly evolving patterns, which restricts their applicability in environments characterized by irregular, stochastic, or rapidly changing dynamics.
- **Capturing multi-scale features of task evolution:** Inspired by the success of Wavelet analysis in time series modeling, the authors aim to utilize its ability to capture signals at multiple scales. This is motivated by the understanding that non-stationary task changes can manifest as both slow, global trends and rapid, fine-grained variations. Traditional methods may struggle to effectively represent both simultaneously.
- **Developing wavelet-domain task representations:** A key objective is to transform sequences of task representations (derived from agent-environment interactions) into the wavelet domain. This transformation is intended to allow wavelet coefficients to explicitly represent both the overarching global trends and the localized, fine-grained variations inherent in non-stationary task evolution.
- **Improving task evolution tracking and prediction:** The research aims to go beyond simple auto-regressive modeling, commonly used in time series forecasting, by devising a wavelet temporal difference (TD) update operator. This operator is motivated by the need for a more robust mechanism to track and predict MDP evolution, which is expected to mitigate the accumulation of prediction errors seen in simpler models.
- **Ensuring theoretical soundness:** A specific objective is to theoretically prove the convergence of the proposed wavelet TD update operator. Furthermore, the work aims to demonstrate that wavelet task representations can lead to improved policies, thereby providing a theoretical foundation for the proposed methodology.
- **Enhancing policy adaptability and sample efficiency:** By integrating the wavelet-derived task representations into policy learning, the goal is to facilitate prompt policy adjustments. This is expected to lead to improved policy adaptability across diverse non-stationary tasks, along with enhanced sample efficiency during training.
- **Comprehensive empirical validation:** The research aims to evaluate WISDOM on a range of diverse and challenging benchmarks to empirically demonstrate its performance advantages in terms of sample efficiency and asymptotic performance compared to existing baselines. This includes tasks with varying state transition dynamics and reward functions, as well as environments with stochastic and irregularly evolving periods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WISDOM framework integrates three main modules: Non-Stationary Task Inference with a Context Encoder, Tracking Task Evolution via a Wavelet Representation Network, and Policy Learning Through Wavelet Task Representations.

**Module A: Non-Stationary Task Inference with Context Encoder**
The agent operates in environments where task distributions $p(\mathcal{T}_t)$ evolve over time, resulting in a sequence of MDPs. The agent lacks prior knowledge of the true task ID $\omega$. A context encoder $e_\eta$, parameterized by $\eta$, infers task-relevant information. It processes a recent history of transitions (context $C$) to generate a task representation sequence $z = [z_0, z_1, \dots, z_T]$. Each $z_i$ in this sequence aims to capture the influence of dynamics and rewards at a specific point, approximating the unknown $\omega$ sequence. The context encoder $e_\eta$ is trained using a variational approximation via KL-divergence, where the context $C$ is sampled from a replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$, and $p(z)$ is a Gaussian prior.

**Module B: Tracking Task Evolution via Wavelet Representation**
Recognizing that the task evolution process can be viewed as a non-stationary signal with underlying frequencies changing over time, WISDOM leverages the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT).
- **Wavelet Representation Network ($Y_\phi$):** This network is designed to perform DWT on the task representation sequence $z$. It consists of two dilated causal convolution networks (Conv1 and Conv2), followed by a linear layer. The convolution kernels ($y_0$ and $y_1$) are learnable, initialized as classical basis functions like the Haar wavelet.
 - $y_0$ acts as a low-pass filter, producing approximation coefficients ($u_m$) that capture low-frequency components and overall trends of task evolution.
 - $y_1$ acts as a high-pass filter, generating detail coefficients ($g_m$) that capture high-frequency components and rapidly evolving local features.
- **Decomposition and Reconstruction:** The network iteratively performs $M$ decompositions, yielding the $M$-th approximation coefficient $u_M$ and a series of detail coefficients $g_{1:M}$. During each decomposition, the network downsamples and preserves recent detail coefficients ($\tilde{g}_m$). Finally, $Y_\phi$ applies a linear transformation to reconstruct these wavelet coefficients back to the time domain, producing an expressive wavelet task representation sequence $\hat{z}$.

**Optimization Objective Design for Wavelet Representation Network $Y_\phi$**
The optimization of $Y_\phi$ is achieved through a combined objective that includes both a novel wavelet Temporal Difference (TD) loss and an auto-regressive (AR) loss.
- **Wavelet TD Update Operator:** A TD-style update operator $F_W(z_t) = z_t + \Gamma W(z_{t+1})$ is defined over the wavelet-based task features learned by the W network (Conv2 layer). This operator explicitly updates $Y_\phi$. The authors theoretically prove that $F$ is a contraction mapping (Theorem 1), ensuring the convergence of wavelet representation updates and contributing to stable learning. This explicit TD update is intended to capture structural regularities in MDP sequences and enhance sample efficiency, potentially not neglecting critical features even with sparse rewards. A target W network $W_{\bar{\mu}}$ is used to stabilize training, similar to DQN.
- **Auto-Regressive (AR) Loss:** An AR loss is incorporated to reinforce long-term temporal dependencies in the task representation sequence. This loss helps prevent the trends captured by $Y_\phi$ from being temporally misaligned due to the relaxed orthogonality of the learnable filters, and empowers $Y_\phi$ to make predictions based on the task representation sequence. It complements the single-step future prediction of the wavelet TD loss by addressing potential accumulated prediction errors over longer horizons.
- **Combined Objective:** The overall objective $J_\phi$ is a weighted sum of the wavelet TD loss and the AR loss, with a balancing coefficient $\alpha_Y$.

**Module C: Policy Learning Through Wavelet Task Representations**
The predicted wavelet task representation $\hat{z}$ generated by $Y_\phi$ is integrated into the policy learning process to enable dynamic policy adjustments.
- **Base RL Algorithm:** WISDOM is based on the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm.
- **Contextual Critic Network ($Q_v$):** The critic network $Q_v$ learns to estimate action-values conditioned on the state, action, and the wavelet task representation $\hat{z}$. Its objective $J_v$ minimizes the squared residual error, using a target Q-value $Q_{target}$ that also incorporates $\hat{z}$.
- **Contextual Policy Network ($\pi_\theta$):** The policy network $\pi_\theta$ learns to select actions conditioned on the state and $\hat{z}$. Its objective $J_\theta$ maximizes expected return and policy entropy.
- **Theoretical Guarantees for Policy Performance:**
 - **Theorem 2:** The research demonstrates that wavelet-domain task representations preserve the ability to indicate policy performance, implying that differences in wavelet-domain features correlate with differences in policy performance.
 - **Theorem 3:** It is proven that the policy $\pi(\cdot|s, \hat{z})$ conditioned on the restored time-domain wavelet task representations $\hat{z}$ improves upon its iterated history policy. This is attributed to $\hat{z}$ filtering out task-irrelevant information and helping the policy focus on essential non-stationary characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of WISDOM was evaluated across diverse benchmarks: Meta-World, Type-1 Diabetes, and MuJoCo. Experiments generally involved non-stationary tasks with stochastic evolution periods.

**Overall Performance**
WISDOM consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency and asymptotic performance compared to existing baselines across various environments. It exhibited adaptability in complex environments characterized by non-stationary and stochastically evolving tasks.

**Meta-World Results**
On the Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks, WISDOM achieved the highest average success rates with low variance, surpassing all baseline methods in adaptability and final performance. This performance is attributed to effective noise filtering and retention of fundamental low-frequency components by the wavelet representations. In contrast, methods like SeCBAD, which rely on rewards for task inference, struggled in environments with complex, fluctuating reward structures (e.g., varying 3D target positions). PEARL and RL$^2$ showed competitive asymptotic performance, indicating their potential in meta-learning policies, but without explicit modeling of task evolution, they did not match WISDOM's efficiency. SAC's competitive performance suggested that inaccuracies in task inference and modeling in other NSRL methods could undermine adaptability in challenging environments. CEMRL struggled with representation collapse when attempting to cluster complex task distributions, and TRIO showed poor overall performance due to challenges in kernel function selection.

**Type-1 Diabetes Results**
In the realistic Type-1 Diabetes environment, which involves controlling blood glucose levels with insulin injections under varying food intake, WISDOM demonstrated efficient adaptability. The wavelet task representations effectively captured trends in blood glucose changes, aiding in appropriate insulin dosage selection. Baselines like SeCBAD and CEMRL struggled with environments exhibiting varying food intake (e.g., 2 meal variations), possibly due to limited distinctiveness in trajectory data for accurate task evolution identification or clustering.

**MuJoCo Results**
On MuJoCo tasks, which involved relatively smaller and narrower distributions of non-stationary parameter adjustments (e.g., changing reward functions or dynamics), WISDOM maintained consistently rapid adaptability and enhanced final performance. CEMRL showed improved clustering capability in these less complex settings, while TRIO and SeCBAD converged faster than PEARL and RL$^2$ due to their explicit task evolution modeling and recurrent encoders. COREP's advantage was less significant here, possibly due to the lower state dimension leading to simpler relationships between graph nodes.

**Ablation Studies**
- **Wavelet Representation Network (Y network):** The Y network provided significant performance gains, indicating that the wavelet task representation effectively reflects non-stationary trends.
- **AR Loss:** Incorporating the AR loss accelerated convergence and improved the final performance of the model.
- **Wavelet TD Loss:** The wavelet TD loss contributed to stabilizing training and reducing variance in performance.
- **Context Encoder Architecture:** An MLP-based encoder (used in WISDOM) demonstrated greater stability compared to an RNN-based encoder, which was susceptible to gradient vanishing and forgetting over time.
- **Variable-Wise Encoding (VWE):** Applying DWT independently to each variate (dimension) of the task representation led to slower convergence and inferior results, suggesting that disrupting cross-variate dependencies hindered effective modeling.

**Adaptability Analysis (Non-Stationary Degrees and RL Backbones)**
- **Non-Stationary Degrees:** As the degree of non-stationarity increased across environments, most models showed a downward trend in final performance. WISDOM, however, maintained consistent adaptability, highlighting the expressiveness of its wavelet task representation and policy robustness.
- **RL Backbones:** WISDOM demonstrated its ability to quickly adapt and converge when integrated with various underlying RL algorithms, while SAC was used as the backbone for fair comparisons across primary experiments.

**Robustness to Noise**
Experiments involving injected Gaussian noise into states showed that WISDOM maintained the highest success rate and rapid convergence, outperforming all baselines under noisy conditions. This robustness is attributed to wavelet-based representation learning, which suppresses noise while retaining fast-varying and task-relevant signals, thereby improving the signal-to-noise ratio.

**Case Study**
A visualization of the task representations tracked by the $Y_\phi$ network in an Inverted Double Pendulum task confirmed its ability to capture intrinsic non-stationary changes. Approximation coefficients ($u$) captured the overall trend of velocity changes, while detail coefficients ($g$) captured sharper variations. The predicted $\hat{z}$ closely aligned with the true trend of task evolution, validating the network's function.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces WISDOM, a novel approach that addresses the challenges of Non-Stationary Reinforcement Learning by leveraging wavelet-domain predictive task representations. The work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Adaptability in Non-Stationary Environments:** By employing a learnable wavelet representation network, WISDOM is designed to capture both long-term trends and short-term fluctuations in task evolution. This capability allows RL agents to adapt flexibly to complex non-stationary tasks, particularly those with stochastic and irregularly evolving periods, which is a common characteristic of real-world scenarios. This moves beyond traditional methods that often assume regular task evolution or struggle with sudden changes.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Asymptotic Performance:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that WISDOM surpasses existing baselines in terms of both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across diverse benchmarks (Meta-World, Type-1 Diabetes, MuJoCo). This indicates that the wavelet-domain representations provide a more informative and stable basis for learning and adaptation.
- **Theoretical Foundations for Wavelet Integration:** The work provides theoretical proofs for the convergence of the wavelet TD update operator and demonstrates policy improvement with wavelet task representations. These theoretical guarantees offer a stronger scientific basis for integrating wavelet analysis into RL and contribute to the understanding of how such representations influence policy learning.
- **Robustness to Noise and Irregularities:** The findings suggest that wavelet-based representation learning inherently offers robustness to environmental noise by effectively suppressing irrelevant signals while retaining crucial task-relevant information. This characteristic is valuable for deploying RL agents in real-world settings where sensory input or environmental dynamics can be inherently noisy.
- **Broader Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across various domains, from robotic manipulation to blood glucose control in diabetic patients, indicates its potential for broad applicability in complex control problems that require continuous adaptation.

**Limitations:**
Despite its demonstrated effectiveness, WISDOM presents certain limitations. The performance can be influenced by the choice of decomposition levels ($M$) within the wavelet representation network. Selecting an appropriate $M$ often requires tuning specific to the environment, as excessive decomposition may inadvertently filter out vital low-frequency components or introduce unnecessary detail, leading to suboptimal performance.

**Future Work:**
The authors propose future research directions to address these limitations and extend the current work:
- Developing adaptive mechanisms that can dynamically adjust the wavelet decomposition levels based on the observed characteristics of the non-stationary environment.
- Exploring more effective strategies for the selective filtering and utilization of detail coefficients to further optimize rapid adaptation capabilities.
