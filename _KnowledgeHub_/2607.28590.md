# VAD: Attributing Visual Evidence for Target Reconstruction in Multimodal On-Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> VAD (Visual Attribution Distillation) introduces a method to refine multimodal knowledge distillation by isolating the visually attributable components of a teacher model's corrections, rather than distilling the full, potentially source-mixed, teacher output. This approach leads to average accuracy gains of 2.40 points for 4B models and 2.80 points for 9B models on fine-grained visual benchmarks, while also preserving general MLLM capabilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from 'perceptual misses,' overlooking visual details or misinterpreting spatial relationships, leading to inaccurate responses.
- Teacher corrections in existing multimodal on-policy distillation (OPD) methods are 'source-mixed,' meaning they are not solely driven by visual evidence but also incorporate linguistic priors and teacher-specific behaviors, hindering effective visual grounding.
- Prior methods struggle to specifically address visual refutation, often focusing only on tokens supported by visual evidence rather than also suppressing incorrect hypotheses.

## Method

- **VAD** uses ==on-policy counterfactual views==, querying a teacher with both an 'evidence-present' crop and an 'evidence-removed' degraded crop for each student-generated prefix.
- It attributes the teacher correction by projecting the full teacher's suggested change onto a ==visual evidence proxy== vector (difference between evidence-present and evidence-removed teacher responses), isolating the visually attributable component.
- A ==student-anchored supervision target== is reconstructed by adding the visually attributed correction and is further refined to separately budget for visual support and refutation of tokens; a weak privileged-teacher regularizer is also applied for stability.

## Results

- VAD consistently surpassed Vision-OPD and visual-advantage weighting methods (VA-OPD, V-Zero) on six fine-grained visual benchmarks, achieving **78.32%** Avg6 for 4B models (**+2.40 points**) and **79.93%** Avg6 for 9B models (**+2.80 points**).
- Semantic analysis showed that the visually attributed correction (rvistr_{vis_t}rvist) selectively concentrated visually grounded and decision-relevant content, with visual attributes, objects, and answer decisions comprising a higher proportion compared to the full teacher correction.
- VAD-trained models maintained or slightly improved generalization on held-out tasks, indicating that fine-grained visual specialization did not compromise broader MLLM capabilities, with positive delta changes at both 4B (**+0.24 points**) and 9B (**+0.23 points**) scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Teacher corrections in multimodal OPD are often 'source-mixed,' and directly distilling the full teacher distribution is inefficient for improving visual grounding.
> - Explicitly estimating and isolating the visually attributable component of a teacher's correction, by contrasting responses to evidence-present and evidence-removed views, creates a more precise learning signal.
> - Reconstructing a student-anchored target that separates visual support from refutation allows for more targeted learning, enabling the student to better understand when visual evidence confirms or contradicts a token.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work operates within the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically addressing challenges in improving their fine-grained visual perception and reasoning capabilities through knowledge distillation. MLLMs, while capable of open-ended visual reasoning, frequently exhibit "perceptual misses" where they overlook visual details, confuse attributes, or misinterpret spatial relationships, leading to inaccurate responses.

Knowledge distillation (KD) is a widely adopted technique for transferring predictive structure from a stronger "teacher" model to a smaller "student" model. On-Policy Distillation (OPD) is a specific variant where the teacher supervises the student on prefixes generated by the student itself, making the supervision directly relevant to the student's deployment trajectory. This is particularly valuable for multimodal reasoning, as early visual errors can significantly derail an MLLM's subsequent generation.

Existing multimodal OPD strategies have demonstrated limitations:
- **Vision-OPD** (Yuan et al., 2026) conditions a teacher on an evidence-centered crop but directly distills its complete next-token distribution. Diagnostics indicate that a substantial portion of the teacher's strongest corrections are not strongly aligned with its evidence-conditioned response, suggesting a "source-mixed" nature where corrections combine visual signals with linguistic priors and teacher-specific effects.
- **VA-OPD** (Liu et al., 2026) and **V-Zero** (Sun et al., 2026) use contrasting informative and degraded visual views to prioritize tokens or trajectories. However, they typically retain the full evidence-present teacher distribution as the underlying target, meaning source-mixed directions can still influence the target. Furthermore, the positive visual advantage used by VA-OPD may fail to capture instances where clear visual evidence should *refute* a student's mistaken token, rather than just supporting a correct alternative.
- **Privileged Distillation** methods (Ye et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Zhang, 2026; Ruan et al., 2026) generally enhance the teacher with information unavailable to the student. While some methods like Anchored Residual OPD separate guidance, they do not specifically attribute teacher corrections to controlled visual interventions.
- **Fine-Grained Visual Perception** improvements (Liu et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2023; Bai et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) have focused on higher native resolution or multi-scale detail. Other approaches (Wu and Xie, 2023; Shen et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2026) actively acquire better views or perform image operations. Region-to-image training (Wei et al., 2026; Yuan et al., 2026; Tian et al., 2026) distills local evidence. VAD differentiates itself by addressing target construction, specifically excluding teacher changes not explained by revealed visual evidence.
- **Counterfactual Visual Supervision** (Leng et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024b; Lee et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2026) uses controlled evidence changes to expose weak grounding. These often target decoding or outcome-driven optimization, differing from VAD's focus on dense on-policy target construction.
- **Decomposed OPD** (Yoon et al., 2026) factors distillation into language-prior and visual-grounding objectives, constructing a visual target from text-only priors and multimodal-to-text-only information gain. VAD, in contrast, projects the privileged teacher-to-student correction onto an evidence-present versus evidence-removed direction for the *same* teacher, anchoring the target to the student's current state, and explicitly budgeting visual support and refutation.

The core gap identified is the need to explicitly estimate *which* component of a teacher's proposed correction is genuinely supported by visual evidence, rather than merely determining *where* or *how strongly* to distill the complete (and potentially source-mixed) teacher distribution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that corrections provided by a privileged-view teacher in multimodal on-policy distillation are often "source-mixed." This means that the teacher's proposed changes to the student's next-token distribution are not solely driven by specific visual evidence, but also incorporate linguistic priors, broader world knowledge, and teacher-specific behavioral patterns that are not directly attributable to a controlled visual input.

The paper identifies several issues arising from this source-mixed nature:
1. **Ineffective Knowledge Transfer**: If a teacher's correction is largely influenced by non-visual factors, directly distilling it might not effectively improve the student's visual grounding. For instance, diagnostic results showed that only 23.2% (for 4B models) and 22.8% (for 9B models) of the strongest teacher corrections in Vision-OPD were strongly aligned with the teacher's evidence-conditioned response. This implies that a significant portion of the teacher's guidance is not directly related to specific visual cues.
2. **Lack of Specificity in Prior Methods**: Existing methods like Vision-OPD distill the entire teacher distribution, assuming all parts are equally relevant to visual learning. Methods like VA-OPD and V-Zero use visual contrast to weight or gate distillation, but still use the complete evidence-present teacher distribution as the target. This leaves the inherent "source-mixed" problem unresolved within the target signal itself.
3. **Inability to Address Visual Refutation**: Current approaches, particularly those relying on "positive visual advantage," primarily focus on tokens that are *supported* by visual evidence. They may not adequately address situations where clear visual evidence should *refute* a student's incorrect hypothesis, lowering its probability. An example presented in the paper illustrates a student identifying a "checkered" pattern when the ground truth is "striped." While the evidence-present teacher correctly identifies "striped," the "checkered" token might not be identified as having a high positive visual advantage, hindering effective correction.

The key objective of this research is to introduce Visual Attribution Distillation (VAD), an algorithm designed to estimate and isolate the *visually attributable* part of a teacher's correction. Instead of simply applying the full teacher correction, VAD aims to reconstruct a learning target that is specifically anchored to the student's current distribution but shifted only by the components demonstrably supported or refuted by a controlled visual intervention.

The contributions of the work are articulated as:
- Identifying source-mixed teacher correction as a central limitation of privileged multimodal OPD and explaining why direct teacher matching and positive visual-advantage weighting do not explicitly estimate visually supported correction components.
- Introducing VAD, which employs a controlled visual-evidence intervention to construct a proxy direction, estimates the aligned component of the privileged teacher correction, reconstructs a student-anchored target, separates support from refutation, and incorporates a weak teacher-correction regularizer for stable optimization.
- Demonstrating consistent performance gains across benchmarks and model scales, supported by semantic token analyses and controlled target studies that validate the interpretation of the proxy-aligned correction and its support-refutation behavior.

In essence, the motivation is to make multimodal knowledge distillation more precise, efficient, and interpretable by ensuring that the student primarily learns from teacher corrections that are directly and demonstrably grounded in visual evidence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Visual Attribution Distillation (VAD) is a counterfactual target-reconstruction algorithm. Its methodology focuses on constructing a refined supervision target by isolating the visually attributable component of a privileged teacher's correction.

The process involves three main stages:
1. **On-Policy Counterfactual Views**:
 - **Student Generation**: The student policy ($\pi_\theta$) is given a full image ($\mathbf{x}^0$) and generates a response sequence. For each token position $t$, the student generates a prefix $\mathbf{y}_{<t}$.
 - **Teacher Queries**: A fixed copy of the initial student model ($\pi_{\bar{\theta}}$) acts as the teacher. For each student-generated prefix $\mathbf{y}_{<t}$, the teacher is queried for its next-token distribution under two distinct visual views:
 - An **evidence-present crop** ($\mathbf{x}^+$): a cropped region that contains the relevant visual evidence.
 - An **evidence-removed/degraded crop** ($\mathbf{x}^-$): a degraded version of the same cropped region (e.g., by downsampling and upsampling) to obscure fine-grained evidence.
 - **Distribution Representation**: The next-token distributions from the full-image student ($\mathbf{p}^0_S$), evidence-present teacher ($\mathbf{p}^+_T$), and evidence-removed teacher ($\mathbf{p}^-_T$) are evaluated on a shared, compact set of top-K candidate tokens ($V_t$). These distributions are then converted to *centered log-probability vectors* ($\phi_t(\mathbf{p})$), which normalize out common logit offsets, preserving pairwise log-odds.

2. **Attributing the Teacher Correction**:
 - **Teacher Correction ($r_t$)**: This is defined as the difference between the centered log-probabilities of the evidence-present teacher and the full-image student: $r_t = \phi_t(\mathbf{p}^+_T) - \phi_t(\mathbf{p}^0_S)$. It represents the complete correction proposed by the privileged teacher relative to the student's current state.
 - **Visual Evidence Proxy ($u_t$)**: This is the core of VAD's attribution. It is defined as the difference between the centered log-probabilities of the evidence-present teacher and the evidence-removed teacher: $u_t = \phi_t(\mathbf{p}^+_T) - \phi_t(\mathbf{p}^-_T)$. Since the teacher parameters and textual context are constant, $u_t$ isolates the teacher's distributional response specifically to the presence of fine-grained visual evidence. $u_t(i) > 0$ implies the evidence supports token $i$, while $u_t(i) < 0$ implies it refutes token $i$.
 - **One-Sided Projection**: The complete teacher correction ($r_t$) is projected onto the visual evidence proxy ($u_t$) to estimate the *visually attributable component* ($r_{vis_t}$). This projection is one-sided, meaning it only retains the component of $r_t$ that aligns positively with $u_t$. If $r_t$ and $u_t$ do not agree (their dot product is non-positive), the attributed component is zero. A small stabilization term ($\zeta$) is added to the denominator. The remaining part of $r_t$ is termed the *proxy-unexplained residual* ($r_{res_t} = r_t - r_{vis_t}$).
 - **One-Sided Supervision Target**: An initial student-anchored target ($q^{one}_{T,t}$) is constructed by adding the (clipped) $r_{vis_t}$ to the student's centered log-probabilities and applying a softmax. This target only incorporates visually attributable shifts. The magnitude of $r_{vis_t}$ defines a "visual-correction budget" ($B_t$).

3. **Budgeted Support and Refutation (Refinement)**:
 - To allow for separate control over supporting and refuting tokens, the $u_t$ vector is split into its positive ($u^+_t$) and negative ($u^-_t$) components.
 - Agreement scores ($s^+_t$, $s^-_t$) between $r_t$ and these branches allocate the visual-correction budget $B_t$. A cap ($\tau^+$) is applied to the support share to prevent uncertain positive evidence from dominating.
 - This refinement yields $r^{VAD}_t$, which is a weighted combination of the normalized support and refutation branches, scaled by $B_t$.
 - The final **reconstructed VAD target** ($q^{VAD}_{T,t}$) is formed by adding the (clipped) $r^{VAD}_t$ to the student's centered log-probabilities and applying a softmax.

**Training Objective and Inference**:
- The primary supervision signal is the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between the detached reconstructed target ($q^{VAD}_{T,t}$) and the student's current distribution ($\mathbf{p}^0_S$).
- A **weak privileged-teacher regularizer** is added to prevent language and output drift observed when using only the visual loss. This regularizer is a JSD between the detached evidence-present teacher distribution ($\mathbf{p}^+_T$) and the student's distribution. Its weight ($a_t$) is inversely proportional to the fraction of the complete teacher correction that was visually attributed, effectively contributing more when less of the correction is visually explained. The total loss is $L = L_{vis} + \lambda L_{reg}$.
- During **inference**, VAD operates as a standard full-image student policy, requiring no additional model calls or auxiliary views. The teacher, crop, and degraded views are exclusively for target construction during training.

**Experimental Setup**:
VAD was trained on Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B models using a synthetic visual question-answering dataset. The evidence-present crop ($\mathbf{x}^+$) was a 2x crop of the relevant region, and the evidence-removed view ($\mathbf{x}^-$) was created by 0.1x bilinear downsampling and nearest-neighbor upsampling of that crop. Key hyperparameters included batch size 96, 8 rollouts per prompt, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-6}$, projection stabilizer $\zeta=10^{-3}$, coordinate bound $c=20$, and weak-regularizer weight $\lambda=0.1$. The positive-branch cap $\tau^+$ was tuned per scale (0.8 for 4B, 0.7 for 9B). Evaluation was conducted on six fine-grained visual benchmarks using an official accuracy pipeline and GPT-OSS-120B as a judge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across five research questions, demonstrating the effectiveness, semantic properties, and robustness of VAD.

**RQ1: Fine-Grained Visual Accuracy**
- **Superior Performance**: VAD consistently outperformed direct privileged-view distillation (Vision-OPD) and visual-advantage weighting methods (VA-OPD, V-Zero) at both 4B and 9B scales.
 - At the 4B scale, VAD achieved an average accuracy of 78.32% across six benchmarks (Avg6), leading the best scale-matched alternative (Vision-OPD) by 2.40 points and Decomposed OPD by 2.95 points.
 - At the 9B scale, VAD reached 79.93% Avg6, surpassing the best scale-matched alternative (V-Zero) by 2.80 points and Decomposed OPD by 2.88 points.
- **Parameter Efficiency**: The 4B VAD checkpoint demonstrated capabilities exceeding larger open-source models (e.g., Qwen3.5-397B, Avg6 76.49%) and even closed-source models like Gemini 3 Flash (77.32%) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (78.04%). The 9B checkpoint further extended this lead to 79.93%.
- **Broad Gains**: VAD showed improvements across all six fine-grained visual benchmarks (V*, Zoom, HR-Bench 4K, HR-Bench 8K, MME-RW EN, MME-RW CN) at both scales, indicating its effectiveness across various types of fine-detail localization, high-resolution perception, and real-world recognition tasks.

**RQ2: Semantics of the Attributed Correction**
- **Selective Concentration of Visual Evidence**: Analysis of the original teacher correction ($r_t$), the visually attributed component ($r_{vis_t}$), and the residual component ($r_{res_t}$) revealed that $r_{vis_t}$ selectively concentrates visually grounded and decision-relevant content.
 - Visual attributes, objects/content, and A-D answer decisions jointly accounted for 42.0% of the relative top-5 composition in $r_{vis_t}$, significantly higher than 26.7% in $r_t$ and 17.7% in $r_{res_t}$.
 - Conversely, linguistic/formatting and artifact/meta semantics were less prominent in $r_{vis_t}$ (58.0%) compared to $r_t$ (73.3%) and $r_{res_t}$ (82.3%).
- **Semantic Separation**: Token-level analysis showed that answer symbols (A-D), visual attributes (e.g., "vertical," "dark," "gray"), and object tokens (e.g., "metal," "square") were more prominent in $r_{vis_t}$. Linguistic scaffolding (e.g., "to," "the," "inside") and prompt- or degradation-related tokens (e.g., "blurred," "pixel") were more prominent in $r_{res_t}$. This supports the interpretation that $r_{vis_t}$ carries the visual evidence-to-decision signal, while $r_{res_t}$ retains the unattributed portion of the teacher correction.

**RQ3: Effects of Alternative Supervision Targets**
- **Branch-Aware Reconstruction as Main Gain**: Controlled experiments with Qwen3.5-4B demonstrated that branch-aware target reconstruction (from $q^{one}_T$ to $q^{VAD}_T$) contributed a 1.00-point gain in Avg6 (from 77.06% to 78.06%) without regularization.
- **Benefit of Regularization**: The weak teacher anchor provided a smaller, but consistent, improvement. Full VAD (with regularization) achieved the highest average of 78.32%, 0.80 points above the regularized one-sided projection.
- **Joint Offline Effect**: Offline studies on answer-token positions indicated that the reconstructed VAD target yielded the largest joint effect on correct-token support and wrong-token suppression. Correct-token support increased from 7.52 points (direct teacher correction) to 7.89 points (VAD target), while wrong-token suppression increased from 6.29 points to 6.61 points. This suggests that VAD strengthens both aspects of correctional guidance.

**RQ4: Generalization of Post-Trained Checkpoints**
- **Preservation of Broad Capabilities**: VAD-trained models maintained or slightly improved generalization on held-out tasks (MMVP, CV-Bench, MMStar, POPE) that were outside the fine-grained suite used for post-training.
 - At 4B, VAD achieved the highest held-out average (79.14%), a +0.24 point improvement over the Base model.
 - At 9B, VAD also achieved the highest average (82.62%), a +0.23 point improvement over the Base model.
- **Consistent Positive Delta**: VAD was the only post-trained method to show a positive change ($\Delta$) in held-out performance at both scales, indicating that its visual specialization did not come at the cost of broader model capabilities.

**RQ5: Effects of Training Choices**
- **Robustness to Hyperparameters**: The performance of VAD was robust around an optimal setting for the weak-regularizer weight ($\lambda=0.10$) and the positive-branch cap ($\tau^+=0.80$ for 4B, $0.70$ for 9B). Nearby configurations remained within 0.20 points of the peak performance.
- **Divergence Choice**: Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) yielded the highest aggregate accuracy at both scales compared to Forward and Reverse KL divergences, demonstrating it as the most balanced default for VAD's objective.

In summary, the results indicate that VAD effectively attributes teacher corrections to visual evidence, leading to substantial and broad improvements in fine-grained visual perception while preserving or enhancing general MLLM capabilities, all without incurring significantly higher training costs compared to existing OPD methods. The semantic analysis provides empirical support for the mechanism of visual attribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Visual Attribution Distillation (VAD) framework introduces a novel approach to knowledge transfer in Multimodal Large Language Models, addressing a core limitation of existing on-policy distillation methods. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several dimensions:

1. **Enhanced Visual Grounding and Precision**: By explicitly disentangling visually attributable corrections from source-mixed teacher signals, VAD enables more precise and targeted learning of visual details. This can lead to MLLMs that are less prone to "perceptual misses" and generate responses more accurately grounded in the visual evidence, a critical factor for reliability in real-world applications.

2. **Improved Model Performance and Efficiency**: VAD consistently outperforms established multimodal OPD methods across various fine-grained visual benchmarks at both 4B and 9B scales. Crucially, it achieves this without requiring significantly larger training budgets or introducing additional inference-time complexity. The ability of a 4B VAD model to surpass larger open- and even closed-source MLLMs on these tasks highlights its parameter efficiency and potential for developing more capable models with fewer resources.

3. **Preservation of Generalization**: A notable impact of VAD is its ability to improve fine-grained visual perception without sacrificing broader MLLM capabilities. The positive gains on held-out, generalist benchmarks indicate that VAD's focused visual training does not lead to "catastrophic forgetting" or an overly narrow specialization, which is vital for the development of versatile MLLMs.

4. **Interpretability of Knowledge Transfer**: The semantic analysis of the attributed correction ($r_{vis_t}$) demonstrates that VAD effectively concentrates task-relevant visual and decision-making information, while non-visual or generic linguistic aspects reside in the residual. This contributes to a clearer understanding of *what* knowledge is being transferred and *why* specific corrections are applied, moving towards more interpretable distillation processes.

5. **Addressing a Fundamental Distillation Problem**: The identification and mitigation of "source-mixed" teacher corrections represent a conceptual advance in on-policy knowledge distillation for multimodal settings. VAD's counterfactual target reconstruction paradigm, which prioritizes *what* to distill over *how strongly* to distill, offers a blueprint for designing more effective and semantically aligned distillation objectives in complex machine learning systems.

**Potential Future Impact**:
- **Robust MLLM Development**: VAD could become a standard technique for post-training MLLMs to enhance their visual perception without compromising other abilities, leading to more robust and reliable systems for tasks like detailed image description, visual question answering, and multimodal dialogue.
- **Foundation for Attributable AI**: The methodology of attributing specific components of a model's correction to controlled interventions could inspire similar approaches in other domains of AI, contributing to the broader goal of building more transparent and explainable AI systems.
- **Reduced Computational Footprint**: By enabling smaller models to achieve performance levels comparable to or exceeding much larger models, VAD supports efforts towards more computationally efficient and sustainable AI research and deployment.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors**:
1. **Compositional Evidence Bias**: The current VAD framework uses a single contrastive vector from one view pair to represent the visual intervention. This might introduce bias if the relevant visual evidence is compositional or requires integration from multiple distinct perspectives. Future work could explore multiple views or learned directional bases for a richer attribution.
2. **Imperfect Semantic Separation**: While VAD achieves significant semantic enrichment in the attributed component, the authors note that it does not yield a perfectly "identifiable separation." The attributed component may still retain some nonvisual teacher effects, and the residual component remains source-mixed. Further research into learned decompositions with explicit grounding constraints might lead to cleaner and more complete attribution.

Despite these limitations, VAD presents a significant step forward in making multimodal knowledge distillation more effective, interpretable, and aligned with the underlying visual task.
