# How Reinforcement Learning After Next-Token Prediction Facilitates Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a theoretical framework to explain why reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, following next-token prediction (NTP) pre-training, enhances the generalization capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). The study demonstrates that RL amplifies rare but correct "chain-of-thought" sequences, leading to increased response lengths and rapid performance improvements, even for tasks where NTP alone fails.

## Problem

- Understanding why LLMs trained solely with next-token prediction often struggle to generalize complex reasoning tasks, especially when explicit 'chain-of-thought' (CoT) demonstrations are scarce in pre-training data.
- Investigating the mechanisms by which reinforcement learning (RL) post-training leads to dramatic, sample-efficient improvements in LLM performance and generalization.
- Explaining the observed phenomenon of increased response length in LLMs when fine-tuned with RL for reasoning tasks.

## Method

- A comprehensive framework combining theoretical analysis using simplified ==autoregressive linear== models with extensive empirical validation on decoder-only transformers (GPT-2, Llama series).
- Used a synthetic XOR parity prediction task with varied proportions of short vs. long 'chain-of-thought' (CoT) sequences, then extended experiments to number multiplication and real-world mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH).
- Models were initially pre-trained with next-token prediction (NTP), followed by fine-tuning using RL algorithms such as STaR, REINFORCE, and GRPO, with correctness-based reward functions.

## Results

- Pre-trained models failed to generalize the parity task under greedy decoding when the proportion of CoT data (`p_cot`) was below a critical threshold (e.g., 1/3), producing short, incorrect answers, but showed 'length calibration' under sampling.
- Switching to RL algorithms immediately increased model performance from random guess to near **100%** accuracy and significantly increased the length of generated responses across synthetic and real-world mathematical reasoning tasks.
- Theoretical proofs for linear autoregressive models confirmed that RL (specifically STaR with CoT correctness reward) enables efficient learning of the parity task in `O(poly(d))` iterations, requiring only `O(log((1-p_cot)/p_cot))` RL rounds for generalization, explicitly linking this success to increased response length.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training with next-token prediction (NTP) alone fails to generalize complex reasoning tasks when 'chain-of-thought' (CoT) demonstrations are rare, due to an estimation limitation rather than approximation capacity.
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) rapidly boosts performance and enables generalization by effectively re-weighting the training data, amplifying the signal from correct, often longer, CoT sequences through its reward-weighted objective.
> - The observed increase in generated response length during RL fine-tuning is an inherent learning phenomenon, driven by optimization pressures that favor longer, more elaborate reasoning paths, rather than solely a need for increased representational depth.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and timely question within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs): the mechanisms behind the remarkable success of reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning applied *after* initial next-token prediction (NTP) pre-training. This two-stage training paradigm, often involving human feedback (RLHF) or AI feedback, has been instrumental in unlocking advanced reasoning capabilities in LLMs, leading to models like OpenAI's o1 and Deepseek's R1.

The broader research landscape for LLMs has seen significant empirical breakthroughs, but a comprehensive theoretical understanding of *why* certain training recipes work remains elusive. Previous theoretical work, such as that by Malach (2024) and Joshi et al. (2025), has shown that autoregressive models can be universal learners, capable of efficiently learning any computable binary function, *provided they have access to perfect "chain-of-thought" (CoT) data*. This paper takes a crucial step in relaxing this strong assumption. It posits a more realistic scenario where elaborate, correct reasoning (CoT) sequences are *present but rare* in the pre-training data, a natural way to view vast internet-scale datasets.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the phenomenon of "length increase" in model responses during RL fine-tuning, often dubbed the model's "thinking process." While previous hypotheses linked this to the computational depth required to approximate complex algorithmic tasks (e.g., Feng et al., Li et al.), this work reframes length increase as a "bona fide learning phenomenon." It argues that length growth is not merely about representation capacity but arises directly from optimization pressures, even for tasks where a shallower representation might suffice. This perspective offers a new lens through which to understand and potentially optimize LLM behavior.

By providing both theoretical proofs and extensive empirical validation, this research bridges the gap between empirical observations and a deeper mechanistic understanding, positioning itself as a foundational contribution to the theoretical underpinnings of advanced LLM training techniques. It also directly informs discussions on sample efficiency and generalization in language models, particularly in reasoning domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a comprehensive framework for studying and explaining the success of the prevalent LLM training paradigm: initial pre-training with a next-token prediction objective, followed by fine-tuning with reinforcement learning algorithms. The authors aim to theoretically expose the underlying optimization mechanisms that drive RL's performance improvements over mere next-token prediction in this context.

Specifically, the paper is motivated by a desire to answer three key questions:

1. **Why is generalization difficult for models during pre-training?** The authors seek to understand why, even with a massive corpus of data, standard next-token prediction can struggle to enable LLMs to generalize on challenging tasks, especially when complex reasoning demonstrations are scarce.
2. **How does reinforcement learning lead to rapid improvement in terms of sample efficiency?** The paper investigates how RL fine-tuning can dramatically boost model capabilities and generalization with a relatively small number of additional samples compared to the initial pre-training phase.
3. **What optimization pressures cause the observed increase in the length of model responses?** The motivation here is to provide a mechanistic explanation for why LLMs, when fine-tuned with RL for reasoning tasks, tend to generate longer, more elaborate "chain-of-thought" responses, which often correlates with improved performance.

To address these questions, the authors simplify the learning setting by focusing on a mixture distribution of short and long "chain-of-thought" sequences encoding a single, well-defined task: predicting the parity of *d* bits (XOR). This choice allows for rigorous theoretical analysis while still capturing essential challenges relevant to LLMs. They then extend their empirical investigation to more complex, real-world mathematical reasoning benchmarks to demonstrate the broader applicability of their findings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a dual approach, combining rigorous theoretical analysis with extensive empirical experimentation, across both simplified and complex task settings.

#### 3.1. Learning Setting and Data Generation

The core learning problem involves predicting the parity (XOR) of *d* bits. The authors design a synthetic data distribution, `D(p_cot)`, which is a mixture of two types of sequences for a given input `x`:
- **Short Sequences:** Comprising only the input bits and the final parity answer `(∏d_i=1 xi, <EOS>)`. These are presented with probability `(1 - p_cot)`.
- **Long Sequences (Chain-of-Thought):** Including the input bits, intermediate computations (e.g., `x1, x1x2...`), and the final parity answer `(x1, x1x2..., ∏d_i=1 xi, <EOS>)`. These represent elaborate demonstrations and are presented with probability `p_cot`.
The parameter `p_cot` (proportion of chain-of-thought) is varied to study its impact on learning, particularly focusing on cases where `p_cot` is small (rare long demonstrations).

#### 3.2. Models

- **Empirical Studies:** Decoder-only autoregressive transformers are used, including smaller GPT-2 variants trained from scratch for the parity and number multiplication tasks, and pre-trained Llama series models (Llama 3.2 3B, Llama 3.1 8B) for mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH). These architectures are representative of current LLMs.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** A simplified "autoregressive linear model" is introduced. This architecture consists of a series of `d+1` linear predictors, where each predictor outputs a token based on the input and previously generated tokens. The feature embedding for each linear model includes (at most) second-degree monomials of the input, designed to capture relevant interactions for the parity task. This simplification allows for mathematical tractability while retaining key autoregressive properties.

#### 3.3. Training Stages and Algorithms

- **Pre-training (Next-Token Prediction - NTP):** In this initial stage, models (both empirical transformers and theoretical linear models) are trained to predict the next token in a sequence sampled from `D(p_cot)`. The objective is the standard logistic loss, often with `ℓ2` regularization in the theoretical setting.
- **Post-training (Reinforcement Learning - RL):** After a certain number of pre-training steps, training switches to RL. Three common RL algorithms are explored empirically:
 - **STaR (Self-Taught Reasoner):** The model generates responses, only correct ones are kept, and the model is fine-tuned on these using the next-token prediction objective. This process is iterated.
 - **REINFORCE:** A standard policy gradient algorithm that maximizes expected reward. The objective is re-framed as a reward-weighted next-token prediction loss.
 - **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** A PPO-like algorithm used for fine-tuning LLMs, focusing on maximizing reward while ensuring policy stability.
 The reward functions used are binary: "end-to-end correctness" (final answer is correct) or "chain-of-thought correctness" (entire generated sequence, including CoT, is valid).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

Performance is evaluated using:
- **Test Accuracy:** Whether the generated final answer is correct.
- **Generated Sequence Length:** The length of responses under greedy decoding or sampling, indicating the extent of "thinking" or CoT generated by the model.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Approach

The theoretical analysis focuses on the simplified linear autoregressive model trained with SGD. It leverages concepts from convex optimization, Lipschitz functions, and Fourier analysis of Boolean functions. The core idea is to:
- Characterize the behavior of SGD on the NTP objective for each linear component.
- Analyze how the `p_cot` parameter influences the model's calibration and greedy decoding strategy.
- Model the STaR algorithm as an iterative re-weighting of the training data distribution, effectively increasing the proportion of "correct" (and often long) sequences, and then re-applying the NTP analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research uncovers several key findings, supported by both extensive empirical simulations and rigorous theoretical proofs in simplified settings:

#### 4.1. Pre-training Limitations and Length Calibration

- **Failure to Generalize:** For challenging tasks like predicting the parity of *d* bits with a large *d* (e.g., *d*=50) and a low proportion of chain-of-thought (CoT) data (`p_cot` < 1/3), mere next-token prediction (pre-training) fails to generalize. Models under greedy decoding perform at random guess levels, generating only short responses. This is explicitly identified as an *estimation limitation* (insufficient samples for this specific objective) rather than an *approximation limitation* (model capacity).
- **Length Calibration:** Despite failing to generalize under greedy decoding, pre-trained models (when sampled with temperature 1.0) exhibit "length calibration." They learn to generate long, correct responses with a probability approximately equal to `p_cot` and short, incorrect responses (random guesses) with probability `(1 - p_cot)`. This leads to a test accuracy under sampling of `(p_cot + 1)/2`. This demonstrates that models *do* learn from rare long demonstrations but do not prioritize them under greedy decoding if `p_cot` is too low.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning's Rapid Improvement and Optimization Mechanisms

- **Immediate Performance Boost:** Switching from pre-training to RL algorithms (STaR, REINFORCE, GRPO) with a correctness reward leads to an immediate and rapid increase in model performance (from random guess to near 100% accuracy) and a concurrent, significant increase in the length of generated responses. This requires a sufficiently mature pre-training checkpoint.
- **Mechanism of Length Increase:** The key insight is that RL effectively *amplifies* the presence of correct long responses in the training data. Since long, CoT-rich responses have a much higher probability of being correct than short, direct answers (which often require learning a "hard" function in one step), the reward-weighted loss functions of RL disproportionately favor and thus up-sample these beneficial long sequences. This positive feedback loop causes the model to generate longer responses, which in turn enables it to generalize by leveraging the intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The number of samples required during the RL post-training phase for generalization is a minuscule fraction of the samples needed for pre-training, highlighting RL's superior sample efficiency in this context.

#### 4.3. Theoretical Validation (Simplified Linear Models)

- **Pre-training Theory:** For autoregressive linear models, the theory proves that if `p_cot < 1/3`, pre-training fails to generalize under greedy decoding, producing short, incorrect answers. Concurrently, it shows that the model remains length-calibrated under sampling. The critical threshold of `p_cot = 1/3` matches empirical observations for transformers.
- **Post-training Theory:** It is theoretically proven that RL (specifically, the STaR algorithm with a chain-of-thought correctness reward) enables these linear models to efficiently learn to predict the parity of *d* bits in `O(poly(d))` SGD iterations, provided `p_cot` is not exponentially small in *d*. This success is explicitly tied to the increase in response length during RL. The analysis shows that only `O(log((1-p_cot)/p_cot))` RL rounds are sufficient for the model to generalize.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Complex Tasks and Real-World LLMs

- **Number Multiplication:** Experiments with GPT-2 models trained from scratch on *n*-digit multiplication tasks show the same pattern: pre-training struggles with low `p_cot`, while RL rapidly improves accuracy and increases response length.
- **Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks (GSM8K & MATH):** Fine-tuning pre-trained Llama models on mixture variations of these benchmarks demonstrates that RL helps models achieve baseline accuracy (similar to models trained on full CoT data) by significantly increasing their average response length. This efficiency is particularly noticeable for low `p_cot` values. The authors also observe that later stages of RL can lead to model generations qualitatively different from the training data, suggesting "out-of-distribution gains" by leveraging broader pre-training knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the theoretical and practical understanding of Large Language Model training, with several potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Foundational Theoretical Understanding

- **First Theoretical Separation:** The paper provides the first theoretical separation between next-token prediction (NTP) alone and NTP combined with reinforcement learning (RL) in the autoregressive setting. This is a crucial step towards building a solid theoretical foundation for LLM capabilities.
- **Mechanistic Explanation for Length Increase:** It offers the first optimization result demonstrating and explaining *why* response length increases during RL fine-tuning of LLMs. This shifts the perspective from merely viewing longer responses as a computational necessity to recognizing them as a fundamental outcome of the learning dynamics.

#### 5.2. Relaxing Assumptions in LLM Theory

- **Beyond Perfect Chain-of-Thought:** Previous theoretical work often assumed access to perfect CoT data. This paper relaxes this to the more realistic scenario of *rare but correct* CoT demonstrations, showing that even this sparse signal is sufficient for efficient learning when combined with RL. This is vital for developing theories that better reflect real-world LLM training on diverse web data.

#### 5.3. Insights for Training Optimization

- **Understanding `p_cot`:** The identification of a critical `p_cot` threshold (e.g., 1/3 for parity) provides a quantitative understanding of when NTP alone fails and when RL becomes indispensable. This could inform data curation strategies or adaptive training schedules.
- **Strategic RL Deployment:** The finding that RL needs a "sufficiently mature" pre-training checkpoint to succeed suggests guidelines for when to initiate RL fine-tuning. Conversely, starting RL too early can lead to unstable or failed learning.
- **Role of Reward Shaping:** The effectiveness of correctness rewards in amplifying beneficial long sequences highlights the importance of well-designed reward functions in RL fine-tuning. The experiments with length penalties also show potential for controlling response length while maintaining accuracy, which is valuable for inference efficiency.

#### 5.4. Re-interpreting Chain-of-Thought

- **Length as a Learning Phenomenon:** By demonstrating that length growth is an inherent learning mechanism even when representation capacity isn't the primary bottleneck, the paper encourages a deeper investigation into how optimization pressures shape the emergence of CoT. This could lead to new ways of inducing or controlling CoT generation.

#### 5.5. Broader Applicability

- The consistent observations across synthetic parity, number multiplication, and complex mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH) suggest that the identified mechanisms are fundamental and broadly applicable to various LLM reasoning tasks.

In summary, this research significantly advances our theoretical understanding of a core LLM training recipe, providing actionable insights into why and how RL boosts reasoning capabilities and influences the generation of elaborate responses. It lays groundwork for future work on more complex real-world data distributions and noise models, ultimately contributing to the design of more effective and efficient LLMs.
