# Spatial-Temporal Aware Visuomotor Diffusion Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A new visuomotor imitation learning method, 4D Diffusion Policy (DP4), integrates a dynamic Gaussian world model to capture 3D spatial structures and 4D spatiotemporal dynamics. This approach significantly improves task success rates and generalization across diverse simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks without increasing inference time.

## Problem

- Existing visual imitation learning methods primarily rely on behavior cloning, limiting their ability to understand underlying physical rules and generalize to novel scenarios.
- Current approaches struggle to inherently capture the complex 3D geometry and dynamic 4D spatiotemporal relationships within interactive environments.
- The lack of explicit spatial-temporal awareness hinders robust object interaction and environment-aware decision-making for robots in varied real-world settings.

## Method

- DP4 introduces a dynamic Gaussian world model, built using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), to provide structured supervision for learning 3D spatial and 4D spatiotemporal perceptions.
- It implements multi-level 3D spatial awareness from single-view RGB-D inputs to reconstruct scene geometry and 4D spatiotemporal awareness to predict future environmental states.
- A denoising diffusion framework is conditioned on these learned structured spatial-temporal representations to generate optimal, physically-grounded action trajectories.

## Results

- DP4 achieved an 84.7% overall success rate on Adroit simulation tasks, a 16.4% improvement over the 3D Diffusion Policy (68.3%).
- It demonstrated an 82.5% overall success rate on DexArt simulation tasks, outperforming 3D Diffusion Policy (68.5%) by 14.0%.
- In real-world robotic tasks, DP4 obtained a 54.6% overall success rate, an 8.6% improvement compared to 3D Diffusion Policy (46.0%), confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling 3D spatial structures and 4D spatiotemporal dynamics through a dynamic Gaussian world model offers richer supervision than pure behavior cloning.
> - Integrating Gaussian Splatting allows for efficient and high-quality representation and prediction of dynamic 3D scenes for robotic policy learning.
> - Conditioning diffusion policies on learned spatial-temporal representations significantly enhances generalization and robustness in complex robotic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual imitation learning (IL) is a critical area in robotics, enabling robots to acquire complex skills by observing expert demonstrations. This field has seen substantial advancements, particularly with the integration of deep learning and computer vision techniques, leading to impressive results in tasks like object grasping, locomotion, and dexterous manipulation. However, a persistent challenge remains: generalizing these learned skills to unseen scenarios, especially when confronted with dynamic and interactive environments.

The existing landscape of visual imitation learning can be broadly categorized by its representational focus:
- **2D Image-based Methods**: These leverage the rich information and ease of acquisition from raw RGB images. While powerful for visual feature extraction, they often struggle to inherently capture the 3D geometry of objects and scenes.
- **3D Representation-based Methods**: Approaches using point clouds or object geometries explicitly encode spatial structure, enhancing spatial reasoning. While an improvement, many still treat trajectories as static sequences of actions, overlooking the dynamic spatiotemporal relationships that arise from environment interactions.
- **Diffusion Models in Robotics**: Building on their success in generative AI (image/video generation), diffusion models have been adapted for policy learning in robotics (e.g., Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy). These models excel at handling complex, multi-modal action spaces and generating diverse action sequences. However, they often rely heavily on behavior cloning from supervised trajectories, implying a limitation in their understanding of underlying physical dynamics.
- **World Models**: This area focuses on learning a predictive model of the environment's dynamics, allowing agents to anticipate future states given current actions. While effective in encoding scene dynamics, early world models often relied on latent space autoencoding, which can be data-intensive and limited by implicit feature capacity.

The paper "Spatial-Temporal Aware Visuomotor Diffusion Policy Learning" directly addresses the limitations of existing methods by explicitly integrating **3D spatial awareness** and **4D spatiotemporal dynamics** into diffusion-based policies. It posits that current visual imitation learning methods, despite their successes, inadequately capture the physical rules governing dynamic environments because they largely rely on "trajectory cloning" without a deep understanding of scene structure or how actions affect future states.

DP4 positions itself as a bridge between these areas, specifically by:
- **Enhancing Diffusion Policies**: It augments traditional diffusion policies with a structured understanding of the world, moving beyond mere behavioral cloning.
- **Leveraging Novel World Models**: It integrates Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), a highly efficient and high-quality rendering technique, into its world model construction. This is a significant departure from prior world models, offering a more explicit and efficient way to represent and predict scene dynamics.
- **Focusing on Generalization**: By learning explicit 3D structures and predicting 4D spatiotemporal changes, DP4 aims to improve the generalization and robustness of robotic policies, which is crucial for deployment in varied real-world scenarios.

In essence, this work argues that true generalization in robotic manipulation requires more than just mimicking observed behaviors; it demands an intrinsic understanding of the physical world—its static structures (3D) and its dynamic evolution over time (4D)—which DP4 aims to provide through its novel integration of a dynamic Gaussian world model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the 4D Diffusion Policy (DP4) is to overcome the limitations of existing visual imitation learning methods in capturing and utilizing **3D spatial structure** and **4D spatiotemporal dependencies** for complex robotic tasks. While current approaches, including advanced diffusion policies, have shown promise, they predominantly rely on "behavior cloning" from supervised historical trajectories. This reliance means they often fail to infer the essential physical properties needed for robust spatial reasoning, effective object interaction, and environment-aware decision-making in real-world, dynamic scenarios.

The key objectives of this research are therefore:
1. **Integrate Spatial-Temporal Awareness into Diffusion Policies**: To develop a novel visual imitation learning framework that explicitly incorporates an understanding of both the 3D structure of the environment and the 4D dynamic changes occurring over time. This moves beyond merely replicating actions to reasoning about their effects.
2. **Learn from a Dynamic Gaussian World Model**: To leverage a structured world model, specifically one based on Gaussian Splatting, that can actively encode 3D spatial structures and 4D spatiotemporal variations. This model serves as a source of rich supervision during the imitation learning process.
3. **Enhance 3D Spatial Perception**: To enable accurate reconstruction of the current scene's geometry from single-view RGB-D inputs, ensuring spatially consistent representations that capture critical structural details.
4. **Enforce 4D Spatiotemporal Awareness**: To allow the system to predict and adaptively refine future environmental states based on the agent's planned trajectories, thereby modeling temporal consistency and dynamic interactions.
5. **Condition Trajectory Generation on Structured Representations**: To ensure that the diffusion policy generates action trajectories not just by mimicking past behaviors, but by reasoning over these learned structured spatial-temporal representations of current and future states.
6. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization**: To demonstrate that the proposed DP4 framework significantly outperforms existing multi-task visual imitation methods across diverse simulated and real-world robotic tasks, thereby setting a new benchmark for generalization and robustness.

In essence, the motivation is to imbue robotic policies with a deeper, more physically grounded understanding of their environment, moving from "what to do" (behavior cloning) to "what will happen if I do this" (world modeling), to ultimately enable more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The 4D Diffusion Policy (DP4) is a novel visual imitation learning method designed to integrate spatial-temporal awareness into diffusion-based policies. It addresses the limitations of existing methods by employing a dynamic Gaussian world model to provide informative supervision for learning 3D spatial and 4D spatiotemporal perceptions from interactive environments. The framework comprises three core components: Multi-level 3D Spatial Awareness, 4D Spatiotemporal Awareness, and Diffusion-based Decision.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

DP4 operates within the visual imitation learning paradigm, where a robot learns skills from expert demonstrations provided as offline datasets of observation-action pairs. The agent receives a single-view RGB-D observation $O^{(t)} = (C^{(t)}, D^{(t)})$ at time $t$ and must predict an optimal continuous action sequence $a^{(t+1)}$. Unlike prior methods that solely rely on behavior cloning, DP4 aims to incorporate a deeper understanding of 3D structures and 4D spatiotemporal dynamics.

#### 3.2. Multi-level 3D Spatial Awareness

To capture the complex 3D structures of the interactive world, DP4 creates multi-level spatial representations from single-view RGB-D input without requiring impractical multi-camera setups or extensive prior environmental data.
- **Point Cloud Generation**: RGB-D images are converted into 3D point clouds using camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters.
- **Learning 3D Representations**:
 - **3D Local Representation**: A cropped point cloud (focused on critical elements like the robot arm) is encoded into a compact 3D local representation using a lightweight MLP network, the **3D Local Encoder**.
 - **3D Global Representation**: The complete point cloud is converted into a $100^3$ voxel grid, which is then encoded by the **3D Global Encoder** into a global representation $v^{(t)}$.
- **Enhancing 3D Spatial Awareness with Gaussian Splatting**: To embed global structural and texture information, DP4 employs a **Generalizable Gaussian Regressor**. This regressor takes the deep volume $v^{(t)}$ as a scene representation to parameterize Gaussian primitives (position, color, rotation, scale, opacity), forming a Gaussian world model based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).
 - **Neural Rendering**: RGB and depth images ($C^{(t)}(p), D^{(t)}(p)$) are rendered from these Gaussian primitives using alpha-blend rendering.
 - **3D Loss ($L_{3D}$)**: The Generalizable Gaussian Regressor and 3D representation are optimized by minimizing the squared difference between the rendered images and the ground-truth RGB ($C^{*(t)}$) and depth ($D^{*(t)}$) observations. This loss ensures the 3D representation emphasizes local visual features while incorporating global structural and texture knowledge.

#### 3.3. 4D Spatiotemporal Awareness

DP4 integrates dynamics into the Gaussian world model to achieve 4D spatiotemporal awareness, enabling future scene reconstruction and providing valuable supervision.
- **Dynamic Gaussian World Model**: This model predicts the future state $s^{(t+1)}$ from the current state $s^{(t)}$ and action $a^{(t)}$. It parameterizes a Gaussian mixture distribution for dynamic Gaussian Splatting.
- **Parameter Propagation with Deformable MLP**: A **deformable MLP** $p_\phi$ is used to estimate changes in Gaussian parameters ($\Delta\theta^{(t)}$) based on the current Gaussian parameters ($\theta^{(t)}$) and the action $a^{(t)}$. This deformation yields future Gaussian parameters $\theta^{(t+1)} = \theta^{(t)} + \Delta\theta^{(t)}$.
- **Future Scene Reconstruction**: A Gaussian renderer $R$ generates predicted RGB and depth images ($C^{(t+1)}(p), D^{(t+1)}(p)$) from $\theta^{(t+1)}$ and the camera pose $w$.
- **4D Loss ($L_{4D}$)**: To model 4D spatiotemporal relationships, consistency supervision is enforced between the predicted future scenes and their real future counterparts ($C^{*(t+1)}, D^{*(t+1)}$). This loss serves as a spatiotemporal enhancement penalty, allowing the multi-level 3D representation to learn physical properties and 4D dynamics.

#### 3.4. Diffusion-based Decision

The learned multi-level 3D representation (local and global) and the current robot state are used to condition a denoising diffusion framework, which predicts the robot's optimal actions.
- **Conditional Denoising Diffusion**: A denoising network $\epsilon_\theta$ progressively converts randomly sampled Gaussian noise $a_K$ into a noise-free action $a_0$ over $K$ iterations, conditioned on the multi-level 3D representation $r$ and robot state $q$.
- **Action Loss ($L_{action}$)**: The denoising network is trained using an MSE loss to predict the noise added to a data point sampled from the dataset.

#### 3.5. Overall Learning Objective and Training Strategy

The overall learning objective for DP4 is a weighted sum of the action, 3D spatial, and 4D spatiotemporal losses:
$L_{DP4} = L_{action} + \lambda_{3D} L_{3D} + \lambda_{4D} L_{4D}$
where $\lambda_{3D}$ and $\lambda_{4D}$ are hyperparameters to balance the different objectives. DP4 employs a joint training strategy, with a warm-up phase (first 500 iterations) where the deformable mapper is frozen to stabilize the 3D representation and Gaussian regressor before simultaneously training the entire Gaussian world model and diffusion-based decision module. Crucially, the Gaussian world model is **only used during training for supervision**; during inference, the integrated spatiotemporal awareness within the multi-level 3D representation directly guides action generation, ensuring minimal inference time overhead.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of DP4 across 17 simulated tasks (173 variants) and 3 real-world robotic tasks demonstrates its significant superiority over existing state-of-the-art visual imitation learning methods.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

- **Adroit Simulation Tasks**: DP4 achieved an impressive overall success rate of **84.7%**, significantly outperforming 2D-based methods (IBC, BCRNN, Diffusion Policy) and 3D-based methods like 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3) which scored 68.3%. For challenging tasks like "Pen," DP4 achieved 75% success, while DP3 only managed 43%.
- **DexArt Simulation Tasks**: DP4 recorded an overall success rate of **82.5%**, showcasing a substantial lead over 2D methods (e.g., Diffusion Policy at 49.0%) and a **14.0%** improvement over 3D Diffusion Policy (68.5%). Tasks like "Faucet" and "Toilet" saw success rates of 84% and 64% respectively, indicating robust dexterous manipulation capabilities.
- **RLBench Simulation Tasks**: DP4 demonstrated state-of-the-art performance, achieving average success rates of **63.3%** and **39.9%** across two sets of challenging tasks. This significantly surpassed prior perception-based (PerAct, GNFactor) and generative approaches (ManiGaussian), demonstrating its ability to accurately predict optimal continuous trajectories from a single RGB-D observation.
- **Real-World Robotic Tasks**: In experiments using a KINOVA GEN2 robot, DP4 outperformed Diffusion Policy and 3D Diffusion Policy across "Grasping Bottles," "Stacking Cups," and "Pouring Water" tasks. DP4 achieved an overall success rate of **54.6%**, an **8.6%** improvement over DP3 (46.0%), confirming its practical applicability and effectiveness in real-world settings.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparisons

- **Visualization of Trajectories**: Qualitative examples illustrated that DP4's generated action sequences accurately complete tasks, unlike DP3, which often failed due to a lack of scene understanding. DP4 effectively interpreted 3D scene structures (e.g., nail positions) and 4D dynamics (e.g., pen rotation), leading to successful task completion (marked in green, versus red for deviations).
- **Visualization of Gaussian World Model**: The visualizations showed that DP4's Gaussian world model successfully captures fundamental spatial structures and spatiotemporal dynamics. Even under the realistic single-view constraint, the renderings, while lacking minute details, effectively represent scene-level 3D structures and 4D dynamics crucial for task success. For example, fine details like a nail's thread are irrelevant to the hammering task, and the model correctly focuses on essential features.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Ablation**: Experiments on Adroit tasks rigorously confirmed the contribution of each proposed component. Starting from a base model, progressively adding RGB supervision, then 3D spatial loss (RGB and Depth supervision), and finally 4D spatiotemporal supervision (DP4) consistently improved success rates across "Hammer," "Door," and "Pen" tasks. Notably, integrating these awareness components did not substantially increase the model's inference time, as the Gaussian world model is only used during training.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis**: An analysis on the "Pen" task revealed optimal hyperparameters for balancing the 3D spatial loss ($\lambda_{3D}$) and 4D spatiotemporal loss ($\lambda_{4D}$), finding that $\lambda_{3D} = 0.1$ and $\lambda_{4D} = 0.01$ yielded peak success rates.

Overall, the findings strongly validate DP4's core hypothesis: explicitly modeling 3D spatial structures and 4D spatiotemporal dynamics through a dynamic Gaussian world model significantly enhances the performance, generalization, and robustness of diffusion-based visuomotor policies across a wide array of complex robotic manipulation tasks in both simulated and real environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of the 4D Diffusion Policy (DP4) represents a significant advancement in visual imitation learning for robotics, with profound implications for the capabilities and deployment of autonomous systems. Its core contribution lies in bridging the critical gap between visual imitation learning and fundamental spatial-temporal physical reasoning.

The key significance and potential impacts include:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: By explicitly modeling 3D spatial structures and 4D temporal dynamics, DP4 moves beyond rote behavior cloning. This intrinsic understanding of the physical world allows robotic policies to generalize much more effectively to unseen scenarios, varied object properties, and dynamic environmental changes. This is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured, unpredictable real-world environments where variations are common.
2. **More Intelligent Robotic Agents**: DP4 enables robots to possess a deeper "awareness" of their surroundings and the consequences of their actions. Instead of merely replicating motions, the robot can reason about the geometry of objects, their interactions, and how the scene will evolve. This capability fosters the development of truly intelligent, context-aware robotic agents.
3. **New Benchmarks for Imitation Learning**: The unprecedented success rates achieved by DP4 across a diverse range of 17 simulated tasks and 3 real-world tasks (including challenging dexterous and deformable object manipulations) set a new benchmark for imitation learning. This provides a strong foundation for future research and challenges the community to integrate similar structured world models.
4. **Practical Real-World Applications**: The demonstrated effectiveness in real-robot tasks, such as grasping, stacking, and pouring, underscores the practical utility of DP4. This work paves the way for robots to perform more complex, multi-stage, and interactive tasks in industries like manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and even domestic settings, where precise manipulation and adaptability are paramount. The collaboration with NeuHelium Co., Ltd further emphasizes this practical orientation.
5. **Integration of Advanced Computer Vision Techniques**: The innovative integration of Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into a dynamic world model for policy learning is a notable methodological contribution. 3DGS offers an efficient and high-quality way to represent and render 3D scenes, proving its value beyond traditional computer graphics applications and into the realm of robotic control.
6. **Reduced Data Dependency (Potential)**: While not explicitly stated as a core finding, by learning fundamental physical properties and dynamic models, DP4 might implicitly reduce the amount of expert demonstration data required compared to pure behavior cloning methods that need to learn every nuance from demonstrations. A more generalized understanding of the world might allow for learning from fewer, more diverse examples.
7. **Foundation for Trustworthy AI**: Given Professor Yanwei Fu's affiliation with the Institute of Trustworthy Embodied AI, this work contributes to the broader goal of making AI systems more reliable and predictable in physical interactions. A robot that understands the physical rules of its environment is inherently more trustworthy and less prone to unexpected failures.

In conclusion, DP4 signifies a move towards more holistic and intelligent robotic policy learning, where robust visual perception and predictive world modeling are not just auxiliary features but integral components of the decision-making process. This research significantly pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with imitation learning, bringing us closer to robots that can operate autonomously and reliably in complex, dynamic human environments.
