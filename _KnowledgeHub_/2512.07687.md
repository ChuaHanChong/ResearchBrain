# HalluShift++: Bridging Language and Vision through Internal Representation Shifts for Hierarchical Hallucinations in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> HalluShift++ detects hallucinations in multimodal LLMs from their own internal states rather than an external judge. It extends distribution-shift features with layer-consistency, attention-concentration Gini, perplexity and token-repetition signals, decomposes captions into object, attribute and relation chunks, and scores each chunk across four classes. AUC-ROC rises 27.7-64.1% over HalluShift across eight MLLMs, reaching 96.5% on LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Problem

- Dominant detectors use external LLM judges like GPT-4, which themselves hallucinate, adapt poorly across domains, and give one verdict without naming the hallucination type.
- Rule-based and binary metrics such as CHAIR and POPE catch only object-existence errors, and are sensitive to prompt design and caption length.
- Internal-state methods stay text-only: HalluShift models distribution shift in LLM decoders and misses visual grounding failures and cross-modal interactions entirely.

## Method

- Twelve new internal features on top of ==HalluShift=='s 62: ==layer-consistency== cosine similarity between early and late decoder layers, ==attention-concentration Gini== over the last three attention layers, perplexity/confidence statistics, and token-repetition ratios.
- **Semantic chunking** splits generated text into object, attribute and relation chunks via ==part-of-speech==, ==named-entity recognition== and ==dependency parsing==, each tagged with word count, chunks-per-image and relative position.
- **Hierarchical ground-truth matching** labels chunks in precedence order (category before attribute before relation); a three-layer ==membership network== maps the 74+3-dimensional vector to four-class probabilistic scores.

## Results

- Across eight MLLMs on MS-COCO and LLaVA, AUC-ROC gains of **27.7-64.1%** over HalluShift; Llama-3.2-11B-Vision reaches **91.7%** and LLaVA-1.5-7B-HF **96.5%** with **94.8%** precision.
- Text-only QA also improves: OPT-6.7B on TruthfulQA goes **89.91** to **92.68** AUC-ROC, beating HaloScope (**73.17**) and CCS* (**63.91**) by wide margins.
- Chunking ablation scales monotonically with granularity: no chunking **0.612**, sentence-level **0.698**, object-only **0.743**, object+attribute **0.798**, complete semantic **0.847** AUC-ROC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Where a claim sits in a generation is highly predictive of its truth: chunk relative position dominated feature importance at 0.2142, an order above any internal-state feature.
> - Finer semantic decomposition monotonically improves detection, so hallucination labels belong on sub-sentence units, not whole responses.
> - Detection quality tracks feature design, not model scale: a 1.66B model gained ~58% AUC-ROC, making internal-state detection viable under tight compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The proliferation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has ushered in a new era of AI capabilities, enabling complex vision-language understanding and generation tasks like image captioning and visual question answering. However, a critical challenge that plagues these advanced models is "hallucination," where models generate linguistically coherent but factually incorrect or inconsistent descriptions relative to the visual input. This phenomenon severely degrades model reliability and user trust, posing significant risks for real-world applications.

The broader research landscape for hallucination detection in MLLMs can be largely divided into two main categories:
1. **External Evaluator Methods:** The dominant approach involves using another LLM (often a powerful general-purpose model like GPT-4) as an external judge to evaluate the factual correctness of MLLM outputs. Methods like GAVIE, FAITHScore, and HaELM fall into this category, typically providing a binary (hallucinated/not) or a single accuracy score.
 - **Limitations:** These methods are inherently susceptible to the evaluator LLM's own hallucination tendencies, face challenges in domain adaptation, are computationally expensive, and often cannot provide fine-grained insights into *what type* of hallucination occurred. Furthermore, they rely on external, often proprietary, models, which can hinder research and practical deployment.
2. **Internal Representation Analysis Methods:** A more nascent but growing area, particularly for text-only LLMs, explores the hypothesis that hallucinated content leaves distinctive, measurable patterns within the model's internal representations (e.g., hidden states, attention weights). Prior works like Haloscope, CCS\*, and HALLUSHIFT have investigated covariance patterns, eigenvalue decomposition, and distribution shifts in LLM layers to detect hallucinations without external supervision.
 - **Limitations (prior work):** While promising for LLMs, adapting these methods directly to MLLMs presents unique challenges. Hallucinations in MLLMs often stem from misaligned visual-linguistic representations and inadequate visual grounding, which are not adequately captured by text-only internal analyses.

HALLUSHIFT++ strategically positions itself to address the critical gap in the MLLM hallucination detection landscape. It significantly advances the internal representation analysis paradigm by extending it to multimodal contexts. By developing novel features specifically tailored to the visual-linguistic interaction, semantic decomposition of generated text, and a hierarchical classification system, HALLUSHIFT++ moves beyond binary detection and the limitations of external evaluators. This work represents a crucial step towards developing robust, interpretable, and self-contained hallucination detection mechanisms for MLLMs, thereby improving their trustworthiness and utility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced, internal-state-based framework, HALLUSHIFT++, for hierarchical hallucination detection in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This framework aims to not only identify the presence of hallucinations but also classify their specific nature across different semantic granularities: category, attribute, and relation.

The key motivations driving this research stem from several critical limitations and challenges observed in the current state of MLLM hallucination detection:

1. **Over-reliance on External LLM Evaluators:** Existing methods predominantly depend on external, often powerful, LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) to act as judges. This approach is problematic because external evaluators can themselves hallucinate, may struggle with domain adaptation, are computationally expensive, and do not integrate seamlessly into MLLM development pipelines. The authors are motivated to move beyond this dependency towards a self-contained, internal detection mechanism.
2. **Lack of Granular Hallucination Analysis:** Current methods typically provide only a binary classification (hallucinated or not) or a single overall accuracy score. This coarse-grained feedback offers little insight into the *type* of hallucination (e.g., inventing an object vs. mislabeling an attribute) which is crucial for targeted model improvement and debugging. The motivation is to provide token-level, hierarchical annotations that can inform more precise architectural design and training adjustments.
3. **Challenges in Multimodal Contexts:** While internal representation analysis has shown promise for hallucination detection in text-only LLMs (e.g., HALLUSHIFT), these methods often fail to capture the complex visual-linguistic interactions specific to MLLMs. Hallucinations in MLLMs frequently arise from inadequate visual grounding and misaligned representations between visual and linguistic modalities. This work is motivated to adapt and extend internal analysis to explicitly address these unique multimodal failure modes.
4. **Need for Robust and Interpretable Features:** The authors hypothesize that hallucinations manifest as measurable irregularities or "distributional shifts" within the internal layer dynamics of MLLMs. The challenge is to derive robust and interpretable features from these internal distributions that reliably distinguish between factually grounded and hallucinated content, and further classify the nature of the hallucination.
5. **Improving MLLM Reliability and User Experience:** Ultimately, the motivation is to enhance the trustworthiness and performance of MLLMs in real-world applications. By accurately detecting and characterizing hallucinations, developers can build more reliable models, leading to better user experiences and safer deployment of AI systems.

In essence, HALLUSHIFT++ seeks to provide a more sophisticated, intrinsic, and actionable approach to hallucination detection, moving beyond simple identification to a deeper understanding of *why* and *how* MLLMs hallucinate, thereby empowering developers to build more robust and factually grounded multimodal AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HALLUSHIFT++ is an enhanced hallucination detection framework that builds upon the foundational concept of internal distribution shifts, extending it from text-only LLMs to the complex domain of MLLMs. The methodology comprises three core components: an extended feature construction phase, a novel semantic chunking strategy, and a hierarchical ground truth matching system feeding into a multi-class membership function.

**A. Feature Construction:**
The framework utilizes a 74-dimensional feature representation derived from the internal states of MLLMs, encompassing hidden states, attention weights, and token probabilities.

- **Original HALLUSHIFT Features (1-62):** These foundational features are adopted from the original HALLUSHIFT, designed to capture distributional shifts within LLM internal layers and token probability-based indicators of hallucination.
- **Enhanced HALLUSHIFT++ Features (63-74):** To address the unique challenges of MLLMs—namely cross-layer inconsistency, attention dispersion, and confidence degradation due to visual-linguistic misalignment—12 novel features are introduced:
 - **Layer Consistency Features (LCF, 2 features):** These measure the alignment between early and late text decoder layers using cosine similarity of flattened hidden states. Disagreement (1-cosine similarity) indicates a loss of visual grounding.
 - **Attention Concentration Features (ACF, 2 features):** Utilizing Gini coefficients on attention weights from the last three attention layers, these features quantify attention dispersion. High Gini values indicate focused attention (desirable), while low values suggest scattered attention (hallucination-prone). Mean and standard deviation of Gini coefficients are extracted.
 - **Perplexity and Confidence Features (5 features):** These detect deteriorating certainty in generated text, a strong indicator of hallucination. They include: Mean Perplexity (MPe), Perplexity Standard Deviation (SPe), Confidence Trend (Tr, slope of max probabilities), Mean Confidence (MPr), and Low-Confidence Token Fraction (LCC, proportion of tokens with max probability < 0.5).
 - **Token Pattern Features (3 features):** These assess the repetitiveness and diversity of generated text. Unique Repetition Ratio (URR), Bi-gram Repetition Ratio (BRR), and Normalized Unique Tokens (NUT) are computed. High repetition and low diversity often signal over-reliance on language priors rather than visual evidence.

**B. Semantic Chunking Strategy:**
A crucial innovation is the decomposition of generated text into semantically meaningful units, moving beyond monolithic analysis. This strategy adapts existing techniques for vision-language tasks:
- **Chunk Types:** Generated text is automatically parsed into three types of chunks: object mentions (nouns/proper nouns), attribute-object pairs (adjective-noun combinations), and relationship patterns (preposition/verb-based connections).
- **NLP Techniques:** Part-of-speech (POS) analysis, Named Entity Recognition (NER), and dependency parsing are employed to identify and extract these chunks.
- **Chunk-Specific Features:** Each chunk is augmented with three contextual features: Chunk Word Count (CWC), Chunks Per Image (CPI), and Chunk Relative Position (CRP), providing insights into semantic density, descriptive granularity, and temporal ordering.

**C. Hierarchical Ground Truth Matching:**
To create supervised labels for training, a hierarchical verification process is implemented, prioritizing different hallucination types:
- **Ground Truth Extraction:** Three functions parse ground truth annotations (G) from datasets into searchable representations: canonical object labels, attribute-object tuples, and relational triplets. Lexical dictionaries for object, attribute, and relation categories aid this process.
- **Chunk Analysis and Verification:** For each generated chunk, comparable information (object, attribute, relation claims) is extracted. The verification proceeds hierarchically:
 1. **Category Hallucination:** If an object mentioned in a chunk does not exist in the ground truth (e.g., "a red car" when no car is present), it's a category hallucination. This takes precedence.
 2. **Attribute Hallucination:** If the object exists but its attributes (e.g., "red" for a blue car) are incorrect, it's an attribute hallucination.
 3. **Relation Hallucination:** If objects and attributes are correct, but their spatial or action relationships are wrong, it's a relation hallucination.
- This ensures accurate, multi-class labeling (Correct, Category, Attribute, Relation) for each semantic chunk.

**D. Membership Function:**
The membership function serves as the classifier, extending the original HALLUSHIFT's binary classification to a multi-class probabilistic scoring system.
- **Input:** The 74-dimensional HALLUSHIFT++ features are combined with the 3 chunk-specific contextual features ($\phi(c_i)$) for each semantic chunk.
- **Architecture:** A three-layer neural network with attention mechanisms is used as the membership estimator. Attention dynamically weighs feature importance across different hallucination types.
- **Output:** Probabilistic scores are assigned across the four hallucination classes: Correct, Category, Attribute, and Relation.

**Experimental Setup:**
The framework is evaluated across eight diverse state-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, LLaVA-1.5-7B-HF) on MS-COCO and LLaVA datasets for multimodal tasks. It is also tested on text-only LLM QA tasks using OPT-6.7B and LLaMA-2-7B on TruthfulQA and TyDiQA. Greedy decoding with a max output length of 64 tokens is used. Training involves an AdamW optimizer, cosine annealing, batch size 32, early stopping, and class balancing techniques (SMOTE, class weighting) to handle dataset imbalances, especially for rare attribute hallucinations. Experiments run on NVIDIA A40 GPUs with mixed-precision training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis of HALLUSHIFT++ yields significant findings across both multimodal and unimodal tasks, demonstrating its superior performance and validating its underlying hypotheses.

**A. Performance on Vision-Language Tasks (Table I):**
HALLUSHIFT++ consistently outperforms the original HALLUSHIFT method across all eight tested MLLMs on both the MS-COCO and LLaVA datasets.
- **Significant AUC-ROC Gains:** The most striking result is the substantial improvement in AUC-ROC, ranging from 27.7% to 64.1% over the baseline HALLUSHIFT. For instance, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision achieved an impressive 91.7% AUC-ROC, a 25.9 percentage point increase from HALLUSHIFT's 65.8%. Similarly, LLaVA-1.5-7B-HF showed a remarkable 96.5% AUC-ROC, a 37.7 percentage point increase from HALLUSHIFT's 58.8% on the LLaVA dataset.
- **Model-Agnostic Effectiveness:** The improvements are consistent across models of varying sizes and architectural paradigms, from smaller models like Kosmos-2-Patch14-224 (1.66B parameters, ~58% gain) to larger models like Llama-3.2-11B-Vision (11B parameters). This validates the hypothesis that the novel multimodal-specific features (cross-layer inconsistency, attention dispersion, and confidence degradation) capture universal hallucination patterns in MLLMs.
- **Practical Implications:** The consistent gains across diverse MLLMs and datasets highlight the robustness and generalizability of HALLUSHIFT++. The fact that even smaller models show significant improvements suggests the approach is computationally efficient and practical for resource-constrained environments.

**B. Performance on Language Model QA Tasks (Table II):**
To assess generalizability, HALLUSHIFT++ was also evaluated on text-only question-answering tasks, comparing it against HALLUSHIFT, Haloscope, and CCS\*.
- **Consistent Improvements:** HALLUSHIFT++ maintained its effectiveness, achieving consistent improvements over HALLUSHIFT. For OPT-6.7B on TruthfulQA, it showed a 2.77 percentage point improvement in AUC-ROC (from 89.91% to 92.68%). Similar, though modest, gains were observed across other models and datasets (e.g., LLaMA-2-7B on TyDiQA-GP improved by 2.05 percentage points).
- **Broader Applicability:** These results confirm that the enhanced feature extraction methodology introduced in HALLUSHIFT++ generalizes beyond multimodal scenarios, making it applicable for hallucination detection in diverse language model architectures and task domains.

**C. Ablation Study: Feature Importance Analysis (Fig. 3):**
An ablation study on the best-performing model (Llama-3.2-11B-Vision) revealed the most influential features for hallucination detection:
- **Chunk Relative Position:** Emerged as the most critical feature (+0.2142 importance score), suggesting that the location of content within a generated response is highly predictive of hallucination. Hallucinated content tends to appear in specific positions.
- **Total Chunks per Image:** The second most dominant feature (+0.0309), indicating that models generating overly detailed or numerous chunks may be more prone to hallucination. This feature effectively captures scenarios where models attempt to describe beyond reliable visual evidence, linked to attention dispersion and confidence degradation features.
- **Mean Token Perplexity:** Showed strong contribution (+0.0175), confirming that models exhibit higher uncertainty when generating ungrounded content.
- **Unique Token Ratio:** Proved effective (+0.0134), as repetitive patterns (indicating reliance on memorized language priors) are characteristic of hallucinated content lacking visual grounding.
- The analysis highlights that semantic context features (from chunking) and the novel HALLUSHIFT++ features significantly contribute to the detection accuracy, providing interpretable insights into the nature of MLLM hallucinations.

**D. Ablation Study: Semantic Chunking Effectiveness (Table III):**
The progressive improvements in detection performance across different granularities of semantic decomposition underscore the value of the chunking strategy:
- **Progressive Gains:** Moving from "No Chunking" (AUC-ROC 0.612) to "Complete Semantic Chunking" (AUC-ROC 0.847) yielded substantial performance gains. Intermediate steps like "Sentence-level" (0.698), "Object-only" (0.743), and "Object + Attribute" (0.798) confirmed the incremental benefit of finer-grained analysis.
- **Validation of Hierarchical Analysis:** This outcome validates the premise that hallucinations manifest at different semantic granularities, and a mechanism to isolate and evaluate these units independently (e.g., distinguishing an incorrect object category from an incorrect attribute of an existing object) is vital for precise detection.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate HALLUSHIFT++'s superior performance in detecting hierarchical hallucinations in MLLMs and its generalizability to LLMs. The ablation studies further provide interpretable insights into which features and methodological components contribute most to this success, particularly emphasizing the importance of multimodal-specific internal states and fine-grained semantic analysis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HALLUSHIFT++ represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, particularly in addressing the critical challenge of hallucination. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Shift from Binary to Hierarchical Hallucination Detection:** The most profound contribution of HALLUSHIFT++ is its departure from traditional binary (hallucinated/not) classification to a hierarchical, fine-grained understanding of hallucinations. By classifying hallucinations into Category, Attribute, and Relation types, the framework provides a nuanced perspective that is crucial for both diagnosis and intervention. This level of detail was largely absent in prior internal-representation-based methods and significantly improves upon external evaluator approaches.

2. **Internal, Self-Contained Detection Mechanism:** HALLUSHIFT++ eliminates the reliance on external LLM evaluators, which are prone to their own hallucinations, costly, and difficult to adapt. By leveraging the internal states of MLLMs, this approach offers a more efficient, robust, and ethical solution, removing the dependency on third-party models and facilitating deployment in resource-constrained or sensitive environments.

3. **Actionable Insights for MLLM Development:** The ability to pinpoint the *type* of hallucination (e.g., an MLLM inventing an object vs. misdescribing its color) provides MLLM developers with actionable insights. This fine-grained feedback can directly inform targeted improvements in training data, model architecture design, and alignment mechanisms, leading to more factually grounded and reliable models. For instance, predominant category hallucinations might suggest issues with object recognition, while attribute hallucinations might point to weaker understanding of descriptive properties.

4. **Novel Multimodal-Specific Features:** The introduction of 12 new features specifically designed to capture cross-layer inconsistency, attention dispersion, and confidence degradation in MLLMs is a key technical innovation. These features effectively bridge the gap between internal representation analysis (historically for text-only LLMs) and the unique challenges of vision-language models, demonstrating that multimodal hallucinations leave distinct, measurable signatures within the model's internal dynamics.

5. **Robust Semantic Decomposition:** The semantic chunking strategy, coupled with hierarchical ground truth matching, is a critical methodological enhancement. It allows for the evaluation of hallucination at a natural linguistic granularity, aligning more closely with how humans perceive and assess factual accuracy. This ensures that the detection mechanism is not only effective but also interpretable in terms of human-understandable semantic units.

6. **Generalizability and Scalability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across a wide range of MLLM architectures (small to large) and its continued strong performance on text-only LLM tasks highlight the generalizability of HALLUSHIFT++. This broad applicability suggests its potential as a universal tool for hallucination detection across different AI models and tasks.

7. **Increased Trust and Reliability:** Ultimately, by providing a more reliable and transparent mechanism for detecting and understanding hallucinations, HALLUSHIFT++ contributes significantly to improving the trustworthiness and real-world applicability of MLLMs. This is vital for deploying AI systems in sensitive domains where factual accuracy is paramount, such as healthcare, education, or autonomous systems.

The open-sourcing of the codebase further amplifies its potential impact by encouraging wider adoption, replication, and further research in internal hallucination detection. HALLUSHIFT++ represents a crucial step towards building more transparent, controllable, and verifiably truthful multimodal AI systems.
