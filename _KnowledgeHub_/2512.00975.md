# MM-ACT: Learn from Multimodal Parallel Generation to Act

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-ACT introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that integrates text, image, and robot actions into a shared token space, enabling efficient parallel generation for real-time robotic control. This framework achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average 96.3% success rate on LIBERO simulation tasks and improved generalization to out-of-domain manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack explicit physical dynamics, limiting their ability to generate precise temporal actions despite strong semantic understanding.
- Current unified VLA approaches suffer from slow inference speeds due to autoregressive generation or architectural complexities arising from hybrid decoding strategies.
- Hybrid VLA models frequently encounter objective misalignment issues when combining different generation paradigms (e.g., autoregressive text with diffusion-based action heads).

## Method

- ==MM-ACT== integrates text, image, and robot states/actions into a unified discrete token space, processed by a single Transformer-based ==mask token predictor== with bidirectional attention.
- It employs a =="Context-Shared Multimodal Learning"== paradigm, where task planning (text) and future image prediction (image) jointly supervise and enhance action generation from a shared context.
- The model utilizes a ==one-step parallel decoding== strategy for action generation, enabling low-latency, real-time robot control at high frequencies (up to 40 Hz).

## Results

- MM-ACT achieved an average **96.3%** success rate on LIBERO simulation tasks, outperforming all baselines, and secured a **72.0%** average success rate in real-world Franka robot experiments.
- The model demonstrated improved out-of-domain generalization on RoboTwin2.0 with a **52.38%** average success rate across bimanual tasks in unseen settings.
- The one-step parallel decoding strategy for action generation achieves real-time inference (**0.22s** for an 8-chunk action) without compromising effectiveness, which is crucial for practical robotic deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly supervising task planning and future image prediction from a shared context significantly boosts action generation performance, with unified text-image-action training providing a +9.25% gain on RoboTwin2.0.
> - A unified token space with parallel decoding across all modalities simplifies VLA model architecture and substantially improves efficiency for real-time robotic control.
> - Different modalities exhibit distinct training dynamics (e.g., text overfitting faster than image), highlighting the necessity for careful loss weighting and scheduling in multimodal training pipelines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards "generalist robotic policies" capable of understanding diverse instructions and interacting effectively with complex environments. A key challenge in this pursuit is bridging the gap between high-level semantic understanding (e.g., from language instructions) and low-level physical interaction (e.g., generating robot actions).

**Existing Paradigms and Their Limitations:**
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Many VLA models [3, 21–23, 25, 30, 49] have emerged, often built upon large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). While these excel at visual and semantic understanding, they frequently lack an explicit model of physical dynamics, limiting their ability to guide temporal action generation. They often rely on integrating action heads or expert modules.
- **Visual-Prediction-Driven Decision and Planning Frameworks (World Models):** Other approaches focus on incorporating visual prediction to model future visual dynamics, thereby improving predictability and planning. However, these "world models" are primarily trained for predictive objectives, leading to limited instruction understanding and sub-task planning capabilities.
- **Unified VLA Approaches:** Recent unified VLA models aim to combine understanding and generation across modalities. However, they often inherit the development paradigms of their base models, leading to architectural complexities and slow inference speeds. For instance, some adopt hybrid strategies (autoregressive for text, parallel for image/action) which require multiple attention mechanisms, while others use fully autoregressive generation for all modalities, making action generation prohibitively slow.

**MM-ACT's Contribution to the Landscape:**
MM-ACT positions itself as a novel "unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model" that addresses the limitations of existing paradigms. It proposes a truly unified approach by integrating text, image, and action into a **shared token space** and performing generation across all three modalities using a **parallel decoding strategy**. This contrasts sharply with hybrid approaches and fully autoregressive methods.

By building upon discrete diffusion models, specifically MMaDA, MM-ACT aims to overcome the "objective misalignment" often found in hybrid VLA models that fine-tune diffusion-based action generation on top of autoregressive (AR) pre-trained backbones. MM-ACT maintains a consistent parallel decoding-based generation objective across all training stages, ensuring better control and generalization.

Furthermore, the introduction of **Context-Shared Multimodal Learning** directly tackles the challenge of enhancing action generation through cross-modal learning. By supervising task planning (text), future image prediction (image), and action generation from a shared context, MM-ACT explicitly models the interdependencies between these modalities. This allows it to leverage semantic understanding and physical prediction to improve robotic control, representing a significant step towards more capable and truly generalist robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **MM-ACT, a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of jointly generating text, image, and robot actions efficiently and effectively.** The authors are motivated by the need for generalist robotic policies that combine both high-level semantic understanding for task planning and robust interaction capabilities with the environment through predictive dynamics.

Specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Addressing Limitations of Current VLA Models:** Existing VLA models, while good at semantic understanding, often lack explicit physical dynamics, limiting their ability to generate precise, temporal actions. Conversely, world models, while excelling in dynamics, lack strong instruction understanding. MM-ACT aims to bridge this gap.
- **Improving Efficiency and Architectural Simplicity:** Current unified VLA approaches suffer from slow inference speeds (due to autoregressive generation) or increased architectural and training complexity (due to hybrid decoding strategies). MM-ACT seeks to simplify the architecture by integrating all three modalities into a shared token space and adopting a unified bidirectional attention mechanism. For action generation, it specifically aims for low-latency inference using a one-step parallel decoding strategy.
- **Enhancing Action Generation through Cross-Modal Learning:** The core idea is that task planning (text) and future image prediction (image) can provide valuable context and supervision for action generation. The proposed "Context-Shared Multimodal Learning" paradigm is designed to explicitly leverage these cross-modal relationships, ensuring that learning from one modality benefits others, particularly action generation.
- **Ensuring Consistent Optimization Objective:** By building upon a discrete diffusion language model (dLLM) like MMaDA, MM-ACT aims to maintain a consistent parallel decoding-based generation objective across all training stages. This avoids the objective misalignment issues prevalent when fine-tuning diffusion-based action heads on top of autoregressive pre-trained backbones.
- **Validation across Diverse Settings:** The research aims to validate MM-ACT's effectiveness in both in-domain and out-of-domain manipulation tasks, across simulation (LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0) and real-world Franka robot setups, assessing its robustness and generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MM-ACT is built upon a Transformer-based mask token predictor, leveraging a unified approach to handle text, image, and robot action generation.

#### 3.1. Model Design

- **Unified Token Space:** Text, image, and robot's proprioceptive states are represented as a single sequence of discrete tokens. This is achieved by concatenating vocabularies from modality-specific tokenizers.
- **Modality-Specific Tokenizers:**
 - **Text:** Uses LLaDA model's tokenizer.
 - **Image:** Employs a pretrained image quantizer from Show-o, encoding 256x256 images into 256 tokens from an 8,192-token codebook.
 - **Robot States/Actions:** Adopts a bin tokenizer, quantizing continuous scalar values (normalized to [-1, 1]) into 2,048 dedicated tokens. This allows for both inputting robot states and outputting continuous action values.
- **Bidirectional Attention:** The model uses bidirectional attention over the full multimodal sequence, providing a unified architecture and simplifying the training pipeline compared to hybrid autoregressive/parallel models.
- **Modal Tokens:** A specific modal token (e.g., `<|mm2a|>` for action, `<|mmu|>` for text, `<|t2i|>` for image) is prepended to the context to specify the target generation modality. A fixed-length `<mask>` token block is appended after the context for generation.

#### 3.2. Context-Shared Multimodal Learning (Training Paradigm)

- **Shared Context:** All generation tasks (task planning, future image prediction, action generation) operate from a shared multimodal context. This context includes robot's multi-view observations, task instructions, text descriptions, and optionally robot's states, formatted as a modality-interleaved token sequence.
- **Unified Optimization Objective:** Unlike approaches combining multiple objectives (e.g., AR text + diffusion image), MM-ACT uses the same optimization objective: a mask token predictor. It takes the context and a masked sequence `x_t` as input and predicts all masked tokens simultaneously.
 - The loss function is a unified cross-entropy loss computed only on the masked tokens across all three modalities (Equation 3). Modality-specific weights `λ_modal` control the impact of each loss.
- **Masking Schedules:**
 - **Text:** Linear mask schedule for text modality, following LLaDA.
 - **Image & Action:** Cosine mask schedule to match noise schedules for continuous denoising.
- **Two-Stage Training Strategy:**
 - **Stage 1:** Initial training focuses exclusively on text and image generation tasks (λ_mm2a = 0) until their losses are low. This pre-trains the model on understanding and visual prediction.
 - **Stage 2:** The primary focus shifts to supervising action generation (λ_mm2a = 1), while `λ_mmu` and `λ_t2i` are set to small values (0.05-0.1) to maintain the generative capabilities of text and image and foster cross-modal learning. This ensures action generation benefits from the contextual understanding learned in Stage 1 and refined in Stage 2.

#### 3.3. Parallel Decoding Strategy

- **One-Step Parallel Decoding for Action:** For action generation, the model predicts all action tokens (representing an action chunk) in a single forward pass by setting `t=1` (fully masked sequence). This prioritizes efficiency, crucial for real-time robot control.
- **Re-mask Parallel Decoding for Text and Image:** For text and image generation, MM-ACT employs a re-mask parallel decoding strategy. This involves multiple forward passes with iterative denoising, where a subset of predicted tokens (those with higher confidence) are retained, and lower-confidence tokens are re-masked for subsequent prediction. This strategy is also explored for action generation in ablation studies to compare effectiveness and efficiency.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:**
 - **Simulation:** LIBERO (Franka arm, long-horizon tasks, spatial/object/goal reasoning), RoboTwin2.0 (bimanual manipulation, out-of-domain generalization with domain randomization).
 - **Real-world:** Franka robot with wrist and third-person cameras on three manipulation tasks (press button, stack blocks, sort vegetables/fruits).
- **Datasets:** Official LIBERO datasets, 500 expert episodes/task for RoboTwin2.0 with automatic subtask/future image annotations, 100 tele-operated trajectories per task for Franka real-robot.
- **Baselines:** Compared against VLM-based VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, π0), Visual Prediction VLA (e.g., CoT-VLA, DreamVLA), and Unified VLA models (e.g., UniVLA, WorldVLA).
- **Training Details:** Batch size 128, action chunk size 8. Training from MMaDA base weights, not large-scale robotic pre-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on simulation and real-world robot setups validate MM-ACT's effectiveness and the benefits of its proposed training paradigm.

#### 4.1. Overall Action Generation Performance

- **LIBERO Simulation:** MM-ACT achieved an average success rate of **96.3%**, significantly outperforming all baselines (e.g., 19.8% over OpenVLA, 2.1% over π0, 0.8% over UniVLA). This demonstrates its strong in-domain manipulation capabilities. Specifically, the "vanilla" MM-ACT achieved 95.0%, and including text-based task planning further boosted Libero-Long performance by **+5.0%** (from 88.0% to 93.0%), leading to the overall 96.3% average.
- **RoboTwin2.0 (Out-of-Domain):** MM-ACT achieved **52.38%** average success rate across eight bimanual tasks in unseen settings, outperforming π0 (48.13%) and OpenVLA-OFT (23.13%). This highlights its generalization capabilities.
- **Franka Real-world Experiments:** MM-ACT secured the highest average success rate of **72.0%** across three real-world tasks, surpassing π0 (70.0%) and OpenVLA-OFT (58.6%). This confirms its applicability and robustness in physical environments.

#### 4.2. Impact of Context-Shared Multimodal Learning

- **Enhancement of Action Generation:** The core finding is that context-shared multimodal learning significantly boosts action generation. On RoboTwin2.0:
 - Joint text-action training: +3.37% improvement.
 - Joint image-action training: +5.62% improvement.
 - Unified text-image-action training: **+9.25%** improvement, achieving the highest success rate of 52.38%. This directly validates the hypothesis that cross-modal learning enhances action performance.
- **Image Quality Assessment:** Qualitatively, generated images closely resemble subgoal images, predicting dynamic environmental changes. Quantitatively (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), image generation quality **improved** in Stage 2 (joint image-action training) compared to Stage 1 (image-only training), suggesting the image modality continuously benefits from the training pipeline.
- **Text Quality Assessment:** In Stage 1 (text-only training), MM-ACT demonstrated strong task planning (81.5% accuracy). However, in Stage 2 (joint text-action training), text generation performance **deteriorated** to 68.7%. This is attributed to the text modality overfitting rapidly in Stage 1 and then struggling to generalize when co-trained with the action modality, which has a slower fitting process.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Action Decoding Strategy:**
 - For a typical action chunk size of 8, the **one-step parallel decoding** strategy was more efficient (0.22s inference time) and equally effective compared to re-mask parallel decoding (1.06s inference time, 0.75% lower success rate).
 - However, for a larger action chunk size of 16, **re-mask parallel decoding** provided a significant **+13.00%** improvement in success rate, although still with a higher inference time (1.06s). This suggests re-masking is beneficial for longer parallel token sequences.
 - Considering real-time robotic control, the one-step parallel decoding with chunk size 8 (40 Hz frequency) was chosen.
- **Text and Image Decoding Strategies:** Both one-step and re-mask decoding strategies for text and image modalities provided beneficial improvements to action generation. Re-mask decoding for images yielded slightly greater benefits, possibly due to better alignment with the base model's pre-training.
- **Robot's State in Context:**
 - Including robot's state in the **image context** further **enhanced** action generation performance (from +5.62% to +8.37%), indicating a beneficial interaction.
 - Conversely, including robot's state in the **text context** **reduced** the beneficial effect on action generation (from +3.37% to +0.37%), suggesting a potential sensitivity or less straightforward integration for text-based planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MM-ACT makes several significant contributions to the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Unified and Simplified Architecture:** MM-ACT offers a novel and compact framework for discrete multimodal generation (text, image, and action). By integrating all three modalities into a shared token space and utilizing a single Transformer-based mask token predictor with bidirectional attention, it eliminates the architectural and training complexities associated with hybrid autoregressive and diffusion models. This simplification makes the development and scaling of generalist robot policies more tractable.

2. **Efficient Real-time Action Generation:** The adoption of a one-step parallel decoding strategy for action generation is a crucial innovation for real-time robotic control. It significantly reduces inference latency, enabling high-frequency control (up to 40 Hz). This addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying VLA models for practical robotic applications, where fast, reactive decision-making is paramount.

3. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** MM-ACT achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across a diverse range of complex robotic manipulation benchmarks, including both in-domain (LIBERO, Franka real-world) and challenging out-of-domain (RoboTwin2.0) settings. Its ability to generalize to unseen environments and bimanual tasks demonstrates its robustness and potential for real-world deployment. The consistent outperformance of baselines validates the architectural design and training paradigm.

4. **Effective Cross-Modal Learning:** The "Context-Shared Multimodal Learning" paradigm is a key conceptual contribution. It empirically demonstrates that jointly supervising task planning (text) and future image prediction (image) from a shared context can significantly boost action generation performance (e.g., +9.25% gain on RoboTwin2.0). This highlights the synergistic potential of multimodal learning for embodied agents, where different modalities can mutually reinforce learning and lead to more intelligent behaviors. It provides a concrete method for VLA models to leverage high-level reasoning (text planning) and predictive understanding (future images) to improve low-level control.

5. **Stepping Stone Towards Generalist Robots:** By effectively integrating semantic understanding, predictive dynamics, and action generation within a unified and efficient framework, MM-ACT brings the field closer to realizing truly generalist robotic policies. It provides a robust foundation upon which future research can build, potentially by incorporating more modalities (e.g., audio, haptics) or scaling to even larger datasets.

6. **Insights into Multimodal Training Dynamics:** The ablation studies and analysis of text and image generation quality offer valuable insights into the training dynamics of multimodal models. The observation that text can overfit quickly while image benefits continuously from joint training highlights the need for careful scheduling and weighting of different modal losses, informing future training strategies for complex multimodal systems.

In conclusion, MM-ACT represents a significant advancement in Vision-Language-Action modeling, offering a compact, efficient, and highly effective framework that leverages cross-modal learning for robust robotic control. Its impact will likely be felt in accelerating the development of more capable and general-purpose robots in both research and practical applications.
