# OccWorld: Learning a 3D Occupancy World Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> OccWorld introduces a generative 3D occupancy world model for autonomous driving, forecasting 4D semantic occupancy and ego vehicle trajectories up to 3 seconds into the future. It significantly outperforms baselines in 4D occupancy prediction and achieves competitive motion planning performance without relying on explicit instance-level or HD map annotations.

## Problem

- Traditional perception-prediction-planning (PPP) pipelines demand extensive, costly ground-truth labels for each module and often provide only coarse, object-level predictions.
- Errors propagate sequentially within modular autonomous driving systems, impacting overall safety and reliability.
- Existing world models lack explicit 3D understanding, struggling to capture fine-grained scene information or jointly model complex dynamic 3D environments and ego motion.

## Method

- A self-supervised ==3D Occupancy Scene Tokenizer==, based on a ==Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)==, converts dense 3D occupancy into compact, discrete spatial tokens.
- A ==Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer==, structured like a ==GPT==, autoregressively predicts future scene tokens and ego movements by modeling ==hierarchical multi-scale spatial-temporal dependencies==.
- **OccWorld** jointly forecasts the future 4D semantic occupancy of the entire scene and the ego vehicle's trajectory, capturing their high-order interactions.

## Results

- OccWorld-O (ground-truth occupancy input) achieved superior 4D occupancy forecasting, with an average mIoU of **17.14%** and IoU of **26.63%** for a 3-second future horizon, significantly outperforming baselines.
- It demonstrated competitive motion planning with an average L2 error of **1.17m**, comparable to state-of-the-art methods that often rely on extensive auxiliary supervision (e.g., HD maps, bounding boxes).
- The framework showed the feasibility of end-to-end self-supervised 4D occupancy forecasting and planning, achieving meaningful performance even when trained solely with self-supervised camera inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D semantic occupancy is an expressive, label-efficient, and versatile representation for a world model, enabling fine-grained scene understanding without requiring explicit instance-level or HD map annotations.
> - A hierarchical spatial-temporal generative transformer can effectively learn and model the complex dynamics of a driving world from discrete scene tokens, facilitating both future scene prediction and ego planning.
> - Jointly predicting future 3D scene states and ego motion within a single world model leads to more coherent and effective decision-making by capturing higher-order interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work sits at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in artificial intelligence for autonomous driving: 3D perception, predictive modeling, and end-to-end learning. It specifically addresses fundamental limitations of the conventional perception-prediction-planning (PPP) pipeline and explores the emerging paradigm of "world models" in the context of self-driving vehicles.

Traditionally, autonomous driving systems are modular, separating perception (identifying objects and building maps), prediction (forecasting object movements), and planning (deciding the ego vehicle's trajectory). While successful, this pipeline suffers from several drawbacks:
- **Annotation Burden:** Each module often requires extensive, labor-intensive, and costly ground-truth labels (e.g., 3D bounding boxes, high-definition maps, semantic segmentation).
- **Information Granularity:** Object-level predictions (e.g., bounding box trajectories) often fail to capture fine-grained scene information, such as deformable objects, complex interactions, or dynamic changes in static elements like drivable areas.
- **Sequential Error Propagation:** Errors at an early stage (perception) can propagate and compound in later stages (prediction, planning), impacting overall system safety and reliability.

Recent advancements have seen a push towards "end-to-end" autonomous driving, where raw sensor data directly informs planning decisions, often through powerful deep learning models. However, many of these still rely on intermediate representations (like BEV features, object queries) that might still be influenced by the need for explicit supervision or lack direct interpretability of future scene states.

The concept of "world models," originating from control engineering and artificial intelligence, aims to learn an internal model of the environment to predict future states given actions and past observations. While world models have shown promise in reinforcement learning and general generative tasks (e.g., 2D image generation using models like StableDiffusion), their application to complex, safety-critical 3D dynamic environments like autonomous driving is challenging. Existing image-based world models lack explicit 3D understanding, and point cloud forecasting models often ignore semantic information and modality versatility.

OccWorld introduces a novel approach by developing a world model specifically within the **3D semantic occupancy space**. This is a significant contribution because:
- It moves beyond object-level predictions to a more **fine-grained, dense 3D representation** of the entire scene, capturing both dynamic agents and static environmental elements (e.g., road structure, vegetation).
- It aims to **reduce reliance on explicit instance-level annotations** by leveraging 3D occupancy, which can be acquired more economically (e.g., from sparse LiDAR scans or even self-supervised methods from cameras), moving towards a more scalable and label-efficient paradigm.
- It **jointly models scene evolution and ego motion**, acknowledging the high-order interactions between the ego vehicle and its environment, rather than treating prediction and planning as separate tasks.
- By using a GPT-like generative transformer, it seeks to learn the underlying dynamics of the driving world in an **autoregressive and interpretable manner**, potentially paving the way for more robust and adaptable self-driving systems.

In essence, OccWorld bridges the gap between high-level conceptual world models and the detailed 3D perception required for autonomous driving, offering a promising alternative to the traditional pipeline with benefits in data efficiency, granularity, and holistic understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the OccWorld framework are:

- **Overcome Limitations of the Traditional Perception-Prediction-Planning (PPP) Pipeline:** The authors explicitly highlight the serial design of the conventional autonomous driving pipeline, which demands ground-truth labels at each stage (e.g., instance-level bounding boxes, high-definition maps) and typically predicts only coarse object movements. This annotation burden and lack of fine-grained scene information are key drivers for a new approach.
- **Develop a Comprehensive World Model for Autonomous Driving:** The core objective is to learn a "world model" that can predict how the entire 3D driving scene evolves over time, rather than just isolated object trajectories. This involves modeling the future states of both dynamic agents (cars, pedestrians) and static environmental elements (drivable areas, sidewalks).
- **Joint Prediction of Scene Evolution and Ego Movement:** A crucial goal is to simultaneously forecast the future 3D scene (4D occupancy) and plan the ego vehicle's trajectory. This joint modeling is hypothesized to capture higher-order interactions between the self-driving car and its surroundings, leading to more coherent and effective decision-making.
- **Leverage 3D Occupancy as a Robust Scene Representation:** The authors are motivated to use 3D semantic occupancy as the foundation for their world model due to three key advantages:
 1. **Expressiveness:** It provides a fine-grained, dense 3D structural and semantic description of the environment, unlike sparse bounding boxes or 2D segmentation maps.
 2. **Efficiency:** It can be economically learned from sparse inputs like LiDAR points or even self-supervision from temporal frames, reducing the need for laborious human annotations.
 3. **Versatility:** It is modality-agnostic, adaptable to inputs from various sensors like monocular cameras, surround cameras, or LiDAR.
- **Facilitate Interpretable End-to-End Autonomous Driving with Minimal Supervision:** By moving towards a self-supervised scene tokenizer and a generative world model, the authors aim to reduce the reliance on explicit human-annotated labels (like instance bounding boxes or HD maps) for both scene understanding and planning. This contributes to the vision of scalable and more "interpretable" end-to-end autonomous driving systems.
- **Pave the Way for Large Driving Models:** Inspired by the success of large generative models (like GPT) in NLP, the research seeks to explore autoregressive generative modeling in the 3D domain for autonomous driving, hinting at future large-scale, foundation models for this domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The OccWorld framework is designed as a novel world model in the 3D semantic occupancy space, comprising two main components: a 3D Occupancy Scene Tokenizer and a Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer.

#### 3.1. Scene Representation: 3D Semantic Occupancy

The core representation `y` is 3D semantic occupancy, which discretizes the 3D space around the ego vehicle into `H × W × D` voxels. Each voxel is assigned a label indicating whether it's occupied and, if so, by what semantic class (e.g., car, pedestrian, drivable surface). This dense representation provides both structural and semantic information, offering a fine-grained understanding of the scene suitable for modeling complex interactions and changes.

#### 3.2. 3D Occupancy Scene Tokenizer

To bridge the gap between low-level 3D occupancy and the high-level concepts required for a world model, the authors propose a self-supervised scene tokenizer based on a **Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)**.
- **BEV Transformation:** For computational efficiency, the 3D occupancy `y` is first transformed into a Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) representation `ŷ ∈ R^(H×W×DC')`. This is achieved by assigning each semantic category a learnable class embedding and concatenating these embeddings along the height dimension.
- **Encoder:** A lightweight Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder processes the BEV representation to extract down-sampled features `ẑ ∈ R^(Hd × Wd × C)`.
- **Vector Quantization:** A crucial step involves quantizing these continuous features into discrete tokens. A learnable codebook `C ∈ R^(N×D)` containing `N` codes is maintained. Each spatial feature `ẑ_ij` from the encoder output is classified to its nearest code `c ∈ C` using an L2 norm. This process converts the continuous features into a compact, discrete scene representation `z ∈ R^(H×W×C)`. Each code in the codebook implicitly represents a high-level concept or pattern in the 3D scene.
- **Decoder:** A 2D deconvolutional decoder then reconstructs the original BEV occupancy `ỹ` from these quantized tokens, progressively upsampling `z` to its original resolution. A softmax layer is applied to classify each voxel into semantic categories.
- **Training:** The VQ-VAE is trained end-to-end using a reconstruction objective, specifically a combination of softmax cross-entropy loss and Lovasz-softmax loss, to ensure accurate reconstruction of the input 3D occupancy.

#### 3.3. Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer

The core of OccWorld is a GPT-like autoregressive transformer designed to predict future scene tokens and ego movements based on historical data.
- **World Tokens:** The output `z` from the scene tokenizer, along with an ego token `z0 ∈ R^C` encoding the ego vehicle's spatial position, forms the "world tokens" `T = {z_i}`. The world model `w` operates on these tokens to predict `T_T+1` given `T_T, ..., T_T-t`.
- **Hierarchical Multi-scale Processing:** The transformer employs a hierarchical architecture to efficiently handle the large number of world tokens and capture multi-level scene information:
 - **Spatial Aggregation:** Within each time step, spatial aggregation modules (e.g., self-attention) are applied to world tokens `T` to model intrinsic dependencies (e.g., how different objects or static elements relate to each other).
 - **Down-sampling:** Scene tokens are progressively down-sampled (e.g., by merging 2x2 windows) over `K` stages to create hierarchical scales `{T0, ..., TK}`, representing the 3D scene at different levels of detail.
- **Spatial-wise Temporal Causal Self-Attention:** For each spatial scale `i`, a sub-world model `wi` applies temporal causal self-attention. This mechanism processes tokens `z_j,i` at a fixed spatial position `j` across `t+1` historical time stamps (`z_T,j,i, ..., z_T-t,j,i`) to predict the corresponding token `ẑ_T+1,j,i` for the next frame. The "causal" aspect means future tokens cannot influence past predictions.
- **U-net Integration:** Finally, a U-net-like structure aggregates the predicted tokens from different scales, ensuring spatial consistency across the generated future scene.
- **Output Decoding:**
 - **3D Occupancy:** The predicted scene tokens `ẑ_T+1` are fed into the pre-trained VQ-VAE decoder `d` to reconstruct the future 3D occupancy `ŷ_T+1 = d(ẑ_T+1)`.
 - **Ego Trajectory:** A separate ego decoder `d_ego` is learned to predict the ego displacement `p̂_T+1 = d_ego(ẑ_T+1,0)` (relative to the current frame) from the forecasted ego token `ẑ_T+1,0`.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

OccWorld adopts a two-stage training strategy:
1. **Stage 1 (Scene Tokenizer Training):** The VQ-VAE encoder `e` and decoder `d` are trained to minimize the reconstruction loss (softmax and Lovasz-softmax) between the input ground-truth 3D occupancy `y` and its reconstruction `d(e(y))`.
2. **Stage 2 (World Model Training):** With the VQ-VAE weights frozen (or used to generate tokens), the spatial-temporal generative transformer `w` and ego decoder `d_ego` are trained. The objective combines:
 - **Scene Token Loss:** Softmax loss between the predicted scene tokens `ẑ_t,j,0` and the ground-truth codes `C(z_t,j,0)` from the codebook for all spatial tokens `j` and time stamps `t`.
 - **Ego Planning Loss:** L2 loss between the predicted ego displacement `d_ego(ẑ_t,0)` and the ground-truth displacement `p_t`.
Masked temporal attention is applied during training to prevent information leakage from future tokens. During inference, the model operates autoregressively, using its own predictions for future frames to generate even further future states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OccWorld was evaluated on two challenging tasks: 4D occupancy forecasting and motion planning, using the nuScenes benchmark and the Occ3D dataset for occupancy.

#### 4.1. 4D Occupancy Forecasting

The task involves predicting 3D occupancy for future frames (up to 3 seconds ahead) given 2 seconds of historical occupancy. Performance was measured using mIoU and IoU.
- **Superior Forecasting Capability:** OccWorld-O (using ground-truth 3D occupancy as input) significantly outperformed the "Copy&Paste" baseline (which simply repeats the current occupancy). For a 3-second future prediction, OccWorld-O achieved an average mIoU of 17.14% and IoU of 26.63%, demonstrating its ability to learn and model complex scene evolutions rather than merely memorizing or replicating static information.
- **End-to-End Vision-Based Results:**
 - OccWorld-D (using TPVFormer predictions from dense 3D occupancy supervision) achieved non-trivial forecasting (mIoU 8.62%, IoU 16.53% avg for 3s future).
 - OccWorld-T (using TPVFormer predictions from sparse semantic LiDAR supervision) showed lower but still meaningful performance (mIoU 3.56%, IoU 8.34% avg).
 - OccWorld-S (using TPVFormer predictions from self-supervised camera inputs, with *no* 3D occupancy supervision during TPVFormer training) demonstrated the potential for entirely self-supervised 4D occupancy forecasting, achieving mIoU of 0.26% and IoU of 5.00% (avg for 3s), despite the extreme difficulty of the task.
- **Visualizations (Figure 1 & 5):** Qualitative results showed OccWorld successfully forecasting movements of surrounding agents (cars, pedestrians) and even completing unseen or evolving map elements like drivable areas. Notably, in some cases, the generated drivable areas appeared "more reasonable" than the ground truth, suggesting the model learns a deeper understanding of scene semantics. However, it struggled with forecasting new vehicles entering the scene, which is inherently difficult without prior input.

#### 4.2. Motion Planning

The task involves predicting the ego vehicle's future trajectory (waypoints) for 3 seconds, evaluated using L2 error (distance to ground truth) and collision rate.
- **Competitive Performance without Map/Instance Supervision:** OccWorld-O (using ground-truth 3D occupancy) achieved an average L2 error of 1.17m (0.64m with VAD metric), which is highly competitive with state-of-the-art methods like UniAD (1.03m) and VAD-Base (1.22m), even though these baselines often rely on extensive auxiliary supervision like HD maps, bounding boxes, and motion tracklets. OccWorld-O significantly outperformed previous occupancy-based methods like OccNet.
- **End-to-End Planning:** OccWorld-D (3D-Occ supervision), OccWorld-T (semantic LiDAR supervision), and OccWorld-S (no explicit 3D-Occ/map/box supervision) also delivered competitive or non-trivial planning results, further highlighting the versatility and potential of the world model approach.
- **Collision Rate:** While L2 error was competitive, OccWorld's collision rate was slightly higher than some SOTA methods. This is attributed to the inherent difficulty of learning precise safety constraints without explicit supervision signals like freespace or bounding box data. However, OccWorld-O achieved a collision rate comparable to OccNet, which *does* use map and box supervision, suggesting the model learns implicit notions of free space from 3D occupancy.
- **Short-Term vs. Long-Term Planning:** OccWorld excelled in short-term planning (e.g., 1s L2 error of 0.43m for OccWorld-O was among the best), but performance degraded faster for longer horizons (e.g., 3s L2 error of 1.99m). This might be due to the generative nature of world models, where diverse future predictions can diverge from a single ground-truth trajectory over time.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Scene Tokenizer Hyperparameters:** The studies confirmed that proper selection of latent spatial resolution, channel dimension, and codebook size is crucial. A codebook size of 512 with a 128-dimensional latent representation and a 50x50 latent spatial resolution yielded the best balance between reconstruction accuracy and forecasting performance. While larger spatial resolutions improved reconstruction, they hindered forecasting by making tokens too low-level.
- **Spatial-Temporal Generative Transformer Components:** All core components were shown to be vital:
 - **Spatial Aggregation:** Discarding spatial attention drastically reduced both forecasting (mIoU 10.07% vs. 17.14%) and planning performance (L2 1.42m vs. 1.17m), highlighting its importance in modeling intra-scene dependencies.
 - **Temporal Attention:** Replacing temporal attention with simple convolutions severely impacted performance (mIoU 8.98%, L2 2.06m), confirming the necessity of long-range temporal modeling.
 - **Joint Modeling of Ego and Scene:** Discarding the ego token entirely (w/o ego) decreased forecasting mIoU (15.13% vs. 17.14%), indicating that joint prediction of ego movement influences the scene forecast. Replacing ego temporal attention with an MLP (w/o ego temporal) led to significantly worse planning (L2 5.89m) and also negatively affected 3D occupancy forecasting, suggesting that accurate ego trajectory prediction is crucial for coherent scene evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OccWorld represents a significant step forward in autonomous driving research, particularly in pushing the boundaries of world models and end-to-end learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering 3D Occupancy World Model:** OccWorld is among the first to successfully implement a world model using 3D semantic occupancy as the primary scene representation. This is crucial for enabling fine-grained, holistic understanding of dynamic 3D environments, moving beyond the limitations of bounding box-based approaches.
- **Reduced Annotation Dependency:** By leveraging 3D occupancy, which can be derived from various sources including self-supervised methods (as demonstrated by OccWorld-S), the framework significantly reduces the reliance on laborious and costly human-annotated instance-level labels (like bounding boxes and HD maps). This makes autonomous driving systems more scalable and adaptable to new environments.
- **Holistic and Interpretable Scene Understanding:** The joint forecasting of 4D occupancy and ego motion allows the model to capture complex interactions between the ego vehicle and its surroundings. The explicit prediction of future 3D scene states offers a more interpretable output compared to abstract latent features, which is critical for safety-critical applications.
- **Foundation for Future End-to-End Systems:** OccWorld demonstrates the feasibility of interpretable end-to-end autonomous driving systems that directly learn to perceive, predict, and plan from sensor inputs, without the traditional modular pipeline. This could lead to more robust and less error-prone systems.
- **Inspiration for Large Driving Models:** By adopting a GPT-like generative transformer and focusing on learning high-level scene tokens, OccWorld paves the way for the development of "large driving models" or "foundation models" for autonomous driving. Such models could potentially be pre-trained on vast datasets (including self-supervised data) and then fine-tuned for specific tasks, similar to the paradigm shift seen in natural language processing.
- **Advancement in 4D Occupancy Forecasting:** The paper formalizes and provides strong baselines for the challenging task of 4D occupancy forecasting, which is vital for understanding future drivable areas, potential obstacles, and dynamic agent behaviors.

While challenges remain, particularly in long-term planning accuracy and collision avoidance without explicit safety supervision, OccWorld provides a compelling vision for how autonomous vehicles can learn to understand and interact with their complex 3D environments more intelligently and efficiently.
