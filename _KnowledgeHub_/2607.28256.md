# When Robots Exchange Meaning: A Demo of Goal-Oriented Semantic Communications for Collaborative Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Toshiba Europe Ltd. demonstrates an end-to-end robot-edge testbed that integrates semantic communication for collaborative robotics, achieving a 42.67x data reduction for visual information while maintaining sufficient quality for semantic mapping and object detection. The system focuses on task-oriented communication, where network utility is measured by application-level outcomes.

## Problem

- Traditional packet-centric communication systems are inefficient for data-rich applications like collaborative robotics, often transmitting irrelevant information.
- Robots generate high volumes of diverse sensory data, leading to significant communication load and potential network congestion.
- A need exists for 6G communication systems to evolve towards task-oriented services, where performance is evaluated by application-level outcomes rather than solely data transport metrics.

## Method

- Implemented robot-side semantic compression using a **VQ-VAE** model to encode RGB frames into compact semantic tokens, drastically reducing visual data payload.
- Utilized an edge node for reconstructing semantic tokens, fusing data (RGB-D, LiDAR, odometry) via ==RTAB-Map== for SLAM, and building an enriched 3D semantic map with object detections.
- Provided a dashboard for real-time monitoring, operator interaction, and high-level mission control, closing the loop between perception, communication, and action.

## Results

- Achieved a **42.67×** data reduction for 320×240 RGB images, compressing 230,400 bytes to a 5,400-byte packed payload.
- The reconstructed images maintained a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of **21.7–21.9 dB**, sufficient for coarse scene awareness and supporting semantic object detection.
- The system successfully created a functional semantic map on the edge, integrating object detections from reconstructed images, and enabled operator interaction via a dashboard for mission control and situational awareness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic compression effectively reduces visual communication load for robots while preserving critical information necessary for higher-level perception tasks like object detection and semantic mapping.
> - Distributing computational loads, with encoding on the robot and heavier processing on an edge node, efficiently leverages edge intelligence for complex AI-driven robotic functions.
> - Application-level metrics such as semantic map utility and mission interaction are more relevant for evaluating task-oriented 6G communication systems than traditional packet-level QoS metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the emerging field of Sixth Generation (6G) wireless communication systems, specifically focusing on the intersection of semantic communication (SemCom), collaborative robotics, and edge intelligence. Current communication systems, often optimized for packet delivery metrics like throughput and latency, are anticipated to evolve in the 6G era towards task-oriented services. In this paradigm, network utility is measured by application-level outcomes and mission success rather than solely by data transport metrics.

Collaborative robotics represents a use-case where this shift is particularly relevant. Robots generate high volumes of diverse sensory data (e.g., camera, depth, LiDAR, odometry), but only a fraction of this information may be directly pertinent to a specific task at a given moment. Traditional communication approaches can lead to network congestion and inefficiencies when transmitting all raw data. Semantic communication is an approach that addresses this by focusing on extracting and transmitting only the "meaning" or task-relevant information, thereby offering potential for significant data compression and more efficient use of communication resources.

The paper contributes to the development of practical testbeds and demonstrations that bridge theoretical concepts of SemCom with real-world robotic applications. It explores how learned semantic representations can reduce communication load while preserving the information essential for robotic functions such as environment perception, mapping, object-level interaction, and multi-robot coordination. This aligns with broader efforts in 6G research to integrate artificial intelligence (AI) directly into the network and leverage embodied AI for physical systems. The work also touches upon the integration with private 5G/Open RAN infrastructures, indicating its relevance to industry efforts in deploying tailored, high-performance networks for specialized applications like robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to demonstrate an end-to-end robot-edge testbed that integrates semantic communication principles into a collaborative robotics scenario. The authors aim to provide a proof-of-concept platform that illustrates how semantic processing functions can be delivered over a networking infrastructure, moving beyond traditional communication metrics to focus on application-level outcomes.

The key motivations driving this research include:

- **Addressing Limitations of Packet-Centric Communication:** Traditional communication systems are optimized for reliable packet delivery, often without considering the semantic content or its relevance to the end application. For collaborative robotics, the success of a mission depends on factors like accurate environment understanding, effective map updates, precise action coordination, and safe mission completion, all of which are influenced by the quality and relevance of communicated information.
- **Leveraging Semantic Communication for Robotics:** The continuous high-rate data streams generated by robots (e.g., RGB-D cameras, LiDAR) present a significant communication load. SemCom offers a solution by focusing on transmitting only the "meaning" or task-relevant information, potentially leading to substantial data compression and more efficient use of network resources. The motivation is to show that this compression can be achieved while preserving critical information for downstream robotic tasks.
- **Developing a Task-Oriented 6G Testbed:** The 6G vision emphasizes task-oriented services where network performance is evaluated based on application outcomes. The authors are motivated to build a practical platform that allows for the study of how communication quality translates into mission execution, environment understanding, and closed-loop operation in a robotic context, rather than just packet delivery metrics.
- **Enabling Edge Intelligence for Robotics:** The architecture distributes computational load between the robot and an edge node. Semantic compression on the robot reduces data transmitted to the edge, where more intensive processing like semantic mapping and object handling can occur. This distribution leverages the computational capabilities of edge infrastructure for complex AI tasks.
- **Facilitating Future Research in Task-Aware Networking:** The project is presented as a "work-in-progress" and a "demo paper," indicating a motivation to provide a foundational platform for future studies. This includes exploring how semantic streams and mission feedback can inform network control, shaping resource allocation and scheduling based on real-time robotic states and tasks.

Specifically, the system is organized around three visible functions, which also serve as sub-objectives:
1. **Semantic compression on the robot:** To reduce the communication load of visual data by encoding it into compact semantic tokens.
2. **Semantic mapping on the edge:** To process reconstructed semantic information, fuse it with other sensor data, and build an enriched 3D map with labeled objects.
3. **Dashboard-based integration for mission control:** To provide an intuitive interface for operators to monitor the system, interact with the semantic map, and issue high-level mission commands, thereby closing the loop between perception, communication, and action.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a robot-edge semantic communication (Sem-Com) testbed for collaborative robotics. The approach integrates hardware, software frameworks, and AI models across several layers.

#### 3.1. System Architecture

The overall architecture (illustrated in Fig. 1) comprises multiple mobile robots and an edge system.
- **Robot Side:** Each mobile robot is equipped with RGB-D cameras and LiDAR sensors. They run ROS 2 for internal communication and control. A key component on the robot is the semantic compression module.
- **Edge Side:** A Jetson Orin edge node serves as the central processing unit. It receives data from the robots, performs data reconstruction, executes Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM), performs semantic mapping, and hosts a dashboard for mission interaction and visualization.
- **Communication Infrastructure:** The system aims for integration with a private 5G/Open RAN setup, where robots act as User Equipment (UEs) and the edge node as a Multi-access Edge Computing (MEC) endpoint. Currently, connectivity carries RGB-D, LiDAR, odometry, Transform (TF), semantic token messages, and dashboard control information, primarily over ROS 2 and UDP sessions.

#### 3.2. Demo Workflow

During a typical operation:
1. The robot publishes conventional telemetry (RGB-D, LiDAR, odometry, TF streams) via ROS 2.
2. In parallel, a semantic encoder on the robot processes RGB frames and produces image tokens.
3. The edge node receives both the conventional telemetry and the semantic tokens.
4. The edge reconstructs the RGB frames from the semantic tokens.
5. The reconstructed visual data, along with other sensor inputs, is fed into the RTAB-Map core for sensor fusion, loop closure, graph optimization, and 3D map building.
6. A semantic mapping module enhances the geometric map by applying a fine-tuned YOLO detector to the reconstructed images, projecting detected objects into the 3D map, and maintaining labeled objects with attributes and confidence.
7. Intermediate and final outputs (e.g., raw and reconstructed views, map growth, semantic objects) are exposed to an operator dashboard.
8. The operator can monitor the scene, compare raw and reconstructed imagery, inspect the map, and issue high-level commands (e.g., start/stop, pause/resume, click-to-go navigation). These commands are routed through a mission API for validation against the map and navigation stack before execution.
9. The system also implements multi-robot RTAB-MAP perception fusion, coordination, and formation control.

#### 3.3. Semantic Compression

- **Model:** A vector-quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) model is used for visual semantic compression and reconstruction.
- **Encoding:** Each 320 × 240 RGB frame is encoded on the robot into an 80 × 60 latent grid, resulting in 4800 discrete tokens. With a codebook size of 512, each token requires 9 bits. The encoder runs using ONNX Runtime for embedded deployment efficiency.
- **Decoding:** The edge node decodes the received token stream using PyTorch to reconstruct the RGB frames.

#### 3.4. Semantic Mapping

- **SLAM Core:** The RTAB-Map core is utilized on the edge node to fuse reconstructed RGB-D, LiDAR, odometry, and TF data, building a 3D geometric map and robot trajectory.
- **Object Detection and Projection:** To enrich the map with semantic information, a fine-tuned YOLO detector is applied to the *reconstructed* images. The detected objects are then associated with depth and pose information, projected into the 3D map frame, and exposed as queryable semantic objects.

#### 3.5. Dashboard and Integration

- The operator dashboard (Fig. 3) provides a consolidated view of the system, including live camera feeds (raw and reconstructed), LiDAR scans, 2D navigation views, and 3D mapping.
- It exposes mission actions, routing them through a mission API to ensure validation against the map and navigation stack.
- The dashboard facilitates real-time comparison of raw and reconstructed imagery against map updates and navigation actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper describes a functional demonstration rather than presenting an exhaustive quantitative evaluation with benchmarks. The key findings and results revolve around the feasibility and utility of the integrated semantic communication system for collaborative robotics.

- **Payload Reduction:** The visual semantic compression pipeline achieved a significant data reduction. A 320 × 240 RGB image, initially 230,400 bytes, was encoded into a packed payload of 5,400 bytes. This represents a 42.67× reduction relative to the model-input RGB bytes.
- **Reconstruction Quality:** Offline tests on saved samples indicated a Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 21.7–21.9 dB for the reconstructed images. Visually, the VQ-VAE model preserved the main scene layout and large objects while discarding fine texture information (Fig. 2). This level of reconstruction quality was deemed sufficient for "coarse scene awareness and proof-of-concept semantic transport."
- **Semantic Map Utility:** The reconstructed visual stream, combined with depth and pose information, successfully supported the creation of a semantic map on the edge. This map incorporated object detections from the reconstructed images, allowing for object-level functions such as semantic search, semantic map inspection, and supporting future go-to-object actions. This finding demonstrates that the information preserved by semantic compression is sufficient for supporting higher-level robotic perception tasks.
- **Mission Interaction and Operator Situational Awareness:** The integrated dashboard facilitated operator monitoring of scene evolution, comparison of raw and reconstructed views, and inspection of map growth. Operators were able to issue high-level commands (e.g., start/stop, pause/resume, click-to-go navigation), which were validated against the map and navigation stack, demonstrating actionable edge outputs. The dashboard design allowed operators to judge the utility of semantic compression in relation to robotic decision support.
- **End-to-End Integration Feasibility:** The demonstration successfully exposed the full path from robot-side visual semantic transport (VQ-VAE token encoding) to edge-side processing (reconstruction, RTAB-Map, semantic object handling) and dashboard-based mission interaction. This indicates the feasibility of integrating these vertical functions over a networking infrastructure.
- **Task-Oriented Observables:** The system demonstrated the ability to expose metrics beyond packet-level QoS, including payload reduction, reconstruction quality, semantic map utility, and mission interaction effectiveness (Table I). These are presented as more relevant application-level outcomes for task-oriented 6G systems.

In summary, the results indicate that semantic representations can reduce visual communication load while retaining sufficient information for environment understanding and operator supervision, demonstrating a practical bridge for 6G experimentation in collaborative robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This demo paper presents a significant step towards the realization of task-oriented 6G systems, particularly in the domain of collaborative robotics. Its potential impact spans several areas:

- **Advancing 6G Research and Development:** The testbed provides a concrete, end-to-end platform for exploring and validating 6G concepts. By moving beyond traditional Quality of Service (QoS) metrics like throughput and latency, the demo focuses on application-level outcomes such as mission success, environment understanding, and operator situational awareness. This aligns with the 6G vision of task-aware networking and offers a practical basis for evaluating communication quality based on its utility to the robotic task.
- **Bridging Semantic Communication and Robotics:** The work demonstrates a practical integration of semantic communication algorithms with real-world robotic applications. It shows that learned semantic representations can effectively compress visual data while preserving enough meaning for complex downstream tasks like 3D mapping, object detection, and semantic navigation. This can accelerate the adoption of SemCom in networked robotics by providing a tangible example of its benefits.
- **Enabling Edge-AI for Robotics:** By distributing computational loads—semantic encoding on the resource-constrained robot and heavy processing (reconstruction, SLAM, semantic mapping) on a more powerful edge node—the system exemplifies an efficient edge intelligence paradigm. This can enable more sophisticated AI-driven robotic capabilities by leveraging centralized processing at the edge while minimizing communication bandwidth requirements from individual robots.
- **Platform for Future Experimentation:** The "work-in-progress" nature of the demo positions it as a foundational platform for future research. It offers integration points for exploring complex topics such as:
 - **Task-aware resource allocation:** How semantic token streams, dashboard feedback, and different robot states (e.g., navigating cluttered areas vs. background mapping) can inform network control and prioritize communication resources.
 - **Energy consumption analysis:** Evaluating the power efficiency of semantic compression and edge processing in real-world deployments.
 - **Multi-robot task execution:** Detailed studies on the coordination and collaboration performance in various scenarios.
 - **Wireless latency characterization:** Understanding the impact of 5G/Open RAN communication paths on end-to-end robotic tasks.
- **Fostering Embodied AI and Physical AI-Enabled Robotics:** By connecting visual semantic transport to map-based object interaction and mission-level control, the platform serves as a practical testbed for embodied AI systems operating in physical environments. It allows researchers to investigate how AI-driven perception and communication directly influence physical actions and task completion for robotic agents.
- **Accelerating Private 5G/Open RAN Deployment:** The testbed is designed with a private 5G/Open RAN target in mind. This provides a concrete example of how specialized network infrastructures can support advanced robotic applications, offering insights into the requirements and benefits of tailored networking solutions for industrial and enterprise use cases.

In conclusion, this work provides a tangible demonstration of how goal-oriented semantic communications can be implemented in a collaborative robotics setting, laying groundwork for future advancements in 6G networking, distributed AI for robotics, and the practical deployment of intelligent autonomous systems.
