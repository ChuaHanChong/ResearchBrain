# Beginning with You: Perceptual-Initialization Improves Vision-Language Representation and Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Vanderbilt University propose a new paradigm for training vision-language models, called perceptual-initialization, which involves first training the vision encoder on human perceptual similarity data before standard web-scale pretraining. This approach led to improvements in zero-shot classification performance by up to 4.0 percentage points and demonstrated superior scaling laws, allowing for more compute-efficient training.

## Problem

- Current vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP rely heavily on noisy, web-scraped image-caption data, which may not accurately reflect human perception.
- Random initialization of deep neural networks creates arbitrary starting points that can hinder alignment with human perceptual space and amplify early training biases.
- Traditional post-hoc alignment methods, such as fine-tuning, are computationally expensive and can distort the VLM's pre-trained representation space, failing to adequately align with human perception.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training paradigm== begins by initializing the vision encoder with knowledge derived from ==human perceptual similarity judgments== using the NIGHTS dataset and a triplet contrastive loss.
- Only the vision encoder's parameters are updated during the first stage, establishing a ==human-aligned initial representation space==.
- The second stage proceeds with standard contrastive vision-language pretraining on web-scale data (YFCC15M), using the perceptually-initialized vision encoder and a randomly initialized text encoder.

## Results

- The perceptually-initialized model improved zero-shot classification accuracy by **+3.8 percentage points** on ImageNet and **+3.5 percentage points** on ImageNet OOD compared to baselines.
- Achieved superior zero-shot retrieval performance across MS-COCO and Flickr30k datasets, with higher recall scores for both image-to-text and text-to-image tasks.
- Demonstrated higher scaling exponents (e.g., ImageNet: β = **0.681** vs. **0.511** for baseline), indicating more efficient learning and faster performance gains as training data increases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initializing vision encoders with human perceptual data leads to more human-aligned representations and superior performance compared to random initialization.
> - Perceptual initialization results in more favorable scaling laws, indicating faster performance improvements with increasing data and greater compute efficiency.
> - Attempting post-hoc alignment through fine-tuning on perceptual data can disrupt learned representations and degrade zero-shot performance, emphasizing the importance of early integration of human perceptual structure.
