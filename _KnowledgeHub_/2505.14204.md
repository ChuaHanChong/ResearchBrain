# Beginning with You: Perceptual-Initialization Improves Vision-Language Representation and Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Vanderbilt University propose a new paradigm for training vision-language models, called perceptual-initialization, which involves first training the vision encoder on human perceptual similarity data before standard web-scale pretraining. This approach led to improvements in zero-shot classification performance by up to 4.0 percentage points and demonstrated superior scaling laws, allowing for more compute-efficient training.

## Problem

- Current vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP rely heavily on noisy, web-scraped image-caption data, which may not accurately reflect human perception.
- Random initialization of deep neural networks creates arbitrary starting points that can hinder alignment with human perceptual space and amplify early training biases.
- Traditional post-hoc alignment methods, such as fine-tuning, are computationally expensive and can distort the VLM's pre-trained representation space, failing to adequately align with human perception.

## Method

- A ==two-stage training paradigm== begins by initializing the vision encoder with knowledge derived from ==human perceptual similarity judgments== using the NIGHTS dataset and a triplet contrastive loss.
- Only the vision encoder's parameters are updated during the first stage, establishing a ==human-aligned initial representation space==.
- The second stage proceeds with standard contrastive vision-language pretraining on web-scale data (YFCC15M), using the perceptually-initialized vision encoder and a randomly initialized text encoder.

## Results

- The perceptually-initialized model improved zero-shot classification accuracy by **+3.8 percentage points** on ImageNet and **+3.5 percentage points** on ImageNet OOD compared to baselines.
- Achieved superior zero-shot retrieval performance across MS-COCO and Flickr30k datasets, with higher recall scores for both image-to-text and text-to-image tasks.
- Demonstrated higher scaling exponents (e.g., ImageNet: β = **0.681** vs. **0.511** for baseline), indicating more efficient learning and faster performance gains as training data increases.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initializing vision encoders with human perceptual data leads to more human-aligned representations and superior performance compared to random initialization.
> - Perceptual initialization results in more favorable scaling laws, indicating faster performance improvements with increasing data and greater compute efficiency.
> - Attempting post-hoc alignment through fine-tuning on perceptual data can disrupt learned representations and degrade zero-shot performance, emphasizing the importance of early integration of human perceptual structure.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Vision-language models (VLMs) like CLIP have revolutionized how artificial intelligence systems understand and connect visual and textual information. These models learn to associate images with their corresponding text descriptions by training on massive datasets of image-caption pairs scraped from the internet. However, a fundamental question remains: should we start with random initialization and hope that web-scale data will eventually align the model with human perception, or can we do better by incorporating human perceptual knowledge from the beginning?

This paper introduces a paradigm shift in training vision-language models through "perceptual-initialization" (PI). Rather than starting with random weights, the authors propose initializing the vision encoder with knowledge derived from human perceptual similarity judgments before proceeding with standard web-scale pretraining. This approach is motivated by research from Vanderbilt University that bridges computer science, data science, and psychology to create AI systems that better align with human perception.

### 2. The Problem with Current Vision-Language Models

Current vision-language models face several fundamental challenges in achieving human-aligned representations:

**Reliance on Noisy Web Data**: Models like CLIP are trained exclusively on image-caption pairs scraped from the internet. While this data is abundant, it often contains noise, inconsistencies, and biases that don't reflect how humans actually perceive and categorize visual information.

**Random Initialization Limitations**: Deep neural networks are highly sensitive to their initial parameter values. The random initialization creates an arbitrary starting point that may not align with the structure of human perceptual space. Early gradient updates tend to amplify directions present in the initialization rather than exploring the full representational space uniformly.

**Post-hoc Alignment Challenges**: Traditional approaches attempt to align models with human perception after the main training phase through fine-tuning. However, this post-hoc alignment can be computationally expensive and may distort the learned representation space that was optimized for the original task.

The authors argue that these limitations stem from a fundamental mismatch between how current AI systems learn (from scratch on noisy web data) and how the target perceptual space is structured (based on human visual processing).

### 3. Perceptual-Initialization Methodology

The perceptual-initialization approach consists of a two-stage training paradigm that fundamentally changes how vision-language models are trained:

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Human Perceptual Initialization

In the first stage, only the vision encoder is trained using human perceptual similarity data:

**Dataset**: The NIGHTS dataset contains 20,000 image triplets with human annotations. Each triplet consists of a reference image and two comparison images, where humans have indicated which comparison image is more similar to the reference through two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) judgments.

**Architecture**: A CLIP model with a Vision Transformer (ViT-B/32) vision encoder and a custom Transformer-based text encoder. Crucially, only the vision encoder parameters are updated during this stage.

**Training Objective**: A triplet contrastive loss that encourages the model to match human similarity preferences:

```
L_triplet = max(0, margin + d(ref, preferred) - d(ref, non-preferred))
```

where `d(·,·)` represents the distance between image embeddings, and the loss encourages the reference image to be closer to the human-preferred image than to the non-preferred one.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Joint Vision-Language Pretraining

The second stage follows standard contrastive vision-language pretraining:

**Dataset**: YFCC15M, containing approximately 15 million image-text pairs from the YFCC100M dataset.

**Architecture**: The full CLIP model, where the vision encoder is initialized with weights from Stage 1, and the text encoder starts with random initialization.

**Training Objective**: The standard symmetric InfoNCE loss used in CLIP:

```
L_InfoNCE = -log(exp(sim(v_i, t_i)/τ) / Σ_j exp(sim(v_i, t_j)/τ))
```

where `v_i` and `t_i` represent the visual and textual embeddings of the i-th image-text pair, `sim(·,·)` is the cosine similarity, and `τ` is a learned temperature parameter.

### 4. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

The authors conducted comprehensive experiments to validate their approach:

**Baseline Models**:
- **Standard YFCC15M Pretraining**: A CLIP model with random initialization trained on YFCC15M
- **Perceptual Fine-tuning**: A CLIP model first trained on YFCC15M, then fine-tuned on NIGHTS data

**Evaluation Benchmarks**:
- **Zero-shot Classification**: 29 image classification datasets spanning five categories (ImageNet, ImageNet OOD, VTAB, Fine-grained & Specialty, Domain & Small)
- **Zero-shot Retrieval**: MS-COCO Captions and Flickr30k for both image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval

**Training Configuration**: Both the perceptual-initialization model and baselines used identical hyperparameters, with 32 epochs of training using the AdamW optimizer, ensuring fair comparison.

### 5. Key Results and Performance Gains

The perceptual-initialization approach demonstrated consistent and significant improvements across multiple evaluation metrics:

#### 5.1. Zero-Shot Classification Performance

The perceptual-initialization model outperformed the baseline on 23 out of 29 classification benchmarks. Key improvements include:

- **ImageNet**: +3.8 percentage points improvement in Top-1 accuracy
- **ImageNet OOD**: +3.5 percentage points, demonstrating better out-of-distribution generalization
- **VTAB**: +2.4 percentage points on this challenging benchmark suite
- **Fine-grained & Specialty**: +3.0 percentage points on datasets requiring detailed visual understanding
- **Domain & Small**: +4.0 percentage points, showing robust performance on specialized domains

#### 5.2. Zero-Shot Retrieval Performance

The model also demonstrated superior performance in image-text retrieval tasks:

For both MS-COCO and Flickr30k datasets, the perceptual-initialization model achieved higher recall scores across all metrics (Recall@1 and Recall@5 for both image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval).

#### 5.3. Ablation Study: Post-hoc vs. Initialization

A critical finding was that perceptual fine-tuning (post-hoc alignment) actually harmed performance. While fine-tuning improved validation accuracy on the NIGHTS dataset itself, it catastrophically disrupted the learned image-text alignment, leading to significantly worse zero-shot classification performance. This validates the authors' hypothesis that incorporating human perceptual structure during initialization is superior to post-hoc alignment approaches.

### 6. Scaling Law Analysis

The scaling law analysis reveals fundamental advantages of perceptual-initialization:

**Higher Initial Performance**: The perceptually-initialized model starts with better performance at small data scales, providing immediate benefits.

**Superior Scaling Exponents**: Across all benchmark families, the perceptual-initialization model achieves higher β values (scaling exponents), indicating faster improvement as more training data is consumed. For example:
- ImageNet: β = 0.681 vs. 0.511 for baseline
- VTAB: β = 0.220 vs. 0.136 for baseline

**Compute Efficiency**: The combination of higher initial performance and better scaling means that perceptual-initialization can achieve target performance levels with less computational resources and training data.

### 7. Implications and Future Directions

This research demonstrates several important principles for building more human-aligned AI systems:

**The Importance of Initialization**: The study provides compelling evidence that initialization matters significantly in deep learning, particularly for vision-language models. By starting with human-aligned representations, models can follow more productive learning trajectories.

**Efficiency Gains**: Perceptual-initialization offers a path to more compute-efficient training. The improved scaling laws suggest that organizations with limited computational resources could achieve better results by incorporating human perceptual data early in training.

**Beyond Fine-tuning**: The failure of post-hoc perceptual fine-tuning highlights the limitations of treating human alignment as an afterthought. Instead, human perceptual structure should be considered a fundamental component of model architecture and training.

**Broader Applications**: The approach could extend beyond vision-language models to other multimodal systems, potentially improving performance in areas like video understanding, robotics, and human-computer interaction.

**Future Research Directions**: The paper opens several avenues for future work, including exploring different types of human behavioral data, investigating the approach with larger model scales, and extending to other modalities like audio or tactile perception.

The research represents a significant step toward creating AI systems that are not just powerful, but also fundamentally aligned with human perception and cognition. By "beginning with you" – incorporating human perceptual knowledge from the start – we can build more effective and trustworthy AI systems.
