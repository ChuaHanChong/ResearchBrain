# Reinforcement Learning with Action Chunking

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed Q-chunking, a method integrating action chunking into temporal difference (TD) reinforcement learning to enable unbiased n-step value backups and enhance online exploration. This approach significantly improved sample efficiency and performance on challenging long-horizon, sparse-reward robotic manipulation tasks compared to existing offline-to-online methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle with effective exploration and sample efficiency in complex, long-horizon tasks, especially with sparse rewards.
- Existing offline-to-online RL methods often suffer from distributional shift and overly pessimistic policies during online fine-tuning.
- Traditional n-step Temporal Difference (TD) learning introduces off-policy bias, making it unstable for accelerating value propagation from diverse data.

## Method

- Introduce **Q-chunking**, which redefines the ==Q-function== and policy to operate on temporally extended ==action sequences (chunks)== of 'h' actions.
- Utilize this chunked Q-function to enable theoretically ==unbiased n-step value backups==, accelerating value propagation without ==off-policy bias==.
- Employ ==behavior regularization==, constraining the policy towards ==temporally coherent action sequences== learned from offline data, to guide online exploration.

## Results

- Q-chunking methods achieved state-of-the-art online sample efficiency, significantly outperforming prior offline-to-online baselines on challenging long-horizon, sparse-reward robotic manipulation tasks.
- The proposed approach consistently outperformed both 1-step TD and naive n-step return variants, demonstrating the stability and effectiveness of its unbiased n-step value estimation.
- Empirical analysis confirmed that Q-chunking generates more temporally coherent action sequences, leading to more structured and diverse exploration compared to single-step baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a Q-function directly on action chunks enables unbiased n-step value propagation, resolving issues of off-policy bias in traditional multi-step TD learning.
> - Constraining policies to generate temporally coherent action sequences, derived from offline data, facilitates more effective and structured online exploration.
> - Action chunking offers a simple yet powerful form of temporal abstraction, potentially simplifying the development of multi-step behaviors compared to complex hierarchical methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) presents a framework for solving tasks based solely on a reward function. However, in complex environments, learning effective policies from scratch through random exploration is often inefficient due to prohibitive computational costs and the difficulty of encountering sparse rewards. This challenge has led to the development of methods that leverage prior offline data to accelerate online learning, termed offline-to-online RL. This setting introduces its own challenges, including distributional shift between offline data and the online policy, and the non-trivial problem of how to utilize offline data for effective online exploration.

In parallel, the field of imitation learning (IL) has increasingly employed action chunking, where policies predict and execute sequences of actions rather than single actions. This technique has been shown to improve policy robustness and better handle non-Markovian behaviors present in real-world demonstrations. While action chunking is less common in traditional RL, due to the Markovian nature of optimal policies in fully observed Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), this work proposes that it can be beneficial for tackling the exploration problem itself.

The paper differentiates its approach from existing methods that incorporate temporal abstraction or action sequencing, such as:
- **Offline-to-online RL algorithms:** While many existing methods pretrain offline and fine-tune online, they often result in overly pessimistic policies that hinder exploration or require complex online tuning of pessimism. This work aims to improve sample efficiency through different mechanisms.
- **RL methods with action chunking:** Prior RL approaches using action chunking typically focus on fine-tuning policies pretrained with IL or apply chunking only to the critic, not the actor. Some methods use Gaussian policies for movement primitives, while others employ multi-level, factorized critic architectures that impose structural assumptions on the action space. This work proposes a more general recipe where both critic and policy operate directly on action chunks without such factorization.
- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) and options framework:** HRL methods learn low-level policies (skills) and a high-level policy that selects among them. A long-standing challenge in HRL is the instability arising from updating both levels simultaneously. This work posits that action chunking can simplify this by collapsing the bi-level optimization into a standard RL objective in a temporally extended action space, while retaining exploration benefits. Previous HRL efforts exploring similar ideas often relied on discrete or heuristically extracted action sequences.
- **Multi-step latent space planning and search:** Model-based RL methods often use learned dynamics models in latent spaces to optimize short-horizon action sequences. In contrast, this work trains a Q-network directly to estimate the value of action sequences without learning a latent dynamics model, focusing on the offline-to-online setting rather than purely online.

This research contributes to the intersection of offline RL, online fine-tuning, and temporal abstraction, proposing a novel application of action chunking to overcome specific challenges in sample efficiency and exploration for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed Q-chunking, that enhances reinforcement learning algorithms for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks, particularly within the offline-to-online RL setting. The authors aim to improve both the sample efficiency of online learning and the effectiveness of exploration by leveraging prior offline data in a specific manner.

The motivation behind Q-chunking stems from several observations regarding the limitations of current RL approaches in this challenging setting:
- **Exploration in complex environments:** Learning from scratch in complex, sparse-reward environments is highly inefficient, as successful task completion often relies on chance before any meaningful learning can occur. Leveraging prior experience is crucial, similar to human learning.
- **Challenges in offline-to-online RL:** While integrating offline data into online learning is promising, it introduces complexities such as distributional shift (where the offline data's policy might differ from the optimal online policy) and the difficulty of utilizing this data to guide effective online exploration. Current methods struggle with balancing pessimism (to avoid exploiting out-of-distribution actions) and sufficient exploration.
- **Underutilization of action chunking in RL:** Action chunking has been effective in imitation learning for modeling complex, non-Markovian behaviors found in expert demonstrations. The authors hypothesize that despite optimal policies in MDPs being Markovian, the *exploration problem* can be better addressed with non-Markovian, temporally extended "skills." Action chunking provides a simple means to achieve this, offering a way to leverage the temporal coherence inherent in offline data.
- **Limitations of n-step return backups:** Standard n-step return methods can accelerate value propagation but suffer from off-policy bias when the data collection policy differs from the current policy. This bias can lead to unstable and inefficient TD learning.

The key objectives are thus to:
1. **Mitigate the exploration challenge:** Develop a mechanism that enables the RL agent to explore its environment with temporally coherent and structured actions, resembling higher-level skills, rather than random single-step actions. This is envisioned to lead to faster discovery of successful trajectories in sparse-reward settings.
2. **Improve stability and efficiency of TD learning:** Address the off-policy bias issue in n-step returns by designing a Q-function that correctly incorporates action sequences, thereby enabling unbiased and accelerated value backups.
3. **Provide a simple and generic recipe:** Offer a straightforward method that can be applied to existing TD-based actor-critic RL algorithms with minimal modifications, making it broadly applicable.

By running RL directly in a "chunked" action space, Q-chunking aims to simultaneously achieve these benefits: the policy can generate temporally coherent actions by being regularized towards consistent behaviors in offline data, and the critic, trained with a standard TD-backup loss, effectively performs unbiased n-step backups.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Q-chunking revolves around applying Q-learning to a temporally extended action space, where both the policy and the critic operate on sequences of actions, or "action chunks." This approach is underpinned by two primary design principles: Q-learning on a temporally extended action space and behavior constraints for temporally coherent exploration.

#### 3.1. Q-learning on a Temporally Extended Action Space

Instead of training a Q-function `Q(s_t, a_t)` and a policy `π(a_t | s_t)` for single actions, Q-chunking extends these to handle sequences of `h` consecutive actions, denoted `a_t:t+h`.
- **Q-Chunking Policy:** `π_ψ(a_t:t+h | s_t)` predicts a sequence of actions `(a_t, a_{t+1}..., a_{t+h-1})` given a state `s_t`.
- **Q-Chunking Critic:** `Q_θ(s_t, a_t:t+h)` estimates the value of executing the entire action sequence `(a_t, a_{t+1}..., a_{t+h-1})` from state `s_t`.

The critic `Q_θ` is trained using a modified Temporal Difference (TD) loss (Equation 4) that structurally resembles an `h`-step return:
`L(θ) = E[ ( Q_θ(s_t, a_t:t+h) - ( Σ_{t'=0}^{h-1} γ^{t'} r_{t+t'} + γ^h Q_¯θ(s_{t+h}, a_{t+h:t+2h}) ) )^2 ]`
Here, `a_{t+h:t+2h}` is sampled from the policy `π_ψ(· | s_{t+h})`.

A crucial distinction is highlighted between this Q-chunking TD backup and traditional n-step returns. While both propagate value `h` steps backward, the Q-chunking critic takes the *entire action sequence* `a_t:t+h` as input. This design eliminates the off-policy bias typically present in naive n-step return estimators (Equation 6) because the Q-function being updated directly corresponds to the full action sequence that yielded the discounted sum of rewards `Σ γ^{t'} r_{t+t'}`. Theorem A.1 formally justifies that this approach yields an unbiased n-step return backup for the chunked Q-function.

#### 3.2. Behavior Constraints for Temporally Coherent Exploration

The second principle addresses exploration by regularizing the action-chunking policy `π_ψ` towards a prior behavior distribution `π_β` derived from the offline data. This constraint `D(π_ψ(a_t:t+h | s_t), π_β(a_t:t+h | s_t)) ≤ ε` (Equation 8) encourages the policy to generate temporally coherent action sequences. The motivation is that offline data often contains non-Markovian, structured behaviors (e.g., from human demonstrations) that can be leveraged as "temporally extended skills" for more effective online exploration, preventing random, localized actions.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementations

Implementing Q-chunking effectively requires a policy capable of capturing complex behavior distributions beyond standard Gaussian policies. The authors address this by training a behavior cloning *flow policy* `f_ξ(s, z, u)` using a flow-matching objective. This flow policy approximates `π_β`. Two main instantiations of Q-chunking are presented:

1. **QC (Q-chunking with implicit KL behavior constraint):**
 - Instead of explicitly parameterizing `π_ψ`, QC implicitly defines it using **best-of-N sampling**.
 - It samples `N` action chunks from the learned behavior flow policy `f_ξ(· | s_t)`.
 - The action chunk `a*` that maximizes `Q(s, a)` among these `N` samples is chosen for execution and for constructing TD targets (Equation 11).
 - This best-of-N sampling method implicitly imposes a KL divergence constraint between the chosen policy and the behavior policy, with `N` controlling the constraint strength.

2. **QC-FQL (Q-chunking with 2-Wasserstein distance behavior constraint):**
 - This variant integrates Q-chunking into the FQL algorithm, which uses a noise-conditioned action prediction model `μ_ψ(s, z)` as the policy `π_ψ`.
 - The policy `μ_ψ` is trained to maximize `Q_θ(s_t, a_t:t+h)` while being regularized towards the behavior flow policy `f_ξ` via a behavior cloning (BC) loss (Equation 13). This BC loss is shown to be an upper bound on the squared 2-Wasserstein distance.
 - The TD loss (Equation 15) remains similar to QC, but the next-state action chunk is generated by `μ_ψ(s_{t+h}, z)`.
 - A hyperparameter `α` directly controls the strength of this regularization.

Both QC and QC-FQL are designed to operate consistently across both offline pre-training and online fine-tuning phases, maintaining a fixed behavior constraint strength. The main difference lies in how the policy is parameterized and constrained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results evaluate Q-chunking methods (QC, QC-FQL, QC-IFQL) against various baselines, including FQL, BFN, RLPD, IQL, ReBRAC, and skill-based methods, on challenging long-horizon, sparse-reward robotic manipulation tasks from OGBench and Robomimic.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison (Q1)

- **Overall Superiority:** Q-chunking methods consistently demonstrated strong performance. QC achieved competitive performance during the offline phase and exhibited high online sample efficiency. Notably, QC outperformed all prior methods on the two most difficult OGBench domains (cube-triple and cube-quadruple) by a significant margin.
- **Comparison to N-step Returns:** All Q-chunking methods (QC, QC-FQL, QC-IFQL) outperformed their corresponding 1-step TD counterparts (BFN, FQL, IFQL) and n-step return counterparts (BFN-n, FQL-n, IFQL-n). This finding suggests that simply using n-step returns without a chunked critic or policy is less effective and can suffer from bias issues, leading to significantly worse performance compared to Q-chunking. This trend was observed across both OGBench and Robomimic tasks.
- **Gaussian Policies:** A naive application of action chunking with Gaussian policies (RLPD-AC) or even with an added behavior cloning loss (QC-RLPD) led to poor performance, underscoring the necessity of more expressive policies (like flow-matching based ones) to leverage action chunking effectively.

#### 4.2. Explanation for Exploration Benefits (Q2)

The research posits that action chunking fosters more temporally coherent actions, leading to improved state coverage and exploration.
- **Qualitative Visualization:** Visualizations of end-effector movements during early training showed that QC generated trajectories with fewer pauses and more diverse state coverage compared to BFN. BFN's trajectories exhibited dense clusters, indicating frequent stops or jittery motions, especially when attempting precise movements.
- **Quantitative Coherency:** A quantitative measure, the average L2 norm of the difference vector between adjacent end-effector positions, confirmed that QC exhibited higher temporal coherence in actions throughout training compared to BFN. This empirical evidence supports the hypothesis that Q-chunking enables exploration with more structured and coherent movements, which is beneficial for navigating sparse-reward environments.

#### 4.3. Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Q3)

- **Action Chunk Length (h):** Experiments with QC-FQL on the cube-triple domain revealed that increasing the chunk length improved performance up to `h=10`. Beyond this, asymptotic performance began to decline, with `h=50` failing to achieve success. The authors suggest that overly large chunk sizes might reduce policy reactivity or make learning the action sequences excessively difficult. `h=5` was selected as a balance between performance and computational cost.
- **Critic Ensemble Size (K):** Increasing the critic ensemble size from `K=2` to `K=10` generally improved the performance of both QC and BFN, indicating the benefit of ensemble-based value estimation.
- **Update-to-Data Ratio (UTD):** Varying the update-to-data ratio did not show a significant improvement in the sample efficiency of QC.

#### 4.4. Resource Efficiency (Appendix E.4)

- QC-FQL demonstrated comparable runtime to baselines like FQL and RLPD for both offline and online training. QC was found to be slower during offline training due to sampling 32 actions per training example (compared to 4 for BFN) but was only about 50% more expensive online.
- The parameter counts for QC and QC-FQL (approximately 4.2M and 5.0M, respectively) were comparable to BFN and FQL, and significantly lower than RLPD (17.2M), which uses a larger critic ensemble.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Q-chunking, a novel and "simple yet effective recipe" for integrating action chunking into temporal difference (TD)-based reinforcement learning algorithms. Its significance lies in demonstrating a practical approach to address critical challenges in long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks within the offline-to-online RL paradigm.

The primary contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Exploration:** By enabling agents to operate in a temporally extended action space, Q-chunking facilitates more structured and coherent exploration. This is particularly impactful for tasks where random, single-step actions are unlikely to lead to rewards, allowing the agent to discover solutions faster and with fewer online interactions. The empirical results on challenging manipulation tasks underscore its ability to achieve higher success rates and better sample efficiency compared to existing methods.
- **Unbiased N-step Value Backups:** The formulation of a chunked Q-function that directly takes action sequences as input offers a mechanism for unbiased n-step returns. This addresses a known limitation of traditional n-step methods (off-policy bias), leading to more stable and efficient value learning and accelerating value propagation across longer horizons. This theoretical justification (Theorem A.1) strengthens the robustness of the Q-chunking approach.
- **Leveraging Offline Data for Temporally Coherent Behaviors:** Q-chunking effectively utilizes the temporal consistency present in offline datasets by employing behavior constraints on action chunks. This allows the agent to learn and leverage implicit "skills" from prior data without the complexities often associated with hierarchical RL architectures, which typically involve bi-level optimization challenges.
- **Generic and Adaptable Recipe:** The Q-chunking recipe is designed to be adaptable to existing TD-based actor-critic RL algorithms with minimal modifications. This general applicability, demonstrated through instantiations like QC and QC-FQL, suggests that the principles can be broadly integrated into various offline-to-online frameworks to enhance their performance.
- **Advancement in Robotic Manipulation:** The successful application to complex robotic manipulation tasks from OGBench and Robomimic demonstrates its practical utility in a domain where long-horizon planning and sparse rewards are prevalent. This could accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

Despite its benefits, the work also identifies promising directions for future research. These include developing mechanisms for automatically determining optimal action chunk boundaries, rather than relying on fixed chunk sizes, and exploring more general non-Markovian policies beyond simple fixed-length chunks, particularly for scenarios requiring high-frequency control feedback. Overall, Q-chunking represents a step towards more effective online exploration from prior offline data, bridging insights from imitation learning with the demands of efficient reinforcement learning.
