# Learning Physics from Pretrained Video Models: A Multimodal Continuous and Sequential World Interaction Models for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysGen, a framework from Sun Yat-sen University and X-Era AI Lab, repurposes pretrained autoregressive video generation models as physics simulators for robotic manipulation tasks. It achieved a 90.8% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and 74% on ManiSkill, outperforming several baselines without requiring action-specific pretraining.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation research faces a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality demonstration data for training robust policies.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often built on LLMs, struggle with the modality gap between symbolic text and continuous physical actions, limiting precision.
- Traditional autoregressive models for continuous signals (like images and actions) can introduce resolution errors and trajectory drift due to discrete tokenization.

## Method

- PhysGen leverages a pretrained autoregressive video generation model as a backbone, adapting it to function as a predictive world interaction model for robotic control.
- It introduces a multimodal continuous representation, unifying video and action in a shared physical embedding space via ==diffusion== processes to avoid quantization errors.
- Architectural innovations include a specialized causal masking scheme for implicit inverse kinematics and Lookahead Multi-Token Prediction (L-MTP) for enhanced temporal coherence and planning horizon.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **90.8%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming WorldVLA by 8.8 percentage points and OpenVLA by 13.8 percentage points, with significant gains on long-horizon tasks.
- Secured an average success rate of **74%** on the ManiSkill benchmark, surpassing ICRT by **12%** and Pi0 by **5%**, with **100%** success on PushCube.
- Demonstrated a **75%** average success rate in real-world Franka Panda arm tasks, matching Pi0 and outperforming OpenVLA, notably achieving **75%** on the challenging "Pick Transparency" task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained autoregressive video generation models implicitly learn physical laws and temporal dynamics, making them effective candidates for predictive world models in robotic control.
> - Continuous multimodal representations facilitate fine-grained control and tighter cross-modal interaction, preserving the inherent structure of continuous signals.
> - A causal masking scheme that allows action tokens to unidirectionally attend to future frame tokens can foster implicit inverse kinematics reasoning for action planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly sought to leverage foundation models, similar to their success in computer vision and natural language processing, to address the challenge of data scarcity in robotics. Acquiring large-scale human demonstration data for robotic tasks is resource-intensive and time-consuming. This has led to two main research directions for knowledge transfer to the action domain.

The first, and currently predominant, direction involves building action policies upon large language models (LLMs), giving rise to Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs). These models typically integrate a lightweight action head with an LLM, mapping language tokens to continuous action outputs. However, the inherent discrepancy between the symbolic nature of text and the precise spatial and temporal requirements of physical control can result in suboptimal alignment for manipulation tasks.

The second direction, which this work explores, is to transfer knowledge from pretrained video generation models. Autoregressive video models learn to iteratively predict future observations based on past contexts, thereby implicitly capturing physical laws, object permanence, and temporal dynamics of the world. Such models possess the characteristics of predictive world models, where their step-by-step state evolution aligns with the sequential decision-making processes required for robotic control. This alignment suggests that knowledge learned from vast video datasets could be effectively transferred to action generation, offering a more "physically grounded" alternative to text-based foundation models.

Prior work has explored video-action joint prediction (e.g., WorldVLA, UWM, UniMimic), often focusing on unifying image and action understanding or generation within a single framework. This research extends this line by specifically repurposing an autoregressive video generator as a predictive physics simulator. A critical technical challenge in these approaches, particularly for continuous signals like images and robot actions, is the choice of representation. Traditional autoregressive models often rely on discrete tokenization, which can introduce resolution errors and accumulate trajectory drift. Recent efforts (e.g., MAR, NOVA) have explored continuous, non-quantized autoregressive generation, particularly for images and video. PhysGen builds upon these advances by integrating continuous representation for both visual observations and actions within a unified autoregressive framework.

Therefore, this work positions itself as an advancement in leveraging visual generative priors for robotic control, aiming to overcome the limitations of language-centric VLAs and the quantization errors of discrete tokenization, thereby facilitating more efficient and physically accurate robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and generalizable framework for robotic manipulation that can learn implicit physical knowledge directly from pretrained video generation models, thereby mitigating the need for extensive, task-specific robotic demonstration data.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key challenges and opportunities in the field:

- **Scarcity of Robotic Data:** The fundamental motivation is the prohibitive cost and labor involved in acquiring large-scale datasets of robot demonstrations. This data scarcity hinders the development of generalist robot policies, contrasting with the abundance of data available in other modalities like text and video. By leveraging existing large-scale datasets from other modalities, the goal is to improve data efficiency for downstream robotic tasks.
- **Limitations of Language-Centric Foundation Models (VLAs):** While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promise by adapting large language models (LLMs) to robotics, they suffer from an intrinsic gap between symbolic text reasoning and the continuous, precise physical control required for manipulation. Text describes the world symbolically, which may not translate effectively to fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding necessary for physical interaction, leading to suboptimal alignment between high-level instructions and low-level actions.
- **Potential of Video Generation Models as World Simulators:** Autoregressive video generation models, by their nature, learn to predict future visual observations based on past frames. This process implicitly requires them to capture fundamental physical laws, object dynamics, and temporal relationships within the environment. The authors hypothesize that these models can effectively serve as "predictive world models" or "physics simulators," making them a more natural and principled foundation for visuomotor control compared to LLMs. Their step-by-step state evolution mirrors the sequential decision-making process in control, potentially enabling efficient transfer of learned world knowledge to action generation.
- **Addressing Discrete vs. Continuous Representation Challenges:** Conventional autoregressive models, particularly those adapting LLM principles, often rely on discrete tokenization for both visual observations and actions. This discretization can introduce resolution errors for continuous signals like images and joint positions, leading to accumulated errors over long trajectories and potentially poor manipulation outcomes. The motivation is to overcome this limitation by developing a multimodal continuous representation that unifies video and action into a shared embedding space, allowing for fine-grained control and preserving the inherent continuity of physical interactions.
- **Enhancing Efficiency and Generalizability:** By extracting implicit physical intuition from pretrained video generators, the researchers aim to develop robotic policies that are more physically grounded, robust, and generalizable to new tasks and environments, especially those involving complex physical interactions (e.g., transparent objects). The ultimate goal is to achieve competitive or superior performance compared to methods requiring extensive action-specific pretraining, while significantly improving data efficiency for fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysGen (Learning Physics from Pretrained Video Generation Models) is an autoregressive framework designed to repurpose pretrained video generation models as predictive physics simulators for robotic manipulation. The methodology integrates multimodal continuous representations with a causal transformer architecture and several architectural enhancements.

#### 3.1. Overview of the PhysGen Framework

PhysGen operates as a unified world–action autoregressive framework. At each timestep, it takes a task instruction, a sequence of past images, and corresponding action chunks as input. It then outputs the next visual state and the action chunk for execution. This autoregressive structure enables the model to function as a predictive world interaction model, explicitly modeling the step-by-step coupled evolution of visual observations and actions.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The core of PhysGen is built upon NOVA, a video autoregressive generation model, leveraging its pretrained weights to inherit physical and temporal knowledge.

- **Tokenizer:**
 - **Language:** Task instructions are tokenized by a frozen Phi language model, generating language tokens $E_l$.
 - **Vision:** Visual observations are tokenized by a frozen 3D-VAE, producing flattened frame tokens $E_{O,n}$.
 - **Action:** An MLP-based action tokenizer projects continuous action chunks into the unified continuous physical embedding space, yielding $K_A$ action tokens $E_{A,n}$. An action chunk $A_n$ consists of $L$ consecutive actions.
- **Physical Autoregression:**
 - **Physical Tokens:** Visual and action tokens are concatenated along the sequence dimension to form joint "physical tokens" $P_n = [E_{O,n}; E_{A,n}]$.
 - **BOA Token:** A learnable Begin of Action (BOA) token is prepended to the action sequence to align lengths and capture the one-step offset between observations and actions.
 - **Autoregressive Prediction:** A Causal Transformer (replicating NOVA’s architecture) parameterizes the conditional distribution $p(P_n | E_l, P_0, \cdot \cdot \cdot, P_{n-1})$, predicting the next physical token based on the preceding context and language instruction.
- **De-Tokenizer:**
 - Unlike methods using discrete vocabularies or direct linear projections, PhysGen estimates conditional distributions for output tokens via a diffusion process, maintaining continuous and generative capabilities.
 - The Transformer's output for the current step, $Z_n$, serves as the conditional vector, encapsulating the entire preceding context.
 - A DiT-based denoising process, conditioned on $Z_n$, is performed to estimate $p(P_n | Z_n)$ by minimizing a squared-error diffusion loss.
 - During inference, the token $P_n$ is sampled via a reverse diffusion process guided by $Z_n$.
 - De-tokenization for frames follows NOVA's reconstruction paradigm, while for actions, a lightweight Action-DiT network is introduced, injecting conditioning vectors via cross-attention.

#### 3.3. Model Design Choices

Several architectural enhancements are introduced to optimize joint autoregressive modeling:

- **Causal Masking:** A specialized causal mask is applied:
 - **Frame:** Within each frame, all patches can attend to each other (chunk-wise full attention).
 - **Action:** Actions within a chunk use a temporal causal mask (earlier actions cannot attend to later ones).
 - **Action to Frame:** Action tokens are allowed to attend to *future* frame tokens unidirectionally. This design aims to implicitly facilitate inverse-kinematics reasoning by conditioning action planning on the desired future visual state.
 - **Cross-Chunk:** A standard temporal causal mask is enforced between different physical token chunks.
- **Lookahead Multi-Token Prediction (L-MTP):** Inspired by lookahead formulations, this strategy integrates multi-token prediction (MTP) for action generation. The action de-tokenizer generates multiple future action tokens in parallel (e.g., 3 tokens). During training, all these predicted tokens are supervised. During inference, only the first token is executed, while the subsequent tokens serve as "lookahead" information to inform and condition future predictions, extending the planning horizon and enhancing temporal coherence.
- **Efficient Training and Inference:**
 - **Training:** A fully parallelized strategy with teacher forcing is used, computing losses for all tokens in a single forward pass. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) fine-tuning is applied to the Transformer backbone to preserve pretrained capabilities and improve efficiency.
 - **Inference:** A KV-cache mechanism is employed to store intermediate features at each layer, enabling efficient autoregressive generation in real-time.

#### 3.4. Loss Function

The overall training objective is the average diffusion loss over the sequence of physical tokens, defined as the sum of the observation loss $L_{obs}$ and the action loss $L_{act}$ for each physical token $P_n$, conditioned on the Transformer's output $Z_n$.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The action chunk size is set to 8. The maximum context length for the Transformer backbone is 2096 tokens, comprising 256 language tokens and 5 physical token packages (each with 360 visual tokens and 8 action tokens). Multi-view observations are concatenated into a single image. PhysGen's backbone is exclusively pretrained on video generation data (without any large-scale action or manipulation datasets) and then fine-tuned on downstream tasks. Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) are used to preserve temporal dependencies, with distinct frequency settings for frame and action tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PhysGen was evaluated through simulation experiments on the LIBERO and ManiSkill benchmarks, real-world experiments, ablation studies, and qualitative analyses.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** PhysGen achieved an average success rate of 90.8%, which was the highest among all compared methods.
 - It surpassed WorldVLA by 8.8 percentage points on average and OpenVLA by 13.8 percentage points.
 - Notably, on the long-horizon LIBERO-Long tasks, PhysGen demonstrated an 18.8-point improvement over WorldVLA.
 - Compared to Pi0-Fast, which relies on an action-pretrained vision-language backbone, PhysGen surpassed it by 4.8% in average success rate, despite its backbone not being pretrained on manipulation data. PhysGen underperformed Pi0-Fast on LIBERO-Spatial, which the authors attribute to potential limitations in the underlying video model's spatial perception.
 - These results indicate that the continuous formulation better preserves the structure of images and actions, fostering cross-modal interaction and knowledge transfer from video generation.
- **ManiSkill Benchmark:** PhysGen achieved an average success rate of 74%, which was competitive with or exceeded several methods that rely on large-scale action pretraining.
 - It outperformed ICRT by 12% and Pi0 by 5% in average success rate.
 - PhysGen achieved a 100% success rate on the PushCube task.
 - These findings suggest that transferring physical priors from pretrained video generation models is effective, enabling sophisticated manipulation competence without exhaustive action-labeled datasets and enhancing data efficiency.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Using a Franka Panda arm, PhysGen was evaluated on four real-world tabletop tasks: Pick Cube, Press Button, Stack Cube, and Pick Transparency.

- PhysGen achieved an average success rate of 75%, matching the performance of Pi0 and surpassing OpenVLA, despite its backbone not being pretrained on any large-scale manipulation datasets. This highlights the data efficiency and strong prior provided by video-derived knowledge.
- Notably, PhysGen outperformed Pi0 on the "Pick Transparency" task (75% vs. 70%), which involves grasping a transparent cube and requires strong physical awareness due to complex visual patterns from refraction and reflection. This suggests that physical intuition acquired from video generation pretraining aids in handling visually challenging and physically demanding manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Libero-Object Task)

- **Video Generation Pretraining:** PhysGen-Full (99.6%) significantly outperformed PhysGen-Zero (86.4%), an ablated variant without pretrained weights. This confirms the critical role of video pretraining in enabling physically grounded manipulation.
- **Token Representation:** PhysGen-Full (99.6%) showed a 5.4% higher success rate than PhysGen-Discrete (94.2%), which used discrete tokenization for actions. This supports the benefit of a shared continuous embedding space for precision and effective cross-modal interaction.
- **Autoregressive Architecture:** PhysGen-Full (99.6%) surpassed PhysGen-NoAR (95.0%), a non-autoregressive variant. This highlights the importance of the autoregressive paradigm in capturing iterative dynamics and producing temporally coherent planning.
- **Lookahead-MTP:** Removing the L-MTP module (PhysGen-STP) resulted in a 3.4% drop in success rate (96.8% vs. 99.6%). This indicates that L-MTP contributes to more stable training convergence and provides a longer planning horizon.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

- **Video Action Alignment:** Visualizations demonstrated a close alignment between predicted video sequences and actual robot executions, showing highly similar motion trajectories and timing of key actions. This suggests fine-grained scene understanding from video pretraining and effective transfer to action planning.
- **Real-World Manipulation:** Qualitative examples of real-world execution confirmed stable and coherent manipulation behaviors, including successful performance on the Pick Transparency task, indicating robustness under visually ambiguous conditions.
- **Attention Map Visualization:** Attention maps showed that different attention heads selectively focus on relevant frame and action tokens at the token level. At the pixel level, attention was concentrated on task-critical regions (e.g., objects, robot arm), indicating spatially relevant feature utilization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces PhysGen, a novel physical autoregressive framework that signifies a shift in leveraging foundational models for robotic control by directly repurposing pretrained video generation models. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Foundation Model Utilization for Robotics:** Instead of primarily relying on large language models (LLMs) with their inherent symbolic-physical gap, PhysGen demonstrates the effectiveness of video generation models as predictive world interaction models. This represents a more physically grounded approach to instilling robotic intelligence.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** By leveraging the implicit physical knowledge encoded in large-scale video datasets during pretraining, PhysGen achieves competitive or superior performance without requiring extensive, expensive, and time-consuming action-specific pretraining datasets. This significantly improves data efficiency for fine-tuning on downstream manipulation tasks, making robotic policy learning more accessible.
- **Improved Precision and Robustness via Continuous Representation:** The introduction of a multimodal continuous representation for both visual observations and actions, modeled via diffusion processes, effectively bridges the gap between discrete video generation and continuous robotic control. This approach mitigates quantization errors, leading to more precise manipulation and stable trajectory generation, particularly evident in physically complex tasks like grasping transparent objects.
- **Advancements in Autoregressive World Modeling:** PhysGen's autoregressive formulation, combined with innovations like causal masking for implicit inverse kinematics and Lookahead Multi-Token Prediction (L-MTP), allows for sophisticated modeling of the coupled dynamics between the robot and its environment. This results in temporally coherent planning and smoother action trajectories over longer horizons.
- **Strong Empirical Performance:** The consistent outperformance of robust baselines on established benchmarks (LIBERO, ManiSkill) and competitive results in real-world scenarios validate the proposed methodology. This positions PhysGen as a state-of-the-art approach in video-based policy learning for robotic manipulation.
- **Potential for Generalizable Robotic Intelligence:** By demonstrating that implicit physical intuition derived from diverse video data can be effectively transferred to various manipulation tasks, PhysGen paves the way for developing more generalizable robotic policies. This could accelerate the development of robots capable of autonomously operating in unstructured and dynamic environments.

In summary, PhysGen offers a promising direction for developing generalizable robotic intelligence by establishing pretrained video generators as effective sequential world interaction models, thereby contributing to the fields of embodied AI and robotic learning.
