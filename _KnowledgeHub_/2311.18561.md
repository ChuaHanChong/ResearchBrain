# Periodic Vibration Gaussian: Dynamic Urban Scene Reconstruction and Real-time Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG) extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with time-dependent position and opacity functions centered on a per-point life peak, unifying static and dynamic urban scene modeling without object boxes or optical flow, achieving state-of-the-art quality and 900x faster rendering than EmerNeRF.

## Problem

- Prior NeRF-based dynamic scene methods rely on manually labeled 3D object boxes or panoptic segmentation, increasing model complexity as object count grows.
- Optical-flow-dependent approaches like SUDS need expensive off-the-shelf flow estimators and separate static/dynamic/environment branches, limiting scalability.
- Existing NeRF and 3DGS dynamic extensions suffer slow training/rendering or fail to jointly capture synergistic static-dynamic interactions in one representation.

## Method

- **Periodic Vibration Gaussian** formulation adds a ==life peak== time and sine-based ==vibrating mean== plus ==Gaussian-decay opacity== to each 3D Gaussian, unifying static/dynamic points via a ==staticness coefficient==.
- ==Temporal smoothing== mechanism estimates a point's state at nearby timestamps via ==average-velocity flow translation==, self-supervising motion trends without optical flow.
- ==Position-aware adaptive control== strategy scales densification/pruning thresholds by distance from scene center, plus ==cube-map sky modeling== and a composite RGB/depth/opacity/velocity loss.

## Results

- On Waymo, PVG beats SUDS by **12.6%** PSNR and **20.8%** lower LPIPS in reconstruction, and beats EmerNeRF by **8.4%** PSNR, **27.3%** lower LPIPS in novel view synthesis.
- On KITTI, PVG improves **16.0%** PSNR and **62.2%** lower LPIPS over SUDS in reconstruction, reaching **59 FPS** versus EmerNeRF's **0.28 FPS**, a roughly **900x** rendering speedup.
- Ablations show removing temporal smoothing drops PSNR from **28.11** to **26.50**, and the sine vibration model outperforms constant (**27.77**) and linear (**27.09**) motion formulations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single differentiable primitive with time-dependent parameters can unify static and dynamic scene modeling instead of hand-engineered architectural separation into branches.
> - Self-supervised local-linearity assumptions over short time windows can substitute for explicit optical flow, reducing dependence on external estimators.
> - Distance-aware adaptive control generalizes point-based splatting to unbounded scenes, showing uniform per-point treatment breaks down at large scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction and rendering of 3D scenes from multi-view images have been significant areas of research in computer vision and graphics, with applications spanning digital maps, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality. Recent advancements have largely been driven by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), which represent 3D scenes as continuous volumetric functions. While NeRFs demonstrate high-quality novel view synthesis for static scenes, they face challenges when applied to dynamic environments due to their computational intensity, slow training and rendering speeds, and difficulties in modeling complex temporal changes.

Extensions of NeRF to dynamic scenes have explored various strategies, including treating time as an additional input to the radiance field or explicitly modeling object movements and deformations. However, these methods often encounter scalability issues in real-world scenarios, particularly for large-scale urban scenes with numerous dynamic elements. Many approaches rely on auxiliary information such as 3D object bounding boxes or optical flow estimations, which can be difficult to obtain accurately, introduce noisy supervision, or increase model complexity. Furthermore, methods that manually separate static and dynamic elements often struggle to capture their inherent correlations and interactions.

A recent development, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), has offered a paradigm shift by representing scenes using explicit 3D Gaussians, combining the rendering quality of volumetric methods with the speed of rasterization. Initially designed for static scenes, 3DGS provides significantly faster training and real-time rendering capabilities compared to NeRF-based approaches. This paper extends the 3DGS framework to address the complexities of dynamic urban scenes, aiming to overcome the limitations of both NeRF-based methods (efficiency, scalability) and prior dynamic 3DGS extensions (reliance on bounding boxes, limited ability to distinguish static/dynamic). It proposes a unified representation that can inherently model both static backgrounds and unconstrained dynamic foregrounds without external supervision for object-level information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified and efficient 3D scene representation model capable of accurately reconstructing and rendering large-scale, dynamic urban environments in real time. The motivations stem from several identified limitations in existing methods:

- **Handling Unconstrained Dynamics:** Dynamic urban scenes feature intricate geometric structures and highly unconstrained movements of various objects (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians, foliage, shadows) in both space and time. Traditional methods often struggle with this complexity, either by focusing only on static scenes or by requiring explicit object-level priors (like 3D bounding boxes or segmentation masks) which are costly and prone to errors.
- **Unified Representation:** Prior dynamic scene models frequently separate static and dynamic components, leading to increased design complexity and potentially suboptimal capture of their synergistic interactions. A key motivation is to create a single, cohesive representation that can inherently model both types of elements without explicit decomposition.
- **Computational Efficiency:** NeRF-based approaches, while capable of high-fidelity rendering, suffer from slow training and rendering speeds, making them impractical for real-time applications or large-scale environments. The motivation is to leverage the efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting while extending it to dynamic scenarios, achieving real-time rendering.
- **Reduced Supervision Dependency:** Many dynamic scene reconstruction methods rely on expensive and often noisy auxiliary data, such as manually labeled object bounding boxes or optical flow estimations. A core motivation is to eliminate this dependency, making the model more robust and applicable to diverse datasets without requiring extensive pre-processing or annotation.
- **Temporal Coherence with Sparse Data:** Training dynamic scene models often involves temporally sparse observations, which can lead to inconsistencies in the reconstructed dynamics. The research aims to develop mechanisms that enhance temporal continuity and robustness even with limited training data.
- **Scalability for Urban Scenes:** Urban environments are typically unbounded and contain both near and distant structures. Existing adaptive control strategies for 3D Gaussians might not be optimal for such large-scale settings, motivating the development of position-aware control.

In summary, the research seeks to introduce a novel dynamic scene representation (PVG) that addresses these challenges by extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to elegantly represent dynamic content, achieve real-time performance, operate without explicit object-level supervision, and ensure temporal coherence and scalability for complex urban scenes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG), builds upon the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework by introducing explicit temporal dynamics.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)

3DGS represents a scene using a collection of 3D Gaussians, each defined by a mean (position) μ, a covariance matrix Σ (decomposed into scale s and rotation q), an opacity o, and a color c. It renders images by projecting these 3D Gaussians onto the image plane to form 2D Gaussians and then performing alpha blending sorted by depth. This process is differentiable, allowing optimization of Gaussian parameters for novel view synthesis.

#### 3.2. Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG) Core Model

PVG extends 3DGS by making the Gaussian's mean (position) and opacity time-dependent, transforming each static 3D Gaussian into a dynamic entity. The per-point learnable parameters are extended to include `{μ, q, s, o, c, τ, β, v}`.

- **Dynamics Introduction: Life Peak (τ)**: Each Gaussian point is assigned a "life peak" τ, representing the specific time at which it has maximum prominence and contributes most actively to the scene. This concept infuses a dynamic nature, allowing Gaussians to appear and disappear or change their influence over time.
- **Periodic Vibration of Mean (μ(t))**: The mean position `μ(t)` is made time-dependent using a periodic sine function:
 `μ(t) = μ + (l / 2π) * sin(2π(t - τ) / l) * v`
 Here, `μ` is the base position, `τ` is the life peak, `l` is a hyper-parameter representing the cycle length (scene prior), and `v` is a learnable parameter representing the vibrating direction and instant velocity at time `τ`. This formulation allows Gaussians to exhibit a vibrating motion around their base position, capturing dynamic movements while inherently preserving a global static property (as the mean over a full cycle is `μ`).
- **Periodic Vibration of Opacity (o(t))**: The opacity `o(t)` is also made time-dependent, decaying exponentially away from the life peak `τ`:
 `o(t) = o * exp(-1/2 * (t - τ)^2 * β^-2)`
 Here, `o` is the base opacity, and `β` is a learnable parameter that governs the Gaussian's lifespan; larger `β` values result in longer lifespans. This mechanism enables Gaussians to appear and fade, contributing differently at various time points.
- **Staticness Coefficient (ρ)**: A derived coefficient `ρ = β / l` quantifies the degree of staticness. Large `ρ` values, especially with small `∥v∥`, indicate static components, as the Gaussian has a long lifespan relative to its vibration cycle. Small `ρ` values correspond to dynamic elements that appear and disappear quickly, executing transient movements around `τ`.

#### 3.3. Position-aware Point Adaptive Control

The standard adaptive control in 3DGS treats all Gaussians uniformly. For large, unbounded urban scenes, PVG introduces a position-aware scaling factor `γ(μ)` based on the distance of a Gaussian's mean `μ` from the scene center. This allows for larger Gaussians at distant locations and smaller ones nearby, enabling efficient representation with fewer points while maintaining accuracy. Densification (cloning or splitting) and pruning operations are adapted to incorporate `γ(μ)`.

#### 3.4. Model Training Enhancements

- **Temporal Smoothing by Intrinsic Motion**: To address sparse training data and enhance temporal consistency, PVG introduces a self-supervision mechanism. Instead of relying on optical flow, it computes an "average velocity" `¯v = v * exp(-ρ/2)`. During training, with a certain probability `η`, the model renders a Gaussian set `H(t)` by translating a previous state `H(t - ∆t)` using `¯v * ∆t`. This acts as a regularization, encouraging Gaussians to learn coherent motion trends between adjacent frames without external flow supervision.
- **Sky Refinement**: To efficiently represent the static sky, a lightweight cube map `f_sky(d)` is used as the background. The final pixel color is a blend of the rendered scene color and the sky color, where `C_f = C + (1 - O)f_sky(d)`. This avoids using extremely large Gaussians for the sky and is physically reasonable.
- **Objective Loss Function**: The overall loss `L` combines several terms:
 - `L1` and `SSIM` loss for RGB image reconstruction.
 - `L_d`: A depth loss term using sparse LiDAR inverse depth maps for geometry awareness.
 - `L_o`: An opacity loss encouraging Gaussians to be either fully opaque (scene elements) or transparent (sky), regularized by a pre-trained sky segmentation mask.
 - `L_¯v`: A sparse velocity loss `∥¯V∥1` applied to the rendered average velocity map. This loss promotes sparsity in `v` and encourages larger `β` (static components), reflecting the prior that most scene elements are static.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the Waymo Open Dataset, KITTI benchmarks, and the Plenoptic video dataset, comparing PVG against various state-of-the-art methods, including NeRF-based and other 3DGS-based models.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (NeRF-based and Static 3DGS)

- **Dynamic Scenes (Waymo & KITTI):**
 - PVG consistently surpassed all baselines (S-NeRF, StreetSurf, 3DGS, NSG, Mars, SUDS, EmerNeRF) across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics for both image reconstruction and novel view synthesis tasks.
 - On Waymo, PVG showed a 12.6% increase in PSNR for image reconstruction and an 8.4% increase in PSNR for novel view synthesis compared to the leading competitors (SUDS and EmerNeRF, respectively).
 - On KITTI, PVG achieved a 16.0% PSNR improvement in image reconstruction over SUDS and an 8.7% PSNR improvement in novel view synthesis over EmerNeRF.
 - **Efficiency:** PVG demonstrated significant efficiency benefits, completing training in approximately one hour on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU and achieving a rendering speed of 50-59 FPS. This represents a substantial acceleration (e.g., 900-fold over EmerNeRF, which runs at 0.053 FPS).
- **Static Scenes (Waymo):** Even though designed for dynamic scenes, PVG also outperformed methods specifically tailored for static scenes (S-NeRF, StreetSurf, 3DGS) in novel view synthesis tasks on Waymo, demonstrating its ability to uniformly manage both static and dynamic environments.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Concurrent Gaussian Splatting Models

PVG was compared against recent 3DGS extensions for dynamic scenes (StreetGaussian, HUGS, Omnire, 4DGS, DeformableGS) on Waymo.
- **Box-based vs. Box-free:** PVG, as a box-free method (not relying on 3D bounding boxes), achieved the best overall performance in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS for both full images and dynamic vehicle regions, outperforming both box-based and other box-free alternatives.
- **Performance Metrics:** PVG attained a PSNR of 36.02 for image reconstruction and 30.95 for novel view synthesis on dynamic scenes, surpassing competitors like StreetGaussian (32.18 / 28.92 PSNR), 4DGS (34.94 / 26.56 PSNR), and DeformableGS (32.34 / 29.52 PSNR).
- **Rendering Speed:** PVG maintained a high rendering speed of 70.4 FPS, comparable to or exceeding other 3DGS methods, while achieving superior reconstruction quality.

#### 4.3. Dynamic Element Analysis

- Visualizations of the velocity map (¯V) and staticness map (ρ) demonstrated PVG's capability to accurately capture moving objects (cars, pedestrians) as well as subtle dynamics like moving light and shadows.
- PVG effectively separated static regions (large ρ, robust to temporal variations) from dynamic regions (small ρ, precisely capturing motion), enabling flexible manipulation such as the removal of dynamic elements from a scene.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Temporal Smoothing:** Deactivating the temporal smoothing mechanism led to a degradation in novel view synthesis quality, confirming its role in enhancing smoothness, consistency, and preventing overfitting.
- **Loss Components:** LiDAR depth loss (`L_d`) and sky refinement contributed to more plausible geometry and anti-aliasing. Velocity loss (`L_¯v`) facilitated sparser velocity fields, simplifying static/dynamic element segmentation.
- **Position-aware Control (PAC):** PAC significantly improved the reconstruction quality of distant scene components.
- **Dynamics Formulation:** The periodic sine function formulation for `μ(t)` outperformed constant and linear motion models, which struggled with either dynamic elements (constant) or static components (linear, causing ghosting).
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The model demonstrated robustness to the choice of cycle length `l` (best around 0.2) and the scale factor threshold `r` for position-aware control, with performance remaining stable across a reasonable range of values. The temporal smoothing probability `η` also showed a stable plateau beyond a certain threshold.

#### 4.5. Plenoptic Video Dataset Evaluation

- PVG achieved competitive PSNR results on the Plenoptic video dataset, even in complex dynamic scenes with human motion and challenging backgrounds, affirming its generalizability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Periodic Vibration Gaussian (PVG) model represents an advancement in dynamic urban scene reconstruction and real-time rendering, addressing several critical limitations of prior work.

- **Unified and Efficient Representation:** PVG offers a single, unified representation for both static and dynamic elements within large-scale urban scenes. By extending 3D Gaussian Splatting with periodic vibration-based temporal dynamics, it achieves a balance of high-quality reconstruction and real-time rendering speeds (e.g., 900-fold acceleration over a leading NeRF-based competitor). This efficiency is particularly significant for applications requiring rapid processing and display.
- **Reduced Supervision Reliance:** A key impact is PVG's ability to operate effectively without requiring manually labeled object bounding boxes or expensive optical flow estimations. This reduces the cost and complexity of data preparation, makes the model more broadly applicable to diverse datasets, and mitigates issues arising from noisy or incomplete supervision.
- **Enhanced Temporal Coherence:** The introduction of a novel temporal smoothing mechanism, based on intrinsic motion rather than external flow, helps maintain temporal consistency in dynamic scene representation, especially when training data is temporally sparse. This contributes to more stable and plausible renderings of dynamic events.
- **Scalability for Urban Environments:** The position-aware adaptive control strategy, combined with sky refinement, enables PVG to handle unbounded urban scenes with both near and distant structures efficiently, faithfully representing expansive environments.
- **Applications:** The capabilities of PVG have direct implications for several fields:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Real-time, high-fidelity reconstruction of dynamic urban scenes is crucial for sensor simulation, perception, and planning modules in autonomous vehicles. PVG's performance and efficiency could facilitate advancements in these areas.
 - **Digital Maps and Virtual Tourism:** Creation of dynamic, immersive digital twins of cities, allowing for interactive exploration and visualization of temporal changes.
 - **Film and Game Production:** Efficient generation of realistic dynamic environments for virtual production and interactive entertainment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By providing a robust and efficient baseline for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting, PVG can serve as a foundation for future work on improving geometric accuracy, integrating more structured priors for complex motions, and enabling efficient editing of dynamic objects, which are acknowledged limitations of the current model.

The research establishes PVG as a competitive model for dynamic urban scene representation, offering a path toward more scalable, efficient, and less supervision-dependent solutions in computer vision and graphics.
