# Pre-training Contextualized World Models with In-the-wild Videos for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ContextWM is a latent dynamics world model that explicitly splits time-invariant context from time-dependent dynamics: a context encoder feeds a randomly sampled frame into the image decoder through multi-scale cross-attention, and dual reward predictors separate exploratory from task-relevant reward regression. Pre-trained on in-the-wild videos such as Something-Something-v2 and fine-tuned with model-based RL, it improves sample efficiency on robotic manipulation, locomotion, and driving.

## Problem

- Prior world-model pre-training used simulated, domain-specific demonstration videos generated by scripted policies, which lack the diversity and scale of internet video data.
- Earlier attempts to pre-train on real videos either underfit during video prediction or produced negligible benefit for downstream visual control tasks.
- Plain latent dynamics models entangle context with dynamics, spending capacity on low-level appearance rather than shared temporal knowledge transferable across visually distinct scenes.

## Method

- **ContextWM**'s ==contextualized latent dynamics== model: the image decoder conditions on both latent state and a context variable while inference stays context-unaware, yielding an ==ELBO== on conditional log-likelihood.
- Context encoder with multi-scale ==cross-attention==: a randomly sampled frame is encoded and injected into decoder features by attention plus residual connection, permitting spatially flexible shortcuts despite motion.
- ==Dual reward predictors==: a behavioral head regresses reward plus the intrinsic exploration bonus for actor-critic learning, a representative head regresses pure reward to keep latents task-relevant.

## Results

- On **six** Meta-world manipulation tasks over **250K** environment steps and **eight** seeds, pre-training beats DreamerV2, and ContextWM exceeds the plain world model on **five of six** tasks.
- Gains survive large domain gaps: DMC Remastered locomotion over **1M** steps and CARLA driving over **150K** steps both improve, and ContextWM outperforms DrQ-v2 and Iso-Dream.
- Ablations: plain world models barely gain from pre-training while ContextWM does; concatenation instead of cross-attention or removing dual reward predictors lowers performance; **1.5k** videos nearly match **162K**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating time-invariant context from dynamics lets a generative world model spend capacity on motion, so pre-trained knowledge transfers across visually dissimilar scenes.
> - Data diversity outweighs domain proximity: lab-collected human motion video caused negative transfer, while diverse object-interaction video helped even distant driving and locomotion tasks.
> - Extra pre-training video gave diminishing returns once dynamics patterns were covered, so scaling should target variety of motion rather than raw frame count.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), representation learning, and unsupervised pre-training, drawing inspiration from successes in computer vision (CV) and natural language processing (NLP).

**Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):** MBRL aims to improve the notoriously poor sample efficiency of Reinforcement Learning (RL) by learning a "world model" of the environment's dynamics. This model can then be used for planning, generating imaginary trajectories, or learning policies more efficiently. Popular world models, such as those in the Dreamer family, typically learn latent dynamics models from visual observations. However, learning an accurate and generalizable world model from scratch (tabula rasa) for complex visual control tasks remains a significant challenge dueating to the high dimensionality and complexity of visual data.

**Pre-training Paradigm:** The "pre-train and fine-tune" paradigm has revolutionized fields like CV (e.g., ImageNet pre-training, self-supervised learning like MoCo) and NLP (e.g., BERT, GPT models). This approach leverages large, diverse, unsupervised datasets to learn powerful, generalizable representations, which are then adapted to specific downstream tasks with limited data. The success of this paradigm has naturally led to explorations of its applicability in RL.

**Bridging the Gap to In-the-Wild Videos:** Recent efforts have started applying pre-training to world models. Notably, Action-free Pre-training from Videos (APV) explored pre-training world models from videos to improve sample efficiency. However, APV and similar works have largely relied on domain-specific or carefully simulated video datasets, which lack the scale and diversity of "in-the-wild" video data available on the internet (e.g., YouTube, Ego4D). Prior attempts to use real-world, uncurated videos for world model pre-training have yielded limited benefits, either due to underfitting or negligible transfer to downstream tasks. This suggests that directly applying existing world model architectures to complex, in-the-wild data is insufficient.

**The Niche of This Work:** This paper directly addresses the challenge of effectively pre-training world models using *abundant, diverse, in-the-wild videos* for efficient visual control. It posits that the complexity of such videos (intricate backgrounds, varying appearances, complex dynamics) prevents standard world models from extracting universally useful "world knowledge." By introducing a novel architecture designed to explicitly handle this complexity, the authors aim to unlock the full potential of large-scale unsupervised video pre-training for MBRL, moving beyond curated or simulated datasets towards truly general-purpose world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable and demonstrate the effective pre-training of world models using diverse, in-the-wild video datasets to significantly improve the sample efficiency of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) on downstream visual control tasks.

The primary motivation stems from the recognition of the immense potential of unsupervised pre-training, similar to its success in computer vision and natural language processing, to alleviate the data scarcity problem in MBRL. While recent efforts have explored world model pre-training from videos (e.g., APV), they have been limited to carefully curated, domain-specific, or simulated datasets. The authors pose the fundamental question: **"Can world models pre-trained on diverse in-the-wild videos benefit sample-efficient learning of downstream visual control tasks?"**

The core challenge they identify is the inherent high complexity and diversity of in-the-wild videos. These videos feature:
- **Intricate backgrounds:** Often dynamic and irrelevant to the core task.
- **Textured appearances:** Highly variable object textures, shapes, and colors.
- **Complicated dynamics:** Diverse movements and interactions.

The authors argue that this complexity, if modeled in a coarse and entangled manner, forces a world model to waste significant capacity on low-level visual details and static contextual information ("what is there"), rather than focusing on essential temporal variations and shared knowledge ("what is happening"). This entanglement hinders knowledge transfer between visually distinct scenes, limiting the generalization capabilities of the world model.

Inspired by biological visual systems that segregate spatial details from temporal changes, the paper's key motivation is to design a world model architecture that explicitly separates the modeling of "context" (time-invariant, static scene information) from "dynamics" (time-dependent, temporal changes), thereby facilitating the extraction of generalizable knowledge from noisy, real-world videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on **Contextualized World Models (ContextWM)**, a novel architecture that explicitly separates the modeling of context and dynamics. This approach is integrated into the "In-the-wild Pre-training from Videos (IPV)" paradigm, which involves pre-training on diverse, uncurated video datasets followed by fine-tuning on downstream visual control tasks.

The ContextWM builds upon the successful **DreamerV2** framework and the **stacked latent model** introduced by APV, extending them with several key components:

1. **Contextualized Latent Dynamics Model:**
 - **Core Idea:** Observations `o_t` are generated conditioned on both a time-dependent latent dynamics variable `z_t` and a time-invariant context variable `c`. This means the image decoder `p_θ(o_t | z_t, c)` uses both dynamic and static information.
 - **Context-Unaware Latent Inference:** Crucially, the variational inference model for `z_t` (the representation model `q_θ(z_t | z_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, o_t)`) *does not* condition on `c`. This forces `z_t` to only capture essential temporal variations not present in `c`, encouraging disentanglement.
 - **Benefits:** This design allows the image decoder to reconstruct complex observations using rich contextual information, while the latent dynamics model focuses on high-level semantic temporal changes, making learned representations more transferable and robust to intricate contexts.

2. **Context Formulation:**
 - For end-to-end visual control, the context `c` is simply a single frame of observation, `o_tilde_t`, randomly sampled from the current trajectory segment. This assumes contextual information is equally present in each frame and encourages the context encoder to be robust to temporal variations.
 - To prevent the decoder from simply copying the context frame (a "shortcut" learning behavior), random Cutout augmentation is applied to the context frame during pre-training for Meta-world tasks.

3. **Multi-scale Cross-Attention Conditioning:**
 - The contextualized image decoder `p_θ(o_t | s_t, c)` (where `s_t` is the action-conditional latent state) is implemented using a U-Net-like architecture.
 - Instead of simple concatenation or summation for shortcut connections, the decoder features `X` are augmented with context features `Z` (extracted from `c` by a separate context encoder) via a **cross-attention mechanism**. This allows flexible, non-aligned information transfer, which is vital as temporal variations (motion, deformation) can alter spatial alignment.
 - To mitigate the quadratic complexity of attention, a random mask is applied to 75% of the context tokens before cross-attention.

4. **Dual Reward Predictors:**
 - To ensure the latent variable `s_t` encodes sufficient task-relevant information (even if static task elements are in `c`), two reward predictors are introduced during fine-tuning:
 - **Behavioral Reward Predictor:** `p_ϕ(r_t + λr_int_t | s_t)` predicts the combined extrinsic and intrinsic rewards, used for behavior learning (actor-critic).
 - **Representative Reward Predictor:** `p_φ(r_t | s_t)` predicts only the pure extrinsic reward. This explicitly compels `s_t` to capture task-relevant information, enhancing its representational quality for the control task.

**Overall Objective:** The ContextWM is optimized with a variational lower bound objective (`L_CWM`) during fine-tuning, which includes the contextualized image reconstruction loss, dual reward losses, and KL divergence terms for both action-free and action-conditional latent dynamics. For pre-training, an action-free variant (`L_CWM-pt`) without action-conditional dynamics and reward predictors is used.

**Implementation Details:** Visual encoders and decoders are implemented as 13-layer ResNets for sufficient capacity. Three diverse in-the-wild video datasets (Something-Something-v2, Human3.6M, YouTube Driving) and an assembled dataset are used for pre-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate In-the-wild Pre-training from Videos (IPV) with ContextWM across various visual control domains: robotic manipulation (Meta-world), robotic locomotion (DMC Remastered), and autonomous driving (CARLA).

1. **IPV Significantly Improves Sample Efficiency:** Pre-training with in-the-wild videos (specifically SSv2) consistently boosts sample efficiency and final performance of MBRL across all evaluated Meta-world tasks compared to learning from scratch (DreamerV2 baseline). This validates the core IPV paradigm.

2. **ContextWM Outperforms Plain World Models:**
 - **Quantitative:** ContextWM consistently surpasses a "plain WM" (DreamerV2-like model used in APV) in terms of sample efficiency and final performance on most Meta-world tasks.
 - **Ablation Study:** A plain WM shows negligible benefits from IPV, indicating that complex contexts hinder knowledge transfer. ContextWM, however, *significantly* improves with pre-training. This strongly supports the motivation for explicit context/dynamics separation.
 - **Contribution of Components:** Ablations confirm that both multi-scale cross-attention and the dual reward predictor structure contribute positively to ContextWM's performance.

3. **Generalization Across Domains:**
 - **DMC Remastered:** ContextWM pre-trained on SSv2 (human interaction videos) yields significant performance gains on DMCR (robotic locomotion), despite a large domain gap. This highlights ContextWM's ability to transfer shared knowledge of context/dynamics modeling.
 - **CARLA:** IPV with ContextWM learns faster in early stages and often achieves higher final performance on CARLA driving tasks across various weather conditions.

4. **Effects of Pre-training Data:**
 - **Dataset Size (Meta-world):** Surprisingly, pre-training with small subsets of SSv2 (1.5k, 15k videos) achieved comparable performance to using the full dataset. The authors hypothesize this is due to the limited diversity in *dynamics patterns* within SSv2 (174 interaction classes), suggesting existing models can learn these dynamics from less data.
 - **Dataset Domain (Meta-world):** While domain-similar data (RLBench) slightly outperforms SSv2, pre-training with the *assembled, more diverse* in-the-wild dataset shows continuous improvement over SSv2, suggesting that increasing diversity (not just quantity) is key for scalable general-purpose world models.
 - **Dataset Domain (DMCR):** Pre-training on Human3.6M (lab-collected human poses) resulted in *negative transfer*, underscoring the importance of *diversity* and truly "in-the-wild" characteristics for beneficial pre-training, as lab data can be too constrained.
 - **Dataset Domain (CARLA):** YouTube Driving (more domain-specific) and assembled data did not significantly outperform SSv2, possibly due to the *higher complexity* of YouTube Driving, which current ContextWM may not fully capture.

5. **Qualitative Analysis:**
 - **Video Prediction:** ContextWM generates higher-fidelity future frames on SSv2, accurately capturing object shape and motion, unlike plain WM which struggles. Cross-attention heatmaps confirm that the model attends to relevant contextual regions for reconstruction.
 - **Video Representations:** t-SNE visualizations show that ContextWM learns disentangled representations, clearly separating videos based on motion direction (e.g., push left vs. push right), while plain WM's representations are entangled.
 - **Compositional Decoding:** ContextWM can successfully combine a new (randomly sampled) context frame with original dynamics information to predict new trajectories, demonstrating a genuine disentanglement of context and dynamics. Plain WM fails at this.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and visual control, particularly in its approach to leveraging real-world, uncurated data.

**Significance:**
1. **Validation of IPV Paradigm:** The paper provides strong empirical evidence that pre-training world models with diverse, in-the-wild videos can indeed significantly improve the sample efficiency and performance of MBRL for downstream visual control tasks. This opens a scalable pathway to building general-purpose world models from the vast quantities of unlabeled video data available online.
2. **Innovative World Model Design (ContextWM):** The ContextWM architecture is a critical innovation that tackles the inherent complexity of in-the-wild videos. By explicitly separating context (static elements like backgrounds, textures) and dynamics (temporal changes like motion), it enables the latent dynamics model to focus on abstract, transferable temporal knowledge. This disentanglement is crucial for generalizing across visually diverse environments.
3. **Enhanced Knowledge Transfer:** The experiments demonstrate effective knowledge transfer even across large domain gaps (e.g., SSv2 pre-training to robotic locomotion). The qualitative analyses confirm that ContextWM learns more disentangled and robust representations, which is key to this improved transferability.
4. **Scalability Implications:** While current dataset sizes for pre-training show diminishing returns for SSv2, the results with the assembled dataset suggest that IPV with ContextWM can continuously improve with greater *diversity* in pre-training data, hinting at a scalable solution for general visual intelligence in agents.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Sample-Efficient RL:** The most immediate impact is on reducing the sample requirements for visual control tasks, making RL more practical for real-world robotics and autonomous systems where data collection is expensive and time-consuming.
- **Generalizable AI Agents:** By fostering the development of general-purpose world models, this work paves the way for agents that can learn broadly applicable "world knowledge" from observing the real world, potentially leading to more versatile and adaptable AI.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** The approach has direct applications in robotic manipulation, locomotion, and autonomous driving, offering a method to imbue robots with a better understanding of physical world dynamics from human videos or other diverse sources.

**Limitations and Broader Impact (as discussed by authors):**
- **Context Modeling Limitations:** Relying on a single randomly selected frame for context might be insufficient for highly dynamic or complex real-world scenes (e.g., autonomous driving). Future work should explore multiple context frames or multimodal inputs.
- **Medium-Scale Models and Data:** The current work uses medium-scale models and datasets. Scaling up to Transformer-based architectures and massive video datasets (e.g., Ego4D, HowTo100M) is a crucial next step to fully realize the potential of IPV.
- **Generative Objective Efficiency:** The authors suggest that generative objectives focusing on image reconstruction might be inefficient in terms of model capacity. Exploring alternative pre-training objectives like contrastive learning or self-prediction could further optimize the learning of dynamics.
- **Societal Concerns:** The authors acknowledge that advancements in world models and video generation could contribute to deepfake technology. Increased automation in robotics and autonomous systems, while offering efficiency and safety, also raises concerns about job displacement and socioeconomic disruption. However, they rightly note that these are long-term concerns, as the field is still in early research stages.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling argument for the viability of pre-training world models with in-the-wild videos, providing a well-designed architecture (ContextWM) that tackles the challenges of real-world visual complexity. It marks a significant step towards creating more intelligent, sample-efficient, and generalizable embodied AI agents.
