# Quantile Advantage Estimation: Stabilizing RLVR for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Quantile Advantage Estimation (QAE) introduces a K-quantile baseline to dynamically regulate policy entropy in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models, preventing both entropy collapse and explosion. It consistently improved pass@1 accuracy across various LLM sizes on mathematical reasoning benchmarks while stabilizing training dynamics.

## Problem

- Policy instability in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLMs, characterized by both entropy collapse (premature sharpening) and entropy explosion (uncontrolled stochasticity).
- The mean-baseline used in value-free RL algorithms improperly penalizes "negative-advantage samples," leading to inefficient exploration and stalled learning.
- Existing token-level entropy controls are one-sided, addressing only collapse or explosion without providing comprehensive, sustained stability or performance gains.

## Method

- Introduced ==Quantile Advantage Estimation== (**QAE**) which replaces the conventional mean reward baseline with a group-wise K-quantile baseline to compute advantages.
- Developed a two-regime gate mechanism where hard queries (low success rate) receive positive advantages for correct responses, while easy queries (high success rate) receive negative advantages for incorrect responses.
- The hyperparameter K acts as a direct control for balancing exploration and exploitation, enabling dynamic regulation of policy entropy.

## Results

- QAE consistently improved pass@1 scores across various Qwen3 LLM sizes (e.g., +**21.5%** on AIME
								’24 for Qwen3-**8B**-Base when integrated with Clip-Higher).
- The method stabilized policy entropy throughout training, preventing early spikes (explosion) and premature collapse, which led to sustained performance gains beyond plateaus observed in baseline methods.
- QAE produced sparse credit assignment, with approximately **80%** of sampled responses receiving zero advantage, indicating an efficient focus on the most informative samples for learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both entropy collapse and its symmetric counterpart, entropy explosion, are critical challenges for stable and scalable RLVR, stemming from a fundamental baseline-design flaw rather than merely token-level heuristics.
> - Entropy regulation can be more effectively achieved by modifying the advantage estimation baseline (from mean to K-quantile) rather than solely relying on token-level controls.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the K-quantile baseline provides "two-sided" entropy safety, curbing excessive growth in low-success regimes and maintaining sufficient entropy in high-success regimes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs). RLVR is an emerging paradigm that aims to enhance LLM reasoning capabilities by providing rewards based on verifiable correctness, a shift from earlier applications in preference alignment. Recent works such as those by Lambert et al. (2024), DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025), and Yang et al. (2025a) have demonstrated the potential of RL in scaling reasoning abilities in LLMs.

A known challenge in RLVR training is policy instability, often characterized by "entropy collapse," where the policy distribution sharpens prematurely, limiting exploration and performance. Previous research has primarily focused on mitigating this collapse through methods like uplifting low-probability tokens (Yu et al., 2025), penalizing collapse-inducing tokens (Cui et al., 2025), or learning primarily from negative samples (Zhu et al., 2025).

The authors contend that these prior approaches are one-sided, addressing only entropy collapse while largely overlooking its "symmetric counterpart," entropy explosion. Entropy explosion, characterized by uncontrolled entropy growth, can lead to inefficient exploration and stalled learning progress. This paper argues that merely preventing collapse is insufficient; effective RLVR necessitates maintaining policy entropy within a productive and stable range. Value-free RL algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024) and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) (Yu et al., 2025), are foundational to current RLVR systems and are central to the analysis in this work. This research positions itself as identifying and addressing a fundamental baseline-design flaw in these value-free RL methods, rather than focusing on token-level heuristics, to achieve a comprehensive solution for entropy regulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust mechanism for regulating policy entropy in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training of Large Language Models (LLMs), thereby preventing both entropy collapse and entropy explosion. The authors contend that current RLVR methods frequently encounter these dual hazards, leading to training instability and limiting sustained performance gains.

The motivation for this work stems from several detailed observations and a critical analysis of existing value-free RL approaches:

- **Dual Entropy Hazard:** The paper highlights that while entropy collapse (premature policy sharpening) is a recognized problem, entropy explosion (uncontrolled stochasticity) is an equally detrimental, yet underexplored, issue. Both extremes impede effective learning and scaling of RLVR. For instance, the authors illustrate that using Clip-Higher with DAPO on Qwen3-8B-Base prevents early collapse but induces an early entropy spike, leading to long-term instability and performance plateaus, indicating that high entropy does not guarantee effective exploration.
- **Root Cause in Mean-Baseline:** The authors trace both entropy collapse and explosion to the mean-baseline used in value-free RL algorithms, such as GRPO and DAPO. They argue that this mean-baseline improperly penalizes "negative-advantage samples" when reward outliers are present. A few high-reward samples can artificially inflate the baseline, causing otherwise competent responses to receive negative advantages, thereby penalizing useful exploration and contributing to entropy collapse. Conversely, an insensitive baseline can exacerbate entropy explosion.
- **Shortcomings of Token-level Controls:** Previous attempts to manage entropy often employ token-level controls (e.g., Clip-Higher). The paper demonstrates that while these controls might prevent early collapse, they do not guarantee sustained reasoning gains. An analysis of anthropomorphic token usage in DAPO shows an early spike in these "aha-moment" markers coinciding with performance gains, but subsequently, their frequency returns to baseline while performance plateaus. This suggests that token-level controls can induce rapid, but ultimately unproductive, entropy growth, leading to homogenized and low-quality exploration patterns where diverse exploratory tokens are replaced by rigid reasoning templates.
- **Disproportionate Role of Negative-Advantage Samples:** Empirical analysis reveals that entropy growth is predominantly driven by negative-advantage samples. These samples exhibit the steepest increase and largest share of policy entropy early in training, indicating over-exploration in the initial phase and insufficient exploitation later.
- **Insufficiency of Hyperparameter Tuning:** Adjusting token-level hyperparameters, such as `ϵ_high` in DAPO, yields only marginal effects on performance and does not resolve the underlying exploration-exploitation tension. This observation reinforces the authors' hypothesis that the problem is fundamentally one of baseline design, not merely hyperparameter tuning.

Based on this analysis, the key objectives are to:
1. Introduce a novel advantage estimation method that can dynamically regulate policy entropy to avoid both collapse and explosion, ensuring it remains within a productive operational range.
2. Replace the problematic mean-baseline in value-free RL with a more robust and controllable alternative.
3. Provide theoretical guarantees for the proposed method's ability to maintain "two-sided" entropy safety.
4. Empirically demonstrate that this modification stabilizes training, improves performance, and clarifies the primary mechanism for scaling RLVR.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is Quantile Advantage Estimation (QAE), which aims to dynamically regulate policy entropy by replacing the conventional mean reward baseline with a group-wise K-quantile baseline. This modification is designed to address the observed instability in value-free RL methods like GRPO and DAPO.

**Formulation of Quantile Advantage Estimation (QAE):**
For a given query `q`, the method first samples a group of `G` responses `{(o_i, R_i)}` from the old policy `π_old(· | q)`. Each response `o_i` is assigned a binary reward `R_i ∈ {0, 1}` (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect). The empirical success rate for the group is calculated as `p(q) = (1/G) Σ R_i`.

Instead of using the mean reward as a baseline, QAE defines a K-quantile baseline, `b_K(q) = Q_K({R_j})`, where `K ∈ (0, 1)` is a hyperparameter. For binary rewards, this K-quantile baseline simplifies to a discrete threshold:
- `b_K(q) = 0` if `p(q) ≤ 1−K`
- `b_K(q) = 1` if `p(q) > 1−K`

The standardized advantage for the i-th sample is then computed as:
`Â_i = (R_i − b_K(q)) / (std({R_j}) + ε)`, where `ε > 0` prevents division by zero.

**Two-Regime Gate Mechanism:**
This quantile-based baseline induces a response-level, two-regime gate that directs updates differently based on query difficulty, which is determined by the success rate `p(q)` relative to `1-K`:

1. **Hard Queries (p(q) ≤ 1−K):** In this regime, `b_K(q) = 0`.
 - Incorrect responses (`R = 0`) receive `Â = 0`.
 - Correct responses (`R = 1`) receive `Â > 0`.
 This regime is exploitation-focused, as it reinforces rare successes and emergent successful trajectories while ignoring failures, which helps prevent entropy explosion by not penalizing potentially useful but low-probability actions.

2. **Easy Queries (p(q) > 1−K):** In this regime, `b_K(q) = 1`.
 - Correct responses (`R = 1`) receive `Â = 0`.
 - Incorrect responses (`R = 0`) receive `Â < 0`.
 This regime is exploration-focused, as it discourages remaining failure modes on queries that are largely solved, pushing the model to diversify and improve from its errors. This helps prevent entropy collapse by maintaining sufficient stochasticity to explore alternative, better solutions.

The hyperparameter `K` thus acts as a direct lever to control the exploration-exploitation balance. A lower `K` means more samples receive positive advantage, encouraging exploitation and potentially reducing entropy. A higher `K` means fewer samples appear successful, promoting diversification and increasing entropy.

**Gradient Analysis and Discriminative Objective:**
The authors analyze QAE from a discriminative perspective, demonstrating how it modifies the GRPO objective. Compared to GRPO, which uses a symmetric query weight `√(p(1-p))` that down-weights both very easy and very hard queries, QAE introduces two crucial changes:
1. It selectively nullifies one of the discriminative terms (either for positive or negative examples) based on the query's difficulty (i.e., `p(q)` relative to `1-K`).
2. It replaces the symmetric weight with asymmetric, monotonic factors (`√(p/(1-p))` for hard queries or `√((1-p)/p)` for easy queries). This shifts the update mechanism from focusing on moderately difficult problems to amplifying signals from rare successes or residual failures.

**Theoretical Analysis: Two-Regime Entropy Safety:**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis demonstrating that QAE offers "two-sided" entropy safety. Under first-order logit updates of a softmax policy, the change in token-averaged policy entropy `∆H(q)` is shown to be strictly increasing with the baseline value `b ∈`.
- **Low-success regime (p(q) ≤ 1−K):** Here, `b_K(q) = 0`. Since `∆H(q; b)` is increasing in `b`, choosing `b_K(q) = 0` (the minimum possible baseline) ensures that `∆H(q; b_K) ≤ ∆H(q; b)` for any other baseline `b`. This property makes QAE "explosion-proof" by curbing excessive entropy growth.
- **High-success regime (p(q) > 1−K):** Here, `b_K(q) = 1`. Choosing `b_K(q) = 1` (the maximum possible baseline) ensures that `∆H(q; b_K) ≥ ∆H(q; b)` for any other baseline `b`. This property makes QAE "collapse-proof" by maintaining sufficient entropy and preventing premature convergence.

The authors highlight that existing token-level controls cannot achieve these two-sided guarantees because they only rescale step sizes without altering the fundamental response-level baseline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Quantile Advantage Estimation (QAE) was conducted on three standard mathematical reasoning benchmarks: AIME’24, AIME’25, and AMC’23. The performance was measured using pass@1 and pass@16 accuracy, and observations were made regarding training dynamics, entropy behavior, and credit assignment sparsity.

**Overall Performance Across Models and Recipes (Table 2):**
- **Consistent Pass@1 Gains:** QAE consistently improved pass@1 scores across various Large Language Model (LLM) sizes, including Qwen3-8B-Base, Qwen3-14B-Base, and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base. These improvements were observed when QAE was integrated as a drop-in replacement for the mean baseline in several existing value-free RL methods, such as Clip-Higher, CLIP-Cov, KL-Cov, and GSPO. For example, QAE boosted Clip-Higher on Qwen3-8B-Base by +21.5% on AIME’24 (from 39.69 to 48.23).
- **Comparable Pass@16:** While pass@1 showed notable gains, pass@16 scores remained largely comparable or experienced modest improvements. This suggests that QAE enhances the efficiency of learning from individual correct responses without necessarily sacrificing the diversity of plausible solutions captured by pass@16.
- **Compatibility:** QAE demonstrated compatibility with existing token-level controls (e.g., CLIP-Cov, KL-Cov) and sequence-level optimization (GSPO), providing additional gains without requiring adjustments to their hyperparameters.

**Training Dynamics and Entropy Safety (Figures 1, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11):**
- **Entropy Stabilization:** A key finding is QAE's ability to stabilize policy entropy throughout training. In contrast to baseline DAPO, which often exhibited an early entropy spike (indicative of explosion) followed by a performance plateau, QAE maintained entropy within a productive and stable range. This behavior was consistent across both Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-14B-Base models.
- **Sustained Performance Gains:** QAE facilitated sustained pass@1 gains, allowing training to progress beyond the performance plateaus observed with standard DAPO. This sustained improvement is directly linked to the stabilized entropy.
- **Negative-Advantage Samples as Instability Driver:** The analysis confirmed that entropy growth in baseline DAPO was predominantly driven by negative-advantage samples. QAE effectively suppressed this component, thereby controlling the overall entropy increase.
- **Productive Exploration:** Unlike the baseline DAPO, which led to a homogenization of "high-entropy tokens" (e.g., shifting from diverse "wait," "perhaps" to rigid "so," "let"), QAE sustained a productive co-growth of diverse exploratory tokens and reasoning accuracy. This indicates that QAE leverages high-entropy branches beneficially and then curbs their proliferation when marginal utility diminishes.

**Response-Level Sparsity: The 80/20 Rule (Figure 5, Right):**
- A notable empirical consequence of QAE is the sparsity of credit assignment. Approximately 80% of sampled responses received zero advantage throughout training with a tuned `K`. This "response-level 80/20 rule" suggests that QAE efficiently concentrates computational effort on a smaller, more informative subset of samples, contributing to its stability and efficiency.

**Ablation Studies (Figure 6):**
- **Two-Regime Validation:** Ablation studies involving one-sided masking mechanisms (POS-MASK and NEG-MASK) provided empirical validation for QAE's two-regime control.
 - With weak high-end clipping (`ϵ_high=0.28`), where entropy explosion is the dominant failure mode, `NEG-MASK` (controlling negative-advantage updates) performed similarly to full QAE, outperforming `POS-MASK`. This indicates the critical role of managing negative advantages in preventing explosion.
 - With strong high-end clipping (`ϵ_high=0.20`), where collapse pressure dominates, `POS-MASK` (controlling positive-advantage updates) performed better. This confirms the theoretical prediction of controlling positive advantages to prevent collapse.

**Quantile Parameter K Analysis (Figure 9):**
- The hyperparameter `K` was shown to effectively control the trade-off between entropy, accuracy, and response length.
 - Large `K` values (e.g., 0.8) led to high entropy, inflated response lengths, and volatile, plateaued training, characteristic of over-exploration.
 - Small `K` values (e.g., 0.2) resulted in low entropy, over-regularization, and limited accuracy gains, consistent with under-exploration or collapse.
 - A value of `K=0.4` (used with `ϵ_high=0.28` in main experiments) achieved a balanced state, yielding bounded entropy, stable response lengths, and sustained accuracy improvements, aligning with the theoretical "two-sided entropy safety."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Quantile Advantage Estimation (QAE) as a mechanism for entropy-safe reasoning in LLMs, presenting several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Reframing Entropy Regulation:** The work fundamentally reframes entropy regulation in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) from a token-level heuristic problem to a baseline-design problem. By demonstrating that modifying the advantage baseline (from mean to K-quantile) effectively controls policy entropy, the authors propose a more principled approach to managing the exploration-exploitation trade-off. This suggests that future research might prioritize baseline design as a primary lever for RL stability.

- **Improved Training Stability and Scalability:** QAE directly addresses a critical challenge in scaling RLVR: the oscillation between entropy collapse and entropy explosion. By providing a mechanism that theoretically and empirically ensures two-sided entropy safety, QAE can lead to more stable and robust training processes. This enhanced stability is crucial for effectively fine-tuning increasingly larger and more complex LLMs for advanced reasoning tasks, potentially enabling sustained performance gains over longer training durations.

- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** The consistent improvement in pass@1 scores across various models and benchmarks indicates that QAE translates theoretical stability into practical performance gains. The observed "response-level 80/20 rule," where approximately 80% of responses receive zero advantage, suggests that QAE significantly sparsifies credit assignment. This concentration of learning signals on the most informative samples can lead to more computationally efficient training, as resources are focused where they provide the most utility.

- **Orthogonality and Compatibility:** QAE is presented as a "drop-in" modification that is orthogonal to existing token-level controls (e.g., Clip-Higher, CLIP-Cov, KL-Cov) and sequence-level optimization methods (e.g., GSPO). Its ability to compose cleanly with these techniques provides practitioners with a versatile tool that can be integrated into current RLVR pipelines without requiring extensive rework or complex hyperparameter tuning of other components. This broad compatibility increases its potential for widespread adoption.

- **Theoretical Foundation for Control:** The theoretical analysis providing two-sided entropy safety guarantees, explaining how the K-quantile baseline achieves minimal entropy increment in low-success regimes (explosion-proof) and maximal increment in high-success regimes (collapse-proof), offers a deeper understanding of the mechanism. This theoretical backing strengthens the methodology and provides a framework for analyzing future entropy control techniques.

- **"Third Knob" for Entropy Control:** The paper suggests that baseline design (specifically, the shift from mean to K-quantile) serves as a "third knob" for entropy control, complementing existing methods like positive/negative ratios or advantage shaping. This expanded toolkit for managing entropy can enable more fine-grained and effective steering of LLM learning.

- **Future Research Avenues:** The identified limitations, such as exploring dynamic K schedules, automatic K adaptation, and integration into other algorithms like PPO, highlight promising directions for future research. These extensions could further generalize QAE's applicability and potentially unlock even greater performance and robustness.

In summary, QAE represents a step forward in addressing fundamental stability issues in RLVR for LLMs. By focusing on the advantage baseline design, it offers a principled, efficient, and compatible method for maintaining productive policy entropy, with the potential to significantly impact the scalability and performance of LLM reasoning systems.
