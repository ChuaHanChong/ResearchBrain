# Right Question is Already Half the Answer: Fully Unsupervised LLM Reasoning Incentivization

> [!summary] Summary
> A fully unsupervised method called EMPO enhances Large Language Model reasoning by minimizing semantic entropy as an intrinsic reward signal. The approach improves mathematical reasoning accuracy on models like Qwen2.5-Math-7B from 30.7% to 48.1% and boosts natural reasoning on benchmarks like MMLU-Pro for Qwen2.5-7B from 32.1% to 50.1%, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding supervised methods.

## Problem

- Enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities typically relies on expensive and time-consuming external supervision, such as human-labeled reasoning traces, verified answers, or pre-trained reward models.
- The high cost and limited scalability of acquiring high-quality supervised data restrict the development of more powerful reasoning LLMs.
- Attempts at self-improvement using pseudo-data can sometimes lead to model collapse or limited performance gains.

## Method

- **EMPO** (==Entropy Minimized Policy Optimization==) is an RL-based method that operates fully unsupervised by minimizing the predictive semantic entropy of LLM generations.
- It samples multiple responses for a given question and groups them into 'meaning clusters' using lightweight rules for math or a small General-Verifier SLM for free-form reasoning.
- Outputs belonging to more probable (consistent) semantic clusters receive higher intrinsic rewards, and optimization focuses on queries with moderate semantic entropy to prevent reward hacking.

## Results

- EMPO improved the average accuracy on mathematical benchmarks from **28.1%** to **42.1%** for Qwen2.5-Math-**1.5B** and from **30.7%** to **48.1%** for Qwen2.5-Math-**7B**, competitively matching or exceeding supervised Instruct models.
- On natural free-form reasoning, EMPO increased Qwen2.5-**7B**'s accuracy on MMLU-Pro from **32.1%** to **50.1%** and on GPQA from **15.9%** to **28.8%**, outperforming supervised fine-tuning baselines.
- Analysis showed a strong negative correlation between the unsupervised semantic entropy objective and true accuracy, validating its effectiveness as a training signal and demonstrating stable learning dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing semantic entropy of LLM outputs can serve as an effective intrinsic reward signal, guiding unsupervised reasoning improvement.
> - Reinforcement learning, whether supervised or unsupervised, primarily refines an LLM's ability to efficiently access and prioritize pre-existing latent reasoning patterns acquired during pre-training, rather than instilling fundamentally new reasoning skills.
> - An entropy thresholding strategy during training helps mitigate reward hacking and maintain output diversity by filtering out both overly confident and highly uncertain predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen remarkable progress in challenging tasks like mathematical and natural language reasoning. A dominant paradigm for enhancing these reasoning capabilities involves a two-stage process: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on carefully curated datasets, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL), often based on human feedback or sophisticated reward models. While effective, this paradigm heavily relies on various forms of **external supervision**, such as human-labeled reasoning traces, verified golden answers, or pre-trained reward models. This dependency leads to significant challenges:
- **High Cost and Time Consumption:** Acquiring high-quality, task-specific supervised data is incredibly expensive and time-consuming, requiring expert annotators.
- **Limited Scalability:** The reliance on supervised data fundamentally limits the scalability of training more powerful reasoning LLMs, especially as model sizes and complexity grow.
- **Risk of Model Collapse:** Attempts at self-improvement using pseudo-data can sometimes lead to model collapse, where the model overfits to its own errors.

To mitigate these issues, recent research has explored various **self-supervised and semi-supervised approaches**. These include:
- **Self-Consistency and Pseudo-Labeling:** Methods that generate high-confidence answers through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and self-consistency, then fine-tune on these pseudo-labels. However, these often yield limited performance gains and carry risks.
- **Self-Rewarding Models:** Approaches where the LLM itself generates rewards or corrections, sometimes requiring a ground-truth verifier or instruction fine-tuning signals for initialization. DeepSeek-R1-Zero, for instance, starts RL directly from a base model but still relies on rule-based rewards or pre-trained reward models.
- **Test-Time Adaptation:** More recent works explore adapting models at inference time, but these are often task-specific (e.g., mathematical tasks) and don't strictly separate training and testing phases.

**EMPO (Entropy Minimized Policy Optimization)** distinguishes itself within this landscape by proposing a **fully unsupervised** approach to LLM reasoning incentivization. Unlike the aforementioned methods, EMPO explicitly aims to eliminate *any* form of external supervision, including human labels, golden answers, or pre-trained reward models that provide correctness feedback. It directly addresses the "critical open question: How can we incentivize LLM reasoning capacities in a fully unsupervised manner?" by leveraging an intrinsic signal – semantic entropy. This positions EMPO as a significant step towards more autonomous and scalable LLM self-improvement, potentially reducing the resource bottleneck in developing advanced reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **fully unsupervised method for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs)**. The authors aim to break the critical dependency of current state-of-the-art LLM reasoning training paradigms on external supervision, such as human-labeled data, verified ground-truth answers, or pre-trained reward models.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Scalability and Cost Barriers:** Current methods for improving LLM reasoning, typically involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), are highly reliant on large-scale, high-quality reasoning datasets. The creation of these datasets through human labeling or sophisticated verification processes is extremely time-consuming and costly, severely limiting the scalability and broader adoption of powerful reasoning models.
- **Seeking Autonomous LLM Improvement:** The authors are inspired by recent advancements like DeepSeek-R1-Zero, which demonstrate robust reasoning capabilities by directly initiating RL from base models. This fuels the motivation to explore if similar success can be achieved *without* the need for rule-based rewards (e.g., verified golden answers) or pre-trained reward models, moving towards true autonomous model improvement.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic Signals:** The core motivation is to discover and exploit an intrinsic signal that can guide LLM optimization for reasoning in the absence of explicit external feedback. The paper posits semantic entropy as this potent intrinsic reward, based on the intuition that a more confident and consistent model (low entropy) is likely to be a more accurate and reliable reasoner.
- **Generalizability Across Reasoning Tasks:** The authors aim for a method versatile enough to apply to diverse reasoning tasks, ranging from deterministic mathematical problems to open-ended, free-form natural language questions, where defining rule-based rewards is significantly harder.

In essence, EMPO seeks to pave the way for a new generation of LLMs that can improve their reasoning skills independently, reducing the reliance on human intervention and specialized datasets, thereby making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and scalable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EMPO (Entropy Minimized Policy Optimization) is an RL-based method designed to enhance LLM reasoning by minimizing the predictive entropy of the model's generations in a latent semantic space. It operates in a fully unsupervised manner, using only unlabeled questions.

**Core Principle: Semantic Entropy Minimization**
The fundamental idea behind EMPO is derived from the established concept of entropy minimization in semi-supervised and unsupervised learning. A robust model should make confident and consistent predictions on unlabeled data. EMPO extends this to LLMs by minimizing *semantic entropy* rather than traditional Shannon entropy.

1. **Sampling Multiple Responses:** Given an unlabeled input question `q`, EMPO samples a group of `G` responses (`o_1..., o_G`) from the current LLM policy model (π_θ).
2. **Semantic Clustering:** Instead of evaluating each response individually for correctness, EMPO focuses on their semantic equivalence. The `G` sampled outputs are grouped into `M` "meaning clusters" (`c_1..., c_M`).
 - For **mathematical reasoning tasks**, semantic equivalence is determined using simple, predefined rules like N-gram matching or regular expressions (e.g., extracting the final numerical answer and comparing). This is computationally lightweight.
 - For **free-form natural reasoning tasks**, a compact Small Language Model (SLM), specifically a "General-Verifier" (a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B-Math model), is used. This SLM takes two model outputs and the original question and determines if the *final predictions* (e.g., contents within `\boxed{}`) are bidirectionally entailing (i.e., semantically equivalent), irrespective of their reasoning traces. The authors emphasize that this SLM acts as a utility for *semantic comparison* and does not provide task-specific "correctness" feedback or gradient guidance, thus maintaining the "unsupervised" nature of EMPO for the reasoning task itself.
3. **Approximating Meaning Probability:** The probability of each meaning cluster `c_j` given question `q` (`p(c_j|q)`) is approximated by the proportion of sampled answers belonging to that cluster: `|c_j| / G`.
4. **Semantic Entropy Calculation:** The semantic entropy `H` is then calculated using this probability distribution: `H = - Σ p(c_j|q) log p(c_j|q)`. A lower entropy indicates higher consistency and confidence in the model's output meanings.
5. **Intrinsic Reward Signal:** The reward `r_i` assigned to an individual output `o_i` is defined as the likelihood of its meaning cluster: `r_i = p(c_j|q)` where `o_i` belongs to `c_j`. This means outputs that belong to higher-probability meaning clusters (i.e., those that the model generates more consistently) receive higher rewards.

**Optimization Objective:**
EMPO optimizes the LLM policy `π_θ` by maximizing an objective similar to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), but with its unique unsupervised reward signal:
`J_EMPO = E [1/|G| Σ (A_i)]`, where `A_i` is the advantage of output `o_i`. The advantage is computed by normalizing rewards within each group: `A_i = (r_i - mean({r_1...,r_G})) / std({r_1...,r_G})`. This incentivizes outputs from more probable (consistent) semantic clusters.

**Mitigating Reward Hacking with Entropy Thresholding:**
A potential risk of unsupervised entropy minimization is "reward hacking," where the model might become overconfident in incorrect but consistent predictions. To counter this, EMPO employs a straightforward **entropy thresholding strategy**:
- Optimization is restricted to prompts where the semantic entropy `H` falls within a moderate range: `δ_low < H < δ_high`.
- **High-entropy filtering (`H > δ_high`):** Queries resulting in extremely high entropy (highly uncertain predictions) are filtered out, as they are prone to being unreliable.
- **Low-entropy filtering (`H < δ_low`):** Queries with already very low entropy are also filtered out. This prevents redundant optimization on already confident predictions and helps maintain output diversity, reducing the risk of overconfidence and potential model collapse.

Unlike GRPO, EMPO removes the KL constraint (which prevents the policy from deviating too far from a reference model) for potentially better performance, a choice also seen in some other advanced RL works.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate EMPO's efficacy in incentivizing LLM reasoning capabilities in a fully unsupervised manner, achieving competitive performance compared to supervised and semi-supervised counterparts.

#### 4.1. Performance on Mathematical Reasoning Tasks

- EMPO was trained on Qwen2.5-Math Base models (1.5B and 7B parameters) using 20,000 unlabeled prompts from the NuminaMath-CoT dataset.
- **Significant Improvement:** EMPO boosted the average accuracy on mathematical benchmarks (Minerva Math, MATH, AMC23, OlympaidBench, AIME24) from:
 - **28.1% to 42.1%** for the Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B Base model.
 - **30.7% to 48.1%** for the Qwen2.5-Math-7B Base model.
- **Competitive with Supervised Models:** Notably, the EMPO-trained models (42.1% for 1.5B and 48.1% for 7B) achieved performance very close to or even slightly exceeding their supervised counterparts, such as Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct (40.5% for 1.5B, 49.4% for 7B). This is particularly significant given that the Instruct models rely on private datasets and multi-stage supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
- **Outperformance of SFT:** EMPO consistently outperformed models trained purely with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the same base models.

#### 4.2. Performance on Natural Free-form Reasoning Tasks

- EMPO was applied to Qwen2.5 Base models (3B, 7B, and 14B parameters) using 18,000 unlabeled prompts from the Natural Reasoning dataset. Semantic clustering for these tasks used a small language model (General-Verifier).
- **Substantial Accuracy Gains:**
 - On **MMLU-Pro**, EMPO improved the accuracy of Qwen2.5-7B Base from 32.1% to 50.1%, and Qwen2.5-14B Base from 32.7% to 58.8%.
 - On the more challenging **GPQA benchmark**, EMPO increased accuracy from 15.9% to 28.8% on the 7B model, and 30.6% to 35.3% on the 14B model.
- **SFT Baseline Issues:** The SFT baseline struggled to consistently improve performance on natural reasoning tasks, hypothesized to be due to noise in the reference responses of the Natural Reasoning training data. This highlights EMPO's robustness in the face of potentially noisy real-world data.
- **Competitive with Supervised RL:** EMPO achieved results comparable to, and in some cases even surpassing, GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), which typically relies on verifiable golden answers.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics and Pass@k Analysis

- **Reliability of Unsupervised Reward:** Analysis of training dynamics showed a strong negative correlation between the unsupervised semantic entropy objective (Eq. 4) and the true accuracy (based on golden answers for evaluation purposes). This validates that minimizing semantic entropy effectively guides the model towards higher accuracy in an unsupervised manner. The training process was stable, with consistently decreased semantic entropy and improved accuracy.
- **Role of Unsupervised Learning in Eliciting Capabilities (Pass@k):** The paper includes a crucial discussion based on pass@k accuracy curves (measuring the probability that at least 1 of the top-k generated solutions is correct).
 - For small to moderate `k` (e.g., 16 or 32), EMPO and supervised RL methods (GRPO) significantly improve pass@k scores compared to the base model, indicating enhanced efficiency in surfacing correct reasoning paths with fewer attempts.
 - However, as `k` becomes substantially large, the performance of RL-trained models (including EMPO) tends to converge with, or is sometimes surpassed by, the base model.
 - This suggests that RL post-training (supervised or unsupervised) primarily **refines the model's ability to efficiently access, prioritize, and consistently select pre-existing latent reasoning patterns** learned during pre-training, rather than fundamentally instilling new reasoning skills. The observed improvements in pass@1 (accuracy) are thus attributed to this enhanced sampling efficiency.

In summary, EMPO demonstrates that a fully unsupervised approach, by leveraging semantic entropy as an intrinsic reward signal, can significantly boost LLM reasoning capabilities to a level competitive with, or even surpassing, methods reliant on expensive external supervision, while also providing insights into *how* these improvements manifest.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of EMPO marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) training, particularly for reasoning tasks, due to its **fully unsupervised nature**. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

- **Scalability and Cost Reduction:** The most immediate and profound impact of EMPO is its ability to bypass the need for expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive human-labeled data, verified golden answers, or pre-trained reward models. This dramatically reduces the barrier to developing and improving LLMs for complex reasoning tasks. It opens the door for continuous, autonomous self-improvement of LLMs, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners, especially in domains where curated data is scarce or proprietary.
- **Enabling Autonomous LLM Improvement:** EMPO provides a concrete step towards truly self-improving LLMs. By deriving an effective intrinsic reward signal (semantic entropy) directly from the model's own outputs and their consistency, it allows LLMs to refine their reasoning processes without external human oversight. This pushes the frontier of AI towards more autonomous learning systems.
- **Robustness to Noisy Data:** As demonstrated by the performance on free-form natural reasoning tasks where SFT struggled due to potential noise in reference responses, EMPO's unsupervised nature can make it more robust to the inherent imperfections of large, uncurated datasets. By focusing on internal consistency, it may be less susceptible to the biases or errors present in external labels.
- **Insights into LLM Learning Mechanisms:** The paper's discussion, particularly the pass@k analysis, offers crucial insights into *how* reinforcement learning, whether supervised or unsupervised, enhances LLM performance. It strongly supports the emerging view that pre-training endows LLMs with the fundamental reasoning capabilities, and subsequent fine-tuning (like EMPO) primarily functions to **elicit and refine these pre-existing abilities**, improving the model's efficiency in consistently selecting and prioritizing correct reasoning pathways. This understanding has implications for future LLM architecture design and training strategies, suggesting that optimizing for diverse and robust pre-training might be key.
- **Broader Applicability of Entropy Minimization:** EMPO validates semantic entropy as a potent intrinsic reward signal for LLMs, extending its application from traditional machine learning and computer vision to complex natural language generation tasks. This could inspire further research into other intrinsic signals for unsupervised LLM optimization.
- **Practical Utility in Resource-Constrained Environments:** For smaller research groups, startups, or applications in low-resource languages where high-quality labeled data is prohibitively expensive or non-existent, EMPO offers a viable and effective alternative to develop competitive reasoning LLMs.

In conclusion, EMPO represents a significant paradigm shift by demonstrating that fully unsupervised methods can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance in LLMs. Its primary impact lies in its potential to democratize access to advanced LLM capabilities by drastically reducing the data annotation burden, fostering greater scalability, and offering a new path toward truly autonomous LLM self-improvement.
