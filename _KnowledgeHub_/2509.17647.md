# VideoArtGS: Building Digital Twins of Articulated Objects from Monocular Video

> [!summary] Summary
> VIDEOARTGS reconstructs high-fidelity, interactable digital twins of articulated objects directly from monocular video by integrating robust motion priors and an articulation-aware 3D Gaussian Splatting framework. The method achieves an approximate two-order-of-magnitude reduction in reconstruction and articulation error compared to previous state-of-the-art approaches.

## Problem

- Reconstructing articulated objects from a single monocular video is fundamentally ill-posed, as camera motion, object geometry, part segmentation, and articulation dynamics are entangled.
- Existing monocular methods often yield distorted geometries, inaccurate part segmentations, or struggle with scalability and generalization to complex, multi-part objects.
- Creating interactable digital twins requires explicit control over object articulation, which is often lacking in general dynamic 3D reconstruction techniques from video.

## Method

- The method employs a novel motion prior guidance pipeline that analyzes, filters, classifies, and clusters 3D tracking trajectories from pre-trained perception models to provide robust initializations for articulation parameters.
- It integrates an explicit articulation model into the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, utilizing a hybrid center-grid part assignment for both movable and complex static parts.
- A joint optimization process refines canonical 3D Gaussians and the articulation-based deformation field using standard rendering losses and tailored tracking losses, including a crucial observation-to-observation consistency loss.

## Results

- VideoArtGS achieves state-of-the-art performance, reducing key error metrics for articulation estimation and mesh reconstruction by approximately two orders of magnitude compared to prior methods.
- On the Video2Articulation-S dataset, it reduced revolute axis error to 0.32 	 0.44 degrees (vs. 13.83 	 28.15 degrees for the previous best) and position error to 0.42 cm (vs. 11.55 cm).
- The approach successfully reconstructs high-fidelity, controllable digital twins of diverse articulated objects from self-captured monocular videos, demonstrating strong generalization to complex multi-part kinematics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently processed motion priors, even from noisy 3D tracks, are critical for making the ill-posed monocular articulated object reconstruction problem tractable and for initializing articulation parameters effectively.
> - A hybrid part assignment strategy, combining center-based assignment for movable parts with a learnable hash grid for complex static base parts, improves segmentation and overall reconstruction robustness.
> - Enforcing temporal consistency through an observation-to-observation tracking loss is more effective for achieving precise and stable joint state estimation than canonical-to-observation supervision alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The problem of reconstructing articulated objects – objects composed of multiple rigid parts connected by joints (e.g., doors, scissors, human bodies) – is a fundamental challenge in computer vision with vast implications for augmented reality (AR), robotics, and interactive scene understanding. VideoArtGS directly addresses a critical frontier within this domain: reconstructing interactable digital twins from **monocular video**.

Traditionally, articulated object reconstruction has followed two main paradigms:

1. **Feed-forward models:** These approaches leverage large, annotated datasets to directly predict articulation parameters. However, their primary limitation lies in scalability and generalization. The sheer combinatorial complexity of real-world objects, diverse articulation types, and varied viewing conditions makes creating sufficiently comprehensive training datasets practically infeasible. This often leads to poor performance on novel, unseen objects or scenarios.
2. **Multi-view or multi-state methods:** These methods benefit from strong geometric constraints by observing objects from multiple viewpoints or in different articulated states. While offering higher accuracy, they demand controlled, often cumbersome data capture setups, making them unsuitable for casual, in-the-wild video inputs. Furthermore, slight misalignments between different states can lead to significant errors.

**The "monocular video" challenge:** The research landscape has increasingly recognized the immense potential of monocular video as an input format due to its simplicity, accessibility, and scalability (e.g., leveraging vast amounts of online video data or direct robot observations). However, this convenience introduces a profound technical hurdle: the reconstruction problem becomes fundamentally **ill-posed**. A single, moving viewpoint entangles four complex factors – camera trajectory, object geometry, part segmentation, and articulation-based part dynamics – making it extremely difficult to disentangle them without strong parallax cues from multi-view data. Previous video-based methods have often yielded distorted geometries, inaccurate part segmentations, or were restricted to overly simplistic objects.

**VideoArtGS's Position and Innovation:**
VideoArtGS directly tackles this ill-posed problem, differentiating itself from prior work by introducing a novel strategy to robustly leverage **motion priors from pre-trained tracking models**. While other methods have explored using 2D or 3D tracks for supervision, VideoArtGS innovatively designs a comprehensive "motion prior guidance pipeline" that not only extracts these tracks but critically analyzes, filters noise, classifies motion types, and clusters them to provide reliable initializations for articulation parameters. This transforms an otherwise intractable joint optimization problem into a well-posed refinement task.

Furthermore, the integration of an explicit articulation model into the recently popularized **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) is a key advancement. While many 4D extensions of 3DGS exist for dynamic scene reconstruction, they often excel at capturing complex, non-rigid motion without offering explicit control over an object's underlying structure. VideoArtGS bridges this gap by enabling high-fidelity reconstruction *with* explicit articulation control, making the reconstructed digital twins truly interactable and meaningful for downstream applications. This approach pushes the boundaries of video-based 3D reconstruction, moving beyond simple non-rigid deformations to accurate structural and kinematic modeling of complex objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable method for **reconstructing high-fidelity digital twins of articulated objects directly from monocular video inputs.** This goal is motivated by several critical needs and challenges in modern computer vision and robotics:

1. **Advancing Intelligent Systems and Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Reconstructing interactable digital twins from simple video inputs can significantly accelerate the development of intelligent systems, particularly in robotics. By providing robots with the ability to model the world accurately through their own visual observations, VideoArtGS can bridge the gap between simulation and real-world application, enabling robots to learn and perform complex manipulation and interaction tasks more effectively.
2. **Unlocking Scalability and Accessibility:** Monocular video offers an attractive input format due to its widespread availability and ease of capture. The ability to learn from the vast amount of videos available online and allow robotic agents to model the world through their own eyes represents a critical frontier. This approach democratizes 3D content creation for articulated objects, moving away from specialized capture setups.
3. **Solving the Ill-Posed Reconstruction Problem:** The core technical challenge is disentangling object geometry, part segmentation, and articulation parameters from the entangled motion cues in a monocular video. This fundamental ambiguity has historically limited the quality and generality of video-based reconstruction methods. The paper aims to overcome this by introducing external motion priors and articulation constraints.
4. **Improving Articulation and Geometry Accuracy:** Existing monocular video methods often struggle with producing accurate geometries, precise part segmentations, and correct articulation parameters. VideoArtGS aims to significantly improve these aspects, providing reconstruction quality that is robust enough for practical applications.
5. **Creating Controllable Digital Assets:** The ultimate goal is not just to reconstruct a static 3D model, but to create a "digital twin" – an interactable asset whose parts can be controlled according to their learned articulation parameters. This enables applications beyond passive viewing, such as interactive AR experiences or robot training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoArtGS introduces a novel approach to reconstruct articulated objects from monocular video, integrating advanced perception models with a sophisticated articulation-based deformation field and robust optimization. The overall pipeline, depicted in Figure 1 of the paper, can be broken down into several key stages:

1. **Upstream Perception for Data Preparation:**
 - Given a monocular video, the method first uses pre-trained perception models to extract fundamental scene information.
 - **VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b):** This model is employed to estimate depths and camera poses for each frame.
 - **TAPIP3D (Zhang et al., 2025):** A pre-trained tracking model is used to obtain 3D tracking trajectories of points across the video sequence. These 3D tracks serve as the crucial "motion prior."

2. **Motion Prior Guidance Pipeline:** This is a central innovation designed to process the noisy 3D tracks and provide reliable initializations for the articulation model.
 - **Motion Pattern Analysis:** Each 3D trajectory is analyzed and classified into one of four categories: static, prismatic, revolute, or noise.
 - Static tracks are identified by minimal displacement.
 - For dynamic tracks, an **adaptive spatial downsampling** method is used to mitigate the issue of densely concentrated points (especially for multi-part objects), which can cause fitting algorithms to collapse.
 - **Line fitting (PCA with RANSAC)** is applied to identify prismatic motion, yielding direction vectors.
 - **Plane and circle fitting (SVD)** is used for revolute motion, yielding axis directions and origins. Tracks are prioritized based on fitting error.
 - **Motion Clustering:** Valid (non-noise) trajectories, along with their classified motion types and parameters, are grouped into distinct parts using K-means clustering. Feature vectors for clustering incorporate starting/average positions, motion directions/origins, and velocities. An iterative filtering strategy, combining directional angle and Euclidean distance, removes outliers to improve clustering quality.
 - **Deformation Field Initialization:** The outputs from motion clustering (axis directions, axis origins, and part centers) are used to robustly initialize the core parameters of the articulation-based deformation field (F).

3. **Articulation-Based Deformation Field (F) with Hybrid Center-Grid Part Assignment:**
 - F models the temporal dynamics by transforming canonical 3D Gaussians (G_c) into their observed states (G_t) at any given time. It comprises a part segmentation module (Sφ) and articulation parameters (Ψ).
 - **Hybrid Center-Grid Part Assignment:** This is another key innovation.
 - For *movable parts*, a center-based approach (similar to ArtGS) uses learnable part centers and Mahalanobis distance for assignment.
 - For the *static base part*, which often has complex, non-centralized geometry, a **learnable hash grid** (H) is used to directly model its spatial region. This hybrid approach allows for robust segmentation by leveraging structured geometric relationships for movable parts and flexible spatial modeling for complex static regions.
 - Articulation is modeled using dual-quaternions for rigid transformations (rotation and translation), with time-variant joint states (θ_t) represented by an MLP on Fourier embeddings for smoothness.

4. **Canonical Gaussian Initialization:** The first N frames of the video, assumed to be static, are used to initialize the canonical 3D Gaussians (G_c) representing the object's base geometry.

5. **Joint Optimization:** The canonical Gaussians (G_c) and the deformation field (F) are jointly optimized across all video frames and tracking trajectories.
 - **Rendering Loss (L_render):** Standard 3DGS rendering losses (L1, D-SSIM, and depth loss) ensure high-quality photometric and geometric reconstruction of the object from rendered views.
 - **Tracking Loss (L_track):** This loss guides the deformation field's optimization using the provided 3D tracks.
 - **Canonical-to-Observation (L_c2o):** Directly supervises the deformation from the canonical state to an observed state, ensuring that the deformed canonical points match the tracked observation points.
 - **Observation-to-Observation (L_o2o):** Enhances temporal consistency by deforming a point from one observed state back to canonical, and then forward to another observed state, encouraging consistency between different observations. This loss is crucial for robustly handling temporal dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoArtGS demonstrates significant advancements in articulated object reconstruction from monocular video, consistently achieving state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks.

**Quantitative Superiority:**
- **Order-of-Magnitude Error Reduction:** The most striking finding is the reduction in key error metrics by approximately *two orders of magnitude* compared to prior state-of-the-art methods. This indicates a qualitative leap in accuracy.

- **Video2Articulation-S Dataset (Simple Articulated Objects):**
 - VideoArtGS dramatically outperforms baselines like ArticulateAnything, RSRD, and Video2Articulation across all articulation estimation (axis direction, axis position, joint state errors) and mesh reconstruction (Chamfer Distance for whole, movable, and static parts) metrics.
 - For instance, on revolute axis error, VideoArtGS achieves 0.32 ± 0.44 degrees, while the next best, Video2Articulation, is 13.83 ± 28.15 degrees. Similar improvements are seen in position (0.42 cm vs 11.55 cm) and joint state errors (1.15 deg vs 10.25 deg).
 - Mesh reconstruction quality also sees substantial improvements (CD-w: 0.29 cm vs 3.45 cm). This validates the effectiveness of the motion prior guidance in providing accurate initialization.

- **VideoArtGS-20 Dataset (Complex Articulated Objects):**
 - The authors introduce a new, more challenging dataset with objects having 2 to 9 movable parts and larger camera motions, specifically designed to test limitations of existing methods.
 - On this complex dataset, VideoArtGS's advantages become even more pronounced. It achieves vastly superior performance in axis error (0.34 ± 0.80 deg vs 43.65 ± 44.72 deg for ArticulateAnything and 48.88 ± 24.18 deg for Video2Articulation) and position error (0.10 ± 0.10 cm vs 15.66 ± 36.20 cm and 37.04 ± 31.82 cm).
 - Even with ArticulateAnything having access to a retrieval database from the same source (PartNet-Mobility), VideoArtGS significantly surpasses it, demonstrating its ability to robustly generalize to complex multi-part kinematics.

**Qualitative Excellence:**
- The qualitative results (Figures 2, 3, 4) visually confirm the quantitative findings. VideoArtGS consistently produces high-fidelity mesh reconstructions, characterized by clean part boundaries and precise articulation.
- It successfully decouples object geometry from time-varying motion, generating controllable digital assets. This is evident in reconstructions of scissors, tables, storage units, cabinets, laptops, and microwaves.
- The method demonstrates strong generalization ability, reconstructing a diverse set of articulated objects from self-captured, real-world monocular videos using a mobile phone camera without LiDAR.

**Ablation Studies:**
- The ablation studies on the VideoArtGS-20 dataset systematically validate the importance of each component:
 - **Motion Prior Guidance:** Removing the entire motion prior guidance (including initialization of centers, joint axes, and deformation field) leads to catastrophic failure, unequivocally confirming its critical role in making the ill-posed problem tractable.
 - **Initialization Components:** Part center initialization is found to be absolutely necessary, while deformation field initialization significantly improves performance. Axis initialization provides a beneficial, though marginal, impact on stability.
 - **Hybrid Center-Grid Assignment:** Replacing the hybrid module with a pure center-based assignment leads to moderate drops in performance, especially for movable part reconstruction and articulation estimation, validating the efficacy of the hybrid design for complex static parts.
 - **Tracking Losses:** The observation-to-observation tracking loss (L_o2o) is more critical for achieving precise and stable joint estimation than the canonical-to-observation loss (L_c2o), highlighting the importance of enforcing temporal consistency directly in the observation space.

In summary, the findings robustly establish VideoArtGS as a new benchmark for video-based articulated object reconstruction, demonstrating its unprecedented accuracy and practical applicability for creating interactable digital twins from casual monocular video.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VideoArtGS research represents a significant leap forward in computer vision and offers profound potential impact across several domains:

1. **Democratization of Digital Twin Creation:** By enabling high-fidelity reconstruction of articulated objects from readily available monocular video, VideoArtGS democratizes the creation of digital twins. This eliminates the need for expensive, specialized multi-view, multi-state capture setups, making advanced 3D modeling accessible to a much wider audience, from enthusiasts to professionals. This has immense potential for creative industries, personal content creation, and small-scale businesses.

2. **Advancement in Robotics and AI for Physical Interaction:**
 - **Sim-to-Real Bridge:** The ability to generate accurate, interactable digital twins from casual video captures is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap in robotics. Robots can now learn to manipulate and interact with articulated objects by observing human demonstrations or their own explorations in the real world, and these learned models can be directly transferred to simulation for further training or analysis.
 - **World Modeling:** This work allows robotic agents to build robust 3D models of articulated objects in their environment directly from their visual observations, fostering a deeper understanding of object kinematics and functionality. This is a crucial step towards truly intelligent and autonomous robotic systems that can adapt to novel objects and situations.

3. **Enhanced Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):**
 - VideoArtGS can significantly enrich AR/VR experiences by allowing users to effortlessly create high-fidelity, interactive 3D models of real-world articulated objects. Imagine scanning a toy car with your phone and immediately having a controllable 3D model in an AR application, or digitizing household appliances for interactive virtual manuals. This capability greatly expands the possibilities for immersive digital content.

4. **Foundation for Future Computer Vision Research:**
 - **Robust Handling of Ill-Posed Problems:** The success of VideoArtGS in addressing the fundamentally ill-posed problem of monocular articulated object reconstruction provides a strong blueprint for tackling other complex inverse problems in computer vision, particularly those involving dynamic scenes and limited input data. The methodology of leveraging noisy motion priors through intelligent analysis and integration is broadly applicable.
 - **Integration of Foundation Models:** The paper's reliance on upstream perception models (VGGT, TAPIP3D) and the exploration of GPT-4o for joint type prediction highlight the growing trend of integrating powerful foundation models into specialized vision tasks. VideoArtGS demonstrates how these components can be effectively orchestrated for complex reconstruction problems.
 - **New Benchmarking:** The introduction of the challenging VideoArtGS-20 dataset provides a valuable resource for the research community, pushing the boundaries of what's possible for multi-part articulated object reconstruction.

5. **Pathways for Future Development:** While groundbreaking, the paper also clearly outlines limitations, such as dependency on upstream trackers and the need for visible motion or static initial frames. These limitations point towards exciting future research directions, including end-to-end learning for joint tracking and reconstruction, or integrating physical priors and generative models to handle motion-scarce or arbitrary video inputs.

In conclusion, VideoArtGS not only achieves unprecedented accuracy in a challenging computer vision task but also lays crucial groundwork for future advancements in AI, robotics, and immersive technologies, making the creation of interactable digital twins from everyday videos a tangible reality.
