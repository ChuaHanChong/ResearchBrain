# RobotArena $\infty$: Scalable Robot Benchmarking via Real-to-Sim Translation

> [!summary] Summary
> RobotArena ∞ is presented as a scalable framework for evaluating generalist robot policies, automatically generating diverse simulated environments from real-world video demonstrations. It employs a dual evaluation strategy combining VLM-guided scoring and large-scale human preference judgments, which was used to assess six open-source VLA policies and identify limitations in their generalization and robustness.

## Problem

- Real-world robot policy evaluation is unscalable, labor-intensive, costly, and lacks reproducibility due to manual setup and resets.
- Existing simulation benchmarks often create a 'closed-world' bias, failing to rigorously evaluate generalist policies trained on diverse real-world data.
- Defining and measuring 'success' in robotics often relies on subjective human judgments that are difficult to quantify at scale.

## Method

- An automated ==real-to-simulation== pipeline converts single-view robot video demonstrations into fully re-executable simulated environments with reconstructed objects and calibrated robot dynamics.
- A dual evaluation strategy combines automated VLM-guided progress scoring with large-scale human pairwise preference judgments, aggregated using the Bradley-Terry model.
- Generated environments can be systematically perturbed along multiple axes (e.g., background, color, object poses) to assess policy robustness and generalization to distribution shifts.

## Results

- Six open-source VLA policies were benchmarked across hundreds of simulated environments and over 8,**500** human preference judgments, revealing varied performance across architectures and datasets.
- Evaluated policies exhibited significant performance drops when tested on out-of-distribution datasets or under systematic environmental perturbations.
- The benchmark highlighted architectural influences, with 
π0 and X-VLA performing well in BridgeSim, RoboVLM excelling in RH20TSim, and indicated that stronger VLM backbones improve robustness to visual shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human preference rankings align with automated VLM scores, indicating the potential for VLM-guided evaluation as a scalable surrogate for direct human assessment.
> - Current VLA policies exhibit limited cross-dataset generalization and are vulnerable to environmental perturbations (background, color shifts, object pose changes).
> - Architectural design and training data play a crucial role in performance, with observations like the 'spatial paradox' suggesting benefits of implicit 3D priors from wrist-camera pre-training for robust manipulation.
