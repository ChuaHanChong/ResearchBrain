# RobotArena $\infty$: Scalable Robot Benchmarking via Real-to-Sim Translation

> [!summary] Summary
> RobotArena ∞ is presented as a scalable framework for evaluating generalist robot policies, automatically generating diverse simulated environments from real-world video demonstrations. It employs a dual evaluation strategy combining VLM-guided scoring and large-scale human preference judgments, which was used to assess six open-source VLA policies and identify limitations in their generalization and robustness.

## Problem

- Real-world robot policy evaluation is unscalable, labor-intensive, costly, and lacks reproducibility due to manual setup and resets.
- Existing simulation benchmarks often create a 'closed-world' bias, failing to rigorously evaluate generalist policies trained on diverse real-world data.
- Defining and measuring 'success' in robotics often relies on subjective human judgments that are difficult to quantify at scale.

## Method

- An automated ==real-to-simulation== pipeline converts single-view robot video demonstrations into fully re-executable simulated environments with reconstructed objects and calibrated robot dynamics.
- A dual evaluation strategy combines automated VLM-guided progress scoring with large-scale human pairwise preference judgments, aggregated using the Bradley-Terry model.
- Generated environments can be systematically perturbed along multiple axes (e.g., background, color, object poses) to assess policy robustness and generalization to distribution shifts.

## Results

- Six open-source VLA policies were benchmarked across hundreds of simulated environments and over 8,**500** human preference judgments, revealing varied performance across architectures and datasets.
- Evaluated policies exhibited significant performance drops when tested on out-of-distribution datasets or under systematic environmental perturbations.
- The benchmark highlighted architectural influences, with π0 and X-VLA performing well in BridgeSim, RoboVLM excelling in RH20TSim, and indicated that stronger VLM backbones improve robustness to visual shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human preference rankings align with automated VLM scores, indicating the potential for VLM-guided evaluation as a scalable surrogate for direct human assessment.
> - Current VLA policies exhibit limited cross-dataset generalization and are vulnerable to environmental perturbations (background, color shifts, object pose changes).
> - Architectural design and training data play a crucial role in performance, with observations like the 'spatial paradox' suggesting benefits of implicit 3D priors from wrist-camera pre-training for robust manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The evaluation of robot policies, particularly as they advance towards generalist capabilities, presents a persistent challenge in robotics research. Real-world testing is constrained by factors such as labor intensity, time requirements, safety concerns, and difficulties in reproducibility. Human operators are often necessary for scene setup, task supervision, and manual resets, which limits the scale and consistency of evaluations. While centralized physical evaluations, like the Amazon Picking Challenge or RoboCup@Home, offer a standardized testing environment, their high cost and logistical demands mean they occur infrequently. This contrasts with fields such as computer vision and natural language processing, which have benefited from standardized, scalable benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, LAION-5B, LMarena) that facilitate rapid progress through consistent metrics and fair comparisons.

Traditional simulation-based benchmarks (e.g., RLBench, CALVIN, Behavior-1K) offer scalability and safety but are typically designed for policies trained and tested within the same simulated environments. This approach can favor specialist policies that exploit specific environmental advantages, potentially limiting their utility for evaluating generalist models trained on diverse real-world or broad simulation data.

Previous work has explored various aspects of real-to-sim translation and robot benchmarking. Efforts like Phone2Proc and Re3Sim aim for high-fidelity scene reconstruction but often rely on multi-view captures, curated object libraries, or fiducial markers, which are impractical for reconstructing typical single-view robot datasets. Other systems, such as RialTo, require human-in-the-loop segmentation or specific robot calibration trajectories. Benchmarks like BEHAVIOR and SIMPLER have made contributions to creating rich simulated environments. BEHAVIOR involved extensive manual asset and environment creation, while SIMPLER focused on reconstructing a limited number of real-world scenes with human effort and hand-designed reward functions.

RobotArena ∞ positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical need for a scalable, reproducible, and continuously evolving benchmark specifically for real-world-trained generalist robot policies. It aims to overcome the limitations of real-world evaluation by shifting VLA assessment into automatically generated simulated environments. By automating environment construction from single-view video demonstrations and integrating both VLM-guided scoring and large-scale human preference judgments, this work seeks to provide a more efficient and rigorous evaluation framework than prior approaches, without requiring extensive manual effort for scene setup or detailed multi-view captures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a scalable and extensible benchmarking framework, named RobotArena ∞, for evaluating generalist robot policies. This framework aims to enable rigorous and reproducible assessment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby facilitating advancements in robot general intelligence.

The motivation for RobotArena ∞ stems from several challenges inherent in contemporary robot policy evaluation:

- **Scalability Limitations of Real-World Evaluation:** Current methods for evaluating robot policies in real-world environments are inherently unscalable. They are resource-intensive, requiring significant human involvement for tasks such as scene setup, manual resets after each trial, and continuous safety supervision. This limits the frequency and diversity of evaluations that can be conducted, hindering comprehensive assessment of increasingly complex generalist policies.
- **Reproducibility and Consistency Issues:** Real-world evaluations often suffer from variability due to slight differences in hardware, camera placement, lighting, and workspace setup across different labs or even different evaluation runs. This lack of standardization makes fair and consistent comparison between different robot policies or across research institutions difficult.
- **Subjectivity of Success Metrics:** Defining and measuring "success" in robotics often relies on nuanced human judgments of execution quality, which can be subjective and difficult to quantify objectively and at scale in physical environments.
- **Gaps in Existing Simulation Benchmarks:** While simulation offers a safe and scalable alternative, existing simulation benchmarks often assume policies are trained and tested within the same simulated environments. This can create a "closed-world" bias, potentially favoring policies specialized to those environments over generalist models trained on diverse real-world data. There is a need for simulation environments that can evaluate policies trained independently of the simulation's origin.
- **High Cost of Centralized Evaluation:** Centralized physical evaluation events, though providing a gold standard for comparison, incur substantial costs for both organizers and participants, leading to infrequent opportunities for widespread benchmarking.
- **Inefficient Human Involvement:** The current paradigm of human involvement in robot evaluation is characterized by tedious, repetitive tasks (e.g., scene setup, resetting). A key motivation is to transform this involvement into a more efficient process, focusing human effort on lightweight preference comparisons rather than manual scene manipulation.

By addressing these issues, RobotArena ∞ seeks to provide a foundation for systematic and continuous evaluation, similar to the role standardized benchmarks have played in accelerating progress in other AI domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RobotArena ∞ introduces a benchmarking framework designed to scale robot evaluation by integrating automated real-to-simulation (real-to-sim) translation with both automated VLM-guided scoring and online human preference feedback. The methodology consists of two main components: an automated real-to-sim pipeline for environment creation and a dual evaluation strategy combining VLM scores with human preferences.

#### 3.1. Real-to-Sim Translation Pipeline

The core of RobotArena ∞ is a fully automated pipeline that converts video demonstrations from real-world robot datasets into corresponding simulated environments within physics engines. This process extracts five key elements from a single RGB image of a static-camera demonstration, along with per-frame robot joint angle trajectories and the robot's URDF file.

- **Automated Robot-Camera Calibration through Differentiable Robot Rendering:**
 - To determine the camera's 6-DoF pose relative to the robot's body, a joint angle-conditioned 3D Gaussian model of the robot is constructed from its URDF file using differentiable rendering, following principles from DR-Robot.
 - The camera's 3D translation and orientation are optimized by minimizing a composite alignment loss between the rendered Gaussian robot model and the video frames. This loss includes an RGB loss for pixel-level appearance, a flow loss for motion consistency (rendered motion fields versus optical flow from video), and a feature loss aligning DINOv2 embeddings.
 - Calibration metadata (if available) is used for initialization; otherwise, a coarse grid search provides a starting point.

- **Object and Scene 3D Reconstruction, Completion, and Physics Estimation:**
 - **Object Segmentation:** Gemini (a Vision-Language Model) is prompted to segment the robot and all task-relevant foreground objects from the video frames.
 - **3D Mesh Generation:** Segmented image crops are super-resolved using InvSR and then converted into textured 3D meshes using Hunyuan-3D.
 - **Object Pose Estimation:** To recover the correct 3D pose of each object, 2D image views of the reconstructed 3D mesh are rendered and compared against the 2D object crop from the real image using MINIMA for correspondence estimation. The view with the most feature matches is selected, and these correspondences are lifted to 3D. Monocular depth estimates from MoGE are scaled using the robot arm's known depth from simulation to achieve metric-scale 3D point clouds. A Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) algorithm is then used to infer the final 3D pose of the objects.
 - **Physical Properties:** Gemini is prompted to infer physical and material properties (e.g., mass, friction coefficients) for the reconstructed objects, which are then incorporated into the simulation environment.
 - **Background Inpainting:** To complete the scene, a static background is generated by inpainting the robot and object regions in the first video frame using the LaMa inpainting model, creating a clean backdrop for the reconstructed assets.

- **System Identification:**
 - To reproduce robot dynamics accurately, system identification is performed to tune the proportional-derivative (PD) controller gains (Kp, Kd). This process aligns simulated end-effector trajectories with those observed in the real demonstration video, using only joint position commands as input.
 - A gradient-free Simulated Annealing (SA) strategy is used, evaluating candidate gains in parallel using the Genesis simulator to minimize the discrepancy in end-effector positions and orientations over time.

#### 3.2. Controllable Domain Perturbations

To assess policy robustness and generalization, the generated simulation environments are systematically perturbed along multiple axes:

- **Background Change (∆BG):** The original scene background is replaced with different inpainted textures, isolating the policy's dependence on contextual appearance cues.
- **Color Shift (∆Color):** The RGB channel configuration of the background is altered (e.g., RGB to BGR conversion at varying intensities) to test robustness against low-level color variations.
- **Object Pose Change (∆ObjPose):** The locations of objects in the scene are randomly permuted, generating multiple scene variants that differ only in object arrangement.

#### 3.3. Evaluation of Robot Trajectories

Robot execution trajectories in these simulated environments are evaluated using two complementary strategies:

- **Automated VLM-guided Scoring:**
 - Proprietary VLMs (specifically Gemini 2.5 Pro) are prompted with an ordered sequence of video frames, simulation state information (object and robot states), and the natural language task instruction.
 - The VLM assigns a progress score (0-100) to each frame, with the initial state explicitly defined as zero progress.
 - A trajectory-level progress estimate is computed as the mean VLM score over the final 30% of frames to focus on task completion or failure.

- **Human Preference Evaluation:**
 - Crowdsourced workers conduct pairwise, double-blind comparisons of two policy execution videos performing the same task in the same simulated environment under identical initial conditions.
 - Evaluators provide a preference label (which policy performed better, or a tie) and a free-form natural language explanation for their choice, enhancing engagement and annotation quality.
 - **Global Ranking from Pairwise Preferences:** The Bradley-Terry (BT) model, a probabilistic framework, is used to aggregate pairwise judgments and infer a global ranking of policies. Each policy is assigned a latent ability score (θ), and these scores are estimated by maximizing the likelihood of observed non-tied comparisons. Confidence intervals for these scores are computed using a robust (sandwich) variance estimator.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RobotArena ∞ benchmark evaluated six open-source VLA policies (Octo, RoboVLM, SpatialVLA, CogAct, X-VLA, π0) across hundreds of automatically generated simulated environments and over 8,500 human preference judgments. The environments were derived from real-world datasets: BridgeV2 (BridgeSim), DROID (DROIDSim), and RH20T (RH20TSim), including both in-distribution and out-of-distribution scenarios, as well as environments with systematic perturbations.

Key findings include:

- **Consistency Between Human and Automated Evaluation:** The human preference rankings of VLAs demonstrated alignment with the automated VLM task progress scores. For instance, π0 and X-VLA were consistently ranked higher by both human evaluators and VLM scores in the BridgeSim environments. This agreement suggests that the automated VLM scoring method can serve as a scalable surrogate for human judgment.
- **Limited Cross-Dataset Generalization:** VLA policies exhibited a significant drop in performance when tested in environments derived from datasets they were not trained on (e.g., DROIDSim and RH20TSim). This indicates that current VLAs tend to specialize to their training data distributions rather than demonstrating true generalist capabilities across diverse scenarios.
- **Vulnerability to Environmental Perturbations:** Performance degraded across all evaluated policies when simulated environments were subjected to perturbations, including changes in background, color shifts, and randomized object poses. This finding suggests that robustness to distribution shifts remains an unresolved challenge for current VLA models.
- **Architectural and Data Design Impact on Performance:** The benchmarking results in BridgeSim and its perturbed variants revealed clear distinctions in performance between architectures, with π0 and X-VLA generally emerging as top performers. However, this superiority was not universal; in RH20TSim, RoboVLM achieved a substantially higher score than other models, while X-VLA's performance was notably poor, indicating that specific architectural choices and training data distributions can lead to varying strengths across different tasks and environments.
- **"Spatial Paradox" Observation:** The models π0 and X-VLA, which had pre-training data including wrist-camera views, demonstrated better spatial reasoning and robustness. This suggests that implicitly learning 3D structural priors through cross-view consistency in pre-training may be more effective for robust manipulation than some explicit 3D inductive biases incorporated in models like SpatialVLA.
- **Backbone Strength and Robustness to Visual Shifts:** Policies equipped with stronger VLM backbones exhibited greater resilience to color perturbations. This implies that these models might be better at relying on invariant structural cues rather than being distracted by superficial appearance features, which can affect less capable models.
- **Overfitting to Task Configurations:** The observed performance degradation under background and object pose perturbations indicates that current VLAs may overfit to the specific visual and spatial setups present in their training datasets. This suggests a reliance on fixed environmental cues rather than robust semantic generalization.
- **Real-World Correlation (Limited Scope):** A limited qualitative comparison for one task ("Put the carrot in the plate") showed consistency between RobotArena ∞ and real-world results: RoboVLM and SpatialVLA succeeded in both, while Octo failed in both, attempting to grasp but consistently missing the target.
- **Rigor Compared to Other Benchmarks:** A comparison with the SIMPLER benchmark revealed that all VLAs achieved higher scores on SIMPLER than on BridgeSim. This discrepancy suggests that SIMPLER's limited number of test environments might lead to an overestimation of policy performance, positioning BridgeSim's broader coverage and diversity as a more rigorous evaluation platform.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RobotArena ∞ presents a significant advancement in the evaluation of robotic manipulation policies, particularly for generalist robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Reproducible Benchmarking:** The framework addresses a critical gap in the robotics field by offering a scalable and reproducible method for evaluating VLA policies. By automating the creation of diverse simulated environments from real-world videos, it overcomes the logistical, safety, and cost constraints inherent in real-world testing. This enables researchers to conduct extensive evaluations and comparative analyses that were previously impractical.
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** Analogous to the impact of standardized benchmarks in computer vision and natural language processing, RobotArena ∞ can accelerate the development of more capable and generalist robot policies. By providing consistent metrics and a common platform for comparison, it allows researchers to iterate on new models more rapidly and objectively assess their progress.
- **Deepened Understanding of Policy Generalization:** The systematic introduction of environmental perturbations (background, color, object pose changes) and cross-dataset evaluation provides valuable insights into the generalization capabilities and robustness of current VLA models. The findings, such as the weak cross-dataset generalization and sensitivity to perturbations, highlight key limitations and direct future research towards developing more robust and truly generalist policies.
- **Efficient Human Feedback Integration:** By shifting human involvement from tedious manual scene setup and resets to lightweight pairwise preference judgments, RobotArena ∞ transforms human feedback into a more scalable and efficient evaluation component. This allows for the capture of nuanced aspects of robot behavior that automated metrics might miss, while optimizing human effort.
- **Extensibility and Future-Proofing:** The modular design of the real-to-sim translation pipeline ensures that RobotArena ∞ can continuously benefit from advances in underlying technologies, such as physics engines, vision-language models, 2D-to-3D generative models, and differentiable rendering. This upgradability ensures the benchmark's long-term relevance and increasing fidelity.
- **Community Resource:** The public release of the benchmark environments and evaluation code is intended to provide the robotics community with an open, extensible platform for rigorous policy evaluation. This fosters collaborative research and promotes transparency and comparability across different research efforts.
- **Informing Policy Design:** The detailed analysis of VLA performance, including observations like the "spatial paradox" and the correlation between backbone strength and robustness, can inform the design of future robot learning architectures. It underscores the importance of learning robust spatial priors and reducing reliance on superficial environmental cues.

While the benchmark acknowledges limitations, such as current difficulties in modeling fine-grained contact dynamics and the initial lack of wrist-camera input support, its foundational contribution is expected to serve as a cornerstone for evaluating the next generation of robotic foundation models.
