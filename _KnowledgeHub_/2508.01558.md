# EvoVLMA: Evolutionary Vision-Language Model Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoVLMA, developed by the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, automates the design of training-free Vision-Language Model adaptation algorithms using a two-stage, LLM-driven evolutionary framework. This system discovers algorithms that consistently outperform manually designed state-of-the-art methods in few-shot classification and domain generalization, achieving accuracy gains up to 1.91 percentage points.

## Problem

- Developing Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation algorithms relies heavily on manual design by human experts, a process that is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and requires specialized domain knowledge.
- Existing parameter-efficient and training-free VLM adaptation methods are predominantly human-engineered, limiting the speed and scope of innovation.
- The search space for effective VLM adaptation algorithms is vast and complex, making manual exploration inefficient.

## Method

- EvoVLMA employs a ==two-stage evolutionary algorithm== that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically generate and refine VLM adaptation algorithm code, represented by both natural language 'thoughts' and Python 'code'.
- It decomposes the complex algorithm search problem into two sequential optimization stages: first for ==feature selection==, then for ==logits computation==, managing the expansive search space more effectively.
- Technical enhancements like low-precision code conversion, web-based parallel execution, and a process monitor ensure efficient and fault-tolerant evaluation of LLM-generated code on GPUs.

## Results

- EvoVLMA-designed algorithms consistently surpassed manually designed state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Tip-Adapter, APE, GDA) in few-shot classification, with gains up to **1.91 percentage points** in recognition accuracy.
- The automatically generated algorithms achieved superior performance on source domains and competitive, well-balanced performance on target domains (ImageNetV2, ImageNet-Sketch) for domain generalization tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of initializing the search with existing algorithms, the effectiveness of the two-stage optimization, and the robustness of discovered algorithms across different visual backbones (ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ViT-B/16).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can generate creative and functional code for VLM adaptation, combining existing concepts and introducing novel terms for both feature selection and logits computation, although they can also produce impractical code.
> - A population-based evolutionary search strategy significantly outperforms individual-based iterative refinement in discovering superior algorithms, demonstrating a better balance between exploration and exploitation.
> - Sequential optimization of interdependent components (feature selection and logits computation) is more effective than attempting joint optimization, which current LLMs struggle with due to increased complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "EvoVLMA" is situated at the intersection of two rapidly evolving fields: **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** and **Large Language Models for Algorithm Design (LLM4AD)**.

In recent years, pre-trained Vision-Language Models, such as CLIP, have emerged as foundational models in computer vision due to their ability to learn powerful, generalized representations from vast amounts of paired image-text data. These VLMs have demonstrated remarkable performance in diverse downstream tasks, including zero-shot and few-shot recognition, and open-vocabulary segmentation. However, effectively transferring knowledge from these large, pre-trained models to specific downstream tasks, especially those with limited labeled data, remains a significant challenge.

To address this, the research community has developed various model adaptation techniques. These generally fall into two categories:
- **Parameter-efficient methods:** These typically involve optimizing a small number of additional parameters rather than fine-tuning the entire model. Examples include prompt tuning (optimizing learnable context vectors) and adapter-based methods (introducing learnable parameters in encoder layers). While effective, these are often gradient-based and can be computationally expensive and slow.
- **Training-free adaptation methods:** A more recent focus has been on methods that do not require gradient-based training, aiming for higher efficiency and suitability for on-device deployment. Notable examples include Tip-Adapter (using a cache model), APE (removing redundant features by minimizing inter-class similarity), and GDA (exploiting Gaussian Discriminant Analysis).

A critical observation highlighted by the authors is that all these existing adaptation methods, despite their effectiveness, are predominantly designed manually by human experts. This manual process is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and requires specialized domain knowledge, thereby hindering the swift advancement and deployment of new adaptation algorithms.

Concurrently, the advent of powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT has spurred a new paradigm in scientific research: the automation of various stages of the research lifecycle, including **automatic algorithm design (LLM4AD)**. This trend has seen LLMs applied to diverse tasks, from literature review and hypothesis generation to combinatorial optimization (e.g., FunSearch, EoH), continuous optimization (LLaMEA), neural architecture search, and even full-pipeline AutoML.

EvoVLMA bridges these two landscapes by applying the powerful code generation capabilities of LLMs to the specific challenge of designing training-free VLM adaptation algorithms. It proposes an automated, evolutionary approach to discover novel and efficient algorithms, thereby addressing the limitations of human-centric design in this critical area. By integrating LLMs with evolutionary computation, EvoVLMA contributes to the broader trend of automating scientific discovery and accelerating the development of advanced AI techniques, specifically within the context of multimodal model adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EvoVLMA stems from a recognized bottleneck in the development of Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation algorithms: the reliance on human experts. While existing adaptation methods (e.g., prompt tuning, adapters, Tip-Adapter, APE, GDA) have significantly advanced the application of VLMs, their manual design is inherently time-consuming, requires deep domain expertise, and slows down the pace of innovation. This manual, iterative process of conceptualization, implementation, and refinement of algorithms impedes rapid progress in VLM adaptation, especially for training-free methods which demand particularly ingenious designs.

Given this context, the **key objectives** of the EvoVLMA research are:

1. **Automate Algorithm Design for VLM Adaptation:** The overarching goal is to move beyond manual expert design and create a system that can automatically discover effective and efficient training-free adaptation algorithms for pre-trained VLMs.
2. **Leverage LLMs for Code Evolution:** To achieve automation, the project aims to exploit the powerful code generation capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) within an evolutionary framework. This involves instructing LLMs to generate and refine algorithm code based on feedback.
3. **Decompose and Conquer the Search Space:** Recognizing the vast and complex search space of possible algorithms, a specific objective is to simplify the problem. EvoVLMA aims to achieve this by identifying core, interdependent components of VLM adaptation (specifically, feature selection and logits computation) and optimizing them sequentially using a two-stage approach.
4. **Ensure Stability and Efficiency of the Search Process:** A practical objective is to develop robust technical infrastructure that can support the continuous generation, execution, and evaluation of LLM-generated code. This includes implementing low-precision code conversion for speed, web-based code execution for fault tolerance, and process monitoring for system stability.
5. **Demonstrate Superiority Over Manual Designs:** Empirically, a crucial objective is to show that the algorithms discovered by EvoVLMA can achieve comparable or, ideally, superior performance compared to state-of-the-art, manually-designed VLM adaptation algorithms.
6. **Pave the Way for Broader Automation:** Ultimately, the research aims to open new avenues for automating the optimization of adaptation algorithms for other pre-trained multimodal models and potentially inspire similar automation efforts across various domains of computer vision and AI.

In essence, the motivation is to accelerate scientific discovery in VLM adaptation by overcoming the limitations of human-centric algorithm design, thereby enabling faster development and deployment of robust and efficient multimodal AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvoVLMA proposes an **Evolutionary Vision-Language Model Adaptation** framework that transforms the challenge of designing VLM adaptation algorithms into an automated code searching task. Inspired by principles of evolutionary computation and recent advancements in LLM-based algorithm design (LLM4AD), EvoVLMA iteratively refines a population of algorithms using LLMs for generative operations.

The core methodology is structured around an **evolutionary algorithm loop**, as depicted in Figure 2 of the paper, comprising initialization, crossover, mutation, and selection. Each "individual" in the population is an algorithm, represented by both its natural language "thoughts" (description) and its corresponding Python "code."

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Evolutionary Algorithm

A key aspect of EvoVLMA's approach is a **two-stage optimization strategy** to manage the expansive search space. The authors identify feature selection and logits computation as two critical, interdependent functions in training-free VLM adaptation. Instead of attempting a joint search, which is highly complex for current LLMs, EvoVLMA sequentially optimizes these components. This involves fixing one component (e.g., using a default feature selection algorithm) while evolving the other (e.g., logits computation), and then potentially vice-versa in subsequent stages. The paper tests both 'fs→logit' (feature selection first, then logits computation) and 'logit→fs' (logits computation first, then feature selection) orders.

#### 3.2. Evolutionary Operators (LLM-Assisted)

1. **Initialization:** The process begins by creating an initial population of algorithms. To provide a strong starting point and prevent the LLM from generating suboptimal solutions in a vast, unstructured code space, existing, publicly available algorithms (e.g., Tip-Adapter, APE, GDA) are used. Their original Python code forms the initial "code," and their paper descriptions form the initial "thoughts." Importantly, additional parameters and hyper-parameters are introduced into these initial function definitions, offering the LLM greater flexibility to explore novel variations during evolution.
2. **Crossover:** This operator fosters exploration by combining ideas from multiple parent algorithms. Two parent algorithms are randomly selected from the current population, with a bias towards those with better fitness (higher rank). An LLM is then prompted to generate a *new* algorithm (both thoughts and code) that draws inspiration from these parents but is explicitly encouraged to be novel and structurally different.
3. **Mutation:** This operator drives refinement and localized exploration by probabilistically modifying an existing algorithm. A single algorithm is selected from the population, and the LLM is prompted to generate a modified version of its thoughts and code, again with an emphasis on novelty.

#### 3.3. LLM Prompt Design and Constraints

The success of LLM-assisted code generation hinges on effective prompt engineering. The authors design structured prompt templates for both crossover and mutation, which include:
- A clear **task description** (e.g., "select the best feature channels" or "devise a good function for computing classification logits").
- Examples of existing algorithms (for crossover).
- Detailed **design requirements**, specifying input/output parameters, their dimensions, and constraints.
- **Additional information** to guide LLM creativity and ensure practical utility. Key constraints enforced on the generated code include:
 - No random operations (for reproducibility).
 - Avoidance of deep nested loops (for runtime efficiency).
 - No learnable parameters (to ensure the generated algorithms remain "training-free").
 - Emphasis on code readability.

#### 3.4. Code Execution and Evaluation (Fitness Function)

To evaluate the quality of a generated algorithm, a **fitness value** is computed by executing its code on holdout image classification datasets (different from downstream validation datasets). The fitness is defined as `1 - arg max_θ ∈ S Acc(logits(D_tst, I, θ1), D_tst)`, where `Acc` is the classification accuracy, `logits` is the generated logits computation function, `I` comes from the generated feature selection function `FS`, and `θ` represents the hyper-parameters. This formulation ensures that the system searches for genuinely creative algorithms rather than merely tuning hyper-parameters.

#### 3.5. Technical Enhancements for Efficiency and Stability

To overcome practical challenges associated with executing LLM-generated code on GPUs, EvoVLMA implements several crucial engineering solutions:
- **Low-precision Code Conversion:** Generated Python code is automatically converted to support fp16 computation using a predefined lookup table for PyTorch functions. This significantly accelerates execution.
- **Web-Based Code Execution:** The core functions (`FS`, `logits`, `Acc`) are encapsulated as independent web services (`/feat_select`, `/logit_comput`, `/eval`). Each service runs in its own process, enhancing fault tolerance.
- **Process Monitoring:** A dedicated monitor continuously checks the health of these web services. If a service becomes unresponsive (e.g., due to a CUDA error in generated code that bypasses Python's `try-except` blocks), its process is immediately killed and restarted, ensuring continuous operation.
- **Parallel Execution:** Multiple instances of each web service are deployed (using Gunicorn), enabling parallel evaluation of different algorithms, which significantly accelerates the overall search process.

#### 3.6. Selection

After the crossover and mutation steps generate new candidate algorithms, the selection operator prunes the population. It selects the top-N individuals with the lowest fitness values (i.e., highest accuracy) to form the population for the next iteration, controlling population size and ensuring the retention of promising algorithms.

#### 3.7. Experimental Settings

Experiments are conducted using ResNet-50 as the default image encoder (with tests on ResNet-101 and ViT-B/16 for robustness). DeepSeek is primarily used as the LLM for code generation, with comparisons to other LLMs like Qwen, GPT-3.5, and GPT4o-mini. Downstream evaluation for few-shot recognition is performed on 11 public datasets, and domain generalization is tested using ImageNet, ImageNetV2, and ImageNet-Sketch. Hyper-parameter optimization for evaluation is performed using Optuna.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted for EvoVLMA yielded several key findings, demonstrating the efficacy and potential of LLM-driven algorithm design for VLM adaptation.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Analysis of LLM-Generated Code

- **Creativity and Basic Functionality:** The LLM successfully generated creative code snippets for both logits computation and feature selection. Examples include a logits computation algorithm that introduces an important feature-channel-weighted alignment term and new weighted logits. For feature selection, an algorithm was generated that considers weighted inter-class divergence, intra-class divergence, and a novel cosine similarity alignment term between visual and textual modalities. This showcases the LLM's ability to combine existing concepts and introduce new ideas, along with basic matrix manipulation skills.
- **Limitations:** The analysis also revealed that current LLMs can generate impractical, redundant, or even functionally incorrect code snippets, indicating that while powerful, their code generation capabilities still have room for improvement in terms of robustness and logical consistency with stated "thoughts."

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance in Few-Shot Classification

- **Outperformance of Manual Algorithms:** EvoVLMA-designed algorithms consistently outperformed their manually-designed counterparts (Tip-Adapter, APE, GDA) in most few-shot classification settings across 11 diverse datasets. The improvements were notable, with gains up to 1.91 percentage points in recognition accuracy (e.g., improving the APE algorithm in the 8-shot setting).
- **SOTA Enhancement:** Even for GDA, which is a state-of-the-art (SOTA) training-free method, EvoVLMA was able to find algorithms that achieved better results, particularly enhancing performance in low-shot settings where GDA traditionally struggles due to inaccurate covariance estimation.
- **Optimization Order:** The order of optimization (feature selection then logits computation, 'fs→logit', versus the reverse, 'logit→fs') showed a slight impact on downstream performance, with 'fs→logit' generally yielding marginally better results.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Performance in Domain Generalization

- **Improved Source Domain Performance:** Algorithms found by EvoVLMA consistently outperformed manual algorithms on the source domain (ImageNet).
- **Competitive Target Domain Performance:** On target domains (ImageNetV2, ImageNet-Sketch), the automatically designed algorithms maintained competitive performance and achieved a better average trade-off between source and target domain performance compared to manual methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Population-Based vs. Iterative Refinement:** EvoVLMA's population-based evolutionary algorithm significantly surpassed an individual-based iterative refinement strategy (commonly used in other LLM4AD works). This suggests that the evolutionary approach better balances exploration and exploitation, leading to higher accuracy and often more token-efficient searches.
- **Effectiveness of Feature Selection:** Optimizing both feature selection and logits computation proved more effective than optimizing logits computation alone. This underscores the importance of intelligent feature selection for VLM adaptation.
- **Two-Stage vs. Joint Searching:** The proposed two-stage sequential optimization strategy was validated as superior to a joint searching approach. Joint searching often failed to find algorithms better than the initialization, indicating that current LLMs struggle with the increased complexity of simultaneously optimizing multiple interdependent components.
- **Impact of Different LLMs:** DeepSeek demonstrated lower code execution error rates and performed better when initialized with APE and GDA, suggesting its strong ability to generate executable code. Other LLMs (Qwen, GPT-3.5, GPT4o-mini) showed competitive or superior results with Tip-Adapter initialization.
- **Importance of Initialization:** Initializing the evolutionary search with existing, well-performing algorithms (Tip-Adapter, APE, GDA) was crucial. Without initialization, a notable degeneration in accuracy occurred, highlighting current LLMs' limitations in designing complex algorithms entirely from scratch without domain-specific guidance.
- **Holdout Dataset Scale:** The number of holdout datasets used for algorithm search (2 vs. 5) had a minimal impact on downstream performance, suggesting that the search process is not overly sensitive to the scale of evaluation data.
- **Backbone Robustness:** Algorithms discovered using a ResNet-50 backbone successfully generalized to other visual backbones like ResNet-101 and ViT-B/16, demonstrating good robustness to shifts in visual feature distributions.
- **Iteration Number:** While additional iterations provided marginal performance gains, the most significant improvements occurred in the first iteration. A single-iteration configuration was adopted as a pragmatic balance between performance and computational efficiency.
- **Time and Token Cost:** GDA-based initialization incurred the highest computational overhead. The majority of the time was spent on code execution and evaluation on GPUs, not LLM token generation, indicating the need for scalable evaluation infrastructure.
- **Time Complexity of Searched Code:** Generated feature selection algorithms showed significant time efficiency improvements due to explicit prompt design that avoided computationally expensive loops. Logit computation exhibited only a marginal slowdown, which had negligible impact on overall inference time as image feature extraction remains the dominant bottleneck. This confirms that LLM-designed algorithms maintain computational efficiency while improving accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvoVLMA research marks a significant step towards the automation of algorithm design within the realm of Vision-Language Model (VLM) adaptation, carrying substantial implications and potential impact across several fronts:

1. **Advancement in Automatic Algorithm Design (LLM4AD):** EvoVLMA is a pioneering work that successfully applies LLM-based code evolution to the complex and practical problem of designing efficient, training-free VLM adaptation algorithms. It provides compelling evidence that LLMs, when integrated into a robust evolutionary framework, can discover algorithms that not only match but often surpass human-designed state-of-the-art methods. This significantly pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with LLM4AD in a real-world, high-impact AI domain.

2. **Accelerating VLM Research and Development:** By automating the traditionally time-consuming and expertise-intensive process of algorithm design, EvoVLMA has the potential to dramatically accelerate the pace of innovation in VLM adaptation. Researchers can rapidly explore a vast space of algorithmic possibilities, leading to faster discovery of more effective and efficient methods for deploying VLMs in various applications, particularly those with limited data or requiring on-device, gradient-free adaptation.

3. **Enhancing VLM Performance and Robustness:** The empirical results demonstrate that EvoVLMA-designed algorithms yield higher accuracy in few-shot classification and better generalization capabilities across different domains and visual backbones. This directly translates to more robust and performant VLM-based systems for real-world tasks. The ability to improve upon even highly optimized manual designs (like GDA) in challenging scenarios (e.g., low-shot learning) is a testament to the framework's power.

4. **Methodological Innovations for LLM-Driven Code Search:** The paper introduces several crucial methodological and engineering contributions that address practical challenges in LLM-driven code evolution:
 - **Two-Stage Decomposition:** The effective "divide-and-conquer" strategy for tackling a large search space by sequentially optimizing interdependent components (feature selection and logits computation) offers a valuable blueprint for other complex algorithm design tasks.
 - **Robust Execution Environment:** The integration of low-precision code conversion, web-based code execution with parallel processing, and an intelligent process monitor provides a robust and efficient infrastructure that is essential for practical, large-scale LLM-based code evaluation. These solutions mitigate common issues like CUDA errors and ensure continuous operation.

5. **Broader Inspirations for Computer Vision and AI:** The success of EvoVLMA can inspire similar automation efforts in other areas of computer vision and AI that heavily rely on manual algorithm design. Many tasks, from optimization heuristics to data augmentation strategies or model architectures, could potentially benefit from an LLM-driven evolutionary approach. This framework could usher in a new era of "AI designing AI," where intelligent systems assist in the very creation of new algorithms.

6. **Highlighting LLM Strengths and Limitations:** While showcasing the immense potential of LLMs, the research also soberly acknowledges their current limitations (e.g., dependence on good initialization, occasional generation of impractical or incorrect code, challenges with joint multi-function optimization). These insights are crucial for guiding future research in developing more capable and reliable LLM-based algorithm design systems.

In conclusion, EvoVLMA represents a significant contribution to the field by demonstrating a viable and effective pathway for automating VLM adaptation algorithm design. Its impact is likely to be felt not only in the improved performance of multimodal AI systems but also in the broader methodological shift towards LLM-assisted scientific discovery, paving the way for more autonomous and efficient AI research.
