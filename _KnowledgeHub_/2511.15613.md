# When to Think and When to Look: Uncertainty-Guided Lookback

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic analysis quantifies the impact of test-time "thinking" in large vision-language models (LVLMs) on visual reasoning across different model sizes and task types. The work introduces an uncertainty-guided lookback decoding strategy that achieves accuracy improvements of 2-6.5 points with a 35-45% reduction in token usage across benchmarks including MMMUval, MathVista, and MMBench.

## Problem

- A lack of systematic understanding exists regarding how test-time 'thinking' truly influences visual reasoning in LVLMs, with current practices often assuming a monotonic benefit.
- The optimal trade-offs between 'breadth' (sampling more reasoning paths) and 'depth' (engaging intensive reasoning modes) for perception-heavy tasks in LVLMs remain underexplored.
- LVLM decoding strategies can lead to inefficient 'long-wrong' reasoning chains and ungrounded textual speculation, similar to issues observed in text-only chain-of-thought, requiring adaptive control.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic, large-scale comparison of ==instruct== vs. ==thinking== modes across 10 state-of-the-art LVLM variants from InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL families on the MMMUval benchmark.
- Developed a training-free, model-agnostic **Uncertainty-Guided Lookback** decoding strategy using a ==token-level visual sensitivity probe== to identify model uncertainty and visual grounding.
- Implemented an adaptive mechanism that, when a ==pause phrase== indicating uncertainty is detected, inserts a ==lookback template== to prompt the model to re-examine visual details and guide reasoning.

## Results

- Increased Pass@1 accuracy on MMMUval by **+2.3 points** for Qwen3-VL 4B (up to **+6.5 points** in specialist domains) while reducing token usage by **~40%**.
- Consistent accuracy improvements of **+2 to +3 absolute points** across 8B and 32B models on MMMUval, with roughly a **one-third reduction in token usage**.
- Gains generalized: **+4 to +6 points** on MathVista and MathVision for 4B and 8B models, and consistent improvements on MMBench, MMStar, and InternVL3.5-Think.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both increasing reasoning path breadth (sampling) and depth (explicit thinking modes) consistently improve LVLM accuracy, with sampling being particularly beneficial for smaller models and thinking for reasoning-centric tasks.
> - 'More thinking' is not always efficient; it can double token usage for smaller models on easy tasks without proportional accuracy gains, and 'long-wrong' trajectories represent a notable inefficiency in recognition-heavy domains.
> - Token-level visual sensitivity can be effectively probed to identify textual cues (pause phrases) that signal model uncertainty or drift from visual grounding, providing a basis for adaptive decoding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are increasingly positioned as general-purpose visual assistants, capable of complex tasks such as interpreting charts, solving exam questions, and reasoning about diagrams. However, despite their impressive capabilities, evaluations such as LMMs-Eval highlight that current LVLMs exhibit brittleness across various domains and task formats. This situation motivates the development of more principled methods for allocating test-time computational resources.

On the language side, "thinking" — which encompasses chain-of-thought (CoT) decoding, self-consistency, and reflection-style prompting — has been established as a crucial element for complex reasoning in large language models (LLMs), yielding substantial performance improvements on text-only benchmarks. Extending these techniques to multimodal settings, however, presents additional challenges. Recent studies indicate that LVLMs inherit LLM failure modes and introduce new cross-modal issues, such notably over-reliance on textual priors and visual hallucination. Some LVLM families, such as Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3, have demonstrated improved visual grounding with increased model capacity.

Efforts in visual CoT have attempted to integrate visual intermediates, with methods like VCoT, Visual Sketchpad, and MathCanvas incorporating drawing capabilities or scratchpads for geometric and spatial reasoning. These approaches typically necessitate additional supervision or specialized tooling. Concurrently, several LVLM families, including Qwen-VL and InternVL3/3.5, have introduced explicit "thinking" modes, reporting strong results on benchmarks like MMMU.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing a gap: the absence of a systematic analysis of how test-time thinking specifically influences visual reasoning in LVLMs. While "thinking" is often presumed beneficial, its effects across model sizes, sampling budgets, and task categories, particularly in terms of trade-offs between computational breadth (more reasoning paths) and depth (stronger reasoning modes), remain underexplored. Furthermore, prior work on text-only CoT suggests that blindly elongating reasoning chains can be detrimental, highlighting a need for adaptive decoding strategies in LVLMs that respond to visual grounding and uncertainty. The research provides a controlled, large-scale comparison and introduces a training-free adaptive decoding strategy to address these identified limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while "thinking" (generating explicit intermediate reasoning chains) has improved performance in both LLMs and LVLMs, there is a lack of systematic understanding of its specific impact on visual reasoning. The authors identify that current practices often assume a monotonic benefit from more thinking, despite evidence of "long-wrong" trajectories where models produce extended, incorrect reasoning chains that may disregard visual input.

The paper articulates three intertwined questions that serve as its core objectives:

1. **When does test-time thinking help visual reasoning?** The motivation here is to move beyond the general assumption of benefit and provide a systematic comparison between standard "instruct" mode and "thinking" modes across various model sizes, sampling budgets, and task categories. This aims to delineate specific conditions under which thinking is advantageous.
2. **How should we trade off breadth versus depth of thinking?** This objective seeks to understand the optimal allocation of test-time computational resources. It explores whether to prioritize sampling a wider array of reasoning paths (breadth) or to engage more intensive, deeper reasoning modes (depth) for perception-heavy tasks. The underlying motivation is to identify efficient strategies for resource utilization.
3. **Can we control thinking for better perception tasks?** Drawing from observations in text-only CoT that unconstrained chain elongation can be inefficient or detrimental, this objective aims to develop LVLM decoding strategies that can adaptively respond to signals of visual grounding and uncertainty. The motivation is to avoid wasteful computation and improve the reliability of reasoning by selectively activating thinking processes.

In pursuing these objectives, the research also aims to:
- Provide a systematic analysis of visual thinking in state-of-the-art LVLMs, distinguishing between the effects of breadth and depth.
- Characterize the "token economy" of visual reasoning, illustrating how language capacity and task difficulty influence the cost and utility of thinking, and identifying scenarios where "long-wrong" versus "quiet-wrong" failures are prevalent.
- Introduce a training-free, model-agnostic decoding strategy that enhances accuracy while potentially reducing the computational overhead associated with thinking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-phase approach: a systematic analysis of existing LVLM thinking behaviors, followed by the development and evaluation of a novel adaptive decoding strategy.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Visual Thinking in LVLMs

- **Models:** The study focuses on 10 variants from two leading open-source LVLM families: InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL. These families were selected due to their reported strong visual reasoning capabilities and explicit "thinking" modes. Models include 4B, 8B, and 32B parameter versions, allowing for controlled comparisons across vision encoder architectures (InternViT vs. Qwen3VLVision), connector architectures (MLP vs. DeepStack), and reasoning modes (instruct vs. thinking/CoT).
- **Dataset:** MMMUval, a diverse multimodal understanding and reasoning benchmark with 30 categories, served as the primary analysis benchmark.
- **Decoding Strategies:**
 - **Multiple-pass decoding:** Each question was evaluated with 10 sampled reasoning paths, following practices in multi-step reasoning.
 - **Temperature and top_p:** Standard settings from the MMMUval leaderboard were used, with only random seeds varied.
 - **Token Budgets:** Substantially larger output-token budgets were allocated (16,384 tokens for Instruct, 32,768 for Reasoning) compared to common evaluators, to minimize truncation and allow for fully articulated reasoning chains. A budget-matched control setting (Thinking truncated to 16,384 tokens) was also used to ensure fairness.
 - **"Breadth" vs. "Depth":** Breadth refers to increasing the number of sampled trajectories (Pass@k), while depth refers to enabling the model's native reasoning mode (e.g., using Qwen3-VL's `<think>` token or InternVL3.5's official reasoning prompt).
- **Analytical Techniques:**
 - **Pass@k accuracy curves:** Used to quantify how performance scales with additional samples.
 - **Mean pass accuracy boxplots:** Summarized overall accuracy and variability across models and reasoning modes.
 - **Category-level Z-scores:** Heatmaps displayed z-scored accuracy across MMMUval disciplines for both Depth (mean accuracy over 10 passes, reflecting single-sample reliability) and Breadth (Pass@10 aggregated accuracy).
 - **Token footprint analysis:** Boxplots illustrated the distribution of total generated tokens per question, segmented by correctness, model family, size, and difficulty (Easy/Medium/Hard).

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Guided Lookback Decoding Strategy

- **Motivation:** Address the observation that increased thinking does not always lead to better outcomes, particularly "long-wrong" trajectories.
- **Token-Level Visual Sensitivity Probe:**
 - **Decomposition:** Each decoded thinking trace was segmented into word-level steps.
 - **Contextual Evaluation:** For each step, the model's perplexity was evaluated under three visual contexts: the original real image (R), a visually mismatched noise image (N), and no image (∅).
 - **Difference Scores:**
 - `Δ_content(s) = PPL_R(s) - PPL_N(s)`: Quantifies how much the *content* of the correct image helps in predicting the next token. A more negative value indicates stronger reliance on actual visual content.
 - `Δ_presence(s) = PPL_N(s) - PPL_∅(s)`: Isolates the effect of merely *having* any image present, indicating sensitivity to visual tokens regardless of semantic content.
 - **Phrase Mining (Offline):**
 - **Pause phrases (P):** Lexical n-grams consistently co-occurring with steps where `|Δ_presence(s)|` is large but `|Δ_content(s)|` is small (indicating general visual sensitivity but lack of content-specific grounding, suggesting uncertainty or drift). Augmented with reflection-style uncertainty markers.
 - **Lookback templates (L):** Multi-token templates consistently co-occurring with highly negative `Δ_content(s)` in correctly solved examples (indicating successful, image-dependent reasoning). These explicitly prompt re-examination of visual details.
- **Lookback-When-Uncertain Decoding (Online):**
 - **Trigger Mechanism:** During autoregressive generation, if the recently generated context contains a pause phrase from P, and the model is still in the thinking phase (not generating a final answer), a lookback template from L is appended to the trace.
 - **Constraints:** To prevent repetitive triggers, at most one lookback is allowed within a defined window of thinking tokens, and triggers are disabled once the final answer phase commences.
 - **Efficiency:** The heavy computation for probe construction and phrase mining is done offline. At inference, the controller uses efficient n-gram matching and occasional prompt insertion, compatible with token-by-token streaming.
- **Parallel Lookback Sampling (Optional):**
 - When a lookback is triggered, M short continuations (of horizon H) are sampled after injecting the lookback phrase.
 - An aggregate visual helpfulness score `V^(m)` is computed for each branch, based on `Δ_content(s)`.
 - The branch with the maximal `V^(m)` is selected, and decoding continues from its state, providing a mechanism for exploring visually grounded alternatives.
- **Baselines:** The proposed method was compared against three text-only adaptive reasoning methods adapted for LVLMs: DEER (Dynamic Early Exit in Reasoning), DeepConf (Deep Think with Confidence), and REFRAIN (discriminator-based early-stopping).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Beyond MMMUval, the method was tested for generality on MMBench and MMStar (broad multimodal capabilities) and MathVista, MathVision, and MathVerse (math-focused visual reasoning datasets).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding LVLM behavior and the effectiveness of the proposed decoding strategy.

#### 4.1. Analysis of Visual Thinking

- **Thinking in Breadth:** Increasing the number of sampled reasoning paths (Pass@k) consistently improved accuracy across all models. Gains were steepest from k=2 to k=6, then tapered off. Smaller models showed disproportionately larger benefits from increased sampling, suggesting that sampling can partially compensate for lower model capacity. In some regimes, a smaller, thinking-enabled model could surpass a larger instruct-only model with sufficient sampling.
- **Thinking in Depth:** Enabling explicit "thinking" modes (depth) consistently boosted the accuracy of all models compared to their instruct-only counterparts. These gains were most significant for smaller models and diminished as model capacity increased. Thinking improved the quality of individual samples and generally led to more stable reasoning traces with reduced variance.
- **Category-Specific Effects:**
 - **Reasoning-centric domains** (e.g., Physics, Math, Engineering, Chemistry, Biology, Clinical/Diagnostics, Finance) benefited substantially from thinking, particularly for smaller and mid-sized models. Thinking enhanced single-sample reliability in these areas.
 - **Recognition-centric categories** (e.g., Literature, History, Art/Art Theory, Music, Sociology) often showed comparable or even superior performance with concise instruct decoding, especially for larger models. In these cases, longer reasoning chains were observed to introduce noise that additional sampling did not effectively mitigate.
- **Token Economy:**
 - Total token usage was primarily influenced by the language model's capacity rather than the visual encoder or connector architecture. Larger models consistently produced shorter and more focused responses.
 - Enabling "thinking" roughly doubled the median token count on easy questions for 4B and 8B models, even when accuracy gains were modest, indicating a higher computational overhead for deliberation in these contexts. The overhead was less wasteful on hard questions.
 - The study identified "budget frontiers," where trade-offs could be made between model capacity, task difficulty, and reasoning mode to achieve similar token footprints. The prevalence of "long-wrong" trajectories, where incorrect answers consumed significant tokens, highlighted inefficiency.

#### 4.2. Results of Uncertainty-Guided Lookback

- **Controller Necessity:** Ablation studies confirmed that uncertainty-guided triggering was superior to periodic lookback insertion. Periodic insertion frequently led to worse performance than even original thinking, suggesting that the precise timing and context of lookback insertion are critical for maintaining semantic continuity and efficacy.
- **Performance on MMMUval:** The proposed method consistently improved both accuracy (Pass@1) and efficiency (reduced token usage) across all Qwen3-VL model sizes.
 - For the 4B model, the lookback variant increased Pass@1 accuracy by +2.3 points while reducing token usage by approximately 40%. With additional sampling, accuracy further improved to +2.7 points with a similar token reduction.
 - At 8B and 32B, overall gains of approximately +2 to +3 absolute points were observed, coupled with roughly a one-third reduction in token usage.
 - Notably, improvements were more pronounced in specialist domains like Diagnostics and Laboratory Medicine or Energy and Power, where Pass@1 could increase by up to +5–6.5 points with 35–40% fewer tokens.
 - These results suggest that the method shifted the Pareto frontier on MMMUval, achieving higher accuracy for a given token budget or requiring fewer tokens for a target accuracy.
- **Cross-Benchmark Generality:**
 - The gains generalized across MMBench and MMStar, demonstrating consistent, albeit smaller, accuracy improvements (a few absolute points) on broad multimodal understanding tasks.
 - On math-focused benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse), the benefits were more substantial, with gains of approximately +4 to +6 points at 4B and 8B on MathVista and MathVision.
 - Transferability was also verified on InternVL3.5-Think models, showing similar gains, indicating the method is not specific to the Qwen3-VL family.
- **Variant Comparison:** The lookback strategy alone provided a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off. The optional parallel lookback sampling further enhanced performance, particularly on math-heavy and difficult MMMUval categories, with a modest increase in token cost relative to lookback alone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic analysis of "thinking" in LVLMs and introduces an adaptive decoding strategy that addresses identified inefficiencies. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Understanding of LVLM Reasoning:** The study provides the first systematic, large-scale analysis of how test-time thinking affects visual reasoning in LVLMs. It clarifies that "more thinking" is not always beneficial, especially in easy or recognition-heavy tasks where it can lead to inefficient "long-wrong" trajectories. This understanding is crucial for the principled development and deployment of LVLMs.
- **Improved Resource Efficiency:** By characterizing the "token economy" and identifying regimes where thinking is wasteful, the research offers insights into optimizing computational resource allocation. The proposed uncertainty-guided lookback method significantly reduces token usage (by 35-45%) while simultaneously improving accuracy, demonstrating a more efficient trade-off between computation and performance. This can lead to lower operational costs and faster inference for LVLM applications.
- **Robustness and Accuracy Gains:** The adaptive decoding strategy, by selectively triggering image-focused prompts when reasoning drifts or uncertainty is detected, enhances visual grounding. This leads to consistent accuracy improvements across a diverse range of benchmarks, including broad multimodal tasks and specialized visual mathematical reasoning. The gains are particularly notable in domains where naive thinking previously underperformed, indicating improved reliability.
- **Practical and Generalizable Solution:** The uncertainty-guided lookback is a training-free and model-agnostic decoding strategy. Its lightweight implementation, based on offline phrase mining and online n-gram matching, makes it compatible with token-by-token streaming and a "plug-and-play" enhancement for existing LVLMs. Its demonstrated transferability across different model families (Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5) and various benchmarks underscores its broad applicability.
- **Informed LVLM Design and Evaluation:** The findings provide empirical guidance for future LVLM architectural design and evaluation methodologies. By highlighting the distinct impacts of "breadth" and "depth" in thinking, and the importance of adaptive control, the work suggests avenues for building more intelligent and context-aware reasoning capabilities into multimodal models.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current LVLMs:** The method directly tackles issues like over-reliance on text priors and ungrounded textual speculation, which are known failure modes in current LVLMs, by actively re-anchoring reasoning to visual information.

While the current implementation requires token-level log-probabilities for probe construction, limiting applicability to closed-source models without such access, the research opens avenues for further work on external, log-probability-free uncertainty estimation or alternative methods for grounding visual reasoning. Overall, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent, efficient, and reliable LVLM systems.
