# Robust-WAM: Bridging Generative Pretraining and Semantic Foresight in World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Robust-WAM introduces a post-training method to enhance the robustness of video-generation-based World-Action Models (WAMs) to visual out-of-distribution conditions. It achieves this by aligning the action stream with appearance-invariant semantic features of future frames, improving average OOD success rates on LIBERO-Plus by up to 9.2% and reducing the real-robot InD-to-OOD performance gap from 22.7 to 2.7 points.

## Problem

- Existing VAE-based World-Action Models (WAMs) leverage powerful dynamics priors from large-scale video generation models but are fragile to visual out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions due to their pixel-level reconstruction objective.
- Semantic-latent WAMs are robust to appearance shifts but cannot directly utilize the benefits of large-scale video generative model pretraining without costly re-pretraining.
- A fundamental trade-off exists between leveraging strong dynamics priors and achieving robustness to visual variations in current WAM architectures.

## Method

- **Robust-WAM** introduces learnable ==semantic foresight query tokens== into the action stream of existing VAE-based WAMs during post-training.
- It aligns the output hidden states of these query tokens with appearance-invariant ==CLS embeddings from a frozen DINOv3== semantic encoder, representing ground-truth future frames.
- ==Shared positional encodings== establish precise temporal correspondence between query tokens and the future action steps they describe.

## Results

- Robust-WAM improved OOD performance for various WAM baselines on LIBERO-Plus, increasing FastWAM's success rate by **9.2%** (to **58.9%**) and GE-Act's by **2.9%** (to **80.9%**), while preserving in-distribution performance.
- It achieved superior OOD generalization compared to other VLA and semantic-latent WAMs, securing the highest LIBERO-Plus success rate (**80.9%** with GE-Act) and randomized RoboTwin success rate (**34.4%** with LingBot-VA).
- On a real robot, Robust-WAM reduced the InD-to-OOD performance gap for GE-Act under colored illumination from **22.7 points to 2.7 points**, improving average OOD success from **57.3% to 80.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The method successfully bridges the gap between strong dynamics priors from VAE-based video pretraining and OOD robustness by integrating appearance-invariant semantic foresight into the action stream.
> - Aligning with DINOv3 CLS embeddings is most effective for semantic foresight, indicating that high-level, class-token-based semantic summaries are critical for robustness over pixel or patch-level features.
> - Precise temporal grounding of future queries, achieved through shared positional encodings and future-frame targets, is crucial for robust action prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on World-Action Models (WAMs). WAMs represent a paradigm that leverages the dynamics priors learned from large-scale video generation models (VGMs) to inform robot control and action generation. This approach benefits from the extensive knowledge encoded in VGMs about how objects move and interact in various environments.

A central challenge in this landscape is a trade-off between the fidelity of learned dynamics and robustness to visual variations. Mainstream WAMs often adapt VGMs trained in a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) latent space. While VAEs excel at pixel-level reconstruction and capture rich appearance details, this objective can make action prediction fragile when confronted with visual out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions such as changes in illumination, texture, or background. The VAE latent space, optimized for visual fidelity, dedicates a significant portion of its representation to appearance details, which are often irrelevant for action execution but can cause instability under visual shifts.

In response to this fragility, an alternative line of research has emerged, constructing WAMs in semantic latent spaces, such as those derived from models like DINO or V-JEPA. These semantic-latent models are inherently more robust to appearance shifts because their representations focus on high-level scene content, like object identity and spatial arrangement, rather than fine-grained visual details. However, these models typically cannot leverage the pre-existing, large-scale dynamics priors learned by VAE-based VGMs, as these priors are embedded within the VAE space. Consequently, semantic-latent WAMs often require costly re-pretraining on extensive video datasets to acquire comparable dynamics knowledge, effectively abandoning the benefits of large-scale VGM pretraining.

This paper positions Robust-WAM as a method designed to bridge this gap. It aims to retain the advantages of large-scale VAE-based VGM pretraining while simultaneously improving robustness to visual OOD conditions. By introducing a semantic alignment objective into the action stream of existing VAE-based WAMs, the work seeks to enable robots to leverage strong dynamics priors without being overly sensitive to appearance changes. This approach is distinct from rebuilding the entire latent space and also from other representation alignment methods in robot learning that focus on spatial structure or general future observation embeddings in standalone policies. Robust-WAM integrates semantic foresight *within* a pretrained video-generation WAM and explicitly couples future queries with corresponding action steps, providing precise temporal grounding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general post-training method, named Robust-WAM, that enhances the robustness of video-generation-based World-Action Models (WAMs) to visual out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions without compromising their in-distribution (InD) performance or requiring costly re-pretraining.

The motivation stems from a fundamental dilemma identified in current WAM architectures:
- **Dilemma of VAE-based WAMs**: These models inherit powerful dynamics priors from large-scale pretrained video generation models (VGMs), which are typically trained in a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) latent space. This VAE space is optimized for pixel reconstruction, leading to representations that encode fine appearance details (e.g., texture, illumination). While beneficial for realistic video generation, this appearance bias makes the subsequent action prediction stream fragile and susceptible to disruption under visual shifts (e.g., photometric or hue changes) that do not alter the underlying task. This fragility limits their real-world applicability where visual conditions vary.
- **Dilemma of Semantic-latent WAMs**: An alternative approach involves building WAMs in a semantic latent space (e.g., DINO, V-JEPA) which is more robust to appearance changes. However, these models cannot directly leverage the extensive dynamics priors acquired through large-scale VGM pretraining, as these priors exist specifically in the VAE latent space. Re-establishing such dynamics knowledge in a new semantic space necessitates expensive pretraining on large-scale video data, negating the efficiency benefits of leveraging existing VGMs.

Robust-WAM is motivated to resolve this trade-off by achieving both strengths simultaneously: retaining the strong dynamics priors from large-scale VAE-based VGM pretraining *and* grounding actions in appearance-invariant dynamics that remain reliable under visual OOD conditions. This aims to create more generalizable and robust robotic policies for real-world deployment.

Specifically, the objectives are to:
- Preserve the VAE-based generative path of existing WAMs to retain large-scale VGM pretraining benefits.
- Introduce a lightweight semantic foresight alignment objective into the action stream to correct for appearance bias directly in the action representation.
- Employ learnable query tokens to bring future-scene semantics into the action stream.
- Align the output hidden states of these query tokens with appearance-invariant semantic features of future ground-truth frames, using DINOv3 CLS embeddings.
- Establish temporal correspondence between query tokens and future steps using shared positional encodings.
- Demonstrate that this approach consistently improves OOD generalization capabilities of various WAM baselines without sacrificing InD performance on simulation benchmarks and a real-robot setup.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Robust-WAM is proposed as a general post-training method designed to enhance the robustness of existing video-generation-based WAMs. The core approach involves augmenting the action stream of a WAM with learnable query tokens and aligning their output with semantic representations of future ground-truth frames.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: World-Action Models (WAMs)

The method builds upon existing WAM architectures, which typically couple a pretrained Video Generation Model (VGM) with an action Diffusion Transformer (DiT). A WAM jointly denoises two streams:
- **Video Stream**: Predicts VAE latents of T<sub>f</sub> future frames (x), derived from the current observation (o<sub>t</sub>) and a language instruction (ℓ). The VAE encoder (E) is frozen, and the video DiT (v<sub>v</sub>θ) regresses the velocity to denoise corrupted latents back to clean ones using a flow matching objective (L<sub>video</sub>).
- **Action Stream**: Predicts an action chunk (a) for the next H control steps. This chunk is represented as L action tokens (h<sub>a</sub>), which are then processed by an action DiT (v<sub>a</sub>θ). The action DiT attends to the hidden states of the video stream (H<sub>v</sub>) and also uses a flow matching objective (L<sub>act</sub>).
The overall WAM objective (L<sub>WAM</sub>) is a weighted sum of L<sub>video</sub> and L<sub>act</sub>. This structure allows action prediction to inherit dynamics priors from the pretrained VGM through shared features.

#### 3.2. Semantic Foresight Alignment

Robust-WAM introduces a representation alignment objective into the action stream while keeping the WAM's VAE tokenization, video-generation objective, and video-action architecture largely intact.

- **Per-Frame Semantic Query Tokens**: K learnable query tokens (q ∈ R<sup>K×d</sup>) are introduced. K is determined by the number of future time steps (T<sub>f</sub>) and camera views (C) (K = T<sub>f</sub> × C). These query tokens are prepended to the noised action tokens, forming an augmented sequence h = [q; h<sub>a</sub>] ∈ R<sup>(K+L)×d</sup>.
- **Shared Positional Encoding (PE)**: To establish temporal correspondence, each query token is assigned the positional encoding (PE) of the action step it describes. The action step (τ<sub>j</sub>) corresponding to future frame j (observation o<sub>t+j∆</sub>) is calculated as min(j∆, L), where L is the action horizon. The positional encoding function Φ(·, p) is applied to the query tokens, reusing the PEs from the WAM's existing action tokens. For action tokens, their original indices (n-K) are used for PE. This ensures that the model understands how far into the future each query is "looking."
- **Semantic Foresight Targets**: For each training sample, the CLS embeddings of a frozen DINOv3 encoder (Siméoni et al., 2025) are extracted from the T<sub>f</sub> ground-truth future frames for each of the C camera views. These embeddings, denoted z* ∈ R<sup>K×d<sub>z</sub></sup>, serve as appearance-invariant summaries of scene content (e.g., objects, spatial arrangement) while disregarding texture and illumination. The DINOv3 ViT-B/16 CLS embedding is used, with a dimension of d<sub>z</sub>=768. These targets are extracted offline once.
- **Alignment Objective**: After the action DiT processes the augmented sequence, a linear head g: R<sup>d</sup> → R<sup>d<sub>z</sub></sup> maps the output hidden states of the K query tokens (h̄<sub>1:K</sub>) to the target space. A per-query cosine alignment loss (L<sub>align</sub>) is then computed between g(h̄<sub>k</sub>) and the corresponding ground-truth DINOv3 CLS embeddings z*<sub>k</sub>:
 L<sub>align</sub> = (1/K) * Σ<sub>k=1..K</sub> (1 - cos(g(h̄<sub>k</sub>), z*<sub>k</sub>))
- **Overall Objective**: This alignment loss is added to the original WAM objective: L = L<sub>WAM</sub> + λ<sub>align</sub> L<sub>align</sub>, where λ<sub>align</sub> is a hyperparameter (set to 0.1).

**Inference**: During inference, the DINOv3 teacher and the linear alignment head (g) are removed. Only the K learnable query tokens remain as inputs to the action stream. This ensures no additional computational cost at inference while allowing the action stream to still benefit from the appearance-invariant foresight learned during training.

#### 3.3. Integration with Different Architectures

Robust-WAM is designed to be plug-and-play across various WAM architectures:
- **Action-Expert WAMs (MoT)**: For Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) WAMs (e.g., GE-Act, FastWAM), the query tokens are directly prepended to the action expert's token sequence. They participate in the expert's self-attention and cross-attend to the video tower in the same manner as the original action tokens.
- **Unified WAMs**: For unified video-action WAMs (e.g., LingBot-VA) that interleave video and action tokens in a shared flex-attention sequence, the queries are inserted into the action segment. They are assigned the same sequence, frame, and noise IDs as their corresponding action tokens, inheriting the attention pattern and preserving autoregressive ordering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Robust-WAM across various WAMs, OOD generalization benchmarks, and a real-robot setup.

#### 4.1. OOD Robustness on LIBERO-Plus (Q1)

- **Setup**: Robust-WAM was applied to two action-expert WAMs, FastWAM and GE-Act. Policies were trained on standard LIBERO suites and evaluated on both clean LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, which includes perturbed tasks across seven axes (camera, lighting, background, object layout, robot initial state, language, sensor noise).
- **Results**:
 - Robust-WAM consistently improved OOD performance on LIBERO-Plus for both baselines. FastWAM's success rate increased from 49.7% to 58.9% (+9.2 points), and GE-Act's from 78.0% to 80.9% (+2.9 points).
 - Notably, GE-Act with Robust-WAM achieved the highest LIBERO-Plus performance (80.9%) among all compared methods, including several VLA (Vision-Language-Action) baselines and the semantic-latent WAM LDA-1B.
 - In-distribution (InD) performance on clean LIBERO also slightly increased or was preserved: FastWAM improved from 97.6% to 97.9% (+0.3), and GE-Act from 96.5% to 97.3% (+0.8). This indicates that OOD improvements did not come at the expense of InD performance.
 - Compared to LDA-1B (a semantic-latent WAM without large-scale VGM pretraining), which achieved 90.6% on clean LIBERO and 45.5% on LIBERO-Plus, Robust-WAM with GE-Act achieved significantly higher results (97.3% and 80.9%, respectively), suggesting the importance of leveraging large-scale video-generation pretraining.
 - Per-axis analysis showed gains across most perturbation axes. For FastWAM, significant gains were observed under sensor noise (+18.9), camera perturbations (+12.4), lighting changes (+11.3), and background changes (+10.3). For GE-Act, improvements were observed in six of seven axes, led by robot initial state (+4.4) and language (+3.5). Improvements under layout and camera perturbations suggested enhanced robustness to geometric and viewpoint variations.

#### 4.2. Generalization Across Different WAM Architectures (Q2)

- **Setup**: Robust-WAM was applied to LingBot-VA, a unified autoregressive WAM based on Wan2.2. Training used RoboTwin clean split demonstrations, and evaluation was on both clean and randomized splits (unseen object poses, textures, lighting).
- **Results**:
 - Robust-WAM improved LingBot-VA's randomized success rate from 29.8% to 34.4% (+4.6 points), while maintaining its 81.2% clean success rate.
 - LingBot-VA with Robust-WAM achieved the highest randomized success rate (34.4%) among all compared methods, including other VLA and WAM baselines.
 - Again, LDA-1B (semantic-latent, no VGM pretraining) performed substantially lower (53.0% clean, 12.3% random) than LingBot-VA with Robust-WAM, reinforcing the benefits of integrating semantic foresight with large-scale VGM priors.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Q3)

- **Setup**: Ablations were conducted on LIBERO-Plus using FastWAM. Variants explored different alignment targets (DINOv3 CLS, DINOv3 Patch, DepthAnything3 (DA3), combined DINOv3 CLS + DA3) and query designs (with/without temporal positional encoding, current-frame vs. future-frame target).
- **Results**:
 - **Alignment Target**: DINOv3 CLS achieved the strongest overall performance, improving the baseline by +9.2 points to 58.9%. DINOv3 Patch (+7.3 points) and DA3 (+7.1 points) also provided substantial gains but were lower than DINOv3 CLS. Combining DINOv3 CLS and DA3 targets resulted in lower performance (56.5%) than DINOv3 CLS alone, suggesting that simple combination did not yield complementary benefits.
 - **Query Design**: The full temporal query design (using future-frame targets with temporal PE) achieved 58.9%. Variants without temporal PE (55.0%, +5.3 points over baseline) and with current-frame targets (52.7%, +3.0 points over baseline) performed worse. This indicates the importance of both future-step indexing and future semantic targets for robust performance.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment (Q4)

- **Setup**: Experiments were conducted on a Franka Research 3 robot across three tabletop tasks (Carrot→Bread, Kiwi→Basket, Stack Cups). Policies were trained under standard illumination and evaluated under standard, purple OOD, and cyan OOD illumination conditions. Baselines were FastWAM and GE-Act; Robust-WAM was built on GE-Act.
- **Results**:
 - Both FastWAM and GE-Act baselines showed sharp degradation under unseen colored illuminations. FastWAM's average success rate dropped from 64.0% to 26.7% (a 37.3-point gap), and GE-Act's from 80.0% to 57.3% (a 22.7-point gap).
 - Robust-WAM, applied to GE-Act, consistently improved OOD performance. It raised the average OOD success rate from 57.3% to 80.0% and dramatically reduced the InD-to-OOD performance gap from 22.7 points to 2.7 points.
 - InD performance was preserved (82.7% with Robust-WAM vs. 80.0% for GE-Act baseline).
 - Improvements were consistent across individual tasks, showing gains of 20 to 28 points in OOD success rate. These results suggest that semantic foresight alignment effectively reduces sensitivity to appearance shifts in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Robust-WAM as a method that addresses a notable limitation in World-Action Models (WAMs): the trade-off between leveraging large-scale video generative pretraining and achieving robustness to visual out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions. The paper demonstrates a solution that allows WAMs to retain the benefits of powerful dynamics priors learned from extensive VAE-based video pretraining while simultaneously enhancing their resilience to changes in appearance.

The significance of Robust-WAM lies in several aspects:
- **Bridging a Fundamental Dilemma**: The research successfully integrates appearance-invariant semantic foresight into VAE-based WAMs without requiring a complete overhaul or costly re-pretraining. This bypasses the need to choose between strong dynamics priors and OOD robustness, offering a path to achieve both.
- **Improved OOD Generalization**: The consistent performance gains observed across multiple simulation benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin clean→random) and a real-world robot setup under various visual shifts (lighting, background, camera viewpoint, object layout, noise) indicate that Robust-WAM can lead to more generalizable robot policies. This is crucial for deploying robotic systems in diverse and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Preservation of In-Distribution Performance**: The method achieves OOD robustness without sacrificing performance on in-distribution tasks, which is a desirable characteristic for practical applications.
- **General Applicability (Plug-and-Play)**: Robust-WAM is described as a general post-training method that can be "plug-and-play" with different WAM architectures (both action-expert and unified models). This broad applicability means it could be integrated into various existing and future WAM frameworks, potentially extending the robustness of a wide range of robot control systems.
- **Efficiency**: By being a post-training method and not requiring re-pretraining of large video models, Robust-WAM offers an efficient way to enhance current WAMs. The use of frozen DINOv3 CLS embeddings and a lightweight alignment objective adds minimal computational overhead during training and no additional cost at inference.
- **Design Insights**: The ablation studies provide insights into effective design choices, such as the superiority of DINOv3 CLS embeddings as an alignment target and the importance of precise temporal grounding for future queries. These findings can inform future research in representation alignment for robot learning.

The potential impact of Robust-WAM is in advancing the capabilities of robotic manipulation in real-world scenarios. By enabling robots to robustly perform tasks despite visual variations, this work contributes to making robotic systems more reliable, adaptable, and deployable outside of controlled laboratory settings. It suggests that complex, learned dynamics from generative models can be effectively combined with semantically robust representations to achieve more intelligent and resilient autonomous agents.
