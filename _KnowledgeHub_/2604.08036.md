# PriPG-RL: Privileged Planner-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Partially Observable Systems with Anytime-Feasible MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called PriPG-RL enables training reactive reinforcement learning policies for partially observable systems by leveraging an anytime-feasible MPC planner to guide a learning agent. This approach, which includes novel imitation and gating mechanisms, allowed a quadruped robot to achieve 100% task success in simulation and successfully navigate obstacle-rich environments in the real world, outperforming standard reinforcement learning methods.

## Problem

- Training model-free deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies under partial observability (POMDPs) is challenging, often leading to instabilities, suboptimal convergence, or complete failure for standard RL algorithms.
- Existing privileged learning or RL from demonstrations (RLfD) methods, when applied to POMDPs, suffer from issues such as vanishing gradients in imitation losses and catastrophic forgetting once guidance is removed.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods might not guarantee feasible solutions if terminated prematurely, limiting their effectiveness as reliable, anytime guidance providers for RL agents.

## Method

- Developed **PriPG-RL**, a framework that integrates an anytime-feasible ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) planner to provide structured guidance to a reactive learning agent (P2P-SAC) during training in partially observable environments.
- Introduced P2P-SAC, a modified Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm that incorporates planner guidance through a dual replay buffer, a deterministic three-phase maturity schedule, a logit-space imitation anchor, and an advantage-based sigmoid gate.
- Utilized an Anytime-feasible MPC (REAP-inspired) as the planner agent, ensuring that feasible control actions are always available, even when terminated early, providing robust and consistent guidance during the RL training process.

## Results

- P2P-SAC achieved a **100%** success rate and **0.0%** crash rate in simulated quadruped navigation, reaching full performance within approximately 1 million environment steps, whereas vanilla SAC and PPO failed to learn the task.
- The learned policy demonstrated slightly improved path optimality (**1.060**±**0.0311**.060 \pm **0.0311**.060±**0.031**) compared to the REAP planner itself (**1.10**±**0.041**.10 \pm **0.041**.10±**0.04**), indicating effective knowledge distillation without strictly bounding performance.
- The policy trained in simulation successfully transferred to a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, demonstrating robust navigation and obstacle avoidance in real-world, partially observable environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging an anytime-feasible planner as a privileged teacher effectively mitigates the challenges of partial observability in model-free RL, enabling robust policy learning in environments where standard RL algorithms fail.
> - Applying imitation loss in the logit space (pre-activation) addresses the vanishing gradient problem encountered with output-space imitation, ensuring effective learning for actions near control boundaries critical for safety.
> - An advantage-based sigmoid gate dynamically modulates planner guidance, allowing the learning agent to surpass the planner's performance in situations where its reactive policy is superior, while retaining essential guidance in aliased states.
