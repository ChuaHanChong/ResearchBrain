# PriPG-RL: Privileged Planner-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Partially Observable Systems with Anytime-Feasible MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called PriPG-RL enables training reactive reinforcement learning policies for partially observable systems by leveraging an anytime-feasible MPC planner to guide a learning agent. This approach, which includes novel imitation and gating mechanisms, allowed a quadruped robot to achieve 100% task success in simulation and successfully navigate obstacle-rich environments in the real world, outperforming standard reinforcement learning methods.

## Problem

- Training model-free deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies under partial observability (POMDPs) is challenging, often leading to instabilities, suboptimal convergence, or complete failure for standard RL algorithms.
- Existing privileged learning or RL from demonstrations (RLfD) methods, when applied to POMDPs, suffer from issues such as vanishing gradients in imitation losses and catastrophic forgetting once guidance is removed.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods might not guarantee feasible solutions if terminated prematurely, limiting their effectiveness as reliable, anytime guidance providers for RL agents.

## Method

- Developed **PriPG-RL**, a framework that integrates an anytime-feasible ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) planner to provide structured guidance to a reactive learning agent (P2P-SAC) during training in partially observable environments.
- Introduced P2P-SAC, a modified Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm that incorporates planner guidance through a dual replay buffer, a deterministic three-phase maturity schedule, a logit-space imitation anchor, and an advantage-based sigmoid gate.
- Utilized an Anytime-feasible MPC (REAP-inspired) as the planner agent, ensuring that feasible control actions are always available, even when terminated early, providing robust and consistent guidance during the RL training process.

## Results

- P2P-SAC achieved a **100%** success rate and **0.0%** crash rate in simulated quadruped navigation, reaching full performance within approximately 1 million environment steps, whereas vanilla SAC and PPO failed to learn the task.
- The learned policy demonstrated slightly improved path optimality (**1.060**±**0.0311**.060 ± **0.0311**.060±**0.031**) compared to the REAP planner itself (**1.10**±**0.041**.10 ± **0.041**.10±**0.04**), indicating effective knowledge distillation without strictly bounding performance.
- The policy trained in simulation successfully transferred to a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, demonstrating robust navigation and obstacle avoidance in real-world, partially observable environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging an anytime-feasible planner as a privileged teacher effectively mitigates the challenges of partial observability in model-free RL, enabling robust policy learning in environments where standard RL algorithms fail.
> - Applying imitation loss in the logit space (pre-activation) addresses the vanishing gradient problem encountered with output-space imitation, ensuring effective learning for actions near control boundaries critical for safety.
> - An advantage-based sigmoid gate dynamically modulates planner guidance, allowing the learning agent to surpass the planner's performance in situations where its reactive policy is superior, while retaining essential guidance in aliased states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a fundamental challenge in model-free deep Reinforcement Learning (RL): training policies under partial observability, a setting formally described as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). In POMDPs, the learning agent's observations do not fully reveal the true environment state, which can lead to severe instabilities in value functions and policy gradients. This informational incompleteness frequently causes standard RL algorithms such as PPO, TD3, and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to fail, resulting in uninformative exploration, convergence to suboptimal local minima, or prohibitively slow learning.

Prior approaches to mitigate partial observability often optimize reactive (memoryless) policies within a surrogate MDP induced by the observation space, inherently accepting an approximation gap. While some theoretical guarantees exist for specific classes of problems (SNS-MDPs) where unobservable components evolve independently, in general continuous control, the surrogate MDP is typically policy-dependent and suffers from pervasive state aliasing.

The concept of privileged learning, where a teacher with access to full state information guides a student with restricted observations, has emerged as a strategy to bridge this optimality gap. Concurrently, the RL community has explored methods like RL from demonstrations (RLfD) and model-based planning to incorporate prior knowledge. However, existing RLfD and regularization frameworks, such as those that regularize an SAC actor towards a heuristic policy, are primarily formulated for fully observable MDPs. When applied to POMDPs, these methods can encounter limitations like vanishing gradients (due to output-space imitation near action boundaries) and catastrophic forgetting (when guidance schedules decay to zero, leaving the agent vulnerable to unmitigated state aliasing).

Separately, the control community has developed anytime optimization methods, such as the Robust-to-Early termination Anytime-feasible MPC (REAP) framework. Unlike standard MPC, which may not guarantee feasible solutions if terminated before convergence, REAP provides guarantees of feasibility and monotonically improving solution quality as computation time increases. The authors identify this property as making REAP a suitable candidate for providing structured guidance to RL agents during training.

This research integrates these two lines of inquiry by developing a framework that leverages an anytime-feasible planner to guide an RL agent in a POMDP setting, aiming to overcome the specific limitations observed in prior attempts at knowledge distillation in partially observable environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general framework, termed Privileged Planner-Guided RL (PriPG-RL), for training reactive RL policies that operate under partial observability. This framework exploits a privileged, anytime-feasible planner agent that is exclusively available during the training phase. The core problem is formalized as finding a reactive policy that achieves high performance while adhering to specific constraints related to deployment and training efficiency.

The detailed objectives of this work are threefold:

1. **Reactive Optimality:** To find a reactive policy, denoted as $\pi_{\theta}^*: S \to \Delta(U)$, that is optimal among all possible reactive policies operating solely on observations $s_t$. The authors explicitly acknowledge that, due to the inherent nature of POMDPs, this objective targets the best reactive policy and not the globally optimal history-dependent policy, thus accepting an inherent optimality gap relative to the full POMDP.
2. **Deployment Autonomy:** To ensure that the learned policy $\pi_{\theta}^*$ can execute autonomously at runtime, relying exclusively on the observable state $s_t$. This means the policy must function without any access to the privileged information ($I_t$) available to the planner agent or the planner agent itself ($\pi_{\text{P}}^{(t_{\text{cpu}})}$). This constraint necessitates effective knowledge distillation from the planner during training, without creating a runtime dependency.
3. **Training Efficiency:** To significantly reduce the sample complexity required to achieve the desired policy performance by effectively exploiting the planner agent $\pi_{\text{P}}^{(t_{\text{cpu}})}$ during the training process. This motivation stems from the high sample inefficiency typically observed in standard RL methods when confronted with the challenges of partial observability.

The motivation for this approach is rooted in several observations regarding the limitations of existing RL methods in POMDP settings:
- Standard model-free RL algorithms struggle with state aliasing, where identical observations correspond to different true states, leading to unstable learning and suboptimal policies.
- Prior privileged learning or RLfD methods, when applied to POMDPs, often suffer from issues such as vanishing gradients in imitation losses (particularly when actions are near control bounds, as these losses are typically applied in output space) and catastrophic forgetting, where learned behaviors degrade once the teacher's guidance is fully removed. The latter occurs because the underlying POMDP challenges resurface without ongoing support.
- The control community's development of anytime-feasible MPC offers a promising avenue for robust, real-time guidance. An anytime-feasible planner can reliably provide feasible (though potentially suboptimal) control actions at any computational budget, making it an ideal candidate to serve as a teacher agent that can adapt to varying computational demands during training while consistently providing valuable, constraint-satisfying advice.

By combining the strengths of privileged learning with the robust guidance of anytime-feasible MPC and addressing the specific technical limitations of previous imitation-based RL approaches, the authors aim to develop a more stable, efficient, and performant RL framework for partially observable control problems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed PriPG-RL framework introduces two agents with asymmetric information structures: a "planner agent" possessing an approximate dynamical model and privileged state information, and a "learning agent" that observes only a lossy projection of the true state. The planner guides the learning agent exclusively during training, with the goal that the learning agent will function autonomously at deployment.

**Planner Agent: Anytime-Feasible MPC (REAP-Based)**
The planner agent $\pi_{\text{P}}^{(t_{\text{cpu}})}$ is an instantiation of an anytime-feasible Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm, inspired by the REAP framework.
- **Model:** It operates on a linear approximate model ($z_{t+1} = Az_t + Bu_t$) of the system dynamics, using the planner's observation $z_t$ and privileged information $I_t$.
- **Optimization Problem:** The planner solves a constrained optimal control problem (7) over a prediction horizon $N$, minimizing a cost function while adhering to state and input constraints, and a terminal constraint set.
- **Anytime Feasibility:** To ensure feasibility regardless of computation time, the problem is formulated using a modified barrier function (12) and solved via a primal-dual gradient flow (13). This dynamical system exponentially converges to the optimal MPC solution and, crucially, guarantees that the computed control sequence $\hat{u}_\rho$ remains feasible for all computation times $\rho$. This property allows the planner to be safely terminated at any point within a computational budget $t_{\text{cpu}}$, providing a feasible control action $u_t^\dagger$.

**Learning Agent: Planner-to-Policy Soft Actor-Critic (P2P-SAC)**
P2P-SAC is a modified SAC algorithm that integrates guidance from the planner agent through four specific mechanisms:

1. **Dual Replay Buffer:**
 - Unlike methods relying on fixed, pre-collected demonstrations, P2P-SAC generates planner data online.
 - It comprises two distinct buffers: `D_P` (planner agent's buffer) and `D_O` (standard online buffer).
 - `D_P` stores transitions where the planner's action $u_t^\dagger$ was executed (behavioral substitution), reflecting the planner's closed-loop dynamics. These transitions are "write-once" and do not expire based on FIFO.
 - `D_O` is a standard FIFO buffer for transitions collected when the learning agent's own policy is executed.
 - Mini-batches for training are sampled as an equal-weight mixture from both `D_P` and `D_O`.

2. **Deterministic Three-Phase Maturity Schedule:**
 - This schedule manages the intensity and nature of planner guidance, addressing the issue of catastrophic forgetting seen in prior work where guidance might decay to zero.
 - **Plateau Phase ($0 \le t \le T_p$):** The agent is "immature" ($M_t = 0$), and a high guidance coefficient ($\beta_t = \beta_0$) is active. During this phase, the planner's action $u_t^\dagger$ replaces the learning agent's action (behavioral substitution), and transitions are stored in `D_P`.
 - **Annealing Phase ($T_p < t \le T_p + T_d$):** $M_t$ remains 0, but $\beta_t$ linearly decays from $\beta_0$ to a non-zero final value $\beta_f$. Behavioral substitution is still active.
 - **Maturity Phase ($t > T_p + T_d$):** The agent becomes "mature" ($M_t = 1$), $\beta_t = \beta_f$. Behavioral substitution is deactivated, and transitions are stored in `D_O`. The non-zero $\beta_f$ ensures that some form of planner guidance persists, preventing a complete return to an unmitigated POMDP.

3. **Logit-Space Imitation Anchor:**
 - This mechanism addresses the vanishing gradient problem encountered when applying imitation losses in the output space (e.g., `tanh` squashed actions). When the planner's actions are close to control bounds, the `tanh` derivative approaches zero, paralyzing learning.
 - P2P-SAC converts the planner's action $u_t^\dagger$ into a "planner agent's logit" $\xi^\dagger$ by performing an inverse `tanh` operation and clipping, ensuring $\xi^\dagger$ remains finite.
 - The imitation loss (17) is then computed in the logit space: $\ell(s, u_t^\dagger) = \frac{1}{p} \| \mu_{\theta}(s) - \xi^\dagger \|_2^2$. This provides a bounded gradient, regardless of the magnitude of the logits, facilitating learning even at safety-critical operating points.

4. **Advantage-Based Sigmoid Gate:**
 - This gate selectively applies the imitation loss, allowing the learning agent to potentially surpass the planner's performance, particularly in regions of informational asymmetry ($G$) where the planner's advantage relies on privileged information.
 - It calculates the soft state value $\hat{V}(s)$ and the planner agent's advantage $\hat{A}^\dagger(s, u^\dagger)$ using the learned critic functions.
 - A sigmoid function maps $\hat{A}^\dagger(s, u^\dagger)$ to a soft weight $m_\phi(s, u^\dagger)$, which is then integrated into a composite gating function $G_\phi(s, u^\dagger; M_t)$.
 - In the immature phase, $G_\phi \equiv 1$, applying uniform guidance. In the mature phase, $G_\phi = m_\phi(s, u^\dagger)$, dynamically suppressing the anchor where the learning agent is performing better (negative advantage) and retaining it where the planner remains superior. This mechanism ensures that the learning agent is not asymptotically bounded by the planner's performance.

**Composite Actor Objective and Theoretical Analysis:**
The actor's loss function (22) combines the standard SAC objective with a planner-regularized term, where the regularization weight is determined by the guidance coefficient $\beta_t$ and the advantage gate $G_\phi$. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) demonstrates that in the mature phase, the policy gradient for this regularized objective is immune to the irreducible aliasing variance that characterizes POMDPs. This means the variance, while present in the overall loss, does not directly influence the gradient used for policy updates, contributing to learning stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PriPG-RL framework, specifically the P2P-SAC algorithm with the REAP-based planner, was evaluated through simulations and real-world deployment on a quadrupedal robot. The task involved autonomous navigation in complex, obstacle-rich environments under partial observability.

**A. Simulation Setup and Metrics:**
- **Platform:** NVIDIA Isaac Lab simulator with a Unitree Go2 quadruped model.
- **Partial Observability:** The learning agent's observations ($s_t$) included its own position and goal position but excluded obstacle positions, robot heading, and joint quantities, thus creating a POMDP environment where the agent is "blind" to obstacles. The planner agent had access to privileged information ($I_t$), including obstacle and boundary geometry.
- **Reward:** A sparse reward function was used, including a step penalty and a control effort penalty, with terminal rewards for reaching the goal (+100) or crashing (-200).
- **Comparisons:** P2P-SAC was compared against vanilla SAC, PPO, and Accelerated SAC. The REAP-based planner itself also served as a baseline for performance comparison.
- **Metrics:** Success rate, crash rate, path optimality (ratio of episode path length to straight-line distance to goal), runtime, and average velocity.

**B. Simulation Results and Discussion:**

1. **Sample Efficiency and Success Rate (Figure 2):**
 - P2P-SAC demonstrated superior sample efficiency, achieving a 100% success rate after approximately 1 million environment steps.
 - In contrast, Accelerated SAC achieved a maximum success rate of about 40%.
 - Vanilla SAC and PPO failed to learn the task, exhibiting negligible success rates throughout training. This result empirically supports the claim that standard RL methods are ineffective in this POMDP setting.
 - The rapid achievement of 100% success by P2P-SAC was attributed, in part, to setting a plateau phase ($T_p = 10^5$ steps) which allowed the planner to collect high-quality initial trajectories, demonstrating the utility of REAP's anytime feasibility.

2. **Final Performance (Table I):**
 - P2P-SAC achieved a 100% success rate and 0.0% crash rate, with an average path optimality of $1.060 \pm 0.031$.
 - Accelerated SAC showed a significantly lower success rate ($35.0 \pm 47.7\%$) and a high crash rate ($65.0 \pm 47.7\%$), with a path optimality of $1.100 \pm 0.073$.
 - The REAP planner (as a baseline) also achieved 100% success and 0.0% crash, with a path optimality of $1.10 \pm 0.04$.
 - These results indicate that P2P-SAC not only matches the planner's reliability in terms of task completion but also slightly surpasses its path optimality, demonstrating effective knowledge distillation without strict performance bounding.

3. **Advantage Gate Behavior:**
 - During the initial annealing phase, the composite gate $G_\phi$ remained at 1, uniformly applying guidance.
 - Upon maturation ($t=T_p$), $G_\phi$ initially dropped to approximately 0.1, suggesting the critic initially estimated the learning agent's policy as superior. It then stabilized around 0.45. This behavior aligns with the theoretical prediction that the gate would tend toward 0.5 in aliased states ($s \in G$), thus asymptotically reducing the imitation bias where the planner's privileged information cannot be replicated by the reactive policy.

**C. Real-World Evaluation:**

- **Deployment:** The policy trained using P2P-SAC in simulation was successfully deployed on a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped robot.
- **Performance:** The robot demonstrated successful navigation in a real-world obstacle-rich environment. It avoided all obstacles and reached the specified goal, while adhering to velocity constraints.
- **Validation:** These hardware experiments (illustrated in Figure 3) confirm the sim-to-real transferability of the learned policy and its robustness in real-world conditions, including maintaining safe trajectories despite partial observability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, PriPG-RL, that significantly contributes to the field of reinforcement learning for control in partially observable environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Core Challenge:** The work directly tackles the fundamental problem of training reactive RL policies in POMDPs, a pervasive issue in real-world robotics and autonomous systems where full state information is rarely available. By demonstrating that P2P-SAC enables robust learning in settings where standard RL algorithms (SAC, PPO) entirely fail, it offers a viable solution to a recognized limitation.
- **Novel Integration of Concepts:** PriPG-RL innovatively integrates an anytime-feasible MPC planner (REAP) as a privileged teacher with a specifically designed SAC-based learning agent (P2P-SAC). This fusion leverages the strengths of model-based control (safety, feasibility, and informed guidance) during training, without imposing runtime dependencies on the complex planner.
- **Overcoming Prior Limitations:** The P2P-SAC algorithm introduces several mechanisms that directly address deficiencies in existing imitation learning and privileged learning approaches for POMDPs:
 - **Logit-Space Imitation Anchor:** Solves the vanishing gradient problem inherent in output-space imitation, ensuring effective learning even for actions near control boundaries, which are often critical for safety.
 - **Advantage-Based Sigmoid Gate:** Provides a principled way to selectively apply planner guidance. This prevents the learning agent from being strictly bounded by the planner's performance, allowing it to surpass the planner where its reactive policy proves superior, while retaining guidance where the planner's privileged information offers a clear advantage (e.g., in aliased states).
 - **Three-Phase Maturity Schedule with Non-Zero Guidance:** Mitigates catastrophic forgetting by maintaining a persistent, albeit attenuated, form of guidance, preventing the agent from reverting to an unmitigated POMDP problem.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating an aliasing-immune gradient is a significant contribution. It shows that the policy gradient for the planner-regularized objective does not contain the irreducible variance caused by state aliasing. This theoretical insight provides a formal basis for the observed stability and effectiveness of P2P-SAC.
- **Practical Validation and Real-World Applicability:** The rigorous validation, encompassing both high-fidelity simulation and successful deployment on a physical Unitree Go2 quadruped in an obstacle-rich environment, underscores the practical utility and robustness of the framework. The ability to transfer policies trained under partial observability to real hardware for safety-critical tasks like obstacle avoidance is highly impactful for robotic applications.
- **Future Directions:** The paper identifies future work in extending PriPG-RL to supervise history-aware architectures and employ time-varying planner agents. This indicates a pathway towards resolving temporal ambiguities in non-stationary environments and potentially achieving even closer approximations to POMDP-optimal policies, further expanding the framework's utility.

In summary, PriPG-RL provides a robust, theoretically grounded, and empirically validated solution for a critical problem in reinforcement learning, with direct implications for the development of more capable and reliable autonomous systems operating in complex, real-world environments with incomplete sensory information.
