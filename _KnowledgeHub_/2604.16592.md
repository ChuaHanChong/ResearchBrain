# Human Cognition in Machines: A Unified Perspective of World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northeastern University propose a conceptual framework to evaluate World Models by grounding them in Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT), systematically classifying existing models based on seven cognitive functions. The framework identifies motivation and meta-cognition as under-researched areas and introduces "Epistemic World Models" for scientific discovery, demonstrating how structured knowledge spaces can be dynamically navigated by agents.

## Problem

- Existing classifications of World Models are often too broad, relying on architectural designs or application domains, which prevents nuanced evaluation of their cognitive capabilities.
- The use of anthropomorphic language to describe World Models lacks a principled framework for objective evaluation, leading to potential overstatements of AI achievements and confusion.
- There is a need to systematically identify specific cognitive functions where current World Models innovate and where critical research gaps remain when compared to human cognition.

## Method

- Developed a novel taxonomy for World Models, grounded in Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT), which categorizes models based on seven specific cognitive functions: memory, perception, language, reasoning, imagining, motivation, and meta-cognition.
- Conducted a comprehensive literature review, applying the CAT-based taxonomy to classify World Models across three domains: Video, Embodied, and the newly introduced Epistemic World Models.
- Proposed a conceptual Unified World Model (UWM) framework that integrates all identified cognitive functions, serving as a roadmap for designing more cognitively complete AI systems.

## Results

- The CAT-based taxonomy successfully classified diverse World Models, revealing distinct innovation patterns across video, embodied, and epistemic domains.
- The analysis revealed that motivation and meta-cognition are critically underdeveloped, with concrete proposals for addressing these gaps using Active Inference for motivation and Global Workspace Theory or agent frameworks for meta-cognition.
- The introduction of Epistemic World Models demonstrates how LLM-powered agents can interact with structured knowledge as a 'world,' providing practical instantiations of meta-cognitive self-monitoring through externalized global workspaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A CAT-based taxonomy provides a finer-grained understanding of World Model capabilities and highlights the specific cognitive functions advanced by different architectures and applications.
> - Motivation (particularly intrinsic motivation) and meta-cognition are identified as drastically under-researched areas in current World Model literature, representing significant gaps in achieving human-like cognitive capabilities.
> - "Epistemic World Models," which operate over structured knowledge spaces for scientific discovery, offer a new paradigm for World Model applications and provide early examples of meta-cognitive mechanisms.
