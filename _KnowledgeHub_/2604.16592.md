# Human Cognition in Machines: A Unified Perspective of World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Northeastern University propose a conceptual framework to evaluate World Models by grounding them in Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT), systematically classifying existing models based on seven cognitive functions. The framework identifies motivation and meta-cognition as under-researched areas and introduces "Epistemic World Models" for scientific discovery, demonstrating how structured knowledge spaces can be dynamically navigated by agents.

## Problem

- Existing classifications of World Models are often too broad, relying on architectural designs or application domains, which prevents nuanced evaluation of their cognitive capabilities.
- The use of anthropomorphic language to describe World Models lacks a principled framework for objective evaluation, leading to potential overstatements of AI achievements and confusion.
- There is a need to systematically identify specific cognitive functions where current World Models innovate and where critical research gaps remain when compared to human cognition.

## Method

- Developed a novel taxonomy for World Models, grounded in Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT), which categorizes models based on seven specific cognitive functions: memory, perception, language, reasoning, imagining, motivation, and meta-cognition.
- Conducted a comprehensive literature review, applying the CAT-based taxonomy to classify World Models across three domains: Video, Embodied, and the newly introduced Epistemic World Models.
- Proposed a conceptual Unified World Model (UWM) framework that integrates all identified cognitive functions, serving as a roadmap for designing more cognitively complete AI systems.

## Results

- The CAT-based taxonomy successfully classified diverse World Models, revealing distinct innovation patterns across video, embodied, and epistemic domains.
- The analysis revealed that motivation and meta-cognition are critically underdeveloped, with concrete proposals for addressing these gaps using Active Inference for motivation and Global Workspace Theory or agent frameworks for meta-cognition.
- The introduction of Epistemic World Models demonstrates how LLM-powered agents can interact with structured knowledge as a 'world,' providing practical instantiations of meta-cognitive self-monitoring through externalized global workspaces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A CAT-based taxonomy provides a finer-grained understanding of World Model capabilities and highlights the specific cognitive functions advanced by different architectures and applications.
> - Motivation (particularly intrinsic motivation) and meta-cognition are identified as drastically under-researched areas in current World Model literature, representing significant gaps in achieving human-like cognitive capabilities.
> - "Epistemic World Models," which operate over structured knowledge spaces for scientific discovery, offer a new paradigm for World Model applications and provide early examples of meta-cognitive mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models have become a fundamental abstraction in the development of intelligent machines. They function by learning internal representations of an external environment to predict its future evolution, including under counterfactual actions. These models are increasingly deployed across diverse applications such as video generation, embodied agents, and language-based reasoning systems, where they are expected to facilitate imagination, decision-making, and adaptive behavior.

However, the rapid expansion of world model research has introduced conceptual ambiguity. Many contemporary world models are described using anthropomorphic or cognitive terminology, such as "understanding scenes," "imagining futures," or "reasoning about actions." While such descriptions can highlight computational capabilities, they frequently obscure the actual mechanisms and limitations of these models. This ambiguity has led to a lack of a standardized vocabulary for evaluating which cognitive functions are genuinely present, absent, or merely aspirational in current world models.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a principled grounding in Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT), a field that identifies key components of cognition. Previous surveys of world models often categorize them by domain application (e.g., video, embodied), architectural distinctions, or abstract taxonomies, but they generally lack a systematic classification based on underlying cognitive functions. This work distinguishes itself by providing a comprehensive review that aligns world model innovations with specific cognitive functions observed in human and machine cognition. By doing so, it aims to clarify the capabilities of existing models and guide future research towards developing more truly human-like cognitive abilities in machines. The paper's contribution is to offer a unified conceptual framework and a novel taxonomy to bring clarity and a shared perspective to the diverse and rapidly evolving field of world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a unified conceptual framework for world models, grounded in the principles of human and machine cognition, to systematically evaluate and guide advancements in the field. This framework aims to integrate all identified cognitive functions: memory, perception, language, reasoning, imagination, motivation, and metacognition.

The motivation behind this work stems from several critical observations in the current landscape of world model research:
- **Conceptual Ambiguity:** The widespread use of cognitive and anthropomorphic language to describe world models, while sometimes evocative, often lacks precise computational grounding. This creates confusion about the actual capabilities of these models and hinders rigorous comparative analysis.
- **Lack of Unified Vocabulary:** The field currently lacks a shared and consistent vocabulary to articulate the specific cognitive functions implemented by different world models. This deficiency makes it challenging to distinguish between concrete achievements and future aspirations, impeding progress towards robust and intelligent machines.
- **Need for Grounded Evaluation:** To move beyond broad claims of "human-like intelligence," a principled account of cognition is necessary. By using Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT) as an organizing lens, the research seeks to enable more precise questions about which cognitive functions are implemented, how they are computationally represented, and where current systems are underdeveloped.
- **Identification of Research Gaps:** A significant motivation is to identify under-researched areas in world model development. The authors specifically hypothesize and later confirm that "motivation (especially intrinsic motivation) and metacognition remain drastically under-researched," highlighting these as critical components for developing autonomous, adaptive, and robust artificial intelligence.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** By identifying these gaps and proposing concrete directions, the work aims to serve as a roadmap for future state-of-the-art research, informing how progress in one cognitive domain might benefit others.
- **Introduction of Epistemic World Models:** A specific objective is to introduce and define "epistemic world models" as a new category. This category focuses on agent frameworks for scientific discovery that operate over structured knowledge, contrasting them with latent world models and providing potential solutions for the identified gaps, particularly concerning metacognition.

In summary, the core motivation is to bring theoretical rigor and a unified perspective to the disparate efforts in world model development, fostering a more structured and goal-oriented approach towards achieving advanced machine cognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is primarily a theoretical and analytical review, underpinned by a novel classification system derived from Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT).

**Core Framework – Cognitive Architecture Theory (CAT):**
The paper grounds its analysis in the functional decomposition of cognition as proposed by Allen Newell, a pioneer in cognitive science. Newell's original list of cognitive components includes perception, memory, language, reasoning, and motivation. The authors expand this list to include **metacognition**, defining it as the capacity for self-reflection, self-evaluation, and self-control, and argue for its paramount role in regulating other cognitive functions. The full set of cognitive functions used are:
- **Perception:** Sensing and encoding observations into a world state.
- **Memory:** Storing past experiences, context, or states.
- **Language:** Using symbols for abstract meaning, communication, and reasoning.
- **Reasoning:** Problem-solving and action selection through inference.
- **Imagination:** Simulating possible or hypothetical futures.
- **Motivation:** Driving behavior based on extrinsic or intrinsic rewards.
- **Metacognition:** Operating over one's own representations, predictions, and decisions (self-reflection, self-evaluation, self-control).

**Taxonomy Development:**
A novel taxonomy is developed by mapping contemporary world model innovations to these seven cognitive functions. The explicit sorting criterion is that "a work is innovative on a cognitive function when the work’s stated contribution operates on that function." Works contributing to multiple functions are noted accordingly. This transparent criterion aims to make the categorization inspectable and debatable.

**Survey Scope:**
The review surveys world model research published since the seminal work by Ha et al. (2018), focusing on highly relevant works presented at top conferences, in significant surveys, or found in prominent repositories. The survey covers three main domains:
1. **Video World Models:** Models that learn compact representations of visual environments and their temporal evolution in 2D, 3D, and hybrid geometric settings.
2. **Embodied World Models:** Models that perceive, act, and anticipate physical world changes, focusing on robotics, navigation, and autonomous driving.
3. **Epistemic World Models (New Category):** Agentic frameworks for scientific discovery that operate over structured knowledge spaces (e.g., literature, databases, experimental outputs) rather than physical environments.

**Unified Cognition Framework for World Models:**
The paper proposes a conceptual "unified world model" framework (Figure 3) which serves as a roadmap for future research. This framework advocates for:
- **Multi-modal Perception:** Encoding observations from various sensory inputs (images, language, audio) into a latent world state ($z_t$).
- **Multi-scalar Memory:** Incorporating short-term memory (e.g., sliding windows of past observations) to capture spatio-temporal dynamics and long-term memory (e.g., queryable maps) for efficient access and temporal coherence.
- **Language Integration:** Using language tokens for inputs, intermediate reasoning, or outputs to facilitate human-in-the-loop interaction and enable metacognition.
- **Imagination for Planning and Transfer:** Leveraging hypothetical reasoning for action rollouts during inference and sim-to-real transfer learning during training, especially when data is scarce.
- **Domain-Specific Reasoning:** Employing state-of-the-art architectures for world state prediction and action selection (e.g., Vision-Language-Action models, World Action Models, diffusion models).
- **Intrinsic Motivation:** Moving beyond extrinsic, hand-crafted rewards by exploring intrinsic mechanisms like Active Inference, which formalizes "minimizing surprise" by minimizing variational free energy, balancing information gain and value (Eq. 7-10).
- **Metacognition via Global Workspaces:** Implementing self-reflection, self-evaluation, and self-control, drawing inspiration from Global Workspace Theory and agent frameworks where language-based workspaces can operate over other cognitive components.

**Mathematical Formalism:** The methodology includes mathematical formulations for world model representation (Eq. 2-5), defining an ideal encoder that optimizes for mutual information while ensuring sufficient reward for downstream tasks, and for reasoning/action selection (Eq. 6), where optimal actions maximize expected rewards over imagined trajectories. It also formalizes Active Inference as a mechanism for intrinsic motivation.

By systematically applying this CAT-based taxonomy and outlining a unified framework, the paper aims to provide both an analytical tool for current research and a constructive guide for future developments in human-like machine cognition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings derived from its comprehensive review and the application of its novel cognitive-centric taxonomy:

**1. A Unified Taxonomy for World Models:**
The paper successfully categorizes a broad spectrum of contemporary world models (published since 2018) across three domains: video, embodied, and the newly defined epistemic world models, based on the seven cognitive functions (perception, memory, language, reasoning, imagination, motivation, and metacognition). This systematic classification, summarized in Tables 3, 4, and 5, provides a coherent framework for understanding the diverse contributions of existing works.

**2. Identification of Critical Research Gaps in Motivation and Metacognition:**
A central finding is the significant under-exploration of two cognitive functions: motivation (particularly intrinsic motivation) and metacognition, across all surveyed world model domains.
- **Motivation:** The vast majority of current world models rely on extrinsic motivation, typically implemented through externally defined reward functions or hand-crafted objectives in Reinforcement Learning (RL) settings. This reliance often leads to issues like reward misalignment and poor generalization. The paper notes a nascent but limited trend towards intrinsic motivation, with only a few works explicitly incorporating Active Inference or similar curiosity-driven mechanisms.
- **Metacognition:** Latent world models (e.g., in video and embodied AI) largely lack explicit mechanisms for self-reflection, self-evaluation, or self-control over their internal computations and predictions. While rudimentary forms of self-correction exist (e.g., inference-time refinement in video generation or model-predictive control in embodied settings), a comprehensive, autonomous metacognitive capability is largely absent.

**3. Introduction and Characterization of Epistemic World Models:**
The paper effectively introduces "epistemic world models" as a distinct and crucial category. These models operate within agentic frameworks for scientific discovery, where the "world" is a structured knowledge space (literature, databases, experimental outputs) and is updated through reasoning and tool interactions. A key finding is that these models provide early instantiations of metacognitive mechanisms. Their use of language-based "Global Workspaces" (inspired by Baars' Global Workspace Theory) allows agents to externalize intermediate reasoning, tool outputs, and shared context, enabling self-reflection, self-evaluation (e.g., through debate agents or reviewer layers), and self-control. This contrasts sharply with the implicit nature of latent world models.

**4. Domain-Specific Trends and Limitations:**
- **Video World Models:** Observed trends include a shift from passive video synthesis to controllable world simulation, emphasizing geometry-aware perception, persistent latent memory, long-horizon rollout, and action-conditioned generation. Limitations persist in implicit dynamics, long-horizon drift, and dependence on additional geometric priors. Motivation and metacognition remain weakly developed.
- **Embodied World Models:** These models face heightened demands for physical grounding. Key developments include multimodal perception (integrating RGB-D, LiDAR for 3D occupancy), advanced memory mechanisms (sparse dynamic queries, decoupling temporal/spatial generation), and language for task instruction and scene representation. Reasoning paradigms span geometric models, language-conditioned planning, and efficient World Action Models (WAMs), with imagination aiding policy learning and synthetic data generation. Efficiency gains are noted through hybrid VLA-WAMs. Limitations include computational inefficiencies, hardware demands, and reliance on costly training data, alongside the general lack of intrinsic motivation.
- **Epistemic World Models:** A notable trend is the move towards explicit, externalized knowledge-state modeling via shared, language-based global workspaces. Language serves as a medium for shared representation and coordination. Perception extends to structured artifacts and user-centric multimodal inputs. Memory is externalized into persistent artifacts, promoting auditability. Imagination generates hypotheses and plans. Reasoning is multi-step and tool-mediated. However, formalization of epistemic state transitions is less clear, motivation is largely extrinsic, and metacognition, while present, is often scaffolded by additional agents or human oversight rather than being fully autonomous.

**5. Intertwined Human and Machine Cognition:**
The report consistently demonstrates how concepts from human cognition (e.g., dual streams for perception, multi-layered thinking, shared intentions for language) inspire and align with advancements in machine cognition. It highlights that while machine cognition functionally emulates human cognition in many aspects, true mechanistic emulation is not the goal. The role of human researchers in setting objectives and designing experiments further emphasizes the "human-in-the-loop" aspect, particularly in epistemic world models.

In essence, the paper provides a clear assessment of the state of world models, pinpointing specific areas where cognitive capabilities are robust, emergent, or critically lacking, offering a structured foundation for future AI development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications for the future direction and understanding of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of world models.

**1. Establishing Conceptual Clarity and Standardization:**
The most immediate impact is the provision of a clear, cognitive-centric taxonomy and a unified conceptual framework. This addresses the current ambiguity in describing world models, offering a "shared vocabulary" that grounds discussion in principled cognitive theory. By moving beyond vague anthropomorphic claims, the research enables more precise evaluation, comparison, and communication within the AI community, potentially standardizing how world models are assessed and benchmarked.

**2. Guiding Future Research Directions:**
The explicit identification of **motivation (especially intrinsic) and metacognition** as critically under-researched areas provides a concrete roadmap for future work. This guidance is invaluable for directing resources and efforts towards developing more advanced and robust AI systems.
- **Intrinsic Motivation:** Research into intrinsic motivation (e.g., via Active Inference) could lead to AI agents that are genuinely curious, self-driven explorers, and capable of learning in unstructured environments without constant human-defined reward signals. This could significantly enhance generalization and autonomy.
- **Metacognition:** The proposed pathways for developing metacognitive abilities, particularly through language-based Global Workspaces in epistemic world models, could lead to AI systems that can monitor their own uncertainty, evaluate the reliability of their predictions, debug their own failures, and regulate their reasoning processes. This would be a crucial step towards more trustworthy, robust, and safe AI, especially in high-stakes applications.

**3. Accelerating Scientific Discovery via Epistemic World Models:**
The introduction and detailed analysis of "epistemic world models" as a new category are highly significant. These models, by formalizing scientific knowledge and processes within agentic frameworks, offer a blueprint for building more effective human-AI collaboration tools for scientific discovery. The observed metacognitive capabilities in these systems, albeit scaffolded, highlight a promising avenue for integrating self-awareness into AI, potentially accelerating research in fields like medicine, materials science, and social sciences.

**4. Fostering a Deeper Understanding of AI Capabilities and Limitations:**
By rigorously comparing machine capabilities to human cognitive functions, the work contributes to a more nuanced understanding of what current AI can and cannot do. This prevents overstatement of AI's "human-like" qualities and helps manage public expectations, which is vital for responsible AI development and deployment. It reinforces the idea that functional emulation does not equate to mechanistic replication of human cognition.

**5. Bridging the Gap Between Cognitive Science and AI Engineering:**
The report strengthens the interdisciplinary link between cognitive science and AI engineering. It demonstrates how theoretical constructs from human cognition (like Newell's CAT and Baars' Global Workspace Theory) can directly inspire practical architectural designs and research objectives in AI, and conversely, how AI experiments can inform our understanding of cognition.

In conclusion, this research is significant for its potential to bring order to a rapidly expanding field, provide clear direction for addressing fundamental challenges in AI autonomy and robustness, and open new avenues for human-AI collaboration in complex domains like scientific discovery, ultimately contributing to the development of more sophisticated, reliable, and interpretable intelligent machines.
