# Mitty: Diffusion-based Human-to-Robot Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Mitty is a Diffusion Transformer that generates robot-execution videos directly from human demonstration videos, end-to-end, without keypoints or trajectories. Human video latents condition robot denoising latents via bidirectional attention inside a Wan 2.2 backbone. Trained on a synthesized human-robot paired dataset, Mitty achieves 84.5% task success versus baselines under 78%.

## Problem

- Prior human-to-robot methods rely on intermediate representations (keypoints, trajectories, depth maps) that discard fine-grained spatio-temporal information from demonstration videos.
- Multi-stage rendering pipelines accumulate errors across estimation, inpainting, and rendering steps, degrading visual and physical realism of generated robot videos.
- Paired human-robot video data is extremely scarce, with the only public dataset containing just 2,600 pairs across nine tasks, limiting generalizable skill learning.

## Method

- ==Bidirectional attention coupling== fuses clean human-video condition tokens with noised robot-video denoising tokens at every ==Diffusion Transformer== layer, enabling cross-domain action translation without action labels.
- A unified Wan 2.2-based framework supports two inference modes, zero-frame ==Human2Robot== generation and first-frame-conditioned ==Human-and-Initial-Image-to-Robot== generation, sharing parameters across both.
- An ==automatic paired-data synthesis pipeline== chains hand-pose estimation, Detectron2/SAM2 hand removal, E2FGVI inpainting, and robot-arm rendering, curated via human-in-the-loop filtering into over 6,000 clips.

## Results

- On Human2Robot, Mitty's task-level success rate reaches **84.5%**, beating Aleph (**78.0%**), Kling (**70.0%**), Mocha (**69.0%**), and the rendering-based Masquerade (**31.5%**).
- Human preference strongly favors Mitty at **68.0%** versus **20.0%** for Masquerade and under **5%** for all video-editing baselines, despite Masquerade's higher **96.5%** embodiment consistency.
- Ablations show removing the human reference video drops success rate from **91%** to **65%** on Human2Robot, while removing the text prompt only mildly hurts performance, down to **88%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct video-to-video conditioning through bidirectional attention can substitute for explicit intermediate representations, avoiding compounding errors inherent to multi-stage pipelines.
> - Visual demonstration conditioning dominates over textual task descriptions for guiding action generation, suggesting language prompts are secondary when rich visual priors are available.
> - High embodiment consistency and high task success rate are not the same axis; a rendering-accurate but error-prone pipeline can still fail tasks more than a visually looser but more coherent generative model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mitty addresses several critical areas within the broader machine learning and robotics research landscape, primarily focusing on **Learning from Demonstration (LfD)** for robots and **generative AI for video synthesis**.

Historically, robot learning from human demonstrations has been a cornerstone of developing more intuitive and generalizable robotic systems. Traditional approaches in this domain often involve extracting intermediate representations from human demonstrations, such as keypoints, trajectories, or depth maps. These representations are then translated into robot actions. However, as the paper highlights, this multi-stage process is prone to **information loss and cumulative errors**, leading to visual and temporal inconsistencies in the generated robot actions. Mitty directly challenges this paradigm by proposing an **end-to-end Human-to-Robot (H2R) video generation** approach that bypasses these intermediate abstractions entirely.

The development of Mitty is deeply intertwined with the rapid advancements in **video generation models**. The paper notes the evolution from early GAN-based methods to UNet-based approaches and, more recently, **Diffusion Transformer architectures**. Mitty builds upon Wan 2.2, a state-of-the-art diffusion-based video generation model pretrained on massive natural video corpora. This leverages the strong visual-temporal priors learned from large-scale natural video data, a trend increasingly observed in applications of large pretrained models to robotics, often referred to as "world modeling" or leveraging "foundation models."

Furthermore, Mitty critically employs **In-Context Learning (ICL)**, a paradigm that has gained significant traction with the success of large language models (LLMs). ICL allows models to adapt to new tasks at inference time by simply conditioning on a few examples, rather than requiring extensive retraining. While widely explored in text and image generation, its application to complex cross-domain video generation, especially for robotics, is a burgeoning area. Mitty's use of ICL allows it to adapt a pretrained video generation model to the H2R task with high data efficiency.

A significant bottleneck in scalable robot learning is **data scarcity**, particularly for finely aligned human-robot paired videos. Real-world robot data collection is notoriously expensive and time-consuming. Mitty addresses this by introducing a novel, automated **data synthesis pipeline** that generates high-quality human-robot paired videos from abundant egocentric human activity datasets like EPIC-Kitchens. This directly tackles the data problem, a common challenge across various robot learning methodologies.

Finally, the work positions itself as an advancement over existing methods for human-to-robot translation and video editing. It directly compares against multi-stage rendering pipelines (like Masquerade) that suffer from error accumulation and generic video editing tools (like Kling, MoCha, Aleph) that struggle with maintaining robotic structural integrity and appearance consistency over time. By demonstrating superior performance in visual fidelity, temporal coherence, and task success rate, Mitty carves out a niche as a robust, specialized solution for the H2R video generation problem, laying a crucial foundation for subsequent policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Mitty: Diffusion-based Human-to-Robot Video Generation" is to **enable end-to-end Human-to-Robot (H2R) video generation directly from human demonstration videos**. This means creating a robotic execution video that accurately mirrors the actions and intent shown in a human demonstration, without relying on any intermediate abstract representations.

The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several key challenges and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Bridging the Human-Robot Gap:** Humans possess an innate ability to acquire new skills by observing others. The vision of scalable and generalizable robot learning hinges on transferring this capability to robots. If robots could directly learn manipulation policies from a single human demonstration video and generate corresponding robot-execution videos, it would unlock a critical path toward cross-task and cross-environment generalization.
2. **Limitations of Intermediate Representations:** Traditional methods for H2R transfer often extract intermediate representations (e.g., keypoints, trajectories, depth maps) from human videos, then use these to condition robot execution. The paper highlights that this approach inherently introduces **information loss** and **cumulative errors**, which severely degrade the temporal and visual consistency of the generated robot videos. Such methods fail to capture the fine-grained spatio-temporal dynamics essential for robust generalization.
3. **Specific Technical Challenges:** The task of end-to-end H2R video generation presents several formidable hurdles:
 - **Appearance and Scene Consistency:** The generated robot video must maintain the visual properties of the original scene while integrating a plausible and stable robot embodiment.
 - **Action and Strategy Alignment:** The robot's actions must accurately follow the human demonstration, despite inherent structural and kinematic differences between human hands and robot arms. This requires adapting human motion to a robotic embodiment realistically.
 - **Data Scarcity:** A major impediment to learning generalizable H2R mappings is the extreme rarity of high-quality, finely aligned human-robot video pairs. While human and robot videos are individually abundant, paired data suitable for supervised learning is scarce, with the largest public dataset containing only about 2,600 pairs across a limited number of tasks.

Mitty is motivated to overcome these challenges by developing a novel framework that not only generates high-fidelity and temporally aligned H2R videos but also provides richer supervision signals than sparse intermediate representations. While the current work focuses on video generation rather than direct robot control, the underlying motivation is to lay a robust foundation for **future video-to-policy inversion**, where these generated videos could eventually be translated into executable control signals for real robots, thus realizing truly end-to-end robot learning from human observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mitty's methodology is characterized by a sophisticated integration of Diffusion Transformers, In-Context Learning, and a novel data synthesis pipeline to achieve end-to-end Human-to-Robot (H2R) video generation.

**A. Overall Architecture and Problem Formulation:**
Mitty frames H2R video generation as a **conditional denoising problem**. Given a human demonstration video ($V_H$) and its corresponding robot execution video ($V_R$), the goal is to model the conditional distribution $p_\theta(V_R | V_H)$. The system supports two inference modes:
1. **H2R (Human2Robot Video Generation):** Directly generates a robot video from a human demonstration without any initial robot frame.
2. **HI2R (Human-and-Initial-Image-to-Robot Video Generation):** Provides an additional initial robot frame to guide the robot's starting state and motion planning, offering finer control.

The core framework is built upon **Wan 2.2**, a state-of-the-art diffusion-based video generation model pretrained on large-scale natural videos. Both human and robot videos are encoded into latent tokens using a shared VAE-based video encoder. Crucially, human latents serve as clean conditioning tokens, while robot latents are the targets for the denoising process. These tokens are concatenated along the temporal dimension and fed into a Diffusion Transformer, enabling cross-modal information flow.

**B. Video In-Context Learning via Bidirectional Attention:**
The heart of Mitty's generative capability lies in its **bidirectional attention mechanism**, which facilitates cross-domain ICL between human and robot modalities.
- **Diffusion Process:** Noise is progressively added *only* to the robot latents ($x_R_t$), keeping the human latents ($z_H_0$) clean as conditioning input. This setup allows the model to learn the conditional generation $p_\theta(V_R | V_H)$ without requiring explicit action or trajectory labels.
- **Token Representation:** Human latents ($z_H_0$) are augmented with temporal and modality embeddings to form "condition tokens" (C). Noisy robot latents ($x_R_t$) are similarly embedded to form "denoise tokens" (D).
- **Bidirectional Attention Coupling:** Within each Diffusion Transformer layer, information is exchanged between C and D through bidirectional attention. Specifically, the model computes attention maps allowing `C` to query `D` and vice versa, effectively enabling the human demonstration to influence the robot's denoising process and vice versa (e.g., ensuring consistent scene context). The updated tokens are then concatenated and passed to subsequent Transformer blocks.
- **Denoising and Reverse Update:** The Diffusion Transformer predicts the noise ($\epsilon_\theta$) on the robot branch, and the reverse diffusion process reconstructs the clean robot video latent ($z_R_0$), which is then decoded by the VAE to produce the final robot video.

**C. Dataset Construction Pipeline:**
To overcome the severe scarcity of human-robot paired videos, Mitty employs an automated paired-data synthesis pipeline, building upon ideas from Masquerade. This pipeline leverages large-scale egocentric human activity datasets (e.g., EPIC-Kitchens) and involves several sequential steps:
1. **Hand Pose Estimation:** 3D hand keypoints and motion trajectories are extracted from egocentric human videos using models like HaMeR.
2. **Hand Segmentation and Removal:** Detectron2 detects human hands, and Segment Anything 2 (SAM2) performs fine-grained segmentation to remove hands and forearms.
3. **Video Inpainting:** An advanced video inpainting model (E2FGVI) fills the removed regions, generating clean background videos.
4. **Pose Mapping:** The extracted hand keypoints are mapped to robot end-effector poses (position, orientation, gripper opening).
5. **Robot Arm Rendering:** Using RobotSuite, robot arms corresponding to the mapped poses are rendered into the inpainted videos.
6. **Human-in-the-Loop Filtering:** To mitigate cumulative errors from the multi-step pipeline, a rigorous human-in-the-loop filtering mechanism audits and removes low-quality samples, ensuring a high-fidelity dataset. The curated videos are then segmented into fixed-length clips for training and testing. This process successfully generates over 6,000 high-quality synthetic human-robot paired videos.

This comprehensive methodology allows Mitty to learn complex H2R mappings end-to-end, capitalize on powerful pretrained generative models, and address the critical data bottleneck in robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Mitty demonstrates compelling results across various quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments, establishing its superiority over existing baselines in Human-to-Robot (H2R) video generation.

**A. Quantitative Performance:**
- **State-of-the-Art Metrics:** On both the Human2Robot dataset and the challenging EPIC-Kitchens dataset (including seen and unseen environments), Mitty consistently outperforms baselines across standard video quality metrics (FVD, PSNR, SSIM, MSE) and, critically, achieves high task success rates (SR).
- **Impact of First-Frame Conditioning (HI2R):** The "Human-and-Initial-Image-to-Robot" (HI2R) mode, which conditions generation on the first robot frame, consistently improves performance. It reduces FVD and MSE, while slightly increasing PSNR, SSIM, and SR, indicating more stable and faithful video generation by providing an explicit initial state for the robot. For instance, on Human2Robot, the TI2V 5B model's SR improved from 85% to 91% with first-frame conditioning.
- **Scaling Benefits:** The larger T2V 14B model (where evaluated) shows further improvements, achieving the best overall performance with the lowest FVD and highest SR (e.g., 93% SR on Human2Robot).
- **Robustness to Diverse Environments:** While EPIC-Kitchens presents a significantly more challenging scenario with diverse scenes, complex environments, and moving camera viewpoints, Mitty still achieves strong performance, including high SR even in unseen environments (89% with T2V 14B), demonstrating its generalization capabilities.

**B. Qualitative Evaluation and Baseline Comparison:**
- **High Visual Fidelity and Temporal Coherence:** Mitty's generated videos accurately preserve scene layout and object interactions, producing smooth, temporally coherent robot motions that closely align with the human demonstrations. The robot arm appearance and structure are maintained consistently throughout the sequences.
- **Superior to Multi-Stage Rendering (Masquerade):** Mitty significantly outperforms multi-stage rendering pipelines like Masquerade. While Masquerade might achieve high *embodiment consistency* (due to direct rendering), its task success rate is notably lower (31.5% for Masquerade vs. 84.5% for Mitty in Table 3) due to heavy error accumulation from hand segmentation, keypoint estimation, inpainting failures, and physically implausible renderings (e.g., abnormal hovering, structural issues, collision artifacts). Mitty, benefiting from its end-to-end learning and curated data, avoids these compounding errors.
- **Superior to Generic Video Editing Methods:** Compared to state-of-the-art video editing methods (Kling, Mocha, Aleph), Mitty shows a substantial lead in task success rate (84.5% vs. max 78% for Aleph) and human preference (68% vs. max 4.8%). Generic editing models, which rely on a single robot reference image and prompts, struggle to maintain the robot arm's appearance and structural consistency throughout a video, often leading to deformation and structural errors. Mitty's paired-data training allows it to learn the complex spatio-temporal dynamics required for consistent robot embodiment.

**C. Ablation Studies and Insights:**
- **Crucial Role of Human Reference Video:** Removing the human reference video from the conditioning input (relying only on initial robot frame and text) leads to a clear degradation in all metrics, particularly on the more diverse EPIC-Kitchens dataset. This highlights that the visual demonstration is the most critical conditioning signal.
- **Minor Impact of Task Description:** Removing textual task descriptions results in only minor performance changes, indicating that Mitty relies more heavily on visual cues for action consistency than explicit language instructions.
- **Separate vs. Mixed Training:** Due to substantial differences in tasks and environments between datasets (e.g., single-arm vs. dual-arm manipulation, varying scene complexity), training the full model separately on each dataset yields better performance than mixed training, suggesting that domain-specific learning remains beneficial for current model capacities.

In summary, Mitty successfully demonstrates the feasibility and advantages of an end-to-end diffusion-based approach for H2R video generation, achieving high fidelity, temporal coherence, and strong generalization, while overcoming the limitations of previous methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mitty represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning and generative AI, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

**A. Advancing Scalable and Generalizable Robot Learning:**
The most profound impact of Mitty lies in its potential to revolutionize how robots acquire new skills. By enabling the generation of high-fidelity robot-execution videos directly from human demonstrations, Mitty paves a critical path toward **scalable and generalizable robot learning from human observations**. This directly addresses the long-standing challenge of making robot learning more accessible and less dependent on costly, specialized robot data collection. Robots that can learn robustly from abundant human videos could rapidly adapt to new tasks and environments, accelerating their deployment in diverse real-world settings.

**B. Establishing a New Paradigm for Human-to-Robot Transfer:**
Mitty is the first end-to-end Human-to-Robot video generation framework built upon a Diffusion Transformer. By successfully bypassing the need for intermediate representations like keypoints or trajectories, it eliminates the associated information loss and cumulative errors that plague traditional methods. This shift towards direct visual-to-visual translation via powerful generative models represents a new paradigm for cross-embodiment skill transfer, promising significantly improved visual and temporal consistency.

**C. Mitigating Data Scarcity through Automated Synthesis:**
The development of an efficient and high-quality automated data synthesis pipeline is a crucial contribution. This pipeline leverages readily available egocentric human video datasets (like EPIC-Kitchens) to generate large-scale human-robot paired videos. This innovative approach effectively addresses the major bottleneck of data scarcity in robot learning, opening up vast reserves of human activity data for training sophisticated robotic policies. This could dramatically reduce the cost and time associated with developing new robot skills.

**D. Foundation for Video-to-Policy Inversion:**
While Mitty currently focuses on video generation rather than direct policy control, the high-fidelity, temporally aligned, and semantically accurate H2R videos it produces are an **essential foundation for future video-to-policy inversion**. These generated videos provide richer, more structured supervisory signals than sparse keypoints or trajectories, which can be invaluable for training real robotic policies. Once the generated videos are reliable enough, they hold the potential to be translated into executable control signals, advancing the H2R task toward a complete video-to-policy solution capable of direct robot control.

**E. Contribution to Generative AI and In-Context Learning:**
Beyond robotics, Mitty demonstrates the remarkable capabilities of Diffusion Transformers and In-Context Learning for complex cross-domain video generation. It expands the applicability of ICL from text and image domains to intricate spatio-temporal tasks in video, showcasing the potential for rapid adaptation and data efficiency in generative models. This work contributes to the broader understanding of how large pretrained generative models can be effectively fine-tuned and conditioned for specialized tasks.

**F. Opening New Research Directions:**
Mitty's success opens several promising avenues for future research, including:
- Integrating explicit action or policy prediction into the generative framework.
- Conducting closed-loop experiments in simulation and on real hardware to evaluate direct control.
- Developing more automated and physically grounded evaluation metrics for H2R tasks.
- Exploring more complex manipulation tasks, multi-robot coordination, and tighter human-robot mapping scenarios.

In conclusion, Mitty not only provides a highly effective solution for Human-to-Robot video generation but also acts as a catalyst for future research, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in learning robotic dexterity from human intelligence and observation.
