# Large Language Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaDA introduces a novel 8-billion parameter diffusion model trained from scratch, demonstrating that diffusion models can achieve performance competitive with state-of-the-art autoregressive models in large-scale language modeling. This work challenges the assumption that LLM capabilities inherently rely on autoregressive architectures and provides a solution to the 'reversal curse'.

## Problem

- The prevailing belief that large language model capabilities (scalability, in-context learning) are exclusive to autoregressive architectures.
- Limitations of autoregressive models, such as the 'reversal curse' where models struggle with tasks requiring bidirectional or inverse reasoning.
- Lack of large-scale diffusion models for language that could rival multi-billion parameter autoregressive models in performance.

## Method

- Developed ==LLaDA==, a ==masked diffusion model (MDM)== for discrete token sequences, utilizing a Transformer backbone without a causal mask.
- Trained an **8-billion** parameter LLaDA model from scratch on a **2.3 trillion** token dataset, optimizing a ==principled cross-entropy loss== that upper bounds the negative log-likelihood.
- Implemented flexible iterative sampling strategies for inference, including a ==low-confidence remasking technique== to balance generation quality and speed.

## Results

- LLaDA 8B Base shows strong in-context learning, outperforming LLaMA2 7B Base and performing comparably to LLaMA3 8B Base on **15** zero/few-shot tasks, particularly in math and Chinese.
- LLaDA 8B Instruct, after supervised fine-tuning, demonstrates competitive instruction-following capabilities, comparable to LLaMA3 8B Instruct, despite lacking reinforcement learning alignment.
- The model effectively mitigates the 'reversal curse', achieving **45.6%** accuracy on a poem reversal task, outperforming GPT-4o (**34.3%**) and showing consistent zero-shot performance in both forward and reversal directions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative modeling principles, rather than the autoregressive formulation, are the core drivers of large language model capabilities and scalability.
> - Diffusion models can natively support intrinsic bidirectional reasoning, offering a principled solution to the 'reversal curse' observed in autoregressive models.
> - A principled probabilistic formulation and standard LLM training pipeline enable masked diffusion models to achieve comparable performance to state-of-the-art autoregressive models at an unprecedented scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of large language models (LLMs) is overwhelmingly dominated by autoregressive models (ARMs), which generate text sequentially, token by token, based on the preceding tokens (the "next-token prediction" paradigm). Models like GPT, LLaMA, and their numerous variants epitomize this approach, having demonstrated remarkable capabilities in language understanding, generation, and complex reasoning. The widespread success of ARMs has led to a conventional belief that their autoregressive nature is fundamental to achieving core LLM properties such as scalability, in-context learning, and instruction-following.

This paper directly challenges this prevailing notion. It positions LLaDA as a strong counter-example, demonstrating that diffusion models, traditionally successful in continuous domains like image generation, can also achieve competitive performance in large-scale language modeling. While discrete diffusion models have been explored for language at smaller scales (e.g., GPT-2 scale), their ability to rival multi-billion parameter ARMs has remained largely unverified. Previous work, including some by the authors' group (Nie et al.), indicated that diffusion models might require significantly more computation than ARMs to achieve similar likelihoods, raising doubts about their scalability for language.

LLaDA's contribution lies in pushing the boundaries of diffusion models for text to an "unprecedented size of 8B parameters," trained "from scratch" under the standard LLM training pipeline. By achieving performance comparable to established ARMs like LLaMA3 8B, LLaDA opens up a "new paradigm for language modeling." This work suggests that the underlying generative modeling principles, rather than the specific autoregressive formulation, are the true drivers of LLM capabilities. Furthermore, it addresses known limitations of ARMs, such as the "reversal curse" (the difficulty of ARMs to perform reversal reasoning tasks due to their left-to-right generation constraint), proposing diffusion models as a potential architectural solution. This research significantly broadens the architectural space for future LLM development and shifts fundamental assumptions within the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **challenge the deeply ingrained notion that the core capabilities of large language models (LLMs)—including scalability, in-context learning, and instruction-following—are inherently dependent on autoregressive models (ARMs)**. The authors seek to demonstrate that alternative generative modeling paradigms, specifically diffusion models, can achieve comparable, if not superior, performance at scale.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:
- **Re-evaluating the Foundation of LLM Capabilities:** The authors argue that the success of LLMs stems from "generative modeling principles" (optimizing a model distribution to capture the true language distribution via maximum likelihood estimation) combined with powerful architectures like Transformers, large model sizes, and vast datasets, rather than being uniquely tied to the autoregressive formulation of "next-token prediction." They propose that if these generative principles are maintained, other model architectures should also exhibit similar scaling properties.
- **Exploring Alternatives to Autoregressive Limitations:** While ARMs are highly effective, they possess inherent limitations. A prominent example is the "reversal curse," where models trained on "A is B" struggle to infer "B is A" due to their strict left-to-right generation process. Diffusion models, with their bidirectional or "all-at-once" prediction capabilities, offer a principled way to overcome such directional biases.
- **Scaling Diffusion Models for Language:** Despite growing interest, diffusion models for language have largely remained at smaller scales or demonstrated limited performance compared to ARMs. The authors are motivated to rigorously test the scalability of masked diffusion models (MDMs) in the context of LLMs, providing the first instance of an 8-billion parameter diffusion language model trained from scratch.
- **Establishing a Principled Generative Approach:** LLaDA leverages a probabilistic formulation that optimizes a likelihood lower bound, offering a theoretically sound generative approach for probabilistic inference. This principled foundation, which is similar to how ARMs are grounded in maximum likelihood estimation, is seen as crucial for robust scalability and the emergence of advanced capabilities.

By introducing LLaDA, the authors aim to expand the architectural possibilities for future LLMs, proving that the generative modeling framework itself, rather than a specific directional generation constraint, is the key enabler for intelligent language behavior at scale.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaDA (Large Language Diffusion with mAsking) is a masked diffusion model (MDM) that operates on discrete token sequences. Its methodology is structured around a novel probabilistic formulation, a standard LLM training pipeline, and flexible inference procedures.

#### 3.1. Probabilistic Formulation

Unlike ARMs that define a model distribution *pθ(x)* as a product of left-to-right conditional probabilities, LLaDA defines its model distribution *pθ(x0)* through a forward process and a reverse process, typical of diffusion models.
- **Forward Process:** This process gradually and independently masks tokens in an original sequence *x0* until the sequence is fully masked at *t=1*. For any time *t ∈ (0, 1]*, each token is masked with probability *t* or remains unmasked with probability *(1-t)*. This linear increase in masking probability is analogous to noise schedules in continuous diffusion.
- **Reverse Process:** This process aims to recover the original data distribution by iteratively predicting and unmasking tokens as *t* moves from 1 (fully masked) to 0 (unmasked). The core of LLaDA is a "mask predictor"—a Transformer-based parametric model *pθ(·|xt)*—which takes a partially masked sequence *xt* as input and predicts all masked tokens simultaneously.
- **Training Objective:** The mask predictor is trained using a cross-entropy loss computed only on the masked tokens, weighted by *1/t*. This loss function is theoretically proven to be an upper bound on the negative log-likelihood of the model distribution, making it a principled objective for generative modeling and connecting it to maximum likelihood estimation, similar to ARMs. This contrasts with some prior MDMs that used heuristic objectives.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture and Training

- **Transformer Backbone:** LLaDA employs a Transformer architecture as its mask predictor, similar to existing LLMs. A key distinction is the *absence of a causal mask*, allowing the model to see the entire input for predictions, facilitating its bidirectional nature.
- **Scaling:** The authors trained two variants: 1B and 8B parameters. The LLaDA 8B model was pre-trained from scratch on an "unprecedented" scale for a diffusion language model.
- **Pre-training:**
 - **Data:** A dataset of 2.3 trillion (T) tokens was used, following data protocols aligned with existing LLMs. The corpus includes web data, books, papers, social media, encyclopedias, mathematics, and code, with a specific mix of English, Chinese, and code. Data quality filtering was performed using manual rules and LLM-based approaches.
 - **Compute:** 0.13 million H800 GPU hours were expended, indicating a substantial investment of computational resources, comparable to ARMs of similar scale.
 - **Masking Strategy:** During pre-training, for each sequence *x0*, a random *t ∈* is sampled, and each token is independently masked with probability *t*. A small fraction (1%) of pre-training data was set to a random length to enhance handling of variable-length data.
 - **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer with a Warmup-Stable-Decay learning rate scheduler was used.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** To enhance instruction-following, LLaDA undergoes SFT on paired data *(prompt, response)*. The model learns to model the conditional distribution *pθ(r0|p0)*.
 - **Mechanism:** During SFT, the prompt tokens remain unmasked, while only the response tokens are independently masked with probability *t*. The loss is computed only on the masked response tokens. This is compatible with pre-training as it can be viewed as a specific masking pattern of the concatenated sequence.
 - **Data:** A dataset of 4.5 million instruction-following pairs (1M human-annotated, 3.5M synthetic) was used, covering diverse domains. A dynamic sequence length strategy with *|EOS|* token padding was adopted to enable automatic response length control.

#### 3.3. Inference

- **Reverse Generation Process (Diffusion Sampling):** Starting from a fully masked response for a given prompt, the model iteratively predicts masked tokens. The total number of sampling steps is a hyperparameter, allowing a trade-off between quality and speed.
- **Remasking Strategy:** At each intermediate step from time *t* to *s*, the model predicts all masked tokens. A fraction *s/t* of these predicted tokens are then remasked in expectation to obtain *rs*. The default strategy used is **low-confidence remasking**, where predicted tokens with the lowest confidence are remarked, inspired by annealing tricks in ARMs. Random remasking is also explored.
- **Flexible Sampling:** LLaDA supports various sampling strategies, including autoregressive, block diffusion, and pure diffusion (the default), without requiring further modifications or training.
- **Conditional Likelihood Evaluation:** For evaluation benchmarks, a low-variance Monte Carlo estimation of an equivalent form of the loss function (Eq. 6) is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on LLaDA, particularly the 8B parameter model, reveal several significant findings that challenge conventional wisdom in LLM development:

- **Strong Scalability and Competitive Performance with ARMs:** LLaDA demonstrates impressive scalability, effectively trained to a compute budget of 10^23 FLOPs. Across diverse tasks, including general language understanding (MMLU, ARC-C, PIQA), mathematics (GSM8K), code generation (HumanEval), and Chinese language tasks (CMMLU), LLaDA's performance trend is highly competitive with self-constructed ARM baselines trained on the same data. On certain tasks like MMLU and GSM8K, LLaDA even exhibits stronger scalability (Figure 3). This directly refutes the idea that the autoregressive paradigm is essential for scaling LLMs.

- **Effective In-Context Learning (Base Model):** The pre-trained LLaDA 8B Base model shows remarkable in-context learning capabilities. It surpasses LLaMA2 7B Base on nearly all 15 standard zero/few-shot learning tasks and performs comparably to LLaMA3 8B Base (Table 1). This indicates that the generative modeling principles implemented by LLaDA can effectively capture the knowledge and patterns necessary for in-context learning, an ability previously strongly associated with ARMs. The paper notes LLaDA's advantages in math and Chinese tasks.

- **Impressive Instruction-Following Abilities (Instruct Model):** After Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on 4.5 million pairs, LLaDA 8B Instruct significantly enhances its ability to follow instructions. While it only undergoes SFT (without additional reinforcement learning alignment used by many top ARMs), it exhibits strong instruction-following in case studies, including coherent multi-turn dialogue generation across multiple languages (Table 3, Table 15, Table 16). Performance after SFT remains competitive with LLaMA3 8B Instruct, although slight gaps exist, which the authors attribute to the lack of RL alignment and potentially suboptimal SFT data quality (Table 2).

- **Breaking the Reversal Curse:** LLaDA effectively addresses the "reversal curse," a known limitation of left-to-right ARMs in tasks requiring reversal reasoning. In a poem completion task (forward and reversal), LLaDA demonstrates consistent zero-shot performance in both directions (51.8% forward, 45.6% reversal). Remarkably, it outperforms GPT-4o on the reversal task (45.6% vs. 34.3%), while GPT-4o shows a significant performance drop in reversal tasks compared to forward tasks (Table 4, Table 17). This is attributed to LLaDA's intrinsic bidirectional modeling capabilities, which treat tokens uniformly without an inductive bias towards left-to-right order.

- **Sampling Efficiency and Flexibility:** LLaDA allows a flexible trade-off between generation quality and speed by adjusting the number of sampling steps. While not necessarily faster than ARMs with KV caching, it can achieve comparable performance to LLaMA3 8B Base with 1.5-1.8x higher throughput on some math tasks when generating 8 tokens per forward pass (Figure 5). Ablation studies confirm that pure diffusion sampling generally yields the best performance (Table 7, Table 8), and the low-confidence remasking strategy consistently outperforms random remasking (Table 9). The model's performance is also found to be insensitive to the generation length hyperparameter (Table 10).

- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) Compatibility:** Although not used in main results for fair comparison with ARMs, LLaDA is shown to be compatible with CFG and consistently benefits from its application, indicating potential for further quality improvements (Table 6).

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate that diffusion models can be a powerful, scalable, and versatile alternative to autoregressive models for developing LLMs, challenging foundational assumptions about language model design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LLaDA represents a **pivotal shift in the fundamental understanding and architectural design of large language models**, offering a fresh perspective that deviates from the long-standing autoregressive paradigm.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Challenging the Autoregressive Dogma:** The most significant contribution is the direct refutation of the hypothesis that core LLM capabilities are solely dependent on autoregressive architectures. By scaling a masked diffusion model to 8 billion parameters and demonstrating competitive performance with leading ARMs like LLaMA3, LLaDA proves that generative modeling principles, rather than a specific left-to-right generation mechanism, are the fundamental drivers of LLM intelligence. This finding is a major scientific innovation, expanding the theoretical scope of what constitutes a "large language model."
- **New Paradigm for Language Modeling:** LLaDA proposes a "new paradigm for language modeling" centered on diffusion processes. This opens up a vast unexplored design space for future LLMs, potentially leading to novel architectures that inherit the benefits of diffusion models.
- **Intrinsic Bidirectional Reasoning:** The inherent bidirectional nature of LLaDA, where it predicts masked tokens simultaneously across the sequence, provides a principled solution to known ARM limitations like the "reversal curse." This capability, demonstrated by outperforming even GPT-4o in reversal tasks, highlights a unique advantage of diffusion-based language models in certain reasoning domains.
- **Theoretical Foundation:** The rigorous probabilistic formulation, optimizing a likelihood lower bound, grounds LLaDA in strong theoretical principles for generative modeling, reinforcing its scientific validity.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Diversification of LLM Architectures:** This work could inspire a new wave of research into non-autoregressive LLMs. Future models might integrate diffusion principles, or explore hybrids, to leverage their unique strengths, moving beyond the current architectural monoculture.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Abilities:** The demonstrated proficiency in reversal reasoning suggests that diffusion models could excel in tasks requiring flexible, non-directional contextual understanding, such as complex logical inference, code synthesis, or creative writing where global coherence is paramount.
- **Improved Controllability and Editability:** Diffusion models are known for their strong controllability in other domains (e.g., image editing). This flexibility could translate to language, allowing for more precise control over aspects like style, tone, length, or content modification in generated text, which is often challenging for ARMs.
- **Efficient Inference Opportunities:** While LLaDA's current inference speed is comparable to ARMs without specific optimizations, the inherent parallelism in diffusion sampling (predicting multiple tokens simultaneously) offers significant avenues for future efficiency gains through algorithmic and hardware optimizations (e.g., specialized hardware for parallel token prediction).
- **Multimodal Integration:** The success of diffusion models in vision and their proven applicability to text suggests a powerful foundation for truly multimodal models that seamlessly generate and understand different data types.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors)

The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also represent significant avenues for future research:
- **Fixed Generation Length:** LLaDA currently requires a user-specified generation length, although the authors find it insensitive to this hyperparameter. Developing adaptive generation length mechanisms would be a practical improvement.
- **Resource Constraints for Direct ARM Comparison:** Full 8B parameter ARM baselines with identical training data were not feasible due to computational constraints, limiting the direct comparative analysis at the largest scale.
- **Lack of Specialized Optimizations:** LLaDA has not yet incorporated specialized attention mechanisms, position embeddings, or system-level architectural optimizations (like KV caching), which are common in ARMs for efficiency.
- **Preliminary Inference Optimizations:** More efficient and controllable sampling algorithms (e.g., distillation, advanced guidance mechanisms) are still preliminary for diffusion LLMs.
- **Absence of Reinforcement Learning Alignment:** LLaDA has not undergone alignment with reinforcement learning (e.g., RLHF), which is crucial for maximizing performance and human alignment in many top-tier LLMs.
- **Further Scaling and Multimodal Data:** The model scale and training data are still smaller than the largest ARM counterparts, suggesting further scaling is needed to fully evaluate its potential. Its ability to process multimodal data remains unexplored.
- **Integration with Advanced Techniques:** Its impact on prompt tuning and integration into agent-based systems, and systematic post-training investigations (e.g., O1-like systems), are yet to be understood.

Despite these limitations, LLaDA's introduction fundamentally expands the architectural design space for LLMs, promising a richer and more diverse future for language AI development.
