# Do You Have Freestyle? Expressive Humanoid Locomotion via Audio Control

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboPerform is a unified framework enabling humanoid robots to perform expressive music-driven dance and speech-driven gestures directly from raw audio. It achieves real-time, physically plausible, and stylistically aligned movements on a Unitree G1 robot by eliminating intermediate motion generation and retargeting steps.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion systems are limited to pre-defined motion clips or sparse language commands, lacking expressive, context-sensitive, and improvisational control.
- Traditional audio-driven motion pipelines suffer from cascaded error accumulation, high inference latency, and loose coupling between high-level acoustic cues and low-level robot actuation.
- Robots currently struggle to intrinsically interpret and translate fine-grained auditory information (rhythm, prosody) into nuanced, physically consistent movements, unlike humans.

## Method

- A retargeting-free, unified framework, RoboPerform, directly generates music-driven dance and speech-driven gestures for humanoids from raw audio using a teacher-student paradigm.
- An Audio-Motion Alignment Module infuses kinematic information into audio latents, and a Delta Mixture of Experts (∆MoE) teacher policy learns robust and diverse motion behaviors.
- A diffusion-based student policy with content-style disentanglement is distilled, using a high-level motion latent for content and aligned audio latents for real-time style modulation.

## Results

- RoboPerform achieved high task success rates (e.g., **0.99** for speech-to-gesture, **0.93** for music-to-dance in IsaacGym) and lower error rates compared to baseline methods, demonstrating superior motion tracking.
- The framework enables 'freestyle' capabilities, allowing the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform expressive dance to unseen music and generate rhythm-aligned co-speech gestures in real-time.
- The diffusion-based student policy, utilizing 2-step DDIM sampling, achieved a low inference latency of approximately **5.3** ms per action step, making real-time deployment practical.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expressive robot motion can be effectively decomposed into a 'content' component (core task from a high-level latent) and an 'audio-driven style' component, allowing for flexible and expressive variations.
> - Eliminating explicit motion reconstruction and retargeting in audio-to-robot control pathways significantly reduces cascaded errors and inference latency, enabling real-time, responsive performance.
> - The Delta Mixture of Experts (∆MoE) architecture, using residual fusion and nested conditional subspace partitioning, effectively learns diverse and complementary motion policies for high-fidelity motion tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of expressive and natural movement has long been a significant challenge in robotics. Historically, humanoid locomotion systems have been constrained to either mimic pre-defined motion clips or follow sparse language commands. While effective for basic tasks, these approaches inherently lack the capacity for expressive, context-sensitive, and improvisational control, limiting robots to being mere motion repliers rather than dynamic performers.

This paper addresses a critical gap in this landscape: the direct, real-time control of humanoid locomotion using audio as a primary, implicit control signal. Existing dominant pipelines for audio-driven motion typically involve a multi-stage process: first, generating human motion from audio (e.g., dance or gesture generation), then retargeting this generated motion to a robot, and finally employing a low-level controller for execution. This cascaded pipeline suffers from several systemic issues:
1. **Cascaded Error Accumulation:** Errors introduced at each stage (motion decoding, retargeting, tracking) propagate and compound, degrading both expressive fidelity and physical consistency.
2. **Significant Inference Latency:** The sequential nature of multi-stage processing introduces considerable delays, hindering real-time deployment and rapid interaction.
3. **Loose Acoustic-Actuation Coupling:** Each module is often optimized in isolation, leading to a disconnect between high-level acoustic cues (rhythm, prosody, emphasis) and low-level joint actuation, failing to preserve fine-grained expressions.

Prior research has made strides in various sub-areas:
- **Humanoid Whole-body Control:** Traditional model-based methods offer precision but limited generalization. Learning-based paradigms, often relying on reinforcement learning (RL), achieve human-like motions but require elaborate reward engineering. Works like OmniH2O and KungfuBot have pushed the boundaries of unified multi-motion policies and robust motion tracking.
- **Modality-driven Locomotion:** Recent efforts have explored language-guided locomotion (e.g., LangWBC, RLPF, RoboGhost), treating locomotion as a generative problem conditioned on text. While impactful, these methods do not directly leverage the rich temporal and stylistic information embedded in audio.

"Do You Have Freestyle?" directly confronts these limitations by proposing a retargeting-free, unified audio-to-locomotion framework. It shifts the paradigm from motion replay to responsive performance by treating audio not as a source for explicit motion reconstruction, but as an *implicit style signal* that modulates and refines core motion content. This approach positions the work as a pioneering effort in enabling humanoids to "freestyle" – to improvise and react fluidly to the nuances of music and speech, thereby significantly advancing the expressiveness and autonomy of robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that humans naturally and expressively respond to sound—a drumbeat prompts a step, a melody invites a leap, and spoken emphasis elicits a gesture. This intrinsic human capability for "freestyle" movement, driven by an intuitive understanding of rhythm, phrasing, and intent, is largely absent in contemporary humanoid robots. Current systems are largely confined to pre-defined motion clips or sparse, high-level commands, fundamentally lacking expressive, context-sensitive control.

The paper identifies three critical shortcomings of existing audio-to-motion pipelines when applied to humanoid robots:
1. **Cascaded Errors:** The sequential process of generating human-like motion from audio, then retargeting it to a robot, and finally controlling the robot introduces cumulative errors, degrading motion fidelity and physical consistency.
2. **High Latency:** The multi-stage inference process results in significant delays, making real-time, responsive interaction impractical.
3. **Loose Coupling:** Acoustic cues (like beat, tempo, prosody) are not tightly integrated with low-level robot actuation, leading to a loss of fine-grained expressive details.

To overcome these challenges, the authors set forth the following key objectives for their **RoboPerform** framework:
- **Unified Audio-to-Locomotion Control:** To develop the first framework that directly generates both music-driven dance and speech-driven co-speech gestures from raw audio, unifying these expressive capabilities within a single system.
- **Eliminate Explicit Motion Reconstruction and Retargeting:** To bypass the error-prone and latency-inducing intermediate steps of generating explicit human motion and then retargeting it to a robot.
- **Achieve Low Latency and High Fidelity:** To ensure that the generated movements are both temporally aligned with the audio and physically plausible, while maintaining real-time performance suitable for robotic deployment.
- **Embody "Motion = Content + Style" Principle:** To fundamentally decompose motion generation into a "content" component (the core task, specified by a high-level latent) and a "style" component (audio signals modulating how the task is performed), allowing for flexible and expressive variations.
- **Enable "Freestyle" Capabilities:** Ultimately, to transform humanoid robots from rigid motion repliers into responsive, improvisational performers capable of reacting dynamically to diverse audio inputs.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to fundamentally reframe humanoid control around audio, making robots more natural, engaging, and expressive in their interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboPerform is presented as a novel, unified audio-to-locomotion framework built upon a teacher-student paradigm, designed to eliminate retargeting and directly generate expressive humanoid actions from audio. Its core insight is "motion = content + style," where audio serves as an implicit style signal modulating a high-level motion content.

The methodology consists of three main components:

1. **Audio-Motion Alignment Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To infuse kinematic information directly into the raw audio latent, enabling it to serve as a direct control signal for robot actions without needing an explicit audio-to-motion generator. This ensures rhythmic consistency between audio and motion.
 - **Mechanism:** An adaptor, implemented as a 6-layer Transformer with temporal attention, processes raw audio latents (`l_audio`). This adaptor is trained to align `l_audio` with motion latents (`l_motion`), which are extracted from a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
 - **Optimization:** The alignment process is optimized using the InfoNCE loss, which encourages corresponding audio-motion latent pairs to be close in the embedding space while pushing apart non-corresponding pairs.

2. **Delta Mixture of Experts (∆MoE) Teacher Policy:**
 - **Purpose:** To learn a robust and diverse set of motion behaviors by specializing experts in distinct conditional input subspaces, ensuring high-fidelity motion tracking.
 - **Architecture:** ∆MoE employs a residual mixture-of-experts design with four MLP-based experts and an MLP gating network. It generalizes Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) to continuous, multi-dimensional conditional settings.
 - **Conditional Subspace Partitioning:** The 3D conditional input `c = [c1, c2, c3]` is partitioned into nested subspaces `{S_i}`. Each expert `e_i` models a policy `π_i(a | c_Si)` that depends solely on its assigned subspace. For instance, `e_1` models the unconditional prior, while `e_4` conditions on the full input.
 - **Residual Fusion:** A gating network dynamically assigns weights (`w_i`) to experts. The final action `a` is computed as a weighted sum of conditional increments `∆a_i = a_i - a_{i-1}`, ensuring mutual complementarity and eliminating redundancy among experts.
 - **Training:** The teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), taking robot state observations and reference motion as inputs, and optimized with a comprehensive reward function including terms for joint position, velocity, body state, contact, and various regularization penalties. Curriculum learning (termination and penalty curricula) and domain randomization are used to enhance robustness.

3. **Diffusion-based Student Policy with Content-Style Disentanglement:**
 - **Purpose:** To generate expressive, physically plausible robot actions in real-time by disentangling motion content and audio-driven style.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** The student policy is trained by distilling knowledge from the ∆MoE teacher policy, following a DAgger-like approach. It learns to denoise actions based on conditioning signals.
 - **Content-Style Decomposition:**
 - **Content:** A high-level motion latent, derived from a pre-trained motion generator (e.g., LaMP-T2M) using semantic descriptions ("The person is dancing"), serves as the primary conditioning signal, defining the core task. This latent is fixed for a given task.
 - **Style:** The aligned audio latents (from the audio adaptor) are injected as external style control signals across multiple layers of the diffusion model's backbone via AdaLN. This modulates the denoising process, steering it towards rhythmically stylized motion.
 - **Architecture & Training:** A 4-layer MLP acts as the backbone of the diffusion model. The policy is trained using an `x_0`-prediction parameterization, minimizing mean squared error between the predicted and actual clean actions.
 - **Inference:** For real-time performance, a 2-step DDIM (Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models) sampling schedule is employed.
 - **Deployment:** The student policy, trained in IsaacGym simulation, is deployed directly on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot for real-world validation, as well as zero-shot transferred to MuJoCo for cross-simulator generalization assessment.

The integrated framework ensures a direct, low-latency, and retargeting-free mapping from audio to physically plausible and stylistically aligned humanoid actions, fulfilling the "motion = content + style" principle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboPerform demonstrates compelling performance across both music-driven dance and speech-driven gesture tasks, validated in simulation (IsaacGym, MuJoCo) and real-world deployment (Unitree G1 humanoid robot).

1. **Audio-Motion Alignment Performance:**
 - The InfoNCE-optimized audio adaptor effectively aligns audio latents with motion latents.
 - High retrieval precision is observed: for Music-Motion, R@1 = 66.7, R@2 = 78.8, R@3 = 83.5; for Speech-Motion, R@1 = 64.6, R@2 = 76.5, R@3 = 82.1. The Multimodal Distance (MM-Dist) is low (1.154 for music, 1.232 for speech), indicating strong semantic alignment.
 - The Beat Alignment Score (BAS) is significantly improved with the adaptor (0.197 for BEAT2 and 0.214 for FineDance) compared to a baseline without it (0.163 and 0.176), confirming better rhythmic coherence.

2. **Motion Tracking Performance in Simulation:**
 - RoboPerform consistently achieves high task success rates and lower errors compared to a baseline using explicit motion generation and retargeting.
 - **Speech-to-Gesture (BEAT2 dataset):** In IsaacGym, RoboPerform achieved a success rate (Succ) of 0.99, E_MPJPE of 0.05, and E_MPKPE of 0.04. The baseline showed 0.98 Succ, 0.07 E_MPJPE, and 0.05 E_MPKPE. Similar improvements were observed in MuJoCo.
 - **Music-to-Dance (FineDance dataset):** In IsaacGym, RoboPerform achieved 0.93 Succ, 0.18 E_MPJPE, and 0.16 E_MPKPE. The baseline achieved 0.88 Succ, 0.24 E_MPJPE, and 0.21 E_MPKPE. These trends held in MuJoCo, demonstrating robust cross-simulator generalization.

3. **Qualitative Results and Freestyle Capability:**
 - Qualitative assessments across IsaacGym, MuJoCo, and the Unitree G1 robot show the policy's ability to adhere to audio rhythm, maintain balance during dynamic transitions, and generalize across different physics engines and hardware.
 - The robot successfully performs freestyle dance to unseen music and generates rhythm-aligned co-speech gestures, demonstrating robust generalization and the "freestyle" capability.

4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Audio-driven vs. Pose-driven:** Explicit pose-driven approaches (motion generation + retargeting) incurred significant computational overhead and degraded tracking performance, validating RoboPerform's retargeting-free design.
 - **∆MoE vs. Vanilla MoE:** ∆MoE consistently yielded more accurate tracking, with t-SNE visualizations confirming that its residual fusion mechanism leads to better-disentangled and complementary expert knowledge compared to vanilla MoE. The optimal number of experts for ∆MoE was found to be 4.
 - **With Content vs. Without Content:** Conditioning the policy on a content latent (semantic description) significantly improved tracking accuracy, confirming the effectiveness of the "motion = content + style" decomposition.
 - **With Adaptor vs. Without Adaptor:** The audio adaptor was crucial for enriching the audio control signal with kinematic information, leading to better tracking and rhythmic alignment.
 - **Diffusion Policy Components:** The diffusion policy outperformed a simple MLP policy, especially in generalization and freestyle tasks. `x_0`-prediction was superior to `ϵ`-prediction as an optimization objective. Deterministic DDIM sampling (2 steps) offered the best balance of performance and low latency (approx. 5.3 ms per action step).

5. **Real-World Deployment:**
 - The student policy was successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating real-time music-to-locomotion and speech-to-locomotion capabilities, transforming the robot into a responsive performer.

In summary, RoboPerform delivers physically plausible, stylistically aligned, and real-time synchronized motion directly from audio, showcasing superior tracking performance, higher inference efficiency, and a novel "freestyle" capability for humanoid robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboPerform framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, pushing the boundaries of expressive and autonomous control. Its significance and potential impact can be elaborated as follows:

1. **Pioneering Unified Audio-to-Locomotion Control:** RoboPerform is the first framework to directly utilize audio as an implicit control modality for both music-driven dance and speech-driven co-speech gestures for humanoids. This unified approach eliminates the need for separate systems and bridges a crucial gap between auditory perception and embodied robot action, moving beyond explicit motion reconstruction and retargeting.

2. **Enhanced Expressiveness and Naturalness:** By enabling humanoids to respond to the rhythm, tempo, and prosody of audio, RoboPerform dramatically increases the expressive capabilities of robots. This allows humanoids to perform natural-looking dance movements aligned with music and generate spontaneous, context-sensitive gestures during speech, making interactions more fluid, intuitive, and engaging.

3. **Technical Innovation in Control Architecture:** The introduction of the **∆MoE teacher policy** is a novel contribution, leveraging residual fusion and nested conditional subspace partitioning to achieve diverse and complementary expert learning. Furthermore, the **content-style disentanglement** within the diffusion-based student policy offers an elegant and effective way to modulate foundational motion tasks with real-time auditory cues, addressing a core challenge in generative control.

4. **Performance and Efficiency Gains:** The retargeting-free and latent-driven design of RoboPerform directly tackles the issues of cascaded errors and high inference latency that plague traditional multi-stage pipelines. By achieving low latency (e.g., 5.3ms per action step) and high fidelity, the framework makes real-time, responsive robotic performance practical, which is critical for dynamic, interactive applications.

5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive experimental validation, including cross-simulator transfer to MuJoCo and real-world deployment on the Unitree G1, underscores the robustness and generalization capabilities of RoboPerform. The ability to "freestyle" to unseen music further demonstrates the policy's capacity to adapt and perform in novel situations.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** More natural and empathetic interactions could emerge. Robots capable of expressive co-speech gestures might be perceived as more intelligent, personable, and trustworthy companions or assistants, enhancing user experience in domestic, educational, and care settings.
- **Entertainment and Performance Robotics:** RoboPerform opens new possibilities for robotic entertainers. Humanoids could perform complex, improvisational dance routines alongside human performers, act as engaging presenters, or star in theatrical productions, creating novel entertainment experiences.
- **Assistive Robotics:** Robots could provide more dynamic and nuanced assistance, for example, by using gestures to clarify instructions or express encouragement, which could be particularly beneficial in educational or therapeutic contexts.
- **Fundamental Robotics Research:** This work establishes a new paradigm for expressive humanoid control, inspiring further research into direct multimodal control signals. It encourages the exploration of how robots can integrate various sensory inputs (vision, touch, and audio) to achieve truly human-like and adaptable behaviors.
- **Generative AI in Robotics:** By successfully applying diffusion models and latent space manipulation to real-time physical control, the paper contributes to the broader field of generative AI, demonstrating its power beyond digital content creation to embodied intelligence.

In conclusion, "Do You Have Freestyle?" successfully demonstrates that humanoid robots can move beyond predefined scripts to become dynamic, expressive performers. By directly translating auditory signals into physically plausible and stylistically rich movements, RoboPerform answers its titular question with a resounding "Yes," paving the way for a future where humanoids are more responsive, engaging, and integrated into our sound-rich world.
