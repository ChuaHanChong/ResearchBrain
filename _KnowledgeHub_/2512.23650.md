# Do You Have Freestyle? Expressive Humanoid Locomotion via Audio Control

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboPerform is a unified framework enabling humanoid robots to perform expressive music-driven dance and speech-driven gestures directly from raw audio. It achieves real-time, physically plausible, and stylistically aligned movements on a Unitree G1 robot by eliminating intermediate motion generation and retargeting steps.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion systems are limited to pre-defined motion clips or sparse language commands, lacking expressive, context-sensitive, and improvisational control.
- Traditional audio-driven motion pipelines suffer from cascaded error accumulation, high inference latency, and loose coupling between high-level acoustic cues and low-level robot actuation.
- Robots currently struggle to intrinsically interpret and translate fine-grained auditory information (rhythm, prosody) into nuanced, physically consistent movements, unlike humans.

## Method

- A retargeting-free, unified framework, RoboPerform, directly generates music-driven dance and speech-driven gestures for humanoids from raw audio using a teacher-student paradigm.
- An Audio-Motion Alignment Module infuses kinematic information into audio latents, and a Delta Mixture of Experts (∆MoE) teacher policy learns robust and diverse motion behaviors.
- A diffusion-based student policy with content-style disentanglement is distilled, using a high-level motion latent for content and aligned audio latents for real-time style modulation.

## Results

- RoboPerform achieved high task success rates (e.g., **0.99** for speech-to-gesture, **0.93** for music-to-dance in IsaacGym) and lower error rates compared to baseline methods, demonstrating superior motion tracking.
- The framework enables 'freestyle' capabilities, allowing the Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform expressive dance to unseen music and generate rhythm-aligned co-speech gestures in real-time.
- The diffusion-based student policy, utilizing 2-step DDIM sampling, achieved a low inference latency of approximately **5.3** ms per action step, making real-time deployment practical.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Expressive robot motion can be effectively decomposed into a 'content' component (core task from a high-level latent) and an 'audio-driven style' component, allowing for flexible and expressive variations.
> - Eliminating explicit motion reconstruction and retargeting in audio-to-robot control pathways significantly reduces cascaded errors and inference latency, enabling real-time, responsive performance.
> - The Delta Mixture of Experts (∆MoE) architecture, using residual fusion and nested conditional subspace partitioning, effectively learns diverse and complementary motion policies for high-fidelity motion tracking.
