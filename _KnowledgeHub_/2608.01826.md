# Multi-View Unified Camera Fields: Geometry-Shaped Action-Facing Representations for RGB-Only Multi-Camera VLA Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Multi-View Unified Camera Fields (MVUCF), a training-only framework that injects metric-depth recoverability and cross-view consistency into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model representations. The framework significantly improved metric depth prediction accuracy to 0.44 cm MAE and cross-view retrieval to 64% Hit@1, leading to a 22.4-point increase in success rate on a robotics robustness benchmark and an overall 81.7% success rate in real-robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current multi-camera Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often use simple concatenation of per-view features, leading to weak metric depth and 3D spatial understanding.
- Features corresponding to the same physical point often lack consistency across different camera views in existing VLA models, hindering coherent 3D scene understanding.
- Complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks demand more precise visual perception than many current VLA models, which are often pretrained on 2D semantic data, can reliably provide.

## Method

- The **Multi-View Unified Camera Fields (MVUCF)** framework introduces a ==geometry injection== stage during training that directly shapes the upper hidden states of the VLA backbone.
- This stage utilizes two complementary objectives: a ==depth objective== to establish within-view metric structure and a ==cross-view correspondence objective== to align tokens observing the same physical point across views, using ground-truth depth and camera calibration data.
- After geometry injection, auxiliary heads, depth observations, and calibration data are removed, and the final policy operates with the original RGB-only inference graph without additional computational cost or hardware requirements.

## Results

- Geometry injection reduced the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for metric depth prediction from **4.9 cm** to **0.44 cm** and improved cross-view retrieval Hit@1 accuracy from **0.4%** to **64%** on held-out data.
- MVUCF improved the average success rate of the GR00T-N1.6 base policy by **1.5 points** on the standard LIBERO benchmark and by **22.4 points** on the LIBERO-Plus robustness benchmark.
- Real-robot experiments demonstrated improved success rates on an Agibot Expedition A2 humanoid robot, achieving an **81.7%** success rate (49/60 trials) compared to **66.7%** (40/60 trials) for the base policy across two tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly injecting metric depth and cross-view correspondence supervision into the action-facing latent fields of a VLA backbone effectively improves its 3D spatial understanding and multi-view coherence.
> - External geometry features alone may not provide sufficient patch identity in deeper VLA layers; direct supervision on the VLA's internal states is crucial for shaping action-relevant representations.
> - The depth and cross-view consistency objectives are synergistic, with their combined application yielding greater improvements in VLA performance than either objective alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a class of robotic manipulation systems that integrate pretrained vision-language backbones with learned action generators, demonstrating capabilities in generalizing across various robotic tasks. Examples include RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, π0, and GR00T. However, the paper identifies that contact-rich manipulation tasks impose more stringent demands on visual perception than many current VLA models are designed to meet.

A primary challenge in such tasks is that a single camera often cannot capture all necessary details of the end effector, objects, and their spatial relationships, particularly under occlusion. Multi-camera observations are typically introduced to address this, providing complementary views of the workspace. Current multi-camera VLA policies commonly fuse information by simply concatenating per-view visual representations. The paper highlights two significant limitations of this common approach:
1. **Weak Metric Depth and 3D Structure:** Many VLA backbones are initialized from vision-language models primarily pretrained on 2D semantic data. Consequently, their hidden states may not reliably recover metric depth and the 3D spatial structure present in multi-camera observations.
2. **Lack of Cross-View Consistency:** Simple concatenation does not explicitly encourage features corresponding to the same physical point to maintain consistency across different camera views. Prior research has indicated that visual foundation models can represent the same surface inconsistently across varying viewpoints.

Existing research has attempted to address these limitations individually. Geometry-aware policies have incorporated 3D cues or transferred geometric priors from models like DUSt3R and VGGT into VLA representations. Concurrently, methods like Selfi have improved cross-view consistency through reprojection-based feature alignment, primarily for tasks such as 3D reconstruction. The authors argue that these prior approaches often address separate prerequisites for constructing a unified multi-camera field—consistent representations across views and metric 3D grounding—but their backbone-shaping mechanisms and representation targets differ, preventing straightforward modular combination. This work aims to introduce a unified framework to simultaneously recover both cross-view consistency and 3D structure directly within the backbone representations that feed the action module.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of multi-camera Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, particularly for tasks involving complex contact interactions, by improving the underlying visual representations. The authors identify several motivations driving this objective:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Multi-Camera VLA Fusion:** The common practice of concatenating per-view visual representations in multi-camera VLA systems is insufficient. This method results in action representations that are weak in metric depth information and inconsistent across different camera views, hindering effective manipulation, especially in scenarios with occlusions or fine-grained interactions.
- **Improving 3D Spatial Understanding:** VLA backbones, typically pretrained on 2D semantic datasets, lack inherent mechanisms to reliably extract metric depth and 3D spatial relationships from multi-camera inputs. For robotic manipulation, precise metric depth and 3D understanding of the workspace, including the end-effector, objects, and targets, are crucial for successful task execution.
- **Enhancing Cross-View Feature Consistency:** Features corresponding to the same physical point in the world often exhibit inconsistencies across different camera views in current VLA models. This inconsistency impedes the robot's ability to form a coherent understanding of the 3D scene from diverse perspectives, which is vital for robust control.
- **Developing a Unified and Efficient Solution:** Existing methods that attempt to inject geometric information or improve cross-view consistency often do so in isolation or require modifications to the inference-time graph, such as additional sensors (e.g., depth cameras) or auxiliary modules. The motivation is to create a unified framework that simultaneously addresses both metric depth and cross-view consistency directly within the VLA backbone, while maintaining the original RGB-only inference graph and runtime efficiency. This ensures that the geometric enhancements do not introduce additional computational cost or hardware requirements during deployment.
- **Shaping Action-Facing Representations:** The authors specifically aim to inject these geometric properties into the "action-facing" latent field of the VLA backbone—the representations directly consumed by the action module. This ensures that the improved geometric understanding is directly available for policy learning and action generation.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a training-only framework, Multi-View Unified Camera Fields (MVUCF), that systematically injects metric-depth recoverability and same-world-point consistency into the core VLA backbone representations. This is intended to enable VLA policies to construct a more effective action-facing multi-view camera field, leading to improved manipulation performance, particularly in complex and robust scenarios, without altering the deployment-time inference process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Multi-View Unified Camera Fields (MVUCF) framework is a training-only approach designed to inject geometric properties into the hidden states of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The methodology operates in two distinct stages: an initial geometry injection stage, followed by standard action module training. The framework is built upon the GR00T-N1.6 model.

#### 3.1. Overview

During the geometry injection stage, the VLA backbone receives synchronized RGB observations, along with corresponding depth maps and camera calibration data (intrinsics and extrinsics). This stage employs two complementary objectives: a depth objective to establish within-view metric structure and a cross-view objective to align tokens observing the same physical point from different cameras. These objectives directly update the upper backbone hidden states (specifically layers 8-15) that subsequently feed the action module, creating a shared action-facing latent field across cameras. Crucially, the auxiliary heads, depth observations, and camera calibration information are removed after this stage. The geometry-shaped backbone is then frozen, and the native action module is trained using only RGB observations. This design ensures that deployment retains the original RGB-only inference graph without additional sensors, modules, or inference computational costs.

#### 3.2. Cross-View Geometry and Target Construction

To generate accurate geometric supervision, target locations are constructed in calibrated raw-camera coordinates and then precisely mapped through the complete image-preprocessing pipeline of the backbone before being associated with tokens. This avoids coordinate mismatch issues.
- For a source pixel `u_raw_i` with ground-truth depth `z*_i`, intrinsics `K_i`, and camera-to-world pose `T_i->w`, the pixel is first back-projected into 3D world coordinates (`P_w`).
- `P_w` is then reprojected into a target view `j` to obtain `u_raw_j`.
- A reprojected target (`u_raw_j`) is considered valid only if it lies within the image bounds, has a valid target-frame depth in `[0.05, 5.0]` meters, and satisfies a `z`-buffer consistency condition (`|Z_j - z*_j(u_raw_j)| < 0.10 m`).
- Accepted `u_raw_j` pixels are then mapped to their corresponding positive token center `q*_j` using a composed transform `T_img->grid`. This transform accounts for all backbone preprocessing steps, including letterboxing, resizing, cropping, smart-resize, patchification, and pixel-unshuffling.
- Camera calibration is used exclusively for constructing these training targets and is not present during action training or deployment.

#### 3.3. Coordinate-Query Depth Head

For each camera view `i`, a depth head predicts metric depth `z_hat_i(q)` and its log variance `s_hat_i(q)` at a continuous grid coordinate `q`.
- The head operates on the action-facing hidden grid `F_i` (from layer 15).
- It processes `F_i` through a `GridTrunk` (LayerNorm, Linear, GELU, 3x3 Conv, GELU) to preserve the grid structure.
- Queries are sampled at `3072` continuous grid locations per view: `50%` uniformly and `50%` importance-sampled to emphasize nearby surfaces and depth discontinuities.
- A query feature `r(q)` is formed by concatenating the sampled `1024-D` feature, local `x/y` differences, and the sub-cell phase `φ(q)`. Absolute position is not provided.
- An MLP with `Softplus` predicts metric depth, and a parallel MLP predicts log variance for uncertainty-aware supervision.
- The depth target is obtained via nearest-neighbor lookup from the ground-truth depth map `D_i`, with invalid locations masked.
- The depth objective `L_depth` is a composite loss, including `SILog` (scaled inverse log), inverse depth (emphasizing nearby workspace), metric gradient (preserving local surface variation), seam, and uncertainty terms.

#### 3.4. Cross-View Correspondence Head

A shared projector maps hidden tokens from `F_i` to `128-D` `L2`-normalized embeddings (`e_s_m` and `e_t_n`).
- Global matching logits `l_mn` are computed as the scaled dot product of source and target embeddings (`10 * (e_s_m)^T e_t_n`).
- Instead of a one-hot target, a Gaussian soft correspondence target `y_mn` is used, centered at the geometrically projected token `q*_j` with `σ = 0.75`. This supervises a spatial neighborhood, tolerating sub-token projection errors.
- The correspondence objective `L_cv` includes `SoftCE` for the soft targets, `hard-negative InfoNCE` (drawing negatives from spatial rings, in-batch, and alternate views), and a `margin loss` to separate the projected match from strong non-positives.
- A local branch is also trained to refine sub-token neighborhoods.
- The outer weight of the cross-view loss is annealed during training, emphasizing alignment early on.

#### 3.5. Training and Deployment Costs

Geometry injection runs for `50k` steps (approximately `18` wall-clock hours). Action training schedules are benchmark-specific. The auxiliary heads and geometry-specific inputs are removed before action training, ensuring that the offline geometry injection stage adds no parameters or FLOPs to the final deployment graph.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive set of findings, spanning representation diagnostics, simulation evaluations across multiple benchmarks, an ablation study, and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. Representation Diagnostics

- **Sufficiency of External Geometry Features:** A diagnostic probe on VGGT features showed that while shallow layers preserved cross-view token identity, deeper layers lost discriminability after global attention mixing (Figure 5). This suggests that deep external geometry features alone may not provide the precise patch identity required for action grounding, motivating direct supervision on action-facing VLA states.
- **Impact of Geometry Injection on VLA States:** Held-out data diagnostics demonstrated that geometry injection significantly reshaped VLA representations (Figure 6).
 - **Depth Recovery:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for metric depth prediction decreased substantially from 4.9 cm (native backbone) to 0.44 cm. The proportion of predictions within 2 cm of ground truth increased from 44% to 97%.
 - **Cross-View Correspondence:** Cross-view retrieval Hit@1 accuracy improved from a near-random 0.4% to 64%, indicating that true correspondences became clearly separable from random pairs and localized around the projected target.
- **Propagation to Action Pathway:** Visualization of action-to-image cross-attention during rollouts (Figure 7) showed that geometry injection concentrated attention more tightly around manipulated objects and targets. This qualitatively suggests that the geometry-shaped representation effectively propagates into the action decision-making process.

#### 4.2. Simulation Evaluation

- **Standard LIBERO:** On the standard LIBERO benchmark, MVUCF improved the average success rate of the GR00T-N1.6 base policy from 97.4% to 98.9% (a +1.5 point gain), averaged over three distinct training seeds (Table 1). The most significant improvement (+4.1 points) was observed on the long-horizon LIBERO-10 suite. Performance on shorter suites was maintained or improved.
- **LIBERO-Plus Robustness:** When evaluated zero-shot on LIBERO-Plus, a robustness benchmark with seven perturbation families, MVUCF improved the average success rate by 22.4 points over the base GR00T-N1.6 (Table 2). Improvements were observed across all seven perturbation categories, indicating enhanced robustness to various visual and environmental changes.
- **RoboTwin Evaluation (Across Action Families):** Across a suite of six RoboTwin tasks, grouped into "Touch," "Move and Place," and "Contact" action families, MVUCF raised the overall success rate from 38.6% (Base) to 61.9% (Ours), a +23.3 point gain (Table 3). All six tasks showed improvement, with notable gains in "Move and Place" (+30.5 points) and "Contact" (+28.5 points) families.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on LIBERO-10 revealed the complementarity of the two geometric objectives (Table 4):
- Using only the depth objective resulted in an average gain of +1.12 points.
- Using only the cross-view objective yielded an average gain of +1.33 points.
- Combining both objectives (Full MVUCF) achieved the largest average gain of +4.12 points, demonstrating that the objectives contribute synergistically.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Experiments

Pilot experiments on an Agibot Expedition A2 humanoid robot, involving two tasks (two-cake placement and cup nesting) with 30 trials per task, provided evidence of practical effectiveness (Figure 8). MVUCF achieved an 81.7% success rate (49/60 trials) compared to 66.7% (40/60 trials) for the base policy. Both tasks showed improvements (17/30 to 21/30 and 23/30 to 28/30), supporting the feasibility of RGB-only deployment with geometry-shaped representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Multi-View Unified Camera Fields (MVUCF) framework introduces a method for enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, demonstrating several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Unified Geometric Grounding:** The work provides a unified, training-only framework to inject both metric-depth recoverability and same-world-point consistency directly into the action-facing latent representations of VLA backbones. This addresses a recognized limitation where VLA models, often pretrained on 2D data, struggle with reliable 3D understanding and cross-view consistency in multi-camera setups. The direct shaping of the backbone ensures that geometric information is intrinsic to the learned representations.

- **Improved Robotic Manipulation Performance:** MVUCF consistently demonstrates performance gains across a range of robotic manipulation benchmarks.
 - The 1.5-point average improvement on standard LIBERO, particularly the 4.1-point gain on long-horizon tasks, suggests enhanced capability for complex, multi-step operations.
 - The 22.4-point average improvement on LIBERO-Plus indicates significantly increased robustness to various visual perturbations (camera, robot, language, light, background, noise, layout), which is critical for real-world deployment where environments are dynamic and unpredictable.
 - The 23.3-point gain across diverse RoboTwin tasks spanning different action families (touch, move-and-place, contact interaction) highlights the framework's effectiveness in tasks requiring precise localization, object interaction, and sustained force, which are often contact-rich and challenging.

- **Efficient and Practical Deployment:** A key aspect of MVUCF is its "training-only" nature. After the geometry injection phase, all auxiliary heads, depth inputs, and camera calibration data are removed. The final policy operates with the original RGB-only inference graph, without any additional sensors, modules, or inference FLOPs. This maintains the computational efficiency and hardware simplicity of RGB-only systems while leveraging geometric supervision during training. This approach makes MVUCF a practical solution for deploying enhanced VLA policies in resource-constrained or real-world robotic systems.

- **Enhanced Representation Quality:** The diagnostic probes confirming stronger depth recovery (MAE reduced from 4.9 cm to 0.44 cm) and significantly improved cross-view matching (Hit@1 from 0.4% to 64%) directly validate that MVUCF produces the intended changes in the VLA model's internal representations. The observed tighter action-to-image attention further suggests that these improved representations effectively propagate to the action generation pathway, leading to better-grounded decisions.

- **Foundational Contribution for Generalist Robots:** By enabling VLA models to form more robust and geometrically aware multi-camera representations, this work contributes to the development of more capable generalist robots. It provides a method to enhance existing VLA architectures (like GR00T-N1.6) with crucial spatial understanding, potentially expanding their applicability to a broader range of complex and delicate manipulation tasks.

- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies areas for future exploration, such as enhancing robustness to corrupted or noisy calibration labels and extending the evaluation to broader open-domain scenarios, indicating avenues for continued development in geometric grounding for VLA models.
