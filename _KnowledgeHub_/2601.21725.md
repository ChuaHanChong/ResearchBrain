# Procedural Pretraining: Warming Up Language Models with Abstract Data

> [!summary] Summary
> Procedural pretraining exposes language models to abstract, algorithmically generated data as an initial warm-up phase, which fosters systematic reasoning skills and improves data efficiency in subsequent semantic pretraining. This approach enables models to achieve comparable performance with significantly less natural language, code, or mathematical data.

## Problem

- Current LLM pretraining simultaneously acquires factual knowledge and abstract reasoning abilities, leading to entangled learning.
- Models can rely on superficial patterns and heuristics rather than robust, systematic reasoning due to this entanglement.
- The process of pretraining on vast web-scale semantic corpora is computationally intensive and costly.

## Method

- The researchers introduce **procedural pretraining**, an initial warm-up phase where language models are exposed to abstract, structured data generated by simple algorithms (e.g., ==formal languages==).
- They train GPT-2-type ==decoder-only transformers== from scratch using a standard ==next-token prediction== objective on this procedural data.
- Two experimental settings are employed: an additive setting to measure gains from additional procedural tokens, and a substitutive setting to assess data-efficient replacement of standard pretraining.

## Results

- Procedural pretraining significantly improved performance on diagnostic algorithmic tasks, with Dyck sequences boosting HAYSTACK accuracy from **10%** to **98%**.
- Models achieved equivalent loss with substantially less semantic data in the substitutive setting (e.g., **55%** for C4, **67%** for CODEPARROT) by adding only **0.1-0.3%** procedural tokens.
- Gains transferred robustly to diverse semantic domains (natural language, code, informal mathematics), scaled across model sizes up to 1.3B parameters, and persisted after fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Procedural pretraining provides an "algorithmic reasoning prior" that effectively disentangles skill acquisition and factual knowledge, accelerating and enhancing subsequent semantic pretraining.
> - Useful information learned during procedural pretraining is localized within specific architectural components, with attention layers often critical for structured data and MLPs for natural language.
> - The benefits stem from the precise structural integrity of the procedural data and the resulting specific weight structures, rather than generic attention sharpening or simple initialization effects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current prevalent approach for pretraining large language models (LLMs) involves direct exposure to vast web-scale corpora. This method typically results in the simultaneous acquisition of rich semantic content and the abstract skills necessary for manipulating this knowledge. However, this entangled learning of knowledge and abstract reasoning abilities has been identified as a potential limitation, contributing to models' reliance on surface-level heuristics rather than systematic reasoning procedures.

Prior research has explored alternatives to standard pretraining on natural language. Hu et al. (2025) demonstrated that "pre-pretraining" LLMs on data generated from formal languages could yield more value per token than natural language. Other studies, such as those by Wu et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2024), have successfully utilized data derived from simple algorithms and cellular automata. The established practice of pretraining on computer code is also recognized for its potential to aid in learning compositional and recursive reasoning (Petty et al., 2024).

This work differentiates itself from previous efforts by treating procedural data not as an imitation of linguistic properties or a direct substitute for standard pretraining, but explicitly as a *complementary warming-up stage*. The paper adopts a broader algorithmic perspective, positing that this initial exposure to abstract, structured data can build essential algorithmic scaffolding without immediate reliance on semantic shortcuts. This approach aims to facilitate and enhance subsequent standard pretraining on semantically-rich corpora, suggesting a path towards disentangling knowledge acquisition from reasoning in LLMs. The research aligns with a concurrent study by Shinnick et al. (2026), which indicates that procedural data may also benefit visual learning, suggesting the possibility of modality-agnostic mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that current LLMs exhibit an entanglement between their acquired knowledge and reasoning abilities during pretraining. This entanglement can lead to models favoring surface-level heuristics over systematic, abstract reasoning, thereby limiting their robustness and generalizability. The authors propose an alternative paradigm, drawing an analogy to human cognitive development where individuals first acquire simple logic and mathematics before engaging in higher-order reasoning.

The central hypothesis is that an initial, lightweight pretraining phase using abstract, structured (procedural) data can "warm up" language models by instilling fundamental algorithmic skills. This "procedural pretraining" is hypothesized to provide a foundational scaffolding that subsequently facilitates and improves the acquisition of rich semantic knowledge during standard pretraining.

The key objectives of the study are detailed as follows:

1. **Diagnose Algorithmic Skill Improvement:** To identify which specific types of procedural data (e.g., formal languages, sequence transformations, memory operations) are most effective in improving particular algorithmic skills in language models. This involves using a set of algorithmic tasks as diagnostic tools and investigating whether these improvements are localized within specific architectural components, such as attention or MLP layers.
2. **Evaluate Transfer to Diverse Semantic Domains:** To assess whether the algorithmic skills gained during procedural pretraining translate into tangible benefits for standard pretraining on diverse semantic datasets, including natural language, computer code, and informal mathematics. This objective includes evaluating the performance gains in terms of perplexity and accuracy, as well as the data efficiency of procedural pretraining (i.e., whether it can reduce the total amount of standard data or computational resources required). The study also aims to validate these findings across different model sizes and data scales.
3. **Localize Transferable Pretrained Information:** To precisely determine where the useful, transferable information from procedural pretraining is encoded within the model's architecture. This involves analyzing the distinct roles of attention layers and MLP layers in processing different types of information (e.g., structured data versus natural language).
4. **Explore Combinatorial Procedural Pretraining:** To investigate methods for combining multiple forms of procedural data to leverage their complementary strengths. This objective aims to lay a path for future work on optimizing mixed-data pretraining strategies.

Collectively, these objectives aim to demonstrate that procedural pretraining is a simple and lightweight means to enhance LLM performance and accelerate their pretraining, ultimately suggesting a method to improve the disentanglement of knowledge acquisition and reasoning capabilities in these models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs GPT-2-type decoder-only transformer models, trained with a standard next-token prediction objective. The experimental setup is structured in two distinct pretraining phases: an initial "procedural pretraining" phase, followed by a "standard pretraining" phase on target semantic data.

**Model Architectures:**
- For diagnostic algorithmic tasks (Section 4), small transformers with 2 layers, 4 attention heads, and a hidden size of 16 were used, except for multiplication tasks which used 4 layers, 8 attention heads, and a hidden size of 512.
- For experiments involving larger pretraining corpora (Section 5 and 6), a GPT-2-small variant with 12 layers, 12 attention heads, and a hidden dimension of 768 was utilized. Further scaling experiments involved 350M and 1.3B parameter models.

**Data Setup:**
- **Procedural Pretraining ($T_1$ tokens):** Models are initially trained on $T_1$ tokens of abstract, structured procedural data. When procedural data consists of input/output pairs, the loss is computed solely on the output tokens.
- **Standard Pretraining ($T_2$ tokens):** Following procedural pretraining, models are trained on $T_2$ tokens from target datasets.
- **Baseline:** A model trained with $T_1 = 0$ (no procedural pretraining) serves as the baseline for comparison.
- **Training Settings:**
 - **Additive setting:** $T_2$ is fixed, and $T_1$ is varied to quantify the performance gain from additional procedural tokens.
 - **Substitutive setting:** $T_2$ is reduced while a much smaller $T_1$ is increased, aiming to match baseline performance and evaluate data efficiency.

**Weight Transfer Settings:**
- **Full-model transfer:** All weights from the procedurally pretrained model are transferred. For procedural-to-semantic transfer, token embeddings are reinitialized due to vocabulary differences; for procedural-to-algorithmic transfer, they are initialized to the mean vector.
- **Selective attention-only or MLP-only transfer:** Only the pretrained weights of attention layers or MLP layers are transferred, while other components are reinitialized. This method is used to localize the storage of useful pretrained information within the architecture.

**Procedural Data Generation:**
Procedural data is generated using simple algorithms designed to produce structurally rich sequences where next-token prediction requires precise symbol manipulation, compositional reasoning, or long-range dependency tracking.
- **Sequence Transformations:** IDENTITY (copy), SET (deduplication), REVERSE, UNION (ordered combination), SORT (ascending order), DELETE (token removal).
- **Memory Operations:** STACK (simulating stack push/pop operations).
- **Formal Languages:** K-DYCK (nested balanced parentheses) and K-DYCK SHUFFLE (non-nested sequences).
- **Cellular Automata:** ECA RULE 110 (binary sequence evolution with deterministic Markovian dynamics).
Input sequence lengths vary, often employing curriculum learning (e.g., 2–20 tokens for Section 4 diagnostic tasks) or fixed lengths (e.g., 8, 16, 32, 64 tokens for Section 5).

**Algorithmic Diagnostic Tasks (Section 4):**
Small models are fine-tuned on these tasks to probe specific capabilities:
- **Memory Recall:** HAYSTACK (needle-in-a-haystack task for long-context retrieval).
- **Arithmetic:** ADDITION (summing two 5-digit integers), REVERSED ADDITION (summing two 10-digit integers with reversed input/output for alignment with autoregression), MULTIPLICATION (product of two 5-digit integers).
- **Logical and Relational Processing:** SORTING (sorting a sequence of 10 integers).

**Semantic Pretraining Corpora (Section 5):**
Larger models are pretrained on these standard datasets:
- **Domain-Specific:** WIKITEXT (natural language), JAVACORPUS (undocumented Java code).
- **Larger and Diverse:** C4 (natural language), CODEPARROT (documented code), DEEPMIND-MATH (informal mathematics).

**Mechanisms Exploration:**
To rule out simplistic explanations for observed benefits (Section 4.3):
- **Attention Sharpening:** Evaluated if benefits arise from generic "sharpening" of attention patterns by comparing procedural pretraining with explicit entropy regularization.
- **Initialization Scale:** Assessed if benefits come from merely adjusting initial weight magnitudes by shuffling weights layer-wise or injecting Gaussian noise, which disrupts structure but preserves distributions.

**Combining Procedural Data (Section 6):**
Two techniques are explored:
- **Data Mixtures:** Pretraining on a mixture of different procedural data types (e.g., SET and UNION) with specified ratios, using a prefix token to indicate data source.
- **Weight Mixtures:** Assembling a new model by combining specific components (e.g., attention layers from one pretrained model, MLP layers from another) based on their localized strengths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings across its various experimental stages, demonstrating the utility and characteristics of procedural pretraining.

### 5. Probing Algorithmic Reasoning with Procedural Pretraining (Section 4.1)

Different types of procedural data were found to significantly enhance specific algorithmic skills. For instance, pretraining on Dyck sequences (balanced brackets) improved context recall on the NEEDLE-IN-A-HAYSTACK task, with accuracy jumping from 10% to 98%. Similarly, ECA RULE 110 data benefited REVERSED ADDITION. The observed improvements were contingent on the structural integrity of the procedural data; shuffling sequences, which preserved token distribution but disrupted structure, caused performance to revert to baseline levels, indicating that the learned structure is crucial.

### 6. Localisation of Pretrained Information in Algorithmic Tasks (Section 4.2)

The benefits of procedural pretraining were found to be localized within specific components of the model architecture. Selective transfer of either attention-only or MLP-only layers sometimes outperformed full-model transfer. For example, in the IDENTITY / HAYSTACK task pairing, attention-only transfer resulted in an 80-percentage point improvement over full-model transfer. Attention layers generally proved to be consistent carriers of useful information across tasks, with REVERSED ADDITION being an exception where MLP-only and full-model transfers were superior.

### 7. Ruling Out Simpler Explanations (Section 4.3)

The observed benefits of procedural pretraining were not attributable to superficial optimization effects.
- **Attention Sharpening:** While procedurally pretrained models exhibited sharper attention patterns, explicit regularization to enforce sharper attention in models trained from scratch did not replicate the performance gains, suggesting that precise attention patterns, rather than generic sharpness, are critical.
- **Initialization Scale:** Shuffling weights layer-wise (preserving magnitude distribution but erasing structure) or gradually adding Gaussian noise to pretrained weights dramatically reduced performance. This indicated that the benefits are encoded in the precise structural organization and values of the weights, rather than merely their scale.

### 8. Complementing or Replacing Standard Data (Section 5)

Procedural pretraining consistently improved and accelerated standard pretraining across various semantic domains and scales.
- **Domain-Specific Corpora (Section 5.1):** On WIKITEXT (natural language) and JAVACORPUS (undocumented code), procedural pretraining significantly outperformed baselines. Small amounts of procedural data, such as UNION and SET, were sufficient, and gains were not directly proportional to the amount of procedural pretraining tokens.
- **Larger Pretraining Corpora (Section 5.2):**
 - **Additive Setting:** Incorporating as little as 0.1–0.3% additional procedural tokens (2–4 million tokens) consistently led to superior performance compared to standard pretraining alone on C4, CODEPARROT, and DEEPMIND-MATH datasets.
 - **Substitutive Setting:** Procedural pretraining demonstrated significant data efficiency. Models achieved the same loss values with 55% of the original C4 data, 67% of CODEPARROT data, and 86% of DEEPMIND-MATH data, implying comparable reductions in FLOPs for semantic pretraining.
 - **Scaling:** These benefits persisted for larger models (up to 1.3B parameters) and across larger data sizes (up to 10.5B tokens).

### 9. Persistence on Downstream Tasks (Section 5.3)

The performance advantages obtained from procedural pretraining on perplexity metrics during standard pretraining were found to persist after downstream fine-tuning. This was observed across representative tasks including language modeling (WikiText-103), general language understanding (GLUE), code completion/generation (PY150, MBPP), and commonsense reasoning (ARC-Easy, HellaSwag). For example, a 124M CodeParrot model with procedural pretraining exhibited non-random accuracy on MBPP, whereas its baseline counterpart showed zero accuracy, suggesting the instilled structure is retained.

### 10. Localisation of Transferable Information in Semantic Domains (Section 5.4)

The study further localized the beneficial information for different semantic domains:
- **Pure Code (JAVACORPUS):** Transferring only attention layers yielded the largest gains in perplexity and code-completion accuracy.
- **Natural Language (WIKITEXT, C4):** Transferring only MLP layers was more effective than attention layers. This finding is notable because MLPs are often associated with storing factual knowledge, raising questions about how abstract procedural data enhances MLPs for natural language processing. MLP-only transfer also required fewer C4 tokens to reach the same perplexity (42% vs. 55% for full-model transfer) and improved BLiMP accuracy.
- **Mixed Domains (CODEPARROT, DEEPMIND-MATH):** For documented code and informal mathematics, which combine natural language with structured data, full-model transfer proved most effective, leveraging benefits from both MLP and attention components.

### 11. Combining Multiple Types of Procedural Data (Section 6)

- **Data Mixtures (Section 6.1):** Experiments showed that pretraining on mixtures of procedural data (e.g., SET and UNION) could outperform the best single-source baselines, indicating that the benefits are cumulative.
- **Weight Mixtures (Section 6.2):** A proof-of-concept demonstrated that modularly combining components from different procedurally pretrained models could yield enhanced performance. For example, combining SET-pretrained attention layers with ECA RULE 110-pretrained MLPs resulted in superior performance across multiple algorithmic tasks (HAYSTACK, ADDITION, REVERSED ADDITION, SORT) compared to single-source models.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for procedural pretraining that offers both practical and theoretical implications for the development of large language models. The core significance lies in demonstrating that an initial exposure to abstract, structured data can serve as a potent "warm-up" phase, leading to measurable improvements in downstream performance and efficiency during subsequent standard pretraining.

**Key Contributions and Practical Impact:**
- **Accelerated Pretraining and Improved Performance:** The consistent finding that procedural pretraining accelerates learning and improves performance across diverse semantic domains (natural language, code, mathematics) is a significant practical contribution. This includes gains in perplexity, accuracy on various downstream tasks (language modeling, code generation, commonsense reasoning), and even grammatical competence.
- **Data Efficiency:** Procedural pretraining substantially reduces the amount of semantic data and corresponding computational resources (FLOPs) required to achieve a given performance level. For instance, models could reach equivalent loss values with significantly less data from C4, CODEPARROT, and DEEPMIND-MATH, translating into substantial resource savings.
- **Lightweight and Simple Approach:** The method is described as simple and lightweight, requiring minimal additional computational overhead compared to the extensive resources typically consumed by large-scale pretraining.

**Theoretical Implications and Future Directions:**
- **Disentangling Knowledge and Reasoning:** The work provides empirical support for the hypothesis that algorithmic reasoning abilities can be instilled somewhat independently of rich world knowledge. This suggests a data-driven approach to inject an "algorithmic reasoning prior" into LLMs, potentially mitigating the limitations arising from the entangled acquisition of knowledge and reasoning.
- **Modular Learning:** The localization of specific skills within attention and MLP layers for different types of data (attention for structured data like code, MLPs for natural language) indicates a modularity in how models acquire and store these foundational capabilities. This finding opens avenues for developing more targeted pretraining strategies or even assembling models from specialized components.
- **New Pretraining Paradigms:** The results motivate the exploration of new pretraining paradigms where models are exposed to fundamental, abstract data before encountering complex, semantically rich information, akin to human learning processes.
- **Beyond Surface-Level Explanations:** By ruling out simplistic explanations like generic attention sharpening or initialisation scale adjustments, the research points towards deeper, more structural changes induced by procedural pretraining, prompting further investigation into the underlying mechanistic interpretability.
- **Combining Procedural Data:** The proof-of-concept experiments on combining multiple procedural data types (both via data mixtures and weight mixtures) lay a foundational path for future work on optimizing composite pretraining strategies, potentially leading to even more robust and versatile models. The concept of "efficient initialisation" suggests that the low Kolmogorov complexity of procedural data might eventually lead to deterministic or closed-form smart initialisation schemes for LLMs.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations:
1. The experiments were conducted with relatively smaller models (up to 1.3B parameters) and lower data-model ratios compared to state-of-the-art LLMs, indicating that further scaling is an important next step.
2. The exploration of combining multiple types of procedural data is currently at a proof-of-concept stage, necessitating further optimization and comprehensive study.

In conclusion, this research offers valuable insights into the fundamental learning processes of LLMs, proposing a principled and efficient method to enhance their capabilities by "warming them up" with abstract, procedural data. The findings contribute to a deeper understanding of how reasoning skills can be fostered in artificial intelligence, with potential long-term impacts on the design and training of more capable and robust language models.
