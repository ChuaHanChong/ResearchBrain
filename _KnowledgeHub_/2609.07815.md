# VoT: Vision-of-Thought for Unified Multimodal Representation Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> VoT (Vision-of-Thought) inserts a discrete visual-thinking layer between a frozen vision-language model and a diffusion transformer. A VoT tokenizer quantizes frozen ViT semantic features using VLM alignment, feature reconstruction, and vector-quantization losses, and a three-branch Mixture-of-Transformers autoregressively predicts 1,024 visual plan tokens before rendering. VoT reaches 0.91 overall on GenEval, above FLUX.1-dev and Mogao.

## Problem

- Text-encoder-plus-diffusion pipelines condition latent noise on static embeddings, giving no explicit interpretable intermediate representation bridging linguistic semantics and low-level visual signals.
- Reconstruction-centric visual tokenizers produce pixel-fragment codes misaligned with VLM semantic space, yielding long redundant sequences and unstable autoregressive next-token prediction.
- Query-based conditioning and post-hoc token alignment lack structural consistency with VLM reasoning, so planning stays separate from generation rather than native to it.

## Method

- **VoT tokenizer** quantizes frozen Qwen2.5-VL ViT features via a 1-layer adapter encoder, ==SimVQ== head with 65,536-entry codebook, and 6-layer semantic decoder.
- ==Closed-loop objective== sums ==feature reconstruction MSE==, ==VLM alignment cross-entropy== on paired captions through the frozen VLM, and standard codebook plus ==commitment vector-quantization loss==.
- **Three-branch Mixture-of-Transformers** (text, VoT, diffusion) with shared key-value attention; VoT experts cloned from frozen VLM experts, trained by branch pretraining then VoT-DiT joint training.

## Results

- GenEval overall **0.91**, beating FLUX.1-dev **0.82**, BAGEL **0.82**, and Mogao **0.89**, with counting **0.86** and position **0.85**.
- Internal AutoEval rises from Mogao baseline **53.12** to **70.37** with VoT tokens, and to **76.22** after supervised fine-tuning and RLHF post-training.
- Ablations: reconstruction loss and adapter lift AutoEval **58.67** to **60.98** to **64.64**; scaling teacher from 2B to 7B reaches **69.32**, decoder depth 6 gives **70.37**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantizing a teacher's semantic features rather than pixels gives discrete codes a language model can read, planning-friendly instead of texture-dominated.
> - Lower autoregressive token-prediction loss does not mean better generation: the most predictable codebook scored worst downstream, so predictability is not utility.
> - Stronger teacher encoders yield cleaner, more compositional discrete vocabularies, but the frozen-backbone design only transfers reasoning to generation, not back.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of unified models capable of both deep understanding and high-fidelity generation in artificial intelligence represents a significant area of research. Current text-to-image (T2I) systems primarily utilize a "text encoder plus diffusion decoder" architecture, where linguistic semantics directly modulate continuous latent noise. While successful for certain applications, these models often lack an explicit and interpretable intermediate representation that bridges the gap between high-level linguistic semantics and low-level visual signals. This deficiency can result in a "modality gap," where a model may possess the semantic knowledge for generation but struggle to render it accurately due to difficulties in transferring structured world knowledge from a large language model (LLM) to a diffusion decoder via static embeddings.

Approaches to unification generally fall into two categories: unified autoregressive models (e.g., Show-o, Janus, Unified-IO) and diffusion-based unification efforts (e.g., those grafting understanding modules onto diffusion backbones). Autoregressive models, by treating images as discrete token sequences, aim to handle both tasks within a single transformer architecture. However, they frequently necessitate a compromise between generation fidelity and understanding depth, as visual codes optimized for reconstruction can conflict with semantics-oriented language modeling. Diffusion-based methods, on the other hand, face challenges in effectively transferring rich, structured VLM knowledge to the decoder through static conditioning alone.

Existing visual tokenization strategies, such as VQ-VAE and VQGAN, are primarily optimized for reconstruction fidelity, compressing images into "pixel codes" that emphasize local textures and low-level details. These reconstruction-centric tokens are often misaligned with the semantic representation space of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), leading to long, semantically redundant sequences that behave more like compressed pixel fragments than meaningful semantic units. This characteristic can hinder stable autoregressive modeling and semantic coherence when VLMs attempt to predict such tokens for generation.

Vision-of-Thought (VoT) positions itself within this landscape by introducing a discrete visual-thinking layer as a semantic bridge. This layer explicitly decouples semantic planning from pixel rendering, aiming to preserve the strengths of both VLMs for multimodal understanding and diffusion transformers for high-quality image synthesis. By generating discrete visual tokens autoregressively, VoT attempts to extend the VLM's reasoning capabilities into the visual domain, akin to "visual chain-of-thought" planning, which differs from direct text-to-image mapping or recent query-based conditioning approaches that lack structural consistency with VLMs for complex reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework that can achieve deep multimodal understanding and high-fidelity image generation by addressing the current limitations in text-to-image systems. The overarching motivation is the creation of AI systems capable of seamlessly generating images from complex instructions, reasoning about visual data, and visualizing multimodal analyses through generated outputs. The authors highlight the potential for such unified systems, citing developments like GPT-4o as indicators of this trajectory.

Specific motivations and objectives include:
- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** To overcome the "modality gap" prevalent in current text-to-image (T2I) paradigms, where the rich, structured world knowledge embedded in a large language model often fails to transfer effectively to the diffusion decoder through static embeddings. This bottleneck leads to situations where the model conceptually "knows" what to generate but struggles to "render" it accurately.
- **Introducing an Explicit, Interpretable Intermediate Representation:** To establish a discrete visual-thinking layer, termed Vision-of-Thought (VoT), between Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and diffusion transformers (DiTs). This layer is designed to allow VLMs to function as multimodal planners, generating discrete VoT tokens that represent high-level visual plans (e.g., objects, layouts) prior to pixel rendering. This objective aims to provide a structured, interpretable interface for controlled generation.
- **Developing a VLM-Aligned VoT Tokenizer:** To design and train a specialized VoT tokenizer that operates within the VLM semantic space, rather than quantizing raw pixels. This tokenizer is intended to generate tokens that are semantically readable by the VLM while preserving the necessary visual information for downstream generation. This involves a closed-loop objective combining VLM alignment, feature reconstruction, and vector-quantization losses to overcome the limitations of traditional reconstruction-centric tokenizers.
- **Enabling Visual Chain-of-Thought Planning:** To allow the model to perform "visual chain-of-thought" by treating visual generation as a next-token prediction task. This capability aims to enable the model to plan image composition step by step before rendering, effectively extending the VLM's reasoning ability into the visual domain.
- **Achieving Efficient Unified Model Integration:** To propose an efficient architectural integration of VoT into modern unified models, specifically by extending the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) paradigm. This involves creating a VoT branch by copying a subset of pretrained VLM experts and freezing the original VLM branch to maintain multimodal understanding capabilities and ensure efficient training with minimal additional computational cost.
- **Improving Semantic Alignment and Controllability:** To demonstrate that the VoT framework enhances semantic alignment between textual prompts and generated images, and provides a more structured interface for interpretable and controllable image generation, particularly for complex compositional tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VoT framework is implemented through a three-stage training process designed to create a discrete visual-thought layer that facilitates semantic planning before visual synthesis.

**Stage 0: VLM-Aligned VoT Tokenizer Training**
The cornerstone of VoT is a discrete visual representation that is compatible with a VLM's semantic space.
- **Semantically Aligned Tokenizer Architecture:**
 - **Foundation & Adapter Encoder:** A frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) from a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL ViT) serves as $E_{frozen}$ to preserve robust semantic features. A lightweight, trainable adapter $E_{adapt}$ (a single-layer ViT block) is appended to bridge the gap between continuous ViT features and the discrete latent space. This adapter projects foundation features onto a manifold suitable for quantization.
 - **SimVQ Head:** A Similarity-based Vector Quantization (SimVQ) mechanism is employed, featuring a learnable codebook $C = \{c_k\}_{K=1}^K \subset \mathbb{R}^d$. Feature vectors are quantized by selecting the codebook entry with the minimum Euclidean distance. An entropy loss regularizes similarity logits to prevent codebook collapse. Gradients are propagated via a straight-through estimator.
 - **Deep Semantic Decoder:** A 6-layer transformer decoder $D$ reconstructs continuous semantic features from the quantized tokens. This deep decoder is intended to invert quantization more effectively in the teacher feature space, allowing tokens to focus on language-grounded semantics while the decoder smooths quantization noise.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Feature Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** A token-wise mean-squared error between the reconstructed features from the decoder and the original frozen teacher ViT features ($f = E_{frozen}(x)$). This loss ensures visual grounding.
 - **VLM Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$):** This objective ensures the VLM can "read" and utilize the discretized representation. The tokenizer-produced visual embeddings are fed into the frozen VLM, and the standard language-model cross-entropy loss is minimized on the paired caption.
 - **Vector-Quantization Loss ($L_{VQ}$):** A standard VQ objective, combining a codebook loss and a commitment loss, used to stabilize codebook learning and prevent collapse.
 - **Total Objective:** The tokenizer is trained with a combined loss: $L_{tok} = L_{rec} + \lambda_{align}L_{align} + \lambda_{VQ}L_{VQ}$. During training, the teacher ViT encoder and VLM remain frozen.

**Stage 1: VoT Branch Pretraining**
Following tokenizer training, the VoT branch is integrated into a unified multimodal architecture.
- **Unified Three-Branch MoT Backbone:** The framework extends the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) paradigm to incorporate three specialized pathways: text (multimodal understanding), VoT (visual token prediction), and diffusion (image synthesis). Each branch ($m \in \{\text{txt, vot, diff}\}$) has independent layer normalization and projections to compute queries, keys, and values. A global context is formed by concatenating key-value pairs from all branches, over which unified attention operates, modulated by branch-specific masks. Causal masking is used for text and VoT branches for autoregressive generation, while the diffusion branch uses bidirectional attention. Outputs are processed through branch-specific feed-forward networks.
- **Initialization:** The backbone is initialized from a pretrained Mogao-14B MoT model. The VoT experts are initialized by copying a subset of pretrained VLM experts, while VoT-specific components (visual token embedding table and prediction head) are randomly initialized.
- **Objective:** Only the VoT branch is warmed up, with the VLM and diffusion branches frozen. The training objective is an autoregressive cross-entropy loss, predicting 1,024 VoT tokens per image from a 65,536-way codebook, using discrete supervision targets extracted by the Stage 0 tokenizer.

**Stage 2: VoT-DiT Joint Training**
In the final stage, the VoT and diffusion branches are jointly optimized.
- **Joint Optimization:** Starting from the Stage 1 checkpoint, parameters in both VoT and diffusion pathways, including their respective experts and shared components, are updated. The VLM branch remains frozen. This joint training allows the diffusion branch to leverage VoT conditioning more effectively, and the VoT branch to refine its representations for improved generation.
- **Combined Objective:** The optimization uses a combined objective comprising the VoT autoregressive prediction loss and the diffusion velocity prediction loss. The diffusion loss backpropagates through shared attention layers, strengthening the coupling between token prediction and rendering quality.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance Support:** Structured conditional dropout is applied during training: VoT tokens are dropped with 0.5 probability, and text tokens with 0.1 probability when VoT is present. Additionally, 0–20% of VoT key-value pairs are randomly masked during attention to prevent over-reliance by the diffusion branch and enhance robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VoT focused on text-to-image generation benchmarks and an ablation study of tokenizer components.

**Text-to-Image Generation**

- **Quantitative Comparison (GenEval Benchmark):** VoT achieved an overall score of 0.91 on the public GenEval benchmark, surpassing both leading generation-specialized models (e.g., FLUX.1-dev at 0.82) and recent unified multimodal models (e.g., BAGEL at 0.82, Mogao at 0.89). Notably, VoT demonstrated advantages in compositionally demanding tasks such as counting, scoring 0.86 compared to Mogao's 0.83. These results are interpreted as supporting the hypothesis that the explicit discrete VoT layer facilitates the transfer of VLM's planning and reasoning capabilities to the generation process, which aids in satisfying structural constraints.
- **Quantitative Comparison (Internal Instruction-Following Benchmark - AutoEval):** On an internal DreamBench suite, VoT achieved an AutoEval score of 76.22%. This performance indicates strong prompt-image coherence and exceeded baseline models such as Seedream 4.0 and GPT-Image-1.
- **Visual Comparison:** Qualitative assessments, particularly in scenarios involving complex layout control and counterfactual generation, showed that VoT could accurately render precise attributes (e.g., facial expressions) and surreal scenes (e.g., "a horse riding an astronaut"). In comparison, GPT-Image-1 reportedly exhibited issues such as attribute leakage or over-reliance on training distribution biases.

**Ablation Study**

The ablation study investigated the contribution of various components within the VoT tokenizer training, focusing on six configurations (Exp.A–F), with results measured by AutoEval scores, VLM alignment loss, and VoT autoregressive loss.

- **Benefits of Reconstruction and Adapters:**
 - The inclusion of a feature reconstruction loss (Exp.B vs. Exp.A) reduced the VLM alignment loss (LM loss) from approximately 1.93 to 1.79 and improved AutoEval from 58.67 to 60.98.
 - Further adding a 1-layer trainable adapter (Exp.C vs. Exp.B) further decreased LM loss to approximately 1.76 and boosted AutoEval to 64.64. These components appear to stabilize token learning and reduce the mismatch with frozen ViT features.
- **VoT Autoregressive Loss vs. Tokenizer Quality:** While Exp.A, which lacked reconstruction and adapter, achieved the lowest final autoregressive loss (approximately 5.08), its AutoEval score (58.67) was significantly lower than configurations with reconstruction and adapters (Exp.B and Exp.C). This suggests that lower autoregressive predictability for the tokens does not necessarily correlate with higher utility for downstream generation, and that reconstruction and the adapter, while potentially increasing autoregressive difficulty, improve semantic alignment and downstream utility by preserving more information.
- **Effect of Teacher VLM Scale:** Scaling the teacher VLM from Qwen2-VL 2B (Exp.C) to Qwen2.5-VL 7B (Exp.D) resulted in a substantial drop in LM loss (from approximately 1.76 to 1.48) and an increase in AutoEval (from 64.64 to 69.32). Exp.D also achieved the lowest VoT autoregressive loss among the reconstruction-based settings. This finding suggests that a more capable teacher VLM provides cleaner semantic supervision, leading to a more structured and autoregressive-friendly visual lexicon.
- **Benefits of VoT and Post-Training:** Directly comparing the original Mogao baseline (AutoEval 53.12) with the VoT model after training (Exp.E), the introduction of VoT tokens resulted in a notable improvement in AutoEval to 70.37. Further post-training, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (Exp.F), incrementally improved the score to 76.22, indicating that post-training techniques complement the benefits derived from VoT tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vision-of-Thought (VoT) framework introduces an approach to unifying multimodal understanding and generation, with several implications for the field of artificial intelligence.

Firstly, VoT addresses a fundamental challenge in current text-to-image systems: the modality gap between high-level linguistic semantics and low-level visual signals. By introducing a discrete visual-thinking layer, VoT creates an explicit and interpretable intermediate representation. This layer allows a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to function as a multimodal planner, generating structured visual plans in the form of VoT tokens before the diffusion decoder renders pixels. This decoupling of semantic reasoning from pixel-space rendering is significant because it aims to reduce the "bottleneck" where models "know" what to generate but struggle to "render" it accurately.

Secondly, the specialized VLM-Aligned VoT Tokenizer represents a departure from traditional reconstruction-centric visual tokenizers. By optimizing tokens in the VLM semantic space with a closed-loop objective (combining VLM alignment, feature reconstruction, and vector-quantization losses), VoT ensures that the discrete tokens are semantically readable by the VLM while preserving necessary visual information. This design choice contributes to improved semantic alignment and is demonstrated to be more effective than simply optimizing for pixel fidelity, especially for complex compositional tasks. The observation that scaling the teacher VLM consistently enhances tokenizer quality and downstream alignment suggests a pathway for future improvements through leveraging more powerful foundation models.

Thirdly, the integration of VoT into a unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, by cloning VLM experts for the VoT branch and freezing the VLM branch, offers an efficient strategy for training. This architecture preserves the VLM's existing multimodal understanding capabilities while enabling dedicated training for visual planning. The concept of "visual chain-of-thought," where the model plans image composition step-by-step, provides enhanced interpretability and controllability in generation, which is a notable advancement over direct text-to-image mapping or purely query-based approaches.

The experimental results, particularly the superior performance on the GenEval benchmark for compositional tasks (such as counting and spatial relations), indicate that VoT can lead to more accurate and coherent image generation, aligning better with complex textual instructions. The qualitative examples further illustrate the capacity for generating images with precise attributes and understanding surreal scenarios, areas where other models have shown limitations.

In conclusion, VoT contributes to the development of next-generation multimodal agents by offering a mechanism for coherent visual thinking and creation. By explicitly structuring the generative process through discrete visual thoughts, the framework provides a pathway towards more interpretable, controllable, and semantically aligned multimodal AI systems. While the current MoT architecture uses a frozen VLM backbone and parallel experts, limiting it to a unidirectional reasoning-to-generation transfer, the authors acknowledge this limitation and suggest exploring bidirectional optimization strategies in future work, which could further enhance the model's overall understanding and generation capabilities.
