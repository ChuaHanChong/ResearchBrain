# 3D Human Interaction Generation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey unifying 3D human interaction generation into three categories, human-scene, human-object, and human-human interaction, by tracing shared foundations (body/entity representations, motion capture, GAN/VAE/diffusion generators) and consolidating methods, datasets, and evaluation metrics into one taxonomy, revealing data scarcity and physical implausibility as the field's core unresolved bottlenecks.

## Problem

- Human-only motion generation ignores environmental context, since real actions are responses to scenes, objects, and other people, not isolated behaviors.
- No prior survey unifies human-scene, human-object, and human-human interaction generation under one taxonomy despite shared representation and generative foundations.
- Existing interaction datasets are small, cover few entity categories, and lack natural-language intent annotations, limiting controllability and generalization.

## Method

- A three-way ==taxonomy== organizing generation methods by interactive entity type, human-scene interaction, human-object interaction, and human-human interaction, each further split into generation, text-conditioned, and prediction sub-tasks.
- A foundational-technology chapter comparing skeletal versus parametric (==SMPL==/==SMPL-X==) human representations, six entity representation formats, and inertial/optical/hybrid motion-capture pipelines.
- A consolidated cross-task benchmarking framework (Tables 1-2) tabulating methods by venue, model family, and dataset, plus a three-group evaluation-metric taxonomy: ==motion quality==, ==physical plausibility==, user studies.

## Results

- The survey catalogs roughly **80** representative interaction-generation methods spanning **2019-2025** across the three HSI/HOI/HHI categories in a single comparison table.
- Nearly **60** datasets are compiled across the three tasks, spanning collection methods (inertial, marker-based, markerless, synthetic, hybrid) and scales from hundreds to millions of frames.
- ==Diffusion==-based architectures dominate the most recent entries in all three categories, replacing earlier GAN- and VAE-based approaches as the prevailing generative backbone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical plausibility failures, sliding, penetration, floating, arise because data-driven generators learn distributional patterns rather than true physical laws like mass and resistance.
> - Progress across HSI, HOI, and HHI is bottlenecked by the same root cause, insufficient large-scale, richly annotated real-world 3D data, not by generative model choice.
> - The field is converging toward unified multi-entity frameworks that jointly model scene, object, and multi-human context rather than treating each interaction type in isolation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Human actions rarely occur in isolation—they are typically performed in relation to environments, objects, or other humans. This contextual nature of human behavior is driving a significant shift in the field of 3D human motion generation, moving from isolated motion synthesis to interaction-aware generation. This survey paper by Fan, Huang, Cai, and Du from Wuhan University provides the first comprehensive overview of data-driven approaches for generating realistic 3D human interactions.

The field of 3D human interaction generation bridges several research areas including computer vision, computer graphics, and embodied AI. As shown in Figure 1, the process involves three key components: interactive entities (scenes, objects, or other humans), generative AI methods, and the resulting interaction motions. The applications for this technology are vast, spanning virtual reality, gaming, animated media, robot learning, and embodied AI.

This survey categorizes the research into three main sub-tasks based on the type of interactive entity:
1. Human-Scene Interaction (HSI)
2. Human-Object Interaction (HOI)
3. Human-Human Interaction (HHI)

### 2. Foundational Technologies

Before diving into specific interaction types, it's essential to understand the fundamental technologies that enable 3D human interaction generation.

#### 2.1. 3D Model Representations

Various 3D model representation methods are employed to capture humans, objects, and scenes:

- **Skeletal Models**: Represent the human body as a kinematic tree of joints and bones, making them computationally efficient for animation but limited in capturing surface details.
- **Parametric Models**: Models like SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) represent the human body using learned shape and pose parameters, allowing for realistic deformation.
- **Mesh Models**: Provide detailed surface representation through vertices and faces, commonly used for scenes and objects.
- **Point Clouds**: Unstructured collections of 3D points sampled from surfaces, useful for representing complex environments.
- **Voxels**: 3D grid structures that divide space into discrete volumetric elements, analogous to 2D pixels.
- **Implicit Representations**: Define surfaces as level sets of continuous functions, enabling smooth interpolation between points.

Each representation has trade-offs in terms of detail, computational efficiency, and ease of manipulation, with the choice depending on the specific interaction generation task.

#### 2.2. Motion Capture Methods

To develop data-driven approaches for human interaction generation, high-quality motion data is essential:

- **Optical Systems**: Use cameras to track markers attached to subjects, providing high accuracy but requiring specialized studio environments.
- **Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs)**: Wearable sensors that measure acceleration and orientation, allowing for capture outside controlled environments.
- **Hybrid Systems**: Combine optical and inertial methods to leverage the strengths of both approaches.
- **Markerless Systems**: Computer vision-based approaches that track human motion without physical markers, improving accessibility.

Recent advances in motion capture technology have enabled the collection of larger and more diverse datasets, driving progress in data-driven interaction generation.

#### 2.3. Generative Models

Deep learning-based generative models form the backbone of modern 3D human interaction generation:

- **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**: Consist of generator and discriminator networks that compete in a minimax game, with the generator learning to produce realistic outputs that the discriminator cannot distinguish from real data.
- **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)**: Learn a compressed latent representation of the data distribution, allowing for controlled generation through sampling from this latent space.
- **Diffusion Models**: Generate data by gradually denoising random noise through a series of steps, providing high-quality and diverse outputs.

The survey notes a recent trend toward diffusion models, which have shown remarkable success in generating realistic and diverse human interaction motions.

### 3. Human-Scene Interaction (HSI)

Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) generation focuses on creating realistic motions of humans interacting with their environment. The key challenges include:

1. **Understanding scene geometry and semantics**: Algorithms must comprehend the 3D structure and functional properties of different scene elements.
2. **Generating physically plausible interactions**: The human motion must respect physical constraints like gravity and avoid penetration with scene elements.
3. **Creating natural and diverse behaviors**: The generated interactions should exhibit the variety and naturalness of real human behavior.

Early approaches to HSI generation used contact-based methods, where scene contacts guide motion synthesis. For example, SAMP (Scene-Aware Motion Prediction) identified potential contact points between humans and the environment to generate realistic sitting or lying down motions.

More recent methods employ full scene representations, often using neural networks to learn the relationship between scene geometry and human motion. For instance, Wang et al. (2021) proposed a system that takes a 3D scene and interaction type as input to generate diverse human motions consistent with the scene geometry.

Text-conditioned HSI generation has emerged as a promising direction, allowing users to specify interactions using natural language descriptions. SceneDiffuser (2023) pioneered this approach, using diffusion models to generate human motions conditioned on text and scene geometry.

There's also growing interest in extensive HSI generation, which aims to create longer and more complex interaction sequences involving multiple scene elements. This reflects the reality that humans typically interact with multiple parts of their environment in sequence.

### 4. Human-Object Interaction (HOI)

Human-Object Interaction (HOI) generation focuses on creating realistic motions of humans manipulating or interacting with objects. This area presents unique challenges:

1. **Modeling object affordances**: Understanding how object properties constrain possible interactions.
2. **Generating precise manipulation**: Ensuring accurate hand positioning and grasping motions.
3. **Creating physically plausible object movements**: The object should move naturally in response to human actions.

Early HOI generation methods relied on motion graphs and databases of captured interactions. More recent approaches employ deep learning to generate novel interactions based on object geometry and semantics.

GRAB (2020) was a landmark dataset and method for HOI generation, capturing detailed hand-object interactions with high precision. This enabled the development of more sophisticated models capable of generating realistic grasping and manipulation actions.

Recent advances include CHOIS (2024), which can generate diverse object interaction sequences based on instruction, and HOI-Diff (2022), which uses diffusion models to generate interactions with various objects while maintaining physical plausibility.

The field is currently moving toward approaches that can handle arbitrary objects without requiring extensive training data for each specific object type. Methods like NIFTY (2024) can generate interactions with novel objects based on their geometric properties.

### 5. Human-Human Interaction (HHI)

Human-Human Interaction (HHI) generation is perhaps the most complex sub-task, as it involves coordinating the motions of multiple humans engaged in social interaction. The key challenges include:

1. **Understanding social dynamics**: Generating motions that reflect social norms and interpersonal relationships.
2. **Creating coordinated movements**: Ensuring that the actions of multiple humans are synchronized and responsive to each other.
3. **Capturing the diversity of social interactions**: From formal handshakes to dance partnerships to competitive sports.

Early approaches to HHI generation used coupled motion graphs to coordinate the actions of multiple characters. Recent deep learning methods have significantly advanced the field.

InterGen (2024) pioneered the use of diffusion models for generating diverse two-person interactions, while FreMotion (2024) extended this to support interactive generation where the motion of one human is fixed, and the model generates a compatible motion for the second human.

Text-guided HHI generation has also emerged as an active research area, allowing the specification of complex social interactions through natural language. TriDi (2024) uses diffusion models to generate three-person interactions based on textual descriptions, representing a significant step toward more complex social scenarios.

The field is currently evolving toward approaches that can model reactive behaviors, where one human's motion is generated in response to another's actions, capturing the dynamic and responsive nature of real human interactions.

### 6. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics

The development of 3D human interaction generation methods relies heavily on high-quality datasets. The survey identifies several key datasets for each interaction type:

- **HSI Datasets**: PROX, SAMP, HUMANISE, CIRCLE
- **HOI Datasets**: GRAB, GraspPose, BEHAVE
- **HHI Datasets**: InterHuman, CMU Panoptic, Aerial People, UBody

These datasets vary in scale, diversity, and capture methodology, with recent efforts focusing on collecting larger and more diverse interaction data.

Evaluating generated interactions presents unique challenges. Common metrics include:

- **Kinematic Metrics**: Measure the smoothness and realism of the generated motion (e.g., joint velocities, accelerations).
- **Physical Plausibility Metrics**: Assess whether the interaction respects physical constraints (e.g., penetration depth, contact consistency).
- **Semantic Alignment Metrics**: Evaluate how well the generated interaction matches the intended semantic meaning.
- **Human Evaluation**: Subjective assessment of realism and naturalness by human observers.

The field is moving toward more comprehensive evaluation frameworks that consider both quantitative metrics and human perception of interaction quality.

### 7. Challenges and Future Directions

Despite significant progress, several challenges remain in 3D human interaction generation:

1. **Data Limitations**: Current datasets often lack diversity in terms of interaction types, participants, and environments.
2. **Physical Realism**: Ensuring generated interactions respect physical laws remains challenging, especially for complex interactions.
3. **Controllability**: Providing intuitive user control over generated interactions while maintaining realism is an ongoing challenge.
4. **Multi-entity Interactions**: Most current methods focus on interactions with a single entity; handling multiple entities simultaneously is an open problem.

The survey identifies several promising future research directions:

- **Multi-modal Conditioning**: Integrating multiple input modalities (text, images, partial motion) to provide richer control over generated interactions.
- **Physically-informed Generation**: Incorporating physics-based constraints and simulation to enhance the realism of generated interactions.
- **Long-term Interaction Planning**: Moving beyond short interaction snippets to generate extended interaction sequences with coherent goals.
- **Cross-entity Interaction**: Developing unified frameworks that can handle HSI, HOI, and HHI within a single model architecture.

### 8. Conclusion

The survey provides a comprehensive overview of the rapidly evolving field of 3D human interaction generation, highlighting the transition from isolated motion synthesis to context-aware interaction generation. This shift mirrors our understanding of human behavior as inherently contextual and interactive.

As shown in Figure 5, the field has experienced explosive growth since 2020, with a particularly notable acceleration in 2023-2024. The emergence of powerful generative models, especially diffusion-based approaches, has been a key driver of recent advances.

The integration of 3D human interaction generation with other fields such as natural language processing, robotics, and embodied AI presents exciting opportunities for future research. As these methods continue to mature, they will enable more immersive virtual experiences, more capable robotic systems, and deeper insights into human behavior.

By synthesizing current research and identifying key challenges, this survey serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners working at the intersection of computer vision, graphics, and human behavior modeling. The field of 3D human interaction generation stands at an exciting inflection point, with the potential to transform how we create and interact with digital humans across numerous applications.
