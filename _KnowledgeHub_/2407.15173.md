# Rethinking Domain Adaptation and Generalization in the Era of CLIP

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework rethinks domain adaptation and generalization for CLIP models by leveraging domain-specific prompts, pseudo-labeling on target data, and parameter-efficient tuning to reduce reliance on labeled source data. It demonstrates improved zero-shot recognition and enhanced generalization across various domains.

## Problem

- Performance degradation of computer vision models when encountering data distributions different from their training data (domain shift).
- Traditional domain adaptation methods often rely heavily on labeled source domain data to learn transferable features.
- The assumptions underlying existing domain adaptation and generalization paradigms needed re-evaluation in the context of CLIP's strong pre-trained capabilities.

## Method

- Enhance CLIP's zero-shot recognition by incorporating ==domain-specific text prompts==, such as 'a sketch of a [class]'.
- Adapt CLIP through ==pseudo-labeling based self-training== on unlabeled target domain data, combined with parameter-efficient ==Task Residual Learning (TaskRes)== for tuning.
- Introduce a ==label-free multi-source domain generalization== framework that disentangles task residuals into domain-shared and domain-specific components, using only the shared component for inference on unseen domains.

## Results

- Domain-aware prompts improved CLIP's zero-shot recognition accuracy by several percentage points across various domains, including Office-Home and DomainNet.
- Pseudo-labeling based self-training achieved competitive or superior performance compared to traditional unsupervised domain adaptation methods, especially with limited source data.
- The label-free multi-source domain generalization framework demonstrated superior performance against baseline methods, effectively leveraging multiple unlabeled domains to enhance generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CLIP's extensive pre-training provides inherently domain-robust representations, shifting the focus of domain adaptation from learning invariant features to efficient adaptation strategies.
> - Incorporating domain-specific knowledge into text prompts significantly enhances CLIP's zero-shot recognition by providing crucial contextual guidance.
> - Effective adaptation of CLIP can prioritize the utilization of unlabeled target domain data over a heavy reliance on labeled source domain data.
