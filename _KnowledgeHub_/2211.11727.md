# Parametric Classification for Generalized Category Discovery: A Baseline Study

> [!summary] Summary
> SimGCD introduces a parametric classification framework for Generalized Category Discovery, achieving state-of-the-art performance by addressing unreliable pseudo-labels and prediction biases. It demonstrates that parametric classifiers can effectively discover novel categories and classify known ones, outperforming prior non-parametric methods while offering greater computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) seeks to simultaneously classify known and discover novel categories within an unlabeled dataset, often relying on limited labeled examples.
- Parametric classifiers, while efficient, were widely considered unsuitable for GCD due to a perceived tendency to overfit known categories, leading to poor discovery of novel classes.
- Previous non-parametric GCD methods, though better for novel categories, incurred high computational costs and lacked efficient joint optimization with feature learning.

## Method

- **SimGCD** proposes a one-stage ==parametric classification== framework that integrates ==DINO pre-trained ViT== features from the backbone for robust representation.
- It employs ==self-distillation== with a ==cosine-scheduled teacher temperature== for generating more reliable and consistent pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples.
- A novel ==entropy regularization== term is added to the ==unsupervised classification loss== to promote uniformly distributed predictions and mitigate inherent class biases.

## Results

- SimGCD achieved state-of-the-art performance across various GCD benchmarks, showing up to a **15%** relative improvement in 'New' class accuracy compared to prior methods (e.g., on Stanford Cars).
- The method demonstrated significant computational efficiency, with inference times drastically reduced compared to non-parametric alternatives (e.g., **18 seconds vs. 9 minutes** on CUB).
- Ablation studies confirmed that self-distillation and entropy regularization were crucial for mitigating prediction biases and achieving balanced, strong performance on both 'Old' and 'New' categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parametric classifiers are not inherently unsuitable for GCD; their past failures were primarily due to unreliable pseudo-labels and specific prediction biases (towards 'Old' classes and imbalanced distributions).
> - Jointly optimizing representation learning and classification is beneficial for GCD, provided that high-quality pseudo-labels guide the training process.
> - Entropy regularization effectively counteracts prediction biases and enhances the model's robustness to variations in the estimated total number of categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge at the intersection of semi-supervised learning (SSL), open-set recognition, and clustering: **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)**. The broader research landscape acknowledges the immense success of deep learning in image recognition, heavily reliant on large-scale labeled datasets. However, the cost and effort of collecting such annotations are prohibitive for many real-world scenarios. This has spurred research into learning paradigms that can effectively leverage abundant unlabeled data.

The evolution of these paradigms can be summarized as:
- **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Assumes labeled instances are available for *all* categories present in the unlabeled data. The goal is to improve classification accuracy by using both labeled and unlabeled samples.
- **Open-Set Semi-Supervised Learning:** Extends SSL by acknowledging that unlabeled data might contain "outlier" categories not seen in the labeled set. However, these outlier categories are merely rejected, not classified or discovered.
- **Novel Category Discovery (NCD):** Focuses on scenarios where the labeled dataset and the unlabeled dataset have *no overlapping* classes. The task is to discover and classify the entirely novel categories in the unlabeled data.
- **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD):** The most challenging and realistic scenario, formalised recently by Vaze et al.. It assumes the unlabeled dataset can contain both categories already present in the labeled set (seen/old classes) and entirely new, similar-yet-distinct categories (novel/new classes). The objective is to simultaneously classify known categories and discover/classify novel ones.

Previous GCD methods have typically approached the problem from two angles:
1. **Representation Learning:** Employing techniques like self-supervised learning (SSL) to learn robust, generalizable feature representations that can distinguish between both seen and novel categories. DINO pre-trained Vision Transformers are a common choice here, as adopted by this paper.
2. **Classifier Construction:** This is where the core debate lies. Earlier works on NCD often used **parametric classifiers** (e.g., a fully connected layer with learnable weights). However, recent influential work, particularly GCD itself, argued that parametric classifiers tend to *overfit to seen categories*, leading to poor performance on novel classes. This led to a preference for **non-parametric classifiers**, such as semi-supervised k-means clustering, which supposedly offered better generalization for novel categories. While non-parametric methods achieved promising results, they suffered from significant computational costs (quadratic complexity in samples) and a lack of joint optimization with representation learning, potentially leading to sub-optimal solutions.

This paper directly challenges the prevailing sentiment that parametric classifiers are unsuitable for GCD. It aims to demonstrate that, with careful design and by addressing specific issues, parametric classification can not only overcome its perceived limitations but also outperform non-parametric alternatives, thereby providing a more efficient and powerful solution to GCD. By revisiting this fundamental assumption, the work seeks to re-orient the research direction towards more potent and scalable parametric approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this study are multifaceted, aiming to dissect, understand, and ultimately overcome the limitations attributed to parametric classifiers in the context of Generalized Category Discovery:

1. **Revisit and Investigate the Failure of Parametric Classifiers:** The core motivation is to critically examine the widely held belief that parametric classifiers are prone to overfitting to 'seen' categories and thus perform poorly in discovering 'novel' ones. The authors aim to systematically understand *why* previous parametric approaches like UNO+ struggled, instead of simply accepting this as an inherent limitation.

2. **Verify Effectiveness of Prior Design Choices under High-Quality Supervision:** The study seeks to determine if key design elements, such as the choice of feature representations (e.g., post-backbone vs. post-projector features) and training paradigms (e.g., decoupled vs. joint representation learning), are inherently flawed or merely ineffective due to other underlying issues. This involves evaluating their performance under varying degrees of "supervision quality," including oracle (perfect) labels.

3. **Identify the Root Cause: Unreliable Pseudo-Labels and Prediction Biases:** Based on their investigations, a crucial objective is to pinpoint the fundamental problem hindering parametric classifiers. The authors hypothesize and then demonstrate that unreliable pseudo-labels generated for unlabelled data, coupled with specific prediction biases, are the key culprits. These biases include:
 - A tendency for the classifier to predict 'Old' classes more frequently than 'New' classes.
 - An imbalanced distribution of pseudo-labels across both 'Old' and 'New' categories.

4. **Propose a Simple yet Effective Parametric Classification Method (SimGCD):** Motivated by the identified issues, a major objective is to develop a streamlined parametric classification framework. This framework aims to be simple in design, building upon established representation learning techniques (like those in GCD), but incorporating novel components to specifically address the pseudo-label unreliability and prediction biases.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance and Robustness:** A concrete goal is to demonstrate that the proposed SimGCD method can surpass existing state-of-the-art methods, including non-parametric ones, on multiple GCD benchmarks. Furthermore, the authors aim to show that their method can achieve strong robustness to variations in the *unknown number of categories*, which is a practical challenge in real-world GCD scenarios.

6. **Establish a Strong Baseline to Facilitate Future Studies:** By demonstrating the efficacy and efficiency of a parametric approach, the authors intend for SimGCD to serve as a robust and accessible baseline. This would encourage future research to build upon parametric classifiers, potentially simplifying model design and accelerating progress in the field, moving away from the computational complexities of non-parametric methods.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to leverage the inherent advantages of parametric classifiers (joint optimization, computational efficiency) while mitigating their historical weaknesses, thereby advancing the practical applicability of generalized category discovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is two-fold: an initial diagnostic investigation into the failures of parametric classifiers, followed by the proposal and rigorous evaluation of a new parametric framework, SimGCD.

#### 3.1. Diagnostic Investigation into Parametric Classifier Failure (Section 3)

The authors meticulously conduct a series of controlled experiments to isolate the factors contributing to the poor performance of previous parametric classifiers for GCD. This involves:

- **Investigation Setting:**
 - **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) Definition:** Clearly defines the problem setting, involving labeled (`D_l` with `Y_l`) and unlabeled (`D_u` with `Y_u`) datasets, where `Y_l` is a subset of `Y_u`. The total number of categories `K_u` is assumed known or estimable.
 - **Representation Learning:** Adopts the strategy from GCD, combining supervised contrastive learning on labeled samples and self-supervised contrastive learning on all samples. The backbone is a ViT-B/16 pre-trained with DINO.
 - **Classifier:** A prototypical classifier (similar to UNO) is used, computing logits based on cosine similarity between features and prototypes.
 - **Training Settings:** Experiments are conducted with varying "supervision qualities": minimal (only `D_l`), oracle (all samples labeled), and two practical pseudo-labeling strategies (self-label based on Sinkhorn-Knopp and self-distillation, which is later proposed in SimGCD).

- **Key Studies and Findings:**
 1. **Which Representation to Build Your Classifier (Sec 3.2):** Compares building the classifier on post-backbone features versus post-projector features.
 - **Finding:** Post-backbone features consistently yield higher 'Old' class accuracy. More importantly, they demonstrate a higher potential for 'New' class accuracy, but *only when high-quality pseudo-labels are available*. This suggests that the projector, in self-supervised learning contexts, might specialize in pretext tasks, while the backbone retains more general information useful for classification.
 2. **Decoupled or Joint Representation Learning (Sec 3.3):** Investigates whether jointly optimizing representation and classification is beneficial or detrimental.
 - **Finding:** Joint training is beneficial *when high-quality supervision (including pseudo-labels) is available*. Conversely, with unreliable pseudo-labels, joint training can degrade performance. This refutes the idea that joint training itself is the problem; instead, the quality of pseudo-labels guiding the joint training is critical.
 3. **The Devil Is in the Biased Predictions (Sec 3.4):** Diagnoses the statistical nature of predictions when parametric classifiers fail.
 - **Finding:** Two significant prediction biases are identified:
 - **Bias towards 'Old' classes:** Models are prone to "False Old" predictions (misclassifying 'New' class samples as 'Old' ones), indicating an over-reliance on known categories.
 - **Imbalanced pseudo-label distribution:** The pseudo-labels generated are highly imbalanced across classes, with some classes receiving many more predictions than others, even for novel categories.

#### 3.2. SimGCD: The Proposed Parametric Classification Method (Section 4)

Based on the diagnostic findings, the authors propose **SimGCD**, a simple yet effective one-stage parametric classification framework.

- **Representation Learning (Sec. 4.1):**
 - This component largely follows GCD, utilizing a combination of **supervised contrastive learning** (L_s_rep) on labeled samples (matching positive pairs by their ground-truth labels) and **self-supervised contrastive learning** (L_u_rep) on all samples (matching augmented views of the same image).
 - The model uses a ViT-B/16 backbone pre-trained with DINO, with only the last block fine-tuned. Features are extracted from the `[CLS]` token.

- **Parametric Classification (Sec. 4.2):**
 - **Prototypical Classifier:** A set of `K` prototypes (one for each total category, `|Y_l U Y_u|`) are randomly initialized. Logits are computed based on the cosine similarity between L2-normalized image features (from the backbone, based on findings in 3.2) and these prototypes.
 - **Self-Distillation for Pseudo-labeling:** This is a crucial improvement over prior methods. For unlabeled samples, pseudo-labels are generated through **self-distillation**: a "teacher" view (another augmented view of the same image) produces sharpened predictions (using a cooler temperature `τ_t`) which serve as soft pseudo-labels (`q'`) for the "student" view (predictions `p` from the main forward pass). This is inspired by consistency-based SSL and deep clustering methods.
 - **Cross-Entropy Loss:** Applied for both labeled samples (using ground truth `y`) and unlabeled samples (using self-distilled pseudo-labels `q'`).
 - **Entropy Regularization:** A key novel component. An entropy maximization term (`-εH(p)`) is added to the unsupervised classification loss. This term encourages the model to produce more uniformly distributed predictions across all possible categories for unlabeled samples, directly addressing the identified prediction biases (imbalanced distributions and bias towards 'Old' classes).
 - **Joint Training:** The representation learning and classification objectives are jointly optimized, leveraging the finding that joint training is beneficial with reliable pseudo-labels.
 - **Teacher Temperature Warmup:** The teacher temperature `τ_t` is initialized higher and gradually warmed up (lowered) with a cosine schedule. This allows for less confident pseudo-labels in the early training stages when the model is less stable, improving reliability.

The overall objective function combines the representation learning loss (`L_rep`) and the classification loss (`L_cls`). The design choices are directly informed by the preceding diagnostic analysis, aiming to overcome the specific failure points of prior parametric approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings and demonstrates compelling results for SimGCD:

1. **Revalidation of Parametric Classifiers for GCD:** The most critical finding is that parametric classifiers *can* indeed achieve state-of-the-art performance in Generalized Category Discovery, directly challenging the prevailing belief that they are inherently prone to overfitting seen categories and thus inferior to non-parametric methods.

2. **Unreliable Pseudo-labels as the Key Failure Factor:** The diagnostic studies conclusively identify that the primary reason for previous parametric classifiers' degraded performance is not their parametric nature itself, but rather the unreliable and biased pseudo-labels generated for novel categories.

3. **Identification of Two Prediction Biases:** The analysis highlights two critical biases:
 - **Bias towards "Old" classes:** Classifiers tend to misclassify novel samples as belonging to known categories ("False Old" errors).
 - **Imbalanced pseudo-label distribution:** Pseudo-labels are unevenly distributed across both seen and novel classes, indicating a lack of uniform discovery.

4. **SimGCD Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Significant Improvement in "New" Class Accuracy:** SimGCD consistently achieves notable improvements in 'New' class accuracy across all tested benchmarks (CIFAR10/100, ImageNet-100, CUB, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft, Herbarium 19, ImageNet-1K). For example, it improves 'New' ACC by ~10% on CIFAR10, ~11% on CIFAR100, ~9% on CUB, and ~15% on Stanford Cars compared to the previous SOTA (GCD).
 - **Balanced Performance:** Unlike some prior parametric methods (e.g., UNO+) that perform well on 'Old' classes but poorly on 'New', SimGCD achieves strong and balanced performance on both 'Old' and 'New' categories.
 - **Outperforms Non-Parametric SOTAs:** SimGCD outperforms GCD (a non-parametric method) across all datasets and metrics, challenging the notion that non-parametric methods are superior for novel category discovery.

5. **Computational Efficiency:** SimGCD, being a parametric method, demonstrates significantly faster inference times compared to non-parametric methods like GCD (e.g., 18s vs. 9m on CUB, 0.6h vs. 7.7h on ImageNet-1K for the unlabelled split), due to avoiding expensive clustering algorithms.

6. **Efficacy of Entropy Regularization:**
 - **Overcoming Prediction Bias:** Ablation studies and error analyses (Fig. 9, 10, 16, 17) show that the entropy regularization term is highly effective in mitigating both identified prediction biases. It reduces "False New" and "False Old" errors and promotes a more balanced pseudo-label distribution that aligns closer to the ground-truth class distribution.
 - **Robustness to Unknown Class Numbers:** The entropy regularizer also imbues SimGCD with strong robustness to variations in the *estimated total number of categories*. This is crucial for real-world deployment where the exact number of novel classes might not be known beforehand. The model intelligently "activates" prototypes close to the true number of categories even when a larger number is provided (Fig. 11, 12, 19).

7. **Impact of Design Choices:**
 - **Post-Backbone Features:** Confirmed to be better for classifier learning, especially when pseudo-label quality is high.
 - **Self-Distillation:** Proved to be a superior pseudo-labeling strategy, offering consistent and significant gains in 'New' class accuracy over simpler self-labeling.
 - **Joint Training:** Demonstrated to be beneficial for refining representations, leading to less ambiguity and more compact clusters (Fig. 14), *provided* high-quality pseudo-labels are available.

8. **Superiority over GCD (even with identical representations):** Ablation studies reveal that even before joint training and with identical representations, SimGCD's classification objective significantly improves over GCD. This is attributed to SimGCD's ability to produce more balanced predictions and better handle tail classes (Fig. 13).

In summary, the main findings highlight that the carefully designed SimGCD, integrating effective pseudo-labeling and entropy regularization, fundamentally shifts the understanding of parametric classifiers' capabilities in GCD, making them a powerful and efficient choice.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the field of Generalized Category Discovery and broader applications of semi-supervised learning in computer vision.

1. **Re-establishing Parametric Classification as a Strong Baseline:** The most profound impact is challenging the prevailing notion that parametric classifiers are inherently ill-suited for GCD due to overfitting. By demonstrating state-of-the-art performance with SimGCD, this paper re-legitimizes and promotes parametric approaches. This shift is crucial because parametric classifiers are generally more computationally efficient during inference and allow for joint optimization of features and classification boundaries, which non-parametric clustering methods often struggle with.

2. **Providing a Simpler and More Efficient Framework:** SimGCD offers a streamlined, one-stage framework that is easier to implement and run than many complex multi-stage or non-parametric methods (e.g., k-means based approaches with their quadratic complexity). Its superior inference speed makes it highly practical for large-scale datasets and real-time applications, where computational resources might be constrained. This simplicity and efficiency could accelerate future research by providing a robust and accessible foundation.

3. **Improved Discovery of Novel Categories:** The significant performance gains, particularly in 'New' class accuracy, mean that SimGCD is better at leveraging unlabeled data to uncover truly novel patterns. This directly contributes to the goal of "generalized category discovery," enabling models to adapt more effectively to evolving data distributions and unknown classes in real-world scenarios.

4. **Enhanced Robustness and Practicality:** The discovery that entropy regularization can enforce robustness to an unknown number of categories is a major practical advantage. In many real-world applications, the exact number of novel classes is not known *a priori*. SimGCD's ability to perform well even with loosely estimated class counts significantly reduces the need for precise hyperparameter tuning, easing deployment.

5. **Deeper Understanding of Classifier Design:** The detailed diagnostic studies provide invaluable insights into the failure modes of parametric classifiers (unreliable pseudo-labels, prediction biases). This understanding is crucial for guiding future research, allowing developers to design more robust and generalizable models not just for GCD, but potentially for other open-world and semi-supervised learning tasks.

6. **Potential Future Research Directions:** The paper openly discusses limitations and future work, which itself is impactful:
 - **Advanced Representation Learning:** The work highlights that even with a strong classification objective, the quality of underlying representations (currently borrowed from GCD) can still be improved. This could spur research into GCD-specific self-supervised pre-training strategies or more sophisticated data augmentations.
 - **Alignment with Human-Defined Categories:** Addressing the challenge of aligning algorithmically discovered categories with human intuition, especially across different granularities or domains, points to a critical area for bridging the gap between AI performance and human interpretability.
 - **Trade-off between Old and New Categories:** The observed trade-off in performance during training suggests a new avenue for optimization, potentially leading to dynamic strategies that balance performance across known and novel classes more effectively.
 - **Ethical Considerations:** Acknowledging biases against minority or long-tailed categories underscores the importance of fairness in discovery systems, prompting research into debiasing techniques for GCD.

In conclusion, this study not only introduces a state-of-the-art method but also fundamentally re-evaluates a core architectural choice in GCD, providing a robust, efficient, and well-understood baseline that is poised to influence the design and development of future category discovery systems.
