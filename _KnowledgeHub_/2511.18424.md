# CrossJEPA: Cross-Modal Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Efficient 3D Representation Learning from 2D Images

> [!summary] Summary
> CrossJEPA introduces a mask-free Joint-embedding Predictive Architecture for efficient 3D representation learning, leveraging a frozen 2D image foundation model to guide a 3D point cloud encoder. It achieves 94.2% accuracy on ModelNet40 and 88.3% on ScanObjectNN using only 14.1M parameters and approximately 6 pretraining GPU hours.

## Problem

- Existing 3D self-supervised learning methods suffer from 3D data scarcity, computational expense, and large model sizes, hindering deployment on resource-constrained devices.
- The full potential of Joint-embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) remains largely unexplored in cross-modal 2D-to-3D learning, partly due to the misconception that masking is a core requirement.
- Current cross-modal approaches are computationally heavy (e.g., reconstruction-based methods) or prone to collapse (e.g., contrastive learning with learnable teachers), making knowledge transfer from 2D inefficient.

## Method

- Developed a ==cross-modal== JEPA that distills knowledge from a frozen 2D image foundation model (teacher) to a learnable 3D point cloud encoder (student) using a lightweight predictor.
- Introduced a 'latent view prediction task' where the predictor infers target 2D image embeddings from 3D features, explicitly conditioned on image-specific latent information like camera pose and color histogram.
- Achieved significant efficiency by freezing the 2D image encoder and precomputing/caching all target image embeddings, drastically reducing learnable parameters and on-the-fly computation.

## Results

- Achieved a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **94.2%** on ModelNet40 and **88.3%** on ScanObjectNN for linear probing, surpassing prior unimodal and cross-modal methods.
- Demonstrated unprecedented pretraining efficiency, requiring only **14.1M** parameters and approximately 6 GPU hours, significantly less than other methods (e.g., Point-JEPA: **46.0M** parameters, ~21 hours).
- Outperformed all prior work across various few-shot learning settings on ModelNet40 (e.g., **97.8%** for 5-way 10-shot), indicating superior generalization from limited labeled data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPA's predictive principle can be effectively applied in a cross-modal setting without relying on input masking, which can even be detrimental in 2D-to-3D knowledge transfer.
> - Explicitly conditioning the predictor with modality-specific nuisance factors (e.g., camera pose) purifies the supervision signal for the point encoder, enabling it to focus on shared semantic features and accelerate convergence.
> - Pre-caching teacher embeddings is a crucial optimization for cross-modal JEPA, transforming it into a highly efficient and practically viable pretraining framework by amortizing the teacher's computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D vision is critical for numerous real-world applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and medical imaging. A central challenge in this domain is effective 3D representation learning, particularly from 3D point clouds. Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm to address the scarcity of large-scale, labeled 3D datasets.

Within 3D SSL, several architectural families exist:
- **Unimodal Approaches:** These methods learn representations solely from point clouds. Examples include Joint-Embedding Architectures (JEAs) like Point2Vec, and generative architectures like Point-BERT and Point-MAE. While effective, they often suffer from the inherent scarcity of 3D data and can be sensitive to hyperparameters, sometimes leading to model collapse. Crucially, the 3D domain currently lacks the powerful "foundation models" seen in 2D image and natural language processing.
- **Cross-Modal 2D-to-3D Learning:** To overcome 3D data limitations and leverage the rich semantics learned by 2D foundation models (e.g., from millions of images), recent work has focused on transferring knowledge from 2D to 3D. These methods include:
 - **Generative Cross-Modal:** Approaches like I2P-MAE and ACT use 2D teachers to guide the reconstruction of 3D points or 2D features. However, reconstructing low-level details in input space is computationally expensive, requires heavy decoders, and can introduce noisy training signals.
 - **JEA-based Cross-Modal:** Methods such as CrossPoint and Pic@Point align multi-modal features in a shared latent space, often employing contrastive learning. These typically involve long pretraining schedules and can be prone to collapse if both teacher and student models are learnable.

The Joint-embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) has recently gained significant traction in SSL for its simplicity, efficiency, and ability to learn rich, abstract representations by predicting latent features rather than raw input. Initial applications of JEPA, such as I-JEPA for images and Point-JEPA for unimodal point clouds, primarily rely on masking parts of the input. This has led to a "misconception" that masking is an intrinsic requirement of JEPA, limiting its exploration in cross-modal settings where direct masking across modalities is non-trivial and often inconsistent.

CrossJEPA positions itself at the intersection of cross-modal learning and JEPA, directly addressing this gap. It proposes a novel, mask-free JEPA-style pretraining strategy specifically designed for 2D-to-3D knowledge transfer. By doing so, it aims to combine the efficiency benefits of JEPA with the data-rich supervision capabilities of 2D foundation models, while circumventing the computational burdens and architectural complexities of existing cross-modal methods. The work explicitly challenges the reliance on masking in JEPA, extending the framework to a new problem domain with a distinct predictive task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind CrossJEPA stems from the limitations observed in existing 3D representation learning paradigms, particularly in real-world applications with operational constraints.
The **key objectives** and underlying **motivations** are:

1. **Address Computational Expense and Model Size:** Current 2D-to-3D cross-modal methods often result in large models that are slow to train and computationally expensive. This hinders their deployment on resource-constrained devices (e.g., edge computing) and contributes to unsustainable AI practices. CrossJEPA aims to develop a significantly more parameter-efficient and faster-to-train framework.
2. **Unlock Cross-Modal Potential of JEPA:** The Joint-embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is lauded for its simplicity and efficiency in unimodal self-supervised learning, yet its application in cross-modal settings (specifically 2D-to-3D) has been largely unexplored. This is partly due to the misconception that masking input is inherent to JEPA. A primary objective is to demonstrate that JEPA's principles can be effectively applied cross-modally without explicit masking.
3. **Enhance Supervision Signal and Learning Robustness:** The goal is to create a stronger, cleaner self-supervision signal for 3D point cloud models. By leveraging a powerful, frozen 2D image foundation model as a "teacher," CrossJEPA aims to distill high-level semantic knowledge more effectively, guiding the 3D student to learn richer and more robust representations.
4. **Decouple Nuisance Factors:** In cross-modal learning, modality-specific "nuisance factors" (e.g., camera pose, color in images) can inject noise into the learning process for the target modality (point clouds). A key objective is to design an architecture that explicitly decouples these nuisance factors from the core semantic content, ensuring a purer gradient flow to the point encoder.
5. **Promote Sustainable AI:** By focusing on efficiency, minimal parameters, and reduced GPU hours, CrossJEPA aims to contribute to more environmentally friendly and accessible AI research and deployment, aligning with the principles of sustainable AI.

In essence, CrossJEPA seeks to forge a new path for efficient and effective 3D representation learning by adapting the powerful JEPA framework to a cross-modal setting, deliberately moving beyond masking-based strategies, and optimizing for computational and parameter efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CrossJEPA proposes a novel cross-modal Joint-embedding Predictive Architecture that distills knowledge from a frozen image foundation model to a learnable point cloud encoder. Its design is deliberately simple and efficient.

**A. Core Architecture:**
The architecture consists of three main components (see Figure 2 in the paper):
1. **Point Encoder (P):** This is the learnable "student" model responsible for generating 3D point cloud representations (`s_p`). It adopts a patchification strategy similar to Point-BERT, where a 3D point cloud is partitioned into local clusters, tokenized, and processed by a standard transformer encoder. The final feature embedding is obtained by combining pooled outputs and a learnable `[CLS]` token.
2. **Image Encoder (I):** This acts as the "teacher." It is a frozen, pretrained 2D image foundation model (specifically, a ViT-B/14 with DinoV2 weights). Its role is to provide stable, high-level target image embeddings (`s_i`) for specific rendered views of a 3D object. Being frozen, it does not learn during pretraining, which significantly stabilizes the supervision signal and reduces the number of learnable parameters.
3. **Predictor (H_p2i):** A lightweight, learnable transformer network that infers image embeddings. It takes the point cloud representation (`s_p`) from the point encoder and *explicitly conditioned latent information* (`z_v`) related to the target view to predict the corresponding image embedding (`s_i_hat`).

**B. Training Objective and Task:**
Unlike traditional JEPA implementations that rely on masking, CrossJEPA introduces a "latent view prediction task." For each 3D object:
- A single point cloud (`p`) is sampled.
- Multiple 2D views (`i_zv`) of the object are pre-rendered from different camera poses (yaw `θ` and pitch `φ`).
- The point encoder `P` generates its representation `s_p`.
- A subset of these 2D views are selected, and their embeddings `s_i_zv` are obtained from the *frozen* image encoder `I`.
- The predictor `H_p2i` is trained to map `s_p` to `s_i_hat_zv`, conditioned on the latent information `z_v` (camera pose and color histogram) corresponding to the target view.
- The learning objective is a Smooth L1 loss (`L_p2i`) between the predicted embedding `s_i_hat_zv` and the target embedding `s_i_zv` across all selected views and objects.

**C. Conditioning with Latent Information (`z_v`):**
A crucial aspect of CrossJEPA is the explicit conditioning of the predictor on latent information specific to the image modality, such as camera pose (yaw and pitch, sinusoidally encoded) and a 16-bin color histogram.
- **Motivation:** This mechanism addresses the problem of "nuisance factors." Image embeddings contain both object-centric semantics (shared with point clouds) and image-specific details (like pose, lighting, color). By providing these image-specific details directly to the predictor, the point encoder is effectively decoupled from learning them. This purifies the supervision signal, allowing the point encoder to focus exclusively on mutual 2D-3D features (e.g., object shape, semantics) and preventing irrelevant gradient components from propagating back.

**D. Datasets and Pre-processing:**
CrossJEPA is pretrained on a combination of ShapeNet (a synthetic dataset of CAD models) and a subset of Objaverse-XL (a large-scale internet 3D dataset).
- For each 3D object, 36 different 2D views are rendered by systematically varying yaw and pitch, while other camera parameters are fixed.
- Point clouds are sampled from these objects and augmented with scaling, translation, jittering, and controlled rotation.

**E. Efficiency Mechanisms:**
1. **Frozen Image Encoder:** By freezing the image teacher, only the point encoder and predictor need to be trained, drastically reducing the number of learnable parameters (to 14.1M total, 8.5M for the point encoder alone).
2. **Target Embedding Caching:** A significant optimization involves precomputing and caching all the image embeddings for all 36 views of every object in the dataset using the frozen image encoder. This eliminates the need for on-the-fly computation of target embeddings during each training iteration, leading to massive amortized speedups and reduced GPU compute/memory overhead.

**F. Architectural Variants Explored (Supplementary Material):**
The authors explored alternative cross-modal JEPA configurations before settling on the proposed pure P2I design:
- **P2I + I2I:** Included an auxiliary image-to-image learning branch where a student image encoder learned from an EMA teacher image encoder. This suffered from model collapse and unstable gradients.
- **P2I + P2P:** Introduced a point-to-point predictive loss alongside the P2I objective. This resulted in imbalanced optimization dynamics and inconsistent gradients between the two loss terms.
The "pure P2I" (CrossJEPA) design was found to be the most stable, efficient, and performant, validating its streamlined approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CrossJEPA demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across multiple 3D understanding tasks, establishing new benchmarks for efficiency and effectiveness.

**A. Performance on Downstream Tasks:**

- **Linear Probing on ModelNet40 (Synthetic Data):** CrossJEPA achieves a new SOTA accuracy of **94.2%**, significantly outperforming recent unimodal and cross-modal methods. Crucially, it achieves this with only **14.1M pretraining parameters** (8.5M in the point encoder) and the **least pretraining GPU hours (approximately 6 hours** on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090). This is a substantial improvement over methods like Point-JEPA (93.6% / 46.0M params / ~21 hours), ReCon (93.5% / 200.6M params / ~12 hours), and I2P-MAE (92.9% / 167.2M params / ~33 hours).
- **Linear Probing on ScanObjectNN (Real-World Data):** CrossJEPA achieves SOTA performance of **88.3% accuracy** on the OBJ-BG setting. This validates the transferability and robustness of its learned representations to real-world, noisy data, despite its compact size and efficient training.
- **Fine-tuning on ModelNet40:** With end-to-end fine-tuning, CrossJEPA achieves **94.3% accuracy**, performing on par with existing SOTA methods, even when using fewer input points (2k vs. 8k for some baselines).
- **Few-Shot Learning on ModelNet40:** CrossJEPA significantly outperforms all prior work across various few-shot settings (5-way/10-way, 10-shot/20-shot protocols), demonstrating its superior ability to generalize from limited labeled data. For example, it achieves **97.8% for 5-way 10-shot**, beating Point-JEPA (97.4%) and ReCon (97.3%).
- **Part Segmentation on ShapeNetPart:** CrossJEPA achieves competitive segmentation scores (84.1% mIoU_C, 85.8% mIoU_I). While not strictly SOTA, the authors attribute this to their smaller encoder size and fewer point clusters compared to some baselines, indicating a strong performance-to-resource trade-off.

**B. Key Findings from Ablation Studies:**

- **Impact of Latent Information:** Providing camera pose and color histogram information to the predictor consistently **improves accuracy (from 93.68% to 94.04%)** and **accelerates convergence (saturation epochs reduced from 93 to 67)**. This empirically validates the hypothesis that explicitly conditioning on nuisance factors purifies the supervision signal for the point encoder.
- **Role of Masking:** Contrary to the common perception, the ablation studies reveal that **CrossJEPA performs best with 0% masking**. Increasing the masking ratio leads to a steady *drop* in accuracy. This strongly supports the paper's claim that masking is not necessary for JEPA and can even introduce cross-modal inconsistencies that harm performance in this specific setting.
- **Parallel View Prediction:** While accuracy remains largely stable, increasing the number of simultaneously predicted views per iteration increases the per-epoch training time. However, it also leads to earlier saturation, indicating that a certain level of multi-view supervision is efficient but excessive redundancy offers diminishing returns. The caching mechanism makes this exploration feasible.
- **Model Scaling:** Larger point encoder and predictor models generally yield higher accuracy, confirming the benefits of increased model capacity, though at the expense of parameter count.
- **Pretraining Dataset Size and Efficiency:** Pretraining on ShapeNet alone already yields strong competitive results (93.27%). Adding a subset of Objaverse-XL further improves accuracy (to 93.84%) and reduces the epochs needed for convergence, demonstrating that CrossJEPA benefits from more data but is not critically dependent on massive datasets to achieve strong performance. Furthermore, CrossJEPA shows impressive data efficiency, maintaining 93.15% accuracy even with only 20% of the full pretraining data, suggesting effective knowledge transfer from the 2D teacher in low-data regimes.
- **Caching Mechanism's Impact:** The caching of image embeddings is shown to be *critical* for efficiency. Without caching, a single epoch's duration increases from ~2 minutes 20 seconds to approximately 16 hours, and the batch size is severely limited. This mechanism transforms CrossJEPA into a practically viable and highly efficient framework.
- **View Sampling Robustness:** CrossJEPA's performance is largely insensitive to the choice of camera parametrization (spherical, cylindrical, Cartesian) used for rendering 2D views, providing flexibility in its application.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CrossJEPA makes several significant contributions to the field of 3D representation learning and self-supervised learning, with notable potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Cross-Modal JEPA Without Masking:** This work is a groundbreaking step, introducing the first JEPA-style framework specifically for image-to-point cross-modal learning that *does not rely on masking*. This directly challenges and rectifies a common misconception about JEPA, broadening its applicability to complex multi-modal scenarios where masking is difficult or detrimental. It demonstrates a new flavor of JEPA based on latent view prediction, proving that JEPA's power lies in its predictive principle, not just masking.
2. **Achieving SOTA Efficiency and Performance:** CrossJEPA sets a new standard for efficient 3D representation learning. By achieving SOTA results on major benchmarks (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN, few-shot learning) with significantly fewer pretraining parameters (14.1M) and drastically reduced pretraining GPU hours (approx. 6 hours), it addresses a critical bottleneck in the field. This unprecedented efficiency makes advanced 3D vision models more accessible, democratizing research and deployment, particularly for resource-constrained environments like edge devices.
3. **Sustainable AI:** The emphasis on minimal compute and parameters aligns with the growing call for "sustainable AI." CrossJEPA offers a blueprint for building high-performing models with a smaller carbon footprint, promoting environmentally conscious research practices.
4. **Cleaner Knowledge Distillation:** The novel approach of conditioning the predictor on target-exclusive latent information (pose, color histogram) provides a cleaner supervision signal. This intelligent decoupling of nuisance factors from shared semantics helps the point encoder learn more robust and task-relevant 3D features, minimizing gradient noise and accelerating convergence.
5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The strong performance across diverse tasks (classification, few-shot learning, segmentation) and datasets (synthetic ModelNet40, real-world ScanObjectNN) highlights the robustness and generalizability of the learned 3D representations. Its superior few-shot learning capabilities, in particular, demonstrate its ability to extract highly transferable features.
6. **Conceptual and Theoretical Validation:** The paper provides intuitive, theoretical (information-theoretic analysis in supplementary material), and empirical justifications for its design choices, including the benefits of nuisance suppression and the inefficiencies of reconstruction-based methods. It also draws compelling analogies to predictive coding in human cognition, providing a deeper understanding of why such architectures are effective.
7. **Foundation for Future Work:** CrossJEPA opens up several promising research avenues. Future work can explore adaptive or learnable projection strategies instead of fixed parameters, potentially leading to even more versatile models. Extending the framework to richer multimodal setups, such as combining LiDAR and camera data for autonomous driving, is a natural and impactful next step, potentially leveraging its efficiency for large-scale, complex real-world scenarios.

In summary, CrossJEPA is a pivotal contribution that advances the state of 3D representation learning by demonstrating that a simple, elegant, and computationally lightweight JEPA framework, thoughtfully adapted to a cross-modal setting without masking, can achieve superior performance and efficiency, paving the way for more sustainable and widely applicable 3D AI.
