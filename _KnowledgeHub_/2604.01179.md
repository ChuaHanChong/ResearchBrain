# A ROS 2 Wrapper for Florence-2: Multi-Mode Local Vision-Language Inference for Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a ROS 2 wrapper for the Florence-2 vision-language model, enabling multi-mode local inference for robotic systems. The wrapper provides flexible interaction patterns and demonstrates practical throughput, achieving approximately 9.75 FPS for object detection with the base model on an RTX 3060 Mobile GPU, making advanced VLM capabilities accessible on consumer-grade hardware.

## Problem

- Integrating advanced vision-language foundation models (VLMs) into practical robot software stacks like ROS 2 presents significant engineering challenges due to incompatible interfaces and deployment complexities.
- Many powerful VLMs require substantial computational resources or constant internet connectivity, limiting their applicability for local deployment on robotic platforms with constrained hardware.
- A lack of dedicated, open-source ROS 2 integrations specifically for compact yet versatile VLMs like Florence-2 restricts their adoption in the robotics community.

## Method

- Development of an open-source ==ROS 2 wrapper== for **Florence-2**, designed for local deployment and enabling a unified interface for diverse vision-language tasks within a single ROS 2 node.
- Implementation of a flexible interaction paradigm combining ==continuous topic-driven processing==, ==synchronous ROS 2 service calls== for on-demand inference, and ==asynchronous ROS 2 actions== for longer requests with feedback.
- The wrapper abstracts **Florence-2**'s ==prompt-based task tokens==, allowing various tasks (e.g., object detection, captioning, OCR) to be executed by modifying the prompt via ROS 2 interfaces.

## Results

- The ROS 2 wrapper demonstrated full functional operation across all three interaction modes: continuous processing, synchronous service calls for on-demand tasks, and asynchronous actions with feedback.
- An end-to-end throughput study for object detection showed average performance of **5.81 FPS** on GTX 1060 Mobile (base model), **9.75 FPS** on RTX 3060 Mobile (base model), and **26.6 FPS** on RTX 3080 Ti Desktop (base model).
- The large **Florence-2** model achieved average throughputs of **2.50 FPS** on GTX 1060 Mobile, **4.21 FPS** on RTX 3060 Mobile, and **11.5 FPS** on RTX 3080 Ti Desktop for object detection, indicating scalability with hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single ROS 2 node can effectively host Florence-2, managing image reception, model inference, and structured output publication, maintaining a short runtime path for integration.
> - Offering multiple ROS 2 communication patterns (topics, services, actions) within one wrapper caters to the diverse real-time and on-demand perceptual needs of robotic applications.
> - Local deployment of a sophisticated VLM like Florence-2 is feasible on consumer-grade GPUs, broadening access to advanced semantic perception for a wider range of robotics practitioners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is positioned at the intersection of foundation models, vision-language models (VLMs), and robotics middleware, specifically the Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2).

**Foundation Models in Robotics:** Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly VLMs, have enabled richer semantic perception capabilities in robotic systems. This represents a shift from traditional task-specific deep learning architectures towards more generalized and flexible perception. Examples cited include embodied multimodal systems such as PaLM-E, RT-2, OpenVLA, and VoxPoser, which demonstrate the utility of integrating sophisticated perceptual representations with robot behaviors for tasks like decision-making, manipulation, and scene understanding. The paper notes the particular relevance of such capabilities for collaborative robotics, where perception extends beyond object recognition to higher-level reasoning about task context, human activity, and intent.

**Integration Challenges in Robotics:** Despite the recognized potential of these advanced models, their practical adoption in robot software stacks faces significant integration hurdles. Models that function effectively in controlled environments, such as Python notebooks, often lack the necessary infrastructure for deployment within a ROS 2 graph, which requires standardized interfaces for camera topics, services, actions, launch files, and reproducible deployment mechanisms. This gap between model availability and system usability is a recurring theme in the robotics community, leading to a body of work focused on creating "wrappers" or middleware components that enable the reuse of existing capabilities within established robotic software frameworks.

**Florence-2's Specific Niche:** Florence-2 is identified as a particularly relevant VLM in this context. It offers a unified, prompt-based interface for a broad range of computer vision and vision-language tasks, including captioning, optical character recognition (OCR), open-vocabulary detection, and grounding. Crucially, Florence-2 maintains a comparatively manageable model size, making it a viable candidate for local deployment in robotic systems where internet connectivity may be unreliable or unavailable, and hardware resources are constrained but not negligible. This distinguishes it from larger multimodal systems that may require more substantial computational infrastructure.

**Addressing an Integration Gap:** While ROS and ROS 2 wrappers exist for other foundation models like Whisper (speech recognition) and Segment Anything Model (SAM) for segmentation, the paper identifies a current lack of dedicated and widely adopted ROS 2 integrations specifically for Florence-2. Therefore, this work aims to fill this specific void by providing a practical, reusable software component for a model well-suited to local robotic perception. The contribution does not involve proposing a new foundation model or a novel embodied AI framework but rather focuses on enabling the practical usability of an existing, compact, and capable VLM within the ROS 2 ecosystem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop and validate a ROS 2 wrapper for the Florence-2 vision-language model, specifically tailored for practical local deployment in robotic systems. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address identified gaps in the current integration landscape for foundation models in robotics.

**Motivation:**

1. **Enabling Richer Robotic Perception:** Foundation vision-language models like Florence-2 offer significantly richer semantic perception capabilities compared to traditional, narrowly specialized deep learning pipelines. These capabilities—ranging from open-vocabulary object detection and grounding to detailed image captioning and OCR—are highly valuable for enhancing robot understanding of complex environments, human activities, and task contexts, particularly in collaborative robotics.
2. **Addressing the Integration Barrier:** Despite the advanced capabilities of these models, their direct integration into operational robot software stacks, such as those built on ROS 2, remains a substantial challenge. The transition from evaluating a model in a Python notebook to deploying it within a ROS 2 graph—with its specific requirements for message types, topic subscriptions, service calls, actions, launch files, and robust, reproducible deployment—is not straightforward. This integration effort is often a limiting factor in the practical adoption of new models.
3. **Leveraging Florence-2's Attributes:** Florence-2 is motivated as a particularly attractive candidate for ROS 2 integration due to its unique combination of features:
 - **Unified Interface:** It provides a single, prompt-based interface for a diverse set of vision-language tasks, simplifying its usage.
 - **Manageable Size:** Compared to many very large multimodal systems, Florence-2 has a relatively manageable model size, making it more feasible for local execution on robotic platforms with limited, but not negligible, hardware resources.
 - **Local Execution Capability:** The ability to run inference locally is crucial for robotic applications where continuous internet connectivity is undesirable or unavailable, enhancing robustness and reducing latency.
4. **Filling a Specific Gap:** The authors identify a current lack of dedicated and well-integrated ROS 2 wrappers for Florence-2, in contrast to some other popular foundation models. This signifies an unmet need within the ROS 2 robotics community for making this specific VLM readily accessible.

**Key Contributions (Specific Objectives):**

Based on these motivations, the authors outline four specific contributions:

1. **Development of an Open ROS 2 Wrapper:** To create an open-source ROS 2 wrapper for Florence-2, explicitly designed for local robotic deployment, ensuring its reusability and accessibility within the community.
2. **Multi-Mode Interaction Design:** To implement a flexible interaction paradigm for the wrapper, combining three distinct ROS 2 communication patterns: continuous topic-driven processing, synchronous service calls for on-demand inference, and asynchronous actions for potentially longer requests with feedback. This aims to cater to diverse robotic application requirements.
3. **Unified Interface for Task Families:** To encapsulate Florence-2's diverse task capabilities (e.g., object detection, captioning, OCR) within a single ROS 2 node, allowing a unified interface where the specific task is selected via a prompt or parameter.
4. **Functional and Performance Validation:** To conduct an initial validation of the wrapper's functionality across its different modes and assess its performance, including a throughput study comparing execution on various consumer-grade GPUs, demonstrating its practical feasibility.

These objectives collectively aim to make Florence-2 a practical and readily deployable component for enhancing perception in ROS 2-based robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this work centers on the design, implementation, and validation of a ROS 2 wrapper that integrates the Florence-2 vision-language model into robotic software architectures.

**Overall Node Architecture:**
The core of the wrapper is a single ROS 2 inference node. This node is responsible for managing the entire inference pipeline:
- **Image Reception:** It subscribes to a configurable `sensor_msgs/Image` topic to receive incoming camera frames.
- **Preprocessing:** Incoming images are converted into a format compatible with the Florence-2 model.
- **Model Inference:** The Florence-2 model is executed using the Hugging Face `transformers` interface.
- **Post-processing:** The raw outputs from Florence-2 are processed into more usable formats.
- **Output Publication:** The processed results are published back into the ROS 2 graph.
This centralized design is intended to maintain a short runtime path and simplify integration into existing camera-based pipelines.

**Interaction Modes:**
A key design principle is the provision of three complementary interaction modes to accommodate various application needs:
1. **Continuous Mode:** In this mode, the node automatically executes a pre-configured Florence-2 task on every incoming image frame. This is suitable for applications requiring a constant stream of semantic interpretations from a camera feed.
2. **Service Mode:** The wrapper exposes a synchronous ROS 2 service, `ExecuteTask`, for on-demand inference. A client node can request a specific Florence-2 task and receive a direct response, which is useful for event-triggered queries or occasional perceptual requests.
3. **Action Mode:** An asynchronous ROS 2 action, `ExecuteTask` action, is provided for potentially longer inference requests. This mode allows client nodes to monitor the progress of the inference task through feedback messages and integrate Florence-2 into higher-level, task-based execution flows.

**ROS 2 Interfaces and Message Design:**
The wrapper offers a defined set of ROS 2 interfaces:
- **Input Topic:** A configurable `/camera/image_raw` topic (`sensor_msgs/Image`) serves as the primary source for image data.
- **On-Demand Interfaces:** The `ExecuteTask` service and `ExecuteTask` action accept requests specifying the target Florence-2 task, optional task-specific text input, and an optional image payload. If no image is provided, the node defaults to processing the most recently subscribed image.
- **Output Topics:**
 - `/results_json`: A generic JSON representation of the inference result is published for all tasks, accommodating Florence-2's heterogeneous output types (e.g., plain text, structured detections).
 - `/detections`: For tasks yielding bounding boxes and labels (e.g., object detection), a standard `vision_msgs/Detection2DArray` message is published, ensuring interoperability with existing ROS 2 detection tools.
 - `/annotated_image`: A visualization topic publishes the input image with overlaid annotations (e.g., bounding boxes, labels) for detection-style outputs.
This hybrid approach aims to provide broad task coverage while maintaining ROS-native interoperability where most beneficial.

**Task Abstraction:**
The wrapper preserves Florence-2's unified, prompt-based task token abstraction. This means a single ROS 2 node can perform multiple vision-language tasks (e.g., object detection, image captioning, OCR) simply by modifying the requested prompt or task token, rather than requiring separate nodes or model servers for each capability.

**Implementation and Deployment:**
The software is structured into two ROS 2 packages:
1. One package contains the custom ROS 2 interface definitions (`.srv`, `.action` files).
2. The second package houses the Florence-2 ROS 2 node itself, along with launch files and example client implementations for both service and action usage.
The runtime backend is implemented in Python, leveraging `rclpy` for ROS 2 communication, `torch` for tensor operations, and the Hugging Face `transformers` library for Florence-2 model interaction. Image conversion is handled by `cv_bridge`. Model loading dynamically selects between CPU and GPU execution and utilizes reduced precision (e.g., FP16) on CUDA-capable devices when appropriate.
Deployment options include:
- **Native Local Installation:** Within a Python virtual environment inside a standard ROS 2 workspace.
- **Docker Containerization:** Both a lightweight configuration and a CUDA-enabled configuration for self-contained GPU deployment are supported, aiming to enhance dependency management and reproducibility.

**Experimental Validation Setup:**
The validation focuses on evaluating the wrapper as a ROS 2 software component, emphasizing end-to-end measurements of its integration and performance, rather than benchmarking Florence-2's intrinsic model quality.
- **Software Environment:** Ubuntu 24.04, ROS 2 Jazzy, Python 3.12, and the Florence-2 implementation from Hugging Face transformers.
- **Input:** Image inputs were provided via standard ROS 2 image topics, using a repeatable image stream (e.g., a `rosbag replay`) to ensure consistent testing conditions across different hardware configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the ROS 2 wrapper for Florence-2 encompassed both functional verification across its interaction modes and a performance study focused on throughput across different GPU hardware.

**Functional Validation Across ROS 2 Modes:**
The wrapper demonstrated functional operation in all three designed interaction modes:
- **Continuous Mode:** The node successfully processed incoming images automatically, publishing both generic JSON outputs and detection-specific outputs (when the task involved bounding boxes and labels). This confirmed its ability to provide a constant stream of semantic information.
- **Service Mode:** In this on-demand mode, the node responded directly to `ExecuteTask` service requests. It was able to perform the requested Florence-2 task using either an image explicitly provided in the service request or, if no image was supplied, the most recently subscribed image. This validates its synchronous, event-driven inference capability.
- **Action Mode:** The `ExecuteTask` action proved functional, with the node emitting intermediate feedback messages during execution and returning a final result object upon task completion. The implementation accepted action cancellation requests, although the authors noted a practical limitation: the actual interruption granularity is constrained by the blocking nature of the underlying model's generation call. This indicates that while cancellation is supported, its immediate effect might vary depending on the model's internal execution phase.

**Performance Observations on Different GPUs:**
An end-to-end throughput study was conducted under continuous image processing to assess the practical operating range of the wrapper on various hardware profiles. The experiment focused on the "Object Detection" (OD) task.
The results for Frames Per Second (FPS) (minimum / average / maximum) on different GPUs and Florence-2 model sizes are as follows:

- **GTX 1060 Mobile (80 W):**
 - Base model: 5.50 - 5.81 - 5.99 FPS
 - Large model: 2.44 - 2.50 - 2.56 FPS
- **RTX 3060 Mobile (80 W):**
 - Base model: 9.23 - 9.75 - 10.1 FPS
 - Large model: 4.05 - 4.21 - 4.29 FPS
- **RTX 3080 Ti Desktop:**
 - Base model: 25.3 - 26.6 - 27.5 FPS
 - Large model: 11.1 - 11.5 - 11.7 FPS

The performance data indicates that the throughput for Florence-2 inference is directly influenced by the GPU capabilities and the model size. Specifically, the RTX 3060 Mobile GPU demonstrated an average throughput of approximately 9.75 FPS for the base model performing object detection. This suggests that achieving near real-time performance (e.g., 10 FPS) with the base model for this task is feasible with a mid-range consumer-grade mobile GPU. The desktop-grade RTX 3080 Ti provided significantly higher throughput for both model sizes, exceeding 25 FPS for the base model and over 11 FPS for the large model. These findings support the claim that local deployment of Florence-2 via the wrapper is feasible with consumer-grade hardware, albeit with varying performance characteristics depending on the specific GPU and model variant.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ROS 2 wrapper for Florence-2 presented in this work holds several points of significance and offers potential impacts on the development and deployment of robotic systems.

**Bridging the Model-to-Robot Gap:** The primary significance lies in addressing the practical challenge of integrating advanced vision-language foundation models into real-world robotic software architectures. By providing a robust, structured ROS 2 interface, the wrapper transforms Florence-2 from a research-oriented model into a readily usable component for roboticists. This helps to reduce the engineering overhead often associated with leveraging state-of-the-art AI capabilities in robotics.

**Enhanced Local Robotic Perception:** The wrapper enables robotic systems to access Florence-2's diverse semantic perception capabilities—including open-vocabulary object detection, image captioning, optical character recognition, and grounding—for local inference. This capability is particularly important for mobile robots or systems operating in environments with limited or no internet connectivity, ensuring robust and low-latency perception without dependence on cloud services. The ability to perform multi-modal local inference can enrich a robot's understanding of its environment and interaction context.

**Flexible and Idiomatic Integration:** The multi-mode interaction design (continuous topic processing, synchronous services, and asynchronous actions) offers flexibility, allowing developers to choose the most appropriate communication pattern for their specific application needs. This adherence to idiomatic ROS 2 design principles enhances the wrapper's usability and facilitates its integration into existing or new robotic pipelines, accommodating scenarios from constant perception streams to event-driven queries and long-running tasks.

**Streamlined Development and Deployment:** By offering a unified interface for multiple Florence-2 task families within a single ROS 2 node, the wrapper simplifies development. Roboticists can leverage a broad range of vision-language capabilities through a consistent API, rather than managing multiple, task-specific model servers. Furthermore, the provision of both native and Docker-based deployment options addresses critical concerns regarding dependency management and reproducibility, streamlining the deployment process across different hardware setups.

**Accessibility to Consumer-Grade Hardware:** The performance validation demonstrates that the base Florence-2 model can achieve practical inference rates (e.g., ~10 FPS for object detection) on consumer-grade mobile GPUs. This finding suggests that advanced vision-language capabilities are becoming accessible to a wider range of robotics practitioners and researchers who may not have access to high-end data center hardware, thereby broadening the adoption potential of foundation models in diverse robotic applications.

**Foundation for Future Robotic Applications:** This wrapper serves as a foundational component for future research and development in areas such as:
- **Advanced Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Enabling robots to understand natural language instructions, interpret human intentions from visual cues, or provide rich textual descriptions of their surroundings.
- **Intelligent Manipulation:** Supporting open-vocabulary object grasping or language-conditioned task planning.
- **Semantic Navigation and Scene Understanding:** Allowing robots to navigate based on high-level semantic descriptions or perform context-aware decision-making.
The open-source availability of the repository further facilitates community engagement, customization, and extension, fostering collaborative development in the field.
