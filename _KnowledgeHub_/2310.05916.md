# Interpreting CLIP's Image Representation via Text-Based Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed a systematic method to interpret CLIP's image representations by decomposing its internal components and grounding them in natural language. This approach revealed that specific attention heads specialize in semantic properties like shape and color, and enabled improving model robustness on the Waterbirds dataset by 25.2% and achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot image segmentation.

## Problem

- Limited understanding of the internal information encoded within large-scale vision-language models like CLIP, hindering debugging and improvement.
- The 'black-box' nature of deep learning models makes it difficult to ascertain why specific decisions are made, particularly at the level of individual internal components.
- Models like CLIP can learn spurious correlations from training data, leading to poor generalization and robustness on out-of-distribution examples.

## Method

- ==Decomposing== CLIP-ViT's image representation into contributions from individual layers, ==attention heads==, and image tokens, leveraging its residual connections and attention mechanism.
- Introducing ==TEXTSPAN==, an algorithm that identifies a sparse, interpretable text basis to semantically label the output space of individual attention heads.
- Applying ==mean-ablation== to quantify the direct effect of model components and generating heatmaps to visualize the contributions of image patches to specific text directions for zero-shot segmentation.

## Results

- Improved worst-group accuracy on the Waterbirds dataset from **47.7%** to **72.9%** for ViT-L by manually ablating 'geolocation' and 'image-location' attention heads.
- Achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot image segmentation on the ImageNet-segmentation dataset, with **75.21%** Pixel Accuracy, outperforming previous methods.
- Discovered and quantitatively validated that a small set of text descriptions can capture over **95%** of the variance in individual attention head outputs, allowing for property-based image retrieval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The direct effects on CLIP's final image representation are concentrated in the last four Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) layers, with MLP layers having negligible direct contribution.
> - Individual attention heads within CLIP-ViT specialize in encoding distinct semantic properties, such as shapes, colors, object counts, and geo-locations, interpretable via text.
> - CLIP exhibits an emergent spatial localization capability, where image patches contribute to the representation's alignment with specific text concepts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving and critical areas within artificial intelligence: vision-language models, explainable AI (XAI), and model robustness.

The foundational context for this work is **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training)**, introduced by Radford et al. (2021). CLIP is a landmark development in vision-language models, demonstrating unprecedented zero-shot transfer capabilities by learning joint representations from vast amounts of image-text pairs. Its ability to perform tasks like image classification, retrieval, and even segmentation without explicit fine-tuning on downstream datasets has made it a central component in numerous applications, from text-to-image generation (e.g., DALL-E, Stable Diffusion) to complex scene understanding. However, as with many powerful deep learning models, CLIP's internal decision-making process remains largely opaque, hindering trust, debugging, and targeted improvement.

This opacity highlights the growing demand for **Explainable AI (XAI)**. The field of XAI broadly seeks to make AI models more transparent and understandable. Traditional XAI methods often generate saliency maps (e.g., Grad-CAM, LRP) to highlight input regions most relevant to a model's output. While useful, these methods typically lack semantic depth, failing to explain *why* specific regions are important or what higher-level concepts the model is relying on. Other XAI approaches explore counterfactuals or try to invert features into the input space. This paper differentiates itself by focusing on **interpreting intermediate representations**, a more challenging but potentially more insightful avenue. Instead of just explaining the *output*, it aims to understand the *internal computations*.

Within the realm of intermediate representation interpretability, prior work has explored visualizing activations, understanding individual neurons (e.g., "circuits" research by Olah et al.), or mapping features to pre-defined concepts. However, interpreting large transformer models like Vision Transformers (ViT), which constitute CLIP's image encoder, adds another layer of complexity due to their attention mechanisms and residual connections. Research on transformer interpretability has started to identify "specialized heads" in attention layers (e.g., Voita et al., 2019), but often without direct semantic grounding in natural language.

A crucial aspect that sets this work apart is its exploitation of CLIP's inherent **multimodal nature**. Unlike many vision models, CLIP's representations exist in a shared vision-language latent space, meaning visual concepts can be directly associated with text. Previous work has touched on this, such as Goh et al. (2021) identifying multimodal neurons that respond to both visual and textual concepts. This paper systematically leverages this property to provide *text-based interpretations* of *individual internal components* (attention heads, image patches), moving beyond just visual inspection to actual semantic labeling.

Finally, the paper contributes to the area of **model robustness**, specifically addressing **spurious correlations**. Machine learning models, including CLIP, can learn shortcuts or rely on superficial correlations in training data (e.g., a "waterbird" always appearing on "water" backgrounds). This leads to poor generalization on out-of-distribution data. Identifying and mitigating such spurious features, as demonstrated with the Waterbirds dataset, is a significant step towards more reliable and trustworthy AI.

In summary, this research combines architectural understanding of ViT (residual connections, attention mechanisms) with CLIP's unique vision-language alignment to provide a novel, scalable method for semantically interpreting internal model components. It then demonstrates how these interpretations can yield actionable insights for improving model robustness and performance on downstream tasks like zero-shot segmentation, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in explainable and robust AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the growing disparity between the remarkable capabilities of large-scale vision-language models like CLIP and our limited understanding of their internal workings. As the authors state, despite CLIP's utility across various downstream tasks—including classification, segmentation, and generation—there remains "a limited understanding of what information is actually encoded in these representations." This lack of transparency is a significant bottleneck for debugging, improving, and ultimately trusting such powerful AI systems.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **To Deeply Understand CLIP's Image Encoder:** The core objective is to move beyond superficial analyses and gain a granular understanding of how CLIP-ViT's image representation is constructed. This involves dissecting the contribution of individual architectural components.

2. **To Decompose the Image Representation Systematically:** The authors aim to break down the complex final image representation into interpretable summands. They leverage key architectural properties of CLIP-ViT, specifically its residual connections (allowing decomposition across layers) and its attention mechanism (allowing decomposition across attention heads and image patches).

3. **To Interpret Decomposed Components via Text:** A central and novel objective is to utilize CLIP's inherent joint vision-language space to assign natural language labels to the functions of these internal components. This allows for a human-interpretable, semantic understanding of what each part of the model is "doing."

4. **To Uncover Emergent Internal Structures:** By performing the decomposition and interpretation, the research seeks to discover emergent properties within CLIP's architecture. This includes identifying attention heads that specialize in specific semantic properties (e.g., shape, color, location) and uncovering the model's emergent spatial localization capabilities.

5. **To Demonstrate the Practical Utility of Interpretability:** A crucial objective is to prove that this deeper understanding is not merely academic but can lead to tangible improvements in model performance and behavior. This is specifically pursued through two applications:
 - **Reducing Spurious Features:** Identifying and mitigating problematic correlations learned by the model (e.g., reliance on background context for object classification).
 - **Improving Downstream Tasks:** Leveraging the understanding for tasks like zero-shot image segmentation.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between CLIP's impressive performance and its black-box nature, thereby fostering a "scalable understanding of transformer models" that can be directly applied to "repair and improve models." This work is driven by the belief that interpretability is a powerful tool for engineering more robust, controllable, and reliable AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper is a systematic, multi-faceted approach that strategically leverages the architectural properties of CLIP's Vision Transformer (ViT) image encoder and its inherent joint vision-language space. The core idea is to decompose the final image representation and then use text to interpret the contributions of individual components.

The approach can be broken down into the following key steps:

#### 3.1. Leveraging CLIP-ViT Preliminaries and Architectural Decompositions

The authors first formalize the CLIP-ViT architecture. They highlight three crucial properties that enable their decomposition:
- **Residual Connections:** The output of ViT is essentially a sum of direct contributions from its layers (MSA and MLP blocks). This allows the final image representation to be expressed as a sum of these direct layer-wise effects.
- **Attention Mechanism:** Within each Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) block, the output is a weighted sum across individual attention heads and input image tokens (patches). This enables further fine-grained decomposition.
- **Joint Vision-Language Space:** Since CLIP's image and text encoders map inputs into a shared latent space, any vector within this space can be interpreted by finding its similarity to various text embeddings.

#### 3.2. Decomposing CLIP Image Representation into Layers

- **Objective:** To identify which layers primarily contribute to the final image representation.
- **Method:** The residual structure of ViT allows the total image representation `M_image(I)` to be expressed as a sum of contributions from the initial class token, all MLP layers, and all MSA layers (Eq. 4).
- **Evaluation (Mean-Ablation):** To quantify the *direct effect* of each component, the authors employ mean-ablation. This involves replacing the output of a specific component (e.g., an MLP or MSA layer) with its average value computed over a dataset (ImageNet validation set). The resulting drop in CLIP's zero-shot classification accuracy indicates the component's significance.
- **Key Finding:** This preliminary analysis revealed that MLP layers have negligible direct effects, and only the *last few (approx. 4) MSA layers* contribute significantly to the final image representation. This finding strategically narrows the focus for subsequent, more detailed analyses.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Decomposition into Heads and Positions

- **Objective:** To decompose the crucial MSA layer contributions into their constituent attention heads and image tokens.
- **Method:** The output of each MSA block is further broken down as a sum over its individual attention heads and input tokens (Eq. 5 & 6). This yields `c_i,l,h`, the direct contribution of token *i* from layer *l*, head *h* to the final representation. These `c` terms live in the joint text-image space.
- **Derived Quantities:**
 - `c_l,h_head = Σ_i c_i,l,h`: Contribution of an individual attention head.
 - `c_i_token = Σ_l,h c_i,l,h`: Contribution of an individual image token (patch).
 These quantities are the basis for the subsequent interpretability methods.

#### 3.4. Decomposition into Attention Heads (Section 4)

- **Objective:** To characterize the semantic role of each attention head.
- **Method (TEXTSPAN Algorithm - Alg. 1):** This is the core interpretability algorithm.
 - It takes the outputs of a specific head (for a batch of images) and a large pool of candidate text descriptions.
 - It greedily selects a set of `m` text descriptions whose CLIP embeddings form a basis that explains the maximum variance in the head's outputs.
 - Crucially, after selecting a text direction, the variance explained by that direction is projected out of the remaining head outputs and candidate text embeddings, ensuring orthogonality and maximizing new information captured.
- **Text Pool Generation:** A diverse set of 3498 sentences generated by prompting ChatGPT-3.5, aiming for general image characteristics. Baselines included common words and random vectors.
- **Evaluation & Applications:**
 - **Quantitative:** Assessing zero-shot ImageNet accuracy when projecting head outputs onto their TEXTSPAN-derived text bases.
 - **Qualitative:** Retrieving images based on high similarity between their head outputs and TEXTSPAN-identified text descriptions.
 - **Reducing Spurious Cues:** Manually identifying "geolocation" or "image-location" heads based on their TEXTSPAN descriptions and mean-ablating their contributions to improve worst-group accuracy on the Waterbirds dataset (a benchmark for robustness to spurious correlations).
 - **Property-Based Image Retrieval:** Demonstrating that specific heads can be used to retrieve images based on properties like color, object count, or texture, rather than holistic similarity.

#### 3.5. Decomposition into Image Tokens (Section 5)

- **Objective:** To visualize which image regions contribute to the output for a given text direction and to perform zero-shot segmentation.
- **Method:** For a given text `t`, a heatmap is generated by computing `⟨c_i_token(I), M_text(t)⟩` for each image token `i`. This intuitively measures how much each image patch contributes to the representation's alignment with `t`.
- **Application (Zero-Shot Segmentation):** The heatmaps are binarized (thresholded) to produce foreground/background segmentations. This is quantitatively evaluated against existing explainability methods on the ImageNet-segmentation dataset.

#### 3.6. Joint Decomposition into Per-Head Image Tokens

- **Objective:** To obtain fine-grained visualizations of the information flow from specific image regions to specific text-labeled semantic directions within individual attention heads.
- **Method:** This combines the two previous decompositions: for a given head `(l, h)` and a TEXTSPAN basis direction `M_text(t)`, the score `⟨c_i,l,h, M_text(t)⟩` is computed for each image token `i`. This visualizes which parts of the image contribute to *that specific head's* encoding of *that specific semantic property*.

In summary, the methodology is a sophisticated chain of decomposition, interpretation, and application. It starts with a broad analysis of layer contributions, refines it to head- and patch-level understanding using a novel text-based interpretation algorithm, and culminates in actionable insights for model improvement and new applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, providing unprecedented insights into CLIP's internal workings and demonstrating the practical utility of interpretability.

1. **Concentration of Direct Effects in Late Attention Layers:**
 - Through mean-ablation experiments, the authors discovered that the MLPs (Multi-Layer Perceptrons) in the ViT architecture have a negligible direct effect on the final CLIP image representation. Ablating all MLPs only resulted in a minor 1-3% drop in zero-shot classification accuracy.
 - Conversely, the direct effect on the output is heavily concentrated in the *last four Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) layers*. Ablating earlier MSA layers had little impact, but removing the final ones drastically reduced performance. This finding is crucial as it significantly narrows the focus for detailed interpretability analysis.

2. **Discovery of Property-Specific Attention Heads:**
 - The core `TEXTSPAN` algorithm successfully found text-interpretable bases for the output space of individual attention heads, particularly within the influential late MSA layers.
 - A striking result was the emergence of **property-specific roles for many attention heads**. Examples include heads specializing in:
 - **Shapes** (e.g., "A semicircular arch," "An isosceles triangle").
 - **Colors** (e.g., "Image with a yellow color," "A charcoal gray color").
 - **Object Counting** (e.g., "Image with six subjects," "An image of the number 3").
 - **Geo-locations** (e.g., "Photo captured in the Arizona desert," "Picture taken in Alberta, Canada").
 - **Textures** (e.g., "Artwork with pointillism technique," "Image with houndstooth patterns").
 - Qualitative verification, by retrieving images most similar to these head-specific text descriptions, confirmed that the retrieved images indeed matched the described property, validating the semantic labels.
 - Quantitatively, projecting head outputs onto these `TEXTSPAN`-derived text bases maintained high zero-shot accuracy (72.77% compared to 75.25% base for ViT-L with 60 descriptions per head), indicating that the text bases capture most of the essential information.

3. **Improved Robustness by Removing Spurious Cues:**
 - Leveraging the identified property-specific heads, the authors demonstrated a direct application: reducing spurious correlations. On the Waterbirds dataset, where models often incorrectly classify waterbirds based on water backgrounds (and vice-versa), they manually identified and mean-ablated "geolocation" and "image-location" heads.
 - This targeted intervention led to a substantial improvement in worst-group accuracy. For ViT-L, the worst-group accuracy increased from 47.7% to a remarkable 72.9% (Table 3), a 25.2% gain, without any re-training. This powerfully illustrates how semantic understanding of internal components can be used to "repair" model behavior.

4. **Property-Based Image Retrieval:**
 - The finding of specialized heads enables a novel form of image retrieval. By computing image similarity based solely on the contributions of a specific head (e.g., a "color" head), the retrieved images are similar not in overall content but according to that particular property. This provides a more nuanced way to query image datasets.

5. **Emergent Spatial Localization and State-of-the-Art Zero-Shot Segmentation:**
 - Decomposition of the representation into individual image tokens (patches) revealed an emergent spatial localization within CLIP. By computing heatmaps representing how much each image token contributes to a specific text direction (e.g., "An image of a tiger"), accurate object localization was observed.
 - This localization capability was translated into a **state-of-the-art zero-shot image segmenter**. The proposed method achieved significantly higher performance across all metrics (Pixel Acc., mIoU, mAP) on the ImageNet-segmentation dataset compared to other CLIP-based zero-shot segmentation and heatmap-based explainability methods (Table 4). For instance, it achieved 75.21% Pixel Accuracy, outperforming the next best (GradCAM) at 70.27%.

6. **Fine-Grained Information Flow Visualization:**
 - The joint decomposition, analyzing per-head image token contributions to `TEXTSPAN` basis directions, provided fine-grained visualizations. These heatmaps confirmed that the highlighted image regions precisely corresponded to the specific property described by the text-labeled basis direction for that particular head. For example, a "shape" head's "isosceles triangle" direction would highlight triangular regions in the image. This validates the semantic meaning attributed to the heads and their internal components.

In summary, the main findings include a clear understanding of which parts of CLIP are most important, the discovery of semantically specialized internal modules (attention heads), and concrete demonstrations of how this knowledge can be leveraged to enhance model robustness and achieve superior performance on challenging downstream tasks like zero-shot segmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper makes a significant contribution to the fields of deep learning interpretability, robustness, and the practical application of vision-language models. Its impact extends beyond theoretical understanding, offering concrete methods for improving and controlling complex AI systems.

1. **Advancement in Model Interpretability for Large Transformers:** The paper provides a novel, systematic, and scalable framework for interpreting the internal representations of large-scale transformer-based vision-language models like CLIP. Unlike many existing XAI methods that provide opaque heatmaps or rely on visual inversions, this work grounds internal model components (attention heads, image patches) directly in natural language semantics. By answering *what* information specific internal modules encode and *how* different image regions contribute to those encodings, it moves towards a truly human-interpretable understanding of model decisions. This is crucial for building trust and accountability in AI systems.

2. **Actionable Insights for Model Repair and Improvement:** A major breakthrough is demonstrating that interpretability can be prescriptive, not just descriptive. The ability to automatically identify attention heads responsible for specific semantic properties (e.g., "geolocation," "shape," "color") allows for targeted interventions.
 - **Robustness Enhancement:** The dramatic improvement in worst-group accuracy on the Waterbirds dataset by simply ablating "spurious" heads illustrates a powerful new paradigm for mitigating learned shortcuts and improving model generalization to out-of-distribution data. This offers a practical, post-training mechanism to enhance robustness without expensive re-training or complex adversarial training schemes.
 - **Performance on Downstream Tasks:** The application to zero-shot image segmentation, achieving state-of-the-art results, shows that a deeper understanding of CLIP's emergent spatial localization leads directly to superior performance on challenging vision tasks. This implies that interpretability can unlock hidden capabilities within existing models.

3. **Scalability and Generalizability of Methods:** The methodology is successfully applied to large CLIP-ViT models (ViT-H-14, L-14, B-16) trained on massive datasets (LAION-2B). This demonstrates its scalability to models used in real-world applications. The principles of decomposing residual networks and leveraging multimodal latent spaces are likely transferable to other large language or vision-language models beyond CLIP, suggesting a broader impact on transformer interpretability.

4. **Enabling New Applications and Control:**
 - **Property-Based Retrieval:** The ability to retrieve images based on specific, semantically-defined properties (e.g., "all images with a red color," "all images containing two objects") opens up new possibilities for fine-grained content search and organization.
 - **Targeted Model Editing:** The work lays groundwork for more sophisticated forms of model editing, where specific undesirable biases or correlations could be removed or reinforced by manipulating identified internal components. This moves towards more controllable AI.

5. **Guiding Future AI Architecture Design:** By revealing which parts of the model learn what types of information, this research can inform the design of future deep learning architectures. For example, knowing that certain attention heads specialize in shape or color could lead to explicit architectural modules designed for these properties, potentially making models more efficient, robust, or interpretable by design.

In conclusion, this paper provides not just an insightful look into the black box of CLIP but also a practical toolkit for enhancing its capabilities and mitigating its flaws. Its primary significance lies in establishing a tangible link between in-depth model understanding and actionable improvements, paving the way for more reliable, controllable, and ultimately, more trustworthy artificial intelligence.
