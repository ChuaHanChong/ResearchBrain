# Interpreting CLIP's Image Representation via Text-Based Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed a systematic method to interpret CLIP's image representations by decomposing its internal components and grounding them in natural language. This approach revealed that specific attention heads specialize in semantic properties like shape and color, and enabled improving model robustness on the Waterbirds dataset by 25.2% and achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot image segmentation.

## Problem

- Limited understanding of the internal information encoded within large-scale vision-language models like CLIP, hindering debugging and improvement.
- The 'black-box' nature of deep learning models makes it difficult to ascertain why specific decisions are made, particularly at the level of individual internal components.
- Models like CLIP can learn spurious correlations from training data, leading to poor generalization and robustness on out-of-distribution examples.

## Method

- ==Decomposing== CLIP-ViT's image representation into contributions from individual layers, ==attention heads==, and image tokens, leveraging its residual connections and attention mechanism.
- Introducing ==TEXTSPAN==, an algorithm that identifies a sparse, interpretable text basis to semantically label the output space of individual attention heads.
- Applying ==mean-ablation== to quantify the direct effect of model components and generating heatmaps to visualize the contributions of image patches to specific text directions for zero-shot segmentation.

## Results

- Improved worst-group accuracy on the Waterbirds dataset from **47.7%** to **72.9%** for ViT-L by manually ablating 'geolocation' and 'image-location' attention heads.
- Achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot image segmentation on the ImageNet-segmentation dataset, with **75.21%** Pixel Accuracy, outperforming previous methods.
- Discovered and quantitatively validated that a small set of text descriptions can capture over **95%** of the variance in individual attention head outputs, allowing for property-based image retrieval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The direct effects on CLIP's final image representation are concentrated in the last four Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) layers, with MLP layers having negligible direct contribution.
> - Individual attention heads within CLIP-ViT specialize in encoding distinct semantic properties, such as shapes, colors, object counts, and geo-locations, interpretable via text.
> - CLIP exhibits an emergent spatial localization capability, where image patches contribute to the representation's alignment with specific text concepts.
