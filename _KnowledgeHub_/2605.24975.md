# Bridging the Gap: Enabling Soft Actor Critic for High Performance Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces principled modifications to the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, enabling it to achieve performance comparable to or exceeding Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in massively parallel simulations for high-performance legged locomotion across diverse robot platforms. The enhanced SAC, called RSL-RL-SAC, successfully closes a long-standing performance gap, offering a sample-efficient alternative for sim-to-real transfer and online learning on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), while effective in simulation, is sample-inefficient due to its on-policy nature, making it impractical for online learning or efficient fine-tuning on real robots.
- Off-policy algorithms like SAC, despite theoretical sample efficiency advantages, historically failed to match PPO's empirical performance and stability in massively parallel simulation environments for complex legged locomotion.
- SAC's underperformance stemmed from issues like miscalibrated action space exploration, biased critic targets due to incorrect handling of episode timeouts, and slow reward propagation in sparse reward settings.

## Method

- The authors proposed a modified SAC implementation (RSL-RL-SAC) that re-parameterizes the policy with tight action bounds derived from robot kinematics and initializes the actor for controlled exploration around default joint configurations.
- A refined approach to handling timeout transitions was introduced, storing pre-reset observations in the replay buffer and modifying critic targets to correctly bootstrap from timed-out states, preventing bias.
- N-step returns were incorporated into the replay buffer to accelerate reward propagation and improve learning stability, especially in tasks with sparse rewards and early terminations.

## Results

- The proposed RSL-RL-SAC successfully closed the performance gap with PPO, achieving comparable or higher cumulative rewards across all seven evaluated legged robot platforms (quadrupeds and humanoids) in IsaacLab simulations.
- SAC demonstrated superior performance over PPO in terms of cumulative reward and convergence speed (iterations) for humanoid robots, attributed to its maximum entropy objective in dense reward scenarios.
- A wall-clock time gap persists, with SAC taking longer to train due to larger network architectures, multiple optimizers, higher update-to-data ratios, and the absence of adaptive learning rate mechanisms in its current form.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precisely aligning the policy's action space with physically meaningful robot joint limits and controlling initial exploration significantly enhances SAC's stability and learning efficiency.
> - Correctly distinguishing between true task failures and artificial simulation timeouts is crucial for unbiased critic target estimation in off-policy learning.
> - Utilizing N-step returns effectively addresses issues of slow reward propagation and noisy one-step Temporal Difference targets in complex, rough-terrain locomotion tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics is increasingly focused on enabling robots to operate autonomously and adaptively in complex, unstructured real-world environments. A key long-term objective is to facilitate online learning directly on physical hardware, allowing robots to continuously adapt to novel terrains and perturbations.

Massively parallel simulation frameworks, such as Isaac Gym and IsaacLab, have become instrumental in accelerating the development of locomotion policies. These environments allow for the training of reinforcement learning (RL) agents across thousands of parallel instances, significantly reducing training time. Within this paradigm, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) has emerged as the dominant training algorithm for legged robots, largely due to its observed stability and scalability in these large-scale simulation settings. PPO has enabled notable achievements across various robot morphologies and tasks, including challenging terrain navigation and complex behaviors.

However, PPO's on-policy nature presents a fundamental limitation: it learns exclusively from data generated by the current policy and discards all previous transitions after each update. This renders PPO inherently sample-inefficient. While this inefficiency is tolerable in data-rich simulation environments, it becomes a significant impediment for real-world deployment where data collection is slow, costly, and subject to real-time constraints. For robots requiring continuous learning on physical hardware, discarding valuable experience is a critical drawback.

Off-policy algorithms, by contrast, offer a solution to this sample inefficiency. They store past experiences in a replay buffer and reuse them over multiple gradient updates, leading to substantially higher sample efficiency. Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), a maximum entropy off-policy algorithm, is a prominent candidate for bridging the gap between simulation training and real-world fine-tuning. The vision is that a single algorithm could pretrain in simulation and then efficiently adapt on hardware without the massive parallelism that PPO necessitates.

Despite these theoretical advantages, SAC and other off-policy methods have historically failed to match PPO's empirical performance in massively parallel simulation environments. Prior research has begun to investigate the reasons for this performance discrepancy, identifying issues such as miscalibrated action space exploration, incorrect handling of episode truncations leading to biased critic targets, and slow reward propagation, particularly in complex terrain tasks. Existing attempts to address these issues often involve task-specific reward engineering, the use of more complex distributional critics, or extensive hyperparameter tuning, which limits their generality and robustness. None of these prior works have systematically closed the performance gap with PPO across a diverse range of robot platforms and tasks using unmodified reward functions.

This work directly addresses this challenge by identifying the root causes of SAC's instability and underperformance in massively parallel simulation. It proposes a set of targeted, principled modifications designed to enable SAC to train stably at scale, ultimately aiming to match or exceed PPO's performance across various legged locomotion scenarios without requiring task-specific adjustments or reward function modifications. By doing so, the authors aim to advance the practical applicability of off-policy reinforcement learning for robotic control, particularly in the context of sim-to-real transfer and online adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) to train stably and achieve performance levels comparable to, or exceeding, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in massively parallel simulation environments for high-performance legged locomotion tasks.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enabling Sim-to-Real Transfer and Online Learning:** Legged robots operating in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments necessitate continuous adaptation to novel terrain and unforeseen perturbations. Online learning on hardware is considered a long-term goal for achieving robust and autonomous robot behavior. PPO, while effective in simulation, is fundamentally sample-inefficient due to its on-policy nature, which involves discarding collected data after each policy update. This characteristic renders PPO impractical for direct online learning or efficient fine-tuning on real robots, where data collection is slow, costly, and resource-intensive. Off-policy algorithms like SAC, which can reuse past experiences stored in a replay buffer, are inherently more sample-efficient. The ability to use the same off-policy algorithm for both extensive simulation pretraining and subsequent efficient online adaptation on physical hardware is a significant motivation for exploring SAC's potential.

2. **Addressing SAC's Performance Gap in Parallel Simulation:** Despite SAC's theoretical advantages in sample efficiency, it has consistently failed to match the empirical performance of PPO in massively parallel simulation settings, a common environment for training legged locomotion policies. This performance disparity has limited SAC's widespread adoption for complex robotic control tasks. The research aims to understand and overcome the technical impediments that contribute to this performance gap, thereby making SAC a viable and competitive alternative to PPO.

3. **Identifying and Resolving Core Instability Factors:** The authors specifically identify three root causes for SAC's suboptimal performance and instability in massively parallel simulation:
- **Miscalibrated Initial Exploration:** Standard SAC implementations can exhibit erratic or inefficient exploratory behaviors early in training. This often stems from a mismatch between the environment's declared action space and the task-relevant range of motion, or from using overly large initial policy standard deviations, causing the squashed Gaussian policy to spread probability mass near action limits. Such miscalibration impedes effective learning from the outset.
- **Biased Critic Targets from Incorrect Truncation Handling:** In simulation, episodes are often artificially terminated after a fixed number of steps (timeouts) for computational reasons, distinct from true task failures. If these timeouts are treated identically to failures, the critic targets become biased, as the value of future states is incorrectly assumed to be zero when it should be bootstrapped. This introduces inaccuracies in value estimation.
- **Slow Reward Propagation:** For challenging rough-terrain locomotion tasks, rewards can be sparse over short horizons, and a single misstep can lead to early termination. Under these conditions, one-step Temporal Difference (TD) targets, traditionally used in SAC, can be noisy and propagate reward signals slowly, hindering stable and efficient learning.

By systematically identifying and addressing these specific technical challenges, the research endeavors to develop a robust SAC implementation that requires no task-specific tuning, can generalize across various robot platforms, and can utilize the same reward functions originally designed for PPO. The ultimate goal is to provide a powerful off-policy reinforcement learning framework that can serve as a foundation for advanced robot learning, particularly for applications requiring online adaptation in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on systematically identifying and modifying specific components of the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm to improve its stability and performance in massively parallel simulation environments for legged locomotion. The work builds upon the existing RSL-RL reinforcement learning library and the IsaacLab simulation framework.

**Base Algorithm:** The foundation is the maximum entropy SAC algorithm. The environment is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The agent aims to maximize an objective that combines expected return with an entropy bonus. The critic is trained using an ensemble of Q-functions with target networks, exponential moving average updates, and clipped double Q-learning. The actor is trained to maximize the expected return plus entropy using the reparameterization trick. The temperature parameter (α), which balances exploration and exploitation, is automatically tuned. The policy is parameterized as a state-dependent Gaussian, with its output squashed by a tanh function to produce actions within a bounded range.

The proposed modifications, collectively forming the "RSL-RL-SAC" framework, address the identified failure modes:

1. **Policy Parameterization and Action Bounds:**
- **Problem:** Standard SAC application to IsaacLab environments often encounters a mismatch between the environment's declared action space and the physiologically realistic or task-relevant range of motion for robot joints. This can lead to either excessively small joint displacements (if actions are unscaled to `(-1, 1)`) or erratic exploration (if overly large action space bounds cause the squashed Gaussian to spread probability mass near these unrealistic limits).
- **Approach:** The authors derive "tight" action bounds directly from the robot's soft joint limits. For each joint, they calculate the distance from the default joint configuration to its soft minimum and maximum limits. These distances are then scaled by the environment's action-manager scale to obtain per-joint policy output bounds. This ensures that the policy's tanh-squashed outputs map to a physically meaningful and task-relevant range. The log-probability calculation is adjusted using the change of variables formula to account for this scaling and tanh transformation.

2. **Actor Initialization:**
- **Problem:** Even with correctly bounded actions, a large initial policy standard deviation ($\sigma_0$) can result in near-uniform exploration over the admissible action range due to the squashing function. This broad, unfocused exploration can be inefficient and destabilizing, particularly early in training.
- **Approach:** The actor network is initialized to encourage exploration concentrated around the default joint configuration with a small, controlled standard deviation. The mean head of the actor network (outputting $\mu_\phi(s)$) is initialized with small random weights and zero bias, ensuring initial actions are close to zero (corresponding to the default configuration) while maintaining weak state-dependency and gradient flow. The log-standard-deviation head (outputting $\tilde{l}_\phi(s)$) is initialized with zero weights and a constant bias, setting the initial exploration scale uniformly to a small value (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.15$) across all action dimensions, independent of the network's hidden state.

3. **Handling Timeout Transitions:**
- **Problem:** Simulation episodes are typically terminated after a fixed number of steps (timeouts) for computational efficiency, distinct from true task failures. If these timeouts are treated as terminations in the Bellman equation, critic targets become biased by incorrectly assuming zero future value at these artificial boundaries. In off-policy learning with a replay buffer, precomputed bootstrap terms become stale as the policy and value function evolve, further exacerbating this bias.
- **Approach:** The RSL-RL `VecEnv` wrapper is modified to expose both the pre-reset observation ($s^p_{t+1}$) and the post-reset observation ($s_{t+1}$) whenever an episode ends. The replay buffer then stores a "corrected next-state" ($\tilde{s}_{t+1}$) which is $s^p_{t+1}$ if the episode timed out, and $s_{t+1}$ otherwise. A timeout flag ($b_t$) and a general termination flag ($d_t$) are also stored. The SAC critic target is then calculated using a modified bootstrap mask ($m_t = b_t + 1 - d_t$). This ensures that for timeouts, bootstrapping occurs from the pre-reset state using the *current* critic and policy, maintaining consistency and preventing bias.

4. **N-step Returns:**
- **Problem:** In rough-terrain locomotion tasks, rewards can be sparse over short time horizons, and episodes may terminate quickly. Under these conditions, one-step TD targets are often noisy and slow to propagate reward signals, hindering stable learning.
- **Approach:** The replay buffer is augmented to construct *n*-step returns on-the-fly. This involves accumulating discounted rewards over *n* steps before bootstrapping from the value function of the state at step $t+n$. The calculation of the *n*-step critic target explicitly incorporates the timeout correction introduced previously: episode failures stop the return, while timeouts allow bootstrapping from the corresponding pre-reset state at the timeout step. This accelerates reward propagation and improves training stability.

**Implementation and Evaluation:** The entire framework is built as an extension to RSL-RL and further includes Random Network Distillation (RND) for exploration, symmetry-based data augmentation, and multi-GPU training support. Evaluation is conducted across seven legged robot platforms (quadrupeds and humanoids) on velocity tracking tasks over rough terrain within IsaacLab. Crucially, the authors reuse the exact same reward functions originally designed for PPO, ensuring a stringent and fair comparison. A single hyperparameter configuration is used across most tasks to demonstrate generalizability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings regarding the performance of the modified SAC algorithm (RSL-RL-SAC) in massively parallel simulation for legged locomotion:

1. **Performance Gap Closure:** The primary finding is that the proposed modifications successfully enable SAC to entirely close the performance gap with PPO. This result holds across all evaluated tasks and robot platforms, which include quadrupeds such as Anymal D, Unitree A1, Unitree Go1, Unitree Go2, and Anymal B, as well as humanoids like Unitree H1 and Unitree G1. The evaluation metrics demonstrate that SAC achieves cumulative reward levels comparable to those of PPO.

2. **Robustness and Generalization:** A significant aspect of the results is the robustness of the proposed approach. SAC achieves PPO-level performance across all quadruped tasks using a single, shared hyperparameter configuration. This indicates that the modifications are principled and effectively address general failure modes, allowing the algorithm to transfer across different robot platforms without requiring task-specific hyperparameter adjustments.

3. **Superior Performance on Humanoids (in iterations):** On humanoid locomotion tasks (Unitree H1 and Unitree G1), SAC not only matches but, in some cases, surpasses PPO in terms of cumulative reward and convergence speed when measured by training iterations. The authors attribute this advantage to the denser and more structured reward designs typically used for humanoids. In such environments, SAC's maximum entropy objective, which encourages broader exploration, provides a beneficial exploratory advantage, leading to higher final returns and faster convergence in terms of training iterations.

4. **Persistent Wall-Clock Time Gap:** Despite achieving comparable or superior performance in terms of cumulative reward per training iteration (sample efficiency), a notable disparity in wall-clock time persists between SAC and PPO. SAC consistently takes longer in real-time to achieve the same performance level. Several factors are identified as contributing to this wall-clock time gap:
- **Larger Network Architectures:** SAC often requires larger neural network architectures compared to PPO for comparable performance, which increases the computational cost of each forward and backward pass during training.
- **Multiple Optimizers and Loss Terms:** Each SAC training iteration involves optimizing multiple components (critic, actor, and temperature), each with its own loss term and optimizer. This results in higher per-iteration overhead compared to PPO, which typically performs a single joint update for its policy and value function.
- **Higher Update-to-Data (UTD) Ratio:** Off-policy replay, while sample-efficient, exposes the agent to a more diverse training data distribution. To extract a stable learning signal from this diversity, SAC typically requires a significantly higher update-to-data (UTD) ratio, meaning more gradient updates are performed for each unit of collected environmental data compared to PPO.
- **Absence of Adaptive Learning Rate Mechanisms:** SAC, in its standard form, lacks adaptive learning rate scheduling mechanisms analogous to those often employed in PPO. This necessitates the use of a fixed and typically smaller learning rate to maintain stability, which can further slow down convergence in terms of wall-clock time.

5. **Variability and Reward Design Considerations:** The research notes that SAC exhibits higher variability across different random seeds compared to PPO. Additionally, for certain tasks, such as G1 locomotion, high cumulative rewards achieved by SAC do not always correlate with perceptually "natural" or desired movement patterns. This observation suggests that the existing PPO-designed reward functions might need to be re-evaluated or refined to elicit optimal motor behaviors when used with SAC, particularly given SAC's different exploration characteristics (e.g., entropy maximization).

6. **Open-Source Release:** The work is accompanied by the open-source release of the RSL-RL-SAC implementation. This extended library includes additional features such as Random Network Distillation (RND) for intrinsic exploration, symmetry-based data augmentation, and multi-GPU training support, providing a valuable resource for the research community.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the field of robotic locomotion and reinforcement learning, particularly in bridging the gap between simulation-based training and real-world robot deployment.

1. **Enabling Off-Policy Learning for High-Performance Legged Locomotion:** The most immediate impact is the demonstrated capability of SAC to achieve performance comparable to, and in some cases surpassing, PPO for complex legged locomotion tasks in massively parallel simulation. This disproves the notion that off-policy methods are inherently unsuited for such high-dimensional, dynamic control problems in this training regime. By identifying and systematically addressing the root causes of SAC's prior instability – namely action space miscalibration, biased critic targets from timeouts, and slow reward propagation – the authors have provided a robust off-policy alternative to the traditionally dominant on-policy PPO.

2. **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer and Online Adaptation:** The success of SAC in simulation directly opens a concrete and promising path towards more effective sim-to-real transfer and continuous online learning on physical robots. Since SAC is an off-policy algorithm, a policy pretrained in simulation can be directly fine-tuned on the real hardware using the exact same algorithm. This eliminates the need for massive environment parallelism during real-world adaptation, which is a critical advantage given the inherent limitations and costs of real robot experiments. This capability is crucial for developing truly adaptive robots that can continuously learn and improve in unstructured real-world environments, representing a significant step towards achieving long-term autonomy.

3. **Algorithmic Advancements and Generalizability:** The principled modifications introduced – tailored actor initialization, precise action bound derivation from robot kinematics, timeout-aware bootstrapping, and the incorporation of n-step returns – are not task-specific heuristics. Instead, they address fundamental algorithmic shortcomings of SAC in the context of massively parallel simulation. Their demonstration of effectiveness across a diverse range of robot platforms (quadrupeds and humanoids) with a largely shared hyperparameter configuration highlights their generalizability and robustness, making them valuable contributions to the broader reinforcement learning toolkit.

4. **Practical Tool for the Research Community:** The accompanying open-source release of RSL-RL-SAC, extended with features such as Random Network Distillation, symmetry-based data augmentation, and multi-GPU training support, provides a readily available and powerful platform for other researchers. This will likely accelerate further research and development in off-policy robot learning, enabling more researchers to explore and build upon these advancements.

5. **Re-evaluation of Training Paradigms:** The work challenges the entrenched reliance on on-policy methods like PPO for large-scale robot learning. By demonstrating the viability of off-policy alternatives, it encourages a re-evaluation of the strengths and weaknesses of different RL algorithms in various stages of robot development, from simulation pretraining to real-world deployment.

6. **Directions for Future Research:** While closing the performance gap in terms of cumulative reward, the research also clearly identifies remaining challenges, such as the persistent wall-clock time disparity, higher variability across random seeds, and potential mismatches between existing reward functions and desired natural behaviors when using SAC's entropy-driven exploration. These identified limitations provide clear and important directions for future research, including algorithmic advancements to reduce computational overhead, strategies for stabilizing training, and novel approaches to reward function design for off-policy agents. Investigating the direct sim-to-real fine-tuning workflow using this enhanced SAC implementation is highlighted as a particularly promising area for future exploration.
