# Perceiver-Actor: A Multi-Task Transformer for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PERCEIVER-ACTOR (PERACT) is a language-conditioned behavior-cloning agent for multi-task 6-DoF robotic manipulation that reformulates the problem as detecting the next best voxel action. The approach leverages a Perceiver Transformer on a voxelized observation space, enabling robust performance across 18 simulated and 7 real-world tasks with only a few demonstrations per task.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation tasks require precise 6-DoF control, which is challenging to learn end-to-end due to high data costs and scarcity.
- Existing end-to-end deep learning methods for robotics (e.g., image-to-action) are highly data-inefficient, often requiring vast datasets that are impractical to collect.
- Traditional object-centric methods struggle with novel or deformable objects, while prior 3D ConvNet approaches for robot control lack a global receptive field for holistic scene understanding.

## Method

- **PERACT** frames 6-DoF manipulation as a classification task by discretizing actions into ==next best voxel action== predictions, operating on extracted ==keyframe actions==.
- It utilizes a ==Perceiver Transformer== to efficiently process high-dimensional multi-view RGB-D inputs fused into a 100x100x100 ==voxel grid==, conditioned on natural language goals via a ==CLIP encoder==.
- Actions (translation, rotation, gripper state, collision avoidance) are predicted from per-voxel features and global pooled features using ==argmax Q-functions==, trained via ==supervised behavior cloning==.

## Results

- PERCEIVER-ACTOR (PERACT) achieved up to a **34x** improvement in success rates over image-to-action baselines and outperformed 3D ConvNet baselines (C2FARM-BC) by **1.33x** to **2.83x** on simulated tasks.
- The system demonstrated high data efficiency, successfully training a multi-task agent on 7 diverse real-world tasks using only **53** total demonstrations, or a few demonstrations per task.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of language conditioning, the Perceiver Transformer's architecture, and accurate keyframe extraction for the agent's performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating robotic manipulation as a 3D voxel-based classification task for "next best voxel action" allows Transformers to operate efficiently with limited data.
> - The Perceiver Transformer's global receptive field on voxelized inputs is crucial for holistic scene understanding, outperforming methods with local receptive fields like 3D ConvNets.
> - Integrating strong 3D structural priors (voxelization) and extracting bottleneck keyframe actions are essential for achieving data-efficient 6-DoF multi-task learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: robotic manipulation, deep learning (specifically Transformers), and language grounding.

**Transformers in AI and Robotics:** Transformers, initially revolutionary in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with models like BERT and GPT-3, have rapidly expanded their influence into computer vision (e.g., Vision Transformers, ViT) and other domains, including protein folding (AlphaFold) and game playing (AlphaStar). Their ability to scale with vast datasets and capture long-range dependencies has led to groundbreaking results. In robotics, however, the direct application of Transformers has been challenging due to the inherent data scarcity and high cost of data collection in real-world manipulation tasks. While generalist agents like DeepMind's Gato have showcased impressive multi-domain capabilities using Transformers, they often require extremely large datasets (tens of thousands of episodes), which are impractical for most robotic setups. PERACT aims to bridge this gap, asking if Transformers can still benefit 6-DoF (Degrees of Freedom) manipulation even with limited data, provided the problem is formulated appropriately.

**Robotic Manipulation Paradigms:**
- **Traditional Object-Centric Approaches:** Historically, robotic perception for manipulation relied on explicit representations like instance segmentations, object classes, and 6D poses. While effective for rigid, known objects, these methods struggle with deformable items, granular materials (e.g., cloth, beans), or novel objects that are difficult to model geometrically.
- **End-to-End Visual Servoing / Behavior Cloning (BC):** More recent trends involve end-to-end learning that directly maps visual observations to robot actions. Examples include BC-Z and Gato. These methods make fewer assumptions about objects but often operate on 2D images and require immense amounts of data to learn robust 6-DoF policies. The paper points out their data inefficiency, often requiring weeks or months of data collection.
- **3D Voxel-Based Approaches:** A parallel line of work, exemplified by C2FARM, has focused on constructing voxelized 3D observation and action spaces. This approach naturally incorporates 3D spatial information and offers improved efficiency for learning visual representations of 3D actions, often using 3D Convolutional Networks (ConvNets). However, existing 3D ConvNet approaches like C2FARM typically use coarse-to-fine schemes, which might limit their global receptive field.

**Language Grounding in Robotics:** The ability to command robots using natural language is a crucial step towards more intuitive and flexible human-robot interaction. Prior work in language grounding often employed disentangled pipelines, where language primarily guided perception modules, or required vast datasets of human demonstrations or autonomous interactions. PERACT seeks to integrate language conditioning directly into an end-to-end behavior cloning framework, aiming for data efficiency.

**PERACT's Niche:** PERACT enters this landscape by proposing a novel synthesis: it combines the representational power of Transformers (specifically, the Perceiver Transformer for its efficiency with high-dimensional inputs) with the data-efficient structural priors offered by 3D voxelized observation and action spaces. By formulating manipulation as a "detecting the next best voxel action" classification task, PERACT aims to enable data-efficient multi-task 6-DoF manipulation conditioned on natural language goals. It explicitly contrasts with 2D image-to-action methods by leveraging 3D structure and distinguishes itself from prior 3D ConvNet methods by employing a Transformer's global receptive field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for PERACT stems from a critical challenge in robotic manipulation: **the scarcity and high cost of training data**, particularly when compared to the vast datasets available for vision and language domains where Transformers have excelled. The authors pose a direct question: "Can manipulation still benefit from Transformers with the right problem formulation?"

**Key motivations driving this research include:**

- **Leveraging Transformer Power in Data-Scarce Robotics:** Transformers have shown unparalleled ability to learn from large, diverse datasets. The authors are motivated to bring this architectural power to robotic manipulation, despite its data limitations, by devising a problem formulation that maximizes data efficiency.
- **Addressing Data Inefficiency of Current End-to-End Approaches:** Existing end-to-end image-to-action agents for 6-DoF tasks (e.g., Gato, BC-Z) often require prohibitively large amounts of data. PERACT seeks to develop an approach that achieves strong multi-task performance with "just a few demonstrations per task."
- **Exploiting 3D Structure for Efficient Learning:** Inspired by how Vision Transformers leverage the 2D structure of image patches, the authors aim to similarly exploit the 3D structure of voxel patches. They hypothesize that this 3D spatial prior will lead to significantly more efficient and robust learning for 6-DoF actions compared to methods operating on 2D images.
- **Integrating Language Conditioning for Generalization:** The ability to interpret natural language commands is crucial for versatile robots. PERACT is motivated to effectively ground language goals in 6-DoF actions within a data-efficient behavior cloning framework, allowing for flexible task specification.
- **Developing Action-Centric Representations:** Moving beyond explicit object-centric representations (which struggle with deformable or novel items), the motivation is to learn "action-centric" representations, focusing on the affordances and actions rather than rigid object definitions.

**The key objectives of the paper, as stated in their contributions, are:**

1. To propose a **novel problem formulation** that enables perceiving, acting, and specifying goals using Transformer architectures for robotic manipulation.
2. To develop an **efficient action-centric framework** capable of grounding language instructions into 6-DoF robot actions.
3. To provide **empirical results** demonstrating the capabilities of multi-task agents across a diverse range of simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PERACT (PERCEIVER-ACTOR) is designed as a language-conditioned behavior-cloning (BC) agent for 6-DoF robotic manipulation. Its core methodology revolves around framing the problem as a classification task where the agent "detects the next best voxel action."

#### 3.1. Observation and Action Space Formulation

- **Keyframe Extraction:** Instead of predicting every continuous action, the system first extracts a set of "keyframe actions" from expert demonstrations. These keyframes represent bottleneck end-effector poses (e.g., where joint velocities are near zero and gripper state hasn't changed). This discretizes the temporal aspect of the task.
- **Voxelization:** Both the observation and action spaces are represented using a voxel grid.
 - **Observation:** RGB-D camera inputs from multiple views (four in simulation, one in real-world) are fused into a 100x100x100 voxel grid (1.0m^3 volume). Each voxel contains 10 channels: RGB (3), point coordinates (3), occupancy (1), and position indices (3). Proprioception data (gripper state, joint positions, timestep) is also incorporated.
 - **Action Discretization:** Keyframe actions (6-DoF pose, gripper state, collision avoidance mode) are discretized:
 - **Translation:** The closest voxel to the gripper's center.
 - **Rotation:** Discretized into 5-degree bins for each of the three Euler angles.
 - **Gripper Open State:** Binary (open/closed).
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Binary (avoid everything/nothing), crucial for tasks involving both contact and non-contact motions.
 - This voxel-based formulation provides a strong structural prior, facilitating multi-view fusion, learning robust action-centric representations, and 6-DoF data augmentation.

#### 3.2. PERACT Agent Architecture (Transformer-based)

PERACT utilizes a Perceiver Transformer (specifically PerceiverIO) as its backbone, designed to handle very high-dimensional inputs efficiently.

- **Language Encoding:** The natural language goal (e.g., "open the middle drawer") is encoded using a pre-trained CLIP language encoder. The resulting language features are then linearly projected.
- **Voxel Encoding:** The raw voxel grid is processed through a 3D convolution layer to increase channel dimensions. It's then split into 3D patches (5x5x5 voxels per patch) using another 3D convolution, akin to how ViT processes 2D image patches. These patches are flattened into a sequence. Proprioception data is also encoded and tiled to match the voxel patch dimensions before concatenation.
- **Perceiver Transformer:**
 - The encoded language features and flattened voxel patch sequence (along with learned positional embeddings) form an extremely long input sequence (e.g., 8077 tokens for 100^3 grid).
 - The Perceiver Transformer efficiently processes this long sequence by first computing cross-attention between the input and a much smaller, fixed set of **latent vectors** (default 2048 latents of 512 dimensions). This is the key to handling high-dimensional inputs without quadratically scaling attention.
 - These latent vectors are then processed through multiple self-attention layers.
 - Finally, the refined latent vectors cross-attend back to the original input sequence dimensions, producing context-rich patch encodings.
- **Voxel Decoding and Action Prediction:**
 - The output patch encodings from the Perceiver are upsampled back to the original 100x100x100 voxel grid dimensions using a 3D convolution and tri-linear upsampling. A skip connection from the initial voxel encoder aids this process, similar to UNet architectures.
 - These per-voxel features are then used to predict discretized actions by forming Q-functions:
 - **Translation:** A 3D convolution maps voxel features to a 100x100x100 Q-function, representing action values for each voxel location. The best translation is found by `argmax` over this Q-function.
 - **Rotation, Gripper, Collision Avoidance:** The voxel features are max-pooled globally, and then passed through separate linear layers to predict Q-values for rotation (72 bins across 3 axes), gripper state (2 bins), and collision avoidance (2 bins). The best action for each is chosen via `argmax`.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Supervised Learning:** PERACT is trained with supervised learning on discrete-time input-action tuples {(v, l, k)} using cross-entropy loss, treating action prediction as a classification task for each action component (translation, rotation, gripper, collision).
- **Data Augmentation:** To enhance robustness and generalization, training samples are augmented with random translation (±0.125m) and yaw rotation (±45°) perturbations.
- **Multi-Task Training Strategy:** To ensure all tasks are adequately represented, a uniform sampling strategy is employed: each training batch consists of one random input-action tuple from a randomly sampled set of tasks, giving equal weighting during gradient updates regardless of task horizon.
- **Resources:** Trained on 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs for 16 days (600K iterations). LAMB optimizer was used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PERACT was extensively evaluated on 18 diverse simulated RLBench tasks (249 variations) and 7 real-world tasks (18 variations).

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Over Baselines

- **Image-to-Action Baselines:** PERACT significantly outperformed Image-BC agents (both CNN and ViT variants), demonstrating a 34x improvement in success rates. Image-BC agents showed near-zero performance on most tasks, highlighting the inefficiency of direct 2D image-to-action mapping for complex 6-DoF tasks without strong 3D priors or extensive data. PERACT's voxel-based approach naturally integrates multi-view observations and enables 6-DoF data augmentation, which are non-trivial for image-based methods.
- **3D ConvNet Baselines (C2FARM-BC):** PERACT consistently outperformed C2FARM-BC, the most competitive 3D ConvNet baseline, with an average improvement of 1.33x with 10 demonstrations and 2.83x with 100 demonstrations. For several tasks, C2FARM-BC's performance even degraded with more data, possibly due to insufficient model capacity to handle the increased task variations.

#### 4.2. Data Efficiency

- PERACT demonstrated remarkable data efficiency, achieving robust multi-task policies with "just a few minutes of training data" (few demonstrations per task).
- For real-world tasks, a single multi-task PERACT agent was trained from scratch on only 53 total demonstrations across 7 diverse tasks, achieving respectable success rates (>65% on simpler tasks).

#### 4.3. Benefits of Global Receptive Field (Perceiver Transformer)

- Ablation studies and direct comparisons revealed that the Perceiver Transformer's global receptive field is crucial for performance.
- On tasks like "open drawer" where distinguishing identical handles requires understanding their global position (top, middle, bottom), PERACT (with its global view) achieved >70% success, while C2FARM-BC (with local receptive fields via coarse-to-fine) performed at chance (~33%), indicating the importance of holistic scene understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Language Conditioning:** Removing language conditioning ("PERACT w/o Lang") led to near-zero performance, confirming its critical role in task specification.
- **Perceiver Transformer:** Ablating the Perceiver (replacing it implicitly, as shown by results) resulted in significantly poorer performance, underscoring its necessity for handling high-dimensional voxel inputs efficiently and enabling the global receptive field.
- **Keyframe Extraction:** The quality of keyframe extraction was vital; using randomly chosen or fixed-interval keyframes led to zero performance, validating the heuristic-based bottleneck keyframe approach.
- **Skip Connections:** Skip connections in the decoder helped the agent train slightly faster.

#### 4.5. Sensitivity Analysis

- **Number of Perceiver Latents:** While 2048 latents were default, 512 latents proved competitive, showcasing the Perceiver's compression capability. More latents generally improved capacity for more tasks.
- **Voxelization Resolution:** Coarser grids (e.g., 32^3) were sufficient for some tasks, but high-precision tasks required the full 100^3 resolution.
- **Data Augmentation:** Rotation perturbations (yaw) generally improved robustness for tasks with high rotational variance but could sometimes hurt performance on tasks with constrained rotations.

#### 4.6. Limitations in Performance

- PERACT, like other agents, still struggled with very high-precision tasks (e.g., "insert peg", "stack cups", "place cups") in the multi-task setting, often achieving near-zero success. This suggests these tasks demand extremely fine motor control. Single-task training on these specific tasks showed slight improvements, indicating that multi-task optimization challenges might contribute to this.
- Common real-world failures included incorrect gripper open actions and the agent exploiting subtle biases in the training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PERACT represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for learning complex 6-DoF skills with unprecedented data efficiency. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Paradigm Shift in Robotic Learning:** By successfully applying the Perceiver Transformer to a 3D voxel-based action detection framework, PERACT demonstrates a novel and effective problem formulation for robotic manipulation. This moves beyond traditional object-centric methods and addresses the limitations of purely 2D image-to-action approaches. It validates the idea that even with limited data, the right problem formulation and architectural choices can unlock the power of Transformers for robotics.
- **Breakthrough in Data Efficiency:** The most impactful contribution is PERACT's remarkable data efficiency. Achieving robust multi-task 6-DoF manipulation with just a "few demonstrations per task" (e.g., 53 total for 7 real-world tasks) drastically lowers the barrier to entry for training advanced robot skills. This makes complex manipulation more accessible to researchers and practitioners who do not have access to massive data collection infrastructure. This is a critical step towards practical robot learning in diverse environments.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The voxel-based formulation naturally enables several crucial properties:
 - **Multi-view fusion:** Seamlessly integrates observations from multiple cameras.
 - **Action-centric representations:** Learns representations focused on actions and affordances, reducing reliance on explicit object models that struggle with deformable or novel items.
 - **6-DoF Data Augmentation:** Allows for physically meaningful data augmentations (translations and rotations in 3D), which significantly boost robustness to scene variations.
 - The global receptive field of the Perceiver Transformer enables holistic scene understanding, crucial for tasks requiring reasoning about relative object positions or properties that are visually identical at a local level.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** PERACT lays a strong foundation for future research directions:
 - **Integration with Pre-trained Models:** The use of CLIP's language encoder opens avenues for leveraging large-scale pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP's vision encoder, R3M) to further enhance generalization to unseen objects and concepts.
 - **Long-Horizon Planning:** The language-conditioned framework could be extended to hierarchical planning, where high-level language instructions are decomposed into sequences of low-level PERACT-executable skills.
 - **Improved Multi-Task Optimization:** Future work can explore more sophisticated dynamic task-weighting schemes (like Auto-λ) to improve performance on high-precision tasks that still pose a challenge in multi-task settings.
 - **Beyond Discretized Actions:** While currently relying on a motion planner for execution, this work could inspire methods for learning continuous control or dynamic manipulation by predicting sequences of actions or integrating learned motion primitives.
- **Addressing the "Data Dilemma":** The paper directly confronts the major bottleneck in robotic learning – the data dilemma. By demonstrating how architectural innovation combined with smart problem formulation can overcome data scarcity, PERACT offers a compelling blueprint for developing more capable and practical robotic systems. The successful real-world validation further cements its potential impact on deploying learning-based manipulation skills outside of simulation.

In conclusion, PERACT is a landmark contribution that pushes the boundaries of data-efficient multi-task robotic manipulation. It effectively harnesses the power of Transformers by adapting them to the unique challenges of robot learning, paving the way for more general-purpose and easily deployable robotic agents.
