# Provably Efficient Online RLHF with One-Pass Reward Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University developed a one-pass reward modeling method for online Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) that maintains constant O(1) computational and storage complexity per iteration. The approach, based on online mirror descent with second-order approximations, demonstrates improved statistical efficiency by a factor of √κ and achieves competitive or superior empirical performance compared to traditional Maximum Likelihood Estimation methods across various online RLHF scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing online RLHF methods incur escalating computational (O(t log t)) and storage (O(t)) costs per iteration due to re-optimizing reward models from scratch with continuously growing datasets.
- This linear scaling of resource demands renders long-term online RLHF impractical, especially for continuous deployment or on resource-constrained devices like edge devices.
- Many current approaches in contextual dueling bandits and online RLHF rely on Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), contributing to the computational burden and potentially limiting statistical efficiency.

## Method

- A one-pass reward modeling method based on ==online mirror descent== (OMD) is proposed, which utilizes a second-order Taylor approximation of the instantaneous loss to enable O(1) computational complexity per iteration without storing historical data.
- The framework is applied and theoretically analyzed across three online RLHF settings: passive data collection, active data collection (using uncertainty-driven query selection), and deployment-time adaptation (balancing exploitation and exploration).
- Practical implementation for high-dimensional LLM feature spaces is achieved using Hessian-Vector Products with conjugate gradient descent for inverse Hessian calculations and rejection sampling for approximating model uncertainty, reducing operations to O(d) per iteration.

## Results

- Theoretically, the method achieves O(1) computational and storage complexity per iteration and demonstrates improved statistical efficiency by a √κ factor compared to prior MLE-based bounds across passive, active, and deployment-time online RLHF.
- Empirically, the OMD-based method showed faster convergence to lower training loss and higher evaluation accuracy in passive data collection, particularly with fewer samples (e.g., T < **10**,**000**), across Llama-3 and Qwen2.5 models.
- For active data collection, the OMD method maintained comparable performance to MLE-based methods while being approximately three times faster, and its action selection strategy consistently outperformed random selection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing the instantaneous loss in OMD with a second-order Taylor approximation allows for a closed-form solution for the parameter update, achieving constant O(1) computational and storage costs per iteration in online RLHF.
> - Integrating second-order information into the OMD updates, captured by a local norm, contributes to a theoretical improvement in statistical efficiency by a factor of √κ over previous MLE-based methods.
> - Approximations like Hessian-Vector Products and rejection sampling can effectively bridge the gap between theoretically efficient algorithms and practical deployment on high-dimensional Large Language Models (LLMs).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as a technique for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences. Traditional RLHF methodologies typically rely on static, pre-collected datasets of human preferences. While these datasets have facilitated initial successes in LLM alignment, they are often characterized by limited coverage. This limitation can hinder the generalization capabilities of the learned reward models, particularly when encountering out-of-distribution samples, thereby constraining the overall effectiveness of the aligned LLMs.

To address the limitations of fixed datasets, online RLHF has been developed as an alternative paradigm. This approach involves iterative data collection and continuous model refinement, a process that has been demonstrated to enhance model performance in practice by systems such as Claude and LLaMA-2. Theoretically, research indicates that online exploration can improve the statistical efficiency of RLHF. Beyond performance improvements, online RLHF is considered a step toward agentic applications, where models can integrate environmental feedback in real time for adaptive reasoning and decision-making.

Despite its benefits in terms of statistical efficiency and adaptability, online RLHF introduces computational challenges. Current online RLHF methods typically necessitate the continuous integration of newly collected data into a growing historical dataset, followed by re-optimization of the reward model from scratch. This approach results in computational and storage costs that scale linearly with the number of iterations (e.g., O(t log t) computational complexity and O(t) storage at iteration t), which can become prohibitive in long-term deployment scenarios, particularly on devices with limited computational and memory resources.

Prior work in related areas includes online RLHF approaches primarily focused on sample efficiency through optimism-driven exploration bonuses, often without addressing the computational complexity. The field of contextual dueling bandits, a framework often used to formalize RLHF, has seen developments in handling contextual information and extending to reinforcement learning settings. However, many existing methods within this framework rely on maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) for reward function learning, which contributes to the linearly increasing computational burden. Active learning, another related area, seeks to reduce labeling costs by selecting informative samples, with approaches categorized into pool-based and stream-based settings. While some active learning techniques have been applied to RLHF, the problem of constant-time per-iteration updates in online RLHF has remained a significant area for development. This research positions itself by aiming to address the escalating computational and storage costs in online RLHF, while simultaneously enhancing statistical efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop online RLHF algorithms that achieve both statistical and computational efficiency. This involves designing methods that can continuously adapt Large Language Models (LLMs) to human preferences without incurring escalating resource demands over time.

The motivation stems from a critical bottleneck in existing online RLHF paradigms: the computational and storage costs grow linearly with the number of iterations. Traditional approaches require storing all historical preference data and re-optimizing the reward model from scratch at each new iteration. This leads to a computational complexity often scaling as O(t log t) and storage requirements scaling as O(t) at iteration `t`. Such scaling makes long-term online RLHF impractical, particularly in scenarios involving continuous interaction or deployment on resource-constrained platforms, such as edge devices, where memory and processing capabilities are inherently limited.

To address this, the specific goals are:
1. **Eliminate the need for historical data storage:** Develop a reward modeling method that processes data in a "one-pass" manner, meaning it does not require retaining all previously collected data.
2. **Achieve constant-time updates per iteration:** Reduce the per-iteration computational cost to O(1), making the update time invariant to the cumulative number of iterations.
3. **Enhance statistical efficiency:** Improve the data efficiency of the learning process, allowing the model to achieve better performance with fewer samples.
4. **Provide theoretical guarantees:** Establish formal proofs demonstrating the improved statistical and computational efficiency of the proposed method.
5. **Develop practical algorithms:** Translate theoretical concepts into empirically viable algorithms suitable for large-scale LLMs, addressing challenges such as high-dimensional feature spaces.
6. **Apply to various online RLHF settings:** Demonstrate the versatility and effectiveness of the proposed approach across different online interaction paradigms, including passive data collection, active data collection, and deployment-time adaptation.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to make online RLHF a more scalable and deployable solution for continuous LLM alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research addresses the computational and storage inefficiencies of online RLHF by reformulating the problem and developing a novel one-pass reward modeling method.

**Problem Formulation:**
The research formulates RLHF as a contextual preference bandit problem. This involves a set of contexts (prompts) `X` and a set of actions (responses) `A`. The learner selects a tuple `(x, a, a')` and receives binary preference feedback `y ∈ {0, 1}`, indicating a preference for `a` over `a'` or vice-versa. The underlying human preference generation is modeled using the Bradley-Terry (BT) model, where the probability of preferring `a` over `a'` is given by a logistic function of the difference in latent rewards `r(x, a)` and `r(x, a')`.
For theoretical tractability, a linear realizable setting is assumed, where the reward function `r(x, a) = ϕ(x, a)⊤θ∗`. Here, `ϕ(x, a)` is a known feature map (e.g., the output of an LLM's penultimate layer), and `θ∗` is an unknown parameter vector. The non-linearity coefficient `κ` is introduced to characterize the learning difficulty, defined as the inverse of the derivative of the logistic function.

**Challenges in Traditional Online RLHF:**
In standard online RLHF, the reward model `eθ_t+1` is typically estimated at each iteration `t` by maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) over the entire historical dataset `D_t+1` (Equation 1). This optimization problem does not have a closed-form solution and requires iterative techniques like gradient descent. Since each gradient evaluation depends on the entire dataset `D_t+1`, the computational cost grows linearly with `t`. To achieve an `ε`-accurate estimate, `O(log(1/ε))` steps are typically needed. If `ε` is set to `1/t` to manage optimization error, the total computational complexity at iteration `t` becomes `O(t log t)`, alongside `O(t)` storage for the dataset.

**Proposed One-Pass Reward Modeling:**
Inspired by advances in logistic bandits and multinomial logit MDPs, the research proposes a one-pass reward modeling method based on the online mirror descent (OMD) framework.

1. **Departure from Implicit OMD:** Previous efforts, such as implicit OMD, approximate cumulative log-loss quadratically and use an update rule (Equation 2) that still requires `O(log t)` computational cost per iteration, as the optimization problem itself does not have a closed-form solution.

2. **Standard OMD with Second-Order Taylor Approximation:** The proposed method (Algorithm 1) replaces the instantaneous loss `ℓ_t(θ)` in the OMD update with a second-order Taylor approximation `eℓ_t(θ)`. This leads to an update rule (Equation 3) that can be solved with a projected gradient step. Specifically, the update involves computing `eθ'_t+1 = eθ_t - η `eH_t^-1` g_t(eθ_t)` and then projecting `eθ'_t+1` onto the parameter space `Θ`.
 - `eH_t` is a local norm defined as `H_t + ηH_t(eθ_t)`, where `H_t` is the cumulative sum of past Hessian matrices `H_i(eθ_i+1)` plus a regularization term `λI`. This local norm captures second-order information, contributing to improved statistical efficiency.
 - The use of this second-order approximation within the standard OMD framework allows for a closed-form solution for `eθ_t+1`, resulting in an `O(1)` computational complexity per iteration. This, combined with the one-pass nature of the updates (eliminating the need to store historical data), addresses the core computational and storage bottlenecks.

**Applications in Three Online RLHF Scenarios:**
The one-pass reward modeling framework is applied to three distinct online RLHF settings:

1. **Passive Data Collection (Algorithm 2):** The learner observes `(x_t, a_t, a'_t, y_t)` provided by the environment. The reward model `eθ_t+1` is updated using Algorithm 1. The policy `π_T+1` is optimized using a "pessimism in the face of uncertainty" principle (Equation 4), which maximizes the estimated reward while penalizing uncertainty quantified by the local norm.

2. **Active Data Collection (Algorithm 3):** The algorithm actively selects `(x_t, a_t, a'_t)` to query for human feedback. The selection strategy (Equation 5) aims to maximize predictive uncertainty under the current reward model, identified by `||ϕ(x, a) - ϕ(x, a')||^2_H_t+1^-1`. The policy `π_T+1` is then derived from the average of past estimated reward functions.

3. **Deployment-Time Adaptation (Algorithm 4):** In this scenario, the learner generates responses to user-provided contexts `x_t` while collecting feedback. The objective is a dual one: maximize immediate rewards (exploitation) and gather informative feedback for model improvement (exploration). The first action `a_t+1` is chosen to maximize the estimated reward (Equation 6). The second action `a'_t+1` balances high estimated reward with maximal distance from `a_t+1` in the feature space, weighted by uncertainty (Equation 7).

**Practical Implementation (Section 6):**
To make the algorithm practical for LLMs with high-dimensional feature spaces (`d=4096` in experiments), approximations are introduced:

1. **Computation of Inverse Hessian:** Directly computing `eH_t^-1` (an `O(d^3)` operation) or even using Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula (an `O(d^2)` operation for rank-one updates) is prohibitive. The research employs the **Hessian-Vector Products (HVP)** combined with **conjugate gradient descent**. This solves the linear system `eH_t v_t = g_t(eθ_t)` to find `v_t = eH_t^-1 g_t(eθ_t)` in approximately `O(d)` per iteration, assuming a small, fixed number of conjugate gradient steps. The sum of past Hessians `∑ H_i` is implicitly handled by adjusting the regularization parameter `λ` with a damping function.

2. **Computation of Model Uncertainty:** Quantifying uncertainty precisely using the inverse Hessian matrix is computationally expensive. A **rejection sampling-based approximation** is used for uncertainty-driven query selection:
 - For active data collection: The method selects responses that represent the highest and lowest predicted rewards (i.e., maximal diversity).
 - For deployment-time adaptation: One response maximizes predicted reward, and another is chosen from a high-reward percentile but distinct from the first to ensure diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical results to support the effectiveness of the proposed one-pass reward modeling method for online RLHF.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The theoretical analysis demonstrates improvements in both statistical and computational efficiency:
- **Estimation Error Bound (Lemma 1):** The proposed one-pass reward modeling method achieves an estimation error bound of `||θ − eθ_t||_H_t ≤ O(√d log(t/δ))`. This implies that `||θ − eθ_t||_V_t ≤ O(√κd)`, where `V_t` is the standard Gram matrix used in prior work. This result improves upon previous MLE-based bounds by a factor of at least `√κ`, where `κ` is the non-linearity coefficient, indicating enhanced statistical efficiency.
- **Passive Data Collection (Theorem 1):** For online RLHF with passive data collection, the algorithm ensures a sub-optimality gap `SubOpt(π_T+1) ≤ O(√d ⋅ ||E_x∼ρ[ϕ(x, π*(x))]||_H_T+1^-1)`. Statistically, this improves existing `O(√dκ ⋅ ||⋅||_V_T+1^-1)` results by a `√κ` factor. Computationally, the algorithm maintains a strict `O(1)` complexity per iteration, a significant improvement over the amortized `O(log T)` complexity of MLE-based methods.
- **Active Data Collection (Theorem 2):** In active data collection, the algorithm achieves a sub-optimality gap `SubOpt(π_T+1) ≤ O(d√(κ/T))`. This matches the statistical efficiency of prior works (e.g., Das et al.) but retains the `O(1)` time and space complexity per round, contrasting with `O(t log t)` time and `O(t)` space for MLE estimators.
- **Deployment-Time Adaptation (Theorem 3):** For deployment-time adaptation, the regret satisfies `Reg_T ≤ O(d√κT)`. This constitutes a `√κ` statistical improvement over prior `O(dκ/√T)` results (Saha et al.) and maintains `O(1)` time and space complexity per round, compared to `O(t log t)` time and `O(t)` space for MLE-based methods.

**Empirical Results:**
Experiments were conducted using Llama-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct models on the Ultrafeedback and Mixture2 datasets, leveraging practical approximations like Hessian-Vector Products and rejection sampling.

- **Passive Data Collection (Figure 2, 8, 11):**
 - The proposed OMD-based method demonstrates faster convergence to lower training loss and achieves higher evaluation accuracy compared to conventional MLE-based methods, particularly in scenarios with fewer training samples (e.g., T < 10,000). This indicates superior statistical efficiency in practice.
 - Results were consistent across different models (Llama-3, Qwen2.5) and datasets (Ultrafeedback, Mixture2).

- **Active Data Collection (Figure 3, 9):**
 - The OMD-based method achieved comparable performance to MLE-based methods.
 - A notable finding is the significant reduction in training time, with the OMD method being approximately three times faster than MLE methods.
 - The active data selection strategy employed by the OMD method consistently outperformed random selection strategies, indicating its ability to identify and leverage informative data points.

- **Deployment-Time Adaptation (Figure 4, 10):**
 - The proposed action selection strategy, designed to balance exploitation (maximizing reward) and exploration (gathering diverse feedback), outperformed baseline selection strategies (random, best-second, best-worst) across both MLE-based and OMD-based methods in terms of average cumulative rewards and win rates.
 - The OMD-based method demonstrated competitive performance with the MLE-based method, further validating its practical applicability without sacrificing performance.

- **Practical Implementation Validation:**
 - The use of Hessian-Vector Products (HVP) combined with conjugate gradient descent effectively reduced the computational burden associated with inverse Hessian calculations from `O(d^3)` or `O(d^2)` to an empirically `O(d)` per-iteration cost.
 - The rejection sampling-based approximation for model uncertainty was shown to be an effective strategy for guiding exploration in active data collection and deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a critical practical limitation in the deployment of online Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Large Language Models (LLMs): the escalating computational and storage costs associated with iterative model updates. By proposing a novel one-pass reward modeling method, the work demonstrates a fundamental shift from linearly growing resource demands to constant-time updates per iteration.

The significance of these findings is multi-faceted:

- **Computational Efficiency:** The reduction of computational complexity from `O(t log t)` to `O(1)` per iteration and storage from `O(t)` to `O(1)` is a substantial improvement. This removes a major bottleneck for long-term online RLHF applications, making continuous adaptation feasible for LLMs that interact over extended periods. This efficiency is particularly impactful for deployment on resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices, where continuous, on-device learning was previously impractical.
- **Statistical Efficiency:** Beyond computational gains, the theoretical guarantees show an improvement in statistical efficiency by a factor of `√κ` (where `κ` is the non-linearity coefficient) compared to prior Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)-based methods. This means the proposed method can achieve comparable or better performance with fewer human feedback samples, which reduces the overall cost of data collection and labeling.
- **Versatility Across Online Settings:** The applicability of the one-pass method across various online RLHF settings—passive data collection, active data collection, and deployment-time adaptation—demonstrates its robustness and potential for broad utility in different practical scenarios where LLMs interact with users.
- **Practical Implementability:** The development of practical approximation techniques, such as using Hessian-Vector Products with conjugate gradient descent for inverse Hessian calculations and rejection sampling for uncertainty estimation, ensures that the theoretically efficient method can be effectively applied to high-dimensional LLM feature spaces.
- **Enabling Adaptive AI Systems:** By making continuous, real-time learning from human feedback more efficient, this work contributes to the development of more adaptive, responsive, and aligned AI systems. These systems could integrate environmental feedback to facilitate real-time interaction, adaptive reasoning, and autonomous decision-making, moving towards more agentic applications of LLMs.

Potential broader impacts include lowering the barrier to entry for institutions with limited computational resources to develop and deploy aligned LLMs, reducing the energy consumption associated with large-scale model retraining, and fostering more dynamically aligned AI agents. However, as with all AI systems that adapt from human feedback, there is a potential for reinforcing biases or being susceptible to adversarial feedback, which necessitates careful design and monitoring in deployment.

Future research directions suggested by the authors include investigating the impact of dynamically changing feature representations (i.e., when the base LLM itself evolves) and extending the framework to other preference models beyond the Bradley-Terry model, such as the Plackett-Luce model, to broaden the applicability of these efficient online RLHF techniques.
