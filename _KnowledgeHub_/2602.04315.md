# GeneralVLA: Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Models with Knowledge-Guided Trajectory Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> GeneralVLA is a hierarchical VLA that skips on-robot data entirely: a VLM+SAM affordance module marks 2D keypoints, an LLM 3DAgent plans a multi-stage 3D end-effector path from projected 3D points plus a retrieved knowledge bank, and a hybrid grasping module executes precise grasps, outperforming VoxPoser on zero-shot manipulation and generating training data rivaling human demonstrations.

## Problem

- Monolithic VLA models need large-scale on-robot demonstration data coupling observations with actions, making them expensive and hard to scale across tasks and viewpoints.
- VLM/LLM planners like VoxPoser or Code-as-Policies produce only coarse value functions or hand-crafted primitives, failing on long-horizon or precise 3D manipulation.
- Existing systems cannot accumulate and reuse experience across tasks, and fine-tuning VLMs for action prediction trades off their visual understanding capability.

## Method

- ==Affordance Segmentation Module (ASM)== fine-tunes an ==MLLM== with a ==SAM decoder== via a special token and an ==iterative positive/negative-point refinement loop== to localize object affordances precisely.
- ==3DAgent== projects ASM's 2D points to 3D via depth, then prompts an LLM (Deepseek R1) to plan a multi-stage end-effector path while retrieving and consolidating skills from a ==KnowledgeBank==.
- ==Hybrid Grasping Module (HGM)== fuses RGB-D point clouds with 3D ranges and grasp-pose candidates, filtering collisions and selecting the pose nearest the object center for precise grasping.

## Results

- GeneralVLA solves all **14** RLBench tasks zero-shot and beats VoxPoser, CAP, and Scaling-up on **10** of **14**, while those baselines cover only **9**, **7**, and **10** tasks respectively.
- Behavior-cloning policies trained on GeneralVLA-generated demonstrations match human RLBench demonstrations within **2.7%** average success rate across **12** tasks.
- Ablations show ASM reaches **63.4%** reference accuracy versus **15-24%** for GPT-4o, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL and SpatialLLM, and removing 3D points from HGM drops success to **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling perception, planning, and control lets each layer exploit a foundation model's strength without sacrificing visual understanding for action prediction.
> - Zero-shot foundation-model pipelines can substitute for costly teleoperated demonstrations, generating training data as good as or better than human data.
> - A persistent, LLM-as-judge-curated knowledge bank enables closed-loop skill accumulation across tasks without weight updates, unlike stateless single-shot planners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is currently grappling with the challenge of achieving open-world generalization, particularly zero-shot capabilities, in manipulation tasks. While large foundation models have demonstrated remarkable success in vision and language domains, translating this level of generalization to physical robots remains elusive. This paper directly addresses this critical gap, positioning GeneralVLA as a significant step toward bridging the divide.

The broader research landscape can be categorized by different approaches to robot learning:
- **Traditional Imitation Learning:** Relies heavily on in-domain robot demonstrations, leading to low generalization to unseen scenarios.
- **Monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Recent approaches, such as RT-1, PaLM-E, and OpenVLA, finetune pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to directly output robot actions. While showing promise in open-world settings, these models are often constrained by the need for large-scale, expensive, on-robot datasets. They also struggle with fine-grained coordinate prediction, long-horizon 3D planning, dynamic and dexterous manipulation due to inference frequency limits, and lack a mechanism to accumulate and reuse experience across tasks. Their generalization to entirely off-domain scenarios is still limited.
- **LLMs and VLMs for High-Level Planning:** Other works leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level macro planning (e.g., Code-as-Policies, Inner Monologue) or VLMs for generating value/reward functions (e.g., VoxPoser). However, LLM planners are often dependent on predefined control primitives, and VLM-based methods can struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks, lacking the ability to perform precise 3D motion control.
- **VLMs for Intermediate Representations:** Some studies focus on predicting intermediate representations like keypoint affordances (e.g., Robopoint) or object trajectories (e.g., RT-trajectory, Track2Act). These improve specificity but often lack comprehensive 3D planning capabilities, including obstacle avoidance.
- **Leveraging Simulation Data:** Efforts are ongoing to use simulation to generate or bootstrap large-scale datasets for imitation learning, aiming to reduce reliance on costly real-world data.

GeneralVLA distinguishes itself by proposing a *hierarchical* VLA architecture, directly contrasting with the limitations of monolithic approaches. It seeks to *decouple* the complex problem of robot manipulation into perception, planning, and control stages, allowing each stage to leverage the strengths of different foundation models. By doing so, GeneralVLA aims to achieve superior zero-shot generalization, eliminate the need for real-world robot data collection for its own operation, and automatically generate high-quality data for training downstream policies. This hierarchical design, infused with knowledge-guided planning, represents an evolution in how foundation models are applied to robotics, addressing key scalability and generalization bottlenecks that hinder widespread robot autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot manipulation framework that exhibits strong open-world generalization, particularly in zero-shot scenarios, a capability currently lacking in robotics compared to the advancements seen in vision and language domains. The authors aim to enable robots to perform diverse, novel tasks without prior demonstrations or real-world data collection.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Achieve Zero-Shot Manipulation:** To enable robots to successfully complete a wide range of manipulation tasks in unfamiliar environments, guided solely by natural language instructions, without task-specific training data.
2. **Generate High-Quality Robotic Data:** To create a scalable method for automatically generating rich, high-quality robot demonstrations (trajectories) that can be used to train more efficient and robust robot policies, thereby addressing the significant data scarcity problem in robotics.
3. **Effectively Leverage Foundation Models:** To design a framework that optimally utilizes the immense prior knowledge and generalization capabilities embedded within large vision-language models (VLMs) and large language models (LLMs), including segment anything models (SAM), for various aspects of robot control, from perception to planning.
4. **Overcome Limitations of Existing VLA Models:** To address the shortcomings of monolithic VLA models, such as their limited zero-shot capability, inability to provide fine-grained spatial coordinates, challenges in long-horizon 3D trajectory planning, and difficulties in accumulating and reusing experience across tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that while foundation models have proven to be powerful "compressed world models" for abstract domains, their full potential has yet to be realized in embodied robotics. The authors believe that a hierarchical approach, which carefully integrates the reasoning and perception strengths of these models, can unlock unprecedented levels of generalization in robot manipulation, making robot deployment more scalable, cost-effective, and adaptable to real-world complexities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GeneralVLA proposes a novel hierarchical vision-language-action (VLA) framework comprising three interconnected modules: the Affordance Segmentation Module (ASM), the Knowledge-Guided Trajectory Planning (3DAgent), and the Path Guided Low-level Policy (enhanced by HGM). This architecture is designed to leverage foundation models effectively for zero-shot manipulation and data generation.

**A. Affordance Segmentation Module (ASM)**
The high-level ASM is responsible for scene perception and identifying object affordances. It addresses the limitation of traditional VLMs in precise object localization and fine-grained segmentation.
- **Foundation Model Integration:** ASM combines a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) like LLaVA with the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for initial scene understanding and segmentation. The MLLM guides SAM by generating a special `<SEG>` token whose hidden representation is decoded into a segmentation mask.
- **Iterative Refinement:** To overcome over-segmentation or under-segmentation errors, ASM employs an iterative refinement mechanism. After an initial segmentation, the MLLM evaluates the result and provides feedback as positive or negative points. These points guide the Interaction Segmentation Tool to refine the mask in subsequent iterations (up to `n` times) until no negative points are generated or sufficient accuracy is achieved.
- **Output:** ASM outputs precise 2D point coordinates representing the affordances of key objects and obstacles, along with their semantic information.

**B. Knowledge-Guided Trajectory Planning (3DAgent)**
The mid-level 3DAgent focuses on task understanding, spatial reasoning, and generating a 3D path for the robot's end-effector. This module addresses the challenges of long-horizon planning and 3D scene understanding.
- **3D Scene Representation:** 2D points from ASM are projected into 3D space using depth information, providing 3D point coordinates and object semantics.
- **LLM for Planning:** A 3D-capable Large Language Model (LLM), such as Deepseek R1, takes the task instruction, 3D point information, and object semantics as input. The LLM then generates a multi-stage 3D trajectory, including gripper open/close states, capable of long-horizon planning and obstacle avoidance. The experiments suggest that at least three 3D points per object are necessary for the LLM to effectively understand its spatial pose.
- **KnowledgeBank:** To facilitate experience accumulation and reuse, 3DAgent integrates a KnowledgeBank.
 - **Retrieval:** During planning, the agent queries the KnowledgeBank with the current task context to retrieve relevant past experiences and skill snippets via embedding-based similarity search. These retrieved items are injected into the LLM's system instructions.
 - **Construction:** Upon task completion, an LLM-as-a-judge labels the outcome (success/failure). New knowledge items are extracted from successful (validated manipulation strategies) and failed (counterfactual signals/pitfalls) experiences.
 - **Consolidation:** Extracted knowledge items are added to the KnowledgeBank, ensuring continuous learning and evolution of skills.

**C. Path Guided Low-level Policy (with HGM - Hybrid Grasping Module)**
The low-level policy is responsible for precise manipulation, guided by the coarse 3D path generated by 3DAgent.
- **Hybrid Grasping Module (HGM):** HGM enhances grasping accuracy.
 - **Multi-modal Fusion:** It fuses RGB images, depth maps, and the 3D spatial range of the object (derived from ASM's 3D points) to obtain a localized point cloud.
 - **Grasp Pose Estimation:** A grasp prediction model (e.g., M2T2/Graspnet) is used to estimate candidate 6-DoF grasp poses.
 - **Refinement:** HGM filters out grasp poses that would cause collisions and selects the optimal grasp pose whose center is closest to the object's center point.
- **Motion Execution:** A motion planner (e.g., OMPL) then uses the 6-DoF grasp pose and the 3D path guidance to move the robot's end-effector precisely to execute the action.

**Overall Dataflow:** The process begins with RGB-D images and a natural language instruction. ASM generates 2D points and semantics. These are converted to 3D points. 3DAgent uses these 3D points and task instruction, along with the KnowledgeBank, to plan a 3D path. Simultaneously, HGM uses the 3D points, RGB, and depth to estimate a precise 6-DoF grasp pose. Finally, the 3D path and 6-DoF pose guide the low-level policy for robot execution. This entire pipeline operates in a zero-shot manner, and its successful trajectories can be recorded as high-quality training data for behavior cloning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GeneralVLA demonstrates strong empirical performance across simulation and real-world tasks, validating its hierarchical design and data generation capabilities.

**A. Zero-shot Performance in Simulation:**
- **Superior Task Coverage:** GeneralVLA successfully generated trajectories for all 14 diverse tasks in the RLBench simulation benchmark, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like VoxPoser (9 tasks), Code-as-Policies (CAP, 7 tasks), and Scaling-up (10 tasks).
- **Higher Success Rates:** GeneralVLA achieved higher success rates in 10 out of the 14 tasks compared to the baselines. For instance, in "Put block," GeneralVLA achieved 93.33% success, significantly higher than CAP's 84.00% or VoxPoser's 70.70%.
- **Importance of ASM:** An ablation study ("GeneralVLA w/o PA") showed that removing ASM's precision affordance localization led to a substantial drop in success rates across tasks (e.g., from 93.33% to 72.00% for "Put block"), confirming ASM's critical role.
- **Multi-Stage Generalization:** The framework successfully generalized to complex multi-object, multi-stage tasks (e.g., as illustrated in Figure 4), demonstrating its ability to handle long-horizon problems.

**B. Behavior Cloning with Demonstrations from GeneralVLA:**
- **High-Quality Data:** Policies trained via behavior cloning using data generated by GeneralVLA performed comparably to those trained with human expert demonstrations (RLBench baseline) across 12 tasks, with an average success rate difference of only 2.7%.
- **Outperforming Autonomous Baselines:** The RVT-2 model trained on GeneralVLA-generated data significantly outperformed models trained on data from other autonomous generation methods (VoxPoser, CAP, Scaling-up).
- **Enhanced Robustness:** Policies trained on GeneralVLA data exhibited a lower standard deviation in success rates (average 6.24) compared to zero-shot deployment (average 11.02), suggesting that distilling GeneralVLA's generated data into a policy improves robustness and efficiency.

**C. Real-world Experiments:**
- **Zero-Shot Real-World Execution:** GeneralVLA successfully generated demonstrations and completed 4 representative real-world tasks (move spray bottle, open drawer, open jar, sort object) on an Agilex-2.0 Piper manipulator.
- **Practical Advantages:** GeneralVLA demonstrated superior performance over baselines like CAP and Robopoint in real-world scenarios, for example, by correctly determining drawer orientation (a task CAP lacked pre-designed primitives for) or calculating object height for placement (a capability Robopoint lacked).

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **ASM Point Location Accuracy:** ASM achieved significantly higher object reference accuracy (63.4%) compared to state-of-the-art multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, SpatialLLM), which ranged from 15.3% to 24.1%. This confirms ASM's ability to generate precise target locations. The study also highlighted the importance of diverse data sources (VQA, LVIS, Pixel Point, Sim, Robot data) for ASM's accuracy, with LVIS contributing significantly to semantic and location information.
- **3DAgent Information Requirements:** Ablations showed that:
 - Using 3D point information (vs. 2D-only) is crucial for trajectory reasoning.
 - Providing at least 3 3D points per object (vs. 1 point) is necessary for the LLM to understand object orientation and spatial pose.
 - Including obstacle information (vs. no obstacles) is vital for obstacle avoidance and correctly inferring manipulation directions (e.g., for pulling an umbrella).
- **HGM Module Contributions:** The HGM ablation demonstrated that multimodal input (RGB, depth, 3D points), collision filtering, and selecting the grasp pose closest to the object's center point are all essential for robust grasping and overall task success.
- **Data Scaling:** Experiments confirmed that increasing the amount of GeneralVLA-generated training data linearly improves the performance of the RVT-2 policy, with a higher rate of change (slope 0.539) than RLBench-generated data (0.178), indicating the quality and scalability of GeneralVLA's generated data.

**E. Failure Analysis:**
- The primary failure mode for GeneralVLA was during the **action execution phase** (e.g., grasping failures, object slipping), indicating that precise physical interaction remains a core challenge.
- **Trajectory prediction failures** (e.g., 3D ambiguity, obstacle collisions due to limited reasoning) were the second most common.
- **Affordance prediction failures** by ASM were the least frequent, thanks to the iterative refinement mechanism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GeneralVLA represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of general-purpose robots, addressing fundamental challenges in generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

**1. Enabling Scalable Robot Learning and Deployment:**
The most profound impact of GeneralVLA is its ability to perform zero-shot manipulation and automatically generate high-quality robot data without requiring real-world robot demonstrations or human intervention. This capability is a game-changer for scalability, as it drastically reduces the cost, time, and human effort traditionally associated with training robots for new tasks. It democratizes access to robot learning, allowing for rapid skill acquisition and deployment in diverse, unstructured environments.

**2. Effective Utilization of Foundation Models' "World Knowledge":**
GeneralVLA provides a robust blueprint for how to effectively harness the vast "world knowledge" embedded in large foundation models (VLMs, LLMs, SAM) for embodied AI. By strategically decomposing the complex robot manipulation problem into distinct hierarchical stages (perception, planning, and control), it overcomes the limitations of monolithic approaches. It demonstrates that integrating the visual understanding of SAM/VLMs with the semantic reasoning and 3D planning capabilities of LLMs can lead to emergent generalizability in physical robots.

**3. Advancing Zero-Shot Generalization in Robotics:**
The empirical results, particularly the superior zero-shot performance in both simulation and real-world tasks compared to state-of-the-art baselines, highlight GeneralVLA's capacity for true generalization. This pushes the boundaries of what robots can achieve without explicit prior training for every new scenario, paving the way for robots that can understand and execute novel instructions in unfamiliar settings.

**4. High-Quality Data Generation for Downstream Policies:**
The finding that GeneralVLA-generated data can train behavior cloning policies as effectively as human demonstrations, and significantly better than other autonomous data generation methods, underscores its utility beyond direct zero-shot execution. This high-quality synthetic data can serve as a robust, scalable, and cost-effective source for training more efficient and robust compact policies, enabling robots to learn new skills faster and with higher fidelity.

**5. Robustness Through Hierarchical Design and Knowledge Guidance:**
The hierarchical architecture, combined with iterative refinement in ASM, knowledge-guided trajectory planning in 3DAgent (via the KnowledgeBank), and refined grasping in HGM, contributes to a more robust and adaptable system. Features like obstacle avoidance and the ability to learn from past experiences (KnowledgeBank) enhance the robot's reliability in dynamic and complex environments.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
The paper also provides valuable insights for future research, particularly in enhancing VLMs' 3D spatial perception capabilities. As GeneralVLA currently relies on VLMs primarily for 2D point estimation, integrating direct 3D pose estimation into VLMs could further improve performance and streamline the perception pipeline.

In conclusion, GeneralVLA marks a crucial step toward building truly generalizable robots. By creatively integrating and leveraging the strengths of foundation models within a hierarchical framework, it addresses the core challenges of data scarcity and zero-shot generalization, offering a scalable and robust pathway for the future of embodied AI.
