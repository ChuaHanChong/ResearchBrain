# A System-Level View on Out-of-Distribution Data in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University present a conceptual framework for addressing out-of-distribution (OOD) data in robotics, emphasizing a system-level perspective over isolated machine learning model performance. The work organizes OOD challenges and outlines key research questions across real-time, episodic, and data lifecycle timescales to foster trustworthy autonomous systems.

## Problem

- Machine learning models integrated into robotic autonomy stacks often exhibit unreliable behavior when encountering data outside their training distribution (OOD), posing risks in safety-critical applications.
- The concept of OOD data lacks a precise and universally agreed-upon definition within robotics, varying across problem formalisms and applications.
- Existing OOD research in machine learning primarily focuses on individual model robustness, neglecting the system-level complexities of robotic feedback loops, dynamic interactions, and consequential physical actions.

## Method

- Proposed a ==system-level perspective== for OOD in robotics, focusing on the overall ==system-level competence== of a robot rather than just individual ML model performance.
- Provided a ==dual conceptualization of OOD data==, distinguishing between ==distributional shifts== (covariate, concept, label shifts) and ==functional uncertainty== (a model's domain of competence).
- Structured OOD challenges and articulated six key research questions across three distinct operational timescales: ==real-time decision-making==, ==episodic closed-loop interaction==, and the ==data lifecycle==.

## Results

- Established a conceptual framework clarifying the OOD problem within a system-level context, formulating **six research questions (RQ1-RQ6)** for reliable, learning-enabled open-world autonomy spanning real-time perception to long-term data management.
- Identified critical gaps in current ML approaches to OOD when applied to robotics, particularly concerning **uncertainty propagation**, **temporal correlations**, and **active system-environment interaction**.
- Called for the development of new, robotics-specific benchmark datasets and simulation platforms to evaluate **closed-loop OOD performance** and capture organic failure modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Addressing OOD in robotics requires a holistic, system-level approach that considers the entire autonomy stack and its dynamic interaction with the environment.
> - Robotic systems introduce unique OOD complexities, such as continuous feedback loops and temporally correlated events, which demand tailored solutions beyond model-centric ML techniques.
> - Effective OOD management involves integrating runtime monitoring, uncertainty-aware decision-making, active mitigation of distributional shifts, and principled data collection for continuous system improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of machine learning (ML) systems into robotic autonomy stacks is increasing, driven by advances in deep learning. ML models are being utilized for tasks such as perception, prediction, and decision-making in complex environments. However, a significant challenge arises when these models encounter data that differs from their training distribution, known as out-of-distribution (OOD) data. OOD inputs can lead to unreliable behavior in learned components, posing risks, particularly in safety-critical applications like autonomous vehicles or home assistance robots.

The broader ML community has extensively researched OOD problems, developing techniques for domain generalization, domain adaptation, anomaly detection, and predictive uncertainty quantification. These efforts often focus on the performance and robustness of individual ML models in isolation, under various forms of distributional shift. While these model-level approaches are fundamental, the unique characteristics of robotic systems introduce additional complexities. Robots operate in a continuous feedback loop, interacting dynamically with environments that present temporally correlated OOD events, and require real-time decision-making with consequential physical actions.

This paper positions itself not as an algorithmic contribution or a comprehensive survey of existing ML or robotics literature on OOD. Instead, it aims to bridge the gap between model-centric ML research on OOD and the system-level requirements of robust robotic autonomy. It argues that addressing OOD in robotics necessitates a holistic, system-level perspective that considers the entire autonomy stack and its interaction with the environment, rather than focusing solely on the reliability of individual learned components. By conceptualizing OOD challenges across different operational timescales and formulating key research questions, this work seeks to establish a framework that guides future research in reliable, learning-enabled open-world autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the increasing reliance on machine learning models within robotic autonomy stacks and the inherent unreliability of these models when confronted with data not represented in their training distribution (OOD data). As robots move towards deployment in unstructured and unpredictable open-world environments, such as autonomous vehicles or domestic assistant robots, ensuring their trustworthiness and safety becomes paramount. The authors identify that current ML models are sensitive to even subtle distributional shifts, and coping with OOD inputs remains a largely unresolved challenge on the path to dependable autonomy. Furthermore, the concept of what constitutes "OOD data" often lacks a precise, universally agreed-upon definition in the context of robotics, varying across problem formalisms and applications.

Against this background, the paper articulates several key objectives:

1. **Demystify the OOD Problem in Robotics:** The authors aim to clarify and concretize the often nebulous notion of the OOD problem specifically within the domain of data-driven robotic systems. This involves drawing explicit connections to existing and emerging approaches within the broader ML community that study the effects of OOD data on learned models.
2. **Advocate for a System-Level Perspective:** A central objective is to argue that roboticists should reason about the overall "system-level competence" of a robot when it operates under OOD conditions. This perspective extends beyond evaluating individual ML model performance, instead considering how OOD data impacts downstream decision-making and how components across the entire autonomy stack can be leveraged to mitigate negative consequences.
3. **Highlight Key Research Questions Across Timescales:** To guide future research, the paper aims to present and elaborate upon critical research questions (RQs) that arise from this system-level view of OOD problems. These RQs are structured across three distinct operational timescales relevant to robotic systems:
- **Real-time Decision-Making:** Addressing immediate challenges in how robots perceive and act based on OOD inputs.
- **Episodic Interaction:** Considering the influence of OOD conditions over extended periods of task execution and sequential decision-making.
- **Data Lifecycle:** Examining how data collected during operation can be utilized to continuously improve the OOD robustness and reliability of robotic systems.
4. **Galvanize Research Interest:** By establishing a clear perspective and context for the OOD challenge in robotics, the authors intend to stimulate greater research activity in this area, which they identify as critical for improving the reliability of autonomous robots.

The paper explicitly states that it is neither an algorithmic contribution nor a comprehensive survey of existing literature. Its motivation is to provide a conceptual framework and highlight gaps that necessitate a shift in focus from isolated model performance to integrated system reliability in the face of OOD data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper adopts a conceptual and analytical approach rather than presenting empirical results or novel algorithms. Its methodology centers on synthesizing existing ML concepts of OOD data, adapting them to the specific challenges of robotics, and articulating a system-level framework for addressing these challenges.

The key steps in their approach include:

1. **Conceptualization of OOD Data:** The authors begin by providing a structured understanding of what makes data OOD. They define two complementary perspectives:
- **Distributional Shifts:** This perspective addresses situations where the test data distribution ($P_{test}$) differs from the training data distribution ($P_{train}$). They categorize these shifts into concept shift (fundamental changes in underlying processes), covariate shift (changes in input distribution $p(x)$ while $p(y|x)$ remains constant), and label shift (changes in output distribution $p(y)$ while $p(x|y)$ remains constant).
- **Functional Uncertainty:** This perspective focuses on characterizing the "domain of competence" of a trained model ($f$), i.e., when and where its individual predictions can be trusted. Functional uncertainty can arise from epistemic uncertainty and can be exacerbated by distributional shifts.
2. **Running Examples:** To concretize the diverse manifestations of OOD challenges in robotics, the paper introduces two conceptual running examples: an autonomous drone delivery service and robotic manipulators assisting in the home. These examples are used throughout the paper to illustrate how different OOD scenarios impact various learning-enabled components and overall system performance.
3. **Review of ML Trends in OOD:** The authors provide a concise overview of current trends in the ML community for tackling OOD problems. This review is structured around two main categories:
- **Coping with Distributional Shift:** Discussing techniques like domain generalization (e.g., distributional robustness, causal inference, foundation models) and domain adaptation (e.g., importance reweighting, invariant feature learning, continual learning, meta-learning).
- **Assessing Functional Uncertainty:** Covering methods such as anomaly detection and predictive uncertainty quantification (e.g., calibration, conformal inference, Bayesian ML approximations).
4. **System-Level Framework for Robotics:** The core of the paper's methodology is the proposal of a system-level perspective for OOD in robotics. This involves:
- **Timescale-Based Analysis:** Structuring the OOD challenges across three distinct operational timescales:
 - **Real-time ML-Enabled Decision Making:** Focuses on immediate impact and runtime monitoring.
 - **Episodic Closed-Loop Interaction:** Considers the influence of OOD conditions over prolonged task execution.
 - **Data Lifecycle:** Addresses long-term improvement through data collection, evaluation, and retraining.
- **Formulation of Research Questions (RQs):** Within each timescale, the authors pose specific, open-ended research questions (RQ1-RQ6) that highlight key challenges and areas for future investigation. These RQs connect the model-level OOD concepts from ML to the system-level realities of robotics (e.g., sensor integration, feedback loops, fallback strategies, policy implications, data management).
- **Discussion of Current Research Gaps:** For each RQ, the authors briefly discuss existing research trends in ML and robotics, identifying where current approaches fall short of addressing the system-level challenges.
5. **Emphasis on Evaluation:** The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for new benchmark datasets and simulation platforms tailored to evaluating closed-loop OOD performance in robotic systems, reflecting the complex, organic failure modes encountered in real-world deployments.

In essence, the methodology involves constructing a conceptual lens through which to view and categorize OOD challenges in robotics, drawing parallels with and distinguishing from existing ML research, and then using this lens to generate a set of focused research directions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a conceptual and perspective paper, its "findings" are not empirical results but rather the proposed framework, identified challenges, and formulated research questions for the robotics community.

The main findings and results are encapsulated in the following points:

1. **Necessity of a System-Level OOD View:** The paper establishes that the OOD problem in robotics cannot be adequately addressed by focusing solely on individual ML model performance. It argues for a "system-level competence" view, where the reliability of the entire autonomy stack under OOD conditions is the primary concern, considering the downstream impact on decision-making and leveraging all available system components for mitigation.
2. **Dual Conceptualization of OOD:** The authors concretize the OOD concept using two complementary perspectives:
- **Distributional Shifts:** Characterized by differences between training and test data distributions (e.g., covariate, concept, label shifts), impacting population-level statistics.
- **Functional Uncertainty:** Reflecting a model's lack of confidence in individual predictions, tied to its domain of competence, and arising from factors like rare inputs or epistemic uncertainty.
3. **Identification of OOD Challenges Across Three Timescales:** The paper systematically organizes OOD challenges into three distinct operational timescales for robotic systems:
- **Real-time ML-Enabled Decision Making:**
 - **RQ 1 (Averting OOD failures through Runtime Monitoring):** The need to leverage full-stack sensory information to detect potential poor performance of ML-reliant decision systems *before* failures occur, requiring goal-oriented measures of uncertainty propagation.
 - **RQ 2 (OOD Aware Decision Making):** The requirement to design decision-making systems compatible with runtime monitors and robust to high functional uncertainty, integrating uncertainty assessment with fallback strategies and redundancy.
- **Episodic Closed-Loop Interaction:**
 - **RQ 3 (Temporally Correlated OOD events):** The challenge of developing methods that account for temporal correlations in inputs when evaluating learned models repeatedly under shifted conditions over an episode, moving beyond i.i.d. assumptions.
 - **RQ 4 (Mitigating Distributional Shifts):** The imperative to construct decision-making algorithms that can actively mitigate distributional shifts between training and deployment, enabling robots to control their interaction with the environment to stay "in-distribution."
- **Data Lifecycle:**
 - **RQ 5 (Leveraging Operational Data):** The problem of effectively using diverse operational data for retraining to improve the robustness and quality of learned models, moving beyond simple data augmentation to approaches like meta-learning or distributionally robust learning.
 - **RQ 6 (Efficient Data Collection):** The necessity of selecting operational data efficiently for storage and labeling to improve models, connecting to active learning and informativeness of uncertain inputs.
4. **Gaps in Existing ML Approaches for Robotics:** The analysis reveals that while ML research offers valuable model-level techniques (e.g., anomaly detection, predictive uncertainty), these often do not directly address system-level concerns such as propagating uncertainty through a complex autonomy stack, handling temporal correlations, facilitating active mitigation of shifts, or optimizing data collection within a robotic feedback loop.
5. **Call for Robotics-Specific OOD Evaluation Benchmarks:** The paper concludes by noting the critical need for new, accessible simulation platforms and benchmark datasets with held-out OOD scenarios that accurately reflect the "organic failure modes" experienced by mature robotic systems in varied real-world deployments.

These findings collectively represent a structured articulation of the OOD problem in robotics, emphasizing its unique complexities and providing a conceptual roadmap for future research directions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of "A System-Level View on Out-of-Distribution Data in Robotics" lies in its potential to reorient and galvanize research efforts towards a more holistic understanding and resolution of OOD challenges in autonomous systems. By shifting the discourse from isolated ML model performance to the overall competence and reliability of the full robotic autonomy stack, the paper provides a framework that is directly relevant to the practical deployment of robots in complex, open-world environments.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Accelerating Trustworthy Autonomy:** By providing a structured and comprehensive view of OOD challenges, the paper can accelerate the development of more trustworthy, reliable, and safe learning-enabled autonomous systems. Addressing OOD at a system level is critical for achieving the level of dependability required for widespread robot deployment in safety-critical applications.
2. **Guiding Interdisciplinary Research:** The paper explicitly connects existing ML research on OOD with the specific requirements of robotics. This can foster greater interdisciplinary collaboration, encouraging ML researchers to consider the unique system-level constraints and opportunities within robotics, and prompting roboticists to leverage and adapt advanced ML techniques more effectively.
3. **Informing System Design and Engineering:** The articulated research questions across real-time, episodic, and data lifecycle timescales offer concrete targets for robotic system architects and engineers. This can lead to the design of new autonomy stacks that intrinsically account for OOD conditions, incorporating elements like runtime monitors, uncertainty-aware decision-making, adaptive policies, and efficient data management from the outset.
4. **Developing Novel Evaluation Methodologies:** The call for robotics-specific OOD evaluation benchmarks is significant. Current ML benchmarks often do not capture the complexity of closed-loop robotic interactions, temporal correlations, or the downstream impact of OOD on physical actions. This paper's perspective can spur the creation of new simulation platforms and real-world testbeds that accurately assess system-level OOD robustness, thereby validating research progress more effectively.
5. **Mitigating Deployment Risks:** A clearer understanding and systematic approach to OOD data can help in mitigating the risks associated with deploying learning-enabled robots in unforeseen conditions. This is crucial for gaining public trust and satisfying regulatory requirements for autonomous systems.
6. **Fostering Continuous Improvement Cycles:** The emphasis on the data lifecycle and efficient data collection strategies can lead to more effective methods for robots to learn from their operational experiences. This means deployed robots can continuously improve their OOD resilience over time, becoming more robust to new environments and tasks through principled data feedback loops.

Overall, this paper contributes by establishing a conceptual foundation and a set of critical research directions, aiming to bridge the gap between theoretical ML advances and the practical demands of reliable robotic autonomy in the real world.
