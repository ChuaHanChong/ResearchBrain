# PhysVideo: Physically Plausible Video Generation with Cross-View Geometry Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysVideo, developed by researchers from Chinese Academy of Sciences and other institutions, introduces a two-stage framework for generating videos with physically plausible and geometrically consistent motion. The method achieves superior physical realism and visual quality, obtaining the highest GPT-4o physical realism score of 0.627, by guiding full video synthesis with physics-aware, multi-view foreground motion, outperforming existing data-driven and physics-engine-based approaches.

## Problem

- Current data-driven video generation models often fail to produce physically plausible and spatially consistent motion, frequently violating fundamental physical principles.
- Existing methods struggle to incorporate 3D geometric and physical principles into pixel-space video generation, leading to inconsistencies across different viewpoints and over time.
- Physics-engine-based simulations provide valid object trajectories but are complex, require explicit 3D representations, and are difficult to integrate into efficient, end-to-end generative frameworks.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, **PhysVideo**, is proposed, which first generates physics-aware orthogonal multi-view foreground videos (**Phys4View**) and then synthesizes full videos with backgrounds (**VideoSyn**).
- **Phys4View** integrates physics-aware attention, conditioned on physical attributes (motion cues, material properties), and geometry-enhanced cross-view attention, utilizing predicted depth maps to enforce multi-view and temporal consistency.
- A large-scale, physics-oriented multi-view video dataset, **PhysMV**, was constructed using 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== and the **TaiChi** physics engine, providing 40,000 distinct motion sequences from four orthogonal viewpoints for training.

## Results

- PhysVideo achieved superior physical realism (GPT-4o Physical Realism score of 0.627) and visual quality (WorldScore Photo 94.09, Motion 93.62) compared to state-of-the-art physics-engine-based and learning-based video generation methods.
- The framework consistently generated videos with physically plausible and coherent object motion, maintaining object integrity and demonstrating realistic interactions with foreground and background elements.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of physics-aware attention, geometry-enhanced cross-view attention, and temporal attention modules to improving physical realism and motion consistency within the Phys4View stage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the generation of physics-conditioned multi-view foreground motion from background-aware video synthesis effectively enhances overall realism and spatiotemporal consistency.
> - Integrating physics-aware attention and geometry-enhanced cross-view attention, guided by predicted depth, provides inductive biases that enable data-driven models to learn and adhere to physical laws without explicit 3D simulation during inference.
> - The use of multi-view data and 3D Gaussian Splatting for dataset creation and as an intermediate structural prior offers crucial supervision for learning 3D geometric consistency within a generative framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in video generation models have led to significant improvements in visual fidelity and temporal coherence, enabling the synthesis of high-quality videos conditioned on various multimodal inputs such as text and images. These models are increasingly considered foundational for applications like interactive content creation, simulation-based reasoning, and embodied artificial intelligence in dynamic visual environments. Approaches like text- and image-conditioned video generation (TI2V) have become prominent, producing content that aligns semantic intent with visual appearance.

Despite progress in perceptual quality, a fundamental challenge persists: ensuring physically plausible and spatially consistent motion in generated videos. Existing models often produce videos that, while visually convincing at a pixel level, may violate basic physical principles. These violations can manifest as implausible accelerations, inconsistent object interactions, or motion patterns that do not account for intrinsic object properties like mass or elasticity. This limitation is attributed to the fact that real-world object motion occurs in three-dimensional space, whereas video observations provide only partial, view-dependent two-dimensional projections.

Current methods to address this issue typically fall into two categories. Data-driven generative models, while excelling in visual quality, often struggle to implicitly learn and enforce complex physical laws. Physics-engine-based methods, in contrast, explicitly use 3D representations of objects and defined material/motion properties to simulate dynamics, thereby ensuring physical plausibility. However, these methods often involve multi-stage pipelines, rely on explicit 3D models, require manual definition of simulation conditions, and can be difficult to scale or integrate into end-to-end learning frameworks. Some recent works attempt to bridge this gap by incorporating physics-aware prompting or optimizing material properties using diffusion priors, but these still face challenges in generating high-quality videos automatically from minimal inputs.

OrthoPhys aims to bridge the gap between these paradigms by enforcing physical plausibility through geometric guidance without relying on explicit 3D reconstruction during inference or full physics simulation in the generative pipeline. It proposes a foreground-first approach that generates synchronized orthogonal-view foreground motion, conditioned on physical attributes, and then uses this as rigid guidance for final video synthesis, aiming to enhance physical realism and spatiotemporal coherence. This approach seeks to provide a more scalable and integrated solution compared to traditional physics-engine methods, while improving physical consistency over purely data-driven generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to generate videos that exhibit not only high visual fidelity but also physically plausible and spatially consistent motion. The authors identify a significant limitation in current video generation models: their struggle to produce motion that adheres to fundamental physical laws, despite advances in perceptual quality. This results in videos that may appear visually appealing but contain implausible dynamics, such as unrealistic accelerations, inconsistent object interactions, or motions that are insensitive to an object's inherent physical properties like mass or elasticity. These shortcomings are particularly evident in scenarios requiring realistic interactions between moving foreground objects and their background context.

The motivation for addressing this limitation stems from the understanding that real-world motion unfolds in 3D space, governed by geometry and material properties that remain consistent across different viewpoints. Existing video generators primarily model dynamics as 2D appearance transformations across frames, which inherently struggles to capture these underlying 3D physical constraints. The authors contend that the mismatch between pixel-space motion modeling and physically constrained motion is a root cause of the problem.

To overcome this, OrthoPhys sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

1. **Enforce Physical Plausibility:** Develop a framework that can generate video content where object motion and interactions are consistent with specified physical attributes (e.g., density, elasticity, acceleration, velocity).
2. **Improve Spatial and Temporal Coherence:** Enhance the consistency of generated motion across both spatial dimensions (multiple viewpoints) and temporal sequences (smooth, continuous motion over time).
3. **Leverage Geometric Guidance without Explicit 3D Reconstruction:** Introduce a method to implicitly ground motion in physical attributes and enforce 3D spatial coherence by generating synchronized orthogonal-view videos of foreground dynamics, without the need to explicitly output full 3D structures or perform costly 3D modeling during inference.
4. **Enable Controllable Video Generation:** Allow users to condition video generation on a single image, a textual prompt, and explicit physical attributes, providing a higher degree of control over the generated content's dynamics.
5. **Facilitate Realistic Foreground-Background Interaction:** Design a two-stage process where physically consistent foreground motion guides the synthesis of the final complete video, enabling data-driven learning of interactions like contact, occlusion, and context-consistent motion.
6. **Construct a Supporting Dataset:** Create a large-scale physics-oriented multi-view video dataset (PhysMV) to train models capable of understanding and generating orthogonal-view dynamics.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to advance video generation beyond mere visual realism to incorporate a crucial aspect of real-world phenomena: physical plausibility, which is essential for more robust and applicable AI systems in dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OrthoPhys is designed as a two-stage feed-forward framework for physically plausible video generation, guided by orthogonal-view geometry.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework: Two-Stage Design

1. **Stage 1: Phys4View Module:** This module generates synchronized, four-view orthogonal videos focusing on foreground dynamics. It incorporates geometry-enhanced attention mechanisms across these views to enforce 3D spatial coherence and implicitly ground motion in specified physical attributes.
2. **Stage 2: VideoSyn Module:** This module utilizes the physically consistent orthogonal foreground videos generated in the first stage as rigid guidance to synthesize the final complete video. It learns to integrate these foreground dynamics seamlessly with the background context, modeling interactions such as contact and occlusion.

#### 3.2. PhysMV Dataset Construction

To train the orthogonal-view video generation model, the authors constructed PhysMV, a large-scale dataset.
- **Scale:** It comprises 40,000 distinct motion sequences, each captured from four orthogonal viewpoints, totaling 160,000 single-view videos.
- **Object Generation:** 10,000 diverse 3D objects were generated and represented as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) models using Trellis. Ten distinct background scenes, also as 3D-GS models, support viewpoint exploration.
- **Physics Simulation:** TaiChi was used as the physics engine, employing the Material Point Method (MPM) solver to simulate object dynamics based on specified physical properties.
- **Video Rendering:** Four-view foreground videos were rendered using 3D-GS capabilities after physics simulation. These videos contain only foreground objects as the backgrounds do not support full 360-degree rendering.
- **Metadata:** For each simulated sample, textual prompts, motion attributes, and material parameters used in the physics simulation were automatically recorded.
- **Post-processing:** Semantic foreground masks were obtained using Grounded-SAM, and relative depth maps were generated with DepthAnything V2. Trellis was used again to generate multi-view videos of segmented foreground objects, serving as multi-view video priors.

#### 3.3. Phys4View Module (Stage 1)

This module generates temporally coherent and geometrically consistent orthogonal-view videos conditioned on an input image (I), a textual prompt (T), and explicit physical attributes (P).

- **Input Preprocessing:**
 - A foreground object mask (M_f) is extracted from the input image using a semantic segmentation model (Grounded-SAM).
 - The foreground image (I_f) is isolated, and a foreground-initialized video (V_f) is created by replicating I_f across the temporal dimension.
 - A pretrained image-to-3D generation model (Trellis) provides a static multi-view video (V_m), serving as 3D structural guidance.
- **Video Prior Conditioning:**
 - V_f and V_m are encoded into latent features (H_f, H_m).
 - These latents are adaptively fused via a lightweight gating mechanism: `H_v = G ⊙ H_f + (1 − G) ⊙ H_m`, where `G` is a gating tensor predicted from the concatenation of H_f and H_m. `H_v` is then injected into the intermediate hidden representation `H_i`.
- **Physical and Semantic Conditioning:**
 - **Physical Attributes:** Object-level physical attributes (acceleration, velocity, density, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) are tokenized into a parameter-free sequence (C_phys). This sequence modulates the intermediate representation `H_c` via a physics-aware attention module: `H_phys = H_c + α_phys · Attn_phys([H_c; C_phys])`.
 - **Textual Conditioning:** Standard text cross-attention, as used in pretrained TI2V models, is applied to `H_phys` using the encoded prompt `C_p`.
- **Geometry-enhanced Cross-view and Temporal Attention:**
 - **Camera Pose Fusion:** Per-view camera pose information is encoded into a learnable embedding, concatenated with the corresponding latent, and fused via a small convolutional block to create pose-aware features.
 - **Geometry-enhanced Cross-view Attention:**
 - A dense depth map (D) is predicted from the latent feature map (H).
 - Each 2D spatial location (u_i, v_i) is lifted to 3D camera coordinates using the predicted depth and camera intrinsic matrix (K).
 - Pairwise geometric distance (d_ij) between 3D points is computed.
 - This distance is converted to a geometry-based affinity weight `w_ij,d = exp(-d_ij^2 / τ)`.
 - A depth-confidence term `w_ij,conf` is introduced to downweight ambiguous correspondences, based on local depth gradients.
 - The final geometry-aware affinity `w_ij` combines `w_ij,d` and `w_ij,conf`.
 - This geometric prior is incorporated into cross-view attention by augmenting attention logits: `s_geo_ij = (Q_i · K_j / √d) + log(w_ij)`, biasing attention towards spatially compatible and geometrically plausible regions.
 - **Temporal Attention:** The feature representation H is partitioned into four components, and independent temporal self-attention is applied to each component to model temporal dependencies, enhancing consistency.

#### 3.4. VideoSyn Module (Stage 2)

This module synthesizes a visually coherent RGB video with realistic foreground-background interactions using the output of Phys4View.

- **Motion Guidance Extraction:**
 - The original-view video (V_1) is extracted from the four-view video (V_4).
 - A frame interpolation model densifies the temporal resolution, creating an extended video sequence (V_1i).
 - A pretrained optical flow estimation model computes the corresponding flow video (V_flow) from V_1i, serving as an explicit motion control signal.
- **Video Generation:** `V_flow` is incorporated as an explicit motion control condition into a diffusion denoising process. The attention module integrates appearance, motion (`H_flow`), and semantic guidance (`C_p`) to predict the final video latent.

#### 3.5. Training Details

- **Phys4View Training:** Uses a standard diffusion objective (L_base) and an additional depth consistency loss (L_depth) based on the scale-invariant correlation between predicted and reference relative depth maps.
- **VideoSyn Training:** Adopts a diffusion-based image-to-video paradigm, training on OpenVid and WISA datasets. It minimizes the mean squared error between the denoised prediction and the ground-truth RGB video, guided by the motion cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate OrthoPhys demonstrated its effectiveness in generating physically plausible and coherent videos compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Quantitative Metrics (WorldScore, VBench):** OrthoPhys exhibited superior motion coherence and visual quality compared to prior video generative models (e.g., CogVideoX, Wan2.2, Force Prompting). It also achieved comparable motion-related results to physics-engine-based methods (e.g., PhysGen, PhysGen3D, OmniPhysGS) while demonstrating higher visual quality (Table 1). Specifically, OrthoPhys scored highest across WorldScore's "Photo" and "Motion" metrics, and VBench's "Motion," "Subject," and "Image" metrics, showing strong temporal coherence and visual fidelity.
- **Physical Plausibility (GPT-4o and Human Evaluation):** Both GPT-4o and a blind user study with 59 participants indicated that OrthoPhys surpassed all competing approaches in physical realism. It also maintained strong alignment with input text and high semantic consistency (Table 2). Qualitatively, existing methods often suffered from issues like texture degradation, incorrect motion dynamics, inconsistent object-background interactions, or object replacement, which OrthoPhys largely mitigated (Fig. 5, Appendix D).
- **Orthogonal-view Video Quality:** In evaluations against 4D generation approaches (DG4D, Diffusion², L4GM), OrthoPhys outperformed these methods in terms of motion consistency and visual quality for generated orthogonal-view videos (Table 3). This indicates its ability to generate motion that adheres to physical principles and produces consistent results from novel viewpoints (Fig. 6).
- **VideoPhy-2 Evaluation:** On an external benchmark for physical commonsense in video generation (VideoPhy-2), OrthoPhys achieved the highest physical plausibility and semantic consistency scores among all evaluated methods (Table 5).
- **Controlled Physical Controllability:** On a synthetic evaluation set, OrthoPhys significantly outperformed DG4D across proxy physical metrics, including trajectory RMSE, contact timing error, and deformation-trend error, demonstrating a more direct quantitative link between physical inputs and generated motion (Table 6).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of key components:

- **Attention Modules:** Incremental addition of physics-aware attention (Phys-Attn), geometry-enhanced cross-view attention (Cross-view Attn), and temporal attention progressively improved physical realism, motion smoothness, image quality, and motion consistency (Table 4, Fig. 8). Physics-aware attention specifically led to a significant improvement in physical realism.
- **Conditional Videos:** Both foreground mask video and orthogonal-view video conditions were found to be effective, providing explicit guidance for foreground object motion and implicitly encoding 3D structural information for spatial consistency (Fig. 9).
- **Finer-grained Design Choices:** Removing mask prior, orthogonal-view prior, gated fusion, or depth-confidence weighting led to degraded performance, particularly in physical plausibility, indicating the necessity of these components (Table 7).
- **Two-stage Design:** The full two-stage OrthoPhys pipeline consistently outperformed a single-stage variant, underscoring the importance of decoupling foreground motion generation from background-aware synthesis (Table 8).
- **Geometry-enhanced Cross-view Attention Analysis:** Detailed ablation showed that cross-view attention alone improved results, and the full geometry-enhanced variant brought further gains by better capturing spatial dependencies (Table 9).
- **Physical Parameter Sensitivity:** Controlled studies demonstrated that varying Young's modulus and density consistently affected generated motion deformation (e.g., stiffer materials deformed less, higher density increased deformation response), indicating the model's sensitivity to specified physical parameters (Tables 10, 11).
- **Dependency on LLM-estimated Attributes:** Comparison with expert-provided attributes showed that GPT-4o estimates provided sufficiently reliable physical initialization, with only marginal additional gains from expert input (Table 12).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OrthoPhys presents a notable contribution to the field of video generation by explicitly addressing the long-standing challenge of physical plausibility and spatial consistency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Physically Plausible Video Generation:** The framework introduces a novel paradigm that moves beyond purely appearance-driven video synthesis. By incorporating orthogonal-view geometry guidance and explicit physical attribute conditioning, OrthoPhys enables the generation of videos where motion and object interactions are more consistent with physical laws, a capability often lacking in prior data-driven models.
- **Bridging the Gap between Generative Models and Physics Engines:** OrthoPhys offers a practical approach that leverages the strengths of generative models (visual fidelity, end-to-end learning) while injecting physics-aware inductive biases, without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction or full physics simulation at inference time. This represents a scalable alternative to complex, multi-stage physics-engine-based pipelines.
- **Enhanced Controllability:** The ability to condition video generation on a single image, a text prompt, and explicit physical attributes (such as material properties, acceleration, and velocity) provides users with a finer degree of control over the dynamic content. This opens avenues for more precise content creation and interactive applications.
- **Introduction of a Novel Dataset (PhysMV):** The creation of PhysMV, a large-scale physics-oriented multi-view video dataset, is a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset, comprising 40,000 scenes with orthogonal viewpoints and rich metadata, provides a valuable resource for training and evaluating future physics-aware video generation and 4D modeling techniques.
- **Potential for Downstream Applications:** The generation of physically consistent orthogonal four-view foreground videos enables various downstream applications. Demonstrations include video editing, where foreground objects can be modified (e.g., rotated) while preserving a stable background, and four-view video generation with simple backgrounds, showcasing cross-view consistency. This suggests applicability in virtual reality, animation, and content creation tools.
- **Impact on Simulation and Embodied AI:** Generating dynamic visual environments with increased physical realism is crucial for advancing simulation-based reasoning, where AI agents need to understand and interact with the physical world, and for embodied AI, where agents operate within dynamic environments. OrthoPhys contributes to building more robust and intelligent AI systems in these domains.

Despite some limitations, such as challenges with complex and highly dynamic backgrounds, OrthoPhys lays a foundation for future research in more holistic scene modeling and hybrid 3D-4D representations, ultimately pushing the boundaries of what is possible in realistic and controllable video synthesis.
