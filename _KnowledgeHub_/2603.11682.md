# Entropy-Preserving Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a formal analysis of policy entropy dynamics in reinforcement learning for large language models, revealing how numerical precision settings like bf16 quantization can distort entropy and lead to training instabilities. It introduces novel entropy-preserving algorithms, REPO and ADAPO, which improve performance on reasoning tasks and enhance models' trainability for sequential learning.

## Problem

- Online policy gradient algorithms for LLMs frequently experience "entropy collapse," where the policy's probability distribution narrows excessively during training.
- This reduction in entropy limits the diversity of explored trajectories, often leading to premature convergence to local optima and impacting pass@k performance.
- Policies with collapsed entropy may lose their capacity for effective sequential learning when fine-tuned on new tasks.

## Method

- The authors formally analyzed the ==entropy dynamics== of various policy gradient objectives, deriving formulas for expected entropy change per update.
- They empirically identified that common bf16 quantization of model outputs introduces a multiplicative upward bias in probability ratio computations, implicitly favoring entropy decrease, and proposed full precision ratio calculations.
- New explicit entropy control mechanisms were developed: REPO (Regulated Entropy Policy Optimization) which modifies the advantage function, and ADAPO (Adaptive Asymmetric Clipping) which dynamically adjusts clipping thresholds.

## Results

- REPO-R and ADAPO consistently achieved higher task performance and maintained more stable policy entropy compared to their GRPO and DAPO baselines across AppWorld and AIME benchmarks.
- Policies trained with entropy preservation retained their exploratory abilities and performed more effectively during sequential fine-tuning on new tasks, unlike those trained with entropy collapse.
- Numerical fixes, including full precision ratio calculations and FP16 training, qualitatively altered entropy dynamics, enabling a numerically stabilized RLOO algorithm to achieve **79%** Test Normal and **71%** Test Challenge on AppWorld with Qwen-3-**32B**, setting a new reported state-of-the-art score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The trajectory of policy entropy during training is a critical indicator of learning effectiveness, with consistently low entropy correlating with lower final test accuracy.
> - Subtle numerical precision issues, specifically bf16 quantization, can significantly distort entropy dynamics by introducing biases in probability ratio calculations, leading to inadvertent entropy collapse.
> - Actively controlling policy entropy through methods like REPO and ADAPO not only improves performance on complex reasoning tasks but also preserves the model's trainability for sequential learning.
