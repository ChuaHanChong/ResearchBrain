# Entropy-Preserving Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a formal analysis of policy entropy dynamics in reinforcement learning for large language models, revealing how numerical precision settings like bf16 quantization can distort entropy and lead to training instabilities. It introduces novel entropy-preserving algorithms, REPO and ADAPO, which improve performance on reasoning tasks and enhance models' trainability for sequential learning.

## Problem

- Online policy gradient algorithms for LLMs frequently experience "entropy collapse," where the policy's probability distribution narrows excessively during training.
- This reduction in entropy limits the diversity of explored trajectories, often leading to premature convergence to local optima and impacting pass@k performance.
- Policies with collapsed entropy may lose their capacity for effective sequential learning when fine-tuned on new tasks.

## Method

- The authors formally analyzed the ==entropy dynamics== of various policy gradient objectives, deriving formulas for expected entropy change per update.
- They empirically identified that common bf16 quantization of model outputs introduces a multiplicative upward bias in probability ratio computations, implicitly favoring entropy decrease, and proposed full precision ratio calculations.
- New explicit entropy control mechanisms were developed: REPO (Regulated Entropy Policy Optimization) which modifies the advantage function, and ADAPO (Adaptive Asymmetric Clipping) which dynamically adjusts clipping thresholds.

## Results

- REPO-R and ADAPO consistently achieved higher task performance and maintained more stable policy entropy compared to their GRPO and DAPO baselines across AppWorld and AIME benchmarks.
- Policies trained with entropy preservation retained their exploratory abilities and performed more effectively during sequential fine-tuning on new tasks, unlike those trained with entropy collapse.
- Numerical fixes, including full precision ratio calculations and FP16 training, qualitatively altered entropy dynamics, enabling a numerically stabilized RLOO algorithm to achieve **79%** Test Normal and **71%** Test Challenge on AppWorld with Qwen-3-**32B**, setting a new reported state-of-the-art score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The trajectory of policy entropy during training is a critical indicator of learning effectiveness, with consistently low entropy correlating with lower final test accuracy.
> - Subtle numerical precision issues, specifically bf16 quantization, can significantly distort entropy dynamics by introducing biases in probability ratio calculations, leading to inadvertent entropy collapse.
> - Actively controlling policy entropy through methods like REPO and ADAPO not only improves performance on complex reasoning tasks but also preserves the model's trainability for sequential learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of reinforcement learning (RL), specifically focusing on policy gradient algorithms applied to language models (LLMs). Policy gradient methods have gained prominence for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, primarily by enabling models to learn from self-generated trajectories, a process intended to foster diverse solution exploration.

A recognized challenge in online policy gradient RL algorithms, such as GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), is "entropy collapse." This phenomenon involves the training process excessively narrowing the policy's probability distribution around already high-probability solutions, thereby reducing the diversity of explored trajectories. This can lead to premature convergence to local optima and often improves `pass@1` performance (the likelihood of generating the single best solution) at the expense of `pass@k` (the likelihood of generating any of the top-k correct solutions), which reflects the diversity of correct outputs (Shao et al., 2024; Dang et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025).

Prior research has explored various strategies to mitigate this issue. Maximum entropy reinforcement learning provides a theoretical framework for balancing exploration and exploitation (Ziebart et al., 2008; Haarnoja et al., 2017; 2018; Eysenbach & Levine, 2022). More recently, modifications to policy gradient algorithms, such as DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) and GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), have aimed to address GRPO's training instabilities and the complex interplay between off-policy drift, importance weight clipping, and entropy. Some studies have directly optimized for `pass@k` performance (Chen et al., 2025b). Other works have investigated the use of auxiliary rewards (He et al., 2025) or different clipping strategies (Yu et al., 2025; Zheng et al., 2025) to protect against entropy collapse. The relationship between the covariance of advantages and probabilities mediating entropy collapse has also been a subject of theoretical inquiry (Cui et al., 2025; Xi et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026).

This paper contributes to this landscape by formally analyzing the entropy dynamics of various policy gradient objectives, identifying subtle but impactful empirical factors, and proposing explicit mechanisms for entropy control. It argues for actively monitoring and controlling entropy throughout training, asserting that the entropy trajectory, rather than just the final entropy value, is a critical indicator of learning effectiveness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that many policy gradient algorithms, while effective for LLM reasoning, tend to reduce policy entropy during training. This reduction in entropy corresponds to a decrease in the diversity of explored trajectories, which can limit the policy's capacity for broader exploration and lead to premature convergence to suboptimal solutions. The paper highlights that a policy constrained in its exploratory abilities may not achieve optimal performance and might lose its capacity for effective sequential learning in new environments.

The authors propose that entropy should be actively monitored and controlled throughout the RL training process, rather than being a passive outcome. This perspective is motivated by empirical evidence suggesting that the cumulative entropy experienced during training, or the "entropy trajectory," is positively correlated with the final policy's test accuracy (Figure 1). A consistently low entropy trajectory is associated with lower performance.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Formal Analysis of Entropy Dynamics**: To formally analyze how the objectives of leading policy gradient algorithms (including PPO, DAPO, and GSPO) influence the entropy dynamics of the policy during training. This objective aims to provide a theoretical understanding of why certain algorithms might experience entropy collapse or preservation.
2. **Identification of Empirical Factors**: To identify practical, implementation-level factors (e.g., numerical precision settings) that significantly affect the behavior of entropy and contribute to previously observed training instabilities in RL systems for LLMs.
3. **Development of Explicit Entropy Control Mechanisms**: To propose and develop new algorithmic mechanisms that allow for explicit and adaptive control over the policy's entropy throughout training. This includes addressing the limitations of existing approaches like a fixed entropy bonus.
4. **Demonstration of Performance and Trainability Benefits**: To empirically demonstrate that models trained with these proposed entropy-preserving methods achieve superior performance on challenging reasoning tasks and retain better trainability for subsequent fine-tuning in new, sequential learning environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research combines theoretical analysis, empirical investigation into implementation details, and the development of novel algorithms for entropy control.

**Theoretical Analysis (Section 3)**
The authors initiate their methodology by analyzing the entropy dynamics of policy gradient algorithms.
- **Entropy Change Derivation**: Theorem 1 provides an approximate formula for the expected change in policy entropy per update, showing it is influenced by a multiplicative relationship between action log-probabilities (mean-centered) and the advantages yielded by those actions. Corollary 1 further simplifies this, indicating that entropy change is proportional to the expectation of `[A(s, a) · L(s, a) · π_θ(a | s)]`.
- **PPO Analysis**: The analysis indicates that PPO's off-policy updates, particularly when repeated, can amplify entropy collapse. However, Theorem 2 demonstrates that PPO's clipping mechanism bounds the change in entropy between the old and new policies, with the bounds directly related to the clipping thresholds (ϵ_low, ϵ_high).
- **DAPO and GSPO Analysis**: DAPO's asymmetric clipping (ϵ_low < ϵ_high) is theoretically shown to implicitly favor entropy increases, thus contributing to entropy stabilization. Similarly, GSPO's trajectory-level and asymmetric clipping (ϵ_GSPO_low < ϵ_GSPO_high) also induce bounds that can stochastically increase entropy, with the bounds dependent on trajectory length.
- **RLOO**: The paper notes that even strictly on-policy algorithms like RLOO are subject to the derived entropy dynamics, implying that if the base policy is highly calibrated, entropy can still decrease.

**Empirical Findings: Implementation Details (Section 4)**
The authors conducted empirical investigations into practical factors affecting entropy dynamics.
- **16-bit Quantization Impact**: They identified that common LLM training stacks (e.g., HuggingFace Accelerate with FSDP2) casting model outputs to `bf16` before probability ratio computation (r = π_new / π_old) introduces a multiplicative upward bias in the observed ratio (`r_observed > r_true`). Theorem 3 proves this bias. This effect translates to an effective asymmetric clipping that systematically favors entropy decrease (ϵ_low > ϵ_high), counteracting algorithms designed for entropy preservation. The proposed solution is to perform ratio calculations in full precision.
- **Float16 vs. Bfloat16 Training**: The research compares `BF16` (common in LLM training for dynamic range) with `FP16` (reported for more accurate gradients). They found `FP16` significantly reduces discrepancies between inference (vLLM) and training policies. The combination of `FP16` training and the log probability rounding fix qualitatively altered DAPO's entropy dynamics, shifting it from collapse to increase (Figure 3).

**Explicit Entropy Control Methods (Section 5)**
The paper proposes two explicit mechanisms to manage entropy, addressing the limitations of a fixed entropy bonus (e.g., memory cost for full logits).
- **REPO (Regulated Entropy Policy Optimization)**: This family of algorithms modifies the advantage function.
 - `A_REPO(s, a) = A(s, a) - β_s · L(s, a)`.
 - **REPO-D (Decorrelate)**: Aims to neutralize the entropy collapse by setting `β_s` to counteract the natural entropy decrease. It is formally equivalent to adding an entropy bonus but is estimated efficiently via REINFORCE using only the sampled token's log-probability, incurring no additional memory cost and potentially reducing gradient variance.
 - **REPO-R (Rescale)**: A practical approximation where advantages are rescaled based on action probabilities: `β_s,a = ζ · |A(s, a)|`. For positive advantages, `A_REPO-R` boosts rare correct actions, while for negative advantages, it reduces penalties on rare incorrect actions (for `ζ > 0`).
 - **Adaptive Controller**: Both REPO variants employ an adaptive bidirectional controller for `ζ` (or `β_s`) that dynamically adjusts its value based on whether the current policy entropy (`H_π_θ`) is above or below an initial target (`H_init_π_θ`). The controller includes sign flipping and magnitude clipping.
- **ADAPO (Adaptive Asymmetric Clipping)**: This method dynamically adjusts the `ϵ_high` threshold of DAPO's asymmetric clipping (keeping `ϵ_low` fixed). It increases `ϵ_high` if entropy is below target and decreases it if entropy is above target, providing bidirectional control.

**Experiments (Section 6)**
The proposed methods were evaluated against state-of-the-art policy gradient algorithms using Qwen-3-8B and Qwen-3-32B models.
- **Environments**:
 - **AppWorld**: An interactive tool-use agent benchmark (Trivedi et al., 2024), using sparse, outcome-based rewards. Training on 90 problems, evaluation on Test Normal (168 tasks) and Test Challenge (417 tasks).
 - **AIME (American Invitational Mathematics Examination)**: Competition-level mathematics problems from NuminaMath-1.5 (Li et al., 2024), with evaluation on AIME 2024 and 2025 (30 problems each). Terminal reward for correct answers, with a limited 4096-token context to increase challenge.
- **Algorithms Tested**: RLOO, GRPO, LOOP, DAPO, GSPO, REPO-R (applied to GRPO), and ADAPO (applied to DAPO).
- **Metrics**: Task Goal Completion (TGC) / Pass@1 (accuracy), and per-token entropy.
- **Sequential Learning**: An experiment was conducted where models pre-trained on one task (AIME or AppWorld) were fine-tuned on the other to assess the retention of trainability.
- **Infrastructure**: Experiments utilized 3 NVIDIA H100 8-GPU nodes for rollout generation (vLLM), learning (FSDP2 with custom RL implementation), and evaluation. LoRA was used for fine-tuning, with AdamW optimizer and gradient clipping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding entropy dynamics, algorithmic performance, and the impact of numerical precision.

- **Entropy Dynamics of Policy Gradient Algorithms**:
 - PPO-like algorithms, specifically GRPO and LOOP, exhibited a rapid reduction in entropy during training, losing up to 90% of initial entropy.
 - Strictly on-policy RLOO maintained considerably more entropy compared to GRPO/LOOP.
 - DAPO and GSPO, incorporating clipping modifications, retained more entropy than GRPO/LOOP. However, DAPO’s entropy could increase uncontrollably in some experiments without specific control mechanisms (Figure 5).

- **Performance of Entropy-Preserving Methods**:
 - REPO-R and ADAPO demonstrated higher performance compared to their off-policy baselines (GRPO and DAPO, respectively). These methods also maintained more stable policy entropy throughout training (Figures 4, 5, 6).
 - There was a consistent positive correlation observed between entropy preservation (maintaining higher cumulative entropy during training) and higher final test accuracy. This correlation was more pronounced in the AppWorld environment, which typically requires more exploration, compared to the AIME benchmark, where base models might already be highly optimized.

- **Impact on Sequential Learning**:
 - Policies trained with GRPO, which experienced significant entropy collapse, demonstrated poor performance when subsequently fine-tuned on a new, different task. This suggests a loss of exploration capability.
 - Conversely, policies trained with DAPO and REPO, which preserved entropy, retained their exploration ability and performed more effectively during sequential fine-tuning (Figure 7).

- **Influence of Numerical Precision**:
 - The empirical investigation revealed that subtle implementation details significantly impact entropy dynamics. Specifically, `bf16` quantization of model outputs (e.g., through FSDP2) introduced a multiplicative upward bias in probability ratios, which effectively led to an asymmetric clipping regime favoring entropy decrease. This finding implies that some previously reported entropy collapse phenomena might have been artifacts of numerical precision rather than fundamental algorithmic properties.
 - The combination of `FP16` training and a fix for `bf16` log-probability rounding qualitatively altered entropy dynamics. For instance, DAPO, which previously showed entropy collapse in one setting, exhibited rapid entropy increase after these fixes (Figure 3, 8).
 - With these numerical improvements, the purely on-policy RLOO algorithm achieved 79% Test Normal and 71% Test Challenge on AppWorld with the Qwen-3-32B model, establishing a new reported state-of-the-art score for this benchmark at the time of submission.
 - A numerical issue related to `softmax` gradient computation for high-probability tokens (where `1-p` rounds to zero in lower precision) was identified, though its practical impact was less significant compared to the other numerical fixes.

- **Observations on Qwen 2.5 vs. Qwen 3 (Appendix E)**:
 - Experiments with Qwen 2.5 32B models showed higher initial success rates on AppWorld compared to Qwen 3 models, but lower overall best results on the challenging test split.
 - Unlike with Qwen 3, LOOP/GRPO did not exhibit rapid entropy collapse when trained with Qwen 2.5, suggesting that base model characteristics can influence entropy dynamics irrespective of the RL algorithm used.
 - The numerical fixes enabled the replication and surpassing of previous LOOP results for Qwen 2.5 32B.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to a deeper understanding of policy gradient reinforcement learning for language models, particularly concerning the critical role of policy entropy.

- **Reframing Entropy in RL Training**: The paper elevates entropy management from a secondary consideration to a "first-class concern" in RL training pipelines. By demonstrating that the trajectory of entropy throughout optimization is more informative than the final entropy value, it shifts the focus towards active, continuous entropy control for effective policy optimization.
- **Enhanced Algorithmic Understanding**: The formal analysis provides a theoretical foundation for understanding why certain policy gradient objectives lead to entropy collapse or preservation. This enhanced understanding can guide the design of more robust and predictable RL algorithms.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness**: The proposed explicit entropy control mechanisms, REPO and ADAPO, lead to improved performance on complex reasoning tasks (AppWorld, AIME) and, importantly, maintain the model's trainability for sequential learning. This implies that models can adapt more effectively to new tasks without losing their exploratory capabilities.
- **Critical Role of Numerical Precision**: The identification and demonstration of how subtle numerical precision issues (e.g., `bf16` output casting, `FP16` vs. `BF16` training) can significantly distort entropy dynamics and lead to training instabilities is a notable contribution. This highlights the importance of meticulous implementation details for robust RL training, especially with large-scale LLMs. The achievement of state-of-the-art results on AppWorld with a numerically stabilized RLOO underscores this impact.
- **Bridging On-Policy and Off-Policy Trade-offs**: While strictly on-policy RLOO demonstrates strong performance with numerical stabilization, it often faces scalability bottlenecks in distributed systems due to synchronous updates. The proposed entropy-preserving mechanisms (REPO, ADAPO) are compatible with weakly on-policy methods, enabling them to approach the performance of strictly on-policy training while retaining the throughput benefits of asynchronous execution.
- **Broader Applicability**: The insights into entropy dynamics and the influence of numerical precision are likely applicable beyond LLMs to other domains employing policy gradient RL, fostering the development of more stable and generalizable reinforcement learning systems.
- **Responsible AI Development**: By fostering diverse and creative solutions through entropy preservation, this work contributes to the development of more robust and beneficial language models, aligning with principles of responsible AI development by mitigating the risk of models converging to narrow, potentially biased, or less comprehensive solution spaces.
