# Learning Action Priors for Cross-embodiment Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage training framework for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enhances robot manipulation policies by pre-training a dedicated action module on unconditioned motion data. This approach significantly boosts average task success rates across diverse robot embodiments by up to 12.7% and improves real-world task performance by over 26% compared to a baseline without motion priors.

## Problem

- Current VLA models often lack explicit motion priors for their action generation modules, leading to simultaneous learning of complex motion dynamics and cross-modal alignment from scratch.
- This results in training instability, slow convergence, and amplified challenges for cross-embodiment generalization due to diverse action spaces and motion distributions.
- Policies exhibit underperformance and unstable behaviors on data-scarce and long-tail manipulation tasks, particularly in real-world settings.

## Method

- A two-stage framework introduces an initial, decoupled "==Action Prior Learning==" stage, training a Transformer-based action encoder-decoder with a flow-matching objective using only unconditioned robot motion trajectories.
- In the "Action-Prior-Guided VLA Training" stage, the pre-trained action decoder is reused as the VLA action head, and a latent alignment distillation loss anchors the VLM's action latent predictions to the learned prior.
- The pre-trained action encoder compresses historical state-action trajectories into a single, motion-aware context token, efficiently expanding the VLM's temporal receptive field without adding significant computational overhead.

## Results

- Average success rate across 13 cross-embodiment tasks improved from **55.3%** (No Action Prior baseline) to **68.0%** (full model), outperforming state-of-the-art GR00T (**48.6%**) and π0.5 (**53.8%**).
- Performance on data-scarce real-world Franka tasks increased from **35.0%** to **66.3%** (average), with "Stack Cups" improving from **25%** to **80%** success.
- Action prior learning reduced initial prediction loss by approximately 8-fold and initial gradient norm by nearly 16-fold in Stage 2 VLA training, leading to significantly faster convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of fundamental robot movement capabilities from cross-modal alignment through explicit action prior pre-training dramatically improves VLA model stability and efficiency.
> - Incorporating motion-aware temporal context via a compact, encoder-generated history token significantly reduces policy hesitation and enhances decisive action-making in long-horizon tasks.
> - The scalability of action prior learning, where increasing action-only data further strengthens the prior, suggests a path for leveraging large, easily collectible datasets to improve generalist robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models typically integrate large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as backbones, leveraging their extensive visual and linguistic semantic knowledge to understand instructions and perceive environments for robotic control. This approach has led to notable successes across diverse robotic platforms, moving towards the development of generalist robotic policies.

However, a recurring challenge in current VLA paradigms is the imbalance in the types of prior knowledge integrated. While VLMs provide rich visual and linguistic priors from their pre-training on vast datasets, the action generation module—responsible for translating high-level commands into low-level physical motions—often lacks a comparable pre-training phase. Typically, action modules are initialized with random weights or transferred parameters from unrelated modalities and are then optimized jointly with the VLM backbone via imitation learning. This means the action module must learn complex physical motion dynamics and temporal structures from scratch, simultaneously with learning the cross-modal alignment between perception, language, and control.

This limitation is exacerbated in cross-embodiment settings, where a single policy must operate across heterogeneous robots with distinct action spaces, kinematic structures, and motion distributions. Learning to manage these diverse action distributions concurrently with cross-modal alignment poses a significant bottleneck for training stability and convergence speed. Existing approaches, such as those leveraging diffusion policies (e.g., CogACT, GR00T) or flow-matching action heads (e.g., π0.5), primarily focus on improving the expressiveness of action generation or adapting to heterogeneous observation/action spaces at the VLA training stage. While some work has explored latent action representations or action-like priors, these often involve visual supervision, future-state prediction, or vision-action policy learning rather than directly modeling the intrinsic statistics of robot motion in a decoupled, action-only manner.

This paper positions itself to address this specific imbalance by proposing a method to explicitly pretrain the action module with motion priors *before* integrating it into the full VLA training pipeline. By decoupling the learning of fundamental movement capabilities from the learning of visual and linguistic conditioning, the authors aim to provide a more stable, efficient, and robust foundation for cross-embodiment VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified structural bottleneck in the standard VLA training pipeline: the absence of an explicit motion prior for the action generation module. Current VLA models, while benefiting from the extensive visual and linguistic priors of their VLM backbones, typically initialize their action modules from scratch. This creates a disparity where the action module must simultaneously discover the intrinsic temporal dynamics of physical motion and establish cross-modal alignment with visual and linguistic inputs. This simultaneous learning objective can lead to several issues:

- **Training Instability:** The action module, initially untrained, generates unstable and poorly structured gradients. These gradients can interfere with the VLM backbone, hindering its ability to consistently align high-level semantic understanding with low-level physical execution.
- **Slow Convergence:** The coupled nature of learning motion dynamics and cross-modal alignment prolongs the training process, as the policy struggles to resolve two complex learning tasks concurrently.
- **Amplified Challenges in Cross-Embodiment Settings:** In scenarios involving multiple, heterogeneous robot embodiments, action spaces and motion distributions vary significantly. The absence of a foundational motion prior makes it even more difficult for the action module to adapt to these diverse physical characteristics during early training.
- **Underperformance on Data-Scarce Tasks:** For tasks with limited demonstration data, particularly in real-world scenarios, the policy may fail to learn stable and effective motion patterns, leading to hesitant or unstable behaviors.

To address these challenges, the key objective of this work is to introduce an explicit action prior learning stage that targets the action module independently, using only action-centric data. This decoupled approach aims to:

1. **Equip the action module with fundamental motion knowledge:** By training the action module solely on unconditioned action trajectories, it can efficiently acquire knowledge about the intrinsic temporal structure and distribution of physical motion, much like an apprentice practicing basic movements without external sensory input.
2. **Facilitate smoother and faster VLA training:** With a pre-trained action module that already encodes plausible motion patterns, the subsequent VLA training can primarily focus on aligning visual and linguistic inputs with an already structured and stable action space, leading to faster convergence and more robust policies.
3. **Enhance cross-embodiment generalization:** By exposing the action module to diverse action distributions from various embodiments during its pre-training, it can build a more generalized understanding of physical motion before facing the complexities of cross-modal alignment across different robot types.
4. **Improve performance on data-scarce and long-tail tasks:** A stable action prior can prevent policies from generating unstable motions when downstream VLA supervision is limited, resulting in more decisive and effective behaviors, especially in real-world applications.
5. **Enable efficient history-aware decision-making:** By reusing the learned action encoder, the system aims to compactly summarize past state-action trajectories into a single, motion-aware context token. This allows the VLM to benefit from an enlarged temporal receptive field without incurring significant computational overhead or requiring it to learn temporal abstraction from raw data.
6. **Demonstrate scalability with action-only data:** The research also aims to investigate whether increasing the volume of unconditioned action data in the prior learning stage leads to a more generalizable action prior and, consequently, improved downstream VLA performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a two-stage training framework designed to instill an action prior into robot manipulation policies before comprehensive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) alignment.

**Stage 1: Learning Action Prior (Action-only Training)**

This initial stage focuses exclusively on training a lightweight action module to learn the temporal structure and distribution of physical motion from unconditioned action trajectories, without any visual observations or language instructions.

- **Action Module Architecture:** A Transformer-based encoder-decoder structure is employed for the action module, parameterized by $\phi = \{\phi_{enc}, \phi_{dec}\}$.
 - **Action Encoder ($\text{E}_{\phi_{enc}}$):** This component is designed to compress an entire action chunk, along with associated proprioceptive states and dataset-specific soft-prompt tokens ($\text{p}_k$), into a single, dense latent embedding $z$. The input sequence $\tau = [s_t, a_t, s_{t+1}, a_{t+1}, \ldots]$ interleaves states and actions to capture both local motion increments and their kinematic context. A learnable summary token `cls` is prepended, and its $\ell_2$-normalized output feature forms the latent action embedding $z$. This design aims to capture global temporal dynamics and macroscopic motion structures.
 - **Flow-Matching-based Action Decoder ($\text{D}_{\phi_{dec}}$):** The decoder is conditioned on the latent embedding $z$ and reconstructs the original action chunk. It utilizes a flow-matching objective, which models a continuous probability path between standard Gaussian noise and the ground-truth actions. The decoder is optimized as a velocity prediction network, minimizing the $\text{L}_{recon}$ loss, which is the squared difference between the predicted velocity field and the target velocity field. This enables flexible modeling of continuous and potentially multi-modal action distributions.
- **Input Data:** The module is trained on interleaved proprioceptive state and action sequences from various robot embodiments (LIBERO, RoboCasa GR1, real-world Franka). Learnable dataset embeddings $\text{p}_k$ are prepended to help disambiguate embodiment-specific action semantics.
- **Objective:** Minimize $\text{L}_{recon}$ to enable the decoder to produce plausible robot motion from the latent embedding $z$.

**Stage 2: Action-Prior-Guided VLA Training**

In the second stage, the action prior learned in Stage 1 is transferred and integrated into a full VLA model.

- **VLA Architecture:** A foundation Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3-VL-2B, serves as the backbone $f_{\theta}$.
- **Inputs:** At each timestep $t$, the VLM receives soft-prompt dataset embeddings $\text{p}_k$, current visual observations $o_t$, a language instruction $l_t$, and a learnable query token $q$.
- **Action Latent Prediction:** The VLM processes these inputs to predict an action latent embedding $z'$, which is the $\ell_2$-normalized output feature corresponding to $q$.
- **Decoder Reuse:** The action decoder $\text{D}_{\phi_{dec}}$ from Stage 1, which has already learned to model plausible robot motion, is directly reused and initialized as the VLA action head. It generates actions conditioned on the VLM's predicted embedding $z'$. The primary prediction loss $\text{L}_{pred}$ is also a flow-matching objective, similar to $\text{L}_{recon}$ but conditioned on $z'$.
- **Latent Alignment Distillation:** To leverage the structured latent space learned in Stage 1, an alignment loss $\text{L}_{align}$ is introduced. The Stage 1 action encoder $\text{E}_{\phi_{enc}}$ is used to extract a ground-truth latent embedding $z$ from the observed trajectory (with stop-gradient applied to prevent the encoder from being pulled by the VLM's current prediction). $\text{L}_{align}$ minimizes the squared difference between the VLM's predicted $z'$ and $sg(z)$, anchoring $z'$ to the learned motion latent space. An auxiliary $\text{L}_{recon}$ is also applied to the encoder-decoder branch to preserve the action latent geometry during joint optimization.
- **Early-step Distillation Strategy:** A monotonically decaying weight $\lambda(k)$ is applied to $\text{L}_{align}$ and the auxiliary $\text{L}_{recon}$. $\lambda(k) = \text{max}(0, 1 - k/N_{decay})$, where $k$ is the training step and $N_{decay}$ is a predefined decay threshold (e.g., 5,000 steps). This strategy ensures that the distillation losses provide strong guidance during early training but gradually phase out, allowing the VLM to refine its representations and the policy end-to-end without being permanently constrained.
- **Total VLA Objective:** The combined loss is $\text{L} = \text{L}_{pred} + \lambda(k)(\text{L}_{align} + \text{L}_{recon})$.
- **Temporal Context Compression with Action Encoder:** The pre-trained action encoder $\text{E}_{\phi_{enc}}$ is reused to compress historical state-action trajectories ($\tau_{hist}$) into a single latent token $z_{hist}$. This compact $z_{hist}$ is then inserted into the VLM input alongside other tokens. This approach injects motion-aware temporal context efficiently, expanding the policy's receptive field without adding significant computational overhead.
- **History-conditioned Training:** During training, the total objective is averaged between evaluations with and without the historical embedding: $\text{L}_{total} = (\text{L}(z_{hist}) + \text{L}(\emptyset))/2$.

**Experimental Setup:**

- **Cross-Embodiment Data:** Experiments are conducted on a demanding setup spanning 13 tasks across two simulated benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa) and a real-world Franka platform. These environments feature diverse embodiment types, action/state representations, and task distributions.
- **Unified Action-State Space:** A unified 37-dimensional action space and 74-dimensional state space are defined, with embodiment-specific vectors mapped to corresponding slots and unused dimensions zero-padded. This enables joint training across heterogeneous platforms.
- **Baselines and Variants:** The method is compared against standard VLA architectures like GR00T and π0.5, as well as internal variants: "No Action Prior" (action module initialized randomly, trained jointly), "Action-State Prior" (Stage 1 with action and state, Stage 2 with distillation), and "Action-State Prior + History" (full model).
- **Training Configuration:** Specific hyperparameters for both stages, including backbone models, chunk sizes, batch sizes, learning rates, and training steps, are detailed (Table II). Stage 1 is considerably more lightweight (2 hours) than Stage 2 (20 hours).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that incorporating action prior learning yields consistent benefits across various performance metrics and training dynamics.

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** The proposed "Action-State Prior" method significantly improved the average success rate across all 13 cross-embodiment tasks, from 55.3% (No Action Prior baseline) to 64.9%. The full model, "Action-State Prior + History," further increased this to 68.0%. This performance exceeded other state-of-the-art methods, GR00T (48.6%) and π0.5 (53.8%).

- **Enhanced Long-Tail and Real-World Performance:** A substantial advantage was observed on data-scarce real-world Franka tasks, which constituted a small fraction (7.6%) of the total training data. The average success rate on these real-world tasks increased from 35.0% (No Action Prior) to 61.3% (Action-State Prior), and further to 66.3% (Action-State Prior + History). Specific examples include "Grasp Coke" improving from 5% to 35% (then 50% with history) and "Stack Cups" improving from 25% to 75% (then 80% with history). Competing methods, particularly π0.5, showed strong simulation performance but struggled significantly on these real-world long-tail tasks, often failing completely.

- **Qualitative Behavior Analysis:** Visual inspection of rollouts revealed that the "No Action Prior" policy exhibited jerky and unstable motions, often freezing or hesitating at critical points. In contrast, policies trained with the action prior produced fast, precise, and stable motions, completing tasks more decisively. The history compression mechanism was particularly effective in reducing hesitation, such as policies failing to commit to a grasp action after achieving alignment. The history token allowed the VLM to infer readiness, leading to timely and decisive actions.

- **Improved Training Dynamics and Optimization Stability:**
 - **Reduced Initial Loss:** Action prior learning led to an approximately 8-fold reduction in initial prediction loss during Stage 2 VLA training (from ~7.1 to ~0.9).
 - **Smoother Gradients:** The initial gradient norm was reduced by nearly 16-fold (from ~1,400 to ~90), indicating more stable and structured gradients backpropagated to the VLM backbone.
 - **Faster Convergence:** Prior-based variants reached target validation Mean Absolute Error (MAE) significantly faster (~700 steps vs. ~2,100 steps for the no-prior baseline), demonstrating that the action prior acts as an effective optimization prior.
 - **Training Efficiency:** Stage 1 action prior learning required only about 2 hours on 8 H200 GPUs, representing approximately 10% of the 20-hour Stage 2 VLA training time. This upfront cost was quickly amortized during Stage 2, with prior-based models outperforming the no-prior baseline earlier in training.

- **Ablation Study Results:**
 - **Proprioceptive State Integration:** Including proprioceptive state in the action encoder's input in Stage 1 improved overall simulation performance (65.4% to 68.3%). This was particularly beneficial for complex embodiments with high-dimensional state spaces (e.g., RoboCasa GR1 tasks), as it provided kinematic context for action deltas.
 - **Encoder-based History Compression:** The learned action encoder proved more effective for history integration (71.5% average success) compared to a naive MLP projection of raw history (68.4%) or no history (68.3%). This indicated that the encoder's ability to compress raw temporal traces into structured motion embeddings was crucial for VLM interpretation.
 - **Distillation Schedule (N_decay):** An early-step distillation strategy, with the alignment losses linearly decaying to zero over the first 5,000 VLA training steps, was found to be optimal. This allowed for strong initial guidance to anchor the VLM to the structured latent space, followed by an unconstrained phase for end-to-end refinement, preventing the prior from overly restricting the VLM's learning.

- **Scaling Action Prior Data:** Increasing the amount of action-only data used in Stage 1 (by 4x) led to a stronger and more generalizable action prior. This resulted in consistently better performance throughout Stage 2 VLA training, allowing the "More Action-State Prior" variant to achieve higher success rates and faster convergence compared to the standard "Action-State Prior" (e.g., reaching 68.8% average success).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the development of robust and efficient generalist robot manipulation policies, particularly in cross-embodiment settings. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addresses a Fundamental VLA Bottleneck:** The work directly addresses the structural imbalance in current VLA models, where the action module lacks a pre-existing motion prior comparable to the visual and linguistic priors from foundation VLMs. By introducing a dedicated action prior learning stage, it provides a more principled approach to equipping robots with fundamental movement capabilities.

- **Enhances Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The ability to learn a unified action prior from heterogeneous robot data significantly improves the generalization capabilities of VLA models across diverse platforms. This is crucial for developing truly generalist robots that can adapt to different physical embodiments without extensive re-training or environment-specific fine-tuning.

- **Improves Training Efficiency and Stability:** Decoupling the learning of intrinsic motion dynamics from cross-modal alignment leads to faster convergence and more stable optimization during the computationally intensive VLA training phase. The lightweight nature of Stage 1 training means this upfront cost is minimal compared to the overall benefits, making policy development more efficient.

- **Boosts Performance on Data-Scarce Real-World Tasks:** The most pronounced impact is demonstrated on long-tail, real-world tasks with limited demonstration data. The learned action prior enables policies to exhibit more stable, decisive, and effective behaviors, mitigating the challenges of uneven data distributions and enhancing the feasibility of deploying VLA models in practical, low-data scenarios.

- **Enables Effective History-Aware Decision-Making:** The reuse of the action encoder as a compact history compressor offers an efficient and semantically rich method for incorporating temporal context. This is vital for long-horizon and sequential manipulation tasks, allowing the policy to infer intent and make more informed decisions based on past actions and states without overwhelming the VLM with raw, high-dimensional history.

- **Demonstrates Scalability of Action Prior Learning:** The finding that scaling action-only data in Stage 1 directly improves downstream VLA performance opens new avenues for research. It suggests that large datasets of unconditioned robot motion, potentially collected at lower cost, could be leveraged to train more robust and generalized action priors, further enhancing the capabilities of future VLA systems.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical and effective framework for improving the stability, efficiency, and generalization of VLA models in complex, multi-robot environments. By ensuring that the action module "knows how to move" before it "learns to see and act," the approach lays a more solid foundation for the development of advanced robotic manipulation capabilities.
