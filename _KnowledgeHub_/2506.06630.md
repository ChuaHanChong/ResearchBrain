# Active Test-time Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Vision-Language Navigation, ATENA, integrates active learning with test-time adaptation, using a novel Mixture Entropy Optimization guided by sparse episodic human feedback and Self-Active Learning for autonomous supervision. This approach significantly improves navigation success rates and efficiency on challenging benchmarks like REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE, outperforming existing test-time adaptation methods by up to 44.98% in success rate for some policies while maintaining low latency.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) policies experience performance degradation when deployed in unfamiliar test-time environments due to distributional shifts.
- Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods, particularly those based on entropy minimization, can lead to accumulated errors and overconfidence in incorrect actions in sequential tasks.
- Applying traditional Active Learning (AL) to online VLN is challenging due to the practical need for low-latency, low-effort human input (sparse episodic feedback rather than dense, step-by-step guidance).

## Method

- ==Introduced ATENA== (Active TEst-time Navigation Agent), an active learning framework for online VLN that integrates sparse human input to guide entropy-based optimization.
- Developed Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) to leverage episodic success/failure feedback by minimizing entropy for successful navigation episodes and maximizing it for failed ones, using a mixture action distribution and a pseudo-expert distribution.
- Proposed Self-Active Learning (SAL), which allows the agent to autonomously determine navigation outcomes for relatively certain predictions via a self-prediction head, reducing reliance on human intervention.

## Results

- ATENA consistently improved navigation performance on REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE benchmarks, with significant gains across metrics like Success Rate (SR) and Shortest Path Length (SPL), e.g., up to **44.98%** SR increase for DUET on REVERIE validation unseen.
- Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) within ATENA achieved superior navigation performance with significantly fewer human queries compared to baselines (e.g., **55.52%** active queries for DUET+MEO vs. **90.34%** for DUET+TENT with AL).
- The framework maintained low additional latency during navigation rollout (**163.73** ms vs. **141.55** ms for baseline DUET) and its self-prediction head demonstrated robust reliability with an **82.19%** overall prediction accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-conditioned entropy optimization, using a mixture of action and pseudo-expert distributions (MEO), effectively amplifies corrective signals and enhances confidence calibration, leading to more robust adaptation than uniform entropy minimization.
> - Sparse, episodic human feedback can be effectively leveraged to guide online test-time adaptation, addressing the practical constraints of detailed human supervision in real-world VLN scenarios.
> - An uncertainty-guided query strategy combined with an autonomous self-prediction head (SAL) enables continuous self-supervision, reducing the overall dependence on human intervention and improving the scalability of active learning in VLN.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a foundational task in embodied AI, requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate through visual environments. Despite advancements in VLN, a significant challenge remains: performance degradation when policies trained on offline datasets are deployed in unfamiliar test-time environments. This issue arises from distributional shifts between training and deployment contexts, which are difficult to address comprehensively through offline training alone due to the impracticality of collecting diverse expert demonstrations across all possible environments.

To mitigate this, Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) has emerged as a strategy. TTA aims to refine a pre-trained model directly during inference using unsupervised signals, such as prediction entropy, consistency, or pseudo-labels. Entropy minimization is a widely adopted TTA technique, operating on the premise that increased model certainty correlates with improved accuracy. However, in sequential tasks like VLN, a uniform application of entropy minimization can lead to accumulated errors and overconfidence in incorrect actions, as the policy may overfit to failure patterns without sufficient contextual grounding. This can cause errors to cascade throughout a navigation episode, reducing resilience.

Active Learning (AL) offers a complementary approach by enabling models to query an oracle, typically a human, for labels on uncertain or informative data points. While AL can provide crucial contextual cues, its application in online test-time navigation faces practical constraints, including latency requirements and the need for intuitive, low-effort human input. Detailed, stepwise expert demonstrations are often not feasible in such real-world online settings.

This work positions itself at the intersection of VLN, TTA, and AL. It seeks to overcome the limitations of conventional entropy-minimization-based TTA by integrating active learning within a test-time adaptation framework. By leveraging sparse, episodic human feedback and developing mechanisms for self-supervision, the proposed method aims to enhance the robustness and adaptability of VLN agents in dynamic, real-world environments, without introducing prohibitive latency or human burden. It particularly focuses on how the core principle of human-robot interaction in VLN can be effectively leveraged for online test-time adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and adaptive framework for Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) that can overcome performance degradation caused by distributional shifts when policies are deployed in unfamiliar test-time environments. This overall objective is driven by several key motivations and leads to specific, actionable goals:

**Motivation 1: Addressing Limitations of Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) Methods**
Current TTA strategies, particularly those based on entropy minimization, often exhibit shortcomings in sequential tasks like VLN. Blindly minimizing entropy can lead to overconfidence in incorrect predictions, causing errors to accumulate and negatively impacting overall navigation performance. The researchers are motivated to provide the necessary contextual cues to enable a VLN agent to properly leverage entropy as a meaningful signal at test time, distinguishing between confident correct actions and confident incorrect actions.

**Motivation 2: Enabling Practical Human-Robot Interaction for Online Adaptation**
The authors identify that while human feedback can be invaluable for active learning, practical constraints in online test-time navigation settings (such as latency during navigation rollout and the need for accessible, low-effort human input) make detailed, stepwise supervision impractical. This motivates the design of an active learning strategy where human feedback is provided as sparse, episodic, binary evaluations (success or failure) rather than dense, step-by-step guidance. The goal is to make human involvement feasible and effective in real-world deployment scenarios.

**Motivation 3: Fostering Continuous Adaptation and Reducing Reliance on Human Intervention**
Traditional active learning methods typically query human feedback only when predictions are uncertain. This approach can overlook subtle errors made with high confidence or lead to periods of inactivity where the agent does not receive guidance. The researchers are motivated to enable the agent to remain actively engaged throughout all iterations for continuous feedback, thereby reducing the burden of human labeling and improving the agent's autonomy and robustness. This requires a mechanism for the agent to assess its own navigation outcomes when human input is not specifically requested.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives are:

- **Introduce ATENA (Active TEst-time Navigation Agent):** To establish the first active learning framework specifically designed for online VLN that integrates human input to guide entropy-based optimization.
- **Develop Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO):** To create a novel technique that effectively leverages sparse episodic feedback by minimizing entropy for successful navigation episodes and maximizing it for failed ones. This involves deriving entropy from a mixture of the action distribution and a pseudo-expert distribution (a one-hot distribution centered on the agent's chosen action) to amplify corrective signals and enhance confidence calibration.
- **Propose Self-Active Learning (SAL):** To implement a strategy where the agent can autonomously determine navigation outcomes for relatively certain predictions. This involves a self-prediction head, initialized at test time and trained online, which allows the agent to self-supervise and receive continuous guidance, thereby reducing reliance on human intervention.
- **Validate Effectiveness on Challenging Benchmarks:** To empirically demonstrate that ATENA significantly outperforms existing test-time adaptation baselines and effectively addresses distributional shifts across challenging VLN benchmarks (REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, ATENA (Active TEst-time Navigation Agent), is a test-time active learning system for online Vision-Language Navigation. It integrates human guidance into an entropy-based optimization process through two core components: Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) and Self-Active Learning (SAL).

#### 3.1. Task Description

The work considers an online VLN scenario where a navigation policy, pre-trained on offline data, encounters a continuous stream of test instructions and environments. At each step `t`, the agent observes `o_t`, selects an action `a_t` based on its policy `π_θ`, and transitions to the next state, forming a trajectory `τ`.

#### 3.2. Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO)

MEO addresses the issue of indiscriminate entropy minimization, which can reinforce incorrect actions. Its approach is to refine the agent's policy using outcome-conditioned entropy signals.

- **Outcome-Conditioned Entropy:** Instead of uniformly minimizing entropy, MEO minimizes entropy for successful navigation episodes to reinforce selected actions and maximizes it for failed episodes to penalize incorrect decisions.
- **Mixture Action Distribution:** A central element of MEO is the "Mixture Action Distribution," `q_mix`. This distribution is a convex combination of the agent's predicted action distribution `π_θ(a | o_t, I)` and a "pseudo-expert distribution" `q_pseudo(a | a_sel_t)`.
 - The `q_pseudo` distribution is a one-hot probability distribution that assigns full probability (1.0) to the agent's currently selected action `a_sel_t`, assuming it is optimal. This is `a_sel_t = arg max_a π_θ(a | o_t, I)`.
 - The mixture is defined as: `q_mix(a | o_t, I) = λq_pseudo(a | a_sel_t) + (1 − λ)π_θ(a | o_t, I)`, where `λ` is a combination weight (0 ≤ `λ` ≤ 1).
 - This formulation sharpens the distribution around the selected action.
- **Mixture Entropy Loss:** The entropy of `q_mix` is calculated and averaged over an episode to obtain `H'(q_mix)`. The loss function `L_mix` is then formulated as: `L_mix = I_success ⋅ H'(q_mix) − (1 − I_success) ⋅ H'(q_mix)`, where `I_success` is a binary indicator (1 for success, 0 for failure). This loss drives the policy to increase confidence in successful actions and decrease confidence in failed actions more aggressively than with `π_θ` alone, amplifying the feedback signal.

#### 3.3. Self-Active Learning (SAL)

SAL addresses the practical constraints of human feedback (cost, latency, sparsity) and the potential for high-confidence errors. It allows the agent to autonomously request or generate feedback.

- **Uncertainty-Guided Query Strategy:**
 - During each navigation step, the entropy of the action distribution `H(π_θ(· | o_t, I))` is stored in an entropy memory.
 - At the end of an episode, the average entropy `¯H` over all steps is computed.
 - If `¯H` exceeds a predefined uncertainty threshold `δ`, the agent queries a human oracle for episodic success/failure feedback (`O_Human(τ)`).
 - Otherwise, for relatively certain episodes, the agent generates its own feedback using a "self-oracle" (`O_Agent(τ)`).
- **Self-Prediction Head (`f_ϕ`):**
 - A neural network head `f_ϕ` is incorporated into the pre-trained policy `π_θ`.
 - It is trained online to predict the episodic outcome (success or failure) from the agent's internal states.
 - The `D`-dimensional hidden state vector `s_t` is averaged over the episode (`s_avg`) and fed into `f_ϕ`.
 - The outcome `I_success` is predicted as 1 if `σ(f_ϕ(s_avg)) > 0.5`, otherwise 0.
- **Training Self-Prediction Head:** The self-prediction head is trained using a binary cross-entropy loss `L_self = -[I_success log(σ(f_ϕ(s_avg))) + (1 − I_success) log(1 − σ(f_ϕ(s_avg)))]`. This loss is applied during test-time adaptation regardless of whether the label source was human or self-generated, enabling continuous self-improvement.
- **Total Adaptation Objective:** The overall adaptation objective combines the mixture entropy loss and the self-prediction loss: `L = L_mix + γL_self`, where `γ` is a balancing weight. This joint objective aims to reinforce correct decisions, penalize errors, and improve the agent’s ability to assess its own performance.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

Experiments are conducted using a batch size of 1 to simulate online test-time scenarios. Hyperparameters like the uncertainty threshold `δ` and mixture weight `λ` are tuned. The method is designed to be lightweight, enabling deployment on lower-powered hardware. For fair comparison, baselines like TENT and FSTTA are re-implemented with episodic updates for TENT and adjusted learning rates for FSTTA due to reported reproducibility issues in their original codebases.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive evaluations of ATENA on three challenging VLN benchmarks: REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE, integrating it with various pre-trained VLN policies.

#### 4.1. Overall Navigation Performance (Tables 1, 2, 3)

- **REVERIE Dataset:** ATENA consistently demonstrates substantial performance increases across various metrics (OSR, SR, SPL, RGSPL) when applied to HAMT, DUET, and GOAT policies. For instance, on the validation unseen split, ATENA improved the SR metric by 3.19% for HAMT, 44.98% for DUET, and 25.72% for GOAT. On the test unseen split, ATENA improved GOAT's performance by 4.59% (OSR), 7.47% (SR), 15.52% (SPL), and 18.13% (RGSPL). In contrast, baselines like TENT and FSTTA often provided minimal gains or, in some cases, hindered navigation performance.
- **R2R Dataset:** ATENA showed superior effectiveness compared to FSTTA. For GOAT on the validation unseen split, ATENA achieved a 2.91% improvement in SPL. For DUET, ATENA achieved the same success rate as FSTTA but with an 11.69% reduction in trajectory length, indicating higher navigation efficiency.
- **R2R-CE Dataset:** Similar trends were observed, with ATENA increasing the SPL for BEVBert by 6.7% on the validation seen split. The authors note that for R2R variants, which rely on dense, step-wise guidance, episodic feedback yields comparatively less significant performance enhancements than for REVERIE.

#### 4.2. Comparison of TTA Methods with Active Learning (Table 4)

- When active learning (AL) feedback was integrated into baseline TTA methods, TENT showed a substantial performance increase, indicating a positive synergy with human interaction.
- FSTTA, however, derived minimal benefit from AL, a result attributed to potential conflicts between its internal gradient modification mechanisms and human-provided guidance for entropy optimization.
- Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) within ATENA demonstrated strong synergy with AL, leading to superior navigation performance across SR, SPL, and RGSPL metrics. Notably, MEO achieved these improvements with significantly fewer human interventions (e.g., 55.52% active queries on Val Unseen for DUET+MEO compared to 90.34% for DUET+TENT with AL), suggesting that the model gains confidence more effectively.

#### 4.3. Effect of Self-Active Learning (SAL) (Table 5)

- Comparing ATENA with and without SAL, the results indicate that SAL contributes notable improvements to navigation performance.
- On the REVERIE validation unseen split, enabling SAL resulted in enhancements of 6.92% in SR, 7.84% in SPL, and 12.32% in RGSPL. This demonstrates that continuously engaging the agent through self-guided adaptation is effective.

#### 4.4. Combination Weight (λ) of Mixture Entropy Optimization (Figure 3)

- Varying the combination weight `λ` in MEO revealed that performance consistently improved as `λ` increased from 0.0 (vanilla entropy).
- The performance peaked at `λ = 0.4`, suggesting an optimal balance between the predicted action distribution and the pseudo-expert distribution.
- Even beyond the peak, the benefits of incorporating the mixture distribution remained evident compared to using vanilla entropy (`λ = 0.0`).

#### 4.5. Sampling Strategies for Feedback Episodes (Figure 4)

- ATENA's uncertainty-based active learning strategy outperformed baselines that used random or consecutive episode sampling. This highlights the effectiveness of selecting informative uncertain episodes for feedback.
- The uncertainty-based strategy also achieved higher SR and RGSPL, and competitive SPL, compared to a full-feedback setting, demonstrating that ATENA can achieve superior performance with reduced supervision.

#### 4.6. Computational Cost (Table 8)

- ATENA introduced minimal additional latency during navigation rollout (163.73 ms) compared to the DUET baseline (141.55 ms).
- This contrasts with FSTTA, which significantly increased navigation latency (from 141.55 ms to 1,155.86 ms).
- While ATENA's adaptation latency (246.96 ms) was slightly higher than TENT's (244.68 ms), this was considered justified by ATENA's substantial performance improvements (e.g., +20.56% SR, +20.45% OSR). The authors conclude that ATENA is a comparatively efficient and practical approach, prioritizing low navigation latency crucial for real-world robotics.

#### 4.7. Leaderboard Results and Self-Prediction Head Precision (Tables 6, 7)

- ATENA, when integrated with GOAT, achieved third place on the REVERIE challenge leaderboard for the test-unseen split, demonstrating competitive performance without requiring extensive pretraining. It outperformed higher-ranked models in SPL and RGSPL, indicating efficiency.
- The Self-Prediction head exhibited robust reliability, with an overall prediction accuracy of 82.19% on the REVERIE validation unseen split. It correctly predicted 90.26% of positive (success) outcomes and 71.46% of negative (failure) outcomes, confirming its suitability as a pseudo-label source for self-supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces ATENA, a framework that addresses critical challenges in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) by effectively integrating active learning with test-time adaptation. The work holds several points of significance and potential impact:

**Addressing Distributional Shifts:** ATENA offers a practical solution to the persistent problem of distributional shifts between offline training and online deployment environments in VLN. By enabling continuous adaptation at test time, the framework allows agents to maintain robust performance in unfamiliar and dynamic real-world settings, which is a fundamental requirement for reliable embodied AI systems.

**Novel Integration of Active and Self-Learning:** The framework's core novelty lies in its dual approach: leveraging episodic, binary human feedback through Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) and enabling autonomous self-supervision via Self-Active Learning (SAL). This hybrid strategy moves beyond traditional active learning's reliance on dense human labels and addresses the limitations of blind entropy minimization.

**Enhanced Confidence Calibration and Decision-Making:** MEO specifically improves confidence calibration by amplifying signals based on episodic success or failure. This explicit reinforcement of correct actions and suppression of incorrect ones leads to more well-grounded and adaptive decision-making throughout navigation. The ability to distinguish between accurate and inaccurate confident predictions is crucial for sequential tasks where errors can compound.

**Practicality for Real-World Deployment:** The design choices prioritize practical implementation. By requesting only sparse, episodic binary feedback, ATENA minimizes the human labeling burden and avoids introducing significant latency during navigation, which is vital for real-time human-robot interaction. The lightweight nature of the method and its compatibility with various pre-trained VLN policies also contribute to its practical applicability.

**Reduced Reliance on Human Intervention:** Self-Active Learning is a notable contribution towards greater agent autonomy. By allowing the agent to evaluate its own navigation outcomes for confident predictions, SAL reduces the overall dependence on continuous human intervention, making the system more scalable and robust in environments where human feedback may be costly, delayed, or unavailable.

**Foundational for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research at the intersection of human-robot interaction, active learning, and automated self-assessment in embodied AI. It opens avenues to explore how similar active and self-supervision mechanisms could be applied to other interactive embodied tasks beyond VLN, such as object manipulation or collaborative tasks, potentially leading to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

**Broader Societal Impact:** By improving the reliability and adaptability of VLN agents, ATENA contributes to the development of more capable and user-centric robots. This could facilitate more effective human-robot collaboration in various domains, from assistive robotics in homes to logistics and exploration in complex environments. However, the authors also acknowledge a limitation regarding the generalizability to less human-interactive tasks and the potential impact of ambiguous or inconsistent user feedback, emphasizing the need for continued research in accurately interpreting human intent.
