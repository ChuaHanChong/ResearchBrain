# Active Test-time Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for Vision-Language Navigation, ATENA, integrates active learning with test-time adaptation, using a novel Mixture Entropy Optimization guided by sparse episodic human feedback and Self-Active Learning for autonomous supervision. This approach significantly improves navigation success rates and efficiency on challenging benchmarks like REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE, outperforming existing test-time adaptation methods by up to 44.98% in success rate for some policies while maintaining low latency.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) policies experience performance degradation when deployed in unfamiliar test-time environments due to distributional shifts.
- Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods, particularly those based on entropy minimization, can lead to accumulated errors and overconfidence in incorrect actions in sequential tasks.
- Applying traditional Active Learning (AL) to online VLN is challenging due to the practical need for low-latency, low-effort human input (sparse episodic feedback rather than dense, step-by-step guidance).

## Method

- ==Introduced ATENA== (Active TEst-time Navigation Agent), an active learning framework for online VLN that integrates sparse human input to guide entropy-based optimization.
- Developed Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) to leverage episodic success/failure feedback by minimizing entropy for successful navigation episodes and maximizing it for failed ones, using a mixture action distribution and a pseudo-expert distribution.
- Proposed Self-Active Learning (SAL), which allows the agent to autonomously determine navigation outcomes for relatively certain predictions via a self-prediction head, reducing reliance on human intervention.

## Results

- ATENA consistently improved navigation performance on REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE benchmarks, with significant gains across metrics like Success Rate (SR) and Shortest Path Length (SPL), e.g., up to **44.98%** SR increase for DUET on REVERIE validation unseen.
- Mixture Entropy Optimization (MEO) within ATENA achieved superior navigation performance with significantly fewer human queries compared to baselines (e.g., **55.52%** active queries for DUET+MEO vs. **90.34%** for DUET+TENT with AL).
- The framework maintained low additional latency during navigation rollout (**163.73** ms vs. **141.55** ms for baseline DUET) and its self-prediction head demonstrated robust reliability with an **82.19%** overall prediction accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-conditioned entropy optimization, using a mixture of action and pseudo-expert distributions (MEO), effectively amplifies corrective signals and enhances confidence calibration, leading to more robust adaptation than uniform entropy minimization.
> - Sparse, episodic human feedback can be effectively leveraged to guide online test-time adaptation, addressing the practical constraints of detailed human supervision in real-world VLN scenarios.
> - An uncertainty-guided query strategy combined with an autonomous self-prediction head (SAL) enables continuous self-supervision, reducing the overall dependence on human intervention and improving the scalability of active learning in VLN.
