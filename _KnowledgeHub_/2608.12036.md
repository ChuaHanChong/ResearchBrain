# Mechanist: AI as a Scientific Instrument for Discovering the Mechanisms of Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> Mechanist is an agentic multi-agent system that uses AI as a scientific instrument to autonomously discover mechanisms of AI intelligence. Hypothesis, experiment, verification and iteration agents are grounded in a 13,000-paper interpretability knowledge graph, 43 million cross-discipline papers, and 32 mechanistic methods. It beats Claude Code and AI Scientist on reproduction reliability, uncovers cross-modal subliminal risk, separable belief heads, and steers Evo2 toward higher alpha-helical content.

## Problem

- Mechanistic exploration of AI models stays largely manual while capabilities advance fast, widening the gap between what models do and what humans can explain or control.
- Existing AI-scientist systems target training recipes or domain science tasks, yielding case-level solutions rather than theory-level mechanism discovery about the AI models themselves.
- Prior automated interpretability only generates and validates descriptions of individual neurons or features at inference time, never exploring general mechanism theories spanning pretraining and diverse behaviors.

## Method

- **Mechanist** four-stage ==multi-agent framework== with an orchestrator dispatching Hypothesis, Experiment, Verification and Iteration agents; each runs in isolated context and communicates through file-based artifacts with resumable completion markers.
- Dual knowledge grounding: an ==interpretability knowledge graph== of about 13,900 papers and blogs with extracted technique, component and target-model nodes, plus ==SciAtlas== covering 43 million papers across 26 disciplines.
- A library of 32 mechanistic methods for analysis, causal intervention and validation, including ==sparse autoencoders==, ==causal tracing==, ==logit lens== and ==Fisher-information ranking== of attention heads.

## Results

- Across 16 reproduced papers, human experts rated Mechanist highest on all four dimensions: **87.2%** data usage, **83.3%** design, **92.2%** execution, **86.5%** analysis, roughly **9-13%** above Claude Code.
- A student fine-tuned only on filtered-safe text from an unsafe teacher gave **48.6%** unsafe multimodal answers versus **20.3%** untuned; banana image generation rose to **25.6%** versus **2.5%**.
- Zeroing head L4.H1 in Pythia-1B cut attributed-belief accuracy from **0.86** to **0.34** with perplexity nearly unchanged; probe-gated amplification gained **+15.3%** versus **+1.6%** for prompt hints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Content-based screening of training data is not a safety guarantee: traits propagate through semantically opposing data and across modalities, evading output filters entirely.
> - Interpretable internal features work as control knobs, but only inside a strength band; beyond it apparent gains rise while generation validity collapses, so calibrate sweeps.
> - Reproduction fidelity depends on domain method knowledge, not general coding skill: generic agents silently substitute cheaper methods and skip intervention-strength calibration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) models has led to their deployment in various critical domains, ranging from scientific problem formulation to human decision-making. Despite their increasing capabilities, the internal mechanisms governing how these models acquire knowledge, form beliefs, reason, and act remain largely opaque. This lack of understanding extends to potential latent risks and unreliable behaviors, particularly in high-stakes applications such as healthcare or chemical manufacturing.

Existing AI research often focuses on either improving AI model performance for specific tasks (AI for AI, e.g., data preparation, model training) or applying AI to solve problems in other scientific disciplines (AI for Science, e.g., molecule design, drug discovery). While some frameworks address automated interpretability, they typically concentrate on describing individual neurons or features at inference time. This work positions itself to bridge a widening gap: the disparity between the speed of AI development and the human capacity to understand and control these systems. It proposes a shift from manual, case-specific mechanistic exploration to autonomous, general mechanism discovery across diverse behaviors and throughout both pretraining and inference stages. This approach aims to move beyond merely improving AI performance to explaining *how* models behave internally, thereby complementing capability-oriented AI systems with mechanistic insights.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Mechanist, an agentic system designed to function as a scientific instrument for the autonomous discovery of mechanisms underlying AI intelligence. The system aims to enable a systematic and scalable approach to understanding AI, contrasting with the predominantly manual and limited scope of current mechanistic interpretability efforts.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges:
- **Lack of Understanding of AI Mechanisms:** Despite their success, the fundamental principles governing how AI models learn, reason, and make decisions are not well understood. This opacity limits the ability to predict, diagnose, and control their behavior.
- **Identification of Latent Risks:** Unclear internal workings can conceal latent risks or unreliable behaviors that may not be apparent through standard benchmarking. Discovering these mechanisms can facilitate early identification of potential hazards, particularly in sensitive domains.
- **Gap between AI Capability and Understanding:** The pace of AI model development far exceeds the current rate of mechanistic understanding, creating a critical gap that necessitates automated tools for exploration.
- **Enabling Adaptive Control and Targeted Improvement:** A deeper understanding of internal mechanisms can allow for more precise control over AI behavior, leading to targeted improvements in performance and reliability, rather than relying solely on empirical trial and error.
- **Advancing Interdisciplinary Discovery:** By understanding the mechanisms of scientific foundation models, it may be possible to steer them more effectively for interdisciplinary applications, moving beyond computationally intensive "generate-and-rerank" paradigms.

Mechanist is motivated by the need to transform mechanistic understanding from a manually intensive process into an autonomous scientific endeavor, with the ultimate goal of developing more capable, reliable, and interpretable AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mechanist is structured as a multi-agent framework comprising a central orchestrator and four specialized agents: a hypothesis generation agent, an experiment agent, a verification agent, and an iteration agent. The system operates through four sequential stages:
1. **Hypothesis Generation:** The hypothesis agent formulates research hypotheses concerning the existence of behavioral phenomena, their underlying mechanisms, or practical applications. This process is grounded in a multi-source retrieval strategy.
 - **Knowledge Graphs:** Mechanist leverages two extensive knowledge graphs:
 - **Interpretability-focused Knowledge Graph:** Curated from approximately 13,000 papers and research blogs, this graph is organized along three axes: object of study (e.g., Transformer, MLP), application scenario (e.g., Safety, Reasoning), and mechanistic analysis method. This graph provides fine-grained concepts specific to mechanistic interpretability. Its construction involved data collection from OpenAlex and research blogs, attribute extraction using an LLM (DeepSeek-V3.2-Thinking), graph construction linking entities and attributes, and quality control via human and LLM evaluation.
 - **Multidisciplinary Knowledge Graph (SciAtlas):** This broader database contains over 43 million papers across 26 disciplines, including psychology, neuroscience, and computer science. It allows Mechanist to draw interdisciplinary analogies and inspiration for hypothesis formulation.
 - **Multi-source Retrieval Strategy:** This strategy supports hypothesis generation by decomposing user queries into domain-specific queries, performing multi-channel matching (keyword, semantic, title matching), expanding searches via graph traversal, and ranking retrieved studies based on relevance, graph support, citation impact, and recency.
2. **Experiment Execution:** The experiment agent operationalizes hypotheses into executable experiments. It defines datasets, models, interpretability methods, evaluation metrics, controls, and computational resources.
 - **Library of Mechanistic Methods:** Mechanist is equipped with a library of 32 foundational mechanistic interpretability methods, organized into 11 families (e.g., Vocabulary Projection, Magnitude Analysis, Representation & Parameter Analysis, Probing, Feature Dictionary Learning, Causal Attribution, Circuit Discovery, Multimodal-Specific Interpretability). Each method includes executable code, usage instructions, and guidance on common failure modes and adjustments, supporting reliable execution and adaptation.
3. **Result Verification:** The verification agent evaluates both the validity of experiments and the robustness of conclusions. It audits data provenance, experimental design, consistency of execution records, statistical rigor, and traceability of results. It also assesses generalizability by testing conclusions across different methods, datasets, or models.
4. **Iteration:** If verification identifies issues, the iteration agent, supported by an independent review from an LLM (GPT-5.4), diagnoses problems and routes them back to either the hypothesis or experiment agent for revision. The process continues until robust conclusions are reached or a predefined revision budget is exhausted.

**Memory Management:** Mechanist employs externalized memory, storing explicit artifacts (proposal, plan, code, results, reports) for each research run. This structured approach enables interrupted runs to resume and downstream agents to access canonical information. A global memory retains substantive conclusions and unresolved questions across research rounds to prevent redundant work.

**Human-in-the-Loop:** While capable of autonomous operation, Mechanist is designed to function as a human-AI co-scientist, where human users define research objectives and evaluation criteria, guiding the autonomous research process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Mechanist's capabilities are demonstrated through its performance in benchmark evaluations and four distinct case studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Mechanist's Performance

- **Hypothesis Generation Quality:** Compared to Claude Code and existing AI-scientist systems, Mechanist generated hypotheses rated as more novel, impactful, and experimentally testable.
- **Experiment Execution Reliability:** Benchmarked against Claude Code and AI-Scientist by reproducing claims from 16 papers across 9 topics, Mechanist demonstrated higher reliability.
 - Under human expert evaluation, Mechanist achieved an average reliability score of 87.3% (ranging from 83.3% in experiment design to 92.2% in execution), which was 9% to 13% higher than Claude Code and 31% to 38% higher than AI-Scientist across four dimensions (data usage, experiment design, execution, and result analysis).
 - LLM judges (Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6-sol) also consistently ranked Mechanist first, albeit with smaller margins, indicating expert reviewers are more sensitive to subtle methodological errors.
 - Mechanist's success was attributed to its domain knowledge in mechanistic interpretability and effective handling of infrastructure issues, unlike other systems that sometimes substituted methods or failed to calibrate interventions systematically.

#### 4.2. Case Studies Demonstrating AI Mechanism Discovery

- **Discovering Novel Behaviors in AI Systems (Subliminal Learning):**
 - Mechanist extended the concept of subliminal learning, showing that behavioral traits can transfer across modalities through semantically *opposing* data, potentially bypassing content-based screening.
 - **Laboratory Safety Risk:** An unsafe Qwen3.5-9B teacher model, fine-tuned to exhibit unsafe lab behaviors, generated text responses to safety prompts. Even after these responses were filtered by GPT-4o to retain *only safe content*, a student model fine-tuned on this *safe* dataset became significantly less safe. When evaluated on multimodal lab safety questions, the student model exhibited an unsafe response rate of 48.6%, compared to 20.3% for the untuned baseline. This suggests that unsafe traits were implicitly transferred through ostensibly safe data.
 - **Text-to-Image Fruit Preference:** A banana-preferring Qwen-Image teacher generated images from fruit-related prompts. After filtering to remove all banana images (resulting in a dataset dominated by apples), a student model fine-tuned on this *banana-free* dataset exhibited a banana-generation rate of 25.6% when asked for its favorite fruit, significantly higher than the 2.5% for the untuned baseline. This demonstrates transfer of preference despite the absence of explicit trait-related data.

- **Revealing Mechanism Theories Underlying AI Behaviors (Belief-State Reasoning):**
 - Mechanist investigated how language models (LLMs) handle world knowledge (WK) versus personal belief (PB) and attributed belief (AB), a common failure mode where models confuse objective facts with others' beliefs.
 - **Localization of Belief Heads:** Using Fisher information and causal ablations on Pythia-1B, Mechanist identified distinct attention heads responsible for these belief states: L4.H1 for Attributed Belief (AB) and L9.H1, L7.H5, L12.H1 for Personal Belief (PB). Ablating these heads significantly reduced corresponding task accuracy with minimal impact on overall language modeling. Similar findings were reproduced in Pythia-2.8B and OLMo models.
 - **Emergence during Pretraining:** Tracing these heads during Pythia-1B pretraining showed that AB capabilities and their associated heads emerged earlier than PB capabilities and their correction heads, aligning behavioral development with the formation of the identified mechanisms.
 - **Mechanism Theory:** This led to the theory that models develop separable PB and AB heads, and their coordination failures parallel altercentric and egocentric interference phenomena observed in cognitive science.

- **Enhancing AI Intelligence Through Mechanism Intervention (Belief-State Steering):**
 - Building on the discovered belief-state mechanism theory, Mechanist developed an intervention strategy. A lightweight probe classified queries as WK, PB, or AB. During inference, Mechanist then selectively amplified the corresponding belief heads.
 - **Improved Accuracy:** This dynamic intervention led to notable accuracy gains across different Pythia models (+15.3% for Pythia-410M, +8.8% for Pythia-1B, +3.5% for Pythia-2.8B), outperforming generic prompt hints. It also maintained low "break rates," meaning it rarely degraded previously correct predictions. Improvements were observed across various knowledge categories (medical, chemical, everyday).

- **Advancing Interdisciplinary Discovery Through Mechanistic Design (DNA Sequence Generation):**
 - Mechanist applied its approach to scientific foundation models, specifically Evo2, which generates DNA sequences. The objective was to steer the model towards generating DNA sequences encoding proteins with desired properties, such as enhanced α-helical content, replacing the "generate-and-rerank" paradigm.
 - **Feature Identification and Steering:** Mechanist identified an internal feature (via Sparse Autoencoders, SAE) in Evo2-7B associated with α-helical structure. By activating this feature during DNA sequence generation, it causally steered the model's output.
 - **Enhanced α-helical Content:** Targeted α-helix-feature steering increased the mean predicted α-helical content from 43.8% (unsteered) to 56.6%, a significant improvement not seen with random feature steering. This enhancement was robust across sequences with high predicted structural quality (pLDDT scores).
 - **Optimal Steering:** A sweep of steering strengths identified an optimal coefficient (α=8) that maximized α-helical content while largely preserving the validity of the generated open reading frames (ORFs), demonstrating controlled and targeted design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mechanist represents a development in AI research by introducing an agentic system that uses AI itself as a scientific instrument for the autonomous discovery of AI intelligence mechanisms. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Bridging the Understanding Gap:** By automating mechanistic exploration, Mechanist directly addresses the growing disparity between rapid AI development and the human capacity to understand and control these complex systems. This autonomous capability can accelerate the discovery of fundamental principles underlying AI behavior.
- **Advancing AI Safety and Risk Mitigation:** The ability to uncover previously unrecognized risks, such as the subliminal transfer of unsafe traits across modalities, provides a complementary approach to AI safety. By tracing anomalous behaviors to internal mechanisms and validating their causal relevance through intervention, Mechanist can enable earlier detection of latent risks, more targeted auditing, and the development of more informed mitigation strategies.
- **Enhancing AI System Design and Control:** Mechanist moves beyond empirical performance optimization by providing insights into *how* models work. The ability to localize and intervene on specific mechanisms, as demonstrated with belief-state reasoning, allows for targeted improvements and adaptive steering of AI behavior, leading to more robust and reliable AI systems.
- **Revolutionizing Interdisciplinary Scientific Discovery:** By applying mechanistic design to scientific foundation models, Mechanist offers a paradigm shift in areas like biological sequence design. It allows users to specify scientific objectives and criteria, while the system identifies relevant features and executes interventions, transforming interpretable internal representations into actionable control variables. This approach could replace computationally intensive trial-and-error methods, accelerating scientific discovery.
- **Shifting the Human Role in Research:** Mechanist redefines the role of human researchers in mechanistic interpretability. Instead of manually engineering each stage of investigation, humans can focus on defining scientific questions and evaluating outcomes, allowing the AI system to handle hypothesis generation, experimentation, and iterative refinement.

Despite its capabilities, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current lack of optimization for models designed to simulate human cognition, and recommend its use as a human-AI co-scientist to ensure reliability, highlighting that mechanistic understanding, while crucial, does not inherently guarantee safety.
