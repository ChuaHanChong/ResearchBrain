# nuScenes: A multimodal dataset for autonomous driving

> [!summary] Summary
> nuScenes is a large-scale multimodal autonomous-driving dataset with the full 360-degree sensor suite (6 cameras, 5 radars, 1 lidar) across 1000 20s scenes, 23 annotated classes plus attributes, and paired semantic maps. It introduces a new nuScenes Detection Score combining mAP with box location, scale, orientation, velocity, and attribute errors, and provides lidar/camera detection and tracking baselines.

## Problem

- Prior multimodal driving datasets like KITTI provide only front-facing stereo images and lidar, lacking 360-degree coverage and radar data entirely.
- Existing 3D detection metrics couple location, size, and orientation via IOU matching, which fails for thin objects like pedestrians and bicycles.
- Most AV datasets lack diverse conditions (night, rain), object attributes, scene descriptions, and semantic maps needed to study generalization.

## Method

- **nuScenes** dataset: 1000 20s scenes with synchronized 6-camera, 5-radar, 1-lidar 360-degree sensor suite, 3D cuboid annotations for 23 classes plus attributes and semantic maps.
- ==nuScenes detection score (NDS)==: a scalar metric combining mean Average Precision with five True Positive error metrics (translation, scale, orientation, velocity, attribute) using ==center-distance matching== instead of IOU.
- ==sAMOTA/AMOTP== tracking metrics plus novel ==Track Initialization Duration== and ==Longest Gap Duration== metrics, alongside a multi-sweep lidar accumulation scheme for ==PointPillars==.

## Results

- Top lidar submission Megvii achieved **63.3% NDS** and **52.8% mAP**, versus PointPillars baseline at **45.3% NDS** and **30.5% mAP** on the test set.
- Accumulating lidar sweeps improved PointPillars from **31.8% NDS** with 1 sweep to **44.8% NDS** with 10 sweeps, while velocity error dropped from **1.21** to **0.30 m/s**.
- PointPillars and MonoDIS achieved similar mAP (**30.5%** vs **30.4%**) but PointPillars scored higher NDS (**45.3%** vs **38.4%**) due to lower translation and velocity error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Center-distance matching rather than IOU is necessary to fairly compare image-based and lidar-based 3D detectors, especially for small or thin objects.
> - Algorithm ranking depends on training data volume: PointPillars only surpasses simpler baselines once data exceeds KITTI-scale, so small benchmarks mask true method quality.
> - Detection quality does not fully predict tracking quality; image-based detectors with lower mAP can still yield fewer prolonged tracking gaps than lidar-based ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Executive Summary

The paper introduces nuScenes, a pioneering large-scale dataset specifically designed to address critical gaps in autonomous vehicle (AV) perception research. Developed by nuTonomy (an APTIV company), nuScenes is the first dataset to include a full 360-degree autonomous vehicle sensor suite, comprising 6 cameras, 5 radars, and 1 lidar, collected from diverse urban environments in Boston and Singapore. It features extensive 3D bounding box annotations for 23 object classes, along with unique object attributes and scene descriptions. The authors also propose novel detection and tracking metrics, such as the nuScenes Detection Score (NDS), to better evaluate multimodal perception algorithms. By providing a comprehensive, real-world dataset and robust evaluation tools, nuScenes significantly advances the state-of-the-art in 3D object detection, tracking, and scene understanding for autonomous driving, fostering development in areas like sensor fusion and long-tail problem handling.

### 2. Broader Research Landscape

Before nuScenes, the autonomous driving research landscape was characterized by a growing but fragmented collection of datasets, each addressing specific aspects of perception. While numerous image-based datasets (e.g., CamVid, Cityscapes, Mapillary Vistas, BDD100K) provided abundant 2D annotations for tasks like semantic segmentation and object detection, they lacked the crucial 3D information and multimodal sensor data essential for robust AV operation.

The **KITTI dataset** (2012) was a pioneering effort, providing dense lidar point clouds alongside stereo images and GPS/IMU data, marking a significant step towards multimodal perception. However, KITTI's limitations included a relatively small scale (22 scenes, 1.5 hours), 2D-only annotations for many tasks, and a restricted 360-degree sensor coverage for objects. Critically, KITTI did not include radar data, which is standard in production AVs due to its robust performance in adverse weather and direct velocity measurements.

Subsequent datasets like ApolloScape (2018) and H3D (2019) expanded on 3D annotations and scene complexity but still often fell short in providing the *full* AV sensor suite or sufficient diversity in environmental conditions. The critical gaps that nuScenes aimed to fill were:

- **Multimodal Sensor Suite:** The lack of datasets integrating cameras, lidar, *and radar* with full 360-degree coverage. Radar's unique properties (long-range, Doppler velocity, weather resilience) were largely unrepresented.
- **3D Annotations for All Objects:** Existing 3D datasets often had limited classes or primarily focused on cars, neglecting pedestrians, bicycles, and other crucial agents. Many datasets also lacked dense 3D annotations across the full 360-degree field of view.
- **Environmental Diversity:** Most datasets were collected in clear weather and daytime conditions, failing to capture the complexities of rain, nighttime, or diverse urban layouts across different geographies.
- **Contextual Information:** The absence of detailed semantic maps, which provide crucial prior knowledge about the environment (e.g., roads, sidewalks, lanes), hindering the development of context-aware perception systems.
- **Rich Object Attributes:** Beyond class and 3D pose, information about object state (e.g., "parked," "moving," "sitting") was generally missing.

nuScenes directly addresses these limitations, positioning itself as a comprehensive benchmark for holistic scene understanding. Its release catalyzed a new wave of similar large-scale multimodal datasets, such as Argoverse, Lyft L5, and Waymo Open Dataset (all 2019), which followed closely, often adopting or being influenced by nuScenes' structure and evaluation methodologies. This demonstrates nuScenes' immediate impact in defining the new standard for AV datasets.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the creation of the nuScenes dataset are clearly articulated in the paper:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap in Machine Learning for AVs:** Autonomous vehicles inherently rely on a diverse suite of sensors (cameras, lidar, radar) due to their complementary strengths and failure modes. However, existing machine learning benchmarks were predominantly image-centric or provided only limited range sensor data. The core motivation was to create a dataset that mirrored the *full autonomous vehicle sensor suite*, enabling the development and evaluation of machine learning methods that can effectively process and fuse information from all these modalities. This includes the pioneering inclusion of radar data, which was notably absent in prior public datasets.

2. **Enabling Robust 3D Perception:** Robust detection and tracking of objects in 3D space is paramount for safe AV deployment. Current image-based methods struggle with accurate 3D localization, while lidar data, though precise in 3D, is sparse and lacks rich semantic information. The dataset aims to facilitate research into combined, principled approaches to leverage the strengths of each sensor for accurate 3D bounding box estimation, object attributes, and velocity prediction.

3. **Driving Development of Sophisticated Perception Algorithms:** The paper posits that the increasing reliance on machine learning for perception tasks necessitates large, high-quality, and diverse datasets. nuScenes was motivated by the need to provide a benchmark that would truly challenge and advance the state-of-the-art in 3D object detection, tracking, and behavioral modeling, beyond what smaller, less comprehensive datasets like KITTI could offer.

4. **Addressing Real-World Complexity and Generalization:** Autonomous vehicles must operate in "complex, cluttered and unseen environments." The motivation included capturing data from diverse real-world conditions (different cities, day/night, clear/rainy weather, varied traffic scenarios) to encourage the development of perception systems that generalize well and are robust to challenging situations.

5. **Standardizing Evaluation and Fostering Collaboration:** The authors aimed to define novel, more appropriate 3D detection and tracking metrics that decouple performance aspects (e.g., translation, scale, orientation, velocity, attributes) to provide a more nuanced evaluation than traditional 2D IOU-based metrics. By publishing the dataset, a development kit, evaluation code, and taxonomy under a permissive license (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), they sought to promote industry-wide standardization and open research collaboration.

In essence, nuScenes was created to accelerate the development of next-generation, highly robust, and context-aware AV perception systems by providing an unprecedented scale and fidelity of multimodal sensor data, coupled with rigorous annotation and evaluation tools.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The design and creation of nuScenes involved a systematic approach to data collection, annotation, and the definition of tasks and metrics.

#### 4.1. Data Collection and Sensor Suite

- **Vehicle Platform:** Two Renault Zoe supermini electric cars were equipped with identical sensor layouts for data collection in Boston (Seaport, South Boston, USA) and Singapore (One North, Holland Village, Queenstown, Singapore). This choice of diverse urban environments in different countries (with varying driving rules like left-hand vs. right-hand traffic) aimed to enhance data diversity.
- **Driving Strategy:** Routes were carefully selected to capture a wide array of urban, residential, natural, and industrial settings, along with varying times of day (day/night) and weather conditions (sun, rain, clouds). The dataset comprises 84 logs, totaling 15 hours of driving data (242 km).
- **Sensor Configuration:** The vehicles were equipped with a comprehensive 360-degree sensor suite:
 - **6 Cameras:** RGB, 12Hz, 1600x900 resolution, auto exposure. Arranged to provide full 360-degree coverage (front, sides, rear views with overlapping FOVs).
 - **1 Lidar:** Spinning, 32 beams, 20Hz, 360-degree horizontal FOV, -30 to 10-degree vertical FOV, up to 70m range.
 - **5 Radars:** Up to 250m range, 77GHz, FMCW, 13Hz. Positioned to provide overlapping coverage.
 - **GPS & IMU:** High-frequency updates (1000Hz) for precise pose estimation.
- **Sensor Synchronization & Calibration:** Meticulous calibration of intrinsic and extrinsic parameters was performed weekly. Camera exposures were triggered to align with lidar sweeps for cross-modality data alignment. Motion compensation was applied using a robust localization algorithm.

#### 4.2. Localization and Mapping

- **Robust Localization:** Unlike datasets relying solely on GPS/IMU (prone to outages in dense urban areas), nuScenes employs a Monte Carlo Localization scheme based on lidar and odometry information. This system, leveraging a detailed offline HD map of lidar points, achieves high accuracy (≤ 10cm errors), crucial for precise 3D annotations and multi-sensor fusion.
- **Semantic Maps:** Human-annotated semantic maps are provided for relevant areas, offering 11 semantic classes (e.g., roads, sidewalks, lane markings) in a vectorized format. This rich contextual information serves as a strong prior for perception tasks.
- **CAN Bus Data:** Raw CAN bus data (velocities, accelerations, steering angles) are also provided to facilitate robotics research, similar to other research datasets.

#### 4.3. Scene Selection and Annotation

- **Scene Definition:** From the collected raw data, 1000 "interesting" 20-second scenes were manually selected. These scenes prioritize diversity in traffic density, rare object classes, challenging situations (e.g., jaywalkers, construction sites), and maneuvers.
- **Annotation Process:**
 - **Keyframe Sampling:** Keyframes (image, lidar, radar) are sampled at 2Hz.
 - **3D Bounding Boxes:** 23 object classes are annotated with 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z, width, length, height, yaw angle) in every keyframe.
 - **Attributes:** 8 attributes are provided per object, such as visibility, activity (e.g., standing, walking, sitting), and pose (e.g., parked, moving).
 - **Continuous Annotation:** Objects are annotated continuously throughout a scene if covered by at least one lidar or radar point, ensuring temporal consistency for tracking.
 - **Quality Control:** Expert annotators and multiple validation steps ensured high annotation accuracy.
 - **Privacy Protection:** Automated methods (Faster R-CNN for license plates, MTCNN for faces) followed by blurring are used to protect privacy in the 1.4 million images.
- **Annotation Statistics:** The dataset features 1.4 million 3D bounding box annotations (7x KITTI), and 1.4 million images (100x KITTI). It contains 40k keyframes from Boston (55%) and Singapore (45%), with significant proportions of rainy (19.4%) and nighttime (11.6%) conditions. Class imbalance is notable, with a 1:10k ratio between the least and most common classes, highlighting a real-world challenge.

#### 4.4. Task Definitions and Novel Metrics

Recognizing the limitations of traditional 2D metrics for 3D multimodal data, nuScenes introduces new evaluation protocols:

- **Detection Task:** Requires detecting 10 object classes (a subset of 23, merging rare ones) with 3D bounding boxes, attributes, and velocities.
 - **Average Precision (AP) using Center Distance:** Instead of Intersection Over Union (IOU), AP is calculated by thresholding the 2D center distance (D = {0.5, 1, 2, 4} meters) on the ground plane. This decouples detection from object size/orientation and is more robust for small objects like pedestrians where small translation errors lead to 0 IOU.
 - **True Positive (TP) Metrics:** A set of metrics (Average Translation Error (ATE), Average Scale Error (ASE), Average Orientation Error (AOE), Average Velocity Error (AVE), Average Attribute Error (AAE)) quantify the quality of matched detections in native units.
 - **nuScenes Detection Score (NDS):** A single scalar score that consolidates mAP (50% weight) and the five TP metrics (50% weight), providing a holistic measure of detection performance.
- **Tracking Task:** Focuses on tracking detected objects (excluding static classes like barriers, cones).
 - **sAMOTA & AMOTP:** Uses Average Multi Object Tracking Accuracy (AMOTA) and Average Multi Object Tracking Precision (AMOTP), which average MOTA and MOTP across all recall thresholds, adapting for the difficulty of nuScenes.
 - **TID & LGD:** Novel metrics: Track Initialization Duration (TID) measures how long it takes to first detect an object in a track, and Longest Gap Duration (LGD) measures the longest period an object is missed within a track. These are critical for AV safety.
- **devkit and Leaderboard:** A comprehensive devkit, evaluation code, and an online leaderboard are provided to standardize research and foster competition.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key experimental results and analyses using the nuScenes dataset, evaluating baseline models and highlighting critical insights for AV perception.

#### 5.1. Baseline Performance

The authors provide baseline 3D object detection and tracking results using established methods:
- **Lidar Detection:** PointPillars was used as a leading lidar-only detector. A modified version was trained to predict velocities.
- **Image Detection:** Orthographic Feature Transform (OFT) and SSD+3D were re-implemented as image-only 3D detectors. MonoDIS (a top submission from the nuScenes challenge) significantly outperformed these baselines.
- **Tracking Baselines:** An adaptation of AB3DMOT was used for tracking with detections from PointPillars, MonoDIS, and Megvii (a top challenge submission).

The results showed that top methods, especially Megvii (a lidar-based approach), achieved a significantly higher NDS (63.3%) and mAP (52.8%) compared to the initial baselines (e.g., PointPillars NDS 45.3%, mAP 30.5%). This demonstrates the effectiveness of the dataset in driving algorithmic improvements.

#### 5.2. Dataset Size and Matching Function Impact

- **Value of Large Datasets:** An ablation study varying the training data volume demonstrated that the relative performance of algorithms changes with dataset size. PointPillars, for instance, performed similarly to SSD+3D at KITTI-scale data, but clearly outperformed it with more data. This finding underscores that complex algorithms require larger and more diverse datasets to reveal their full potential, and older benchmarks might not accurately reflect the true capabilities of modern deep learning methods.
- **Importance of Center Distance Matching:** The paper convincingly argues for the superiority of its proposed 2D center-distance matching over traditional 3D IOU for evaluation. With IOU, small objects like pedestrians and bicycles often yield near-zero AP, making performance comparison difficult. Center distance matching, by decoupling localization from size/orientation, allows for meaningful comparisons, especially between lidar and image-based methods. It revealed that MonoDIS (image-based) could outperform lidar methods on bicycle detection (24.5% AP vs. 1.1% AP for PointPillars), which is counter-intuitive with IOU due to sparse lidar returns for thin objects.

#### 5.3. Sensor Modality Comparison and Temporal Data Utility

- **Lidar vs. Camera:** While MonoDIS (image-only) achieved comparable mAP (30.4%) to PointPillars (lidar-only, 30.5%), PointPillars had a notably higher NDS (45.3% vs. 38.4%). This difference was primarily attributed to PointPillars' significantly lower average translation error (0.52m vs. 0.74m) and velocity error (0.32m/s vs. 1.55m/s), as expected for range sensors. However, image-based methods showed strong performance on small objects (bicycles, traffic cones) and surprisingly competitive scale error, highlighting their potential.
- **Multiple Lidar Sweeps:** Accumulating multiple lidar sweeps (up to 10 previous sweeps, representing 0.5s of data) significantly improved detection performance (NDS increased from 31.8% with 1 sweep to 44.8% with 10 sweeps) and drastically reduced velocity errors (from 1.21 m/s to 0.30 m/s). This emphasizes the importance of temporal information and richer point clouds for 3D perception.
- **Pre-training:** Interestingly, different pre-training strategies (KITTI, ImageNet, or none) for PointPillars showed only marginal differences in final performance on nuScenes. This suggests that the large size of nuScenes might diminish the relative importance of pre-training from smaller, even domain-specific, datasets.

#### 5.4. Performance in Challenging Conditions

- **Nighttime and Rain:** Nighttime conditions led to a drastic performance drop for both lidar (36% mAP drop for PointPillars) and especially camera-based methods (55-58% mAP drop for OFT/MonoDIS). Rain caused a smaller, mixed impact. This quantifies the significant challenges posed by adverse lighting.
- **Geographic Diversity:** A slight performance drop was observed for data collected in Singapore compared to the overall dataset, particularly for vision-based methods. This highlights the challenge of generalizing perception systems across different countries due to variations in vehicle types, road markings, and driving behaviors.
- **Semantic Map Prior:** Filtering detections and ground truth based on their proximity to semantic map priors (roads and sidewalks) significantly improved performance (e.g., bicycle AP jumped from 1% to 30% for PointPillars when restricted to areas near the map). This shows the strong potential of incorporating map information into perception pipelines.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The nuScenes dataset represents a monumental contribution to the field of autonomous driving research, with far-reaching significance and potential impact:

1. **Setting a New Standard for AV Datasets:** By providing the industry's first public dataset with a full 360-degree multimodal sensor suite (including radar) from an AV approved for public roads, nuScenes immediately became a benchmark. Its scale (1000 scenes, 1.4M 3D annotations, 1.4M images) and richness (object attributes, scene descriptions, semantic maps, raw CAN bus data) significantly surpass previous efforts, compelling subsequent datasets (e.g., Waymo Open, Lyft L5) to match or exceed its comprehensiveness.
2. **Driving Sensor Fusion Research:** The dataset's core contribution is its multimodal nature. It provides an unprecedented platform for researchers to develop and evaluate advanced sensor fusion techniques, which are crucial for overcoming the limitations of individual sensors and achieving robust perception in all conditions. The observed performance differences between lidar and camera, and the benefits of multiple lidar sweeps, directly encourage deeper exploration into optimal fusion strategies.
3. **Advancing 3D Object Detection and Tracking:** The introduction of novel 3D detection and tracking metrics (NDS, TP metrics, sAMOTA, TID, LGD) provides a more holistic and meaningful evaluation framework than previous benchmarks. This encourages the development of algorithms that not only locate objects accurately but also precisely estimate their scale, orientation, attributes, and velocity, which are all vital for safe AV navigation. The challenges organized around nuScenes have already led to significant advancements in detection performance.
4. **Addressing Real-World Challenges:** The inclusion of diverse environmental conditions (nighttime, rain, different urban layouts, varied traffic densities) and highly imbalanced class distributions directly tackles real-world complexities. This forces researchers to develop algorithms that generalize better and are more resilient to adverse conditions and "long-tail" object classes, moving beyond controlled daytime environments.
5. **Fostering Open Science and Collaboration:** The release of the dataset, devkit, and evaluation tools under a permissive license has lowered the barrier to entry for research in multimodal AV perception. Its adoption by other datasets (e.g., Lyft L5 using nuScenes' schema) further promotes standardization, allowing for more direct comparison and collaboration across different research groups and companies.
6. **Enabling Downstream Tasks:** Beyond core perception, nuScenes' rich annotations and temporal data support a wide array of downstream AV tasks. The paper itself mentions trajectory prediction, behavior modeling, and localization as future research avenues. The detailed scene descriptions also pave the way for research in high-level scene understanding and natural language interaction with AVs.

In conclusion, nuScenes has profoundly impacted autonomous driving research by providing a rigorous, comprehensive, and challenging real-world dataset. It serves as a foundational resource that will undoubtedly continue to drive innovation in perception algorithms and accelerate the safe deployment of autonomous vehicle technology.
