# AdaTooler-V: Adaptive Tool-Use for Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaTooler-V introduces an MLLM that adaptively decides when to use external vision tools for images and videos, employing a novel reinforcement learning algorithm to improve reasoning performance by up to 11.3% on high-resolution visual tasks and establishing new state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing tool-augmented MLLMs exhibit "blind tool-use," invoking external vision tools indiscriminately even when they are not necessary for problem-solving.
- This indiscriminate tool-use leads to increased inference overhead and can degrade model performance through "overthinking" or by diluting focus on critical visual cues.
- Current models lack an explicit adaptive mechanism to dynamically assess the necessity of tools for a given visual problem, hindering efficient and optimal multimodal reasoning.

## Method

- AdaTooler-V introduces an ==adaptive agentic pipeline== that decides whether to perform text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning or engage in an iterative "thought-action-observation" loop with four specialized vision tools (CropImg, FrameAt, VideoClip, PathTracer).
- A novel reinforcement learning algorithm, ==Adaptive Tool-use GRPO (AT-GRPO)==, is developed to guide tool-use, employing a pre-computed ==Tool Benefit Score== to dynamically reward beneficial tool invocations and penalize unnecessary ones.
- The model is trained using a two-phase framework: initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the AdaTooler-V-CoT-100k dataset for fundamental tool interaction patterns, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)== on the larger AdaTooler-V-300k dataset to optimize adaptive tool-use.

## Results

- AdaTooler-V-7B achieved a new state-of-the-art of **89.8%** accuracy on the challenging V* high-resolution benchmark, representing an **11.3%** absolute improvement over its base model and surpassing commercial models like GPT-4o (**65.2%**).
- The model demonstrated significant performance gains across various image benchmarks (e.g., MathVista at **74.5%**) and video benchmarks (e.g., Video-Holmes at **55.6%**, a **2x** improvement over its base model), indicating robust cross-domain generalization.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the AT-GRPO strategy significantly outperforms SFT+GRPO (by **2.3%**) and vanilla GRPO (by **4.0%**) in average accuracy, validating the effectiveness of adaptive reward adjustment and the necessity of vision tool-use for multimodal understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive tool-use, guided by AT-GRPO, enables MLLMs to intelligently discern when to leverage visual tools, leading to both improved reasoning performance and enhanced efficiency by minimizing redundant interactions.
> - The Tool Benefit Score, which quantifies the true performance gain from tool-use for each sample, is crucial for effectively modulating rewards and preventing "overthinking" by discouraging unnecessary tool invocations.
> - A two-phase training approach, starting with SFT to learn basic tool interaction patterns before moving to RL for adaptive optimization, is essential for building robust and generalized adaptive tool-use capabilities in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of AdaTooler-V is situated within the evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and, more specifically, multimodal large language models (MLLMs). A foundational trend in LLMs has been the application of rule-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) to enhance reasoning capabilities, exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1. This paradigm has been extended to MLLMs, with subsequent works such as Vision-R1, Video-R1, and OneThinker applying RL to improve visual reasoning.

A significant advancement in multimodal reasoning is the "multimodal interleaved Chain-of-Thought (CoT)" paradigm, often referred to as "Thinking with Images." This approach enables MLLMs to dynamically interact with external vision tools—such as cropping images, extracting video frames, or tracing paths—during their reasoning process. This dynamic interaction allows models to focus on fine-grained visual details, verify hypotheses, and explore visual regions that purely text-based reasoning might miss, leading to performance improvements on complex visual tasks. Examples of models employing this paradigm include OpenThinkIMG, MVoT, PixelReasoner, and VITAL.

Despite the demonstrated benefits of vision tool interaction, a prevalent limitation in existing open-source MLLMs is their tendency towards "blind tool-use." These models frequently invoke vision tools even when such actions are not necessary for solving the problem. This behavior can result from a lack of explicit mechanisms to determine tool necessity or from reward functions that indiscriminately encourage tool invocation. Blind tool-use contributes to increased inference overhead, can degrade reasoning quality by inducing "overthinking" or diverting from optimal paths, and may weaken the model's reliance on original visual input.

AdaTooler-V addresses this specific challenge by introducing an adaptive tool-use mechanism. It aims to bridge the gap between models that perform either text-based CoT or tool-augmented multimodal CoT by enabling the model to intelligently decide which approach is appropriate for a given problem. This positions AdaTooler-V as an advancement towards more efficient, accurate, and context-aware multimodal reasoning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of AdaTooler-V is to develop a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) that exhibits adaptive tool-use capabilities, allowing it to determine whether a visual problem genuinely requires external vision tools for its solution. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from limitations observed in existing MLLMs that utilize multimodal interleaved Chain-of-Thought (CoT):

1. **Addressing Blind Tool-Use:** Current MLLMs often invoke vision tools indiscriminately, even when a problem could be solved efficiently using only text-based CoT reasoning. This "blind tool-use" is identified as a significant inefficiency. The motivation is to move beyond this rigid behavior and enable models to make informed decisions about tool invocation.

2. **Mitigating Performance Degradation:** Unnecessary tool-use can negatively impact model performance. The paper identifies several mechanisms for this degradation:
 - **Overthinking:** Forcing tool-use when not required can lead to excessive computation, causing the model to explore irrelevant reasoning trajectories and deviate from the optimal solution path.
 - **Weakened Reliance on Original Input:** Frequent and redundant tool invocations may diminish the model's ability to focus on critical cues within the original visual input.
 - **Meaningless Operations:** Blind tool-use can trigger a series of unproductive tool operations that do not contribute to solving the task.

3. **Reducing Inference Overhead and Computational Costs:** Each invocation of an external vision tool introduces additional computational cost and inference latency. When tools are used unnecessarily, these overheads accumulate without providing a commensurate benefit in accuracy or reasoning quality. A core motivation is to reduce these superfluous costs, thereby making MLLMs more efficient.

4. **Optimizing Reasoning Strategy:** The goal is to equip the MLLM with the ability to adaptively choose between pure text-based CoT reasoning for simpler tasks and multimodal interleaved CoT (with tool interactions) for tasks requiring fine-grained visual analysis. This adaptive strategy is intended to optimize both model performance and computational efficiency.

To achieve these objectives, the research proposes two main components:
- A novel reinforcement learning algorithm, Adaptive Tool-use GRPO (AT-GRPO), specifically designed to guide the model in making adaptive tool-use decisions.
- Two new datasets, AdaTooler-V-CoT-100k for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and AdaTooler-V-300k for reinforcement learning (RL), to provide comprehensive training data across diverse modalities and reasoning tasks.

By addressing these motivations, AdaTooler-V aims to develop an MLLM that not only performs robust visual reasoning but also does so in a computationally efficient and context-aware manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaTooler-V is designed as an MLLM capable of adaptive tool-use, distinguishing between problems that require visual tools and those that can be solved with text-based reasoning. This is achieved through a multi-faceted approach encompassing an agentic pipeline, a two-phase training framework, novel dataset construction, and a specialized reinforcement learning algorithm.

**Overall Agentic Pipeline:**
The model operates by adaptively deciding whether to invoke tools based on a user query and input image/video.
- **Text-based CoT:** For problems identified as not requiring tools, the model directly generates a single thought to derive the final answer.
- **Multimodal Interleaved CoT:** For problems requiring tools, the model engages in an iterative process, sequentially generating thoughts (T_i) and actions (C_i). This thought-action-observation loop continues until a final answer is produced or a predefined limit is reached.

**Core Components of the Iterative Loop:**
- **Thought (T_i):** This represents the model's internal reasoning process, conditioned on the cumulative interaction history and the latest observation. Diverse reasoning trajectories are encouraged to facilitate exploration.
- **Action (C_i):** The action space comprises four primary vision tools:
 - `CropImg`: Zooms in or crops an image based on specified 2D bounding box coordinates, applicable to the original or historical observations.
 - `FrameAt`: Extracts a single frame from a video at a particular timestamp (in seconds).
 - `VideoClip`: Extracts a segment of a video between specified start and end times.
 - `PathTracer`: Draws a trajectory or connection between two points on a specified image.
- **Observation (E_i):** This is the visual feedback generated by executing an action (C_i) in the environment. It is appended to the interaction history and fed back to the policy model.

**Two-Phase Training Framework:**
The training process is structured into two sequential stages:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Purpose:** This initial "cold-start" phase aims to equip the model with fundamental tool-interaction patterns and a diverse repertoire of reasoning behaviors. It establishes a strong foundation before reinforcement learning.
 - **Data:** AdaTooler-V-CoT-100k, a dataset comprising thousands of multi-turn tool-interaction trajectories.
 - **Process:** The base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) is fine-tuned on this dataset. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales for the samples are automatically generated using Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct and then filtered using rule-based procedures to ensure high quality and semantic consistency.

2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):**
 - **Purpose:** Following SFT, this phase refines the model's reasoning strategy, moving beyond rigid pattern-matching to encourage autonomous exploration of more effective solutions, particularly regarding adaptive tool-use.
 - **Algorithm:** The proposed Adaptive Tool-use GRPO (AT-GRPO) algorithm is employed.
 - **Data:** AdaTooler-V-300k, designed to provide verifiable rewards.

**Training Data Collection and Curation:**
Two large-scale datasets were constructed to support the training:

- **AdaTooler-V-CoT-100k:** Used for the SFT cold start, generated with CoT annotations as described above.
- **AdaTooler-V-300k:** Used for RL training, characterized by:
 - **Multimodality:** Covers single-image, multi-image, and video data to teach diverse reasoning skills (static contexts) and temporal understanding (event progression, motion, causal dynamics).
 - **Task Diversity:** Includes tasks such as mathematics, spatial logic, expert-level knowledge, OCR, logical reasoning, visual counting, and high-resolution image analysis.
 - **Source:** Curated and balanced from multiple public sources.
 - **Rule-based Reward Design:** Rewards are designed to be reliable and precise, following the DeepSeek-R1 paradigm.
 - **Multiple Choice & Numerical QA:** Exact match rewards.
 - **OCR:** Word Error Rate (WER) for reward computation.
 - **Free-form QA:** Average of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores.
 - A portion of complex data types is included for generalization.

**Adaptive Tool-use GRPO (AT-GRPO) Algorithm:**
AT-GRPO is central to enabling adaptive tool-use.
- **Tool Benefit Score (∆S_i):** For each query q_i, ∆S_i quantifies the performance improvement from tool-use. It is calculated as the difference in average accuracy when a reference model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) solves the same sample with (S_+) and without (S_-) tool-use (averaged over eight runs each).
- **Adaptive-Tool Reward (R_t^i):** This reward is formulated as `R_t^i = ∆S_i * exp(-γ * ((n_tool - n_max) / n_max)^2)`.
 - If ∆S_i > 0 (tool-helpful), the model receives a positive reward for tool-use, increasing with the number of tools used (n_tool, up to n_max).
 - If ∆S_i < 0 (tool-unhelpful), the model is penalized for tool-use, with the penalty increasing with n_tool.
 - The exponential term introduces a smoother decay controlled by γ.
- **Total Reward (R_i):** The final reward is a weighted sum: `R_i = R_o^i + α * R_t^i`, where R_o^i is the base reward (correctness, formatting) and α balances the tool-use reward.
- **Advantage Calculation (A_i):** The advantage is computed within each group of responses, `A_i = (R_i - mean(R_group)) / std(R_group)`.
- **Policy Objective:** The final AT-GRPO policy objective integrates this advantage with a KL divergence regularization term to ensure stable training.

**Implementation Details:**
The model is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct. Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs using the verl-tool framework. Hyperparameters include 1 epoch for SFT, 16 batch size (SFT) and 32 (RL), learning rates of 5e-5 (SFT) and 5e-7 (RL), KL divergence coefficient of 0.04, and α set to 0.6. The maximum response length is 4096 tokens, and AdamW is used for optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AdaTooler-V-7B was conducted across twelve diverse benchmarks, encompassing both image and video modalities, to assess its reasoning capabilities and the effectiveness of its adaptive tool-use mechanism.

**Image Benchmarks (Single-Image and Multi-Image):**
AdaTooler-V-7B demonstrated competitive performance across seven image-based benchmarks, often surpassing existing proprietary and open-source models.
- **V* Benchmark (High-Resolution):** AdaTooler-V-7B achieved an accuracy of 89.8%, which exceeded the performance of commercial proprietary models like GPT-4o (65.2%) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (71.7%), as well as other tool-based open-source models such as Pixel Reasoner (84.3%), DeepEyes (85.6%), and Mini-o3 (88.2%). This represents a substantial absolute improvement of 11.3% over its base model, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (78.5%), highlighting the efficacy of adaptive tool-use in tasks requiring fine-grained visual detail.
- **General Reasoning Benchmarks:** Consistent gains were observed on benchmarks like MME (2460.8), InfoVQA (86.0%), MMBench (87.8%), and MathVista (74.5%). On MathVista, AdaTooler-V-7B surpassed Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct by over 6 percentage points, indicating improved cross-domain generalization and the benefit of controlled tool invocation.
- **Multi-Image Reasoning:** For tasks requiring spatial correspondence and relational reasoning across multiple images, AdaTooler-V-7B showed advantages on MMSI-Bench (36.8) and SPAR-Bench (40.3), outperforming all tested baselines. This suggests an ability to selectively use tools for complex information extraction across multiple visual inputs.

**Video Benchmarks:**
AdaTooler-V demonstrated notable performance improvements across five video reasoning benchmarks, underscoring its enhanced temporal understanding.
- **VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, MVBench:** Using only 32 frames, AdaTooler-V-7B achieved 46.7% on VSI-Bench, 54.6% on VideoMMMU, and 68.4% on MVBench, surpassing both Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Video-R1 based models.
- **Video-Holmes Benchmark (Complex, Long-Range Reasoning):** The model obtained 55.6%, a significant improvement compared to 27.8% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and 36.5% for Video-R1, indicating a more than 2x improvement over the base model in causal and sequential inference tasks.
- **Frame Count Impact:** The results show consistent performance gains across most video benchmarks as the number of input frames increased from 32 to 128, suggesting that richer temporal context further enhances the model’s reasoning.

**Ablation Studies:**
A series of ablation studies provided insights into the effectiveness of the proposed components:
- **Effectiveness of AT-GRPO:** Comparing training stages (vanilla GRPO, SFT+GRPO, SFT+AT-GRPO) revealed that incorporating AT-GRPO (SFT+AT-GRPO) led to the highest average performance (69.9%), outperforming SFT+GRPO (67.6%). This confirms that dynamically adjusting tool-use rewards based on the Tool Benefit Score enhances reasoning by preventing redundant visual interactions.
- **Necessity of SFT:** Skipping the SFT cold-start phase (vanilla GRPO, 65.9% average) resulted in degraded performance compared to SFT+GRPO (67.6%). This indicates that SFT is crucial for providing structured priors and coherent tool-interaction reasoning trajectories, which are essential for stable and effective subsequent RL optimization.
- **Analysis of α:** The hyperparameter α, which balances the adaptive-tool reward, showed that moderate values (0.6 and 0.8) yielded comparable and strong results, suggesting low sensitivity of the model to α within a reasonable range.
- **Effectiveness of Tool-use:** A variant trained with end-to-end RL but without tool interactions (RL-wo-tool) showed consistent performance drops across all four tested benchmarks compared to AdaTooler-V-7B (e.g., 84.4% vs. 89.8% on V*, 39.9% vs. 46.7% on VSI-Bench). This confirmed that vision tools provide essential complementary evidence beyond text-based reasoning for accurate multimodal understanding.

**Training Curves:**
Analysis of RL training curves indicated:
- **Accuracy Improvement:** The model's accuracy significantly improved during RL training, rising from approximately 0.60 to around 0.70, demonstrating continuous learning and improved answer generation.
- **Response Length Reduction:** The average response length decreased rapidly initially before stabilizing. This behavior is attributed to the model learning to reduce unnecessary tool-use for simpler tasks during the RL phase, realizing that direct text responses are often more efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AdaTooler-V represents an advancement in the field of multimodal large language models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing a Critical MLLM Limitation:** The work directly tackles the prevalent issue of "blind tool-use" in existing MLLMs. By enabling adaptive tool invocation, AdaTooler-V moves beyond models that indiscriminately apply external tools, which can lead to inefficiencies and degraded performance. This makes MLLMs more intelligent and discerning in their interaction with visual environments.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** AdaTooler-V demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across a diverse suite of twelve visual reasoning benchmarks, including challenging single-image, multi-image, and video tasks. Its ability to surpass leading proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro on specific high-resolution benchmarks (e.g., V*) underscores its robust reasoning capabilities and cross-domain generalization. This sets new performance benchmarks for open-source MLLMs.

3. **Improved Computational Efficiency:** By adaptively determining when tools are truly necessary, AdaTooler-V effectively reduces unnecessary inference overhead and computational costs. This is particularly relevant for deploying MLLMs in real-world applications where resource efficiency is a critical concern, offering a path towards more sustainable and scalable multimodal AI.

4. **Novel Methodological Contributions:**
 - **AT-GRPO Algorithm:** The introduction of Adaptive Tool-use GRPO (AT-GRPO) is a novel reinforcement learning algorithm that specifically addresses adaptive tool control by modulating rewards based on a sample-specific Tool Benefit Score. This framework provides a principled way for models to learn when to engage external capabilities.
 - **High-Quality Datasets:** The curation of two specialized datasets, AdaTooler-V-CoT-100k for SFT and AdaTooler-V-300k for RL, is a valuable contribution. These datasets, covering diverse modalities and verifiable rewards, support the development and evaluation of advanced multimodal reasoning.

5. **Advancing Agentic MLLMs:** This research contributes to the broader trend of developing agentic MLLMs that can reason, plan, and interact dynamically with their environment. The adaptive decision-making process for tool-use is a key component for building more autonomous and versatile AI agents.

6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of all code, models, and data is a significant contribution to the research community. This enables reproducibility, facilitates further research, and lowers the barrier for others to build upon AdaTooler-V's advancements, potentially accelerating innovation in tool-augmented MLLMs and multimodal reasoning.

In summary, AdaTooler-V provides a compelling demonstration of how intelligent tool management can lead to more capable and efficient multimodal AI. Its methodological innovations and strong empirical results offer a promising foundation for future research in advanced MLLM architectures and reasoning strategies.
