# Y-BotFrame: An Extensible Embodied Agent Framework for Quadruped Robot Assistants

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xidian University developed Y-BotFrame, an extensible embodied agent framework for quadruped robots that integrates multimodal perception and an LLM-based cognitive core. This framework translates natural language instructions into robot actions, supporting intuitive human-robot interaction and a modular, distributed system design for deploying general-purpose intelligent ground assistants.

## Problem

- Existing intelligent systems for highly mobile quadruped robots are specialized (e.g., path planning, locomotion control, task-specific policies), lacking the generality and adaptability for diverse, real-world assistance tasks.
- Current end-to-end vision-language-action (VLA) models for robots are heavily dependent on specific task distributions and extensive training data, limiting their extensibility, interpretability, and stable deployment in open, dynamic environments.
- The lack of intuitive and efficient human-robot interaction paradigms, beyond traditional remote controllers, hinders seamless collaboration and accessibility for general users.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical== and modular framework (**Y-BotFrame**) was designed, decoupling a large language model (LLM) for high-level cognitive planning from low-level robot execution modules.
- An LLM-based task planner translates natural language instructions into sequences of executable actions by selecting and invoking modules from a 'Tool Factory' of defined robot capabilities, ensuring contextual consistency with historical interactions.
- The 'Tool Factory' organizes the quadruped's functionalities into standardized modules for speech interaction, autonomous navigation (including online mapping and path planning), and embodied question answering, each with clear interfaces.

## Results

- Successfully integrated multimodal perception, combining speech, vision (RGB observations), and LiDAR data to enable comprehensive environmental understanding.
- Implemented an LLM-centric cognitive core capable of understanding natural language instructions, contextual reasoning, and translating high-level commands into executable robot action plans.
- Demonstrated functional modules for speech interaction (ASR, TTS, LLM-based dialogue), autonomous navigation (LiDAR/GNSS/IMU fusion, A* path planning, online mapping), and embodied question answering (multi-view RGB scene description with Qwen3-VL-Flash and LLM reasoning).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hierarchical decoupling of the cognitive LLM from encapsulated execution modules enhances the system's interpretability, extensibility, and stability, overcoming limitations of monolithic end-to-end approaches.
> - Standardizing robot capabilities into 'plug-and-play' modules with well-defined interfaces facilitates modular upgrades and iterative development, allowing for easy integration of new functionalities.
> - A distributed system infrastructure, offloading computationally intensive tasks to a remote server, reduces onboard computational load on the robot, improving deployment flexibility and operational efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Y-BotFrame is situated within the intersection of robotics, artificial intelligence, and human-robot interaction, specifically focusing on the capabilities of quadruped robots as intelligent mobile platforms. Quadruped robots are recognized for their ability to navigate diverse and complex terrains, including unstructured environments such as stairs, gravel paths, and grasslands, due to their inherent mobility and flexibility. This makes them suitable candidates for deploying general-purpose embodied agents in real-world scenarios.

Current research in intelligent systems for quadruped robots often concentrates on specialized areas. Some approaches focus on high-level path planning, optimizing routes and trajectories within known or perceived environments. Others delve into locomotion control, refining the robot's gait patterns and stability across varying surfaces. A distinct category involves task-specific policy learning, where robots are trained for particular functions, often requiring large datasets tailored to those tasks. These systems, while effective in their domains, tend to exhibit limitations in generality and adaptability to new or evolving requirements.

More recent advancements have introduced vision-language-action (VLA) models, which attempt to integrate perception, planning, and control in an end-to-end manner. While these models can achieve performance in constrained scenarios, they typically depend on specific task distributions and extensive training data. This reliance can hinder their extensibility, interpretability, and stable deployment in open, dynamic environments where general-purpose functionality and adaptability are critical.

Y-BotFrame addresses these identified gaps by proposing a hierarchical and modular framework. Instead of an end-to-end, data-driven approach, it decouples the cognitive planning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) from the low-level execution modules of the quadruped robot. This design aims to enhance the system's interpretability, extensibility, and stability, allowing for the integration of multimodal perception (speech, vision, LiDAR) and the translation of natural language instructions into executable robot actions. By doing so, Y-BotFrame seeks to advance the development of general-purpose, instruction-driven embodied agents that can operate effectively as intelligent ground assistants, overcoming the constraints of specialized or data-intensive end-to-end systems. This work contributes to the broader goal of making robotic systems more versatile, robust, and capable of natural interaction in unstructured human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of Y-BotFrame stems from the recognition of quadruped robots' potential as highly mobile and flexible ground-based intelligent platforms, coupled with the existing limitations of current robotic control and AI paradigms. While quadruped robots demonstrate strong adaptability to complex terrains, existing intelligent systems often lack the generality, extensibility, and intuitive human-robot interaction capabilities required for widespread real-world deployment as versatile assistants.

Specific motivations identified by the authors include:
- **Leveraging Quadruped Mobility:** Quadruped robots possess superior mobility and traversability in complex, unstructured environments compared to conventional ground robots, making them ideal platforms for mobile deployment of various algorithms.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Systems:** Most intelligent systems for quadruped robots focus on specific aspects like path planning, locomotion control, or task-specific policy learning. While effective in their niches, these systems often lack the comprehensive capabilities for general-purpose assistance.
- **Overcoming Challenges of End-to-End Models:** Recent vision-language-action models, though promising, typically depend on specific task distributions and large-scale training data. This dependence limits their extensibility, interpretability, and stable deployment in open, dynamic environments, where a general-purpose assistant needs to handle a wide array of tasks.
- **Enabling Natural Human-Robot Interaction:** The necessity for a more intuitive and efficient interaction paradigm, moving beyond remote controllers, to facilitate seamless human-robot collaboration. This involves enabling natural language communication and multimodal feedback.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for Y-BotFrame are:
- **Develop an Extensible Embodied Platform:** To create a framework, Y-BotFrame, that transforms a quadruped robot into an intelligent ground assistant capable of performing diverse tasks.
- **Integrate Multimodal Perception:** To equip the robot with advanced perception capabilities by integrating speech, vision (visual feedback), and LiDAR data to enable comprehensive environmental understanding.
- **Implement LLM as a Cognitive Core:** To utilize a large language model (LLM) as the central cognitive engine, responsible for environmental understanding, contextual reasoning, and complex task planning based on high-level instructions.
- **Translate Natural Language to Embodied Actions:** To establish a mechanism that effectively maps user natural-language instructions into a sequence of executable embodied task units that the robot can carry out.
- **Facilitate Natural Interaction:** To support natural human-robot interaction primarily through voice commands and visual feedback, thereby eliminating the need for traditional remote controllers and enhancing usability.
- **Ensure Extensibility and Modularity:** To design a framework that is highly extensible, allowing for the "plug-and-play" integration of new functional modules, supporting modular upgrades, and facilitating iterative development of robot capabilities.
- **Provide a Reference Implementation:** To offer a concrete example and framework for the real-world deployment of general-purpose, instruction-driven embodied agents on quadruped robots.
- **Improve Interpretability and Stability:** To overcome the strong dependence on large-scale task-specific data and improve the interpretability, extensibility, and stability of the system during real-world deployment, differentiating it from end-to-end approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Y-BotFrame is designed as a hierarchical embodied platform, integrating advanced AI capabilities with robotic locomotion and perception. The system's architecture is structured to allow for modularity, extensibility, and distributed computation.

#### 3.1. System Framework Overview

The framework consists of several interconnected components: multimodal human-robot interaction, an LLM-based task planner, executable tool modules, and robot-side execution feedback. The core principle involves a hierarchical decoupling of cognitive planning from low-level robot execution.

- **Lower Level:** This level encapsulates fundamental robot capabilities into distinct, executable modules. These modules are designed with clearly defined input and output interfaces, representing atomic actions or perceptions the robot can perform. Examples include navigation, environmental perception, and embodied question answering.
- **Upper Level:** An LLM-based agent operates at this level, functioning as the primary task planner. It processes user natural-language instructions, considers historical interaction records, and incorporates environmental priors to generate structured task plans.

#### 3.2. LLM Task Planner

The task planner is implemented using a single large language model. Its function is to translate abstract user instructions into a sequence of concrete, executable module invocations.

- **Module Descriptions:** The system maintains a structured repository of callable modules. Each module description includes its specific function, required input parameters, applicable conditions for its use, and any execution constraints.
- **Plan Generation:** Upon receiving a natural-language instruction from the user, the LLM analyzes the intent. It then selects appropriate modules from the repository, binds the necessary interfaces and parameters, and generates a structured task plan.
- **Contextual Consistency:** To ensure coherence over extended interactions, the planner integrates historical plans and execution records. This includes information about previously invoked modules and their feedback, allowing for context-aware decision-making.
- **Execution Monitoring and Replanning:** The low-level execution modules are responsible for real-time control and adherence to safety constraints. These modules continuously report task progress, identify exceptions, and communicate final results back to the task planner. This feedback loop supports ongoing monitoring and enables dynamic replanning in response to changes or errors.

#### 3.3. Tool Factory

The Tool Factory organizes the quadruped robot's executable capabilities into a set of standardized modules with well-defined interfaces. These modules are categorized into speech interaction, autonomous navigation, and embodied question answering.

- **Speech Interaction Module:** This module facilitates natural language communication between the human and the robot.
 - **ASR and TTS:** It employs Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) to convert spoken commands into text and Text-to-Speech (TTS) to synthesize natural language responses from the robot.
 - **Dialogue Understanding:** An LLM is used to interpret the nuances of human dialogue and generate appropriate responses.
 - **Extensibility:** The module is designed to incorporate additional features such as a knowledge base, voiceprint recognition for user identification, and a dialogue memory for long-term interaction context.

- **Autonomous Navigation Module:** This module enables the robot to move safely and intelligently within its environment.
 - **Localization:** It achieves global pose estimation by fusing measurements from LiDAR, Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS), and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU).
 - **Environmental Mapping:** A local obstacle-aware grid map is constructed dynamically from LiDAR point clouds, representing the immediate surroundings.
 - **Path Planning:** The A* algorithm is utilized to generate feasible paths within the perceived environment.
 - **Online Updates:** The map and planned path are continuously updated in real-time based on new perceptual data, allowing the robot to navigate complex environments without requiring pre-built maps.

- **Embodied Question Answering Module:** This module allows the robot to answer questions about its physical surroundings.
 - **Perception:** After reaching a specified location, the robot performs a rotational scan, capturing multi-view RGB observations of the scene.
 - **Scene Description:** Qwen3-VL-Flash, a vision-language model, is used to generate textual descriptions from these visual observations.
 - **Answer Generation:** The generated scene descriptions, along with the user's question and the robot's viewing angles, are then fused and processed by an LLM to generate the final answer.

#### 3.4. System Workflow and Infrastructure

The interaction model primarily revolves around voice dialogue.
- **Request Processing:** When a user request is received, the task planner first queries the available module interfaces to identify suitable functional modules.
- **Module Invocation:** If a match is found, the planner generates corresponding module invocation instructions.
- **Open-Ended Dialogue:** If no suitable execution module can be matched for a given request, the system defaults to parsing the request as an open-ended dialogue task. In this scenario, the LLM generates a natural-language response, which is then delivered to the user via speech synthesis.
- **Distributed Architecture:** The system employs a distributed infrastructure. The task planner invokes functional modules synchronously, while receiving asynchronous feedback over the same local area network.
 - **Deployment Flexibility:** Modules can be deployed either directly on the robot or on a remote server.
 - **Resource Optimization:** Computationally intensive tasks, such as complex model inference and visual understanding (e.g., scene description), are offloaded to a remote server. The robot itself handles lighter tasks, including basic perception, motion control, and the transmission of results. This distributed design strategy aims to reduce onboard computational load, enhance system extensibility, improve deployment flexibility, and optimize operational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper "Y-BotFrame: An Extensible Embodied Agent Framework for Quadruped Robot Assistants" presents a conceptual design and implementation framework for an intelligent quadruped robot assistant. The primary findings are related to the architectural design and the demonstrated integration of various advanced capabilities, rather than empirical performance metrics from extensive experimental evaluations.

The main findings can be summarized as follows:

- **Integrated Multimodal Perception:** The framework successfully integrates diverse multimodal perception capabilities, including speech, vision (via RGB observations), and LiDAR, to provide the robot with a comprehensive understanding of its environment. This integration forms the basis for rich environmental interaction.
- **LLM-Centric Cognitive Core:** The system effectively utilizes a large language model (LLM) as the central cognitive core. This LLM is responsible for complex cognitive functions such as understanding natural-language instructions, performing contextual reasoning based on interaction history, and generating structured task plans for the robot.
- **Mapping Natural Language to Embodied Actions:** Y-BotFrame demonstrates a methodology for translating high-level, natural-language user instructions into low-level, executable embodied task units. This process is facilitated by the LLM task planner interacting with a "Tool Factory" of defined robot capabilities.
- **Support for Natural Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework supports intuitive human-robot interaction primarily through voice commands and visual feedback. This design eliminates the need for a remote controller, aiming to make human-robot collaboration more efficient and accessible.
- **Modular and Extensible Architecture:** The core finding regarding the system's architecture is its high extensibility and modularity. The hierarchical design, with a clear separation between the LLM planner and encapsulated execution modules, facilitates "plug-and-play" integration of new functionalities, supports modular upgrades, and enables iterative development cycles. This modularity addresses the limitations of monolithic or end-to-end systems.
- **Demonstrated Capabilities:** The paper describes the implementation of key functional modules within the Tool Factory:
 - **Speech Interaction:** Enabling natural communication through ASR, TTS, and LLM-based dialogue understanding.
 - **Autonomous Navigation:** Providing localization, path planning using A*, and safe motion, including online map updates from LiDAR and sensor fusion (LiDAR, GNSS, IMU) for operation in complex, unmapped environments.
 - **Embodied Question Answering:** Allowing the robot to answer questions about its surroundings by combining multi-view RGB observations, scene descriptions generated by a vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-Flash), and LLM reasoning.
- **Distributed System Infrastructure:** The proposed distributed design, where heavy computational tasks (e.g., model inference, visual understanding) are offloaded to a remote server while the robot handles lightweight perception and motion control, is presented as a functional approach. This infrastructure aims to reduce onboard computation requirements, thereby enhancing deployment flexibility and operational efficiency.

While the paper details the architectural components and their intended functions, it does not provide quantitative results or experimental validation of the system's performance, robustness, or efficiency in real-world scenarios. The findings primarily consist of the description of the framework's design and its potential capabilities as a reference implementation. A supplementary video is referenced, which would visually illustrate the system's operations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Y-BotFrame represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, particularly in the domain of quadruped robot development. Its primary significance lies in proposing a highly modular and extensible framework that aims to overcome several limitations inherent in existing approaches to robot control and intelligence.

The potential impacts of this work are manifold:

- **Advancement in General-Purpose Embodied Agents:** By integrating multimodal perception, LLM-based cognitive planning, and robust execution capabilities on a highly mobile quadruped platform, Y-BotFrame offers a pathway toward developing truly general-purpose embodied agents. This moves beyond task-specific solutions or end-to-end models that often struggle with versatility and generalization in open environments. It offers a structured approach to building robots that can adapt to a wider array of user instructions and environmental conditions.

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The emphasis on natural language interaction via voice commands and visual feedback has the potential to fundamentally change how humans interact with complex robotic systems. By removing the dependency on specialized remote controllers or programming interfaces, the system could make quadruped robots more accessible, intuitive, and user-friendly for a broader range of applications and users. This improved interaction paradigm fosters more efficient and natural human-robot collaboration.

- **Improved Interpretability and Stability:** The hierarchical decoupling of high-level LLM planning from low-level execution modules enhances the interpretability of the robot's actions. Unlike black-box end-to-end systems, Y-BotFrame's modular design allows for a clearer understanding of how natural language commands are translated into robot behaviors. This transparency, coupled with the system's focus on stability in real-world deployment, contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy robotic assistants.

- **Facilitating Real-World Deployment:** The framework addresses critical challenges for deploying intelligent robots in real-world, unstructured environments. The autonomous navigation capabilities, including online mapping and path planning without pre-built maps, and the robust perception stack enable the robot to operate in dynamic and unknown terrains. Furthermore, the distributed system infrastructure, which offloads heavy computation to a remote server, is a practical design choice that reduces onboard computational burden and extends operational endurance, making real-world deployment more feasible.

- **Accelerating Future Robotics Research:** Y-BotFrame serves as a reference implementation for instruction-driven embodied agents. Its highly extensible, "plug-and-play" modular design encourages future research and development. Researchers can easily integrate new perception modules, planning algorithms, or robot capabilities into the framework, fostering iterative development and innovation in areas such as advanced LLM integration, novel multimodal fusion techniques, and sophisticated robot behaviors.

- **Broadening Application Domains:** The capabilities demonstrated by Y-BotFrame, particularly its ability to traverse complex terrains, understand natural language, and perform embodied question answering, open up new possibilities for quadruped robot applications. These could include mobile assistance in homes or workplaces, environmental monitoring and inspection in challenging industrial or natural settings, search and rescue operations, and collaborative tasks where intelligent, adaptable mobile platforms are required.

In summary, Y-BotFrame represents a step towards creating more intelligent, adaptable, and naturally interactive quadruped robot assistants, with implications for both the theoretical development of embodied AI and the practical deployment of robotic systems in diverse, real-world scenarios.
