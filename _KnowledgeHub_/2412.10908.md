# Do large language vision models understand 3D shapes?

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from the Weizmann Institute assessed the ability of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) to recognize identical 3D shapes under varying orientations, materials, and environments. While LVLMs performed significantly above random chance, their accuracy consistently fell short of human performance, especially when multiple visual properties were simultaneously altered.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) demonstrate impressive visual understanding but sometimes struggle with fundamental perception tasks, such as understanding invariant 3D shapes.
- It is unclear if LVLMs possess an abstract comprehension of 3D shapes that is robust to changes in viewpoint, material, or environmental factors, which is critical for real-world interaction.
- Human-level 3D shape recognition is a foundational skill for autonomous systems and requires identifying objects irrespective of superficial visual variations.

## Method

- A systematic ==multi-choice visual test== was developed using procedurally generated 3D objects, rendered with diverse ==PBR materials== and ==HDRI environments== via Blender.
- Seven distinct test categories were created to isolate and evaluate the impact of varied ==orientations==, ==materials/textures==, ==environments/illumination==, and combinations thereof on 3D shape matching.
- Prominent LVLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3.5-sonnet, LLama3.2-90b) were evaluated using an ==automated API-based query system== and benchmarked against human performance.

## Results

- Top LVLMs achieved high accuracies (e.g., **98-100%**) when only orientation or only texture varied, and maintained robustness to background changes.
- Accuracy dropped to **82-89%** when both orientation and texture varied, and further to **74-83%** when all factors were simultaneously changed.
- LLama3.2-90b consistently performed lower, with accuracies ranging from **4**4%** to 82%** across different test categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LVLMs demonstrate a partial understanding of 3D shapes, performing significantly above random chance (33% accuracy), but consistently below human accuracy (near 100%).
> - The most challenging scenarios for LVLMs involved simultaneous variations in both orientation and material/texture, leading to notable performance drops across models.
> - LVLMs showed robustness to changes in background environments and illumination, effectively separating objects from their surroundings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) such as GPT, Claude, Gemini, and LLama represents a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, aiming to achieve a general visual understanding of the world. These models are designed to analyze complex visual scenes and answer diverse questions, often demonstrating expert-level capabilities across various domains. However, despite their advanced performance, LVLMs have also exhibited limitations in fundamental visual understanding tasks, sometimes performing significantly below human levels in basic perception challenges. Previous research has highlighted instances where LVLMs struggle with geometric information, exhibit biases towards texture over shape, or experience "hallucinations" – generating plausible but factually incorrect outputs based on visual input.

Understanding 3D shapes is a foundational aspect of human perception, essential for interacting with the physical world and a prerequisite for autonomous systems like robots and self-driving cars. While existing object recognition systems can identify objects, the ability to recognize their underlying 3D structure regardless of viewpoint, material, or environmental factors represents a deeper form of visual comprehension. This work specifically investigates whether LVLMs possess this abstract understanding of 3D shapes, moving beyond simple object classification to assess their capacity for invariance to orientation, material, and illumination changes. By focusing on this core perceptual skill, the study contributes to the ongoing effort to characterize the capabilities and limitations of state-of-the-art LVLMs, particularly in relation to their foundational visual intelligence and readiness for real-world applications. It builds upon previous benchmarks and evaluations that explore various aspects of LVLM visual understanding, aiming to pinpoint a specific area of potential weakness or strength.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) truly understand 3D shapes. This overarching question is broken down into specific challenges designed to test the models' ability to recognize and match identical 3D shapes under varying conditions. The central motivation stems from the recognition that a general visual understanding of the world is fundamental for any intelligent system that needs to interact autonomously within a physical environment. For LVLMs, which are positioned as leading approaches for general visual intelligence, discerning their depth of understanding in such a basic perceptual task is crucial.

The study aims to determine if LVLMs can identify objects that share the exact same 3D shape, even when these objects are presented with:
1. **Different orientations (viewpoints):** Can a model recognize an object's intrinsic shape when it is rotated or viewed from a different angle?
2. **Different materials or textures:** Can the model disregard surface appearance (color, texture, material properties) and focus solely on the underlying geometric form?
3. **Different environmental conditions (backgrounds and illumination):** Can the model robustly identify the object's shape without being distracted by changes in lighting or surrounding context?

The authors emphasize that this question differs from standard object recognition tasks, which often focus on identifying a specific instance of an object rather than its invariant 3D structure. For humans, recognizing 3D shapes despite these variations is a fundamental skill learned early in development and is essential for daily interactions. By assessing LVLMs on this task, the research seeks to:
- **Gauge the extent of abstract 3D understanding:** Determine if models have developed an abstract representation of shape that is robust to superficial changes.
- **Identify specific failure modes:** Pinpoint which types of variations (orientation, texture, environment, or combinations thereof) pose the greatest challenge to current LVLMs.
- **Inform future model development:** Provide insights into areas where LVLMs may require direct training or architectural improvements to achieve more robust and human-like visual perception.
- **Benchmark leading foundation models:** Provide a comparative evaluation of prominent LVLMs (GPT, Gemini, Claude, LLama) against a human baseline on this specific task.

Ultimately, the motivation is to understand the foundational visual intelligence of these advanced AI systems, which is critical for their reliable deployment in real-world applications that demand a nuanced comprehension of physical space and objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic methodology centered on the procedural generation of a multi-choice visual test and its application to prominent Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs), with a comparison to human performance.

#### 3.1. Test Design

The core of the evaluation is a four-panel image test. Each test image consists of a 2x2 grid, with panels labeled A, B, C, and D. Panel A displays a reference object. The model is then asked to identify which of the panels (B, C, or D) contains an object with an identical 3D shape to the object in panel A. Of the three choice panels, only one depicts the same 3D object as panel A, but with specific variations (orientation, texture, environment, or a combination), while the other two panels feature entirely different 3D objects. This design forces the model to focus on shape identity despite superficial differences.

#### 3.2. Image Generation

A key aspect of the methodology is the use of Computer-Generated Imagery (CGI) via Blender 4.3. This approach enables the generation of a large, diverse, and controlled dataset, allowing for precise manipulation of object properties.
- **3D Objects:** A large collection of 3D objects was sourced from the Objaverse dataset.
- **Materials/Textures:** 60,000 unique Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials/textures were obtained from the Vastexture dataset. These materials were procedurally applied to the 3D objects.
- **Environments/Illumination:** 600 HDRI panoramic backgrounds from HDRI Haven were used to create diverse illumination and background settings for the scenes.
- **Scene Composition:** For each image, an object was placed at the center, assigned a random orientation, covered with a random PBR texture, and illuminated by a random HDRI background. Object sizes were normalized to maintain a similar radius within the images.

#### 3.3. Test Categories

To isolate and evaluate the impact of different visual properties on 3D shape recognition, the researchers created seven distinct sets of tests:
1. **Varied Orientations, Original Textures:** Objects maintain their original unique materials and colors but are shown with different orientations. (Figure 2)
2. **Varied Orientations, Same Material:** All objects in a given test share the same material/texture and background illumination, but their orientations vary. (Figure 3)
3. **Varied Textures, Same Orientation:** Objects maintain the same orientation and illumination but have different textures/materials applied. (Figure 4)
4. **Varied Orientations and Textures (Same Environment):** Objects are presented with different orientations and textures/materials, while the environment remains constant. (Figure 5)
5. **Varied Backgrounds, Same Orientation and Materials:** Objects have identical orientations and materials but are set against different background environments and illumination. (Figure 6)
6. **Varied Everything:** Objects have different orientations, textures, and environments. (Figure 7)
7. **Same Everything:** Essentially identical images of the same object (used as a baseline for perfect matching).

Tests 2, 4, and 6 are designed to specifically challenge models to rely solely on 3D shape, as texture/color cues are either removed or varied. Tests 1, 3, 5, and 7 allow for the potential use of color, texture, or 2D projection characteristics in recognition.

#### 3.4. Model Testing

- **Automatic Evaluation:** The entire testing process, including image generation and querying models, was automated using Python APIs.
- **Number of Tests:** 1000 unique images were generated and used for each of the seven test categories.
- **Models Evaluated:** GPT-4o, GPT-4 mini, Gemini-1.5-pro, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Claude-3.5-sonnet, and LLama3.2-90b were tested.

#### 3.5. Prompt Selection

An analysis of prompt phrasing was conducted, revealing that short, general prompts yielded better accuracy (up to an 8% difference) than longer, more detailed instructions. The optimal prompt used was: "Which of the panels contains an object with an identical 3D shape to the object in panel A. Your answer must come as a single letter." The researchers inferred that models understood the task from their accuracy being well above random (33%) and their consistent explanations when asked to elaborate. Strategies were developed to handle cases where models provided long answers or refused to respond, primarily involving repeated queries and requests to shorten answers.

#### 3.6. Human Baseline

Human participants were also tested on the same image sets to establish a performance benchmark. Their accuracy was near 100%, with minor deviations attributed to a small percentage (1-2%) of unclear or technically flawed CGI images.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation yielded several key findings regarding the 3D shape understanding capabilities of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs):

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Relative to Humans and Random Chance

All tested LVLMs demonstrated an understanding of 3D shapes significantly above random chance (33% accuracy), indicating they have acquired some abstract shape recognition capability. However, their performance consistently lagged behind human accuracy, which was nearly 100% across all test categories. This suggests that while LVLMs possess some grasp of 3D form, their understanding remains partial compared to human perception.

#### 4.2. Model-Specific Performance

Among the evaluated models:
- **Gemini-1.5-pro, Gemini-2.0-Flash, and GPT-4o** generally exhibited the highest performance, often achieving accuracies in the high 80s and 90s, and even 100% in some simpler test cases.
- **GPT-4 mini** also performed well, though slightly behind its more advanced counterpart.
- **Claude-3.5-sonnet** showed strong performance, comparable to GPT-4 mini in many aspects.
- **LLama3.2-90b** consistently performed lower than the other models, with accuracies ranging from 44% to 82%, indicating a lesser degree of 3D shape understanding in this specific evaluation.

#### 4.3. Impact of Different Visual Variations (Table 1 Analysis)

The systematic variation of properties provided insights into the models' robustness:
- **Varied Orientations, Original Textures (Test 1):** When only orientation changed but original textures and colors were maintained, models like Gemini and GPT-4o achieved 100% accuracy, and Claude-3.5-sonnet reached 98%. This indicates strong rotational invariance when texture cues are present.
- **Varied Orientations, Same Material (Test 2):** When orientation changed and all objects were rendered with the same material (removing texture as a cue), performance remained high for top models (Gemini and GPT-4o at 98%, Claude at 97%). This suggests models can identify shapes despite orientation changes even without unique texture cues, relying purely on shape.
- **Varied Textures, Same Orientation (Test 3):** When orientation was constant but textures varied, models performed very well (Gemini-1.5-pro and Gemini-2.0-Flash at 99%, GPT-4o at 97%, Claude at 93%). This demonstrates strong material/texture invariance when viewpoint is fixed.
- **Combined Orientation and Texture Changes (Test 4 & 6):** This category presented the most significant challenge to the models.
 - **Test 4 (Varied Orientations and Textures, Same Environment):** Accuracy dropped notably across all models. GPT-4o achieved 89%, Gemini models around 87%, Claude 82%, and LLama 51%. This drop highlights the difficulty when both orientation and material cues are simultaneously altered.
 - **Test 6 (Varied Everything: Orientation, Texture, Environment):** This scenario, combining all major variations, resulted in the lowest accuracy for all models. GPT-4o (82%), Gemini-1.5-pro (83%), Gemini-2.0-Flash (77%), Claude (74%), and LLama (44-45%) showed their most constrained performance. This indicates that the combination of multiple transformations significantly impairs their ability to match 3D shapes.
- **Varied Backgrounds (Test 5):** Changes in background and illumination (HDRI) had the least impact on model accuracy. Models generally performed very well (e.g., GPT-4o 96%, Gemini 93%, Claude 88%). This suggests that LVLMs are robust to changes in scene context and lighting, effectively separating the object from its surroundings.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Model Explanations (Appendix)

When models were prompted to explain their choices, two main types of responses were observed:
- **Transformational Descriptions:** Some models attempted to describe rotations and transformations linking the reference object to the matched object. However, these explanations often included inaccuracies, such as referring to resizing or mirroring, even though neither was applied in the test image generation. This suggests models may generate plausible-sounding but not necessarily accurate internal reasoning processes or representations.
- **Feature/Class Descriptions:** Other explanations focused on describing generic 3D features (e.g., "flat," "dish-like") or the object's class. While these indicated an understanding of the objects depicted, the features were often too general to precisely differentiate between similar 3D shapes, especially for objects within the same class.
Despite these limitations in explanations, the high accuracy (above random) and consistent responses when the task was easier suggest that models generally understood the core task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding of Large Vision Language Models' (LVLMs) fundamental visual intelligence by systematically evaluating their capacity for abstract 3D shape recognition. The findings carry several significant implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Clarification of LVLM Capabilities and Limitations

The study demonstrates that current state-of-the-art LVLMs have acquired a measurable, albeit partial, understanding of 3D shapes. Their performance significantly surpasses random chance, indicating that they can abstract some geometric properties from 2D images. However, the consistent and considerable performance gap compared to humans, especially when objects undergo simultaneous changes in orientation and material/texture, highlights a fundamental limitation. This clarifies that while LVLMs possess impressive general visual understanding capabilities, they do not yet achieve human-level robustness in basic perceptual tasks requiring invariant 3D shape recognition.

#### 5.2. Identification of Specific Failure Modes

The detailed breakdown of test categories reveals that LVLMs are largely robust to changes in orientation when texture cues are consistent, and robust to changes in texture when orientation is consistent. They are also highly robust to variations in background and illumination. The critical challenge emerges when both orientation and material/texture are altered simultaneously. This specific failure mode suggests that current models may struggle to decouple shape from surface properties and viewpoint, potentially relying on a combination of 2D features and learned texture patterns rather than a true, robust 3D representation.

#### 5.3. Implications for Downstream Applications

A robust understanding of 3D shapes is essential for numerous real-world applications, including:
- **Robotics:** For tasks like object manipulation, grasping, and navigation in dynamic environments, robots need to recognize objects irrespective of their appearance or viewpoint. The current limitations of LVLMs could hinder their effectiveness in such embodied AI applications.
- **Autonomous Vehicles:** Accurate perception of objects in varying lighting conditions and from different perspectives is critical for safe operation.
- **Computer-Aided Design and Manufacturing:** Systems requiring precise shape matching or analysis could be impacted.
- **General Human-Computer Interaction:** If LVLMs are to serve as general visual assistants, they need a more complete understanding of physical objects.
The observed lack of comprehensive 3D understanding in LVLMs suggests that their performance in these downstream tasks might be negatively impacted, potentially leading to errors or less reliable behavior in complex scenarios.

#### 5.4. Future Directions for Model Improvement

The study implicitly suggests that the abstract 3D shape recognition ability might not fully "emerge" as a byproduct of learning other, broader visual tasks, as some might expect. Therefore, the findings advocate for the necessity of directly training LVLMs on tasks designed to foster robust 3D understanding. The methodology, particularly the procedural generation of a large, diverse, and controlled synthetic dataset, offers a practical pathway for this. Such datasets can be used not only for benchmarking but also for targeted training, allowing models to learn invariance to orientation, texture, and environmental changes more effectively. This could lead to:
- **Development of dedicated training curricula:** Integrating specific 3D shape understanding tasks into LVLM training pipelines.
- **Architectural innovations:** Exploring new model architectures or representation learning techniques that prioritize invariant 3D shape encoding.
- **Improved benchmarks:** The provided benchmark and code serve as a valuable resource for the research community to further evaluate and improve LVLMs on this fundamental aspect of visual intelligence.

In conclusion, this work provides a quantitative assessment of a critical gap in current LVLM capabilities, highlighting the need for continued research and development to bridge the divide between current AI visual perception and human-level understanding of the physical world.
