# Do large language vision models understand 3D shapes?

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from the Weizmann Institute assessed the ability of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) to recognize identical 3D shapes under varying orientations, materials, and environments. While LVLMs performed significantly above random chance, their accuracy consistently fell short of human performance, especially when multiple visual properties were simultaneously altered.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) demonstrate impressive visual understanding but sometimes struggle with fundamental perception tasks, such as understanding invariant 3D shapes.
- It is unclear if LVLMs possess an abstract comprehension of 3D shapes that is robust to changes in viewpoint, material, or environmental factors, which is critical for real-world interaction.
- Human-level 3D shape recognition is a foundational skill for autonomous systems and requires identifying objects irrespective of superficial visual variations.

## Method

- A systematic ==multi-choice visual test== was developed using procedurally generated 3D objects, rendered with diverse ==PBR materials== and ==HDRI environments== via Blender.
- Seven distinct test categories were created to isolate and evaluate the impact of varied ==orientations==, ==materials/textures==, ==environments/illumination==, and combinations thereof on 3D shape matching.
- Prominent LVLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3.5-sonnet, LLama3.2-90b) were evaluated using an ==automated API-based query system== and benchmarked against human performance.

## Results

- Top LVLMs achieved high accuracies (e.g., **98-100%**) when only orientation or only texture varied, and maintained robustness to background changes.
- Accuracy dropped to **82-89%** when both orientation and texture varied, and further to **74-83%** when all factors were simultaneously changed.
- LLama3.2-90b consistently performed lower, with accuracies ranging from **44% to 82%** across different test categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LVLMs demonstrate a partial understanding of 3D shapes, performing significantly above random chance (33% accuracy), but consistently below human accuracy (near 100%).
> - The most challenging scenarios for LVLMs involved simultaneous variations in both orientation and material/texture, leading to notable performance drops across models.
> - LVLMs showed robustness to changes in background environments and illumination, effectively separating objects from their surroundings.
