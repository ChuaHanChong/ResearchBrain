# How Well Does GPT-4o Understand Vision? Evaluating Multimodal Foundation Models on Standard Computer Vision Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EPFL researchers assessed the visual comprehension of Multimodal Foundation Models (MFMs) like GPT-4o across standard computer vision tasks using a novel "prompt chaining" framework. Their evaluation revealed that MFMs, despite being strong generalists, fall short of specialist vision models, exhibiting particular weaknesses in geometric understanding while performing better on semantic tasks; GPT-4o consistently outperformed other non-reasoning MFMs.

## Problem

- Lack of comprehensive quantitative evaluation for Multimodal Foundation Models (MFMs) on detailed, pixel-level computer vision tasks.
- Incompatibility of text-output MFMs with traditional vision tasks requiring dense pixel predictions (e.g., masks, depth maps).
- Proprietary nature of leading MFMs (API-only access) prevents direct architectural modification or fine-tuning for standard vision benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed a ==prompt chaining== framework that translates complex vision tasks into multiple simpler, text-promptable sub-tasks for MFMs.
- Implemented specific prompt chaining strategies for tasks like object detection (==recursive zooming==), semantic segmentation (superpixel classification with multi-scale pyramids), and depth/surface normal prediction (pairwise relative ranking).
- Established control baselines, including specialist vision models, specialists constrained by prompt chaining, and an oracle, for fair and calibrated comparisons.

## Results

- GPT-4o emerged as the top performer among non-reasoning MFMs, leading in **4 out of 6** evaluated tasks, followed by Gemini 2.0 Flash and 1.5 Pro.
- MFMs exhibit a notable performance gap compared to state-of-the-art specialist models across all tasks, even when specialists operate under identical prompt chaining constraints.
- Image generation capabilities of GPT-4o (DALL-E 3) for dense vision outputs show significant limitations, including spatial misalignments, hallucinations, and lack of precise correspondence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MFMs serve as respectable generalists but significantly underperform specialized vision models, even when subjected to the same methodological constraints.
> - MFMs demonstrate stronger capabilities in semantic tasks (e.g., classification, segmentation) compared to geometric tasks (e.g., depth, surface normals).
> - Enhanced reasoning capabilities in newer MFM architectures (e.g., OpenAI's o1, o3, o4-mini) correlate with improved performance on geometric understanding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in multimodal foundation models (MFMs), such as GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, have demonstrated substantial progress across various domains. While their language capabilities have been extensively benchmarked and analyzed, a comprehensive, quantitatively calibrated understanding of their visual understanding abilities remains less explored. Existing vision benchmarks for MFMs primarily focus on text-based tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA) or image classification, which often primarily assess the models' language proficiency rather than their core visual comprehension, and limit direct comparison with vision-only specialist models due to their text-only output formats. Furthermore, these benchmarks typically do not delve into diverse aspects of visual understanding beyond semantics, such as 3D geometry, grouping, or dense pixel-wise predictions.

This research addresses these limitations by establishing a standardized framework to evaluate MFMs on a wider array of established computer vision tasks that necessitate dense pixel-level outputs, including semantic segmentation, object detection, image classification, depth prediction, and surface normal prediction. The work is positioned as a foundational effort to provide a quantitative and holistic assessment of MFM vision capabilities, enabling a direct and calibrated comparison with dedicated vision specialist models. A key challenge in this landscape is the proprietary nature of many leading MFMs, which are accessible only via APIs without weight access, thereby preventing traditional adaptation or fine-tuning for specific vision tasks that do not natively produce text. The proposed "prompt chaining" methodology seeks to overcome this by translating vision tasks into text-promptable, API-compatible formats.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to rigorously benchmark the visual understanding capabilities of contemporary multimodal foundation models (MFMs) on a set of standard computer vision tasks. This involves moving beyond common text-centric evaluations (like VQA) to assess how well these models perform on tasks requiring more detailed and dense visual comprehension.

The detailed objectives and motivations include:

- **Quantitative Evaluation of Diverse Vision Tasks:** To provide a well-calibrated quantitative understanding of MFM performance across a diverse range of vision tasks, specifically:
 - **Semantic Tasks:** Image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and grouping.
 - **Geometric Tasks:** Depth prediction and surface normal prediction.
 This aims to identify strengths and weaknesses across different facets of visual understanding (semantics, 3D structure, object delineation).
- **Benchmarking Against Established Standards:** To evaluate MFMs using well-established datasets (e.g., ImageNet, COCO, Hypersim) and compare their performance against state-of-the-art specialist vision models. This direct comparison is intended to contextualize MFM capabilities within the broader computer vision field.
- **Addressing MFM Output and Access Limitations:** Many leading MFMs are proprietary (API-only access) and primarily designed to output text. This poses a significant challenge for tasks requiring non-textual outputs (e.g., masks, bounding boxes, depth maps). The research is motivated by the need to develop a methodology that translates these vision tasks into a text-promptable format compatible with API-level MFM interaction.
- **Developing a Standardized Benchmarking Framework:** To create a generic and standardized prompt chaining framework that can be applied to any MFM capable of image input and text output. This framework aims to ensure consistent evaluation across different MFMs and enable future benchmarking as new models emerge.
- **Analyzing Prompt Sensitivity and Generalization:** To investigate how MFM performance varies with different prompt formulations and to assess their generalization capabilities to "in-the-wild" images not likely to be part of their training data. This helps in understanding the robustness and practical applicability of these models.
- **Identifying Failure Modes:** To systematically analyze common failure patterns in MFM visual outputs, particularly for complex tasks like dense segmentation or 3D geometry prediction, to inform future model development.

In summary, the motivation stems from a recognized gap in the systematic and comprehensive evaluation of MFM visual intelligence, particularly on tasks requiring granular, non-textual outputs, and the need for a method to achieve such evaluation given the current operational constraints of many advanced MFMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this research is "prompt chaining," designed to enable multimodal foundation models (MFMs) that primarily output text to solve standard computer vision tasks requiring versatile, non-textual outputs (e.g., segmentation masks, bounding boxes, depth maps). This approach addresses the limitations of proprietary MFMs accessible only via API. The central idea is to decompose complex vision tasks into multiple simpler, text-solvable sub-tasks, leveraging the MFM's strength in image classification.

**General Strategy:**
Each task is broken down into a sequence of prompts. The MFM is queried sequentially, with the output of one step informing the input of the next. This allows MFMs to incrementally build up complex visual outputs from a series of text-based decisions.

**Task-Specific Prompt Chaining Implementations:**

- **Image Classification:** The MFM is presented with an image and a list of candidate classes, then prompted to identify the correct class. For efficiency, multiple images are sometimes grouped and classified jointly within a single sequence.
- **Object Detection:** This task is divided into two stages:
 1. **Object Identification:** The MFM identifies all objects present in the input image (a multi-label classification task). Different strategies are explored for this, including querying the entire image or dividing it into regions and querying each independently.
 2. **Bounding Box Regression (Recursive Zooming):** For each identified object, a recursive zooming method is employed. The image is iteratively divided into a grid (e.g., 3x3), and the MFM is queried to identify which grid cells contain the object. Cells without the object are discarded, effectively narrowing the search space. This process is repeated with progressively finer grid resolutions until the object's precise bounding box is located.
- **Semantic Segmentation:** To avoid prohibitively expensive per-pixel querying, the image is first segmented into *superpixels* using an unsupervised clustering algorithm (e.g., SLIC). The MFM's task is then to assign a semantic label to each superpixel. This is done by classifying superpixels in batches. To overcome MFMs' "blurry vision" and struggle with fine-grained details, each superpixel is presented to the MFM within a "multi-scale pyramid" that includes a crop of the superpixel, its surrounding context, and the full image. Consistency across batches is maintained by including predictions from previously processed batches in the prompt chain.
- **Grouping:** Given an image and a query point on an object, the MFM identifies other pixels belonging to the same object or background. Similar to segmentation, superpixels are used. A graph is constructed where superpixels are nodes, and edges connect neighbors. Starting from the superpixel containing the query point, the MFM iteratively expands the group by deciding whether adjacent superpixels belong to the same entity, resembling a Breadth-First Search (BFS) approach.
- **Depth Prediction:** This task involves predicting relative depth. The image is superpixelated. Random pairs of superpixels are sampled, and the MFM is queried to rank them based on their relative distance from the camera (e.g., "superpixel A is closer than superpixel B"). These pairwise rankings are then globalized into a coherent depth map by minimizing an objective function, which incorporates a smoothness regularization term.
- **Surface Normal Prediction:** This follows a similar ranking approach to depth prediction. For each superpixel pair, the MFM determines their relative alignment with three standard camera-relative basis vectors (right, up, forward). These pairwise alignments for each direction are then globalized using an optimization algorithm, resulting in three distinct surface normal maps.

**Control Baselines and Considerations:**

To contextualize MFM performance and account for the artificial constraints introduced by superpixelation and prompt chaining, several control baselines are included:

- **Vision Specialist:** Reports the performance of state-of-the-art specialist vision models for each task.
- **Vision Specialist + Chain:** Applies the same algorithmic constraints (e.g., superpixels, recursive zooming) to a vision specialist model. This provides a calibrated "apples-to-apples" comparison.
- **Oracle + Chain:** Assumes the MFM provides ground-truth answers at each sub-task within the prompt chain, isolating the limitations of the MFM itself from the chosen granularity of the chaining algorithm.
- **Blind Guess:** Prompts the model with a blank image to assess inherent biases and determine if it genuinely utilizes visual content.

**Evaluated Models and Datasets:**
The study evaluates several closed-weight MFMs (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) and open-weight models (Qwen2-VL, Llama 3.2). Additionally, multimodal reasoning models (o1, o3, o4-mini) are assessed on smaller subsets. Evaluations utilize standard datasets: ImageNet and its variants (ImageNet-v2, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-S, ImageNet-C, ImageNet-3DCC) for classification; COCO for object detection, semantic segmentation, and grouping; and Hypersim for depth and surface normal prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of multimodal foundation models (MFMs) on standard computer vision tasks using the prompt chaining framework revealed several key insights into their visual understanding capabilities:

1. **Performance Gap with Specialists:** Across all six evaluated tasks (image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, grouping, depth prediction, and surface normal prediction), MFMs consistently lagged significantly behind state-of-the-art specialist vision models. This gap persisted even when the specialist models were subjected to the same algorithmic constraints imposed by the prompt chaining framework (e.g., using superpixels).
2. **MFMs as Respectable Generalists:** Despite not matching specialist performance, the MFMs demonstrated a respectable capability as generalists. Their performance was generally good across a broad range of tasks, particularly when considering that they are presumably trained primarily on image-text-based tasks (e.g., VQA) rather than dense pixel-level prediction.
3. **Semantic vs. Geometric Understanding:** A notable finding was the differential performance between semantic and geometric tasks. MFMs performed considerably better on semantic tasks (classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, grouping) compared to geometric tasks (depth prediction, surface normal prediction). This suggests a stronger grasp of high-level semantic information than detailed 3D spatial understanding.
4. **Top Performers Among Non-Reasoning Models:** GPT-4o emerged as the best-performing model among the non-reasoning MFMs, securing the top position in 4 out of 6 tasks. Gemini 2.0 Flash and Gemini 1.5 Pro also demonstrated strong performance, often ranking closely behind GPT-4o.
5. **Improvements with Reasoning Models:** Newer multimodal reasoning models, such as o1, o3, and o4-mini, exhibited improved performance, particularly in geometric tasks, where they noticeably outperformed non-reasoning MFMs. On a representative subset, o1 and o3 slightly surpassed GPT-4o in most semantic tasks and showed significant gains in geometric understanding. This indicates a potential pathway for MFMs to enhance their 3D comprehension.
6. **Impact of Prompt Chaining:** The prompt chaining techniques significantly improved MFM performance compared to direct prompting, validating the utility of the proposed methodology for eliciting visual capabilities from text-output models. The study also observed that better-performing models generally exhibited less sensitivity to variations in prompt wording, suggesting a more robust underlying visual understanding.
7. **GPT-4o with Native Image Generation:** An analysis of GPT-4o's native image generation capabilities (where the model outputs an image rather than text) revealed several failure modes. These included hallucinated objects, inaccurate predictions, and spatial misalignments between input and output, suggesting that while promising, this capability is not yet robust enough for direct application to precise vision tasks.
8. **Observed Failure Modes:** Beyond image generation issues, systematic failure patterns were noted:
 - **"Blurry Vision":** MFMs struggled with fine-grained details and localization in semantic segmentation when only superpixel outlines were provided, highlighting a limitation in precise visual acuity.
 - **"Ceiling Bias":** In depth prediction, a consistent bias was observed where models tended to attribute greater depth to pixels located higher in the image.
 - **Coordinate-Frame Ambiguities:** For surface normal prediction, models frequently inverted x- and y-axes (e.g., confusing left-facing with right-facing surfaces). Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting could partially mitigate these errors.
9. **Generalization to Novel Data:** Evaluations on "in-the-wild" images, released after the models' knowledge cutoff dates, demonstrated good generalization performance, suggesting that the observed results are not substantially distorted by potential data contamination with standard datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes a novel and systematic framework for evaluating the visual understanding capabilities of multimodal foundation models (MFMs) beyond traditional text-centric benchmarks. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Standardized Benchmarking Framework:** The proposed prompt chaining methodology offers a standardized, API-compatible approach to quantitatively assess MFMs on established computer vision tasks. This is crucial for comparing proprietary models without direct weight access and for fostering consistent evaluation across the rapidly evolving MFM landscape. It establishes a baseline for future research and model development.
2. **Holistic Understanding of MFM Visual Capabilities:** By evaluating MFMs across a diverse set of semantic and geometric vision tasks, the study provides a more comprehensive picture of their strengths and weaknesses. This helps to move beyond anecdotal observations and offers calibrated quantitative data, revealing that while MFMs are impressive generalists with strong semantic understanding, they currently lag significantly in fine-grained geometric comprehension.
3. **Guidance for MFM Development:** The detailed findings, particularly regarding the performance disparity between semantic and geometric tasks and the observed failure modes (e.g., "blurry vision," "ceiling bias," coordinate-frame ambiguities), can directly inform the training and architectural design of future MFMs. Developers can leverage these insights to focus on improving specific aspects of visual understanding, especially in 3D perception and precise localization.
4. **Identifying Promising Directions:** The improved performance of reasoning models (like o1, o3, o4-mini) in geometric tasks suggests that incorporating advanced reasoning capabilities into MFMs could be a fruitful avenue for closing the gap with specialist models in 3D understanding. This highlights the importance of not just scaling but also architectural innovations in multimodal learning.
5. **Contextualization for Research Community:** The direct comparison with specialist vision models, even under the same constraints introduced by prompt chaining, clearly contextualizes the current state of MFMs within the broader computer vision field. It provides a realistic perspective on their current capabilities and the remaining challenges, preventing overestimation of their visual intelligence solely based on impressive but often qualitative demonstrations.
6. **Open Science Contribution:** The open-sourcing of the evaluation code and prompt chaining tools empowers the broader research community to reproduce the results, benchmark new MFMs, and further investigate various aspects of multimodal visual understanding, thereby accelerating progress in the field.
7. **Addressing Data Contamination Concerns:** The "in-the-wild" evaluation provides reassurance that the conclusions drawn are likely robust against potential data contamination issues with standard datasets, strengthening the validity of the benchmark's findings.

In conclusion, this work provides critical insights and tools for understanding, evaluating, and ultimately advancing the visual intelligence of multimodal foundation models, charting a path for their evolution toward more robust and comprehensive visual comprehension.
