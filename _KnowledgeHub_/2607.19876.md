# KineBench: Benchmarking Embodied World Models via IDM-Free Kinematic Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> KineBench introduces an IDM-free closed-loop framework for evaluating Embodied World Models (EWMs), mitigating attribution ambiguity by replacing learned action extraction with an explicit kinematic grounding pipeline. The framework incorporates robot-centric 3D kinematic metrics and a four-suite benchmark, revealing that current EWMs struggle with complex contact dynamics and exhibit non-uniform scaling behaviors across tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks for Embodied World Models (EWMs) primarily focus on pixel-level fidelity and spatiotemporal consistency, which are insufficient to assess physical plausibility or actionable potential for robotic control.
- Current closed-loop EWM evaluation frameworks rely heavily on Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) for action extraction, leading to attribution ambiguity where execution failures are difficult to disentangle between EWM inaccuracies and IDM generalization limitations.
- A lack of robot-centric metrics means EWMs are evaluated primarily on visual appearance, failing to capture critical 3D kinematic properties like trajectory smoothness or adherence to physical manipulability constraints.

## Method

- **KineBench** implements an ==IDM-free closed-loop evaluation== framework using a modular geometric pipeline to explicitly recover 6D end-effector poses from generated videos, integrating 2D instance segmentation, monocular depth estimation, and CAD-based pose tracking.
- The framework incorporates robot-centric 3D kinematic metrics: ==Spectral Arc Length (SPARC)== to quantify trajectory smoothness in the frequency domain and the ==Maruyama Manipulability Index== to assess kinematic dexterity and singularity avoidance.
- A structured benchmark comprising 20 manipulation tasks across four suites (basic execution, task transfer, visual out-of-distribution generalization, and complexity-conditioned scaling) is designed for comprehensive EWM analysis in the ManiSkill3 simulator.

## Results

- The KineBench pipeline demonstrated substantially lower position error compared to an IDM baseline, with an estimated depth-based translational error of **1.5–3 cm** and rotational error of approximately **ten degrees**, indicating greater robustness under visual perturbations.
- Larger EWMs achieved higher success rates on complex tasks in basic execution, but all models struggled with contact-rich dynamics and experienced significant degradation (e.g., Wan 2.2 dropping from **60.0%** to **30.0%** on "OpenBoxHard") under visual out-of-distribution generalization.
- SPARC effectively diagnosed catastrophic physical hallucinations in zero-shot models, while the Maruyama Manipulability Index revealed that capacity-limited models often pushed the robot towards singularities despite generating visually plausible motions, exposing a lack of embodiment awareness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit, modular kinematic grounding pipeline significantly reduces attribution ambiguity and offers a more robust and diagnosable interface for EWM evaluation compared to learned Inverse Dynamics Models.
> - Classical 3D kinematic metrics like SPARC and Manipulability Index provide specialized diagnostic signals, effectively identifying physical hallucinations, temporal fluency issues, and violations of robot kinematic constraints that pixel-based metrics miss.
> - EWM performance gains from increasing data and compute are non-uniform and task-dependent, with increasing data sometimes proving detrimental for simpler tasks, challenging common scaling assumptions and highlighting the need for tailored strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) aims to equip agents with a predictive understanding of environmental dynamics, a concept central to Embodied World Models (EWMs). Recent advancements in scalable generative architectures and visual foundation models (VFMs), trained on extensive internet-scale data, have demonstrated capabilities in spatiotemporal prediction. This has led to a growing research paradigm that leverages these video generation models as predictive priors for physical intelligence and embodied control. These models serve both as pre-training backbones for Vision-Language-Action models—hypothesized to yield stronger action policies due to richer spatiotemporal priors—and as model-based planners for guiding downstream task execution through predictive rollouts.

However, this shift introduces a critical evaluation challenge: assessing the actionable potential and physical plausibility of these video foundation models, rather than only their visual generation quality. Existing video generation benchmarks primarily focus on pixel-level fidelity and spatiotemporal consistency, which are insufficient for robotic control. More recent embodied benchmarks, such as EWMBench and RBench, employ vision models and Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to assess semantic correctness in an *open-loop* setting, evaluating visual scene consistency and motion correctness. While an improvement, these still do not verify physical feasibility. Physics-grounded open-loop benchmarks attempt to measure plausibility without full simulation by using physics-grounded prompts or proxies like optical flow, but these remain measures of visual plausibility rather than actual physical executability.

A limited number of frameworks have pursued *closed-loop* evaluation, grounding generated sequences into physical simulators to directly assess executability. Noteworthy examples include World-in-World and WoW-World-Eval. These frameworks highlight that visual quality does not consistently correlate with task success, underscoring the necessity of execution-based evaluation. A common methodological bottleneck in these closed-loop approaches is their almost exclusive reliance on Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs)—either end-to-end neural mappings or explicit tracking-based methods—to extract robot actions from generated RGB frames. This IDM-based protocol introduces an attribution ambiguity: when closed-loop execution fails, it is often unclear whether the failure stems from the world model generating physically implausible visuals or from the IDM's poor generalization to novel, out-of-distribution trajectories produced by generative models.

KineBench addresses this specific limitation by proposing an IDM-free closed-loop evaluation framework. It aims to resolve the attribution ambiguity by replacing learned IDM-based action extraction with a modular, explicit geometric grounding pipeline. Furthermore, KineBench integrates classical 3D kinematic metrics, which traditionally characterize movement smoothness and dexterity in robotics, into the evaluation of generative video models, thereby shifting the evaluation paradigm from solely visual appearance to kinematic fidelity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a rigorous and transparent methodology for evaluating the physical consistency and actionable potential of Embodied World Models (EWMs).

The core motivation stems from the limitations identified in current EWM evaluation paradigms:
1. **Inadequate Assessment of Physical Plausibility:** Existing video generation benchmarks predominantly focus on pixel-level fidelity and spatiotemporal consistency. These metrics are insufficient to determine if a generated video sequence represents physically plausible and kinematically feasible robot movements that can be executed in a real or simulated environment. The shift from general video generation to embodied control necessitates a deeper assessment beyond visual appearance.
2. **Attribution Ambiguity in Closed-Loop Evaluation:** While closed-loop evaluation, which involves executing generated actions in a physics simulator, offers a more faithful assessment of physical plausibility, current frameworks heavily rely on Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) for action extraction. IDMs learn complex mappings from 2D pixel space to 3D kinematic space.
 - **Brittleness of IDMs:** IDMs are prone to overfitting to their training data's specific action distributions. When faced with novel or out-of-distribution trajectories generated by EWMs, especially with unseen objects or scenarios, these learned IDMs can fail to reliably extract accurate actions.
 - **Confounding Factors:** This unreliability creates an unavoidable attribution ambiguity. When a closed-loop execution fails, it becomes difficult to ascertain whether the failure originates from inaccuracies within the EWM (e.g., generating physically impossible visual sequences) or from errors in the IDM (e.g., misinterpreting a valid visual sequence into an incorrect action). This ambiguity impedes effective diagnosis and improvement of EWMs.

To address these motivations, KineBench aims to achieve several key objectives:
- **Develop an IDM-Free Closed-Loop Benchmark:** Create a framework that can connect generated videos to physics simulation without relying on learned IDMs for action extraction, thereby reducing attribution ambiguity.
- **Implement Explicit Kinematic Grounding:** Design a modular pipeline that directly extracts 6D end-effector poses from generated video frames using cascaded visual foundation models, ensuring a more stable and diagnosable interface.
- **Introduce Robot-Centric Kinematic Evaluation Metrics:** Incorporate classical 3D kinematic metrics, specifically Spectral Arc Length (SPARC) for trajectory smoothness and the Maruyama Manipulability Index for kinematic feasibility and dexterity, to provide a deeper, robot-centric assessment of generated motions.
- **Design a Structured Benchmark for Comprehensive EWM Analysis:** Create a set of 20 diverse manipulation tasks organized into four progressive evaluation suites (basic execution, task transfer, visual out-of-distribution generalization, and complexity-conditioned scaling) to systematically probe the capabilities and limitations of EWMs.
- **Investigate EWM Scaling Behaviors:** Analyze how increasing data and compute resources affects EWM performance across different task complexities, providing empirical guidance for future model development and data-scaling strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KineBench is an IDM-free closed-loop evaluation framework designed to assess the actionable capabilities and physical consistency of Embodied World Models (EWMs). It comprises three main components: an explicit kinematic grounding pipeline, a robot-centric kinematic evaluation system, and a four-suite benchmark of diverse manipulation tasks.

#### 3.1. Explicit Kinematic Grounding Pipeline

To circumvent the generalization limitations and attribution ambiguity of IDMs, KineBench employs a modular geometric pipeline to recover 6D end-effector motion. This pipeline integrates several visual perception models:

1. **Masking and Depth Recovery:**
 - **2D Instance Segmentation:** A fine-tuned YOLO model is used to accurately segment the end-effector mask within each generated video frame. This leverages the availability of exact CAD models for the gripper, eliminating the need for manual annotation.
 - **Monocular Depth Estimation:** A two-stage fine-tuned MoGeV2 model is applied within the simulation domain to generate high-precision absolute metric depth maps from the segmented frames. The second stage specifically amplifies the loss on fine-grained geometric details for improved fidelity.

2. **CAD-based Pose Matching and Geometric Filtering:**
 - **6D Pose Tracking:** Given the segmentation mask, the known CAD model of the gripper, and the estimated depth map, FoundationPose is utilized to extract the 6D pose (position and orientation) of the gripper. FoundationPose operates on a "Render-and-Compare" paradigm, iteratively refining the pose in SE(3) space by minimizing photometric and geometric residuals between rendered CAD templates and the observed generated frames.
 - **Geometric Regularization:** This CAD-constrained pose-tracking procedure provides a geometric regularization effect. It enforces strict alignment with rigid-body constraints in 3D space, which helps to suppress high-frequency temporal jitter or minor local non-rigid pixel deformations often present in generated videos. Conversely, significant physical hallucinations (e.g., gripper vanishing or large discontinuous spatial motion) fundamentally violate rigid-body mechanics and lead to unstable or infeasible pose estimates. This process acts as a low-pass filter, focusing on physically meaningful motion.

3. **Closed-Loop Execution:** The extracted 6D pose sequence (actions) is then directly injected into the ManiSkill3 physics simulator. The simulator executes these actions, renders the next observation, and initiates the subsequent generation-and-rollout cycle for the EWM, thus closing the loop without an IDM.

#### 3.2. Robot-Centric Kinematic Evaluation

Beyond execution-based assessment, KineBench introduces a robot-centric perspective by characterizing the physical properties of generated motions in 3D space, rather than relying solely on 2D visual metrics. Two classical 3D kinematic metrics are incorporated:

1. **Spectral Arc Length (SPARC):**
 - **Purpose:** To measure trajectory smoothness in the frequency domain. Traditional smoothness metrics based on high-order temporal derivatives of position signals are overly sensitive to high-frequency noise common in video generation, making them unreliable.
 - **Mechanism:** SPARC is defined as the arc length of the normalized Fourier magnitude spectrum of the velocity signal. Smooth actions, adhering to principles of minimum energy, concentrate their energy in low frequencies, resulting in a flat spectrum and a SPARC value approaching zero.
 - **Interpretation:** A lower SPARC value indicates smoother motion. Conversely, hallucinations like stuttering or teleportation introduce high-frequency harmonics, making the spectrum convoluted and decreasing the SPARC value. SPARC thus rigorously scrutinizes temporal consistency.

2. **Maruyama Manipulability Index:**
 - **Purpose:** To characterize kinematic dexterity and feasibility, particularly the robot's ability to avoid singular configurations. Smoothness alone does not guarantee successful manipulation; a robot must operate within its kinematic limits.
 - **Mechanism:** The manipulability index (w = sqrt(det(J(q)J^T(q)))) is based on the determinant of the Jacobian matrix J(q), representing the volume of the end-effector's velocity ellipsoid. The video-generated 6D poses are input into an inverse kinematics (IK) solver. If the generated poses exceed the robot's physical joint constraints, the manipulability index will sharply decline.
 - **Interpretation:** The integral of manipulability over a trajectory directly reflects the EWM's implicit understanding of the robot's kinematic boundaries. Lower values (or sharp declines) indicate poor manipulability or attempts to reach kinematically infeasible configurations.

#### 3.3. Four-Suite Benchmark Design

To systematically evaluate EWM capabilities, 20 diverse manipulation tasks within the ManiSkill3 physics simulator are designed and organized into four evaluation suites:

1. **Suite 0: Basic Execution:** Tasks are strictly identically distributed (I.I.D.) across training and evaluation sets, covering all tasks, object instances, and backgrounds. This suite establishes a fundamental kinematic execution baseline to quantify a model's ability to reliably reproduce demonstrated dynamics without domain shift.

2. **Suite 1: Task Transfer:** This suite assesses zero-shot transfer to conceptually related but distinct tasks. For example, a model trained on "CloseBox" might be evaluated on "OpenBoxEasy-v2" (novel orientations) or "CloseLaptopHard." This probes the model's capacity for instruction understanding, spatial extrapolation, and physical causality reasoning.

3. **Suite 2: Visual OOD Generalization:** This suite separates visual representation robustness from physical reasoning. Training data uses 50% of randomized asset libraries (object instances, background textures), while evaluation is performed on the remaining disjoint 50%. This strictly out-of-distribution (OOD) visual setting tests kinematic generation degradation under novel textures, geometries, and lighting.

4. **Suite 3: Complexity Scaling:** This suite uses an I.I.D. task distribution (similar to Suite 0) but systematically varies training data volume and compute budget (via training duration). It examines how intrinsic task complexity (e.g., degrees of freedom, contact dynamics) influences performance gains from increased data and compute, revealing complexity-conditioned empirical scaling trends.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Evaluation Environment and Tasks:** ManiSkill3 physics simulator with 20 diverse manipulation tasks, including basic reaching, rigid body grasping (with/without obstacles), multi-axis articulated object manipulations (hinges, sliders), and long-horizon compositional tasks. Tasks incorporate randomized object instances and background textures with varying difficulty levels.
- **Hardware and Model Pool:** Evaluation conducted on four NVIDIA A100 GPUs, benchmarking state-of-the-art video foundation models including Wan 2.1 (1.3B), Wan 2.2 (5B), CogVideoX (2B), and their LoRA-finetuned variants (CogVideoX 5B-LoRA, Wan 2.2 5B-LoRA), as well as API models (Wan 2.6, Hailuo-V2).
- **KineBench Pipeline Details:** Utilizes YOLOv11 for 2D mask extraction, a two-stage fine-tuned MoGeV2 for high-fidelity metric depth estimation, and FoundationPose for zero-shot 6D pose tracking. This decoupled approach aims for efficient and stable closed-loop evaluation.
- **Generalization and Robustness Analysis of KineBench:** The robustness of the KineBench pipeline itself is analyzed by comparing it against an IDM baseline (Dino3DFlowIDM) on unseen simulator-rendered trajectories and under controlled visual perturbations (gripper deformation, occlusion, contrast, blur, noise). Ablation studies are also conducted on depth estimation quality to isolate error sources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using KineBench yielded several findings regarding the robustness of the evaluation pipeline, the performance of EWMs across different generalization settings, the utility of robot-centric kinematic metrics, and complexity-conditioned scaling behaviors.

#### 4.1. Generalization and Robustness of the KineBench Pipeline

- **Comparison with IDMs:** The KineBench pipeline demonstrated substantially lower position error compared to the Dino3DFlowIDM baseline across seven unseen test tasks and under controlled visual perturbations (e.g., gripper deformation, 20% and 50% occlusion, low contrast, motion blur, noise). Although rotation mean error was occasionally slightly higher, its distribution was more concentrated with fewer long-tail failures, indicating greater robustness.
- **Ablation of Depth Estimation:** When comparing pose extraction using simulator ground-truth depth versus MoGeV2-estimated depth, the translational error increased from approximately centimeter-level to 1.5–3 cm. This suggests that the depth estimation stage is a primary source of translational error within the KineBench pipeline. However, the CAD-constrained pose tracker (FoundationPose) remained comparatively stable. The rotational error for the MoGeV2-based pipeline was approximately ten degrees.
- **Interpretability:** The modular nature of KineBench's pipeline allows for localized and measurable error sources (e.g., in depth estimation), which is not possible with end-to-end IDMs, whose failures are difficult to attribute to specific components.

#### 4.2. Main Results: Physical Execution and Generalization

- **Base Physical Execution (Suite 0):** Larger models generally achieved higher success rates on complex tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation or long-horizon sequencing. However, performance significantly dropped on contact-rich tasks (e.g., StackCube, PickFruits), indicating that current video models struggle with modeling complex friction and collision dynamics, despite capturing kinematic priors. Full-parameter fine-tuning generally outperformed LoRA variants in this suite, suggesting benefits for acquiring task-specific execution dynamics.
- **Task and Visual Generalization (Suites 1 & 2):**
 - In Suite 2 (Visual OOD Generalization), models showed robustness on simple tasks like "CloseBox" but experienced substantial degradation on tasks requiring precise affordance localization under visual shifts (e.g., Wan 2.2 dropping from 60.0% to 30.0% on "OpenBoxHard" with unseen assets). This suggests that models may rely on appearance-specific features rather than learning transferable 3D geometric affordances.
 - Cross-task transfer in Suite 1 remained challenging for all evaluated models. Notably, LoRA-tuned models sometimes outperformed their fully fine-tuned counterparts in transfer tasks, which might indicate a trade-off where LoRA preserves more general pre-trained visual and motion priors beneficial for transfer, while full fine-tuning leads to over-specialization.

#### 4.3. Robot-Centric Kinematic Evaluation

- **Spectral Arc Length (SPARC):**
 - For zero-shot and under-optimized models, SPARC strongly correlated with success rates, effectively diagnosing catastrophic physical hallucinations stemming from poor motion fluency.
 - For fully fine-tuned models, SPARC scores tended to saturate, indicating they generate kinematically smooth trajectories. In these cases, execution failures primarily arose from semantic misalignments rather than local jitter. SPARC proved to be a specialized metric for evaluating fine-grained temporal fluency.
- **Maruyama Manipulability Index:**
 - Fully fine-tuned models effectively internalized the robot's 7-DoF structural boundaries, consistently maintaining low manipulability costs.
 - Zero-shot or capacity-limited LoRA models, despite generating visually plausible motions, frequently pushed the robot towards singularities or kinematically unreachable points due to inherent stochasticity, leading to execution failures.
 - This index served as a crucial metric for assessing embodiment awareness, exposing the risks of unconstrained generative randomness in EWMs by indicating violations of spatial constraints.

These two metrics collectively established a comprehensive 3D evaluation paradigm, with SPARC scrutinizing dynamic motion details and Manipulability bounding spatial positioning and embodiment constraints, offering insights into the causes of success or failure in the physical world.

#### 4.4. Complexity-Conditioned Scaling Behaviors (Suite 3)

- Experiments varying training data volume (expert trajectories from 10 to 100) and optimization steps (1,500 to 15,000) for the Wan 2.1 1.3B architecture revealed task-dependent and nonlinear scaling trends.
- **Simple Tasks (e.g., OpenBoxEasy):** Increasing homogeneous training trajectories from 10 to 100 actually reduced the success rate (from 100% to 83.33%), and additional optimization steps provided limited or negative gains. This suggests potential overspecialization when tasks are simple and distribution is narrow.
- **Complex Tasks (e.g., StoreCube-v2):** Increasing the optimization budget significantly improved success rates (from 13.34% to 69.52%). This indicates that tasks with more diverse motion and interaction requirements benefit from longer training.
- Overall, the marginal benefits of increasing data and compute were found to depend on task complexity and trajectory diversity, implying that EWM scaling is not uniformly monotonic.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KineBench represents a methodological contribution to the evaluation of Embodied World Models (EWMs) and has several significant implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics.

Firstly, its core contribution is **reducing the attribution ambiguity** inherent in existing closed-loop EWM evaluation frameworks. By replacing brittle, learned Inverse Dynamics Models (IDMs) with an explicit, modular kinematic grounding pipeline, KineBench provides a more stable, robust, and diagnosable interface for extracting actionable poses from generated videos. This transparency allows researchers to more clearly distinguish between errors originating from the EWM's predictive capabilities and those stemming from the action extraction process, which is crucial for targeted model improvement.

Secondly, KineBench introduces a **novel, robot-centric evaluation paradigm** that moves beyond traditional 2D pixel-based metrics. By directly analyzing 3D end-effector poses, it assesses physical plausibility and kinematic feasibility rather than merely visual appearance. The incorporation of classical robotics metrics like Spectral Arc Length (SPARC) and the Maruyama Manipulability Index provides specialized diagnostic signals. SPARC offers insights into the temporal fluency and smoothness of generated motions, helping to identify catastrophic physical hallucinations. The Manipulability Index evaluates the model's implicit understanding of robot kinematic constraints and embodiment awareness, identifying trajectories that would lead to singularities or unfeasible configurations. These metrics offer a deeper understanding of *why* an EWM succeeds or fails in a physical context.

Thirdly, the **structured four-suite benchmark design** (basic execution, task transfer, visual OOD generalization, and complexity scaling) provides a comprehensive framework for systematically probing different facets of EWM capabilities. This allows for a granular analysis of how EWMs perform under varied conditions, including their ability to generalize to novel tasks, handle visual appearance shifts, and scale with increasing data and compute.

Finally, the **empirical observations on complexity-conditioned nonlinear scaling behaviors** offer practical guidance for future EWM development. The finding that increasing data and compute does not yield uniform or monotonic benefits across all task complexities, and can even be detrimental for simpler tasks, challenges common scaling assumptions from other domains (e.g., language models). This suggests that optimal data and compute allocation strategies for EWMs may need to be highly tailored to task characteristics and desired generalization properties.

In summary, KineBench provides a transparent and robust evaluation framework that can accelerate the development of physically grounded and actionable world models. By shifting the focus to executable kinematics and offering detailed diagnostic tools, it enables researchers to build EWMs that are not only visually compelling but also truly capable of supporting intelligent embodied agents in complex physical environments.
