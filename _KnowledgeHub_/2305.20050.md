# Let's Verify Step by Step

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at OpenAI demonstrated that training language models with process supervision, which evaluates and rewards each step of a model's reasoning, leads to more reliable and capable models than traditional outcome supervision. Their method achieved 78.2% accuracy on the challenging MATH dataset, outperforming outcome-supervised models and generalizing well to new problems, while also making the process more data-efficient through active learning.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often produce plausible-sounding but incorrect reasoning, known as hallucination, particularly in tasks requiring precise, step-by-step reasoning like complex mathematics.
- Traditional outcome supervision, which only rewards the final correct answer, can lead models to develop faulty reasoning processes or shortcuts without ensuring the validity of intermediate steps.
- It was unclear whether evaluating each reasoning step (process supervision) or only the final answer (outcome supervision) would yield more reliable and capable language models for complex reasoning.

## Method

- The OpenAI research team introduced and advocated for ==process supervision==, training ==process-supervised reward models (PRMs)== to evaluate each step of a solution, in contrast to ==outcome-supervised reward models (ORMs)== that only check the final answer.
- They used the challenging MATH dataset as a testbed, applying a ==best-of-N== evaluation approach where reward models selected the highest-ranked solution from N generated candidates.
- To address the cost of granular feedback, they implemented ==active learning== techniques that prioritized human labeling of the most informative examples, particularly ==convincing wrong-answer solutions==, and released the **PRM800K** dataset for further research.

## Results

- Process-supervised reward models (PRMs) achieved **78.2%** accuracy on the challenging MATH test set using a best-of-1800 evaluation, outperforming outcome-supervised reward models (ORMs) which reached **72.4%** accuracy.
- Active learning strategies for process supervision demonstrated a **2.6x** improvement in data efficiency, allowing for more effective training with reduced human labeling effort.
- PRMs consistently outperformed ORMs across all problem difficulty levels and maintained an advantage (**69.6%** vs **65.9%**) on out-of-distribution STEM questions, demonstrating robust generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process supervision, which evaluates and rewards each step of a model's reasoning, consistently leads to more reliable and capable language models for complex reasoning tasks compared to outcome supervision.
> - Active learning techniques can significantly improve the data efficiency of process supervision, enabling more effective reward models with fewer human-labeled examples.
> - Process supervision enhances generalization capabilities, showing improved performance across varying problem difficulties and out-of-distribution questions, and aligns with AI safety goals by fostering transparent and correct reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

As large language models (LLMs) continue to advance in capabilities, ensuring they can reason reliably remains a significant challenge. Models often produce plausible-sounding but incorrect reasoning, a phenomenon commonly referred to as "hallucination." This issue becomes particularly concerning when these models are deployed in contexts requiring precise, step-by-step reasoning, such as solving complex mathematical problems.

The research paper "Let's Verify Step by Step" by a team of researchers at OpenAI (including Hunter Lightman, Vineet Kosaraju, Yura Burda, and Karl Cobbe, among others) presents a groundbreaking approach to improving the reliability of language models' reasoning processes. The researchers introduce and advocate for process supervision—a method that evaluates and rewards the correctness of each step in a model's reasoning—as opposed to the more common outcome supervision, which only judges the final answer.

### 2. Understanding Process vs Outcome Supervision

When training language models to perform complex reasoning tasks, there are two primary approaches to providing feedback:

1. **Outcome Supervision**: The model is rewarded based solely on whether its final answer is correct. This approach is simpler but can lead models to develop shortcuts or faulty reasoning processes that happen to yield correct answers.

2. **Process Supervision**: The model is evaluated on each step of its reasoning process. This approach requires more granular feedback but potentially leads to more reliable and transparent reasoning.

The core question this research addresses is: Which approach produces more reliable and capable language models for complex reasoning tasks? To investigate this, the authors train and compare two types of reward models:

- **Outcome-supervised Reward Models (ORMs)**: These models are trained to predict whether a solution reaches the correct final answer.
- **Process-supervised Reward Models (PRMs)**: These models are trained to evaluate each reasoning step independently.

### 3. Research Methodology

The researchers focused on mathematical reasoning as a test case for their comparison, using the challenging MATH dataset, which contains competition-level mathematics problems requiring sophisticated multi-step reasoning. Their methodology involved several key components:

1. **Creating Reward Models**: They trained both process and outcome-supervised reward models using GPT-4 as a base model.

2. **Best-of-N Evaluation**: The trained reward models were used to rank N different potential solutions to each problem, selecting the highest-ranked solution. This "best-of-N" approach allows the model to generate multiple reasoning paths and select the most promising one.

3. **Human Feedback Collection**: Process supervision requires human feedback on each reasoning step. The researchers employed skilled data labelers to assess the correctness of reasoning steps, creating labeled data for training.

4. **Active Learning**: To improve data efficiency, they explored active learning techniques that prioritize labeling the most informative examples—particularly focusing on "convincing wrong-answer solutions" that might appear correct but contain subtle reasoning errors.

### 4. Key Findings

The results of this research were striking and conclusive:

As shown in Figure 2, process-supervised reward models significantly outperformed outcome-supervised models across all test conditions. The best PRM achieved 78.2% accuracy on the MATH test set using the best-of-1800 evaluation method, compared to 72.4% for the best ORM—a substantial improvement.

The performance gap widened as the number of candidate solutions increased, indicating that PRMs are more effective at identifying high-quality reasoning among many options. This finding suggests that process supervision leads to reward models that can better distinguish subtle differences in reasoning quality.

### 5. Active Learning for Process Supervision

A potential limitation of process supervision is the increased cost of obtaining human feedback for each reasoning step. To address this, the researchers investigated active learning techniques to improve data efficiency:

Their active learning approach significantly improved data efficiency, achieving a 2.6x improvement in performance compared to uniform sampling of solutions for labeling. By focusing human feedback on the most informative examples, particularly those with convincing but incorrect reasoning, they were able to train more effective reward models with fewer labeled examples.

The left side of Figure 3 demonstrates how PRM with active learning (red line) outperforms other approaches, especially when the number of labeled examples is limited.

### 6. Generalization Capabilities

An important question is whether the benefits of process supervision extend beyond the training distribution. The researchers tested this by examining performance across problem difficulty levels:

As shown in Figure 4, PRMs consistently outperformed ORMs across all difficulty levels, with the advantage becoming more pronounced for harder problems (quintiles 3, 4, and 5). This suggests that process supervision is particularly valuable for complex reasoning tasks that require many correct steps to arrive at a solution.

They also tested generalization on out-of-distribution STEM questions that weren't part of the training data, finding that PRMs maintained their advantage with 69.6% accuracy compared to ORM's 65.9%.

### 7. The PRM800K Dataset

To facilitate further research in this area, the authors released the PRM800K dataset—a collection of over 800,000 step-by-step labeled reasoning traces. This dataset includes human judgments on the correctness of individual reasoning steps, providing a valuable resource for training and evaluating process-supervised models.

Examples from the dataset show the detailed nature of the step-by-step evaluations.

### 8. Implications for AI Safety

The research has significant implications for AI safety and alignment. Process supervision represents a more transparent approach to training AI systems, as it ensures that models not only reach correct conclusions but do so through valid reasoning processes.

This addresses a key concern in AI alignment: models might learn to produce desired outputs through incorrect or unintended reasoning, potentially leading to failures when deployed in new contexts. By rewarding correct reasoning processes, we can better ensure that models are learning the intended reasoning patterns rather than shortcuts or heuristics that happen to work on the training data.

Importantly, the authors found that process supervision actually improves model performance rather than imposing an "alignment tax" (a performance penalty in exchange for better alignment with human values). This finding challenges the notion that making AI systems more aligned necessarily comes at the cost of capability.

### 9. Conclusion

"Let's Verify Step by Step" makes a compelling case for process supervision as a superior approach to training language models for complex reasoning tasks. The research demonstrates that evaluating and rewarding each step in a model's reasoning process leads to more reliable and capable models compared to only judging the final answer.

Key takeaways from this research include:

1. Process-supervised reward models consistently outperform outcome-supervised models on complex mathematical reasoning tasks.

2. Active learning techniques can significantly improve the data efficiency of process supervision, making it more practical to implement.

3. The benefits of process supervision extend to out-of-distribution problems, suggesting robust generalization capabilities.

4. Process supervision aligns with AI safety goals by ensuring models learn correct reasoning processes rather than shortcuts.

5. The release of the PRM800K dataset enables further research in this promising direction.

This work represents an important advance in training more reliable AI systems that can perform complex reasoning tasks. By promoting transparency in how models arrive at their conclusions, process supervision not only improves performance but also enhances our ability to trust and understand AI systems—a critical requirement as these systems take on increasingly important roles in our society.
