# Visually-grounded Humanoid Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The Peking University and Carnegie Mellon University-led research team developed a framework for visually-grounded humanoid agents, enabling digital humans to autonomously perceive and act in novel 3D environments using only visual observations and specified goals. The system integrates an occlusion-aware semantic 3D scene reconstruction pipeline with a two-layer agent model for context-aware planning and controllable full-body motion generation, achieving approximately 30% higher navigation success rates than prior methods on a new benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing digital human systems primarily feature passive animation or rely on scripted controls, limiting their adaptability and generalization in novel environments.
- Current VLM/LLM-based agents are often disembodied, lacking visual grounding, real-world perception-action coupling, and context-aware adaptability for complex 3D environments.
- There is a scarcity of photorealistic, semantically structured, and interactive 3D environments, especially large-scale outdoor scenes, suitable for benchmarking embodied human interaction.

## Method

- The framework uses a ==World Layer== for ==occlusion-aware semantic 3D scene reconstruction== (leveraging ==3D Gaussian Splatting==, ==SAM==, and VLM for open-vocabulary annotation).
- The ==Agent Layer== implements a two-level approach: a ==VLM-based context-aware high-level planner== (utilizing ==spatial-aware visual prompting==) and a ==motion diffusion model== low-level controller.
- A comprehensive benchmark is introduced for evaluating humanoid-scene interaction, including diverse scenes, avatars, and single/multi-goal visual navigation tasks.

## Results

- The Agent Layer achieved an average Success Rate approximately **30%** higher than state-of-the-art Vision-and-Language Navigation baselines on SmallCity tasks, with comparable or lower Collision Rates.
- The method significantly outperformed baselines in multi-goal navigation and demonstrated robust generalization across diverse outdoor (XGRIDS) and indoor (SAGE-3D) environments.
- The World Layer's occlusion-aware semantic reconstruction significantly outperformed other 3DGS-based feature-lifting methods in mean IoU and mean Accuracy for large-scale outdoor scene segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coupled two-layer architecture effectively bridges detailed semantic 3D environment reconstruction with first-person visual perception and goal-driven embodied actions for digital human agents.
> - Occlusion-aware masks and strategic view selection are crucial for robust and accurate instance-level semantic segmentation and open-vocabulary annotation in challenging large-scale outdoor scenes.
> - Spatial-aware visual prompting and an iterative reasoning mechanism significantly enhance VLM-based planning, enabling long-horizon goal completion, collision avoidance, and coherence in dynamic and occluded environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation and animation of digital humans have been subjects of study for decades, supporting applications in augmented/virtual reality, telepresence, and robotic training. However, the majority of existing systems are characterized by passive animation, often relying on pre-defined states, scripted controls, or motions derived from third-person data (e.g., motion capture). This passive nature restricts their ability to adapt to novel environments and generalize across diverse scenarios, posing a fundamental limitation for scaling their utility.

Concurrently, advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have facilitated high-level reasoning and dialogue for digital agents. Nevertheless, these systems frequently operate in a disembodied manner, constrained by symbolic reasoning or scripted scenarios. They often lack visual grounding, real-world perception-action coupling, and context-aware adaptability, which are critical for effective interaction in complex 3D environments. While some efforts integrate VLMs with visual inputs for motion planning, their effectiveness in intricate environments is limited. Consequently, a gap exists in the research landscape for a unified framework that spans semantic reasoning to embodied digital humans, integrating perception, decision, and action into a continuous cycle for autonomous adaptation within complex, real-world 3D environments.

Furthermore, the development of robust interactive environments for embodied agents faces challenges. Existing large-scale 3D reconstructions, often based on techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), frequently suffer from occlusions and incomplete semantic information. Datasets for autonomous driving, while providing 3D annotations, are typically vehicle-centric and do not adequately support diverse human-scene interactions. The creation of photorealistic, semantically structured, and compositional environments with accurate physical meshes, particularly for large-scale outdoor scenes, remains an area requiring further development to facilitate embodied human interaction.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel paradigm for visually-grounded humanoid agents, aiming to enable digital humans to actively behave using only visual observations and specified goals in novel scenes. It specifically tackles the challenges of passive animation, disembodied reasoning, and the absence of semantically rich, interactive 3D environments for humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop digital humans that can actively behave using solely visual observations and specified goals within novel 3D environments. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and aims to address key challenges:

1. **Transition from Passive to Active Digital Humans**: The primary motivation is to overcome the limitations of passively animated digital human systems. The goal is to create agents that perceive and act from a first-person perspective, employ autonomous decision-making, and interact with realistic environments in a goal-directed manner, similar to how real humans utilize visual observations and short-term goals.
2. **Scalable Population of 3D Environments**: The project seeks to enable the large-scale instantiation of digital humans in any 3D environment, allowing them to exhibit spontaneous, natural, and goal-directed behaviors. This addresses the scalability issues inherent in systems relying on privileged state or scripted control.
3. **Embodied AI for Humanoids**: The work aims to extend the principles of embodied AI, which have driven progress in robotics, to the digital human domain. This involves replicating human capabilities at multiple levels: visual perception, reasoning, and movement within realistic 3D worlds.
4. **Grounding VLMs in Real-World Interaction**: A motivation is to integrate the high-level reasoning capabilities of VLMs with visual grounding and real-world perception-action coupling. This addresses the challenge of current VLM-based systems often being disembodied and lacking continuous interaction with the physical world.
5. **Creation of Semantically Rich, Interactive 3D Environments**: The research is motivated by the critical absence of suitable environments for benchmarking embodied human interaction, especially for large-scale outdoor scenes. The objective is to reconstruct environments that are photorealistic, semantically structured, compositional, and equipped with physical meshes for collision detection.
6. **Addressing Challenges in Semantic Scene Reconstruction**: Specific technical challenges include severe occlusion and sparse supervisory signals in large-scale outdoor scenes, which hinder accurate semantic feature propagation and learning for open-world landmarks. The project aims to develop an occlusion-aware semantic reconstruction pipeline to overcome these issues.
7. **Developing Robust Perception-Planning-Action Loops**: For humanoid agents, the objective is to design a unified loop that couples first-person perception, planning, and action. This includes mitigating VLM limitations in visual grounding and memory for decision-making and translating abstract goals into realistic full-body actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, "Visually-grounded Humanoid Agents," operates on a coupled two-layer paradigm: the World Layer and the Agent Layer.

**The World Layer: Environment and Avatar Generation**
The World Layer establishes the interactive substrate by reconstructing semantically rich 3D environments and populating them with animatable human avatars.

1. **Occlusion-Aware Semantic Scene Reconstruction**:
 - **Foundation**: The framework builds upon 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representations (specifically CityGaussian) to reconstruct photorealistic city scenes from real-world videos. This process also involves extracting collision meshes from the reconstructed geometry to facilitate physical interaction.
 - **Semantic Scene Feature Learning**: To augment 3DGS with instance-level semantics, the method leverages 2D instance masks obtained from Segment Anything Model (SAM). These 2D masks guide the learning of feature vectors for each 3D Gaussian. Contrastive learning is employed, consisting of an intra-mask smoothing loss (aligning pixel-wise features within a mask) and an inter-mask contrastive loss (separating features of different instances).
 - **Coarse-to-fine Codebook Discretization**: To ensure consistent instance features across all Gaussians belonging to the same object, a two-level codebook discretization strategy is used. A coarse level clusters Gaussians based on their learned features and 3D coordinates, followed by a fine-level quantization within each coarse cluster using only instance features. A quantization loss enforces feature map consistency between continuous and quantized features.
 - **Occlusion-Aware Masks and View Selection**: To address challenges posed by severe occlusions and sparse supervision in large outdoor scenes:
 - *Occlusion-Aware Cluster Masks*: For a target cluster, other scene objects are rendered as depth-only occluders. This generates a 2D binary mask isolating the visible regions of the target cluster, and the quantization loss is applied only within this mask, preventing feature contamination.
 - *Strategic View Selection*: An occlusion-aware visibility score is pre-computed for each cluster-view pair. Training for a coarse cluster is then focused only on views where its visible pixel count exceeds a specified threshold, prioritizing high-quality views.
 - **Automated Open-Vocabulary Annotation**: After 3D semantic segmentation, each instance is automatically annotated using a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) via a context-aware visual prompting mechanism. This highlights the target object's contour while dimming the background to preserve spatial context, enabling the VLM to generate detailed, discriminative descriptions.

2. **Avatar Reconstruction and Agent Placement**:
 - The framework reconstructs animatable human avatars from real-world human videos (e.g., PeopleSnapshot, GaussianAvatar). Each avatar is modeled as a collection of Gaussian primitives, represented in a canonical space and animated using SMPL parameters via Linear Blend Skinning.
 - These reconstructed avatars are randomly placed into the semantically enriched 3D scenes to create diverse human-scene interaction scenarios, with their behaviors subsequently controlled by the Agent Layer.

**The Agent Layer: Autonomous Behavior**
The Agent Layer equips the avatars with first-person visual perception and goal-driven planning capabilities, enabling autonomous and adaptive action. This layer is designed as a two-level framework, mirroring cognitive paradigms of high-level planning ("slow thinking") and low-level execution ("fast execution").

1. **Context-Aware Action Planning (High-Level Planner)**:
 - **VLM Utilization**: A VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is employed in a training-free, zero-shot manner for high-level planning. Planning is framed as a selection problem over discrete human-centric action primitives, each comprising a motion type and a canonical direction vector.
 - **Spatial-Aware Visual Prompting**: To ground VLM planning in first-person perception and overcome limitations in spatial reasoning:
 - *Physically Viable Action Proposals*: The system segments traversable ground using SAM, samples 2D candidate paths on it, projects them to 3D points using depth maps, and prunes paths shorter than a safety threshold.
 - *Open-Vocabulary Localization*: The VLM is given visual input where the target object is highlighted with a bounding box (e.g., green 'GOAL'), clarifying agent-goal relations.
 - *Social-Aware Extension*: For social navigation, dynamic humans are detected and highlighted with distinct bounding boxes (e.g., red 'HUMAN'), injecting their spatial information into the visual prompt.
 - **Iterative Reasoning**: Inspired by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, an iterative reasoning mechanism provides foresight for long-horizon goals. At each timestep, the VLM's text prompt is dynamically updated with the high-level language goal, a memory buffer of previous plans and actions, and a query to update its plan based on current observations. This enables multi-step planning, obstacle avoidance, and handling target occlusion, including a "stop and wait" primitive for social interaction.

2. **Controllable Human Motion Generation (Low-Level Controller)**:
 - **Waypoint Generation**: The high-level planner's output (action primitive, direction) is translated into a sequence of waypoints by linearly interpolating between the agent's current and target 3D positions.
 - **Motion Diffusion Model**: These waypoints and a motion type text prompt (e.g., "walk") condition a motion diffusion model (MDM-SMPL) to synthesize a plausible full-body motion sequence, producing SMPL pose parameters.
 - **Training-Free Spatial Guidance**: During inference, a guidance mechanism ensures adherence to waypoints, text consistency, and temporal smoothness, without requiring additional training. This involves applying gradient descent iterations to refine the denoised motion based on trajectory, orientation, and initial pose alignment losses.

**Benchmark Design**
A comprehensive benchmark is introduced to evaluate humanoid-scene interaction.
- **Scenes**: Curated from SmallCity (primary large-scale outdoor scene), XGRIDS (other outdoor scenes), and SAGE-3D (indoor scenes), selected based on rendering quality, interaction range, semantic richness, and collision mesh availability.
- **Avatars**: 14 animatable GS-based avatars covering diverse appearances.
- **Tasks**:
 - Three single-goal visual navigation tasks on SmallCity (SimNav, ObstNav, SocialNav) with increasing complexity.
 - A multi-goal benchmark on SmallCity with 2–5 consecutive landmarks per episode.
 - Generalization evaluations on XGRIDS and SAGE-3D for basic navigation.
- **Metrics**: Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), Collision Rate (CR). For multi-goal: Progress Rate (PR), Progress weighted by Path Length (PPL). For World Layer semantic evaluation: Mean IoU (mIoU) and Mean Accuracy (mAcc) on manually curated pseudo ground-truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates the capabilities of the Visually-grounded Humanoid Agents across both its World and Agent Layers.

**Agent Layer: Visually Grounded Navigation**
- **Single-Goal Navigation**: On the SmallCity dataset, the proposed method consistently surpassed state-of-the-art Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) baselines (NaVILA, NaVid, Uni-NaVid). It achieved an average Success Rate (SR) approximately 30% higher than the strongest baseline across SimNav, ObstNav, and SocialNav tasks. The Collision Rate (CR) was either on par with or lower than the baselines, suggesting effective goal-oriented planning and collision avoidance, even without fine-tuning VLMs on expert navigation trajectories. Occasional collisions in crowded dynamic scenes were observed, attributed to agents' limited field-of-view blind spots for nearby obstacles and challenges in reacting to fast-moving pedestrians.
- **Long-Horizon Planning**: In the multi-goal benchmark, the method significantly outperformed all baselines. This was attributed to the iterative reasoning mechanism, which re-evaluates scene state at each sub-goal, and visual prompting, which continuously anchors the spatial location of the next target, thereby preserving planning coherence over longer episodes.
- **Generalization**: The Agent Layer demonstrated consistent advantages when evaluated on diverse environments, including outdoor scenes from XGRIDS and indoor scenes from SAGE-3D, confirming its robustness across various settings.
- **Ablation of Spatial-Aware Visual Prompting**:
 - Spatial-aware visual prompting consistently improved SR, particularly in tasks with obstacles (ObstNav). Without it, agents tended to lose track of the goal after detours, indicating its role in injecting strong semantic cues about goal position.
 - The inclusion of 3D-aware filtering for action proposals significantly reduced the Collision Rate (e.g., from 53.3% to 13.3% in SimNav), highlighting its necessity for selecting physically plausible and safe paths.
 - A discrete action space formulation yielded substantially better performance (68.3% SR) compared to a continuous regression approach (37.5% SR), suggesting that VLMs are more effective at visual question answering-style choices than precise continuous value regression.
 - An optimal number of 9 candidate actions was identified; sparser options limited maneuverability, while denser options introduced visual clutter, negatively impacting performance.
- **Ablation of Iterative Reasoning**: Integrating iterative reasoning led to substantial improvements in SR, SPL, and CR. It aided in achieving long-horizon goals, prevented myopic straight-line paths, and reduced collisions by promoting cautious navigation around obstacles.
- **Effect of Locomotion Style**: The framework supported diverse locomotion styles. While a "Running" style naturally resulted in a higher CR due to increased speed, a "Slow" motion prioritized safety with the lowest CR. Notably, even the "Running" style outperformed the strongest baseline, indicating the robustness of the planning framework across varying locomotion dynamics.
- **Impact of VLM Backbones**: When evaluating different VLM backbones, all general-purpose VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen2.5-VL) integrated within the framework outperformed training-based navigation specialists (NaVILA, NaVid, Uni-NaVid). Qwen2.5-VL-72B, the default model, achieved the best results, likely due to its high-resolution image processing capabilities beneficial for recognizing distant goals. GPT-4o showed lower performance, attributed to difficulties in initial goal grounding.

**World Layer: Semantic Scene Reconstruction**
- **Comparison with Baselines**: Feature-lifting methods designed for indoor or small-scale scenes (Feature-3DGS, Gradient-Weighted 3DGS) exhibited severe performance degradation in the large-scale outdoor setting of SmallCity. OpenGaussian performed more competitively but was limited by its lack of explicit occlusion handling. The proposed full method significantly outperformed all baselines in both mean IoU (mIoU) and mean Accuracy (mAcc).
- **Ablation of Occlusion-Aware Masks and View Selection**:
 - Occlusion-aware masks were critical in preventing erroneous feature propagation, which could lead to fragmented and noisy object segmentations.
 - Strategic view selection complemented the masks by prioritizing frames with high instance visibility, enhancing the model's ability to learn fine details and recover thin or sparsely observed objects (e.g., trash cans).
 - The combination of both modules yielded the best performance, producing semantically cleaner and more complete 3D reconstructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a paradigm for Visually-grounded Humanoid Agents, representing a step towards active, autonomous digital humans in reconstructed 3D environments.

- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Digital Human Modeling**: The framework is presented as the first to successfully integrate first-person visual perception, spatially-aware planning, and physically executable full-body actions into a continuous loop for embodied digital humans in complex, real-world 3D environments. This moves beyond passively animated digital human systems and disembodied AI agents, fostering human-centric embodied AI.
- **Novelty in 3D Scene Understanding**: The introduction of an occlusion-aware semantic scene reconstruction pipeline, including occlusion-aware masks and strategic view selection, contributes to robust and accurate 3D instance segmentation and open-vocabulary annotation in large-scale, challenging outdoor environments. This addresses a recognized gap in existing 3D reconstruction and semantic understanding methods.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark**: The development of a detailed benchmark, spanning diverse real-world and reconstructed 3D environments with various navigation complexities (SimNav, ObstNav, SocialNav, Multi-Goal), provides a standardized platform for evaluating embodied human-scene interaction. This benchmark is intended to establish a foundation for future research and systematic evaluation of embodied human systems.
- **Future Research Opportunities**: The framework opens up several avenues for further research, including:
 - Extending embodied skills to include richer interactions such as conversations and physical manipulation of scene objects.
 - Exploring human-centric robotic learning, where the simulated agents and reconstructed scenes could inform the development and training of real-world humanoid robots.
 - Advancing large-scale, real-time rendering and reconstruction techniques for gigapixel-scale world modeling to support highly dynamic and continuous viewpoint changes.
- **Potential Applications**:
 - **Next-generation Simulation**: The work contributes to the development of next-generation simulation systems capable of modeling autonomous humanoid agents interacting in realistic scenes, enabling structured "what-if" analyses in open-world settings.
 - **Urban Services**: The ability of embodied agents to navigate complex public spaces in a socially aware manner has implications for urban planning, design of assistive mobility systems, and the deployment of public-facing robotic services, potentially contributing to safer and more efficient urban environments.
 - **Societal Impact**: By improving the integration of autonomous systems into human-populated environments, the research could support accessibility for mobility-impaired individuals and contribute to more equitable urban infrastructures.

This work offers a perspective on populating scenes with digital humans in an active manner, potentially establishing a foundation for the systematic creation and evaluation of future embodied human systems across various domains.
