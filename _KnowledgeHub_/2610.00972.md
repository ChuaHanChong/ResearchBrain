# VeriHarness: Scaling Agentic Verification for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> VeriHarness turns the generator's own LLM into an agentic verifier by giving it a workspace, evidence tools, and reusable verification skills. A disagreement resolver tests competing claims across rollouts against environment evidence, while a consensus challenger probes shared claims and omitted requirements; a fresh-context adjudicator selects and revises the final artifact. Across five long-horizon workspace benchmarks, it beats all selection baselines and lifts single-rollout scores by over 6 points.

## Problem

- Majority voting and self-consistency trust the most common answer, yet 34% of unanimous claims are wrong and the most frequent disputed value is correct only 47% of the time.
- LLM-as-a-judge and tournament methods score rollouts by reading them, relying on the model's existing beliefs instead of checkable evidence in source files, data, and task constraints.
- At the frontier no stronger judge exists, same-model self-checks tend to be confirmatory, and the executable tests used for code and math rarely exist for reports or spreadsheets.

## Method

- ==Verification harness== (workspace, evidence tools, protocol, skill library) wraps the frozen generator model, exposing every rollout's artifact, final state, and trace as read-only files for recomputation and source tracing.
- ==Disagreement resolver== and ==consensus challenger== run in separate contexts: the resolver picks high-information checks to eliminate competing values; the challenger proposes how unanimous values, readings, or omissions could fail and tests them.
- ==Fresh-context adjudication== outputs a base rollout, an evidence-backed revision plan, and an unresolved list; an 18-skill human-authored ==skill library== self-evolves from development failure feedback, scored on held-out tasks.

## Results

- Selection alone raises single-rollout averages by **4.4 points** (Gemini 3.5 Flash) and **4.1 points** (Claude Opus 4.8), beating LLM-as-a-Verifier and every baseline in all ten model-benchmark settings.
- Evidence-backed revision lifts average gains to **6.2** and **6.4 points**, up to **+11.7** on APEX-Agents with Opus; an agentic verifier with identical environment access recovers only about **half** the gain.
- Ablation with Opus: resolver alone reaches **54.3**, challenger alone **52.6**, both **56.1**; skills evolved from an empty library add **11.0 points** on APEX-Agents and beat the human-authored library.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rollout disagreement is a cheap pointer to where verification effort pays off: selection gains grow with claim entropy, while unanimous pools gain almost nothing from selection alone.
> - Tool access alone does not make a good verifier; a protocol that divides checking labor plus artifact-specific failure knowledge is what turns environment access into accuracy.
> - Same-model verifiers stop where generators stopped, checking correct intermediates while errors sit downstream; useful skills forbid treating a recomputation that matches the pool as confirmation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Judging a long, file-based deliverable can mean tracing claims to sources, recomputing figures, and checking requirements across documents. That work often has no simple answer key or unit test. A polished answer can still rely on a superseded source, or share a wrong unit with every other attempt.

The authors give the generator's *same* model a verification workspace, evidence tools, and reusable checking skills. They then direct it to investigate both disagreements among attempts and errors the attempts may all share. The verifier does not see reference answers or grading rubrics at test time. This harness, VeriHarness, was evaluated on five workspace benchmarks with Gemini 3.5 Flash and Claude Opus 4.8 as both generator and verifier. It has the highest primary selection score among the evaluated baselines in all ten model–benchmark settings.

Illustrative worked example, not a benchmark result. R1 and R2 report USD 100m from a draft, and R3 reports USD 120m from the final report. A version-history check finds that the final report supersedes the draft. Source metadata identifies EUR, which contradicts all three USD labels. A fresh-context adjudicator delivers EUR 120m with a verification record.

### 2. Two investigations, one delivery

A *claim* is a task-relevant assertion in an artifact: a value, an interpretation, or whether a requirement is met. The workspace exposes candidate artifacts, their final states and action traces, task inputs, and environmental files. Tools let the verifier trace sources, recompute quantities, inspect metadata, and run code. Tools do the checking, while skills suggest what to check.

The model works in separate contexts. The disagreement resolver traces competing claims to sources or calculations and chooses checks that distinguish the alternatives. The consensus challenger proposes ways a unanimous value, interpretation, or omission could be wrong, then checks those possibilities against the environment. A fresh context then reviews both evidence records, selects a base artifact, and makes only evidence-supported revisions. Unsettled claims remain marked unresolved in the verification record.

### 3. Why checking votes matters

The authors ran a claim-level analysis on ten-rollout pools from Claude Opus 4.8 on APEX-Agents. It covered 917 consensus claims and 775 disputed claims, with correctness assigned using the benchmark's rubric grades. In this analysis, 34% of consensus values were judged incorrect. A correct candidate appeared in 74% of disputed claims, but the most frequent value was correct in only 47%. These are percentages of claims, not whole-artifact accuracy.

Claude Opus 4.8, ten-rollout APEX-Agents pools. Consensus claims (n=917): 66% correct, 34% wrong. Disputed claims (n=775): 74% have a correct candidate, but the most frequent candidate is correct in 47%.

For the method comparison, all methods receive the same frozen pool of ten rollouts per task and model. Scores use each benchmark's native scale, and "Avg" is an unweighted mean of the five benchmark scores. With Gemini 3.5 Flash, the five-benchmark averages are:

- 51.6 for VeriHarness selection
- 49.5 for LLM-as-a-Verifier
- 49.4 for an agentic verifier given the same workspace and evidence tools but not the protocol or skills
- 47.2 for a single rollout

On Workspace-Bench Lite, which reports mean rubric score, VeriHarness selection scores 62.0 versus 60.4 for LLM-as-a-Verifier. Revision, an added editing pass, scores 65.4. That is an 8.9-point gain over the single-rollout score of 56.5.

Revision improves every model–benchmark setting. It raises the unweighted average gain over a single rollout to 6.2 points with Flash and 6.4 with Opus. These are benchmark-score gains, not fewer generated tokens, fewer attempts, or reduced elapsed time.

The comparison with the same-environment-access verifier shows that workspace access alone is not sufficient. It does not isolate which missing component explains the difference.

Table 2: three-seed means on each benchmark's native scale. For Flash, the separate "With revision" block gives VeriHarness Avg 53.4, which is +6.2 over the single-rollout pool.

### 4. Scope

This study covers two models, five benchmarks, and ten rollouts per task, with verification-time access to neither grader nor rubric. Verification adds multi-turn work and wall-clock time. The harness returns an artifact and a record of checks, findings, and unresolved issues. It does not provide a calibrated scalar score or a guarantee of correctness.
