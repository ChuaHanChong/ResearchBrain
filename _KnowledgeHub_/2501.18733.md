# Integrating LMM Planners and 3D Skill Policies for Generalizable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LMM-3DP is a robotic framework that combines Large Multimodal Models for high-level planning with 3D feature fields for low-level control, enabling robots to execute diverse manipulation tasks in complex, dynamic environments. The system demonstrates robust adaptation to visual changes and achieves enhanced task success rates in real-world kitchen scenarios.

## Problem

- Bridging high-level task understanding (language instructions) with precise low-level continuous robotic control in real-world environments.
- Existing LLM-based systems lack visual understanding, limiting adaptation to dynamic environments and tasks requiring visual reasoning.
- Robotic systems often rely on predefined primitive skills, limiting generalizability and applicability to novel, open-world manipulation tasks.

## Method

- An LMM-3DP framework integrating an LMM-based high-level planner (GPT-4V) with a 3D semantic-geometric skill policy for low-level control.
- A closed-loop system incorporating continuous visual feedback, a critic agent for plan refinement, and a memory system for in-context learning.
- Low-level control leverages a unified 3D and semantic representation (DINO, PointNext, CLIP) to predict keyframe-based 6-DOF poses via a 3D transformer.

## Results

- Achieved high accuracy (**90%** without distractors, **56%** with) on individual manipulation skills, outperforming baselines like OWL-v2 and Voxposer.
- Demonstrated strong generalizability in skill composition, successfully combining untrained object-location pairs with 70-**90%** accuracy rates.
- Showed superior performance on long-horizon tasks, achieving an average success rate of **56.5%** compared to 7-**10%** for baseline approaches (SayCan, Voxposer), with planning module near **100%** accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closed-loop integration of high-level LMM planning with low-level 3D policies allows for dynamic adaptation to environmental changes and self-correction.
> - A critic agent, operating solely on visual input, can effectively identify and prompt correction of LMM planning flaws, improving plan robustness.
> - Representing actions as keyframe predictions based on fused 3D semantic and geometric features provides a sample-efficient and robust method for learning low-level manipulation skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has long pursued the goal of creating robots capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks in real-world environments. Traditional approaches have struggled with the fundamental challenge of bridging high-level task understanding with precise low-level control. This paper by Li et al. from UC San Diego introduces LMM-3DP, a framework that addresses this challenge by integrating Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for planning with 3D feature fields for skill execution.

The work represents a significant step toward autonomous robotic systems that can understand complex instructions, adapt to dynamic environments, and execute precise manipulation tasks without constant human supervision. By combining the reasoning capabilities of foundation models with robust 3D perception and control, LMM-3DP demonstrates substantial improvements in both task success rates and generalization capabilities compared to existing approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Current robotic manipulation systems face several critical limitations that prevent their deployment in real-world scenarios. Large Language Models (LLMs) have shown promise in task planning but lack visual understanding, making them unable to adapt to environmental changes or handle scenarios that require visual reasoning. Additionally, most existing systems rely on predefined primitive skills, limiting their applicability to open-world environments where novel manipulation tasks may arise.

The disconnect between high-level symbolic reasoning and low-level continuous control represents a fundamental challenge. While LLMs excel at decomposing abstract tasks into language instructions, translating these instructions into precise robot actions in complex 3D environments remains difficult. This gap is particularly pronounced when dealing with occlusions, spatial relationships, and dynamic environmental changes that require real-time adaptation.

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have emerged as a potential solution by incorporating visual understanding alongside language processing. However, effectively integrating these models with robust low-level control policies that can handle the geometric complexity of real-world manipulation tasks remains an open challenge.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The LMM-3DP Framework

LMM-3DP addresses the integration challenge through a comprehensive framework that combines three key components: an LMM-based high-level planner, a 3D skill policy for low-level control, and an effective integration mechanism that enables seamless communication between these components.

The framework operates through a closed-loop system where the high-level planner continuously receives visual feedback from the environment, allowing it to adapt plans dynamically. A critic agent provides additional oversight by identifying potential flaws in proposed plans, while a memory system enables learning from past experiences. The low-level control system leverages both semantic and geometric 3D representations to execute precise manipulation skills.

#### 3.2. High-Level Planning with Large Multimodal Models

The high-level planning component utilizes GPT-4V as the primary planner, integrated within the robot's execution loop to enable continuous adaptation based on visual feedback. This closed-loop design allows the system to monitor ongoing events and adjust plans when environmental conditions change or when low-level execution fails.

The critic agent plays a crucial role in ensuring plan quality by taking only visual observations and proposed plans as input to assess feasibility. When problematic steps are identified, the critic's reasoning is fed back to the planner, prompting generation of updated, more accurate plans. This mechanism helps correct common planner biases, such as blindly following instructions even when environmental conditions contradict them.

The memory system stores historical critiques of the planner's outputs, serving as a learning mechanism for in-context improvement. This allows the system to learn from past mistakes and maintain consistency over long-horizon tasks. Additionally, the framework maintains a skill library that can be expanded when new capabilities are identified as necessary for task completion.

#### 3.3. Low-Level Control with 3D Semantic Representation

The low-level control system implements a language-conditioned multi-task 3D policy trained through imitation learning from human demonstrations. Rather than predicting continuous control actions directly, the system converts the problem into keyframe-based action prediction, which proves more sample-efficient and robust.

The vision and language encoder creates a unified 3D and semantic representation by combining:

- **Semantic Features**: Extracted using pre-trained vision foundation models (DINO) and back-projected into 3D space
- **Geometric Features**: Learned through point-based networks (PointNext) to capture local 3D structures
- **Language Embeddings**: Generated using CLIP to process instructions from the high-level planner

The 3D transformer architecture processes fused point features, language embeddings, and robot proprioception to predict 6-DOF poses for the next best keyframe. The model discretizes the continuous action space by scoring query points in the gripper's workspace, using attention mechanisms to focus on relevant local structures.

The training objective combines cross-entropy losses for translation, rotation, gripper openness, and collision avoidance, with label smoothing to prevent overfitting and handle demonstration noise.

#### 3.4. Integration Strategy

The integration between high-level planning and low-level control occurs through the 3D transformer's joint attention mechanism. Language embeddings representing the high-level planner's intent are attended together with the unified 3D and semantic feature field, allowing the low-level policy to interpret high-level instructions within the context of the visual scene.

This direct conditioning enables the execution of language-conditioned skills while maintaining the rich 3D understanding necessary for precise manipulation. The integration is further strengthened by the shared semantic representation that bridges the gap between symbolic language instructions and continuous physical actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive real-world experiments using a Franka Emika Panda robot in kitchen environments, evaluating performance across individual skills, skill composition, high-level planning, and long-horizon tasks.

For individual skills, LMM-3DP achieved high accuracy rates across five distinct manipulation primitives (grasping, placing, turning, opening, closing), significantly outperforming baselines like OWL-v2 and Voxposer. The system achieved 90% accuracy for grasping without distractors and maintained 56% accuracy even with environmental distractors present.

The skill composition experiments demonstrated strong generalizability, with the system successfully combining objects and locations that were not directly trained together. For example, the robot could place milk in a sink or drawer after training on separate object-location pairs, achieving 70-90% accuracy rates.

In high-level planning comparisons, LMM-3DP showed superior performance compared to LLM baselines (SayCan, Voxposer), particularly on tasks requiring visual reasoning. On tasks needing visual understanding, LMM-3DP achieved 80% accuracy while baselines scored 0-50%.

For long-horizon tasks, LMM-3DP achieved an average success rate of 56.5% compared to 7% for SayCan + OWL-v2 and 10% for Voxposer. The planning module achieved nearly 100% accuracy, with most failures attributed to accumulated errors in low-level execution over multiple steps.

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of visual feedback and the critic agent. In scenarios with environmental disturbances or misaligned instructions, LMM-3DP's closed-loop planning and critic mechanism enabled successful task completion while baselines failed entirely.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Contributions and Impact

LMM-3DP represents a substantial advancement in embodied AI and robotics by demonstrating several key capabilities that address longstanding challenges in the field. The framework's ability to operate autonomously while adapting to dynamic environments significantly reduces the need for human intervention, moving toward truly autonomous robotic systems.

The data-efficient learning of generalizable low-level skills addresses a major bottleneck in robot deployment. The system's ability to acquire new skills from limited demonstrations and generalize them to novel objects and environments makes it more practical for real-world applications where extensive training data may not be available.

The effective integration of high-level reasoning with low-level control provides a template for future research in embodied AI. The joint attention mechanism successfully bridges symbolic reasoning and continuous control, enabling robots to understand and execute complex instructions in visually complex environments.

The framework's robustness to real-world challenges, including environmental disturbances, execution failures, and ambiguous instructions, makes it suitable for deployment outside controlled laboratory settings. This reliability is crucial for practical applications in industrial automation, service robotics, and hazardous environments.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

Despite its achievements, LMM-3DP faces several limitations that present opportunities for future research. The system's effectiveness still depends on careful prompt engineering for the LMM, suggesting that more robust methods for human-robot interaction could further improve performance.

The keyframe-based approach may struggle with tasks requiring highly continuous, fine-grained trajectories. Future work could explore hybrid approaches that combine keyframe prediction with continuous control for tasks requiring smooth motion profiles.

While the system demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, extending skills to truly novel items with very limited demonstrations remains challenging. Continued research into few-shot learning and meta-learning approaches could address this limitation.

The framework's reliance on pre-trained foundation models also presents both opportunities and challenges. As these models continue to improve, LMM-3DP could benefit from enhanced capabilities, but the system's performance is also constrained by the limitations of these underlying models.

Future directions could include exploring more sophisticated reasoning mechanisms, incorporating tactile and force feedback for more robust manipulation, and developing methods for learning from robot-robot interaction in multi-agent scenarios. The expansion of the skill library concept could also lead to more comprehensive lifelong learning systems capable of continuous capability acquisition.
