# Seeing What Matters: Visual Preference Policy Optimization for Visual Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViPO (Visual Preference Policy Optimization) refines Group Relative Policy Optimization for visual generation by introducing a Perceptual Structuring Module, which transforms scalar preference feedback into spatially and temporally structured advantages. This approach enhances visual fidelity and motion quality, consistently outperforming conventional GRPO on human preference metrics for both image and video synthesis.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO methods for visual generation assign a single scalar reward to an entire image or video, failing to account for their rich internal spatial and temporal structures.
- This coarse feedback creates a "spatial credit assignment problem," making it difficult to correct localized visual artifacts and capture fine-grained perceptual cues.
- Reward models typically output scalar scores, preventing RL frameworks from fully leveraging rich spatial information for fine-grained policy optimization.

## Method

- **ViPO** integrates a ==Perceptual Structuring Module== (PSM) into the GRPO framework, designed to capture the selective nature of human visual preference.
- The PSM comprises a Visual Preference Extractor (VPE) that derives spatial features and a Visual Preference Allocator (VPA) that generates region-specific importance maps.
- These allocation maps spatially and temporally distribute the global scalar advantage, directing policy optimization towards perceptually significant regions within images and videos.

## Results

- ViPO consistently outperformed vanilla GRPO, achieving higher scores on human preference metrics (e.g., HPSv2.1, ImageReward, PickScore) for image generation and significant gains in Visual Quality and Motion Quality for video generation.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated ViPO's ability to generate more detailed, realistic, and preference-aligned visual content, correcting localized artifacts and maintaining structural integrity.
- Experiments with a "redness reward" showed ViPO's enhanced robustness, preventing semantic degradation and structural collapse observed in DanceGRPO under strong, biased gradient signals.
- The Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM) introduced minimal training overhead, increasing step time by 1.0-**4.8%** and peak memory by less than **4.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human visual perception is inherently selective and spatially biased; leveraging this property through structured feedback can significantly improve generative model alignment with preferences.
> - Converting a global scalar advantage into spatially and temporally resolved advantages enables more precise credit assignment for high-dimensional visual content.
> - Applying optimization pressure selectively to perceptually important regions enhances generative fidelity and robustness against semantic degradation under strong reward signals.
