# Seeing What Matters: Visual Preference Policy Optimization for Visual Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViPO (Visual Preference Policy Optimization) refines Group Relative Policy Optimization for visual generation by introducing a Perceptual Structuring Module, which transforms scalar preference feedback into spatially and temporally structured advantages. This approach enhances visual fidelity and motion quality, consistently outperforming conventional GRPO on human preference metrics for both image and video synthesis.

## Problem

- Existing GRPO methods for visual generation assign a single scalar reward to an entire image or video, failing to account for their rich internal spatial and temporal structures.
- This coarse feedback creates a "spatial credit assignment problem," making it difficult to correct localized visual artifacts and capture fine-grained perceptual cues.
- Reward models typically output scalar scores, preventing RL frameworks from fully leveraging rich spatial information for fine-grained policy optimization.

## Method

- **ViPO** integrates a ==Perceptual Structuring Module== (PSM) into the GRPO framework, designed to capture the selective nature of human visual preference.
- The PSM comprises a Visual Preference Extractor (VPE) that derives spatial features and a Visual Preference Allocator (VPA) that generates region-specific importance maps.
- These allocation maps spatially and temporally distribute the global scalar advantage, directing policy optimization towards perceptually significant regions within images and videos.

## Results

- ViPO consistently outperformed vanilla GRPO, achieving higher scores on human preference metrics (e.g., HPSv2.1, ImageReward, PickScore) for image generation and significant gains in Visual Quality and Motion Quality for video generation.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated ViPO's ability to generate more detailed, realistic, and preference-aligned visual content, correcting localized artifacts and maintaining structural integrity.
- Experiments with a "redness reward" showed ViPO's enhanced robustness, preventing semantic degradation and structural collapse observed in DanceGRPO under strong, biased gradient signals.
- The Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM) introduced minimal training overhead, increasing step time by 1.0-**4.8%** and peak memory by less than **4.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human visual perception is inherently selective and spatially biased; leveraging this property through structured feedback can significantly improve generative model alignment with preferences.
> - Converting a global scalar advantage into spatially and temporally resolved advantages enables more precise credit assignment for high-dimensional visual content.
> - Applying optimization pressure selectively to perceptually important regions enhances generative fidelity and robustness against semantic degradation under strong reward signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) has gained prominence in post-training visual generative models to align their outputs with human preferences. Within this domain, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is increasingly utilized due to its group-wise comparison-based advantage formulation, which has been observed to improve optimization stability and sample quality in tasks like language generation. Recent advancements have successfully adapted GRPO to diffusion and flow-based visual generators, demonstrating its potential for reinforcement-driven alignment in image and video synthesis.

However, a fundamental limitation in the direct application of GRPO to visual generation is that its original design, optimized for token-level or sequence-level outputs (e.g., text), assigns a single scalar advantage to an entire visual sample. This approach overlooks the intrinsic spatial and temporal structures present in images and videos. Consequently, existing GRPO implementations for visual tasks treat each visual entity as a holistic unit, providing coarse supervision that is not sensitive to localized artifacts or fine-grained perceptual cues. This results in a "spatial credit assignment problem," where all pixels receive an identical scalar advantage, leading to uniform weighting and potentially misdirected gradients that limit the model's ability to correct specific errors or enhance particular regions.

The broader research landscape includes significant work in visual perception modeling, such as saliency maps, attention mechanisms in deep networks, and perceptual loss functions that approximate human perceptual similarity. Concurrently, the development of visual reward models (e.g., PickScore, HPSv2 for images; VideoAlign, VideoScore for videos) aims to capture human visual preferences. While these reward models can sometimes capture fine-grained cues, their output is typically a scalar score, which is then collapsed into a single value by current GRPO-style frameworks. This prevents the rich spatial information encoded within these advanced visual reward models from being fully leveraged during policy optimization.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing a method that bridges the structured nature of visual data and perceptual insights with the policy optimization process. It aims to develop a framework that can exploit the spatial and temporal dimensions of visual content to provide more granular, perception-guided feedback, thereby improving the alignment of generative models with human preferences beyond what scalar-reward GRPO can achieve. The work positions itself as an enhancement to the GRPO paradigm, making it more suitable for the complexities of visual synthesis while maintaining its core benefits.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations of applying existing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) frameworks directly to visual generative models. When applied to visual content, such as images or videos, GRPO typically assigns a single scalar reward to the entire sample. This approach has several inherent drawbacks:

1. **Coarse Supervision and Lack of Granularity:** Treating each image or video as a holistic entity with a single scalar reward ignores the rich spatial and temporal structures of visual content. This coarse level of supervision provides insufficient information for addressing localized imperfections or modeling fine-grained perceptual cues. For example, if a generated image contains a small, distracting artifact in one corner, a scalar reward might not provide specific enough feedback to correct only that region, potentially leading to inefficient or misdirected optimization across the entire image.
2. **Spatial Credit Assignment Problem:** In conventional GRPO, all pixels or regions within a visual sample implicitly share the same scalar advantage. This implies a uniform contribution to the overall perceptual quality, disregarding that different regions (e.g., foreground objects versus background) have varying degrees of importance to human perception and preference. This uniform weighting can generate indiscriminate gradients that might amplify irrelevant cues or fail to adequately correct critical regions, thereby limiting the generator's capacity to produce perceptually faithful and semantically consistent outputs.
3. **Inability to Leverage Fine-Grained Perceptual Cues:** While modern visual reward models and perceptual modeling techniques can capture detailed spatial and semantic information, current GRPO-style alignment frameworks are unable to fully exploit these rich, structured cues due to their reliance on a scalar reward output. The fine-grained evidence from these models is collapsed into a single value, losing its spatial or temporal specificity.

To overcome these limitations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Reformulate Advantage Representation:** To move beyond the scalar advantage, reformulate the advantage representation in GRPO to incorporate spatial and temporal structure, making it suitable for image and video generation.
2. **Introduce Spatial Credit Allocation:** Develop a mechanism for fine-grained, region-aware credit assignment, allowing the policy optimization to differentiate feedback across perceptually distinct regions within a visual sample.
3. **Enable Perception-Guided Optimization:** Integrate perceptual insights (derived from pretrained vision backbones) into the advantage assignment process, so that optimization pressure is intelligently redistributed towards perceptually important regions.
4. **Maintain Stability and Compatibility:** Ensure that the proposed framework, Visual Preference Policy Optimization (ViPO), preserves the stability and simplicity of the original GRPO algorithm and is fully compatible with existing GRPO training pipelines without requiring dense pixel-level supervision or explicit region annotations.
5. **Improve Perceptual Fidelity and Alignment:** Ultimately, achieve stronger alignment with human visual judgment, enhance perceptual fidelity, and improve generalization capabilities of visual generative models across both image and video tasks.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to provide a more expressive and informative learning signal for visual generation, leading to higher quality and more perceptually aligned synthetic content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Visual Preference Policy Optimization (ViPO), is an enhanced GRPO framework designed to provide structured, pixel-level advantages for visual content generation. It reformulates the advantage representation and credit-assignment mechanism of GRPO to leverage the spatial and temporal structure inherent in visual data.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of GRPO for Visual Generation

The paper first outlines the standard application of GRPO to visual generation. The denoising process in diffusion and rectified flow models is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). A generative policy $\pi_\theta$ samples a group of outputs $\{o^1..., o^G\}$ for a given prompt $c$. The objective function $J(\theta)$ is based on a clipped policy gradient formulation, typically:
$J (\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\{o_i\}^G_{i=1} \sim \pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|c), a_{t,i} \sim \pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot|s_{t,i})} [ \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^G \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \min(\rho_{t,i} A_i, \text{clip}(\rho_{t,i}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A_i)]$
where $\rho_{t,i}$ is the likelihood ratio, and $A_i$ is the scalar advantage function for output $o_i$, calculated using group-wise rewards:
$A_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1..., r_G\})}{\text{std}(\{r_1..., r_G\})}$
For flow-matching models, which typically use deterministic Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs), a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) formulation is adopted to introduce the stochastic exploration necessary for RL training. This involves converting the reverse-time ODE into an SDE with a controlled stochasticity term ($\epsilon_t dw$), allowing for policy gradient optimization.

#### 3.2. Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM)

To address the lack of spatial awareness in scalar advantages, ViPO introduces the Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM). PSM extracts visual preference cues from generated content and encodes them into a preference allocation map, which is then used for structured advantage assignment. PSM consists of two main components:

1. **Visual Preference Extractor (VPE):** Given a generated image or video frame $x$, the VPE (denoted as $\Phi$) uses a pretrained vision backbone (e.g., DINOv2, SAM for Transformer-based; ResNet for CNN-based) to produce feature embeddings.
 - **For Transformer-based models (DINOv2, SAM):** The VPE outputs patch-level features $F \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D}$. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to $F$ to retain the top-K components, $Z = \text{PCA}(F) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times K}$, along with their explained variance ratios $\lambda = (\lambda_1..., \lambda_K)$. Each component $z_j$ is then normalized and inverted to emphasize regions with lower PCA projections (often corresponding to salient content): $z'_j = \frac{\max(z_j) - z_j}{\max(z_j) - \min(z_j)}$.
 - **For CNN-based models (ResNet):** Intermediate feature maps $F \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times H_f \times W_f}$ are extracted from a designated layer. An activation map $S$ is obtained via channel-weighted aggregation: $S = \sum_{c=1}^C \alpha_c F_c$, where weights $\alpha_c$ are derived from global average pooling followed by softmax.
2. **Visual Preference Allocator (VPA):** The VPA aggregates the extracted preference cues into a spatial map $S$.
 - **For PCA-based features:** The normalized components $z'_j$ are aggregated using a variance-weighted summation: $S = \text{Reshape}(\sum_{j=1}^K \lambda_j z'_j)$.
 - The resulting map $S$ is optionally smoothed (e.g., with a Gaussian kernel) and upsampled to the latent resolution, forming the final preference allocation map $M$. For video, maps are computed per frame and temporally aligned to create a spatio-temporal volume $M \in \mathbb{R}^{T_l \times H_l \times W_l}$. This process distills region relevance without dense annotations.

#### 3.3. Visual Preference Policy Optimization (ViPO) Objective

ViPO modifies the GRPO objective to incorporate the structured allocation map $M$. Instead of a single scalar advantage $A_i$ for an entire sample $x_i$, ViPO introduces a spatially (and temporally for video) resolved advantage $A_p^i$:
$A_p^i = M(p) A_i$
Here, $p$ indexes a latent-space position, and $M(p)$ is the regional weight from the allocation map at that position. This effectively links the scalar group advantage $A_i$ with the perceptual relevance inferred by $M$. The modified ViPO objective is:
$J (\theta) = \mathbb{E}[ \frac{1}{G T_s |P|} \sum_{i=1}^G \sum_{t=1}^{T_s} \sum_{p \in P} \min(\rho^p_{t,i} A^p_i, \text{clip}(\rho^p_{t,i}, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)A^p_i)]$
where $T_s$ is the number of diffusion/flow steps and $\rho^p_{t,i}$ is the local likelihood ratio.
This formulation ensures that the optimization direction remains consistent within each sample (by multiplying $A_i$ with $M(p)$), prevents gradient interference from mixed-sign rewards, and maintains plug-and-play compatibility with existing GRPO implementations. It allows gradient updates to focus on perceptually significant regions across space and time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations across image and video generation benchmarks, along with ablation studies to validate the design choices.

#### 4.1. Human Preference Reward Evaluation

Experiments were conducted using Flux.1-dev for image generation (fine-tuned with HPSv2.1) and Wan2.1-T2V-14B-480P for video generation (fine-tuned with VideoAlign). DanceGRPO was used as a primary baseline, alongside the original generative models.

- **Quantitative Results (Image Generation - Table 1):**
 - ViPO variants (using DINOv2, SAM, or ResNet as PSM backbones) consistently outperformed the original Flux model and DanceGRPO across in-domain (HPSv2.1) and out-of-domain (PickScore, ImageReward) metrics.
 - The DINO-based ViPO variant showed the strongest overall performance, achieving the highest HPSv2.1 and ImageReward scores.
 - The ResNet-based variant performed well, particularly on out-of-domain PickScore.
 - The SAM-based variant, while generally weaker than DINO or ResNet variants, still surpassed DanceGRPO, suggesting that its focus on low-level content or boundaries might be less effective for high-level perceptual alignment compared to DINOv2's semantic representations.
- **Quantitative Results (Video Generation - Table 2 & Table 6):**
 - ViPO (using DINOv2 in PSM) surpassed both Wan2.1 and DanceGRPO in in-domain metrics (Visual Quality (VQ), Motion Quality (MQ)) and out-of-domain VBench metrics (Semantic, Quality, Total scores).
 - Detailed VBench results (Table 6) indicated substantial improvements in Dynamic Degree, Imaging Quality, Multiple Objects, Spatial Relationship, and Overall Consistency, supporting ViPO's ability to enhance both visual fidelity and motion realism.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 3, Figure 4, Figure 6, Figure 7):**
 - **Image Generation:** ViPO consistently produced results with greater detail, realism, and alignment with human preferences. For example, in comparison to DanceGRPO which sometimes added unnatural elements or duplications, ViPO rendered objects more realistically and ensured coherent compositions.
 - **Video Generation:** ViPO demonstrated significant improvements in visual fidelity and motion quality. Examples included refined object rendering, more realistic background elements in dynamic scenes, and natural, fluid motion without semantic distortions (e.g., duplicated or broken limbs) that sometimes appeared in DanceGRPO outputs.
 - These qualitative improvements were attributed to the PSM's ability to focus reward attribution on perceptually salient regions, leading to differentiated refinement rather than uniform updates across the entire frame.

#### 4.2. Redness Reward Experiment (Figure 5, Figure 10)

Experiments with a rule-based "redness reward" function showed that ViPO better preserved the semantic content and structural integrity of generated images throughout training. In contrast, DanceGRPO outputs tended to suffer from semantic degradation or structural collapse, especially under strong color biases. This suggests ViPO's region-differentiated optimization is more robust to global gradient signals.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3, Table 4)

A series of ablation studies on the Flux model explored the components of the PSM:

- **Visual Preference Allocation Map Necessity:**
 - Replacing the allocation map with an all-ones map (uniform weighting) significantly degraded performance, confirming that the benefit stems from semantically meaningful, fine-grained allocation, not just pixel-wise decomposition.
 - Applying the allocation map directly to the reward instead of the advantage also resulted in a performance drop, indicating that applying it to the advantage maintains stable relative signals while enabling fine-grained semantic allocation.
- **Aggregation Strategies:**
 - Variance-weighted aggregation (which prioritizes components explaining more variance) yielded higher out-of-domain scores compared to simple averaging of principal components. This highlights the importance of weighting semantic components based on their explanatory power.
- **Number of Principal Components (K):**
 - Varying K (1 to 5) showed that K=3 offered a robust balance across metrics, providing good interpretability and competitive performance without the variability observed with more components.
- **Spatial Smoothing:**
 - Moderate Gaussian smoothing (σ=1) generally improved robustness across metrics, while no smoothing or overly aggressive smoothing (σ=2) could lead to slightly less optimal performance. Smoothing helps regularize local jitter or noisy activations in projected feature maps.

#### 4.4. Training Overhead (Table 5)

The PSM introduces minimal computational overhead. For image generation, training step time increased by 1.0–1.8%, and peak memory usage by less than 4.0%. For video generation, the step time increased by 4.8%, and peak memory usage by 4.5%. This indicates that ViPO is lightweight and compatible with existing pipelines without significantly impacting training efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Visual Preference Policy Optimization (ViPO), a novel framework that significantly enhances the application of reinforcement learning to visual generative models, particularly those leveraging Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation in RL for Visual Generation:** ViPO directly tackles the fundamental problem of scalar-only feedback in GRPO when applied to visual data. By transforming coarse scalar advantages into structured, pixel-level, and temporally aware advantages, it provides a more granular and informative learning signal. This is a scientific advancement in how spatial credit assignment is handled within RL for high-dimensional visual synthesis tasks.
2. **Improved Perceptual Fidelity and Human Alignment:** The method consistently demonstrates superior performance over vanilla GRPO and base generative models, yielding outputs with richer details, greater realism, and stronger alignment with human visual preferences across both image and video generation. This implies a notable improvement in the quality and aesthetic appeal of AI-generated visual content.
3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** ViPO not only improves performance on in-domain preference rewards but also shows better generalization capabilities on out-of-domain evaluations. The robustness demonstrated in the redness reward experiment, where semantic integrity was preserved even under strong biases, indicates that ViPO's region-differentiated optimization leads to more stable and coherent generative models.
4. **Modular and Lightweight Design:** The Perceptual Structuring Module (PSM) is architecture-agnostic, allowing the integration of various pretrained vision backbones. Its low computational overhead (minor increases in training time and memory) ensures full compatibility with existing GRPO pipelines, making it an accessible and practical enhancement for current generative model development.
5. **Bridge Between Perception and Policy Learning:** ViPO effectively bridges modern perceptual modeling with reinforcement learning, allowing detailed visual cues to directly influence policy updates. This integration represents a promising direction for creating AI systems that "see what matters" in a way analogous to human visual attention.
6. **Future Research Directions:** The principles introduced by ViPO—structured feedback, region-aware policy learning, and fine-grained perceptual alignment—suggest significant avenues for future research. This could include developing more sophisticated perceptual structuring modules, exploring dynamic attention mechanisms within the RL framework, and applying these concepts to a wider range of high-dimensional generative tasks beyond images and videos. For example, it could be extended to 3D content generation or interactive visual environments where fine-grained control and understanding of visual importance are critical.

In conclusion, ViPO offers a practical and theoretically grounded advancement that refines the application of reinforcement learning to visual generation, paving the way for more perceptually aligned, high-fidelity, and robust generative AI models.
