# CARP: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> CARP introduces a visuomotor policy learning method that leverages coarse-to-fine autoregressive prediction with multi-scale action tokenization. This approach achieves performance on par with diffusion models while demonstrating a 10-fold increase in inference speed and utilizing significantly fewer parameters across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive models (AMs) for robotic control are efficient but often struggle with long-range dependencies and global action structure, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Diffusion models (DMs) achieve high accuracy in visuomotor policy learning but incur significant computational costs and inference latency due to iterative denoising, hindering real-time robotic deployment.
- A critical dilemma exists in balancing high action generation performance and real-time inference efficiency for practical robotic applications.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is proposed, starting with ==Multi-Scale Action Tokenization== using a VQ-VAE and 1D CNNs to convert continuous action sequences into discrete, multi-scale latent representations.
- The second stage utilizes a GPT-style transformer for Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction, generating action token maps from coarse to fine granularity by conditioning on prior scales and historical observations.
- This prediction process, formulated as "next-scale" rather than "next-token," employs Cross-Entropy loss to preserve multimodality and relaxes rigid Markovian assumptions for more holistic trajectory generation.

## Results

- CARP consistently matched or surpassed state-of-the-art diffusion policies in task success rates across various single and multi-task simulations (e.g., Robomimic, Kitchen, MimicGen).
- It achieved approximately 10 times faster inference speeds and utilized only 1-**5%** of the parameters compared to diffusion models, making it highly efficient for real-time applications.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance on real-world UR5e and Franka Panda robots, showing up to a **10%** improvement in success rates over diffusion policies while maintaining high inference efficiency and generating smooth, multimodal trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical, multi-scale action tokenization effectively bridges the gap between local temporal dependencies and global action structure, overcoming the "myopic" nature of traditional autoregressive models.
> - Re-framing autoregressive generation as a coarse-to-fine, "next-scale" prediction process allows for rapid, holistic trajectory generation and refinement with significantly fewer steps than diffusion models.
> - Leveraging Cross-Entropy loss for policy learning preserves multimodal behavior, enabling the generation of diverse and contextually appropriate actions for complex robotic tasks.
