# CARP: Visuomotor Policy Learning via Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> CARP introduces a visuomotor policy learning method that leverages coarse-to-fine autoregressive prediction with multi-scale action tokenization. This approach achieves performance on par with diffusion models while demonstrating a 10-fold increase in inference speed and utilizing significantly fewer parameters across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing autoregressive models (AMs) for robotic control are efficient but often struggle with long-range dependencies and global action structure, leading to suboptimal performance.
- Diffusion models (DMs) achieve high accuracy in visuomotor policy learning but incur significant computational costs and inference latency due to iterative denoising, hindering real-time robotic deployment.
- A critical dilemma exists in balancing high action generation performance and real-time inference efficiency for practical robotic applications.

## Method

- A two-stage framework is proposed, starting with ==Multi-Scale Action Tokenization== using a VQ-VAE and 1D CNNs to convert continuous action sequences into discrete, multi-scale latent representations.
- The second stage utilizes a GPT-style transformer for Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction, generating action token maps from coarse to fine granularity by conditioning on prior scales and historical observations.
- This prediction process, formulated as "next-scale" rather than "next-token," employs Cross-Entropy loss to preserve multimodality and relaxes rigid Markovian assumptions for more holistic trajectory generation.

## Results

- CARP consistently matched or surpassed state-of-the-art diffusion policies in task success rates across various single and multi-task simulations (e.g., Robomimic, Kitchen, MimicGen).
- It achieved approximately 10 times faster inference speeds and utilized only 1-**5%** of the parameters compared to diffusion models, making it highly efficient for real-time applications.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance on real-world UR5e and Franka Panda robots, showing up to a **10%** improvement in success rates over diffusion policies while maintaining high inference efficiency and generating smooth, multimodal trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical, multi-scale action tokenization effectively bridges the gap between local temporal dependencies and global action structure, overcoming the "myopic" nature of traditional autoregressive models.
> - Re-framing autoregressive generation as a coarse-to-fine, "next-scale" prediction process allows for rapid, holistic trajectory generation and refinement with significantly fewer steps than diffusion models.
> - Leveraging Cross-Entropy loss for policy learning preserves multimodal behavior, enabling the generation of diverse and contextually appropriate actions for complex robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic visuomotor policy learning has seen significant advancements, particularly with the adoption of generative models from the vision community. This paper, CARP, positions itself at the intersection of two prominent generative modeling paradigms: Autoregressive Modeling (AM) and Diffusion Modeling (DM), aiming to resolve their inherent trade-offs for robotic applications.

**Autoregressive Models (AM)**, often implemented with GPT-style decoders and techniques like action chunking, are highly efficient, flexible, and have mature exploration due to lower computational requirements. However, the paper points out that their "next-token prediction" paradigm often struggles to capture long-range dependencies, global action structure, and temporal coherence. This limitation can lead to poor performance in complex robotic tasks, as their "myopic" nature restricts generalizability in scenarios requiring bidirectional reasoning or holistic understanding of action sequences. Examples of conventional AM in robotics include Behavior Transformers (BET) and those used in Decision Transformers.

In contrast, **Diffusion Models (DM)** have recently emerged as a powerful alternative, demonstrating impressive performance and precision in visuomotor policy learning. By modeling multimodal distributions and gradually refining action sequences from noise, DMs like Diffusion Policy (DP) bridge the accuracy gap left by AMs. However, their reliance on multiple sequential denoising steps makes them computationally intensive, leading to significant runtime inefficiency. This is a critical drawback for real-time robotic tasks, especially in environments with constrained onboard computing. Furthermore, the rigid Markovian assumption of DDPMs limits their flexibility and can lead to cumulative errors over long time horizons.

**CARP's Contribution to the Landscape:**
CARP proposes a novel generative paradigm that directly addresses this fundamental trade-off. It aims to combine the high performance and multimodal capabilities characteristic of DM with the inference efficiency and flexibility inherent in AM. The core idea is to redefine autoregressive action generation as a **coarse-to-fine, next-scale prediction** process. This approach is inspired by recent advancements in visual autoregressive modeling (e.g., VAR) which have shown that AR models can achieve performance comparable to, or even surpass, diffusion models in image generation.

The broader research landscape for visuomotor policy learning also includes approaches like Implicit Behavior Cloning (IBC), which uses energy-based models, and various forms of explicit policies (e.g., those using action discretization or mixture density networks). While these offer different trade-offs, they often face issues like action space explosion, hyperparameter sensitivity, or training complexity.

By introducing multi-scale action tokenization and a coarse-to-fine autoregressive prediction mechanism, CARP provides a fresh perspective. It seeks to develop a robust, efficient, and flexible framework that can pave the way for more adaptive and versatile visuomotor policies, especially appealing for real-world deployment where both speed and accuracy are paramount. The paper's emphasis on GPT-style architectures also aligns with the growing trend of leveraging large language model principles for broader AI tasks, including embodied intelligence and vision-language-action (VLA) models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind CARP stems from the inherent limitations and trade-offs observed in existing generative models for robotic visuomotor policy learning. The research team identifies a critical dilemma:
- **Autoregressive Models (AMs)**, while efficient and flexible, often suffer from **suboptimal performance** due to their inability to effectively capture long-range dependencies, global action structure, and temporal coherence in action sequences. Their "next-token prediction" approach can be myopic, leading to less precise and less stable actions.
- **Diffusion Models (DMs)**, on the other hand, achieve **high performance and accuracy** by effectively modeling multimodal distributions and refining actions iteratively. However, this iterative denoising process incurs **significant computational costs and inefficiency**, making them impractical for real-time inference in many robotic applications, especially on resource-constrained hardware. They also lack flexibility due to rigid generative processes.

Given this context, the **key objectives** of the CARP project are:
1. **Achieve DM-level Performance with AM-level Efficiency:** To develop a novel policy learning paradigm that can generate highly accurate and smooth actions, matching or even surpassing the performance of state-of-the-art diffusion-based policies, while maintaining the inference efficiency characteristic of autoregressive models.
2. **Enhance Flexibility:** To create a model that is flexible and adaptable, avoiding the complex constraints and rigid generative processes often associated with diffusion models, and leveraging the inherent adaptability of GPT-style architectures.
3. **Address AM's Limitations:** To overcome the "myopic" limitations of traditional autoregressive models by explicitly capturing global action structure and maintaining temporal locality across the entire action sequence.
4. **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalizability:** To validate the proposed approach across diverse settings, including single-task and multi-task scenarios, state-based and image-based simulation benchmarks, and real-world robotic tasks.

In essence, CARP is motivated by the need for a practical and powerful generative policy for robotics that does not force a compromise between high accuracy and real-time operational speed, thereby enabling more capable and deployable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CARP (Coarse-to-Fine AutoRegressive Policy) introduces a novel two-stage framework to achieve its objectives: **Multi-Scale Action Tokenization** and **Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction**.

**Stage 1: Multi-Scale Action Tokenization**
The first stage focuses on creating a rich, hierarchical representation of action sequences, moving beyond simple step-by-step actions.
- **Action Sequence Encoding:** Instead of raw actions, CARP learns multi-scale representations of the entire action sequence. An action autoencoder, based on the VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder) architecture, is employed.
- **Temporal 1D CNNs:** Given the inherent temporal dependency in action sequences and the orthogonality of action dimensions (e.g., position vs. rotation), a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is used for both the encoder and decoder. This captures local correlations along the time dimension, preserving temporal locality.
- **Multi-Scale Quantization:** A modified multi-scale quantization layer, inspired by residual-style designs, encodes the action sequence into *K* discrete token maps (R = (r1, r2..., rK)). Each token map r_k represents the sequence at a different scale or granularity (e.g., r1 being coarse, rK being fine). A shared codebook `Z` is used across all scales.
- **Robust Rotation Representation:** To handle the discontinuities in rotation space (e.g., Euler angles, quaternions), `rotation6d` is utilized for stable tokenization. Cosine similarity is used as the distance function in the quantizer for stability.
- **Orthogonal Dimension Handling:** For enhanced stability during training, a separate VQ-VAE is trained for each dimension of the action space, rather than a single joint VQ-VAE for all dimensions.
- **Loss Function:** The VQ-VAE is trained using a standard loss, comprising a reconstruction loss (L_recon) to minimize the difference between original and reconstructed actions, a quantization loss (L_quant) to align quantized features, and a commitment loss (L_commit) to prevent codebook collapse.
- **Benefit:** This stage effectively converts continuous action sequences into discrete, multi-scale latent representations, capturing both local temporal dependencies and global structural features, thus addressing the "myopic" nature of traditional AMs.

**Stage 2: Coarse-to-Fine Autoregressive Prediction**
Once the action tokenizer is trained, the second stage leverages these multi-scale token maps for a novel prediction strategy.
- **GPT-Style Transformer:** A standard decoder-only transformer, akin to GPT-2, is used as the core prediction model.
- **Next-Scale Prediction:** The model reformulates autoregressive prediction from "next-token" to "next-scale." It predicts action token maps progressively from the coarsest (r1) to the finest (rK) granularity. The likelihood is factorized as p(r1..., rK) = Π p(r_k | r1..., r_{k-1}; s_O), where each scale's prediction is conditioned on all previously predicted coarser scales and historical observations (s_O).
- **Relaxed Markovian Assumption:** Unlike DMs that strictly rely on the previous step (Markovian), CARP allows each scale `r_k` to depend on *all* prior scales `r_1:k-1`, enabling better context integration and requiring fewer refinement steps.
- **State Conditioning:** Historical states `s_O` are used to generate the initial coarse-scale token map and then as adaptive normalization for subsequent predictions, grounding the action generation in the current observation.
- **Loss Function:** A standard **Cross-Entropy loss** is used for training the transformer. This choice is crucial as it naturally preserves multimodality, unlike MSE loss which tends to enforce a unimodal distribution detrimental to diverse robotic tasks.
- **Inference:** During inference, the transformer autoregressively predicts token maps from coarse to fine without teacher forcing. Kv-caching is employed to optimize computation. The full set of predicted token maps (r1:K) is then passed through the VQ-VAE decoder to yield continuous, executable actions.
- **Benefit:** This approach enables holistic trajectory generation, refining high-level intentions into fine-grained details efficiently. It produces high-quality trajectories with significantly fewer steps compared to DMs by modeling actions directly in a low-dimensional latent space, mitigating trajectory anomalies.

**Implementation Details:**
- `rotation6d` for stable rotation representation.
- Cosine similarity for quantization distance.
- Separate VQVAE for each action dimension.
- EMA (Exponential Moving Average) is used during training for stability and performance.
- Inference leverages kv-caching and no causal mask is needed, enabling faster prediction.

This two-stage approach allows CARP to leverage the strengths of both generative paradigms: the efficient and flexible structure of autoregressive models, combined with a hierarchical representation and prediction strategy that facilitates high-quality, stable, and multimodal action generation, traditionally associated with diffusion models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations of CARP across various settings consistently demonstrate its effectiveness in balancing high performance, efficiency, and flexibility, addressing the research questions posed in the paper.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Single-Task Simulations (Robomimic, Kitchen, Push-T)

- **Accuracy (RQ1):** CARP consistently achieved competitive, often superior, success rates compared to state-of-the-art diffusion policies (DP-C, DP-T) and significantly outperformed traditional autoregressive policies (BET). For instance, in the state-based Robomimic tasks (Lift, Can, Square), CARP achieved 1.00, 1.00, and 0.98 success rates, matching or slightly surpassing DP variants (Table 1). Similar trends were observed in image-based settings (Table 2) and the multi-stage Kitchen task, where CARP matched DP's near-perfect performance for p1-p4 (Table 3).
- **Efficiency (RQ2):** A standout finding is CARP's remarkable inference efficiency. It demonstrated approximately **10 times faster inference speed** compared to diffusion policies. For example, in the state-based Square task, CARP inferred 400 actions in 3.07 seconds, whereas DP-C took 35.21 seconds and DP-T took 37.83 seconds (Table 1). This efficiency gain was consistent across all single-task benchmarks, including image-based scenarios (e.g., 4.83 s for CARP vs. 47.37 s for DP-C in image-based Lift, Table 2).
- **Model Size:** CARP also achieved these results with significantly fewer parameters, typically using only **1-5% of the parameters** required by diffusion models (e.g., 0.65M for CARP vs. 65.88M for DP-C in state-based tasks, Table 1).
- **Multimodal Behavior (Push-T):** On the Push-T task, designed to assess multimodal behavior, CARP generated diverse and consistent predictions from the same initialization, outperforming baselines in both task success (0.88 for CARP vs. 0.84 for DP-T) and inference speed (2.66s vs. 29.56s) (Figure 5). This highlights the effectiveness of using Cross-Entropy loss for preserving multimodality.
- **Trajectory Quality:** Visualizations showed that CARP produced smoother and more consistent trajectories than Diffusion Policy (Figure 6, left panels), and achieved accurate predictions within just 4 coarse-to-fine steps, demonstrating superior efficiency over DP's numerous and often redundant denoising steps (Figure 6, right panels).
- **Data Efficiency:** CARP consistently outperformed baselines across varying training dataset sizes, demonstrating robustness to data scarcity (Figure 12).
- **Training Efficiency:** While CARP includes an additional tokenizer pretraining phase, the total training cost, when amortized across tasks or when considering only the policy learning stage, was comparable to that of DP (Figure 11).

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Multi-Task Simulations (MimicGen)

- **Performance (RQ1) & Flexibility (RQ3):** CARP achieved up to a **25% average improvement in success rates** compared to state-of-the-art diffusion-based multi-task policies like Sparse Diffusion Policy (SDP) and Task-Conditioned Diffusion (TCD) (Table 4). For example, CARP achieved an average success rate of 0.85 across 8 tasks, significantly higher than SDP's 0.78 and TCD's 0.68. This demonstrates CARP's strong generalization and adaptability to diverse tasks with minimal architectural modifications (adding a task embedding).
- **Efficiency (RQ2):** In multi-task settings, CARP maintained its efficiency advantage, showing **over 10 times faster inference speed** (6.92 s for CARP vs. 112.39 s for SDP for 400 actions) and using only **10% of the parameters** compared to SDP (16.08M vs. 159.85M) (Table 4).
- **Fine-Grained Manipulation:** For tasks requiring high precision like Nut Assembly and Threading, CARP outperformed diffusion methods by up to 20% in success rates and consistently showed lower mean error and reduced variance in insertion precision (Tables 8, 9), further emphasizing its capability in complex, contact-rich scenarios.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Real-World Tasks (UR5e, Franka Emika Panda)

- **Real-world Applicability (RQ1):** CARP demonstrated robust performance on real-world manipulation tasks (Cup, Bowl on UR5e, and One Leg on Franka Panda). It achieved comparable or up to a **10% improvement in success rate** over Diffusion Policy (DP) (Figure 7).
- **Real-time Efficiency (RQ2):** On limited computational resources (NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2060 GPU), CARP achieved approximately **8 times faster inference** than DP, making it highly suitable for real-time robotic applications where onboard computation is constrained (Figure 7). Visualizations confirmed smooth and successful trajectories in these real-world scenarios (Figure 18). CARP also showed better performance in precision-critical phases like "Grasp" and "Insert" on the Franka Panda arm (Figure 20).

In summary, CARP consistently delivers high task performance, superior inference efficiency, and notable flexibility across a comprehensive range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CARP represents a significant advancement in robotic visuomotor policy learning, primarily by **effectively resolving the long-standing trade-off between high performance and high inference efficiency** that has challenged previous autoregressive and diffusion-based approaches. This achievement has several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Real-time, High-Performance Robotics:** The most immediate impact of CARP is its ability to deliver DM-like accuracy with AM-like inference speeds. This makes it highly suitable for **real-time robotic applications** where rapid, precise action generation is critical and computational resources might be limited (e.g., edge devices, mobile robots, factory automation). Previously, developers often had to choose between slow but accurate policies or fast but imprecise ones; CARP eliminates this compromise, paving the way for more sophisticated and responsive robotic behaviors in practical settings.

2. **Novel Generative Paradigm:** The introduction of a "coarse-to-fine, next-scale autoregressive prediction" paradigm is a methodological breakthrough. By carefully designing multi-scale action tokenization and relaxing the strict Markovian assumptions of diffusion models, CARP offers an elegant and efficient way to capture both global structure and temporal locality in action sequences. This new approach could inspire further research into hierarchical generative modeling across various domains beyond robotics.

3. **Enhanced Flexibility and Generalizability:** CARP's foundation on GPT-style decoder-only transformers makes it inherently flexible. Its seamless transition from single-task to multi-task learning with minimal modifications, as demonstrated by its superior performance in multi-task benchmarks, highlights its potential for building **more generalizable and versatile robot policies**. This flexibility also positions CARP well for future integration with multimodal inputs (e.g., tactile, auditory information) and for addressing long-term dependency reasoning, moving towards more intelligent and adaptive embodied AI systems.

4. **Reduced Computational Overhead:** The significantly lower parameter count of CARP compared to diffusion models (often 1-5% of parameters) is crucial for resource-constrained environments. Combined with faster inference, this translates to lower energy consumption and hardware requirements, making advanced robotic control more accessible and scalable.

5. **Multimodality Preservation:** By utilizing Cross-Entropy loss, CARP effectively preserves the potential for multimodal predictions, addressing a weakness of traditional autoregressive models trained with MSE. This allows CARP to generate diverse and contextually appropriate actions, which is vital for tasks with multiple valid solutions or contact-rich dynamics.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper concludes by outlining promising avenues for future work, which themselves highlight CARP's foundational significance:
 - **Architectural Simplification:** Developing a unified one-stage model could further streamline the framework.
 - **Expanded Multimodal Capacity:** Deeper exploration of CARP's multimodal reasoning capabilities, especially in tasks requiring diverse output distributions.
 - **Leveraging GPT-style Capabilities:** Exploiting in-context learning for few-shot and zero-shot generalization, and integrating with broader vision-language-action (VLA) models for general-purpose embodied intelligence (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA).
 - **Scaling Laws:** Applying scaling laws from existing GPT-style models to CARP, suggesting substantial performance gains with increased model capacity and larger datasets.

In conclusion, CARP offers a compelling solution to a critical problem in robotic policy learning. Its superior balance of performance and efficiency, coupled with inherent flexibility, positions it as a strong candidate for next-generation robotic control systems and a catalyst for further research into advanced generative AI for embodied intelligence.
