# Evolution Strategies at Scale: LLM Fine-Tuning Beyond Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Cognizant AI Lab demonstrated that Evolution Strategies can successfully fine-tune Large Language Models with up to 8 billion parameters in their full parameter space. This method consistently outperformed or matched state-of-the-art Reinforcement Learning baselines on various tasks, showing increased robustness and stability, even with a small population size.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) based fine-tuning of LLMs often suffers from low sample efficiency, high variance with long-horizon rewards, sensitivity to base LLM choice, reward hacking, and training instability.
- A long-standing assumption in the AI community was that Evolution Strategies (ES) were not scalable for fine-tuning modern Large Language Models with billions of parameters, limiting their application.
- Prior attempts to apply ES to LLMs resorted to dimensionality reduction, such as tuning only the last layer or low-dimensional adapters, or searching in action space instead of full parameter space.

## Method

- The authors implemented a simplified variant of ==Evolution Strategies (ES)== to directly optimize the full parameter space of LLMs, without using dimensionality reduction.
- They introduced seven key implementation details, including ==noise retrieval with random seeds==, ==parallel evaluations==, and ==layer-level in-place parameter perturbation==, to manage memory and enable scalability to billions of parameters.
- The approach intentionally avoided common algorithmic enhancements to demonstrate the fundamental efficacy of the core ES algorithm for LLM fine-tuning at scale.

## Results

- Achieved full-parameter fine-tuning of LLMs (up to 8 billion parameters), overturning the assumption that ES is unscalable for such model sizes.
- Consistently outperformed PPO, GRPO, and Dr.GRPO variants on the Countdown task across Qwen2.5 (**0.5B**-**7B**) and Llama3 (**1B**-**8B**) models, demonstrating superior performance on long-horizon rewards.
- Showed superior reward-KL divergence tradeoffs and resistance to reward hacking on the Conciseness task compared to GRPO, and achieved significant performance improvements on math reasoning benchmarks and puzzle tasks (e.g., ARC-AGI accuracy increased from **0.2%** to **29.5%** on Qwen-2.5-**14B**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolution Strategies can effectively scale to fine-tune LLMs with billions of parameters, using a remarkably small population size (e.g., 30) for successful optimization.
> - ES provides greater robustness, stability, and resistance to reward hacking compared to gradient-based RL, especially for tasks with sparse, long-horizon outcome rewards.
> - Operating as an inference-only mechanism, ES offers significant engineering advantages, including substantial GPU memory savings and massive parallelization capabilities for LLM training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) is a rapidly evolving field, with the predominant paradigm currently dominated by **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**. Techniques such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), often combined with Human Feedback (RLHF), have been instrumental in aligning LLMs with human preferences and adapting them to specific downstream tasks. This approach has led to the development of many state-of-the-art LLMs.

However, the authors point out several inherent challenges associated with RL-based fine-tuning. These include low sample efficiency and high variance when dealing with long-horizon or outcome-only rewards (common in reasoning tasks), sensitivity to the choice of base LLM, susceptibility to "reward hacking" (where models exploit loopholes in the reward function rather than achieving the desired behavior), and training instability across multiple runs. These limitations suggest a need for alternative fine-tuning methodologies that can address these issues while maintaining or improving performance.

**Evolution Strategies (ES)**, a class of population-based zeroth-order optimization algorithms, has long been recognized for its advantages in traditional control and gaming problems, such as natural parallelization, tolerance for long-horizon rewards, broad exploration, and robustness. Despite these theoretical benefits, ES has largely been "overlooked" for LLM fine-tuning due to a pervasive and long-standing assumption: that it **does not scale** to modern model sizes with billions of parameters. Previous successful applications of ES to neural networks typically involved models with only a few million parameters. Attempts to use ES with LLMs have often resorted to dimensionality reduction, such as fine-tuning only the last layer or low-dimensional adapters, or performing evolutionary search in action space rather than parameter space.

This paper directly challenges and **overturns this widespread assumption**. It represents a critical departure from the current RL-centric paradigm by demonstrating the first successful application of ES to **full-parameter fine-tuning of LLMs at the billion-parameter scale, without dimensionality reduction**. By doing so, it fundamentally expands the design space for post-training algorithms, introducing a "backpropagation-free post-training paradigm" that offers a distinct alternative to gradient-based RL methods. This work positions ES not just as a viable alternative but as a powerful, distinct approach that can address RL's shortcomings and open new avenues for LLM development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **demonstrate that Evolution Strategies (ES) can successfully scale to fine-tune Large Language Models (LLMs) with billions of parameters, specifically through direct optimization of their full parameter space without any form of dimensionality reduction**. This directly aims to challenge and disprove the prevailing assumption within the AI community that ES methods are inherently unscalable for modern, large-scale models.

Derived from this overarching goal, the authors are motivated to explore whether ES can offer superior performance and robustness compared to established Reinforcement Learning (RL) implementations, which currently dominate LLM fine-tuning. Their specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing RL's limitations:** They aim to show that ES can mitigate several known weaknesses of RL-based fine-tuning, such as low sample efficiency and high variance with long-horizon/delayed rewards, sensitivity to the choice of base LLM, susceptibility to reward hacking, and training instability.
- **Demonstrating effectiveness with outcome-only rewards:** A key objective is to prove ES's efficacy in tasks where only sparse, long-horizon outcome rewards are available, which are particularly challenging for gradient-based RL methods.
- **Evaluating robustness across diverse base LLMs:** They seek to establish that ES can consistently provide good fine-tuning performance across various LLM families and sizes, unlike RL which often exhibits inconsistent results.
- **Investigating resistance to reward hacking:** The paper aims to show that ES, by optimizing a solution distribution rather than a single solution, is less prone to reward hacking, leading to more desirable and aligned model behaviors.
- **Assessing training stability and cost efficiency:** They intend to demonstrate that ES fine-tuning is more consistent across different runs, thereby reducing the computational and developmental cost associated with hyperparameter tuning and repeated experiments.
- **Leveraging backpropagation-free optimization:** A foundational motivation is to highlight the advantages of an inference-only, gradient-free fine-tuning mechanism, which simplifies implementation, saves GPU memory, and enables highly parallelized training.

Ultimately, the core motivation is to establish ES as a powerful, scalable, and general post-training paradigm for LLMs, thereby expanding the algorithmic toolkit available to researchers and practitioners beyond the current gradient-based RL approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around implementing a **simplified variant of Evolution Strategies (ES)**, specifically inspired by Natural Evolution Strategies (NES) and OpenAI ES, but without many common algorithmic enhancements. This deliberate simplification aims to isolate and demonstrate the fundamental scalability and efficacy of ES itself, rather than relying on auxiliary improvements.

The fundamental ES algorithm used operates as follows (as detailed in Algorithm 1):
1. **Population Sampling:** In each iteration, `N` perturbed models are created by adding random Gaussian noise (εn) to the current model parameters (θt-1). This noise is i.i.d. (independently and identically distributed) in each parameter dimension and scaled by a hyperparameter `σ`.
2. **Reward Evaluation:** Each perturbed model is evaluated to obtain a reward score (Rn) from the target reward function R(·).
3. **Reward Normalization:** The collected rewards (Rn) for the current population are normalized using z-scores.
4. **Parameter Update:** The model parameters are updated by aggregating the sampled perturbations, weighted by their normalized reward scores. The update rule is simplified to `θt ← θt-1 + α · (1/N) * Σ(Rn * εn)`, where `α` is the learning rate.

To enable ES to scale to billions of parameters and operate efficiently, the authors introduce **seven key implementation details** (Algorithm 2 in Appendix A.1):

1. **Noise Retrieval with Random Seeds:** Instead of storing large perturbation tensors, only random seeds are stored. This significantly reduces GPU memory usage, as the exact noise can be regenerated on demand.
2. **Parallel Evaluations:** The `N` perturbed models are evaluated fully in parallel across multiple processes/GPUs, with each process assigned a unique random seed. This capitalizes on ES's inherent parallelizability.
3. **Layer-level In-place Perturbation and Restoration:** To manage peak GPU memory usage, model parameters are perturbed one layer at a time in-place. After evaluation, the original parameters are restored by subtracting the same noise, again in-place and layer by layer. This avoids needing to hold entire perturbed models in memory.
4. **Reward Normalization:** Rewards are normalized using z-scores within each iteration, ensuring a consistent reward scale across different iterations and tasks, which aids stable optimization.
5. **Greedy Decoding:** Perturbed models use greedy decoding for response generation during reward evaluation. This ensures deterministic evaluation based purely on parameter space exploration, rather than variations from action space stochasticity.
6. **Decomposition of Parameter Update:** The aggregated update of model parameters is also performed in-place and decomposed layer by layer, further minimizing peak GPU memory requirements.
7. **Learning Rate Digestion:** The `1/σ` term from the standard ES update equation is absorbed into the learning rate `α`, simplifying the computational and parametric setup.

Notably, the authors intentionally **avoided common algorithmic enhancements** found in other ES implementations (e.g., rank transformation, mirrored sampling, weight decay, virtual batch normalization, Adam optimizer). This choice emphasizes that the core ES algorithm, with its memory-efficient and parallelized implementation, is sufficient to achieve strong performance at scale, providing a clean baseline for future work.

The evaluation approach involves comprehensive empirical studies across various LLMs (Qwen2.5, Llama3 families) and tasks, directly comparing ES performance against state-of-the-art RL baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence that Evolution Strategies (ES) can effectively scale to fine-tune Large Language Models (LLMs) with billions of parameters, outperforming or matching state-of-the-art Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods across multiple dimensions. The main findings are:

1. **Scalability to Billion-Parameter Models:** The most significant finding is the successful application of ES to full-parameter fine-tuning of LLMs (up to 8 billion parameters) without dimensionality reduction. This overturns the long-held assumption that ES is unscalable for modern model sizes.
2. **Superior Performance on Long-Horizon Rewards (Countdown Task):**
 - ES consistently and substantially improved over PPO, GRPO, and Dr.GRPO (including their variants) across all tested Qwen2.5 (0.5B–7B) and Llama3 (1B–8B) models in the Countdown task. This task, with its sparse, long-horizon outcome rewards, is particularly challenging for gradient-based RL methods.
 - ES demonstrated robustness across different LLM families and sizes, while RL methods often showed inconsistent performance and required extensive hyperparameter tuning for each model.
3. **Extremely Small Population Size:** Contrary to prior ES applications that required populations of 10,000+ for models with millions of parameters, this work achieved strong results with a remarkably small population size of just 30 in a multi-billion-parameter space. This makes the approach feasible without extensive compute.
4. **Robustness Against Reward Hacking (Conciseness Task):**
 - In the conciseness fine-tuning task, ES achieved superior reward-KL divergence tradeoffs (a dominant Pareto front) compared to GRPO.
 - Crucially, ES achieved these superior tradeoffs *without* any explicit KL divergence penalty, whereas GRPO often resorted to "reward hacking" (producing short but nonsensical responses) when the KL penalty was too low. ES did not exhibit reward hacking despite optimizing only for conciseness, suggesting it finds fundamentally different types of solutions.
5. **Improved Training Stability and Consistency:**
 - ES fine-tuning showed significantly greater consistency across independent runs (lower standard deviation in reward and KL divergence) compared to GRPO. This reduces the cost and uncertainty associated with fine-tuning LLMs.
6. **Inference-Only Optimization:** ES operates as an inference-only fine-tuning mechanism, requiring no gradient calculations. This saves significant GPU memory and simplifies the engineering pipeline compared to gradient-based RL.
7. **Competitive Performance on Math Reasoning Benchmarks:**
 - ES fine-tuned models on the MATH dataset achieved significant improvements over base models and were competitive with strong, established RL baselines (SimpleRL-Zero, OpenReasoner, OatZero) across various math reasoning benchmarks (OlympiadBench, MATH500, Minerva, AIME2024, AMC). This highlights ES's generality in enhancing LLM reasoning.
8. **Solving Challenging Puzzle Problems:**
 - ES fine-tuning significantly improved performance on two challenging puzzle tasks: ARC-AGI (from 0.2% to 29.5% accuracy on Qwen-2.5-14B) and Sudoku (from 2.5% to 69.5% accuracy on Qwen-2.5-3B). This demonstrates ES's broad applicability to diverse problem types beyond standard language tasks.

Overall, the findings establish ES as a powerful, scalable, robust, and general fine-tuning method that consistently outperforms or is competitive with RL, while offering distinct algorithmic and engineering advantages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper marks a pivotal moment in the field of LLM fine-tuning by fundamentally challenging and disproving the long-standing assumption that Evolution Strategies (ES) cannot scale to modern, billion-parameter models. Its significance and potential impact are profound, spanning algorithmic advancements, engineering benefits, and broader societal implications.

**Algorithmic and Theoretical Significance:**

- **New Paradigm for LLM Fine-Tuning:** The paper establishes ES as a viable and powerful backpropagation-free alternative to gradient-based Reinforcement Learning (RL). This expands the algorithmic design space for post-training LLMs, offering a fresh perspective on optimization in high-dimensional, complex landscapes.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Stability:** ES's inherent advantages, such as tolerance for long-horizon rewards, robustness across diverse base LLMs, and consistent performance across runs, directly address key limitations of current RL methods. Its exploration in parameter space (rather than action space) leads to lower variance in rollouts and more reliable gradient estimation, effectively smoothing out jagged reward landscapes.
- **Resistance to Reward Hacking:** By optimizing a distribution of solutions, ES is intrinsically less susceptible to reward hacking, a critical issue in AI safety and alignment. This makes ES-tuned models potentially more aligned with intended objectives and less prone to exploitative behaviors.
- **Insights into LLM Optimization:** The counterintuitive finding that a small population size (e.g., 30) can effectively optimize billions of parameters opens new research directions. This could be linked to the concept of low intrinsic dimensionality in LLMs, offering deeper insights into how these massive models learn and generalize.
- **Unsupervised Fine-Tuning Potential:** The gradient-free nature of ES opens the door to novel unsupervised fine-tuning based on internal LLM behaviors (e.g., confidence, semantic density), which is not possible with action-space RL. This could lead to more autonomous and powerful LLM self-improvement mechanisms.

**Engineering and Practical Impact:**

- **Democratization of LLM Fine-Tuning:** ES significantly lowers the barrier to entry for fine-tuning LLMs. Unlike RL, which demands expert mathematical understanding and extensive hyperparameter tuning, ES requires only assigning a score to a model's output. This simplicity can democratize LLM deployment, enabling non-experts to develop customized AI applications.
- **Reduced Computational Overhead:** Being an inference-only mechanism, ES eliminates the need for gradient calculation and communication, saving substantial GPU memory. This is crucial for scaling to even larger models and reduces the engineering complexity associated with distributed training setups prevalent in modern RL frameworks.
- **Massive Parallelization:** ES is trivially parallelizable, allowing for straightforward scaling across thousands of GPUs or even data centers. This can dramatically reduce wall-clock training time or enable the use of much larger populations for more extensive exploration.
- **Specialized Inference Kernels:** The inference-only nature of ES allows for the development of specialized inference kernels optimized for repeated forward passes and parameter perturbations, potentially leading to further performance gains not achievable with gradient-based training.

**Broader Societal Impact:**

- **Improved AI Safety and Alignment:** The reduced susceptibility to reward hacking makes ES a promising tool for developing more ethically aligned and safer LLMs. It can help maintain ethical guardrails and potentially simplify the process of fine-tuning for desired ethical behaviors, even for non-experts.
- **Accelerated AI Development:** By providing a robust, scalable, and easier-to-use fine-tuning method, ES can accelerate the development and deployment of LLMs across scientific, engineering, and commercial workflows, fostering innovation across various sectors.

In conclusion, this paper fundamentally shifts the understanding of ES's capabilities, positioning it as a foundational and powerful alternative to RL for LLM post-training. It promises a future where LLM fine-tuning is more accessible, stable, robust, and potentially safer, opening up new avenues for AI research and application.
