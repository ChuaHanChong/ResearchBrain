# Debiasing 6-DOF IMU via Hierarchical Learning of Continuous Bias Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep learning framework learns continuous dynamics of 6-DOF IMU biases using Neural Ordinary Differential Equations on Lie groups. The approach debiases IMU data without requiring ground truth bias, leveraging only pose ground truth for training, and significantly improves pure IMU integration accuracy and Visual-Inertial Odometry performance.

## Problem

- Low-cost 6-DOF IMUs exhibit significant, complex, and time-varying biases that lead to rapid error accumulation and drift in integrated pose estimates.
- Traditional IMU calibration models oversimplify bias dynamics, failing to capture real-world complexities.
- Prior learning-based debiasing methods either generalize poorly due to implicit learning of motion patterns or demand elusive ground truth bias data for explicit bias modeling.

## Method

- A deep learning framework explicitly models continuous 6-DOF IMU bias dynamics using **Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs)**, utilizing ==Lie group theory== to integrate IMU kinematics on the ==SO(3) manifold== for geometrically consistent debiasing.
- A novel loss formulation uses readily available pose ground truth instead of direct bias ground truth for training, overcoming a key data acquisition challenge.
- A ==hierarchical training strategy==, debiasing gyroscope errors before accelerometer errors, improves training efficiency and network design.

## Results

- Reduced pure IMU integration orientation errors by over **90%** (e.g., EUROC average AOE from **120.78 deg** to **2.40 deg**) and consistently outperformed baselines on public datasets.
- Improved the accuracy and robustness of Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) systems, notably enabling successful VIO operation on TUM-VI where raw IMU data caused failures.
- Demonstrated generalization on an unseen real-world dataset (FETCH), maintaining accuracy advantages over baselines, while noting that simple linear models can be competitive for simple motion patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly learning continuous IMU bias dynamics via Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) on Lie groups is effective and geometrically consistent.
> - Accurate IMU debiasing can be achieved by training with readily available pose ground truth, circumventing the need for elusive ground truth bias data.
> - A hierarchical training approach, starting with gyroscope debiasing, simplifies the optimization of coupled IMU kinematic models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) are ubiquitous in modern robotics, serving as foundational sensors for state estimation in applications ranging from Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) to legged robot control. However, the pervasive issue of noise and, more critically, time-varying biases in low-cost IMUs significantly compromises their accuracy, leading to drift in pose estimates. This problem is exacerbated in scenarios where other sensors (e.g., cameras in VIO) might fail due to environmental conditions, leaving the IMU as the sole source of motion information.

Historically, IMU inaccuracies have been addressed through **model-based calibration**. These methods typically model biases as constant or follow a simple Brownian motion. While effective for static or slowly varying biases, this simplified approach often falls short in capturing the complex, non-linear, and time-varying nature of real-world IMU biases, which can be influenced by temperature, vibration, and motion patterns.

With the rise of deep learning, researchers have increasingly turned to **learning-based calibration methods** to overcome the limitations of traditional models:
- **Implicit Learning of Correction Terms:** Many approaches, such as those by Brossard et al., Huang et al., and Liu et al., employ neural networks (e.g., CNNs, LSTMs, TCNs) to learn a direct correction term for raw IMU data. While showing promise, a significant drawback is their inability to distinguish whether the learned correction compensates for IMU-intrinsic errors or for specific motion patterns present in the training data. This can lead to poor generalization across different motion contexts.
- **Learning Motion Patterns from IMU Data:** Another line of research (e.g., Yan et al., Herath et al., Liu et al.) focuses on learning displacement or full pose directly from IMU data. These methods are typically data-intensive, motion-context-sensitive, and sometimes rely on already calibrated IMU data, limiting their scope for raw IMU debiasing.

More recently, the concept of **explicitly modeling IMU bias evolution** using neural networks has emerged, as proposed by Buchanan et al.. This approach aims to learn the underlying bias dynamics themselves, which ideally leads to motion-independent calibration. However, a major bottleneck of this explicit learning paradigm has been the stringent requirement for ground truth bias data during training. Obtaining precise bias ground truth is notoriously difficult, often necessitating complex multi-sensor fusion algorithms, which introduces dependency on the accuracy and assumptions of those fusion methods.

This paper distinguishes itself by addressing this critical challenge head-on. It *explicitly* learns the continuous dynamics of IMU biases but crucially *bypasses the need for ground truth bias data*. Instead, it leverages widely available pose ground truth, making the approach significantly more practical and interpretable. By adopting a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) framework and extending it to the matrix Lie group, the authors provide a principled and efficient way to model continuous bias evolution, thus pushing the boundaries of deep learning for robust IMU calibration. This work represents a significant step towards enabling more reliable and generalizable IMU-based state estimation in diverse robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a deep learning-based framework for the **online debiasing of 6-degrees-of-freedom (6-DOF) IMU gyroscopes and accelerometers**. This is motivated by several critical challenges inherent in IMU-based state estimation for robotic applications:

1. **Inaccuracy of Low-Cost IMUs:** Low-cost IMUs, commonly used in many robotic systems, suffer from significant noise and time-varying biases. These inaccuracies lead to rapid accumulation of errors in integrated pose estimates, compromising the performance of crucial tasks like Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) and pure inertial navigation.
2. **Limitations of Traditional Calibration:** Conventional IMU calibration models often simplify bias dynamics (e.g., as constant or Brownian motion), failing to capture complex, non-linear, and context-dependent variations influenced by factors like temperature and vibration.
3. **Ambiguity in Implicit Learning Methods:** Prior learning-based methods that implicitly learn a correction term from raw IMU data struggle to differentiate between corrections for IMU-intrinsic errors and those for specific motion patterns. This leads to models that are less interpretable and generalize poorly to unseen motion contexts.
4. **Data Dependency of Explicit Bias Learning:** While explicit bias modeling (learning the bias dynamics directly) offers a more interpretable and motion-independent alternative, existing approaches (e.g., Buchanan et al.) demand ground truth bias data for training. Such data is rarely available and is often derived from other sensor fusion algorithms, making the bias estimation dependent on the accuracy of the fusion process itself.
5. **Criticality in Sensor Failure Scenarios:** In environments where other sensors like cameras may fail (e.g., low light, texture-less environments), the IMU becomes the sole source of odometry information. In such cases, the accuracy of raw IMU measurements is paramount for maintaining robust state estimation.

To address these motivations, the authors outline four key contributions:

1. **Novel Loss Formulation without Bias Ground Truth:** Proposing a new loss function that leverages readily available pose ground truth, thereby bypassing the problematic requirement for explicit bias ground truth during training. This loss formulation also features a hierarchical structure for improved training efficiency.
2. **Continuous Bias Dynamics with NODE on Lie Algebra:** Modeling bias dynamics as continuous vector fields in the Lie algebra using a Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) framework. This approach offers a lightweight network architecture, enabling the learning of continuous bias trajectories and delivering superior performance.
3. **Comprehensive Validation:** Evaluating the proposed method on two widely recognized public datasets (EUROC, TUM-VI) and one real-world experiment (FETCH dataset). This demonstrates the method's ability to provide more accurate IMU measurements and its generalization capabilities.
4. **Open-Source Release:** Providing an open-source implementation of their method to facilitate reproducibility and further research in the community.

In essence, the core objective is to make explicit, learning-based IMU debiasing practical and effective by solving the critical problem of bias ground truth availability, while simultaneously leveraging advanced mathematical tools (Lie group theory and NODEs) for a robust and efficient solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces a novel deep learning framework to explicitly learn and compensate for IMU biases, built upon Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) and Lie group theory.

#### 3.1. IMU Measurement Model and Bias Dynamics

The conventional IMU measurement model is adopted: $\tilde{\omega}_t = \omega_t + b^g_t + n^g_t$ and $\tilde{a}_t = a_t + b^a_t + n^a_t$, where $\tilde{\omega}_t$ and $\tilde{a}_t$ are raw measurements, $\omega_t$ and $a_t$ are true values, $b^g_t$ and $b^a_t$ are biases, and $n^g_t$ and $n^a_t$ are Gaussian noise.
Crucially, instead of modeling biases as stochastic processes (like Brownian motion), the authors propose a **deterministic, non-linear dynamics model** for the biases:
$\dot{b}^g_t = f_g(b^g_t, \tilde{\omega}_t, \dot{\tilde{\omega}}_t)$
$\dot{b}^a_t = f_a(b^a_t, \tilde{a}_t, \dot{\tilde{a}}_t)$
Here, $f_g$ and $f_a$ are neural networks that learn these continuous bias dynamics. The inclusion of input derivatives ($\dot{\tilde{\omega}}_t$, $\dot{\tilde{a}}_t$) is a unique aspect, enabling the model to capture bias components that depend on the rate of change of measurements, such as those arising from axis misalignment.

#### 3.2. Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (NODEs) for Bias Learning

The core idea is to treat the bias dynamics as an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE), where the neural networks $f_g$ and $f_a$ parameterize the vector fields. The NODE framework allows learning these vector fields by integrating the ODE over time and comparing the output to a ground truth.
To handle the discrete nature of raw IMU measurements ($\tilde{u}_i$), a **causal cubic Hermite spline** is employed to interpolate the discrete inputs into continuous functions of time $S_{\tilde{u}}(t)$. This ensures continuous derivatives, which is beneficial for NODE convergence and enables online inference.

#### 3.3. Solving ODEs on Matrix Lie Group (SO(3))

A significant challenge arises because IMU kinematics involve orientation, represented by elements of the Special Orthogonal Group SO(3), which is a non-Euclidean manifold. Standard NODEs operate in Euclidean space. To bridge this gap, the authors leverage a **Lie group method**. The orientation ODE $\dot{R}_t = R_t \omega_t^{\times}$ (where $R_t \in SO(3)$) is transformed into an ODE on its Lie algebra so(3) (a Euclidean vector space): $\dot{\xi}_t = J_r^{-1}(\xi_t) \omega_t$, where $\xi_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and $J_r^{-1}(\cdot)$ is the inverse of the right Jacobian of SO(3). The orientation $R_t$ can then be recovered by $R_t = R_0 \text{Exp}(\xi_t)$. This allows the use of standard ODE solvers for the neural network part while respecting the underlying geometry of orientation. A "chart change" mechanism is implemented, resetting the local coordinate $\xi_t$ to zero when its norm approaches a singularity (e.g., $\pi$), to maintain numerical stability.

#### 3.4. Loss Construction: Bypassing Bias Ground Truth

This is a critical innovation. Instead of requiring ground truth bias, the loss function is formulated based on readily available **pose ground truth** (orientation $R_k$, velocity $v_k$, and position $p_k$). The estimated pose $(\hat{R}_k, \hat{v}_k, \hat{p}_k)$ is obtained by integrating the full coupled ODE system (IMU kinematics with debiased measurements and the learned bias dynamics).
The loss functions are Mean Squared Error (MSE) based:
- Orientation Loss ($L_R$): $L_R = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N \|\text{Log}(\hat{R}_k R_k^T)\|_2^2$
- Velocity and Position Loss ($L_{v,p}$): $L_{v,p} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^N (\|\hat{p}_k - p_k\|_2^2 + \|\hat{v}_k - v_k\|_2^2)$
The total loss is $L = L_R + L_{v,p}$. This allows the neural network parameters for bias dynamics to be optimized solely from pose ground truth.

#### 3.5. Hierarchical Training Strategy

To manage the complexity of training a continuous recurrent neural network, a two-stage hierarchical approach is adopted, leveraging the kinematic dependencies:
1. **Gyroscope Debiasing (Rotation):** The gyroscope bias dynamics ($f_g$) are trained first, using only the orientation loss ($L_R$). This involves solving the coupled ODE for $\dot{\xi}_t$ and $\dot{b}^g_t$.
2. **Accelerometer Debiasing (Velocity & Position):** Once $f_g$ is trained and fixed, the accelerometer bias dynamics ($f_a$) are trained using the velocity and position loss ($L_{v,p}$). This stage involves solving the full ODE system including $f_g$ (fixed), $f_a$, and the pose kinematics.
This hierarchical approach simplifies network design, accelerates convergence, and naturally aligns with IMU kinematics.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **Network Structure:** Simple Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) with residual connections are used for $f_g$ and $f_a$, keeping the networks lightweight.
- **Training Segments:** Instead of integrating over entire trajectories, training is performed on short, overlapping segments of the sequence (e.g., $N=16$ IMU samples). This significantly reduces computational cost and memory usage.
- **Initial Bias Conditions:** For each training segment, approximate initial bias conditions are derived from the pose ground truth and raw IMU data. The noise in these approximations is considered a form of data augmentation, making the method robust.
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a StepLR scheduler for learning rate.

In summary, the methodology combines state-of-the-art deep learning (NODEs) with foundational robotics principles (Lie group kinematics) and a clever loss formulation to enable practical and interpretable learning of IMU bias dynamics without the need for elusive bias ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed debiasing method is rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments across two public datasets (EUROC and TUM-VI) and one real-world dataset (FETCH), comparing its performance against raw IMU data, a simple linear calibration model, and an existing CNN-based implicit debiasing method (M.B.). Evaluation metrics include Absolute Orientation Error (AOE), Absolute Position Error (APE), Relative Orientation Error (ROE), and Relative Position Error (RPE).

#### 4.1. Performance in Pure IMU Integration

This scenario isolates the IMU's contribution, mimicking situations where other sensors fail.
- **Significant Accuracy Gains:** The proposed method consistently and significantly outperforms all baselines in both absolute and relative orientation errors across both EUROC and TUM-VI datasets. For instance, on EUROC, average AOE drops from 120.78 deg (raw) and 4.22 deg (linear) to 2.40 deg, and on TUM-VI, from 56.06 deg (raw) to 1.79 deg. This demonstrates highly accurate debiased angular velocity.
- **Orientation Robustness:** The results show that pure integration with debiased IMU data yields accurate orientation over long test trajectories, closely matching ground truth.
- **Position and Velocity Challenges:** While orientation is greatly improved, long-term position estimates remain unreliable due to the inherent challenges of double integration and sensitivity to initial conditions and remaining biases. Velocity estimates are reliable only over short intervals, diverging over longer durations, although the z-axis velocity often shows the smallest error. This highlights that IMU-only integration for long-term position tracking is fundamentally difficult, even with debiasing.

#### 4.2. Performance in Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO)

The debiased IMU measurements from the proposed method are fed into OpenVINS, a state-of-the-art VIO algorithm.
- **Consistent Improvement:** The proposed method yields better absolute and relative errors in both orientation and position within the VIO framework, compared to using raw IMU data or data debiased by baselines.
- **Robustness Enhancement:** Notably, using raw IMU data caused the OpenVINS algorithm to fail on the TUM-VI dataset in the experimental settings, whereas the debiased data enabled successful operation with improved accuracy. This demonstrates the method's ability to enhance the robustness of multi-sensor fusion systems.
- **Reduced Impact:** The performance improvement in VIO is less dramatic than in pure IMU integration. This is expected, as the vision sensor also provides significant pose information, and the VIO algorithm itself incorporates bias estimation capabilities. Nevertheless, the debiased IMU data provides a cleaner input, contributing to overall improved and more stable VIO performance.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capability (FETCH Dataset)

To test generalization, the model trained on EUROC data was applied to the unseen real-world FETCH dataset (mobile robot).
- **Positive Generalization:** The proposed method performed well on FETCH, maintaining its accuracy advantage over raw IMU and M.B..
- **Comparison to Linear Model:** Interestingly, on the FETCH dataset, the simple linear model performed comparably and even achieved better positional accuracy than the proposed learning-based method. The authors attribute this to the *simplicity of motion patterns* in the FETCH dataset. They hypothesize that learning-based methods, while powerful, will inevitably capture and leverage motion patterns. If these patterns are simple, a linear model might suffice. This is supported by the fact that the linear model performed worse on the more diverse EUROC and human-motion-rich TUM-VI datasets, where complex patterns necessitate advanced learning. This suggests a practical guideline: try a linear model first; if insufficient, then deploy a learning-based approach.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Discussion on Implementation

- **Integration Method:** No significant performance difference between simple Euler integration and more advanced methods was observed. This is likely due to the high IMU data frequency and the overwhelming impact of IMU noise and bias, diminishing the relative contribution of integration precision.
- **Integration Interval Length:** Accuracy improves as the integration length (N) increases from very short (e.g., N=4) due to noise sensitivity. However, accuracy saturates around N=10-16. Longer intervals increase training time and GPU memory consumption (linearly). N=16 was chosen as a balance.
- **Data Pre-processing:** Smoothing noisy ground truth (especially critical for TUM-VI) was found to improve training outcomes, indicating the sensitivity to initial conditions used in the segment-based training.
- **Method Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the method is device-specific, still implicitly learns motion pattern characteristics to some extent (which can limit advantages in simple motion contexts), and suffers from increasing training time/memory with longer integration intervals (common in RNN-like architectures).

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that the proposed method significantly enhances IMU measurement accuracy by effectively debiasing both gyroscopes and accelerometers. It achieves superior performance in pure IMU integration and contributes to more robust and accurate VIO systems, showcasing its practical utility and generalization capabilities, while also highlighting the nuanced interplay between learning complexity and motion pattern diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of IMU calibration and state estimation for robotics, with several layers of significance and potential impact:

1. **Practical Breakthrough in Explicit Bias Learning:** The most profound contribution is the elimination of the need for ground truth bias data for explicit bias learning. This addresses a critical limitation that has historically hampered the practicality and widespread adoption of such methods. By formulating a loss function based solely on readily available pose ground truth, the authors make explicit bias modeling feasible and more interpretable, allowing for a clearer understanding of what the network is learning (true IMU characteristics vs. motion patterns).

2. **Enhanced IMU Measurement Accuracy and Robustness:**
 - **Pure IMU Integration:** The dramatic improvement in pure IMU integration, particularly for orientation, is highly impactful. In situations where vision, LiDAR, or GPS signals are unavailable or compromised (e.g., in feature-less environments, GPS-denied zones, underwater, or underground), the debiased IMU data can provide significantly more accurate and reliable short-to-medium-term odometry, crucial for emergency navigation, temporary localization, or control.
 - **Multi-Sensor Fusion (VIO):** By providing cleaner, debiased IMU measurements, the method acts as a robust front-end for multi-sensor fusion algorithms like VIO. This not only improves the overall accuracy of the estimated pose but also enhances the robustness of these systems, preventing failures that might occur with highly biased raw IMU data (as demonstrated on the TUM-VI dataset).

3. **Theoretical Advancement in Geometric Deep Learning:** The extension of the Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (NODE) framework to handle dynamics on matrix Lie groups (specifically SO(3)) is a notable theoretical contribution. This allows the integration of IMU kinematics, which inherently reside on a manifold, directly into a deep learning framework. This paves the way for more geometrically consistent and robust deep learning models for state estimation and other applications involving non-Euclidean spaces.

4. **Efficiency and Interpretability of Continuous Dynamics:** Modeling bias as continuous dynamics using NODEs results in lightweight neural networks compared to traditional RNNs or CNNs that process discrete windows of data. This reduced model complexity is beneficial for computational resource requirements, potentially enabling deployment on edge devices or for real-time applications. The explicit nature of learning bias dynamics also contributes to better interpretability of the learned corrections.

5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The provision of an open-source implementation (UMich-CURLY/Debias IMU.git) is a crucial step for the robotics and machine learning communities. It facilitates reproducibility, encourages further research, and allows other researchers and practitioners to build upon this work, integrating it into their own systems.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Improved Robotic Autonomy:** More accurate and reliable IMU data translates directly to better state estimation, which is fundamental for robotic control, path planning, and navigation in complex and dynamic environments (e.g., for MAVs, legged robots, autonomous vehicles, and mobile manipulators).
- **Advanced AR/VR and Human-Computer Interaction:** Technologies relying heavily on IMUs for head tracking, hand tracking, and full-body motion capture will benefit from enhanced accuracy and reduced drift, leading to more immersive and responsive user experiences.
- **Smart Wearables and Health Monitoring:** For applications like activity tracking, gait analysis, and posture monitoring, more precise IMU data will enable more accurate and clinically relevant insights.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a strong foundation for integrating learned dynamics with traditional estimation frameworks (e.g., Invariant Extended Kalman Filters), which could lead to hybrid systems combining the robustness of model-based methods with the adaptability of data-driven approaches. The planned extension to incorporate covariance propagation and the adjoint method for memory efficiency would further increase the practical utility of this framework.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust, principled, and practical solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics. By cleverly addressing the data requirements and leveraging modern deep learning and geometric tools, it significantly advances the state-of-the-art in IMU debiasing, promising to enhance the capabilities and reliability of inertial sensing systems across a wide range of applications.
