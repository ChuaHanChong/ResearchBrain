# Flexible Motion In-betweening with Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CondMDI, a masked conditional diffusion model from the University of British Columbia, generates high-quality, diverse human motions by precisely in-betweening arbitrary spatio-temporally sparse and partial keyframe constraints. The model is explicitly trained with randomized keyframe conditions, enabling flexible control for animators while maintaining fast inference speeds and achieving strong adherence to given poses.

## Problem

- Generating realistic and plausible motion sequences that connect user-defined keyframes remains a labor-intensive and technically challenging task in character animation.
- Existing deep learning methods for motion in-betweening often lack practical flexibility, struggling to handle arbitrary temporal and spatial sparsity of keyframes or partial pose specifications.
- Integrating precise spatial constraints into generative models like diffusion models without sacrificing motion quality, diversity, or incurring high computational costs at inference time has been a challenge.

## Method

- A masked conditional diffusion model, CondMDI, is proposed, building upon the GMD architecture and utilizing a global-root motion representation.
- Keyframe constraints are integrated directly by replacing observed parts of noisy motion samples with keyframe values at each denoising step and concatenating a binary observation mask to the model input.
- The model is trained with a '==Random Mask Generator==' that samples arbitrary numbers and temporal locations of keyframes, as well as random subsets of observed joints, allowing it to generalize to diverse sparse and partial keyframe scenarios.

## Results

- CondMDI achieves competitive performance for unconditioned text-to-motion synthesis, with an FID score of **0.2538** and R-precision of **0.6450**, demonstrating high generative quality.
- The model generates high-quality, smooth motions that precisely adhere to sparse and partial keyframes, significantly reducing Keyframe Error as constraint density increases (e.g., **0.0754** with 20 full keyframes).
- Ablation studies confirm that explicit training with flexible keyframe scenarios yields superior motion quality (FID **0.1731**) and adherence compared to inference-time conditioning techniques, while offering faster inference (54.39s) than dedicated joint control models like OmniControl.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training a diffusion model with randomized sparse and partial keyframe conditions is crucial for achieving high flexibility and adherence to constraints at inference time, outperforming methods relying solely on inference-time conditioning hacks.
> - Utilizing a global-root motion representation simplifies the direct incorporation of absolute spatio-temporal constraints, avoiding cumulative errors inherent in relative representations.
> - Concatenating a binary observation mask alongside direct keyframe imputation into noisy samples allows the diffusion model to effectively learn which parts are conditioned and generate coherent motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Motion synthesis and character animation are fundamental and enduring challenges in computer graphics, spanning decades of research. The core problem addressed by this paper, "motion in-betweening," involves generating realistic and plausible motion sequences that connect user-defined keyframes. This task has historically been labor-intensive and technically challenging.

**Traditional Motion Synthesis:** Early approaches relied on kinematic methods, motion graphs, and motion blending. Motion graphs [Arikan and Forsyth 2002; Kovar et al. 2002] precompute feasible transitions between motion clips, allowing for synthesis via search or dynamic programming. Motion matching [Büttner and Clavet 2015] searches for frames that best fit the current context. These methods, while effective, often face memory and scalability issues, requiring large motion databases or expensive runtime optimizations.

**Deep Learning for Motion Synthesis:** The advent of large-scale motion capture datasets and advances in deep learning have revolutionized the field.
- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)** were among the first deep learning models applied to motion synthesis and keyframe completion [Harvey et al. 2020; Holden et al. 2016]. While capable, RNNs often struggle with modeling long-term dependencies, limiting them to shorter animations.
- **Generative Models** like **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)** and **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** have also been explored for motion in-betweening [He et al. 2022; Li et al. 2021; Zhou et al. 2020], offering more diverse outputs.
- **Transformer-based architectures** [Duan et al. 2021; Oreshkin et al. 2023], inspired by their success in natural language processing, have shown promise in modeling long-term dependencies by predicting entire motion trajectories at once. A key limitation across many of these deep learning methods, however, is their tendency to be restricted to fixed keyframe patterns or representations, hindering practical flexibility.

**Diffusion Models in Motion Synthesis:** Most recently, **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)** have emerged as powerful generative models, demonstrating impressive capabilities in generating diverse and realistic human motions [Dabral et al. 2023; Tevet et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2022]. Diffusion models excel at incorporating various forms of conditioning, from text-to-image generation with guidance [Nichol et al. 2021] to image completion via inpainting [Lugmayr et al. 2022]. This inherent flexibility makes them highly suitable for conditional motion generation tasks.

**Challenges in Motion In-betweening with Diffusion Models:** While powerful, applying diffusion models to motion in-betweening, especially with spatial constraints like keyframes, still presents specific challenges. Previous diffusion-based methods like MDM [Tevet et al. 2023] or PriorMDM [Shafir et al. 2023] struggled with precise keyframe adherence when using inference-time imputation or with representations that are relative to the previous frame, leading to issues like foot sliding. Some methods, like GMD [Karunratanakul et al. 2023b], supported sparse keyframes but were limited to root (pelvis) position, not full pose. OmniControl [Xie et al. 2023] offers full-pose spatial conditioning but requires a separate embedding module and relies on repeated guidance, increasing inference time and complexity.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a simpler, unified diffusion model that explicitly learns to handle flexible keyframe constraints (both temporal and spatial sparsity, including partial poses) as part of its training, rather than relying solely on inference-time hacks or specialized architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop a unified diffusion model for motion in-betweening:** The goal is to create a single model capable of generating diverse and high-quality human motions.
2. **Achieve flexible conditioning:** The model should accommodate a wide range of user-specified spatial constraints, including:
 - **Arbitrary dense-or-sparse keyframe placement:** Users should be able to place keyframes at any temporal interval.
 - **Partial keyframe constraints:** The model should handle cases where only a subset of joints are specified at a keyframe (spatial sparsity).
 - **Text conditioning:** The generated motions should also be guidable by natural language prompts.
3. **Generate precise and diverse motions:** The output motions must accurately conform to the given keyframes while maintaining naturalness, smoothness, and offering diverse interpretations for the same constraints.
4. **Improve efficiency and simplicity:** The proposed method aims to be computationally efficient at inference time and possess a simpler architecture compared to some prior diffusion-based control methods.

The motivation stems from the recognition that motion in-betweening remains a demanding task in character animation. Existing deep learning approaches, while advanced, often lack the practical flexibility required by animators due to limitations in handling arbitrary keyframe sparsity, partial pose specifications, or the inherent difficulty of integrating precise spatial constraints into generative models like diffusion models without sacrificing motion quality or incurring high computational costs. This work aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a "simple unified model" that integrates flexibility directly into the training process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach: Conditional Motion Diffusion In-betweening (CondMDI)

The authors propose **Conditional Motion Diffusion In-betweening (CondMDI)**, a masked conditional diffusion model designed for flexible motion synthesis.

**Problem Definition and Motion Representation:**
The task is to generate a human motion sequence `x` of `N` frames, where each frame `x_i` consists of `J` joints, each with `D` features. This generation is conditioned on a text prompt `p` and an observation control signal `c`. The control signal `c` is spatio-temporally sparse, meaning it contains only a subset of `k ≤ N` keyframes and `j ≤ J` joints per keyframe.

A critical aspect of the methodology is the **motion representation**. Unlike many previous models that use relative root joint positions and orientations (relative to the previous frame), CondMDI converts this to a **global-root representation**. This change, validated by previous work (GMD), simplifies the incorporation of temporally sparse global spatial constraints, as absolute positions and orientations can be directly specified and maintained without cumulative error issues inherent to relative representations over long sequences. The HumanML3D dataset, which represents each frame as a 263-dimensional feature vector (including root translation/rotation, local joint positions/rotations, global joint velocities, and foot contact), is preprocessed to use this global-root representation.

**The CondMDI Model:**
CondMDI is an explicit conditional diffusion model built upon the **GMD (Guided Motion Diffusion)** architecture [Karunratanakul et al. 2023b] as its backbone. GMD is a UNet-based diffusion model using 1D convolutions and Adaptive Group Normalization (AdaGN), demonstrating strong performance in text-conditioned motion generation.

The core of CondMDI's conditioning mechanism is its "masked conditional" approach:
1. **Keyframe Signal Integration:** At each denoising step `t` in the reverse diffusion process, the noisy motion sample `x_t` is directly modified. The observed parts of `x_t` (corresponding to the keyframes `c`) are replaced with the observed keyframe values. This is represented as `~x_t = m ⊙ c + (1 − m) ⊙ x_t`, where `m` is a binary mask.
2. **Observation Mask Concatenation:** To inform the diffusion model `G_θ` about *which* parts of the input are observed, the binary observation mask `m` is concatenated with the modified noisy sample `~x_t` and fed into the model as `⟨~x_t, m⟩`.
3. **Text Conditioning:** Text prompts `p` are encoded using a pre-trained CLIP model [Radford et al. 2021] and provided as an additional input to the diffusion model, enabling text-guided motion generation alongside keyframe constraints.
4. **Classifier-Free Guidance:** For increased flexibility over the strength of conditioning, classifier-free guidance is employed. This involves training the model to jointly predict both conditional and unconditional outputs (by setting `p = ∅` for a fraction of training samples). At inference, a weighted combination of these predictions allows tuning the balance between adherence to the text prompt and sample diversity.

**Training and Inference Strategy:**
- **Flexible Training:** A key innovation for achieving flexibility at inference time is training CondMDI with **randomly sampled partial keyframes**. During training, a "Random Mask Generator" procedure is used to:
 - Randomly sample the number of keyframes `k` within the motion sequence length.
 - Randomly pick the temporal locations of these `k` keyframes.
 - Randomly sample the number of observed joints `j` for these keyframes.
 - Randomly pick the specific `j` joints to be observed.
 This comprehensive randomization during training allows the model to learn to generalize to a wide variety of sparse and partial keyframe scenarios at inference.
- **Unconditioned Generation Capability:** For 10% of training samples, the keyframe conditioning signal `c` is set to `∅`. This trains the model to also perform unconditioned (text-only) motion generation, enhancing its versatility.
- **Efficient Inference:** The authors highlight that their method maintains fast inference speed, a crucial practical advantage, especially when compared to alternative diffusion-based methods that rely on iterative guidance applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CondMDI is performed on the HumanML3D dataset, a large collection of text-annotated human motion sequences. Standard evaluation metrics for conditional motion generation are used, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), R-Precision (Top-3), Diversity, Foot Skating Ratio, and Keyframe Error.

**Quantitative Performance on Text-to-Motion Synthesis (Unconditioned):**
- CondMDI achieves competitive performance for text-conditioned motion synthesis without any keyframe input (Table 1). It yields an FID score of 0.2538, R-precision of 0.6450, and Diversity of 9.7489, comparable to or better than state-of-the-art models like GMD (0.235 FID) and MLD (0.473 FID) for this specific task. This baseline demonstrates the quality of the underlying generative model.

**Sparse and Partial Keyframe In-betweening:**
- **Flexible Keyframes:** CondMDI demonstrates strong performance across various keyframing schemes (Table 2).
 - As the number of randomly placed full keyframes increases (from `K=1` to `K=20`), the Keyframe Error decreases significantly (0.3739 to 0.0754), indicating strong adherence to constraints.
 - Motion quality metrics (FID, R-precision, Diversity) generally remain comparable to or slightly worse than unconditioned synthesis, which is expected as denser constraints can limit generation diversity but ideally improve precision.
- **Partial Keyframes (Joint Control):** The model effectively handles partial keyframe specifications.
 - Conditioning on only the root joint trajectory ("Root Joint") or sparse VR joints (head and wrists, "VR Joints") results in minimal Keyframe Error (0.0525 and 0.0422, respectively) while maintaining good motion quality.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 4) show natural motions generated even from highly sparse joint information.
- **Qualitative Results:** Figures 1 and 3 illustrate the generation of high-quality, smooth, and natural motions that precisely follow sparse keyframes, even for complex actions like karate kicks or yoga poses, and across significant temporal gaps (e.g., 40 frames).

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art Diffusion Models:**
- Direct comparison with all SOTA diffusion models for keyframe in-betweening is challenging due to varying representations (global vs. relative coordinates) and supported constraint types.
- However, when compared specifically on the task of root joint trajectory control (Table 3), CondMDI (FID 0.2474, Keyframe Error 0.0525) shows **comparable or superior performance** to OmniControl (FID 0.322, Keyframe Error 0.0367), which is a recent dedicated joint control model. Crucially, CondMDI achieves this with a **simpler architecture and relatively faster inference speed** (54.39s vs. 183.79s for OmniControl, Table 6).

**Ablation Studies of Conditioning Methods:**
A comprehensive ablation study investigates the effectiveness of CondMDI's explicit conditional training against inference-time conditioning techniques (Table 4, Figure 5):
- **Pure Imputation (IMPC=0):** Replacing keyframes at every denoising step results in extremely low Keyframe Error but catastrophically high FID (8.6204), indicating highly unnatural, jerky motions that largely ignore the diffusion process between keyframes.
- **Imputation (IMP):** Stopping replacement at denoising step 1 improves FID (0.3600) but leads to very high Keyframe Error (0.5150), suggesting the model mostly ignores the keyframes.
- **Imputation with Reconstruction Guidance (IMP+RecG):** Adding reconstruction guidance improves both motion quality and keyframe adherence compared to pure imputation, achieving low Keyframe Error (0.0034) but still with a relatively high FID (1.7072) and visible jitters.
- **CondMDI (trained with random frames only):** A variant of CondMDI trained with full keyframes at random temporal locations shows strong performance (FID 0.1822, Keyframe Error 0.1165).
- **CondMDI (full model, trained with random frames and joints):** The proposed CondMDI model, trained with randomized full *and partial* keyframes, achieves the **best overall balance** of motion quality (FID 0.1731, R-precision 0.6823, Diversity 9.3053) and keyframe adherence (Keyframe Error 0.1789), outperforming all inference-time methods significantly. This highlights the importance of explicitly training the model with flexible keyframe scenarios.

**Qualitative Observations and Diversity:**
- The model can generate diverse samples for the same initial keyframes (Figure 6), allowing animators to explore different movement interpretations.
- Text conditioning effectively guides the overall motion style even when only sparse keyframe constraints are provided (Figure 7), demonstrating the combined power of text and spatial control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CondMDI represents a significant step forward in the practical application of generative AI for character animation, particularly in the domain of motion in-betweening.

**Strengths and Advantages:**
- **Unprecedented Flexibility:** The most critical impact is the ability to handle **arbitrary spatio-temporal keyframe sparsity and partial joint specifications** within a single, unified framework. This level of flexibility is crucial for real-world animation workflows, where animators rarely work with rigidly predefined keyframe patterns.
- **High-Quality and Diverse Motion Generation:** The model consistently generates realistic, smooth, and high-quality human motions that precisely adhere to constraints while offering diversity, which is essential for creative exploration.
- **Simplicity and Efficiency:** By integrating the conditioning directly into the model's training rather than relying on complex inference-time guidance or specialized embedding modules, CondMDI offers a simpler architecture and faster inference compared to some state-of-the-art diffusion-based control methods. This makes it more amenable to adoption in production environments.
- **Unified Control:** The combination of text conditioning with flexible spatial keyframes provides a powerful and intuitive control interface for animators, bridging semantic guidance with precise pose control.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Models:** It further demonstrates the immense potential of diffusion models for complex conditional generation tasks in graphics and animation. Its modularity, built on a GMD backbone, suggests it can readily benefit from future improvements in underlying motion diffusion models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper also acknowledges several limitations, which present avenues for future research:
- **Minor Footskate and Jitter:** Despite improvements, some highly dynamic motions still exhibit minor footskate and jitter. Future work could incorporate explicit footskate losses, smoothness regularization, or leverage physics-based simulation to improve realism. Filtering outlier motions (like skating/swimming) from training data or providing contextual information could also help.
- **Redundant Data Representation:** The current data representation (263-dimensional feature vector for all joints) means that conditioning on a small number of features (e.g., a single joint) still requires providing all 263 features, potentially leading to sparse observed values being treated as noise. More intelligent or non-redundant representations for partial keyframes could address this.
- **Randomized Keyframe Selection in Training:** The fully randomized keyframe selection during training, while enabling flexibility, might not optimally reflect the specific patterns animators use in practice. Grounding the randomization to more practical keyframe combinations could further enhance performance and realism.

In conclusion, CondMDI offers a highly practical and effective solution for motion in-betweening, significantly improving flexibility and control for animators while demonstrating the power of diffusion models in this domain. Its simple yet powerful approach is likely to influence future research and development in AI-driven character animation tools.
