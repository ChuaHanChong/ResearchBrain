# Flexible Motion In-betweening with Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> CondMDI, a masked conditional diffusion model from the University of British Columbia, generates high-quality, diverse human motions by precisely in-betweening arbitrary spatio-temporally sparse and partial keyframe constraints. The model is explicitly trained with randomized keyframe conditions, enabling flexible control for animators while maintaining fast inference speeds and achieving strong adherence to given poses.

## Problem

- Generating realistic and plausible motion sequences that connect user-defined keyframes remains a labor-intensive and technically challenging task in character animation.
- Existing deep learning methods for motion in-betweening often lack practical flexibility, struggling to handle arbitrary temporal and spatial sparsity of keyframes or partial pose specifications.
- Integrating precise spatial constraints into generative models like diffusion models without sacrificing motion quality, diversity, or incurring high computational costs at inference time has been a challenge.

## Method

- A masked conditional diffusion model, CondMDI, is proposed, building upon the GMD architecture and utilizing a global-root motion representation.
- Keyframe constraints are integrated directly by replacing observed parts of noisy motion samples with keyframe values at each denoising step and concatenating a binary observation mask to the model input.
- The model is trained with a '==Random Mask Generator==' that samples arbitrary numbers and temporal locations of keyframes, as well as random subsets of observed joints, allowing it to generalize to diverse sparse and partial keyframe scenarios.

## Results

- CondMDI achieves competitive performance for unconditioned text-to-motion synthesis, with an FID score of **0.2538** and R-precision of **0.6450**, demonstrating high generative quality.
- The model generates high-quality, smooth motions that precisely adhere to sparse and partial keyframes, significantly reducing Keyframe Error as constraint density increases (e.g., **0.0754** with 20 full keyframes).
- Ablation studies confirm that explicit training with flexible keyframe scenarios yields superior motion quality (FID **0.1731**) and adherence compared to inference-time conditioning techniques, while offering faster inference (54.39s) than dedicated joint control models like OmniControl.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training a diffusion model with randomized sparse and partial keyframe conditions is crucial for achieving high flexibility and adherence to constraints at inference time, outperforming methods relying solely on inference-time conditioning hacks.
> - Utilizing a global-root motion representation simplifies the direct incorporation of absolute spatio-temporal constraints, avoiding cumulative errors inherent in relative representations.
> - Concatenating a binary observation mask alongside direct keyframe imputation into noisy samples allows the diffusion model to effectively learn which parts are conditioned and generate coherent motion.
