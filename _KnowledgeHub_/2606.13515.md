# MaskWAM: Unifying Mask Prompting and Prediction for World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MaskWAM introduces an end-to-end world-action model that enhances robotic control by integrating future mask prediction with visual prompting. The approach achieves improved policy generalization and resolves referential ambiguity by providing explicit semantic grounding and precise spatial anchoring across diverse manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current World-Action Models (WAMs) rely on weakly structured RGB reconstruction, which treats all visual regions equally and can entangle task-relevant objects with background clutter.
- Textual instructions in robotic control often lack the precision for exact spatial anchoring, leading to referential ambiguity in cluttered environments or when distinguishing visually similar objects.
- Existing methods for robust spatial grounding often treat visual cues or intermediate representations as isolated targets or require per-frame conditional masks, lacking a unified approach within WAMs.

## Method

- **MaskWAM** extends the standard WAM architecture to jointly predict action chunks, future RGB frames, and future masks, unifying ==mask prompting== and prediction.
- Masks are rendered as RGB-compatible images and encoded by the same frozen causal 3D VAE encoder as RGB frames, with their latents concatenated to form a unified latent representation.
- An input mask dropout strategy during training enables a single policy to dynamically incorporate an initial visual prompt (target mask) for disambiguation or operate solely on RGB and language inputs for clear instructions.

## Results

- MaskWAM achieved a **98.4%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and **92.2%** on RoboTwin 2.0, outperforming various state-of-the-art VLA and WAM baselines.
- The model demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization in real-world tasks, maintaining **90.4%** success with distractors, **74.6%** with novel instances, and **81.7%** under lighting changes, significantly surpassing baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that joint RGB and mask prediction is essential for optimal performance, and future mask prediction is critical for effectively grounding visual prompts, as a prompt alone without prediction yielded only **21.6%** success in ambiguous tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit future mask prediction provides semantic supervision that forces the policy to prioritize and attend to task-relevant regions, thereby abstracting environmental noise and strengthening grounding.
> - Integrating first-frame visual prompts (e.g., target object masks) serves as a precise spatial anchor, substantially reducing referential ambiguity, particularly in language-ambiguous scenarios.
> - Direct mask conditioning offers more effective and fine-grained guidance for WAMs compared to relying solely on text or abstract coordinate prompts, leading to stronger performance and better generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the field of robotic control, specifically focusing on World Action Models (WAMs). WAMs represent a paradigm for robotic control that couples action generation with the prediction of future video observations. This approach is distinct from traditional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, as WAMs leverage the modeling of future observations as a proxy task to enhance underlying representations. By forecasting plausible futures, WAMs aim to capture physical dynamics and task-relevant temporal structures, facilitating transfer to downstream control tasks.

Despite the appeal of WAMs, the paper identifies two fundamental spatial limitations in current implementations:
1. **Lack of Explicit Grounding in Pure Visual Predictions**: Existing WAMs primarily supervise visual futures through RGB reconstruction. This provides weakly structured supervision, treating all regions equally rather than prioritizing task-relevant objects. Consequently, target objects can remain entangled with background clutter, potentially degrading policy performance and necessitating auxiliary semantic supervision.
2. **Inherent Descriptive Limits of Textual Conditioning**: While textual instructions provide semantic context, they often lack the precision required for exact spatial anchoring, particularly in cluttered environments or when distinguishing visually similar objects. This referential ambiguity can lead to suboptimal policy guidance.

Prior work has attempted to achieve robust spatial grounding through two main avenues: utilizing visual cues as static inputs (visual prompting via point clicks, bounding boxes, or masks) or predicting intermediate representations (such as keypoints, trajectories, or affordance maps). However, current methods often treat these as isolated targets or require per-frame conditional masks.

MaskWAM aims to advance the state of WAMs by directly addressing these spatial bottlenecks. It integrates masks as both explicit inputs (visual prompts) and explicit predictions, thereby providing a unified framework that combines both semantic supervision and precise spatial anchoring. This approach seeks to bridge the gap between high-dimensional pixel data and the lower-dimensional spatial structure crucial for effective control, thus enhancing generalization and precision in robotic manipulation beyond what is achievable with traditional RGB-only WAMs or text-only VLA/WAM policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end world-action model, termed MaskWAM, that achieves precise robotic control and robust policy generalization by unifying future mask prediction and visual prompting. This objective is motivated by the identified limitations of current World Action Models (WAMs) in handling spatial information and referential ambiguity.

The specific motivations and corresponding objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Lack of Explicit Grounding in Pure Visual Predictions**:
 - **Motivation**: Current WAMs primarily rely on predicting future RGB frames. While this implicitly captures spatial priors, the supervision signal is weakly structured, assigning equal importance to all pixels. This means that task-relevant objects can be obscured or conflated with background clutter, making it difficult for the policy to consistently identify and act upon the intended target. To achieve reliable target isolation and improve performance, a more explicit and semantic form of supervision is needed.
 - **Objective**: To introduce explicit semantic supervision by predicting future masks alongside RGB frames. This forces the policy to inherently prioritize and attend to task-relevant regions, abstracting away environmental noise and providing a stronger grounding signal for action generation.

2. **Overcoming the Inherent Descriptive Limits of Textual Conditioning**:
 - **Motivation**: Textual instructions, while crucial for conveying high-level task goals, are inherently limited in their ability to provide precise spatial information. In cluttered scenes with multiple similar objects (e.g., "give me the red bottle" when several red bottles are present), language alone often leads to referential ambiguity. This imprecision can result in a policy struggling to identify the exact target, leading to errors and reduced robustness.
 - **Objective**: To establish a precise spatial anchor that substantially reduces referential ambiguity by integrating first-frame visual prompts, such as target object masks. This visual input directly designates the target object or contact region, providing unambiguous guidance to the policy even in complex or cluttered environments.

3. **Leveraging WAMs' Vision-Driven Nature**:
 - **Motivation**: WAMs are fundamentally vision-driven architectures, processing visual inputs to predict future visual states. Introducing visual masks as a conditioning mechanism is hypothesized to align more naturally with this visual prediction process compared to relying solely on text or abstract coordinate prompts.
 - **Objective**: To demonstrate that direct mask conditioning provides more effective and fine-grained guidance for WAMs, leading to stronger performance and better generalization than visual-prompted VLA baselines or purely text-conditioned WAMs/VLAs.

In summary, MaskWAM aims to enhance robotic manipulation by developing a unified framework that provides both semantic task grounding through explicit future mask prediction and precise spatial anchoring through initial visual prompts, thereby overcoming the limitations of existing WAMs in handling spatial ambiguity and visual noise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MaskWAM introduces an end-to-end world-action model designed to jointly predict action chunks, future RGB frames, and future masks. This is achieved by integrating masks as both explicit inputs (visual prompts) and predictive targets.

**Core Architecture and Joint Prediction:**
MaskWAM extends the Wan 2.2 architecture, a standard world-action model, by incorporating mask processing capabilities. The model parameterizes the joint distribution of future actions, RGB frames, and masks, conditioned on the current RGB observation, an optional first-frame target mask, the current proprioceptive state, and a language instruction:
$p_\theta(a_{1:K}, I_{1:T}, M_{1:T} | I_0, M_0, s_0, \ell)$
Here, $I_0$ is the current RGB observation, $M_0$ is the optional first-frame target mask, $s_0$ is the proprioceptive state, $\ell$ is the language instruction, $a_{1:K}$ are predicted action chunks, $I_{1:T}$ are future RGB frames, and $M_{1:T}$ are future masks over a prediction horizon $T$.

**Unified RGB and Mask Encoding:**
To seamlessly integrate masks into the existing video backbone, MaskWAM employs a strategy where masks are treated as RGB-compatible images.
- **Mask Representation**: Task-relevant masks are rendered as three-channel images using a fixed color palette, with the background assigned a distinct color. This allows the mask sequence to have the same temporal horizon and spatial resolution as the corresponding RGB observations.
- **Shared Encoder**: Both RGB frames and these rendered mask frames are encoded by the *same frozen causal 3D VAE encoder* ($E$). This produces RGB latents ($z_v$) and mask latents ($z_m$).
- **Latent Concatenation**: The RGB and mask latents are then concatenated along the channel dimension to form a unified latent representation: $z = [z_v; z_m]$. This design aligns mask information with the pretrained visual latent space, enabling the reuse of the VAE and transformer interface with minimal architectural modifications. Each latent position can thus encode both appearance and task-relevant spatial guidance.
- **Patch Embedding Expansion**: The input patch embedding of the pretrained video backbone is expanded from $C$ to $2C$ channels to accommodate the concatenated latents. The original $C$ visual channels inherit pretrained weights, while the added mask channels are initialized to zero, allowing gradual incorporation of mask information during fine-tuning. The output head is similarly expanded to $2C$ channels to predict both RGB and mask latent velocities.

**Initial Masks as Policy Conditions (Visual Prompting):**
At deployment, the optional first-frame target mask ($M_0$) serves as a visual prompt or anchor.
- **Source of Prompt**: $M_0$ can be generated from various sources, such as a text phrase, point click, bounding box, or a coarse mask provided by a segmentation model (e.g., SAM-3). This generation occurs only once at the beginning of an episode.
- **Purpose**: This prompt explicitly designates the target object or interaction region, particularly in language-ambiguous scenarios (e.g., distinguishing identical objects or specific sub-parts).
- **Mechanism**: The initial mask prompt initializes the mask channel at the first frame. The world model then propagates this target specification through its predictions of future RGB frames, future masks, and actions.

**Unified Training via Mask Dropout:**
To enable a single policy to handle both tasks requiring visual prompts (language-ambiguous) and those that do not (language-clear), an input mask dropout strategy is employed during training. With a probability of $p = 0.5$, the initial target mask $M_0$ is replaced with a zero tensor. This unified training regime allows the same model to dynamically incorporate a mask prompt for disambiguation or operate solely on RGB and language inputs when the instruction is clear.

**Training Objective: Joint Flow Matching:**
MaskWAM is trained end-to-end using a unified flow matching objective. The architecture is based on a Mixture of Transformers (MoT), comprising two interacting branches:
- **Visual Branch**: Denoises the combined visual (RGB) and mask representations. Language features, extracted via a frozen T5 text encoder, are injected into this branch via cross-attention.
- **Action Expert**: A lightweight branch dedicated to denoising action chunks. Proprioceptive state and noisy actions are processed and fed into this expert, which then uses joint attention mechanisms over the visual context from the visual branch to predict action velocities.

Crucially, the flow matching objective decouples noise scheduling across two domains:
- **Visual Domain**: RGB and mask streams are synchronized under a shared visual timestep ($\tau_v$), ensuring spatiotemporal alignment of appearance and structural masks.
- **Action Domain**: An independent timestep ($\tau_a$) is sampled for action denoising. This decoupling enables the action expert to condition trajectory denoising on visual contexts at various noise levels, providing the theoretical basis for a partial denoising inference strategy.

The overall training objective is a sum of individual flow-matching losses for RGB videos ($L_{video}$), mask representations ($L_{mask}$), and action trajectories ($L_{act}$): $L = L_{video} + L_{mask} + L_{act}$.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Frozen Components**: During training, the Video VAE and the T5 text encoder remain frozen.
- **Input/Output Dimensions**: The model processes 384x320 RGB frames with a prediction horizon of $T=8$.
- **Efficient Inference**: To minimize latency during deployment, MaskWAM utilizes a partial-denoising decoding strategy. Instead of generating full future frames, the model performs only one denoising step on the joint RGB-mask stream to extract intermediate, task-aware visual latents. The action expert then uses these partially denoised latents, along with language instructions and proprioception, to generate the action chunk. This avoids the computational cost of full video generation while retaining the mask-grounded world state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MaskWAM's performance was evaluated across simulated benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks, demonstrating improvements over baseline models, particularly in tasks involving spatial ambiguity and generalization.

**Performance on LIBERO Benchmark:**
- MaskWAM achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 98.4% on the LIBERO benchmark.
- It outperformed various VLA models (e.g., WorldVLA, GR00T-N1, $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) and other WAMs (e.g., Motus, FastWAM).
- An ablation study showed that integrating the auxiliary mask prediction objective alone, without visual prompts at deployment (comparing MaskWAM's RGB-only variant at 97.3% to the full MaskWAM at 98.4%), resulted in improved policy learning. This suggests that mask supervision enhances the base policy by focusing attention on task-relevant regions, thereby improving visual grounding and execution robustness.

**Performance on RoboTwin 2.0 Benchmark:**
- MaskWAM achieved a 92.2% average success rate across six randomized RoboTwin 2.0 tasks.
- This performance surpassed $\pi_0$ by 19.4% and FastWAM by 4.5%.
- Ablations indicated that while a Mask-only variant (88.8%) natively outperformed an RGB-only variant (87.3%), the full MaskWAM, which unifies both modalities, achieved the highest success rate. This finding suggests that RGB futures capture essential dynamics, but auxiliary mask futures are crucial for directing the model's focus to task-relevant regions, with the joint representation yielding optimal performance.

**Real-world Evaluation (Eight Challenging Manipulation Tasks):**

1. **Language-clear Tasks (Tasks 1-4):**
 - MaskWAM demonstrated competitive performance, achieving an average success rate of 84.3%.
 - It outperformed state-of-the-art VLA models ($\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$) and prior WAM architectures (FastWAM).
 - Compared to its own RGB-only variant, integrating future mask prediction consistently improved success rates (e.g., from 86% to 91% on Task 1 and from 76% to 81% on Task 3). This indicates that joint mask prediction stabilizes and enhances the policy even in unambiguous tasks by providing explicit semantic grounding.

2. **Language-ambiguous Tasks (Tasks 5-8, requiring explicit spatial prompting):**
 - **In-Distribution Performance**: MaskWAM achieved a 92.9% average success rate. A comparison with baselines using textual coordinate prompts revealed that the language-driven $\pi_0$-coord (41.7%) performed better than the vision-driven FastWAM-coord (26.3%), supporting the hypothesis that spatial disambiguation in WAMs is more effectively injected visually through masks rather than text.
 - **Zero-shot Generalization**: MaskWAM demonstrated robust generalization across challenging scenarios:
 - **Distractors**: Maintained a 90.4% success rate when unseen, task-irrelevant objects were introduced, significantly outperforming the $\pi_0$-mask baseline (52.9%). This indicates strong spatial grounding amidst visual clutter.
 - **Novel Instances**: Achieved a 74.6% success rate when training targets were replaced with unseen objects of varying geometries or related categories, substantially surpassing $\pi_0$-mask (44.6%). This highlights robust category-level structural skill transfer.
 - **Lighting**: Showed strong invariance with an 81.7% success rate under altered environmental illumination.
 - Across all ambiguous settings, MaskWAM's mask-prediction mechanism consistently outperformed both visual-prompted VLA and textual coordinate baselines in resolving language ambiguity and enhancing visual generalization.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Q1: Necessity of paired RGB and mask prediction**: Joint RGB and mask prediction was found essential. The full MaskWAM (98.4% on LIBERO) surpassed isolated RGB-only (97.3%) and Mask-only (97.6%) variants, demonstrating that combining both modalities creates a synergy that improves spatial precision and regularizes the latent space.
- **Q2: Necessity of future mask prediction for grounding visual prompts**: Future mask prediction was critical for effective grounding. In language-ambiguous real-world tasks, providing a mask prompt without future mask prediction (MaskWAM-no-pred) resulted in a success rate of only 21.6%, compared to 84.9% with the full MaskWAM. This indicates that a visual prompt alone is insufficient without the explicit prediction objective to ensure the model reliably utilizes the visual guidance.
- **Q3: Impact of different prompt formulations**: Mask conditioning proved superior to spatial coordinate text. In language-ambiguous tasks, mask conditioning (84.9% success) significantly outperformed coordinate-augmented text prompts (18.2%). Visualizations showed that text-coordinate prompts led to dispersed attention, while mask conditioning maintained a sharply localized, temporally consistent focus on the target. The model also demonstrated robustness against varying input mask qualities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MaskWAM represents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in the domain of World Action Models, by addressing fundamental spatial and referential challenges. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced World Model Capabilities**: By integrating future mask prediction into the latent space of a unified Mixture of Transformers, MaskWAM advances the capabilities of WAMs. It provides models with a more semantically grounded understanding of future states, explicitly focusing on task-relevant objects rather than treating all visual information equally. This shift from "predicting what happens" to "predicting what matters" is a crucial step for more effective and robust robotic control.

2. **Improved Policy Generalization**: The research demonstrates that MaskWAM exhibits strong generalization across various challenging conditions, including:
 - **Distractors**: Maintaining performance in cluttered scenes with unseen, task-irrelevant objects.
 - **Novel Instances**: Transferring skills to unseen objects of varying geometries or categories.
 - **Lighting Changes**: Exhibiting robustness to appearance shifts caused by different illumination conditions.
 These generalization capabilities are important for deploying robots in diverse and unstructured real-world environments, where variations are common.

3. **Robust Resolution of Language Ambiguity**: MaskWAM offers a mechanism to resolve referential ambiguity inherent in textual instructions. By allowing for optional first-frame visual prompts (e.g., masks), the model can be precisely guided to the intended target object or region, even when language alone is insufficient. This capability is essential for intuitive and reliable human-robot interaction, enabling users to specify targets unambiguously through visual cues.

4. **Unified and Efficient Robotic Control**: The architecture allows a single policy to handle both language-clear tasks (without explicit visual prompts) and language-ambiguous tasks (with visual prompts), simplifying policy deployment. Furthermore, the partial-denoising inference strategy minimizes latency, making MaskWAM a more practical option for real-time robotic applications compared to methods requiring full video generation at test time.

5. **New Design Principles for World Models**: The findings underscore the importance of object-centric semantic supervision in world models. The ablations clearly show that both the act of predicting masks and conditioning on them are essential for effective spatial grounding. This suggests that future world model designs for robotics could benefit from explicitly incorporating intermediate, semantically rich representations beyond raw pixels.

In conclusion, MaskWAM's advancements in unifying mask prompting and prediction offer a more precise, robust, and generalizable paradigm for robotic manipulation. Its demonstrated performance in both simulated and real-world scenarios suggests a potential to broaden the scope of tasks that robots can reliably perform, especially in complex and ambiguous environments.
