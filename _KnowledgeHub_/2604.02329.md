# Generative World Renderer

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a large-scale dataset, "Generative World Renderer," comprising over 4 million frames from AAA games with synchronized RGB and five G-buffer channels, alongside a novel VLM-based evaluation protocol. The work demonstrates that training on this dataset substantially improves inverse rendering performance and G-buffer-conditioned forward synthesis, enabling more robust material decomposition, precise geometry, and enhanced real-world game editing with complex atmospheric effects.

## Problem

- Scaling bidirectional rendering (inverse and forward) to "in-the-wild" scenarios is hindered by a data bottleneck.
- Existing synthetic datasets lack sufficient scale, diversity, temporal continuity, scene complexity, and realistic environmental conditions (e.g., adverse weather).
- This data scarcity creates a "domain gap," causing models to struggle with real-world complexities, leading to imperfect material decomposition, geometric inaccuracies, and temporal flickering.

## Method

- Created **Generative World Renderer**, a dataset of over 4 million 720p/30fps frames from AAA games, synchronized with RGB and five ==G-buffer channels== (depth, normals, albedo, metallic, roughness).
- Developed a ==non-intrusive pipeline== for data acquisition by intercepting runtime G-buffers at the ==graphics API level== using ReShade and employing a ==dual-screen stitched capture== for strict temporal synchronization.
- Introduced a novel ==VLM-based evaluation protocol== using Gemini 3 Pro to assess inverse rendering performance on real-world videos without ground truth, validated by a user study with CG experts.

## Results

- Fine-tuning state-of-the-art models on the dataset significantly improves inverse rendering, yielding superior depth, normal, and albedo estimation on benchmarks (e.g., lower AbsRel and RMSE for depth, higher PSNR for albedo).
- The enhanced G-buffers from the proposed method lead to better relighting consistency and advanced game editing capabilities, including the seamless integration of complex volumetric effects like fog and rain.
- VLM-based evaluation on 40 real-world video cases showed improved semantic correctness, appearance quality, and temporal consistency for metallic and roughness predictions, validated by **70-85%** agreement rates in a user study.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curating long-duration, diverse video sequences with synchronized high-fidelity G-buffers from commercial games effectively bridges the "sim-to-real" domain gap for generative rendering tasks.
> - A non-intrusive, graphics API-level interception method combined with multi-screen recording enables scalable and temporally synchronized capture of complex G-buffers, overcoming traditional data extraction limitations.
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer a scalable and reliable proxy for human judgment in evaluating inverse rendering generalization on real-world videos, especially for material properties where ground truth is unavailable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of digital world modeling is fundamentally concerned with two interconnected tasks: forward rendering and inverse rendering. Forward rendering involves synthesizing photorealistic images from scene attributes such as geometry, materials, and lighting, typically governed by the rendering equation. Conversely, inverse rendering aims to decompose observed images back into these constituent physical components. Recent advancements in generative models, particularly diffusion models, have begun to integrate these two tasks into unified frameworks, leveraging intermediate representations like G-buffers to provide explicit geometric and material guidance for controllable synthesis and as supervision targets for decomposition.

Despite these developments, scaling bidirectional rendering to "in-the-wild" scenarios presents a significant challenge. The primary limitation identified in the existing research landscape is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and temporally continuous video sequences that are synchronized with high-fidelity ground-truth G-buffers. Previous synthetic datasets often exhibit limitations in scene complexity, feature static camera trajectories, employ simplified material models, and frequently lack diverse environmental conditions such as adverse weather (e.g., fog, rain, snow). These shortcomings contribute to a persistent "domain gap," where models trained on such data struggle to generalize to the complexities of real-world videos, leading to issues like imperfect "delighting" in cluttered environments, inaccuracies in fine-grained geometry, or temporal flickering during rapid motion. This data bottleneck is noted as a factor limiting the potential of current models to function as comprehensive generative world renderers.

This work addresses this specific data bottleneck by introducing a new large-scale, dynamic dataset. Unlike prior short-clip collections or image-centric datasets, this dataset emphasizes long, uninterrupted video sequences with synchronized multi-channel G-buffers. By curating data from visually complex AAA games and developing a non-intrusive capture pipeline, the authors aim to provide the necessary supervision for robust material decomposition and flexible G-buffer-conditioned forward synthesis, including complex volumetric effects. The work also contributes a novel evaluation protocol that utilizes Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to assess real-world performance, a domain where traditional per-frame metrics are insufficient due to the absence of ground truth. This positions the paper as an effort to bridge the sim-to-real gap and enhance the practical applicability of inverse and forward rendering technologies in dynamic, complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research is to address the "data bottleneck" that hinders the scalability of bidirectional rendering, encompassing both inverse and forward rendering, to real-world scenarios. The existing limitations in available datasets, characterized by restricted scene complexity, static camera movements, simplified material properties, and an absence of diverse environmental conditions (e.g., fog, rain, snow), lead to a significant domain gap. Models trained on such data often fail to generalize effectively to the intricacies of "in-the-wild" videos, resulting in issues like inaccurate material decomposition, geometric distortion, and temporal inconsistencies. This data scarcity is identified as a critical barrier preventing current models from evolving into robust "generative world renderers."

To overcome this, the paper sets out several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale, Continuous Video Dataset:** The primary objective is to create a substantial dataset comprising over 4 million frames at 720p resolution and 30 frames per second. This dataset is designed to feature continuous, long-duration video sequences synchronized with five high-fidelity G-buffer channels (depth, normals, albedo, metallic, and roughness) and corresponding RGB frames. The dataset specifically targets diverse urban and natural environments, varying atmospheric conditions, and long temporal trajectories to support both video inverse rendering and G-buffer-conditioned forward synthesis.

2. **Establish an Efficient Data Curation Pipeline:** The work aims to develop a novel, non-intrusive pipeline for scalable data acquisition. This pipeline intercepts runtime G-buffers at the graphics API level, avoiding the need for game decompilation or asset extraction. A key aspect is a dual-screen stitched capture method to record high-resolution buffers efficiently and with minimal quality degradation, ensuring strict temporal synchronization across all data channels.

3. **Enhance Rendering Performance and Evaluation:** The objective is to demonstrate that training and fine-tuning state-of-the-art models on the proposed dataset significantly improves their performance in both inverse rendering (robust geometry and material decomposition) and G-buffer-guided forward synthesis (controllable generation, including complex volumetric effects). Furthermore, recognizing the difficulties in evaluating real-world inverse rendering without ground truth, the paper aims to introduce a novel VLM-based assessment protocol. This protocol is intended to measure semantic correctness, spatial fidelity, and temporal consistency, thereby providing a reliable proxy for human judgment in complex, real-world scenarios. The inclusion of motion-blur variants in the dataset is also motivated by the need to ensure models are resilient to common real-world imaging degradations.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived limitations of current rendering models due to insufficient and unrealistic training data, while the objectives focus on developing a comprehensive dataset and an associated evaluation framework to enable more robust, high-fidelity, and generalizable generative rendering capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the construction of a large-scale, dynamic dataset from commercial video games and the development of a novel evaluation protocol for real-world scenarios.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

The dataset, named "Generative World Renderer," is curated from two AAA games, Cyberpunk 2077 and Black Myth: Wukong, to ensure visual complexity and diversity. It comprises over 4 million frames at 720p/30fps, with synchronized RGB and five G-buffer channels: depth, normals, albedo, metallic, and roughness.

- **G-buffer Interception:** The process begins with intercepting the rendering pipeline at the graphics API level using ReShade. Modern games often utilize proprietary G-buffer packing and encodings, necessitating an initial offline frame analysis with RenderDoc to identify render passes and their associated render-target attachments, including formats and dimensions. Game-specific ReShade add-ons are then implemented to hook graphics API callbacks, monitor per-frame render-target bindings, and identify desired G-buffers based on stable invariants like consistent format, extent, and recurrent binding patterns. Camera-space normals are reconstructed from world-space normal buffers using inverse projection and finite differences during the ReShade effects stage, as direct view matrix access is not reliably available. To prevent channel coupling artifacts during compression, metallic and roughness maps are decoupled and rendered into spatially distinct screen regions.

- **Synchronized Multi-Screen Recording:** To manage the high data volume and ensure strict temporal synchronization, a "mosaic" compositing strategy is used. All G-buffers are rendered onto a unified canvas, which is then recorded using hardware-accelerated capture software (OBS) at a near-lossless bitrate. To achieve 720p resolution for each channel, two 2K monitors are stitched together. A center-crop is applied to the source buffers before tiling to preserve the intended aspect ratio, as expanding the display area typically increases the game's field of view.

- **Scene Traversal Strategies:** Different strategies are employed for each game to maximize scene diversity. For Cyberpunk 2077, a semi-automated driving setup is used with long-range waypoints to generate continuous trajectories with variable speeds, complemented by sequences of player walking and indoor scene capture. In Black Myth: Wukong, exploration sequences from completed save files are captured, intentionally avoiding combat to focus on diverse environments and routes.

- **Dataset Statistics and Annotation:** The dataset is richly annotated with meta-information covering scene type (indoor/outdoor), weather conditions (sunny, cloudy, foggy, rainy, snowy), camera and scene motion dynamics (four categories), and dominant texture cues. These annotations are generated by uniformly sampling five timestamped frames per video clip and feeding them into Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct. Distribution analysis for metallic, roughness, and luminance is performed, revealing characteristic differences between the two games (e.g., Cyberpunk 2077's metallic urban scenes vs. Black Myth: Wukong's rough natural scenes). Clips with static content or excessively low luminance are filtered out.

- **Dataset Post-processing:** The RGB frames are initially captured with in-game motion blur disabled to provide sharp, canonical observations. To enhance realism and bridge the sim-to-real gap, an offline motion-blurred RGB variant is synthesized by interpolating 8 sub-frames using RIFE, averaging them in the linear domain, and converting back to RGB.

#### 3.2. VLM-based Evaluation on Real-Scene Test Cases

To evaluate the generalization performance of inverse renderers on real-world videos where ground truth G-buffers are unavailable, a VLM-based assessment protocol is introduced.

- **Motivation for VLM:** Traditional metrics are inapplicable without ground truth, and human studies are labor-intensive and require domain expertise. VLMs are leveraged for their extensive material-related world knowledge and ability to provide scalable, relative comparisons. The evaluation focuses on metallic and roughness, as these properties exhibit strong semantic and appearance priors that facilitate consistent pairwise preferences. VLMs can utilize global context and localized cues to identify subtle failure patterns.

- **Protocol:** Gemini 3 Pro is selected as the judge model for its video understanding and temporal reasoning capabilities. For each test video, a fixed-layout grid presenting the RGB reference alongside the method outputs is synchronously played. The VLM is prompted to rate predictions based on three criteria: (i) temporal consistency (e.g., flicker, jitter), (ii) spatial quality (e.g., edge alignment, detail), and (iii) semantic plausibility (e.g., correct material identification, disambiguation from glossy plastics/wet surfaces). The VLM provides structured scores and rankings for each modality. A detailed prompt is provided to guide the VLM's judgment based on expert knowledge of material properties.

- **User Study Validation:** A user study with 25 CG experts is conducted to validate the VLM's accuracy. Experts perform pairwise preference tests between DiffusionRenderer and the fine-tuned model for metallic and roughness. The agreement rate between expert judgments and VLM predictions is reported, generally showing alignment, albeit with lower agreement for roughness due to its often ambiguous visual cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed dataset and methodology.

#### 4.1. Training and Experimental Setup

The primary baseline for inverse rendering is DiffusionRenderer, a video inverse rendering method. Two diffusion-based image inverse rendering models are also included. The official DiffusionRenderer implementation is fine-tuned from pre-trained weights, using fixed-length clips of 57 frames at 24 FPS and 1280×720 resolution. Training is conducted on Cyberpunk 2077 data, and testing on Black Myth: Wukong. A motion-augmented variant of the fine-tuned model is selected as the final model due to its consistent performance improvements. A longer-clip variant (113 frames) is also trained, showing enhancements for long-video inference. For game editing, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct generates captions, and a Wan 2.1-T2V-1.3B model is adapted with G-buffers as conditional inputs, fine-tuned on Black Myth: Wukong at 832×480 (480p) and 16 FPS using 81-frame clips.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation of Inverse Rendering

The evaluation uses standard metrics for each modality on synthetic benchmarks with ground truth. Depth is evaluated in disparity space using AbsRel, RMSE, RMSE-log, and threshold accuracies (δ < 1.25^n). Albedo uses PSNR, LPIPS, and their scale-invariant counterparts. Normals are assessed via mean angular error and accuracy at 11.25°. Metallic and roughness use RMSE and MAE.

- **Black Myth Wukong Benchmark (Table 1):** On a held-out test set of 39 video clips from Black Myth: Wukong, the fine-tuned model demonstrates superior performance in depth (lower AbsRel, RMSE, RMSE-log; higher δ accuracies) and normal estimation (lower AngularError, higher Acc@11.25°). It also achieves the strongest scale-invariant albedo scores and significantly improves metallic and roughness accuracy (lower RMSE, MAE).
- **Sintel Benchmark (Table 2):** On the MPI-Sintel final pass, the fine-tuned model achieves superior overall performance for both depth (lower RMSE, RMSE-log; higher δ accuracies) and albedo (higher PSNR, lower LPIPS, including scale-invariant variants). Sky regions are excluded from albedo evaluation due to annotation inconsistencies.
- **Real-World Video Evaluation (Table 3):** Using Gemini 3 Pro as a VLM judge on 40 real-world video cases, fine-tuning on the dataset improves all VLM evaluation metrics (semantic correctness, appearance quality, temporal consistency) for both roughness and metallic. Motion augmentation further improves results, particularly for metallic appearance and roughness temporal consistency.
- **User Study (Table 4):** A user study with 25 CG experts validates the VLM-based evaluation. Expert judgments show an 85% agreement rate with VLM preferences for metallic predictions when the VLM favored the proposed model, and 70% when it favored DiffusionRenderer. For roughness, agreement rates were 75% and 61%, respectively, indicating a general alignment with VLM, with slightly lower agreement for the more ambiguous roughness cues.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Evaluation of Inverse Rendering (Figures 4, 5)

Qualitative comparisons on real-world video sequences illustrate the method's capabilities. The proposed method generates cleaner albedo maps with more thorough delighting and reconstructs more precise depth and normals, preserving structures while resisting outdoor illumination artifacts. Metallic and roughness predictions are semantically accurate and robust to atmospheric disruptions like smoke and volumetric scattering, corroborating quantitative findings.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study of Inverse Renderer (Table 5, Figure 8)

An ablation study on motion effects reveals that motion augmentation improves most synthetic metrics for depth and albedo and enhances real-video generalization, leading to higher temporal stability and reduced flicker under strong motion blur.

#### 4.5. Evaluation of Relighting (Figure 6)

When using the improved G-buffers estimated by the proposed inverse renderer, an off-the-shelf forward renderer (DiffusionRenderer's frozen forward renderer) synthesizes images with significantly better consistency with target environment maps compared to those generated from baseline G-buffers. This improvement is attributed to the enhanced ability of the data-centric paradigm to decouple intrinsic scene properties from environmental illumination, leading to cleaner G-buffers.

#### 4.6. Evaluation on Game Editing (Figure 7)

For high-fidelity video game editing, the G-buffer-conditioned model fine-tuned on the dataset achieves a superior balance between editability and visual fidelity compared to video-to-video baselines (ControlNet-based, SDEdit-style, DiffusionRenderer-based). G-buffers are demonstrated to be superior conditioning inputs, providing stable spatial and material cues that avoid temporal inconsistencies seen with RGB-derived edge maps or excessive deviation common with SDEdit. The model's ability to learn flexible priors from the dataset, including complex in-game visual effects like volumetric fog and rain, enables it to seamlessly hallucinate and integrate rich atmospheric effects during editing, which the DiffusionRenderer baseline struggles with due to its reliance on environment maps.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to the fields of generative rendering and computer vision by addressing a critical data bottleneck that has limited the scalability and realism of bidirectional rendering systems. The introduction of a large-scale, dynamic dataset derived from AAA games marks a step in providing high-fidelity, temporally coherent, and diverse training data that better reflects "in-the-wild" complexities than previous synthetic datasets.

The significance of this research lies in several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Domain Gap:** The dataset and associated methodologies are designed to bridge the persistent domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world scenarios. By providing explicit geometric and material guidance through synchronized G-buffers, it enables models to learn robust material decomposition and flexible priors for G-buffer-conditioned forward synthesis.

2. **Advancing Bidirectional Rendering:** The work supports advancements in both inverse and forward rendering. For inverse rendering, the dense supervision from the dataset facilitates more accurate and robust decomposition of geometry and materials in complex, dynamic scenes. For forward rendering, it allows generative models to synthesize photorealistic images and complex volumetric effects (e.g., fog, rain) that are often challenging for simplified physics-based renderers.

3. **Enhanced Realism and Generalization:** The inclusion of motion-blur variants in the dataset ensures that models trained on this data are more resilient to common real-world imaging degradations, contributing to better generalization capabilities across diverse visual conditions. The quantitative and qualitative results suggest that fine-tuning state-of-the-art models on this dataset significantly improves their performance and ability to handle unseen game environments.

4. **Novel Evaluation Paradigm:** The proposed VLM-based evaluation protocol is a significant methodological contribution. It provides a scalable and robust means to assess the real-world performance of inverse rendering without ground truth, leveraging the semantic and contextual understanding of VLMs. Its demonstrated correlation with human judgment establishes a valuable tool for future research in areas where traditional metrics are inadequate.

5. **Practical Applications and Future Research:** The demonstration of game editing as a practical application highlights the potential for controllable generative editing, allowing users to manipulate game styles, lighting, and visual effects using G-buffers and text prompts. The release of the dataset under a non-commercial license and the open-sourcing of the data curation toolkit are intended to promote transparency, reproducibility, and further research, facilitating the continuous expansion and diversification of such datasets for world simulation and generative AI.

In summary, this research provides a foundational dataset and an innovative evaluation method that collectively offer a pathway towards more reliable, temporally coherent, and generalizable generative world rendering, with potential impacts on areas ranging from content creation to advanced simulation.
