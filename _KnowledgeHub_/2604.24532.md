# A Reward-Free Viewpoint on Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University and MIT integrated Reward-Free Reinforcement Learning (RFRL) into Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) by adapting the Forward-Backward algorithm into a new framework called MORL-FB. This approach achieved superior performance and data efficiency across various continuous and discrete control tasks, showcasing robust generalization to unseen preferences and zero-shot transfer capabilities across tasks with different objective counts.

## Problem

- Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning (MORL) struggles with efficiently learning policies that adapt to diverse, often unknown, user preferences for multiple conflicting objectives.
- Existing MORL methods often suffer from inefficient exploration or limited generalization when trained on sparse preference samples.
- Direct application of Reward-Free Reinforcement Learning (RFRL) to MORL yields suboptimal performance because vanilla RFRL exploration does not adequately prioritize states critical for MORL-relevant, preference-weighted rewards.

## Method

- The proposed **MORL-FB** method systematically adapts the ==Forward-Backward== (FB) RFRL algorithm, designed to learn universal policy representations, to the MORL setting.
- It introduces 'Preference-Guided Exploration' (PG-Explore), which uses mini-batch sampled preferences from the replay buffer to generate relevant latent vectors, directing exploration towards policies aligned with MORL objectives.
- An auxiliary Q loss is incorporated, utilizing observed scalarized multi-objective rewards to enhance representation learning efficiency and directly connect learned FB representations to task-specific value functions.

## Results

- MORL-FB consistently achieved superior Utility (UT), Hypervolume (HV), and Episodic Dominance (ED) scores across diverse continuous and discrete control tasks compared to state-of-the-art MORL methods.
- The method demonstrated high data efficiency, reaching performance levels comparable to or exceeding baselines within 3 million environment steps, significantly fewer than the **63** million steps required for **21** separate single-objective policies.
- It exhibited robust generalization, maintaining performance with limited preference training, and showed effective zero-shot cross-objective transfer, adapting to tasks with additional reward terms without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RFRL's objective to learn optimal policies for any reward function can serve as an effective auxiliary task for MORL, promoting broader knowledge sharing and generalization beyond predefined scalarizations.
> - Effective integration of RFRL into MORL requires specific algorithmic enhancements, such as guided exploration and direct utilization of observed multi-objective rewards, to overcome the limitations of naive RFRL application.
> - Disentangled representations of environmental dynamics, learned through the adapted FB framework, enable robust generalization to unseen preferences and facilitate zero-shot transfer across tasks with varying numbers of objectives.
