# Lux Post Facto: Learning Portrait Performance Relighting with Conditional Video Diffusion and a Hybrid Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Lux Post Facto, developed by Netflix Eyeline Studios and Johns Hopkins University, introduces a video diffusion model that achieves photorealistic and temporally consistent portrait relighting. The system leverages a novel lighting injection mechanism and a hybrid dataset to enable precise control over High Dynamic Range (HDR) lighting environments, outperforming prior methods in visual quality and consistency without requiring specialized dynamic capture setups.

## Problem

- Producing photorealistic and temporally consistent lighting effects in portrait videos is challenging due to complex light transport and maintaining stability across frames.
- Existing high-fidelity relighting methods rely on expensive, per-subject dynamic light stage captures (OLAT data), limiting their accessibility and scalability.
- Current data-driven approaches struggle to offer precise, fine-grained control over High Dynamic Range (HDR) lighting environments or ensure temporal consistency in generated videos.

## Method

- A two-stage conditional ==video diffusion model==, based on ==Stable Video Diffusion==, is employed with separate models for delighting (albedo generation) and relighting (applying new illumination).
- A novel lighting control module tokenizes HDR maps into "light embeddings" and injects them via ==cross-attention== into the diffusion U-Net, enabling precise illumination control.
- A hybrid dataset and training strategy combine lighting-rich static OLAT images with motion-rich in-the-wild portrait videos to overcome dynamic paired video data scarcity and learn temporal consistency.

## Results

- Lux Post Facto quantitatively outperformed state-of-the-art image relighting methods (Total Relighting, SwitchLight) with an LPIPS of **0.1158** and a PSNR of **24.62** dB, indicating higher fidelity and perceptual quality.
- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that the method generates more realistic highlights, skin tones, finer details, and better-formed shadows with superior temporal stability compared to prior video relighting models like NVPR and SwitchLight.
- Ablation studies confirmed the essential contributions of the proposed HDR map tokenization lighting module and the hybrid dataset training strategy for achieving high-fidelity, precise lighting control and robust temporal consistency across video frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise and fine-grained control over complex HDR lighting within video diffusion models can be achieved by tokenizing HDR maps and integrating them via cross-attention.
> - Training on a hybrid dataset, combining static OLAT data for lighting supervision with in-the-wild videos for motion and temporal consistency, effectively addresses the scarcity of dynamic paired video data.
> - An autoregressive generation scheme, along with a specialized spatio-temporal U-Net design, is crucial for maintaining temporal coherence and photorealism when relighting extended video sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of portrait relighting aims to computationally modify the illumination of a subject in an image or video. This capability is significant for visual storytelling, film production, and general content creation, as lighting influences scene depth, mood, and visual impact.

Historically, accurate relighting relied on specialized capture setups such as light stages, which record a subject's reflectance field under dense arrays of lighting conditions (One-Light-At-A-Time, or OLAT). Pioneering work by Debevec et al. (2000) and later extensions to dynamic scenarios (Wenger et al., 2005) demonstrated cinematic relighting of facial performances. However, these methods are resource-intensive, requiring complex equipment and per-subject capture, which limits their scalability and generalizability to new subjects or in-the-wild content.

The research landscape has shifted towards data-driven and deep learning approaches to overcome these limitations. Early efforts involved transferring reflectance information using techniques like quotient images or intrinsic decomposition. More recently, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been applied to image relighting, enabling novel lighting effects and shadow manipulation. While successful for static images, applying these image-based methods to video often results in temporal inconsistencies or a loss of photorealism due to frame-by-frame processing.

Video relighting presents additional challenges, primarily maintaining temporal consistency across frames while producing photorealistic results. Existing video relighting methods often involve trade-offs between spatial quality and temporal stability. Some extend image relighting models with temporal smoothing, which can lead to blurry details, while others simplify lighting conditions or rely on scarce dynamic OLAT datasets, which are expensive to acquire. Neural Video Portrait Relighting (NVPR) integrates temporal consistency losses but may compromise facial detail. More recent 3D-based methods leveraging representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting require multi-view capture for both training and inference, again limiting generalization and accessibility.

Concurrently, the emergence of video diffusion models has demonstrated success in generating temporally coherent and high-quality videos from various inputs, including text. However, these models typically lack fine-grained control over specific lighting parameters like High Dynamic Range (HDR) environmental maps. Methods attempting lighting control often use conventional image conditioning (e.g., CLIP embeddings) which may not capture HDR details, or rely on explicit proxy geometry, which is prone to estimation errors.

"Lux Post Facto" positions itself within this context by addressing the challenges of video portrait relighting through the integration of state-of-the-art conditional video diffusion models and a novel hybrid dataset training strategy. The work aims to achieve both photorealism and temporal stability without requiring complex, per-subject dynamic OLAT data, and to provide precise lighting control, distinguishing it from prior works that either struggle with video consistency, lack fine-grained lighting control, or depend on extensive dynamic paired video data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust video relighting method capable of producing photorealistic and temporally consistent lighting effects on arbitrary portrait videos. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in existing relighting techniques:

1. **Accessibility for Content Creators:** The authors aim to democratize advanced lighting control, making it a simple post-production process accessible to a wider range of content creators beyond those with professional filming expertise and expensive equipment. This necessitates a method that can process in-the-wild portrait videos without specialized capture.
2. **Addressing Photorealism and Temporal Consistency in Video:** A significant challenge in video portrait relighting is simultaneously achieving high photorealism in complex facial reflections and consistent lighting effects across a dynamic video sequence. Existing methods often compromise on one aspect, either producing unrealistic lighting or exhibiting temporal flickering and instability when applied frame-by-frame.
3. **Fine-Grained Lighting Control:** While modern video diffusion models can generate high-quality videos, they typically lack precise control over specific lighting parameters. The goal is to enable users to define target lighting using High Dynamic Range (HDR) maps, which are rich in spatial details and intensity information, unlike simpler text prompts or background images. Current conditioning methods like CLIP embeddings struggle to represent HDR pixel values and spatial details, while pixel-aligned control maps often rely on error-prone explicit proxy geometry.
4. **Overcoming Data Scarcity for Training:** Training robust video relighting models traditionally requires extensive paired video datasets (e.g., dynamic OLAT data), which consist of videos of subjects performing under diverse, precisely controlled lighting conditions. Such datasets are expensive and challenging to acquire, especially at movie frame rates and across varied subjects and expressions. The motivation is to devise a training strategy that circumvents the need for such scarce paired video data.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- To design a novel conditional video diffusion model architecture that can generate plausible and photorealistic relighting solutions from ill-posed relighting problems.
- To develop a new lighting injection mechanism that allows for precise and fine-grained control over the target HDR lighting environment within the diffusion model.
- To introduce a hybrid dataset and an associated training approach that can effectively learn complex relighting and temporal modeling from more accessible data sources: static OLAT images and in-the-wild portrait performance videos. This approach seeks to avoid the intensive effort of acquiring dynamic paired video datasets.

By achieving these objectives, Lux Post Facto aims to provide a practical and high-performing solution for video portrait relighting, making professional-grade lighting adjustments accessible in post-production.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Lux Post Facto implements a two-stage approach for video portrait relighting, building upon a conditional video diffusion model. This architecture is designed to manage the complexities of light transport across both space and time, utilizing a hybrid dataset for training.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The method comprises two independently trained diffusion models:
1. **Video Delighting Model:** Takes an input portrait video and outputs a shading-free "albedo" video.
2. **Video Relighting Model:** Takes the predicted albedo video and a target HDR map as a lighting condition, then generates the relit video.
Both models share a common backbone based on the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) architecture, but differ in their specific inputs and conditioning.

#### 3.2. Model Design

The core of both models is a denoising U-Net adapted from SVD, a state-of-the-art video diffusion model known for its spatio-temporal generative capabilities.
- **Spatio-Temporal Conditioning:** To condition the denoising process on the input video, additional input channels are concatenated to the first convolutional layer of the U-Net. For handling long video sequences and enabling autoregressive prediction, binary masks are introduced as additional input. These masks indicate which frames are original input and which are previously generated predictions, helping the model differentiate and maintain temporal consistency across prediction windows.
- **Lighting Control Module (for Relighting Model):** This module is crucial for precise control over the target lighting using an HDR map. Instead of using generic image embeddings or pixel-aligned control maps, the HDR map is tokenized:
 - Each directional "light token" is derived by summing intensities over a small local area of the HDR map.
 - These tokens are then embedded into high-dimensional "light embeddings" using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
 - Positional encodings representing the average direction of each light are concatenated with these embeddings.
 - The combined "light embedding" is then passed to the denoising U-Net through cross-attention layers, similar to how text embeddings are used in text-to-video diffusion models. This design allows for a richer and more precise representation of lighting compared to alternative methods.

#### 3.3. Long Sequence Prediction

Video diffusion models typically operate on fixed-length video clips. To process longer videos, Lux Post Facto employs an iterative, autoregressive scheme. For a model trained on a clip length of L frames (e.g., L=30), subsequent frames are generated by replacing the first T frames (e.g., T=4) of the input window with previously predicted frames. This strategy ensures temporal coherence across extended video sequences.

#### 3.4. Learning Relighting on a Hybrid Dataset

Training typically requires paired videos (lit, albedo, HDR map), which are difficult to acquire. Lux Post Facto addresses this with a novel hybrid dataset and training strategy:

#### 3.4.1. Hybrid Video Data Creation

The hybrid dataset consists of two types of videos:
- **Lighting-Rich Dataset (D_l):** Generated from static OLAT image captures of 67 diverse subjects under various expressions and poses, using 36 frontal cameras. Image-based relighting is applied with 769 HDR maps from PolyHeaven to create lit images. Corresponding albedo images are derived from flat-lit captures. To convert these static images into videos, synthetic camera motions (zooming, panning) are simulated. This dataset provides accurate paired {V_l, V_a, E_l} for lighting supervision but limited real-world motion.
- **Motion-Rich Dataset (D_m):** Consists of 15,000 in-the-wild talking head videos from VFHQ, providing diverse natural motion patterns but with unknown lighting. To create paired training samples, an image delighting model (a Stable Diffusion-based model trained on D_l) is used to predict pseudo-albedo frames for each video. This results in {V_l, V_a} pairs, where V_a is a pseudo-albedo and V_l is the real lit video, but without known HDR maps (E_l). The pseudo-albedos are not temporally consistent on their own.

#### 3.4.2. Training with Hybrid Data

The hybrid dataset poses two challenges: the absence of E_l for D_m, and the temporal inconsistency of pseudo-albedo in D_m. These are addressed by introducing an auxiliary task: reference-based appearance copy.
- **Reference-based Appearance Copy:** Instead of an HDR map, a reference frame (F_i_l) from a lit video (V_l) is used to condition the model. A CNN encoder converts F_i_l into an embedding (L_ref), which is then used to condition the network to "copy" the appearance. This task does not require known HDR maps and implicitly encourages temporal consistency.
- **Video Relighting Training:** The relighting model is trained to perform both HDR-based relighting (on D_l, using E_l embeddings) and reference-based appearance copy (on D_m and D_l, using L_ref embeddings). Conditioning choice is randomized, allowing the model to leverage D_m for learning temporal consistency in motion-rich scenarios, even without explicit HDR maps.
- **Video Delighting Training:** The delighting model is similarly trained. While D_m provides pseudo-albedo targets, which can be inconsistent, joint training on D_l for precise delighting and on D_m/D_l for appearance copy helps the model learn temporal consistency for albedo generation.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

Both models are initialized with pre-trained SVD weights. A two-stage training schedule is employed:
1. **Stage 1 (Warm-up):** 200K steps on short 2-frame sequences, optimizing all model weights for rapid task adaptation.
2. **Stage 2 (Refinement):** 50K iterations on longer 30-frame sequences, specifically optimizing temporal layers to enhance consistency.
The models are optimized using v-prediction objectives and the AdamW optimizer. Inference uses a DDIM sampler with 30 diffusion steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Lux Post Facto demonstrates its capabilities through extensive evaluations, comparing against state-of-the-art image and video relighting methods.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **Video Relighting:** Qualitative comparisons were conducted against NVPR (a video relighting model trained on dynamic OLAT data) and SwitchLight (an improved commercial video relighting model). Lux Post Facto consistently produced results with more realistic highlights, skin tones, finer details, and better-formed shadows. The supplementary material further indicated superior temporal stability.
- **Image Relighting:** When Lux Post Facto was used for single-image relighting (by treating images as static videos), quantitative and qualitative evaluations were performed against Total Relighting (TR) and the SwitchLight image model.
 - **Quantitative Results:** Lux Post Facto outperformed both TR and SwitchLight across all evaluation metrics (LPIPS↓, NIQE↓, PSNR↑, SSIM↑). For instance, it achieved an LPIPS of 0.1158 compared to TR's 0.1794 and SwitchLight's 0.2129, and a PSNR of 24.62 dB compared to TR's 22.44 dB and SwitchLight's 19.87 dB. This indicates higher fidelity and perceptual quality even with the added constraint of temporal consistency training.
 - **Qualitative Results:** On both controlled test sets (using left-out OLATs with ground truth) and in-the-wild portrait images, Lux Post Facto's results were observed to be closer to ground truth or reference images, preserving skin tones better and generating more plausible highlights and shadows than competing methods.

#### 4.2. Additional Evaluations

- **Consistent Albedo Prediction:** The hybrid training strategy enabled Lux Post Facto to predict temporally consistent albedo channels. The model successfully removed existing shading while preserving facial details across video frames.
- **Precise Lighting Control:** The novel lighting injection mechanism demonstrated high controllability. The model could faithfully render complex light transport effects, including specular reflections and cast shadows, when conditioned with directional light sources or diffuse light sources (resulting in softer shadows). This was shown with rotating point lights, where the lighting effects coherently followed the light source's movement.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Lighting Control Module:** An ablation study compared the proposed HDR map tokenization and cross-attention mechanism against a common CLIP-based image encoder for lighting conditioning. The quantitative results (Table 2) showed that the proposed method significantly outperformed CLIP image encoding across all metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.1158 vs 0.1747, PSNR 24.62 dB vs 20.73 dB). Qualitatively, CLIP-based encoding struggled to accurately capture lighting intensity and direction, leading to less precise lighting control, whereas the proposed method produced high-fidelity effects that closely followed the given HDR map.
- **Hybrid Dataset Training Strategy:** An ablation study demonstrated the importance of the hybrid dataset training strategy. Without training on the motion-rich in-the-wild videos (i.e., solely training on the lighting-rich static OLAT data with simulated camera motions), the model struggled to handle subject movement in real videos, resulting in blurry and less photorealistic outputs. The hybrid training was shown to be crucial for generalization to natural motions and achieving sharper, temporally consistent results. Further temporal consistency metrics (lighting error, light instability, LPIPS between adjacent frames, warping error) confirmed that the complete model (with spatio-temporal design and hybrid training) achieved superior temporal consistency and quality compared to ablations without these components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Lux Post Facto represents an advancement in video portrait relighting, offering a method that addresses several key challenges in the field.

**Technological Advancement:** The work introduces a novel approach to achieving both photorealism and temporal consistency in video relighting, which have historically been difficult to reconcile. By leveraging and adapting a state-of-the-art video diffusion model (SVD) with a specialized lighting injection mechanism and a unique hybrid dataset training strategy, the method demonstrates a robust solution to the ill-posed problem of inverse rendering for video. The precise lighting control, demonstrated through HDR map conditioning, extends the functionality of generative models for specific visual effects tasks.

**Accessibility and Democratization of Visual Effects:** A significant impact of Lux Post Facto lies in its potential to make sophisticated lighting design accessible to a broader audience. By enabling high-fidelity relighting as a post-production step for standard portrait videos, the method removes the requirement for specialized and expensive capture equipment (like light stages for dynamic performances) and expert lighting teams. This can empower everyday content creators, independent filmmakers, and other users to achieve cinematic-quality lighting effects, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for advanced visual storytelling.

**Practicality in Training Data Management:** The innovative hybrid dataset and training strategy address a critical bottleneck in learning video relighting: the scarcity and high cost of acquiring high-quality, paired dynamic OLAT video datasets. By strategically combining readily available static OLAT images (for lighting supervision) and diverse in-the-wild portrait videos (for temporal consistency and motion generalization), the method provides a more practical and scalable training paradigm. This reduces the resource overhead typically associated with developing such capabilities.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The approach lays a foundation for future research in several areas, including:
- **Real-time Applications:** While the iterative nature of diffusion models currently limits real-time application, the paper suggests exploring more efficient architectures or distillation techniques to improve runtime efficiency, which could open doors for live production workflows.
- **Higher Resolution Generation:** The current resolution limit (512x768 pixels) due to GPU memory could be overcome by integrating off-the-shelf super-resolution models, further enhancing the applicability of the method to professional high-definition content.
- **Broader Generalization:** Addressing challenging cases like partial facial occlusions by accessories, which the current model finds difficult due to data limitations, could lead to more robust and generalized relighting solutions.
- **Advanced Lighting Effects:** Investigating the generation of complex lighting effects like foreign shadows, which are beyond the current light stage representation, could push the boundaries of achievable relighting realism.

**Ethical Considerations:** The authors acknowledge the potential for misuse of such a powerful generative tool, particularly in creating deepfakes or misleading videos. This recognition is a responsible step, underscoring the importance of advocating for responsible usage, clear content labeling, and the development of robust detection mechanisms as these technologies become more accessible.

In summary, Lux Post Facto contributes a practical, high-performance, and accessible solution to video portrait relighting, addressing key technical and logistical challenges within the field and broadening the creative possibilities for content generation.
