# Lux Post Facto: Learning Portrait Performance Relighting with Conditional Video Diffusion and a Hybrid Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Lux Post Facto, developed by Netflix Eyeline Studios and Johns Hopkins University, introduces a video diffusion model that achieves photorealistic and temporally consistent portrait relighting. The system leverages a novel lighting injection mechanism and a hybrid dataset to enable precise control over High Dynamic Range (HDR) lighting environments, outperforming prior methods in visual quality and consistency without requiring specialized dynamic capture setups.

## Problem

- Producing photorealistic and temporally consistent lighting effects in portrait videos is challenging due to complex light transport and maintaining stability across frames.
- Existing high-fidelity relighting methods rely on expensive, per-subject dynamic light stage captures (OLAT data), limiting their accessibility and scalability.
- Current data-driven approaches struggle to offer precise, fine-grained control over High Dynamic Range (HDR) lighting environments or ensure temporal consistency in generated videos.

## Method

- A two-stage conditional ==video diffusion model==, based on ==Stable Video Diffusion==, is employed with separate models for delighting (albedo generation) and relighting (applying new illumination).
- A novel lighting control module tokenizes HDR maps into "light embeddings" and injects them via ==cross-attention== into the diffusion U-Net, enabling precise illumination control.
- A hybrid dataset and training strategy combine lighting-rich static OLAT images with motion-rich in-the-wild portrait videos to overcome dynamic paired video data scarcity and learn temporal consistency.

## Results

- Lux Post Facto quantitatively outperformed state-of-the-art image relighting methods (Total Relighting, SwitchLight) with an LPIPS of **0.1158** and a PSNR of **24.62** dB, indicating higher fidelity and perceptual quality.
- Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that the method generates more realistic highlights, skin tones, finer details, and better-formed shadows with superior temporal stability compared to prior video relighting models like NVPR and SwitchLight.
- Ablation studies confirmed the essential contributions of the proposed HDR map tokenization lighting module and the hybrid dataset training strategy for achieving high-fidelity, precise lighting control and robust temporal consistency across video frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Precise and fine-grained control over complex HDR lighting within video diffusion models can be achieved by tokenizing HDR maps and integrating them via cross-attention.
> - Training on a hybrid dataset, combining static OLAT data for lighting supervision with in-the-wild videos for motion and temporal consistency, effectively addresses the scarcity of dynamic paired video data.
> - An autoregressive generation scheme, along with a specialized spatio-temporal U-Net design, is crucial for maintaining temporal coherence and photorealism when relighting extended video sequences.
