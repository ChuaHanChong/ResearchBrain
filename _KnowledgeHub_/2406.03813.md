# Touch100k: A Large-Scale Touch-Language-Vision Dataset for Touch-Centric Multimodal Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Touch100k, a large-scale dataset containing 100,147 entries that pair tactile, visual, and multi-granularity language data to address the scarcity of rich data in tactile AI. They develop TLV-Link, a pre-training method that achieves state-of-the-art performance in material property identification and robot grasping prediction, improving zero-shot rough/smooth classification accuracy by 14.0% and linear probing robot grasping accuracy by 12.2% over existing baselines.

## Problem

- Tactile perception research has been less explored compared to vision or audio, despite its importance for human and robotic interaction.
- Existing tactile datasets and models incorporating language typically use simple classification labels, limiting the depth of cross-modal associations between touch, vision, and natural language.
- A lack of large-scale, paired touch-language-vision datasets with rich semantic descriptions hinders the development of more sophisticated touch-centric multimodal AI.

## Method

- Constructed Touch100k, the first large-scale dataset (100,147 entries) integrating tactile, visual, and multi-granularity (sentence and phrase-level) natural language descriptions, generated and refined using GPT-4V and human correction.
- Proposed TLV-Link, a pre-training method for GelSight sensors, which uses a curriculum learning strategy to progressively transfer knowledge from a pre-trained vision encoder to a touch encoder.
- Employed contrastive learning to align the learned curriculum representation (from touch and vision) with multi-granularity language features for robust cross-modal understanding.

## Results

- TLV-Link achieved 67.2% accuracy for material classification and 93.1% for hard/soft classification in linear probing, outperforming the best baselines by 4.2% and 1.1% respectively.
- The method demonstrated a 94.5% accuracy in robot grasping prediction (linear probing), representing a 12.2% improvement over previous state-of-the-art.
- In zero-shot settings, TLV-Link improved rough/smooth classification accuracy by 14.0% to 77.8% and material classification by 4.2% to 70.0%, showcasing strong generalization capabilities. The study also validated that increasing dataset scale directly improves downstream performance, particularly for zero-shot tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A large-scale dataset featuring rich, multi-granularity language descriptions (sentence and phrase-level) is crucial for developing semantically informed tactile representations.
> - Curriculum learning, specifically a teacher-student paradigm transferring knowledge from a vision encoder to a touch encoder, significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of learned tactile representations.
> - Rigorous data quality enhancement, including AI-driven consistency checks and manual correction, is essential for ensuring the accuracy and practicality of generated language descriptions in multimodal datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile perception is a fundamental sense for humans, providing rich information about the physical world, crucial for interaction and manipulation. In robotics, tactile sensing is equally vital for enabling precise control, delicate manipulation, and safe interaction with diverse objects. Despite its acknowledged importance, research into tactile perception remains comparatively less explored than that of visual or auditory perception.

The existing body of work in tactile sensing primarily focuses on integrating touch with vision, aiming to leverage the strengths of both modalities for tasks like object recognition, material identification, and manipulation. However, a significant gap exists in the research landscape concerning the explicit incorporation of the language modality alongside touch and vision. While some works do involve language, its representation is often limited to simple classification labels (e.g., "soft," "rough") rather than rich, descriptive natural language expressions. This limitation hinders the development of more sophisticated cross-modal associations, which are essential for true human-like understanding and interaction.

This paper addresses this critical gap by extending multimodal representation learning to include tactile, visual, and *multi-granularity* language data. Prior work in multimodal alignment has predominantly concentrated on vision-language connections, with recent efforts expanding to include modalities like audio, depth, or thermal data (e.g., ImageBind, LanguageBind). However, the integration of touch, especially with rich natural language descriptions, has largely been overlooked. By constructing Touch100k, the first large-scale dataset encompassing these three modalities with multi-granularity language, the authors significantly advance the field of multimodal learning by providing a foundational resource.

Furthermore, the paper innovatively applies curriculum learning, a training strategy that gradually increases the difficulty of tasks to enhance model generalization, to the novel context of multimodal alignment involving touch, vision, and language. While curriculum learning has been successfully applied in various machine learning tasks, its application to complex multimodal fusion, particularly involving a less-explored modality like touch, is a new contribution. This work thus not only provides critical data infrastructure but also pushes methodological boundaries in multimodal representation learning and tactile AI for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the recognized importance of tactile perception for both human and robotic interaction with the environment, contrasted with the relative underdevelopment of tactile research compared to other sensory modalities like vision and audition. Specifically, the authors identify a critical void in current tactile studies: the limited exploration of the language domain. Existing datasets and models, when incorporating language, typically restrict it to simple textual classification labels, thereby overlooking the rich, descriptive semantics that natural language can offer regarding tactile sensations. This limitation severely constrains the depth of cross-modal associations that can be learned between touch, vision, and language.

To address this gap, the paper sets out with several key objectives:
1. **To construct a large-scale, paired touch-language-vision dataset:** The primary objective is to create "Touch100k," a dataset comprising 100,147 entries, featuring tactile sensation descriptions in multiple granularities (i.e., natural sentence-level expressions with rich semantics, and phrase-level descriptions capturing key features). This dataset aims to be the first of its kind to integrate these three modalities at such a large scale with detailed linguistic annotations.
2. **To propose a novel pre-training method for tactile representation:** Based on the Touch100k dataset, the authors aim to develop "Touch-Language-Vision Representation Learning through Curriculum Linking (TLV-Link)." This method is designed to learn a robust and versatile tactile representation specifically for the GelSight sensor.
3. **To capture intricate cross-modal relationships:** A central goal of TLV-Link is to effectively capture and align the relationships between tactile, language, and visual modalities, moving beyond simple classification to a deeper, semantically rich understanding.
4. **To demonstrate the effectiveness of the dataset and method:** The research aims to empirically evaluate the performance of the learned representations across various downstream tasks. These include material property identification (e.g., classification of material types, hardness, roughness) and robot grasping prediction, focusing on both direct tactile representation assessment (linear probing) and zero-shot touch understanding capabilities.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a foundational dataset and an effective methodology that can significantly advance touch-centric multimodal perception, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research paper introduces a novel dataset, Touch100k, and a corresponding pre-training method, TLV-Link, to learn touch-centric multimodal representations. The methodology is meticulously structured across three main stages for data construction and two primary components for the pre-training method.

**Touch100k Dataset Construction:**
The construction of Touch100k, a dataset of 100,147 paired touch-language-vision entries, involved three stages:
1. **Stage I: Data Collection:** The foundational vision-touch data was curated from existing publicly available datasets: TAG (91,982 entries with classification labels) and VisGel (10,000 entries, for which object names were manually labeled). All tactile observations were collected using the GelSight sensor.
2. **Stage II: Multi-granularity Description Generation:** To add the language modality, GPT-4V (gpt-4-turbo, April and May 2024 versions) was employed. Visual images from the collected data were used as input, alongside carefully designed prompts (detailed in Appendix B). The model generated textual descriptions at two granularities:
 - **Sentence-level:** Natural language expressions providing high-level, rich semantics, including contextual and dynamic relationships.
 - **Phrase-level:** Concise descriptions capturing key features of tactile sensations.
 Random selection from a pool of prompts ensured diversity in the generated content.
3. **Stage III: Multi-step Data Quality Enhancement:** To guarantee the accuracy and practicality of the generated descriptions, a robust quality enhancement process was implemented:
 - **Pattern Filtering and Machine Correction:** Regular expressions were used to identify and filter out descriptions with erroneous patterns (e.g., multilanguage confusion, special markers, semantic redundancy). Problematic data was then re-entered into GPT-4V for automated correction.
 - **Consistency Assessment and Manual Correction:** Gemini 1.0 Pro, another powerful multimodal model, acted as a referee to assess the consistency between the input visual image and the GPT-4V generated descriptions. Human workers were then hired to identify and correct any inconsistencies.
 - **Completeness Assessment and Manual Correction:** Finally, human evaluators assessed the descriptions for completeness, ensuring they conveyed various aspects of tactile sensations (e.g., touch point locations, texture features). Any identified deficiencies were manually refined and supplemented. This rigorous process yielded 100,147 valid touch-language-vision data entries.

**TLV-Link Pre-training Method:**
TLV-Link aims to learn a tactile representation for the GelSight sensor by linking tactile, language, and visual modalities through a two-stage protocol:
1. **Curriculum Representation for Tactile Encoding:** This stage is inspired by curriculum learning and leverages the inherent semantic overlap between visual and tactile data. A teacher-student paradigm is adopted:
 - The **vision encoder** (pre-trained, for its robust visual understanding) serves as the teacher model.
 - The **touch encoder** acts as the student model.
 - The curriculum representation `x` is defined as a weighted combination of the visual representation `x(v)` and the tactile representation `x(t)`: `x = β_i * x(v) + (1 - β_i) * x(t)`.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** The weight `β_i` (representing the teacher's influence) starts high (`β_1`) and linearly decays to `β_min` (set to 0) over `N` training steps: `β_i = β_1 - (i/N) * (β_1 - β_min)`. This gradually shifts learning responsibility from the vision encoder to the improving touch encoder.
2. **Modality Alignment:** This stage aligns the curriculum representation `x` with the language modality.
 - **Text Encoding:** Multi-granularity textual descriptions are processed by a *frozen* text encoder from OpenCLIP-large. This pre-trained text encoder generates a final text feature `y` after fusing sentence-level and phrase-level representations.
 - **Contrastive Learning:** InfoNCE loss is used to optimize the alignment between the curriculum representation `x` and the text feature `y`.
 - **Encoder Optimization:** The touch encoder undergoes *full training*. The vision encoder is fine-tuned using LoRA to adapt it to tactile scenarios, while the text encoder parameters remain frozen due to its strong pre-trained capabilities.

**Implementation Details:**
For both visual and tactile modalities, a 24-layer, 1024-dimensional Vision Transformer (ViT) with a patch size of 14 is used, initialized from OpenCLIP-large. Tactile observations are treated as RGB images. The AdamW optimizer with a base learning rate of 2e-4, warm-up, and cosine decay is employed. Models are trained with a batch size of 96 on 2 Nvidia A40 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Touch100k and TLV-Link focused on two task categories: material property identification and robot grasping prediction, under both linear probing and zero-shot settings. The findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed dataset and method.

**Tactile Representation Quality (Linear Probing):**
In the linear probing setting, where a lightweight linear classifier is trained on top of frozen touch encoder features, TLV-Link demonstrates superior performance:
- **Material Property Identification:**
 - Material Classification (20 classes): TLV-Link achieved 67.2% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (VIT-LENS-2) by a significant 4.2%.
 - Hard/Soft Classification: TLV-Link scored 93.1%, surpassing VIT-LENS-2 by 1.1%.
 - Rough/Smooth Classification: TLV-Link reached 84.7%, very close to the best baseline's 85.1%, indicating competitive performance.
- **Robot Grasping Prediction:** TLV-Link achieved a remarkable 94.5% accuracy, representing a substantial improvement of 12.2% over the best baseline (UniTouch), showcasing its strong capabilities in this critical robotics task.

**Zero-Shot Touch Understanding:**
The zero-shot evaluation rigorously tests the generalization capabilities of the touch encoder to unseen data distributions. TLV-Link consistently delivered superior performance:
- **Material Property Identification:**
 - Material Classification: TLV-Link achieved 70.0% accuracy, a 4.2% improvement over the best baseline (VIT-LENS-2).
 - Hard/Soft Classification: TLV-Link scored 79.3%, 4.6% higher than VIT-LENS-2.
 - Rough/Smooth Classification: A striking 77.8% accuracy was achieved, marking a 14.0% increase over VIT-LENS-2, demonstrating exceptional zero-shot generalization for texture properties.
- **Robot Grasping Prediction:** TLV-Link achieved 65.4%, marginally trailing UniTouch (65.5%) but still delivering robust zero-shot performance in a challenging task.

**Dataset Analysis:**
- **Comparison to TAG dataset:** Using a refined baseline method (VT CMC + TTCL), training on Touch100k consistently yielded better results (e.g., 64.1% vs. 60.8% for material, 61.3% vs. 43.4% for robot grasping) compared to using the TAG dataset. This strongly validates Touch100k's value as a richer and more effective resource for training tactile representations.
- **Impact of Dataset Scale:** Experiments with reduced dataset sizes (25k, 50k, 75k) showed that increasing the data scale positively influences downstream performance, particularly for zero-shot tasks. For instance, zero-shot material classification accuracy rose from 26.5% (25k) to 70.0% (100k), and robot grasping from 44.7% to 65.4%, underscoring the benefits of a large-scale dataset for generalization.

**Tactile Representation Analysis:**
t-SNE projections revealed that TLV-Link effectively separates categories for binary classification tasks like Hard/Soft and Rough/Smooth. However, for multi-classification and tasks with significantly different data distributions (e.g., complex robot manipulation), the generalization ability of the representations showed some limitations, indicating areas for future improvement.

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on the curriculum representation module demonstrated its critical contribution. Removing this module led to a performance drop across all tasks in both linear probing and zero-shot settings. The most significant decline was observed in zero-shot robot grasping, where accuracy plummeted by 21.2% (from 65.4% to 44.2%), decisively proving the effectiveness of the curriculum learning approach in enhancing generalization and knowledge transfer.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that Touch100k is a valuable resource, and TLV-Link is an effective pre-training method, achieving state-of-the-art performance and strong generalization in touch-centric multimodal tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Touch100k and the TLV-Link pre-training method represents a significant leap forward in the field of multimodal perception, particularly for tactile AI and robotics. The contributions hold substantial significance and promise considerable impact:

1. **Novel and Unprecedented Dataset:** Touch100k stands out as the first large-scale (100k entries) paired touch-language-vision dataset featuring tactile sensation descriptions in multiple granularities. This addresses a critical data scarcity issue that has historically hampered advanced research in tactile perception. By providing rich, natural language annotations alongside visual and tactile data, Touch100k enables a much deeper, semantically informed understanding of tactile sensations than previously possible with simple classification labels. This dataset is poised to become a foundational resource for future research, catalyzing new developments in touch-centric multimodal learning.

2. **State-of-the-Art Multimodal Representation Learning:** The proposed TLV-Link method, leveraging curriculum learning and contrastive alignment, establishes a new state-of-the-art in touch-centric multimodal representation learning. Its impressive performance on tasks like material property identification and robot grasping prediction, especially in zero-shot settings, showcases the robustness and generalization capabilities of the learned tactile representations. This advancement means that robots can potentially acquire a more nuanced "understanding" of objects through touch, akin to human perception.

3. **Innovative Application of Curriculum Learning:** The successful integration of a teacher-student curriculum paradigm for knowledge transfer from visual to tactile modalities in a complex multimodal alignment setting is a methodological innovation. This demonstrates that curriculum learning can effectively generalize to new modalities and enhance the learning efficiency and performance of deep learning models in complex, data-scarce domains.

4. **Advancements in Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The primary impact of this work lies in its potential to revolutionize robotics. More precise and generalized tactile representations can lead to:
 - **Enhanced Manipulation:** Robots equipped with better tactile understanding can perform delicate assembly tasks, handle fragile objects with greater care, and execute complex manipulation sequences in unstructured environments.
 - **Improved Grasping Stability:** The significant gains in robot grasping prediction directly translate to more reliable and adaptable robotic grippers, crucial for automation in manufacturing, logistics, and service industries.
 - **Richer Human-Robot Collaboration:** A robot capable of describing what it "feels" or understanding natural language instructions related to tactile properties can foster more intuitive and effective collaboration between humans and robots. This could extend to assistive technologies, where robots could better interpret and respond to human touch.

5. **Broader Multimodal AI Understanding:** By successfully integrating touch into a comprehensive multimodal framework, this research contributes to the broader goal of building AI systems that can perceive and interpret the world through diverse sensory channels. This holistic understanding is essential for developing general-purpose AI and intelligent agents that can operate effectively in complex, real-world environments.

While the current work is specifically designed for GelSight sensors, the authors acknowledge this limitation and highlight future work on enhancing transferability across diverse sensor types. This forward-looking perspective, coupled with the foundational dataset and effective methodology, positions "Touch100k" as a pivotal contribution that will inspire and enable a new generation of research in tactile perception, multimodal AI, and intelligent robotics.
