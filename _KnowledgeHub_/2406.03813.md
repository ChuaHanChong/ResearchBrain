# Touch100k: A Large-Scale Touch-Language-Vision Dataset for Touch-Centric Multimodal Representation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Touch100k, a large-scale dataset containing 100,147 entries that pair tactile, visual, and multi-granularity language data to address the scarcity of rich data in tactile AI. They develop TLV-Link, a pre-training method that achieves state-of-the-art performance in material property identification and robot grasping prediction, improving zero-shot rough/smooth classification accuracy by 14.0% and linear probing robot grasping accuracy by 12.2% over existing baselines.

## Problem

- Tactile perception research has been less explored compared to vision or audio, despite its importance for human and robotic interaction.
- Existing tactile datasets and models incorporating language typically use simple classification labels, limiting the depth of cross-modal associations between touch, vision, and natural language.
- A lack of large-scale, paired touch-language-vision datasets with rich semantic descriptions hinders the development of more sophisticated touch-centric multimodal AI.

## Method

- Constructed Touch100k, the first large-scale dataset (100,147 entries) integrating tactile, visual, and multi-granularity (sentence and phrase-level) natural language descriptions, generated and refined using GPT-4V and human correction.
- Proposed TLV-Link, a pre-training method for GelSight sensors, which uses a curriculum learning strategy to progressively transfer knowledge from a pre-trained vision encoder to a touch encoder.
- Employed contrastive learning to align the learned curriculum representation (from touch and vision) with multi-granularity language features for robust cross-modal understanding.

## Results

- TLV-Link achieved 67.2% accuracy for material classification and 93.1% for hard/soft classification in linear probing, outperforming the best baselines by 4.2% and 1.1% respectively.
- The method demonstrated a 94.5% accuracy in robot grasping prediction (linear probing), representing a 12.2% improvement over previous state-of-the-art.
- In zero-shot settings, TLV-Link improved rough/smooth classification accuracy by 14.0% to 77.8% and material classification by 4.2% to 70.0%, showcasing strong generalization capabilities. The study also validated that increasing dataset scale directly improves downstream performance, particularly for zero-shot tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A large-scale dataset featuring rich, multi-granularity language descriptions (sentence and phrase-level) is crucial for developing semantically informed tactile representations.
> - Curriculum learning, specifically a teacher-student paradigm transferring knowledge from a vision encoder to a touch encoder, significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of learned tactile representations.
> - Rigorous data quality enhancement, including AI-driven consistency checks and manual correction, is essential for ensuring the accuracy and practicality of generated language descriptions in multimodal datasets.
