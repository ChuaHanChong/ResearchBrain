# OXE-AugE: A Large-Scale Robot Augmentation of OXE for Scaling Cross-Embodiment Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley developed OXE-AugE, a large-scale augmented dataset created using the AugE-Toolkit pipeline, to address data imbalance in robot learning datasets by generating physically accurate demonstrations for diverse robot embodiments. Policies fine-tuned on OXE-AugE achieved average success rate improvements of 24-45% on unseen robot-gripper configurations in real-world experiments, significantly enhancing robustness and cross-embodiment generalization.

## Problem

- Large-scale robot datasets like Open X-Embodiment (OXE) suffer from severe data imbalance, with a few robot types dominating the majority of trajectories.
- This imbalance limits the ability of generalist robot policies to effectively generalize across diverse robot embodiments without costly data collection or fine-tuning for each new platform.
- Existing robot data augmentation methods often lack kinematic validity, physical accuracy, or scalability to many target robots.

## Method

- Developed AugE-Toolkit, an open-source, scalable pipeline that fuses learned and simulation-based masks and utilizes automatic base position tuning for physically accurate robot augmentation.
- Created **OXE-AugE**, a large-scale dataset by augmenting 16 popular OXE datasets with up to 9 new robot embodiments, tripling the original data volume to over 4.4 million trajectories.
- Employed physics-based rendering within MuJoCo Playground for augmented robot replay, ensuring physically accurate poses and visual realism over artifact-prone generative models.

## Results

- Simulation studies showed N-robot augmentation consistently improved robustness on the source robot and achieved generalization performance on unseen robots that rivaled or surpassed transfer to explicitly augmented target robots.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that OpenVLA and Pi0 foundation models fine-tuned on OXE-AugE achieved average success rate improvements of **24%** and **45%**, respectively, on unseen robot-gripper configurations.
- Policies fine-tuned on OXE-AugE achieved high average success rates (75-**82%**) on a novel 'Robotiq++' embodiment, outperforming baseline training and demonstrating superior performance compared to diffusion-based augmentation (which led to a 27-**30%** drop in success).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling the number and diversity of robot augmentations significantly improves policy robustness against visual perturbations on the source robot, fostering learning of task-relevant spatial geometry.
> - Diverse robot augmentation facilitates the learning of embodiment-agnostic visual representations and spatial reasoning, enabling effective zero-shot generalization to previously unseen robot embodiments.
> - Physically accurate, simulation-based augmentation is crucial for generating effective robot training data, outperforming diffusion-based methods that introduce visual artifacts and geometric inconsistencies.
