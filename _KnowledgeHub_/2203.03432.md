# Learning Solution Manifolds for Control Problems via Energy Minimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for learning solution manifolds for control problems is introduced, minimizing the integral of an energy function over the input space. This enables neural network policies to instantly generate solutions for complex robotic tasks like inverse kinematics and trajectory optimization, achieving faster execution and more consistent results compared to traditional behavioral cloning.

## Problem

- Traditional optimization-based control for robotics is computationally demanding, preventing real-time execution in dynamic scenarios.
- Existing machine learning approaches like Behavioral Cloning (BC) struggle with multimodal solution spaces, leading to conflicting training data and erratic approximation landscapes.
- Rapidly generating optimal control solutions after slight input changes introduces unacceptable delays for real-time robotic applications.

## Method

- The learning of a neural network policy is formulated as minimizing the integral of the control objective's ==energy function== over the entire input parameter space.
- The energy integral minimization is reinterpreted as a supervised learning problem using dynamic, converging targets derived from single optimization steps on the current policy output.
- Strategic sampling techniques, including incremental and rejection sampling, are employed to improve domain coverage, manage multimodality, and actively remove conflicting data points during training.

## Results

- The proposed method achieves significantly lower and more stable position errors across various robotic tasks compared to Behavioral Cloning and Dagger, with benefits becoming more pronounced in complex problems.
- Learned policies generate smoother and lower-energy objective landscapes by effectively mitigating conflicting regions, leading to better generalization.
- Using the learned policies to warm-start UR5 Kinematic Trajectory Optimization reduces optimization time from an average of 47ms to 3ms, demonstrating practical real-time acceleration while maintaining high accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly minimizing an integrated energy function allows for robust policy learning that inherently addresses multimodal solution spaces without relying on fixed 'expert' demonstrations.
> - Reinterpreting energy minimization as behavioral cloning with dynamic, optimization-derived targets provides a stable and numerically sound learning framework.
> - Strategic sampling methods, particularly rejection sampling, are crucial for producing smooth, consistent, and lower-energy objective landscapes, preventing interpolation artifacts from conflicting data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the intersection of optimization-based control for robotics and machine learning. Optimization algorithms are frequently employed in robotics for tasks such as inverse kinematics (IK), trajectory optimization (TO), and model predictive control (MPC). These methods can address complex, non-linear problems and underpin the motion pipelines of advanced robotic systems. However, a significant limitation of these optimization algorithms is their computational demand, which often precludes their direct use in real-time applications requiring rapid responses to dynamic input changes.

To overcome this real-time constraint, machine learning techniques, particularly imitation learning, have been explored to distill complex optimal solutions into fast-executing policies. Behavioral cloning (BC) is a common approach where a neural network is trained via supervised learning on a dataset of expert solutions generated by optimization algorithms. However, BC faces challenges, notably poor generalization when the dataset contains conflicting samples. This issue frequently arises in tasks with multimodal solution spaces, where multiple optimal solutions exist for a given input (e.g., multiple configurations for an IK target), leading to an erratic approximation landscape and interpolation artifacts.

This paper positions itself as an alternative to conventional BC, aiming to provide a more robust and efficient method for learning solution manifolds. It addresses the shortcomings of BC, particularly the problem of conflicting data arising from multimodality, by reformulating the learning task. While other approaches like Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) or f-divergence minimization have attempted to handle multimodality, or methods like Soft-Q Imitation Learning (SQIL) and Implicit Behavioral Cloning (IBC) offer alternative imitation learning paradigms, this work proposes a distinct gradient-based framework. The focus is on learning *trajectory-based policies* (producing an entire sequence of actions at once) rather than reactive one-step policies, differentiating it from some prior gradient-based policy search methods. Furthermore, it aims to accelerate optimization-based control, similar to other works but with an emphasis on both kinematic and dynamic problems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable instantaneous execution of optimization-based control for real-time robotic applications by leveraging machine learning. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational Bottlenecks:** Traditional optimization algorithms, while powerful and flexible, are too computationally intensive for real-time control. Tasks like re-planning an inverse kinematics solution or generating a new trajectory after a slight input change can introduce unacceptable delays. The paper aims to bridge this gap by learning policies that can be queried rapidly.

2. **Overcoming Behavioral Cloning Limitations:** Existing machine learning approaches, particularly behavioral cloning (BC), are often used to distill optimal solutions into neural networks. However, the authors identify significant shortcomings in BC:
 - **Incompatible Local Minima:** When a control problem has multiple valid solutions (multimodality), BC struggles to learn a consistent policy. Training data derived from different optimal solutions for the same input can be conflicting.
 - **Interpolation Artifacts:** Conflicting samples lead to an erratic approximation landscape, where interpolating between different optimal modes results in sub-optimal or infeasible solutions. This manifests as high-energy regions in the learned policy's output space.
 - **Insufficient Coverage:** Standard data generation for BC might not adequately cover the entire state space of interest, leading to generalization issues.

3. **Developing a Robust and Numerically Stable Learning Framework:** Motivated by the instability and poor generalization of BC, the authors seek an alternative that is both efficient and robust. Instead of merely mimicking expert solutions, the objective is to learn a functional mapping that directly satisfies the underlying control objective across the entire domain of interest.

4. **Focus on Trajectory-Based Policies:** The research specifically targets policies that output an entire sequence of actions (trajectories) in a single step, rather than iterative, one-step policies. This is relevant for many robotic tasks requiring coordinated movements over time.

To achieve these objectives, the paper proposes a novel formulation: learning solution manifolds by minimizing the integral of the control objective's energy terms over the space of all relevant problem inputs. This approach is intended to inherently address the challenges of multimodality and conflicting samples by not relying on pre-sampled "correct" solutions, but rather by continuously refining a policy to satisfy the energy landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on formulating the learning of solution manifolds as an energy minimization problem integrated over the space of problem inputs, and then solving this with a novel combination of adaptive sampling and gradient-based optimization.

**A. Solving Individual Problem Instances:**
The paper begins by defining a general optimal control problem where an action trajectory $\bar{a}^*$ is sought to minimize an energy function $E(\bar{s}(\bar{a}, p), \bar{a}, p)$. Here, $\bar{s}$ is the state trajectory, $\bar{a}$ is the control trajectory, and $p$ represents input parameters. For a simple Inverse Kinematics (IK) problem, $E$ is defined as the squared norm of the end-effector position error and a regularization term for joint angles. Individual instances of this problem can be solved using standard gradient-based optimization methods:
- **First-order update rule:** Gradient descent.
- **Second-order update rule:** Newton's method, often using the Gauss-Newton approximation of the Hessian for guaranteed descent. These updates are iterative, starting from an initial guess and refining the action $\bar{a}$.

**B. Learning the Optimal Solution Space:**
The core innovation is to learn a neural network policy $\bar{\pi}_\theta$ (parameterized by $\theta$) that directly outputs optimal actions for any given input $p$. This learning process is formulated as minimizing the integral of the energy function over the entire space of input parameters $\mathcal{P}$:
$\theta^* = \arg \min_\theta \int_\mathcal{P} E(\bar{s}(\bar{\pi}_\theta, p), \bar{\pi}_\theta, p) dp$

This integral is approximated using Monte-Carlo integration as a finite sum over $M$ samples:
$\theta^* \approx \arg \min_\theta \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M E(\bar{s}(\bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m), \bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m)$

The gradient of this sum with respect to $\theta$ can be computed using the chain rule. Crucially, the authors reinterpret this energy minimization in parameter space $\theta$ as a form of Behavioral Cloning (BC) with *dynamic, converging targets*. Instead of using pre-computed, fixed expert solutions as targets, the targets $T_m$ are derived from applying a single step of the individual problem's optimization (gradient descent or Newton's method) to the *current output of the policy* $\bar{\pi}_\theta(p_m)$.
- **First-order target:** $T_m = \bar{\pi}_\theta(p_m) - \alpha_{\bar{a}} \frac{dE}{d\bar{\pi}_\theta}(\bar{s}(\bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m), \bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m)$
- **Second-order target:** $T_m = \bar{\pi}_\theta(p_m) - \alpha_{\bar{a}} H^{-1} \frac{dE}{d\bar{\pi}_\theta}(\bar{s}(\bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m), \bar{\pi}_\theta, p_m)$, where $H$ is the Hessian (or its Gauss-Newton approximation).

By substituting these dynamic targets into a standard supervised learning update rule for $\theta$, the authors show that it aligns with the first-order update rule for the energy integral minimization. This interpretation allows the use of line-search procedures on $\alpha_{\bar{a}}$ to guarantee monotonic improvement of the energy for individual target generation, stabilizing the training process. The second-order target formulation can be viewed as minimizing a weighted squared error loss, with the inverse Hessian acting as a weighting matrix.

**C. Strategic Sampling for Policy Learning:**
To effectively approximate the energy integral and handle multimodality, several sampling strategies are proposed:
- **Static Sampling:** Samples $p_m$ are chosen once and remain fixed. Poisson-disk sampling (PDS) is employed to ensure an even distribution of samples for better domain coverage and conflict detection.
- **Dynamic Sampling:** Samples $p_m$ are re-sampled at each iteration from a continuous distribution, similar to Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with batches.
- **Incremental Sampling:** The size of the input sample set is gradually increased. Training starts with a small "seed" sample that yields low energy, and then new samples are added if they are close to the existing set, expanding the domain slowly. This aims to bootstrap a uniform solution manifold.
- **Rejection Sampling (Algorithm 2):** This method actively identifies and removes "conflicting" samples from the training dataset. It works by:
 1. For each sample, finding its nearest neighbors within a radius $r$.
 2. Interpolating the sample and its neighbors, then evaluating a discrepancy metric $D_m$ (e.g., position error) for this averaged point and its averaged target.
 3. If $D_m$ exceeds a threshold (average $D_m + \epsilon$), the sample and its neighbors are rejected. PDS helps in setting $r$ (twice the Poisson disk radius) and interpreting $D_m$ (position error). This mechanism is applied at every iteration after targets are updated.

**D. Experimental Setups:**
The methodology is evaluated on a series of robotic control problems:
- **Planar IK for n-link mechanisms (n=2 to 5):** Learning IK solutions for planar serial robots. Inputs are 2D end-effector targets, outputs are joint angles. The energy function includes end-effector position error and joint angle regularization. Increased links introduce higher dimensionality and potential conflicts.
- **IK for UR5 serial robot with self-collision:** Learning IK for a 6-DoF UR5 robot. Inputs are 3D Cartesian targets, outputs are 6 joint angles. The energy function incorporates joint limits, collision avoidance (using a unilateral barrier function and collision primitives), joint angle regularization, and position/orientation error.
- **Kinematic Trajectory Optimization (KTO) for UR5:** Learning feasible trajectories for the UR5 robot from a start configuration to a target Cartesian position. The policy outputs a sequence of joint angles. The energy function includes feasibility constraints (joint position, velocity, acceleration, jerk limits using bilateral barrier functions), smoothness objectives (small velocities, accelerations, jerks), collision avoidance, and position/orientation errors at start/end.

**E. Network Architecture:**
For all experiments, a neural network with two hidden layers, 512 nodes, and ReLU activation functions was used. The output layer uses tanh activations. Joint angles are encoded as (sin $\theta_i$, cos $\theta_i$) to handle periodicity, which also helps mitigate conflicting data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings based on comparisons against Behavioral Cloning (BC) and Dataset aggregation (Dagger), evaluating average position error, energy landscapes, and computational performance.

**A. Performance Comparison (Position Error):**
- **Overall Performance:** The proposed method, especially with incremental sampling and rejection sampling, generally achieves lower and more stable position errors compared to BC and Dagger across various robotic control problems (Figure 5).
- **Initial Convergence:** "Ours + Incremental + Rejection Sampling" exhibits a slower initial convergence than BC. This is attributed to BC starting with a fully labeled dataset covering the entire domain, whereas the rejection sampling method gradually expands its coverage from a small region. However, over more training steps, the proposed method surpasses BC's performance.
- **Stability and Variance:** The "Ours" methods, particularly with rejection sampling, tend to show lower variance in position error, indicating more consistent learning and generalization. BC and Dagger often display higher and more erratic error curves.
- **Problem Complexity:** The benefits of the proposed method become more pronounced with increasing problem complexity, such as the 4-Link, 5-Link IK problems, and the UR5 Kinematic Trajectory Optimization (KTO).

**B. Energy Landscape Consistency:**
- **Reduction of Conflicts:** Figure 1 and Figure 6 illustrate a primary benefit: the proposed approach produces smoother and lower-energy objective landscapes compared to BC. BC's landscapes often feature high-energy "elbow-shaped" conflicting regions resulting from interpolating between multimodal solutions. In contrast, the sampling schemes, particularly rejection sampling, mitigate these conflicts, leading to more uniform landscapes.
- **Impact of Sampling Strategies:** While static and dynamic sampling already improve landscapes over BC, incremental and rejection sampling further refine them, achieving lower error and variance.

**C. Conflict Detection Effectiveness:**
- **Detection Capability:** Figure 3 demonstrates that the proposed conflict detection mechanism can identify conflicting data points effectively, even with a relatively sparse number of test samples. This is crucial for higher-dimensional input spaces where dense sampling is impractical.
- **Origin Conflicts:** The mechanism also correctly flags regions with inherently ambiguous solutions, such as the origin in an IK problem (where infinite solutions exist), as conflicting.

**D. Warm-starting Optimization:**
- **Significant Speedup:** When the learned policies are used to warm-start the Kinematic Trajectory Optimization (KTO) problem for the UR5 robot, there is a substantial reduction in optimization time, from an average of 47ms to 3ms. This speedup is achieved while maintaining high accuracy (0.05 mm position error and 0.05 degrees orientation error), indicating that the learned policies provide effective initial guesses for full optimization.

**E. Broader Class of Systems:**
- **Generality:** The framework's generality is demonstrated by its application to diverse dynamic problems beyond kinematics (Figure 7). This includes dragging a point mass, repositioning and reorienting a rigid body box on a frictional surface, and reorienting a box with a flexible attachment. This suggests the method's applicability to a wider range of control problems formulated as energy minimization.

**F. Comparison with Multimodal Approaches (IBC):**
- **Performance vs. IBC:** When compared against Implicit Behavioral Cloning (IBC), a method designed to preserve multimodality, the proposed energy minimization approach achieves significantly lower position errors and faster computation times for planar n-Link IK problems (Table I).
 - For example, on the 5-Link IK problem, the proposed method achieves an error of 0.64 mm in 3 minutes, while IBC has an error of 76.6 mm and takes 103 minutes.
- **Trade-offs:** While IBC aims to preserve multimodality, the presented method focuses on learning one consistent solution manifold. The results indicate that for tasks where a single robust solution is sufficient, the proposed method provides a more efficient and accurate outcome under the given experimental conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers several advancements and potential impacts on the fields of robotics and machine learning for control:

1. **Enabling Real-time Optimization-Based Control:** The primary significance lies in its ability to convert computationally expensive optimization problems into fast-executing neural network policies. This facilitates the deployment of complex control algorithms in real-time robotic applications, where rapid reaction times are crucial. By achieving average KTO times of 3ms for the UR5, the method demonstrates a path towards instantaneous control from initially slow optimization.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Behavioral Cloning:** The work directly tackles fundamental issues of traditional behavioral cloning, specifically the problems of conflicting data and erratic solution landscapes arising from multimodal solution spaces. By reformulating the learning problem as an integrated energy minimization and using dynamic, second-order targets, it offers a more robust and numerically stable alternative.

3. **Improved Solution Manifold Consistency:** The development of strategic sampling techniques, particularly incremental and rejection sampling, leads to the learning of smoother, more consistent, and lower-energy solution manifolds. This directly translates to better generalization capabilities of the learned policies across the input domain.

4. **Novel Integration of Optimization and Learning:** The approach elegantly reinterprets the energy integral minimization as a supervised learning problem with a "converging supervisor" that provides dynamic, optimization-derived targets. This hybrid approach leverages the strengths of both optimization (guaranteed descent, robustness) and machine learning (function approximation, speed).

5. **Generalizability Across Problem Domains:** The demonstration of the method's applicability to a range of problems, from simple planar IK to complex UR5 trajectory optimization with collision avoidance and various dynamic systems, suggests its broad utility beyond specific robotic platforms or task types.

6. **Potential for Warm-Starting Existing Optimizers:** The ability of learned policies to serve as effective warm-starts for full optimization routines offers a significant practical impact. It provides a means to quickly obtain a highly accurate initial guess, thereby reducing the computational cost of online re-optimization without sacrificing solution quality.

7. **Foundation for Advanced Multimodal Learning:** While the current method simplifies multimodality by learning one consistent manifold, the developed conflict detection mechanism could serve as a basis for future research. It might be used to cluster different solution modes, paving the way for learning multiple solution manifolds or adaptive sampling in regions of high conflict.

Overall, this work contributes a robust methodology for distilling complex optimization-based control into performant neural network policies, addressing key challenges in real-time robotics and providing a framework for future advancements in intelligent control systems.
