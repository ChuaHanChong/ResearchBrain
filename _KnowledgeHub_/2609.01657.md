# NeoMME: A Single-Tower Multimodal-Native Multilingual Foundation Encoder for Efficient Fine-Tuning and Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> NeoMME is a family of 260M and 800M-parameter bidirectional encoders that feed multilingual text tokens and raw 32x32 image patches through one shared Transformer, with no pretrained vision tower, trained from scratch with an image-conditioned masked discrete-diffusion text objective at 16,384-token context. Fine-tuned with joint dense and late-interaction heads, NeoMME-Retriever reaches 0.523 (260M) and 0.556 (800M) nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3.

## Problem

- Visual document retrievers like ColPali repurpose generative VLMs as encoders, inheriting the parameter and compute overhead of a causal decoder for a non-generative task.
- Dual-tower CLIP/SigLIP designs and ModernVBERT still depend on separately pretrained vision towers, giving modalities asymmetric computational paths and fragmenting the pretraining, fine-tuning and serving lifecycle.
- Prior shared-Transformer multimodal models initialize from pretrained ViTs, use externally tokenized images, or target generation, so none pairs raw patches with from-scratch retrieval training.

## Method

- ==Single-tower== backbone: factorized text embeddings and a 2-layer MLP patch stem feed one bidirectional Transformer with ==2D RoPE==, ==grouped-query attention==, and interleaved 256/1024 ==sliding-window== plus global layers.
- ==Image-conditioned masked discrete-diffusion pretraining==: text segments draw corruption rate from U(0,1), multimodal segments from U(0.30,1) to suppress text-only shortcuts, with ==inverse-rate loss weighting== and no pixel target.
- **NeoMME-Retriever** adds jointly trained ==late-interaction== (128-d per token) and ==Matryoshka== dense mean-pooled heads on one forward pass, plus **Late-Interaction Kernels** that tile ==MaxSim== without materializing the similarity tensor.

## Results

- NeoMME-Retriever 260M scores **0.523** nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3, beating the best sub-300M model by **26.1 points** and sitting within **0.2 points** of the **14.4x** larger ColQwen2.5.
- At 2048x2048 on an L40S the 260M model indexes **51.3 pages/s**, **1.97x** ColModernVBERT; pool factor 8 with int8 queries and binary documents cuts **1536.7 kB** to **6.0 kB** while retaining **95.19%**.
- Input-ablation probes give **38.4** and **40.5 point** image gain at **90%** text masking; transfer reaches **75.3 mean** on 17 language tasks but only **13.2** frozen 16-shot natural-image accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A from-scratch shared bidirectional Transformer over raw patches can rival retrievers over ten times larger, so an inherited vision tower buys parameters more than retrieval quality.
> - Conditioning solely through masked-text denoising leaves no usable frozen image space; without a direct visual target, natural-image transfer stays weak while document transfer is fast.
> - Page images hold far more redundant patch vectors than text holds redundant tokens, so visual late-interaction indexes tolerate aggressive pooling and binary document quantization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal machine learning has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of large language models (LLMs) and visual language models (VLMs). Many contemporary multimodal models build upon architectures initially designed for generative vision-language tasks, typically combining separately pretrained vision encoders with causal language models. Examples include CLIP and SigLIP, which align independent image and text towers, and generative VLMs like Flamingo and BLIP-2, which often project visual encoder outputs to a causal language model.

When these VLM architectures are repurposed for non-generative tasks such as visual document retrieval, they often carry over parameter and compute overhead that is not optimized for the specific task. For instance, models like ColPali adapt VLMs for retrieval, while ModernVBERT (a 250M-parameter model) replaces the causal decoder with a bidirectional encoder but still retains a pretrained visual tower (SigLIP2).

An alternative approach involves processing image and text tokens within a single, shared Transformer encoder. This design has been explored in bidirectional architectures such as ViLT, OneR, and M3AE, as well as in more recent tower-free VLMs like Fuyu and EVEv2, which feed projected image patches directly into generative backbones. These single-tower designs offer the advantage of a unified computational path and model lifecycle across modalities.

In the realm of text representation, bidirectional encoders like BERT established masked bidirectional pretraining, with subsequent models like Sentence-BERT and ColBERT adapting these for dense and late-interaction retrieval. ModernBERT and other contemporary models have extended these architectures with longer contexts and broader language coverage. There is an ongoing discussion regarding the comparative strengths of native masked encoders versus causal decoders (or decoder-to-encoder adaptations) for various tasks, with evidence suggesting encoders excel in classification and retrieval, while decoders are stronger for generation.

The training objective for text representations has also evolved, with masked diffusion models (e.g., DiffusionBERT, MDLM, LLaDA) demonstrating effectiveness for denoising and representation learning. Multimodal extensions of diffusion models exist (e.g., Masked Diffusion Captioning, UniDisc), but typically operate on externally tokenized images or primarily target generation.

The present work introduces NeoMME to address a specific gap: the absence of a system that combines continuous raw image patches and multilingual text processed by a single, shared bidirectional Transformer encoder, trained from random initialization using an image-conditioned masked-text diffusion objective, and subsequently evaluated for both dense and late-interaction visual document retrieval. NeoMME aims to provide a natively multimodal and multilingual foundation encoder that is efficient for fine-tuning and inference in non-generative tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that many existing multimodal models, particularly those adapted for non-generative tasks like visual document retrieval, often inherit architectural complexities and computational overhead from their generative vision-language modeling origins. Specifically, these models frequently rely on dual-tower designs with separately pretrained vision encoders coupled with causal language models. When applied to tasks such as visual document retrieval, this approach can be inefficient, both in terms of parameter count and computational demands.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Multimodal-Native, Multilingual, Single-Tower Foundation Encoder**: To introduce a family of bidirectional multimodal encoders, named NeoMME, that are designed from scratch. These models are intended to process multilingual text and raw image patches within a single, unified bidirectional Transformer encoder, thereby avoiding the overhead associated with separate modality-specific towers or causal decoders.
2. **Ensure Efficiency for Fine-Tuning and Inference**: To optimize the NeoMME architecture for efficient downstream fine-tuning and inference. This includes incorporating features like long context windows (16,384 tokens) to handle high-resolution visual documents and dynamic-resolution image processing to maintain aspect ratios and manage patch counts effectively.
3. **Employ a Novel Pretraining Objective**: To pretrain NeoMME models from scratch using a masked discrete-diffusion text objective. For multimodal examples, this text-denoising objective is explicitly conditioned on visible image patches, aiming to encourage the model to leverage visual information for textual understanding. The objective does not involve pixel reconstruction or masked image modeling.
4. **Demonstrate Downstream Retrieval Capabilities**: To validate NeoMME's effectiveness by fine-tuning it for visual document retrieval. This involves developing NeoMME-Retriever, which can output both dense pooled representations and late-interaction multi-vector representations from a single backbone forward pass, providing deployment flexibility.
5. **Achieve High Performance and Efficiency in Retrieval**: To evaluate NeoMME-Retriever on standard visual document retrieval benchmarks (e.g., ViDoRe v3) and demonstrate competitive or superior performance relative to existing models, particularly considering model size. Additionally, to investigate and optimize aspects of retrieval efficiency, including document indexing throughput, representation storage, and compression techniques.
6. **Contribute to Open Science**: To release the pretrained NeoMME backbone and retrieval-compatible checkpoints under an open-source license, contributing the models to the Hugging Face Transformers library to facilitate further research and adoption.

By pursuing these objectives, the researchers aim to provide a more streamlined, efficient, and versatile foundation model for multimodal understanding, particularly for retrieval tasks that benefit from tight integration of visual and textual information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology behind NeoMME involves a comprehensive approach encompassing architecture design, a novel pretraining strategy, and a tailored fine-tuning process for retrieval tasks, all optimized for efficiency.

#### 3.1. Architecture (NeoMME)

NeoMME is built around a single bidirectional Transformer encoder, specifically designed for long contexts and multimodal inputs.
- **Shared Multimodal Inputs**: Text tokens and raw RGB image patches (32x32 pixels) are mapped into a shared hidden space.
 - **Text Processing**: Text tokens use an ALBERT-style factorized embedding (256-dimensional lookup projected to model width), which minimizes model size. A tied, factorized masked-token decoder is used for output, reusing embedding parameters.
 - **Image Processing**: Each image is converted to RGB and partitioned into non-overlapping 32x32 patches. These raw patch values are projected to the model input space via layer normalization followed by a 2-layer MLP, trained jointly from scratch without a pretrained vision encoder or patch-merging module. Structural tokens delimit input sections and patch rows.
 - **Dynamic Resolution**: The image pipeline randomly samples a longest-side cap (between 1,024 and 2,048 pixels) for each example, preserving aspect ratio and downsampling only if necessary. This exposes the model to varied image sizes.
- **2D Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)**: RoPE is extended to two coordinate axes for multimodal inputs, with consecutive frequency pairs alternating between axes. Text uses diagonal coordinates (i, i), while images use (row, col).
- **Modern Bidirectional Backbone**:
 - **Long-Context Attention**: Supports a maximum context length of 16,384 tokens. Most layers use symmetric sliding-window attention (half-window alternates between 256 and 1,024 tokens), while every sixth layer and the final layer use global attention.
 - **Attention Mechanisms**: Uses Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) (260M: 16 query, 4 KV heads; 800M: 28 query, 7 KV heads). Queries and keys are independently root-mean-square normalized (QK normalization).
 - **Block Structure**: Each block employs parameter-free root-mean-square pre-normalization, a squared-ReLU MLP, and a query-dependent elementwise sigmoid gate for attention output. Residual branches are scaled by (2L)^-1/2 (Depth-µP component).
 - **Token-Indexed Value Embeddings**: A shared embedding table, indexed by input token identifiers, is added to value vectors in the first and last global-attention layers.
 - **Residual Mixing**: Each block forms a learned scalar mixture of the current residual stream and the initial normalized multimodal input.
- **Model Sizes**: Two main models are developed: NeoMME-260M (17 layers, 1,024 hidden width) and NeoMME-800M (20 layers, 1,792 hidden width).
- **Weight Initialization**: Attention output and MLP down projections are initialized to zero. Factorized word tables are drawn from a normal distribution with specific standard deviation.

#### 3.2. Pretraining

The pretraining phase trains the shared backbone from random initialization on a diverse dataset mixture.
- **Pretraining Objective**: A discrete masked-diffusion denoiser over text.
 - For text-only segments, a corruption rate 𝜌 is drawn from U(0,1).
 - For multimodal segments, 𝜌 is drawn from U(0.30, 1), with the higher minimum rate intended to reduce reliance on visible textual context and encourage the use of image evidence.
 - Eligible text positions are replaced by a mask token, and loss is computed only at these positions. No masked image modeling or pixel prediction is used.
- **Dataset Mixture**: Comprises approximately 524 billion packed input tokens.
 - **Text-only stream (55%)**: Includes multilingual web text (FineWeb-Edu, FineWeb2-HQ), PDF text (FinePDFs), synthetic educational material (Cosmopedia-v2), encyclopedic text (Wikipedia), mathematics (FineMath), question answering datasets (MMLU, MedMCQA), and code (StarCoderData).
 - **Multimodal stream (45%)**: Combines mixed visual-text collections (FineVision), document content (PDFA + LightOnOCR, DocAtlas), OCR data, and natural images (PixelProse).
- **Data Pipeline and Optimization**: Documents are packed into 16,384-position streams with variable-length attention boundaries. Images are decoded and patchified in background workers. Techniques like FlashAttention-3, Liger Kernel's fused linear cross-entropy, and `torch.compile` are used to maximize GPU utilization.
- **Training Settings**: Performed on AWS p5.48xlarge instances with H100 accelerators. Utilizes bfloat16 precision. Employs NorMuon and MasterAdamW optimizers, a warmup-stable-decay (WSD) learning rate schedule, and gradient clipping.
- **Image Sensitivity Analysis**: Cross-modal input-ablation probes measure how much image presence improves masked-token recovery, indicating the model's reliance on visual cues.

#### 3.3. Retrieval Fine-Tuning (NeoMME-Retriever)

To evaluate downstream capabilities, NeoMME is fine-tuned for document retrieval.
- **Retrieval Architecture**: The final hidden states from the backbone feed into two jointly trained heads:
 - **Late-Interaction Head**: A linear layer projects hidden states to 128 dimensions, followed by L2 normalization. Generates one vector per token/patch for MeanMaxSim scoring.
 - **Dense Head**: Mean pooling averages final hidden states, followed by L2 normalization. Trained using Matryoshka representation learning across multiple dimensions (128, 256, 512, 1024 for 260M; plus 1792 for 800M).
- **Deployment Flexibility**: A single backbone pass produces both dense and late-interaction representations, allowing flexibility for different retrieval strategies (e.g., dense for large-scale ANN, late-interaction for reranking).
- **Dataset Mixture**: Retrieval training combines text-only (1/3 batch share) and multimodal (2/3 batch share) query-document pairs. Text sources include English, multilingual, and code retrieval datasets (e.g., mLateOn). Multimodal sources include visual document datasets (ColPali, VisRAG) and multilingual document images. The mixture is decontaminated against evaluation benchmarks.
- **Query Augmentation**: Qwen3.5-9B is used to generate augmented queries for multimodal examples, adding variety in query types, formats, and languages.
- **Hard Negatives**: Text examples use provided hard negatives. Visual hard negatives are self-mined in a two-stage process using the fine-tuned model itself.
- **Contrastive Objective**: An InfoNCE loss (L_retrieval = L_late + L_dense) is used, with both terms weighted equally. Matryoshka training is applied to the dense head.
- **Optimization**: GradCache is employed to manage memory for large-batch contrastive training. Late-Interaction Kernels (LIK) are used to fuse MaxSim computations, reducing VRAM usage and runtime for late-interaction scoring.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol

- **Benchmarks**: Evaluated on ViDoRe v3, v2, and v1 for visual document retrieval (nDCG@10/5), and BEIR-15 for text retrieval (nDCG@10).
- **Settings**: For ViDoRe, a downscale-only longest-side cap of 2,048 pixels and exact all-pairs MeanMaxSim scoring are used. For BEIR-15, 8,192-token limits, with a compressed FastPLAID index followed by exact rescoring for late-interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across various aspects of multimodal encoding and retrieval:

#### 4.1. Visual Document Retrieval Performance

- **NeoMME-Retriever 260M**: Achieved 0.523 nDCG@10 on the ViDoRe v3 benchmark. This performance surpasses all other evaluated models strictly below 800M parameters. For instance, it notably outperforms ColModernVBERT (250M) and ColSmol-256M. The 260M model's score is comparable to that of ColQwen2.5-v0.2 (3.75B parameters), demonstrating competitive quality at a substantially reduced model size (14.4x smaller).
- **NeoMME-Retriever 800M**: Achieved 0.556 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3. This places it competitively, within 0.9 points of the slightly larger Vultron Flash (~850M parameters) and 3.2 points above the 3.75B-parameter ColQwen2.5.
- **Performance Scaling**: Increasing model size from 260M to 800M consistently improved retrieval scores across all ViDoRe benchmarks (3.3 points on v3, 3.7 points on v2, 1.5 points on v1).

#### 4.2. Text Retrieval Performance

- The same NeoMME encoder backbone demonstrates transferability to text retrieval. On the BEIR-15 benchmark:
 - NeoMME-260M achieved 0.4881 nDCG@10 with late-interaction and 0.3055 with dense retrieval.
 - NeoMME-800M achieved 0.5126 nDCG@10 with late-interaction and 0.3686 with dense retrieval.
- Late-interaction consistently exceeded dense retrieval performance by a notable margin (18.3 points for 260M, 14.4 points for 800M) on BEIR-15.

#### 4.3. Tokenization Efficiency

NeoMME's custom-trained tokenizer exhibited superior compression for text. When evaluated on 14 target languages from FLORES-200 devtest, it emitted significantly fewer tokens per UTF-8 byte compared to ModernBERT (44.4% fewer), LFM2.5-Encoder-230M (39.4% fewer), mmBERT-base (6.3% fewer), and EuroBERT-210m (16.9% fewer).

#### 4.4. Image Sensitivity During Pretraining

Using a cross-modal input-ablation probe, both NeoMME models showed positive mean image gain, meaning visible image patches improved masked-token recovery. This gain increased with higher text corruption rates, reaching 38.4 percentage points for NeoMME-260M and 40.5 percentage points for NeoMME-800M at 90% masking, indicating effective utilization of visual information for textual denoising.

#### 4.5. Resolution Impact

Analysis of image resolution showed a trade-off between quality and indexing cost. Reducing the longest-side cap from 2,048 to 1,536 pixels resulted in a minimal quality loss (at most 1.8% in nDCG@10) while significantly reducing the number of vectors (approximately 57% fewer for a square page). Larger reductions in resolution (e.g., to 768 pixels) led to more substantial quality degradation (11.7% to 36.1%).

#### 4.6. Compression and Storage for Late-Interaction Embeddings

- **Hierarchical Token Pooling**: Reduced document vector storage by approximately 7x (at pool factor 7) while retaining over 99% of ViDoRe v3 nDCG@10, suggesting high effectiveness for visual document retrieval.
- **Asymmetric Quantization**: Quantizing document vectors to int8 reduced storage by 3.9x, and to binary representation by 32x, both while preserving over 99.96% of float32 nDCG@10 when queries were int8.
- **Combined Compression**: A combination of pool factor 8 and asymmetric quantization (int8 queries, binary documents) for NeoMME-260M reduced embedding size from ~1.5 MB to 6 kB per page (a 255x compression) while retaining over 95% of the original retrieval quality.

#### 4.7. Indexing Efficiency

- **Throughput**: On an NVIDIA L40S GPU with a 2048x2048 input size, NeoMME-260M achieved 51.3 pages per second, approximately 1.97 times the throughput of ColModernVBERT (26.0 pages/sec). NeoMME-800M also demonstrated high throughput, encoding pages at 21.2 pages per second at 2048x2048, or 39.2 pages per second at 1536x1536.
- **Query-Encoding Latency**: NeoMME models demonstrated low query-encoding latency, competitive with or superior to other visual retrievers across NVIDIA L40S, CPU, and Apple M5 Pro platforms.

#### 4.8. Interpretability

Similarity maps of late-interaction scores revealed emergent OCR-like behavior in NeoMME-260M. Query tokens activated relevant text regions and structural elements within document images, suggesting the model learns to identify and use textual content visually.

#### 4.9. Other Downstream Tasks (NeoMME-260M)

- **Language Transfer**: On a 17-task suite (GLUE, SuperGLUE, multilingual tasks), NeoMME-260M achieved a mean of 75.3, within 4 points of the specialized LFM2.5-Encoder-230M text model. It also showed evidence of transfer to long-document classification (LEDGAR) and token labeling (CoNLL-2003).
- **Visual Transfer**: While frozen natural-image classification accuracy was low (13.2%), fine-tuning on natural image tasks yielded improvements (e.g., 77.1% on Food101). Performance was stronger for document images, with fine-tuning on RVL-CDIP (6,000 examples) raising accuracy to 81.5%.

#### 4.10. Dual-Head Training Ablation

Jointly training the late-interaction and dense heads for retrieval improved late-interaction performance (1.38 nDCG@10 points gain on ViDoRe v3) compared to training the late-interaction head alone. Dense retrieval performance remained largely consistent or slightly reduced in the joint training setup.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces NeoMME, a multimodal-native and multilingual foundation encoder, which represents a notable contribution to the development of efficient and versatile AI models for diverse data types. The significance and potential impact of this work can be understood through several key aspects:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Multimodal Architecture Design

NeoMME's single-tower, bidirectional Transformer architecture for jointly processing raw image patches and multilingual text offers a streamlined approach compared to traditional multi-tower or VLM-repurposed models. This design choice aims to reduce architectural complexity and computational overhead, making the models more efficient for fine-tuning and inference in non-generative applications. This shift towards a unified backbone suggests a pathway for developing more integrated and efficient multimodal understanding systems.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Efficiency for Retrieval Systems

The study demonstrates significant improvements in efficiency for visual document retrieval. The ability to encode high-resolution pages at substantially higher throughput (e.g., NeoMME-260M achieving nearly 2x the throughput of ColModernVBERT) reduces the computational resources and time required for indexing large document corpora. Furthermore, the demonstrated effectiveness of hierarchical token pooling and asymmetric quantization in compressing multimodal embeddings (up to 255x compression while retaining over 95% quality) addresses a critical challenge in scaling late-interaction retrieval to massive datasets. This makes advanced retrieval methods more practical for real-world deployment.

#### 5.3. Versatility and Deployment Flexibility

The capability of NeoMME-Retriever to generate both dense and late-interaction representations from a single forward pass provides significant deployment flexibility. Dense embeddings can be used for rapid, large-scale approximate nearest-neighbor searches, while more expressive late-interaction representations can be employed for precise reranking, catering to different stages of a retrieval pipeline. This adaptability allows practitioners to balance speed and accuracy based on specific application requirements.

#### 5.4. Multilingual and Multimodal Capabilities from Scratch

Training NeoMME from scratch on a diverse mixture of multilingual text, code, mathematics, natural images, and document images showcases its potential as a robust foundation model. The masked discrete-diffusion text objective, conditioned on visible image patches, facilitates cross-modal learning without relying on pixel-level reconstruction. This foundational pretraining enables the model to effectively understand and relate information across modalities and languages, as evidenced by its performance on both visual document and text retrieval.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contribution and Research Enablement

The release of NeoMME model checkpoints and their integration into the Hugging Face Transformers library under an Apache 2.0 license is a significant contribution to the broader research community. This provides an accessible, high-performing foundation for researchers and developers to build upon, accelerating further innovation in efficient multimodal and multilingual representation learning.

#### 5.6. Potential Impact on Visual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

The advancements in efficient visual document retrieval directly impact Visual RAG systems. By enabling faster indexing and more compact storage of document pages, NeoMME can facilitate quicker retrieval of relevant visual evidence. This, in turn, can enhance the ability of VLMs to answer complex queries by grounding their responses in specific parts of documents, potentially reducing hallucinations and improving factual accuracy in multimodal generative tasks.

#### 5.7. Identified Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also highlight avenues for future research:
- **Pretraining Scale**: NeoMME's pretraining data volume is significantly smaller than some large text-only models, suggesting potential for further gains with extended training or larger datasets.
- **Visual Objective**: The absence of explicit image content prediction or reconstruction objectives might limit transferability to certain natural-image tasks, indicating a need for comparative studies of different visual objectives.
- **Retrieval Supervision Scale**: The amount of retrieval-specific supervision data is less than state-of-the-art text retrieval systems, implying that performance could be further improved with larger, more diverse retrieval training sets.
- **Mixed-Modality Corpora Evaluation**: Future work could evaluate NeoMME on benchmarks involving mixed-modality corpora to assess its ability to retrieve across text chunks and image pages simultaneously.
- **Data and Language Coverage**: The need for larger, multilingual visual document datasets and broader language coverage for visual retrieval is identified.
- **Ablation Studies and Distillation**: Further ablations to isolate the impact of specific architectural, data, and training choices, as well as investigating distillation techniques from the 800M model to the 260M model, could provide deeper insights.
- **Bias and Safety**: Acknowledgment of potential data biases and safety concerns (e.g., generation of harmful text) suggests areas for dedicated evaluation and mitigation strategies.

In conclusion, NeoMME presents an efficient and effective single-tower multimodal-native multilingual encoder that significantly contributes to advancing retrieval systems by improving throughput and storage efficiency, while offering a flexible and openly accessible foundation for future research in multimodal AI.
