# Joint-Aligned Latent Action: Towards Scalable VLA Pretraining in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing the JALA framework, researchers developed a scalable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pretraining method that learns "Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions" from diverse human videos without relying on explicit visual reconstruction. This approach, utilizing a 7.5 million-sample hybrid dataset, significantly improves hand motion generation and robot manipulation performance in both simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face scalability limitations due to the scarcity and limited diversity of robot demonstration data.
- Utilizing abundant human demonstration videos is challenging due to a "quality-variety trade-off" between precisely labeled but limited lab data and diverse but unannotated in-the-wild footage.
- Existing latent action extraction methods relying on visual reconstruction are inefficient and struggle with the subtlety of fine-grained human hand motions.

## Method

- The **JALA** framework learns "==Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions==" by aligning predictive embeddings from the VLA context with latent actions derived from an ==Inverse Dynamics== Model (LAP).
- This approach bypasses pixel-level reconstruction for action representation, making the latent space more behavior-centric and efficient.
- A hybrid dataset, UniHand-Mix, was created, merging 7.5 million samples of precisely labeled lab data and diverse in-the-wild human videos for scalable pretraining.

## Results

- JALA-dino exhibited superior generalization in hand motion generation on unconstrained in-the-wild videos, showing substantially better performance across metrics like MPJPE and MDE than baselines.
- In simulation, JALA achieved an average **96.9%** success rate on LIBERO Two-View and **92.3%** on Single-View, outperforming prior VLA methods and demonstrating efficient transfer with human-only pretraining.
- Real-world robot experiments showed JALA consistently achieved the best completion rates and maintained robustness to visual distribution shifts, exhibiting advanced behaviors like self-correction during manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint alignment of predictive VLA embeddings with inverse dynamics-inferred latent actions is more effective and efficient than pixel-level reconstruction for learning robust action representations.
> - A unified latent action space can be effectively learned from heterogeneous human data, allowing scalability by combining precise lab annotations with diverse in-the-wild videos.
> - Asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) updates between the Latent Action Perceiver and Latent State Perceiver stabilize the alignment process, preventing one module from dominating.
