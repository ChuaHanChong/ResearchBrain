# Joint-Aligned Latent Action: Towards Scalable VLA Pretraining in the Wild

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing the JALA framework, researchers developed a scalable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pretraining method that learns "Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions" from diverse human videos without relying on explicit visual reconstruction. This approach, utilizing a 7.5 million-sample hybrid dataset, significantly improves hand motion generation and robot manipulation performance in both simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face scalability limitations due to the scarcity and limited diversity of robot demonstration data.
- Utilizing abundant human demonstration videos is challenging due to a "quality-variety trade-off" between precisely labeled but limited lab data and diverse but unannotated in-the-wild footage.
- Existing latent action extraction methods relying on visual reconstruction are inefficient and struggle with the subtlety of fine-grained human hand motions.

## Method

- The **JALA** framework learns "==Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions==" by aligning predictive embeddings from the VLA context with latent actions derived from an ==Inverse Dynamics== Model (LAP).
- This approach bypasses pixel-level reconstruction for action representation, making the latent space more behavior-centric and efficient.
- A hybrid dataset, UniHand-Mix, was created, merging 7.5 million samples of precisely labeled lab data and diverse in-the-wild human videos for scalable pretraining.

## Results

- JALA-dino exhibited superior generalization in hand motion generation on unconstrained in-the-wild videos, showing substantially better performance across metrics like MPJPE and MDE than baselines.
- In simulation, JALA achieved an average **96.9%** success rate on LIBERO Two-View and **92.3%** on Single-View, outperforming prior VLA methods and demonstrating efficient transfer with human-only pretraining.
- Real-world robot experiments showed JALA consistently achieved the best completion rates and maintained robustness to visual distribution shifts, exhibiting advanced behaviors like self-correction during manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint alignment of predictive VLA embeddings with inverse dynamics-inferred latent actions is more effective and efficient than pixel-level reconstruction for learning robust action representations.
> - A unified latent action space can be effectively learned from heterogeneous human data, allowing scalability by combining precise lab annotations with diverse in-the-wild videos.
> - Asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) updates between the Latent Action Perceiver and Latent State Perceiver stabilize the alignment process, preventing one module from dominating.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses one of the most significant bottlenecks in the development of generalist robots capable of diverse manipulation tasks: the scarcity and limited diversity of large-scale robot demonstration data. The broader research landscape for robotics has seen considerable excitement around Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs), which aim to adapt the success of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to robotic control. However, unlike the vast datasets available for vision and language tasks, robot datasets are orders of magnitude smaller and less diverse, impeding the scalability of VLA pretraining.

To circumvent this data limitation, a growing body of work has explored leveraging human demonstration videos. These videos are abundant, cost-effective, and offer a richer spectrum of skills than typical robot datasets. However, existing methods utilizing human videos face a crucial "quality-variety trade-off":
- **Lab-collected datasets** provide precise 3D hand tracking and explicit labels but are limited in scale and constrained to controlled, often tabletop, scenarios. They offer strong physical grounding but lack diversity.
- **In-the-wild videos** offer immense diversity and natural behaviors but critically lack reliable action labels or precise hand tracking, making direct application challenging.

Prior approaches to learning from human videos can be broadly categorized:
- **Indirect learning:** Some methods extract generic visual representations or build world models from human videos. While scalable, their connection to concrete physical actions is often weak or implicit.
- **Latent action spaces:** More direct approaches exploit inverse dynamics models (IDMs) to infer latent actions from visual transitions. These are often coupled with forward dynamics models (FDMs) that reconstruct future frames, aiming to ensure latent actions encode action-relevant information. However, the paper argues that this reconstruction-based paradigm is inefficient for fine-grained human manipulation, as the FDM struggles with subtle hand motions and can introduce noise, degrading latent action quality.

JALA distinguishes itself by directly tackling this quality-variety trade-off and improving upon existing latent action extraction methods. It proposes a novel perspective by learning "Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions" that bypass explicit visual dynamic reconstruction. Instead of heavy reliance on pixel-level reconstruction, JALA learns a predictive action embedding that is simultaneously aligned with inverse dynamics and, when available, real action labels. This allows it to construct a unified latent action space that can effectively learn from both precisely annotated lab data and diverse, unlabeled in-the-wild videos. By bridging these complementary data sources, JALA positions itself as a scalable pathway for VLA pretraining, aiming to unlock the full potential of human demonstration data for generalist robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily their inability to scale due to the scarcity and lack of diversity in robot data. While human manipulation videos are recognized as a rich and cost-effective alternative, their effective utilization is hampered by a fundamental "quality-variety trade-off." This trade-off forces researchers to choose between small, precisely-labeled datasets (e.g., lab-collected) and vast, diverse in-the-wild footage with unreliable or absent action labels.

The core question driving this work is: **How can we effectively combine these complementary human data sources to scale VLA pretraining?**

The authors identify specific shortcomings in existing approaches:
- **Inefficiency of reconstruction-based methods:** Current methods, such as those relying on forward dynamics models (FDMs) to reconstruct future frames from latent actions, struggle with the subtlety and variability of fine-grained human hand motions. Learning an accurate FDM is challenging and can introduce noise, rather than stably grounding the latent actions. This makes the latent actions less reliable for capturing true action semantics.
- **Limited scope of precisely-labeled data:** While lab-collected data offers strong physical grounding through accurate 3D hand tracking, its collection is expensive, limiting both its scale and the diversity of tasks and environments it covers.

Inspired by how humans learn manipulation through transferable action patterns rather than memorizing every visual detail, the key objectives of JALA are:
1. **Develop a Novel Latent Action Paradigm:** To introduce a framework (JALA) that learns "Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions" by replacing reconstruction-driven pipelines with joint alignment between a predictive embedding from the VLA context and latent actions derived from an inverse dynamics model (IDM). This aims to shape a latent action space that is predictable from context while remaining informative of inverse dynamics, all without reconstructing pixels.
2. **Enable Learning from Heterogeneous Human Data:** To create a unified latent action space that consistently supports learning from both precisely annotated lab-collected videos and diverse, unlabeled (or sparsely labeled) in-the-wild videos, thereby overcoming the quality-variety trade-off.
3. **Construct a Large-scale Hybrid Dataset:** To introduce UniHand-Mix, a hybrid 7.5 million-sample corpus (>2,000 hours) that blends laboratory and in-the-wild footage, providing the necessary scale and diversity for VLA pretraining.
4. **Demonstrate Improved Generalization and Performance:** To empirically show that JALA generates more realistic hand motions in both controlled and unconstrained scenarios, and significantly improves downstream robot manipulation performance in both simulation and real-world tasks, indicating a scalable pathway for VLA pretraining from human data.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to build more capable and adaptable VLA models that can leverage the wealth of human demonstration data available, ultimately accelerating the development of generalist robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The JALA framework introduces a novel approach to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pretraining, centered on learning "Jointly-Aligned Latent Actions" from heterogeneous human video data. Its core design principle is to replace reconstruction-heavy pipelines with a joint alignment mechanism, fostering a behavior-centric latent space.

**VLA Architecture and Action Representation:**
JALA is built upon a Transformer-based VLA, which processes visual tokens, instructional text tokens, and motion tokens. Human hand movements are modeled using MANO parameters, a standard representation for hand poses. These continuous pose sequences are discretized into fixed-length chunks, then tokenized into 128 motion tokens per chunk (64 for wrist, 64 for fingers) using Group-Residual Vector Quantization (GRVQ). This tokenized representation allows the VLA to learn motion patterns.

**Joint Alignment with Latent Actions:**
The central innovation is the joint alignment of a "predictive embedding" with "latent actions."
- **Predictive Embedding (h):** During pretraining, intermediate hidden states from a preselected attention layer of the VLA, corresponding to masked motion chunks, serve as predictive embeddings. These embeddings implicitly capture chunk-level movement patterns based on the VLA's contextual understanding.
- **Latent Action Perceiver (LAP):** A Perceiver module functions as an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM). For each motion chunk, LAP takes the start and end frames (v_t, v_{t+δ}) as input and outputs a sequence of latent action vectors (z_i,k). These latent actions are designed to capture the chunk-level transition dynamics without requiring explicit pixel reconstruction.
- **Latent State Perceiver (LSP):** To ensure better alignment and alleviate the VLM's predictive burden, an additional Latent State Perceiver (LSP) is introduced. LSP shares the same 2-layer Perceiver architecture (and weights) with LAP. Instead of boundary frames, LSP takes a duplicated initial frame (v_0, v_0) to process initial visual context and map it into the same latent action space as LAP.

**Training Objectives:**
JALA employs a hybrid training objective to learn from both annotated and unannotated human videos:
- **Masked Chunk Prediction (MCP):** For data with hand tracking labels, a masked token modeling objective (L_MCP) is applied. This involves masking motion tokens within a chunk and training the VLA to predict the original tokens, similar to GR-1. This ensures the predictive embeddings (h) encode explicit motion semantics.
- **Latent Action Alignment:** The predictive embeddings (h_i,k) are directly aligned with the latent actions (z_i,k) from LAP using an L1 loss (L_Align). This bridges motion patterns from hidden states with visual dynamics from latent actions, providing a transition-aware, behavior-centric signal.
- **Hybrid Loss:** The overall pretraining loss (L) combines these objectives: `L = 1_labeled · L_MCP + λL_Align`. The `1_labeled` indicator ensures L_MCP is only applied when hand tracking labels are available. For in-the-wild videos without labels, only L_Align is active.

**Decoupled Updates for Stability:**
To stabilize the alignment between LAP and LSP, which process heterogeneous signals, an asymmetric Exponential Moving Average (EMA) update mechanism is used:
- The backbone weights of LAP are updated via EMA from LSP's backbone weights, ensuring consistency with the predictive context.
- The learnable query weights of LSP are updated via EMA from LAP's query weights, allowing LSP to inherit action-grounding capabilities.
This design prevents one side from dominating the training and helps modules converge to a coherent latent space.

**Masking Scheme and Inference:**
A hybrid masking scheme is used for MCP: a single target chunk has a variable masking ratio, while subsequent chunks have a fixed 5% masking. For unlabeled videos, entire motion chunks are masked. During inference for motion generation, JALA uses a multiple-pass decoding strategy, generating ~5% of tokens per pass, which are then ensembled.

**Two-Phase Training Strategy:**
1. **Pre-training on Hybrid Data:** The VLA is pretrained on UniHand-Mix using the combined objective, establishing a unified latent action space.
2. **Post-training with Flow Matching:** For robot manipulation tasks, the pretrained VLA backbone's predictive embeddings (h_i,k) are fed as conditional inputs to a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) policy head. This policy is trained with a flow-matching objective to adapt the learned dynamics knowledge to generate continuous robot-specific actions, efficiently transferring the pretrained latent action space to robot tasks.

**UniHand-Mix Dataset:**
To enable large-scale pretraining, the authors created UniHand-Mix, a hybrid dataset of 7.5 million samples (>2,000 hours) combining:
- **Lab-Annotated Subset (5M+ samples):** Sourced from ~1,000 hours of lab videos (e.g., Arctic, Taco, Dex-YCB). Processed through a pipeline of hand-pose standardization (to MANO), hierarchical task labeling, and instructional sample generation (motion generation, description, continuation).
- **In-the-Wild Subset (2.5M samples):** Curated from ~1,123 hours of Ego4D. Processed via a two-stage filtering and annotation pipeline: visual filtering (WiLoR), hand-centric activity validation, and Gemini-2.5-Flash for instruction generation. Approximately 10% of these clips include pseudo hand-pose annotations using HaWoR.

This comprehensive methodology, from data curation to novel architectural design and a two-phase training strategy, underpins JALA's ability to effectively learn from diverse human manipulation videos.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

JALA demonstrates significant improvements across hand motion generation, simulation-based robot manipulation, and real-world robot tasks, validating its core hypotheses regarding scalable pretraining from human videos.

#### 4.1. Hand Motion Generation

- **Superior Generalization to Wild Data:** On the "Wild split" (unseen, in-the-wild Ego4D videos), JALA variants (JALA-dino, JALA-vjepa) achieve substantially better performance than all baselines across all metrics (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, MWTE, MDE). While all models degrade on the Wild split compared to the Lab split due to increased complexity, JALA exhibits a significantly smaller performance drop. This confirms that joint-aligned latent actions effectively capture transferable manipulation priors beyond curated lab settings.
- **Robustness of Joint Alignment:** Ablation studies highlight the critical role of joint alignment. Simply adding more visual features (Being-H0+dino) or switching prediction paradigms (JALA w/o align) yields only mild improvements. Crucially, JALA w/o latent (which attempts to use predicted action tokens as pseudo-labels for unlabeled data) performs notably worse, especially on the Wild split, indicating that naive scaling with unlabeled video without proper structural supervision can be detrimental. Joint alignment provides a stable and transferable information source.
- **Qualitative and Latent Space Coherence:** t-SNE visualizations (Fig. 4) show that the predictive embeddings (h) and latent actions (z) cluster in closely aligned regions, with Wild samples expanding the Lab manifold in an integrated manner. This confirms a unified and coherent latent action space. Qualitative results (Fig. 5) demonstrate realistic and diverse hand motions in complex wild scenarios (guitar playing, knitting, stirring) and precise control in lab tasks, corroborating quantitative findings.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

JALA consistently outperforms reconstruction-based baselines and ablations across LIBERO, RoboCasa, and GR1 tabletop tasks.
- **LIBERO (Two-View):** JALA-dino achieves an impressive 96.9% average success rate, matching or surpassing stronger baselines (e.g., UniVLA, GR00T N1.5) despite being a lighter model and not pretraining on robotic data. LAPA† (LAPA retrained on JALA's backbone and data) trails JALA significantly (83.5% vs. 96.9%), emphasizing the superior efficacy of JALA's alignment objective over reconstruction. The decoupled EMA update (JALA w/o dec) is shown to be critical, with its removal causing a sharp performance collapse (56.6%).
- **LIBERO (Single-View):** In this more challenging setting, JALA-dino sets a new state-of-the-art with 92.3% average success for models with comparable backbone sizes (<3B parameters). It particularly excels on long-horizon tasks (Long suite, 87.2%). JALA outperforms even UniVLA-human (trained only on human data) and approaches UniVLA-full (which incorporates large-scale robot datasets), demonstrating the efficiency of its human-only pretraining.
- **RoboCasa:** JALA consistently shows stronger performance, especially on synthetic data, where the domain gap is larger, indicating better generalization.
- **GR1 Tabletop:** For dexterous manipulation tasks with an end-effector morphologically similar to a human hand, JALA achieves the highest success rate (26.33%), substantially outperforming GR00T N1.5 (20.41%), LAPA (11.42%), and Being-H0 (12.91%). This highlights JALA's particular strength in transferring human-derived priors to dexterous robot embodiments.
- **Scalability to Unconstrained Videos:** Ablation studies on LIBERO (Fig. 6 left) show a consistent improvement in downstream success rates as the proportion of in-the-wild data in pretraining increases, confirming JALA's strong scaling potential.
- **Predictive Embedding Layer Choice:** When using different hidden layers as inputs to the flow-matching head (Fig. 6 right), the 19th layer (which was used for alignment during pretraining) yields the best transfer performance, suggesting that this layer concentrates generalized action cues.
- **Efficiency:** JALA's pretraining is more efficient than reconstruction-based methods (e.g., LAPA†), requiring less than 80% of the wall-clock time while achieving better performance.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Experiments

JALA is deployed on a Franka Research 3 arm with an Inspire dexterous hand for three multi-step tasks (Put-Three-Obj, Wipe-Board, Water-Plant).
- **Consistent Outperformance:** JALA-dino achieves the best completion rates in every task, consistently outperforming strong baselines (Being-H0, GR00T N1.5) across both seen and unseen settings.
- **Robustness to Visual Shifts:** A crucial finding is JALA's superior robustness to visual distribution shifts. In unseen variants (changed tablecloth, marker color), JALA-dino's performance remains largely stable, whereas baselines degrade sharply. This suggests JALA learns more action-relevant dynamics rather than superficial appearance cues.
- **Learned Behavioral Competence:** Qualitative analysis of successful rollouts (Figs. 9, 10) reveals advanced behaviors, such as self-corrective motions during grasping under visual shifts and iterative refinement during wiping, indicating the internalization of feedback-driven strategies not explicitly supervised.
- **Identified Failure Modes:** Remaining limitations include spatial offsets leading to imperfect contact and unstable grasps, insufficient planar contact during wiping, and incorrect affordance reasoning (e.g., misaligning a spray bottle), highlighting challenges in precise alignment and fine-grained contact stability (Fig. 11).

In summary, JALA successfully addresses the VLA data bottleneck by proposing an effective and efficient pretraining paradigm that leverages the full spectrum of human video data. Its empirical success across diverse benchmarks and real-world tasks underscores its ability to extract transferable, behavior-centric priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The JALA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and robotic learning, with substantial potential impact across several dimensions:

- **Paradigm Shift in Latent Action Learning:** JALA introduces a novel paradigm for extracting latent actions from human videos. By emphasizing "joint alignment" between predictive embeddings and inverse-dynamics-derived latent actions, it effectively bypasses the inefficiencies and noise associated with full visual dynamic reconstruction. This shift from reconstruction-heavy to alignment-centric learning provides a more direct, behavior-centric signal, which is shown to be both more effective and more computationally efficient. This new approach could inspire future VLA architectures to reconsider how they extract and represent action knowledge from complex visual data.

- **Scalable VLA Pretraining from Human Data:** JALA demonstrates a practical and truly scalable pathway for VLA pretraining. Its ability to effectively combine vast, diverse, and often unlabeled in-the-wild human videos with smaller, precisely-annotated lab datasets is a critical breakthrough. The introduction of UniHand-Mix, a large-scale hybrid dataset of over 7.5 million samples, is itself a significant contribution that enables this scale and diversity. This means that the rich and abundant source of human demonstration videos can now be more fully leveraged, accelerating the development of generalist robot foundation models.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness for Robotics:** The consistent outperformance of JALA on challenging robot manipulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa, GR1) in simulation, and especially in real-world settings, highlights its superior generalization capabilities. JALA achieves state-of-the-art or competitive results with comparable or smaller model sizes, often without any pretraining on robotic data. Its demonstrated robustness to visual distribution shifts in real-world tasks (e.g., changing tablecloths or marker colors) is particularly impactful. This indicates that JALA learns more fundamental, action-relevant dynamics rather than superficial appearance cues, which is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unconstrained environments.

- **More Efficient Resource Utilization:** Beyond performance gains, JALA offers improved training efficiency. By focusing on behavior-centric alignment rather than pixel-level reconstruction, it requires less wall-clock time for pretraining while yielding better results. This efficiency is vital for large-scale model development, making VLA research more accessible and sustainable.

- **Bridging Human and Robot Skill Transfer:** JALA provides a stronger and more effective bridge for transferring human manipulation skills to robots. Its success, particularly with dexterous hands (like on GR1 tasks), suggests that the learned human priors are highly relevant to complex robotic embodiments. This could lead to robots that can more intuitively interpret and replicate human actions, reducing the need for extensive robot-specific demonstrations.

- **Future Research Directions:** The findings open significant avenues for future research. The established unified latent action space and the validated approach to handling heterogeneous data lay the groundwork for exploring even larger and more diverse human corpora. It encourages further investigation into optimal representation learning for action, the role of different visual backbones, and methods for further enhancing the fidelity and transferability of human motion priors.

In conclusion, JALA represents a substantial step towards realizing the vision of generalist robots. By providing a scalable, efficient, and robust method for pretraining VLAs from the vast repository of human video data, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing capable and adaptable robotic systems, with profound implications for automation, human-robot interaction, and embodied AI.
