# From Flatland to Space: Teaching Vision-Language Models to Perceive and Reason in 3D

> [!summary] Summary
> A 3D-driven data generation pipeline yields SPAR-7M, a large-scale 2D spatial dataset, and SPAR-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models. Training VLMs on SPAR-7M enables them to develop robust 3D spatial perception and reasoning from 2D inputs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on existing spatial benchmarks and competitive results on 3D tasks, without requiring architectural modifications.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate limited ability to perceive and reason about the 3D physical world from 2D inputs.
- Integrating explicit 3D representations into VLMs faces challenges due to data scarcity, architectural complexity, and integration difficulties.
- Existing 2D-based spatial datasets and benchmarks lack the precision, diversity, or comprehensive scope needed to robustly train and evaluate fundamental 3D spatial understanding in VLMs.

## Method

- Developed a novel ==3D-driven data generation pipeline== that transforms 3D scene data into diverse and precisely annotated 2D spatial question-answering (QA) pairs.
- Constructed **SPAR-7M**, a large-scale dataset containing over ==7 million QA pairs== across ==33 spatial task types==, supporting single-view, multi-view, and video inputs.
- Created **SPAR-Bench**, a rigorously validated benchmark subset of SPAR-7M, for comprehensive evaluation of spatial perception and reasoning in Vision-Language Models.

## Results

- Models pretrained on SPAR-mix achieved **state-of-the-art** performance on 2D spatial benchmarks (e.g., **89.08** on CV-Bench 3D), outperforming baselines while maintaining general 2D capabilities.
- VLMs trained with SPAR-7M showed competitive results on 3D-specific tasks like spatial QA (**90.7 CIDEr** on ScanQA) and 3D grounding (**48.8 Acc@0.25** on ScanRefer), comparable to or surpassing models using explicit 3D inputs.
- Probing experiments revealed a significant reduction in Average Position Error (**1.05m vs. 2.22m**) for BEV coordinate prediction from multi-view RGB, indicating the learning of implicit 3D representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models can develop robust 3D spatial perception and reasoning capabilities from 2D inputs alone, eliminating the need for explicit 3D representations or architectural changes during inference.
> - A scalable 3D-to-2D data generation pipeline can effectively bridge the gap between abundant 2D images and precise 3D spatial ground truth, providing rich supervision for VLMs.
> - Training on sufficiently diverse and accurate 2D spatial data allows VLMs to implicitly learn and utilize 3D structures, evident in improved bird's-eye-view coordinate predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has seen rapid advancements in understanding and generating content across modalities. However, a significant frontier remains the capability of these models to genuinely perceive and reason about the three-dimensional (3D) physical world. This paper directly addresses a critical gap: while VLMs excel at processing 2D images and text, their spatial understanding—the ability to infer depth, distance, object relationships, and camera poses—is often limited.

Existing research largely falls into two categories for enhancing spatial understanding in VLMs:
1. **Direct 3D Representation Integration:** This approach involves incorporating explicit 3D data like point clouds or meshes into the VLM architecture, often through dedicated 3D encoders. While conceptually intuitive, this method faces challenges such as the scarcity and suboptimal quality of large-scale 3D datasets for pretraining, the inherent complexity of designing effective 3D encoders, and difficulties in seamlessly integrating these modifications into existing 2D-centric VLM frameworks. Examples include 3D-LLM, LEO, and Scene-LLM, which leverage 3D information directly.
2. **2D-only Spatial Reasoning:** A smaller but growing line of research attempts to teach VLMs 3D spatial understanding *implicitly* from 2D visual inputs, akin to how humans perceive 3D from 2D retinal images. This approach avoids the aforementioned challenges of 3D data but has been hindered by the lack of sufficiently diverse and accurately annotated 2D datasets specifically designed to train for 3D spatial concepts. Prior 2D spatial datasets often rely on estimated 3D annotations, lack numerical precision, or focus predominantly on high-level semantic reasoning, neglecting fundamental spatial perception.

This work positions itself firmly within the second category, arguing that explicit 3D representations might not be necessary if VLMs can learn robust spatial priors from well-designed 2D data with precise 3D ground truth. It differentiates itself from other 2D-based approaches (e.g., SpatialVLM, SpatialR-GPT) by providing a more structured and accurate assessment of spatial understanding, emphasizing both low-level spatial perception and high-level reasoning, and supporting diverse input formats including single-view, multi-view, and video.

Furthermore, the paper tackles the limitations of existing spatial benchmarks, many of which focus solely on high-level reasoning, overlook fundamental perception tasks, or struggle with multi-view evaluation due to reliance on internet-collected images without precise 3D ground truth. By introducing SPAR-7M and SPAR-Bench, the authors aim to provide a comprehensive, rigorous, and scalable solution that complements both direct 3D input methods and existing 2D spatial understanding efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are multifaceted:

1. **Address the Spatial Perception Deficiencies in VLMs:** The core motivation is the observation that despite significant advancements in general vision-language understanding, current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) still demonstrate limited spatial perception and reasoning capabilities, which is crucial for embodied intelligence and interaction with the physical world.

2. **Challenge the Necessity of Explicit 3D Representations:** The authors question whether integrating explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds) into VLMs is truly necessary. They are motivated by the human ability to infer 3D space from 2D observations and seek to empower VLMs to acquire similar spatial understanding directly from large-scale, spatially relevant 2D data without modifying their core architecture for 3D inputs.

3. **Develop a Novel 2D Spatial Data Generation Pipeline:** A key objective is to create a robust and scalable pipeline capable of generating diverse and precisely annotated 2D spatial data from existing 3D scene data. This pipeline is crucial for overcoming the scarcity and quality issues associated with 3D data and for providing rich supervision for spatial tasks.

4. **Construct a Large-Scale, Diverse Spatial Dataset (SPAR-7M):** The research aims to leverage the new pipeline to build SPAR-7M, a comprehensive dataset that covers a broad spectrum of spatial tasks, ranging from basic perception (depth, distance, relative positions) to complex reasoning (spatial imagination, camera pose inference). It emphasizes both numerical and semantic representations and includes single-view, multi-view, and video inputs, structured across three difficulty levels.

5. **Introduce a Comprehensive Spatial Benchmark (SPAR-Bench):** To enable rigorous and holistic evaluation, another objective is to create SPAR-Bench. This benchmark is designed to overcome the limitations of existing benchmarks by covering both fundamental spatial perception and higher-order reasoning, supporting both single-view and multi-view inputs, and ensuring high data quality through manual validation.

6. **Enhance VLMs' Spatial Understanding and Generalization:** Ultimately, the goal is to demonstrate that training VLMs on SPAR-7M can significantly improve their spatial understanding capabilities, leading to state-of-the-art performance on existing 2D spatial benchmarks and competitive results even on 3D task-specific datasets, without requiring explicit 3D inputs during inference. This also includes probing whether models trained on SPAR-7M develop implicit 3D representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around a novel 3D-driven data generation pipeline and its application in creating the SPAR-7M dataset and SPAR-Bench benchmark.

#### 3.1. 3D-Driven Data Generation Pipeline

The pipeline systematically converts 3D scene data into diverse 2D spatial question-answering (QA) pairs. It consists of three main stages:

1. **Filtering and Subsampling:** To reduce data redundancy, especially in video sequences, images with minimal camera movement are filtered out. Camera poses (translation and rotation) are compared against thresholds to retain only images that offer sufficiently distinct viewpoints. This is akin to keyframing, ensuring viewpoint diversity without excessive overlap.
2. **Extracting 3D Information:** Global 3D information from each scene (e.g., 3D mesh, object bounding boxes, camera parameters) is extracted and organized into a unified data structure. This structure comprises "image records" (metadata like camera parameters, visible objects) and "object records" (ID, category, associated image indices). This unified representation facilitates cross-view retrieval and accurate 3D ground-truth annotations. Object visibility and valid projection constraints (e.g., fraction of visible vertices, minimum projected area, depth range) are enforced to ensure high-quality 2D-to-3D mappings.
3. **Multi-tasks Generation:** This is the core annotation step, where diverse QA pairs are automatically generated from the structured 3D information.
 - **Task Configuration:** Each task is defined by type (e.g., depth prediction, spatial relations), input image count (single-view, multi-view, video), question format (fill, select, sentence), and relation type (object-to-object (OO) or object-to-camera (OC)).
 - **Annotation Logic:** For each task, specific procedures are followed to derive precise 3D ground truth. For instance:
 - **Object Spatial Relation:** 3D object centers are transformed into a common camera view, and relationships (above-below, left-right, near-far, front-behind) are determined based on spatial coordinates and distances.
 - **Depth Prediction/Distance Inference:** Absolute or relative depth/distance values are directly calculated from 3D object centers in the camera coordinate system.
 - **Spatial Volume Inference:** Object dimensions (length, width, height) are derived from 3D bounding boxes.
 - **Spatial Imagination:** Involves simulating camera movements and orientations to generate questions about object relationships *after* a hypothetical viewpoint change.
 - **Position Matching/View Change Inference/Camera Pose:** These tasks leverage camera poses and 3D object locations to generate questions about object correspondence across views, camera displacement, and camera projection coordinates/depth.
 - **Navigation/Room Size/Object Count/Object Frame Location:** These tasks also use scene metadata for instruction generation, area calculation, counting, and temporal object tracking.
 - **Template Filling:** Human-curated templates (expanded and refined with GPT) are used, with placeholders filled by computed spatial information and object labels, ensuring diversity and accuracy in question phrasing.

#### 3.2. Spatial Dataset Construction (SPAR-7M)

- **Sources:** SPAR-7M is built from 4,000+ indoor 3D scenes from ScanNet, ScanNet++, and Structured3D.
- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises 33 task types, categorized into low-level perception, medium-level structured reasoning, and high-level imagination, totaling over 7 million QA pairs.
- **Input Formats:** Supports single-view, multi-view (3–5 images per QA), and video inputs to assess different facets of spatial understanding.
- **Granularity:** Emphasizes both numerical (e.g., depth, distance) and semantic (e.g., "left of") representations.

#### 3.3. Spatial Benchmark Construction (SPAR-Bench)

- **Subset of SPAR-7M:** SPAR-Bench is a comprehensive benchmark derived from 20 tasks sampled from the SPAR-7M validation set.
- **Quality Control:** It includes 7,207 carefully curated questions, manually validated to ensure clarity, accuracy, and factual consistency, achieving a 90.1% acceptance rate.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Tasks are formulated as numerical prediction (evaluated by Mean Relative Absolute Error - MRA) or classification (evaluated by accuracy). Video-based tasks are excluded to focus purely on spatial reasoning.

#### 3.4. 3D Grounding with VLMs

A multi-view-based 3D grounding approach is proposed to enable VLMs to localize objects in 3D. This reformulates 3D grounding as a text generation problem:
- **Frame Selection and Inference:** Given a video input, the VLM selects the most suitable single frame and infers 2D UV coordinates, depth, and size for the target object.
- **3D Box Recovery:** Using the camera pose of the selected frame, the 3D bounding box is recovered.
- **Optional Refinement:** An optional refinement step matches the predicted 3D bounding box with existing proposal bounding boxes to obtain a more precise final result.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Pretraining Strategy:** A "SPAR-mix" pretraining dataset is used, combining 40% uniformly sampled data from SPAR-7M with 60% general 2D vision-language data (EMOVA configuration) to maintain generalization.
- **Base Model:** InternVL2.5-8B is used as the base VLM.
- **Training Resources:** Pretraining involved 2 million samples over 6,100 NPU-hours on 64 NPUs.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of the SPAR-7M data ratio during pretraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that training VLMs on the SPAR-7M dataset significantly enhances their spatial understanding capabilities, yielding state-of-the-art results on several benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Existing 2D Spatial Benchmarks

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The model pretrained on SPAR-mix (InternVL2.5-8B + SPAR-mix) achieved state-of-the-art results on CV-Bench 3D and VSI-Bench, outperforming strong baselines like GPT-4o, LLaVA-OV, and InternVL2-8B. For instance, on CV-Bench 3D, SPAR-mix achieved 89.08, compared to 78.50 for the Base VLM, and 64.83 for Base VLM + EMOVA-2M.
- **Generalization Preservation:** While specifically enhancing spatial reasoning, the model maintained competitive performance on general 2D benchmarks (e.g., MMBench, TextVQA), with only minor drops (within 1%), indicating that spatial supervision does not compromise general VLM capabilities.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** Pretraining with SPAR-7M improved spatial understanding across diverse, out-of-domain datasets used in CV-Bench 3D (e.g., Hypersim, SUNRGBD, nuScenes). Notably, an 18-point gain was observed on the outdoor nuScenes dataset, highlighting strong generalization of learned geometric cues beyond indoor scenes.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on SPAR-Bench

- **Human vs. Model Performance:** Human performance (67.27 average accuracy/MRA) consistently surpassed all models on SPAR-Bench across all difficulty levels. The strongest model (InternVL2.5-8B + SPAR-mix) achieved an average of 63.25, demonstrating that human spatial reasoning remains a high bar.
- **Impact of SPAR-mix:** Fine-tuning InternVL2.5-8B on SPAR-mix significantly boosted performance across low- (65.53), medium- (63.01), and high-level (61.29) tasks. This indicates that SPAR-7M introduces valuable inductive bias for spatial reasoning.
- **Uneven Gains:** Gains were particularly significant in tasks requiring multi-view reasoning (e.g., View Change Inference: 6.32% to 37.14%) and depth prediction. Interestingly, the fine-tuned model surpassed human performance in depth prediction and distance estimation (MRA-based metrics), which favor numerically optimized models over human intuition. However, tasks involving viewpoint changes and spatial imagination remain highly challenging, indicating limitations in multi-view fusion and compositional reasoning.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on 3D-Specific Tasks

- **Spatial QA (ScanQA, SQA3D):** Despite not incorporating explicit 3D information, the SPAR-mix model achieved competitive scores on 3D spatial QA benchmarks like ScanQA and SQA3D, performing on par with or even surpassing some models that *do* use 3D inputs (e.g., it achieved 90.7 CIDEr on ScanQA, comparable to LLaVA-3D's 91.7 and better than 3D-LLM's 69.4).
- **3D Grounding (ScanRefer):** The model showed strong capabilities in 3D grounding, locating objects in scenes from textual descriptions. With an optional refinement step, SPAR-mix achieved 48.8 Acc@0.25 and 43.1 Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, performing competitively with models explicitly designed for 3D grounding, showcasing its ability to perform complex generative tasks without 3D input.

#### 4.4. Probing Implicit 3D Representations

- **BEV Coordinate Prediction:** To probe for learned implicit 3D structures, the model was tasked with predicting bird's-eye-view (BEV) 2D coordinates of objects from multi-view RGB images (without 3D inputs at inference).
- **Reduced Localization Error:** The SPAR-mix model significantly reduced the Average Position Error (APE) (mean APE 1.05m vs. 2.22m for base VLM). This improvement was more pronounced at medium-to-far ranges where depth and occlusion are challenging, suggesting that SPAR-mix helps the model aggregate cues across views to infer scene-consistent geometry.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

- **SPAR Ratio Impact:** Reducing the SPAR-7M data proportion from 60% to 40% in the SPAR-mix dataset, while slightly improving 2D general benchmarks, led to performance drops on 3D-specific benchmarks (CV-Bench 3D) and across all SPAR-Bench difficulty levels, particularly on middle and high-level tasks. This confirms that a sufficient proportion of SPAR-7M data is crucial for reinforcing generalizable spatial priors and robust geometric alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models and embodied AI, particularly in bridging the gap between 2D visual understanding and 3D spatial reasoning. Its potential impacts are substantial:

1. **Enabling 3D Spatial Understanding in Existing 2D VLMs:** The most profound impact is demonstrating that VLMs, traditionally designed for 2D image processing, can acquire robust 3D spatial perception and reasoning capabilities *without requiring architectural modifications or explicit 3D inputs during inference*. This opens the door for a vast ecosystem of existing VLMs to be enhanced with 3D understanding, accelerating their application in real-world scenarios.

2. **Addressing 3D Data Scarcity and Complexity:** By deriving rich 2D spatial supervision from 3D ground truth, the proposed pipeline offers a scalable solution to the persistent challenges of 3D data scarcity, uneven quality, and the complexity of 3D model design. This approach makes advanced spatial training more accessible and efficient.

3. **Comprehensive Dataset and Benchmark for Spatial AI:** SPAR-7M and SPAR-Bench provide invaluable resources for the research community. SPAR-7M's diversity in tasks, input formats, and difficulty levels, coupled with precise 3D ground truth, will become a standard for training spatially-aware VLMs. SPAR-Bench offers a rigorous and holistic evaluation framework, pushing the boundaries of what VLMs can achieve in spatial AI and fostering reproducible research.

4. **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** Spatial understanding is a cornerstone of embodied intelligence, enabling agents to navigate, interact with objects, and comprehend environments. By improving VLMs' ability to reason about 3D space, this work directly contributes to developing more intelligent, adaptable, and safer robotic systems and embodied agents that can operate effectively in complex physical environments.

5. **Insights into Implicit 3D Representations:** The probing experiments, which show that the model learns implicit 3D structures and can predict BEV coordinates from 2D inputs, offer valuable cognitive insights. This suggests that VLMs can build an internal, scene-consistent layout of the environment, even without explicit 3D supervision, furthering our understanding of how AI models perceive and represent the world.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors:**
The authors transparently acknowledge that the current work primarily focuses on static, QA-based spatial reasoning from images, not continuous or interactive reasoning crucial for embodied agents. The monocular nature of inputs means metric 3D tasks might suffer from scale ambiguity, often requiring additional cues or normalized targets. Furthermore, the data generation pipeline's reliance on 3D ground truth (depth, intrinsics, poses) might limit its applicability in domains where such information is unavailable.

Despite these limitations, this research represents a significant leap towards developing more spatially intelligent VLMs, offering a practical and effective pathway to empower current models with a deeper understanding of the 3D world.
