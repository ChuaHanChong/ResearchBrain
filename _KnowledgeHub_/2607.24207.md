# FloAff-Kitchen: Bridging Navigation and Manipulation via Canonical and Progressive Floor Affordance Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> FloAff-Kitchen introduces a framework that integrates Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR) and Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL) to improve robot base placement for mobile manipulation. The approach achieved significantly higher success rates (up to 85% overall) across diverse manipulation tasks compared to baselines, while also demonstrating robustness to scene variations and camera viewpoints.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based Floor Affordance (FloAff) prediction methods generate ambiguous scene representations due to irrelevant spatial context and inconsistent object orientations.
- Learning across diverse manipulation skills suffers from entanglement of shared and task-specific knowledge, limiting transferability of learned affordance priors.
- Current benchmarks for FloAff prediction lack comprehensive evaluation for viewpoint robustness and generalization across varying scene layouts.

## Method

- A ==Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR)== is proposed to transform egocentric observations into consistent, orientation-invariant representations by cropping local geometry and aligning target orientations.
- A ==Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL)== strategy learns transferable FloAff priors from a foundational task (pick-and-place) and progressively adapts them to complex, heterogeneous skills, using ==feature distillation== to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- The **FloAff-Kitchen** benchmark was established using RoboCasa, offering cross-scene and multi-view observations to systematically evaluate viewpoint robustness and generalization for tasks like pick-and-place, opening doors, and closing drawers.

## Results

- The proposed method reduced RMSE from **0.147** to **0.073** and improved PCC from **0.680** to **0.796** on the MoMa-Kitchen benchmark for FloAff prediction, outperforming existing baselines.
- For multi-task mobile manipulation, the framework achieved an overall success rate of **0.85** on the FloAff-Kitchen Styles benchmark (vs. NavAff's **0.37**) and **0.79** on the Layouts benchmark (vs. NavAff's **0.27**), demonstrating strong generalization and robustness to scene variations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both target-centered cropping and orientation alignment within CFAR, as well as feature distillation in PFAL, are critical components for the method's performance and transferability across tasks and environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Canonicalizing interaction geometry by eliminating irrelevant spatial context and achieving orientation consistency dramatically reduces representation ambiguity, leading to more accurate FloAff predictions.
> - Progressive learning with feature distillation effectively disentangles shared spatial reasoning principles from task-specific interaction patterns, improving the transferability of learned affordance priors across heterogeneous manipulation skills.
> - A comprehensive benchmark incorporating scene variations and multi-view observations is crucial for evaluating the robustness and generalization capabilities of FloAff prediction systems in realistic mobile manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile Manipulation (MoMa) requires robots to determine optimal base placements not merely for navigation feasibility, but specifically to maximize the success of subsequent manipulation tasks. Floor Affordance (FloAff) prediction, which involves estimating ground regions suitable for robot base placement based on egocentric observations, is a foundational capability for achieving effective MoMa.

Prior research in navigation has advanced robot movement through techniques such as motion planning, semantic mapping, and learning-based policies, including the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) for object-goal navigation. However, these methods primarily prioritize navigation feasibility, potentially leading to suboptimal robot positions for manipulation. Concurrently, advancements in learning-based robotic manipulation, utilizing large-scale demonstrations, reinforcement learning, and foundation models, have improved policy generalization. Nevertheless, the success of manipulation policies remains contingent on appropriate robot base placement, as factors like viewpoint, visibility, reachability, and surrounding geometry directly influence task execution.

Efforts to bridge navigation and manipulation have explored explicit reasoning about robot base placement. Early approaches relied on geometric planning, such as inverse reachability maps. More recent methods have incorporated perception and learning, with examples including Navi2Gaze for VLM-based navigation goal prediction, MoMa-Pos for optimizing articulated-object interactions, and MoMa-Kitchen, which established an initial benchmark for FloAff prediction. N2M further investigated policy-aware base placement learned from manipulation rollouts.

Despite these developments, existing learning-based FloAff prediction methods face limitations. These include the generation of ambiguous scene representations due to the inclusion of irrelevant spatial context and the entanglement of shared and task-specific knowledge across diverse manipulation skills, which constrains the transferability of learned affordance priors. Additionally, current benchmarks predominantly focus on front-view observations, leaving the impact of viewpoint variation on FloAff prediction largely unexplored. This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing novel representation learning and progressive learning strategies, alongside a more comprehensive evaluation benchmark.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified framework for FloAff prediction from egocentric multimodal perception that robustly bridges navigation and manipulation tasks. This framework aims to enable robots to identify optimal base placements that maximize manipulation success.

The motivation for this work stems from two core challenges identified in existing FloAff prediction methods:

1. **Representation Ambiguity**: FloAff is primarily determined by the local interaction geometry surrounding a manipulation target. Existing methods, however, often process entire scene point clouds, which introduces irrelevant spatial context. Furthermore, identical interaction geometries, when observed under different object orientations, can result in inconsistent point cloud representations. This inconsistency creates "representation ambiguity," compelling models to simultaneously discern affordance-relevant geometry and learn invariance to arbitrary orientations. The authors hypothesize that eliminating this ambiguity through a canonical representation would improve prediction accuracy and generalization.

2. **Limited Transferability Across Heterogeneous Manipulation Skills**: Robots are required to perform a variety of manipulation tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, opening doors, closing drawers). While these tasks possess distinct interaction dynamics, they often share fundamental spatial requirements for robot base placement, such as reachability, collision avoidance, visibility, and overall manipulability. Current multi-task learning approaches tend to optimize all skills concurrently, which can intermingle shared spatial reasoning principles with task-specific interaction patterns. This entanglement, according to the authors, hinders the transferability of learned FloAff priors to novel or diverse manipulation skills.

To address these challenges and facilitate rigorous evaluation, a third motivation was to establish a more comprehensive benchmark. Existing datasets primarily focus on single viewpoint evaluations and limited scene variations. Therefore, a key objective was to create a benchmark that supports systematic evaluation of viewpoint robustness and generalization across diverse scene layouts and furniture styles.

In response to these motivations, the research specifically aims to:
- Propose a **Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR)** to transform egocentric observations into a consistent, orientation-invariant representation that preserves only affordance-relevant local geometry.
- Develop a **Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL)** strategy to learn transferable FloAff priors from a foundational manipulation task and progressively adapt them to more complex and heterogeneous downstream skills, thereby disentangling shared and task-specific knowledge.
- Establish the **FloAff-Kitchen benchmark**, incorporating cross-scene and multi-view observations to enable systematic evaluation of viewpoint robustness and scene generalization capabilities of FloAff prediction systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for FloAff prediction integrates Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR) and Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL) into a unified system.

**Problem Formulation**:
The goal is to predict a Floor Affordance (FloAff) map `A ∈^(H×W)` from an egocentric multimodal observation `X = {I, D, S, o}`, where `I` and `D` are RGB and depth observations, `S` is the robot proprioceptive state, and `o` is the target object. This observation is first converted into an egocentric point cloud `P = Ψ(I, D, o)`. The framework learns a mapping `fθ : {P, S} → A`, optimized by minimizing a FloAff prediction loss `Laff`.

**Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR)**:
CFAR aims to mitigate representation ambiguity by transforming raw observations into a canonical representation `T(P, S)` before feeding it to the FloAff predictor. This transformation is designed with two properties:
1. **Affordance Relevance**: It retains only geometry crucial for FloAff prediction, discarding irrelevant spatial context.
2. **Orientation Consistency**: It maps scenes with equivalent affordance patterns into consistent representations, irrespective of the target's observed orientation.

The canonical transformation `T(·)` is implemented through two steps:
- **Eliminating Irrelevant Spatial Context**: A target-centered local representation `P_local` is constructed by retaining only points within a predefined sphere of radius `r` centered at the manipulation target's position `t`. This operation focuses the representation on the immediate interaction geometry.
- **Eliminating Orientation Ambiguity**: The `P_local` point cloud is rotated to align the target's front-facing direction with a canonical axis (e.g., negative y-axis). This rotation, `R(π - θo)`, uses the target yaw angle `θo`. During inference, `θo` is estimated by a lightweight orientation predictor called **AxisNet**, which takes the cropped point cloud and target-object features as input, predicting an `ℓ2`-normalized orientation vector. AxisNet is a PointNet-style network trained to minimize MSE loss against ground-truth orientations.

**Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL)**:
PFAL addresses the challenge of learning transferable FloAff priors across heterogeneous manipulation skills by leveraging a curriculum-based approach. It assumes that FloAff representations can be decomposed into shared priors (`z_s`) and task-specific constraints (`z_t`).

- **Shared FloAff Priors Learning**: Pick-and-place (PnPC2C) is chosen as the foundational task because it primarily involves generic spatial reasoning. The FloAff predictor is initially optimized on the PnPC2C dataset (`D_pnp`) using a combined loss `Laff = L_mse + βL_sdl`. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss quantifies prediction accuracy, while the Soft Dice Loss (SDL) promotes spatial continuity of the predicted feasible regions. The parameters learned from this stage (`θ_pnp`) serve as an initialization for subsequent stages.
- **Progressive FloAff Priors Learning**: Starting from `θ_pnp`, the model is progressively adapted to more specialized tasks, specifically joint optimization for door-related tasks (OpenSingleDoor, CloseDoubleDoor) and CloseDrawer. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of the shared priors during adaptation, a **feature distillation objective (L_shared)** is introduced. This loss encourages the current stage's feature extractor (`g_θk(P̂)`) to maintain similarity with the previous stage's feature extractor (`g_θk-1(P̂)`). The overall objective for this stage is `L_pro = Laff + λL_shared`. This progressive adaptation, combined with distillation, aims to learn task-specific patterns while preserving transferable spatial reasoning capabilities.

**FloAff-Kitchen Benchmark**:
To enable systematic evaluation of viewpoint robustness and scene generalization, the authors established the FloAff-Kitchen benchmark based on RoboCasa. It focuses on kitchen environments due to their diverse manipulation requirements.

- **Tasks**: Four representative MoMa tasks: PnPCounterToCab (PnPC2C), OpenSingleDoor (OpenSD), CloseDrawer (CloseD), and CloseDoubleDoor (CloseDD).
- **Scene Generation**: Configurable kitchen environments with variations in layouts and furniture styles. Two benchmark settings:
 - **Styles Benchmark**: Varies furniture styles, textures, and object appearances while preserving kitchen layouts.
 - **Layouts Benchmark**: Varies kitchen layouts.
- **Rollout-based FloAff Annotation**: Candidate robot poses are generated on the floor and filtered for physical feasibility (collision-free, reachability). A pre-trained task-specific manipulation policy then evaluates these feasible poses, assigning a binary label (success/failure). These labels are projected onto a grid to create a Ground-Truth (GT) FloAff map, with Gaussian smoothing applied for spatial continuity.
- **Multi-View Observation Collection**: Observations are collected from both front-view and side-view camera positions. Front-view provides relatively complete visibility, while side-view introduces greater occlusion and viewpoint ambiguity. Both viewpoints share the same rollout-generated FloAff labels, enabling explicit evaluation of viewpoint robustness.
- **Benchmark Statistics**: The benchmark comprises 24,782 RGB-D observations with FloAff annotations, divided into Styles (13,610 samples) and Layouts (11,172 samples). Train/test splits are performed at the scene level, with five training scenes and one unseen testing scene per task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments on three benchmark settings: the public MoMa-Kitchen benchmark and their proposed FloAff-Kitchen Styles and Layouts benchmarks.

**Comparative Studies on FloAff Prediction (Table 1)**:
The proposed method consistently demonstrated superior performance across all evaluation metrics (RMSE, logMSE, PCC, SIM) compared to baselines like PointNet++, VoteNet, H3DNet, and NavAff.
- On the **MoMa-Kitchen benchmark**, the method substantially reduced RMSE from 0.147 (NavAff) to 0.073 and improved PCC from 0.680 to 0.796, indicating enhanced accuracy.
- On the **FloAff-Kitchen (Styles) benchmark**, the method achieved an RMSE of 0.029 and PCC of 0.863, outperforming NavAff (RMSE 0.064, PCC 0.395) despite significant appearance variations. This suggests the representation is less sensitive to texture and style changes.
- On the more challenging **FloAff-Kitchen (Layouts) benchmark**, which involves structural variations, the method yielded an RMSE of 0.035 and PCC of 0.738, significantly better than NavAff (RMSE 0.080, PCC 0.238). This indicates robust FloAff representation under both appearance and structural variations.

**Comparative Studies on FloAff-grounded Multi-task MoMa (Table 2)**:
The predicted FloAff maps were used to determine robot base placements for downstream MoMa tasks, evaluated by Success Rate (SR).
- The proposed method consistently achieved the highest success rates across all benchmark settings and viewpoints, approaching the Oracle policy's performance.
- On the **Styles benchmark**, it achieved an overall SR of 0.85, compared to NavAff's 0.37.
- On the **Layouts benchmark**, it achieved an overall SR of 0.79, compared to NavAff's 0.27, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen kitchen layouts.
- The results also indicated robustness to viewpoint variations: on Styles, both front-view and side-view achieved an 85% SR. On Layouts, the gap between front-view (81%) and side-view (77%) remained relatively small, unlike baselines which showed larger performance drops.

**Comparative Studies on FloAff-grounded Single-task MoMa (Table 3)**:
To isolate the contribution of CFAR, single-task training experiments were conducted.
- CFAR consistently achieved the best performance across all manipulation tasks (PnPC2C, OpenSD, CloseD, CloseDD) under both Styles and Layouts benchmarks.
- Improvements were particularly notable for articulated-object manipulation tasks (OpenSD, CloseDD), which are sensitive to target orientation. CFAR's canonical representation reduced orientation-induced variations, leading to more reliable FloAff predictions. For CloseD, the method achieved near-saturated performance.
- These results suggest that canonical interaction geometry learned by CFAR generalizes well to unseen interaction configurations and structural variations.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 4 and 5)**:
Ablation studies validated the contribution of each proposed component. Removing any module consistently degraded performance.
- **On MoMa-Kitchen (Table 4)**:
 - Removing the Soft Dice Loss (SDL) resulted in the largest performance drop, indicating the importance of region-level supervision for capturing complete feasible regions.
 - Removing Feature Distillation (FD) also degraded metrics, supporting the benefit of preserving shared FloAff priors.
 - Removing point cloud cropping showed a slight degradation, suggesting its benefit is less pronounced in constrained scenes.
- **On FloAff-Kitchen (Layouts) (Table 5)**:
 - Removing either target-centered cropping (`w/o Crop`) or orientation alignment (`w/o Align`) significantly reduced PCC and SIM, underscoring the importance of both CFAR components for consistent geometry under layout variations.
 - Removing FD still degraded performance, confirming the benefit of transferable FloAff priors even under significant structural scene variations.
- **Parametric studies (Supplementary)** on the crop radius indicated optimal radii differed between Styles (1.30 m) and Layouts (1.20 m), highlighting the need to balance relevant geometry and irrelevant context depending on scene variations.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that CFAR effectively learns compact canonical representations by reducing irrelevant spatial variations, while PFAL improves transferability across heterogeneous manipulation skills, leading to robust FloAff prediction and superior downstream MoMa performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses fundamental limitations in current mobile manipulation systems, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robot Autonomy**: By providing a more accurate and robust method for Floor Affordance (FloAff) prediction, the framework enables robots to determine optimal base placements more effectively. This directly translates to higher success rates in downstream manipulation tasks, particularly in complex and varied environments, thereby advancing the autonomy of mobile manipulation robots.

2. **Robustness to Environmental and Viewpoint Variations**: The introduction of the Canonical Floor Affordance Representation (CFAR) significantly improves the model's ability to handle irrelevant spatial context and arbitrary object orientations. This leads to representations that are more consistent and less susceptible to variations in furniture styles, scene layouts, and observation viewpoints (front-view vs. side-view), which are common challenges in real-world deployments. This robustness is crucial for generalist robots operating in unstructured settings.

3. **Improved Transferability of Skills**: The Progressive Floor Affordance Learning (PFAL) strategy offers a method to disentangle shared and task-specific affordance knowledge. By learning transferable priors from a foundation task and progressively adapting them, the framework enhances the generalization capability across a range of heterogeneous manipulation skills. This approach could reduce the data and training effort required to teach new manipulation tasks to robots.

4. **Novel Research Benchmark**: The establishment of the cross-scene, multi-view FloAff-Kitchen benchmark is a notable contribution to the robotics research community. This benchmark, which includes diverse manipulation skills, scene layouts, furniture styles, and viewpoints, provides a standardized and challenging testbed for future research. Its public release (data, protocols, code) will facilitate systematic evaluation and accelerate advancements in viewpoint robustness and scene generalization for mobile manipulation.

5. **Foundational Methods for Future Robotics**: The principles of canonical representation learning and progressive skill transfer could have broader applications beyond FloAff prediction. They may inform perception and learning strategies in other robotics domains where consistent understanding of interaction geometry and efficient skill generalization are required.

In summary, this work provides both methodological advancements and a crucial evaluation resource that collectively contribute to more intelligent, robust, and adaptable mobile manipulation systems, paving the way for their deployment in more diverse and challenging real-world scenarios.
