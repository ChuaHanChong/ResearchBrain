# GigaWorld-Policy-0.5: A Faster and Stronger WAM Empowered by AutoResearch

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at GigaAI and Tsinghua University developed GigaWorld-Policy-0.5, an action-centered World Action Model designed for faster and more robust robot control. The system achieves a 0.85 success rate on fruit-picking and 0.80 on long-horizon tasks, while demonstrating a low inference latency of 85 ms on an RTX 4090, enabled by a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture and optimized deployment.

## Problem

- Existing World Action Models (WAMs) suffer from high computational overhead due to explicit future video generation or iterative denoising during inference.
- The substantial inference latency in WAMs hinders their deployment in real-time, closed-loop robot control systems.
- Balancing the benefits of rich visual dynamics supervision during training with the need for efficient, low-latency action generation at inference time remains a challenge.

## Method

- **GigaWorld-Policy-0.5** utilizes an action-centered ==World Action Model== formulation with a ==causal masking== strategy, allowing for action-only decoding during inference to achieve low latency.
- A ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture separates visual dynamics modeling into a 'Visual Expert' and action generation into an 'Action Expert' for specialized, efficient computation.
- The approach integrates a two-stage training pipeline (robot-data pretraining with mixed ==Action-Conditioned World Modeling==, followed by target-robot post-training) and an agent-based **AutoResearch** pipeline for systematic hyperparameter optimization and an optimized C++ inference runtime.

## Results

- GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 achieved an average success rate of **0.85** on text-following fruit-picking tasks and **0.89** on object-placement tasks, outperforming several baselines.
- On long-horizon tasks, the model demonstrated an average success rate of **0.80**, representing a **33%** relative improvement over the strongest baseline.
- Inference latency was reduced to **85 ms** on an RTX 4090 with a C++ runtime, making it **53%** faster than FastWAM, showcasing enhanced real-time deployment capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual dynamics for dense supervision during training from action-only generation during inference enables efficient real-time robot control without sacrificing physically grounded action learning.
> - A specialized Mixture-of-Transformers architecture can reduce active computation for action-only inference by selectively activating relevant experts, improving efficiency despite potentially increasing total parameters.
> - Explicitly modeling how actions induce visual state changes through mixed Action-Conditioned World Modeling pretraining strengthens action-visual coupling, leading to more robust and transferable action representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of robot policy learning, specifically within the domain of World Action Models (WAMs). World models, which learn predictive representations of physical environments from visual data, have demonstrated progress in capturing spatiotemporal priors about object motion and interaction dynamics. This predictive capability is considered beneficial for robotic control, as policies can anticipate environmental changes resulting from their actions.

Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have begun to integrate world models to augment policy learning. While earlier VLA methods primarily relied on sparse action supervision from demonstrations, incorporating world model predictions provides richer temporal context and grounds policy learning in future scene evolution.

World Action Models (WAMs) represent a further development, where robot actions and future observations are jointly modeled within a unified framework. This approach allows action representations to be learned in conjunction with their visual consequences, enabling future visual dynamics to serve as dense supervision for generating physically grounded actions. Examples of this line of work include Motus, UWM, and VideoVLA.

A recurring challenge in deploying existing WAMs is the computational overhead associated with explicit future video generation, iterative denoising, or predictive rollout during inference. This design limits real-time closed-loop control due to the high cost of processing video tokens compared to action tokens. Prior work, such as GigaWorld-Policy and Fast-WAM, has explored strategies to decouple future prediction between training and inference, utilizing visual dynamics for supervision during training but avoiding explicit video generation at inference time.

GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 builds upon this direction, specifically inheriting the action-centered formulation of GigaWorld-Policy. It aims to refine the balance between leveraging the benefits of action-dynamics coupling during training and achieving efficient, low-latency inference. The introduction of specialized architectures and systematic optimization pipelines in GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 represents an effort to advance WAMs towards practical, real-time deployment on edge computing platforms, addressing both performance and efficiency aspects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an enhanced action-centered World Action Model, GigaWorld-Policy-0.5, that improves inference efficiency for robot control while retaining the benefits of dense visual dynamics supervision during training. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Addressing Inference Latency in WAMs:** Existing WAMs commonly generate future videos explicitly at inference time. This process is computationally intensive, leading to substantial overhead that hinders real-time, closed-loop robot deployment. A core motivation is to overcome this limitation by enabling low-latency action generation without sacrificing the physically grounded action learning provided by future visual dynamics. GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 aims to achieve this by employing an action-only decoding pathway during inference, building on the action-centered causal token structure.

2. **Preserving Training Benefits of Future Visual Dynamics:** While optimizing for inference speed, it is crucial to maintain the strong supervision and physical grounding that future visual dynamics provide during training. The joint modeling of actions and future visual observations is considered beneficial for learning robust and transferable action representations. GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 is motivated to preserve this training advantage by utilizing future visual dynamics as dense supervision during the training phase.

3. **Strengthening Action-Dynamics Coupling and Transferability:** The research seeks to improve the relationship between visual dynamics and robot actions. By enhancing this coupling, the model can learn action representations that are more consistent with expected scene evolution, thereby improving their transferability for downstream policy learning. This is motivated by the observation that predictive world knowledge is an important component for developing more capable robot policies.

4. **Improving Inference Efficiency through Architectural Specialization:** To achieve lower inference latency, there is a motivation to design a model architecture that can selectively activate necessary computational pathways. The Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture is introduced to separate visual dynamics modeling and action generation into specialized components (experts). This is intended to reduce active computation during action-only inference by avoiding unnecessary visual dynamics calculations.

5. **Systematizing and Accelerating Hyperparameter Tuning:** The process of identifying optimal training configurations for complex models like WAMs, which jointly optimize multiple objectives, can be time-consuming and labor-intensive. A motivation is to streamline this experimental search process. The integration of an agent-based AutoResearch pipeline aims to systematically search training configurations, reduce manual intervention, and efficiently identify effective hyperparameter settings.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for WAMs that are both performant in learning complex robotic tasks and efficient enough for real-world, real-time deployment, aiming to mitigate the computational bottlenecks that have limited their practical application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 builds upon the action-centered World Action Model (WAM) formulation of GigaWorld-Policy, integrating several methodological advancements in its architecture, training pipeline, and optimization strategies.

#### 3.1. Core Formulation and Causal Structure

The model is designed to jointly predict an action chunk $\hat{a}_{t:t+p-1}$ and future visual observations $\hat{o}_{comp}_{t+\Delta:t+K\Delta}$ given current multi-view observations, proprioceptive state, and a language instruction. The composite observation is formed by concatenating left, front, and right camera views. A key aspect is the causal masking strategy: future visual tokens are permitted to attend to action tokens, allowing them to be conditioned on robot actions, while action tokens are explicitly prevented from attending to future visual tokens. This design enables future visual prediction to be an optional component during inference, facilitating action-only decoding for low-latency control. The model is optimized using the flow matching framework, where modality-specific flow timesteps are sampled for action and future visual tokens to regress the model-predicted velocity field to the ground-truth flow velocity.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture: Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)

GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 introduces a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that diverges from a fully shared Transformer backbone. This architecture separates the two primary modeling components:
- **Visual Expert:** Specializes in processing current and future visual tokens, focusing on scene evolution in the latent visual space.
- **Action Expert:** Concentrates on action-token denoising and action-chunk prediction.

Each expert possesses its own cross-attention and feed-forward network (FFN) modules for modality-specific language conditioning and nonlinear transformations. The experts are interconnected through multi-modal self-attention, which follows the action-centered causal mask to regulate information flow. The visual expert is initialized with pretrained visual weights from GigaWorld-1, a large-scale world model, to provide a general world-model prior. The action expert's parameters are initialized from the corresponding visual-expert weights, with dimension adjustments where necessary.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

The training process involves two stages, preceded by the initialization of the visual expert:
1. **Robot-Data Pretraining:** GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 is pretrained on approximately 2,000 hours of filtered open-source and internally collected real-robot data. This stage adapts the initial world-model prior to robot-centric scenarios. A mixed training strategy is employed, combining Action-Conditioned World Modeling (AC-WM) with standard WAM training. In AC-WM, future visual evolution is predicted conditioned on robot actions, aiming to strengthen the model’s understanding of how actions induce scene changes.
2. **Target-Robot Post-Training:** The model undergoes further training on target real-robot trajectories, which include aligned observations, language instructions, robot states, and action sequences. During this stage, the model jointly optimizes action prediction and future visual dynamics modeling under the action-centered causal structure. The action objective supports instruction-conditioned action generation for closed-loop control, while the future-visual objective provides auxiliary supervision to ensure action representations are consistent with plausible scene evolution. For deployment, an action-only inference path is used.

#### 3.4. Inference Acceleration

To achieve low-latency real-world deployment on edge computing platforms, the inference stack is optimized through:
- **KV Caching:** Key-value states of attention layers are cached after encoding the initial visual and language context, avoiding redundant computations in subsequent autoregressive action generation steps.
- **Graph Compilation:** `torch.compile` is applied to optimize the inference graph, which reduces Python-level dispatch overhead and enables backend optimizations such as operator fusion.
- **Lightweight C++ Runtime:** The optimized policy is deployed within a C++ runtime environment that integrates image preprocessing, tensor construction, model execution, KV-cache management, and action post-processing into a unified native pipeline. This reduces Python dependencies and runtime overhead.

#### 3.5. AutoResearch Pipeline

An agent-based AutoResearch pipeline is implemented to systematically explore and optimize training hyperparameters, such as learning rate and warmup schedule. This automated process involves:
- Generating candidate configurations.
- Launching short pilot runs (e.g., 1K steps).
- Monitoring validation metrics (e.g., action MSE).
- Comparing candidate performances.
- Selecting promising configurations for longer, extended training runs.
This iterative approach is designed to efficiently derive a reliable training recipe, reducing manual intervention and time expenditure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 demonstrates improvements in both real-world robot control performance and inference efficiency compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Real-World Robot Control Performance

Experiments were conducted on an AgileX PiPER 6-DoF robotic arm across text-following and long-horizon manipulation tasks. Success Rate (SR) was the primary metric, with graded scoring for text-following tasks.

- **Text-Following (Fruit-Picking Task):** GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 achieved an average success rate of 0.85. This represented an improvement over baselines, including $\pi_{0.5}$ (0.76), Motus (0.80), FastWAM (0.78), and GigaWorld-Policy (0.80). The model consistently obtained the highest success rate across all six fruit categories, with notable gains on objects like lemon and avocado, indicating improved language grounding for visually distinct objects.
- **Text-Following (Object-Placement Task):** On this more compositional task, GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 achieved an average success rate of 0.89, surpassing Motus (0.83), the strongest baseline in this category. It demonstrated the highest success rate for every instruction, with particular improvements observed for "bowl-to-basket" and "fork-to-basket" instructions. This suggests enhanced grounding of both object identities and spatial goal specifications.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** For tasks requiring multiple temporally dependent steps (e.g., "Food Heating," "Tableware Arrangement"), GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 achieved an average success rate of 0.80. This constituted an absolute improvement of 0.20 over the strongest baseline, which achieved 0.60, representing a 33% relative improvement. This result indicates the method's effectiveness in maintaining coherent action sequences and handling intermediate state transitions for extended tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Mixed AC-WM and WAM Pretraining:** An ablation comparing standard WAM pretraining with a mixed AC-WM and WAM pretraining strategy showed that the mixed approach yielded a model that converged faster and achieved higher success rates during post-training. The model with mixed pretraining reached a success rate of 0.85, demonstrating that explicitly modeling how actions drive visual state transitions leads to more transferable action representations and more sample-efficient downstream policy learning.

- **Effect of MoT Architecture:** The Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture was evaluated for its impact on inference efficiency.
 - With KV caching and `torch.compile` enabled, GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 achieved an inference latency of 189 ms on an A100 GPU, a 17.5% speedup compared to FastWAM (229 ms). It also outperformed $\pi_{0.5}$ (225 ms).
 - On a local RTX 4090, latency was 110 ms, matching $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - Further optimization with a C++ deployment reduced latency to 85 ms on the RTX 4090, making it 23% faster than $\pi_{0.5}$ and 53% faster than FastWAM. This indicates that despite potentially increasing total parameter count, the specialized experts of the MoT architecture, combined with inference optimizations, enable reduced computation for action-only inference.

- **AutoResearch-Driven Hyperparameter Study:** The AutoResearch pipeline systematically explored hyperparameters for the "pick the fruit" task.
 - A 1K-step pilot sweep over learning rates identified $6 \times 10^{-5}$ as achieving the lowest evaluation action MSE (0.409764), balancing training action loss and visual loss.
 - Subsequent batch-size sweeps did not yield further improvements.
 - Extended training with the selected hyperparameters ($6 \times 10^{-5}$ learning rate, batch size 16) showed that the model achieved its best validation performance at 30K training steps. This process demonstrated the pipeline's ability to efficiently identify effective training configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 represents a step in advancing World Action Models (WAMs) for robot control, particularly by addressing the trade-off between leveraging rich visual dynamics for training and achieving efficient real-time inference.

The work's primary significance lies in demonstrating a practical pathway toward more deployable WAMs. By preserving the use of future visual dynamics as dense supervision during training while enabling an action-only decoding pathway at inference, GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 maintains the benefits of physically grounded action learning without incurring the substantial computational overhead previously associated with explicit future video generation. The achievement of an 85 ms inference latency on an RTX 4090, particularly with C++ deployment, indicates that real-time closed-loop robot control on edge devices can be supported by this class of models. This could broaden the applicability of WAMs in real-world robotic systems where low latency is a critical requirement.

The introduction of the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture is notable. By separating visual dynamics modeling and action generation into specialized experts, the model facilitates more efficient computation during action-only inference, even with an increased total parameter count. This architectural design provides a modular approach to model complexity and computational resource allocation, potentially influencing future designs of multi-modal robot control models.

Furthermore, the mixed Action-Conditioned World Modeling (AC-WM) and WAM pretraining strategy is shown to strengthen the coupling between actions and visual changes. This contributes to more transferable action representations, which could lead to more data-efficient policy learning on downstream tasks. Such transferability is valuable in robotics, where collecting large, diverse datasets can be challenging.

The implementation of an agent-based AutoResearch pipeline offers a systematic and efficient method for hyperparameter optimization. This approach reduces the manual effort and time required for experimental configuration, which is a common bottleneck in complex deep learning research. The successful application of AutoResearch in this work suggests its potential for broader adoption in identifying robust training recipes for various neural network architectures and tasks, thereby improving research efficiency and reproducibility.

Overall, GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 contributes to the development of robot control systems that are both highly capable in understanding complex environments and sufficiently efficient for practical deployment, bridging a gap between theoretical modeling power and real-world operational constraints.
