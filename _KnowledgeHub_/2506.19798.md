# CoCo4D: Comprehensive and Complex 4D Scene Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoCo4D introduces a framework for generating comprehensive and complex 4D scenes featuring dynamic foregrounds and evolving backgrounds. This method reduces generation time to approximately 1 hour, outperforming prior score distillation methods by up to 23x while achieving superior motion quality and scene complexity as validated by VBench metrics and user studies.

## Problem

- Existing 4D scene generation methods primarily focus on isolated object-level animation or dynamic scenes with restricted novel views.
- Current scene-level 4D generation approaches are computationally expensive, often requiring 4 to 24 hours per scene, and struggle with rich ambient dynamics or complex background motion.
- Unified approaches often fail to effectively model the disparate motion patterns between rapidly moving foreground objects and gradually changing backgrounds.

## Method

- A framework that decouples the synthesis of dynamic foreground objects and evolving backgrounds, applying specialized pipelines for their distinct motion patterns.
- A "progressive outpaint" strategy that iteratively expands and animates background scenes by combining 2D video inpainting with depth-guided ==3D Gaussian== projection and refinement.
- Combines a static 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== representation with a learned deformation network for foreground motion, supervised by multi-view diffusion (**Zero123**) and rigidity losses.

## Results

- CoCo4D achieves 4D scene generation in approximately 1 hour, a substantial reduction from the 4-24 hours required by prior score distillation methods.
- The method demonstrates superior motion quality (Motion Smoothness: 92.37%, Dynamic Degree: 70.95%) and aesthetic quality (63.86%) compared to state-of-the-art baselines in VBench evaluations.
- CoCo4D successfully generates complex scenes featuring coherent foreground motions (e.g., walking anime girl) and dynamic, wide-ranging backgrounds (e.g., bursting fireworks, flowing river).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foreground objects exhibit rapid, articulated motion, while backgrounds undergo gradual changes, necessitating specialized pipelines for each within a 4D scene.
> - An iterative "inpaint-project-optimize" scheme, termed progressive outpaint, can effectively expand and animate 4D background scenes from limited initial information.
> - Leveraging 3D Gaussian Splatting combined with deformation networks and multi-view diffusion guidance enables efficient and high-quality 4D content generation for both foregrounds and backgrounds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 4D scene generation, encompassing both spatial (3D) and temporal (1D) dynamics, is a rapidly evolving frontier in computer vision and graphics. CoCo4D positions itself at the forefront of this landscape by addressing significant limitations of existing approaches, particularly concerning the generation of "comprehensive" (wide view range) and "complex" (dynamic foreground and background with sophisticated motion) scenes.

Historically, 4D content generation has largely focused on either:
- **Object-level generation:** Methods like Animate124, Consistent4D, and SV4D primarily aim to generate isolated dynamic 3D objects, often in a simplified "video-to-4D" or "image-to-4D" setting. While successful for single entities, they fall short of synthesizing entire, interactive environments. CoCo4D acknowledges this progress and leverages insights from 4D object generation for its foreground module.
- **Limited dynamic scene synthesis:** Early attempts at scene-level 4D generation, such as those based on score distillation (e.g., Singer et al., 4D-fy, Dream-in-4D), optimize a 4D representation using priors from 2D or video diffusion models. While these methods can achieve multi-view consistency, they are notoriously computationally expensive (often taking 4-24 hours per scene) and typically struggle with rich ambient dynamics or complex background motion, often simplifying backgrounds or assuming static environments.
- **Learning-based methods:** More recent approaches (e.g., Free4D, PaintScene4D, VividDream) employ multi-view video diffusion models or novel view synthesis techniques to generate full scenes. These are generally more efficient than optimization-based methods and can enable richer background motion. However, they often require massive datasets for training and can struggle with multi-view consistency across wide camera ranges, or encounter issues with stereo initialization and distorted geometries when extrapolating.

CoCo4D directly tackles these challenges by proposing a novel, decoupled approach. Its key insight—that foreground objects exhibit rapid, articulated motion while backgrounds undergo gradual changes—allows for specialized pipelines for each component. This contrasts with monolithic approaches that struggle to capture both types of dynamics simultaneously and efficiently. By integrating a "progressive outpaint" strategy, CoCo4D also extends the view range and introduces dynamic backgrounds, a common limitation in previous works. Furthermore, by achieving generation times under one hour, CoCo4D significantly improves the practicality compared to slower optimization-based methods, making it competitive with faster learning-based methods while delivering superior quality and complexity. This work represents a crucial step towards generating fully immersive, consistent, and high-fidelity dynamic 4D environments, bridging the gap between isolated object animation and complete scene synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of CoCo4D stem from the inherent limitations and unmet needs within the existing landscape of 4D scene generation. The authors clearly articulate these as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Existing 4D Synthesis:** The core motivation is to move beyond the constraints of current methods, which predominantly focus on object-level generation or dynamic scene synthesis with restricted novel views. This narrow scope limits the ability to create truly immersive and multi-view consistent dynamic 4D scenes.
2. **Generate Comprehensive and Complex 4D Scenes:** The method aims to produce 4D scenes that are both "comprehensive" (meaning they support a wide view range, allowing for 360-degree observation) and "complex" (meaning they feature both highly dynamic, articulated foreground objects and evolving, ambient backgrounds with sophisticated motion).
3. **Address Disparate Motion Patterns:** A critical observation driving CoCo4D is that foreground objects (e.g., a walking anime girl) typically exhibit rapid, articulated motion, whereas background elements (e.g., swaying trees, flowing water) change more gradually and subtly. Current unified approaches struggle to model these distinct dynamics effectively. CoCo4D is motivated to design specialized pipelines for each, acknowledging their unique characteristics.
4. **Achieve Multi-View Consistency and Immersiveness:** A recurring challenge in generative models for 3D/4D content is maintaining consistency across different viewpoints and over time. CoCo4D aims to deliver high-quality and consistent multi-view rendering at each timestamp, crucial for creating truly immersive experiences.
5. **Improve Computational Efficiency:** Existing optimization-based methods are prohibitively slow, often requiring 4 to 24 hours to generate a single scene. A key objective for CoCo4D is to significantly reduce this time, making the generation process more practical and accessible for users, targeting a generation time under one hour.
6. **Provide Flexible Input Conditions:** The framework is designed to generate 4D scenes from text prompts, with the added flexibility of including an optional reference image, catering to diverse creative inputs.
7. **Enable Seamless Integration of Foreground and Background:** Beyond generating separate components, CoCo4D is motivated to ensure a realistic and coherent blending of the dynamic foreground object within the evolving background, achieved through optimized parametric trajectories and depth knowledge.

In essence, CoCo4D is motivated by the desire to push the boundaries of 4D content creation, making it more detailed, dynamic, consistent, and computationally feasible for a wider range of applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoCo4D introduces a novel framework for comprehensive and complex 4D scene generation by strategically decoupling the synthesis of dynamic foreground objects and evolving backgrounds. The methodology is built upon a crucial observation regarding the distinct motion patterns of scene components. The overall approach can be broken down into four main stages: Reference Video Generation, Dynamic Foreground Generation, Dynamic Background Synthesis, and Foreground and Background Composition.

#### 3.1. Reference Video Generation

The process begins by generating an initial "reference video" based on user input.
- If both a text prompt and an image are provided, an Image-to-Video (I2V) model (e.g., DynamiCrafter) is used.
- If only a text prompt is given, a Text-to-Video (T2V) model (e.g., CogVideoX) is employed, or an image is first generated from text and then converted to video via an I2V model.
This reference video ($V_{ref}$) serves as the primary guidance for both foreground and background synthesis. Following its generation, the reference video is disentangled into foreground frames ($V_{fg}$), background frames ($V_{bg}$), and foreground masks ($M_{fg}$) using a segmentation model (SAM2).

#### 3.2. Dynamic Foreground Generation

This module is dedicated to generating an articulated 3D object sequence that matches the appearance and motion from the foreground reference video ($V_{fg}$).
- **Static 3D Foreground Object Reconstruction:** A static 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation ($G_f$) of the foreground object is initialized. It is then optimized using a combination of L2 loss against the first foreground frame ($I_1^{fg}$) from the "seen view" and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss. The SDS loss leverages a multi-view diffusion model (Zero123) to hallucinate unseen views, ensuring plausible geometry for the static 3D object.
- **Foreground Object Motion Optimization:** To animate the static 3D object, a deformation network ($D_f$) is learned. This network takes the static Gaussians ($G_f$) and a timestamp as input to produce deformed Gaussians ($G_t^f$). The parameters of $G_f$ are frozen during this stage. The motion is supervised by an L2 reconstruction loss against the foreground reference video ($V_{fg}$) from the seen view. Additionally, SDS loss with Zero123 is applied to unseen views at each timestamp (using $I_t^{fg}$ as condition) to maintain geometric plausibility during motion. A rigidity loss ($L_{rigid}$) is also incorporated to prevent excessive deformation between neighboring Gaussians, ensuring realistic motion.
- **Foreground Object Trajectory Learning:** To accurately position the animated foreground object within the scene, a parametric trajectory (shift $\tau$ and scale $s$) is learned for each timestamp. This trajectory is refined by optimizing it with the RGB video alignment loss (Eq. 3), ensuring precise alignment with the reference video.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Background Synthesis

Given that background scenes typically exhibit gradual, slow-moving changes, a distinct strategy is employed for their generation.
- **3D Background Scene Initialization:** The background in the first frame ($I_1^{bg}$) of the reference video is projected into 3D space using an estimated depth map ($D_1^{bg}$) obtained from a depth estimation model. This creates an initial point cloud from which background Gaussians ($G_b$) are initialized. This initial 3DGS representation is then refined using L2 loss against $I_1^{bg}$ and SDS loss (with Stable Diffusion v2.1) to enhance quality and ensure a plausible reference view.
- **Progressive Outpaint:** This is a novel, iterative "inpaint-project-optimize" scheme designed to simultaneously expand and animate the background scene.
 1. **Initial Animation:** The optimized static 3D background scene ($G_b$) is equipped with a deformation network ($D_b$) and initially optimized with L1 reconstruction loss and total-variation regularization, similar to 4D Gaussian Splatting.
 2. **Outpainting Loop:** In each iteration, the method defines a set of "outpaint" camera poses ($C_{out}$). For each camera pose, it renders a video sequence ($V_c$) and a mask sequence ($M_c$) from the current 4D background representation.
 3. **Image-Guided Video Inpainting:** To fill the empty regions in the rendered videos, a pseudo-video generation strategy is used. First, the initial frame of the rendered video ($x_c^1$) is inpainted using a 2D image diffusion model (SDXL) guided by the text prompt. This inpainted region is then copied and pasted into subsequent frames of $V_c$ to create a pseudo-video. Finally, this pseudo-video is refined by a video inpainting model (COCOCO) with a small amount of added noise, ensuring temporal consistency.
 4. **Depth-Guided 3D Projection:** To integrate the inpainted 2D content into the 3D scene, the inpainted pixels from the first frame of the refined video are projected back into 3D space. Crucially, a depth inpainting model is used to fill in missing depth information in the rendered depth map, allowing for seamless integration of new Gaussians into $G_b$.
 5. **Refinement and Iteration:** The fused static background Gaussians ($G_b$) are then refined using L1 and SDS losses (with the inpainted image as reference). Subsequently, the deformation network ($D_b$) is optimized alongside the Gaussians using the reference video and the newly curated novel view videos. This process is repeated incrementally (e.g., 2-3 rounds) to expand the scene gradually, improving quality and consistency.

#### 3.4. Foreground and Background Composition

The final step involves seamlessly combining the optimized dynamic foreground object and background scene using depth information. The depth map from the first frame of the original reference video ($D_{ref}$) is used as a guide. A depth shift ($d_f$) and scaling factor ($\epsilon$) are calculated based on the relative depth ranges of foreground and background within $D_{ref}$ and the rendered foreground/background depth maps. These parameters are used to adjust the foreground Gaussians' screen space position and scale, ensuring accurate placement and proportional sizing within the combined scene. Finally, the two 4D Gaussian representations are composed during rendering to form the comprehensive and complex target 4D scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

CoCo4D demonstrates significant advancements in 4D scene generation, achieving superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of quality, complexity, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

The evaluation involved comparisons against score distillation-based methods (4D-fy, Dream-in-4D) and learning-based methods (Free4D). The quantitative assessment utilized VBench metrics (Motion Smoothness, Dynamic Degree, Aesthetic Quality) and CLIP Score for text-video alignment.

- **Efficiency:** CoCo4D significantly reduces the time cost for 4D scene generation. While score distillation methods take 13.5-24 hours per scene, and some learning-based methods take 3-3.5 hours, CoCo4D achieves generation in approximately **1 hour**, matching the efficiency of Free4D. This drastic reduction in time is a critical step towards practical applications.
- **Overall Quality Metrics (Table 1):**
 - **CLIP Score:** CoCo4D achieves a competitive 23.47%, slightly outperforming Dream-in-4D (23.58%) and significantly better than 4D-fy (22.32%) and Free4D (22.06%). This indicates strong text-to-video alignment.
 - **Motion Smoothness:** CoCo4D scored 92.37%, demonstrating superior motion quality compared to all baselines (Dream-in-4D: 89.84%, Free4D: 82.18%, 4D-fy: 79.62%). This highlights its capability to generate realistic and fluid motions.
 - **Dynamic Degree:** CoCo4D achieved the highest Dynamic Degree at 70.95%, indicating its success in generating scenes with rich and complex motion, especially for articulated movements, far surpassing Dream-in-4D (56.32%), Free4D (66.39%), and 4D-fy (43.29%).
 - **Aesthetic Quality:** CoCo4D also showed the best Aesthetic Quality at 63.86%, indicating visually appealing results, closely followed by Free4D (60.97%).
- **User Study (Table 2):** A user study involving 30 participants rated motion quality, multi-view consistency, and visual quality.
 - CoCo4D received the highest scores for **Motion Quality** (7.04) and **Visual Quality** (7.84), confirming its qualitative superiority in these aspects.
 - For **Multi-view Consistency**, CoCo4D scored 7.26, competitive with Dream-in-4D (7.45) but slightly behind 4D-fy (7.82). This suggests that while score-distillation methods excel in consistency, CoCo4D offers a strong balance across all metrics.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visual comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrate CoCo4D's ability to generate complex and coherent motions across wide camera ranges, which existing methods struggle with.
- Score distillation methods (Dream-in-4D) show good multi-view consistency but fail to produce sophisticated motion.
- Free4D occasionally fails due to issues with multi-view video generation or stereo initialization, producing inconsistent or distorted results.
- CoCo4D successfully generates scenes with an "anime girl walking in a cherry blossom scene" or a "teddy bear dancing" with intricate foreground motion while maintaining dynamic backgrounds (e.g., bursting fireworks).
- Figure 5 illustrates the successful disentanglement of foreground and background, showing that both components are individually dynamic and consistent across views and time.
- Figure 8 showcases the "4DGS outpaint with customized text prompt" feature, demonstrating the ability to guide the background expansion with specific textual descriptions (e.g., "river flowing nearby"), yielding an "ideal 4D scene."

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies validate the effectiveness of key methodological components:
- **Pseudo Video Generation (Figure 6):** The image-inpainting guided strategy for video inpainting significantly improves consistency in the expanded background regions compared to naive video inpainting, which often produces inconsistent results.
- **Depth Inpainting (Figure 7):** Incorporating a depth inpainting model for 3D Gaussian projection ensures seamless point cloud fusion and prevents artifacts, a notable improvement over methods without this feature.
- **Training Losses:** The SDS loss for background synthesis and the re-use of the deformation network during progressive outpaint contribute to enhanced quality, novel view synthesis capabilities, and reduced training time for the dynamic background.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoCo4D represents a significant step forward in the domain of generative AI for 3D and 4D content, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in creating dynamic, immersive digital environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Comprehensive and Complex 4D Scene Generation:** By successfully synthesizing dynamic foregrounds with articulated motion and evolving, wide-ranging backgrounds, CoCo4D addresses a critical unmet need. Previous methods often excelled at one aspect (e.g., object animation) but faltered at scene-level complexity or vice-versa. CoCo4D's disentangled approach offers a robust solution for holistic 4D scene creation.

2. **Bridging the Efficiency-Quality Gap:** The most impactful contribution from a practical standpoint is CoCo4D's ability to generate high-quality, complex 4D scenes in under an hour. This drastically reduced computational cost compared to optimization-based methods (which took 4-24 hours) makes 4D scene generation more accessible for researchers, artists, and developers, fostering faster iteration and experimentation. This efficiency positions CoCo4D as a truly practical tool.

3. **Novel Disentangled Architecture:** The core insight of separating foreground and background synthesis based on their distinct motion characteristics is a novel and effective architectural design. This allows for specialized treatment of each component, leading to higher fidelity and consistency, and potentially paving the way for more modular and scalable 4D content creation pipelines.

4. **Advancements in 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS):** By building upon the efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting and extending it to 4D, CoCo4D further solidifies 4DGS as a viable and powerful representation for dynamic scenes. The "progressive outpaint" technique for background expansion is particularly innovative, demonstrating how to incrementally build and animate large, consistent 4D environments from limited initial information.

5. **Enhanced Realism and Consistency:** The rigorous attention to detail in maintaining multi-view consistency, motion smoothness, and visual quality (validated by quantitative metrics and user studies) means that the generated scenes are more plausible and immersive. This is crucial for applications requiring high visual fidelity.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** The ability to rapidly generate comprehensive and dynamic 4D scenes can revolutionize content creation for VR and AR experiences, enabling highly interactive and realistic virtual worlds that respond to user input and time.
- **Filmmaking and Animation:** CoCo4D can accelerate pre-visualization, digital set extension, and special effects production in film and animation, offering artists powerful tools to generate complex dynamic environments with unprecedented speed.
- **Gaming:** Game developers could leverage CoCo4D for procedural generation of dynamic game environments, adding depth and realism to virtual worlds without extensive manual design.
- **Simulation and Robotics:** Realistic 4D scene generation can provide richer training data and environments for robotics, autonomous driving simulations, and other AI research fields that benefit from dynamic, high-fidelity virtual scenarios.
- **Democratization of 4D Content Creation:** By making the process faster and more effective, CoCo4D lowers the barrier to entry for creators, enabling a wider range of individuals to experiment with and produce complex 4D content.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by authors):** While highly impactful, the authors candidly acknowledge a few limitations: occasional undesired foreground results due to upstream model (Zero123) errors, artifacts in the final scene from inaccurate depth maps during background optimization, and minor misalignments during scene composition. Addressing these areas would further enhance the robustness and quality of CoCo4D.

In conclusion, CoCo4D represents a significant leap towards a future where generating rich, dynamic, and immersive digital worlds from simple inputs becomes a practical reality, with broad implications across entertainment, technology, and scientific research.
