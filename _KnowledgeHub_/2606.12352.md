# CHORUS: Decentralized Multi-Embodiment Collaboration with One VLA Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University introduce CHORUS, a framework enabling decentralized collaboration among diverse robot teams using a single Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy. This approach improves reactive coordination by leveraging VLA priors, scales to three heterogeneous robots, and outperforms centralized VLA policies and from-scratch imitation learning methods.

## Problem

- Centralized multi-robot control approaches face scalability limitations and require constant inter-robot communication at inference, leading to latency and robustness issues.
- Existing decentralized methods often rely on strong inference-time assumptions like shared proprioceptive state or third-person camera views, complicating real-world deployment.
- Leveraging powerful single-robot Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model priors for truly decentralized, multi-embodiment, and mobile multi-robot collaboration remained an open challenge.

## Method

- **CHORUS** adapts a pretrained VLA backbone (specifically, π_{0.5}) into a single, ==shared policy== that controls multiple robots.
- Each robot runs an independent copy of the policy, conditioned only on its local observations and a unique robot-identifying prompt, eliminating inference-time communication.
- Training data consists of individual robot trajectories extracted from synchronously logged multi-robot demonstrations, which are then pooled to fine-tune the shared VLA policy using a flow-matching loss.

## Results

- CHORUS achieved a 64 percentage point higher mean task success rate compared to a decentralized diffusion policy baseline, demonstrating the value of VLA priors.
- The shared policy in CHORUS improved handover recovery rate by **40%** (17/20 vs. 9/20) over separate policies when a teammate was perturbed, indicating enhanced reactive coordination.
- The framework successfully scaled to a three-robot team for a complex basket transport task, achieving a **90%** success rate without any architectural modifications, showcasing its scalability and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained visuomotor priors from VLA models are highly effective for multi-robot collaboration, significantly improving performance over learning from scratch, even for out-of-distribution tasks.
> - Training a single, weight-shared policy across diverse robots fosters a shared representation that enhances reactivity and adaptation to dynamic teammate behavior.
> - Decentralized execution with local observations maintains input distributions closer to the VLA backbone's pretraining data, leading to superior performance compared to centralized approaches requiring concatenated, multi-robot observations.
