# Point-RFT: Improving Multimodal Reasoning with Visually Grounded Reinforcement Finetuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Point-RFT is a framework for multimodal reasoning that explicitly grounds Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps to specific visual coordinates, enhancing reasoning accuracy and interpretability. This approach achieves 90.04% accuracy on the ChartQA dataset, outperforming previous methods and generalizing well to out-of-domain visual reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-language models often suffer from visual hallucinations and inadequate integration of visual information when generating reasoning chains in multimodal tasks.
- Traditional text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fails to leverage rich spatial and quantitative visual information, leading to factual inaccuracies in multimodal contexts.
- There is a lack of mechanisms to verify if textual descriptions in reasoning chains accurately reflect visual content, complicating error diagnosis.

## Method

- Introduces ==Point-RFT==, a two-stage training framework combining supervised format finetuning with reinforcement learning.
- Develops the ==Point-CoT dataset==, containing 71,000 diverse visual reasoning problems with step-by-step rationales explicitly grounded to ==visual coordinates== using GPT-4o for reasoning and Molmo-7B for localization.
- Utilizes ==Group-wise Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== for reinforcement finetuning, employing a dual reward system to optimize for both point-grounded CoT structure adherence and final answer accuracy.

## Results

- Achieved **90.04%** accuracy on the ChartQA dataset, representing a **19.16 percentage point** improvement over the format-finetuned baseline (**70.88%**) and **6.12 percentage points** over text-only reinforcement learning (**83.92%**).
- Demonstrated strong generalization capabilities by consistently outperforming alternative approaches on out-of-domain benchmarks including CharXiv, PlotQA, IconQA, and TabMWP.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both visual grounding and reinforcement finetuning are crucial for optimal performance and interpretability, validating the complete Point-RFT framework design.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding reasoning steps to visual coordinates significantly reduces visual hallucinations and improves factual accuracy in multimodal AI.
> - Connecting textual reasoning to precise visual elements enables better error diagnosis, distinguishing between perception and reasoning failures.
> - Reinforcement learning with dual rewards (format adherence and accuracy) can effectively optimize for both structural adherence (visual grounding) and correct logical reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Multimodal reasoning tasks require artificial intelligence systems to understand and integrate both visual and textual information effectively. While large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in text-based reasoning through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, their application to multimodal scenarios faces significant challenges. Vision-language models often suffer from visual hallucinations and inadequate integration of visual information when generating reasoning chains.

This paper introduces Point-RFT, a framework that addresses these limitations by explicitly grounding CoT reasoning to visual elements. Rather than relying solely on textual descriptions, Point-RFT associates each reasoning step with specific coordinates in the image, creating a more robust connection between visual perception and logical reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Problem with Text-Only Chain-of-Thought

Traditional CoT reasoning in multimodal contexts faces several critical limitations. When vision-language models attempt to reason about visual content, they often generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect descriptions of what they observe. This phenomenon, known as visual hallucination, undermines the reliability of the entire reasoning process.

For example, when analyzing a chart showing enrollment ratios over time, a text-only approach might incorrectly state values or relationships between data points. Without explicit grounding to visual elements, the model has no mechanism to verify that its textual descriptions accurately reflect the visual content.

Furthermore, text-only CoT fails to leverage the rich spatial and visual information available in images. Charts, graphs, tables, and other visual documents contain precise quantitative information that requires accurate visual parsing to extract meaningful insights.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Point-RFT Framework Overview

Point-RFT introduces a two-stage training framework that combines supervised format finetuning with reinforcement learning. The key innovation lies in creating reasoning chains that explicitly connect each step to specific visual coordinates, ensuring that logical reasoning remains grounded in actual visual content.

The framework operates on the principle that effective multimodal reasoning should mirror human cognitive processes. When humans analyze visual documents, they naturally point to or focus on specific elements while explaining their reasoning. Point-RFT formalizes this intuitive process by requiring the model to specify exact pixel coordinates for each reasoning step.

This approach provides several advantages: it reduces visual hallucinations by forcing the model to reference actual visual elements, enables better error diagnosis by separating perception errors from reasoning errors, and improves interpretability by showing exactly which visual elements informed each reasoning step.

#### 3.2. Point-CoT Dataset Creation

The foundation of Point-RFT is the Point-CoT dataset, containing 71,000 diverse visual reasoning problems with step-by-step rationales explicitly grounded to visual elements. The dataset creation process involves a sophisticated pipeline that combines the reasoning capabilities of GPT-4o with the visual localization abilities of Molmo-7B.

The data generation process begins with collecting diverse visual documents including charts, graphs, tables, and other structured visual content. For each image, researchers generate complex reasoning questions that require multi-step analysis. GPT-4o then creates detailed reasoning chains that break down the problem-solving process into logical steps.

The crucial innovation comes in the second stage, where Molmo-7B analyzes each reasoning step and identifies the specific visual elements referenced. For instance, when a reasoning step mentions "the bar representing Finland," Molmo-7B locates the exact coordinates of that bar in the chart. This process creates a precise mapping between textual reasoning and visual elements.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Finetuning with GRPO

After supervised finetuning on the Point-CoT dataset, Point-RFT undergoes reinforcement finetuning using Group-wise Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). This stage optimizes the model's ability to generate accurate, well-grounded reasoning chains through a dual reward system.

The reward mechanism consists of two components: a format reward that encourages adherence to the point-grounded CoT structure, and an accuracy reward that promotes correct final answers. The format reward ensures that the model consistently provides visual coordinates for its reasoning steps, while the accuracy reward drives overall performance improvement.

GRPO operates by comparing multiple candidate responses and updating the model's policy to favor higher-reward generations. This approach is particularly effective for multimodal reasoning because it can optimize both the quality of visual grounding and the correctness of logical reasoning simultaneously.

The reinforcement learning process uses the ChartQA dataset, which provides a substantial collection of chart-based question-answering tasks. By training on this domain-specific data, Point-RFT learns to apply its visually grounded reasoning capabilities effectively to chart interpretation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

Point-RFT demonstrates substantial improvements across multiple benchmarks. On the ChartQA dataset, Point-RFT achieves 90.04% accuracy, significantly outperforming the format-finetuned baseline (70.88%) and text-only reinforcement learning (83.92%). This represents a 19.16 percentage point improvement over the baseline and a 6.12 percentage point improvement over text-only approaches.

The model's superior performance extends to out-of-domain evaluations, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. On CharXiv, PlotQA, IconQA, and TabMWP benchmarks, Point-RFT consistently outperforms alternative approaches, indicating that visually grounded reasoning transfers effectively across different types of visual documents.

Ablation studies confirm the importance of both visual grounding and reinforcement finetuning. Models trained without visual grounding show significantly lower performance, while those without reinforcement finetuning fail to achieve optimal accuracy. This validates the complete Point-RFT framework design.

The enhanced pointing accuracy not only contributes to better answers but also provides increased interpretability. Researchers can now distinguish between perception errors (incorrect visual localization) and reasoning errors (faulty logical steps), enabling more targeted model improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Implications and Applications

Point-RFT represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI with broad implications for various applications. The framework's ability to provide interpretable, visually grounded reasoning makes it particularly valuable for domains where explainability is crucial.

In educational technology, Point-RFT could assist students in understanding complex visual materials by providing step-by-step explanations that clearly indicate which visual elements support each reasoning step. This capability extends to scientific research, where accurate interpretation of charts, graphs, and experimental data is essential.

Business intelligence applications could benefit from Point-RFT's ability to analyze financial charts, market data, and performance metrics with both accuracy and transparency. The model's capacity to explain its reasoning process through visual grounding makes it suitable for decision-support systems where stakeholders need to understand the basis for AI-generated insights.

Accessibility applications represent another promising domain. Point-RFT could help visually impaired individuals understand visual content by providing detailed, spatially grounded descriptions of charts, diagrams, and other visual materials.

The framework also advances the broader field of multimodal AI by demonstrating how explicit visual grounding can enhance reasoning capabilities. This approach could inspire similar techniques in other domains, such as video understanding, robotic manipulation, and augmented reality applications.

Point-RFT's success suggests that future multimodal AI systems should incorporate explicit mechanisms for connecting reasoning processes to sensory inputs. This principle extends beyond vision to other modalities, potentially informing the development of audio-grounded reasoning for speech analysis or tactile-grounded reasoning for robotic applications.

The work establishes a new standard for multimodal reasoning evaluation by emphasizing both accuracy and interpretability. Future research in this area will likely adopt similar frameworks that require models to demonstrate not just correct answers but also proper grounding in relevant sensory information.
