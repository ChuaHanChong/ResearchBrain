# World Model-based Perception for Visual Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> World Model-based Perception (WMP) integrates a learned world model with a reinforcement learning framework, enabling legged robots to perform vision-based locomotion on complex terrains. The system successfully transfers from simulation to a real Unitree A1 robot and consistently outperforms baseline methods across various challenging environments, including gaps, climbs, and tilts.

## Problem

- Legged robots relying solely on proprioceptive feedback struggle with complex terrains that require advanced planning based on visual information.
- Learning control policies directly from high-dimensional visual inputs using standard reinforcement learning is data-inefficient.
- Existing privileged learning frameworks create a performance gap between training (with extra information) and real-world deployment, and sim-to-real transfer remains challenging.

## Method

- A World Model-based Perception (WMP) framework combines a learned world model with a reinforcement learning policy for vision-based legged locomotion.
- A Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) is trained in simulation to learn compact, predictive latent representations of future depth images and proprioception.
- A policy network uses the world model's recurrent state and current proprioception to generate robot control actions, trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with domain randomization.

## Results

- The simulation-trained world model accurately predicts real-world depth images across various terrains, demonstrating successful perceptual transfer.
- The full WMP system successfully transferred to a real Unitree A1 robot, enabling robust navigation across various complex real-world terrains like gaps, climbs, and tilts.
- WMP consistently achieved higher success rates compared to baseline methods, including student and blind policies, showing particular advantages in increasingly challenging visual locomotion tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model can learn distinct latent representations for various terrain types from visual and proprioceptive data, aiding environmental understanding.
> - Optimizing the world model update interval (e.g., 8-10 control steps) balances performance and computational cost.
> - Both visual (depth images) and proprioceptive information are crucial for the world model to accurately capture environmental dynamics and improve robot performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Enabling legged robots to navigate complex, unstructured environments remains one of the most challenging problems in robotics. While significant progress has been made in legged locomotion using proprioceptive information (internal sensory data about joint positions and forces), these approaches struggle when confronted with complex terrains that require visual perception.

#### 1.2. Background and Challenges

Traditional approaches to legged locomotion face several limitations:

1. **Proprioception Limitations**: Methods relying solely on proprioceptive feedback struggle with terrains requiring advanced planning based on visual information.

2. **Data Inefficiency**: Learning policies directly from high-dimensional visual inputs using standard reinforcement learning (RL) is extremely data-inefficient.

3. **Privileged Learning Drawbacks**: Existing approaches often use privileged learning frameworks, where robots are trained with additional information not available during deployment. This creates a performance gap between teacher and student policies.

4. **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots remains challenging due to the reality gap in visual and physical dynamics.

The proposed WMP framework aims to overcome these limitations by learning a world model that can efficiently capture the essential dynamics of the environment while being transferable to real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Proposed Approach

This paper introduces World Model-based Perception (WMP), a novel framework that addresses the challenges of vision-based legged locomotion by incorporating a world model that learns predictive representations of the environment. The approach draws inspiration from how animals navigate by building mental models of their surroundings, enabling them to anticipate and adapt to environmental changes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. World Model-based Perception Framework

WMP integrates a world model with a reinforcement learning framework to enable vision-based legged locomotion. The core idea is to:

1. Learn a compact and meaningful representation of the environment through a world model
2. Use this representation to inform the robot's control policy
3. Enable successful transfer from simulation to the real world

The WMP framework consists of two main components:

#### 3.2. World Model

The world model is implemented as a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) that learns to predict future observations based on past observations and actions. The model operates at a lower frequency than the control policy to reduce computational demands.

The world model includes:
- **Perception Encoder (E)**: Encodes visual and proprioceptive information into a latent space
- **Perception Decoder (D)**: Reconstructs observations from the latent space
- **Recurrent State (h)**: Captures temporal dependencies in the environment
- **Stochastic State (z)**: Incorporates uncertainty and variability in predictions

The latent representations capture key features of the environment that are relevant for locomotion tasks, such as terrain geometry and obstacles.

#### 3.3. Policy Network

The policy network takes as input:
- The recurrent state from the world model, which contains information about the environment
- The robot's proprioceptive information, which provides data about its current physical state

The policy outputs control actions for the robot's joints. By incorporating the world model's state, the policy can make decisions based on a rich understanding of the environment rather than raw visual inputs.

#### 3.4. Training Process

Training the WMP framework involves several key steps:

1. **World Model Training**: The world model is trained entirely in simulation to predict future observations (depth images and proprioception) from past observations and actions.

2. **Policy Learning**: Using the pretrained world model, a policy is trained with reinforcement learning. Specifically, the asymmetric actor-critic framework with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed.

3. **Domain Randomization**: To improve robustness and facilitate sim-to-real transfer, domain randomization techniques are applied during training. These include randomizing physical parameters, terrain properties, and visual characteristics.

The model's internal representation captures meaningful features about the environment, as visualized through dimensionality reduction techniques. Figure 3 shows how the model learns to distinguish between different terrain types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

In simulation evaluations, the WMP approach demonstrated several key findings:

1. **Model Interval Optimization**: The researchers analyzed different update intervals for the world model, finding that update intervals around 8-10 control steps provided the best balance between performance and computational efficiency.

2. **Training Duration**: The performance improved with increased training duration, plateauing after sufficient training steps, suggesting successful convergence of the learning process.

3. **Prediction Accuracy**: The world model successfully predicted depth images for various terrains, matching the ground truth closely even after many prediction steps.

#### 4.2. Real-World Transfer

A critical aspect of the research is the transfer of the trained system to a real Unitree A1 quadrupedal robot. The authors found that:

1. **Prediction Capability**: The world model, trained entirely in simulation, could accurately predict real-world depth images across various terrains, demonstrating successful transfer of perceptual capabilities.

2. **Control Transfer**: The complete WMP system transferred successfully to the real robot, enabling it to navigate complex terrains including gaps, climbs, tilts, and crawl spaces.

#### 4.3. Performance Evaluation

The WMP approach was evaluated against several baselines:

1. **Student Policy**: A neural network trained to mimic a privileged teacher policy
2. **Blind Policy**: A policy using only proprioceptive information without visual input
3. **WMP without Proprioception**: An ablated version of WMP that doesn't predict proprioception

The evaluation involved increasing the difficulty of various terrains until failure:

Key findings included:

- WMP consistently outperformed all baselines across different terrain types
- The performance gap widened as terrain difficulty increased
- The blind policy failed almost immediately in visually demanding terrains
- Including proprioception prediction in the world model was beneficial for performance

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

The researchers conducted several ablation studies to understand the contribution of different components:

1. **Depth Image Importance**: Removing depth images from the world model resulted in significantly worse performance, highlighting the essential role of visual information for complex terrain navigation.

2. **Proprioception Prediction**: Removing proprioception prediction from the world model reduced performance, suggesting that predicting proprioception helps the model capture physical properties of the environment.

3. **Model Interval**: Testing different intervals for world model updates revealed a trade-off between computational efficiency and performance, with shorter intervals generally yielding better results.

These studies confirmed that all components of the WMP framework contribute meaningfully to its overall performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Real-World Applications

The system demonstrated robust performance in diverse outdoor environments, showcasing its practical utility:

The successful deployment in outdoor settings highlights the potential of WMP for real-world applications such as search and rescue, inspection tasks, and exploration of difficult-to-access environments.

#### 5.2. Future Directions

The authors identify several promising directions for future research:

1. **Mixed Training Data**: Training the world model with both simulated and real-world data could further improve real-world performance.

2. **Additional Sensory Modalities**: Incorporating other forms of perception beyond vision, such as touch sensing, could expand the system's capabilities.

3. **Task Generalization**: Extending the framework to handle a broader range of locomotion tasks and environments would increase its practical utility.

4. **Dynamic Environments**: Improving the ability to handle dynamic elements in the environment, such as moving obstacles or changing lighting conditions.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

The World Model-based Perception framework represents a significant advancement in vision-based legged locomotion. By learning a world model that captures essential environmental dynamics, WMP enables more data-efficient learning and successful sim-to-real transfer. The approach outperforms traditional methods, especially in challenging terrains that require visual perception.

The key contributions of this work include:

1. A novel framework that integrates world models with reinforcement learning for legged locomotion
2. Demonstration of successful sim-to-real transfer of both perceptual and control capabilities
3. Superior performance compared to existing approaches, particularly in complex terrains
4. Insights into the importance of different components through careful ablation studies

This research opens new possibilities for deploying legged robots in complex, unstructured environments, bringing us closer to the vision of robots that can navigate the real world with the agility and adaptability of animals.
