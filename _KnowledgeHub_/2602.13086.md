# UniManip: General-Purpose Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation with Agentic Operational Graph

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanyang Technological University researchers developed UniManip, a robotics framework that enables robust zero-shot manipulation by integrating high-level semantic reasoning with precise physical interaction via a Bi-level Agentic Operational Graph and closed-loop recovery. It achieved a 93.75% success rate in zero-shot tasks, outperforming leading baselines, and successfully transferred to mobile manipulation platforms without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models struggle with zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution conditions due to a lack of explicit state grounding and interpretability.
- Hierarchical planning methods often lack dynamic physical grounding and robust mechanisms for diagnosing and recovering from execution errors, leading to brittleness in real-world scenarios.
- Bridging the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and precise, low-level physical interaction remains challenging for long-horizon and dexterous robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- Introduced UniManip, a framework built upon a Bi-level Agentic Operational Graph (AOG) comprising an Agentic Logic Layer for high-level reasoning and a Semantic-Operational State Graph for dynamic state representation.
- Developed a 'task-to-motion bridge' that integrates single-view conservative scene reconstruction, safety-aware trajectory generation, and parameter-relaxed inverse kinematics (RIK) for robust physical execution.
- Implemented a closed-loop reflection and autonomous recovery mechanism within the AOG for anomaly detection, state verification, and error-informed replanning (local or global fixes).

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of 93.75% in zero-shot manipulation tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art VLA baselines like NORA-1.5-FAST (71.25%).
- Demonstrated an average task success rate of 82.5% in cluttered scenarios, surpassing hierarchical baselines such as ReKep (47.5%), MOKA (57.5%), and VoxPoser (55.0%).
- Successfully enabled zero-shot transfer to mobile manipulation platforms in office environments without fine-tuning, performing complex tasks while adapting to dynamic base positions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A bi-level agentic architecture that couples procedural and episodic memory significantly enhances the robot's ability to reason semantically and maintain dynamic physical grounding.
> - Integrating conservative scene reconstruction, safety-aware planning, and relaxed inverse kinematics (RIK) in a 'task-to-motion bridge' is crucial for reliable and collision-free execution in cluttered environments.
> - Autonomous reflection and recovery, driven by continuous verification and adaptive replanning, is essential for achieving high success rates and robustness in long-horizon and unscripted manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation aims to enable robots to perform diverse tasks in unstructured, open-world environments. A significant challenge within this domain is achieving zero-shot generalization, which refers to a robot's ability to adapt to novel objects, tasks, and environments without prior task-specific training or fine-tuning. Current research approaches primarily fall into two paradigms: data-driven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and structured hierarchical planning frameworks.

VLA models, such as RT-1, PaLM-E, and OpenVLA, aim to map raw sensory observations directly to low-level robot actions, leveraging large datasets and deep learning architectures. While these models have demonstrated success in specific or lightly adapted settings, they often operate as "black boxes" with limited explicit state grounding. This characteristic can lead to brittleness when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions or requiring long-horizon verification and recovery mechanisms. Their generalization capability is fundamentally bounded by their training data distribution, making it challenging to adapt to unseen kinematics or dynamics without extensive, high-quality demonstrations.

Conversely, hierarchical planning methods, exemplified by approaches like VoxPoser and ReKep, decompose complex tasks into symbolic plans, often utilizing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or formal planning languages like PDDL. These frameworks typically separate high-level planning from low-level execution. This separation can result in a lack of dynamic physical grounding and closed-loop feedback, rendering them vulnerable to failures when low-level primitives do not execute as expected or when environmental conditions change unpredictably. Such systems often lack robust mechanisms for diagnosing and recovering from execution errors.

UniManip positions itself to address the limitations of both paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level semantic reasoning and precise, low-level physical interaction by integrating an agentic, graph-based workflow. The paper highlights the need for a system that can efficiently represent scenario conditions and spatial operations for VLM reasoning while seamlessly connecting this reasoning to robust low-level manipulation execution. By incorporating continuous perception, verification, and autonomous reflection, UniManip seeks to provide the agentic capacity required for robust zero-shot performance in dynamic, unscripted environments, moving beyond open-loop execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose robotic manipulation framework, UniManip, capable of robust zero-shot generalization across diverse objects, tasks, and robot embodiments, including transfer from fixed-base to mobile manipulation, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning or reconfiguration.

This overarching objective is motivated by several key challenges in current robotic manipulation systems:
- **Bridging Semantic and Physical Interaction:** Robots need to translate abstract human commands into precise physical actions in unstructured environments. Existing methods often struggle to maintain consistency between high-level semantic intent and low-level physical reality.
- **Addressing Zero-Shot Generalization Limitations:** Current approaches exhibit limitations in adapting to novel scenarios. End-to-end VLA models frequently fail under out-of-distribution conditions (unseen visual data, free-form commands), while hierarchical planners lack the semantic flexibility to handle unexpected environmental variations or execution faults. The research aims to overcome this "fundamental disconnect" and "semantic rigidity."
- **Ensuring Precision and Robustness for Long-Horizon Tasks:** Many complex real-world tasks involve multiple sequential steps where minor deviations can lead to cascading failures. Existing systems often lack the precision for long-horizon tasks and mechanisms to recover from partial failures, leading to low success rates.
- **Enabling Adaptive Interaction with Dynamic Environments:** The motivation extends to endowing robots with an "agentic capacity"—the ability to continuously perceive, verify, and autonomously reflect on their actions to realign high-level intent with the unscripted physical world. This includes dynamic scene understanding and adaptation to execution errors.
- **Supporting Diverse Robot Embodiments:** The goal is to develop a framework that is hardware-agnostic and can transfer capabilities between different robot platforms (e.g., fixed-base manipulators and mobile manipulators) without significant modifications or retraining. This reduces the deployment barrier for general-purpose robots.

In summary, the motivation is to create a more cognitively aligned, robust, and adaptable manipulation system that can operate effectively in open-world settings by integrating high-level reasoning with reliable low-level execution and incorporating autonomous recovery mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniManip is a framework structured around a Bi-level Agentic Operational Graph (AOG), designed to unify semantic reasoning with physical grounding. The AOG comprises two interacting layers: an Agentic Logic Layer (ALG) for high-level reasoning and task orchestration, and a Semantic-Operational State Graph (SOSG) for dynamic state representation and physical grounding. The system operates through three logically connected phases: multimodal scene instantiation, agentic primitive execution, and closed-loop reflection and recovery.

**A. Bi-level Agentic Operational Graph (AOG)**
- **Agentic Logic Layer (ALG):** This layer functions as the system's procedural and semantic memory. It is responsible for high-level reasoning, orchestrating task flow, verifying outcomes, and managing recovery from partial failures. It guides the robot through a Perceive-Plan-Act-Reflect cycle based on natural language commands and available tools.
- **Semantic-Operational State Graph (SOSG):** This layer serves as the dynamic episodic memory, maintaining an adaptive, grounded representation of object states and spatial affordances. Each node (v_i) in the SOSG represents a physical entity, parameterized by an augmented state vector (x_i) containing semantic (class, role, context), kinematic (articulation, grasping aperture, pose), intrinsic physical (color, geometry, material), and operational part decomposition (sub-components, functional descriptions) properties. Edges represent dynamic relationships or operations.

**B. Multimodal Scene Instantiation and Task Decomposition**
1. **Dynamic SOSG Update and Scene Parsing:** The SOSG is updated online using a hierarchical open-vocabulary perception pipeline. A VLM-based open-vocabulary detector performs coarse retrieval of candidate regions based on natural language context (τ_i). A promptable segmentation model then refines these regions into pixel-accurate masks, which, combined with synchronized depth data from a single wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, enable robust 3D pose estimation and affordance detection. For articulated objects, the episodic mechanism state over operational parts is also refreshed after interactions.
2. **Task Decomposition:** The ALG translates a free-form natural language command (L_h) into a sequence of graph-conditioned sub-tasks. Sub-tasks are encoded as either directed edges (e_ij) for Transit operations (move to target configuration) or self-loops (e_ii) for Manipulation operations (execute precise relative motions like pushing/pulling, specified by a 6-DoF increment relative to a base or end-effector frame). This graph-based representation provides explicit, compositional, and verifiable plans.

**C. Agentic Primitive Execution and Motion Synthesis**
1. **Action Abstraction through Agentic Operational Primitives:** The framework uses a primitive library (T_c) for atomic spatial operations.
 - **Objective-Oriented Motion:** Directs the end-effector (EEF) to a target pose (R_target) grounded in the SOSG, with optional gripper actions.
 - **Operation-Oriented Motion:** Executes precise relative motions (translation and rotation increments) for interaction-centric tasks (e.g., pushing, pulling).
 - **Navigation Primitive (for mobile manipulators):** An augmented primitive (N) for mobile robots to navigate to a target entity (O_target) and achieve a desired docking clearance (d_goal) using visual servoing, driving the base to a manipulation-feasible configuration.
2. **Agentic-Guided Motion Synthesis:** This module maps a target EEF pose to a collision-free waypoint sequence.
 - **Defensive Reconstruction of Occluded Areas:** From a single RGB-D observation, a conservative volumetric occupancy map (M_final) is constructed. Raw point clouds are voxelized, and 3D morphological closing is applied. A safety heuristic enforces vertical support under occupied voxels, treating space below as non-traversable to mitigate collision risks from unobserved cavities.
 - **Safety-Aware Trajectory Generation:** The conservative occupancy map is converted into an Euclidean Signed Distance Field (ESDF) (Φ). An A* algorithm computes a discrete path (P*) in this ESDF, enforcing a minimum clearance (r_safe) to avoid collisions. Orientation is synthesized using Spherical Linear Interpolation (Slerp) to ensure smooth rotational transitions along the path.
 - **Parameter-Relaxed Inverse Kinematics (RIK):** To address IK failures near kinematic singularities or workspace boundaries, a relaxed IK strategy is employed. The solver initially attempts convergence with strict position and orientation tolerances. If it fails, these tolerances are iteratively relaxed by predefined factors until a feasible joint configuration is found or a safety budget is exceeded. This prioritizes continuity of execution over exact pose matching in challenging regions.

**D. Agentic Verification and Autonomous Recovery**
UniManip integrates a closed-loop failure reflection and recovery mechanism within the AOG.
1. **Anomaly Detection and State Verification:** A verification operator (F_verify) evaluates whether the intended post-conditions of an operation are satisfied. This combines geometric consistency checks (e.g., EEF pose, gripper state) with visual-semantic consistency, using a VLM-based verifier to assess if the SOSG has transitioned as expected based on post-execution observations.
2. **Agentic Reflection via Graph and Memory:** Upon a detected failure, a reflection operator (Ψ) updates the episodic memory (SOSG) to reflect the observed outcome (G_ref_t) and produces a semantic diagnosis (D_err). This ensures subsequent reasoning is grounded in reality.
3. **Error-Informed Reflection and Agentic Recovery:** Based on the diagnosis (D_err) and updated episodic state (G_ref_t), the ALG selects a recovery strategy:
 - **Local Fix:** For minor deviations (e.g., small pose errors), the agent computes a corrective adjustment (δo) by back-projecting the execution error into the operation parameter space, then reissues a repaired operation.
 - **Global Fix:** For high-level divergences (e.g., target displacement, scene topology changes), the system discards the remaining plan and regenerates a new operation sequence, conditioned on the updated episodic memory, the original command, and retrieved experience.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of UniManip was conducted using a Galaxea A1 robotic arm with a two-finger gripper and an Intel RealSense D435 RGB-D camera in an eye-in-hand configuration. For mobile manipulation, a Realman RM65 arm on a differential-drive base was used. Experiments focused on zero-shot manipulation, robustness in cluttered scenes, long-horizon and dexterous tasks, cross-embodiment transfer, and an ablation study.

**A. Zero-shot Manipulation Performance (vs. VLA baselines)**
UniManip was compared against state-of-the-art end-to-end VLA models: π0, NORA, and NORA-1.5. Evaluations covered tasks like "Put S in S" (seen objects in seen containers), "Put U in U" (unseen objects in unseen containers), "Put U in S," and "Move U to U."
- UniManip achieved an average success rate (SR) of 93.75%, outperforming NORA-1.5-FAST (the strongest baseline) at 71.25%.
- UniManip demonstrated a lower distractor grasping rate of 7.78%, indicating a reduced tendency to interact with irrelevant objects. This was attributed to its grounded task representation within the Bi-level AOG, which queries object-centric constraints.
- The results suggested that the explicit task-to-motion bridging in UniManip, including conservative occupancy completion and safety-aware planning, contributed to execution reliability beyond semantic correctness.

**B. Robustness Evaluation in Cluttered Scenarios (vs. Hierarchical baselines)**
UniManip was benchmarked against hierarchical open-vocabulary manipulation frameworks: ReKep, MOKA, and VoxPoser, in cluttered desktop scenes with multiple distractors.
- UniManip achieved an average task success rate (TSR) of 82.5%, which was higher than ReKep (47.5%), MOKA (57.5%), and VoxPoser (55.0%).
- This performance was attributed to the AOG's architecture, which enhances task resilience through plan adaptation and verified intermediate outcomes.
- The task-to-motion bridge, incorporating conservative occupancy and safety-aware planning with relaxed IK, was also identified as reducing geometric brittleness and avoiding collision-induced failures in cluttered environments.

**C. Evaluation on Long-Horizon and Dexterous Tasks**
UniManip was tested on complex tasks involving sequential skill composition, precise contact control, and closed-loop recovery. These included sorting waste, inserting objects, pouring water, preparing breakfast, sorting breads, pressing buttons, and opening/closing drawers.
- An ablation study was conducted to quantify the contributions of specific modules: Parameter-relaxed Inverse Kinematics (RIK), Collision-Free Planning (CFP), and Failure Detection & Recovery (FDR).
 - **RIK:** Contributed to reachability robustness by mitigating IK failures near singularities or workspace boundaries, particularly for collision-free trajectory tracking and articulated object manipulation.
 - **CFP:** Improved safety and consistency in cluttered environments by planning in a conservative ESDF, preventing trajectories from entering unobserved cavities.
 - **FDR:** Significantly improved long-horizon success (e.g., trash sorting, breakfast preparation) by preventing error accumulation through AOG-driven verification, reflection, and recovery.
- The full UniManip system (with RIK, CFP, and FDR) attained an 80% average success rate across 90 trials in the ablation suite, highlighting the complementary roles of these components.

**D. Mobile Manipulation in Office Environments**
UniManip was deployed on a mobile manipulator, demonstrating zero-shot transfer without fine-tuning or reconfiguration.
- The system utilized a mapless reactive navigation strategy (Visual Servoing) with YOLOE for real-time object detection and relative centroid estimation.
- The mobile base navigated to randomly selected docking points in an office, performed tasks such as preparing breakfast, and tidying up a bench.
- The eye-in-hand camera configuration was identified as crucial for maintaining perceptual consistency despite changes in the mobile base's position and viewpoint.

**E. Failure Analysis**
Analysis of failure modes across all trials (before recovery actions) revealed:
- **Grasp Failure (16.3%)**: Most predominant, primarily due to single-view depth estimation limitations with transparent or reflective objects, leading to inaccurate gripper pose.
- **Motion Error (9.7%)**: Largely caused by coupling errors between the mobile base and manipulator, leading to kinematic singularities or invalid IK solutions when the base docked suboptimally.
- **Grasp Collision (5.3%)**: Occurred in dense environments where gripper fingers made unintended contact with adjacent obstacles.
- **Placement Error (4.3%)**: Often during the release phase, due to unstable contact dynamics.
- **Grounding Error (4.7%)**: A low rate, validating the Bi-level AOG's effectiveness in semantic understanding. Failures occurred when VLMs misidentified visually similar objects or struggled with ambiguous spatial descriptions.
The distribution of failures underscored the necessity of the reflection and recovery mechanism, as a significant portion (30%) stemmed from execution-based issues that a static planner would not address.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniManip contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by presenting a framework that integrates high-level semantic reasoning with precise low-level physical interaction. Its primary significance lies in its demonstrated ability to achieve robust zero-shot generalization across diverse objects, tasks, and robot embodiments, including transfer to mobile manipulation, without task-specific fine-tuning or reconfiguration. This capability addresses a fundamental challenge in deploying robotic systems to unstructured, open-world environments.

The introduction of the Bi-level Agentic Operational Graph (AOG) as an agentic infrastructure represents a notable architectural contribution. By explicitly coupling an Agentic Logic Layer (ALG) with a Semantic-Operational State Graph (SOSG), the system mimics cognitive interplay between procedural and episodic memory, enabling dynamic task decomposition, continuous environmental synchronization, and sophisticated reasoning. This unified approach mitigates the limitations of both end-to-end VLA models (lack of precision, interpretability) and traditional hierarchical planners (semantic rigidity, lack of dynamic physical grounding).

The proposed task-to-motion bridge, incorporating single-view conservative scene reconstruction, safety-aware motion planning, and parameter-relaxed inverse kinematics, enhances the reliability of physical execution. This is particularly impactful for operations in cluttered or partially observed environments where collision avoidance and kinematic feasibility are critical. The closed-loop reflection and autonomous recovery mechanism further increases robustness, converting potential terminal failures into recoverable states through error diagnosis and adaptive replanning.

The experimental results demonstrate that UniManip consistently outperforms state-of-the-art VLA and hierarchical baselines in success rates, particularly in scenarios involving unseen objects, clutter, and long-horizon tasks. The successful zero-shot transfer to mobile manipulation highlights the framework's versatility and potential for deployment in real-world scenarios requiring mobility.

The open-source nature of the project (indicated by the project page) has the potential to facilitate further research and development in agentic robotics, offering a foundation for building more intelligent, adaptive, and reliable robotic systems. By addressing key bottlenecks in current robotic manipulation research, UniManip lays groundwork for future advancements in areas such as contact robustness, uncertainty-aware state estimation, and whole-body planning in complex multi-robot settings.
