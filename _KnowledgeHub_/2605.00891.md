# X2SAM: Any Segmentation in Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-Sen University, Peng Cheng Laboratory, and Meituan Inc. developed X2SAM, a unified multimodal large language model that performs "any-segmentation" across both images and videos using conversational and visual prompts. This framework improves video grounded conversation generation by 21.5 mIoU over VideoGLaMM and establishes strong performance on diverse image and video segmentation tasks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack dense, pixel-level perception for fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding across images and videos.
- Foundation segmentation models require low-level visual prompts and do not natively interpret complex conversational instructions.
- Existing segmentation MLLMs are fragmented, with separate models for images and videos, often lacking unified prompt integration and struggling with temporal consistency in videos.

## Method

- Introduced **X2SAM**, a unified segmentation-oriented MLLM framework supporting both textual instructions and visual prompts for images and videos.
- Implemented a ==Mask Memory module== with a ==First-In-First-Out== (FIFO) strategy to maintain temporal coherence of segmentation masks in video sequences.
- Utilized a ==dual-branch visual extraction== architecture, combining a Vision Encoder for global understanding and a Mask Encoder for fine-grained features, integrated with an LLM and a redesigned Mask Decoder.

## Results

- X2SAM achieved a 21.5 mIoU improvement over VideoGLaMM in video grounded conversation generation (V-GCG) and a 14.2 J&F improvement over HyperSeg in video reasoning segmentation.
- The model showed substantial gains in video visual grounded segmentation (V-VGD), with 74.4 AP on YT-VIS19 and 57.8 AP on VIPSeg for box prompts, surpassing SAM2-H by considerable margins.
- X2SAM demonstrated competitive performance in image open-vocabulary segmentation (31.2 PQ) and secured state-of-the-art results among segmentation-based MLLMs across various image and video chat benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework can integrate complex multi-modal instructions (textual and visual prompts) to achieve "any-segmentation" capabilities across both images and videos.
> - Maintaining temporal coherence in video segmentation is effectively achieved through a Mask Memory module that stores and refines guided vision features across frames.
> - An adaptive unified joint training strategy with dimension-shifting and modality-aware batching enables efficient co-training on diverse image and video datasets.
