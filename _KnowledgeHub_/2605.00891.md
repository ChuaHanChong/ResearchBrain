# X2SAM: Any Segmentation in Images and Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sun Yat-Sen University, Peng Cheng Laboratory, and Meituan Inc. developed X2SAM, a unified multimodal large language model that performs "any-segmentation" across both images and videos using conversational and visual prompts. This framework improves video grounded conversation generation by 21.5 mIoU over VideoGLaMM and establishes strong performance on diverse image and video segmentation tasks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack dense, pixel-level perception for fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding across images and videos.
- Foundation segmentation models require low-level visual prompts and do not natively interpret complex conversational instructions.
- Existing segmentation MLLMs are fragmented, with separate models for images and videos, often lacking unified prompt integration and struggling with temporal consistency in videos.

## Method

- Introduced **X2SAM**, a unified segmentation-oriented MLLM framework supporting both textual instructions and visual prompts for images and videos.
- Implemented a ==Mask Memory module== with a ==First-In-First-Out== (FIFO) strategy to maintain temporal coherence of segmentation masks in video sequences.
- Utilized a ==dual-branch visual extraction== architecture, combining a Vision Encoder for global understanding and a Mask Encoder for fine-grained features, integrated with an LLM and a redesigned Mask Decoder.

## Results

- X2SAM achieved a 21.5 mIoU improvement over VideoGLaMM in video grounded conversation generation (V-GCG) and a 14.2 J&F improvement over HyperSeg in video reasoning segmentation.
- The model showed substantial gains in video visual grounded segmentation (V-VGD), with 74.4 AP on YT-VIS19 and 57.8 AP on VIPSeg for box prompts, surpassing SAM2-H by considerable margins.
- X2SAM demonstrated competitive performance in image open-vocabulary segmentation (31.2 PQ) and secured state-of-the-art results among segmentation-based MLLMs across various image and video chat benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework can integrate complex multi-modal instructions (textual and visual prompts) to achieve "any-segmentation" capabilities across both images and videos.
> - Maintaining temporal coherence in video segmentation is effectively achieved through a Mask Memory module that stores and refines guided vision features across frames.
> - An adaptive unified joint training strategy with dimension-shifting and modality-aware batching enables efficient co-training on diverse image and video datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed substantial progress in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which demonstrate capabilities in image-level visual understanding and reasoning. However, a limitation exists in their ability to perform dense, pixel-level perception and generate fine-grained outputs for precise spatial and temporal comprehension across both static images and dynamic video sequences.

Concurrently, foundation segmentation models, such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM) series (e.g., SAM and its video-extended successor SAM2), are capable of producing high-quality segmentation masks. These models typically rely on explicit low-level visual prompts, such as points or bounding boxes, and do not inherently interpret complex conversational instructions.

A developing area involves bridging the gap between language understanding and mask generation through segmentation MLLMs. Current approaches, however, often present a fragmented landscape. Image segmentation MLLMs (e.g., LISA) process textual instructions but are generally restricted to static images and frequently lack support for visual prompts. Conversely, video segmentation MLLMs (e.g., VISA, VideoLISA) enable temporal text-to-mask generation but typically operate without a unified architecture that supports both images and videos or incorporates visual prompts alongside textual instructions. Furthermore, many video-centric models employ frame-by-frame decoding, which can lead to inconsistencies in mask generation and object tracking across video frames.

X2SAM is introduced to address this fragmentation and the identified limitations. It aims to develop a unified framework that interprets complex multi-modal instructions, encompassing both textual and visual prompts, for segmentation across images and videos. Unlike SAM2, which focuses on promptable segmentation with memory-based propagation but lacks language-driven reasoning, or X-SAM, which supports MLLM-based segmentation with multi-modal prompts but is image-centric, X2SAM seeks to integrate these capabilities within a single instruction-following framework. Its approach is not a direct cascade of existing models but a joint optimization of grounding, decoding, and memory mechanisms designed for temporally consistent, instruction-based mask generation across both modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce X2SAM, a unified segmentation-oriented Multi-modal Large Language Model (MLLM) framework. This framework aims to extend "any-segmentation" capabilities, traditionally applied to images, to dynamic video sequences, supporting diverse segmentation tasks driven by both conversational instructions and visual prompts.

The motivation for this research stems from several limitations observed in the current landscape of AI models:

- **Limited Pixel-Level Perception in MLLMs:** While contemporary MLLMs have demonstrated robust capabilities in global visual understanding (e.g., image captioning, visual question answering), their ability to generate dense, pixel-level outputs for precise spatial and temporal comprehension remains underdeveloped. This restricts their applicability to fine-grained tasks across static images and dynamic video content.
- **Dependence on Low-Level Prompts in Foundation Segmentation Models:** Foundation models like SAM are effective at generating high-quality segmentation masks. However, their operation typically requires explicit, low-level visual prompts (e.g., points, boxes). They do not natively interpret complex, conversational textual instructions, limiting their utility in more natural, language-driven interaction scenarios.
- **Fragmented Architectures in Existing Segmentation MLLMs:** The current generation of MLLMs designed for segmentation is structurally fragmented. Image-specific segmentation MLLMs (e.g., LISA) can process textual instructions but are confined to static images and often lack comprehensive support for interactive visual prompts. Conversely, video-specific segmentation MLLMs (e.g., VISA, VideoLISA) address temporal text-to-mask generation but typically do not offer a unified architecture for both images and videos, nor do they fully integrate visual prompting. This fragmentation necessitates separate models for different modalities and prompt types, hindering the development of truly generalist perception systems.
- **Challenges in Maintaining Temporal Coherence for Video Segmentation:** Existing video segmentation MLLMs often employ frame-by-frame decoding strategies. This approach struggles to systematically store and track multi-modal guided features, which is essential for maintaining robust mask consistency and temporal coherence across continuous video frames, especially in scenarios with occlusions or significant appearance changes.

To address these motivations, X2SAM is designed to tackle three specific technical challenges:

1. **Comprehensive Prompt Integration:** The framework seeks to augment MLLMs to process interleaved textual instructions and visual prompts (V-Prompts) uniformly across both image and video inputs.
2. **Spatio-Temporal Task Formulation:** X2SAM aims to cast diverse image segmentation paradigms into a shared formulation capable of representing and tracking targets consistently over time in video sequences.
3. **Temporal Coherence via Mask Memory:** A critical aspect is the introduction of a Mask Memory module. This module is intended to move beyond independent frame-by-frame decoding by storing guided vision features and interacting with the Mask Decoder, thereby ensuring mask consistency across video sequences.

Furthermore, a specific objective is the introduction of the **Video Visual Grounded (V-VGD) segmentation benchmark**. This benchmark is designed to evaluate a model's capacity to segment object tracks in videos based on interactive visual prompts, aiming to advance research in grounding and tracking objects across frames through direct visual interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

X2SAM is conceptualized as a novel segmentation-oriented MLLM that extends segmentation capabilities from static images to dynamic video sequences through a unified framework.

#### 3.1. Formulation

X2SAM takes a textual or visual prompt alongside either a single image or a video sequence as input. The textual prompt provides natural language instructions for the segmentation task, while the visual prompt offers interactive cues (e.g., points, boxes) to specify objects of interest. The output comprises a contextual language response and a corresponding pixel-level segmentation mask. To standardize various tasks, X2SAM employs a unified formulation where objects of interest are conditional states, and instructions are contextual inputs. Special tokens `<p>` and `</p>` demarcate object conditions, and a `<SEG>` token indicates the segmentation mask. The LLM's output for `<SEG>` serves as a directive for the mask decoder, and task-specific templates facilitate aligned language generation.

#### 3.2. Framework Architecture

As depicted in Figure 3 of the paper, X2SAM features a dual-branch visual extraction architecture:
- **Vision Encoder (fv):** Extracts global visual representations (Zv). This component is based on Qwen3-VL-4B, processing visual inputs augmented with timestamps and partitioned into spatial patches, which are then projected into latent embeddings.
- **Mask Encoder (gm):** Captures fine-grained visual features (Zm) for dense prediction. It adopts the robust and lightweight mask encoder from SAM2, processing videos frame-wise to extract high-resolution mask features.
- **Vision Projector:** Projects the global features (Zv) into a format suitable for the LLM (Hv).
- **Region Sampler (gr):** A parameter-free module that extracts localized visual prompt embeddings (Hr) from the Mask Encoder's high-resolution features (Zm) using point-sampling and adaptive pooling.
- **Large Language Model (fϕ):** Receives projected global features (Hv), region features (Hr), and tokenized textual embeddings (Hq). Based on Qwen3-VL, this LLM auto-regressively generates the language response (Yq) and a dedicated latent embedding for the `<SEG>` token.
- **MLLM Projector:** Transforms the LLM's `<SEG>` latent embedding into a prompt token embedding (Zp), acting as a semantic bridge.
- **Mask Decoder (gψ):** Synthesizes the segmentation mask (Ym). Redesigned from X-SAM, it integrates Zp, learnable mask queries (Qm), and temporally refined visual features (Zw). It includes structured Query-to-Image Attention and Token-to-Image Attention modules, with zero-initialization for the latter's parameters to ensure stable integration.
- **Mask Memory Module (gω):** This module is central to maintaining temporal coherence in video segmentation. As detailed in Figure 4, it operates as a temporal cache with a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) strategy.
 - **Memory Attention:** Attends to guided vision features from preceding frames to produce temporally-refined vision features for the current frame.
 - **Mask Decoder:** Generates the current frame's segmentation mask based on these refined features and the LLM-derived segmentation token.
 - **Memory Encoder:** Encodes downsampled vision features and current frame mask logits into guided vision features.
 - **Memory Bank:** Stores these guided vision features for processed frames.

#### 3.3. Training Methodology

The training process for X2SAM consists of two phases:
- **Agnostic Segmentor Training:** This initial phase provides a stable initialization for the mask decoder. The mask encoder remains frozen, and only the mask decoder is optimized using mask-level supervision on the SA-1B dataset. The loss function, `L_mask`, combines binary cross-entropy loss (`L_bce`) and Dice loss (`L_dice`).
- **Unified Joint Training:** X2SAM is co-trained on heterogeneous image and video datasets under a unified optimization framework.
 - **Dimension-shifting pipeline:** Visual input tensors (B×T×H×W×C) are transposed and split into frame-level tensors (B×H×W×C) for frame-wise processing by the mask encoder, with temporal dependencies handled by the mask memory.
 - **Modality-aware batching and gradient accumulation:** A base per-device batch size of 1 is set for video samples, while image batches are scaled by a multiplier. Image batches are updated every step, and video gradients are accumulated over multiple steps to manage memory.
 - **Temporal-aware sampler:** Groups video clips of similar lengths to reduce padding.
 - **Optimization:** Optimizes projectors, LoRA parameters of the LLM, mask encoder, mask decoder, and mask memory.
 - **Joint Objective:** `L_joint` integrates auto-regressive loss (`L_ar`) for language generation, mask loss (`L_mask`) for segmentation, and focal loss (`L_cls`) for mask classification. Dataset-balanced resampling is applied.

#### 3.4. Video Visual Grounded (V-VGD) Segmentation Benchmark

The work introduces the V-VGD segmentation task and constructs two datasets, YT19-VGD and VIPSeg-VGD, from YT-VIS19 and VIPSeg. These datasets extend original annotations into a visual-prompt-driven format, where each object trajectory is paired with four automatically generated visual prompts (point, scribe, box, mask) from its first visible frame, with the full spatio-temporal mask sequence as supervision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of X2SAM encompassed a broad range of image and video segmentation tasks, including generic, open-vocabulary, referring, reasoning, grounded conversation generation, object-centric, and visual grounded segmentation, alongside image and video chat capabilities.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Table 6)

- **Image Segmentation:** X2SAM exhibited competitive performance with image-centric generalist MLLMs like X-SAM. It demonstrated improvements in image open-vocabulary segmentation (I-OV), with its PQ score increasing from 20.9 to 31.2 compared to X-SAM. Performance on generic (I-Gen.) and referring (I-Ref.) segmentation tasks was maintained, and strong results (67.1/65.2 mIoU) were achieved on I-GCG.
- **Video Segmentation:** X2SAM generally surpassed existing MLLM-based video generalists. It showed improved V-Ref. scores on both Ref-YT21 and Ref-DV17 compared to UniPixel-7B and achieved strong V-Gen. performance with 65.1 mIoU. Notably, in video grounded conversation generation (V-GCG), X2SAM improved over VideoGLaMM by 21.5 mIoU (75.8 vs. 54.3).

#### 4.2. Reasoning Segmentation (Table 7)

- **Image Reasoning Segmentation (I-Rea. Seg.):** X2SAM achieved 64.5 cIoU and 71.1 gIoU on validation, outperforming HyperSeg by +7.8 cIoU and +11.9 gIoU. It also established new test results for both short and long queries.
- **Video Reasoning Segmentation (V-Rea. Seg.):** A score of 69.9 J&F was achieved on ReVOS, an improvement of +14.2 points over HyperSeg and surpassing the video-specialist ReferFormer-B. This indicates effective integration of LLM-based reasoning with spatio-temporal mask tracking.

#### 4.3. Out-of-Domain Segmentation (Table 8)

- X2SAM showed generalization capabilities on unseen datasets and novel categories. It improved performance on gRefCOCO for image referring segmentation compared to ReLA, PSALM, and X-SAM. On ADE20K, it achieved 31.2 PQ, 38.2 mIoU, and 20.2 mAP for image open-vocabulary segmentation, outperforming ODISE and X-SAM. In the video domain, X2SAM obtained 60.3 AP on YT-VIS-21 for video open-vocabulary segmentation, exceeding OMG-Seg and HyperSeg.

#### 4.4. Visual Grounded Segmentation (Table 9)

- **Image Visual Grounded Segmentation (I-VGD Seg.):** X2SAM performed comparably to the image-specialized X-SAM model on the COCO benchmark, achieving AP scores of 45.9 and 48.5 for point and box prompts, respectively.
- **Video Visual Grounded Segmentation (V-VGD Seg.):** Significant improvements were observed over SAM2-H (a non-MLLM specialist) under the V-VGD protocol. For box prompts, X2SAM achieved 74.4 AP on YT-VIS19 and 57.8 AP on VIPSeg, substantially higher than SAM2-H's 54.0 AP and 40.4 AP. These gains support the effectiveness of the region sampler and mask memory modules in propagating temporal visual prompts.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Mask Decoder (Table 2):** The Token-to-Image (T2I) attention module with a zero-initialization strategy consistently outperformed the baseline and random initialization, leading to improved image and video task performance.
- **Joint Training (Table 3):** The unified joint training strategy reduced training costs by 36.5% (from approximately 5.2K to 3.3K GPU hours) compared to a simple joint training method, while maintaining competitive image performance and improving video metrics.
- **Mask Memory (Table 4):** Mask guidance in the memory module provided consistent gains across tasks, particularly improving V-Ref. YT21 by +9.7 J&F. Class guidance further enhanced semantic discrimination, and a multi-scale design achieved the best overall performance.
- **Memory Size (Table 5):** An increase in memory size from 1 to 6 generally benefited tasks requiring longer temporal context, such as open-vocabulary and reasoning-oriented video understanding. However, increasing the memory size to 8 frames sometimes introduced redundant cues, leading to decreased performance in some metrics, indicating that a moderate memory size provides a better trade-off. A memory size of 6 frames was adopted as the final configuration.

#### 4.6. Chat Capabilities (Table 20, 21)

- **Image Chat:** X2SAM achieved state-of-the-art performance among segmentation-based MLLMs on most image chat benchmarks, performing comparably to, and in some cases exceeding, chat-specialized models.
- **Video Chat:** As the sole segmentation-based MLLM evaluated, X2SAM demonstrated strong accuracy across various video chat benchmarks, outperforming several chat-centric video MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of X2SAM represents a notable step in the field of multimodal AI by addressing the current fragmentation in segmentation capabilities across images and videos. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Perception Framework:** X2SAM provides a unified framework that extends the "any-segmentation" paradigm from static images to dynamic video sequences. This addresses a critical gap in existing MLLMs, which often specialize in either image-level or pixel-level understanding, or are restricted to a single modality. By integrating both textual and visual prompts, X2SAM offers a more versatile and intuitive interface for diverse segmentation tasks.
- **Enhanced Temporal Coherence:** The introduction of the Mask Memory module is a key innovation for video segmentation. This module enables the model to maintain temporal consistency of segmentation masks across video frames, mitigating the limitations of traditional frame-by-frame processing that can lead to flickering or identity shifts. This capability is crucial for accurately tracking objects and understanding dynamic scenes.
- **New Benchmark for Visual Grounding in Videos:** The proposed Video Visual Grounded (V-VGD) segmentation benchmark provides a standardized protocol for evaluating MLLMs' ability to ground and segment object tracks in videos based on interactive visual prompts. This benchmark can stimulate further research and development in interactive video understanding and object tracking.
- **Efficient Multi-Modal Training:** The adaptive unified joint training strategy allows for efficient co-training of X2SAM on heterogeneous image and video datasets. This approach optimizes resource utilization and reduces computational costs associated with training large models across diverse data types, while maintaining balanced performance across modalities.
- **Broadened Application Scope:** By enabling pixel-level spatio-temporal understanding from complex multi-modal instructions, X2SAM has the potential to impact various application domains:
 - **Content Creation and Editing:** Facilitating precise object selection and manipulation in video editing workflows.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improving environmental perception, object interaction, and navigation in dynamic settings.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Assisting in the segmentation and tracking of anatomical structures or anomalies in medical videos.
 - **Surveillance and Analysis:** Enabling more granular analysis of events and object behaviors in video feeds.
- **Baseline for Future Research:** X2SAM establishes a practical baseline for unified pixel-level spatio-temporal understanding. Its strong performance across a wide array of image and video segmentation tasks, coupled with preserved general chat abilities, can motivate future research into more robust, scalable, and efficient generalist models for dense prediction tasks.

The work also points to future directions, such as exploring more efficient training strategies, integrating lightweight backbones, and developing adaptive long-range memory mechanisms, which could further enhance the model's scalability, robustness, and ability to handle extended video sequences and complex real-world scenarios.
