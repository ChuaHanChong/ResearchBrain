# Let Humanoids Hike! Integrative Skill Development on Complex Trails

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified learning framework enables humanoid robots to autonomously navigate complex hiking trails by integrating visual perception, decision-making, and motor control through hierarchical reinforcement learning and privileged learning techniques, demonstrating emergent motor skills and comparable performance to oracle policies across diverse terrains in simulated environments.

## Problem

- Current humanoid robots struggle to combine high-level navigation with low-level motor control in unstructured environments
- Existing approaches tend to separate locomotion and navigation, limiting adaptability to varying terrain
- Traditional navigation relies on scene mapping or rigid world geometry, and LLMs/VLMs enabling high-level planning lack the physical grounding needed for real-time motor adaptation

## Method

- LEGO-H framework unifies navigation and locomotion through hierarchical reinforcement learning
- ==Temporal Vision Transformer (TC-ViT)== for visual-based navigation combined with privileged learning using Hierarchical Latent Matching
- Real-time adaptation of motor skills based on proprioceptive feedback and terrain conditions

## Results

- Framework achieves comparable or better performance than oracle policies trained with privileged information
- Successfully demonstrates autonomous hiking across diverse simulated trails using different humanoid robot morphologies
- Ablation studies confirm TC-ViT is essential for coordinating navigation and locomotion, while Hierarchical Latent Matching promotes stability and safety through coordinated joint actions

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision is essential for successful navigation - performance degrades significantly without visual input
> - Unified learning approach enables emergence of diverse motor skills (walking, jumping, leaning) adapted to terrain
> - Hierarchical Latent Matching promotes coordinated joint actions aligned with robot's physical structure

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a significant gap in the field of humanoid robotics: the integration of high-level navigation with low-level motor control for complex, unstructured environments. The current research landscape is fragmented, with locomotion research often focusing on isolated motor skills in simplified environments and navigation research overlooking the challenges of real-world embodiment and terrain variability.

The paper directly tackles these limitations by proposing "humanoid hiking" as a compelling testbed for embodied autonomy. This framework builds upon and extends existing research in several key areas:

- **Humanoid Locomotion:** It acknowledges prior work on walking, running, parkour, and climbing, but criticizes the lack of adaptability to varying terrains and the reliance on engineered behaviors or user commands.
- **Humanoid Navigation:** The paper points out the limitations of traditional navigation methods based on scene mapping or rigid world geometry. It also notes that while Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) show promise for high-level planning, they often lack the physical grounding needed for real-time adaptability and fine-grained motor control.
- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL):** This paper advances hierarchical RL. Existing methods often oversimplify environments or restrict low-level control to basic skills like walking, limiting adaptability.
- **Privileged Learning (PL):** This paper proposes a novel approach to Privileged Learning by developing a Hierarchical Latent Matching Metric. Existing distillation approaches supervise global behaviors or per-joint accuracy, often ignoring inter-joint dependencies.

By unifying these research areas within a single learning framework, LEGO-H, the authors aim to bridge the gap between high-level planning and low-level execution, enabling humanoid robots to navigate complex trails autonomously. It positions itself by highlighting that prior works consider subsets of these capabilities, whereas LEGO-H unifies all three. This work is inline with current trends of Embodied AI where grounding AI systems with a physical body improves the perception, decision making, and interactive capabilities of AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learning framework that enables humanoid robots to hike autonomously on complex trails. This overarching goal is driven by the following motivations:

- **Addressing Limitations of Current Humanoid Research:** Current humanoid robots struggle to integrate low-level motor skills with high-level navigation, limiting their ability to operate in real-world environments.
- **Creating a Challenging Testbed for Embodied Autonomy:** Hiking on complex trails demands balance, agility, adaptive decision-making, and the ability to handle unpredictable terrain, making it an ideal testbed for advancing humanoid autonomy.
- **Developing Integrative Skill Development:** The authors aim to create a framework that drives the integrative development of visual perception, decision-making, and motor execution in humanoid robots.
- **Real-World Applications:** Hiking-capable robots could explore remote areas, assist in rescue missions, and guide individuals along rugged paths, highlighting the potential for real-world impact.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel learning framework, LEGO-H, to achieve autonomous humanoid hiking. The key elements of their approach include:

- **Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL):** LEGO-H uses HRL to unify navigation and locomotion. A high-level navigation module anticipates future local goals based on visual cues and global objectives, while a low-level motor skill module executes actions in real-time, adapting to proprioceptive feedback and terrain conditions.
- **Temporal Vision Transformer Variant (TC-ViT):** The navigation module is implemented using TC-ViT, a variant of the Temporal Information Conditioned Vision Transformer. TC-ViT combines tokenization with embodied reinforcement learning to model navigation goals, and temporal-spatial relations, considering the robot’s past, present, and future states for sequential anticipation.
- **Privileged Learning (PL) with Hierarchical Latent Matching (HLM):** A teacher policy is trained with privileged information (e.g., known foothold locations) to develop diverse, optimal behaviors. A student policy then learns to replicate these behaviors using only proprioception and onboard perception. HLM, a novel metric learning approach, is used to distill policy based on action rationality by utilizing structured latent representations and masked reconstruction via VAEs to enforce relational consistency across joints.
- **Simulated Environment:** The research is conducted in a simulated environment using the Isaac Gym simulator. They construct diverse trails in the Nvidia Isaac Gym Simulator using a procedural generation approach.
- **Robots:** Experiments are conducted using two humanoid robots from Unitree, the H1 and the G1, with distinct body scales and mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of LEGO-H through a series of experiments in simulated environments. Their main findings include:

- **LEGO-H enables autonomous humanoid hiking:** The framework successfully integrates visual perception, decision-making, and motor execution, allowing humanoid robots to traverse complex trails autonomously.
- **TC-ViT is essential for basic hiking functionality:** Ablation studies show that TC-ViT plays a crucial role in balancing global goals, physical state, and visual perception, which is crucial for coordination between navigation and locomotion.
- **HLM enhances stability and safety:** The Hierarchical Latent Matching metric promotes coordinated joint actions that align with the robot's physical structure, leading to more stable and collision-free movements.
- **LEGO-H rivals the oracle policy:** In terms of efficiency and safety, LEGO-H performs comparably to or slightly better than an oracle policy trained with privileged information, demonstrating its effectiveness and capacity.
- **Emergent motor skills:** The humanoid robots develop diverse motor skills (e.g., walking, stepping, jumping, leaping, leaning) to adapt to different terrains.
- **Vision is essential:** Performance of the robot is severely degraded without vision. The performance gap between LEGO-H and EP-H across metrics underscores the importance of structured visual information.
- **Unified learning is vital for adaptability:** Without a unified framework and a separated locomotion feedback is not enough for decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of humanoid robotics and has the potential for significant impact:

- **Hiking as a new testbed:** Proposing humanoid hiking as a new, challenging, and relevant testbed for embodied autonomy pushes the boundaries of robot capabilities.
- **Introduction of LEGO-H:** The LEGO-H framework provides a novel and effective approach for integrating navigation and locomotion in humanoid robots. It creates a learning framework for autonomous humanoid hiking.
- **Advancement of HRL and PL:** The authors introduce TC-ViT, a temporal vision transformer variant tailored for HRL, and Hierarchical Latent Matching, enhancing Privileged Learning, to address the limitations of existing methods.
- **Demonstration of robustness and versatility:** The research demonstrates the robustness and versatility of LEGO-H across diverse simulated trails and humanoid morphologies, establishing it as a baseline for future humanoid research.
- **Potential for real-world applications:** The development of hiking-capable robots could have significant impact in areas such as search and rescue, remote exploration, and assistance for individuals with mobility impairments.

The paper opens several avenues for future research, including:

- **Exploring whole-body control:** Extending the framework to incorporate whole-body control could further enhance the robot's agility and adaptability.
- **Investigating long-horizon exploration:** Adapting the framework for kilometer-scale trails, where sustained adaptability and energy efficiency are crucial.
- **Real-world deployment:** Testing and refining the framework in real-world environments to close the sim-to-real gap.

Overall, this research represents a significant step forward in the development of embodied autonomy for humanoid robots, offering a promising approach for enabling robots to navigate and operate effectively in complex, unstructured environments.
