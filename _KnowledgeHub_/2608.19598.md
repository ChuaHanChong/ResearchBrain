# PEA-DPO: Perception-Enhanced Alignment Direct Preference Optimization for MLLMs Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> PEA-DPO fixes visual insensitivity in multimodal DPO by adding a Visual context Preference Optimization term alongside standard response-quality DPO. Rejected images are built via CLIP-guided masking that removes the most critical visual region. Joint training on LLaVA-v1.5 7B/13B cuts hallucination rates 72-92% across MMHalBench, Object HalBench, and AMBER versus base models.

## Problem

- Standard multimodal DPO treats the image as fixed conditioning rather than a preference target, so gradients never push the model to discriminate visual context quality.
- Existing rejected-image construction (blockwise masking, rotation, cropping, blacking out) either retains too much original context or destroys all information, producing weak or degenerate preference signal.
- Prior visual-aware DPO variants like mDPO and V-DPO incorporate some visual signal but lack a theoretical account of how or why visual insensitivity arises and persists after training.

## Method

- Formalizes ==visual insensitivity== via an image-based likelihood ratio and derives two failure modes, ==Across-Image Insensitivity== and ==Within-Image Insensitivity==, as theorems bounding response/image-level preference margins.
- Constructs ==Perception-Enhanced Preference Data== by randomly masking the chosen image n times and selecting the candidate with lowest CLIP embedding similarity to the original as the rejected image.
- Introduces a ==Joint Optimization Objective== combining ==Response-quality Preference Optimization== and ==Visual-context Preference Optimization==, reformulated reference-free with length-normalized log-probabilities, ReLU margins, and target-margin hyperparameters (**mPEA-DPO**).

## Results

- On LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B, mPEA-DPO cuts MMHalBench hallucination rate by **33%**, Object HalBench response/object-level CHAIR by **92%/87%**, and AMBER CHAIR/hallucination/cognition by **76%/72%/86%** versus base models.
- mPEA-DPO wins on **87.5%** of hallucination metrics for the 7B model and **75.0%** for the 13B model against RLHF/RLAIF baselines including RLAIF-V, OPA-DPO, and DAMA.
- Ablation shows both loss terms matter (score drops from **3.02** to **2.44** without response-quality term, to **2.81** without visual-context term) and CLIP-guided masking beats blockwise, rotation, crop, and blackness rejected-image strategies at alpha=0.2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating the image purely as conditioning context in preference optimization silently caps how much a model can be pushed to ground responses in visual evidence.
> - Rejected-sample quality for contrastive alignment hinges on removing the most semantically critical content, not merely perturbing it; naive corruption strategies underperform targeted, similarity-guided removal.
> - Reducing hallucination via stronger visual grounding trades off against response coverage, models get more conservative and may omit ambiguous but true details.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) represents a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, enabling systems to process and reason about both visual and textual information. A critical challenge in deploying these models is aligning their outputs with human preferences and values, particularly in ensuring factual correctness and mitigating "hallucinations" where models generate content ungrounded in visual evidence.

The broader research landscape for MLLM alignment largely builds upon techniques established for text-only Large Language Models (LLMs), with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) being a prominent method. DPO, which simplifies Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) by directly optimizing a policy based on preference pairs without an explicit reward model, has been widely adopted. Existing approaches typically construct multimodal preference datasets by pairing images with preferred and dispreferred textual responses, then apply a DPO-like loss function.

However, the authors identify a gap in these existing multimodal DPO applications. They observe that directly applying DPO often treats the image as a static conditioning factor rather than an integral component for preference evaluation. This can lead to visual insensitivity, where MLLMs generate responses that are weakly grounded in the visual input. Previous work in multimodal DPO (e.g., mDPO, V-DPO) has begun to incorporate visual signals, and research on preference data construction has explored generating rejected images through various transformations (e.g., MFPO, LPOI, OPA-DPO). However, these methods often employ coarse-grained transformations or rely on computationally intensive processes, and do not fully address the specific theoretical manifestations of visual insensitivity identified in this paper.

PEA-DPO situates itself within this context by formalizing the problem of visual insensitivity, decomposing it into "Across-Image Insensitivity" and "Within-Image Insensitivity," and proposing a targeted solution. It extends the DPO framework by explicitly incorporating visual preference signals, derived from carefully constructed "perception-enhanced preference data," into a joint optimization objective. This work attempts to provide both a theoretical foundation for understanding visual insensitivity and a practical methodology to mitigate it, aiming to improve the visual grounding and reduce hallucinations in MLLMs more effectively than previous methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the alignment of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) with human preferences by directly addressing the problem of "visual insensitivity." The authors identify that current methods for multimodal preference optimization, which largely adapt Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) from text-only settings, often treat images merely as contextual input rather than a primary source of preference. This leads to MLLMs generating responses that are not adequately grounded in the visual content.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that MLLMs trained with standard DPO can exhibit a significant limitation: they frequently fail to distinguish between an original image and a modified version where critical visual context has been removed. The paper terms this phenomenon "visual insensitivity." This insensitivity is problematic because it can lead to two specific failure modes, which are formalized and theoretically analyzed:

1. **Across-Image Insensitivity:** This occurs when the model assigns nearly identical preference scores across distinct images, even when these images convey different critical visual information. For example, a model might describe an "outdoor reading activity" and an "outdoor picnic" with very similar responses, failing to capture the discriminative visual cues that differentiate the two scenes. This indicates a lack of ability to differentiate between broadly different visual contexts.
2. **Within-Image Insensitivity:** This manifests when the model fails to discriminate semantically critical visual cues from irrelevant ones within the *same* image. An example is a model confusing a "screwdriver" with a "toilet brush" or "plunger" even when the correct object is clearly visible in the image. This leads to visually ungrounded responses that misinterpret or hallucinate specific details about objects or actions within a single visual context.

The key motivations are thus:
- To formally define and analyze these two manifestations of visual insensitivity in MLLMs.
- To develop a novel alignment framework that explicitly leverages visual preference signals to overcome these identified limitations.
- To enable MLLMs to generate responses that are not only high-quality in terms of language but are also precisely grounded in and sensitive to the visual evidence presented in the input image.
- To mitigate hallucination, a common issue in MLLMs, by enhancing their ability to accurately perceive and utilize visual context.
- To provide a theoretical basis demonstrating that the proposed method can provably address both Across-Image and Within-Image Insensitivity.

By addressing these issues, the authors aim to improve the reliability, factual accuracy, and overall trustworthiness of MLLMs in real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

To address the identified visual insensitivity issues, the authors propose **Perception-Enhanced Alignment DPO (PEA-DPO)**, a framework designed to jointly optimize response quality preferences and visual context preferences. The methodology consists of two main components:

#### 3.1. Construction of Perception-Enhanced Preference Data

This component focuses on generating high-quality "rejected images" (m_l) that specifically lack critical visual context, in contrast to the "chosen images" (m_w, the original images). This process aims to create meaningful visual grounding preference pairs.

1. **Perturbed Image Generation:** For each original image (m_w), a candidate set of *n* perturbed images (M_p = {m_k^p}_{k=1}^n) is created. Each perturbed image (m_p) is generated by applying a random binary mask (P) of a fixed proportion to the original image (m_p = m_w ⊙ (1 - P)). This process ensures that different parts of the visual information are removed in each candidate.
2. **Rejected Image Selection using CLIP Embeddings:** To identify the most impactful removal of visual context, the semantic similarity between the original image (m_w) and each perturbed image (m_k^p) is quantified.
 - CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) embeddings are used: v_w = f_CLIP(m_w) and v_k^p = f_CLIP(m_k^p) for the original and perturbed images, respectively. These embeddings are ℓ2-normalized.
 - The similarity score (s_k) is calculated as the cosine similarity between the embeddings: s_k = cos(v_w, v_k^p).
 - The perturbed image with the *lowest* similarity score (m_l = arg min_{m_k^p ∈ M_p} s_k) is selected as the rejected image. This selection criterion ensures that the rejected image corresponds to a version where the most semantically critical visual context, as perceived by CLIP, has been removed, creating a strong contrast for visual preference learning.

This process results in perception-enhanced preference data in the format (x, m_l, m_w, y_w), where x is the prompt, m_w is the original image, m_l is the context-reduced rejected image, and y_w is the preferred textual response.

#### 3.2. Joint Optimization Objective

PEA-DPO combines two complementary preference learning terms to simultaneously enhance response quality and visual sensitivity.

1. **Response Quality Preference Optimization (RPO):** This is the standard multimodal DPO loss (L_RPO) derived from Equation 3. Given the same text-image input (x, m_w), the model learns to distinguish high-quality responses (y_w) from lower-quality ones (y_l). The term h(x, m, y) = β log (π_θ(y|x,m) / π_ref(y|x,m)) is used to represent the reward.
 L_RPO = -E_D [log σ(h(x, m_w, y_w) - h(x, m_w, y_l))]
2. **Visual Context Preference Optimization (VPO):** This novel term (L_VPO) leverages the perception-enhanced preference data (D_m). Given the same text (x) and the same preferred response (y_w), the model is optimized to prefer the response conditioned on the original, complete visual context (m_w) over the context-reduced image (m_l).
 L_VPO = -E_{D_m} [log σ(h(x, m_w, y_w) - h(x, m_l, y_w))]
3. **PEA-DPO Objective:** The total objective (L_PEA-DPO) is a linear combination of RPO and VPO, controlled by a weighting hyperparameter α.
 L_PEA-DPO = L_RPO + α · L_VPO

#### 3.3. Modified PEA-DPO (mPEA-DPO)

To improve computational efficiency and controllability, the authors introduce three modifications, resulting in mPEA-DPO:
1. **Reference Model Removal:** Log-probability ratios are replaced with length-normalized log-probabilities (h_m(x, m, y) = log π_θ(y|x,m) / |y|), eliminating the need for a reference model.
2. **ReLU Activation:** The sigmoid function is replaced with a ReLU activation, which filters out trivial data points and is intended to prevent overfitting.
3. **Target Margins:** A temperature parameter (β) is removed, and target reward margins {γ_r, γ_m} are introduced for RPO and VPO respectively. These margins enforce a minimum separation between preferred and rejected responses/images.

The modified PEA-DPO loss function (L_mPEA-DPO) is given as:
L_mPEA-DPO = E_D [ReLU[-(h_m(x, m_w, y_w) - h_m(x, m_w, y_l) - γ_r)]] + αE_{D_m} [ReLU[-(h_m(x, m_w, y_w) - h_m(x, m_l, y_w) - γ_m)]]

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Section 5) demonstrating how mPEA-DPO mitigates visual insensitivity. It defines an "image-level margin" (G_m) that quantifies the model's increased confidence in a preferred response when conditioning on the correct image versus a context-reduced one. Similarly, it revisits the "response-level margin" (G_r). The analysis establishes that Across-Image Insensitivity correlates with a small G_m, and Within-Image Insensitivity correlates with a small G_r. The mPEA-DPO objective is explicitly designed to enlarge both G_m (via L_mVPO) and G_r (via L_mRPO), thereby directly addressing the two failure modes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PEA-DPO (specifically mPEA-DPO) was conducted using LLaVA-v1.5 models (7B and 13B parameters) as base MLLMs. Training data included a LLaVA-1.5 preference dataset, augmented with perception-enhanced preference data constructed by masking 9% of images 30 times. The models were fine-tuned for 5,000 steps.

The main findings and results are summarized as follows:

#### 4.1. Main Results (Table 1)

- **Significant Hallucination Reduction:** PEA-DPO consistently and substantially reduced hallucinations across all evaluated benchmarks for both the 7B and 13B models.
 - For the 7B model, it reduced the hallucination rate (Hal.) on MMHalBench by 33%, CHAIR scores (CHAIR_s, CHAIR_i) on Object HalBench by 92% and 87% respectively, and CHAIR, hallucination rate, and human cognition (Cog.) on AMBER by 76%, 72%, and 86% respectively, compared to the base LLaVA-v1.5-7B. Similar improvements were observed for the 13B model.
- **Outperformance Against Baselines:** PEA-DPO demonstrated competitive or superior performance compared to a wide range of existing MLLM alignment methods (including hallucination-specific, RLHF/RLAIF-based approaches like mDPO, OPA-DPO, DAMA) and strong commercial models (GPT-4V, Gemini 2.5-Pro).
 - For LLaVA-v1.5-13B, mPEA-DPO achieved the best performance on 75.0% of the hallucination metrics. For LLaVA-v1.5-7B, this increased to 87.5%.
 - Specifically, mPEA-DPO (7B) achieved a MMHalBench Score of 3.02 (compared to GPT-4V's 3.49 and Gemini 2.5-Pro's 3.55) with a hallucination rate of 0.36 (compared to GPT-4V's 0.28 and Gemini 2.5-Pro's 0.28). While proprietary models still show strong performance, mPEA-DPO significantly closed the gap for open-source models.
- **Trade-off in Coverage:** The enhancements in hallucination reduction led to a slight compromise in object coverage metrics (e.g., AMBER Cover. for 7B: 46.7% vs. LLaVA-v1.5-7B's 51.0%). This suggests that the model adopts a more conservative generation strategy, prioritizing factual correctness over potentially ambiguous details.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Component Combination (Table 2):**
 - Both Response quality Preference Optimization (mRPO) and Visual context Preference Optimization (mVPO) individually contributed to performance improvements over the base model on MMHalBench and AMBER.
 - The combination of both components in mPEA-DPO yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating their synergistic effect in enhancing alignment and reducing hallucinations.
- **Impact of Visual Context Preference Optimization Weight (α) (Figure 4):**
 - The experiments explored different values for the weighting hyperparameter α. The optimal performance across various metrics (MMHal Score, Hal. and AMBER C., Cog.) was observed when α was set to 0.2.
- **Impact of Rejected Image Construction Strategy (Table 3, Figure 5):**
 - The proposed CLIP-based strategy for generating rejected images (by removing critical visual context) significantly outperformed other generic strategies (Blockwise, Rotation, Crop, Blackness).
 - Strategies that retained significant visual context (Blockwise, Rotation, Crop) or completely eliminated all information (Blackness) resulted in poorer optimization, highlighting the importance of selectively removing *key* visual context for effective visual preference learning.

#### 4.3. Impact of Data Scale (Figure 6)

- mPEA-DPO demonstrated effectiveness even with a relatively small training dataset (2,800 instances), outperforming the baseline.
- Performance consistently improved with increasing data scale, indicating scalability and significant performance gains with more data.

#### 4.4. Attribution of Hallucination Reduction (Table 4)

- To address concerns that hallucination reduction might simply be due to shorter, less informative responses, a comparison on Object HalBench was conducted, including Recall.
- mPEA-DPO achieved superior CHAIR metrics (CHAIRs 4.3, CHAIRi 3.2) while maintaining recall performance (52.30%) comparable to or better than strong baselines like DAMA (Recall 54.50) and OPA-DPO (Recall 43.29). This indicates that the reduction in hallucination is genuinely due to improved visual grounding rather than a trivial reduction in response length.

#### 4.5. Analysis of General Capability (Table 5)

- Evaluation on general capability benchmarks (MMStar, AI2D, LLaVA-Bench, MMMU) showed that mPEA-DPO not only reduced hallucinations but also preserved and even slightly improved the base model's general capabilities (e.g., MMStar: 32.8 vs 30.3; LLaVA-Bench: 86.5 vs 82.3), without significant degradation on MMMU. This suggests that the visual preference optimization strategy enhances the model's instruction-following ability without negatively impacting other core functionalities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PEA-DPO framework presents a significant advancement in the alignment of multimodal large language models, offering both theoretical insights and practical solutions to critical limitations.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Formalization of Visual Insensitivity:** The paper's primary contribution is the formal identification and decomposition of "visual insensitivity" into "Across-Image Insensitivity" and "Within-Image Insensitivity." This provides a structured understanding of how MLLMs fail to adequately utilize visual context in preference optimization.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical analysis connecting these two forms of insensitivity to specific margins (image-level and response-level) and demonstrating how the PEA-DPO objective directly addresses them provides a strong foundation for the proposed method.
- **Effective Mitigation of Hallucinations:** PEA-DPO substantially reduces hallucinations across various benchmarks and model scales. This directly tackles one of the most pressing issues in MLLMs, enhancing their trustworthiness and reliability.
- **Novel Data Construction Strategy:** The CLIP-based method for generating "perception-enhanced preference data" by selectively removing *critical* visual context is a novel and effective approach. It moves beyond generic data augmentation to create highly informative preference pairs that specifically target visual grounding.
- **Preservation of General Capabilities:** Unlike some alignment methods that may compromise a model's broader abilities, PEA-DPO maintains or even slightly improves the general instruction-following capabilities of the base MLLM, indicating a well-balanced optimization strategy.
- **Competitive Performance with Proprietary Models:** PEA-DPO enables open-source LLaVA models to achieve performance levels competitive with, and in some metrics surpass, strong commercial models like GPT-4V and Gemini 2.5-Pro in multimodal alignment, particularly concerning hallucination reduction.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Trustworthy AI Systems:** By significantly reducing hallucinations, PEA-DPO can lead to more reliable and factually accurate MLLMs, which is crucial for applications where errors or misinformation derived from visual misinterpretations could have serious consequences (e.g., medical diagnostics, autonomous driving, legal analysis of visual evidence).
- **Improved User Experience:** Users of MLLMs will benefit from models that are better grounded in the visual input, providing more precise, relevant, and believable responses, thereby enhancing the overall interaction quality.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The formalization of visual insensitivity and the proposed solution provide a clear direction for future research in multimodal alignment. It highlights the importance of explicitly modeling visual preference signals and developing more sophisticated methods for generating visual preference data.
- **Broader Adoption of MLLMs:** Increased reliability and reduced hallucination can accelerate the adoption of MLLMs in various industries and research fields, expanding their utility in tasks requiring robust visual-textual understanding and reasoning.
- **Advancements in Alignment Techniques:** The modifications to DPO, particularly the integration of visual context preferences and the mPEA-DPO objective, could inspire new approaches for preference optimization in other complex, multi-modal, or multi-faceted AI systems.

While the method involves some computational overhead for data construction and might lead to a slightly conservative generation strategy (as indicated by the coverage trade-off), its fundamental contribution to making MLLMs more visually sensitive and less prone to hallucination is substantial. These limitations also present clear avenues for future research to further refine the efficiency and balance of the approach.
