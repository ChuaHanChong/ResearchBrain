# VisuLogic: A Benchmark for Evaluating Visual Reasoning in Multi-modal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark called VisuLogic evaluates true visual reasoning capabilities in multimodal language models through carefully designed tasks that prevent language-based shortcuts, revealing that current MLLMs achieve only 30% accuracy compared to 51.4% human performance while demonstrating how reinforcement learning can help bridge this gap.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks allow MLLMs to solve visual tasks using language shortcuts rather than true visual reasoning
- Current evaluation metrics overestimate MLLMs' visual reasoning abilities by conflating them with basic perception or language-based solutions

## Method

- Created ==VisuLogic== benchmark with tasks that inherently require ==vision-centric reasoning== and cannot be solved through text alone
- Implemented ==reinforcement learning fine-tuning== approach to improve visual reasoning capabilities

## Results

- Current MLLMs achieve only **30%** accuracy on VisuLogic compared to **51.4%** human performance
- Models struggle most with spatial reasoning (LLMs) and stylistic reasoning (MLLMs)
- Reinforcement learning fine-tuning significantly improves performance by enabling iterative hypothesis revision
- Hint prompts boost performance but models still struggle to construct coherent reasoning chains

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Most MLLMs perform only slightly above random chance (25%) on genuine visual reasoning tasks
> - Text-only LLMs with detailed image descriptions barely exceed random baseline, confirming the necessity of true visual reasoning
> - Chain-of-thought prompting shows minimal benefit for visual reasoning tasks

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Context:** Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have seen rapid progress in recent years, demonstrating impressive capabilities in understanding and generating content across different modalities (text, image, audio, etc.). A crucial aspect of intelligence is reasoning, which includes visual reasoning for MLLMs.
- **Problem Addressed:** The paper directly addresses the limitations of existing benchmarks in evaluating the genuine visual reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. Current benchmarks often:
 - Rely on tasks that can be solved using language-based shortcuts, without truly requiring vision-centric reasoning.
 - Conflate visual reasoning with object localization or simple perceptual tasks.
 - Allow MLLMs to translate visual information into text and then rely on standard language reasoning, leading to an overestimation of visual reasoning abilities.
- **Contribution:** This research contributes by:
 - Introducing a novel benchmark, VisuLogic, specifically designed to evaluate visual reasoning abilities in MLLMs.
 - Focusing on tasks that are inherently difficult to articulate using language, forcing models to engage in genuine vision-centric reasoning.
 - Providing a rigorous evaluation and analysis of leading MLLMs on the benchmark, revealing significant gaps in their visual reasoning capabilities.
 - Offering a supplementary training dataset and reinforcement learning baselines to encourage further research in this area.
- **Related Work:** The paper clearly positions itself within the context of existing research on MLLMs and multimodal benchmarks. It acknowledges the progress made in MLLM development and the evolution of benchmarks, while highlighting the specific deficiencies that VisuLogic aims to address. It also cites relevant work on reinforcement learning for enhancing reasoning capabilities in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The primary objective of this research is to create a benchmark that can accurately and reliably evaluate the visual reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.
- **Motivation:** The motivation stems from the understanding that:
 - Visual reasoning is a core component of human intelligence and a critical capability for advanced multimodal models.
 - Current MLLMs often fall short of human-level visual reasoning, and this deficiency is not accurately reflected in existing evaluation metrics.
 - Developing more robust visual reasoning capabilities is essential for building truly intelligent and versatile AI systems.
 - Lack of current benchmarks allows for language-based "shortcuts" leading to inflated results that do not represent the true visual reasoning ability of the model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **VisuLogic Benchmark Construction:**
 - **Data Collection:** Sourcing questions from publicly available online resources, adhering to licensing regulations.
 - **Data Cleaning:** Removing noise, irrelevant content, and extraneous HTML markup.
 - **Data Structuring:** Standardizing text and images in JSON Lines format.
 - **Quality Control:**
 - Image Verification: Ensuring image existence and correct formatting.
 - Duplicate Removal: Eliminating redundant entries based on text and image similarity (using perceptual hashing).
 - Manual Inspection: Thorough human review to confirm validity and reliability.
 - **Data Taxonomy:** Categorizing questions into six primary classes based on the reasoning skills required: Quantitative Reasoning, Spatial Reasoning, Positional Reasoning, Attribute Reasoning, Stylistic Reasoning, and Other.
- **Evaluation Protocol:**
 - Establishing reference performance levels with human participants and random selection baselines.
 - Evaluating a range of open-source and closed-source LLMs and MLLMs.
 - Using detailed descriptions of image generated by GPT-4o for LLM evaluation to test the necessity of vision data for visual reasoning tasks.
 - Employing different prompting paradigms: non-CoT, CoT, and hint prompts.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Implementing a reinforcement learning fine-tuning step on a supplementary training dataset to improve visual reasoning capabilities.
 - Applying REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) algorithm, a model-free algorithm, to maintain robustness to noise and KL constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Low MLLM Performance:** Most MLLMs score below 30% accuracy on VisuLogic, only slightly above the random baseline (25%) and far below human performance (51.4%).
- **Ineffectiveness of Textual Reasoning:** Text-only LLMs supplied with detailed descriptions of images barely exceed the random chance baseline, demonstrating the insufficiency of textual reasoning alone.
- **Limited Impact of CoT Prompting:** Chain-of-thought prompting yields minimal improvements in visual reasoning, suggesting a need for CoT techniques tailored to multimodal data.
- **Potential of Hint Prompts:** Hint prompts can boost model performance, but models still struggle to construct coherent reasoning chains.
- **Benefits of Reinforcement Learning:** Reinforcement learning fine-tuning significantly improves visual reasoning performance, outperforming both open-source and closed-source counterparts. Models are able to capture state transitions and revise intermediate hyptheses iteratively.
- **Error Distribution Analysis:** LLMs struggle most with Spatial Reasoning, while MLLMs struggle most with Stylistic Reasoning. Human error patterns differ from both LLMs and MLLMs.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** LLMs often omit critical visual details, while MLLMs struggle to infer evolving relationships among shapes. Reinforcement learning encourages deeper, stepwise logical reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Significance:**
 - VisuLogic provides a more rigorous and realistic evaluation of MLLMs' visual reasoning abilities, addressing the limitations of existing benchmarks.
 - The findings reveal a significant gap between current MLLM performance and human-level visual reasoning, highlighting the need for further research in this area.
 - The research demonstrates the promise of reinforcement learning for enhancing MLLMs' visual reasoning capabilities.
- **Potential Impact:**
 - VisuLogic can serve as a valuable tool for guiding future research and development efforts in MLLMs, promoting the creation of more robust and capable models.
 - The supplementary training dataset and reinforcement learning baselines can accelerate progress in visual reasoning research.
 - Improved visual reasoning capabilities in MLLMs can have a wide range of applications in areas such as robotics, autonomous driving, medical image analysis, and assistive technology.
 - The research can encourage the development of more human-like AI systems that can effectively perceive, reason, and interact with the visual world.
 - The development of reliable, vision-centric reasoning, could lead to downstream applications such as reducing the dependence on textual descriptions and potentially reduce language bias.
