# HydroShear: Hydroelastic Shear Simulation for Tactile Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> HydroShear presents a computationally efficient, physics-based method for simulating tactile shear, capturing complex phenomena like stick-slip transitions and path-dependent force buildup for vision-based sensors. Policies trained with HydroShear achieve an average 93% zero-shot sim-to-real success rate across diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming existing tactile simulation methods.

## Problem

- Existing tactile simulators for vision-based sensors often lack dynamic fidelity in modeling tactile shear, leading to a substantial sim-to-real gap for contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- High-fidelity simulation methods are computationally prohibitive for scalable reinforcement learning, while faster approximations oversimplify elastomer behavior, failing to capture path-dependent deformation or stick-slip transitions.
- Current fast methods are often limited in representing full 6-DoF object-sensor interactions, restricting the realism and applicability of simulated tactile data for general dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- **HydroShear** models tactile shear fields by decomposing marker displacement into dilation and shear components, tracking object on-surface points in the elastomer frame.
- It employs ==recursive force tracking== that accounts for viscoelasticity, enforces physical constraints like non-negative normal forces and Coulomb friction, and uses ==Signed Distance Functions (SDFs)== to track object motion history.
- The method uses a sequential ==system identification== procedure to calibrate its four physical parameters (λ_d, λ_s, K, μ) from real-world data, enabling accurate simulation of diverse shear modes.

## Results

- HydroShear consistently outperformed baselines (TacSL, FOTS) in tactile shear simulation accuracy across all tested shear types, achieving lower RMSE and higher cosine similarity (e.g., **1.000 px** RMSE for dilation, **1.576 px** RMSE for rolling shear).
- Policies trained with HydroShear achieved superior zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, demonstrating an average **93%** success rate across four challenging manipulation tasks (e.g., **29/30** for Bin Packing, **30/30** for Drawer Pulling).
- The simulation method is computationally efficient enough for scalable RL training, being significantly faster than original FOTS implementations, with further potential for optimization in batched SDF calculations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately simulating path-dependent force and shear buildup, along with stick-slip transitions, is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap in tactile-driven contact-rich robotic manipulation.
> - A physics-based approach that combines recursive force tracking on indenter surface points with SDFs can efficiently model complex SE(3) interactions, capturing viscoelastic properties and tactile shadowing.
> - Utilizing Asymmetric Actor-Critic Distillation with a contact penalty curriculum during training enables robust and safe zero-shot transfer of tactile reinforcement learning policies to real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge in robot learning: the sim-to-real transfer of reinforcement learning (RL) policies for contact-rich manipulation tasks. While sim-to-real paradigms have advanced robot locomotion, their application to dexterous manipulation, particularly involving tactile sensing, has been limited. This limitation primarily stems from discrepancies between simulated contact dynamics and the high-fidelity tactile feedback observed in real-world interactions.

Existing tactile simulation methods often exhibit a dichotomy: visual fidelity for vision-based tactile sensors has progressed, but dynamic fidelity, especially concerning tactile shear, remains a bottleneck. Current approaches for simulating vision-based tactile sensor images, such as those that focus on RGB rendering quality, typically provide overly simplistic models of force and shear. Consequently, these models introduce a substantial sim-to-real gap for tasks requiring precise contact and force reasoning.

More accurate methods, like Finite Element Methods (FEM), are computationally intensive, rendering them impractical for the iterative training demands of scalable reinforcement learning. Faster, penalty-based approximations often simplify shear modeling, focusing on instantaneous penetration and velocity. These models typically fail to capture complex, path-dependent phenomena such as the viscoelastic deformation of elastomers, stick-slip transitions, or tactile shadowing effects. Furthermore, certain marker-based simulation methods, such as FOTS, have been limited to tracking object motion in SE(2), which is insufficient for representing the full SE(3) complexity involved in dexterous manipulation, including rolling motions.

HydroShear positions itself within this landscape by introducing a method designed to overcome these limitations. It aims to provide a computationally efficient, high-fidelity tactile shear simulation that accurately models critical physical phenomena (stick-slip, path-dependent force/shear buildup, full SE(3) interactions), thereby bridging the reality gap and enabling robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of RL policies for a wider range of contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reliable zero-shot sim-to-real policy transfer for contact-rich manipulation tasks that rely on tactile feedback. The authors identify the accurate simulation of tactile shear as the critical bottleneck preventing this transfer.

The motivation for developing HydroShear stems from several identified deficiencies in the state of the art:

- **Inadequate Shear Modeling in Current Simulators**: Existing tactile simulators for vision-based sensors primarily focus on rendering realistic RGB images, which capture contact geometry but largely fail to accurately model tactile shear forces. Tactile shear, which represents the tangential forces between the sensor and an object, is crucial for tasks requiring fine manipulation, slippage detection, and precise force control.
- **Computational Expense of High-Fidelity Methods**: While methods like FEM can accurately simulate complex tactile interactions, their computational cost makes them prohibitive for large-scale reinforcement learning, where millions of simulation steps are often required.
- **Simplistic Nature of Fast Approximation Methods**: Faster, penalty-based or other approximate physical models often oversimplify elastomer behavior. They typically do not account for path-dependent deformation (hysteresis), stick-slip transitions, or the complex force buildup that characterizes real-world tactile interactions. These simplifications lead to a significant discrepancy between simulated and real tactile feedback, resulting in poor sim-to-real transfer.
- **Limited Representation of Object-Sensor Interactions**: Some existing fast methods are limited in the types of object motion they can model (e.g., FOTS is restricted to SE(2)), precluding the simulation of full 6-DoF (SE(3)) interactions like rolling. This limitation restricts the realism and applicability of simulated tactile data for general manipulation.
- **Need for Physics-Based, Computationally Efficient Shear Simulation**: The authors seek to develop a physics-based model that can generate realistic force fields from arbitrary watertight geometries, while remaining computationally efficient enough for RL training and being agnostic to the underlying physics engine.

In response to these motivations, HydroShear was developed with the following specific objectives:
- To accurately model stick-slip transitions in tactile contact.
- To simulate path-dependent force and shear buildup, capturing the viscoelastic properties of sensor elastomers.
- To enable the simulation of full SE(3) object-sensor interactions, including translation and rotation in all axes.
- To provide a computationally efficient framework suitable for training reinforcement learning policies at scale.
- To leverage Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) for representing complex geometries and tracking object motion history across the sensor membrane.
- To demonstrate that policies trained with HydroShear can achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across a diverse set of contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming existing tactile simulation methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HydroShear's methodology focuses on simulating tactile shear fields by modeling the physical interaction between an indenter object and the soft elastomer of a tactile sensor. The core idea is to track the displacements of indenter on-surface points as they interact with the sensor membrane. The total marker displacement field (M_t) is decomposed into a dilation field (M_d_t) and a shear field (M_s_t). The model operates on a grid of N tactile query points (markers) on the elastomer membrane {p_i} and aims to predict their associated 2D tactile shear vectors {s_i} = (d_x, d_y).

**A. Dilation (M_d_t)**
Dilation models the displacement of markers along the sensor normal, primarily due to the indenter's penetration into the elastomer.
- **Contact Detection**: A tactile grid point p_i is considered in contact if its signed distance function value with respect to the indenter, ϕ_I(p_i), is negative.
- **Field Aggregation**: The dilation vector field is computed by summing the influence of all in-contact tactile grid points. Each point's contribution is proportional to its penetration depth (−ϕ_I(p_c_i)) and weighted by an exponential decay factor, exp{−λ_d * ||v_c_i||^2}. This factor causes the effect to dissipate with distance from the contact point, thereby modeling tactile shadowing effects observed in real elastomers.

**B. Shear (M_s_t)**
The shear field is modeled using an extension of hydroelastic contact principles, which tracks forceful interactions and object motion history.
- **Indenter Surface Points**: The method tracks contact forces on a set of M indenter on-surface points {o_j,t} in the elastomer frame. These points' coordinates are derived by transforming local indenter surface points (¯o_j) using the indenter's pose (E_X_I_t).
- **Recursive Force Tracking**: Contact forces {f_j,t} are recursively tracked over time, considering the indenter's pose history (E_X_I_t, E_X_I_t-1). This formulation accounts for the viscoelasticity of the elastomer. The base case assumes no contact at t=0, so f_j,0 = 0.
- **In-Contact Displacement Calculation**: To update f_j,t, the displacement of indenter surface points (o_j,t - o_j,t-1) is calculated. Only the portion of this displacement that is in contact with the elastomer (d_j,t) is considered. This "in-contact fraction" (α_j,t) is determined using the elastomer's signed distance function (ϕ_E), specifically by the ReLU of SDF differences, allowing for precise tracking during full contact, no contact, or partial entry/exit.
- **Force Conversion**: The in-contact displacement d_j,t is decomposed into normal (d_n_j,t) and tangential (d_xy_j,t) components. These components are then scaled by the elastomer's viscoelastic stiffness parameters (E for normal, K for tangential) and the surface area (A_j) associated with each indenter point to calculate raw forces (f_n_j,t, f_xy_j,t).
- **Physical Constraints**:
 - Normal forces are lower-bounded by zero using a ReLU function (¯f_n_j,t), preventing "pulling" forces.
 - Coulomb friction is enforced on tangential forces (¯f_xy_j,t) by clipping their magnitude based on the normal force and a friction coefficient (μ).
 - Contact forces are reset to zero if the indenter surface point loses contact with the elastomer (Heaviside function).
- **Projection Displacement Tracker**: A separate, but related, tracker (f_j,t) is maintained. This tracker is conceptualized as tracking the displacements of projected indenter points on the elastomer surface (o_j,t = o_j,t + f_j,t) and is used to model slippage. It uses the same recursive force function F but with stiffness parameters E, K, A_j set to 1, and a coefficient μ_hat for slippage.
- **Simplification for Shear Field**: A key simplification is introduced: if E = K, A_j = A (uniform area), and μ = μ_hat, the actual contact forces (f_j,t) can be recovered by simply scaling the projected displacements (f_j,t) by KA.
- **Shear Field Computation**: The final shear vector field (M_s_t) is computed by aggregating the effects of each constrained contact force (f_j,t) at its corresponding projected indenter surface point (o_j,t). Similar to dilation, this aggregation is weighted by penetration depth (−ϕ_E(o_k_i,t)) and an exponential decay factor (exp{−λ_s * ||v_k_i||^2}) to account for tactile shadowing.
- **Model Parameters**: The complete HydroShear model requires 4 parameters: λ_d, λ_s, K, and μ.

**C. Calibration**
A sequential system identification procedure is used to calibrate the 4 model parameters using real-world data, simplifying optimization.
- **λ_d Calibration**: Real-world data (Y_d_i) is collected by vertically pressing the indenter into the elastomer at various positions. λ_d is optimized via least-squares to minimize the difference between simulated and real dilation.
- **λ_s and K Calibration**: Real-world data (Y_s_i) is collected by applying combined normal and tangential motions. Y_s_i is rescaled, and λ_s is optimized first, followed by K without rescaling, both using least-squares against the full model with previously calibrated parameters.
- **μ Calibration**: Real-world data (Y_μ_i) is collected during controlled slip motions. μ is then optimized via least-squares to match the simulated and real shear fields.

**D. Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning**
- **RL Framework**: Contact-rich manipulation tasks are formulated as infinite-horizon discrete-time Partially-Observable Markov Decision Processes.
- **Training Platform**: Policies are trained entirely in Isaac Gym, leveraging GPU-parallelized environments, using the PPO algorithm.
- **Teacher-Student Distillation**: An Asymmetric Actor-Critic Distillation (AACD) approach is employed:
 - **Teacher Training**: A teacher actor-critic is initially trained with access to privileged state information (e.g., ground-truth contact forces, object poses). A contact penalty curriculum is used: initial training without penalties for efficient task success, followed by finetuning with contact penalties to promote safer, less aggressive policies that make controlled contact and avoid sensor damage.
 - **Student Training**: A student actor is trained from scratch using the finetuned teacher's critic. The student policy observes only proprioceptive states (end-effector pose), goal pose, and high-dimensional tactile feedback from HydroShear (no privileged information).
- **Zero-Shot Deployment**: The trained student actor is deployed directly to the real robot without any modifications or fine-tuning.
- **Gravity Effect Augmentation**: For displacement-based shear algorithms (including HydroShear and FOTS), an augmentation technique is implemented in simulation to account for the real-world effect of object gravity deforming the elastomer while grasped, which would otherwise not be captured.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HydroShear was conducted across two main axes: its accuracy in simulating diverse modes of real-world tactile shear and its efficacy in enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of RL policies.

**A. Calibration and Real-world Shear Evaluation**

- **Setup**: Calibration and evaluation utilized a 7-DoF KUKA MED LBR robot equipped with a 35-mm spherical indenter and a GelSight Mini tactile sensor. A digital twin environment replicated the real-world setup for baseline comparisons.
- **Calibration**: The model parameters (λ_d, λ_s, K, μ) were calibrated sequentially using a controlled dataset of 30 real-world samples covering dilation, combined normal/tangential shearing, and slip motions.
- **Baselines**: HydroShear's simulation accuracy was compared against two established tactile shear simulation methods: TacSL (a penalty-based force field model) and FOTS (a marker-based model tracking SE(2) motion). Each baseline was calibrated using its original procedure on the same dataset.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance was assessed using per-taxel Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and mean cosine similarity (CS) between predicted and ground-truth shear vector fields over a test set of 40 samples (10 each for dilation, translational shear, torsional shear, and rolling shear).
- **Results (Table I)**:
 - **HydroShear** consistently demonstrated superior performance across all shear types, achieving the lowest RMSE and highest CS values. For instance, HydroShear's RMSE for dilation was 1.000 px (CS: 0.976), compared to FOTS at 1.333 px (CS: 0.976) and TacSL at 2.187 px (CS: 0.323). For rolling shear, HydroShear had an RMSE of 1.576 px (CS: 0.904), significantly better than FOTS at 2.841 px (CS: 0.561) and TacSL at 3.861 px (CS: 0.137).
 - **TacSL** exhibited the highest errors, which the authors attributed to its lack of explicit tactile shadowing effects in its formulation.
 - **FOTS** performed comparably to HydroShear for purely in-plane object motions where SE(2) assumptions were more valid. However, its accuracy notably decreased under tilt motions and other out-of-plane SE(3) interactions, which are not directly modeled in its formulation.
 - **Qualitative Analysis**: The authors note that HydroShear's use of object SDFs and tracking of on-surface contact points enabled a more precise simulation of tactile shear, particularly for complex contact geometries and interactions.

**B. Zero-shot Sim-to-Real RL**

- **Real-world Setup**: Experiments were conducted on a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot equipped with GelSight Mini tactile sensors on its gripper.
- **Tasks**: Four contact-rich manipulation tasks were used:
 1. **Peg Insertion**: Insertion of a cylindrical peg with in-hand pose uncertainty into a socket.
 2. **Bin Packing**: Inserting a block into a bin crowded with other objects, requiring multi-object interaction.
 3. **Book Shelving**: Lateral insertion of a book into a shelf, involving full contact patches and gravity orthogonal to insertion.
 4. **Drawer Pulling**: Fine gripper control and reaction to slippage under external force perturbations.
- **RL Policy Baselines**: Five distinct RL policies were trained and evaluated:
 1. TacSL Gray: Image-based policy using grayscale tactile images.
 2. TacSL Shear: Shear-based policy using normalized per-taxel shear vectors.
 3. Original FOTS: Shear-based policy using the original FOTS formulation (reimplemented for parallelization).
 4. FOTS (Reimpl.): An improved, parallelized FOTS variant that re-centers shear around the initial contact patch.
 5. HydroShear: Shear-based policy trained with HydroShear.
- **Training Protocol**: All student policies for each task were trained using Asymmetric Actor-Critic Distillation (AACD), leveraging the same teacher critic checkpoint that was finetuned with a contact penalty curriculum.
- **Results (Table II)**:
 - **HydroShear policies** demonstrated superior zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average success rate of 93% (112 out of 120 trials across all tasks). It achieved the highest success rates for Peg Insertion (25/30), Bin Packing (29/30), Book Shelving (28/30), and perfect success for Drawer Pulling (30/30).
 - **TacSL Gray** performed moderately on Peg Insertion (16/30) and Bin Packing (16/30) but exhibited poor transfer on Book Shelving (6/30) and Drawer Pulling (3/30), indicating limitations for tasks requiring explicit shear direction, accumulation, or slip cues.
 - **TacSL Shear** performed strongly on Peg Insertion (19/30), Book Shelving (23/30), and Drawer Pulling (24/30), but significantly degraded on Bin Packing (4/30). This was attributed to its per-taxel shear normalization, which discards crucial magnitude and spatial structure information, especially detrimental for tasks involving large shear and torsional interactions.
 - **Original FOTS** policies performed poorly on Peg Insertion (1/30) and Bin Packing (5/30), with moderate performance on Book Shelving (20/30) and Drawer Pulling (15/30). Its performance was inconsistent, dependent on the alignment between the object frame center and the actual contact patch.
 - **FOTS (Reimpl.)** significantly improved performance for tasks with stable contacts (Peg: 20/30, Bin: 24/30, Book: 26/30) due to its re-centering of shear. However, its performance deteriorated for Drawer Pulling (3/30) where the assumption of a single, well-defined contact patch broke down under frequent contact transitions or slip.
 - **Ablation on Contact Penalty**: The authors found that finetuning the teacher policies with a contact penalty curriculum was crucial. This curriculum led to safer student policies that made controlled contact and exhibited emergent goal-searching behaviors, preventing aggressive interactions that could damage real sensors.

**C. Speed Comparison (Table X)**

- A comparison of computational speed between FOTS, FOTS (Reimpl.), and HydroShear was conducted by measuring the average compute time for tactile shear field calculation during exemplar rollouts in the Peg Insertion task across varying numbers of parallel environments (256, 512, 1024).
- **FOTS (Reimpl.)** demonstrated the fastest and most consistent performance (mean 11-12 ms across batch sizes), attributed to its optimized batched operations.
- **HydroShear** was significantly faster than the original FOTS but slower than FOTS (Reimpl.) (mean 33-103 ms). The increase in compute time with more environments was linked to bottlenecks in its SDF calculation, despite batching.
- **Original FOTS** was the slowest (mean 122-446 ms) due to a lack of GPU-parallelized batched operations in its original implementation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HydroShear represents a notable advancement in tactile simulation for robotic manipulation, offering several significant implications:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Sensing**: The primary significance of HydroShear lies in its demonstrated ability to bridge the sim-to-real gap for tactile feedback. By accurately modeling complex physical phenomena such as stick-slip transitions, path-dependent force and shear buildup, and full SE(3) object-sensor interactions, it provides a more faithful representation of real-world tactile events than previous methods. This fidelity is critical for developing robotic policies that can operate effectively in physical environments after being trained solely in simulation.
- **Enabling Complex Contact-Rich Manipulation**: The success of HydroShear in enabling zero-shot policy transfer across diverse and challenging contact-rich tasks (peg insertion, bin packing, book shelving, drawer pulling) highlights its potential to unlock new capabilities in robotic manipulation. These tasks require precise reasoning over extrinsic contacts, slippage, and fine force modulation, areas where accurate tactile shear feedback is indispensable.
- **Robustness and Generalization of RL Policies**: Policies trained with HydroShear exhibit enhanced robustness and generalization. The model's capacity to capture the nuances of tactile dynamics, including hysteresis and varied contact geometries, allows RL agents to develop more adaptable behaviors, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection and iterative policy refinement.
- **Computational Efficiency for Scalable RL**: Despite its increased fidelity, HydroShear is designed to be computationally efficient and GPU-parallelizable. This allows for large-scale reinforcement learning training, a prerequisite for developing complex skills, without incurring the prohibitive computational costs associated with high-fidelity methods like FEM. Further improvements in batched SDF algorithms could enhance this efficiency even more.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterous Manipulation**: HydroShear provides a robust foundation for future research in tactile-driven dexterous manipulation. By enabling more realistic simulation of tactile interactions, it can accelerate the development of advanced robotic skills that rely heavily on haptic feedback, potentially leading to robots capable of performing more intricate and adaptive tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Advancing Simulator Development**: The methodology contributes to the broader field of physics-based simulation by extending hydroelastic contact models with non-holonomic properties and robust tracking mechanisms, particularly for full SE(3) object geometries.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point towards future research directions:
- The method's effectiveness relies on the underlying physics engine supporting penetrating contact simulation, such as Isaac Gym's Kelvin-Voigt model. Integrating with other physics engines that natively support hydroelastic contacts could be a future extension.
- The use of SDFs for object and elastomer representation means that the accuracy is tied to SDF resolution, impacting computational speed. Exploring alternative representations for deformable objects could be beneficial.
- The current model assumes a flat elastomer surface, which could be extended to curved elastomers for dexterous hand fingertips.
- The work focuses on vision-based tactile sensors that utilize deformation cues; while extensions to other tactile sensor types are possible, they are outside the scope of this study.
