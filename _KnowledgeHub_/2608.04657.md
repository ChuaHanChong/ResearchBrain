# MobileWAM: Bridging World Action Models to Mobile Manipulation with Chain-of-Foresight

> [!summary] Summary
> MobileWAM introduces a mixture-of-transformers architecture that adapts video-generation World Action Models for whole-body mobile manipulation. It integrates a Chain-of-Foresight module for dense temporal supervision and a Mobile Mixture-of-Experts to manage locomotion and manipulation dynamics, achieving a 73.0% mean success rate on the ManiSkill-HAB benchmark and demonstrating faster inference and real-world transferability.

## Problem

- World Action Models (WAMs) have primarily been confined to fixed-base, tabletop manipulation tasks, struggling to transfer to dynamic, scene-scale mobile manipulation environments.
- Mobile manipulation presents unique challenges including viewpoint changes due to ego-motion, multimodal action distributions (many paths to a goal), and long-horizon causal dependencies where early errors can propagate.
- Existing mobile manipulation approaches, whether modular or end-to-end, often rely on hand-designed interfaces, dynamics-blind visual encoders, or multi-stage training, leading to limitations in coherence and generalization.

## Method

- **MobileWAM** employs a ==mixture-of-transformers== architecture, coupling a pretrained video diffusion transformer (world expert) with a lightweight action-denoising transformer (action expert) for whole-body action chunk output.
- A ==Mobile Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== replaces feed-forward layers in the action expert with shared, locomotion, and manipulation experts, using a soft router to handle heterogeneous dynamics based on motion intent.
- ==Chain-of-Foresight (CoF)== introduces an auxiliary, training-only objective that uses a recurrent, serially chained module to predict future latents, densifying temporal supervision and enhancing long-range causal consistency.

## Results

- MobileWAM achieved a **73.0%** mean success rate on the ManiSkill-HAB benchmark, outperforming existing baselines like AnchorVLA (**64.0%**) and AC-DiT (**55.6%**) using only RGB inputs and single-stage training.
- Ablation studies showed Chain-of-Foresight improved mean success rate by **3.5 points** and the Mobile MoE further added **4.1 points**, demonstrating their compounded benefits for tasks requiring base-arm coordination.
- The model exhibited faster inference latency (**938 ms**), representing a **5.3x** speedup over Motus and **8.7x** over LingBot-VA due to its decoupled denoising scheme, and successfully transferred to a real ARX Lift2 mobile manipulator for diverse household tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adapting pretrained video diffusion transformers, with specialized architectural components, can effectively extend their capabilities from fixed-base to whole-body mobile manipulation tasks.
> - A soft mixture-of-experts design allows for the reconciliation of distinct locomotion and manipulation dynamics within a single policy, enabling specialized learning without severing the structural coherence of whole-body control.
> - Dense, recurrent temporal supervision via a 'Chain-of-Foresight' mechanism can instill a stronger understanding of long-range spatio-temporal and causal dependencies in learned representations at zero inference-time cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenges of integrating World Action Models (WAMs) with mobile manipulation robotics. WAMs, which leverage video generation backbones to internalize physical priors and synthesize future states, have demonstrated success in robot learning. However, their application has largely been confined to fixed-base, tabletop manipulation tasks. Mobile manipulation, by contrast, requires a robot to simultaneously execute locomotion and whole-body manipulation in dynamic, scene-scale environments. This domain presents distinct challenges, including viewpoint changes due to ego-motion, multimodal action distributions (as multiple base-arm paths can achieve the same goal), and long-horizon causal dependencies where early errors can propagate.

Traditional mobile manipulation approaches often fall into two categories:
1. **Modular systems:** These decouple navigation from manipulation, often relying on trajectory optimization, base-pose optimization, or high-level planners (e.g., LLM/VLM planners). While offering structured control, they often involve hand-designed interfaces that can lead to error propagation between stages.
2. **End-to-end systems:** These learn whole-body control directly through methods such as dense policies, anchored diffusion, memory-augmented or reasoning-augmented Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While promising, these often depend on dynamics-blind visual encoders, 3D point clouds, two-stage training, or privileged ground-truth segmentation.

The paper identifies a gap: despite the ability of video-generative diffusion transformers to internalize physical priors and predict coherent futures—a capability seemingly well-suited for the long-horizon and dynamic nature of mobile manipulation—their benefits have not been systematically translated to this domain. Existing WAM successes are predominantly in fixed-base tasks, while mobile manipulation still predominantly relies on approaches that may not fully capture scene-scale dynamics and the complex interplay between locomotion and manipulation. MobileWAM aims to bridge this gap by systematically adapting video-generation WAMs for whole-body mobile manipulation, providing an alternative to reliance on hand-engineered coordination or explicit 3D representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MobileWAM is to bridge the gap between World Action Models (WAMs), which excel at learning physical priors from internet-scale video, and the demands of whole-body mobile manipulation. The authors argue that mobile manipulation is not merely an additive combination of navigation and fixed-base manipulation, but rather a multiplicative challenge requiring novel solutions for coupling these two elements.

The motivation stems from three specific challenges inherent to mobile manipulation:
1. **Viewpoint Consistency under Ego-Motion:** Unlike tabletop setups with fixed cameras, mobile robots' cameras continuously move. This necessitates perception models that can maintain consistent representations of the world despite relentless ego-motion, which dynamics-blind visual encoders may struggle with.
2. **Multimodal Action Distributions:** With a mobile base, the robot has many degrees of freedom, meaning numerous base-arm trajectories can achieve the same goal. This leads to a significantly more multimodal action distribution compared to fixed-base tasks, complicating policy learning.
3. **Long-Horizon Causality:** Mobile manipulation tasks are characterized by deep causal dependencies. An error in an early navigation step can silently undermine the success of a later manipulation phase (e.g., a precise grasp). This requires representations that can encode how the world will evolve over extended causal chains, beyond short-horizon vision-to-action mappings.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out the following key objectives:
- **Adapt WAMs for Mobile Manipulation:** Develop a WAM architecture that can effectively translate internet-scale motion priors into whole-body control for mobile manipulators, moving beyond its current confinement to tabletop tasks.
- **Reconcile Heterogeneous Dynamics:** Design a mechanism within the action expert to address the distinct dynamics of locomotion (navigating free space) and manipulation (precise contact tasks) without destructive interference, allowing for specialized learning while maintaining coherence.
- **Densify Temporal Supervision:** Introduce an auxiliary objective that strengthens long-range spatio-temporal and causal consistency in the learned representations, explicitly guiding the model to understand future world evolution without incurring inference-time cost.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate that the proposed MobileWAM can surpass existing mobile manipulation policies on established benchmarks using simpler, RGB-only inputs and single-stage training.
- **Validate Real-World Transferability and Efficiency:** Show that MobileWAM can fine-tune to a real mobile manipulator and perform diverse tasks with strong generalization, while also achieving inference speeds suitable for real-time deployment by decoupling video generation from action inference.

These objectives collectively aim to establish a more robust and principled approach to mobile manipulation by leveraging the predictive capabilities of world models where they are most critically needed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MobileWAM is a mixture-of-transformers architecture designed to adapt video-generation World Action Models (WAMs) to mobile manipulation. Its core components include a pretrained video diffusion transformer (world expert), a lightweight action expert, a Mobile Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) for heterogeneous dynamics, and a Chain-of-Foresight (CoF) module for dense temporal supervision.

#### 3.1. Overview

At each timestep, the robot receives observations from head and wrist RGB cameras, proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, base odometry), and a language instruction. The policy outputs a whole-body action chunk, commanding the arm, gripper, head, torso, and base. During training, the model also observes a future video segment. Both action and video modalities are trained using flow matching. MobileWAM couples the world expert with the action expert via layerwise joint attention and employs an asymmetric attention mask to allow for decoupled video and action denoising, enabling faster inference. CoF serves as a training-only recurrent supervision signal.

#### 3.2. World Expert

The backbone is a pretrained text-and-image-to-video diffusion transformer (L=30 blocks, width 3072). A frozen 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses input video frames (384x640 RGB from two views composited side-by-side) 16x spatially and 4x temporally. One latent tick of the VAE output corresponds to one action chunk (H=4). The current frame is input as a clean latent (timestep zero), while subsequent frames are supplied with noise, requiring the backbone to denoise the future given the present. A frozen T5-family encoder processes the language instruction, which is then injected into the backbone via per-block cross-attention.

#### 3.3. Action Expert and Mobile MoE

A lightweight action-denoising transformer (width 1024) operates in parallel with the world expert. In each of its 30 layers, its action tokens engage in shared self-attention with the world expert's visual tokens, following a mixture-of-transformers paradigm. Noisy action chunks are linearly embedded into action tokens. The robot's proprioceptive state is appended to the text context for cross-attention.

To address the distinct dynamics of locomotion and manipulation, every feed-forward layer within the action expert is replaced with a **three-expert mixture**: a shared expert, a locomotion expert, and a manipulation expert. A soft router, which processes the mean-pooled noisy action embedding, generates soft weights via a temperature-scaled softmax to determine the contribution of each expert. Experts are initialized as clones of the dense layer, and the router is zero-initialized to allow specialization to emerge from data. No explicit load-balancing loss is used.

#### 3.4. Chain-of-Foresight (CoF)

CoF introduces an auxiliary objective to densify temporal supervision. It taps hidden states from four uniformly spaced layers ({4, 12, 20, 30}) of the world expert's backbone for current-observation tokens. These tapped features are fused via a two-layer MLP `g` to create an initial belief state `h_0`.

Depth-specific modules (F_1..., F_K), each consisting of three transformer blocks, are chained to predict future latents. At each step `k`, `F_k` takes the belief `h_{k-1}` and a noised target future latent `z_k`, cross-attends to the language, proprioceptive state, and current observation, and outputs a velocity estimate and an updated foresight belief `h_k`. This RNN-style chain forces `h_k` to summarize the evolving world, acting as a latent dynamics model. The belief `h_k` is the sole information conduit between consecutive foresight steps.

#### 3.5. Attention Mask and CoF Interaction

The joint attention mask is asymmetric:
- Current observation tokens attend only to themselves.
- Future video tokens attend to all visual tokens.
- Action tokens attend only to current observation tokens.
- No video token attends to any action token.
- Each foresight step `f_k` (for k ≥ 1) attends to the current observation and its predecessor's belief `f_{k-1}`, while `f_0` attends to the current observation and itself.

This design ensures that the current-observation representation read by the policy remains identical during both training and deployment. At inference time, the world expert functions as a "current-frame encoder": per-layer key-value pairs are cached from a single backbone pass, and action tokens are iteratively denoised against this cache. The CoF module and future video generation are entirely discarded, resulting in significant speedups. CoF's gradients flow through the fusion MLP into the tapped backbone layers, influencing the representations that the action expert consumes, thereby shaping the backbone to encode scene dynamics without direct inference-time cost.

#### 3.6. Losses

Both video and action modalities are trained using **flow matching**. For a clean sample `x` (video latent or action chunk), a noisy interpolant `x_τ` is created, and the network learns to predict the velocity field `ˆv` that approximates `v = ε - x`. The losses are:
- `L_v = E ||ˆv_vid - v_vid||^2` for video.
- `L_a = E ||ˆv_act - v_act||^2` for action.
CoF adds a depth-decayed term to the total objective:
- `L_CoF = Σ_{k=1}^K w_k E ||ˆv_k - v_k||^2`, where `w_1 > ... > w_K` discounts farther futures.
The total objective is `L = L_v + L_a + λ L_CoF`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of MobileWAM was evaluated through comparisons with state-of-the-art methods on a benchmark, extensive ablation studies, real-robot deployment, and qualitative analysis of generated futures.

#### 4.1. Comparison with the State of the Art

On the ManiSkill-HAB SetTable benchmark (seven subtask-object-scene combinations), MobileWAM achieved a mean success rate (S.R.) of 73.0%. This result surpassed all listed baselines, including AnchorVLA (64.0%) and AC-DiT (55.6%). MobileWAM demonstrated robustness, avoiding the single-digit success rates on particular subtasks observed in other baselines. The performance was achieved using only RGB inputs and a single-stage training process, in contrast to methods that rely on 3D point clouds or multi-stage training curricula.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Component Contribution:** Adding Chain-of-Foresight (CoF) to a plain WAM improved the mean S.R. from 65.4% to 68.9% (+3.5 points). Subsequent integration of the Mobile Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) further increased the mean S.R. to 73.0% (+4.1 points). These improvements were observed to compound, particularly on tasks requiring coordination between base repositioning and precise manipulation (e.g., Place Apple).
- **Foresight Design:**
 - **Parallel vs. Serial Foresight:** Simply extending the prediction horizon with parallel, unchained future supervision yielded a modest gain (50.2% to 52.3% mean S.R. on a reduced budget). However, serial chaining, where each foresight step conditions on its predecessor's belief, was found to be crucial.
 - **MLP vs. Transformer for Chaining:** Using an MLP for chaining the belief state resulted in a performance degradation (52.3% to 46.3%), suggesting the MLP bottleneck was too crude. Employing a transformer-based module for foresight restored expressiveness and delivered the best result (58.2%), indicating that richer belief states are necessary for effective causal chaining.
 - **Layer Selection for Belief State:** Tapping four uniformly spaced backbone layers (4, 12, 20, 30) for the initial belief `h_0` proved most effective (58.2% mean S.R.). Indiscriminate concatenation of all 30 layers (37.1%) or selection of layers from a single region (e.g., first, middle, or last four) performed worse, suggesting a trade-off between coverage of geometry-to-semantics spectrum and gradient noise.
 - **Chain Length:** Performance was stable for chain lengths K=1 to K=4, with K=3 yielding the peak mean S.R. (58.2%). Longer chains beyond the near-term causal window provided diminishing returns.
- **Mobile MoE vs. Two Action Experts:** An alternative architectural separation into two distinct action experts (locomotion and manipulation) with various attention masks (bidirectional, one-way) consistently underperformed the Mobile MoE (e.g., 58.2% for MoE vs. 48.8% for bidirectional two experts). This suggests that a hard split halves effective data, severs shared parameters, and restricts coordination, whereas soft routing over a shared expert maintains a coherent trajectory-level representation.

#### 4.3. Deployment Efficiency

MobileWAM exhibited faster inference latency compared to "imagine-then-execute" WAMs. On an NVIDIA A800, MobileWAM completed one prediction cycle in 938 ms. This represented a 5.3x speedup over Motus (4950 ms) and an 8.7x speedup over LingBot-VA (8126 ms), primarily due to its decoupled denoising scheme that discards video generation and foresight at inference time.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Experiments

MobileWAM was fine-tuned on teleoperated demonstrations collected with an ARX Lift2 mobile manipulator for five household tasks of increasing complexity (T_a to T_e). MobileWAM consistently outperformed the π_0.5 baseline on every task. The performance margin was most significant on the longest-horizon task, T_e (compound open–fetch–deposit–close sequence), where MobileWAM achieved a 15% success rate while π_0.5 never succeeded. This indicates that the benefits of MobileWAM's causally chained representations become more pronounced with increasing task causal depth.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis of Generated Futures

During fridge-picking tasks, MobileWAM's video predictions (even though unused at inference) demonstrated superior spatio-temporal consistency compared to a fine-tuned-only Wan backbone. MobileWAM's predictions maintained consistent object scaling with distance and preserved object silhouettes despite robot ego-motion, while the baseline exhibited perspective violations and shape distortions. This suggests that CoF enhances the backbone's ability to encode physically consistent scene dynamics, which contributes to the action policy's performance.

#### 4.6. Failure Analysis and Limitations

Failures in MobileWAM primarily occurred during pick-and-place subtasks. Approximately 40% of failures were attributed to localization errors (missing grasp/release tolerances), 25% to collision interference, 20% to lack of recovery from initial failures, 10% to failure to restore rest poses, and 5% to premature drops. These failures clustered around target poses at the robot's workspace fringes, where high precision and accurate inverse kinematics are critical. Open and close tasks, with their coarser geometry, exhibited fewer failures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MobileWAM represents a step towards adapting World Action Models (WAMs) for mobile manipulation, an area where such models have previously been underutilized. The work demonstrates that leveraging pretrained video diffusion transformers, combined with tailored architectural and supervisory designs, can significantly advance performance in complex robotic tasks requiring simultaneous locomotion and manipulation.

The significance of MobileWAM stems from several key aspects:
- **Bridging WAMs to Mobile Manipulation:** By systematically adapting video-generation WAMs, MobileWAM extends the applicability of these powerful, pre-trained models from fixed-base to whole-body mobile manipulation. This offers a path to transferring internet-scale motion priors to complex, real-world robotic scenarios.
- **Enhanced Temporal and Causal Consistency:** The introduction of Chain-of-Foresight (CoF) provides a novel mechanism for densifying temporal supervision during training. This recurrent, serially chained future-latent prediction objective helps instill a stronger understanding of long-range spatio-temporal and causal dependencies in the robot's representations, which is crucial for long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks. Its zero inference cost makes it a practical solution for improving model capabilities without impacting real-time performance.
- **Effective Handling of Heterogeneous Dynamics:** The Mobile Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design addresses the inherent conflict between the distinct dynamics of locomotion and manipulation. By softly routing feed-forward layers across shared, locomotion, and manipulation experts based on motion intent, MobileWAM allows for specialization without severing the structural coherence of whole-body control. This improves performance, particularly in tasks requiring fine-grained base-arm coordination.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance with Leaner Inputs:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on ManiSkill-HAB using only RGB-only inputs and single-stage training is significant. This demonstrates that MobileWAM can infer spatio-temporal consistency directly from visual data and internal priors, reducing the reliance on explicit 3D representations, two-stage training, or privileged information often used in other approaches.
- **Practical Deployment Efficiency:** The decoupled video-action denoising scheme allows MobileWAM to operate as a pure current-frame encoder at inference, resulting in substantial speedups (5-8x faster than imagine-then-execute WAMs). This makes the model more viable for real-time deployment on mobile platforms, where computational resources can be constrained.
- **Real-World Generalization:** The successful transfer and outperformance of MobileWAM on a real ARX Lift2 mobile manipulator across diverse, increasing-horizon household tasks validate its practical utility and generalization capabilities beyond simulation. The observed performance gains on longer-horizon tasks underscore the value of the causally chained representations.

The potential impact of this work is broad, influencing the development of more capable and general-purpose mobile manipulators. It suggests that future research could further explore post-training with reinforcement learning or interactive corrections, integrate richer geometric grounding beyond RGB, and apply CoF during large-scale WAM pretraining. MobileWAM posits that mobile manipulation is not just a harder benchmark but the natural environment for world action models, capable of demonstrating their full potential.
