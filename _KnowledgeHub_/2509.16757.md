# HDMI: Learning Interactive Humanoid Whole-Body Control from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed HDMI, a framework enabling humanoid robots to acquire complex, whole-body, contact-rich interaction skills directly from monocular human demonstration videos. It achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like door traversal and object manipulation on a Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating adaptability to environmental variations.

## Problem

- Integrating locomotion and manipulation for whole-body, contact-rich humanoid-object interaction (HOI) remains a significant challenge, with many prior solutions being task-specific.
- Learning complex HOI skills suffers from data scarcity, as collecting robot-specific demonstrations or manually programming behaviors for diverse interactions is unscalable.
- Reinforcement learning faces difficulties in training humanoids to establish stable contact and balance objects, especially when initial motion references from videos are kinematically imperfect.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline transforms raw human videos into structured reference datasets, then trains a whole-body control policy via reinforcement learning for robot-object co-tracking, and finally deploys it zero-shot to real humanoids.
- The framework utilizes existing tools for human pose estimation and retargeting to generate robot and object reference trajectories from monocular RGB videos.
- Reinforcement learning employs a unified object representation, a residual action space, and a unified interaction reward to enhance training stability, sample efficiency, and robust physical contact.

## Results

- HDMI policies successfully executed various complex tasks on a real Unitree G1 robot, including 67 consecutive door open/traverse runs and multi-stage sequences like 'Truman's Bow,' demonstrating high robustness.
- Real-world experiments showed adaptability to environmental variations (e.g., removed floorboard) and random positional offsets (10–30 cm), adjusting robot actions precisely for tasks like door traversal.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the interaction reward was crucial for handling imperfect video references and guiding precise contact, while the residual action space was vital for stable learning and efficient convergence in challenging poses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified object representation, expressing object pose and contact points relative to the robot's root, ensures spatial invariance and generalizes across diverse object types (rigid/articulated, fixed/floating).
> - Employing a residual action space, where the policy predicts corrective offsets to reference joint positions, significantly stabilizes RL training and enables learning of challenging poses (e.g., kneeling) more efficiently.
> - A unified interaction reward actively guides the policy to establish and maintain stable, physically plausible contact with objects, effectively compensating for imperfections in kinematically retargeted human video references.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen remarkable progress, particularly in specialized domains such as agile locomotion and dexterous manipulation. However, integrating these capabilities into *whole-body, contact-rich humanoid-object interaction (HOI)* remains a significant challenge, representing a frontier for current research. This paper directly addresses this gap.

**Current State of the Art and Identified Gaps:**

- **Humanoid Locomotion:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) and imitation learning from human data have led to highly agile and robust locomotion skills, including walking, running, jumping, and parkour [1–3, 13–16]. These methods often focus on free-space movement or interaction with the ground plane.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** Similarly, advances in data collection and RL have enabled dexterous manipulation policies, particularly for upper-body tasks [4–6, 18–20]. These typically involve fine motor control with manipulators.
- **The Loco-Manipulation Challenge:** Bridging the gap between isolated locomotion and manipulation—to achieve seamless *loco-manipulation* where the robot moves and interacts with objects simultaneously in contact-rich settings—is complex. Prior efforts in this area have often been limited:
 - **Task-Specific Solutions:** Many approaches rely on extensive reward engineering or trajectory optimization tailored to specific tasks, such as box transport. This limits their generality and scalability to diverse interactions.
 - **Specialized Architectures:** Some works introduce specific architectural designs like skill blending or hierarchical control to decouple low-level tracking from high-level task policies [3, 22–24]. While effective for certain scenarios, these can add complexity and might not generalize universally.
 - **Robustness Approaches:** More recent work has explored dual-agent RL or adaptive policies for human-humanoid collaboration [25–27], aiming for increased robustness but often within constrained interaction contexts.
- **Learning from Human Videos:** Human videos offer a scalable and abundant source of demonstration data. While methods exist for learning locomotion skills from videos [1, 28–30] and manipulation skills from videos [31–33], they typically fall short in the context of whole-body HOI:
 - Locomotion-focused methods do not explicitly model object dynamics.
 - Manipulation-focused methods are often constrained to upper-body interactions, neglecting the potential of whole-body coordination and the expanded workspace it offers.

**HDMI's Contribution to the Landscape:**

HDMI positions itself as a **general, end-to-end framework** that directly tackles the limitations of previous works. By learning *whole-body humanoid-object interaction skills* directly from *monocular RGB human videos*, it aims to unify locomotion and manipulation in a contact-rich environment. It distinguishes itself by explicitly modeling human-object dynamics and co-tracking trajectories, moving beyond the traditional separation of these domains. This work represents a significant step towards enabling humanoids to robustly perform a wide array of interactive tasks in unstructured human environments, using a scalable data source that bypasses the need for specialized robot demonstrations or extensive manual reward engineering for each new task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the HDMI research is to **enable robust whole-body humanoid-object interaction (HOI) for humanoid robots**. This overarching goal is driven by the vision of humanoid robots assisting humans in diverse real-world environments, where interaction with objects and the environment is paramount.

The authors articulate several key motivations for pursuing this objective and for the specific design choices within HDMI:

- **Unleashing Humanoid Potential:** Humanoid robots possess human-like morphology, making them versatile for human environments. However, their full potential remains untapped without the ability to robustly interact with objects, beyond simple locomotion or isolated manipulation.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Learning complex HOI skills requires vast amounts of data. Manually programming or collecting robot-specific demonstrations for every possible interaction is impractical and unscalable. Human-object interaction data, especially with detailed 3D human and object motions, is scarce compared to free-space locomotion data. The motivation is to leverage the abundance of readily available *human videos* as a scalable and natural source of demonstrations.
- **Overcoming RL Training Challenges for HOI:** Learning whole-body interaction tasks presents unique difficulties for reinforcement learning:
 - **Guiding Desired Contact:** Unlike free-space tasks, HOI inherently involves intricate contact dynamics. Imperfect motion references from videos can make it difficult for RL policies to learn stable and precise contact behaviors.
 - **Balancing with Objects:** Interacting with objects, especially in challenging poses or with varying loads, requires sophisticated balance control, which is hard to learn from scratch with traditional RL.
 - **Limitations of Prior Approaches:** Existing HOI methods often rely on task-specific motion reference generation or extensive manual reward engineering, limiting their generality and requiring significant effort for each new skill. The motivation is to create a more general framework that can acquire diverse interactive skills without such explicit task-specific programming.
- **Developing a General and Simple Framework:** The authors explicitly aim for a "simple and general framework" that can support interaction with different body parts (hand, foot) and diverse object types (articulated or rigid, fixed or floating). This generality is motivated by the desire to move beyond highly specialized solutions towards a more broadly applicable paradigm for humanoid control.

In essence, HDMI seeks to bypass the limitations of traditional approaches by offering a unified, video-driven imitation learning framework that addresses the core challenges of data acquisition and robust RL training for whole-body humanoid-object interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HDMI framework is designed as a simple yet general three-stage pipeline to acquire interactive humanoid skills directly from monocular RGB human videos.

**Stage (i): Scalable Skill Specification from Human Videos**
The first stage focuses on transforming raw, unconstrained human videos into structured reference datasets suitable for robot learning.
- **Data Source:** Monocular RGB videos of humans interacting with objects.
- **Pose Estimation & Retargeting:** The framework utilizes existing state-of-the-art tools:
 - **GVHMR:** For accurate SMPL-pose estimation (human body model).
 - **LocoMujoco:** For retargeting these human motions to the robot's kinematics.
- **Post-processing and Annotation:** The extracted data undergoes further processing to create a comprehensive reference motion dataset. Each frame `t` in this dataset provides:
 - A reference state `s_ref_t` comprising:
 - `s_robot`: Robot's reference root position (`p_robot`), root orientation (`q_robot`), and joint positions (`θ_ref_t`).
 - `s_obj`: Object's position (`p_obj`) and orientation (`q_obj`). For articulated objects (e.g., doors), this also includes their joint states (`θ_obj`).
 - Desired contact points `p_contact_t`, defined in the object's local frame, indicating where the robot is intended to make contact.
 - A binary signal `c_t ∈ {0, 1}` indicating whether contact is intended at frame `t`.

**Stage (ii): Interactive Skill Learning via Robot-Object Co-Tracking**
This stage involves training a whole-body control policy using reinforcement learning (RL) to simultaneously track the reference trajectories of both the robot and the object.
- **Formulation:** The problem is framed as a robot-object co-tracking problem, similar to DeepMimic-style imitation learning.
- **RL Setup:**
 - **Environment:** All policies are trained in IsaacSim.
 - **Optimization:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used.
 - **Episode Initialization:** Robot and object are initialized from a random frame `s_ref_t` of the reference motion, with small random perturbations to enhance robustness.
 - **Observations:** The policy receives:
 - Proprioceptive state (`s_proprio_t`) of the robot.
 - A phase variable `ϕ ∈` indicating progress through the motion.
 - **Unified Object Representation (Key Design 1):** The object's pose, expressed relative to the robot's local root frame, is provided. This spatial invariance aids generalization across diverse objects. Additionally, the reference contact points (`p_contact_t`) are transformed into the robot's root frame and provided to guide interaction.
 - **Termination:** Episodes terminate if robot or object states deviate excessively from the reference motion (tracking-error-based termination) or if contact is lost prematurely.
- **Key Designs for Robustness and Generality:** To address the specific challenges of whole-body object interaction, HDMI introduces three critical components:
 - **1. Unified Object Representation:** As described above, by representing object pose and target contact points relative to the robot's local root frame, the framework achieves spatial invariance. This allows the same policy architecture to generalize across various objects (rigid/articulated, fixed/floating) and facilitates future integration with on-board sensory inputs.
 - **2. Residual Action Space:** Instead of predicting absolute joint targets, the policy learns to output a *corrective offset* (`a_t`) that is added to the *reference joint positions* (`θ_ref_t`) from the kinematic trajectory: `θ_target_t = θ_ref_t + a_t`. This grounds initial exploration around the current reference pose, preventing abrupt, destabilizing "pop-up" behaviors when initialized in challenging poses (e.g., kneeling). This significantly improves sample efficiency and convergence speed for complex motions.
 - **3. Unified Interaction Reward (`R_interaction`):** Reference motions from video retargeting can be kinematically imperfect (e.g., lacking precise contact or containing penetrations). To compensate, this reward component encourages the policy to establish and maintain stable contact when interaction is intended (`c_t = 1`). For each active end-effector `i`, it combines:
 - A **position term**: Rewards proximity between the end-effector `p_eef,i` and its target contact point `p_target,i` on the object.
 - A **force term**: Promotes sufficient but not excessive contact forces (`F_contact,i`), capped by a threshold `F_thres` for safety.
 This reward encourages physically plausible and stable interactions, even with imperfect kinematic references.
- **Reward Structure & Regularization:** The overall reward function includes robot tracking, object tracking, the unified interaction reward, and various regularization penalties (action rate L2, joint position limits L1, joint velocity penalty, joint torque limits L1, feet impact force L2, feet slip, feet air time). These guide stable and natural robot behavior.
- **Domain Randomization:** To further enhance robustness for sim-to-real transfer, the robot's and object's inertial and friction properties are randomized during training.

**Stage (iii): Zero-Shot Deployment on Real Humanoid Robots**
The policies trained in simulation are directly deployed on real Unitree G1 humanoid robots without any real-world fine-tuning.
- **Hardware:** Unitree G1 humanoid.
- **Real-world Sensing:** Motion capture (mocap) markers are attached to the robot's pelvis and manipulated objects to acquire global poses. These object poses are then transformed into the robot's root frame, matching the observation space learned in simulation.

This structured, multi-stage approach, leveraging human video data, robust RL training with novel components, and direct sim-to-real transfer, forms the core of the HDMI methodology.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted in both simulation and the real world on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrate the significant robustness and generality of the HDMI framework.

**Real-World Experiments (Unitree G1):** HDMI successfully acquired and executed various complex whole-body interaction tasks.

- **Case I: Door Open and Traversal:**
 - The robot achieved an impressive **67 consecutive door runs (approximately 34 minutes)** before failure. This included pushing the door open with its hand, walking through, turning, kicking the door open with its foot, and passing again.
 - **Robustness:** The policy demonstrated resilience to environmental variations, successfully performing about 7 runs even after a wooden floorboard was removed, indicating adaptability to terrain changes. It also handled random positional offsets (10–30 cm) at the start of each round, adjusting footsteps and arm movements precisely.
- **Case II: Box Loco-Manipulation:**
 - This category showcased the robot's ability to integrate whole-body motions for manipulating varied box-like objects (different heights, weights, and shapes).
 - **Suitcase manipulation:** The robot executed 7 consecutive successful runs, exhibiting smooth transitions between kneeling, lifting, and walking while carrying a load.
 - **Bread box carrying:** Completed 2 full trials, with the primary challenge being a rapid 180° turn.
 - **Foam mats relocation:** The robot successfully walked forward, grasped the mats, sidestepped, and placed them down.
 - These tasks highlight HDMI's capability for seamless whole-body coordination involving grasping, locomotion, and manipulation.
- **Case III: Truman's Bow:**
 - A highly complex, multi-stage task requiring a long sequence of behaviors: stair climbing, performing a bow, sitting on the stair, waving, jumping off, and walking back.
 - The learned policy successfully executed the entire sequence 3 times continuously.
 - **Contact-rich and expressive:** This demonstrated the framework's ability to handle dynamic and contact-rich interactions (climbing and sitting on stairs) alongside precise full-body pose control for expressive gestures. Light human support was occasionally applied during stair climbing due to inherent risks.

**Simulation Ablation Studies:** These experiments critically evaluated the contribution of HDMI's key design components.

- **Interaction Reward:**
 - **Handling Imperfect References:** The interaction reward proved crucial for tasks where reference motions from videos might be imperfect (e.g., failing to show a precise grasp). It enabled the policy to *deviate from the flawed reference* to establish proper contact. Without this reward, the policy rigidly mimicked the imperfect reference, leading to interaction failure.
 - **Guiding Precise Contact:** For tasks like pushing a box, where precise end-effector placement on an object's edge is critical, the interaction reward was essential. It guided the policy to achieve accurate and stable contact, preventing unstable interactions that occurred without it.
- **Residual Action Space:**
 - **Stable and Efficient Training:** The residual action space consistently led to lower joint and body tracking errors and significantly faster convergence to high success rates across various tasks.
 - **Learning Challenging Poses:** It was vital for learning motions involving challenging poses, such as kneeling (e.g., for suitcase manipulation). Without the residual action space, policies often converged to suboptimal strategies (e.g., bending at the waist instead of fully kneeling) or failed due to unstable "pop-up" behaviors at the start of episodes, generating low-quality training data. By centering exploration around the reference pose, the residual action space allowed stable learning of complex deviations from a standing posture.

Overall, the results establish HDMI as a robust and general framework, capable of learning diverse, contact-rich whole-body interaction skills for humanoids directly from human videos, successfully transferring these skills from simulation to real hardware. The ablation studies confirm the critical role of the unified object representation, residual action space, and unified interaction reward in achieving this performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HDMI framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in enabling complex and versatile interactions with the environment. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering General Framework for HOI from Videos:** HDMI is presented as the first general framework that enables learning *autonomous whole-body humanoid-object interaction skills directly from human videos*. This is a crucial step forward, addressing the long-standing challenge of data scarcity and the difficulty of programming contact-rich behaviors.
- **Enhanced Humanoid Versatility and Capabilities:** By enabling robots to robustly perform tasks like opening doors, carrying objects, and executing complex multi-stage sequences such as Truman's Bow, HDMI significantly expands the practical capabilities of humanoid robots. This brings them closer to being truly versatile assistants in human environments, capable of performing a wide array of useful and complex tasks.
- **Leveraging Abundant Human Data:** The ability to learn directly from monocular RGB human videos is a powerful paradigm shift. It democratizes the process of skill acquisition, moving away from expensive, robot-specific data collection or expert-driven demonstrations. This makes it scalable and accessible, potentially accelerating the development of new humanoid skills.
- **Innovative Technical Solutions:** The framework's core innovations—the unified object representation, residual action space, and unified interaction reward—are key contributions that tackle fundamental challenges in HOI learning:
 - **Unified Object Representation:** Promotes generality across diverse object types and interaction modes.
 - **Residual Action Space:** Crucially enhances RL training stability and efficiency, particularly for complex and challenging poses, accelerating learning and enabling behaviors previously difficult to acquire.
 - **Unified Interaction Reward:** Addresses the critical problem of imperfect kinematic references from videos by actively guiding the policy towards stable and precise physical contact, a prerequisite for successful interaction.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot deployment of policies trained in simulation to a real Unitree G1 humanoid, demonstrated across numerous diverse tasks and under varying environmental conditions, underscores the robustness of the learned policies and the effectiveness of the training methodology (including domain randomization).
- **Foundation for Future Research:** HDMI establishes a strong baseline and opens up several promising avenues for future research:
 - **Towards On-Board Sensing:** The stated limitation of relying on ground-truth motion capture data for object poses highlights the next critical step: integrating direct perception from on-board sensors like cameras. This would eliminate the need for external instrumentation and enable deployment in truly unconstrained environments.
 - **Generalist Policies:** Moving from "one policy per skill" to training unified generalist models that can perform a wide range of interactions based on diverse skill data is another ambitious and impactful future direction.

In conclusion, HDMI significantly advances the state of the art in humanoid control by providing a general, robust, and data-efficient framework for learning complex whole-body object interaction skills from human videos. Its impact will be felt in the accelerated development of more capable and autonomous humanoid robots, bridging the gap between impressive individual skills and integrated, intelligent interaction with the physical world.
