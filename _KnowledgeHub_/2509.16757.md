# HDMI: Learning Interactive Humanoid Whole-Body Control from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed HDMI, a framework enabling humanoid robots to acquire complex, whole-body, contact-rich interaction skills directly from monocular human demonstration videos. It achieves robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like door traversal and object manipulation on a Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating adaptability to environmental variations.

## Problem

- Integrating locomotion and manipulation for whole-body, contact-rich humanoid-object interaction (HOI) remains a significant challenge, with many prior solutions being task-specific.
- Learning complex HOI skills suffers from data scarcity, as collecting robot-specific demonstrations or manually programming behaviors for diverse interactions is unscalable.
- Reinforcement learning faces difficulties in training humanoids to establish stable contact and balance objects, especially when initial motion references from videos are kinematically imperfect.

## Method

- A three-stage pipeline transforms raw human videos into structured reference datasets, then trains a whole-body control policy via reinforcement learning for robot-object co-tracking, and finally deploys it zero-shot to real humanoids.
- The framework utilizes existing tools for human pose estimation and retargeting to generate robot and object reference trajectories from monocular RGB videos.
- Reinforcement learning employs a unified object representation, a residual action space, and a unified interaction reward to enhance training stability, sample efficiency, and robust physical contact.

## Results

- HDMI policies successfully executed various complex tasks on a real Unitree G1 robot, including 67 consecutive door open/traverse runs and multi-stage sequences like 'Truman's Bow,' demonstrating high robustness.
- Real-world experiments showed adaptability to environmental variations (e.g., removed floorboard) and random positional offsets (10–30 cm), adjusting robot actions precisely for tasks like door traversal.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the interaction reward was crucial for handling imperfect video references and guiding precise contact, while the residual action space was vital for stable learning and efficient convergence in challenging poses.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified object representation, expressing object pose and contact points relative to the robot's root, ensures spatial invariance and generalizes across diverse object types (rigid/articulated, fixed/floating).
> - Employing a residual action space, where the policy predicts corrective offsets to reference joint positions, significantly stabilizes RL training and enables learning of challenging poses (e.g., kneeling) more efficiently.
> - A unified interaction reward actively guides the policy to establish and maintain stable, physically plausible contact with objects, effectively compensating for imperfections in kinematically retargeted human video references.
