# Physics-Informed Deformable Gaussian Splatting: Towards Unified Constitutive Laws for Time-Evolving Material Field

> [!summary] Summary
> Physics-Informed Deformable Gaussian Splatting (PIDG) unifies explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting with continuum mechanics to enable physically consistent dynamic novel-view synthesis from monocular video. The framework, developed by researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China and UIUC, achieves superior rendering quality and physical consistency, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in metrics like PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and predicts coherent velocity and stress fields.

## Problem

- Existing dynamic novel-view synthesis methods struggle to infer complex 3D motion patterns from sparse monocular video inputs due to inadequate supervision.
- Current data-driven approaches often lack physical consistency, yielding unrealistic deformations and dynamic behaviors for objects like fluids or elastic materials.
- Relying solely on 2D visual supervision makes it challenging to uniformly constrain 3D particle states over time, leading to arbitrary deviations and unstable scene reconstruction.

## Method

- Employs a 4D decomposed hash encoding and a static-dynamic decoupling strategy for efficient representation and optimization of dynamic scenes.
- Treats each 3D Gaussian as a Lagrangian material particle within a canonical hash space, embedding continuum mechanics constitutive laws via a time-evolving material field.
- Integrates a Lagrangian particle flow matching loss, derived from a refined backward optical flow decomposition, to provide direct supervision for the predicted velocity field.

## Results

- PIDG demonstrated superior average quantitative results (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across custom physics-driven scenarios, HyperNeRF real-world data, and D-NeRF synthetic benchmarks.
- The physics-informed material field consistently improved dynamic novel-view synthesis and enhanced future prediction capabilities for baseline methods.
- The framework successfully models both spatio-temporal structure and intrinsic physical properties, predicting coherent velocity and stress fields in addition to high-fidelity renderings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating continuum mechanics' constitutive laws directly into 3D Gaussian Splatting enables the recovery of physically consistent spatio-temporal dynamics and intrinsic material properties.
> - Modeling Gaussians as Lagrangian particles allows for the enforcement of physical laws like the Cauchy momentum equation, acting as a robust regularizer for dynamic scenes.
> - Refined optical flow decomposition provides a viable and artifact-free surrogate supervision signal for 3D particle motion, applicable to monocular video without ground-truth motion priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the rapidly evolving field of **novel-view synthesis** and **3D scene representation**, with a particular focus on **dynamic scenes**. The broader landscape has seen significant advancements, transitioning from implicit representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to explicit representations such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS).

Initially, methods like NeRF (Mildenhall et al. 2020) demonstrated remarkable success in reconstructing static 3D scenes and synthesizing novel views, but were computationally intensive and struggled with dynamic content. Dynamic NeRF variants (e.g., Pumarola et al. 2021, Park et al. 2021b) emerged to address this, often by mapping dynamic scenes to a static canonical space and learning deformation fields. While these improved dynamic reconstruction, they often treated scenes as Eulerian volumes, limiting physical consistency to 2D projections.

The advent of 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. 2023) revolutionized real-time rendering of explicit scene representations. This led to numerous extensions for dynamic scenes, such as DynamicGS (Luiten et al. 2024), deformation-field approaches (Wu et al. 2024, Yang et al. 2024b), and methods leveraging low-rank assumptions (Huang et al. 2024) or 4D decomposed hash coding (Xu et al. 2024, Dai et al. 2025). These data-driven methods, while achieving high-fidelity rendering, often simplify motion to rigid-body transformations or struggle to capture complex, physics-driven deformation patterns, leading to results that lack physical realism or consistency, especially from sparse monocular inputs. Furthermore, purely visual supervision from monocular videos can result in ambiguous 3D particle states and deviations from true motion patterns.

To overcome these limitations, a parallel trend in computer graphics and vision research has been the integration of **physics-informed modeling**. Earlier physics-guided methods, often based on Material Point Methods (MPM) (e.g., Xie et al. 2024), Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) (e.g., Chu et al. 2022), or spring-mass models (e.g., Zhong et al. 2025), aimed to embed physical realism. However, these often required strict boundary conditions, multi-view or RGB-D inputs, or relied on fixed material properties, limiting their generalization to diverse, complex dynamic scenes from monocular video.

This paper, "Physics-Informed Deformable Gaussian Splatting (PIDG)," positions itself at the intersection of these fields. It attempts to bridge the gap between efficient explicit dynamic 3DGS representations and robust physical modeling. By treating each Gaussian as a **Lagrangian material point** and embedding **continuum mechanics constitutive laws** through a time-evolving material field, PIDG aims to provide a unified, physically consistent framework. It differentiates itself by integrating these physical principles directly into the 3DGS optimization pipeline, supervised by readily available 2D optical flow (as a proxy for motion), making it highly adaptable to monocular video inputs without relying on predefined material properties or expensive multi-view setups. This work represents a significant step towards creating dynamic scene representations that are not only visually accurate but also physically plausible and generalizable, overcoming a core challenge in the current landscape of 4D novel-view synthesis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **faithfully embed physical dynamics into spatio-temporal scene representations** for dynamic novel-view synthesis. The authors aim to go beyond mere visual reconstruction to accurately capture the evolution of intrinsic physical properties across space and time within dynamic scenes.

The primary motivation for PIDG stems from significant challenges faced by existing novel-view synthesis methods in dynamic scenes, particularly when dealing with monocular video inputs:

1. **Inadequate Supervision for Complex Motion Patterns:** Sparse-view observations from monocular videos are often insufficient to supervise complex, diverse motion patterns in 3D. Purely data-driven approaches tend to struggle with inferring plausible 3D motion and geometry without strong priors.
2. **Lack of Physical Consistency:** The synthesis results from existing methods often suffer from inconsistencies in physical dynamics. They typically simplify motion to rigid body transformations or learn deformations without explicit physical constraints, leading to unrealistic behaviors for deformable objects, fluids, or elastic materials. This results in visually plausible but physically inaccurate dynamic models.
3. **Inability to Uniformly Constrain 3D Particles:** Relying solely on 2D visual supervision makes it difficult to uniformly constrain the 3D state of Gaussian particles over time, leading to arbitrary deviations from their original motion patterns and instability in reconstruction.

To address these challenges, the authors investigate two key questions that define the core objectives of this paper, as explicitly stated in Section 3.3:

1. **"How can we model the positions and time-varying deformations of Gaussian particles without any known ground-truth particle motion priors?"** This question motivates the development of a framework that can learn intrinsic motion properties from visual cues, rather than relying on external physical simulations or annotations.
2. **"What kind of boundary conditions or surrogate supervision can enable physically meaningful and generalizable modeling of dynamic materials?"** This drives the search for effective physics-based constraints and data-fitting terms that ensure physical plausibility and robustness across diverse dynamic scenarios, without requiring explicit, scene-specific boundary conditions or multi-view ground truth data.

In response to these motivations, PIDG proposes to model dynamic 3D Gaussians as time-evolving Lagrangian particles, explicitly embedding continuum mechanics constitutive laws. This approach aims to recover not only spatio-temporal structure but also intrinsic physical properties, leading to more consistent, convergent, and generalizable dynamic behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Physics-Informed Deformable Gaussian Splatting (PIDG) method introduces a novel, fully differentiable pipeline for monocular dynamic novel-view synthesis that unifies efficient scene representation with physically consistent motion modeling. The approach is built upon three core components: Dynamic Modeling in Canonical Hash Space, Physics-Informed Gaussian Representation, and Lagrangian Particle Flow Matching.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Modeling in Canonical Hash Space

PIDG starts by efficiently representing dynamic scenes using a **4D decomposed hash encoding**, inspired by Grid4D. Instead of costly 4D MLPs or low-rank decompositions, this method maps each Gaussian coordinate (x, y, z, t) to four separate 3D hash grids (G_xyz, G_xyt, G_yzt, G_xzt). This significantly reduces memory complexity from O(n^4) to O(n^3) while maintaining reconstruction accuracy. A spatial MLP extracts a context feature (G_xyz) to compute a directional attention weight, which then modulates a raw temporal encoding from a temporal MLP (G_xyt, G_yzt, G_xzt). A multi-head MLP decoder processes this combined feature to predict a rotation (R_x), translation (T_x), scale update (∆s), and rotation increment (∆r), which collectively define the deformation field for canonical Gaussians.

To further optimize efficiency and accuracy, a **static-dynamic decoupling strategy** is employed. In a two-stage process, all Gaussians are initially densified and jointly optimized for both static geometry and dynamic deformation. Subsequently, densification is paused, and a dynamic mask isolates moving regions. During refinement, only masked Gaussians update their deformation fields, while unmasked (static) Gaussians have their rendering parameters fine-tuned within the canonical hash space. This explicit separation of static and dynamic components allows for efficient physical modeling of dynamics without compromising static scene representation.

#### 3.2. Physics-Informed Gaussian Representation

The core innovation of PIDG lies in its treatment of each Gaussian as a **Lagrangian material particle** within the canonical hash space, explicitly embedding constitutive laws from continuum mechanics. This moves beyond merely modeling deformation to understanding the underlying physics.
A **time-evolving material field** is introduced, modeled by a multi-head MLP (`f_θ`). This network ingests a feature vector composed of normalized 4D coordinates (from six learnable tensors representing spatial and temporal planes), a Fourier time encoding for high-frequency temporal variations, and a learnable index embedding unique to each Gaussian particle. The `f_θ` MLP then jointly predicts the 3D **velocity vector `v(x, t)`** and the six independent components of the **Cauchy stress tensor `σ(x, t)`** for each Gaussian particle.

To enforce physical consistency, the **Cauchy momentum residual (L_CMR)** is integrated as a physics constraint. This term penalizes the L2 norm of the residual of the Cauchy momentum equation (Newton's second law for continua: `ρ(∂v/∂t + (v · ∇)v) - ∇ · σ = 0`). By treating velocity and constitutive stress as independent intrinsic attributes predicted by the material field, this loss term ensures that each Gaussian particle's motion and internal states evolve continuously in a physically consistent manner, eliminating the need for costly re-matching after densification.

#### 3.3. Lagrangian Particle Flow Matching

While the Cauchy momentum residual enforces physical laws, it might not always converge to unique or physically meaningful solutions on its own, especially without strong boundary conditions. To provide direct supervision for the predicted velocity field, PIDG introduces **Lagrangian particle flow matching**.

First, an improved **optical flow decomposition** strategy is used. Unlike prior forward-warping methods that can introduce artifacts, PIDG computes *backward motion flow* from `I_t+1` to `I_t`, warps it to `I_t`, and applies a motion mask. This results in an artifact-free, pseudo-ground-truth motion flow in the reference frame `I_t`.

Second, two types of rendered flows are computed and matched against this pseudo-ground-truth motion flow:
- **Gaussian Flow (`f_low_g`):** This is computed by propagating the top-K Gaussians (ranked by splatting weights) from time `t` to `t+1` according to their learned position updates, based on the principle of invariant normalized coordinates from GaussianFlow (Gao et al. 2024).
- **Velocity Flow (`f_low_v`):** This advects each Gaussian by its predicted velocity (`v`) from the time-evolving material field and projects it to `I_t`.

The **Lagrangian particle flow matching loss (L_LPFM)** is defined as the L1 norm discrepancy between both Gaussian flow and velocity flow and the ground-truth motion flow (with motion mask). This data-fitting term helps the predicted velocity and stress fields of the time-evolving material field converge reliably to physically plausible values, providing generalizable, physics-grounded regularization without explicit boundary conditions.

The entire system integrates these components into a fully differentiable pipeline, jointly optimizing rendering consistency (L_renders) and physical consistency (L_CMR + L_LPFM).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on a custom physics-driven dataset (PIDG), as well as standard synthetic (D-NeRF) and real-world (HyperNeRF) datasets, demonstrate the significant advantages of PIDG in achieving physically consistent and high-quality monocular dynamic novel-view synthesis.

#### 4.1. Performance on Custom PIDG Physics-Driven Dataset

- **Quantitative Superiority:** PIDG consistently achieves the best average results across various physics-driven scenarios, including human motion, cloth simulation, fluid smoke, soft-body dynamics, and elastic mechanics. It outperforms state-of-the-art methods like Grid4D, GaussianPredict, SC-GS, and D-2DGS in metrics such as PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS (Table 1).
- **Ablation Effectiveness:**
 - The static-dynamic decoupling (PIDG w/o L_CMR + L_LPFM) shows consistent gains, highlighting the benefit of separating static and dynamic components.
 - The time-evolving material field constrained by L_CMR alone (PIDG w/o L_LPFM) further improves performance, especially in non-conventional continuum cases.
 - The full PIDG model, including L_LPFM, yields the highest structural similarity, particularly in challenging fluid, soft-body, and elastic scenarios where deformation fields alone are insufficient.
- **Qualitative Consistency:** Visual results (Fig. 4, A6) show that PIDG models diverse dynamic motions while maintaining high-fidelity reconstruction in static regions, leading to coherent and generalizable motion representation, unlike baselines that struggle with complex physical deformations or specific motion patterns.

#### 4.2. Performance on HyperNeRF Real-World Dataset

- **Best Average Results:** PIDG achieves the best average quantitative results for monocular dynamic novel-view synthesis on the HyperNeRF dataset (Table 2).
- **Structural Similarity Improvement:** The incorporation of Lagrangian particle flow matching (L_LPFM) leads to a clear improvement in structural similarity, validating its effectiveness as a supervision strategy for real-world scenarios without ground-truth particle motion.
- **Qualitative Alignment:** Visual analysis (Fig. 5, A8) reveals significant improvements, with motion flow, velocity flow, and Gaussian flow showing more coherent and intuitive alignment compared to methods like MotionGS.

#### 4.3. Performance on D-NeRF Synthetic Dataset (Future Prediction and Regularization)

- **Improved Dynamic Novel-View Synthesis:** When the time-evolving material field (constrained by L_CMR only, as optical flow is unreliable due to discontinuous viewpoints) is plugged into baselines like GaussianPredict, SC-GS, and Grid4D, it yields consistent improvements in PSNR and SSIM across most synthetic scenes (Table 3, A4), acting as a physically consistent regularizer.
- **Enhanced Future Prediction:** The L_CMR-only material field also enhances future prediction capabilities, improving both MLP and GCN variants of GaussianPredict by strengthening Gaussian particle consistency during deformation and mitigating mode collapse (Table 3, A5).
- **Visualization of Physical Fields:** PIDG simultaneously models spatio-temporal structure and intrinsic physical properties, providing not only satisfactory RGB and depth renderings but also predicting coherent velocity and stress fields, where dynamic particles exhibit consistent velocity directions and magnitudes (Fig. A9).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Constitutive Relation:** Ablating the stress (σ) component from L_CMR simplifies the constraint to `∇·v=0`, enforcing smoothness but failing to describe real material interactions, demonstrating the importance of the full Cauchy momentum equation (Fig. 6).
- **t-SNE Analysis:** t-SNE embeddings show that PIDG preserves richer dynamics in the canonical space and maintains them after deformation, attributable to dynamic decoupling and inheritable embeddings of indices, velocities, and stresses.
- **Efficiency:** PIDG maintains competitive training efficiency, memory consumption, and rendering FPS compared to other state-of-the-art methods (Table A1).
- **Architectural Ablation:** Optimal performance is achieved with specific multi-head decoder depth and temporal grid hash encoder levels, demonstrating the robustness of the chosen architecture (Table A2).
- **Scaling Threshold Adjustment:** A simple adjustment to the Gaussian scaling threshold significantly reduces depth noise and improves geometric reconstruction by filtering noisy Gaussians (Fig. A4).

Overall, the findings underscore that PIDG not only achieves superior rendering quality in monocular dynamic novel-view synthesis but also significantly enhances physical consistency and generalization across a wide range of dynamic scenarios, from simple synthetic motions to complex real-world deformations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Physics-Informed Deformable Gaussian Splatting (PIDG)" paper presents a significant advancement in dynamic 3D scene representation and novel-view synthesis by explicitly integrating physical principles. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Unifying Data-Driven and Physics-Based Modeling:** PIDG is one of the first works to successfully unify an explicit, efficient scene representation (3D Gaussian Splatting) with time-evolving Lagrangian constitutive laws from continuum mechanics. This bridges a critical gap between purely data-driven methods, which often lack physical realism, and physics-based simulations, which are typically computationally expensive or require extensive ground-truth data.

**2. Enhanced Physical Consistency and Generalization:** By treating Gaussians as Lagrangian material points and enforcing the Cauchy momentum equation, PIDG ensures that generated dynamic scenes are not only visually plausible but also adhere to fundamental physical laws. This leads to significantly more consistent and realistic motion patterns, especially for deformable objects, fluids, and elastic bodies. The method demonstrates strong generalization across diverse physical scenarios (fluid, soft-body, elastic, cloth, human motion) from monocular input, a major challenge for prior works.

**3. Novel Surrogate Supervision for Motion:** The introduction of Lagrangian particle flow matching, guided by a refined backward optical flow decomposition, provides a powerful and artifact-free surrogate supervision signal. This innovatively addresses the challenge of supervising 3D particle motion from sparse 2D monocular videos without relying on expensive ground-truth 3D motion priors or strict boundary conditions, making the method broadly applicable.

**4. Intrinsic Property Modeling:** Beyond geometric and appearance reconstruction, PIDG is capable of predicting intrinsic physical properties such as velocity and stress fields. This opens up new avenues for understanding material behavior, performing inverse material property estimation, and generating more complex, interactive simulations grounded in real-world observations.

**5. Broadening Applications in Immersive Technologies:**
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** The ability to reconstruct physically consistent dynamic scenes from monocular video can greatly enhance the realism and interactivity of VR/AR content, allowing for dynamic virtual objects that behave realistically within real environments.
- **Game Development:** Game developers can leverage this technology to create more believable character animations, environmental effects (e.g., cloth, water, smoke), and physically accurate interactions with dynamic elements.
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** Creating physically realistic "world models" for AI agents or robots from observed videos can significantly improve their training and decision-making capabilities in dynamic environments.
- **Digital Twins and Engineering:** The method offers potential for creating digital twins of real-world dynamic systems, allowing for analysis and prediction of physical phenomena, such as material stress under deformation, in engineering and scientific applications.

**6. Advancing Research Paradigms:** The paper implicitly advocates for more comprehensive evaluation metrics in novel-view synthesis, moving beyond mere 2D image quality (PSNR, SSIM) to include 3D structural accuracy and physical consistency. This pushes the research community towards a more holistic understanding of 4D scene reconstruction.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While groundbreaking, the method still faces limitations. The optimization-based pipeline is computationally intensive, limiting scalability to very large scenes or real-time applications. The current PINN-based constitutive model, while effective, does not fully capture highly complex material behaviors like non-linear elastoplasticity. Future work will likely focus on lightweight feed-forward network architectures for real-time performance, integrating more advanced physical simulation techniques, and using scene segmentation with renormalization-group approaches to unify Gaussian particle ensembles for even more coherent and physically faithful reconstructions.

In conclusion, PIDG represents a significant step forward in making dynamic 3D content generation more realistic, generalizable, and physically grounded. Its novel integration of Lagrangian mechanics with Gaussian Splatting offers a powerful framework with far-reaching implications across various scientific and technological domains.
