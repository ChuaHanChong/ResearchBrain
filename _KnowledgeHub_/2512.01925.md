# Rectifying LLM Thought from Lens of Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> REPRO, a framework from Monash University and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, enhances large language model reasoning efficiency by reframing Chain-of-Thought processes as an optimization problem. The method achieves approximately 50% reduction in reasoning token cost and demonstrates improved performance across various benchmarks, including AIME24, GPQA-Diamond, and MBPP, for diverse LLM architectures.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) using long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting frequently "overthink," producing excessive tokens or unnecessarily long reasoning sequences.
- These inefficient reasoning paths lead to increased computational costs, higher latency, and reduced applicability in time-sensitive scenarios.
- Suboptimal reasoning patterns hinder efficient convergence to solutions, conceptually akin to optimization processes becoming trapped in local optima or oscillating around saddle points.

## Method

- Formulating a ==surrogate objective== function (J~) that tracks the LLM's confidence in the correct answer through its perplexity over the ground-truth token sequence.
- Developing a dual scoring mechanism, comprising a Magnitude Score (S_magn) for optimization progress and a Stability Score (S_stab) for detecting oscillatory behavior in reasoning.
- Integrating these scores into an entropy-based process-level reward system (REPRO), which is applied to selected high-entropy segments of reasoning trajectories during RL post-training.

## Results

- REPRO consistently improved LLM performance across PPO, REINFORCE++, and GRPO algorithms, showing gains such as PPO on AIME24 (**34.8%** to **36.3%**) and GRPO on GPQA-Diamond (**34.5%** to **37.0%**).
- It reduced reasoning token consumption by approximately **50%** compared to vanilla GRPO while achieving superior accuracy on various benchmarks.
- The method significantly decreased suboptimal thinking behaviors like "backtracking," promoted more linear thought processes, and maintained higher, more stable policy entropy during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM Chain-of-Thought reasoning can be effectively modeled as an iterative optimization process, allowing for the analysis and rectification of suboptimal trajectories.
> - Measuring the magnitude and stability of the model's confidence in the ground truth at each step provides a robust proxy for its internal optimization quality.
> - Applying process-level rewards to high-entropy segments of reasoning chains offers a targeted and efficient way to refine reasoning without excessive computational overhead or noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) have been significantly driven by their emergent reasoning capabilities, particularly through the application of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. This approach enables LLMs to explore complex problems and reflect on their reasoning processes, which is considered a pivotal step towards artificial general intelligence (AGI). State-of-the-art LLMs, including models from OpenAI, DeepSeek-AI, Kimi-Team, and Gemini, leverage long-CoT prompting to enhance their reasoning performance. Much of this improvement is attributed to reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) pipelines, which allow LLMs to refine their reasoning steps based on terminal feedback.

Despite these advancements, long-CoT LLMs frequently exhibit suboptimal reasoning behaviors. A notable issue is "overthinking," characterized by the generation of excessive tokens or unnecessarily protracted reasoning chains. These extended sequences often contribute minimally to problem resolution, leading to increased computational costs, higher latency, and limited applicability in time-sensitive domains. Prior research has identified this as a challenge, with models sometimes producing thousands of tokens in response to simple queries.

This work conceptualizes these suboptimal reasoning patterns through an optimization framework, drawing on perspectives from existing literature that treats LLM reasoning trajectories as optimization processes. In this paradigm, CoT is viewed as a variant of gradient descent, where each reasoning step constitutes an optimization update. From this viewpoint, suboptimal reasoning manifests as oscillations around saddle points or local optima, hindering efficient convergence to an optimal solution.

The current paper introduces REPRO (Rectifying Process-level Reward) to address these limitations. It contributes to the growing body of research focused on enhancing LLM reasoning and efficiency. While existing methods often rely on explicit process reward models or length-based regularization to improve efficiency, REPRO proposes a novel, model-agnostic approach. It differs from prior process reward models by not requiring additional trainable models and from length-based regularization by providing more nuanced, process-level feedback, aiming to refine reasoning patterns without unduly penalizing necessary steps. Thus, REPRO seeks to offer a plug-and-play solution that can be integrated into prevalent RLVR pipelines to guide LLMs towards more efficient and stable reasoning processes during post-training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed inefficiencies in the reasoning processes of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Large Language Models (LLMs). While these models exhibit emergent reasoning capabilities crucial for complex tasks, they frequently suffer from suboptimal behaviors, such as "overthinking." This phenomenon involves generating an excessive number of tokens or excessively prolonged reasoning chains that do not significantly contribute to problem resolution. These protracted reasoning paths lead to substantial computational costs, increased latency, and hinder the applicability of LLMs in contexts requiring timely responses.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel conceptual framework: analyzing LLM reasoning processes through an optimization lens. Within this framework, CoT is conceptualized as a gradient descent procedure, where each reasoning step represents an iterative update aimed at problem resolution. From this perspective, suboptimal reasoning behaviors, like overthinking, are reinterpreted as the model's internal optimization process becoming trapped in local optima or oscillating around saddle points, thereby preventing efficient convergence to the optimal solution.

Building upon this motivation and conceptualization, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Formulate a Surrogate Objective Function:** To define a measurable proxy metric, denoted as `J~`, that effectively captures the LLM's internal state during the reasoning process. This function is designed to quantify the model's evolving confidence in the ground-truth answer by measuring its perplexity over the correct token sequence, thereby serving as an observable indicator of the optimization trajectory.

2. **Develop a Dual Scoring Mechanism for Optimization Quality:** To introduce a quantitative system capable of assessing the quality of the LLM's reasoning process. This involves developing two distinct scores:
 - **Magnitude Score (`S_magn`)**: To measure the intensity or overall progress of the optimization, reflecting whether reasoning steps lead to substantial improvements in the objective function.
 - **Stability Score (`S_stab`)**: To evaluate the smoothness and efficiency of the optimization, quantifying the degree of oscillatory or redundant behavior in the reasoning steps.

3. **Integrate Scores into a Process-Level Reward System:** To combine the Magnitude and Stability Scores into a composite process-level reward that can be seamlessly incorporated into existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) pipelines. This reward is intended to provide fine-grained feedback during post-training, encouraging optimal reasoning behaviors.

4. **Enhance Reasoning Performance and Efficiency:** To empirically demonstrate that the proposed REPRO framework, through its process-level reward, consistently improves LLM reasoning performance across diverse benchmarks and mitigates suboptimal reasoning behaviors, leading to more concise and effective reasoning trajectories.

5. **Ensure Plug-and-Play Compatibility:** To design REPRO as a flexible and generalizable method compatible with various reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO) and diverse LLM architectures, facilitating its broad adoption.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to refine LLM thought processes, making them more efficient, reliable, and capable of tackling complex problems with optimized resource utilization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, REPRO (Rectifying Process-level Reward), aims to refine LLM reasoning during post-training by framing the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process as an optimization procedure.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The authors conceptualize the generation of a reasoning sequence (`τ_thinking`) by an LLM (`π_θ`) for a given question (`q`) as an optimization process. Each reasoning step is considered an iterative update to the LLM's internal states, aiming to increase the likelihood of generating the correct answer (`a`). This is framed as optimizing an objective function `J(π_θ, q, τ, a)` such that `θ* = arg max_θ J(π_θ, q, τ, a)`. The actual optimization process is acknowledged to be complex.

#### 3.2. Objective Function Definition

To monitor the implicit optimization process, a surrogate objective function, `J~`, is introduced. `J~(π_θ, q, τ_(≤t), a)` is defined as the average log probability (or negative perplexity) of the ground-truth answer tokens given the question `q` and the reasoning trajectory up to step `t`. This metric quantifies the model's confidence in the ground truth as it processes its reasoning steps. Empirical evidence is provided to support `J~` as an effective proxy, showing a gradual decrease in `-J~` (corresponding to an increase in `J~`) as reasoning trajectory length increases.

#### 3.3. Quantifying Optimization Process

The core of REPRO is a scoring mechanism `S` that evaluates the quality of the optimization process based on the dynamics of `J~` over a reasoning trajectory `τ`. This mechanism comprises two components:

- **Magnitude Score (`S_magn`)**: This score assesses the "intensity" or net improvement of the optimization process. For a partial trajectory `τ_(≤t)`, `S_magn,(t)` measures the relative increase of `J~` compared to a baseline `J_b(q)`, which is the direct probabilistic prediction of the ground truth without any CoT. The relative increase is normalized using the `tanh` function to constrain the score to (0, 1]. A higher `S_magn` indicates more substantial benefits from the reasoning steps.

- **Stability Score (`S_stab`)**: This score quantifies the "stability" of the optimization, detecting oscillatory behaviors. It is calculated using Kendall's Tau Correlation Coefficient between the sequence of `J~` values and their corresponding step indices. A score close to 1 indicates smooth, effective updates, while a score near 0 suggests ineffective or oscillating updates. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) smoothing can be applied to `J~` values to mitigate noise.

These two scores are combined into a final composite score `S = (1 - w) * S_magn + w * S_stab`, where `w` is a weighting factor (set to 0.5 in experiments).

#### 3.4. Learning with Rectifying Process-level Reward

The composite score `S` is integrated into the reinforcement learning (RL) training pipeline as a process reward.

- **Entropy-Based Selection Strategy**: To manage computational overhead and avoid introducing excessive noise from token-level reward calculation, `S` is computed only for selected segments of the reasoning trajectory. The `τ_thinking` sequence is divided into multiple segments (e.g., by two-line breaks). The top-k segments are chosen based on the entropy of their first token. High-entropy tokens are considered critical decision points where suboptimal optimization is more likely to occur, thus providing more informative reward signals.

- **Integrating `S` into Reward**: For the selected segments, individual scores `S_j` are computed. The rectifying process-level reward `˜r_j` for a segment `j` is defined as the gain from that segment: `S_j - S_{j-1}` for `j > 1`, and `S_1` for `j = 1`. This `˜r_j` penalizes suboptimal thinking and encourages optimal progress. These rewards are then normalized across trajectories within a group/batch to prevent interference from probabilistic prediction mismatches between different questions and to facilitate stable policy updates.

- **Overall Advantage Calculation**: The normalized process-level rewards contribute to a process-level advantage `˜A_t`. This is combined with the verifiable outcome advantage `A` (derived from the correctness of the overall trajectory) to form a total advantage `ˆA_t = A + α * ˜A_t`, where `α` is a weighting factor. The LLM parameters are then updated using `ˆA_t` via the clipped surrogate objective function (as in PPO or other critic-free RL algorithms). An empirical example (Example 3.1) illustrates that steps with low `˜r` often involve self-doubt or redundancy, while high `˜r` steps correspond to critical advancements in reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of REPRO was evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple reinforcement learning algorithms and diverse Large Language Models (LLMs), on benchmarks spanning mathematics, science, and coding.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **LLMs**: Experiments were conducted on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B, Hunyuan-1.8B-Instruct, and MobileLLM-R1-950M, covering various architectures and scales.
- **RL Algorithms**: PPO, REINFORCE++, REINFORCE++ Baseline, and GRPO were utilized.
- **Benchmarks**:
 - **Mathematical Reasoning**: AIME24, AIME25, MATH500, LiveMathBench.
 - **Scientific Reasoning**: GPQA-Diamond.
 - **Code Reasoning**: MBPP, LiveCodeBench.
- **Training Data**: Approximately 40,000 high-quality mathematical samples from the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset.
- **Hyperparameters**: Default `w=0.5` and `α=0.1`, with top-10 segments selected for reward calculation.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness and Generalization of REPRO

- **Consistent Improvements Across RL Algorithms**: REPRO consistently enhanced the performance of all tested RL baselines. For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, PPO with REPRO improved AIME24 accuracy from 34.8% to 36.3% and MATH500 from 86.9% to 87.7%. Similar gains were observed with REINFORCE++ and GRPO, indicating REPRO's algorithm-agnostic nature.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Benchmarks**: Beyond in-domain mathematical tasks, REPRO demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-domain benchmarks. For instance, GRPO with REPRO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B improved GPQA-Diamond accuracy from 34.5% to 37.0% and MBPP from 62.5% to 65.4%.
- **Generalization to Diverse LLMs**: REPRO's effectiveness extended across various LLM families and sizes, from 950M (MobileLLM-R1-950M) to 8B parameters (Qwen3-8B). This scalability suggests that REPRO provides a general mechanism for reasoning enhancement.
- **Performance on Zero-RLVR Models**: When applied to base models like Qwen3-4B-Base, REPRO demonstrated comparable or slightly better performance than vanilla GRPO, while exhibiting a more modest growth in token cost, suggesting its potential for developing efficient reasoning LLMs.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Magnitude Score & Stability Score**: Experiments varying the weighting factor `w` showed that REPRO consistently outperformed baselines across all `w` values. Performance was slightly higher when more emphasis was placed on the Magnitude Score (lower `w`), indicating that optimization intensity may be a more critical factor.
- **Impact of REPRO Weight (`α`)**: REPRO maintained relatively stable performance across different `α` values, demonstrating robustness to the balance between outcome and process-level advantages.
- **Impact of Number of Selected Segments (`k`)**: While increasing `k` yielded slight performance improvements, these gains became marginal beyond `k=20`, suggesting a trade-off between computational cost and performance. `k=10` was adopted as a balanced choice.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis of Thinking Behaviors

- **Improved Token Efficiency**: REPRO reduced reasoning token cost during both training and inference across all benchmarks and LLMs. For instance, REPRO reduced token consumption by approximately 50% compared to vanilla GRPO while achieving superior performance. This indicates promotion of more concise reasoning trajectories.
- **Reduction in Suboptimal Thinking Behaviors**: Quantitative analysis of thinking patterns revealed that REPRO significantly decreased the prevalence of "backtracking" patterns (indicative of ineffective or excessive reasoning) during training.
- **Entropy Dynamics**: REPRO sustained a significantly higher and more stable entropy level throughout training compared to vanilla GRPO, mitigating rapid policy collapse and fostering sustained exploration.
- **Case Studies**: Qualitative analysis highlighted that REPRO-trained models generated responses with fewer inefficient and suboptimal reasoning steps (e.g., self-doubt, redundant re-examinations), resulting in more linear, efficient thought processes and reduced errors compared to models trained with vanilla RL algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The REPRO framework contributes to the field of large language model (LLM) research by offering a method for refining reasoning processes during post-training. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Performance**: REPRO consistently improves the reasoning accuracy of LLMs across diverse and challenging domains, including mathematics, science, and coding. This indicates a potential to unlock more reliable and robust problem-solving capabilities in advanced LLM applications.

2. **Increased Computational Efficiency**: By addressing suboptimal reasoning behaviors such as "overthinking" and excessively protracted thought chains, REPRO leads to a reduction in generated tokens and, consequently, lower computational costs and latency. This makes LLMs more practical for deployment in resource-constrained environments or time-sensitive applications where efficiency is critical.

3. **Broad Applicability and Generalizability**: The plug-and-play nature of REPRO, demonstrated by its compatibility with various reinforcement learning algorithms (PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO) and diverse LLM architectures (from compact 950M models to larger 8B models), suggests its broad utility. This characteristic could allow existing LLMs to benefit from REPRO without extensive architectural modifications.

4. **Novel Conceptual Framework**: Framing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning as an optimization process, akin to gradient descent, provides a new theoretical lens for understanding and addressing LLM reasoning deficiencies. This perspective may inspire further research into the underlying mechanisms of LLM thought and more principled approaches to its improvement.

5. **Refinement of Reasoning Behaviors**: Beyond mere performance gains, REPRO's ability to reduce suboptimal thinking patterns, such as backtracking and redundant re-examination, and to maintain stable policy entropy, suggests a deeper refinement of the LLM's internal cognitive processes. This could lead to more human-like, coherent, and less circuitous reasoning.

6. **Contribution to Low-Cost Reasoning LLMs**: The observed modest token cost growth and consistent performance improvements when applying REPRO to base models suggest its potential in developing efficient, low-cost reasoning LLMs, which is a significant practical objective in the field.

While the paper acknowledges limitations regarding scalability to even larger LLMs and applicability to subjective reasoning tasks, REPRO represents a step towards creating more intelligent, efficient, and reliable LLM reasoning systems. Its approach of providing nuanced, process-level feedback based on optimization dynamics offers an alternative to purely outcome-based or length-penalized training methods.
