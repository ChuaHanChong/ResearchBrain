# Rectifying LLM Thought from Lens of Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> REPRO, a framework from Monash University and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory, enhances large language model reasoning efficiency by reframing Chain-of-Thought processes as an optimization problem. The method achieves approximately 50% reduction in reasoning token cost and demonstrates improved performance across various benchmarks, including AIME24, GPQA-Diamond, and MBPP, for diverse LLM architectures.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) using long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting frequently "overthink," producing excessive tokens or unnecessarily long reasoning sequences.
- These inefficient reasoning paths lead to increased computational costs, higher latency, and reduced applicability in time-sensitive scenarios.
- Suboptimal reasoning patterns hinder efficient convergence to solutions, conceptually akin to optimization processes becoming trapped in local optima or oscillating around saddle points.

## Method

- Formulating a ==surrogate objective== function (J~) that tracks the LLM's confidence in the correct answer through its perplexity over the ground-truth token sequence.
- Developing a dual scoring mechanism, comprising a Magnitude Score (S_magn) for optimization progress and a Stability Score (S_stab) for detecting oscillatory behavior in reasoning.
- Integrating these scores into an entropy-based process-level reward system (REPRO), which is applied to selected high-entropy segments of reasoning trajectories during RL post-training.

## Results

- REPRO consistently improved LLM performance across PPO, REINFORCE++, and GRPO algorithms, showing gains such as PPO on AIME24 (**34.8%** to **36.3%**) and GRPO on GPQA-Diamond (**34.5%** to **37.0%**).
- It reduced reasoning token consumption by approximately **50%** compared to vanilla GRPO while achieving superior accuracy on various benchmarks.
- The method significantly decreased suboptimal thinking behaviors like "backtracking," promoted more linear thought processes, and maintained higher, more stable policy entropy during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM Chain-of-Thought reasoning can be effectively modeled as an iterative optimization process, allowing for the analysis and rectification of suboptimal trajectories.
> - Measuring the magnitude and stability of the model's confidence in the ground truth at each step provides a robust proxy for its internal optimization quality.
> - Applying process-level rewards to high-entropy segments of reasoning chains offers a targeted and efficient way to refine reasoning without excessive computational overhead or noise.
