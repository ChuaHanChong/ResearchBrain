# Real-IKEA: Physical Fidelity is the Prerequisite for Robust Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Real-IKEA is a simulation dataset and framework treating physical fidelity as a first-class goal for articulated manipulation. It supplies 1,079 articulated configurations built from 83 authentic IKEA handles and knobs via a six-step workflow, with COACD convex-decomposed collision meshes, a bidirectional surface-deviation metric, and calibrated joint damping/friction. A PPO policy trained on these assets discovers hooking and levering form-closure strategies that survive high joint resistance.

## Problem

- Existing articulated datasets use synthetic or simplified CAD assets whose geometric distributions diverge from real domestic furniture, creating out-of-distribution risk for policies.
- Coarse convex-hull collision meshes seal off holes, grooves and cavities, so simulators silently forbid the geometry-exploiting contacts robots need for form closure.
- Prior benchmarks omit configurable joint damping and friction, letting friction-dominated parallel-jaw pull policies look successful while they slip under real mechanical resistance.

## Method

- Six-step asset workflow: component segmentation, structural cleaning of self-colliding internals, pre-decomposition scaling/clipping, ==COACD== collision generation, joint parameterization with calibrated damping/friction, and ==MuJoCo== XML integration.
- ==Bidirectional surface-deviation metric== computes mean nearest-neighbor distances between densely sampled visual-mesh and collision-mesh points in both directions, quantifying collision-shell swelling per asset.
- Privileged ==PPO== policy observes handle position, drawer joint state, gripper aperture and stage one-hot, emits 5D incremental end-effector plus gripper actions through ==inverse kinematics== under a four-stage log-shaped reward.

## Results

- Under high joint resistance (damping **10**, friction **20**), GraspGen and heuristic friction pulling both hit **0%** success on knobs and finger-pull handles, while human teleoperation retains **38%** and **35%** full success.
- On two-point handles human teleoperation succeeds **100%** at every resistance level, versus **11%** for GraspGen and **0%** for the heuristic baseline at high resistance.
- The best RL checkpoint (iteration **2550**) reaches near-**100%** zero-shot success across all three resistance profiles, whereas policies trained on coarse meshes converge to fragile friction pulling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Collision-mesh approximation error is a hidden benchmark confound: swollen convex hulls remove affordances, so the strategy space itself, not just the visuals, is misspecified.
> - Robustness comes from converting actuator effort into torque via mechanical interlocking; friction-only grasps guarantee neither force nor form closure and fail once resistance exceeds the frictional limit.
> - Resistance should be a swept simulation axis, since low-friction defaults mask policy fragility and let weak strategies score perfectly in easy settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic manipulation policies often relies on large-scale data generation through simulation environments due to the scalability and cost-effectiveness they offer. This approach, often termed the "data pyramid paradigm," has demonstrated success in fields such as locomotion and navigation. However, achieving robust performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks, where physical interactions (e.g., contact location, leverage, and mechanical resistance) are critical, has remained a significant challenge.

A persistent issue in this domain is the "physics gap" or "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in simplified simulations fail to generalize to the complexities of the real world. While prior work has addressed visual domain gaps and improved visual fidelity or task diversity in simulation datasets (e.g., PartNet-Mobility, AdaManip, ManiSkill), a fundamental limitation persists in the physical fidelity of simulated articulated objects. Existing datasets often employ synthetic assets or simplified CAD models with coarse collision meshes. These simplifications, such as treating non-convex features as solid convex volumes, lead to inaccurate contact geometries and dynamics, effectively "sealing off" crucial interaction spaces (e.g., holes, grooves) necessary for advanced manipulation strategies.

Contemporary robotic policies frequently default to simple parallel-jaw grasps followed by linear pulling actions. These friction-dominated strategies are inherently fragile because they rely on surface friction, which provides neither force nor form closure. Consequently, these methods are prone to failure when faced with real-world mechanical resistance (e.g., heavy drawers) or low-friction materials. In contrast, human manipulation robustness often derives from exploiting object geometry through behaviors like "hooking" fingers into handle gaps or "levering" against surfaces, which provide mechanical interlocking and form closure, offering resistance to slipping.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by asserting that accurate physical fidelity, specifically regarding contact geometry and realistic joint dynamics, is a prerequisite for learning robust, geometry-exploiting manipulation policies. It addresses the overlooked limitations of existing simulation benchmarks by providing a framework designed to reproduce real-world physical challenges and enable the discovery of robust manipulation strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to emphasize and establish physical realism in articulated interaction as a critical component for robust policy learning in robotics. The authors contend that the current physics gap in simulations, particularly concerning contact geometry and joint dynamics, fundamentally limits the types of robust manipulation strategies that can be learned.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are:

- **Highlighting the Criticality of Physical Fidelity:** The work is motivated by the observation that many failures in contact-rich robotic manipulation are mechanical, rather than purely visual, stemming from discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics. The authors aim to demonstrate that accurate physical modeling is not merely an improvement but a prerequisite for enabling robust behaviors.
- **Addressing Deficiencies in Existing Datasets:** Current articulated object benchmarks are identified as lacking in physical fidelity. Datasets often feature synthetic or simplified assets with coarse collision meshes that do not accurately represent real-world objects, particularly in regions crucial for contact interactions like handle holes or fine grooves. This leads to out-of-distribution risks for policies deployed in real domestic environments.
- **Enabling Geometry-Exploiting Manipulation:** Conventional, friction-dominated manipulation strategies are shown to be fragile and ineffective under realistic mechanical resistance. The research aims to develop a simulation environment that allows robots to discover and exploit mechanical advantage through form-closure strategies, analogous to how humans use "hooking" or "levering" to interact with objects. This requires re-enabling critical affordances that are often "sealed off" in simplified simulations.
- **Developing a High-Fidelity Dataset and Simulation Framework:** The core objective is to introduce "Real-IKEA," a comprehensive framework that prioritizes physical accuracy. This involves:
 - **Creating a Physically Accurate Dataset:** Constructing a large-scale library of articulated assets (1,079 configurations) derived from authentic IKEA products, ensuring that geometric distributions are strictly aligned with reality.
 - **Implementing High-Fidelity Contact Modeling:** Replacing conventional coarse convex hulls with precise convex decompositions for collision meshes. This includes introducing a bidirectional surface-deviation metric (E_Q→P, E_P→Q) to quantify collision mesh accuracy and ensure that non-convex features and cavities are preserved.
 - **Calibrating Realistic Joint Dynamics:** Establishing resistance-calibrated configurations with varying damping and friction parameters to simulate real-world mechanical resistance accurately.
- **Validating the Necessity of Physical Fidelity for Robustness:** Through empirical validation using a Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy, the authors aim to demonstrate that training on high-fidelity assets enables the emergence of robust, form-closure strategies that succeed where friction-dominated approaches fail. This validates the hypothesis that physical fidelity is the foundational driver of manipulation robustness.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the "physics gap" in simulation, particularly for contact-rich tasks, by meticulously reconstructing the physical properties of real-world articulated objects, thereby enabling the development and evaluation of truly robust robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of Real-IKEA, a dataset and simulation framework designed with physical accuracy as a primary objective. The approach involves a multi-step process for asset construction, precise contact modeling, calibrated joint dynamics, and empirical validation through a Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework.

**A. Dataset Construction: Grounding Assets in Reality**
1. **Selection of Authentic Products:** The authors chose authentic IKEA products, specifically handles, knobs, and cabinets, due to their globally standardized yet diverse nature and modular design philosophy. This ensures a realistic geometric distribution rather than synthetic shapes.
2. **Modular Combinatorial Pairing:** A library of 83 authentic IKEA handles and knobs was curated. By systematically combining these with IKEA cabinet base units, 1,079 articulated configurations were generated. This approach aims to provide a large-scale and morphologically diverse dataset.
3. **Six-Step Physical Workflow for Asset Generation:** Each high-fidelity articulated asset undergoes a meticulous six-step processing workflow:
 - **Component Segmentation and Formatting:** Manually segmenting visual meshes into distinct components (cabinet base, drawers, doors, interactive parts) and converting formats with texture maps.
 - **Structural Cleaning for Simulation:** Manual refinement to remove internal features that would cause problematic self-collisions in the simulator while preserving external interaction surfaces.
 - **Pre-Collision Scaling and Clipping:** Manual scaling or clipping of specific joint regions to counteract the "swelling" effect of approximate convex decomposition, preventing tightly fitted components from getting stuck.
 - **Collision Mesh Generation:** Application of the COACD algorithm to cleaned non-convex visual meshes to produce high-fidelity convex decomposition, generating hundreds of convex primitives per asset.
 - **Assembly and Joint Parameterization:** Manual assembly of components, accurate determination of joint positions, axes, and realistic ranges, with crucial calibration of joint damping and friction parameters to reflect real-world mechanical resistance.
 - **Simulation Integration:** Assembly of visual meshes, accurate collision meshes, and joint kinematics into a unified XML format compatible with the MuJoCo simulator.

**B. Closing the Contact-Geometry Gap**
1. **High-Fidelity Collision Meshes:** The COACD algorithm is used to generate collision meshes consisting of hundreds of high-fidelity convex primitives. This is a departure from previous datasets that often use simplified convex hulls, which "fill in" non-convex features critical for interaction.
2. **Bidirectional Surface Deviation Metric:** To quantitatively evaluate the accuracy of collision meshes, a novel bidirectional metric is introduced:
 - E_Q→P: Measures the outward deviation (mesh swelling) from the collision shell (Q) to the ground-truth visual mesh (P). For each point q ∈ Q, its nearest-neighbor distance to P is computed.
 - E_P→Q: Symmetrically measures the deviation from P to Q, providing a comprehensive measure of geometric discrepancy. This metric is used to demonstrate that Real-IKEA's meshes significantly reduce deviation compared to baselines, preserving critical cavities.
3. **Calibrated Joint Resistance:** The framework includes configurable joint resistance parameters (damping and friction) that can be varied, enabling evaluation under different levels of mechanical challenge.

**C. Evaluating Robustness: Case Study with Drawer Opening**
1. **Task Definition:** A canonical task of opening a drawer with a parallel-jaw gripper is selected.
2. **Resistance Modes:** Three distinct resistance settings are configured: Smooth (damping=2, friction=5), Normal (damping=5, friction=10), and High Resistance (damping=10, friction=20).
3. **Policy Comparisons:** Three types of policies are compared:
 - **Human Teleoperation:** Serves as an upper bound for performance, demonstrating robust human capabilities.
 - **Ground-truth Baselines (Heuristic):** Implements heuristic friction-based pulling strategies.
 - **GraspGen:** A state-of-the-art model predicting 6-DoF grasp poses, representing modern data-driven approaches.

**D. Discovering Robustness: Reinforcement Learning Paradigm**
1. **RL Framework:** A Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework is designed to empirically validate that high-fidelity assets enable geometry-exploiting strategies. The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is utilized for policy training.
2. **Privileged State Observation:** The RL agent receives a low-dimensional privileged state observation, including precise handle coordinates, drawer joint states, the 6D pose of the end-effector, gripper aperture, previous action, and a four-dimensional staged reward indicator. This provides comprehensive information to facilitate complex interaction.
3. **Action Parameterization:** The policy outputs a 5D continuous action vector (∆x, ∆y, ∆z, ∆pitch, and gripper action). These are scaled to physical increments (e.g., α_pitch = 0.04 rad, α_trans = 0.01 m) and mapped to physical movements via an Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver.
4. **Staged Reward Curriculum:** Rewards are structured into four stages to encourage a coarse ordering of actions:
 - Stage 1: Approach a pre-grasp region with an open gripper.
 - Stage 2: Refine approach to the handle neighborhood.
 - Stage 3: Achieve a grasp-ready closure.
 - Stage 4: Open the drawer, with a logarithmic reward to prioritize overcoming static friction. Monotonicity is preserved across stage boundaries.
5. **Domain Randomization and Two-Phase Training:** Training proceeds in two phases. Initially, handle and robot base poses are fixed to stabilize learning. After 900 policy iterations, per-episode pose perturbations are introduced (domain randomization) to enhance policy robustness and generalization.
6. **Policy Selection:** Intermediate checkpoints are evaluated zero-shot across the three joint resistance profiles (Smooth, Normal, High Resistance) to select the most robust policy for deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the impact of physical fidelity on robotic manipulation robustness:

**A. Geometric Fidelity and Collision Accuracy**
- **Quantified Improvement:** The bidirectional surface deviation metric (E_Q→P) demonstrated that Real-IKEA's meticulously processed meshes achieved significantly lower geometric "swelling" compared to baseline collision meshes. Baseline meshes exhibited massive deviations, particularly in mechanically vital regions such as inner handle loops, deep grooves, and curved support structures.
- **Preservation of Affordances:** Real-IKEA's optimized meshes successfully preserved critical cavities and non-convex features, which are typically "filled in" by coarse approximations in standard simulators. This re-enabled the physical affordances necessary for form-closure strategies (e.g., a robot finger passing through a handle).
- **High-Resolution Basis:** The interactive parts in Real-IKEA maintain significantly higher vertex and face counts compared to standard datasets, providing the high-resolution basis required for robust collision modeling.

**B. Performance of Manipulation Policies under Joint Resistance**
- **Collapse of Friction-Based Strategies:** In the drawer opening task, both the state-of-the-art GraspGen and heuristic friction-based baselines performed adequately in the "Smooth" resistance setting. However, their success rates drastically collapsed under "Normal" and "High Resistance" conditions, often reaching 0% success for knobs and finger-pull handles under high friction. This confirmed that relying solely on surface friction for pinching is insufficient when the required pulling force exceeds the frictional limit.
- **Robustness of Human Teleoperation:** In contrast, human teleoperation maintained high success rates across all resistance levels and handle types, achieving perfect success on two-point handles even under "High Resistance." This was attributed to humans exploiting form-closure by inserting fingers or bracing against curvature, creating mechanical locks independent of surface friction.
- **Dependence on Handle Type:** The fragility of friction-based methods was more pronounced for knobs and finger-pull handles, which offer fewer opportunities for form-closure without precise geometric interaction. Two-point handles, offering clearer "hooking" affordances, showed relatively better, though still limited, performance from automated policies under high resistance, while human teleoperation remained consistently robust.

**C. Emergence of Form-Closure Strategies via Reinforcement Learning**
- **Qualitative Shift in Learned Behavior:** When the RL policy was trained on standard, simplified collision meshes, it converged to naive, friction-based pulling behaviors that failed under realistic resistance.
- **Discovery of Geometry Exploitation:** However, when trained on Real-IKEA assets with their high-fidelity contact geometry and calibrated resistance, the RL policy discovered distinct local optima. Specifically, for two-point handles, the agent learned to thread its gripper or "finger" completely through the handle loop before initiating a pull. This "hooking" behavior represents a form-closure strategy.
- **Empirical Proof of Concept:** The emergence of these advanced mechanical interactions (e.g., hooking, leveraging) demonstrated that physical fidelity in the simulation environment is a prerequisite for the natural discovery of human-level robust manipulation strategies. The RL agent, given the appropriate physical modeling, was able to escape the "friction trap" and exploit object geometry for mechanical advantage.
- **Policy Robustness:** Evaluation of RL checkpoints showed that the selected policy (Checkpoint 2550) achieved near-perfect success rates across all three joint resistance profiles (Smooth, Normal, High Resistance), indicating generalization and robustness to varying physical conditions once geometry-exploiting strategies were learned.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Real-IKEA framework, and the findings presented in this paper, hold several significant implications for the field of robotic manipulation and reinforcement learning:

- **Elevating the Importance of Physical Fidelity:** The work unequivocally demonstrates that physical fidelity, specifically accurate contact geometry and calibrated joint dynamics, is not a minor detail but a fundamental prerequisite for developing robust manipulation policies. It shifts focus from purely visual fidelity or task diversity to the underlying physical realism of the simulation environment.
- **Bridging the "Physics Gap" in Simulation:** Real-IKEA provides a concrete solution for significantly narrowing the "physics gap" in contact-rich manipulation tasks. By meticulously constructing digital twins of real-world objects with precise collision meshes and realistic joint resistance, it enables simulations to reproduce challenges that were previously masked by oversimplification.
- **Enabling Discovery of Advanced Manipulation Strategies:** The empirical demonstration that an RL agent, when trained on high-fidelity assets, naturally discovers complex, geometry-exploiting strategies (e.g., hooking, levering) is a critical step. This suggests that with sufficiently accurate physical modeling, robots can learn to achieve mechanical interlocking (form closure) rather than relying on fragile friction-based interactions. This has the potential to unlock a new class of robust manipulation behaviors previously inaccessible in simulation.
- **New Benchmark for Robustness Research:** Real-IKEA serves as a critical benchmark for future research in robust manipulation. Its large-scale, high-fidelity library of articulated configurations and the quantitative metrics for collision accuracy provide a standardized platform for developing and evaluating policies capable of human-level robustness in articulated object tasks. Researchers can now train and test agents in environments that faithfully reproduce real-world mechanical challenges.
- **Implications for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By ensuring greater fidelity in physical interactions, Real-IKEA is expected to improve the sim-to-real transferability of learned manipulation policies. Policies developed in this more realistic simulation environment are likely to exhibit better performance when deployed on physical robots.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** The insights from Real-IKEA contribute to the broader field of embodied AI by emphasizing that an accurate physical understanding of the environment is essential for intelligent agents to perform complex, contact-rich tasks with reliability and resilience. This could lead to more capable and adaptable robots in domestic and industrial settings.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work is likely to inspire further research into developing more sophisticated collision modeling techniques, methods for automatic calibration of physical parameters, and novel RL algorithms specifically designed to exploit complex physical interactions enabled by high-fidelity simulations.
