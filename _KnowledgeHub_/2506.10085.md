# VITA: Zero-Shot Value Functions via Test-Time Adaptation of Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Imperial College London developed VITA, a method for zero-shot value function learning that improves Vision-Language Models' generalization and temporal reasoning via test-time adaptation. VITA effectively provides reward shaping for offline reinforcement learning, achieving an interquartile mean return of 0.815 on Meta-World MT10, which exceeds the simulation's native fuzzy-logic dense rewards.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with frozen pre-trained representations struggle to generalize effectively to novel, out-of-distribution robotic tasks, environments, or robot embodiments.
- Current VLM-based approaches often lack robust temporal reasoning, failing to capture sequential context necessary for accurate task progress estimation and distinguishing visually similar states at different task stages.
- Developing generalist robotic policies or value functions typically requires extensive expert demonstrations, large-scale domain-specific fine-tuning, or massive multimodal pre-training, which are resource-intensive.

## Method

- **VITA** combines a frozen contrastive VLM (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32) with a lightweight, meta-learned adaptation module and a regression head for goal-conditioned value function estimation.
- The adaptation module is updated online at each test-time step via a single gradient step on a self-supervised reconstruction loss, which implicitly encodes temporal history into its parameters.
- A dissimilarity-based sampling strategy is used during meta-training to construct mini-batches from visually diverse sub-trajectories, mitigating shortcut learning and encouraging reliance on semantic cues.

## Results

- VITA demonstrated superior Value Order Correlation (VOC) for predicting task progress across diverse out-of-distribution environments, tasks, and robot embodiments, outperforming autoregressive VLMs and recurrent VLM baselines in most scenarios.
- The method achieved perfect discrimination (BinVOC = 1.0) between expert and non-expert trajectories, consistently assigning higher VOC scores to optimal paths across five scripted datasets.
- Utilized for zero-shot reward shaping in offline reinforcement learning on Meta-World MT10, VITA yielded an interquartile mean return of **0.815**, surpassing the simulation's native fuzzy-logic dense rewards (**0.779**) and other CLIP-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time adaptation of a small, meta-learned module can effectively imbue frozen VLM representations with both generalization capabilities and robust temporal reasoning without extensive retraining.
> - Implicitly retaining temporal context within the adapter's parameters through sequential, per-timestep updates is more effective for value function estimation and less prone to overfitting than explicit recurrent models or memoryless adaptation.
> - Mitigating shortcut learning through dissimilarity-based sampling during meta-training is crucial for developing value functions that rely on meaningful semantic and temporal understanding rather than superficial chronological cues.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist agents capable of performing diverse tasks in complex environments is a central aim in artificial intelligence research, particularly in robotics and embodied AI. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated strong generalization capabilities by leveraging large-scale, unstructured web data, offering a potential pathway toward more adaptable robotic policies. However, achieving comparable success in robotics has been challenging due to the typical reliance on expert demonstrations or extensive supervised training for state-of-the-art methods.

World models represent a promising direction for enabling agents to learn in simulated environments and generate realistic visual trajectories at scale. A critical challenge within this framework is to enable agents to learn effectively from these vast video datasets without substantial human supervision. Current research explores two main approaches: inferring actions directly from expert visual trajectories or learning a universal goal-conditioned value function. The latter approach formulates goal-conditioned value estimation as predicting task progress from visual observations and natural language descriptions, serving as a zero-shot reward shaping or supervision signal for reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning.

Prior work has utilized pre-trained contrastive VLMs for zero-shot reward shaping by measuring semantic similarity between observations and task descriptions. However, these methods often fail to capture the temporal context necessary for distinguishing visually similar states at different task stages. Autoregressive VLMs attempt to incorporate temporal context by processing entire trajectories, but they can inherit biases (e.g., toward monotonically increasing predictions) from their chronologically ordered pre-training datasets. A common limitation across many VLM architectures is their reliance on frozen pre-trained representations for zero-shot prediction, which can constrain their generalization ability and temporal reasoning. To address this, previous efforts have focused on large-scale pre-training, domain-specific fine-tuning, or demonstration-based reward specification.

This work addresses the limitations of existing VLM-based approaches by introducing a method that enhances both generalization and temporal reasoning without requiring extensive pre-training or task-specific demonstrations. It builds upon test-time adaptation paradigms, extending their application from static image classification to sequence modeling and goal-conditioned value function learning, where temporal context is crucial. By focusing on adapting a lightweight module at inference time, VITA aims to improve value estimation in a zero-shot setting, thereby offering a more flexible and adaptable solution compared to methods that rely solely on fixed pre-trained representations or complex autoregressive models for temporal context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce VITA, a zero-shot value function learning method designed to enhance both the generalization capabilities and temporal reasoning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation tasks.

The motivation for developing VITA stems from several identified limitations in the current application of VLMs for goal-conditioned value function estimation:

1. **Limited Generalization of Frozen VLM Representations:** While VLMs show promise for zero-shot goal-conditioned value functions by learning from large-scale web data, their pre-trained representations are often frozen. This rigidity restricts their ability to generalize effectively to new, out-of-distribution tasks, environments, or robot embodiments encountered at inference time. The research aims to overcome this by enabling dynamic adaptation to novel contexts.

2. **Inadequate Temporal Reasoning in Current VLM Approaches:** Existing VLM-based methods, particularly those relying on contrastive similarity, often fail to capture the sequential temporal context crucial for accurate progress estimation. For instance, distinguishing between visually similar states that occur at different points in a task (e.g., folding versus unfolding an object) requires robust temporal understanding. Autoregressive VLMs attempt to address this by conditioning on entire trajectories, but they can suffer from biases toward monotonically increasing predictions, inherent from their pre-training data. VITA seeks to explicitly encode trajectory history to address these temporal reasoning deficiencies.

3. **Reducing Reliance on Extensive Supervision and Pre-training:** Many state-of-the-art methods for learning generalist policies in robotics or for value function estimation require either extensive expert demonstrations, large-scale domain-specific fine-tuning, or massive multimodal pre-training. The motivation is to develop a method that achieves strong generalization without these resource-intensive requirements, allowing for more efficient and adaptable deployment in new scenarios.

4. **Mitigating Shortcut Learning:** Expert visual trajectories often contain high redundancy in consecutive frames, which can lead models to exploit "shortcut cues" by overfitting to common visual patterns rather than learning robust semantic or temporal understanding. A key motivation is to design a training strategy that encourages reliance on meaningful semantic cues and discourages overfitting to spurious correlations.

5. **Enabling Scalable Learning from Videos without Human Supervision:** The broader goal of enabling agents to learn from video data at scale without relying on human supervision remains a significant challenge. By developing a generalizable zero-shot value function, this work aims to provide a critical component for effectively learning from large volumes of unstructured visual data, facilitating the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems.

In essence, VITA is motivated by the need for a value function estimator that is not only generalizable across diverse conditions but also possesses strong temporal reasoning capabilities, all while operating efficiently in a zero-shot manner at inference time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VITA (Zero-shot Value Functions via Test-Time Adaptation) proposes a method for learning goal-conditioned value functions by enhancing generalization and temporal reasoning through test-time adaptation of Vision-Language Models. The approach combines a pre-trained VLM with a lightweight adaptation module and a regression head, trained using a meta-learning framework and a dissimilarity-based sampling strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The VITA value function estimator consists of three primary components:

1. **Multimodal Input Representation:** A frozen contrastive Vision-Language Model, specifically CLIP (OpenCLIP ViT-B/32), is used as the backbone. This encoder extracts joint visual-language representations from visual observations (`o_t`) and a natural language task description (`g`). At each timestep `t`, the visual embedding `ϕ_v(o_t)` and language embedding `ϕ_g(g)` are concatenated to form a joint multimodal representation `z_t ∈ ℝ^(2d)`. CLIP's pre-training objective, which aligns image-text pairs, positions semantically closer observations nearer to the goal description in the shared embedding space.

2. **Test-Time Adaptation Module (`f_adapt`):** To adapt the multimodal representations to specific semantic and temporal contexts encountered during inference, a lightweight adaptation module `f_adapt` is employed. This module is updated online at each timestep `t` via a single gradient step on a meta-learned self-supervised loss `ℓ_self`. The self-supervised loss is formulated as a reconstruction objective:
 `ℓ_self(z_t; θ_(t-1), P_K, P_V) = ||f_adapt(P_K z_t; θ_(t-1)) - P_V z_t||^2`,
 where `θ_(t-1)` are the parameters of `f_adapt` before the current update, and `P_K ∈ ℝ^(d' × 2d)` and `P_V ∈ ℝ^(d' × 2d)` are learnable linear projections that generate a perturbed input view and a target, respectively. Through sequential updates (`θ_t = θ_(t-1) - η∇_θ ℓ_self(z_t; θ_(t-1))`), `f_adapt` implicitly retains information from past visual observations, effectively encoding temporal history into its parameters.

3. **Zero-Shot Value Function Estimator Head:** After test-time adaptation, the input `z_t` is first mapped into an adaptation space `ℝ^(d')` by another meta-learned linear projection `P_Q ∈ ℝ^(d' × 2d)`. This representation `P_Q z_t` is then processed by the adapted `f_adapt` module. Finally, a two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) regression head `h` outputs the predicted goal-conditioned value `V(z_t; g) = h(f_adapt(P_Q z_t; θ_t))`. During inference, the regression head `h` remains frozen, while `f_adapt` is updated online.

#### 3.2. Training Procedure

The model is trained using a gradient-based meta-learning approach, optimizing `ℓ_self` such that test-time adaptation improves the supervised prediction loss `ℓ_pred`.

1. **Meta-Learning:** The training objective minimizes a combined loss that includes the supervised prediction loss `ℓ_pred` (mean squared error between predicted values and normalized progress labels `y_t` from expert demonstrations, `y_t = t/T`) and the self-supervised loss `ℓ_self`, weighted by a scalar `λ`. The meta-learning framework differentiates through the adaptation updates of `f_adapt` to optimize its initial parameters `θ_0`, the linear projections `P_K`, `P_V`, `P_Q`, and the regression head `h`. This process enables the model to learn how to adapt its internal representations at test time to improve value function estimation.

2. **Dissimilarity-Based Sampling:** To mitigate shortcut learning—where the model might overfit to redundant, chronologically ordered frames in expert trajectories—a dissimilarity-based sampling strategy is introduced. Instead of using all frames or randomly sampled ones, mini-batches are constructed from the most visually diverse sub-trajectories within each expert video.
 Given a sequence of multimodal representations `{z_1..., z_T}`, fixed-length sub-trajectories are extracted using a sliding window. A diverse subset of size `k` is then selected by maximizing pairwise dissimilarity. This is approximated by assigning each window `w` a score equal to its total dissimilarity with all other windows and selecting the `k` highest-scoring windows. This strategy increases intra-batch variance and encourages the model to rely on semantic cues rather than chronological position alone.

#### 3.3. Training Setup

VITA is trained on a curated subset of 2,986 expert visual demonstrations from the BridgeData V2 dataset, focusing on pick-and-place manipulation tasks within four configurations of the ToyKitchen environment, using a WidowX 250 robot. OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 is used as the frozen backbone. The training objective combines `ℓ_pred` and `ℓ_self` with `λ_self = 0.5`. Dissimilarity-based sampling uses a window size `w_tr = 8`, `k = 8` sub-trajectories, and a stride `s = 1`. At test time, a single gradient step (`t_ep = 1`) with a learning rate `η = 0.1` is used for adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VITA focused on its core capabilities: generalization under distribution shifts, differentiation between expert and non-expert trajectories, and zero-shot reward shaping for offline reinforcement learning.

#### 4.1. Generalization Under Distribution Shifts

VITA's generalization ability was assessed across novel environments, tasks, and robot embodiments using diverse subsets of the BridgeData V2 dataset. Performance was measured using the Value Order Correlation (VOC) metric, which quantifies the Spearman rank correlation between predicted task progress and chronological frame order.

- **Comparison with Baselines:** VITA and CLIP-GRU consistently outperformed autoregressive VLM-based methods (GVL-0S and GVL-1S) and CLIP-based baselines lacking temporal modeling (VLM-CL, VLM-RM, CLIP-FT). This highlights the importance of preserving temporal order for effective progress estimation.
- **VITA vs. CLIP-GRU:** VITA demonstrated superior performance over CLIP-GRU on the majority of evaluation datasets (6 out of 10), indicating that encoding temporal history via test-time adaptation can be more effective than modeling it through recurrent hidden states for certain scenarios.
- **GVL Performance:** GVL methods showed inconsistent performance, performing well on folding tasks but less effectively on stacking and pick-and-place tasks. This suggests a potential bias in the autoregressive VLM used by GVL.
- **Consistency and Robustness:** VITA maintained consistent performance across various task types and distribution shifts (environment shifts, embodiment shifts, and combined shifts), indicating stronger generalization compared to in-context learning approaches like GVL.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** On the long-horizon `ms sweep` task, VITA achieved the highest VOC score, outperforming CLIP-GRU, which further supports the effectiveness of its test-time adaptation for complex, sequential tasks.
- **Embodiment Shifts:** VITA exhibited strong generalization under embodiment shifts. In some cases (e.g., `dt tk pnp`), VITA and CLIP-GRU even exceeded their in-distribution performance, suggesting that learned temporal context can transfer across different robot morphologies.

#### 4.2. Differentiating Expert from Non-Expert Trajectories

The ability to distinguish expert (optimal) from suboptimal visual trajectories was evaluated using the BinVOC metric, defined as `1` if `VOC_exp > VOC_subopt` and `0` otherwise, averaged across five scripted (non-expert) datasets.

- **Discriminative Success:** VITA, GVL-0S, and GVL-1S achieved perfect discrimination (BinVOC = 1.0), consistently assigning higher VOC scores to expert trajectories.
- **VITA vs. CLIP-GRU:** VITA outperformed CLIP-GRU, suggesting that its implicit memory mechanism through sequential test-time updates may be more robust to overfitting temporal shortcuts than explicit recurrent hidden states.
- **CLIP-based Baselines:** Methods lacking temporal modeling (VLM-CL, VLM-RM) performed poorly, while CLIP-FT was more reliable but still failed on one task, indicating sensitivity to object distribution shifts.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Reward Shaping for Offline RL

VITA's utility for zero-shot reward shaping was evaluated on the Meta-World MT10 benchmark for multi-task robot learning. Policies were trained using Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) with VITA-derived dense rewards. Performance was measured by the interquartile mean (IQM) of returns across all task-seed pairs.

- **Superior Performance:** VITA achieved the highest IQM return (0.815) among all evaluated methods, including CLIP-based baselines and the fuzzy-logic dense rewards provided by Meta-World (META-WL, IQM = 0.779).
- **Real-to-Sim Transfer:** This result demonstrates VITA's capacity to generalize effectively from real-world robotic manipulation data (used for VITA training) to provide effective reward shaping in a simulated environment, outperforming even the simulation's native dense rewards.
- **Practicality:** The high inference cost of proprietary autoregressive VLMs like Gemini 1.5 Pro (used in GVL) made a direct large-scale comparison in the RL setting infeasible, highlighting VITA's more practical inference profile.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

1. **Effect of Dissimilarity-Based Sampling:** The proposed dissimilarity-based sampling strategy was compared against full-trajectory and random sampling for its impact on discriminative performance.
 - Dissimilarity-based sampling (with `w_tr=8`, `k=8`) achieved perfect discriminative performance, outperforming both full-trajectory sampling (which performed worst due to overfitting to global temporal cues) and random sampling. This confirms its role in mitigating shortcut learning and promoting reliance on semantic diversity.

2. **Effect of Temporal Memory:** Different test-time adaptation strategies for incorporating temporal context were evaluated.
 - VITA (incremental, implicit memory via sequential, per-timestep updates without reset) consistently outperformed trajectory-level adaptation (TTT-TR, single batched update), memoryless adaptation (TTT-RS, reset at every step), and explicit memory (TTT-EX, reset at every step, local window update).
 - This result underscores the importance of incrementally accumulating temporal context in the adapter parameters for effective value function estimation, rather than discarding temporal order or resetting memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces VITA, a novel approach to zero-shot value function learning that addresses key limitations in existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) applications for robotic control. The findings demonstrate a significant advancement in the generalization and temporal reasoning capabilities of value functions without requiring extensive task-specific data or large-scale pre-training.

**Significance of the Work:**

- **Enhanced Generalization and Temporal Reasoning:** VITA successfully enhances the generalization and temporal reasoning of contrastive VLMs for zero-shot value function estimation. By employing test-time adaptation, VITA enables lightweight adaptation to both semantic and temporal contexts of trajectories at inference, a capability often limited by frozen pre-trained VLM representations or the biases of autoregressive VLMs.
- **Zero-Shot Adaptation to Distribution Shifts:** The method demonstrates robust generalization from a single training environment to diverse out-of-distribution tasks, environments, and robot embodiments in robotic manipulation scenarios. This capability reduces the need for collecting and labeling data for every new task or robot configuration.
- **Improved Discriminative Power:** VITA exhibits a strong ability to distinguish between expert and non-expert visual trajectories, which is crucial for identifying optimal behaviors and providing meaningful feedback in learning systems.
- **Effective Zero-Shot Reward Shaping:** The application of VITA’s zero-shot value estimates for reward shaping in offline reinforcement learning on the Meta-World MT10 benchmark resulted in multi-task policies that surpassed the performance of policies trained with the simulation’s native fuzzy-logic dense rewards. This highlights the potential of VITA to provide high-quality supervision for downstream RL tasks, even when trained on real-world data and applied to simulated environments.
- **Efficient Temporal Memory Mechanism:** VITA’s implicit memory, formed by sequential test-time updates encoding trajectory history into the adapter's parameters, proved more effective and generalizable than explicit temporal modeling via recurrent hidden states (CLIP-GRU) or other test-time adaptation variants that do not preserve temporal order.
- **Practicality:** The lightweight nature of the adaptation module ensures negligible inference-time overhead, making the approach potentially applicable in scenarios where real-time performance is a consideration.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Scalable Robot Learning:** VITA provides a pathway towards more scalable robot learning from large, unstructured video datasets without the heavy reliance on human supervision or expert demonstrations that typically characterize current approaches. This could accelerate the development of more generalist robot policies.
- **Robust Robotic Control:** By offering generalizable and temporally aware value functions, VITA can contribute to the creation of more robust and adaptable robotic control systems that can operate effectively in novel and dynamic environments.
- **Advancement in RL and Imitation Learning:** Its efficacy in zero-shot reward shaping suggests VITA could serve as a valuable tool for facilitating offline reinforcement learning and imitation learning, particularly in settings where traditional reward engineering is challenging or expert demonstrations are scarce.
- **Broader Applicability for VLMs:** The underlying principle of meta-learned test-time adaptation for enhancing generalization and temporal reasoning is agnostic to the specific VLM backbone. This suggests VITA's methodology could be applied to other pre-trained multimodal representations, potentially extending their utility across various domains beyond robotics.
- **Inspiring Future Research:** The work opens avenues for further research into test-time adaptation for vision-language models, particularly in the context of training agents within world models and exploring its applicability in real-time closed-loop control settings and more complex RL environments. A theoretical analysis of the dissimilarity-based sampling strategy could also further solidify its impact on mitigating shortcut learning.
