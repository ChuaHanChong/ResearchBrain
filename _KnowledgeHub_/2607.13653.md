# Exploratory, Communicative, and Deployable: Vision-Driven Embodied Agents for Open-World Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The REAL framework and agent, developed by researchers from Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and other institutions, enables vision-driven embodied agents to perform open-world mobile manipulation by incorporating oracle-free perception and integrated human-in-the-loop communication. This approach achieves a 78.3% end-to-end success rate in real-world deployment on an Ark LIFT2 mobile robot, demonstrating robust zero-shot transferability to novel household scenarios.

## Problem

- Embodied agents often rely on privileged simulator information (e.g., global object lists, oracle APIs) or complete task instructions, hindering real-world deployment and sim-to-real transfer.
- Existing frameworks struggle with active exploration in unstructured environments, often using text-centric planning that lacks precise visual disambiguation for cluttered scenes.
- Human-robot collaboration is limited by the assumption of explicit and unambiguous user instructions, neglecting the need for dynamic, context-aware interaction for clarification.

## Method

- Introduces **REAL**, an agentic framework with an ==oracle-free perceptual API toolchain== for multi-level active exploration based on raw RGB observations.
- Integrates a simulated user, driven by ==Gemini-3-Flash==, to provide ambiguous instructions and dynamic feedback, enabling the agent to proactively clarify intent through multi-turn dialogue.
- Employs a two-stage training pipeline combining ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== for tool alignment and ==Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO)== with environment-grounded rewards for adaptive reasoning.

## Results

- REAL-Bench, a new benchmark with **241** tasks covering exploration, distractors, articulation, and interaction, was introduced for comprehensive evaluation.
- GSPO training on top of SFT improved success rates on distractor-rich FODP tasks to **33.9%** and significantly boosted interaction-heavy SUL tasks to **56.9%**, outperforming zero-shot commercial VLMs.
- The framework demonstrated robust zero-shot transferability, achieving a **78.3%** end-to-end success rate over **60** real-world episodes on an Ark LIFT2 mobile manipulator without unrecoverable system crashes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining SFT for initial tool alignment with GSPO-based reinforcement learning improves robustness to visual ambiguity and generalizes exploration capabilities beyond direct expert trajectories.
> - Integrating a simulated user and providing social rewards with a query budget enables agents to learn proactive communicative behavior for intent alignment, surpassing zero-shot baselines.
> - A sim-to-real consistent environment design, using standardized interfaces and hierarchical execution, allows for robust zero-shot transfer of high-level cognitive policies to physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied agents capable of operating in complex, real-world environments is a significant area within artificial intelligence. Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have enabled agents to perform various tasks by leveraging their reasoning, planning, and visual perception capabilities. Early embodied agent frameworks primarily utilized LLMs in text-based simulators, relying on textual perception and tool calling. However, the inherent limitations of pure-text interactions, particularly regarding spatial and physical grounding, led to the adoption of VLMs for richer visual perception in high-fidelity simulation environments.

Despite these advancements, a notable gap persists between simulated environments and real-world deployment. Existing benchmarks often simplify two critical dimensions: active exploration and communicative interaction. Many frameworks provide agents with privileged simulator information, such as global object lists, oracle category-finding APIs, or target 3D poses, which are not available in physical environments. This reliance on non-privileged information hinders effective sim-to-real transfer. While some recent works adopt non-privileged paradigms using standard sensor inputs, their exploration and planning often remain text-centric, struggling with precise visual disambiguation in cluttered scenes.

The second limitation is the assumption of explicit and unambiguous user instructions. Current benchmarks typically treat task execution as an open-loop process, overlooking the need for dynamic intent alignment. In practical scenarios, human users often provide incomplete or ambiguous goals, necessitating active, context-aware interaction for clarification. Although some studies have introduced simulated users, their focus has largely been on text-based preference elicitation or memorization, rather than co-optimizing communication with physical mobile manipulation tasks.

This paper introduces REAL, an agentic framework designed to address these identified limitations by focusing on deployability through oracle-free perception and integrated human-in-the-loop communication. It positions itself as a step toward bridging the reality gap for long-horizon mobile manipulation by creating a more realistic training and evaluation environment that explicitly accounts for active visual exploration and dynamic user interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate an embodied agent framework, termed REAL (exploRatory, communicativE, And, deployLable), that is specifically designed for open-world mobile manipulation in real-world settings. This overarching goal is motivated by several key challenges in the current embodied AI landscape:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Existing embodied agent frameworks frequently rely on privileged simulator states (e.g., access to global object lists, precise 3D object poses, or oracle perception APIs) or assume complete task instructions. Such idealizations bypass many realistic deployment challenges, leading to performance degradation and failures when transferring trained policies to physical robots. A core motivation is to eliminate these unrealistic assumptions by establishing a sim-to-real-consistent environment API that operates without oracle perception.
- **Enabling Active Exploration:** In unstructured, open-world environments, agents cannot rely on predefined maps or complete object inventories. They must actively explore their surroundings to discover objects and gather necessary information. The research aims to equip agents with a deployable toolchain for multi-level active exploration based solely on raw RGB observations, encompassing navigation, object detection, and visual prompting for manipulation. This necessitates grounding discovered objects in visual space rather than relying on privileged 3D coordinates.
- **Facilitating Communicative Interaction:** Real-world human-robot collaboration often involves ambiguous instructions due to users' incomplete information or evolving preferences. Existing benchmarks frequently assume explicit and unambiguous commands, neglecting the need for dynamic intent alignment. A key objective is to integrate a simulated user into the environment to provide initially ambiguous instructions and offer dynamic, context-aware natural language feedback, thereby training the agent to proactively clarify intent through multi-turn dialogue.
- **Developing a Robust Training Paradigm:** To optimize agent performance under these realistic constraints, the research seeks to develop an automated and scalable data generation and filtering pipeline. This pipeline is designed to construct diverse exploration and interaction tasks, enabling a multi-stage training approach. The motivation is to align the agent with a deployable tool interface and enhance adaptive reasoning and error recovery through a combination of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and online reinforcement learning (RL).
- **Comprehensive Evaluation and Real-World Validation:** To rigorously assess the capabilities of the proposed framework and agent, a comprehensive benchmark (REAL-Bench) is introduced. This benchmark spans various task complexities, including active exploration, visual distraction, articulated manipulation, and interactive disambiguation. Ultimately, the motivation extends to physically deploying and evaluating the trained agent on a mobile manipulator to validate its zero-shot transferability and the effectiveness of the sim-to-real-consistent design in real household scenarios.

In summary, the research aims to advance embodied AI by creating a more realistic, robust, and deployable agent that can actively explore, communicate to disambiguate intent, and perform mobile manipulation tasks in open-world settings, thereby bridging the current gap between simulation and real-world application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces REAL, an agentic framework that incorporates a closed-loop environment, a multi-stage training pipeline, and a comprehensive evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. Environment Design

The environment is built upon GRUtopia, featuring high-fidelity rendering and rigid-body physics. It adheres to two core principles: an oracle-free perceptual API toolchain and an integrated simulated user for natural language interaction.

- **Privilege-Free Environment Modeling:**
 - **Initial Observation:** Agents receive a prior list of receptacle IDs and semantic categories (e.g., `table_2`), which are physically obtainable (e.g., from prior robot scans). However, they lack access to global object lists, target 3D poses, simulator object states, or goal locations. Fine-grained appearance of receptacles and object distributions are also not provided.
 - **Multi-level Active Exploration:** A toolchain enables active exploration without oracle APIs. These tools include:
 - `nav_to`: Inter-receptacle navigation based on structural priors.
 - `walk_around`: Intra-receptacle scanning using open-vocabulary 2D perception to bypass occlusions and detect objects from multiple viewpoints around a receptacle.
 - `show_object_by_category`: Isolates objects of a user-defined category within the current view.
 - `gaze_at`: Moves the agent towards a selected target to center it in the camera's view, considering physical distance.
 - **Vision-Grounded Interaction:** Detected objects are back-projected from 2D segmentation masks into 3D space, forming a temporary "exploration map." Objects in this map are highlighted in RGB/SoM observations with visual IDs, allowing the agent to distinguish similar objects and parameterize manipulation skills (e.g., `pick`, `open`) using these IDs.
 - **Simulated User for Collaborative Tasks:** A simulated user, driven by Gemini-3-Flash, is integrated into the environment dynamics. It provides ambiguous initial instructions, monitors task progress, and clarifies objectives based on real-time exploration results. A task-specific profile defines user preferences and choices. The server synchronizes with the agent's interaction history and physical observations to maintain a shared context. The agent can use an `ask` action to query the user for clarification, which generates natural language guidance based on shared context and profile.

#### 3.2. Agent Training

The decision-making process is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) to minimize inference overhead and mitigate context explosion in long-horizon tasks.

- **Problem Formulation and Context Management:**
 - **Closed-Loop Execution:** The VLM policy maps current observation `o_t` to a formatted tool call `a_t`. A tool executor validates and dispatches calls, providing immediate feedback for invalid actions.
 - **Observation and State Aggregation:** `o_t` is partitioned into `o_env_t` (immediate perceptual results, execution feedback) and `o_hist_t` (compressed historical state). `o_hist_t` is managed via self-summarization: `h_t = (Σ_t, task_t)`, where `Σ_t` is a summary of completed work and `task_t` is an analysis of the active phase. This bounds the per-step prompt size and records only successful actions.
 - **Scalable Asset Library and Task Instantiation:** Uses 7 GRScenes (11 room types, 100 receptacles) and over 2,500 objects from MesaTask. Scene state is represented as a World Graph (WG). LLMs generate (source, destination, object) triplets for cross-receptacle object rearrangement tasks. Distractors (object and receptacle level) are introduced.
 - **SFT Data Synthesis:** A rule-based planner generates expert action sequences in the simulator. An automated Social Annotation phase uses LLMs to rewrite instructions for deliberate vagueness, injecting `ask` actions and simulated user responses into trajectories to train communicative behavior. Gemini-3-pro annotates internal reasoning traces and structured historical states `h_t` for the trajectories.

- **Paradigm Alignment via SFT:**
 - A Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct VLM serves as the backbone.
 - Training involves minimizing the negative log-likelihood of expert trajectories (`L_SFT`) through behavior cloning. The vision encoder is frozen. The model generates internal reasoning (`<think>...</think>`) and a JSON output for `h_t` and `a_t`.

- **Reinforcement Learning for Skill Optimization:**
 - Addresses limitations of SFT, such as distribution shift and scarce interactive data.
 - **Algorithm Selection:** Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) is adopted over GRPO. GSPO uses a length-normalized sequence ratio for credit assignment, reducing variance and enabling valid updates during long rollouts, which promotes adaptive replanning and proactive interaction.
 - **Environment-Grounded Reward Shaping:** A dense, step-wise reward `r_t` is designed based on changes in the World Graph (WG). Positive rewards are given for matching `δ_t = WG_t - WG_{t-1}` against task objectives, and for intermediate physical progress (e.g., picking an object). Penalties are applied for efficiency (`-λ_time`), failed actions, and placing objects incorrectly. The `ask` tool receives a reward for valid disambiguation queries, subject to a difficulty-conditioned budget.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment via Sim-to-Real Transfer

The trained agent is deployed on a physical Ark LIFT2 dual-arm mobile robot.

- **Seamless Transfer Through Standardized Interfaces:** A Model Context Protocol (MCP) server is used for tool invocation, keeping the high-level cognitive framework identical between simulation and the physical robot. The physical world acts as an alternative backend to the simulator without retraining the high-level policy.
- **Hierarchical Execution with Specialized Physical Policies:** While high-level planning is zero-shot transferred, continuous physical dynamics use dedicated real-world implementations. Atomic manipulation commands are executed by a fine-tuned `π_0.5` base model, allowing the VLM to focus on visual grounding, state tracking, and task planning.
- **Physical Infrastructure:** ConceptGraph with Intel RealSense D455 for semantic 3D scene graph, Odin1 depth camera for SLAM (relocalization error < 1 cm), A* for global planning, DWA for local obstacle avoidance, PID controllers for mobility. Grounded-SAM-2 is used for open-vocabulary segmentation and mask generation, providing visual prompts for the VLA model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the REAL framework and its trained agent through a new benchmark, comparisons with commercial VLMs, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Construction

REAL-Bench was introduced, comprising 241 task instances across four families:
- **Furniture-Distractor Pick-and-Place (FDP):** 72 tasks, cluttered mobile manipulation.
- **Furniture & Object-Distractor Pick-and-Place (FODP):** 56 tasks, open-vocabulary, full-category distractors.
- **Furniture-Distractor Open/Close (FDO):** 48 tasks, articulation-centric manipulation.
- **Simulator-User-Loop (SUL):** 65 tasks, designed with instruction ambiguity requiring clarification.

#### 4.2. Results and Analysis of SFT and RL Training

Experiments compared strong open-source and commercial VLMs under zero-shot prompting with the proposed Qwen3-VL-8B agent after SFT and GSPO training.

- **Tool Alignment and Behavior-Cloning Overfitting:**
 - Zero-shot Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct showed negligible success (0-1.5%) on manipulation and interactive tasks.
 - One epoch of SFT significantly improved tool interface alignment (e.g., FDP to 45.8%).
 - A second SFT epoch further improved FDP to 65.3% but decreased performance on distractor-rich FODP tasks (from 30.4% to 28.6%), indicating overfitting to expert trajectories and reduced robustness to visual ambiguity.

- **Reinforcement Learning Restores Generalization:**
 - Applying closed-loop GSPO to the single-epoch SFT checkpoint recovered exploration capability. FODP success increased to 33.9%, while maintaining tool-calling performance comparable to the more heavily SFT-tuned model. This suggests that environmental feedback promotes adaptive problem-solving over rote memorization.

- **Social Reward Improves Proactive Intent Alignment:**
 - GSPO training had the most significant impact on the interaction-heavy SUL split, where the agent achieved a 56.9% success rate. This outcome surpassed all proprietary zero-shot baselines, including the teacher model used for SFT annotation. The integration of a difficulty-conditioned query budget and explicit rewards for resolving ambiguity enabled the agent to learn when to proactively query the simulated user.

- **Dynamic Manipulation Reveals a Vision Bottleneck:**
 - Success rates in the articulation-heavy FDO split remained lower (41.7% after SFT+RL) compared to other task types. This suggests that dynamic visual state changes (e.g., opening/closing doors) pose a challenge for the agent, potentially due to the frozen vision encoder, which limits the base model's visual grounding capabilities for multimodal abilities.

- **Failure Mode Taxonomy:**
 - Manual analysis of 100 SFT+RL evaluation episodes revealed that most failures fell under "Object Confusion" (25%), followed by "Key Action Missing" (17%), "Lost Memory" (9%), and "Other Error" (2%). These categories indicate that cluttered visual grounding, procedural completeness, and long-horizon memory remain bottlenecks.

#### 4.3. Real-World Deployment via Sim-to-Real Transfer

The framework was deployed on a physical Ark LIFT2 dual-arm mobile robot.

- **End-to-End Success:** The high-level policy achieved a 78.3% end-to-end success rate over 60 real-world episodes, with zero unrecoverable system crashes.
- **Zero-Shot Transferability:** The physical trials demonstrated robust zero-shot transferability to unseen household scenarios, validating that the sim-to-real-consistent framework design effectively bridges the reality gap for long-horizon mobile manipulation.
- **Hierarchical Execution Reliability:** The low-level primitive layer, implemented by a fine-tuned `π_0.5` model, was executable in 85.3% of 600 VLA primitive executions.
- **Causal Reasoning:** Physical trials illustrated causal planning, such as preparing a microwave (opening the door) before fetching the food item, and human-robot disambiguation for multiple candidate objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents REAL, a framework designed to enhance the deployability of vision-driven embodied agents for open-world mobile manipulation. Its significance and potential impact stem from addressing critical limitations in current embodied AI research:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By eliminating reliance on oracle perceptual APIs and privileged simulator states, REAL establishes a more realistic training paradigm. This sim-to-real consistent environment design directly facilitates zero-shot transfer to physical robotic systems, reducing the effort and resources typically required for real-world deployment. This approach can lead to more practical and readily deployable robotic agents for household and other unstructured environments.
- **Enabling Adaptive and Robust Exploration:** The multi-level active exploration toolchain, grounded in raw RGB observations, allows agents to actively discover and disambiguate objects in cluttered and unfamiliar environments. This capability is fundamental for general-purpose robots operating in dynamic real-world settings where complete information is rarely available upfront.
- **Fostering Human-Robot Collaboration:** The integration of a simulated user for dynamic, context-aware interaction addresses the challenge of ambiguous user instructions. By training agents to proactively query for clarification, REAL promotes the development of more collaborative and user-friendly robots that can effectively align with human intent, even under uncertainty. This is crucial for integrating robots into human-centric environments.
- **Advancing Training Methodologies:** The two-stage training pipeline, combining supervised fine-tuning for tool-use alignment with online reinforcement learning (GSPO) for adaptive reasoning and error recovery, offers a robust approach to optimizing complex embodied policies. The use of environment-grounded reward shaping and GSPO's ability to handle long-horizon trajectories contribute to the development of more intelligent and resilient agents.
- **Providing a Comprehensive Evaluation Tool:** The introduction of REAL-Bench, a benchmark designed with challenging tasks covering active exploration, visual distraction, articulated manipulation, and interactive disambiguation, provides a valuable resource for future research. It enables systematic and rigorous evaluation of embodied agents against realistic deployment scenarios.
- **Validation through Real-World Deployment:** The successful deployment and high end-to-end success rate on a physical dual-arm mobile robot (Ark LIFT2) provide empirical validation of the framework's design principles. This demonstration of robust zero-shot transferability is a tangible step toward realizing general-purpose embodied agents.

Despite these advancements, the work also identifies limitations, such as the current task space being restricted to cross-receptacle rearrangement (lacking richer compositional constraints), the simulated user operating within a bounded behavioral envelope, and the monolithic modeling of receptacles without part-level distinction. Addressing these limitations in future work could further enhance the capabilities and versatility of embodied agents. Overall, REAL contributes to a more practical and generalizable approach to developing embodied AI, impacting fields such as service robotics, logistics, and human-robot interaction.
