# Adaptive Guidance Accelerates Reinforcement Learning of Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper investigates how large language models learn complex reasoning when trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), formally decomposing performance gains into 'self-distillation' and 'capability gain'. The authors introduce 'Guide-GRPO,' an adaptive online RL algorithm that strategically injects natural language hints when unguided attempts fail, demonstrating superior generalization on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and a consistent performance lead across model scales up to 72B parameters.

## Problem

- The precise mechanisms driving performance gains in Large Language Models (LLMs) trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) were poorly understood, particularly the balance between refining existing knowledge and acquiring new capabilities.
- Accelerating the acquisition of genuinely new problem-solving abilities in LLMs during RLVR training remained a challenge, as existing methods often struggled to efficiently help models overcome problems they could not initially solve.
- Developing an effective and scalable method to inject targeted learning signals that improve LLMs' reasoning without requiring constant guidance or relying solely on off-policy data was needed.

## Method

- The paper formally decomposes RLVR's performance gains into =='self-distillation'== (improving pass@k to pass@1) and =='capability gain'== (solving previously unsolvable problems), enabling a quantitative understanding of learning mechanisms.
- A new class of online RL training algorithms, =='Guide'== (specifically ==Guide-GRPO==), is introduced, which adaptively injects natural language hints into the model's context only for problems where all initial unguided rollouts fail.
- Guide-GRPO uses ==importance sampling== to optimize the policy for contexts where hints are absent, ensuring that learning from guided trajectories translates to improved performance in unguided inference scenarios.

## Results

- Empirical analysis confirmed that self-distillation is the dominant driver of RLVR learning across Qwen 2.5 models (0.5B to 72B parameters), with capability gain being a minority contributor.
- Guide-GRPO consistently achieved superior performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming standard GRPO, Filter-GRPO, and SFT, with a macro-average Pass@1 of **51.03%** and Pass@16 of **70.15%**.
- Guide-GRPO demonstrated robust scalability, maintaining its performance lead over vanilla GRPO with increased context length (4K to 8K) and model size (7B to 32B), achieving **3.39 percentage points** higher macro-average Pass@1 for the 32B model with 8K context.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) performance gains are predominantly driven by 'self-distillation,' where models learn to convert problems solvable with multiple attempts (pass@k) into single-attempt solutions (pass@1).
> - Genuine 'capability gain,' solving problems previously unsolvable even with multiple attempts, contributes a smaller but present portion to RLVR's learning progress across various model scales.
> - Adaptive, in-context guidance applied selectively when all unguided rollouts fail, combined with importance sampling, accelerates the acquisition of new problem-solving abilities without requiring hints at inference time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes the research paper "Adaptive Guidance Accelerates Reinforcement Learning of Reasoning Models" (arXiv:2506.13923v2). The paper investigates the mechanisms by which large language models (LLMs) learn to solve complex reasoning problems when trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The authors formally decompose RLVR's performance gains into "self-distillation" (improving pass@k to pass@1) and "capability gain" (solving previously unsolvable problems). Their empirical findings, across diverse model scales (0.5B to 72B parameters) and domains (math, science, code), reveal that self-distillation is the dominant driver of learning progress.

Leveraging this insight, the paper introduces "Guide," a novel class of online RL training algorithms, with a specific focus on Guide-GRPO. Guide adaptively injects natural language hints into the model's context only for problems where all initial unguided rollouts fail. It then uses importance sampling to optimize the policy for contexts where hints are absent. Guide-GRPO empirically demonstrates significant improvements in generalization on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, outperforming vanilla GRPO and other baselines, and showing robustness with increased context length and model size. The work provides a clearer understanding of RLVR's learning dynamics and offers a practical method to accelerate genuine capability gains in reasoning models.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), reinforcement learning (RL), and advanced reasoning capabilities. It contributes significantly to the evolving understanding and optimization of RL for LLMs, specifically focusing on the relatively new paradigm of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

**Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The field of applying RL to LLMs has seen rapid advancements. Initially, RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) became a standard for aligning LLMs with human preferences. More recently, RL from AI Feedback (RLAIF) and RL from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) have emerged, particularly for tasks where objective, verifiable outcomes (like correct math answers or executable code) are available. Pioneering works like OpenAI-o1 and DeepSeek-R1 have demonstrated the state-of-the-art performance of RLVR in complex reasoning domains such as mathematics, science, and coding. This paper explicitly builds upon these successes, acknowledging that RLVR models surpass traditional SFT or RLHF on challenging benchmarks due to the scalability of verifiable rewards.

**Understanding RLVR's Mechanisms:** A crucial debate in the current landscape revolves around *why* RLVR improves LLM performance. Prior work, such as Yue et al., suggested that RLVR's gains were almost entirely attributable to the distillation of the base model's existing knowledge, essentially "compressing pass@k into pass@1." This paper takes a more nuanced stance, formalizing the improvements as a sum of two orthogonal effects: "distillation" and "genuine capability gain." By investigating how these effects evolve with model scale, the authors directly address and refine previous hypotheses about RLVR's underlying learning mechanisms. They confirm that self-distillation is dominant but also show that true "capability gain" – solving problems previously unsolvable even with multiple attempts – does exist, albeit as a smaller contributor.

**Improving RL Efficiency with Off-Policy Data and Guidance:** The paper draws inspiration from the rich history of using off-policy data in RL to improve training efficiency. It differentiates itself from methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and other off-policy techniques by focusing on "guidance in context" rather than solely on fully off-policy data. While some methods use expert iteration or self-training to distill knowledge, Guide introduces a novel approach of adaptively injecting *hints* on failure, which still requires the model to derive the solution chain from scratch. This strategy aims to bridge the gap between purely on-policy and fully off-policy learning, offering a way to acquire new problem-solving skills without relying on a more powerful teacher model for direct distillation. The theoretical analysis of Guide's learning efficiency further solidifies its contribution to the methodological landscape of RL for LLMs.

In summary, this work provides a more detailed empirical and theoretical understanding of RLVR's learning dynamics and introduces a practical, effective algorithm that accelerates the acquisition of new reasoning capabilities in LLMs, contributing to the broader goal of building more capable and efficient AI reasoning agents.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's research is driven by a two-fold primary motivation:
1. **To understand the drivers of performance gains in RLVR-trained reasoning models:** Despite RLVR's demonstrated success in improving LLM reasoning, the precise mechanisms driving these improvements and how they interact with model scale have remained "poorly understood." The authors aim to formalize and empirically analyze these mechanisms.
2. **To leverage these insights to accelerate the learning of new problems during RLVR:** Once the learning mechanisms are better understood, the goal is to develop an effective method to enhance the model's ability to solve problems it previously could not.

These motivations lead to two specific, main research questions:

1. **Self-distillation or capability gain?** To what extent does learning during RLVR merely redistribute probability mass among outputs the model already knows (termed "self-distillation"), versus genuinely expanding the model’s problem-solving capabilities (termed "capability gain")? This question seeks to quantify the relative contributions of these two effects.

2. **Do guidance-conditioned trajectories on failure accelerate learning?** If a policy is given selective guidance only when it completely fails a problem, while still requiring it to generate trajectories from its current policy state, can this accelerate knowledge acquisition more effectively than using fully off-policy data, providing no guidance, or always providing guidance? This question explores the efficacy of targeted, in-context guidance as a training signal.

By addressing these questions, the authors aim to make three key contributions:
- Decompose and quantify RLVR's improvements into self-distillation and capability gain, showing the former dominates.
- Demonstrate that selective guidance significantly improves pass@k rates, enabling the discovery of otherwise unreachable successful trajectories.
- Introduce "Guide," a new training algorithm that synthesizes these insights to accelerate RLVR by expanding the pool of reachable solutions for self-distillation and enabling the model to learn them without guidance in future inferences.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a combination of formal definitions, extensive empirical analysis, and algorithm development to address its research questions.

#### 4.1. Decomposing RLVR Gains

To understand how RLVR drives LLMs to solve new tasks, the authors formalize performance gains using a net reward metric ($R_{net}$). This metric measures the change in accuracy (progress on unsolved problems minus regression on previously solved problems) before and after RL.

The core of this decomposition lies in distinguishing two components of "progress" on initially unsolved problems:
- **Self-Distillation:** Occurs when an RL-trained policy solves a problem (pass@1) that the initial policy could only solve with multiple attempts (pass@k, where k is the number of rollouts used during training, here k=16). This represents the model learning to convert existing latent knowledge into reliable single-attempt solutions.
- **Capability Gain:** Occurs when an RL-trained policy solves a problem (pass@1) that the initial policy could *not* solve even with multiple attempts (pass@k=0). This represents the model acquiring genuinely new problem-solving abilities.

**Experimental Setup for Decomposition:**
- **Base Models:** Qwen 2.5 models at five scales: 0.5B, 3B, 7B, 32B, and 72B parameters. These models were first fine-tuned with one epoch of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the AMPS dataset to ensure proper reasoning output formatting.
- **Training:** 256 steps using the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) objective, a variant of PPO, on a large dataset of >500,000 reasoning problems across math, STEM, and coding domains (Table 8).
- **Evaluation:** Performance was evaluated every 16 training steps on a suite of benchmarks including GSM8K, MATH500, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, GPQA-DIAMOND, OLYMPIADBENCH, LEETCODE, LIVECODEBENCH, and HUMANEVAL.
- **Measurement:** To quantify self-distillation and capability gain, 100 rollouts were generated for every test problem at temperatures 1.0 (for pass@k) and 0.0 (for pass@1). For each of 10 independent trials, pass@1 was determined by randomly sampling one temperature 0.0 rollout, and pass@16 by randomly sampling 16 temperature 1.0 rollouts and checking for any correct solution. Results were aggregated to report mean and standard error.

#### 4.2. Guide: Adaptive Guidance Algorithm

Inspired by the finding that self-distillation dominates, the authors hypothesize that increasing the proportion of correct rollouts during RL training, especially for hard problems, can accelerate learning. They propose "Guide," a new class of online RL training algorithms.

**Core Idea of Guide-GRPO (Algorithm 1):**
- **Conditional Guidance Injection:** Guide-GRPO injects natural language hints (`guid` suffix) into the prompt context (`<q, guid>`) *only* when all `k` initial unguided rollouts for a given problem fail. This focuses learning signal on the hardest, currently unsolvable problems.
- **Importance Weighting (Off-Policy Correction):** Since the guided trajectories are "off-policy" relative to the desired on-policy performance *without* guidance, a critical component is the importance weight (Equation 6). This weight `(π_θ(o_i,t | q, o_i,<t)) / (π_θ_old(o_i,t | q, h, o_i,<t))` corrects the gradient to ensure that samples drawn *with* guidance still optimize performance *without* guidance.
- **GRPO Objective Modification:** The standard GRPO objective is modified to incorporate these conditionally guided, importance-weighted trajectories.
- **Theoretical Guarantee:** Theorem 1 formally proves that Guide-GRPO improves learning efficiency over vanilla GRPO, with gains being larger for harder prompts, when guided advantages are positive, and when the baseline success probability is non-zero (allowing credit propagation).

**Guidance Generation:**
- Hints were generated using GPT-4o, prompted as an "expert math tutor." The prompt instructs GPT-4o to provide conceptual and strategic hints (3-5 hints in ascending order of directness) without revealing direct calculations or solution paths (Appendix G). This ensures the model still "derives a solution chain from scratch."

**Experimental Setup for Guide-GRPO:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, fine-tuned similarly to the decomposition experiments. Experiments were also scaled to Qwen-2.5-Math-32B and longer context lengths (4K to 8K).
- **Training Data:** Default subset of OpenR1-Math-220k (93.7K math reasoning tasks), which includes prompts, ground-truth answers, and human-authored reference solutions.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Standard GRPO:** Vanilla GRPO training.
 - **GRPO with Filtering:** A technique that discards prompts where all rollouts are either entirely correct or entirely incorrect, aiming for more efficient training.
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Trained directly on human-authored reference solutions.
- **Evaluation:** Pass@1 (greedy decoding) and Pass@16 (temperature 1.0) on a range of math benchmarks (same as decomposition).
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Guidance Thresholds:** Compared "All Incorrect" (only when all standard rollouts fail), "Mostly Incorrect" (accuracy < 25%), and "Always" (unconditional guidance).
 - **Policy Loss Formulation:** Investigated different importance weighting and PPO-clipping configurations to understand training stability (Appendix D).

The comprehensive experimental design, combining large-scale model testing, formal decomposition, novel algorithm development, and careful ablations, provides a robust basis for the paper's conclusions.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

#### 5.1. RLVR Learning Mechanisms

The empirical analysis of RLVR's drivers across various Qwen model scales (0.5B to 72B) yielded clear insights:
- **Self-Distillation Dominates:** For all evaluated model sizes, the majority of performance improvement from RLVR stems from self-distillation. This means models primarily learn to convert problems they could already solve with multiple attempts (pass@16) into problems they can solve reliably with a single attempt (pass@1). As shown in Figure 1, the "distillation" component is consistently much larger than "capability gain."
- **Capability Gain Exists but is Minor:** While self-distillation is dominant, models do learn to solve *some* problems they could not solve at initialization, even with multiple attempts (capability gain). However, this remains a minority contributor at every scale, indicating RLVR primarily refines existing knowledge rather than discovering truly novel solutions for a given `k` value.
- **Headroom and Model Scale:** The absolute count of problems that can be improved via RLVR (the "unsolved set" or "headroom") decreases significantly as model size increases (e.g., 3195 for 0.5B vs. 1532 for 72B). Since each model converts a similar *fraction* of its unsolved problems, the absolute gain in pass@1 inevitably drops for larger models. This suggests that for stronger models, introducing harder examples is crucial to replenish the "unsolved set" and expose new reasoning gaps.

#### 5.2. Guide-GRPO Performance and Scalability

The Guide algorithm, particularly Guide-GRPO, demonstrated significant empirical advantages:
- **Increased Correct Rollouts with Guidance:** Initial validation (Figure 2) showed that providing targeted, in-context guidance significantly increases the number of correct rollouts. This improvement applies to both capability gain (solving more previously unsolvable questions) and self-distillation (reinforcing consistency on already solvable ones).
- **Superior Test-Time Performance:** Guide-GRPO consistently outperformed all baselines (Filter-GRPO, vanilla GRPO, SFT, and the Base model) across a wide range of math benchmarks (Table 1).
 - It achieved the highest macro-average Pass@1 (51.03%) and Pass@16 (70.15%) scores, and similarly for micro-averages.
 - Specific strong gains were observed on challenging benchmarks, e.g., a 3% absolute improvement in Pass@1 on Olympiad-level questions and a 13% improvement in Pass@16 on AIME 25 compared to the next best baseline. This suggests Guide-GRPO promotes better exploration and solution diversity, crucial for reasoning-centric domains.
- **Scalability with Resources:** The benefits of Guide-GRPO were shown to scale effectively with increased context length (from 4K to 8K) and model size (from 7B to 32B). As seen in Table 2, Guide-GRPO consistently maintained a performance lead over vanilla GRPO in these larger configurations, demonstrating robust generalization. For the 32B model with 8K context, Guide-GRPO achieved 3.39 percentage points higher macro-average Pass@1 than GRPO.

#### 5.3. Training Dynamics and Ablations

Insights from training dynamics and ablation studies further explained Guide's efficacy:
- **Converging to Better Performance:** Figure 3 illustrates that Guide-GRPO, despite sometimes showing lower initial rollout accuracy without guidance, ultimately surpasses standard GRPO methods as training progresses. This "crossover" indicates that the selective guidance injection effectively updates policy weights, enabling the model to perform better independently without requiring guidance at inference time.
- **Preserved Exploratory Capacity:** Guide-GRPO maintains consistently higher generation entropy throughout training while steadily increasing response length (Figure 3). This suggests the method preserves the model's exploratory capacity for novel solutions, contributing to its superior generalization.
- **Critical Loss Formulation Factors:** Investigations into policy loss formulations (Appendix D, Figure 4) revealed two critical factors for stable training:
 - **Correct Importance Weighting:** Importance weights for guided trajectories must be computed relative to the *sampling distribution* (prompt + guidance in context), not just the prompt alone. Incorrect weighting leads to significant instability.
 - **PPO-Clip Deactivation for Guided Trajectories:** Applying PPO-clipping to guided trajectories caused training divergence. Guided trajectories inherently generate smaller probability ratios, and clipping could artificially inflate these, leading to unstable gradient updates. Removing ratio clipping empirically stabilized training.
- **Optimal Guidance Threshold:** Ablation studies on guidance thresholds (Table 3) confirmed that applying guidance *only when all standard rollouts fail* ("All Incorrect" strategy) yields optimal performance. Unconditional guidance ("Always") significantly impairs results, likely by preventing the model from developing robust independent reasoning. Selective guidance provides crucial signal for truly difficult problems while incentivizing independent exploration in other cases.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of LLM development, particularly for reasoning tasks:

- **Refined Understanding of RLVR:** The formal decomposition and empirical validation of "self-distillation" versus "capability gain" provide a more granular and accurate understanding of how RLVR contributes to LLM performance. This insight clarifies that RLVR primarily refines and consolidates existing, albeit latent, problem-solving abilities (pass@k to pass@1), rather than fundamentally expanding the model's *absolute* reasoning capacity for a given `k`. This understanding is crucial for designing more effective training strategies.

- **Accelerating Genuine Capability Gain:** By identifying that capability gain is a minority contributor, the paper motivates the need for methods that specifically target this aspect. "Guide" directly addresses this by strategically injecting guidance for problems that are truly beyond the model's current capabilities. This enables the model to explore and discover solutions it previously could not reach, thereby accelerating true knowledge acquisition and expanding the model's "solution space."

- **Practical and Generalizable Algorithm:** Guide-GRPO provides a practical and empirically proven method to improve LLM reasoning. Its key strength is the ability to leverage human-authored hints (or potentially AI-generated ones) during training to boost performance *without requiring guidance at test time*. This is a significant advantage for real-world deployment, as it avoids the computational overhead or logistical challenges of requiring hints during inference.

- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** By focusing guidance on the hardest problems (where all unguided rollouts fail), Guide-GRPO intelligently allocates its learning signal. This targeted approach, combined with principled importance sampling, likely leads to more efficient use of training data and computational resources compared to methods that might provide guidance indiscriminately.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work:
 - **Dynamic Guidance:** Investigating dynamic guidance generation targeted at specific reasoning failures could lead to even more efficient learning.
 - **Domain Generality:** Testing Guide's effectiveness in other complex domains like code generation, agentic tasks, or robotics could demonstrate its broader applicability.
 - **Scaling Laws for Guidance:** Further scaling studies are needed to fully understand how Guide's effectiveness varies with much larger model sizes, context lengths beyond 8K, and increasing computational resources.
 - **Impact of Hint Quality:** Deeper analysis into the quality and nature of the hints themselves could yield further improvements.

In conclusion, "Adaptive Guidance Accelerates Reinforcement Learning of Reasoning Models" not only deepens our scientific understanding of LLM learning with RL but also provides a powerful and practical tool to enhance the reasoning capabilities of these models, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex problem-solving domains.
