# The EPIC-KITCHENS Dataset: Collection, Challenges and Baselines

> [!summary] Summary
> The EPIC-KITCHENS dataset offers the largest collection of egocentric kitchen videos, comprising 55 hours of unscripted daily activities from 32 participants, densely annotated with 39,564 action segments and 454,158 object bounding boxes. It establishes benchmarks for object detection, action recognition, and anticipation, revealing challenges in model generalization to unseen environments and the value of participant narrations for understanding human intent.

## Problem

- There was a scarcity of large-scale, richly annotated video datasets for egocentric (first-person) action understanding.
- Existing video datasets for daily activities often contained scripted actions or short, isolated clips, failing to capture the natural spontaneity and multi-tasking behaviors of real-world human interactions.
- Models lacked robust benchmarks to evaluate generalization to novel environments and understand fine-grained distinctions in human actions and intentions.

## Method

- Collected 55 hours of unscripted, continuous ==egocentric video== and stereo audio from 32 participants in their diverse home kitchens across multiple countries, capturing authentic daily routines.
- Implemented a multi-stage ==annotation pipeline== that uniquely leveraged ==participant self-narrations== (post-recording speech) for detailed intentions, which were then crowd-sourced for precise ==temporal action localization== and ==active object bounding box== annotations.
- Manually grouped free-form verbs and nouns from narrations into ==125 verb classes and 331 noun classes== to standardize the vocabulary for fine-grained action understanding.

## Results

- Object detection baselines using Faster R-CNN achieved modest performance (e.g., **28.06% mAP@0.5** for seen kitchens) and struggled with few-shot classes, highlighting the difficulty of detecting active objects in egocentric views.
- Action recognition baselines demonstrated that multimodal fusion (RGB+Flow+Audio) improved Top-1 action accuracy to **26.06%** on seen kitchens, compared to **22.44%** for RGB alone, with temporal models showing significant gains for verb classification.
- Action anticipation proved highly challenging, with Top-1 action accuracy for predicting an action 1 second in advance reaching only **6.25%** on seen kitchens, indicating a significant gap in current models' ability to predict future human behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Participant self-narration provides invaluable ground truth for human intentions and fine-grained action details, offering a unique resource for understanding the "why" behind actions.
> - The "unseen" kitchen test split reveals that current state-of-the-art models struggle significantly with generalization to novel environments and zero-shot classes, underscoring the need for more robust AI.
> - Multimodal fusion (RGB, optical flow, and audio) consistently improves action recognition performance, and temporal modeling architectures are crucial for capturing the dynamic nature of fine-grained actions in egocentric video.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The EPIC-KITCHENS dataset marks a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, specifically addressing the challenges of video understanding and egocentric perception. The broader research landscape has seen substantial progress in image-based tasks (e.g., classification, detection) largely due to large-scale benchmarks like ImageNet and MS-COCO. However, video understanding has lagged due to the scarcity of richly annotated datasets that capture the temporal, interactive, and contextual complexities of real-world activities.

Existing video datasets for action recognition (e.g., YouTube-8M, Kinetics, Something-Something) often feature short, single-action clips or are scripted, leading to less natural human behaviors. Datasets like Charades and Hollywood in Homes attempt to capture daily actions in homes but are still largely scripted, lacking the natural progression, multi-tasking, and spontaneity of real life.

Within egocentric vision, datasets have historically been much smaller and more constrained, often captured in single, controlled environments (e.g., EGTEA Gaze+, GTEA Gaze+, BEOID, CMU). The ADL dataset (Activities of Daily Living) is a notable predecessor, also featuring egocentric recordings in native environments. However, EPIC-KITCHENS massively scales up this concept, boasting 11.5 million frames (55 hours of video) compared to ADL's 1 million frames, and orders of magnitude more action segments and object bounding boxes.

EPIC-KITCHENS distinguishes itself by:
- **Scale and Diversity:** It is the largest egocentric video benchmark to date, featuring data from 32 participants across 4 countries and 10 nationalities in their native, unscripted kitchen environments. This diversity captures a wide array of kitchen habits, cooking styles, and environmental variations.
- **Naturalistic Behavior:** Crucially, the videos depict non-scripted daily activities, recorded every time a participant entered their kitchen. This captures genuine multi-tasking (e.g., washing dishes amidst cooking), search behaviors, decision-making, and even unexpected events, which are absent in scripted datasets.
- **Rich Multimodal Annotation:** Beyond video, it includes stereo audio and densely annotated action segments and object bounding boxes. The unique aspect of having participants narrate their own videos post-recording provides unparalleled ground-truth for intentions, a critical component for understanding human behavior.
- **Focus on Generalizability:** The dataset explicitly defines "seen" and "unseen" kitchen test splits, pushing models to generalize to novel environments, a fundamental challenge for real-world applications of AI.

By offering unscripted, long-duration, multi-tasking interactions with dense, intention-rich annotations across diverse real-world settings, EPIC-KITCHENS provides a crucial resource that bridges the gap between controlled lab settings and the complexity of daily human activities. It positions itself as a foundational benchmark for advancing research in fine-grained action recognition, object interaction, intention prediction, and contextual understanding in egocentric vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of the EPIC-KITCHENS dataset is to push the boundaries of egocentric video understanding by providing a large-scale, richly annotated, and uniquely diverse benchmark of natural human activities.

The primary motivations behind its creation are multifaceted:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Video Understanding:** The authors identify a significant bottleneck in video understanding research: the lack of sufficiently large and diverse annotated datasets, especially for egocentric (first-person) perspectives. Existing benchmarks are often limited in duration, contain scripted actions, or lack comprehensive annotations, thus hindering the development of robust deep learning models.
- **Capturing True Human Intent and Natural Interaction:** Traditional datasets, even for daily activities, often rely on scripted scenarios, which fail to capture the spontaneity, multi-tasking, and varied ways humans perform mundane tasks. By having participants record *all* their daily kitchen activities without a script, EPIC-KITCHENS captures genuine human intentions, goals, and the fluid, often parallel, nature of real-world actions (e.g., "washing a few dishes amidst cooking"). The participant narrations, collected post-recording, are designed to reflect these true intentions, offering a unique "ground-truth of thought."
- **Enabling Fine-Grained Action and Object Understanding:** The dataset aims to facilitate research into fine-grained distinctions between similar actions (e.g., "closing a tap" vs. "opening it up") and detailed object interactions. Dense annotations of both action segments and active object bounding boxes, combined with verb-noun classifications, provide the necessary granularity.
- **Promoting Generalizability to Novel Environments:** A critical motivation is to challenge models to generalize beyond previously seen environments. The inclusion of "seen" and "unseen" kitchen test splits directly addresses the need for robust models that can function effectively in diverse, unencountered real-world settings.
- **Stimulating Research Beyond Traditional Action Recognition:** While providing benchmarks for object detection, action recognition, and anticipation, the unscripted and untrimmed nature of the recordings motivates broader research. This includes understanding routines, predicting future actions, reasoning about objects, and even inferring cognitive states like attention and intention, thereby moving towards a more holistic understanding of human activity.
- **Encouraging Multimodal Learning:** The dataset's inclusion of video, audio, and participant narrations encourages the development of multimodal learning approaches that can leverage various streams of information for more accurate and robust understanding.

In essence, EPIC-KITCHENS was created to provide a more realistic, challenging, and comprehensive foundation for developing intelligent systems capable of understanding human behavior from a first-person perspective, with direct implications for applications in smart homes, assistive technologies, and human-robot interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the EPIC-KITCHENS dataset involved a meticulous, multi-stage methodology for data collection and dense annotation, designed to capture naturalistic human activities and intentions.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

- **Participants and Environment:** 32 individuals volunteered to record their daily kitchen activities in their native kitchen environments across 4 cities (Bristol/UK, Toronto/Canada, Catania/Italy, Seattle/USA) between May and November 2017. Participants represented 10 different nationalities, ensuring a high degree of diversity in cooking styles and kitchen layouts.
- **Recording Protocol:** Participants were instructed to start recording immediately before entering their kitchen and stop only upon leaving, for at least three consecutive days. This "always-on" approach captured non-scripted, untrimmed, and authentic daily routines, including multi-tasking and non-goal-oriented behaviors. Identity-disclosing items were removed.
- **Equipment:** Head-mounted GoPro cameras were used to capture egocentric video and stereo audio. The camera settings were largely standardized (Full HD 1920x1080 resolution, 59.94fps, linear field of view), though minor variations occurred due to participant adjustments.
- **Data Volume:** The collection resulted in 55 hours of video, totaling 11.5 million frames, with an average of 1.7 hours of recording per participant across 13.6 sequences.

#### 3.2. Annotation Pipeline

The annotation process was designed to leverage participants' unique insight into their own actions and intentions, followed by crowd-sourcing for refinement.

- **Participant Narrations (Coarse Annotation):** After completing their recordings, each participant watched their own videos and narrated the actions they performed using a hand-held recording device. This "post-filming narration" (referred to as "speech") was chosen over written captions for its speed and naturalness, reflecting true intention without disturbing the original activity. Narrations were collected in English or the participant's native language (5 languages in total).
- **Transcription and Translation:** Automatic speech-to-text APIs proved inaccurate due to the informal language and limited context. Thus, Amazon Mechanical Turk (AMT) workers manually transcribed the speech. YouTube's automatic closed caption alignment tool was then used to produce accurate timings for the narrations. Non-English narrations were also translated by AMT workers.
- **Action Segment Annotations (Temporal Localization):** For each narrated sentence, AMT workers were tasked with precisely adjusting the start and end times of the corresponding action segment within the video. To ensure quality, 4 annotators (K_a=4) were assigned to each task, trained with a tutorial on temporal localization. A "union of best agreements" consensus function (Eq. 7) was employed to combine annotations, prioritizing agreement and ensuring sufficiently encompassing temporal bounds. This yielded 39,564 densely labeled action segments (average length 3.7 seconds), noting that 24% of these overlapped, highlighting concurrent activities.
- **Active Object Bounding Box Annotations:** Nouns from the narrations corresponded to objects involved in the actions. For each such object, bounding boxes were drawn by AMT workers in frames surrounding the action segment ([t_s - 2s, t_e + 2s]). To maintain consistency, the same annotator worked on consecutive frames for a given object, and a maximum agreement metric (Eq. 8) selected the best annotator out of 3 (K_o=3). In total, 454,158 bounding boxes were collected, along with 125,375 "true negative" labels for objects that were absent.
- **Verb and Noun Class Grouping:** Given the free-form and multi-lingual nature of narrations, participants used diverse vocabulary (e.g., 'put', 'place', 'put-down'). Automated semantic clustering methods (WordNet, Word2Vec, Lesk) were found insufficient due to limited context. Therefore, verbs and nouns were manually grouped into minimally overlapping semantic classes (125 verb classes, C_V, and 331 noun classes, C_N). This involved preprocessing compound nouns and iteratively merging/splitting categories until authors were satisfied with the semantic coherence.

#### 3.3. Annotation Quality Assurance

To quantify the reliability of the annotations, 300 random samples were manually assessed for correctness. The reported error rates were:
- Action Segment Boundaries: 5.7%
- Object Bounding Boxes: 6.3%
- Verb Classes: 3.3%
- Noun Classes: 6.0%
These error rates are considered comparable to other large-scale datasets, indicating a high standard of annotation quality for the complexity involved.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper establishes three key challenges—Object Detection, Action Recognition, and Action Anticipation—and evaluates several baselines across two test splits: **Seen Kitchens (S1)** and **Unseen Kitchens (S2)**. S1 assesses performance within familiar environments, while S2 evaluates generalization to entirely new kitchens/participants, including zero-shot classes (actions/objects not seen in training).

#### 4.1. Object Detection Benchmark

- **Methodology:** Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-101 backbone, pre-trained on MS-COCO, was used to detect active objects (nouns from narrations).
- **Results (Table 6, 7, Fig. 12):**
 - Performance, measured by mean Average Precision (mAP), was generally low. At IoU > 0.5, mAP was 28.06% for S1 and 28.57% for S2. Even at a very low IoU threshold (>0.05), performance remained relatively low, suggesting the inherent difficulty of detecting active objects in egocentric views, which often involves occlusions, fine-grained details, and diverse viewpoints.
 - Many-shot classes (≥100 bounding boxes in training) significantly outperformed few-shot classes (<100 bounding boxes), with few-shot performance falling below 10% mAP. This highlights a critical challenge for learning novel objects from limited examples.
 - The comparable performance between S1 and S2 suggests that Faster R-CNN exhibits reasonable generalization capability across different kitchen environments for this task.
 - Analysis by bounding box size showed comparable performance across different object sizes, with slightly better results for smaller objects.
 - Qualitative examples (Fig. 12) illustrated successful detections, even under occlusion, but also highlighted common failure cases due to object ambiguity or difficulty in distinguishing similar items.

#### 4.2. Action Recognition Benchmark

- **Methodology:** Four temporal network architectures were benchmarked: Two-stream CNN (2SCNN), Temporal Segment Networks (TSN), Temporal Relational Networks (TRN), and Temporal Shift Module (TSM). These were evaluated using RGB, optical flow, and audio modalities, as well as their fusions. Predictions were made for verbs, nouns, and their combination (action).
- **Modality and Fusion Results (Table 8, Fig. 14):**
 - **Modality Strengths:** Flow achieved the best performance for verb classification, while RGB was strongest for noun classification. Audio, despite being a wearable camera stream, showed surprisingly good performance, especially for verb classification on S1.
 - **Generalization Gap:** Noun classification performance dropped notably for both RGB and audio on the unseen S2 split, making flow the most transferable modality to novel environments. Interestingly, audio sometimes outperformed RGB for verb accuracy on S2.
 - **Multimodal Fusion:** Fusing all three modalities (RGB+Flow+Audio) consistently outperformed individual modalities across all metrics (e.g., S1 Top-1 Action Accuracy improved from 22.44% (RGB) to 26.06% (Fusion)).
 - **Challenge:** Despite fusion, the overall action recognition (correctly classifying both verb and noun) remained challenging (S1: 26.06% Top-1 accuracy, S2: 15.61%). Confusion matrices (Fig. 14) visually reinforced how fusion reduces confusion across top classes.
- **Temporal Model Results (Table 9, Fig. 13):**
 - **Importance of Temporal Modeling:** Architectures explicitly designed for temporal modeling (TRN, TSM) showed significant improvements for verb classification over simpler models like 2SCNN and TSN (e.g., 6.4-8.0% increase in Top-1 verb accuracy for S1). This underscores the necessity of capturing temporal dynamics for understanding fine-grained actions in egocentric videos.
 - **Less Impact on Nouns:** Temporal modeling had less pronounced effects on noun classification, as object appearance is often more static.
 - **Generalization of Temporal Models:** While TSM generally outperformed TRN on S1, TRN showed better performance on S2 for more metrics, suggesting TSM might be less generalizable to unseen actions.
 - Qualitative examples (Fig. 13) highlighted successes (e.g., TSM correctly predicting 'open bottle') and failures due to object ambiguity (e.g., 'get fork' misclassified as 'put tomato') or visual similarity between actions.

#### 4.3. Action Anticipation Benchmark

- **Methodology:** The task was to predict an action before it starts (anticipation time τ_a = 1s), based on an observed video segment (observation time τ_o varying from 0.5s to 2s). Adapted versions of 2SCNN, TSN, TRN, Deep Multimodal Regressor (DMR), and Encoder-Decoder (ED) architectures were evaluated.
- **Results (Table 10, Fig. 15, 16):**
 - **High Difficulty:** As expected, anticipation results were significantly lower than recognition results, emphasizing the inherent difficulty of predicting future actions in complex, unscripted environments.
 - **Model Performance:** No single method clearly dominated across all metrics. ED showed some advantage in S1 for action-related metrics but not in S2. DMR, despite being designed for anticipation, performed poorly, suggesting that regression-based future representation prediction might be less suitable for the high variability of egocentric video.
 - **Observation Time Sensitivity:** Simpler models (2SCNN, TSN) struggled to exploit longer observation times effectively, especially for action-based measures. TRN, with its more principled aggregation of segments, was less sensitive to observation time in S1 and even benefited from longer observation times in S2, indicating a better capacity for reasoning about past context for future prediction.
 - Qualitative analysis (Fig. 16) showed successful anticipations when the target object was clearly visible and indicative (e.g., "wash cup"). Failures often stemmed from the inherent ambiguity of the future or the need for a much longer temporal context to infer high-level goals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EPIC-KITCHENS dataset represents a landmark contribution to the computer vision and artificial intelligence communities, with profound significance and wide-ranging potential impact:

- **Pioneering Egocentric Vision Research:** By providing the largest, most diverse, and most naturalistic egocentric dataset to date, EPIC-KITCHENS sets a new standard for research in first-person vision. It moves the field beyond controlled, scripted scenarios to address the complexities of real-world human behavior and interaction.
- **Advancing Fine-Grained Understanding:** The dense, detailed annotations of action segments, active object bounding boxes, and fine-grained verb-noun classifications enable research into subtle distinctions in human actions and precise object interactions. This level of detail is crucial for developing truly intelligent systems that can understand the "how" and "what" of human activity.
- **Benchmarking Generalizability:** The explicit "seen" and "unseen" kitchen test splits directly tackle the critical challenge of model generalization to novel environments. This is a fundamental requirement for deploying AI systems in real-world applications and will drive the development of more robust and adaptable algorithms.
- **Highlighting Current Limitations and Future Directions:** The baseline results, while demonstrating the utility of existing methods, also starkly reveal their limitations, particularly for action anticipation and for handling the diversity and naturalness of the data. This highlights exciting avenues for future research in areas such as:
 - **Temporal Modeling:** The clear performance gains from TRN and TSM emphasize the need for more sophisticated temporal reasoning architectures.
 - **Multimodal Fusion:** The benefits of combining RGB, flow, and audio modalities underscore the potential for richer contextual understanding.
 - **Long-term Reasoning and Intention Prediction:** The challenges in action anticipation point to the need for models that can reason about longer temporal contexts and infer underlying human intentions and goals.
 - **Few-Shot/Zero-Shot Learning:** The poor performance on few-shot object classes calls for innovative approaches to learn from limited examples, crucial for adapting to new objects in an open-world setting.
- **Enabling New Research Tasks:** The "task-agnostic" approach to data collection, where the data was gathered first and then tasks defined, allows for diverse explorations beyond the initial benchmarks. The authors explicitly mention potential future work in dense captioning, skill understanding, multi-day routine modeling, video object reasoning, action retrieval, visual learning of novel words, unsupervised domain adaptation, and environmental affordances. These tasks are essential for building truly intelligent assistive technologies or smart environments.
- **Driving Real-World Applications:** The insights gained from EPIC-KITCHENS can directly contribute to the development of applications such as:
 - **Smart Home Systems:** Proactive assistance, appliance control, and safety warnings based on anticipated user actions.
 - **Assisted Living Technologies:** Monitoring daily activities for elderly or individuals with disabilities, providing guidance or detecting anomalies.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Robots better understanding human intentions and adapting their actions in shared workspaces.
 - **Skill Transfer and Learning:** Analyzing expert demonstrations to teach skills or provide feedback.

In conclusion, EPIC-KITCHENS is not merely a dataset; it is a catalyst for fundamental research, providing a challenging yet rich foundation for the next generation of AI systems capable of perceiving and understanding human activity in the complexity of the real world.
