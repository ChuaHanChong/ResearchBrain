# PAI-Bench: A Comprehensive Benchmark For Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Tech and Carnegie Mellon University introduce PAI-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate the perception and prediction capabilities of multimodal large language models and video generative models within Physical AI domains. The evaluation reveals that current AI models achieve high visual fidelity but consistently struggle with physical plausibility and understanding, underscoring a substantial gap between current AI capabilities and human-level physical intelligence.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) and video generative models (VGMs) primarily focus on abstract reasoning or aesthetic quality, insufficiently assessing their core Physical AI abilities like real-world physics perception and prediction.
- There is a lack of unified evaluation frameworks that integrate both perception and prediction, using physically plausible dynamics and real-world data, to provide a holistic view of AI's readiness for physical interaction.
- Current benchmarks do not rigorously evaluate the fundamental understanding of physical laws, complex interactions, and predictive accuracy in practical, specialized Physical AI scenarios.

## Method

- **PAI-Bench** introduces a three-track benchmark: PAI-Bench-G for video generation (assessing predictive physical plausibility), PAI-Bench-C for conditional video generation (evaluating fidelity to control signals), and PAI-Bench-U for physical video understanding (probing MLLM perception).
- The benchmark curates real-world video data from Physical AI domains (e.g., autonomous driving, robotics) with MLLM-assisted and manual annotation for diverse tasks and questions.
- Evaluation utilizes a combination of metrics, including Quality Scores (visual fidelity), Domain Scores (physical plausibility via an "MLLM-as-Judge" paradigm), Control Fidelity (modality-specific metrics), and Accuracy for understanding tasks.

## Results

- Video generative models achieved high Quality Scores, competitive with source videos, but significantly underperformed in Domain Scores (physical plausibility) compared to real content.
- Conditional video generative models demonstrated improved visual quality when using multi-signal conditioning, but noisy control signals (e.g., segmentation maps) could lead to decreased fidelity.
- All evaluated MLLMs lagged substantially behind the human baseline (**93.2%** accuracy) on Physical AI understanding tasks, and models performed at random guessing levels with text-only input, confirming the necessity of visual grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generative models exhibit high visual quality and temporal consistency but consistently fall short in generating physically plausible content, indicating a fundamental lack of understanding of real-world dynamics.
> - Multi-modal large language models show a significant performance gap compared to human baselines in physical common sense and embodied reasoning tasks, suggesting that Physical AI is not yet a primary optimization focus for the field.
> - Integrated visual reasoning is critical for MLLMs, as text-only 'thinking modes' can sometimes degrade performance in complex, visually-demanding physical reasoning tasks due to insufficient grounding in fine-grained visual details.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence is progressively moving towards developing systems that can interact with and understand the physical world, moving beyond purely digital tasks. This paradigm is encapsulated by "Physical AI," which aims to develop models capable of perceiving, predicting, and acting within real-world dynamics. Historically, AI systems have excelled in controlled, data-rich digital environments, but their transition to messy, unpredictable reality presents significant challenges.

This paper identifies a critical gap in the current research landscape: the insufficient understanding and evaluation of how modern multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) and video generative models (VGMs) support the core abilities required for Physical AI, namely perception and prediction of real-world physics. Existing benchmarks for MLLMs tend to focus on abstract reasoning or simple daily-life perception, leaving their efficacy in specialized Physical AI applications largely unexplored. Similarly, current benchmarks for VGMs primarily assess aesthetic appeal and temporal consistency, neglecting a rigorous evaluation of their fundamental understanding of physical laws, complex interactions, and predictive accuracy in practical scenarios.

PAI-Bench directly addresses these deficiencies by introducing a unified and comprehensive benchmark. It bridges the gap between general-purpose multimodal AI evaluation and the specific demands of Physical AI. By explicitly focusing on physically plausible dynamics, domain-specific reasoning, and real-world data across generation, conditional generation, and understanding tasks, PAI-Bench provides a much-needed framework for:
- **Advancing World Models:** It rigorously tests the implicit world models learned by VGMs, which are crucial for forecasting future states in robotics and autonomous driving.
- **Grounding MLLMs:** It moves MLLM evaluation beyond abstract reasoning to physically grounded understanding, critical for embodied AI agents.
- **Unifying Evaluation:** Unlike fragmented benchmarks that assess prediction or perception in isolation, PAI-Bench integrates both, offering a holistic view of AI's readiness for physical interaction.

This work sets a new standard for evaluating AI systems designed for real-world interaction, pushing the research community to develop models that not only generate visually convincing outputs but also embody a deep understanding of physical laws.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core mission of Physical AI is to enable intelligent algorithms to perceive, predict, and act directly within the physical world. The authors argue that for AI systems to truly mature beyond mere information processors, they must overcome the limitations of digital simulations and engage with the complexities of reality. This paper focuses specifically on the perception and prediction components of this mission, primarily through visual signals in video streams.

The key objectives and motivations behind introducing PAI-Bench are:

- **To systematically evaluate the perception and prediction capabilities of current state-of-the-art AI models in Physical AI domains.** The authors observed a lack of dedicated benchmarks that truly test physical plausibility, causal interpretation, and forecasting abilities in MLLMs and VGMs.
- **To provide a unified and comprehensive benchmark framework.** Existing evaluations are often fragmented, assessing only prediction or perception, or focusing on narrow aspects like visual fidelity rather than physical coherence. PAI-Bench aims to integrate these dimensions.
- **To ground evaluation in physically meaningful tasks and real-world data.** The benchmark is explicitly designed using real-world video captures (e.g., dashcam recordings, robotics data) and task-aligned metrics that capture physical plausibility and domain-specific reasoning, moving beyond aesthetic evaluations.
- **To highlight key gaps and challenges in current AI systems.** By conducting a systematic assessment, the authors aim to identify where current models (VGMs and MLLMs) fall short in handling the perceptual and predictive demands of Physical AI.
- **To serve as a realistic foundation and catalyst for future research.** PAI-Bench is intended to guide the development of future AI systems that can internalize underlying physics and enable robust action in the physical world.

To achieve these objectives, PAI-Bench is structured into three distinct tracks:
- **PAI-Bench-G (Generation):** To evaluate prediction capabilities via video generative models, assessing both visual quality and physical plausibility.
- **PAI-Bench-C (Conditional Generation):** To further probe prediction by evaluating the fidelity of conditional video generative models to explicit control signals.
- **PAI-Bench-U (Understanding):** To evaluate perception capabilities of MLLMs on video understanding tasks specific to Physical AI, including physical common sense and embodied reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PAI-Bench is structured into three distinct tracks, each with specific objectives, data curation strategies, and evaluation metrics designed to assess different facets of Physical AI. The unified design principle across all tracks is to ground evaluation in physically meaningful tasks using real-world data.

#### 3.1. PAI-Bench-G: Video Generation

- **Objective:** To evaluate the prediction capability of Video Generative Models (VGMs) by forecasting physically plausible states.
- **Data Curation:** Videos are curated from Physical AI domains (autonomous driving, robotics, industrial applications) using open-source datasets (e.g., EgoExo4D, Agibot) and public web sources. A two-stage annotation process involves using an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) for initial video captioning, followed by rigorous manual curation. QA pairs for Domain Score assessment are generated leveraging an ontology and MLLM-based candidate generation, with manual refinement. The dataset comprises 1,044 video-prompt pairs and 5,636 QA pairs across 6 domains.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Quality Score:** Adopts protocols from VBench and VBench++ to assess visual fidelity, temporal consistency, and semantic alignment. Metrics include Subject Consistency (DINO features), Background Consistency (CLIP features), Motion Smoothness (frame interpolation MAE), Aesthetic Quality (LAION aesthetic predictor), Imaging Quality (MUSIQ predictor), Overall Consistency (ViCLIP video-text consistency), Image-to-Video Subject (DINO features), and Image-to-Video Background (DreamSim features).
 - **Domain Score:** Assesses physical plausibility using an "MLLM-as-Judge" paradigm. Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct is used to answer curated QA pairs derived from the ground truth. The score is the MLLM's accuracy against expected binary responses, quantifying adherence to physical and semantic constraints.
- **Model Evaluation:** 15 leading VGMs (proprietary like Veo3, open-source like DynamicCrafter, LTX-Video, MAGI, CogVideoX, Cosmos-Predict, Wan) are evaluated. An arena-based human study with ELO ratings validates the alignment of the proposed metrics with human preferences.

#### 3.2. PAI-Bench-C: Conditional Video Generation

- **Objective:** To evaluate the fidelity of conditional VGMs to input control signals, assessing faithfulness, visual quality, and generative diversity.
- **Data Curation:** 600 videos sampled from AgiBot (robotics), OpenDV (autonomous driving), and Ego-Exo-4D (ego-centric tasks). Ground-truth control signals (blur, edge, depth, segmentation) are extracted using modality-specific models (Blur Kernel, Canny, Video Depth Anything, GroundingDINO with SAM2). For diversity, an MLLM-in-the-loop pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) generates base and variant captions, followed by manual refinement.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Control Fidelity:** Blur SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure for blurred video), Edge F1 (F1 score for Canny edge maps), Depth si-RMSE (scale-invariant Root Mean Squared Error for depth maps), and Mask mIoU (mean Intersection over Union for segmentation maps).
 - **Visual Quality:** DOVER (technical reference-free video quality assessment).
 - **Generation Diversity:** LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) between generated videos under identical conditions.
- **Model Evaluation:** 4 conditional VGMs from Cosmos-Transfer and Wan-Fun families are evaluated across five conditioning signals (blur, edge, depth, segmentation, and an "All" combination).

#### 3.3. PAI-Bench-U: Physical Video Understanding

- **Objective:** To evaluate the perception capabilities of MLLMs on video understanding tasks requiring physical common sense and embodied reasoning.
- **Data Curation:**
 - **Physical Common Sense Reasoning:** Over 1,000 videos from online sources, manually annotated with 5,737 questions based on an ontology (Space, Time, Physical World). Refined to 604 high-quality QA pairs across 426 videos.
 - **Embodied Reasoning:** 601 videos from existing datasets (RoboVQA, RoboFail, BridgeData, AgiBot, HoloAssist, proprietary AV dataset), with 610 manually annotated QA pairs. Focus on predicting action effects (task-completion verification, next-plausible action) and adherence to physical constraints (action affordance). Unified question templates and action granularity (hierarchical taxonomy) are enforced.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy on multiple-choice questions within the defined common sense and embodied reasoning categories.
- **Model Evaluation:** 21 MLLMs (proprietary like Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o, GPT-5; open-source like Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, Cosmos-Reason1, InternVL3.5, GLM-4.5V). LMMs-Eval framework used, with 16 video frames as input (8 for InternVL3.5). Qwen3-8B is used for post-processing MLLM responses. A human baseline is established.
- **Bias Analysis:** Evaluated models with varying input frame counts (0 to 32) to confirm that performance requires visual grounding and temporal context, effectively neutralizing language priors and static bias.
- **Ablation Study:** Investigated the impact of "thinking mode" (generating an intermediate rationale) on MLLM performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation across the three PAI-Bench tracks revealed several critical insights into the current state of Physical AI:

- **Video Generative Models (VGMs) excel in visual quality but lack physical plausibility (PAI-Bench-G).**
 - Leading VGMs (e.g., Veo3, Wan2.2-I2V-A14B, Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B) achieved high Quality Scores, often competitive with source videos, demonstrating strong visual fidelity and aesthetic appeal.
 - However, all evaluated models significantly underperformed in Domain Score compared to real-world source videos. This highlights a critical deficiency: while VGMs can synthesize visually convincing content, they often fail to adhere to fundamental physical laws and complex real-world dynamics.
 - Human evaluation confirmed a strong correlation between PAI-Bench's metrics and human preferences for both video quality and physical plausibility.

- **Multi-signal conditioning enhances video quality in conditional generation, but noisy signals can degrade fidelity (PAI-Bench-C).**
 - For Cosmos-Transfer models, using an "All" condition (combining blur, edge, depth, and segmentation signals) consistently resulted in the highest Quality Scores, outperforming single control signals. This suggests that providing complementary control signals can significantly improve reconstruction quality.
 - Conversely, using segmentation maps as a control signal led to the lowest Mask mIoU for Cosmos-Transfer models. The authors hypothesize this is due to segmentation maps often being the noisiest supervision signal, with state-of-the-art segmentation models sometimes producing masks with lower temporal consistency, thus degrading model performance.

- **Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lag significantly behind human performance in physical understanding (PAI-Bench-U).**
 - There is a substantial performance gap between all MLLMs and the human baseline (93.2% accuracy) on Physical AI video understanding tasks. Even top proprietary models like GPT-5 could not close this gap.
 - Surprisingly, proprietary models did not uniformly outperform open-source models; for instance, Qwen3-VL-235B achieved a higher accuracy than GPT-5 in some overall metrics. This suggests that Physical AI has not yet been a primary optimization focus for the broader AI field, leading to a "community-wide data gap" or a failure of current models to learn the specific capabilities required.
 - PAI-Bench-U was validated as an unbiased benchmark: models performed at random guessing levels with text-only input (zero frames) and showed significant improvement with even one frame, confirming the necessity of visual and temporal context.

- **Textual thinking alone does not guarantee performance gains; integrated visual reasoning is crucial (PAI-Bench-U).**
 - Ablation studies showed that enabling a textual-only "thinking mode" (generating intermediate rationales) unexpectedly led to slight performance degradation for some Qwen3-VL models, particularly in embodied reasoning tasks.
 - This is attributed to the high demands of these tasks, requiring fine-grained visual details. If the vision modules fail to capture these nuances, subsequent textual reasoning lacks proper grounding.
 - In contrast, GPT-5, which employs a more integrated textual and visual thinking process, showed clear performance gains with increased reasoning effort, underscoring the necessity of advancing visual thinking capabilities for Physical AI.

**Overall Conclusion:** The findings from PAI-Bench strongly indicate that **Physical AI remains a largely unsolved domain**, regardless of whether models are proprietary or open-source. While visual fidelity is improving, a deep, physically grounded understanding and predictive capability are still in their nascent stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PAI-Bench represents a significant contribution to the advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in its transition towards embodied agency and real-world interaction. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

- **Establishing a Rigorous Baseline:** By providing a unified, comprehensive, and physically grounded benchmark, PAI-Bench sets a new standard for evaluating AI models. This allows researchers to rigorously assess the true capabilities of current and future systems in critical areas of perception and prediction for Physical AI.
- **Highlighting Critical Gaps:** The study's clear findings—that VGMs struggle with physical plausibility despite high visual fidelity, and MLLMs lag significantly behind human performance in physical understanding—pinpoint crucial areas where current AI models are deficient. This diagnostic capability is invaluable for guiding research efforts.
- **Driving Future Research Directions:** PAI-Bench is designed to be a catalyst for innovation. By exposing the limitations of current models, it motivates the research community to:
 - **Develop truly physics-aware generative models:** Future VGMs should aim to internalize underlying physical laws rather than merely replicating visual appearance.
 - **Enhance physically grounded reasoning in MLLMs:** MLLMs need to improve their capacity for common sense reasoning about the physical world and robust embodied forecasting.
 - **Improve multimodal integration:** The findings on visual thinking highlight the need for more deeply integrated visual and textual reasoning within MLLMs, rather than sequential or superficial reasoning.
 - **Address data challenges:** The "community-wide data gap" hypothesis suggests a need for focused, large-scale data collection efforts specifically targeting physical interactions and dynamics.
- **Accelerating Real-World Applications:** Progress in Physical AI is fundamental for numerous high-impact applications, including autonomous vehicles, robotics, industrial automation, and assistive technologies. By providing a benchmark that more realistically assesses these capabilities, PAI-Bench will help accelerate the development of more reliable, safe, and truly intelligent autonomous systems.
- **Fostering Collaboration and Open Science:** The release of the PAI-Bench dataset, code, and a leaderboard encourages open competition and collaboration within the research community, facilitating faster progress. The acknowledgement of NVIDIA Research and the Cosmos team also points towards the benchmark's role in industrial AI development.

In summary, PAI-Bench moves the conversation in AI evaluation beyond superficial metrics to essential, physically meaningful criteria. It provides a robust framework to evaluate whether AI models merely "see" the world or genuinely "understand" its underlying physics, thus serving as a critical tool for bridging the gap towards truly intelligent and capable Physical AI.
