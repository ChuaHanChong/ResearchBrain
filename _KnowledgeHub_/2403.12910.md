# Yell At Your Robot: Improving On-the-Fly from Language Corrections

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a hierarchical robot learning framework, Yell At Your Robot (YAY Robot), that enables real-time adaptation and continuous policy improvement from natural language corrections. The system demonstrated up to 50% increases in task success rates during human intervention and subsequent autonomous performance improvements of up to 45% across complex bimanual manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Achieving high success rates for complex, long-horizon robotic tasks is challenging due to brittleness and compounding errors over many steps.
- Scalably acquiring high-quality training data for robust high-level policies, which are often costly or not sufficiently grounded for real robot behaviors.
- Enabling intuitive and accessible human intervention to correct robot failures and facilitate learning without requiring specialized interfaces or programming.

## Method

- A hierarchical learning architecture with a low-level language-conditioned behavior cloning (LCBC) policy for executing actions and a high-level policy for generating language instructions.
- An on-the-fly mechanism allowing human verbal commands to temporarily override the high-level policy's output, feeding corrections directly to the low-level policy for immediate adaptation.
- An iterative fine-tuning process, analogous to DAgger, where the high-level policy is retrained on a dataset augmented with human language corrections, leading to continuous autonomous improvement.

## Results

- On-the-fly language corrections significantly improved success rates by 25-**50%** in Bag Packing, 30-**45%** in Trail Mix Preparation, and 15-**25%** in Plate Cleaning tasks on real robots.
- Iterative fine-tuning with collected language corrections enhanced the autonomous policy's success rate by 20-**45%** in Bag Packing, 15-**20%** in Trail Mix, and 15-**25%** in Plate Cleaning.
- The hierarchical YAY Robot framework consistently outperformed flat imitation learning policies (ACT) on long-horizon tasks, demonstrating superior management of compounding errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging natural language as a real-time corrective feedback mechanism allows for scalable human-robot interaction and on-the-fly adaptation.
> - Training a learned high-level policy on human language corrections enables continuous improvement of autonomous robot behavior, outperforming pre-scripted or ungrounded VLM approaches.
> - Hierarchical policies with language-conditioned low-level skills effectively manage compounding errors in complex, long-horizon tasks, providing robustness over flat policy architectures.
