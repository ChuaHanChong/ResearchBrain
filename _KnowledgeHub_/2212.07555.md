# IMos: Intent-Driven Full-Body Motion Synthesis for Human-Object Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> IMoS synthesizes full-body human motion for object interactions from text instructions naming an intent and object. Two decoupled conditional variational autoregressors generate arm and body motion separately, conditioned on CLIP intent embeddings, while an optimization module fits the object's 6-DoF pose into the hands. It beats ACTOR, Action2Motion and TEMOS on all metrics and wins 80% of perceptual realism votes.

## Problem

- Full-body action synthesis methods condition on action labels alone and ignore objects entirely, so generated motion never accounts for what the character holds.
- Hand-object grasp synthesis models only wrist and finger movement; attaching those hands to a body yields uncanny, physically implausible sequences for intents needing head and torso.
- Full-body grasping works stop at the moment of grasp, leaving motion after contact, the part carrying the actual intent, entirely unaddressed.

## Method

- A **Condition Encoder** maps a one-hot object category, a ==CLIP== embedding of the intent word, and ==SMPL-X== body shape parameters through MLPs into one 400-dimensional conditioning vector.
- Decoupled **Arm Synthesis** and **Body Synthesis** Modules, both ==conditional variational autoregressors== over the previous four frames, sample separate 32-d and 100-d latents; the body branch adds position-encoded joint ==self-attention==.
- An **Object Optimizer Module** solves per frame for object rotation, translation and hand pose under constant hand-object inter-vertex distance, first-frame contact, and temporal-smoothness energies.

## Results

- On the GRAB test split, IMoS reaches **0.03** MPJPE, **3.82** AVE and **0.27** FID versus ACTOR's **0.09**, **8.05** and **0.67**, with recognition accuracy **0.87** against ground-truth **0.97**.
- In a 75-participant study over 30 sequences, **80%** judged IMoS most realistic and **81.6%** best matched to the instruction label, over ACTOR and Action2Motion.
- Ablations confirm each part: random label vectors instead of CLIP give **0.39** FID, no self-attention **0.38**, one whole-body module **0.42**, all above the full model's **0.27**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling limbs that drive an interaction from the rest of the body into separate latent spaces buys precision and diversity a single whole-body generator loses.
> - Pretrained language embeddings act as a semantic regularizer: semantically close intents map to similar motions, a structure randomly initialized label vectors cannot supply.
> - Coordinated head-hand motion emerged without explicit supervision, questioning hand-crafted head-direction terms, though L1 losses smooth away high-frequency wrist motions like shaking or squeezing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human motion synthesis has seen rapid advancements, driven by applications in virtual reality, gaming, film production, and robotics. This paper, "IMoS," addresses a critical, yet previously underserved, niche within this landscape: the synthesis of *full-body motion for human characters interacting with handheld objects based on high-level textual instructions, and specifically, the motion that occurs *after* an object has been grasped.*

Existing research can be broadly categorized, and IMoS positions itself by addressing the limitations of each:

- **Human Pose Forecasting and Synthesis:** Many methods predict future poses from past sequences or generate motions conditioned on abstract action labels (e.g., "walking," "jumping") or sentences. However, these methods typically **do not consider explicit object interactions**, or if they do, they are often with large, static environmental objects (e.g., chairs, walls) rather than handheld items requiring fine motor skills.
- **Hand-Object Grasp Synthesis:** A significant body of work focuses solely on synthesizing plausible hand and finger movements for grasping objects. While sophisticated, these methods are **inherently limited to just the hands** and do not generate the coordinated full-body motion required for complex interactions. Simply attaching a synthesized hand motion to a rigid body often results in "uncanny and physically implausible" animations.
- **Full-Body Grasp Synthesis:** More recent efforts, such as GOAL and SAGA, have begun to tackle full-body motion leading up to a grasp. Crucially, these methods typically **synthesize motion *only until the moment of grasping***. They do not address the subsequent actions a character performs with the object (e.g., drinking from a cup *after* grasping it, inspecting an apple, or passing an object). This represents a significant gap because a character's intent often manifests *after* the initial grasp.
- **Human-Object 3D Interaction Modeling with Large Objects:** Some methods model interactions with larger scene objects (e.g., sitting on a couch, interacting with a wall). These are usually driven by low-level character control or geometric constraints and do not translate well to fine-grained, intent-driven interactions with handheld objects.

IMoS directly bridges these gaps. It is presented as the *first* framework to combine **intent-driven full-body motion synthesis** with **object manipulation**, including the critical aspect of **6-DoF object pose optimization** throughout the interaction, and supporting both single and two-handed interactions, even for scenarios like "off-handing" (passing an object between hands). This holistic approach sets it apart and significantly advances the state-of-the-art in creating realistic, dynamic virtual character animations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of IMoS is to develop a framework that can synthesize plausible, diverse, and full-body motion sequences of virtual human characters interacting with 3D objects based on simple, text-based instructions.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and demands:

- **Plausible and Diverse Object Interaction:** Humans constantly interact with objects in myriad ways. The ability to synthesize these interactions plausibly and with natural diversity (e.g., different ways of holding, using, or inspecting an object) is crucial for realistic virtual environments.
- **Bridging the Instruction-to-Motion Gap:** The goal is to move beyond low-level motion control or simple action labels to a more intuitive, high-level, intent-driven input. Textual instructions (e.g., "eat the apple," "drink from the cup") offer a natural language interface for content creation.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current Methods:** As discussed in the previous section, existing methods fall short. Full-body motion synthesis often neglects objects, while object interaction methods focus narrowly on hand grasps. Methods that do consider full-body grasping stop at the initial contact, leaving the actual "interaction" phase unaddressed.
- **Efficiency in Content Creation:** Manually animating diverse 3D character-object interactions is an extremely time-consuming and labor-intensive process for content creators in games, film, and virtual reality. An automated, instruction-driven system could dramatically reduce this overhead and enable richer, more dynamic virtual worlds.
- **Importance of Full-Body Coordination:** Realistic interactions involve more than just hands. Actions like eating, drinking, or inspecting an object require coordinated movements of the head, torso, and arms. Ignoring the full body leads to unnatural and "uncanny" animations.
- **Generalizability:** The framework aims to be generalizable across a diverse set of objects and intentions, recognizing that similar intentions might involve different objects, and similar objects can be used for different intentions.

In essence, IMoS seeks to empower virtual characters with intelligent, context-aware interaction capabilities that mimic human behavior, driven by user-friendly semantic inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The IMoS framework is designed as an "intent-driven full-body motion generator" that takes textual instructions and initial body/object states as input to produce full-body motion sequences and corresponding 6-DoF object poses. It achieves this through four interconnected modules:

1. **3D Human Body and Object Representation:**
 - **Human:** The **SMPL-X parametric body model** [PCG*19] is used, representing the full body (including hands and face) with parameters for body shape ($\beta$), root translation ($\mathbf{t}$), and joint rotations ($\mathbf{r}$). Joint rotations are represented using 6D continuous representations [ZBL*19] for stability.
 - **Object:** Objects are downsampled to 300 vertices for efficient processing, and their pose is represented by a 6-DoF (rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}$ and translation vector $\mathbf{T}$).

2. **Condition Encoder:**
 - **Input:** Object category (51-dimensional one-hot vector), intended action label (English word), and body shape parameters ($\beta$).
 - **Action Encoding:** The action label is passed through a **pre-trained CLIP encoder** [RKH*21] to obtain 512-dimensional embeddings. This leverages CLIP's robust semantic understanding from a large text-image corpus, allowing for better generalization and capturing relationships between semantically similar actions.
 - **Output:** A compact, encoded feature vector ($\phi$) of 400 dimensions, serving as the conditioning signal for subsequent motion generation modules.

3. **Decoupled Conditional Variational Autoregressors (Motion Generator):**
 - **Core Idea:** The system employs two separate, conditional variational autoregressors to model the complex interdependencies of human-object interaction more precisely, recognizing that arms are primary movers.
 - **Arm Synthesis Module:**
 - **Type:** Conditional Variational Autoregressor.
 - **Input:** Encoded condition ($\phi$) and the past *k* frames of synthesized arm poses and 6-DoF object positions.
 - **Function:** An encoder maps the input to the parameters ($\mu_a, \sigma_a$) of a latent normal distribution. A decoder samples a latent vector ($z_a$) and generates the arm pose for the current frame. This variational nature allows for diverse motion generation.
 - **Body Synthesis Module:**
 - **Type:** Conditional Variational Autoregressor.
 - **Input:** Encoded condition ($\phi$), the past *k* frames of *synthesized arm poses* (from the Arm Synthesis Module), *position-encoded self-attention mapping* of all body joints from past *k* frames, and past object positions.
 - **Self-Attention:** A crucial component to model fine-grained correlations between all body parts (head, torso, legs) for a coherent full-body action (e.g., tilting the head back when drinking).
 - **Function:** Similar VAE structure to the Arm Synthesis Module, generating the pose for the rest of the body.
 - **Full Pose Assembly:** The synthesized arm and body poses are concatenated to form the full-body pose for the current frame.

4. **Object Optimizer Module:**
 - **Purpose:** To determine the plausible 6-DoF pose of the object relative to the character's hands for each frame.
 - **Assumptions:** (a) Object is initially at rest and upright, (b) inter-vertex distances between hand and object remain constant during interaction (suitable for static grasps, not dynamic ones like slipping).
 - **Optimization:** Minimizes an objective function combining three energy terms:
 - **$E_d$ (Distance):** Enforces constant inter-vertex distances between hand and object from the initial grasp.
 - **$E_c$ (Contact):** Ensures that vertices identified as being in contact in the initial frame remain in contact.
 - **$E_r$ (Regularization):** L2 regularizers on changes in object and hand poses to maintain temporal consistency.
 - **Output:** Optimized object rotation and translation parameters, which also condition the future motion synthesis steps. Hand poses are initialized with a state-of-the-art grasp estimator.

**Training and Inference:**
- The model is trained on the GRAB dataset [TGBT20], which contains sequences of human-object interactions with various intents.
- Training involves minimizing a weighted sum of KL divergence losses (for the VAEs) and pose/velocity reconstruction losses.
- During inference, the model autoregressively generates motion for novel intent-object pairs and body shapes, sampling diverse motions from the latent space.

This modular and decoupled approach, particularly the use of VAEs and CLIP embeddings, allows IMoS to generate diverse, semantically coherent, and physically plausible full-body human-object interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The IMoS framework demonstrates significant advancements in intent-driven full-body human-object motion synthesis, supported by both quantitative evaluations and a perceptual user study.

- **Quantitative State-of-the-Art:**
 - IMoS consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines (ACTOR [PBV21], Action2Motion [GZW*20], TEMOS [PBV22]) across all key metrics:
 - **Mean Per-Joint Positional Error (MPJPE):** IMoS achieves a lower MPJPE (0.03 ± 0.005), indicating higher accuracy in joint positions compared to baselines (e.g., ACTOR: 0.09).
 - **Average Variance Error (AVE):** Lower AVE (3.82 ± 0.004) suggests better consistency and reduced variance in generated joint positions.
 - **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** A significantly lower FID (0.27 ± 0.002) indicates that IMoS produces more natural and realistic motions closer to the ground truth distribution.
 - **Recognition Accuracy:** Higher accuracy (0.87 ± 0.011) implies that the synthesized actions are clearly recognizable and semantically faithful to the input intent.
 - **Diversity and Multimodality:** IMoS shows diversity (1.11 ± 0.015) and multimodality (0.28 ± 0.015) values closer to ground truth, demonstrating its ability to generate varied and realistic motions for the same instruction.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Importance of CLIP Embeddings (Ablation 1):** Randomly initialized action vectors (instead of CLIP) led to worse performance in all metrics (e.g., MPJPE 0.05 vs 0.03 for Ours), highlighting CLIP's critical role in providing robust, semantically meaningful intent conditioning.
 - **Effectiveness of Self-Attention (Ablation 2):** Removing position-encoded self-attention from the Body Synthesis module degraded performance (e.g., MPJPE 0.04 vs 0.03), affirming its contribution to modeling subtle, correlated body movements.
 - **Superiority of Decoupled Synthesis (Ablation 3):** Training a single module for the entire body (instead of decoupled arm and body modules) also resulted in poorer performance (e.g., MPJPE 0.05 vs 0.03), proving that decoupling is vital for precise arm movements and overall coordination.

- **Perceptual Study (User Study):**
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** Users strongly preferred IMoS animations. In 80% of responses, IMoS was chosen as "most realistic," and in 81.6%, it was deemed to "best correspond with the input instruction label," significantly outperforming ACTOR and Action2Motion.
 - **Comparison with Ground-Truth:** In a direct comparison with ground-truth motions, 47.5% of participants rated IMoS's best syntheses as "indistinguishable" in terms of realism. While ground truth was generally preferred (36.9%), IMoS was preferred over ground truth in 15.6% of cases, particularly for actions with abundant training data like "eating an apple with one hand."

- **Qualitative Contributions:**
 - **Diversity:** The model successfully generates diverse motion sequences for the same input instruction by leveraging its variational latent space, showcasing variations in head, arm, and torso angles.
 - **Both-Handed Interactions & Off-Handing:** IMoS is the first to plausibly synthesize full-body motions for both-handed interactions and successfully handles complex scenarios like "off-handing" where an object is passed between hands. The decoupled arm synthesis contributes significantly to this capability.
 - **Plausible Head Motions:** The model implicitly learns visually plausible head orientations and hand-head coordination without explicit supervision, indicating the robustness of its joint learning approach.

- **Identified Limitations:**
 - **High-Frequency Motions:** The model struggles with rare intents involving high-frequency wrist or finger movements (e.g., "squeeze," "shake"), attributed to the L1 loss smoothing out high-frequency components and insufficient training data in GRAB for these actions.
 - **Dynamic Grasps:** The current Object Optimizer Module assumes constant inter-vertex distances, limiting its ability to handle dynamic grasping scenarios with hand slipping or relative object motion (e.g., "rotating" a cube).
 - **GRAB Dataset Precision:** Acknowledges that the ground-truth contacts in the GRAB dataset are not always perfectly precise, sometimes showing inter-penetrations or lack of contact.
 - **Long-Term Motion:** The framework currently does not address synthesizing long-term motion sequences (minutes-long) involving a complex series of actions with an object.

These findings solidify IMoS as a substantial step forward in generating realistic and controllable human-object interactions in virtual environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IMoS framework represents a significant leap forward in the field of 3D human motion synthesis, particularly at the intersection of character animation and human-object interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be seen across several domains:

- **Novelty and Advancement of State-of-the-Art:** IMoS fills a crucial gap in existing research by being the *first* to synthesize intent-driven, *full-body* human motion involving *object manipulation after the grasp*, including 6-DoF object pose optimization. This goes beyond prior work that either focused only on hands, full-body grasping *until* contact, or full-body motion without specific object interaction. This holistic approach sets a new benchmark for realism and control in virtual character animation.

- **Enabling Realistic Virtual Characters:** By allowing virtual characters to interact plausibly and diversely with objects based on semantic instructions, IMoS dramatically enhances the realism and interactivity of virtual agents. This is critical for creating more immersive and believable experiences in:
 - **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Animators can generate complex, subtle, and diverse character-object interactions with greatly reduced manual effort, accelerating content creation for films, commercials, and digital media.
 - **Video Games and Metaverse:** Game developers can create more dynamic and believable NPCs (Non-Player Characters) and avatar interactions, leading to richer gameplay and more engaging virtual worlds.
 - **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** Enables more intuitive and realistic interactions with virtual objects, improving user immersion and experience.

- **Impact on Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to generate human-like interaction trajectories from intent can be invaluable for robotics research. It can:
 - **Inform Robot Learning:** Provide synthetic data or guidance for training robots to perform human-like manipulations.
 - **Improve Human-Robot Collaboration:** Help robots anticipate human intentions and actions during collaborative tasks, leading to more fluid and effective partnerships.
 - **Enhance Robot Teleoperation:** Offer more natural ways to control robotic avatars or telepresence systems.

- **Advancements in AI and Machine Learning:**
 - The successful integration of CLIP embeddings for semantic understanding of intent showcases the power of large pre-trained language models in driving physical actions.
 - The decoupled variational autoregressive architecture is a sophisticated approach to managing the complexity and diversity of full-body movements.
 - The learned implicit coordination of body parts (e.g., head motions without explicit supervision) points to robust, data-driven representations of human behavior.

- **Future Research Directions:** IMoS opens up several exciting avenues for future work, including:
 - **Dynamic Grasps:** Extending the framework to handle more complex scenarios where objects slip, rotate, or deform in the hand.
 - **Long-Term Sequential Actions:** Developing methods to synthesize longer sequences of interactions, where characters perform multiple, interconnected actions with objects.
 - **Finer-Grained Control:** Exploring more descriptive sentence embeddings (e.g., "a person passes the bowl using the right hand") to allow for even greater control over motion generation.
 - **Multi-Object Interactions:** Extending to scenarios where characters interact with multiple objects simultaneously or sequentially.

In conclusion, IMoS is a foundational contribution that significantly pushes the boundaries of virtual character animation. By providing a robust, instruction-driven system for generating full-body human-object interactions, it offers a powerful tool for creators and researchers alike, paving the way for more intelligent, realistic, and interactive virtual environments.
