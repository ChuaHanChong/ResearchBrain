# Learning Loco-Manipulation From SMPC Demonstrations With Sparse Offline-to-Online RL

> [!summary] Summary
> The method uses sample-based model predictive control (SMPC), tuned interactively in simulation, as an automated expert generating millions of loco-manipulation demonstrations. These bootstrap an off-policy TD3 agent trained purely on sparse task rewards, layered above a frozen whole-body stability controller. Learned policies converge without any reward shaping, finish tasks faster than the SMPC teacher, and transfer to an arm-equipped Spot quadruped and a G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Scaling reinforcement learning to loco-manipulation demands dense reward shaping, where each reward tweak requires a full lengthy training cycle, making iterative tuning prohibitively slow.
- Optimal control tunes quickly but its computational cost blocks direct deployment on high-degree-of-freedom hardware, and formulations stay limited to prespecified contacts or narrow tasks.
- Demonstration-based alternatives rely on teleoperation or motion retargeting, which scale poorly to non-anthropomorphic platforms like arm-equipped quadrupeds and cover only tabletop manipulation.

## Method

- A strictly ==sparse reward== (0 at goal, -1 otherwise, -2/(1-gamma) on crash) trains a modified ==FastTD3== off-policy agent, with expert transitions filling 50% of the replay buffer.
- ==Tiled GPU-parallel SMPC data collection== broadcasts each tile's task state across sampled spline action rollouts, keeps elite trajectories, and yields one million expert samples per hour.
- A ==hierarchical stack== pairs the high-level delta-action policy with a frozen ==ReLIC whole-body stabilization controller==, plus ==tanh-bounded critics== clipped to the reward's known return limits.

## Results

- Policies reach near-**100%** simulation success across five tasks on both robots; without SMPC data, sparse-reward learning fails to learn anything at all.
- Learned policies beat the SMPC teacher on task-completion time, with some tasks improving over **50%** and standard deviation of duration dropping **11-45%**.
- The hardest task, tire rolling, needs **4M** samples (**4** GPU hours) and collapses below a **50%** expert ratio; multimodal expert data causes complete training failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A cheaply tunable model-based controller can replace reward engineering entirely: it supplies the exploration signal, letting sparse rewards define the true unbiased objective.
> - Expert data must be phased out early (10% success threshold); retaining it past that point stalls fine-grained improvement as the policy distribution shifts away.
> - Demonstration unimodality matters more than demonstration success rate, since off-policy actors output unimodal distributions that cannot absorb a planner's multiple solution modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of locomotion and manipulation capabilities is identified as a crucial area for developing autonomous robots capable of operating in complex, unstructured environments. Existing research in robotic control often bifurcates into two primary paradigms: Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Model Predictive Control (MPC).

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** offers advantages in learning complex, fast-executing, and robust behaviors, especially when combined with domain randomization. However, scaling pure RL to complex loco-manipulation tasks is frequently hindered by the challenges of reward shaping. The manual, iterative process of designing dense reward functions to guide exploration is time-consuming and resource-intensive, often requiring extensive training cycles to evaluate minor adjustments. Current RL developments in loco-manipulation largely focus on imitation learning or trajectory tracking, where the RL agent learns to follow predefined paths or demonstrations, rather than discovering behaviors purely from sparse task objectives. This limits their generality to the quality and availability of the provided demonstrations.

**Model Predictive Control (MPC)**, including its sample-based variants (SMPC), provides a systematic way to achieve loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., pushing, grasping) by leveraging known system dynamics. MPC strategies are generally easier to tune in an interactive setting due to their direct relation to the system model. However, their computational cost, particularly for high-degree-of-freedom systems and complex, contact-rich interactions, typically prevents direct real-time deployment on hardware. They also require accurate model knowledge and are often limited to pre-specified contacts or narrowly defined task formulations.

This research aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It proposes a method that leverages the rapid tuning capabilities of optimal control (SMPC) in simulation to generate high-quality, diverse expert data, which then bootstraps an RL agent. This offline-to-online RL approach seeks to overcome the reward-shaping bottleneck of traditional RL while enabling the deployment of policies that are robust, execute quickly, and can potentially surpass the performance of the expert demonstrations by optimizing directly for sparse task rewards. The work distinguishes itself from other demonstration-based RL methods that typically rely on human demonstrations or algorithmic planners for stationary, end-effector, or tabletop manipulation, by focusing on dynamic, whole-body loco-manipulation for diverse robot morphologies using a unified SMPC framework. The paper builds on advancements in offline-to-online RL and efficient off-policy architectures like FastTD3/FastSAC, extending them to complex loco-manipulation tasks with sparse rewards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for training robust loco-manipulation policies using exclusively sparse task rewards, thereby eliminating the need for extensive and time-consuming manual reward tuning. This objective is motivated by several critical limitations in current robotics research:

1. **Bottleneck of Dense Reward Shaping:** Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for complex tasks often rely on carefully engineered dense reward functions to guide the agent's exploration. This manual process is slow, requiring significant effort to design, test, and iterate on reward signals, with each evaluation demanding lengthy training cycles. The authors seek to bypass this "reward-tuning bottleneck" to accelerate skill acquisition and enable broader application of RL in robotics.

2. **Exploration Challenges in High-Dimensional Spaces:** Training RL agents with sparse rewards from scratch in high-dimensional continuous action spaces, characteristic of whole-body loco-manipulation (combining legs and arms), presents a severe exploration challenge. Without dense feedback, agents struggle to discover successful task completion strategies. The paper aims to resolve this fundamental exploration problem.

3. **Limitations of Direct Optimal Control Deployment:** While optimal control strategies like Sample-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) are tunable and capable of generating complex behaviors in simulation, their high computational cost typically prohibits direct deployment on real hardware for high-degree-of-freedom systems in real-time. The motivation is to retain the tuning benefits of SMPC while achieving the fast execution speeds characteristic of learned neural network policies.

4. **Scalability of Data Collection:** Many demonstration-based RL methods rely on human teleoperation or motion retargeting for data generation. These methods often scale poorly to non-anthropomorphic robots (e.g., arm-equipped quadrupeds) or to a wide variety of complex loco-manipulation tasks, introducing a restrictive limitation. The authors are motivated to find an automated, highly scalable method for generating expert demonstrations.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the following key objectives and contributions:

- **Tuning-Free RL Training:** Introduce a framework that leverages SMPC as an automated, easily tunable data generator in simulation. This allows for the use of sparse-reward off-policy RL, completely circumventing the manual reward-tuning bottlenecks of standard RL pipelines.
- **Optimal Loco-Manipulation Policies:** Empirically demonstrate that bootstrapping sparse off-policy RL with SMPC datasets yields hardware-deployable policies. A specific objective is to show that these policies can ultimately surpass the performance of the original optimal control teacher by strictly optimizing the true task objective.
- **Validation Across Real-World Platforms:** Validate the robustness and generality of the approach across diverse robot morphologies. This includes deploying learned skills on real hardware for both an arm-equipped Spot quadruped and a G1 humanoid.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to combine the strengths of rapid tuning from optimal control with the fast execution and robustness of RL policies trained with domain randomization, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in autonomous loco-manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on an offline-to-online Reinforcement Learning framework that utilizes Sample-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) to generate expert demonstrations, thereby enabling the training of RL agents with sparse rewards.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Control Architecture

A hierarchical control architecture is employed, decoupling high-level task objectives from low-level stabilization. This structure is consistent across both data collection and deployment, allowing for interchangeable use of the SMPC expert or the RL agent as the high-level policy.

- **High-Level Task Policy:** This policy is responsible for overall manipulation and navigation. Its action space consists of deltas to current desired values: planar base velocity (∆vcmd ∈ R3), arm joint positions (∆qarmcmd ∈ Rna for an na-degree-of-freedom arm), torso height (∆hcmd ∈ R), and torso pitch (∆pcmd ∈ R). For the Spot quadruped, torso height and pitch commands are fixed.
- **Low-Level Stabilization Controller:** Kinematic commands from the high-level policy are passed to a whole-body maneuvering controller. This controller, trained via the ReLIC framework, is frozen after its initial training. It strictly tracks the commanded arm motion and base velocity while dynamically adjusting leg joints (qlegs) to maintain dynamic stability. This allows the high-level policy to focus solely on task completion.

#### 3.2. Training Off-Policy RL with Sparse Rewards

The high-level loco-manipulation task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and trained in simulation using MuJoCo Warp and mjlab.

- **Sparse Reward Function:** A strictly sparse reward function is defined to eliminate manual reward engineering:
 - 0 at goal achievement.
 - -2/(1-γ) if the robot crashes (torso height or tilt exceeds predefined limits), leading to episode termination.
 - -1 otherwise.
 This structure ensures agents prefer constant negative rewards over crashing and incentivizes goal completion.
- **Offline-to-Online Training Strategy:** To address the exploration challenge of sparse rewards in high-dimensional action spaces, an offline-to-online strategy is used, employing a modified FastTD3 architecture.
 - **Expert Data Injection:** During initial training phases, 50% of the transitions in the replay buffer are replaced with pre-collected expert data (Section 4.3). This stabilizes critic networks and facilitates meaningful policy gradient estimates before the agent randomly discovers successful trajectories.
 - **Curriculum for Expert Data Phase-Out:** A curriculum phases out the expert data once the agent achieves a sufficient empirical success rate (e.g., 10% in experiments), transitioning to pure online learning. This prevents over-reliance on demonstrations and allows the policy to optimize further.
- **Policy Parameterization:** The high-level policy outputs action deltas (e.g., ∆vcmd) rather than absolute targets. This parameterization allows for explicit enforcement of maximum desired accelerations and speeds within the action space, contributing to safe and deployable behaviors.
- **Bounded Critic Networks:** The outputs of the critic networks are structurally bounded to the theoretical maximum (0) and minimum (-2/(1-γ)) possible returns, which are known due to the sparse reward formulation. This bounding stabilizes training, especially when hyperparameters are suboptimal, by mitigating unbounded overestimation in Q-functions.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To achieve reliable transfer to hardware, domain randomization is applied to object mass, size, and friction. An asymmetric actor-critic setup is utilized, where the actor receives noisy observations, while the critic receives exact observations from the simulation.

#### 3.3. Data Collection with Sampling-Based Model Predictive Control (SMPC)

SMPC is used as an automated expert to generate large-scale demonstration datasets in simulation, bypassing the need for manual teleoperation.

- **SMPC Formulation:** A streamlined SMPC formulation, similar to Howell et al. (2022), is used, incorporating random sampling and a warm-start strategy.
- **Rapid Tuning:** The SMPC is guided by easily formulated dense cost functions, which can be interactively tuned in minutes (achieving ~0.5x real-time performance on an RTX 5090 GPU). This allows for rapid generation of expert data without requiring policy training.
- **Massively Parallelized Data Collection:** Data collection is scaled using a massively parallelized tiled approach in vectorized MuJoCo Warp environments (Figure 3). This architecture enables generating approximately one million samples per hour, allowing the required offline data for bootstrapping the RL agent to be collected within four hours on a single GPU.
- **Action Sampling:** The SMPC samples actions as splines over a planning horizon, rather than independent step-wise commands, to generate smooth behaviors. After the first iteration, future actions are sampled around a shifted last best action trajectory. Only the first Ne actions are executed before resampling, resulting in a 5 Hz policy with 50 Hz action chunks.
- **Reward Discounting:** Future rewards in sampled trajectories are discounted to bias the objective towards trajectories that achieve success sooner.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Simulation states are cached, and all simulations are periodically reset to the state of the most successful sample after a full rollout, saving computational resources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the proposed framework for learning loco-manipulation skills.

#### 4.1. Real-World Deployment on Diverse Platforms

The framework's viability was demonstrated through the successful deployment of learned policies on real hardware. Five diverse tasks requiring complex, whole-body coordination were designed: Spot reach, Spot box pushing, Spot tire uprighting, Spot tire rolling, and G1 humanoid box pushing.
- **Success Rates:** In simulation, policies consistently achieved near-perfect success rates, exhibiting low variance across different random seeds. The number of samples required for satisfactory performance increased with task complexity.
- **Hardware Transfer:** Policies trained exclusively on sparse rewards were reliably deployed on physical hardware for all five tasks and both robot platforms (arm-equipped Spot quadruped and Unitree G1 humanoid). This indicates the robustness and generalization of the hierarchical architecture and offline-to-online training pipeline to real-world dynamics without platform-specific reward engineering.

#### 4.2. Q1: Do agents trained on sparse rewards improve over seen data?

The performance of the sparse-reward trained policies was compared against the SMPC expert used for data generation, focusing on average task-completion time.
- **Performance Superiority:** The trained policies consistently outperformed the SMPC expert in terms of task completion time across all tasks (Figure 5). Some tasks showed improvements exceeding 50%.
- **Consistency:** Learned policies also demonstrated increased consistency, with a reduction in the standard deviation of task duration ranging from 11% to 45%. This improved consistency was attributed to the RL agent optimizing specifically for the task objective without biases from surrogate rewards, while SMPC’s variance was linked to suboptimal trajectories arising from its limited parallel exploration.

#### 4.3. Q2: How much data is required for task learning?

An ablation study investigated the impact of the volume of expert data on training performance across different tasks.
- **Correlation with Task Complexity:** The required dataset size correlated directly with task complexity (Figure 6). Easier tasks could be solved with relatively small datasets (e.g., hundreds of thousands of samples).
- **Demand for Complex Tasks:** More complex whole-body manipulation tasks, such as tire rolling, required significantly larger datasets (e.g., four million samples collected in four GPU hours) to converge successfully. This suggests that complex tasks with wider state distributions necessitate greater dataset coverage for accurate policy gradient estimation by the critic.

#### 4.4. Q3: How important is the quality of the data?

The influence of SMPC trajectory quality, varied by reducing the number of sampling environments during data generation, was analyzed. Lower sampling environments lead to less exploration and potentially lower-quality expert datasets.
- **Robustness:** Most tasks exhibited relative robustness to lower-quality data (Figure 7).
- **Sensitivity for Coordination Tasks:** However, tasks requiring high coordination, such as tire rolling, showed a noticeable drop in success rates with reduced data quality. This indicates that the required data quality is directly correlated with the task’s precision requirements.

#### 4.5. Q4: How does multimodality in the data affect training?

The impact of multimodal SMPC data, where the expert demonstrates various ways to achieve a task, was investigated.
- **Negative Impact of Multimodality:** Policies trained on multimodal datasets failed to learn viable strategies (Figure 8). The SMPC's inherent multimodality, not being observation-dependent, created a structural mismatch with RL policies that rely on unimodal action distributions.
- **Necessity of Unimodality:** Enforcing a single behavioral mode in the demonstration data (e.g., through stricter SMPC costs that can be tuned interactively) was found to be necessary for successful offline initialization and subsequent learning by the RL agent.

#### 4.6. Extended Ablations (from Appendix)

- **Percentage of Expert Data:** The choice of expert data ratio (before phase-out) had little effect on simpler tasks. However, the tire-rolling task was sensitive, benefiting significantly from a higher initial expert data ratio (e.g., 50%) and failing to converge below 50%. Higher initial ratios were posited to improve value estimation around successful states.
- **Bounded Critic Networks:** Bounding the critic’s Q-value predictions to theoretical limits (min/max possible returns) stabilized training and increased the range of hyperparameters that yielded successful runs, preventing sharp performance drops observed with unbounded critics (Figure 10).
- **Phase-Out Threshold:** Keeping SMPC data in the replay buffer for too long negatively impacted training performance (Figure 11). While essential for initial bootstrapping, prolonging its presence beyond a certain success rate (e.g., 10%) slowed down or even prevented convergence for more complex tasks. This suggests that the SMPC data becomes detrimental once the policy has narrowed its strategy and the data distribution shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a significant bottleneck in the development of complex robotic behaviors, particularly for loco-manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Elimination of Manual Reward Tuning:** By demonstrating a viable approach to learn complex loco-manipulation skills using strictly sparse rewards, the work bypasses the labor-intensive and time-consuming process of manual reward shaping in RL. This can significantly accelerate the development and deployment cycle for new robotic capabilities.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The methodology positions Sample-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) as a powerful, scalable, and automated data generator for the RL community. SMPC's interactive tunability in near real-time and its ability to be massively parallelized to produce millions of expert samples efficiently, offers an alternative to restrictive data collection methods like human teleoperation or motion capture, especially for non-anthropomorphic robots.
- **Enhanced Policy Performance:** The finding that sparse-reward RL policies can quantitatively surpass the performance of their expert SMPC teachers is notable. By optimizing directly for the true task objective without the biases introduced by dense surrogate rewards, the learned policies achieve faster task completion and increased consistency, indicating a path towards more optimal and robust robotic behaviors.
- **Broad Applicability and Robustness:** Validation across diverse robot morphologies (quadruped and humanoid) and a range of challenging whole-body tasks demonstrates the robustness and generality of the proposed hierarchical architecture and offline-to-online training pipeline. This suggests a broad potential for application in various robotic domains requiring sophisticated physical interaction.
- **Algorithmic Insights for Offline RL:** The ablation studies provide valuable algorithmic insights into effectively leveraging expert demonstrations in an offline-to-online RL context. Specifically, the findings on the correlation between task complexity, required dataset size and quality, the benefits of bounded critics, and the critical importance of filtering multimodal behaviors in demonstration data, offer guidance for future research and practical implementations in offline RL.
- **Advancement in Autonomous Robotics:** By enabling the acquisition of complex and agile loco-manipulation skills for deployment on real hardware, this work contributes to the broader goal of deploying autonomous robots in unstructured environments where adaptability, dynamic stability, and precise interaction with objects are paramount. It offers a stepping stone towards robots that can learn to perform tasks without continuous human intervention for reward design.
- **Limitations and Future Directions:** While significant, the work acknowledges limitations such as the local optimality of learned behaviors (tied to the dataset distribution), sensitivity to hyperparameters, and the reliance on a frozen low-level controller (limiting adaptation). Future work could explore unfreezing low-level controllers for further adaptation or integrating vision-based state information for deployment in more unstructured, perception-driven environments.
