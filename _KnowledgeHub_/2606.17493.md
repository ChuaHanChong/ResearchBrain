# When Robots Sleep: Offline Skill Consolidation for Shared-Policy Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Sleeping Robots" framework introduces an offline skill consolidation approach for shared-policy robot learning, addressing the issue of "skill-coupling collapse" where inter-skill reliability degrades during sequential learning. It uses compact frozen memories to preserve knowledge without full trajectory replay, achieving over 60% relative improvement in Average Final Success Rate and a 2.0 times higher Pairwise Reliability Score on Meta-World MT5 compared to strong baselines.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to continually learn new skills sequentially without catastrophically forgetting previously acquired knowledge.
- A failure mode termed "skill-coupling collapse" occurs where shared policies lose the coordinated structure among related skills, even if individual skill success is maintained.
- The need for a single, shared policy in long-term robot deployment without access to previous task environments, rewards, demonstrations, or full trajectories.

## Method

- A "==wake-sleep==" framework, **Sleeping Robots**, separates online skill acquisition (wake) from offline knowledge consolidation (sleep) without environmental interaction.
- Compact frozen skill memories (critic memories for RL, actor memories for IL) are created at the end of the wake phase, providing differentiable surrogate objectives for past skills.
- Nash-bargained gradient coordination is applied during sleep to combine gradients from all frozen memories, promoting policy updates that maintain agreement across the entire skill repertoire.

## Results

- On Meta-World MT5, Sleeping Robots achieved an Average Final Success Rate (AFSR) of **0.578**, a >**60%** relative improvement, and a Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS) of **0.245**, approximately **2.0** times higher than the best non-oracle baseline.
- For vision-conditioned imitation learning on SurgicAI, the framework yielded the highest AFSR (**0.410**) and best Backward Transfer (BWT) (-**0.338**) among continual methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the offline sleep phase is crucial, more than doubling AFSR and substantially improving BWT compared to a wake-only approach, and validating the contributions of Nash negotiation, adaptive anchoring, and local excitability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Skill-coupling collapse" is a critical failure mode in continual robot learning, specifically affecting the shared policy's ability to reliably coordinate related skills.
> - Offline consolidation, leveraging compact frozen memories and multi-objective gradient coordination, can effectively mitigate forgetting and shared-policy degradation without requiring replay of full trajectories or access to original task data.
> - Adaptive sleep anchoring and local excitability mechanisms further stabilize the shared policy during consolidation, balancing the preservation of old knowledge with the integration of new skills.
