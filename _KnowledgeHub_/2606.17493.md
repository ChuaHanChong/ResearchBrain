# When Robots Sleep: Offline Skill Consolidation for Shared-Policy Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Sleeping Robots" framework introduces an offline skill consolidation approach for shared-policy robot learning, addressing the issue of "skill-coupling collapse" where inter-skill reliability degrades during sequential learning. It uses compact frozen memories to preserve knowledge without full trajectory replay, achieving over 60% relative improvement in Average Final Success Rate and a 2.0 times higher Pairwise Reliability Score on Meta-World MT5 compared to strong baselines.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to continually learn new skills sequentially without catastrophically forgetting previously acquired knowledge.
- A failure mode termed "skill-coupling collapse" occurs where shared policies lose the coordinated structure among related skills, even if individual skill success is maintained.
- The need for a single, shared policy in long-term robot deployment without access to previous task environments, rewards, demonstrations, or full trajectories.

## Method

- A "==wake-sleep==" framework, **Sleeping Robots**, separates online skill acquisition (wake) from offline knowledge consolidation (sleep) without environmental interaction.
- Compact frozen skill memories (critic memories for RL, actor memories for IL) are created at the end of the wake phase, providing differentiable surrogate objectives for past skills.
- Nash-bargained gradient coordination is applied during sleep to combine gradients from all frozen memories, promoting policy updates that maintain agreement across the entire skill repertoire.

## Results

- On Meta-World MT5, Sleeping Robots achieved an Average Final Success Rate (AFSR) of **0.578**, a >**60%** relative improvement, and a Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS) of **0.245**, approximately **2.0** times higher than the best non-oracle baseline.
- For vision-conditioned imitation learning on SurgicAI, the framework yielded the highest AFSR (**0.410**) and best Backward Transfer (BWT) (-**0.338**) among continual methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the offline sleep phase is crucial, more than doubling AFSR and substantially improving BWT compared to a wake-only approach, and validating the contributions of Nash negotiation, adaptive anchoring, and local excitability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - "Skill-coupling collapse" is a critical failure mode in continual robot learning, specifically affecting the shared policy's ability to reliably coordinate related skills.
> - Offline consolidation, leveraging compact frozen memories and multi-objective gradient coordination, can effectively mitigate forgetting and shared-policy degradation without requiring replay of full trajectories or access to original task data.
> - Adaptive sleep anchoring and local excitability mechanisms further stabilize the shared policy during consolidation, balancing the preservation of old knowledge with the integration of new skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses the challenge of continual learning in robotics, specifically focusing on the long-term deployment of robots that must acquire new skills sequentially while maintaining a single, coherent policy. The broader field of continual learning aims to enable models to learn new tasks without catastrophically forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Within robotics, this translates to robots that can expand their repertoire over time, adapting to new users, environments, or tasks.

Existing continual learning methods typically fall into several categories:
- **Regularization and Gradient Projection:** These methods constrain updates to protect parameters important for prior tasks (e.g., EWC, SI, MAS, GEM, A-GEM).
- **Replay Methods:** These approaches revisit stored experience, examples, or transitions from previous tasks (e.g., DER, CLEAR).
- **Distillation Methods:** These methods aim to preserve the outputs or knowledge from earlier models when learning new tasks (e.g., LwF, Progress & Compress, Reset & Distill).
- **Modular Methods:** These approaches use task-specific components like heads, routing mechanisms, adapters, or expert networks to handle different skills, shifting coordination to module selection (e.g., PackNet, HAT, LOTUS, LEGION, AtomicVLA, CompoNet).
- **Multi-Task Gradient Methods:** These methods coordinate gradients from multiple tasks when all task objectives are simultaneously available (e.g., PCGrad, Nash-MTL).

This research differentiates itself by focusing on a specific failure mode termed "skill-coupling collapse," where a shared policy maintains non-trivial individual skill success but loses the coordinated structure necessary for reliable interaction among related skills. Many existing methods primarily evaluate per-task retention and may not adequately address this shared-policy reliability. Furthermore, this work operates under realistic constraints where previous environments, rewards, demonstrations, or trajectories may no longer be accessible, and the deployed policy must remain a single, shared controller without task-specific components or replay of full trajectories. It adapts multi-task gradient coordination to a sequential setting where only compact frozen memories are available, separating skill acquisition (wake) from offline consolidation (sleep).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. Identify and address "skill-coupling collapse," a failure mode in sequential robot skill learning where the shared-policy compatibility among related skills deteriorates, even if individual skill success remains.
2. Develop a framework that enables robots to learn new skills sequentially without access to previous task data (e.g., environments, rewards, demonstrations, or trajectories).
3. Ensure that the robot's deployed policy remains a single, shared controller, avoiding the use of task-specific heads, routing mechanisms, or adapters that might complicate coordination or deployment.
4. Propose an offline consolidation mechanism that preserves the shared structure and reliability of a growing skill repertoire.
5. Evaluate the framework's effectiveness not only in terms of individual skill retention (average success, backward transfer) but also in terms of the shared policy's ability to reliably coordinate related skills.

The motivation for this work stems from the transition of robots from fixed-task deployment to long-lived, adaptive operation in diverse settings such as households, assistance, or industrial manipulation. In these scenarios, robots need to continually expand their capabilities without being re-engineered or losing previously acquired knowledge. Current continual learning approaches often focus on retaining individual task performance, which the authors argue is insufficient for complex robotic systems that rely on the interplay and coordination of multiple skills. The absence of previous task data, a common real-world constraint, further motivates the development of methods that can consolidate knowledge efficiently using compact representations. The "skill-coupling collapse" concept highlights a gap in current evaluation methodologies, emphasizing the need for metrics beyond average task success to assess the true utility of a robot's learned repertoire.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed "Sleeping Robots," is a wake-sleep framework designed for offline consolidation in continual robot skill learning with a single shared policy. It separates the process into online skill acquisition ("wake") and offline knowledge consolidation ("sleep").

**Wake Phase and Frozen Skill Memories:**
During the "wake" phase, the robot learns a new skill `T_k` using current-skill data. The learning substrate depends on the domain:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** For state-based manipulation tasks (e.g., Meta-World MT5), standard RL updates (e.g., SAC) are performed using only current-skill data. No stored memories are consulted, and no previous trajectories are replayed.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** For vision-conditioned control tasks (e.g., SurgicAI), wake uses current-skill behavior cloning. If previous actor memories are available, output-space distillation on their stored observation buffers may also be applied during wake.

At the end of the wake phase, the learned skill is stored as a compact **frozen memory (M_k)**. These memories are designed to provide differentiable sleep objectives without storing full trajectories:
- **For RL actors:** A frozen skill critic `M_Q_k = (Q_ϕ_k, B_k, ν_k)` is stored. `Q_ϕ_k` is the frozen value critic, `B_k` is an unordered buffer of states, and `ν_k` is the critic normalizer snapshot. This buffer contains no actions, rewards, or transitions.
- **For IL actors:** A frozen actor snapshot `M_π_k = (π_θ_wake_k, B_k)` is stored. `π_θ_wake_k` is the wake-end policy, and `B_k` contains unordered observations (e.g., robot states, images).

**Sleep Objectives from Frozen Memories:**
The "sleep" phase commences when `k ≥ 2`. This phase involves no environment interaction and no memory updates. Each frozen memory `M_i` from the set of all learned skills `S_k = {M_i}i=1^k` defines a differentiable surrogate objective `L_i(θ)` for the current shared policy `π_θ`.
- **For RL memories:** The objective is `L_Q_i(θ) = −E_{s∼B_i}[Q_ϕ_i(ν_i(s), π_θ(s))]`. This evaluates the current policy's actions through the frozen critic on stored states.
- **For IL memories:** The objective is `L_π_i(θ) = E_{o∼B_i}[∥π_θ(o) − π_θ_wake_i(o)∥^2_2]`. This uses frozen-actor distillation to minimize the difference between the current policy's outputs and those of the frozen expert actor on stored observations.
The gradient `g_i = ∇_θ L_i(θ)` is then derived for each skill.

**Nash-Bargained Sleep Consolidation:**
To combine gradients from multiple frozen memories without a single memory dominating, the method employs a Nash-bargained gradient coordination strategy, adapted for the sequential setting. Each memory gradient `g_i` is normalized as `ˆg_i = g_i / (∥g_i∥_2 + ϵ)`. Convex weights `α*` are chosen by maximizing a log-product objective: `α* = arg max_α∈Δ_k-1 Σ_{i=1}^k log(⟨ˆg_i, Σ_{j=1}^k α_j ˆg_j⟩ + ϵ_Nash)`. This objective promotes directions that maintain agreement with all stored skill memories. The policy is then updated in the resulting sleep direction `g* = Σ_{i=1}^k α*_i ˆg_i` using an Adam optimizer. A fixed sleep budget, gradient normalization, numerical flooring, and early stopping are used to control updates.

**Adaptive Sleep Anchoring:**
To prevent overly aggressive policy shifts, an adaptive sleep anchoring mechanism is used. If the current policy `θ` is too close to any previous wake-end snapshot `θ_wake_i` (i.e., `min_{i<k} ∥θ − θ_wake_i∥^2_2 < τ`), a conservative pull (`λ_k`) is added toward the previous consolidated checkpoint `θ_sleep_k-1`. This anchoring term is incorporated into the sleep objective.

**Local Excitability for Feedforward RL Actors:**
For feedforward RL actors, a local excitability variable `e_i` is introduced for each hidden neuron (`y_i = e_i σ(w^⊤_i x)`). Unlike learnable gains, `e_i` is updated only through local activity statistics to regulate activation levels towards a target `α_target`, fostering homeostatic stability. This mechanism is not applied to transformer-based imitation policies.

At inference, Sleeping Robots utilizes a single shared policy `π_θ` without any task-specific routing, adapters, or separate policy heads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Sleeping Robots was evaluated on two distinct five-skill streams: Meta-World MT5 (state-based RL) and SurgicAI (vision-conditioned IL). The evaluation metrics included Average Final Success Rate (AFSR), Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS), Backward Transfer (BWT), and Synergy Preservation Index (SPI).

**Meta-World MT5 Results (SAC policies, critic memories):**
- **Average Final Success Rate (AFSR):** Sleeping Robots achieved an AFSR of 0.578 ± 0.016, a significant improvement of over 60% relative to the strongest non-oracle baseline, Reset & Distill (0.353 ± 0.018).
- **Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS):** This metric specifically targets "skill-coupling collapse." Sleeping Robots obtained a PRS of 0.245 ± 0.020, which is approximately 2.0 times higher than the best baseline PRS (Reset & Distill at 0.123 ± 0.013). This indicates improved shared-repertoire reliability among related skills.
- **Synergy Preservation Index (SPI):** Sleeping Robots achieved the highest SPI of 0.767 ± 0.017, outperforming the strongest baseline (0.504 ± 0.060). This suggests better preservation of the reference-skill trajectory subspace.
- **Backward Transfer (BWT):** While PCGrad showed the highest BWT, it exhibited much lower AFSR, PRS, and SPI, indicating that BWT alone might not fully capture shared-policy reliability. Sleeping Robots achieved a BWT of -0.280 ± 0.020.
- **Skill-Coupling Collapse Observation:** The results across baselines often showed that AFSR decreased as new skills arrived, while PRS dropped more sharply, supporting the concept of skill-coupling collapse where isolated skill competence is retained, but inter-skill reliability diminishes.

**SurgicAI Results (ACT policies, actor memories):**
- **Average Final Success Rate (AFSR):** Sleeping Robots achieved the highest AFSR among continual methods at 0.410 ± 0.010.
- **Backward Transfer (BWT):** It also obtained the best BWT at -0.338 ± 0.024.
- **Comparison to SR Wake-Only:** The ablation showed that offline sleep more than doubled AFSR and substantially improved BWT compared to SR Wake-Only, confirming the contribution of the consolidation phase beyond wake-time distillation.
- **Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS):** While Sequential BC had the highest non-oracle PRS (0.129 ± 0.014), Sleeping Robots was competitive at 0.117 ± 0.012.
- **Synergy Preservation Index (SPI):** M2Distill had the highest non-oracle SPI, but Sleeping Robots presented a stronger balance across AFSR and BWT.
Overall, Sleeping Robots demonstrated a robust balance between retention and reliability on SurgicAI, without task-specific heads, routing, or trajectory replay.

**Ablation Studies (MT5):**
- **Wake-only:** Performed the worst, with low AFSR, zero PRS, and highly negative BWT, highlighting the necessity of a consolidation mechanism.
- **Mean-gradient sleep:** Showed improvement over wake-only but was inferior to full Sleeping Robots, supporting the use of scale-normalized Nash negotiation.
- **Removal of anchor:** Resulted in lower AFSR, PRS, and SPI.
- **Removal of excitability:** Led to the highest PRS but reduced AFSR, BWT, and especially SPI. Disabling sleep-time excitability improved BWT but significantly reduced AFSR and PRS.
- **Full Sleeping Robots:** Provided the strongest overall balance, achieving the best AFSR and SPI, with second-best PRS and BWT.

**Robustness Checks:**
Evaluation on a reverse MT5 skill order and an extended MT8 stream demonstrated that Sleeping Robots maintained non-trivial final success, pairwise reliability, and trajectory-structure preservation, suggesting that the observed trends are not limited to a specific skill order or stream length.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of continual robot learning by identifying "skill-coupling collapse" as a critical failure mode, distinct from catastrophic forgetting, that affects the reliability of shared policies in multi-skill robotic systems. By introducing the "Sleeping Robots" framework, the authors provide a novel approach for mitigating this issue through offline skill consolidation.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Addressing Shared-Policy Reliability:** The framework prioritizes the preservation of a coherent, shared policy structure, enabling robots to reliably coordinate multiple skills rather than merely retaining individual task capabilities. This is crucial for long-lived, adaptive robots operating in complex, dynamic environments where inter-skill coordination is essential.
- **Efficiency in Data-Scarce Settings:** By utilizing compact frozen skill memories (critic memories for RL, actor memories for IL) and avoiding replay of full trajectories or access to original environments, the method offers a practical solution for scenarios where previous task data is unavailable or expensive to store and retrieve.
- **Algorithmic Innovation:** The adaptation of Nash-bargained gradient coordination for sequential learning, combined with adaptive sleep anchoring and local excitability, provides a robust mechanism for balancing the preservation of old skills with the acquisition of new ones during offline consolidation.
- **Cross-Domain Applicability:** The successful application across both state-based reinforcement learning (Meta-World MT5) and vision-conditioned imitation learning (SurgicAI) suggests the generality of the consolidation principle across different robotic learning substrates.
- **Improved Evaluation Metrics:** The introduction and emphasis on the Pairwise Reliability Score (PRS) and Synergy Preservation Index (SPI) highlight the need for a more comprehensive evaluation of continual robot learning, moving beyond isolated task success to assess the functional integrity of a robot's learned repertoire.

The potential impact of "Sleeping Robots" includes enabling more robust and adaptable robotic systems capable of continuous skill expansion in real-world applications such as household assistance, medical surgery, and industrial manipulation. Robots equipped with this framework could learn new capabilities on demand, personalize to user needs, and maintain a high level of operational reliability over extended deployments, contributing to the development of truly autonomous and lifelong learning robots.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge several limitations, including the potential for frozen memories (especially critics) to extrapolate outside their reliable regions, leading to a recovery-conservatism trade-off. While mitigating strategies like state/observation buffers, gradient normalization, bounded updates, early stopping, and adaptive anchoring are employed, future work could explore uncertainty-aware memories, conservative critic objectives, adaptive sleep schedules, on-policy buffer regeneration, and trajectory-level distillation. The evaluation is also primarily limited to specific skill streams and pair sets.
