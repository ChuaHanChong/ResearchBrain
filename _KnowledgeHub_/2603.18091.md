# Action Draft and Verify: A Self-Verifying Framework for Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Action-Draft-and-Verify (ADV) framework enhances Vision-Language-Action models by using a diffusion expert to draft multiple action candidates which an auto-regressive VLM then verifies and selects based on perplexity scores. This approach improved success rates by an average of +4.3% in simulation and +19.7% in real-world tasks, particularly in out-of-distribution environments, by filtering suboptimal actions.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while precise, exhibit reduced robustness and underfitting behaviors (e.g., fewer recovery attempts, more collisions) in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments.
- Auto-regressive VLA models demonstrate more stable corrective behavior under distribution shifts but generally achieve lower success rates and can be slower than diffusion-based methods.
- Existing hybrid VLA approaches often involve complex co-training or direct prediction fusion, which can be sensitive to disagreements between different model predictions.

## Method

- The Action-Draft-and-Verify (**ADV**) framework co-trains an auto-regressive VLM backbone and a lightweight ==diffusion== action expert using a combined loss function.
- A two-stage inference procedure: a 'Draft' phase where the ==diffusion== expert generates multiple candidate action chunks, and a 'Verify' phase where the VLM scores these candidates based on a perplexity-style metric and selects the best one.
- Introduced Textual FAST, a novel discrete action tokenization method that compresses continuous action chunks into text-aligned tokens, improving the VLM's reliability for perplexity-based scoring.

## Results

- ADV consistently improved success rates, averaging +4.3 percentage points in simulation (e.g., +3.5 on RoboTwin2.0 Hard OOD tasks) and +17.1 points in real-world settings compared to diffusion-based inference.
- In OOD environments (RoboTwin2.0 Hard), ADV significantly reduced average success steps by 99.9, increased recovery attempts by 1.6, and reduced pre-grasp environmental collisions by 26.5 percentage points.
- Textual FAST achieved a **96.8%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming other tokenization methods like FAST (**94.4%**) and VLA-0 (**94.9%**) for VLM verification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM verification primarily acts as a 'failure-mode filter,' effectively discarding suboptimal or erroneous action drafts proposed by the diffusion expert, rather than consistently identifying the single optimal trajectory.
> - The VLM's perplexity-style score is a reliable metric for filtering candidate actions, showing an inverse correlation with task success and demonstrating robustness to noisy inputs.
> - Representing action chunks as text-aligned tokens via Textual FAST enhances the stability and reliability of the VLM's perplexity-based verification by aligning with its pre-training distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on robotic control. Current VLA models generally fall into two categories: auto-regressive models and diffusion models.

Auto-regressive VLA models typically leverage the semantic understanding capabilities and strong generalization priors of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by predicting discrete action tokens sequentially. While robust, this token-by-token generation can be slower and less precise for low-level continuous control.

Diffusion-based VLA models, in contrast, utilize dedicated diffusion action experts to generate high-precision, temporally smooth continuous action chunks efficiently. These models, exemplified by architectures like π (Black et al., 2025b) and GR00T (Bjorck et al., 2025), have demonstrated performance in both simulated and real-world environments. However, a limitation of diffusion-based VLAs is their tendency to exhibit reduced robustness and underfitting behaviors, such as decreased recovery attempts and increased jitter collisions, when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. This is attributed to their potentially underutilized VLM priors for structured generalization, compared to auto-regressive models. Furthermore, the stochastic nature of diffusion inference can lead to less deterministic action execution.

Recent research has explored hybrid approaches to combine the strengths of both paradigms. Some methods involve co-training VLMs to output discrete action tokens alongside a diffusion expert for continuous actions (e.g., π
0.5, G0) during the training phase. Others fuse diffusion and auto-regressive predictions at inference time (e.g., HybridVLA). This paper positions itself as an alternative hybrid approach, specifically addressing the inference stage. Unlike fusion methods that average predictions, which can be sensitive to disagreement between predictors, this work proposes a "decoding-as-selection" paradigm where a diffusion expert proposes multiple candidates, and the VLM then verifies and selects the most suitable one. This approach draws parallels with reranking in large language model decoding and best-of-N selection strategies.

Additionally, the paper contributes to the area of discrete action tokenization. It builds upon methods like FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025), which compresses action sequences for efficiency, and VLA-0 (Goyal et al., 2025), which maps actions to text-like representations to better align with VLM pre-training distributions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation of this research is to develop a VLA framework that combines the high-precision continuous action generation of diffusion models with the robustness and generalization capabilities of auto-regressive VLMs, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments.

The authors identify specific limitations in existing VLA models:
- **Diffusion-based VLA models:** While effective for high-precision continuous control, they can struggle in OOD environments. Experiments conducted on RoboTwin2.0 tasks demonstrate that diffusion methods show significantly reduced recovery attempts (dropping from 4.5 to 0.4 in hard OOD tasks) and increased pre-grasp environmental collisions (from 0.0% to 29.7% in hard OOD tasks). This suggests an inability to effectively leverage VLM priors for structured generalization and a susceptibility to stochastic inference leading to suboptimal trajectories.
- **Auto-regressive VLA models:** These models show more stable corrective behavior under distribution shift, with higher recovery attempts and fewer pre-grasp collisions. However, they generally exhibit lower success rates compared to diffusion-based methods in both in-distribution and OOD settings, and can be slower.

To address these issues, the primary objective is to develop a lightweight inference framework that:
1. **Leverages the strengths of both paradigms:** It aims to achieve the high success rates and precision of diffusion models while integrating the stable, corrective behaviors and robustness of auto-regressive VLMs under distribution shifts.
2. **Mitigates failure modes in OOD environments:** Specifically, it seeks to improve the model's ability to recover from execution failures, reduce environmental disruptions caused by imprecise actions (like gripper jitter), and maintain task consistency when faced with unfamiliar conditions.
3. **Provides a practical solution:** The framework should introduce minimal overhead to maintain a high control rate (steps per second), ensuring smooth and responsive robot motions.
4. **Offers mechanistic insights:** The research aims to understand why combining these paradigms improves robustness, specifically investigating the role of VLM-based verification in filtering suboptimal actions.
5. **Demonstrates broad applicability:** The proposed framework should be applicable to various VLM backbones and pre-trained VLAs, showing consistent performance improvements across different simulation and real-world benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Action-Draft-and-Verify (ADV)**, integrates a diffusion action expert with an auto-regressive VLM backbone. It involves a co-training objective and a two-stage inference procedure: Draft and Verify, facilitated by a novel discrete action tokenization method called Textual FAST.

#### 3.1. Co-training Objective

The VLA system consists of a VLM backbone (θ) and a diffusion action expert (ϕ). The training objective combines both auto-regressive and diffusion losses:
- **Auto-regressive objective (L_VLM):** Minimizes the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of target action tokens (converted using Textual FAST) given the observation, instruction, and previous tokens. This trains the VLM to predict discrete action sequences.
- **Diffusion objective (L_dif):** Trains the diffusion action expert using a flow-matching loss. The VLM provides a final-layer hidden feature (f_c) that conditions the diffusion expert. This trains the diffusion expert to generate continuous action chunks.
- **Co-training loss (L_co-train):** L_co-train = L_VLM(θ) + β L_dif(θ, ϕ). A weighting factor β is used for the diffusion loss to synchronize optimization, as the auto-regressive component typically converges faster.

#### 3.2. Draft and Verify Inference Procedure

The ADV framework operates in two distinct stages at inference time:

1. **Draft Phase:**
 - The VLM extracts a hidden feature representation (f_c) from the current observation (o) and instruction (ℓ).
 - Conditioned on f_c, the diffusion action expert generates multiple candidate action chunks, S = {A_0..., A_M-1}. This is achieved by varying the injected noise during the diffusion sampling process, leading to a diverse set of plausible action trajectories.
 - The diffusion expert is designed to be lightweight, using a few denoising steps (e.g., 4 steps) and generating a moderate number of candidates (e.g., M=16). This design aims to keep the drafting overhead relatively small compared to a single VLM forward pass.

2. **Verify Phase:**
 - Each candidate action chunk (A_n) from the draft phase is converted into a sequence of discrete tokens (˜A_n) using Textual FAST (described below).
 - All tokenized candidates are then scored in parallel within a single batched VLM forward pass.
 - The VLM computes a length-normalized perplexity-style score (C_A_n) for each candidate. This score is calculated as the exponential of the negative average log-likelihood of the tokens in the sequence, conditioned on the observation, instruction, and preceding tokens within that chunk.
 - The action chunk with the lowest perplexity-style score is selected as the final output (A\* = arg min_A_n∈S C_A_n). This selection mechanism allows the VLM to act as a verifier, filtering out low-quality or erroneous trajectories proposed by the diffusion expert while retaining its high-precision continuous control.

#### 3.3. Discrete Action Tokenization: Textual FAST

To enable reliable likelihood-based verification by the VLM, the authors introduce Textual FAST, which builds upon existing tokenization schemes:
- **FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025):** The initial step involves encoding continuous action chunks into compact discrete tokens. This is done by applying quantile normalization, Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to capture temporal structure, quantization of DCT coefficients, flattening the sparse matrix, and finally applying Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) for further compression.
- **VLA-0 (Goyal et al., 2025):** Textual FAST then incorporates the idea of text-aligned representations from VLA-0. The compressed discrete tokens generated by FAST are rendered as plain text strings.
- **VLM Re-tokenization:** This text string representation of actions is subsequently re-tokenized using the standard tokenizer of the pretrained VLM backbone.

This two-stage tokenization approach provides two benefits: (i) FAST-style compression efficiently captures temporal structure and motion range, and (ii) representing actions as text aligns them better with the VLM's pre-training distribution, avoiding the introduction of new, low-frequency action-only vocabulary items. This alignment improves the stability and reliability of the VLM's perplexity-based scoring during verification. While Textual FAST might result in longer token sequences than FAST alone, this does not impede the ADV verification process since it uses a single batched forward pass for scoring, rather than token-by-token decoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates ADV across simulation (LIBERO, RoboTwin2.0) and real-world environments, using Qwen2.5-VL-3B, InternVL3.5-2B, and π
0.5
as VLM/VLA backbones.

#### 4.1. Q1: ADV Performance

- **Overall Success Rate:** ADV consistently improves success rates across all evaluated benchmarks and backbones compared to both auto-regressive (Model-FAST) and diffusion-based (Model-Diffusion) baselines under matched settings.
 - **LIBERO Simulation:** ADV improved success rate by an average of +2.7 percentage points (Qwen2.5-VL: +2.9; InternVL3.5: +3.1; π
 0.5
 : +1.9) over diffusion-based inference.
 - **RoboTwin2.0 Simulation:** ADV achieved an average increase of +9.2 points on Easy tasks and +3.5 points on Hard (OOD) tasks for Qwen2.5-VL compared to diffusion.
 - **Real-World Tasks:** ADV demonstrated an average improvement of +17.1 percentage points (Qwen2.5-VL: +21.9; InternVL3.5: +17.5; π
 0.5
 : +12.0) over diffusion-based inference.
- **Efficiency:** While ADV introduces additional inference overhead due to drafting multiple candidates and a single VLM scoring pass, it maintains high control rates (Steps per Second, Hz) sufficient for smooth robot motions (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-ADV: 41.0Hz on LIBERO, 53.3Hz on RoboTwin2.0, 14.4Hz real-world, compared to Qwen2.5-VL-Diffusion: 68.4Hz, 94.7Hz, 26.1Hz respectively). In OOD environments, ADV often completes tasks in fewer average steps (e.g., RoboTwin2.0 Hard: Avg. Success Steps reduced by 99.9 for ADV), partially offsetting the per-chunk latency.
- **Generalizability:** ADV demonstrated effectiveness for both general VLMs without embodied pre-training (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5) and VLAs with embodied pre-training (π
 0.5
 ), indicating broad applicability.

#### 4.2. Q2: ADV Mechanism Analysis

The analysis suggests that VLM verification improves diffusion-based inference primarily by filtering out suboptimal or erroneous draft actions.
- **"Discarding the bad rather than finding the single best":** Experiments on LIBERO-Long showed that selecting the best-ranked action by perplexity score yielded the highest success rate (94.6%). However, the success rate only marginally decreased for K=2 (94.8%) and K=3 (94.2%), but dropped significantly for K≥4. This indicates that the VLM verifier is effective at identifying and rejecting evidently poor action chunks, even if it does not always pinpoint the single optimal candidate.
- **Trajectory Keeping in OOD Environments:**
 - **Real-World (Unseen):** ADV improved success rates by an average of +16.4 points over diffusion-based baselines in tasks with unseen instructions and objects.
 - **RoboTwin2.0 (Hard):** Across four OOD tasks, ADV increased average success rate by +12.0 points, reduced Avg. Success Steps by 99.9, increased Recovery Attempts by 1.6, and reduced Env. Collision Before First Grasp by 26.5 points compared to diffusion-based inference. This suggests ADV enhances proactive re-grasping and reduces environmental disturbance under unseen conditions.
- **Fault Rescue within In-Distribution Environments:** An experiment inducing OOD states by using long action chunks (32 steps) in the real-world "push blocks" task showed that ADV provided a recovery advantage. When two long chunks were executed, ADV achieved a 33% success rate compared to 17% for diffusion. With one long chunk, ADV reached 83% success versus 33%. This indicates ADV's ability to steer the system back to a correct trajectory from perturbed states, provided the environment has not been irreversibly altered.

#### 4.3. Q3: Scoring Reliability

The perplexity-style score (Eq. (4)) was found to be a reliable metric for filtering candidate actions.
- **Correlation with Success:** Successful trajectories generated by Qwen2.5-VL-Diffusion on LIBERO exhibited substantially lower average perplexity-style scores (1.465) compared to failed trajectories (2.110), indicating an inverse relationship between score and task success.
- **Noise Robustness:** When random noise was injected into all but one candidate action chunk on LIBERO, the VLM verifier's "Chosen Rate" for the noise-free chunk increased with larger noise magnitudes (e.g., 12.6% for 0.1% noise to 99.6% for 10% noise). This demonstrates ADV's ability to detect and reject noisy candidates, improving the likelihood of executing a successful trajectory irrespective of the noise level.

#### 4.4. Q4: Tokenization Impact

Textual FAST consistently outperformed alternative action tokenization schemes for ADV verification.
- **Comparative Performance:** On LIBERO, Textual FAST achieved a 96.8% success rate, which was +2.4 points higher than FAST (94.4%) and +1.9 points higher than VLA-0 (94.9%).
- **Underlying Factors:** The authors attribute these gains to (i) improved alignment with the VLM pre-training distribution due to text-rendered action representations, leading to more reliable perplexity-style scoring, and (ii) efficient action compression reducing verification noise from redundant tokens. Direct action space discretization (Action Bins, 93.0% success rate) performed worse than a diffusion expert without any verification (Qwen2.5-VL-Diffusion, 93.9%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Action Draft-and-Verify (ADV) framework represents an advancement in Vision-Language-Action models by proposing an inference mechanism that integrates the strengths of diffusion action experts and auto-regressive Vision-Language Models.

The primary significance lies in its demonstrated ability to **improve robustness and generalization under distribution shifts** in robotic manipulation tasks. By allowing a diffusion expert to propose multiple action candidates and then using a VLM for perplexity-style verification, ADV mitigates common failure modes associated with diffusion-based policies in unseen or perturbed environments. This leads to quantifiable improvements in success rates across diverse simulated and real-world benchmarks, with an average increase of +4.3% in simulation and +19.7% in real-world scenarios over diffusion-based baselines under matched settings.

The mechanistic insights provided suggest that the VLM verifier functions as a **"failure-mode filter"**, reliably rejecting anomalous or low-quality action drafts. This mechanism enhances error recovery and maintains task-consistent trajectories, even when the underlying diffusion sampler is stochastic or when small errors push the system into rarely seen states.

Furthermore, the introduction of **Textual FAST** as a discrete action tokenization method contributes to the reliability of VLM-based scoring by aligning action representations with the VLM's pre-training distribution while retaining compression efficiency. The broad effectiveness of ADV across different VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5) and pre-trained VLAs (π
0.5
) suggests its potential as a generalizable framework for enhancing existing and future VLA architectures.

Potential impacts include:
- **More reliable robotic systems:** The improved robustness in OOD environments could lead to more dependable robotic agents capable of operating in less structured or variable real-world settings.
- **Enhanced VLA model development:** The framework offers a method for leveraging the complementary strengths of different VLA paradigms, potentially informing the design of future hybrid architectures.
- **Accelerated deployment:** By enhancing the reliability of VLA models, the work could contribute to faster and safer deployment of intelligent robots in various applications, from industrial automation to household assistance.

Despite these gains, the authors acknowledge limitations: ADV's performance is contingent on the underlying VLA's ability to propose at least one viable candidate; if all drafts are non-viable, verification cannot salvage a successful trajectory. Additionally, ADV introduces inference latency due to drafting multiple candidates and a batched VLM scoring pass. However, this overhead can be mitigated by efficient chunking and execution strategies and is often compensated by the framework's tendency to complete tasks in fewer steps.
