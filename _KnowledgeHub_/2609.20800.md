# JEPA-Anything: Learning Predictive Models across Different Worlds

> [!summary] Summary
> JEPA-Anything is a domain-agnostic latent world-model framework built on orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF): the JEPA target embedding is split into orthogonal learned subspaces, each predicted by a dedicated head, then recombined via pseudoinverse synthesis into one complete latent state. Across seven domains it beats matched monolithic JEPA on all 10 dynamics tasks, cuts Interventional Pong single-intervention error 34.8%, and leads all four molecular rollouts.

## Problem

- Predictive world models remain domain-specific: vision, biology, clinical, molecular, and weather systems each use bespoke predictors, with no shared learning principle spanning radically different systems.
- Standard JEPA predicts one monolithic target embedding through a single predictor, so easily predicted or high-variance structure can dominate optimization while weaker predictive structure receives conflicting gradients.
- Factorized alternatives fall short: beta-VAE and slot methods need semantic inductive biases, unidentifiable without supervision, while unconstrained multi-head predictors duplicate directions and destabilize state recombination.

## Method

- **Orthogonal Predictive Factorization (OPF)**: K learned projectors split the ==stop-gradient EMA target== into orthogonal r-dimensional subspaces, each regressed by a dedicated predictor head from the shared context representation.
- ==Additive OPF loss==: per-factor regression plus within- and cross-factor ==orthogonality penalties==, a ==factor-activity hinge floor== on projected targets, and an ==online-encoder variance term== against collapse, added onto each domain's base loss.
- ==Pseudoinverse state synthesis== with domain adapters: predicted factors recombine via ==Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse== into one complete latent state for decoding, ==CEM planning==, or rollout; adapters supply tokens, descriptors, and context-target views.

## Results

- On CITRIS Interventional Pong, one-step MSE drops **34.8%** for single interventions and **12.9%** for unseen combined interventions versus matched JEPA; six-step rollout MSE drops **8.6%**.
- Dynamics gains: APEBench Burgers held-out error falls **49.5%** and Kuramoto-Sivashinsky **13.2%**; molecular 100-step RMSD is lowest on all four systems, e.g. paracetamol **1.868** to **1.776 Å**.
- Orthogonality audit: capacity-matched unconstrained heads give condition number **438.5** versus **1.00005** for OPF; CEM planning improves on Walker2d and HalfCheetah but Hopper favors standard JEPA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating latent world-state design as capacity allocation, splitting one target into orthogonal predicted subspaces, is a cheap drop-in upgrade to existing JEPA pipelines, costing under 0.3% extra parameters.
> - Multi-head predictors need explicit orthogonality to recombine safely: without it heads duplicate directions and synthesis error amplifies, so factor geometry, not head count alone, determines synthesis stability.
> - Gains are consistent but modest and narrow with rollout horizon; orthogonal factors do not establish causal mechanisms, so treat factor-nominated discoveries as hypotheses needing external validation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position within the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the development of latent world models: models that summarize an observed system in an internal representation and use that representation to predict hidden, future, or intervention-induced states. World models have traditionally been developed for particular settings, such as visual control, robotics, reinforcement learning, physical simulation, or biological data. Examples include recurrent latent dynamics models, Dreamer-style model-based reinforcement learning, TD-MPC2, DINO-WM, and V-JEPA-based predictive models.

JEPA-Anything builds on joint-embedding predictive architectures, or JEPAs. In a conventional JEPA, an encoder represents a context, a target encoder represents a related target state, and a predictor learns to map the context representation to the target representation. Because the prediction occurs in latent space rather than pixel or observation space, the model can focus on predictable structure and avoid reconstructing irrelevant noise or high-variance details.

The paper’s central argument is that the standard JEPA formulation is too monolithic for many complex systems. A single target embedding and prediction pathway may mix several types of predictable information, such as spatial location, object identity, temporal change, entity-specific responses, or physical scales. JEPA-Anything therefore introduces orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF), which divides a target representation into learned complementary subspaces. Each subspace has its own predictor, and the resulting factor predictions are recombined into a complete latent state.

The approach is related to disentangled representation learning, object-centric representations, causal representation learning, and redundancy-reduction methods such as Barlow Twins and VICReg. However, it does not assume that the factors correspond to known objects, causal variables, or human-interpretable concepts. Instead, factor identities are intended to emerge from predictive utility and orthogonality constraints. This distinction is important because unsupervised semantic disentanglement is generally not identifiable without suitable inductive biases or supervision.

### 2. Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Central Objective

The primary objective is to determine whether a common latent predictive-learning principle can support world modeling across substantially different domains. The authors ask whether models for images, cells, patient histories, robot control, molecular trajectories, physical fields, and weather can use the same factorized predictive core, while allowing domain-specific input processing and context–target definitions.

The proposed framework is intended to support three uses of a learned state:

1. **Terminal readout**, in which an encoder representation is used for classification, clustering, or event-risk prediction.
2. **Operational world modeling**, in which predicted latent states are decoded, used for planning, or repeatedly fed back into the model for multi-step rollout.
3. **Scientific analysis**, in which explicit factor coordinates are examined for intervention hypotheses or physical regularities.

#### 2.2. Motivation for Factorization

The paper identifies several limitations of a single predictive pathway. Different components of a state may have unequal predictability, operate at different spatial or temporal scales, or involve different entities. In a monolithic representation, easily predicted or high-variance components may dominate the training signal, while less prominent but useful components receive weaker or conflicting gradients. Multiple prediction heads alone may not solve this problem, because unconstrained heads can learn overlapping directions or fail to cover the full state space.

OPF is intended to address this capacity-allocation problem. It assigns different portions of the latent target to separate branches while retaining a shared context representation. The authors also seek a mathematically stable method for recombining factor predictions. If the learned factor subspaces are orthogonal, the synthesis map is well-conditioned, preserves the full target representation, and does not amplify prediction errors through an ill-conditioned coordinate system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Common Interface

Each domain-specific adapter converts raw data into content tokens and, where relevant, structural descriptors. Descriptors may encode patch positions, timestamps, graph locations, entity identities, or other indices. A domain-specific view sampler then selects context elements and target elements. The common interface is therefore a context–target prediction problem, regardless of whether the target is a masked image region, a future patient state, a next molecular configuration, or a future physical field.

An online encoder maps the context to a representation \(z_c\). A target encoder maps the target to \(z_t\). The target encoder is updated by an exponential moving average of the online encoder and receives no direct gradient. This follows the teacher–student structure used in JEPA methods.

#### 3.2. Orthogonal Predictive Factorization

The target representation has dimension \(d\) and is divided into \(K\) factors of width \(r\), with \(Kr=d\). Learned projectors \(P_k\) analyze the target representation into factor coordinates:

\[
z_t^{(k)} = P_k^\top z_t.
\]

Each factor has a dedicated predictor \(q_k\), which maps the shared context representation, and optionally a target descriptor, to a predicted factor \(\hat z_t^{(k)}\). The predicted factors are concatenated and converted into a complete predicted state using the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of the combined projection matrix.

The training objective includes four main components. First, a factor-wise prediction loss directly regresses each predicted factor toward its target. Second, an orthogonality loss encourages orthonormal columns within each factor and orthogonality between different factors. Third, a factor-activity loss imposes a minimum variance on projected target coordinates, discouraging inactive branches. Fourth, an online encoder-variance term discourages collapse of the trainable representation. These terms are added to the original loss used by each domain implementation.

For temporal, action-conditioned, or intervention-conditioned tasks, exogenous inputs are incorporated into context tokens or target descriptors. The factor predictors then generate the next factorized state, which is synthesized and used as input for the next transition. This creates an autoregressive latent rollout.

#### 3.3. Experimental Design

The experiments are organized into three groups. Group I examines terminal representations in controlled visual binding, single-cell biology, and longitudinal clinical forecasting. Group II evaluates intervention prediction, out-of-distribution dynamics, control, physical-field prediction, weather, and molecular rollouts. Group III examines whether the factors can support scientifically testable analysis.

Comparisons generally use matched standard JEPA baselines with the same adapters, encoders, data, splits, optimization budgets, and downstream readouts. The main difference is whether the latent target is predicted through one monolithic pathway or through OPF. Most experiments use four factors, although the intervention Pong experiment uses five and the locomotion models use four eight-dimensional factors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Terminal Representation Tasks

In controlled visual binding using MuJoCo scenes, JEPA-Anything improved held-out-cell injective accuracy, reduced collapse rate, and improved grid recovery relative to standard JEPA for both DINOv3 and SigLIP2 backbones. With DINOv3, injective accuracy increased from 0.572 to 0.581, collapse decreased from 0.426 to 0.417, and recovery increased from 0.645 to 0.659. With SigLIP2, injective accuracy increased from 0.483 to 0.490 and recovery from 0.679 to 0.688, while collapse decreased from 0.514 to 0.503.

In single-cell representation learning, JEPA-Anything produced the highest reported clustering and perturbation-prediction scores. On PBMC-10K, the average biological clustering score was 0.8301 after fine-tuning and 0.7752 in the zero-shot setting, compared with 0.7830 and 0.7194 for Cell-JEPA. Pearson correlation for perturbation-response prediction increased to 0.814 on Norman and 0.942 on Adamson, compared with 0.787 and 0.937 for Cell-JEPA.

For longitudinal clinical forecasting, the model predicted a synthesized future patient state and used it to estimate risks for more than 1,000 future clinical events. The factorized model achieved a higher mean precision–recall area under the curve than the matched monolithic JEPA across the event vocabulary. The paper does not provide the exact aggregate PRAUC values in the supplied text, but reports improvement across the broad event set.

#### 4.2. Intervention and Dynamics

On CITRIS Interventional Pong, JEPA-Anything reduced single-intervention one-step mean squared error from 0.009541 to 0.006218, corresponding to a 34.83% reduction. For combinations of interventions withheld from training, error decreased from 0.009441 to 0.008223, a 12.90% reduction. Six-step free-rollout error decreased from 0.009478 to 0.008665, an 8.58% reduction.

The mechanism audit showed that OPF produced nearly zero cross-factor overlap, a minimum singular value of approximately 1, a condition number near 1, and near-zero synthesis error when exact factor coordinates were used. In contrast, unconstrained multi-head prediction produced overlapping subspaces, a minimum singular value of 0.00513, a condition number of 438.52, and a transpose-synthesis normalized error of 0.7886.

Across ten matched dynamics tasks involving robotics, visual dynamics, PDEs, and weather, JEPA-Anything improved the reported metrics on all tasks. In representative six-step diagnostics, PDEBench Burgers error at step six decreased from 0.006369 to 0.004014, while shallow-water error decreased from 0.010510 to 0.006522. In an additional APEBench evaluation, Burgers late-state error decreased by approximately 49.5% and six-step rollout error by approximately 44.7%. Improvements on Kuramoto–Sivashinsky late-state prediction were approximately 13.2%. A separate 50-step Burgers test showed smaller but persistent gains on both in-distribution and high-frequency out-of-distribution data.

In continuous-control planning, JEPA-Anything achieved higher mean cross-entropy-method returns on Walker2d and HalfCheetah but not Hopper. This indicates that planning benefits were environment-dependent rather than uniform. Factor masking increased rollout error in all three locomotion environments, and masking most factors reduced HalfCheetah planning return, suggesting that the learned branches were active and functionally used.

For molecular forecasting, JEPA-Anything achieved the lowest one-step displacement mean absolute error and lowest 100-step final-position RMSD for water, quartz, paracetamol, and benzene. For example, 100-step RMSD decreased from 2.536 to 2.459 Å for water and from 1.868 to 1.776 Å for paracetamol relative to the matched TrajCast-JEPA condition.

#### 4.3. Scientific Analysis

In a biological application, factor-level analysis nominated a combined IL-18 and NT5E/CD73-blockade intervention. The paper reports supporting results in Huh7–PBMC co-cultures, patient-derived organoids, tumor fragments, and immunocompetent mice, including increased tumor-cell killing and immune-cell activation.

In an orbital-dynamics analysis, spectral modes derived from latent factors recovered the Keplerian scaling relation \(f \propto a^{-3/2}\). The fitted slope was −1.4991 with \(R^2=0.9999999\), closely matching the theoretical exponent of −1.5.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper’s main contribution is architectural rather than domain-specific. It proposes that a shared predictive interface can be retained across domains while factorization organizes the latent state according to predictive structure. The same model design can therefore support representation learning, intervention-conditioned forecasting, planning, long-horizon simulation, and scientific diagnostics.

The results suggest potential applications in robotics, climate and physical simulation, molecular modeling, clinical risk forecasting, and biological experiment design. The explicit synthesis map is particularly relevant for recurrent world models, because poorly conditioned factor systems can amplify errors during rollout. The scientific-analysis experiments also indicate a possible role for predictive latent factors in generating experimentally testable hypotheses.

Several qualifications remain. Domain-specific adapters, encoders, and context–target samplers are still required, so the framework does not eliminate domain engineering. Orthogonality does not establish that factors are causal, semantically meaningful, or uniquely identifiable. Control gains were mixed, and some scientific analyses, including the orbital example, were based on limited runs. Future work will need to evaluate uncertainty calibration, robustness under distribution shift, factor revision when new evidence appears, and whether model-guided interventions improve experimental efficiency. Consequently, the paper supports OPF as a general capacity-organization mechanism, but further independent validation is needed before treating it as a universally transferable world-modeling solution.
