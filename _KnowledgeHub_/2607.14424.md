# ConFlow: Constraints-Guided Learning with Flow Matching for Motion Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> ConFlow introduces a framework for robot motion generation that integrates task-specific constraints and negative demonstrations directly into the training objective of flow matching models. This approach, combined with a conditional Gaussian Process source distribution, yielded significantly lower collision rates and inherently smoother, more stable trajectories on a two-robot navigation task.

## Problem

- Existing generative motion models exhibit a mismatch between training objectives and inference-time constraints, leading to out-of-distribution or physically implausible behaviors.
- Current methods rely heavily on costly expert demonstrations and often fail to leverage valuable information from non-expert or negative demonstrations (constraint violations).
- Standard generative models using unstructured Gaussian source distributions frequently produce trajectories lacking intrinsic smoothness and consistent structural properties.

## Method

- **ConFlow** modifies the standard ==Flow Matching objective== to include a ==constraint-guided loss==, incorporating ==differentiable constraint functions== activated specifically for constraint-violating demonstrations.
- It replaces the traditional Gaussian source distribution with a ==conditional Gaussian Process (CGP)==, which inherently instills temporal correlations and enables conditioning on trajectory endpoints for improved smoothness and boundary adherence.
- The framework effectively leverages infeasible (negative) demonstrations as supervision, teaching the model to avoid undesirable behaviors and improving overall constraint satisfaction.

## Results

- ConFlow achieved a robot-collision rate of **0.016** even without inference-time guidance, demonstrating superior internalization of collision avoidance compared to baselines (**0.031** for standard FM).
- The use of a Conditional Gaussian Process source reduced the robot-collision rate by approximately **46%** and improved endpoint adherence by over an order of magnitude (e.g., start error from **0.0267** to **0.0006**).
- Incorporating negative demonstrations with ConFlow consistently lowered collision rates, while their naive inclusion into a standard Flow Matching model degraded performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding deployment-time constraints directly into the training objective of flow matching models significantly mitigates the training-inference mismatch, leading to more robust and inherently compliant motion generation.
> - Utilizing a conditional Gaussian Process as the source distribution for flow matching can substantially enhance trajectory structural properties, such as smoothness and endpoint consistency, by encoding prior knowledge.
> - Negative demonstrations, when integrated through a constraint-guided objective, provide valuable contrastive signals, improving data efficiency and overall constraint satisfaction without degrading performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "ConFlow" is situated within the rapidly evolving field of generative modeling for robot motion generation. Recent advancements in methods such as diffusion models and flow matching have demonstrated considerable potential for modeling complex motion distributions and enabling inference-time guidance. This guidance allows robotic policies to adapt to new tasks, like obstacle avoidance or waypoint adherence, without extensive retraining, making these approaches attractive for deployment in unstructured environments.

However, a key challenge in current guidance-based frameworks is the inherent misalignment between the objective optimized during training and the constraints enforced at deployment. When constraints are primarily applied at inference time, they modify the sampling process but do not fundamentally alter the underlying learned distribution. This can result in the generation of out-of-distribution behaviors that may be infeasible or physically implausible. Existing solutions, such as improved inference procedures or post-hoc fine-tuning, have only partially addressed this discrepancy, often leading to suboptimal outcomes or additional computational overhead.

Furthermore, modern generative models typically demand large datasets, and in robotics, these are frequently sourced from expert demonstrations. The collection of such data is often costly and labor-intensive, limiting scalability. This reliance overlooks potentially valuable information contained in non-expert interactions, particularly negative demonstrations, which can provide contrastive signals regarding constraint violations and help models differentiate between feasible and infeasible motions.

"ConFlow" aims to address these identified limitations by integrating constraint information directly into the training phase of flow matching, rather than solely relying on inference-time guidance. This approach aligns with a growing interest in "training-time guidance," which seeks to embed constraint awareness into the model's fundamental learning process. Prior work in this area has explored methods such as sequential quadratic programming for constraint projection or energy guidance in reinforcement learning, as well as enhancing source distribution expressivity in normalizing flows. ConFlow seeks to advance this by developing a more comprehensive framework that combines a constraint-aware training objective with a structured source distribution, specifically tailored for robot motion generation and capable of leveraging diverse demonstration data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of ConFlow is to mitigate the training–inference mismatch observed in existing generative motion generation frameworks. Current methods often train a generative model on a dataset of expert demonstrations and then rely on inference-time guidance to enforce task-specific constraints, such as obstacle avoidance or desired end-effector poses. While this approach offers flexibility, it effectively modifies the sampling process externally without fundamentally altering the underlying distribution learned during training. This can lead to the generation of trajectories that are out-of-distribution, physically implausible, or violate critical constraints despite guidance attempts.

A secondary motivation is to improve the data efficiency and robustness of robot learning by moving beyond exclusive reliance on costly expert demonstrations. Traditional generative models often require extensive expert data, but the authors observe that non-expert interactions, especially those that result in constraint violations (negative demonstrations), can provide valuable supervision. Current frameworks are not optimally designed to leverage such data, and naive inclusion can degrade performance.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives for the ConFlow framework are:

1. **Integrate Constraint Information into Training:** To directly incorporate task-specific constraints and environmental requirements (e.g., collision avoidance) into the flow matching training objective. This aims to encourage the learned velocity field to generate motions that are inherently compliant with these constraints from the outset.
2. **Mitigate Training–Inference Mismatch:** By transferring the enforcement of constraints from inference-time guidance into the training objective, the framework seeks to align the learned motion distribution more closely with deployment-time requirements, thereby reducing the discrepancy that can lead to infeasible behaviors.
3. **Enhance Trajectory Structural Properties via Source Distribution:** To improve the intrinsic quality and structural properties of generated trajectories, such as smoothness and adherence to boundary conditions. This is achieved by replacing the standard, unstructured Gaussian source distribution in flow matching with a conditional Gaussian Process (GP), which can encode desired prior knowledge about trajectory characteristics.
4. **Leverage Negative Demonstrations for Improved Constraint Satisfaction:** To develop a mechanism that effectively utilizes infeasible or constraint-violating demonstrations as negative supervision. This allows the model to learn what behaviors to avoid, improving constraint satisfaction and generalization without demanding additional expert data collection.
5. **Develop a Robust and Generalizable Motion Generator:** The overarching objective is to produce a generative model that can reliably generate smooth, diverse, and feasible robot trajectories that satisfy prescribed task and motion constraints, exhibiting superior generalization and robustness to distribution shifts compared to existing methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ConFlow builds upon the foundations of Flow Matching (FM), a generative modeling framework that learns a continuous-time vector field to transform samples from a simple source distribution (typically a standard Gaussian) to a target data distribution. Unlike diffusion models, FM directly regresses this velocity field, simplifying training and often leading to faster inference. The core FM objective involves training a neural network $u_\theta(x, t)$ to approximate the ground-truth velocity field $u_t(x_t|x_0, x_1)$ along a probability path connecting a source sample $x_0$ and a data sample $x_1$, by minimizing a mean squared error (MSE) loss.

ConFlow introduces two complementary modifications to this standard conditional flow matching paradigm to achieve constraint-guided learning:

1. **Constraint-Guided Objective:**
 To embed constraints directly into the learning process, ConFlow modifies the standard FM loss. The proposed constraint-guided objective, $L_{CG}$, incorporates a correction term into the target velocity:
 $$L_{CG} = \text{MSE}\left[ u_{\theta,t} - (u_t - \lambda_c M(x_1)u_{c,t}) \right]$$
 Here, $u_{\theta,t}$ is the predicted velocity field, $u_t$ is the standard FM target velocity, and $\lambda_c$ is a coefficient controlling the strength of the constraint guidance. The crucial element is $u_{c,t} = \nabla_{x_t} C(x_t)$, which represents a constraint-induced correction derived from a differentiable constraint function $C(\cdot)$.
 A binary violation indicator, $M(x_1)$, is introduced to differentiate between feasible and infeasible demonstrations. $M(x_1)$ is 1 if the target trajectory $x_1$ violates a constraint, and 0 otherwise. This design ensures that the constraint correction term is only activated for constraint-violating demonstrations. This prevents over-regularization of feasible trajectories while enabling the effective use of negative demonstrations to inform the model about undesirable behaviors.
 For collision avoidance, ConFlow employs a SoftPlus formulation for $C(x_t)$, specifically:
 $$C_\gamma(x_t) = - \frac{1}{\gamma} \log\left( 1 + \exp\left( -\gamma (d(x_t) - r)\right)\right)$$
 where $d(x_t)$ is the minimum distance between robots (or robot links) and $r$ is a safety radius. The SoftPlus function provides smoother gradients compared to ReLU-based penalties, which is beneficial for training. The parameter $\gamma$ controls the sharpness of the transition around the safety boundary. The gradient $u_{c,t}$ derived from this function effectively pushes trajectories away from collision configurations.

2. **Constraint-Injected Source Distribution:**
 To instill structural motion priors into the generative process, ConFlow replaces the standard Gaussian source distribution $p_0$ with a conditional Gaussian Process (CGP), denoted $p_{CGP}$. A key limitation of the standard Gaussian is its assumption of independent noise across trajectory waypoints, which can lead to arbitrarily varying neighboring states and high-frequency artifacts.
 By using a GP as the source distribution, temporal correlations between neighboring waypoints are introduced inherently, encouraging smoother trajectory structures from the initial sampling stage. Each trajectory dimension is modeled independently as $f_d(s) \sim \text{GP}(0, k(s,s'))$, where $k(\cdot, \cdot)$ is an RBF kernel.
 Furthermore, ConFlow optionally conditions the GP on the trajectory endpoints. Given a target trajectory $x_1$ with start state $y_{start}$ and goal state $y_{goal}$, the source trajectory $x_0$ is sampled from $p_{CGP}(x | x(s_0)=y_{start}, x(s_{T-1})=y_{goal})$. This conditioning ensures that both the source ($x_0$) and target ($x_1$) trajectories share identical start and goal states. This significantly simplifies the transport problem for the learned flow, allowing it to concentrate on refining the intermediate trajectory segments while inherently preserving smoothness and endpoint consistency.

**Training Procedure:**
The training algorithm involves sampling a target trajectory $x_1$ from the dataset and a source trajectory $x_0$ from the (conditional) GP. An intermediate state $x_t$ and its ground-truth velocity $\dot{x}_t$ are computed. The constraint guidance $g_t = \nabla_{x_t} C(x_t)$ is then calculated, and a feasibility mask $M(x_1)$ determines if this guidance is applied. The guided target velocity is constructed as $u_t = \dot{x}_t + \lambda M(x_1) g_t$, and the network parameters $\theta$ are updated by minimizing the squared difference between the predicted velocity field $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ and this guided target velocity.

**Inference:**
A notable aspect of ConFlow is its inference phase. Because constraints are learned during training, the model can generate feasible trajectories with minimal or no additional inference-time guidance. Sampling typically involves initializing from a conditional GP prior that adheres to prescribed start and goal states, and then integrating the learned velocity field. Optional inference-time guidance can still be incorporated for enhanced safety in demanding scenarios, by adding $\lambda \nabla_{x_t} C(x_t)$ to the learned velocity field $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ during sampling.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was validated on a two-robot navigation task where two robots exchange positions in a 2D workspace. The dataset comprised trajectories of 32 waypoints for 4 state dimensions, with approximately 30% containing robot-robot collisions (infeasible demonstrations). Baselines included standard Flow Matching (FM) with a Gaussian source and without constraint-guided learning, as well as ablations of ConFlow to isolate the effects of negative data and the CGP source. Evaluation metrics focused on robot-collision rate, object-collision rate (with obstacles), smoothness, and endpoint errors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that ConFlow consistently achieves improved performance in motion generation, particularly regarding constraint satisfaction and trajectory quality.

**Overall Performance and Robustness (Table I):**
ConFlow, specifically when trained with both positive and negative demonstrations (ConFlow (D+ ∪ D-)), exhibited the lowest collision rates across all evaluated inference settings: no guidance, robot-avoidance guidance, and obstacle-avoidance guidance. This finding supports the core hypothesis that integrating constraint guidance directly into the training objective effectively reduces the training–deployment mismatch, leading to improved generalization and robustness. The results also indicate that the benefits of this training-time constraint integration are complementary to, rather than replaceable by, inference-time guidance.

**Impact of Negative Demonstrations:**
The comparison between ConFlow (D+ ∪ D-) and ConFlow (D+) highlighted the efficacy of incorporating infeasible demonstrations as negative supervision. ConFlow (D+ ∪ D-) consistently outperformed its variant trained solely on feasible data. Conversely, naive inclusion of negative examples into a standard Flow Matching baseline (FM (D+ ∪ D-)) resulted in a degradation of performance, indicating that a specific framework like ConFlow's constraint-guided objective is necessary to effectively leverage such data.

**Internalization of Collision Avoidance:**
A significant observation was that ConFlow achieved a substantially lower robot-collision rate (0.016) even without any inference-time guidance, compared to the standard Flow Matching baseline (0.031). This suggests that the collision-avoidance behavior was internalized by the learned velocity field during training, rather than being solely enforced by test-time corrections. This characteristic makes ConFlow robust even in scenarios where deployment-time guidance might be unavailable or imperfect.

**Ablation Study on Source Distributions (Table II and Fig. 2):**
The ablation study on source distributions confirmed the benefits of replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a Conditional Gaussian Process (CGP).
- **Reduced Collision Rate:** The robot-collision rate decreased by approximately 46% (from 0.0288 to 0.0156) when using CGP.
- **Improved Smoothness:** Both median and 95th-percentile smoothness metrics showed substantial improvement, indicating that CGP priors lead to inherently smoother trajectories.
- **Enhanced Endpoint Adherence:** Endpoint errors (start and end errors per robot) decreased by more than an order of magnitude (e.g., start error from 0.0267 to 0.0006). This demonstrates that conditioning the source distribution on the start and goal states effectively preserves task boundary conditions during generation.

Qualitative visualizations further supported these quantitative findings, showing that trajectories generated with CGP were noticeably smoother with fewer local oscillations compared to those from a standard Gaussian prior. The conditioning also mitigated issues observed with unconstrained GP priors, which could generate trajectories drifting away from the data manifold, leading to unstable samples. These results collectively indicate that embedding structured priors into the source distribution significantly enhances the quality and stability of the learned flow.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ConFlow represents a significant contribution to the field of robot motion generation and generative modeling by addressing several critical limitations of current approaches.

1. **Mitigating the Training–Inference Gap:** ConFlow's central contribution is its ability to integrate deployment-time constraints directly into the training objective. This fundamentally shifts constraint enforcement from an inference-time correction to an intrinsic property of the learned model. This mitigation of the training–inference mismatch is crucial for developing robot systems that can reliably operate in complex, dynamic, and safety-critical environments where out-of-distribution or constraint-violating behaviors are unacceptable.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Robustness:** By demonstrating consistently lower collision rates and improved motion quality, ConFlow offers a pathway to inherently safer robot motion generation. The internalization of collision avoidance during training means that generated trajectories are more robust, even in the absence of explicit inference-time guidance. This property is vital for autonomous systems that must operate reliably under varying operational conditions and with limited real-time computational resources for complex guidance.

3. **Improved Data Efficiency through Negative Supervision:** The framework's capacity to effectively leverage infeasible (negative) demonstrations is a notable advancement. This expands the pool of usable training data beyond costly expert-only collections. By learning from "what not to do," ConFlow can potentially achieve better generalization and constraint satisfaction with less reliance on meticulously curated positive examples, thereby contributing to more scalable robot learning pipelines.

4. **Advancement in Flow-Based Generative Models:** ConFlow extends the utility of Flow Matching by showing how sophisticated modifications to both the training objective and the source distribution can embed domain-specific knowledge and structural priors. The use of a conditional Gaussian Process as a source distribution is particularly impactful, demonstrating that careful design of the generative process's initial conditions can significantly improve the quality, smoothness, and boundary adherence of generated outputs, reducing the burden on the learned flow to correct fundamental structural properties.

5. **New Paradigm for Structured Generative Learning:** More broadly, ConFlow proposes a promising direction for generative modeling in robotics and beyond: jointly designing the training objective and source distribution to embed task-relevant structure. This holistic approach contrasts with treating these components as independent elements and suggests a powerful strategy for building more capable and reliable generative models.

**Potential Future Impact:**
The principles demonstrated by ConFlow have the potential to be extended to higher-dimensional robotic systems, such as articulated manipulators, and to real-world platforms, which would further validate its applicability and impact. By enabling robots to learn inherently safer and more efficient motions from diverse data, ConFlow contributes to the development of more autonomous, capable, and trustworthy robotic systems across various applications, including manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.
