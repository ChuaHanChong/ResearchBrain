# REMAC: Self-Reflective and Self-Evolving Multi-Agent Collaboration for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University, CMU, UNC-Chapel Hill, and UC Berkeley introduce REMAC, a framework enabling multi-robot systems to execute long-horizon manipulation tasks through self-reflection and adaptation, achieving 40% higher success rates and 52.7% improved execution efficiency compared to single-robot baselines without task-specific prompting.

## Problem

- Existing VLM-based robot control systems struggle with dynamic environments and unexpected changes during long-horizon tasks
- Current approaches rely heavily on detailed task-specific prompts and prior environmental knowledge
- Integrating multi-robot coordination with VLM-based adaptive planning remains underexplored, leaving single-robot systems unable to exploit parallel execution for efficiency gains

## Method

- ==Three-stage framework== combining ==scene exploration==, ==task planning with check mechanisms==, and ==iterative self-evolution==
- Integration of VLMs for visual understanding with LLMs for reasoning and planning in a ==multi-agent system==
- ==Self-reflection module== for pre/post-condition checks and plan refinement

## Results

- Achieved **40%** higher task success rate and **52.7%** improved execution efficiency compared to the single-robot baseline across diverse tasks
- Demonstrated effectiveness across **4** task categories with **27** task styles and **50+** objects
- Reduced need for task-specific prompting while maintaining high performance

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-robot coordination reduces average subtask duration by 20% through optimized task decomposition
> - Self-evolution mechanism improves planning efficiency by reducing initial plan length 35-62%
> - Storing reflections in long-term memory enables continuous learning and performance improvement

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work builds upon several key areas in robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** VLMs have emerged as a promising tool for robot control, enabling robots to understand and execute complex tasks described in natural language by combining visual information with linguistic instructions. This paper addresses the limitations of existing VLM-based approaches in handling long-horizon tasks and dynamic environments.
- **Long-Horizon Task Planning:** This is a challenging area in robotics that involves planning and executing tasks with many steps and complex dependencies. Existing methods often struggle with unforeseen changes in the environment or unexpected task conditions. The paper addresses the need for more adaptable and efficient planning frameworks for long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- **Multi-Robot Collaboration:** Coordinating multiple robots to perform tasks efficiently is a well-established field, but integrating it with VLM-based planning and adaptation is relatively new. This paper contributes by proposing a framework that enables robots to coordinate and collaborate based on environmental understanding and task requirements, dynamically adapting plans to maximize efficiency.
- **Self-Reflection and Self-Evolution:** These concepts, borrowed from cognitive science and machine learning, are becoming increasingly important in robotics. By enabling robots to reflect on their past performance and adapt their future behavior, researchers aim to create more robust and intelligent systems. This paper incorporates these principles into a multi-agent planning framework, allowing robots to continuously improve their performance through iterative refinement.

The paper directly addresses the limitations of existing methods that rely on prior environmental knowledge or carefully designed task-specific prompts, making them struggle with dynamic scene changes or unexpected task conditions. By proposing REMAC, the authors aim to fill the gap in adaptability and efficiency for scene-agnostic multi-robot long-horizon task planning and execution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the research are:

- **Develop an adaptive multi-agent planning framework (REMAC) for long-horizon robot manipulation.** The framework should enable efficient, scene-agnostic planning and execution without relying on detailed, task-specific prompts.
- **Improve robots' adaptability to dynamic scene changes and unexpected task conditions.** The system should be able to handle situations where the initial plan becomes infeasible due to environmental changes or unforeseen circumstances.
- **Enhance task execution efficiency through multi-robot collaboration.** The framework should enable robots to coordinate tasks in parallel, maximizing the overall efficiency of task execution.
- **Create a self-reflection module for pre- and post-condition checks.** This module should evaluate progress, refine plans, and identify potential planning errors.
- **Implement a self-evolution module for dynamically adapting plans based on scene-specific reasoning.** This module should allow the robots to learn from their past experiences and improve their future performance.
- **Benchmark the proposed framework against state-of-the-art reasoning models.** The authors aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of REMAC by comparing its performance to existing methods in a realistic multi-agent environment.

The motivation behind this research stems from the limitations of existing VLM-based approaches for long-horizon robot manipulation in dynamic and unpredictable environments. The authors recognize that current methods often struggle with adaptability and efficiency, leading to frequent replanning and suboptimal performance. By developing REMAC, they aim to create a more robust and intelligent system that can handle complex tasks in real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel framework called REMAC, which comprises three interconnected stages:

- **Scene Exploration and Information Gathering:** Robots explore the environment guided by the task description. They use cameras to identify and record relevant items, providing the system with the necessary information to understand the scene.
- **Task Planning and Check Mechanism:** A large language model (LLM) decomposes the high-level task description into subtasks, using the gathered environmental information. Pre-condition and post-condition checks are implemented to assess the feasibility of the plan and the successful execution of subtasks.
- **Iterative Self-Evolving Framework:** The system iteratively refines the initial plan based on accumulated reflections from the pre- and post-condition checks. The reflections are stored in a long-term memory module to guide the planning in subsequent iterations.

The methodology combines the strengths of VLMs, LLMs, and multi-agent systems. The VLMs provide visual understanding, the LLMs provide reasoning and planning capabilities, and the multi-agent system enables collaboration and parallel execution. The self-reflection and self-evolution mechanisms allow the system to continuously learn and improve its performance.

To validate REMAC's effectiveness, the authors built a multi-agent environment based on RoboCasa, featuring 4 task categories with 27 task styles and 50+ different objects. They benchmarked state-of-the-art reasoning models, including DeepSeek-R1, o3-mini, QwQ, and Grok3, demonstrating REMAC's superiority.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that REMAC significantly improves the performance of long-horizon robot manipulation tasks:

- **Increased Task Success Rate:** REMAC boosts the average success rate by 40% over the single robot baseline without any task-specific prompting or finetuning.
- **Enhanced Execution Efficiency:** REMAC improves execution efficiency by 52.7% compared to the single robot baseline.
- **Improved Subtask Completion Rate:** The self-reflection and self-evolution mechanisms significantly increase the completion ratio of subtasks within initial plans.
- **Reduced Length of Initial Plan:** The self-evolution mechanism improves planning efficiency by reducing the length of the initial plan (i.e., the number of subtasks) by 35–62%.
- **Effective Multi-Robot Collaboration:** Transitioning from single-robot (RE) systems to multi-robot (REMAC) coordination reduced average subtask duration by approximately 20% through optimized task decomposition.

The comparisons among different reasoning models show that Grok3 outperforms o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1, and QwQ, demonstrating remarkable reflection ability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has significant implications for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Improved Adaptability and Efficiency:** REMAC addresses the critical limitations of existing VLM-based approaches for long-horizon robot manipulation, enabling robots to handle complex tasks in dynamic and unpredictable environments.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By eliminating the need for detailed, task-specific prompts, REMAC makes it easier for humans to interact with robots and delegate complex tasks.
- **Increased Autonomy:** The self-reflection and self-evolution mechanisms allow robots to learn from their past experiences and improve their future performance, leading to more autonomous and intelligent systems.
- **Practical Applications:** The framework can be applied to a wide range of real-world scenarios, including household robots, manufacturing automation, and search and rescue operations.
- **Advancement of AI and Robotics Research:** The research contributes to the ongoing development of AI and robotics by providing a novel framework for multi-agent planning, self-reflection, and self-evolution.

The development of REMAC represents a significant step towards creating more robust, adaptable, and efficient robot systems. By combining the strengths of VLMs, LLMs, and multi-agent systems, the authors have developed a framework that can handle complex tasks in real-world settings, paving the way for more autonomous and intelligent robots.

This research is likely to inspire further work in the areas of VLM-based robotics, long-horizon task planning, multi-agent collaboration, and self-learning systems. It has the potential to significantly impact the development of future robotic systems and their applications in various industries.
