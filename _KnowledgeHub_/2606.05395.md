# VASO: Formally Verifiable Self-Evolving Skills for Physical AI Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Texas at Austin and Iowa State University introduce VASO, a framework that enables the formal verification and self-evolution of large language model (LLM)-generated robot skills by using formal counterexamples as direct feedback for skill refinement. This approach achieves high safety scores (up to 97%) for physical AI agents and effectively generalizes optimized skills to novel tasks.

## Problem

- LLM-generated robot skills, while easy to create from natural language, can harbor safety-critical flaws during physical execution.
- Existing skill evolution methods rely on 'soft' feedback (e.g., execution outcomes), providing only trace-level evidence without formal guarantees for temporal safety specifications under untested conditions.
- Integrating formal verification into the skill improvement loop is challenging due to incompatible consumer requirements (model checkers, LLM planners, robots) and verification typically acting as a post-planning filter without improving the reusable skill itself.

## Method

- **VASO** defines a structured skill representation that explicitly couples formal (==temporal logic==, symbolic transition systems) and planner-facing (natural language, code-like) interfaces via a semantic contract and a proposition-aligned labeling function.
- The framework employs a two-level verification process: an initial skill-level check for logical feasibility and a plan-level check for behavioral correctness against global and local LTL specifications.
- Formal verification counterexamples are translated into '==textual gradients==' which serve as direct feedback to the foundation model, guiding the iterative, prompt-level refinement of the reusable skill's semantic contract.

## Results

- Initial skill feasibility improved from **89%** to **97%** after one verification feedback iteration, with optimized skills achieving approximately **95%** safety scores while maintaining **97%** task feasibility.
- VASO achieved over **90%** safety within 7 optimization steps, outperforming baseline prompt optimization (LAD-VF) which plateaued near **85%**, and demonstrated robust generalization (92-**100%** safety) to unseen task prompts.
- The framework achieved high safety with a small number of optimization samples (fewer than 100) and short timeframes (under 20 minutes per skill) and showed automatic proposition alignment performed on par with handcrafted mappings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formal verification counterexamples can serve as an effective optimization signal for refining the underlying reusable skill contract, moving beyond simply rejecting individual flawed plans.
> - Optimizing a structured skill representation (semantic contract) is more effective and efficient for improving safety compliance compared to directly refining flat natural language prompts.
> - Foundation models can automatically generate proposition-aligned labeling functions comparable to handcrafted versions, making formal verification integration into robotics more practical and reducing reliance on domain experts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of embodied AI agents capable of executing complex tasks in physical environments increasingly relies on reusable robot skills, which act as high-level modules translating open-ended instructions into long-horizon behaviors. Foundation models have significantly reduced the effort required to author these skills from natural language, enabling the rapid generation of skill descriptions, code templates, or executable command sequences. However, this ease of creation has not translated into a corresponding ease of assurance or trustworthiness. Generated skills, while appearing plausible in natural language, can harbor safety-critical flaws during physical execution.

Existing methods for skill evolution in foundation model-driven agents primarily rely on soft feedback mechanisms, such such as execution outcomes, unit tests, environmental rewards, or Large Language Model (LLM) self-critique. While these approaches are effective in expanding an agent's capabilities by identifying successful behaviors in sampled tasks, they offer only trace-level evidence. This means they demonstrate a skill's functionality on observed executions but do not provide guarantees regarding its adherence to temporal safety specifications under untested or novel conditions. This limitation is particularly critical in robotics, where even rare violations of safety rules can have significant consequences. The paper highlights that even skills refined through execution-style feedback can exhibit specification violations in a notable percentage of cases.

Formal verification techniques offer a potential solution by rigorously checking system properties against logical specifications. Prior work in LLM planning and plan verification has focused on generating executable action sequences or code policies and, in parallel, compiling LLM outputs into formal models for model checking. These efforts typically operate at the plan level, treating verification as a final pass/fail filter for individual generated plans, with counterexamples serving as rejection certificates rather than mechanisms for improving the underlying skill. Similarly, prompt and model optimization methods use textual or formal feedback to refine model inputs or update model parameters. However, these improvements are often absorbed into a specific prompt or hidden model parameters, preventing their exposure as an interpretable, reusable skill artifact. Skill evolution frameworks, while focusing on building reusable skill libraries, still rely on trace-level supervision, leaving formal safety gaps unaddressed.

VASO positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental gap between formal verification and the self-evolution of reusable LLM-generated skills for physical AI agents. It proposes a novel skill representation that bridges the requirements of model checkers (symbolic transition systems, temporal logic) with those of language model planners (natural language or code-like behavioral priors) and physical robots (executable APIs). By enabling formal verification counterexamples to directly act as optimization feedback for the reusable skill contract itself, VASO aims to move beyond verifying one-off plans or fine-tuning model weights, thus contributing to the development of more trustworthy and robust physical AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables the formal verification and self-evolution of LLM-generated robot skills, thereby increasing their trustworthiness and reliability for deployment in physical AI agents. The work is driven by several motivations:

1. **Bridging the Creation-Trust Gap:** Foundation models have significantly lowered the barrier to creating robot skills from natural language instructions. However, this ease of creation has not been matched by a similar reduction in the cost or complexity of assuring these skills, particularly in terms of safety and adherence to specifications. A skill that appears coherent in language may conceal critical safety failures during physical execution, such as a ground robot failing to stop upon observing a person or a quadcopter exceeding its altitude limits.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Skill Improvement Loops:** Current skill evolution methodologies predominantly rely on "soft" feedback mechanisms like execution outcomes, unit tests, environment rewards, or LLM self-critique. While beneficial for expanding capabilities by identifying successful sampled behaviors, these signals provide only "trace-level evidence." They can confirm that a skill worked in specific observed instances but do not guarantee that the skill will consistently induce plans that satisfy temporal safety contracts under untested conditions or rare edge cases. For physical systems, where safety is paramount, this distinction is critical, as many successful rollouts cannot compensate for a single, severe safety violation.
3. **Integrating Formal Verification into the Evolution Loop:** A natural progression is to incorporate formal verification, which offers rigorous guarantees, into the skill evolution process. However, integrating formal verification directly into the skill improvement loop presents several challenges:
 - **Incompatible Consumer Requirements:** A robot skill must simultaneously cater to a model checker (requiring symbolic transition systems and temporal logic), a language model planner (requiring natural language or code-like behavioral priors), and a physical robot (requiring executable APIs or trajectories).
 - **Limited Impact of Post-Planning Verification:** If verification is applied only after a plan is generated, it functions merely as a pass/fail filter for that specific plan, without providing a mechanism to improve the reusable skill itself.
 - **Lack of Interpretable Feedback for Reusable Skills:** When feedback is used solely to tune a planner prompt or fine-tune a model, the learned improvement is not exposed as an interpretable skill artifact that can be inspected, reused, and verified independently across different task instances. The paper identifies a missing representation that would allow formal counterexamples to directly inform and refine the reusable skill.
4. **Enabling Inspectable, Reusable, and Verifiable Skill Improvement:** The overarching motivation is to move beyond merely creating skills to enabling skills to improve in a manner that is inspectable by humans, reusable across diverse tasks, and formally verifiable to ensure safety and correctness. This necessitates a framework where formal verification acts as a primary optimization signal for the skill's underlying contract, rather than just an external check.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VASO (Verification-Guided Automated Skill Optimization) introduces a closed-loop framework for the verifiable self-evolution of LLM-generated skills for physical AI agents. Its core approach revolves around a structured skill representation and a two-level verification process that uses formal counterexamples as feedback for skill refinement.

**Skill Definition and Representation:**
A skill `sk` is formally defined as a tuple `(G, ψ_sk, C_sk)`:
- `G = {φ_1..., φ_m}`: A set of **global specifications** expressed as Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) formulas over a set of atomic propositions (AP). These define constraints that must always be satisfied.
- `ψ_sk`: A **local rule** specified as an LTL formula over AP, representing the intended behavior or goal of the particular skill.
- `C_sk`: The **semantic contract**, which is central to the skill, coupling its formal and planner-facing interfaces. It consists of:
 - `L_sk`: A **proposition-aligned labeling function** that maps robot execution states, observations, and control signals to the set of atomic propositions holding at a given time. This function serves as the bridge between the physical robot's state and the formal logical propositions.
 - `C_p_sk`: A **text-based plan template** that defines a semantic behavioral prior, guiding the LLM planner in generating executable plans.

**VASO Framework Operation:**
The framework operates in a closed loop, iteratively refining `ψ_sk` and `C_sk` through verification feedback while keeping the global specifications `G` and the foundation model weights frozen.

1. **Skill Generation:** Given a task prompt `τ`, a set of system commands `S`, and global specifications `G`, a foundation model `F_skill` generates an initial skill `sk = (G, ψ_sk, C_sk)`.

2. **Skill-Level Verification (Logical Feasibility Check):**
 - Before any plan is sampled, VASO performs a preliminary check to ensure the generated skill specification is logically feasible. This means verifying that the local rule `ψ_sk` is consistent with the global specifications `G`.
 - A combined specification `Φ_sk = (∧_{φ∈G} φ) ∧ ψ_sk` is formed.
 - A universal transition system `M` is constructed, representing all possible proposition transitions over AP.
 - A model checker is then used to verify `M |= ¬Φ_sk`. If this property holds, it implies no execution trace can satisfy `Φ_sk`, indicating an infeasible skill.
 - If infeasible, `F_skill` is re-queried to refine the local rule. If feasible, the model checker provides a satisfying trace as a witness, and the process proceeds. This stage filters out logically contradictory skills early, reducing downstream failures.

3. **Plan-Level Behavior Verification:**
 - For a logically feasible skill, `F_plan` (a language model planner) generates an executable plan `π` guided by the semantic contract `C_sk` and the task prompt `τ`.
 - The generated plan `π` is compiled into an automaton `A_π`. Each state in `A_π` corresponds to an execution state, and transitions represent executable behaviors. The proposition-aligned labeling function `L_sk` maps each state to the atomic propositions satisfied at that point.
 - A formal model checker (e.g., NuSMV) then verifies `A_π |= G ∧ ψ_sk`.
 - If verification fails, the model checker returns a counterexample trace, which highlights the specific sequence of events leading to a specification violation.

4. **Semantic Contract Refinement:**
 - The counterexample trace from the plan-level verification is translated into a **textual gradient** `g`. This gradient is a natural-language description of the identified specification violation (e.g., "violate specification: G(person observed -> X stop)").
 - This textual gradient `g` serves as feedback for `F_skill`, guiding the iterative refinement of the semantic contract: `C^(k+1)_sk = F_skill(C^(k)_sk, g, τ)`.
 - This process effectively acts as a textual gradient descent, where `F_skill` updates the semantic contract based on formal verification feedback, without requiring parameter updates or gradient backpropagation within the foundation model itself. The optimization operates entirely at the prompt level.

**Key Features:**
- **Automatic Proposition Alignment:** `F_skill` is employed to automatically generate `L_sk` (proposition alignment functions) from APIs and specifications, reducing the need for manual, expert-driven mappings.
- **Physical Agent Validation:** The framework is evaluated on real robotic platforms: the Clearpath Jackal ground robot and a PX4 quadcopter, demonstrating its applicability to physical AI agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of VASO was conducted on two robotic platforms, the Clearpath Jackal ground robot and a PX4 quadcopter, across 11 temporal-logic specifications and 400 generated plans. The findings address four primary questions:

1. **Does verification feedback improve skills?**
 - Initial skill generation using `F_skill` resulted in 89% of generated skills satisfying all contracts. After just one iteration of verification feedback, this feasibility improved to 97%, demonstrating rapid convergence.
 - To quantify skill quality, a "safety score" (fraction of specifications satisfied by a plan) was defined. Comparing 100 plans from initial skills against 100 from optimized skills (after 7 optimization steps), optimized skills achieved approximately 95% safety scores. This represents a substantial improvement over initial skills, while maintaining a high task feasibility rate of 97%. This indicates that verification feedback contributes to improved skill quality rather than merely promoting conservative, safe behaviors that might compromise task completion.

2. **Does optimizing a skill outperform flat prompt optimization on natural language prompts?**
 - VASO was compared against LAD-VF, a baseline prompt optimization method that directly refines natural-language instructions without an explicit structured skill representation.
 - VASO achieved over 90% safety within 7 optimization steps. In contrast, LAD-VF plateaued near 85% safety even after 10 steps. This suggests that optimizing a structured skill representation, as opposed to directly refining flat natural language prompts, provides a more effective and efficient optimization target for enhancing safety compliance.

3. **Can optimized skills generalize to unseen task prompts?**
 - The reusability of optimized skills was evaluated on four representative skills using 5 prompts seen during optimization and 5 new, unseen prompts from the same task family.
 - Optimized skills demonstrated robust generalization, achieving 92–100% safety and 80–96% task completion on these new, held-out prompts. This result indicates that the learned improvements are embedded within the reusable skill contract, allowing the optimized skills to guide future plan generation for novel task instances without requiring further refinement.

4. **Does automatic proposition alignment provide a practical alternative to handcrafted mappings?**
 - A key practical aspect of formal verification is the creation of proposition-aligned labeling functions (`L_sk`) that map low-level robot controls to symbolic propositions. `F_skill` was used to automatically generate these functions.
 - A comparison between automatically generated `L_sk` and meticulously handcrafted versions revealed that automatic alignment achieved comparable performance. Automatically generated alignment resulted in a safety score of 96.8% and a task completion rate of 86.5%, which is on par with the 97.2% safety and 85.3% task completion achieved with handcrafted alignment. This finding suggests that automatic proposition alignment can render verification-guided skill optimization practical without requiring domain experts to manually define low-level symbolic abstractions, reducing deployment overhead.

**Planning Baseline Comparison:**
- VASO was benchmarked against several zero-shot planners (LLM+P, ReAct, LLM-Planner) and learning-based methods (DSPy, RoboInstruct, RLVF).
- VASO achieved the highest safety score (96.6%) while maintaining a competitive task completion rate.
- Compared to learning-based baselines, VASO demonstrated a more favorable safety-efficiency trade-off, achieving high safety without the need for parameter fine-tuning. For instance, VASO reached a high safety score with only 100 optimization samples, requiring approximately one-fourth of the training time compared to fine-tuning approaches like RLVF.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of VASO contributes to the field of AI and robotics by addressing a fundamental challenge in the deployment of autonomous systems: ensuring the trustworthiness and safety of AI-generated behaviors in physical environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Formal Verification and LLM Skill Evolution:** VASO represents a framework that integrates formal verification into the self-evolution loop of LLM-generated robot skills. This connection addresses a gap in existing research, where formal verification typically serves as a post-hoc filter for individual plans, rather than an active mechanism for refining reusable skills. This shift means that formal guarantees can influence the underlying skill definitions, leading to more inherently safe and compliant behaviors.

2. **Enhancing Trustworthiness of Physical AI Agents:** For physical robots, the implications of unsafe behaviors can be severe. By explicitly incorporating formal verification feedback, VASO provides a systematic method to improve LLM-generated skills to satisfy temporal safety contracts, even under untested conditions. This capability is critical for increasing confidence in deploying AI agents in safety-critical applications, ranging from autonomous navigation to human-robot collaboration.

3. **Enabling Reusable and Interpretable Skill Refinement:** Unlike methods that absorb improvements into opaque model parameters or transient prompts, VASO refines an explicit, structured skill contract. This makes the improvements inspectable, reusable across different task instances, and verifiable independently of the original task prompt or specific LLM parameters. This contributes to the development of robust and generalizable skill libraries for embodied agents.

4. **Efficiency in Skill Optimization:** The framework demonstrates that high levels of specification compliance (e.g., 97.2% safety score) can be achieved with a relatively small number of optimization samples (fewer than 100) and in a short timeframe (under 20 minutes per skill). This efficiency, particularly when compared to fine-tuning-based approaches like RLVF, lowers the computational and temporal cost of developing verified robot skills.

5. **Practical Integration of Formal Methods:** The ability of foundation models to automatically generate proposition-aligned labeling functions, which achieve performance comparable to manually crafted mappings, simplifies the integration of formal verification tools. This reduces the burden on domain experts to manually define low-level symbolic abstractions, making formal verification more accessible and practical for AI-driven robotics.

6. **Advancing Foundational Capabilities for Robotics:** By making verification an intrinsic part of skill development, VASO provides a pathway for LLM-powered robots to learn and adapt skills that are not only capable but also reliably safe and compliant with desired specifications. This is a foundational step towards more autonomous, robust, and trustworthy robotic systems.

The limitations of the current work, such as the reliance on unverified AI-generated labeling functions and the focus on sequential skill execution, also point to clear directions for future research. Addressing these could further enhance the robustness and applicability of verification-guided skill evolution, for instance, by incorporating uncertainty signals into proposition grounding or extending the framework to multi-agent, concurrent skill interactions.
