# Sampling-Based System Identification with Active Exploration for Legged Robot Sim2Real Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and Google DeepMind developed SPI-Active, a framework that improves sim-to-real transfer for legged robots by combining sampling-based system identification with active exploration. This approach, which does not require differentiable physics or specialized sensing, led to policies exhibiting 42-63% performance improvements on physical robots across various locomotion tasks.

## Problem

- The sim-to-real gap causes performance degradation when policies trained in simulation are deployed on real-world legged robots, due to modeling inaccuracies, parameter uncertainty, and unmodeled dynamics.
- Traditional system identification methods for legged robots often require differentiable physics simulators, direct torque measurements, or struggle to collect sufficiently informative data for accurate parameter estimation.
- Existing methods like Domain Randomization (DR) or basic System Identification (Sys-ID) often fall short in precisely capturing complex contact dynamics and non-linearities inherent in legged locomotion.

## Method

- Carnegie Mellon University and Google DeepMind developed SPI-Active (Sampling-based Parameter Identification with Active Exploration), a two-stage framework for principled system identification.
- The framework combines an initial sampling-based parameter identification stage, which minimizes state discrepancies between real and simulated trajectories, with an active exploration and refinement stage.
- An active exploration strategy uses the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) to quantify information content and guides the collection of highly informative trajectories for improved parameter estimation, without requiring differentiable simulators.

## Results

- Policies fine-tuned with SPI-Active parameters showed performance improvements of 42-63% across various locomotion tasks on quadruped (Go1) and humanoid (H1) robots.
- SPI-Active achieved lower tracking errors in global root position and per-joint angles, demonstrating improved prediction accuracy compared to baseline methods.
- The active exploration strategy effectively generated more informative trajectories for parameter estimation, leading to more accurate models compared to random data collection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - FIM-guided active exploration efficiently identifies informative trajectories, reducing the data needed for accurate parameter estimation by focusing on motions that best excite system dynamics.
> - A sampling-based system identification approach, utilizing multi-horizon prediction and CMA-ES optimization, can accurately estimate physical parameters without requiring differentiable physics simulators or specialized torque sensors.
> - Accurate system identification of physical parameters like mass distribution, friction, and actuator dynamics is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap and improving the performance of learned policies on legged robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robots—known as sim-to-real transfer—remains one of the most significant challenges in robotic learning. This challenge is particularly acute for legged robots, where complex contact dynamics and physical parameters significantly impact performance. Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and Google DeepMind have developed a novel approach called SPI-Active (Sampling-based Parameter Identification with Active Exploration) that addresses this challenge through a principled system identification framework.

The SPI-Active framework provides a systematic method for estimating physical parameters of legged robots without requiring specialized sensing equipment or differentiable physics simulators. By combining sampling-based optimization with an active exploration strategy, the approach efficiently identifies parameters that minimize the discrepancy between simulated and real-world robot behavior, significantly improving the performance of learned policies when deployed on physical robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The sim-to-real gap refers to the discrepancy between robot behavior in simulation versus the real world. This gap arises from several factors:

1. **Modeling inaccuracies**: Simplified physics models in simulation cannot capture all real-world phenomena
2. **Parameter uncertainty**: Physical parameters like mass, friction, and motor dynamics are often estimated or approximated
3. **Unmodeled dynamics**: Environmental factors and wear-and-tear effects that aren't represented in simulation

Existing approaches to address this gap generally fall into three categories:

- **Domain Randomization (DR)**: Training policies with randomized simulation parameters to increase robustness
- **System Identification (Sys-ID)**: Estimating physical parameters to improve simulation accuracy
- **Adaptive Policy Learning**: Fine-tuning policies online using real-world feedback

Traditional system identification methods for legged robots face several limitations:

- They often require differentiable physics simulators
- Many approaches depend on direct torque measurements
- It's challenging to collect informative data for parameter estimation
- Complex contact dynamics are difficult to model accurately

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPI-Active framework addresses these limitations through a two-stage approach that combines sampling-based optimization with active exploration.

The framework consists of two main stages:

1. **Initial Parameter Identification**:
 - Collect real-world trajectories using motion priors or pre-trained policies
 - Estimate physical parameters by minimizing state discrepancies between real and simulated rollouts
 - Leverage parallel sampling for efficient parameter space exploration

2. **Active Exploration and Refinement**:
 - Use an active exploration strategy to identify the most informative trajectories
 - Maximize the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) to guide data collection
 - Optimize command sequences for a pre-trained policy to generate informative motions
 - Refine parameter estimates using the enriched dataset

#### 3.1. System Identification Methodology

The core of the SPI-Active framework is a sampling-based parameter identification approach that enables accurate estimation of physical parameters without requiring differentiable dynamics or specialized sensing.

The method minimizes a cost function that represents the discrepancy between real and simulated trajectories:

$$J_i(\theta) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{h=1}^{H_k} \| x_{i,k}^r(h) - \hat{x}_{i,k}^s(h, \theta) \|^2_{\Sigma_x^{-1}}$$

Where:
- $x_{i,k}^r(h)$ is the real-world state at horizon step $h$ in trajectory segment $k$
- $\hat{x}_{i,k}^s(h, \theta)$ is the simulated state with parameters $\theta$
- $\Sigma_x^{-1}$ is a weighting matrix for different state components
- $H_k$ is the prediction horizon length for segment $k$

Key innovations in the methodology include:

1. **Multi-horizon prediction**: Using trajectory segments with varying horizon lengths to capture both short-term and long-term dynamics
2. **Log-Cholesky decomposition**: Reparameterizing the pseudo-mass matrix to ensure positive-definiteness
3. **Actuator dynamics modeling**: Using a hyperbolic tangent function to model non-linearities in high-torque regimes
4. **CMA-ES optimization**: Employing a covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategy for parameter optimization

#### 3.2. Active Exploration Strategy

A key contribution of the SPI-Active framework is the active exploration strategy that identifies the most informative trajectories for parameter estimation.

Rather than collecting random trajectories, the framework uses the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) to quantify the information content of a trajectory with respect to the parameters being estimated:

$$F_I(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p(\tau|\theta)} \left[ \nabla_\theta \log p(\tau|\theta) \nabla_\theta \log p(\tau|\theta)^T \right]$$

The active exploration strategy optimizes command sequences for a pre-trained policy to maximize the FIM, ensuring that the collected data efficiently excites the relevant dynamics of the system.

The optimization problem for finding the most informative command sequence $c_{1:T}$ can be expressed as:

$$c_{1:T}^* = \arg\max_{c_{1:T}} F_I(\theta, c_{1:T})$$

The CMA-ES algorithm is used to solve this optimization problem efficiently, resulting in command sequences that generate trajectories with high information content.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SPI-Active framework was evaluated on both quadruped (Go1) and humanoid (H1) robots across various locomotion tasks, including forward jumping, yaw jumping, velocity tracking, and attitude tracking.

The results demonstrate significant improvements in both prediction accuracy and sim-to-real transfer performance:

1. **Improved Prediction Accuracy**:
 - SPI-Active achieved lower tracking errors in global root position and per-joint angles
 - The actively collected trajectories led to more accurate parameter estimates compared to random data collection

2. **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Performance**:
 - Policies fine-tuned with SPI-Active parameters showed performance improvements of 42-63% across locomotion tasks
 - The framework successfully generalized across different robot platforms and tasks

3. **Effectiveness of Active Exploration**:
 - The active exploration strategy resulted in more informative trajectories compared to random data collection
 - FIM-guided exploration led to parameter estimates that better captured the real-world dynamics

4. **Importance of Multi-Horizon Prediction**:
 - Using trajectory segments with varying horizon lengths improved the prediction accuracy
 - An ablation study showed that combining different horizon lengths outperformed fixed-horizon approaches

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SPI-Active framework represents a significant advancement in sim-to-real transfer for legged robots with several important implications:

1. **Improved Legged Robot Performance**: By enabling more accurate system identification, the framework allows legged robots to achieve higher performance on complex locomotion tasks.

2. **Reduced Manual Tuning**: The sampling-based approach eliminates the need for extensive manual parameter tuning, making it easier to deploy learning-based policies on real robots.

3. **Broader Applicability**: Unlike many existing methods, SPI-Active does not require differentiable physics or specialized sensing equipment, making it applicable to a wider range of robotic platforms.

4. **Data Efficiency**: The active exploration strategy significantly improves data efficiency, reducing the amount of real-world data needed for accurate parameter estimation.

The framework has potential applications in various domains, including:

- **Search and rescue operations**: Enabling legged robots to navigate challenging terrains with improved stability and precision
- **Industrial inspection**: Allowing robots to perform complex maneuvers in constrained environments
- **Personal assistance**: Enhancing the mobility capabilities of legged robots for home assistance applications
- **Research platforms**: Providing a more reliable foundation for further research in legged locomotion

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, the SPI-Active framework has several limitations that could be addressed in future work:

1. **Computational Complexity**: The sampling-based optimization approach is computationally intensive, which could limit its applicability in resource-constrained settings.

2. **Model Fidelity**: While the framework improves simulation accuracy, it is still limited by the underlying physics model in the simulator.

3. **Online Adaptation**: The current implementation performs system identification offline, which does not account for changes in robot dynamics during operation.

Future research directions could include:

- **Extending active trajectory excitation to humanoid systems**: Adapting the active exploration strategy for more complex humanoid robots with higher degrees of freedom
- **Enabling online or adaptive identification**: Developing methods for continuously updating parameter estimates during robot operation
- **Exploring more sample-efficient optimization algorithms**: Investigating alternative optimization approaches to reduce the computational requirements
- **Incorporating uncertainty estimation**: Quantifying the uncertainty in parameter estimates to inform robust policy learning

By addressing these limitations, the SPI-Active framework could further bridge the sim-to-real gap and enable more widespread deployment of learning-based policies on real-world legged robots.
