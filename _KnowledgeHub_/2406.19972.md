# HumanVLA: Towards Vision-Language Directed Object Rearrangement by Physical Humanoid

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces HumanVLA, a system enabling physical humanoids to perform general object rearrangement tasks directed by natural language and egocentric vision. It demonstrates the first Vision-Language-Action model for physical humanoids, achieving up to 60.2% success on unseen tasks by leveraging a teacher-student framework, active perception, and a new Human-in-the-Room (HITR) dataset.

## Problem

- Existing human-scene interaction (HSI) research often relies on pre-defined object dynamics or privileged information (ground-truth states), limiting real-world applicability for general object rearrangement.
- Applying Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to high-dimensional, physical humanoids for complex tasks directed by natural language and egocentric vision remained largely unexplored.
- The lack of suitable datasets for general object rearrangement tasks by physical humanoids hindered research in this emerging area.

## Method

- A teacher-student framework is employed, where a state-based teacher policy learns robust, generalizable object rearrangement using goal-conditioned RL with adversarial motion priors (AMP) and enhanced techniques like geometry encoding and a carry curriculum.
- The teacher's knowledge is distilled into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) student policy using online ==behavior cloning== (DAgger), which operates on egocentric vision and natural language instructions.
- Novel techniques like active rendering improve the student's visual perception by actively directing the humanoid's gaze towards target objects, and in-context path planning guides navigation in complex environments.

## Results

- The HumanVLA-Teacher achieved **79.3%** success and 19.3cm precision on unseen rearrangement tasks, outperforming a re-implemented InterPhys baseline (**59.5%** success, 52.5cm precision).
- The HumanVLA (student) model achieved **60.2%** success and 57.0cm precision on unseen tasks, demonstrating its ability to generalize to novel scene compositions, visual appearances, and language instructions.
- Ablation studies showed that geometry encoding, carry curriculum pre-training, in-context path planning, active rendering, and online learning (DAgger) were crucial for high performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General object rearrangement by humanoids benefits significantly from geometry-aware policies, carry curriculum pre-training for robust interaction, and in-context path planning for navigation.
> - Distilling a high-performance state-based teacher policy into a vision-language-action (VLA) student requires active perception techniques, such as active rendering, and robust online learning methods like DAgger to overcome covariate shift.
> - Physical humanoids can effectively interpret natural language commands and egocentric visual inputs to perform complex manipulation tasks, even with high-dimensional control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Human-Scene Interaction (HSI), physics-based motion synthesis, embodied AI, room rearrangement, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

- **Human-Scene Interaction (HSI):** Previous HSI research has seen success in generating expressive humanoid control and interaction with static scenes (e.g., sitting, lying) or specific objects (e.g., carrying a box, throwing a ball). However, these works are often limited to pre-defined object dynamics or require privileged information (ground-truth object/goal states), hindering generalization and real-world applicability. HumanVLA directly tackles this limitation by aiming for *general object rearrangement* with *diverse objects* and *varied dynamics*.
- **Motion Synthesis:** The field of motion synthesis is broadly divided into kinematic (visually plausible) and physics-based (physically plausible) methods. This work belongs to the latter, emphasizing physical realism and control within a simulator. It builds upon advancements in physics-based character control, particularly the Adversarial Motion Prior (AMP) framework, which enables learning natural state transitions from motion data. The paper extends AMP's capabilities to handle complex, generalized object interactions, which is a key challenge for physics-based methods.
- **Embodied AI and Room Rearrangement:** Research in embodied AI and room rearrangement has traditionally focused on simpler robot embodiments (e.g., wheeled robots with grippers) capable of navigation and manipulation of small objects on smooth terrain. These platforms often lack the bipedal locomotion and robust interaction capabilities of humanoids. HumanVLA pioneers complex rearrangement tasks with a *human-like embodiment*, leveraging bipedal locomotion and stronger interaction motors to manipulate larger objects like chairs and tables. This addresses a critical gap in embodied AI for more human-centric tasks.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** VLA models, which map visual and linguistic inputs to actions, have shown impressive results in embodied AI and robotics, particularly for desktop manipulation tasks with simpler grippers. However, their application to high-dimensional, complex humanoids remains largely unexplored. HumanVLA breaks new ground by developing the *first VLA model for physical humanoids* to perform tasks directed by egocentric vision and natural language, pushing towards a more natural and intuitive human-robot interface.

In essence, HumanVLA aims to bridge the gap between sophisticated human-like motor control and high-level, human-understandable instructions. It moves beyond specific, pre-programmed interactions or reliance on ideal sensor data, working towards a more generalized and practically deployable system for humanoids to assist in everyday environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind HumanVLA are:

1. **Enable General Object Rearrangement by Physical Humanoids:** The primary goal is to empower physical humanoids to perform a wide variety of object rearrangement tasks in complex indoor environments. This involves interacting with diverse objects of varying geometries, poses, and weights, moving them across a room, and achieving desired goal states, going beyond the limitations of existing HSI techniques focused on specific objects or static interactions.
2. **Move Beyond Privileged Information for Humanoid Control:** Existing advanced HSI methods often rely on privileged information such as ground-truth object and goal states, which are unavailable in real-world deployments without specialized external localization devices. A key motivation is to shift towards more practical, easily deployable perception methods.
3. **Develop Vision-Language Directed Humanoid Control:** To achieve practical deployability, the authors aim to direct humanoid controls using natural language instructions and egocentric vision. This aligns with the long-term vision of truly intelligent humanoids that can understand human commands, perceive their surroundings like humans do, and execute tasks autonomously.
4. **Create a Benchmark Dataset for Humanoid Rearrangement:** Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets for general object rearrangement tasks by physical humanoids, the authors aim to introduce a novel dataset, Human-in-the-Room (HITR), to facilitate research in this emerging area.

These objectives are driven by the desire to develop more comprehensive and practical HSI applications, leading to more capable and versatile humanoid robots that can genuinely assist humans in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HumanVLA employs a teacher-student framework, splitting the learning process into two phases to address the complexity of learning high-dimensional vision-language-action policies for physical humanoids.

**Phase 1: State-based Teacher Policy Learning (HumanVLA-Teacher)**
A state-based teacher policy ($\pi_{tch}$) is trained using goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) combined with Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP) within a Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) paradigm.
- **Goal-conditioned RL + AMP:** The teacher policy receives oracle scene states (precise object pose, geometry, navigation waypoints, goal coordinates). It is trained to maximize a reward function that combines a goal-conditioned task reward ($r_G$) for task completion and a style reward ($r_S$) derived from an adversarial discriminator (D) to encourage realistic, human-like motions.
- **Improved Techniques for General Manipulation:** To overcome limitations of vanilla AMP and facilitate generalized object rearrangement, several key insights are introduced:
 - **Geometry Encoding:** Object geometry is encoded using Basis Point Set (BPS) representations, augmented with position, rotation, and velocities, allowing the policy to be "geometry-aware" and generalize to diverse objects.
 - **Carry Curriculum Pre-training:** A simpler "carry curriculum" is designed to pre-train the policy on the initial steps of locomotion and robust object contact/lifting. This shortens the learning horizon and provides a strong prior for subsequent object relocation, particularly for objects that need to be lifted rather than pushed.
 - **Style Reward Clipping:** To prevent the policy from getting stuck imitating insignificant motions when task progress is difficult, a style reward clipping mechanism is proposed. It prioritizes the task reward by setting an upper bound for the style reward, ensuring the agent focuses on high-level task execution.
 - **In-context Path Planning:** For navigation in complex, cluttered rooms, an A\* algorithm is used on a 2D obstacle map derived from scene point clouds. This provides navigation waypoints to guide the humanoid, avoiding collisions and enabling efficient locomotion.

**Phase 2: Distilling into Vision-Language-Action Model (HumanVLA-Student)**
The knowledge from the HumanVLA-Teacher is distilled into a Vision-Language-Action (HumanVLA) student network ($\pi_{vla}$) that operates with practical egocentric vision and natural language instructions.
- **Behavior Cloning (DAgger):** The student policy is trained using behavior cloning, mimicking the teacher's actions. To mitigate the covariate shift problem common in offline behavior cloning, the authors employ the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) framework, an online learning approach that iteratively collects data under the student policy and augments the training dataset.
- **Active Rendering:** A novel active rendering technique is proposed to improve the perception quality of the egocentric camera. It analytically calculates the head-to-object direction and uses inverse kinematics to derive an "active rendering action" for the neck joint. This action is mixed with the teacher's neck action to encourage the camera to focus on the target object, providing more informative visual observations for the student policy.
- **Network Architecture:** HumanVLA uses an EfficientNet-B0 for image encoding and a frozen bert-base-uncased for language encoding. These features, along with proprioception and the last action, are fed into an MLP action decoder.

**Human-in-the-Room (HITR) Dataset:**
A new dataset is created to support HumanVLA.
- **Procedural Generation:** HITR includes four room layouts (bedroom, living room, kitchen, warehouse) populated with replaceable objects from HSSD assets. Tasks are generated by randomly relocating an object, defining initial and goal states.
- **LLM-generated Instructions:** GPT-4-vision is used to generate natural language instructions by analyzing concatenated images of initial and goal scenes, with manual review for quality control.
- **Statistics:** The dataset comprises 615 tasks, 50 static objects, and 34 movable objects (sizes 21-126cm, weights 5-20kg), specifically designed for humanoid interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in IsaacGym using the HITR dataset, demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of HumanVLA.

- **Box Loco-Manipulation Comparison:**
 - HumanVLA-Teacher achieved a 98.1% success rate with 4.2cm precision and 4.6s execution time in box rearrangement tasks, significantly outperforming a re-implemented InterPhys baseline (97.8% success, 12.6cm precision, 5.3s execution time) and even the officially reported InterPhys results. This established the teacher's strong foundation.

- **Ablation Study (on training split):**
 - **HumanVLA-Teacher:** The full teacher policy achieved 85.9% success and 14.4cm precision.
 - **Geometry Encoding:** Essential for object generalization; its removal caused a ~20% drop in success rate (to 64.5%) and significantly worse precision (43.4cm).
 - **Carry Curriculum Pre-training:** Also crucial for object manipulation; without it, success dropped ~20% (to 66.3%) with very poor precision (73.4cm).
 - **Style Reward Clipping:** Improves task execution prioritization, resulting in a 6% success rate decrease (to 79.9%) without it.
 - **In-context Path Planning:** Critical for navigation in complex scenes; its removal led to an 18.5% drop in success (to 67.8%).
 - **HumanVLA (Student):** The full student policy achieved 74.8% success and 42.6cm precision.
 - **Active Rendering:** Highly important for student perception; its absence led to a 6.9% drop in success (to 67.9%).
 - **Online Learning (DAgger):** Crucial for overcoming covariate shift; an offline behavior cloning baseline performed extremely poorly (15.3% success, 145.0cm precision).

- **Generalizing to Unseen Tasks (on test split):**
 - **HumanVLA-Teacher:** Showed strong generalizability with only minor drops in performance (79.3% success, 19.3cm precision), outperforming InterPhys (59.5% success, 52.5cm precision).
 - **HumanVLA (Student):** Maintained a respectable 60.2% success rate and 57.0cm precision on unseen tasks, significantly outperforming baselines without active rendering (56.7% success) and offline GC-BC (10.2% success). This demonstrates its ability to generalize to novel scene compositions, visual appearances, and language instructions.
 - **Unseen data analysis (additional):** The method generalized well to unseen texts and visual appearance of objects, but struggled more with entirely unseen object categories (different geometry) and novel scene layouts, indicating areas for future improvement in extreme generalization.

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations demonstrated HumanVLA's capability to move various objects (pot, vase, chair, box) in response to natural language instructions, with clear progression through locomotion, interaction, and relocation phases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanVLA represents a significant step forward in the field of embodied AI and humanoid robotics, with several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering General Object Rearrangement for Physical Humanoids:** This work addresses a long-standing challenge by enabling complex, general object manipulation by humanoids in cluttered environments. This capability is foundational for practical humanoid applications in homes, offices, and industrial settings.
2. **Bridging Perception and Control for Humanoids:** By developing the first vision-language-action model for physical humanoids, HumanVLA demonstrates a viable pathway to intuitive, human-like instruction following without relying on privileged internal states. This moves humanoid control closer to real-world deployability.
3. **Introduction of the HITR Dataset:** The creation of the Human-in-the-Room (HITR) dataset is a crucial contribution. It provides a much-needed benchmark for future research in humanoid-centric object rearrangement, fostering further development in generalized manipulation and vision-language understanding for complex physical agents.
4. **Novel Technical Contributions:** The proposed techniques—geometry encoding, carry curriculum pre-training, style reward clipping, in-context path planning, and active rendering—are innovative solutions to specific challenges in learning robust, generalizable humanoid policies from complex data and distilling them into VLA models. Active rendering, in particular, highlights the importance of active perception for effective VLA learning.
5. **Implications for Real-World Humanoid Robots:** The ability of HumanVLA to perform tasks directed by natural language and egocentric vision makes it highly relevant for future humanoid robots designed to assist humans. Such robots could understand commands like "Clean the room" or "Place the laptop on the desk" and execute them physically, leading to safer, more efficient, and more versatile human-robot collaboration.
6. **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This research pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by moving beyond simpler embodiments to tackle the complexities of human-like physical interaction and locomotion in response to high-level cognitive inputs.

While the current system has limitations (e.g., spherical hands, single-object tasks), it lays a robust foundation for future work, including dexterous manipulation, long-horizon multi-object interaction, and integrated planning and memory modules. The ethical consideration of safety in real-world humanoid deployment is also acknowledged, underscoring the broader societal impact of this line of research.
