# SimuScene: Simulation-Ready Compositional 3D Scene Reconstruction from a Single Image

> [!summary] Summary
> SimuScene reconstructs simulation-ready compositional 3D scenes from one RGB image by putting a physics engine inside the reconstruction loop: gravity-induced displacement and penetration measured in sequential per-object simulation become diagnostic signals driving gravity-axis stretching or DPO-fine-tuned amodal shape resampling. It reaches state-of-the-art post-simulation alignment and stability, for example 0.507 versus 0.218 strict ABO and 2.5% penetration on GenWild.

## Problem

- Single-image lifters recover plausible per-object shapes, but composed scenes collapse under simulation: objects sink through supports, hover in mid-air, or interpenetrate neighbors.
- Existing physics-aware pipelines apply constraints only post-hoc at the layout level, adjusting where objects sit while leaving erroneous underlying object geometry uncorrected.
- A monocular view is fundamentally ambiguous about occluded geometry and absolute depth, and no available dataset readily teaches the physical plausibility that would resolve it.

## Method

- ==Sequential diagnostic simulation== releases one object at a time under gravity with previously placed objects frozen as static colliders, halting at first contact to yield clean penetration and displacement signals.
- Two-tier physics-informed shape correction: normalized gravity-axis displacement below 0.15 triggers ==OBB-axis stretching==, while larger displacement triggers ==OBB-guided amodal resampling== from a physics-expanded crop mask.
- ==Flow-matching DPO== fine-tuning of the ==SAM3D== shape branch via ==LoRA== adapters on 16.6K synthetic latent preference pairs contrasting occlusion-failed against completed amodal shapes.

## Results

- On GenWild, strict ABO rises to **0.507** versus **0.218** for SAM3D, mean displacement drops to **0.128**, and penetrated-object ratio falls from **16.2%** to **2.5%**.
- Gains hold cross-dataset: GraspClutter6D strict ABO **0.248** versus **0.087** with **10.3%** penetration, and AriaDigitalTwin 3D strict ABO **0.293** versus **0.051** at **8.8%** penetration.
- Ablation lifts ABO from **0.263** to **0.409** with alignment, **0.440** with physics, **0.532** with resampling; VLM pairwise judging scores resampled meshes **1083** Elo, **71.9%** win rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simulator can serve as a measurement instrument, not merely a validator: physical violations invert into quantitative geometric error signals that supervise reconstruction itself.
> - Sequential freeze-at-first-contact processing keeps diagnostic signals clean; letting all objects settle jointly yields chaotic dynamics that destroy the link between violation and cause.
> - Greedy per-object ordering cannot revise early estimates using later evidence, so early mistakes persist; joint scene-level optimization remains the open direction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The reconstruction of 3D scenes from 2D images is a well-established area within computer vision and graphics, with particular relevance to robotics and embodied AI. Current research approaches generally fall into two categories: reconstructing an entire scene as a single, inseparable mesh, or lifting individual objects independently to form a compositional scene. While methods in both categories have advanced in generating visually plausible 3D representations, a common limitation has been the lack of physical plausibility and simulation-readiness of the reconstructed scenes.

Existing single-image scene reconstructors, such as those relying on unified mesh generation or individual object lifting like SAM3D, often produce scenes where objects interpenetrate, hover, or sink when subjected to physical simulation. These inaccuracies arise from fundamental ambiguities in single-image input regarding occluded geometry and absolute depth, and the absence of physical plausibility considerations in many existing datasets. Some recent efforts have begun to integrate physics into the reconstruction process. However, these often treat physical consistency as a post-hoc layout correction, adjusting object positions without rectifying underlying geometric inaccuracies. This approach is limited because incorrect object shapes can lead to persistent physical instabilities despite layout adjustments.

SimuScene addresses this gap by integrating physics simulation directly into the generative process for both shape and layout estimation, rather than treating it as a final validation step. It proposes a feedback loop where physical violations diagnosed by the simulator drive geometric corrections. This positions SimuScene as an advancement in 3D scene reconstruction by moving beyond merely visually plausible outputs to generate scenes that are functionally stable and interactive within physics environments, thereby enabling direct deployment in downstream applications like robotic manipulation and virtual reality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reconstruct physically plausible, simulation-ready compositional 3D scenes from a single RGB image. This output is intended to be directly compatible with physics simulators, facilitating downstream tasks such as reinforcement learning and imitation learning for robot manipulation, as well as interactive VR/AR setups that require objects to respond accurately to contact and gravity.

The motivation behind SimuScene stems from critical bottlenecks observed in existing single-image 3D reconstruction methods. While these methods can generate visually convincing per-object shapes, their compositional outputs frequently exhibit physical instabilities. Objects may interpenetrate, float above surfaces, or sink into their supports. These issues, identified as interpenetrating, hovering, or sinking objects, lead to scenes that collapse under physical simulation. These instabilities are attributed to two main challenges:
1. **Monocular Ambiguity and Occlusion:** A single image inherently provides insufficient information to fully resolve occluded geometry and determine absolute depth, leading to errors in reconstructed object shapes and poses.
2. **Lack of Physical Plausibility in Datasets:** Existing datasets used for 3D reconstruction often do not explicitly encode physical constraints, making it difficult for models to learn physically consistent scene generation.

Current physics-aware methods attempt to resolve these issues by performing post-hoc layout corrections. However, this approach is insufficient because it adjusts object positions without correcting fundamental geometric errors in the objects themselves. When an object's reconstructed shape is inherently inaccurate, merely adjusting its pose cannot achieve true physical stability.

The authors identify an opportunity in this limitation: the physics simulation that reveals these artifacts can also quantify them. For instance, the distance an object falls or the depth of its interpenetration provides diagnostic signals indicating geometric error. Instead of merely cleaning up these artifacts, the research is motivated to leverage these diagnostic measurements to guide shape and scale correction during the reconstruction process itself. This "physics in the loop" approach aims to mitigate accumulated reconstruction errors and produce stable, simulation-ready 3D scenes suitable for embodied AI and interactive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimuScene operates as a compositional 3D reconstruction pipeline that integrates physics simulation directly into the shape and layout estimation process. The methodology can be broken down into several stages:

#### 3.1. Decomposed Scene Initialization

The pipeline begins by segmenting and reconstructing base structures (e.g., tables, shelves) separately from other objects. An MLLM-based image editor first removes non-base objects from the input image, producing a decluttered image ($I_{base}$). SAM3D is then applied to $I_{base}$ to reconstruct these fixed base colliders. For the remaining objects, RAM++ and a VLM generate instance labels, SAM3 produces segmentation masks ($M_i$), and SAM3D lifts each instance into an initial 3D canonical mesh ($M^{init}_i$), scale ($s^{init}_i$), translation ($t^{init}_i$), and rotation ($R^{init}_i$). Monocular depth ($D$) is obtained using MoGe. A VLM is also used to assign pose-DoF labels (free, point-anchored, or line-anchored) to each object, informing the simulator about appropriate constraints.

#### 3.2. Pose Refinement Before Simulation

Before physics simulation, object poses are refined against image evidence. Depth ($D$) and camera intrinsics ($K$) from MoGe are used to back-project pixels within each segmentation mask ($M_i$) to form an object point cloud ($P_i$). An initial alignment is performed to solve for isotropic scale ($s^*_i$) and translation ($t^*_i$) by aligning the canonical mesh ($M^{init}_i$) with $P_i$. Rotation is not robustly recoverable from point-cloud alignment alone, so FoundationPose is subsequently used to refine the translation ($t^{pre}_i$) and rotation ($R^{pre}_i$) for each object, taking the scaled mesh and previous alignments as initializations. This stage aims to produce an image-faithful configuration ($S_{pre}$) prior to physical validation.

#### 3.3. Diagnostic Simulation: Physics as a Probe

The pre-simulation scene ($S_{pre}$) is generally not physically valid. SimuScene uses a physics engine as a diagnostic tool to identify and measure residual errors. Objects are processed sequentially, ordered by the lowest point of their world-frame bounding box along the gravity direction. For each active object, previously placed objects (including base structures) are frozen as static colliders, and the simulation halts at the first frame of contact to maintain a clean diagnostic signal.

- **Penetration Resolution:** For each active object $i$, the simulator calculates a displacement ($\Delta t^{pen}_i$) to resolve interpenetration with already placed objects.
- **Gravity-Based Diagnostics:** After penetration resolution, object $i$ is released under gravity. If the object is "free" (6-DoF), it is frozen at the first contact, yielding a gravity-induced displacement ($\Delta t^{grv}_i$). For "wall-anchored" objects (point-anchored or line-anchored), constrained simulation settles them, yielding a gravity-induced rotation ($\Delta R^{grv}_i$) and displacement.
- **Displacement-Based Shape Correction Criterion:** The physics-induced translation is used as a diagnostic signal. The object's Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) in $S_{pre}$ ($B^{pre}_i$) is computed. A normalized gravity-axis displacement ($\rho_i$) is calculated based on the total displacement and the OBB's length along the gravity axis. A threshold of $\rho_i \ge 0.15$ indicates a significant shape-sampling failure, triggering amodal shape resampling. If $\rho_i < 0.15$, the error is deemed minor, prompting gravity-axis stretching.

#### 3.4. Physics-Informed Shape Correction

Based on the diagnostic signal, one of two correction mechanisms is applied:

- **Gravity-axis Stretch:** If $\rho_i < 0.15$, the canonical mesh is anisotropically stretched along the gravity-aligned OBB axis by a factor derived from the signed displacement ($\eta_i$). The stretched mesh is then renormalized, and the object's scale and translation are updated to fix the OBB face opposite to gravity.
- **Amodal Shape Resampling:** If $\rho_i \ge 0.15$, indicating a significant shape error often due to severe occlusion, the shape is resampled. A desired OBB ($B^{str}_i$) is computed by stretching $B^{pre}_i$ along the gravity axis by the simulated displacement. An auxiliary crop mask ($M'_i$) is constructed by augmenting the modal segmentation mask ($M_i$) with the projected lowest part of $B^{str}_i$. SAM3D is rerun with this physics-informed crop, and the resampled mesh ($M^{re}_i$), scale ($s^{re}_i$), and rotation ($R^{re}_i$) are used, with the translation ($t^{re}_i$) set to the center of $B^{str}_i$.
 - **SAM3D Fine-tuning:** SAM3D's shape branch is fine-tuned using a Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) style objective with flow-matching loss (FM-DPO). This training leverages synthetic preference pairs: occlusion-completion pairs (contrasting an occluded shape latent with an edited full-object completion latent) and clean preservation pairs (regularizing original modal-mask reconstruction). Only LoRA adapters within SAM3D's attention projections are trained to preserve the base model's capabilities.

#### 3.5. Final Composition

After individual object correction, the final simulation-ready scene is composed. Wall-anchored objects retain their initial geometry and diagnostic-simulated constrained pose. Free objects are replaced by their stretched or resampled states. A final sequential penetration resolution is run to address any overlaps introduced by shape correction. Unlike the diagnostic phase, the final simulation allows all free objects to be simultaneously dynamic (6-DoF rigid bodies) and settle jointly under gravity for a fixed duration, while anchored objects follow their rotational constraints. The resulting stable configuration defines the final simulation-ready compositional 3D scene.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SimuScene pipeline was evaluated extensively on diverse datasets and metrics, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating physically stable and geometrically aligned 3D scenes.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

SimuScene demonstrated superior performance across all evaluated datasets: GraspClutter6D, Aria Digital Twin (ADT), and GenWild.
- **Physical Stability and Alignment:** The method achieved the highest Average Best Overlap (ABO$_{fh/fo}$) and the lowest penetration ratios compared to baselines (SAM3D, Gen3DSR, 3D-RE-GEN). ABO$_{fh/fo}$ is a strict metric that explicitly zeroes out scores for invalid instances (e.g., interpenetrating objects). SimuScene yielded ABO$_{fh/fo}$ scores of 0.248 (GraspClutter6D), 0.293 (AriaDigitalTwin), and 0.507 (GenWild), significantly outperforming SAM3D (0.087, 0.051, 0.218 respectively).
- **Reduced Penetration:** SimuScene achieved notably lower object penetration ratios across all datasets: 10.3% (GraspClutter6D), 8.8% (AriaDigitalTwin), and 2.5% (GenWild). This compares favorably to SAM3D's 24.5%, 16.4%, and 16.2% respectively.
- **Improved Dynamics:** The method showed lower Mean Displacement (D$_{mean}$) and Peak Energy (E$_{pk}$) after simulation, indicating fewer residual settling errors and dynamic artifacts. For instance, on GenWild, SimuScene achieved D$_{mean}$ of 0.128 and E$_{pk}$ of 0.420, compared to SAM3D's 0.555 and 2.957.
- **Robustness to Backgrounds:** Baselines were evaluated with their native backgrounds and also with SimuScene's reconstructed base meshes ("our bg"). Even with improved backgrounds, baselines often showed significant instability (e.g., Gen3DSR's low ABO and high penetration, or 3D-RE-GEN's high energy errors), highlighting the limitations of their core object reconstruction and composition.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Performance

Visual comparisons reinforced the quantitative findings.
- **Physical Plausibility and Alignment:** SimuScene's reconstructions maintained precise, static alignment with the input image while also being physically stable. Objects settled correctly on surfaces without interpenetration or hovering.
- **Occlusion Handling:** The method accurately anchored even heavily occluded items, reconstructing complete 3D geometry that aligned with their expected hidden extent.
- **Baseline Limitations:** Baselines frequently resulted in physically unstable scenes. Objects often appeared fused, scattering due to severe collisions during simulation, or exhibiting incomplete geometry.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the GenWild dataset progressively demonstrated the contribution of each pipeline component:
- **Pose Alignment (+Align.):** This stage provided the largest initial improvement in visual alignment and resolved deep interpenetrations, increasing ABO from 0.263 (SAM3D) to 0.409.
- **Penetration Resolution + Gravity Simulation (+Pen.+Grv.):** Stabilized dynamics further (ABO 0.440), but underlying geometric inaccuracies persisted.
- **Physics-Informed Shape Correction (+Re. (Full)):** The full model, incorporating shape correction, achieved the highest alignment scores (ABO 0.532) and lowest displacement error, indicating that replacing flawed meshes via amodal resampling effectively resolved remaining distortions.
- **Mesh Completion Quality:** A VLM-based pairwise comparison evaluated the plausibility of occluded geometries after resampling. SimuScene's amodal resampling achieved a substantially higher Elo score (1083.4) and win rate (71.9%) compared to raw SAM3D (931.5 Elo, 41.3% win rate) and simple stretching (908.9 Elo, 36.8% win rate), confirming its ability to recover complete and visually plausible 3D shapes.

#### 4.4. Applications

The physically stable 3D reconstructions from SimuScene were demonstrated to unlock direct utility in downstream robotics and interactive applications:
- **Physics-Based Character Control:** Reconstructed scenes were loaded into a physics simulator to train humanoid control policies for dexterous human-object interaction. The stability of the assets allowed for the generation of physics-based character motion in novel environments.
- **Robot-arm Manipulation:** SimuScene's outputs were used as input for a closed-loop VLM agent for text-guided 6D pose prediction. The object-complete and physically grounded reconstructions provided stable grasp targets and enabled collision-free trajectories for robotic arm manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SimuScene represents a notable advancement in single-image 3D scene reconstruction by redefining the role of physical simulation. Instead of merely validating reconstructed scenes, the work reframes physical simulation as an active, in-the-loop diagnostic signal that inverts physical violations into actionable geometric corrections. This addresses a core challenge of monocular perception: resolving structural ambiguities and ensuring physical plausibility when depth and occluded geometry are underspecified.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to produce genuinely "simulation-ready" compositional 3D scenes. This output directly addresses a critical bottleneck for robotic manipulation, where the absence of physically accurate and interactive virtual environments has historically hindered the development and training of intelligent agents. By generating scenes that can be immediately dropped into any physics simulator as a collection of separately addressable rigid bodies with consistent contact geometry, SimuScene eliminates the need for manual scene authoring, which is typically a time-consuming and labor-intensive process.

The potential impact of SimuScene is multifaceted:
- **Robotics and Embodied AI:** It provides a foundation for accelerated training of robot manipulation policies, humanoid control, and other embodied AI tasks in diverse, realistic virtual environments derived directly from real-world images. This can significantly reduce the data collection requirements and increase the realism of simulation-to-real transfer.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality (VR/AR):** The ability to reconstruct interactive 3D scenes from single images can enhance the development of VR/AR applications, allowing users to interact with virtual objects in a physically consistent manner within environments derived from photographs.
- **Content Creation:** It offers a pathway for automating aspects of 3D content generation for simulations and games, converting 2D images into interactive 3D assets more efficiently.
- **Future Research:** The sequential, per-object protocol that integrates physical dynamics as a generative supervisory signal provides a scalable foundation for future research. The authors suggest joint scene-level optimization as a natural next step, which could further refine the global consistency and interaction dynamics within reconstructed scenes.

In summary, SimuScene contributes a novel paradigm for 3D scene reconstruction that emphasizes physical realism and functional utility over mere visual fidelity, holding implications for practical applications in robotics, virtual environments, and the broader field of generative AI.
