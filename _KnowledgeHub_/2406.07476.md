# VideoLLaMA 2: Advancing Spatial-Temporal Modeling and Audio Understanding in Video-LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Developed by DAMO Academy, Alibaba Group, VideoLLaMA 2 is a new family of Video Large Language Models that enhances spatial-temporal modeling and audio understanding. It achieves competitive performance across various video and audio benchmarks, such as outperforming LLaVA-NeXT-Video on EgoSchema by 7.8 percentage points and Qwen-Audio on Clotho-AQA by 12.21 percentage points with substantially less training data.

## Problem

- Current Video-LLMs struggle to effectively process temporal dynamics due to limitations in fusing features across different frames.
- Many existing Video-LLMs frequently overlook the crucial contextual cues provided by synchronous audio streams.
- This leads to incomplete multimodal understanding and hinders accurate interpretation of complex video scenarios and prediction of future states from video data for AI models and LLMs alike, limiting general applicability and efficiency overall (missing temporal-context and audio cues).

## Method

- Developed a ==dual-branch framework==, **VideoLLaMA 2**, independently processing visual and auditory inputs before integrating them within an ==instruction-finetuned LLM== backbone, supporting multiple LLM backbones (Mistral, Mixtral, Qwen2-Instruct) and vision encoders (CLIP ViT-L/14, SigLIP SO400M-384).
- Implemented a novel ==Spatial-Temporal Convolution (STC) Connector== for the visual branch, designed to preserve spatial-temporal order, reduce token count, and alleviate information loss during feature extraction and for better computational efficiency.
- Integrated a dedicated ==Audio-Language Branch== with a ==BEATs encoder== and ==MLP projector==, trained via comprehensive ==multi-stage training== (video-language, audio-language, and a critical ==audio-video joint training== phase) to progressively build multimodal capability.

## Results

- VideoLLaMA 2-7B achieved an accuracy of **51.7%** on the EgoSchema benchmark for multi-choice video question answering, outperforming the previous open-source state-of-the-art LLaVA-NeXT-Video (**43.9%**).
- For audio-only QA (AQA), VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) achieved **70.11%** on Clotho-AQA, surpassing Qwen-Audio (**57.90%**) despite using significantly less training data.
- In joint audio-video QA (OE-AVQA), VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) achieved **79.2%** on MUSIC-QA and **70.9%** on VGGSound; VideoLLaMA 2-7B outperformed GPT4-V (**43.7%→53.4%**) on MV-Bench, improved Perception-Test to **51.4%** from **38.3%** prior SOTA, and scored **2.57** correctness / **2.61** detailedness on MSVC video captioning (GPT-assisted eval).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Designing a Spatial-Temporal Convolution (STC) Connector tailored for video, which maintains spatial-temporal order and efficiently compresses tokens, is more effective than simpler visual feature aggregation methods.
> - Integrating and jointly training a dedicated Audio Branch allows for robust audio understanding, significantly improving multimodal comprehension even with comparatively less audio-specific training data.
> - A multi-stage training methodology that progressively builds multimodal capabilities, including separate and joint fine-tuning phases for video, audio, and audio-visual data, leads to competitive performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed remarkable progress in recent years, particularly in Large Language Models (LLMs) which have transformed various industries. Within this landscape, models focused on static image understanding (Image-LLMs) have achieved near-human capabilities, leading to breakthroughs in diverse applications from medical imaging to autonomous driving. However, the domain of video understanding and generation remains comparatively nascent. Videos present a significantly more complex challenge than static images due to the inherent temporal dynamics and the integration of synchronous audio streams, which profoundly enrich the information content but also introduce substantial computational and interpretative complexities.

Current Video-LLMs, while making strides, are often constrained by two primary limitations: their struggle to effectively process temporal dynamics due to limited capabilities in fusing features across different frames, and their frequent oversight of the crucial contextual cues provided by audio streams. This leads to an incomplete multimodal understanding and hinders their ability to accurately interpret complex scenarios or predict future states.

VideoLLaMA 2 is positioned as a direct response to these limitations. Building upon its predecessor, VideoLLaMA (Zhang et al., 2023), this work aims to significantly enhance spatial-temporal modeling and audio understanding within Video-LLMs. It seeks to bridge the performance gap between mature Image-LLMs and the less developed Video-LLMs by introducing architectural innovations tailored for video's unique characteristics. Specifically, it distinguishes itself from many existing Video-LLMs that might rely on simpler linear projections or Q-formers for visual feature aggregation by proposing a more sophisticated Spatial-Temporal Convolution (STC) Connector. This architectural choice is a deliberate attempt to move beyond merely adapting image-centric approaches to video and instead, design components that inherently capture the sequential and dynamic nature of video data. Furthermore, the explicit integration and joint training of an Audio Branch represent a crucial step towards holistic multimodal understanding, moving beyond visual-only analyses that miss vital auditory cues.

By releasing their models publicly, the authors contribute to the open-source community, facilitating further research and development in this challenging yet critical area of AI. This work aligns with the broader trend of developing generalist AI models capable of handling diverse multimodal inputs and tasks, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can comprehend and articulate beyond pure text.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **present VideoLLaMA 2**, a new family of Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs) specifically designed to **enhance spatial-temporal modeling and audio understanding** in tasks involving video and audio. This overarching goal can be broken down into several specific objectives:

- **Improve Spatial-Temporal Modeling:** To develop a novel mechanism capable of effectively capturing the intricate spatial and temporal dynamics present in video data. This involves moving beyond simple frame-by-frame processing or inefficient token aggregation methods.
- **Integrate Robust Audio Understanding:** To seamlessly incorporate audio cues into the model's multimodal understanding capabilities through the introduction and joint training of a dedicated Audio Branch. This aims to overcome the common oversight of audio as a rich source of contextual information in many existing Video-LLMs.
- **Achieve Competitive Performance:** To demonstrate that VideoLLaMA 2 can achieve competitive results across a range of video and audio-oriented benchmarks, including multiple-choice video question answering (MC-VQA), open-ended video question answering (OE-VQA), video captioning (VC), audio-only question answering (AQA), and audio-video question answering (OE-AVQA). The aim is to outperform existing open-source models and approach the performance of proprietary, state-of-the-art systems.
- **Promote Further Research:** To release all developed models publicly to foster and facilitate future research and development in the field of multimodal LLMs.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several critical observations regarding the current state of multimodal AI:

- **Gap in Video Understanding:** Despite significant advancements in AI, particularly in image recognition and generation, video understanding lags due to its inherent complexities (temporal dynamics, audio streams). Current Video-LLMs struggle to fully capitalize on available temporal information and integrate audio effectively.
- **Limitations of Existing Video-LLMs:** Many contemporary Video-LLMs fail to adequately fuse features across frames, leading to a suboptimal understanding of temporal patterns. The neglect of audio streams, which are vital for comprehensive scene understanding, further limits their effectiveness.
- **Need for Efficiency and Contextual Integrity:** There is a clear demand for more advanced Video-LLMs that can handle the complexities of multimodal video data without compromising processing efficiency or the integrity of contextual information. Models need to not only comprehend but also articulate the rich narratives embedded in video content.
- **Iterative Improvement:** As a successor to VideoLLaMA, this work is motivated by the desire to refine and build upon previous foundations, addressing identified weaknesses and pushing the state-of-the-art further.

By addressing these motivations, VideoLLaMA 2 aims to set a new standard for intelligent video understanding systems, demonstrating a profound comprehension of video content by effectively processing the interplay of visual and auditory signals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VideoLLaMA 2 adopts a sophisticated dual-branch framework, inspired by its predecessor, designed to independently process visual and auditory inputs before integrating them within a powerful language model. This modularity ensures streamlined training and flexibility for future expansions.

#### 3.1. Architecture

The core architecture consists of a Vision-Language Branch and an Audio-Language Branch, both connecting to an instruction-finetuned Large Language Model (LLM) backbone. Critically, cross-modal interactions are primarily handled by the highly capable LLM itself, rather than by early fusion mechanisms. The LLM backbones chosen for VideoLLaMA 2 variants include Mistral-Instruct, Mixtral-Instruct, and Qwen2-Instruct, reflecting a commitment to leveraging powerful, open-source language models.

- **Vision-Language Branch:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** The model uses pre-trained image-level CLIP (ViT-L/14) or SigLIP (SO400M-384) as its vision backbone. The choice of an image encoder allows for flexible frame sampling strategies and feature aggregation.
 - **Frame Processing:** A fixed number of frames are consistently sampled from each video, padded, and resized to 336x336 pixels before being fed into the image encoder.
 - **Spatial-Temporal Convolution (STC) Connector:** This is a key innovation of VideoLLaMA 2, replacing the Q-former used in VideoLLaMA 1. The STC Connector is designed based on three principles:
 1. **Maintaining Spatial-Temporal Order:** Avoids resampling operations (like those in Q-formers) to preserve the inherent order of visual tokens, crucial for autoregressive LLMs.
 2. **Reducing Token Count:** Incorporates 3D downsampling operators to compress spatial-temporal tokens, improving computational efficiency for the LLM.
 3. **Alleviating Information Loss:** Employs RegStage (a robust convolution block) before and after the 3D downsampling to preserve local visual patterns during compression.
 - An empirical study validated the effectiveness of using 3D convolution with RegStage blocks, showing superior average performance across video QA benchmarks compared to 2D alternatives or 3D pooling.

- **Audio-Language Branch:**
 - **Audio Preprocessing:** Raw audio signals are transformed into 128-bin fbank spectrograms.
 - **Audio Encoder:** BEATs (a state-of-the-art audio encoder) is used to extract detailed audio features and temporal dynamics, ensuring alignment with the STC Connector's temporal focus.
 - **Audio Projector:** A Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) block with two linear layers aligns the BEATs features with the LLM's hidden dimension.

#### 3.2. Training Methodology

VideoLLaMA 2 undergoes a comprehensive multi-stage training process, progressively building multimodal capabilities.

- **Video-Language Training (Two Stages):**
 1. **Pre-training:** Uses a large-scale, weakly labeled dataset of web-crawled image-text and video-text pairs (e.g., Panda-70M, WebVid-10M, InternVid-10M, CC-3M). During this stage, the visual encoder and LLM are frozen, with only the STC Connector being optimized to minimize cross-entropy loss of text tokens.
 2. **Multi-task Fine-tuning:** Incorporates high-quality, fine-grained multimodal annotations from diverse datasets covering video captioning (VideoChat), video classification (Kinetics-710), VQA (NExTQA, EgoQA), and instruction following (Valley, VideoChatGPT). It also mixes image captioning (ShareGPT4V) and image VQA/instruction data (LLaVA) to maintain static visual understanding, and pure text data (Magpie, ALLaVA) to enhance instruction following. Here, the visual encoder is frozen, while the LLM and STC Connector are optimized.

- **Audio-Language Training (Two Stages):**
 1. **Pre-training:** Leverages WavCaps (approx. 400,000 samples) for audio captioning. The audio encoder and LLM are frozen, focusing optimization exclusively on the audio projector to align audio data with textual representations.
 2. **Multi-task Fine-tuning:** Employs a variety of datasets for different audio processing tasks, including audio QA (ClothoAQA), audio captioning (AudioCaps, Clotho, MusicCaps), and sound event classification (VGGSound, UrbanSound8K, ESC50). The audio encoder and projector are optimized, with the LLM remaining frozen.

- **Audio-Video Joint Training (Third Phase):**
 This critical phase integrates audio and visual modalities by fine-tuning on a rich mixture of audio-visual (e.g., AVQA, AVSD, VGGSound, AVInstruct), video-only, image-text, audio-text, and pure text datasets. Audio tracks are extracted from videos and aligned. A specific batch sampling ratio (2:1:1 for audio-visual:visual:audio data) ensures comprehensive coverage. The video encoder remains frozen, while the audio/video projectors, audio encoder, and the LLM itself are unfrozen and optimized. This stage is crucial for developing a deep understanding of multimodal content and interactions.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

VideoLLaMA 2 is built upon the LLaVA 1.5 library. Empirically set hyperparameters include a global batch size of 1,024 and a learning rate of 1e-3 for pre-training, and 2,048 and 2e-5 for fine-tuning. Training durations are kept relatively short (1 epoch for pre-training, up to 3 for video fine-tuning, 2 for audio fine-tuning, and 2 for audio-visual joint fine-tuning), indicating a focus on efficiency and effective transfer learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VideoLLaMA 2 demonstrates significant advancements in multimodal understanding, consistently achieving competitive results across various video and audio benchmarks. The key findings are as follows:

#### 4.1. Video Understanding (MC-VQA, OE-VQA, and VC)

- **Superiority over Open-Source Models in MC-VQA:** VideoLLaMA 2 exhibits substantial improvements over existing open-source models in Multi-choice Video Question Answering.
 - On the **EgoSchema** benchmark, VideoLLaMA 2-7B achieved an accuracy of 51.7%, significantly outperforming the previous state-of-the-art LLaVA-NeXT-Video (43.9%).
 - Similarly, on **Perception-Test** and **MV-Bench**, VideoLLaMA 2-7B achieved accuracies of 51.4% and 53.4% respectively, surpassing other open-source counterparts.
 - Notably, VideoLLaMA 2-7B even outperformed the proprietary model GPT4-V (43.7%) on the MV-Bench dataset.
 - Scaling up the LLM backbone from Mistral (7B) to Mixtral (8×7B) further boosted performance across these benchmarks.
- **Competitive in Video Captioning (VC):** On the **Multi-Source Video Caption (MSVC)** benchmark, VideoLLaMA 2-7B scored 2.57 in correctness and 2.61 in detailedness (using GPT-assisted evaluation). While slightly below GPT4-V, these scores were higher than all other open-source models, highlighting its strong ability to interpret dynamic video content.
- **Strong Performance in Open-Ended Video QA (OE-VQA):**
 - VideoLLaMA 2-7B achieved 71.7% accuracy with a score of 3.9 on the **MSVD** dataset, outperforming other open-source models like LLAVA-NeXT-Video (67.8%/3.5) and VideoChat2 (70.0%/3.9).
 - On **ActivityNet** and **Video-ChatGPT** benchmarks, while performing strongly (e.g., 49.9% accuracy on ActivityNet and 3.09 correctness on Video-ChatGPT for 7B), VideoLLaMA 2 was occasionally slightly outperformed by models like LLaVA-NeXT-Video in specific metrics. This suggests that models heavily trained on static image data might have an edge in video tasks that rely more on static visual information.

#### 4.2. Audio Understanding (AQA and OE-AVQA)

- **Exceptional Efficiency in Audio-Only QA (AQA):** VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) demonstrated remarkable efficiency and advanced learning capabilities in audio-only question answering, despite being trained on significantly less data compared to baselines.
 - On **Clotho-AQA**, VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) achieved 70.11%, a substantial improvement over Qwen-Audio (57.90%), despite using only 5k hours of multimodal data compared to Qwen-Audio's 137k hours.
 - Similar outperformance was observed on **TUT2017** (78.40% vs. 64.90%).
 - On **VocalSound**, VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) achieved 93.19%, closely matching Qwen2-Audio (93.92%) despite an enormous difference in training data hours (5k vs. 520k). These results underscore the effectiveness of the VideoLLaMA framework's audio integration.
- **Significant Advancements in Open-Ended Audio-Video QA (OE-AVQA):** VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) showed substantial superiority over existing open-source models in joint audio-video question answering tasks.
 - It achieved a strong 79.2% on the **MUSIC-QA** benchmark, even outperforming CREAM which used specialized model fine-tuning.
 - On the **AVSD** benchmark, it scored 57.2%, demonstrating adeptness at integrating visual context with audio for coherent scene descriptions.
 - VideoLLaMA 2-AV (7B) led on the **VGGSound** benchmark with 70.9%, significantly higher than AV-LLM's 47.6%, indicating a profound understanding and synthesis of information across both modalities for dynamic interactions.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Case studies showcased VideoLLaMA 2's impressive multimodal instruction-following capabilities, including:
- **Global Scene Understanding:** Detailed descriptions of objects, atmosphere, and movements.
- **Spatial-Temporal Orientation Awareness:** Correctly judging dynamic changes like a car's turning direction.
- **Commonsense Reasoning:** Inferring morning time based on activities and natural lighting.
- **Spatial-Temporal Fine-grained Recognition:** Accurately identifying text on a specific object at a precise moment in the video.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VideoLLaMA 2 represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for video-language modeling. Its significance can be understood through its technical contributions and its broader implications for future research and applications.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced Multimodal Comprehension:** By meticulously designing a Spatial-Temporal Convolution (STC) Connector and integrating a jointly trained Audio Branch, VideoLLaMA 2 substantially improves the model's ability to comprehend the complex interplay of visual and auditory signals in dynamic environments. This holistic approach addresses critical shortcomings in previous Video-LLMs that often overlooked crucial temporal dynamics and audio cues.
- **Architectural Innovation for Video:** The STC Connector is a notable architectural improvement. Unlike simpler token aggregation methods, it is specifically engineered to maintain spatial-temporal order, efficiently reduce token count, and minimize information loss during downsampling. This tailored design for video data is key to its robust performance.
- **Competitive Performance and Efficiency:** VideoLLaMA 2 consistently outperforms open-source models of similar size across a wide array of benchmarks (MC-VQA, OE-VQA, VC, AQA, OE-AVQA). Crucially, its performance often approaches or even surpasses that of proprietary models on several metrics. The impressive results in audio understanding, achieved with significantly fewer training hours compared to dedicated audio-LLMs, highlight the model's exceptional efficiency and the effectiveness of its integrated multimodal learning framework.
- **Foundational Open-Source Model:** The decision to publicly release all models is a major contribution to the research community. This open-sourcing allows other researchers to build upon VideoLLaMA 2, replicate results, and accelerate further innovation in multimodal AI. It democratizes access to state-of-the-art video-language modeling capabilities.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Video Understanding Research:** VideoLLaMA 2 lays a stronger foundational layer for future research in video-language modeling. Its success in capturing spatial-temporal dynamics and integrating audio provides a robust baseline and inspiration for developing even more sophisticated models.
- **Catalyst for Specialized Applications:** As a powerful generalist model, VideoLLaMA 2 can serve as a robust backbone for tackling more specialized and challenging problems in video analysis. These include:
 - **Long Video Understanding:** Its enhanced temporal modeling could be crucial for comprehending narratives and events across extended video durations.
 - **Video Agents:** It could empower AI agents to interact with and reason about video content, leading to more intelligent conversational assistants or instructional systems.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** The precise spatial-temporal and auditory understanding can contribute to more robust perception systems for self-driving vehicles, enabling better situational awareness and decision-making.
 - **Motion Understanding:** Improved capabilities in discerning fine-grained motions from video data could benefit applications in sports analysis, human-computer interaction, and surveillance.
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Robots could leverage VideoLLaMA 2's understanding to interpret visual and auditory cues from their environment, leading to more adaptive and capable manipulation tasks.
- **Accessible Multimodal AI Development:** The efficiency gains demonstrated, particularly in audio training, could lower the computational barrier for developing high-performing multimodal models. This could enable more research groups and smaller entities to contribute to the field.
- **Real-world Applications:** For a technology conglomerate like Alibaba, the advancements in VideoLLaMA 2 hold immense potential for enhancing various products and services. This includes improved content moderation, video search and recommendation, smart surveillance, virtual assistants that can understand video and audio inputs, and more engaging user experiences through advanced video analysis.

In conclusion, VideoLLaMA 2 not only marks a significant step forward in technical capability for video-language models but also serves as a critical open-source resource that promises to accelerate the entire field of multimodal AI, pushing towards more comprehensive and intelligent understanding of dynamic, real-world media.
