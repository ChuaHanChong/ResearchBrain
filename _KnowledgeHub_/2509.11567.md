# Shape control of simulated multi-segment continuum robots via Koopman operators with per-segment projection

> [!summary] Summary
> A data-driven Koopman operator approach enables real-time, closed-loop shape control for simulated multi-segment tendon-driven soft continuum robots. This method incorporates a per-segment projection scheme that improves model accuracy by an order of magnitude and facilitates reaching complex target shapes with multiple segments.

## Problem

- Controlling the full, continuous shape of soft continuum robots is computationally expensive due to their infinite degrees of freedom and highly nonlinear dynamics, making real-time control challenging.
- Previous Koopman operator applications for soft robots primarily focused on low-dimensional end-effector control or made unrealistic assumptions about actuation for whole-shape control.
- Existing data-driven and physics-based control methods struggle with the computational demands and nonlinearities inherent in multi-segment soft robots, limiting their practical real-time application.

## Method

- A data-driven ==Koopman== operator approach transforms the robot's nonlinear dynamics into a globally linear representation in a lifted observable space, enabling the use of efficient linear Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- A per-segment projection scheme projects each segment's state into its local coordinate frame, aiming to linearize local dynamics and improve the accuracy of the identified Koopman model for multi-segment robots.
- The observable dictionary is structured to guarantee a control-affine Koopman model, using the change in control input (∆u) as the input variable, which is then integrated into an MPC formulation for shape tracking.

## Results

- The per-segment projection scheme enhanced Koopman model accuracy, reducing the mean squared unprojected shape error by an order of magnitude.
- The method successfully controlled simulated multi-segment robots (up to 5 segments) to various complex target shapes with high qualitative and quantitative fidelity.
- The study demonstrated the feasibility of computationally efficient closed-loop shape control for simulated tendon-driven soft robots, implying potential for real-time implementation, despite observed steady-state inaccuracies in the Koopman models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The per-segment projection scheme significantly improves Koopman model accuracy by an order of magnitude, effectively mitigating the challenges posed by highly nonlinear dynamics in multi-segment soft robots.
> - Using the change in control input (∆u) for the Koopman operator, in conjunction with the projection scheme, yields models with the highest accuracy compared to using absolute control inputs.
> - While MPC with Koopman models enables computationally efficient shape control, inherent steady-state errors in the identified Koopman models prevent the controller from maintaining the absolute lowest possible error, highlighting an area for future improvement.
