# Shape control of simulated multi-segment continuum robots via Koopman operators with per-segment projection

> [!summary] Summary
> A data-driven Koopman operator approach enables real-time, closed-loop shape control for simulated multi-segment tendon-driven soft continuum robots. This method incorporates a per-segment projection scheme that improves model accuracy by an order of magnitude and facilitates reaching complex target shapes with multiple segments.

## Problem

- Controlling the full, continuous shape of soft continuum robots is computationally expensive due to their infinite degrees of freedom and highly nonlinear dynamics, making real-time control challenging.
- Previous Koopman operator applications for soft robots primarily focused on low-dimensional end-effector control or made unrealistic assumptions about actuation for whole-shape control.
- Existing data-driven and physics-based control methods struggle with the computational demands and nonlinearities inherent in multi-segment soft robots, limiting their practical real-time application.

## Method

- A data-driven ==Koopman== operator approach transforms the robot's nonlinear dynamics into a globally linear representation in a lifted observable space, enabling the use of efficient linear Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- A per-segment projection scheme projects each segment's state into its local coordinate frame, aiming to linearize local dynamics and improve the accuracy of the identified Koopman model for multi-segment robots.
- The observable dictionary is structured to guarantee a control-affine Koopman model, using the change in control input (∆u) as the input variable, which is then integrated into an MPC formulation for shape tracking.

## Results

- The per-segment projection scheme enhanced Koopman model accuracy, reducing the mean squared unprojected shape error by an order of magnitude.
- The method successfully controlled simulated multi-segment robots (up to 5 segments) to various complex target shapes with high qualitative and quantitative fidelity.
- The study demonstrated the feasibility of computationally efficient closed-loop shape control for simulated tendon-driven soft robots, implying potential for real-time implementation, despite observed steady-state inaccuracies in the Koopman models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The per-segment projection scheme significantly improves Koopman model accuracy by an order of magnitude, effectively mitigating the challenges posed by highly nonlinear dynamics in multi-segment soft robots.
> - Using the change in control input (∆u) for the Koopman operator, in conjunction with the projection scheme, yields models with the highest accuracy compared to using absolute control inputs.
> - While MPC with Koopman models enables computationally efficient shape control, inherent steady-state errors in the identified Koopman models prevent the controller from maintaining the absolute lowest possible error, highlighting an area for future improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of soft continuum robotics is characterized by the development of robots that exhibit continuous, compliant motions, suitable for applications requiring delicate manipulation, safe human interaction, and adaptation to diverse environments, such as minimally invasive surgery. However, the control of these systems, particularly their full shape, presents significant challenges due to their inherent infinite degrees of freedom and highly nonlinear dynamics.

Traditional physics-based models, such as the Cosserat rod model or Kirchhoff rod model, describe soft robot dynamics using partial differential equations (PDEs), which are computationally intensive to solve. While reduced-order models exist to improve computational speed, they still typically rely on nonlinear control techniques that can be slow, especially for high-dimensional shape control tasks. Finite element methods (FEM) also face high computational demands for real-time control due to the required spatial and temporal resolution.

Data-driven approaches offer an alternative by learning approximations of system dynamics. Deep neural networks have been employed but can be less interpretable and challenging to integrate into classical control frameworks due to their nonlinear and heavyweight nature. Koopman operator-based methods present a distinct advantage by transforming nonlinear system dynamics into a globally linear representation in a lifted, higher-dimensional observable space. This linearity allows for the application of computationally efficient linear control techniques.

Previous work utilizing Koopman operators for soft robot control has primarily focused on end-effector (tip) control, which typically involves low-dimensional state variables and does not address the complexity of whole-shape control. Some research has applied Koopman operators to shape control but often relies on the assumption of arbitrary external forces being applicable at various points along the robot's body, which may not represent realistic actuation mechanisms for tendon-driven robots. This paper positions itself within this context by aiming to achieve real-time, whole-shape control of multi-segment tendon-driven soft continuum robots using data-driven Koopman models, specifically addressing the computational efficiency and realistic actuation constraints that limit existing methods. It seeks to bridge the gap between the theoretical advantages of Koopman operators and their practical application to full shape control of complex soft robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a data-driven Koopman operator-based approach for the real-time, closed-loop shape control of simulated multi-segment tendon-driven soft continuum robots. This objective is motivated by several challenges and limitations in the current state of soft robot control:

1. **High Computational Cost of Shape Control:** Soft robots possess high or infinite degrees of freedom, making their full shape control computationally expensive with traditional physics-based models (e.g., Cosserat/Kirchhoff rod models, FEM). These models involve solving complex nonlinear PDEs in real time, which is often infeasible for practical applications.
2. **Limitations of Existing Control Strategies:** While some rigid robots achieve real-time task-space control (e.g., tip control), extending this to whole-shape control for soft robots is difficult. Reduced-order models, while faster, still rely on nonlinear control techniques that are computationally burdensome for high-dimensional shape references. Data-driven methods like deep neural networks offer alternatives but often lack interpretability and are challenging to integrate into efficient classical control frameworks.
3. **Incompleteness of Prior Koopman Applications:** Previous applications of Koopman operators to soft robots have predominantly focused on end-effector control, utilizing low-dimensional state variables that cannot adequately represent the continuous shape of the robot. Efforts towards shape control with Koopman operators have sometimes made strong assumptions regarding actuation (e.g., arbitrary forces along the body), which do not always align with common actuation methods like tendon-driven systems.
4. **Need for Biocompatible and Compliant Motion:** Soft continuum robots are inherently designed for biocompatible motion, non-destructive manipulation, and safer human interaction. Efficient and robust shape control is crucial to fully leverage these advantages in diverse applications such as minimally invasive surgery or environmental adaptation.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines two specific contributions:
1. The development of a **per-segment projection scheme** designed to improve the accuracy of Koopman models for soft robots by accounting for their multi-segment structure and highly nonlinear dynamics.
2. The demonstration of **shape control for tendon-driven soft robotic arms** composed of one to five independent segments, showcasing the method's ability to handle increasing complexity.

The overarching goal is to enable computationally efficient closed-loop control of soft robot shapes, potentially paving the way for practical real-time shape control in physical soft robot prototypes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a data-driven approach leveraging Koopman operator theory combined with model predictive control (MPC), specifically designed for multi-segment tendon-driven soft continuum robots.

#### 3.1. Simulation Setup

- **Robot Model:** Simulated multi-segment tendon-driven soft robots are modeled using the Kirchhoff rod model, incorporating tendon forces. The dynamics are solved using the implicit BDF-α method.
- **Components:** The robot is conceptualized with a backbone, tendons, and tendon routing disks. Each segment has its own set of three tendons (at 0°, 120°, 240°) that apply forces only along that segment's length.
- **Physical Parameters:** Key parameters for the simulated robots include a length of 1.0m per segment, Young’s modulus of 200 GPa, density of 8000 kg/m³, backbone radius of 1.0cm, and tendon offset of 4cm. Spatial discretization per segment is 100 points, with a time step of 0.01s.

#### 3.2. Data Collection

- **Trajectory Generation:** For each robot configuration (1 to 5 segments), 480 trajectories, totaling 120,000 snapshots of `(x(t), x(t + ∆t), u(t))`, are collected.
- **Control Input Strategy:** Initial control inputs are zero, and then five unique control inputs are randomly sampled from a uniform distribution `U(0, 8)` Newtons for each tendon.
- **Ramp-and-Hold:** The control inputs `u(t)` are applied using a ramp-and-hold sequence: linearly interpolating between sampled inputs for a ramp time (`t_r = 40∆t = 0.4s`) and then holding for a hold time (`t_h = 10∆t = 0.1s`). This generates dynamic behavior for training.
- **State Variable:** The state variable `x` consists of the concatenation of 10 position vectors uniformly sampled along each segment of the backbone, defining the robot's shape. This choice is made for ease of observation and reduced dimensionality compared to including orientation or other physical variables.

#### 3.3. Koopman Operator Identification

- **Theory:** The Koopman operator is an infinite-dimensional linear operator that propagates a dictionary of nonlinear observable functions `θ(x, u)` forward in time.
- **Finite Approximation (EDMD):** An approximate finite-dimensional Koopman operator `K_∆t` is identified from the collected data using Extended Dynamic Mode Decomposition (EDMD). This involves solving a least squares problem: `K_∆t ≈ Θ(X', U)Θ†(X, U)`.
- **Regularization:** To mitigate overfitting, outliers, and noisy data, the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regularization (`L1` norm) is incorporated into the least squares problem, promoting sparsity and improving stability.
- **Control Affine Model:** The dictionary of observables `θ(x, u)` is structured to guarantee a control-affine Koopman model. It is defined as a concatenation of time-delayed coordinates of the state `x` and the control input `u`: `θ(x_k) = [x_k; x_{k-1}; x_{k-2}]` for `θ_x(x_k)`, resulting in `θ(x, u) = [θ_x(x); u]`. This allows the learned dynamics in the lifted space to be expressed as `θ_x(x_{k+1}) = Aθ_x(x_k) + Bu_k`, enabling linear control techniques. The control input `u` is chosen as `∆u = u_k - u_{k-1}` rather than `u_k` to be zero-centered, which is shown to improve accuracy.

#### 3.4. Per-segment Projection Scheme

- **Problem:** Directly using concatenated positions of the entire robot as a state variable can lead to large model errors due to highly nonlinear dynamics, especially for distal segments reacting to forces on proximal segments.
- **Solution:** Each segment is projected into its local coordinate frame. For each position `p(s)` along the arclength `s` of a segment, a transformed position `p'(s) = R⁻¹(s₀)(p(s) - p(s₀))` is computed, where `s₀` is the segment's origin and `R(s₀)` is its orientation.
- **Rationale:** This procedure aims to linearize the dynamics locally, ensuring that the effects of tendon forces on a segment are primarily observed within that segment's local frame. This promotes sparsity in the learned Koopman control matrix and improves the accuracy of the identified model. After control in the projected space, the full state of the robot can be reconstructed by inverting this projection using the segment's original orientation `R(s₀)`.

#### 3.5. Model Predictive Control (MPC) Formulation

- **Objective:** MPC is used to control the robot to desired target shapes (`r_k+1`). It minimizes a cost function over a finite prediction horizon `H`.
- **Cost Function:** The cost function penalizes the difference between the projected lifted state (`Cz_k+1`) and the reference shape (`r_k+1`), as well as the change in control inputs (`∆u_k`).
 `J = Σ_{k=1}^H (Cz_{k+1} - r_{k+1})ᵀQ(Cz_{k+1} - r_{k+1}) + ∆u_kᵀR∆u_k`
 where `z_k = θ_x(x_k)` is the lifted state, `Q` and `R` are positive definite weight matrices, and `C` is a projection matrix that maps the lifted state back to the original state (`x_k = Cz_k`). The state `x` is explicitly included in the lifted state `θ_x(x)` to ensure `C` exists.
- **Optimization:** The control-affine Koopman model converts the MPC problem into a convex quadratic program, which can be solved efficiently using algorithms like OSQP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated the proposed method through numerical experiments focusing on shape control of simulated tendon-driven soft robotic arms with varying numbers of segments (1 to 5).

#### 4.1. Efficacy of Per-Segment Projection Scheme

- **Model Accuracy:** A comparison of Koopman model accuracy (mean squared unprojected shape error) as a function of prediction horizon demonstrated that the per-segment projection scheme significantly improves model accuracy.
- **Control Input Choice:** Using `∆u` (change in control input) instead of `u` (absolute control input) as the control input for the Koopman operator, in conjunction with the projection scheme, yielded models with the highest accuracy. The combination of `∆u` and projection resulted in an order of magnitude more accurate models than without projection (Fig. 5).

#### 4.2. Shape Control Performance

- **Qualitative Results:** The method successfully controlled multi-segment robots (3, 4, and 5 segments) to various target shapes, including complex shapes resembling letters (e.g., "R" and "S"). The final shapes of the robot were consistently observed to be qualitatively and quantitatively close to the reference shapes (Fig. 6).
- **Quantitative Results (Convergence):**
 - **Projection Impact:** Control algorithms utilizing the per-segment projection method consistently improved the shape error by several orders of magnitude over a two-second real-time simulation period. Without the projection method, shape error for multiple-segment robots did not significantly improve (Fig. 7.a vs. 7.b, 7.c).
 - **Weight Matrix Influence:** The convergence behavior was found to be dependent on the choice of the gain matrix `Q` in the MPC cost function.
 - Using `Q` proportional to the identity matrix (`Q = 20I`) with the projection scheme led to improved shape error (Fig. 7.b).
 - A weighted diagonal `Q` matrix, which applied larger penalties to segments closer to the fixed end and smaller penalties to distal segments, demonstrated faster initial convergence. However, this approach also highlighted inherent steady-state model error in the Koopman models, preventing the lowest possible error from being maintained (Fig. 7.c).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The study implies that the method realizes computationally efficient closed-loop control, demonstrating the feasibility of real-time shape control for soft robots by leveraging linear MPC with Koopman models.

#### 4.3. Identified Limitations

- **Koopman Model Inaccuracies (Steady-State Error):** Despite the improvements, the identified Koopman operator models exhibited steady-state errors. For fixed control inputs, the data-driven model often failed to converge to the true steady states of the system. For instance, when control inputs were fixed at zero from an initial configuration, the Koopman model predicted significant deviation from this initial (and true steady) state (Fig. 8).
- **Impact on MPC Stability:** These model inaccuracies affected the stability of the MPC controller, preventing it from maintaining the absolute lowest obtainable error and sometimes causing spikes in shape error during simulations (e.g., Fig. 6.g, 6.a, 6.b, 6.c, 6.i, and 6.e showing transient error increases even after initial convergence).
- **Spurious Eigenvalues:** Identified Koopman models, particularly in low data regimes, can yield spurious eigenvalues, contributing to instability when used directly.
- **Open Problems:** The choice of optimal dictionary functions for Koopman models remains a system-dependent open problem. High data requirements for Koopman models could also limit practical application to physical prototypes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the control of soft continuum robots, particularly in addressing the complex challenge of whole-shape control.

- **Enabling Real-time Shape Control:** By successfully demonstrating computationally efficient closed-loop shape control for multi-segment tendon-driven soft robots in simulation, this work contributes to overcoming a significant barrier in soft robotics. The use of Koopman operators to linearize the dynamics of highly nonlinear systems, combined with linear Model Predictive Control, offers a pathway towards real-time control that is often unachievable with traditional physics-based or purely nonlinear data-driven methods.
- **Addressing Nonlinearity Challenges:** The introduction of a per-segment projection scheme is a key innovation. This method effectively mitigates the highly nonlinear dynamics that arise from multi-segment continuum robots by transforming state variables into local coordinate frames, thereby improving the accuracy of the learned Koopman models by an order of magnitude. This approach makes the Koopman framework more robust and applicable to the inherent complexities of soft robots.
- **Foundational for Practical Applications:** The ability to precisely control the continuous shape of soft robots has substantial implications for various fields.
 - **Minimally Invasive Surgery:** Precise shape control allows soft robots to navigate complex anatomical structures, adapt to diverse geometric constraints, and perform delicate manipulations with reduced risk of damage to tissues.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** The compliant nature of soft robots, coupled with accurate shape control, enhances safety and adaptability in collaborative tasks or in environments with humans.
 - **Adaptive Manipulation:** Robots capable of diverse shape changes can adapt to various environments and objects, broadening their utility in complex tasks.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work identifies critical limitations, particularly regarding the steady-state accuracy of Koopman models and the need for principled dictionary function choices. These identified limitations serve as clear directions for future research, including investigating methods to mitigate steady-state errors (e.g., using static Koopman operators as pre-gain terms) and exploring adaptive dictionary learning.
- **Experimental Validation:** The stated intention for future work to experimentally validate this approach on physical prototypes, using both simulated and observed data, underscores the practical relevance of this research and its potential transition from simulation to real-world applications.

Overall, this research represents a significant step towards realizing robust and efficient shape control for soft continuum robots, opening avenues for their broader adoption in applications requiring compliant, dexterous, and adaptable robotic systems.
