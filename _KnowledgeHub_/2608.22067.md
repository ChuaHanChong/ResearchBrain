# DELE-w0.5: Inferring Action from Future Latent State for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DELE-w0.5 is a world-action model that drops video generation entirely: instead of synthesizing dense future frames, it predicts a single compact future latent state, encoded by DINO-v3, and jointly denoises it with the action chunk through a dual-stream flow-matching transformer. Future-state tokens are dropped at inference. Across 640 real-robot trials on four long-horizon tasks it reaches 62.5% full-task success and 81.3% macro stage progress.

## Problem

- Video-generation world-action models optimize dense visual trajectory reconstruction as a surrogate for control, a substantially harder problem than the low-dimensional action actually executed.
- Multi-frame video latents dominate sequence length, memory, and compute, so joint world-action training inherits the heavy cost of large video generators.
- Iterative denoising over high-dimensional future video latents bottlenecks the control loop, and compact backbones or distillation reduce cost without removing the underlying redundancy.

## Method

- **DELE-w0.5** reformulates world-action modeling as joint prediction of an action chunk and one future latent state, replacing the video posterior over intermediate frames with a single post-interaction state.
- ==Dual-stream architecture==: a condition stream fuses language and multi-view current observation via ==single-stream attention== with ==AdaLN-modulated QKV==; a noise stream denoises noised action plus future latent.
- ==Flow-matching== objective regresses velocity fields for action and future latent, weighted by lambda, under an ==attention mask== that hides future-state tokens from action tokens so they can be dropped at inference.

## Results

- Across **640** real-robot trials on an Astribot S1 dual-arm robot, achieves **62.5%** overall success and **81.3%** macro progress, beating the best baseline by **32.5** and **20.1** points.
- Wins all four tasks: **80%** door, **65%** Pepsi retrieval, **45%** add-ice, **60%** microwave popcorn, versus **30%** best-baseline overall success from Xiaomi Robotics-0 and **26.3%** from GigaWorld-Policy-0.5.
- Median core inference latency is **87.5 ms** on an RTX 4090, faster than GigaWorld-Policy-0.5, pi-0.5, LingBot-VLA2, and HY-VLA while achieving far higher success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model for control needs the post-action physical state, not the visual transition to it; intermediate frames cost capacity without specifying the outcome.
> - Auxiliary prediction targets can be training-only: masking future-state tokens out of the action pathway lets you drop them at inference for free latency.
> - Stage-wise retention exposes what aggregate success hides: baselines open appliances reliably yet collapse at handoff, insertion, and terminal-state stages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on robotic manipulation. Within this domain, two primary model architectures are widely adopted: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs).

VLA models typically operate as a three-step framework. They process multimodal inputs, including human language instructions and current robot observations, using a vision-language model (VLM) for task planning. Subsequently, an action expert decodes this planning into executable robot commands. While VLA models leverage the semantic knowledge of large pre-trained VLMs, they often require extensive data for adaptation to downstream tasks. This reliance can lead to limitations in generalization, with performance degrading when visual conditions, colors, or tasks change significantly, as indicated by empirical studies.

WAMs represent an alternative approach, aiming to unify world state evolution prediction with action generation within a single end-to-end learning objective. A dominant variant of recent WAMs integrates robot control with video generation backbones, predicting dense future visual trajectories alongside robot actions. This approach introduces useful spatiotemporal priors, leveraging the rich information embedded in video data. However, this line of WAMs also introduces a structural mismatch. The objective of dense visual generation is fundamentally different from the objective of robot control. Future videos are high-dimensional and appearance-sensitive, with many intermediate frames describing visual transitions rather than the critical physical consequences that dictate subsequent actions. This necessitates that the model solves a computationally intensive problem of visual reconstruction before extracting a compact action signal. This process incurs significant computational costs, memory consumption, and latency during inference, as it requires iterative denoising over high-dimensional video-action latents. Existing efforts to mitigate these costs, such as compact backbones or latent downsampling, reduce the burden but do not eliminate the underlying redundancy of video synthesis.

DELE-w0.5 positions itself by challenging the necessity of video generation in WAMs for robotic manipulation. It argues that for effective robot control, a world model's objective should not be to reproduce the visual appearance of the world at every intermediate moment, but rather to predict the *physical state* that the world will attain after an action is executed. This work proposes a more direct formulation that models action-relevant future states and infers robot actions without relying on intermediate video generation, thereby addressing the computational and conceptual limitations identified in video-generation-based WAMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a more efficient and direct world-action model for robotic manipulation by inferring robot actions from predicted future latent states, rather than from generated future visual trajectories. The motivation for this objective stems from a critical analysis of the limitations inherent in existing video-generation-based World-Action Models (WAMs).

The authors argue that current video-generation-based WAMs formulate an "unnecessary intermediate objective" for robotic manipulation. While predicting future videos might provide rich spatiotemporal priors, it implicitly treats detailed visual evolution as the primary target for world modeling. For a robot, however, the ultimate goal is not to visually reproduce every intermediate moment of an interaction, but to predict the *physical outcome*—how the world will change after an action is executed.

Specifically, the key motivations and limitations addressed are:

- **High-Dimensional Visual Redundancy**: Future videos are characterized by high dimensionality and sensitivity to appearance. Most intermediate frames within a generated video sequence describe the visual transition between states, offering limited direct information about the final physical consequence that dictates subsequent robot actions. This forces the model to learn the distribution of complete visual evolution, a task that is substantially more complex than directly modeling action-relevant future states.
- **Structural Mismatch**: There is a fundamental mismatch between the objective of visual generation and the objective of robot control. The model is compelled to first reconstruct dense visual evolution, and then extract a compact action signal from this high-dimensional representation. This surrogate objective diverts model capacity and computational resources towards visual details that may not be directly relevant to the physical action outcomes.
- **Computational Overhead**: Multi-frame video latents are significantly larger than corresponding robot action sequences. This disparity leads to increased sequence length, memory consumption, and overall computational budget for joint world-action learning. Consequently, training these WAMs inherits the substantial cost associated with large video generators. Although techniques like compact backbones and latent downsampling exist, they reduce the cost of the pipeline without eliminating its underlying redundancy.
- **Inference Latency**: During real-time inference, video-generation-based WAMs require iterative denoising over high-dimensional future video-action latents. This process bottlenecks the control loop, as the robot must wait for the synthesis of a visual trajectory that it does not itself execute. Low-latency inference is crucial for real-world robotic deployment.
- **Suboptimal Feature Organization**: Features optimized primarily for visual reconstruction are not necessarily organized in a manner most effective for low-level robot control. This can lead to a less direct and less efficient mapping from perceived world state to actionable commands.

To address these issues, DELE-w0.5 is motivated by the insight that a world model for robotic manipulation should focus on predicting the future *physical state* relevant to subsequent actions. The proposed model aims to infer robot actions directly from a compact future latent state, bypassing the need for generating intermediate video frames. This formulation is intended to provide a more compact and efficient paradigm for world-action modeling, leading to cheaper training, lower-latency inference, and improved control capabilities by directly linking world understanding to action generation through action-relevant future states.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DELE-w0.5 models robotic manipulation as a probabilistic inference problem where the objective is to infer robot actions from predicted future states, specifically $p_\theta(A_t, O_{t+\Delta t} | O_t, q_t, L)$. The framework employs a dual-stream attention mechanism, comprising a condition stream and a noise stream, designed to avoid explicit video generation.

#### 3.1. Input and Tokenization

- **Language Instruction (L):** Task instructions (e.g., "put the flowers into the vase") are tokenized using Qwen3 and then passed through a projection layer to obtain language tokens $l$ of a consistent hidden dimension.
- **Current Observation (O_t):** Multi-view RGB images (three views in experiments: head-mounted and two wrist-mounted) are encoded by a Vision Encoder (DINO-v3 in this work). The resulting latent vision representation $\hat{z}_t$ is then projected to the same hidden dimension as the language tokens, denoted as $z_t$.
- **Proprioception (q_t):** This refers to the robot's internal state (e.g., joint positions). While listed as an input, its direct tokenization method isn't explicitly detailed, but it conditions the overall policy.
- **Action Chunk (A_t):** An action chunk consists of a sequence of future actions $A_t = [a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+H}]$, where $H=60$. For a single-arm robot, each action $a$ is a 7-DoF vector (3D positional displacement, 3D Euler angle rotation, 1D binary gripper state). For dual-arm, it's 14-DoF.
- **Next State (O_{t+Δt}):** The observation of the world at a future time step, which is also encoded into a latent space $\hat{z}_{t+\Delta t}$ using the Vision Encoder.

#### 3.2. Framework Architecture

The core of DELE-w0.5 is built upon a dual-stream architecture:

- **Condition Stream Block:**
 - **Input Processing:** The projected language tokens $l$ and current vision tokens $z_t$ are concatenated to form $Z_c_t = \text{cat}(l, z_t)$.
 - **Single-Stream Attention:** This concatenated sequence $Z_c_t$ is fed into a standard self-attention block (Transformer encoder). This block refines the multimodal information, enabling cross-modal interactions to produce $\hat{Z}_c_t$.
 - **AdaLN and Output:** The refined representation $\hat{Z}_c_t$ is modulated by AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization) using a timestep embedding for $\tau=1$ (denoted as $t_c$). This modulation generates the Query ($Q_c$), Key ($K_c$), and Value ($V_c$) matrices for the condition stream. QKNorm (L2 normalization) is applied to $Q_c$ and $K_c$ before multiplication.

- **Noise Stream Block:**
 - **Noise Addition:** For both the action chunk and the future latent state, noise is added based on a uniform random timestep $\tau \sim U$ and Gaussian noise $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$.
 - Noisy Action: $A^\tau_t = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\epsilon$.
 - Noisy Future Latent State: The true future observation $O_{t+\Delta t}$ is encoded to $\hat{z}_{t+\Delta t}$, then noisy version is created as $z^\tau_{t+\Delta t} = \tau \hat{z}_{t+\Delta t} + (1 - \tau)\epsilon$.
 - **Processing:** These noisy representations ($A^\tau_t$ and $z^\tau_{t+\Delta t}$) are processed through a similar single-stream attention mechanism, also modulated by AdaLN, to generate Query ($Q_n$), Key ($K_n$), and Value ($V_n$) for the noise stream, utilizing the random timestep $\tau$.

#### 3.3. Joint Attention and Flow Matching Objective

- **Concatenation:** The $Q, K, V$ matrices from both streams are concatenated ($Q=\text{cat}(Q_c, Q_n)$, $K=\text{cat}(K_c, K_n)$, $V=\text{cat}(V_c, V_n)$).
- **Masked Attention:** These concatenated matrices are processed by a joint masked attention block. This block produces an output that is then passed through a projection layer to yield the predicted action chunk $\hat{A}^\tau_t$ and the predicted next latent observation $\hat{z}^\tau_{t+\Delta t}$.
- **Flow Matching Objective:** The model is trained to regress the velocity field.
 - Action Loss ($L_a$): Minimizes the squared difference between the predicted action velocity field ($u_a$) and the target velocity field ($A_t - \epsilon$).
 - Observation Loss ($L_o$): Minimizes the squared difference between the predicted observation velocity field ($o_a$) and the target velocity field ($\hat{z}_{t+\Delta t} - \epsilon$).
 - Total Loss: $L = L_a + \lambda L_o$, where $\lambda$ is a balancing weight.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference Protocols

- **Training:** An attention mask ensures that the condition tokens (language, current observation) attend bidirectionally among themselves but do not access future information. Action tokens attend to the condition group and themselves, but not to predicted future observations. Crucially, the predicted future observation tokens attend to *all* token groups (language, current observation, action), allowing the model to learn state transitions conditioned on full context.
- **Inference:** During inference, the predicted future observation token groups are *removed* from the sequence. The model then predicts the action sequence solely based on the language instruction and current multi-view observations, without generating any visual tokens. This design is intended to shorten the inference sequence, eliminate unnecessary visual generation, and enable low-latency control.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Robot and Tasks:** Experiments were conducted on an Astribot S1 dual-arm robot across four long-horizon manipulation tasks: door opening, Pepsi retrieval, adding ice to a cup, and microwave popcorn. These tasks represent varied difficulties including articulated interaction, object retrieval, bimanual transfer, tool use, and constrained insertion.
- **Baselines:** DELE-w0.5 was compared against seven Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies: GigaWorld-Policy-0.5 (GWP0.5), Xiaomi Robotics-0 (XR0), $\pi_{0.5}$, LingBot-VLA2, Hy-Embodied-0.5-VLA (HY-VLA), Spirit-v1.5, and GR00T-N1.7.
- **Evaluation:** All methods were subjected to identical fine-tuning data (three epochs), evaluation protocols (robot embodiment, task instructions, scene resets, 180-second execution budget), and metrics.
- **Metrics:** Two metrics were used: full-task success rate (whether the task-specific complete condition was met) and normalized ordered-stage progress (the highest consecutively completed stage divided by total stages, macro-averaged across tasks). A total of 640 real-robot trials (8 methods × 4 tasks × 20 trials) were performed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, encompassing 640 real-robot trials across four distinct long-horizon manipulation tasks, indicate that DELE-w0.5 performed effectively compared to the evaluated baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- DELE-w0.5 achieved an overall full-task success rate of 62.5% (50 out of 80 trials).
- It attained a macro-averaged normalized ordered-stage progress of 81.3%.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- DELE-w0.5 surpassed the strongest baseline in both key metrics. For macro progress, it outperformed GWP0.5 (61.3%) by 20.1 percentage points. For full-task success, it exceeded XR0 (30.0%) by 32.5 percentage points.
- Its performance was consistently superior across all four tasks when compared to the other seven VLA policies.

#### 4.3. Task-wise and Stage-wise Analysis

- **Door Opening:** DELE-w0.5 achieved 80.0% full success and 95.0% normalized progress. It reached the coordinated rotation-and-push stage in all 20 trials, with its failures occurring only at the final sustained-opening condition. In contrast, several baselines grasped the handle but failed to synchronize rotation and pushing, or to maintain the required opening angle.
- **Pepsi Retrieval:** DELE-w0.5 completed 65.0% of trials, with 82.0% normalized progress. It successfully opened the refrigerator and extracted the can in 18 out of 20 trials. The primary failure points were the right-to-left handoff and the final door closure while retaining the can. XR0 and GWP0.5 were the strongest baselines, but with lower success rates (30.0% and 25.0% respectively).
- **Adding Ice:** This task involved the longest sequence. DELE-w0.5 achieved 45.0% full success and 63.3% normalized progress, completing the six-stage task in 9 out of 20 trials. It picked up the scoop in 19 trials and acquired ice in 11. Baselines showed significantly lower success, with GWP0.5 being the strongest at 20.0% success and 50.0% progress. Failures in baselines frequently occurred at repeated bimanual coordination, tool use, precise release, or scene restoration.
- **Microwave Popcorn:** DELE-w0.5 obtained 60.0% full success and 85.0% normalized progress. It opened the microwave and picked up the package in every trial, closing the microwave in 16 trials, and starting heating in 12. Baselines like GWP0.5 and XR0 achieved 15.0% and 20.0% success respectively. Many baselines could open the appliance but failed during package insertion, door closure, or heating activation.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

- DELE-w0.5 exhibited a median inference time of 87.5 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 4090. This was faster than GWP0.5, $\pi_{0.5}$, LingBot-VLA2, and HY-VLA, and comparable to Spirit-v1.5, despite achieving substantially higher success rates. Inference latency excludes network transfer, image decoding, action projection, inverse kinematics, trajectory processing, and robot execution.
- Conditional on successful completion, DELE-w0.5 showed competitive measured mean completion times across tasks, e.g., 16.66 s for Door and 46.27 s for Add Ice.

#### 4.5. Failure Analysis

- Analysis of unsuccessful trials for baselines revealed consistent bottlenecks:
 - Door opening: Failure during coordinated handle rotation and pushing.
 - Pepsi retrieval: Issues during object handoff or final refrigerator closure.
 - Adding ice: Difficulty before ice acquisition and pouring.
 - Microwave popcorn: Failures during insertion, door closure, or heating activation.
- For DELE-w0.5, while it reached later stages more often, its remaining failures were observed during object transfer, precise release, or meeting the final task condition. These findings suggest that robust long-horizon performance relies on both individual skill execution and reliable transitions between interaction stages.

#### 4.6. Early Signs of Emergence

- During additional intervention rollouts, DELE-w0.5 exhibited adaptive behaviors not explicitly present in the training demonstrations. Examples include retaining a popcorn package and reopening a closed microwave door to complete the task, or using the package itself to push a narrowly open door wider instead of reverting to the demonstrated handle-opening routine. These behaviors suggest goal-conditioned behavioral recomposition in response to unseen intermediate states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DELE-w0.5, a world-action model that re-evaluates the role of future prediction in robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Conceptual Shift in World Modeling for Robotics:** The work challenges the conventional assumption that dense video generation is a necessary intermediate objective for world models in robotic control. By proposing that a world model should predict the *physical outcome* or *action-relevant future state* rather than the complete visual evolution, DELE-w0.5 advocates for a more direct and task-specific formulation. This reframing suggests that optimizing for visual fidelity across every intermediate frame may introduce unnecessary complexity without proportional benefits for control.

- **Improved Efficiency and Real-world Deployability:** The removal of high-dimensional visual redundancy and the costly iterative video generation process inherent in many video-based WAMs contribute to significantly cheaper training and lower-latency inference. This efficiency is a critical factor for the practical deployment of robot intelligence in real-world scenarios, where real-time responsiveness and resource constraints are paramount. The reported median inference time of 87.5 ms, combined with high success rates, demonstrates progress toward this goal.

- **Enhanced Performance on Complex Long-Horizon Tasks:** DELE-w0.5 consistently outperformed a range of representative VLA baselines across four challenging, long-horizon manipulation tasks involving contact-rich interactions, bimanual coordination, and tool use. The substantial improvements in both full-task success (32.5 percentage points over the strongest baseline) and normalized ordered-stage progress (20.1 percentage points) indicate the practical effectiveness and robustness of the future-state-driven approach. This suggests that focusing on action-relevant physical outcomes enables more reliable long-term planning and execution.

- **Evidence of Goal-Conditioned Adaptability:** The observed "early signs of emergence," where the policy adapted its interaction strategy to unseen intermediate states (e.g., re-opening a microwave door or using an object to push a door), suggests an improved capacity for goal-conditioned behavioral recomposition. This adaptability is crucial for robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments where deviations from demonstrated trajectories are common. This implies a step towards more generalized and robust robot intelligence that can maintain task objectives under varying conditions.

- **Promising Direction for Future Research:** By demonstrating that a compact future latent state can effectively bridge world understanding and action generation, DELE-w0.5 offers a promising research direction. It suggests that future efforts in embodied AI might benefit from exploring more abstract, action-centric representations of the world's state rather than relying on comprehensive visual simulations. This could pave the way for more scalable and computationally efficient architectures for robot learning and control.
