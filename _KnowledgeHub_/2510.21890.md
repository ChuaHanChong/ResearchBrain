# The Principles of Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Authored by leading researchers from Sony AI, OpenAI, and Stanford, this monograph synthesizes the rapidly evolving field of diffusion models by clarifying their theoretical foundations and unifying diverse formulations into a single continuous-time generative framework. It systematically covers the origins, unifies variational, score-based, and flow-based perspectives, and outlines advancements in sampling and generation techniques.

## Problem

- The rapid proliferation of diverse diffusion model formulations (e.g., DDPM, NCSN, Flow Matching) led to a fragmented literature.
- Researchers and practitioners found it challenging to grasp the underlying mathematical connections and theoretical coherence across different approaches.
- A lack of a systematic and unified understanding hindered entry into the field and efficient progress in research.

## Method

- The monograph provides a comprehensive and authoritative review, acting as a pedagogical guide for the field of diffusion models.
- It rigorously demonstrates the mathematical equivalence of ==variational, score-based, and flow-based approaches== under a ==continuous-time framework==.
- It clarifies the theoretical underpinnings, including the central roles of the ==score function==, SDEs, ODEs, and the ==Fokker-Planck equation==, and systematically covers practical advancements like guidance and efficient sampling.

## Results

- The monograph conclusively demonstrates the mathematical equivalence between **DDPM**, **NCSN/Score SDE**, and **Flow Matching** paradigms, showing they are manifestations of a unified continuous-time generative process.
- It establishes the Fokker-Planck equation as the universal underlying law governing the evolution of marginal probability densities in diffusion models, regardless of stochastic or deterministic dynamics.
- The work systematically categorizes and explains advancements in diffusion models, including guidance mechanisms for controllable generation, training-free acceleration methods (numerical solvers), and training-based acceleration techniques (distillation, learning from scratch like Consistency Models).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Variational, score-based, and flow-based diffusion models are mathematically equivalent formulations of a single continuous-time generative process, all implicitly or explicitly learning a time-dependent vector field.
> - The 'conditioning trick,' which transforms intractable marginal objectives into tractable conditional ones, is a foundational enabler for training across all major diffusion model formulations.
> - The generation process can be fundamentally understood as solving a differential equation (SDE or Probability Flow ODE), providing a principled basis for analyzing and accelerating sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep generative modeling has experienced rapid growth, with diffusion models emerging as a prominent paradigm across various domains, including machine learning, computer vision, and natural language processing. The literature on diffusion models is extensive and distributed, highlighting progress in theoretical foundations, architectural designs, implementation practices, and diverse applications. This dispersed nature of knowledge can present challenges for researchers and practitioners seeking a cohesive understanding of the subject.

"The Principles of Diffusion Models" positions itself as a foundational text intended to synthesize this expansive and diverse body of work. Rather than serving as an exhaustive survey of all diffusion model variants or domain-specific applications, the book focuses on elucidating the core conceptual and mathematical principles that underpin the field. It aims to trace the origins of diffusion models, clarify the interrelations between different formulations, and develop a unified framework that connects intuitive insights with rigorous mathematical grounding. This approach is intended to provide a stable conceptual foundation from which further developments can be understood, thereby serving as both a principled reference for experienced researchers and an accessible entry point for students and newcomers to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "The Principles of Diffusion Models" is to provide a principled and unified foundation for understanding diffusion models. The motivation for this work stems from the rapid expansion and fragmented nature of the diffusion model literature, which has resulted in a broad but often disparate understanding of the field's core theoretical underpinnings.

The book outlines several central themes to achieve its objective:

- **Elucidating Core Concepts and Formulations:** To present the essential concepts and mathematical formulations that are fundamental to diffusion model research. The aim is to equip readers with a foundational understanding necessary to navigate the broader literature, focusing on the underlying principles rather than an exhaustive catalog of specific variants or applications.
- **Unifying Diverse Perspectives:** To demonstrate that diffusion models, at their core, represent a gradual transformation process from noise to data. This central idea has been developed through three primary perspectives: the variational view, the score-based view, and the flow-based view. The book seeks to trace the origins of these formulations, clarify their interrelationships, and integrate them into a coherent understanding that bridges intuitive insight with mathematical rigor.
- **Advancing Practical Applications:** Building upon the foundational principles, the book aims to examine how diffusion models can be further developed to enhance the efficiency of sample generation, provide greater control over the generative process, and inspire novel forms of generative modeling rooted in diffusion principles. This includes addressing challenges related to sampling speed and the ability to guide generation based on specific conditions or preferences.

The intended audience for this book includes researchers, graduate students, and practitioners who possess a basic understanding of deep learning or deep generative modeling. By providing a principled and mathematically grounded analysis, the authors aim to enable readers to interpret various diffusion model formulations within a cohesive framework, confidently apply existing models, and pursue new research directions in this evolving area. The book's scope explicitly excludes rapidly changing topics such as architectural specifics, hyperparameter tuning, empirical benchmark comparisons, or hardware engineering, prioritizing durable conceptual foundations over transient technical details.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The book employs a structured expository methodology, organizing the complex landscape of diffusion models into a progressive narrative across four main parts. This approach systematically builds conceptual and mathematical foundations, tracing the evolution of diffusion models from their origins to advanced topics in sampling and generative efficiency.

- **Part A: Introduction to Deep Generative Modeling.** This initial section establishes the context by reviewing fundamental goals, mathematical setups (e.g., Kullback–Leibler divergence, maximum likelihood estimation, Fisher divergence), and challenges inherent in deep generative modeling, particularly the intractability of normalizing constants. It introduces prominent generative models such as Energy-Based Models (EBMs), Autoregressive Models, Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Normalizing Flows (NFs), and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), classifying them as either explicit or implicit models. This part sets the stage by identifying VAEs, EBMs, and NFs as conceptual precursors to diffusion models.

- **Part B: Origins and Foundations of Diffusion Models.** This core section develops the three primary perspectives on diffusion models:
 - **Variational View (Chapter 2):** Traces the lineage from VAEs to Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). It explains VAEs, introduces the fixed forward noising process and the learned reverse denoising process of DDPMs, and details the derivation of DDPM's training objective through a tractable Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and the use of conditional probabilities. The section covers epsilon- and x-prediction parameterizations and discusses the iterative sampling process.
 - **Score-Based View (Chapters 3 & 4):** Connects diffusion models to EBMs through the concept of the score function. Chapter 3 introduces score matching and Denoising Score Matching (DSM), highlighting the role of noise injection and Tweedie's formula. It describes Noise Conditional Score Networks (NCSN) and their multi-noise level training and annealed Langevin sampling. Chapter 4 unifies discrete-time methods under the continuous-time Score Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) framework, deriving forward and reverse-time SDEs and introducing the deterministic Probability Flow Ordinary Differential Equation (PF-ODE). It explains how the Fokker-Planck equation ensures marginal consistency and details continuous-time training and sampling.
 - **Flow-Based View (Chapter 5):** Explores the lineage from NFs and Neural ODEs (NODEs) to Flow Matching (FM). It reviews NFs and NODEs, emphasizing the change-of-variables formula and the instantaneous change-of-variables (continuity equation). The FM framework is introduced as a method to learn a velocity field for continuous mass transport between arbitrary distributions, leveraging a conditional strategy for tractable training. The concept of "Reflow" for straightening trajectories is also discussed.
 - **Unifying Principles (Chapter 6):** This chapter synthesizes the three perspectives, formally demonstrating their mathematical equivalences. It highlights the "conditional trick" as a common strategy for tractable training across all formulations and systemizes the various parameterizations (noise, clean data, score, velocity prediction). The Fokker-Planck equation is established as the universal governing principle for density evolution in continuous-time diffusion models.

- **Part C: Sampling of Diffusion Models.** This part addresses practical aspects of controlling and accelerating diffusion model inference without additional training.
 - **Guidance and Controllable Generation (Chapter 8):** Explores methods for steering generative trajectories, including Classifier Guidance, Classifier-Free Guidance, and training-free guidance techniques. It also discusses model alignment using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and its adaptation to diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO).
 - **Sophisticated Solvers for Fast Sampling (Chapter 9):** Frames sampling as an ODE/SDE solving problem and introduces advanced numerical integrators to reduce computational cost. Methods such as Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM), Diffusion Exponential Integrator Samplers (DEIS), and the DPM-Solver family are detailed, along with time-parallel approaches like ParaDiGMs.

- **Part D: Toward Learning Fast Diffusion-Based Generators.** This final part investigates training-based methods for accelerating generation.
 - **Distillation-Based Methods (Chapter 10):** Explores techniques for training a fast student generator to mimic a slow, pre-trained teacher diffusion model. This includes distribution-based distillation (e.g., Variational Score Distillation) and flow map-level distillation (e.g., Progressive Distillation).
 - **Learning Fast Generators from Scratch (Chapter 11):** Focuses on methods that directly learn efficient, few-step generative maps without relying on a pre-trained teacher. This section details Consistency Models (CM), Consistency Trajectory Models (CTM), and Mean Flow (MF), emphasizing their shared objective of learning direct flow maps.

The methodology throughout the book is characterized by a strong emphasis on theoretical grounding, mathematical derivation, and the establishment of conceptual equivalences. Appendices provide foundational background on differential equations, Itô calculus, and Girsanov's theorem, ensuring accessibility for the target audience.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

"The Principles of Diffusion Models" synthesizes a comprehensive understanding of diffusion models, presenting several key findings and establishing unifying principles across diverse formulations:

- **Unified Theoretical Framework:** The book demonstrates that the variational (DDPM), score-based (NCSN, Score SDE), and flow-based (Flow Matching) perspectives on diffusion models are not merely analogous but are mathematically equivalent. This unification is rooted in the shared objective of learning a time-dependent vector field that effectively transports a simple prior distribution to the complex data distribution.
- **The "Conditional Trick" as a Universal Solution:** A fundamental finding is the pervasive use of a "conditioning trick" across all diffusion model formulations. This technique transforms otherwise intractable marginal objectives (e.g., KL divergence between marginals, score matching against marginal scores, flow matching against marginal velocities) into tractable regression problems by conditioning on the initial clean data sample (x_0). This allows for efficient, simulation-free training across various methods.
- **Equivalence of Parameterizations and Forward Flows:** The book establishes that common neural network parameterizations (e.g., epsilon-prediction, x-prediction, score prediction, velocity prediction) are algebraically equivalent and interconvertible. Furthermore, all affine forward noise injection schedules (such as those used in VE, VP SDEs, or Flow Matching) are shown to be equivalent up to time reparameterization and spatial rescaling. This implies that many seemingly distinct design choices are different expressions of the same underlying mathematical target.
- **The Fokker-Planck Equation as the Unifying Principle:** A central result is the identification of the Fokker-Planck equation (and its deterministic counterpart, the continuity equation) as the universal governing law for the evolution of probability densities in continuous-time diffusion models. This equation ensures that the marginal distributions generated by both stochastic and deterministic reverse processes consistently retrace the path defined by the forward noising process.
- **Generation as Differential Equation Solving:** The book frames the sampling process in diffusion models as inherently equivalent to numerically solving an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) (the Probability Flow ODE) or a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) in reverse time. This insight underpins the development of both control strategies and acceleration techniques.
- **Principled Acceleration and Control:**
 - **Guidance:** Controllable generation is systematically explained through the Bayesian decomposition of the conditional score, providing a unified framework for methods like Classifier Guidance, Classifier-Free Guidance, and various training-free guidance approaches.
 - **Fast Solvers:** The analysis of sampling as ODE/SDE solving leads to the application of advanced numerical methods (e.g., exponential integrators, multistep schemes, log-SNR reparameterization). These techniques, exemplified by DDIM, DEIS, and the DPM-Solver family, significantly reduce the number of function evaluations required for high-quality sample generation.
 - **Training-Based Acceleration:** The book introduces two paradigms: distillation-based methods (e.g., Progressive Distillation), which train a fast student model from a slow teacher, and learning-from-scratch methods (e.g., Consistency Models, Consistency Trajectory Models, Mean Flow), which directly learn efficient, few-step generative maps.
- **Generalization to Discrete State Spaces:** The book extends the core probability-transport principle to finite discrete state spaces. It demonstrates that the same three perspectives (variational, score/ratio, flow) apply, with analogous mathematical objects (e.g., Markov transition kernels, probability ratios, jump rates, and the master equation) taking the place of their continuous counterparts.

These findings collectively establish a cohesive theoretical foundation for diffusion models, clarifying their operational mechanisms and providing a systematic lens for their analysis and development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"The Principles of Diffusion Models" is poised to have a substantial impact on the field of deep generative modeling and artificial intelligence more broadly. Its significance lies in its comprehensive synthesis of a rapidly evolving and fragmented area, aiming to elevate the collective understanding of diffusion models from an algorithmic collection to a unified scientific discipline.

- **Standardization and Educational Foundation:** By consolidating diverse formulations into a coherent framework, the book will serve as a foundational text and a critical educational resource. This will standardize terminology, clarify core concepts, and make the complex theory of diffusion models more accessible to students, new researchers, and practitioners, fostering a shared understanding that can accelerate future progress.
- **Advancement of Research Paradigms:** The book's clear articulation of unifying principles, such as the "conditional trick" and the role of the Fokker-Planck equation, is expected to encourage more principled research. By clarifying the mathematical equivalences between different approaches, it can stimulate cross-pollination of ideas and lead to the development of novel algorithms and architectural designs that leverage insights from across the various perspectives.
- **Improved Generative Model Design and Performance:** The detailed analysis of optimal parameterizations, noise schedules, training objectives, and advanced sampling techniques provides actionable insights for model developers. This can lead to the creation of more robust, efficient, and higher-fidelity diffusion models that are better aligned with practical application requirements, such as real-time content generation or data augmentation.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Practicality:** The synthesis of knowledge on guidance mechanisms and acceleration strategies directly addresses critical limitations of early diffusion models. By enabling faster sampling and more precise control over generated outputs, the book contributes to making diffusion models significantly more practical and widely deployable in real-world scenarios across various industries.
- **Conceptual Groundwork for Future AI:** The exploration of the "density-transport backbone" and its extension to discrete state spaces suggests a more general framework for generative modeling. This conceptual generalization could inspire future research in new data modalities, develop generative processes beyond current diffusion paradigms, and inform the design of AI systems capable of understanding and manipulating complex data distributions in novel ways.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** While focusing on theoretical principles, the book's emphasis on practical challenges like sampling speed and control provides a bridge between abstract mathematical concepts and their real-world implications. This balance ensures that theoretical advancements are directly relevant to engineering challenges in generative AI.

In essence, "The Principles of Diffusion Models" is set to become a seminal work that not only clarifies the current state of diffusion models but also lays a robust theoretical and conceptual groundwork for their continued evolution and impact across scientific and technological domains.
