# TWIST: Teleoperated Whole-Body Imitation System

> [!summary] Summary
> A teleoperation system enables real-time whole-body control of humanoid robots through human motion imitation, combining reinforcement learning with behavior cloning to achieve coordinated movements across diverse tasks while maintaining a 0.9-second latency on the Unitree G1 platform.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid robot teleoperation systems struggle with coordinated whole-body control, often limiting themselves to isolated locomotion or manipulation tasks
- Traditional approaches lack accurate real-time tracking and adaptation capabilities for complex human-like movements
- Prior systems often rely on modular controllers that restrict robots to isolated skill sets, lacking the robustness needed for real-time whole-body teleoperation

## Method

- Three-stage system combining motion dataset curation, simulation-based controller training, and real-world deployment
- Teacher-student framework where privileged teacher policy trains with future motion frames while student policy operates on single-frame inputs
- Online motion retargeting system that jointly optimizes 3D joint positions and orientations

## Results

- Enabled diverse whole-body skills including manipulation, locomotion, and expressive motion on Unitree G1, with successful sim-to-sim transfer to the Booster T1 embodiment
- Achieved superior tracking accuracy compared to pure RL or BC approaches
- Maintained approximately **0.9**-second total teleoperation delay

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding even small amounts of online-retargeted in-house motion capture data substantially reduces tracking errors
> - Domain randomization of physical parameters during training improves sim-to-real transfer
> - Simultaneous optimization of positions and orientations in motion retargeting yields smoother reference motions

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The "TWIST" paper directly addresses the challenges in humanoid robot teleoperation, which is a subfield of robotics that focuses on enabling humans to remotely control robots. This research sits at the intersection of several key areas:

- **Humanoid Robotics:** The paper aims to advance the capabilities of humanoid robots, making them more versatile and capable of performing complex, human-like tasks. This aligns with the broader goal of developing robots that can effectively operate in human environments.
- **Teleoperation:** The work contributes to the development of more intuitive and effective teleoperation systems. Traditional teleoperation often suffers from limitations in dexterity and coordination. TWIST aims to overcome these limitations by enabling whole-body control through imitation learning.
- **Imitation Learning:** The paper utilizes imitation learning techniques to train the robot controller. This approach leverages human motion data to guide the robot's learning process, enabling it to acquire complex motor skills.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** Reinforcement learning is combined with behavior cloning to train the controller, enabling it to adapt to real-world conditions and improve its performance over time.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The research addresses the challenge of transferring controllers trained in simulation to real-world robots. Domain randomization and other techniques are used to bridge the gap between the simulated and real environments.
- **Motion Retargeting:** Accurately converting human motion to robot motion is a key component of the system. The paper explores different motion retargeting techniques, including offline and online methods.

**Related Works:**

The paper references several related works, highlighting the current state of the art in humanoid robot teleoperation and control. These include:

- Learning-Based Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Robots
- Teleoperation Systems for Humanoid Robots

The authors position TWIST as an advancement over existing systems by addressing limitations in coordinated whole-body control and robustness. They argue that TWIST enables more versatile and human-like skills compared to previous approaches that often rely on modular controllers or lack accurate real-time tracking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a teleoperation system (TWIST) that enables humanoid robots to perform versatile, coordinated, whole-body skills by imitating human motions in real time.
- **Motivation:**
 - **Enable General-Purpose Robots:** The authors are motivated by the need for general-purpose robots that can live and work alongside humans in household environments. They believe that enabling humanoid robots to perform complex, coordinated actions is crucial for achieving this goal.
 - **Overcome Limitations of Existing Systems:** Current humanoid teleoperation systems often fall short of enabling coordinated whole-body behavior, typically limiting themselves to isolated locomotion or manipulation tasks. TWIST aims to address these limitations by providing a more comprehensive and integrated approach.
 - **Leverage Human Motion as an Interface:** Human motion provides an ideal interface for controlling all degrees of freedom of a humanoid robot. The authors aim to leverage human motion capture data to create a natural and intuitive teleoperation system.
 - **Robustness and Adaptability:** The authors want to develop a robust, adaptive, and responsive whole-body controller that can handle the challenges of real-time teleoperation and adapt to different human movements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TWIST system consists of three main stages:

1. **Curating Humanoid Motion Datasets:**
 - **Data Sources:** Publicly available MoCap datasets (AMASS, OMOMO) and a small, in-house dataset.
 - **Motion Retargeting:** Offline retargeting for large-scale datasets (similar to PHC) and online retargeting for the in-house dataset. The online retargeter jointly optimizes 3D joint positions and orientations.
2. **Training a Whole-Body Controller in Simulation:**
 - **Two-Stage Approach:** A teacher-student framework is used. The teacher policy is trained with privileged access to future motion frames, and the student policy is trained using reinforcement learning and behavior cloning, relying only on a single reference frame at each time step.
 - **Privileged Teacher Policy:** Trained via RL with future reference motion frames as input.
 - **Deployable Student Policy:** Optimized using RL and behavior cloning (BC) to imitate the teacher policy.
 - **Reward Structure:** Emphasizes accurate tracking while penalizing artifacts such as jitter and foot slippage.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Randomization of parameters such as base mass, friction, motor strength, and gravity to improve sim-to-real transfer.
3. **Real-World Humanoid Teleoperation:**
 - **Online Streaming and Retargeting:** High-quality human motions are captured at 120Hz using OptiTrack and retargeted into humanoid motions at 50Hz.
 - **Policy Deployment:** The student policy takes the retargeted motions as input and outputs joint position commands at 50Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Versatile Whole-Body Skills:** TWIST enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to perform a wide range of diverse, human-like skills, including whole-body manipulation, legged manipulation, locomotion, and expressive motion.
- **Superior Tracking Accuracy and Motion Smoothness:** The RL+BC controller achieves superior tracking accuracy and motion smoothness compared to pure RL or BC (DAgger) approaches.
- **Importance of In-House MoCap Data:** Adding even a small set of in-house MoCap sequences, retargeted online to mimic real teleoperation, substantially reduces tracking errors.
- **Learning to Apply Force:** Training policies with end-effector perturbations improves stability during stationary poses and contact-rich tasks.
- **Better Online Retargeter, Better Tracking:** Improving the online retargeter by simultaneously optimizing 3D positions and orientations yields smoother humanoid reference motions and reduces tracking errors.
- **General Framework:** TWIST is proven to be a general framework for diverse embodiments by showing sim-to-sim results for the Booster T1 robot.
- **Teleoperation Delay:** The total teleoperation delay of the system is approximately 0.9 seconds, with the major overhead arising from generating tracking goals.
- **Enhanced Reachability:** The system demonstrates exceptional whole-body reachability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement in Humanoid Robot Teleoperation:** TWIST represents a significant advancement in humanoid robot teleoperation, enabling more versatile, coordinated, and human-like skills than previously achieved.
- **Potential for General-Purpose Robots:** The system contributes to the development of general-purpose robots that can effectively operate in human environments, performing tasks such as household chores, assistance for the elderly, and disaster response.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** By providing a more natural and intuitive teleoperation interface, TWIST can improve human-robot interaction and make it easier for humans to control and collaborate with robots.
- **Applications in Various Fields:** The system has potential applications in various fields, including:
 - **Manufacturing:** Teleoperating robots for complex assembly tasks.
 - **Healthcare:** Assisting surgeons with remote procedures.
 - **Exploration:** Remotely exploring dangerous environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The research provides a valuable foundation for future research in humanoid robot teleoperation, imitation learning, and sim-to-real transfer.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations of the current system, including:

- **Lack of Robotic Feedback:** The system lacks robotic egocentric vision and tactile feedback, which can limit the effectiveness of teleoperation in certain situations.
- **Hardware Reliability:** The current generation of humanoid hardware cannot sustain long-term continuous operation.
- **Dependence on MoCap Systems:** The method relies on a motion capture system, which is not portable and difficult to democratize.

Future work will focus on addressing these limitations, including:

- **Incorporating Robotic Feedback:** Adding robotic egocentric vision and tactile feedback to the system.
- **Improving Hardware Reliability:** Developing more robust and reliable humanoid hardware.
- **Exploring RGB-Based Pose Estimation:** Investigating RGB-based pose estimation techniques to reduce the dependence on MoCap systems.
- **Learning Visuomotor Policies:** Study how to learn visuomotor policies with combined TWIST-collected teleoperation data and egocentric human data.

**Conclusion:**

The "TWIST" paper presents a novel and effective teleoperation system for humanoid robots. By combining imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and motion retargeting techniques, the authors have achieved a significant advancement in the field. The system enables humanoid robots to perform a wide range of versatile, coordinated, whole-body skills, opening up new possibilities for human-robot collaboration and the development of general-purpose robots.
