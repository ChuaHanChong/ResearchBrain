# Conformalized Interactive Imitation Learning: Handling Expert Shift and Intermittent Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> The ConformalDAgger framework enables robots to adaptively quantify uncertainty and strategically query human experts during interactive imitation learning, even with intermittent feedback. Utilizing the new Intermittent Quantile Tracking (IQT) algorithm, this approach helps robots detect distribution shifts and quickly adapt policies, resulting in increased expert feedback requests (e.g., from ~20% to ~60% in simulation upon expert shift) and improved learning efficiency in simulated and hardware tasks.

## Problem

- Robot policies trained on expert demonstrations often degrade in performance due to distribution shifts when deployed in dynamic or new environments, or when the expert's behavior changes.
- Prior uncertainty quantification methods in interactive imitation learning (IIL) do not adapt their estimates online using received feedback, potentially leading to overconfident predictions in the face of deployment-time shifts.
- Existing online conformal prediction algorithms assume continuous access to ground-truth labels, which is not suitable for interactive robot learning where human feedback is inherently intermittent.

## Method

- Developed Intermittent Quantile Tracking (IQT), a novel online ==conformal prediction== algorithm that extends Quantile Tracking (QT) to handle intermittent labels by scaling updates with the observation probability and only applying them when labels are observed.
- Integrated IQT into an interactive imitation learning framework, named ConformalDAgger, which treats the robot's policy as the base model for IQT and uses the calibrated uncertainty (prediction interval size) to trigger expert queries.
- Designed an adaptive querying mechanism where the robot's decision to query the expert is based on the size of the calibrated uncertainty interval, allowing for targeted and efficient feedback collection.

## Results

- In simulated expert shift tasks, ConformalDAgger adaptively increased expert feedback requests (e.g., from approximately **20%** to **60%** of trajectory length) and achieved lower miscoverage rates (max **0.4** vs. baselines max **1.0**), leading to faster policy alignment with the shifted expert.
- On a 7-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulator performing a sponging task, ConformalDAgger significantly increased robot-requested interventions (up to ~35 timesteps of human feedback) when the expert changed their wiping strategy, enabling the robot to learn the new pattern more effectively.
- ConformalDAgger maintained minimal querying behavior and exhibited performance comparable to other methods in stationary expert scenarios, demonstrating that it does not excessively query when uncertainty is genuinely low.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermittent Quantile Tracking (IQT) maintains asymptotic coverage guarantees for uncertainty estimates even when ground-truth labels are observed probabilistically or intermittently, providing theoretical reliability for online interactive learning.
> - Calibrated uncertainty estimates derived from online conformal prediction can effectively detect distribution shifts, particularly changes in expert policy, serving as a robust signal for adaptively querying human experts.
> - Directly incorporating intermittent human feedback into the online uncertainty estimation process allows for real-time adaptation of the robot's confidence, leading to more efficient data collection and faster policy retraining.
