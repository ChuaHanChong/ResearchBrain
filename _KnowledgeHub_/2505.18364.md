# ImLPR: Image-based LiDAR Place Recognition using Vision Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ImLPR introduces a LiDAR Place Recognition pipeline that leverages a novel three-channel Range Image View (RIV) representation and lightweight MultiConv adapters to adapt a pre-trained DINOv2 Vision Foundation Model. The approach consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art LPR methods and generalizes robustly across diverse environments and unseen sensor types, demonstrating the power of effectively adapting vision foundation models to the LiDAR domain.

## Problem

- Traditional LPR methods often lack generalization capabilities across diverse environments, sensor types, and scene variations.
- There is an absence of general-purpose 3D foundation models suitable for large-scale outdoor LPR tasks.
- A significant modality gap hinders the direct and effective application of powerful 2D Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) to 3D LiDAR data, often leading to information loss or inefficient adaptation.

## Method

- The **ImLPR** pipeline converts raw LiDAR point clouds into a novel three-channel ==Range Image View (RIV)== representation, encoding reflectivity, range, and a normal ratio for semantic, geometric, and structural information.
- It adapts a pre-trained ==DINOv2 Vision Transformer (ViT)== by inserting lightweight ==MultiConv adapters== into its frozen layers and fine-tuning only the final two blocks, preserving pre-trained knowledge while bridging the domain gap.
- A ==Patch-InfoNCE loss== is introduced for patch-level contrastive learning, complementing a global objective (==Truncated SmoothAP==) to enhance the discriminability and robustness of both local and global features.

## Results

- ImLPR consistently outperformed state-of-the-art LPR methods across intra-session and inter-session tasks on the HeLiPR dataset, achieving average Recall@1 up to **0.990** and F1 scores up to **0.996**.
- The method demonstrated strong generalization to unseen datasets (MulRan DCC, NCLT) and varied sensor types, maintaining high F1 scores and competitive Recall@1 despite domain shifts.
- The three-channel RIV representation, MultiConv adapters, and Patch-InfoNCE loss were identified as critical components, with the RIV outperforming BEV, adapters preserving VFM knowledge, and patch-level learning enhancing feature robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed multi-channel 2D representation (RIV) can effectively translate 3D LiDAR information into a format compatible with RGB-pretrained VFMs, preserving crucial cues for place recognition.
> - Lightweight, strategically placed adapters enable efficient adaptation of powerful 2D VFMs to new modalities like LiDAR, retaining their extensive pre-trained knowledge without catastrophic forgetting.
> - Integrating patch-level contrastive learning with global descriptor learning significantly improves feature discriminability and robustness, leading to more precise and generalized place recognition performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Place recognition is a fundamental capability for autonomous systems, enabling robots to determine if a location has been previously visited and to localize themselves within a pre-existing map. This field is broadly categorized into Visual Place Recognition (VPR) and LiDAR Place Recognition (LPR).

Recent advancements in VPR have extensively leveraged Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), such as DINOv2 and CLIP, which are pre-trained on vast image datasets. These models have demonstrated an ability to extract robust and transferable features from natural images, leading to significant performance enhancements in VPR tasks.

In contrast, LPR has traditionally relied on task-specific models or techniques tailored for 3D point clouds or their 2D projections (e.g., Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) or Range Image Views (RIV)). The development of 3D foundation models for LiDAR data has been limited, and existing ones are often computationally intensive or designed for specific tasks like object detection or indoor scene analysis, making them less suitable for large-scale outdoor LPR. Directly adapting 2D VFMs to LiDAR data presents a significant modality gap, as LiDAR images (even 2D projections) differ substantially from natural RGB images in terms of channel composition, sparsity, and visual characteristics. Previous attempts to project LiDAR data into 2D images for use with vision models often struggled to fully leverage the pre-trained knowledge of VFMs due to these domain differences, especially with single-channel or overly large multi-channel inputs.

ImLPR addresses this gap by proposing a novel pipeline that effectively bridges the modality differences, allowing a pre-trained 2D VFM (DINOv2) to be applied to LiDAR data for robust place recognition. This approach positions ImLPR as a method that seeks to bring the benefits of foundation models, particularly their generalization capabilities and robust feature extraction, to the LPR domain, thereby overcoming limitations of traditional, domain-specific LPR methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel LiDAR Place Recognition (LPR) pipeline, named ImLPR, that leverages the robust and transferable feature extraction capabilities of pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). The work aims to overcome the inherent challenges of applying 2D VFMs to 3D LiDAR data while retaining the majority of the VFM's pre-trained knowledge.

This objective is motivated by several key limitations observed in the current LPR landscape:

- **Limited Generalization of Traditional LPR Methods:** Conventional LPR approaches often employ specialized architectures trained on specific datasets. While effective within their trained domains, these methods tend to exhibit reduced performance and struggle to adapt to significant scene variations, such as environmental changes or sensor differences, when deployed in diverse outdoor conditions or unseen domains. This lack of broad applicability necessitates more robust and generalizable feature descriptors.
- **Absence of Suitable 3D Foundation Models:** Unlike the visual domain, the 3D domain lacks general-purpose foundation models pre-trained on massive, diverse datasets that are directly applicable to LPR tasks. Existing 3D foundation models are typically focused on object-level perception or indoor scenes and often entail heavy computational costs, rendering them impractical for large-scale LPR.
- **Challenges in Adapting 2D VFMs to LiDAR Data:** Directly utilizing powerful 2D VFMs with LiDAR point clouds is hindered by a substantial modality gap. While projecting 3D point clouds into 2D representations (e.g., Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) or Range Image View (RIV)) is a common strategy, traditional approaches often fail to create 2D images that effectively leverage the rich pre-trained knowledge of RGB-oriented VFMs. Issues include information loss during projection, the use of single-channel inputs, or overly complex multi-channel inputs that do not align with the VFM's training data. This leads to inefficient adaptation or catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge if the VFM is extensively fine-tuned.

To address these motivations, ImLPR proposes a multi-faceted approach:

- **Tailored RIV Representation:** Introduce a novel three-channel Range Image View (RIV) representation for LiDAR point clouds. This representation encodes reflectivity, range, and normal ratio, which are designed to capture semantic, geometric, and structural information in a format that is more amenable to RGB-pretrained VFMs.
- **Effective VFM Adaptation:** Employ a pre-trained DINOv2 VFM as the core feature extractor. To bridge the domain gap without extensive fine-tuning, lightweight MultiConv adapters are strategically inserted into the VFM. This design preserves the majority of the VFM's pre-trained weights, mitigating catastrophic forgetting while enabling effective adaptation to the LiDAR domain.
- **Enhanced Feature Learning with Patch-InfoNCE Loss:** Propose a patch-level contrastive loss, Patch-InfoNCE, which operates on local feature embeddings. This loss is designed to enhance the discriminability and robustness of learned LiDAR features by encouraging consistent representations across corresponding RIV patches, thereby improving local context understanding.

By achieving these objectives, ImLPR aims to set a new standard for LPR performance, particularly in terms of generalization and robustness across diverse environmental conditions and sensor types.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ImLPR is structured as a pipeline that converts raw LiDAR point clouds into a specific 2D image representation, extracts robust features using an adapted Vision Foundation Model (VFM), aggregates these features into a global descriptor, and employs a multi-level contrastive loss for training.

#### 3.1. Overview of ImLPR

The process begins by rendering a LiDAR scan into a three-channel Range Image View (RIV) image. A VFM-based encoder, denoted as *f*, extracts patch-level features from this RIV image. These patch features are then pooled by an aggregator, *h*, to form a global descriptor. The entire mapping function, Ω = *h* ◦ *f*, is optimized using metric learning to ensure that spatially close locations yield descriptors with small distances, and vice-versa, at both global and local feature levels.

#### 3.2. Input Data Processing

Raw LiDAR point clouds, consisting of points *pᵢ* = (*xᵢ*, *yᵢ*, *zᵢ*), are converted into RIV pixel coordinates (*uᵢ*, *vᵢ*) using a standard spherical projection formula involving the point's range (*rᵢ*), vertical field of view (FOV), and image dimensions (*W*, *H*). To enrich the geometric and semantic information, each pixel in the RIV image is assigned three channels:
- **Reflectivity:** Provides semantic cues related to surface properties of objects.
- **Range:** Encodes geometric features such as distance from the sensor and depth variations.
- **Normal Ratio:** A scalar derived from the logarithmic ratio of the largest to smallest singular values of the covariance matrix of *k*-nearest neighboring points. This compact representation summarizes local geometric variations like planarity and edge-like structures, offering a computationally efficient alternative to multi-channel normal vectors.
This three-channel image serves as the input to the subsequent feature extraction stage. For datasets lacking reflectivity, normalized intensity is used as a substitute.

#### 3.3. Feature Extraction with Adapted DINO ViT

ImLPR utilizes a DINO ViT-S/14 model, which is pre-trained on RGB images, as its feature extractor. To adapt this VFM to LiDAR RIV images while preserving its extensive pre-trained knowledge, a specific fine-tuning strategy is employed:
- **Limited Fine-tuning:** Only the final two transformer blocks of the DINOv2 model are fine-tuned. The remaining layers are frozen.
- **MultiConv Adapters (MCAs):** Lightweight convolutional adapters are inserted at regular intervals (every three blocks) among the frozen transformer layers. These adapters refine intermediate patch representations, effectively bridging the domain gap between natural RGB images (on which DINOv2 was pre-trained) and the new LiDAR RIV inputs. The adapters process patch features while token features remain untouched. This design minimizes the number of trainable parameters, preventing catastrophic forgetting and reducing computational overhead compared to full model fine-tuning.

#### 3.4. Feature Aggregation with Optimal Transport

The refined patch features, *F* ∈ ℝ^(H'W'×C), extracted by the adapted DINOv2, are aggregated into a global representation using an optimal transport approach, similar to SALAD.
- A convolutional layer first maps *F* to a score matrix *S* ∈ ℝ^(n×m), which represents the assignment probabilities of local features to *m* learnable cluster centers.
- The Sinkhorn algorithm is then applied to *S* to obtain an optimized assignment matrix *R* ∈ ℝ^(n×m), effectively determining how patches are grouped into clusters.
- Aggregated cluster features, *V* ∈ ℝ^(m×l), are computed as a weighted sum of intermediate feature embeddings (*¯F*) using *R*.
- A compact global embedding *G* ∈ ℝ^e is generated from the global token via linear layers.
- The final global descriptor *g* is formed by concatenating the flattened local cluster features and the global embedding (*g* = [*V*.flatten, *G*]), capturing both fine-grained local patterns and robust global context.

#### 3.5. Patch-level Contrastive Learning

To enhance both local (patch feature-level) and global (descriptor-level) discriminability, ImLPR introduces a Patch-InfoNCE loss in addition to a global objective (Truncated SmoothAP, L_TSAP).
- **Positive Patch Mining:** From two RIV images, corresponding 3D point clouds are transformed to a common world frame using ground truth poses refined by Iterative Closest Point (ICP). Positive patch pairs (*p₁*, *p₂*) are selected if their valid overlapping pixels exceed a threshold (ρ_valid = 0.5) and their mean range difference is below an adaptive threshold (τ_r) computed using Median Absolute Deviation (MAD), ensuring geometric consistency and range coherence.
- **Negative Patch Mining:** Negative patch pairs are selected by enforcing large vertical and horizontal distances from positive patches, considering cylindrical geometry to handle image boundaries.
- **Patch-InfoNCE Loss:** This loss, an extension of InfoNCE, operates on local embeddings of positive and hard negative patch pairs. It encourages high cosine similarity between positive pairs and low similarity with negative pairs, promoting discriminative patch-level embeddings. The final objective function is a weighted sum of Patch-InfoNCE (L_P) and L_TSAP (L_final = L_P + λL_TSAP), balancing local and global learning. Data augmentations, including random yaw rotation, square, cylindrical, and line masking, are also applied to enhance robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several main findings regarding ImLPR's performance and the contribution of its design elements:

- **Superior Performance in Intra- and Inter-Session LPR:** ImLPR consistently outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) LPR methods (LoGG3D-Net, MinkLoc3Dv2, CASSPR, BEVPlace++) across multiple evaluation metrics (Recall@1 and F1 score) on the HeLiPR dataset for both intra-session (same environment, different times within a session) and inter-session (different environments, different times) place recognition. For example, in intra-session tasks, ImLPR achieved an average Recall@1 of 0.976 and F1 score of 0.989 on Roundabout-O and Town-O, surpassing BEVPlace++ (0.952 R@1, 0.977 F1). In inter-session scenarios, ImLPR showed average Recall@1 and F1 scores of 0.990 and 0.996 on HeLiPR Roundabout-O, and 0.989 and 0.995 on HeLiPR Town-O, significantly outperforming all baselines. The F1-Recall curves further illustrate ImLPR's superior balance of precision and recall.

- **Robust Generalization to Unseen Sensors and Environments:** ImLPR demonstrated strong generalization capabilities when tested on unseen datasets (MulRan DCC, NCLT, and HeLiPR Roundabout-V) without additional training. Despite domain shifts, sensor differences, and varying data quality (e.g., discrete intensity values in NCLT or occlusions in MulRan), ImLPR maintained the highest average F1 score and competitive Recall@1, outperforming other methods that showed inconsistent performance across these diverse datasets. This resilience is attributed to its RIV representation and VFM with adapters.

- **Contribution of RIV Image Channels:** Ablation studies confirmed that each of the three RIV channels—reflectivity, range, and normal ratio—contributes distinctly to LPR performance. Reflectivity provides a semantic baseline, range significantly enhances geometric depth understanding, and the normal ratio further refines accuracy by encoding local 3D structural details. The combination of all three channels consistently yielded the best results, highlighting their complementary nature.

- **Effectiveness of RIV Representation over BEV:** The RIV representation consistently outperformed the BEV representation for LPR. Visualizations showed that BEV struggles with distorted features and misidentified empty pixels, while RIV produces more consistent features across scans, better retaining spatial and semantic patterns. The Patch-InfoNCE loss, designed for RIV, further enhanced performance by leveraging range-aware supervision.

- **Importance of MultiConv Adapters:** The MultiConv adapters were crucial for effectively bridging the sensor domain gap and preserving the pre-trained knowledge of DINOv2. Models trained with adapters showed significant performance improvements compared to those without, even with the same amount of fine-tuning. The optimal strategy involved using adapters with minimal fine-tuning of transformer blocks (e.g., last two blocks), as extensive fine-tuning could lead to overfitting and reduced generalization on some datasets.

- **Impact of Data Augmentation:** Various data augmentation strategies, including random yaw rotation, square masking, cylindrical masking, and line masking, cumulatively enhanced ImLPR's robustness. Yaw rotation improved resilience to orientation changes, while masking techniques helped the model handle occlusions and sparse scene representations effectively.

- **Optimal DINOv2 Backbone Dimension and Computational Efficiency:** The DINO ViT-S/14 backbone (384 feature dimension) provided an optimal balance between performance and computational efficiency, achieving top-tier accuracy with the lowest inference time (18.1 ms). Larger backbones (ViT-B/14, ViT-L/14) did not yield proportional accuracy improvements but increased computational cost, suggesting that excessive complexity might lead to overfitting or less effective training on LiDAR RIV images. ImLPR's inference time was comparable to or faster than several SOTA methods while achieving higher Recall@1.

- **Comparison with VPR Models:** Direct application of existing VPR models (SALAD, BoQ, SelaVPR) using RIV inputs resulted in lower LPR performance compared to ImLPR. This finding underscores that a naive adaptation is insufficient; explicit domain adaptation strategies (like ImLPR's adapters) and LiDAR-aware feature learning (e.g., Patch-InfoNCE) are essential for achieving high performance with LiDAR data.

- **Comparison with 3D Foundation Model:** ImLPR, leveraging a 2D VFM, outperformed a re-trained 3D foundation model (PointTransformerV3) in both LPR performance and computational efficiency. ImLPR was lighter (half the parameters) and four times faster, demonstrating the significant advantage of effectively adapting a VFM with massive pre-trained knowledge over 3D-specific models trained on smaller LiDAR datasets.

- **Robustness to Yaw Variation and Spatial Precision:** ImLPR exhibited inherent yaw robustness due to the translation-equivariant nature of its ViT backbone and the horizontal shift-invariance of its optimal transport aggregation. It maintained superior performance even under stringent spatial distance thresholds (e.g., 1m), indicating high localization accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ImLPR framework represents a notable advancement in the field of LiDAR Place Recognition (LPR) with several significant implications:

- **Paradigm Shift in LPR:** ImLPR introduces a paradigm shift by moving LPR beyond traditional task-specific 3D sparse convolution or Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) based methods. Instead, it demonstrates the effectiveness of leveraging powerful pre-trained 2D Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) for LiDAR data. This approach opens new avenues for LPR research by drawing on the extensive knowledge embedded in VFMs trained on vast natural image datasets.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The core contribution of ImLPR lies in its ability to achieve robust and generalizable LPR performance. By effectively bridging the modality gap between LiDAR and natural images through a tailored three-channel Range Image View (RIV) representation and lightweight MultiConv adapters, ImLPR significantly mitigates issues of domain shift. This enables systems to perform reliably in diverse environments, under varying environmental conditions, and with different sensor characteristics, which is crucial for real-world autonomous navigation.

- **Methodological Innovations:** The paper introduces several key methodological innovations:
 - **Novel RIV Representation:** The meticulously designed three-channel RIV (reflectivity, range, normal ratio) effectively translates 3D LiDAR information into a 2D format compatible with RGB-pretrained VFMs, preserving crucial semantic, geometric, and structural cues.
 - **Efficient VFM Adaptation:** The strategy of inserting lightweight MultiConv adapters while largely freezing the pre-trained DINOv2 model offers an efficient solution to adapt VFMs to new modalities without catastrophic forgetting or excessive computational cost.
 - **Patch-level Contrastive Learning:** The proposed Patch-InfoNCE loss enhances local feature discriminability, complementing global descriptor learning and leading to more precise place recognition.

- **Practical Implications for Autonomous Systems:** The superior performance of ImLPR in both intra-session and inter-session scenarios, coupled with its robust generalization across different datasets and sensors, has direct practical implications for autonomous vehicles, robotics, and mapping applications. It promises more reliable initial localization estimates, improved loop closure in SLAM systems, and enhanced ability for robots to operate in previously visited but altered environments. The low inference time (e.g., 18.1 ms for ViT-S/14) also ensures its suitability for real-time applications.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** ImLPR's success in integrating VFMs into the LiDAR domain sets a precedent for further exploration. The paper suggests future work could involve integrating ImLPR with other foundation models (e.g., segmentation models for re-ranking) or developing hierarchical pipelines, which could lead to even more sophisticated and robust place recognition systems.

- **Addressing Limitations:** While primarily focused on homogeneous LPR (same sensor type), the work lays the groundwork for addressing more complex challenges like heterogeneous LPR, which involves matching across different LiDAR sensor types. The strategies for robust adaptation and feature learning will be valuable for future developments in this area.

Overall, ImLPR represents a significant step towards more generalized, robust, and computationally efficient LPR by strategically leveraging the power of Vision Foundation Models, thereby enhancing the capabilities of autonomous systems to operate intelligently in complex, dynamic environments.
