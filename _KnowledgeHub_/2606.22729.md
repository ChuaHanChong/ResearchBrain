# Temporal Logic Guidance for Action-Only Diffusion Policies with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A method enables action-only diffusion policies to satisfy temporal logic constraints at inference time by integrating a separately trained, differentiable world model for state prediction. This approach reduced constraint violations from 84% to 4% while maintaining task success in a can transport task, ensuring objects like cans remain upright.

## Problem

- Modern robot learning policies, especially action-only diffusion models, lack mechanisms for selecting specific desired behaviors from their diverse learned distributions at inference time.
- Existing Signal Temporal Logic (STL) guidance methods for diffusion policies typically require joint generation of actions and states, making them incompatible with prevalent action-only diffusion policy architectures.
- Robots need to adapt to user-specific preferences and dynamic safety constraints in human environments without extensive and costly retraining for each new scenario.

## Method

- A separately trained, differentiable world model is used to predict future state trajectories from the action sequences proposed by an action-only diffusion policy.
- The predicted states are then used to calculate the robustness of a given ==Signal Temporal Logic (STL)== specification, and its gradient is injected into the diffusion policy's denoising process to steer action generation.
- A few additional gradient ascent steps are applied directly to the final action sequence after the diffusion denoising process to further refine it for constraint satisfaction.

## Results

- The proposed guidance method reduced uprightness constraint violations from **84.0%** (base policy) to **4.0%** in a can transport task.
- The average tilt of the can was improved from **8.51**° (base policy) to **1.93**° when guidance was applied, indicating precise adherence to the upright orientation constraint.
- Task success rate was maintained at **100%** with guidance, matching the unguided base policy and outperforming the Sample & Rank baseline (**98.0%**), demonstrating no performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling action generation from state prediction, through a differentiable world model, allows state-dependent formal guidance (like STL) to be applied to a wider range of modern action-only robot policies.
> - Gradient-based guidance within the diffusion denoising process is significantly more effective at enforcing complex temporal constraints than simple sample-and-rank baseline methods.
> - This inference-time guidance can drastically improve adherence to specified constraints (e.g., safety, preferences) without compromising the robot's ability to successfully complete the primary task.
