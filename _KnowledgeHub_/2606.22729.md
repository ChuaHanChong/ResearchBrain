# Temporal Logic Guidance for Action-Only Diffusion Policies with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A method enables action-only diffusion policies to satisfy temporal logic constraints at inference time by integrating a separately trained, differentiable world model for state prediction. This approach reduced constraint violations from 84% to 4% while maintaining task success in a can transport task, ensuring objects like cans remain upright.

## Problem

- Modern robot learning policies, especially action-only diffusion models, lack mechanisms for selecting specific desired behaviors from their diverse learned distributions at inference time.
- Existing Signal Temporal Logic (STL) guidance methods for diffusion policies typically require joint generation of actions and states, making them incompatible with prevalent action-only diffusion policy architectures.
- Robots need to adapt to user-specific preferences and dynamic safety constraints in human environments without extensive and costly retraining for each new scenario.

## Method

- A separately trained, differentiable world model is used to predict future state trajectories from the action sequences proposed by an action-only diffusion policy.
- The predicted states are then used to calculate the robustness of a given ==Signal Temporal Logic (STL)== specification, and its gradient is injected into the diffusion policy's denoising process to steer action generation.
- A few additional gradient ascent steps are applied directly to the final action sequence after the diffusion denoising process to further refine it for constraint satisfaction.

## Results

- The proposed guidance method reduced uprightness constraint violations from **84.0%** (base policy) to **4.0%** in a can transport task.
- The average tilt of the can was improved from **8.51**° (base policy) to **1.93**° when guidance was applied, indicating precise adherence to the upright orientation constraint.
- Task success rate was maintained at **100%** with guidance, matching the unguided base policy and outperforming the Sample & Rank baseline (**98.0%**), demonstrating no performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling action generation from state prediction, through a differentiable world model, allows state-dependent formal guidance (like STL) to be applied to a wider range of modern action-only robot policies.
> - Gradient-based guidance within the diffusion denoising process is significantly more effective at enforcing complex temporal constraints than simple sample-and-rank baseline methods.
> - This inference-time guidance can drastically improve adherence to specified constraints (e.g., safety, preferences) without compromising the robot's ability to successfully complete the primary task.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robots into human environments necessitates their ability to adapt behavior to diverse and often user-specific preferences without extensive retraining. This requirement is particularly pertinent in human-robot interaction (HRI) scenarios where users may possess varying needs or safety considerations. Modern robot learning methodologies, such as diffusion policies, have demonstrated the capacity to learn from varied demonstrations and generate multimodal behaviors, reflecting a distribution of valid task executions. However, while these policies can represent diverse behaviors, they typically lack mechanisms for reliably selecting a *specific* desired behavior mode at inference time. This limitation motivates the development of inference-time guidance mechanisms to steer policy behavior toward particular objectives without requiring resource-intensive retraining.

Within the landscape of inference-time guidance for diffusion models, various approaches have been explored. These include using classifier models, auxiliary networks, visual observations from goal states, and Signal Temporal Logic (STL) expressions as guidance objectives. STL is recognized for its structured and expressive capabilities in specifying complex tasks and safety constraints, and its potential for natural language interfaces in HRI.

Existing methods for STL-based diffusion guidance, however, often require the diffusion policy to jointly generate future actions and states. This joint generation increases the computational complexity and training cost of the policy model itself. Furthermore, many contemporary diffusion policies are designed to generate only action sequences, rendering direct application of state-dependent STL guidance methods incompatible. This research addresses this specific limitation by proposing a novel guidance method tailored for action-only diffusion policies. It integrates a separately learned world model to enable the differentiable evaluation of STL robustness, thereby providing a mechanism to steer action-only policies toward constraint satisfaction without altering the core policy architecture or requiring retraining. This approach positions itself as an advancement in making formal verification and specification methods, such as STL, more broadly applicable to a wider range of modern robot learning policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective method for guiding action-only diffusion policies at inference time to satisfy specified temporal logic constraints, without requiring modifications or retraining of the underlying policy. This objective is driven by several motivations related to enhancing robot adaptability, usability, and safety in human-centric environments:

Firstly, a significant motivation stems from the need for robots to exhibit flexible and adaptable behavior in human environments. As robots become increasingly integrated into personal and shared spaces, their operation must accommodate individual preferences, which can vary considerably across users and situations. For instance, a household robot might need to adjust its dish-loading strategy based on whether a user prioritizes fragile item safety ("space dishes far apart") or efficiency ("stack them tightly"). Diffusion policies are capable of learning and representing such diverse behaviors, but they often lack the intrinsic mechanism to reliably select a specific mode of behavior corresponding to a user's real-time preference during deployment. The research aims to provide this inference-time control, allowing the same robot policy to accommodate varied preferences without requiring costly and time-consuming retraining for each new scenario or user.

Secondly, the researchers are motivated by the utility and expressiveness of Signal Temporal Logic (STL) for specifying complex robot behaviors and safety constraints. STL offers a formal framework to quantify the satisfaction of temporal properties over continuous signals, making it suitable for expressing requirements such as "the robot must not spill the drink" or "the can must remain upright." Furthermore, prior work has demonstrated the feasibility of translating natural language commands into STL specifications, making STL a promising tool for intuitive human-robot interaction. However, a critical limitation in existing STL-based guidance methods for diffusion policies is their reliance on policies that explicitly generate both future actions *and* states. This joint action-state generation adds significant complexity to the diffusion model architecture and training, and renders these methods incompatible with a large class of modern diffusion policies that are designed to produce only action sequences.

Therefore, the core motivation for this specific work is to bridge this gap: to enable the powerful, expressive guidance capabilities of STL to be applied to the widely adopted and simpler action-only diffusion policies. By introducing a separate, learned world model to predict states from actions, the proposed method aims to unlock STL-based guidance for a broader range of robotic systems, facilitating more nuanced control over robot behavior at inference time. This approach seeks to improve constraint adherence and robot safety without compromising the underlying task performance, thereby contributing to more reliable and user-friendly robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology focuses on enabling inference-time Signal Temporal Logic (STL) guidance for diffusion policies that generate only action sequences, rather than joint action-state sequences. This is achieved by incorporating a separately trained, differentiable world model to predict future states, which are then used for STL robustness evaluation and gradient-based steering of the diffusion process.

The core of the approach builds upon the standard inference-time guidance framework for diffusion models. A diffusion policy, denoted as π(a_{t:t+H} | s_t), generates an action sequence a_{t:t+H} of a specified horizon H, conditioned on the current state s_t. The policy's denoising process, which refines a noisy action sequence a_k at step k into a cleaner version a_{k-1}, is typically described by:
a_{k-1} = μ_θ(a_k, s_t, k) + σ_kϵ
where μ_θ is the denoising model, σ_k is the variance for step k, and ϵ ∼ N(0, I) is random noise.

The proposed method modifies this standard denoising update by introducing a gradient-based guidance term:
a_{k-1} = μ_θ(a_k, s_t, k) + λ∇_{a_k} J(ϕ, a_k, s_t) + σ_kϵ
Here, λ is a hyperparameter controlling the guidance strength. The term ∇_{a_k} J(ϕ, a_k, s_t) represents the gradient of a guidance objective J with respect to the action sequence a_k, which is then injected into the denoising process to steer the policy toward constraint satisfaction.

The guidance objective J is defined based on the STL robustness measure. For an STL specification ϕ, the robustness measure, ρ_ϕ(τ), quantifies the degree to which a trajectory τ satisfies ϕ. The objective is formulated as:
J(ϕ, a_{t:t+H}, s_t) = ρ_ϕ(ˆs_{t+1:t+H})
where ˆs_{t+1:t+H} denotes the predicted future state trajectory induced by the proposed action sequence a_{t:t+H}.

A critical component of this methodology is the use of a *separately trained world model*, denoted as f_θ(s_t, a_t). This world model approximates the environment dynamics by predicting the next state ˆs_{t+1} given the current state s_t and action a_t. It is trained independently using the same data distribution from which the diffusion policy learns. By iteratively applying this world model over the action sequence a_{t:t+H}, the full predicted state trajectory ˆs_{t+1:t+H} can be generated from the current state s_t and the candidate action sequence.

The differentiability of the entire process is key. STL robustness measures are typically differentiable with respect to the state trajectory. Since the world model f_θ is also differentiable, the predicted state trajectory ˆs_{t+1:t+H} is differentiable with respect to the input action sequence a_{t:t+H}. This chain of differentiability allows for the computation of the gradient ∇_{a_k} J, which is then used to steer the diffusion process.

Additionally, after the diffusion denoising process is complete and the final action sequence a_0 is obtained, the method applies a small number of gradient ascent steps directly on J with respect to a_0. This supplementary step further refines the action sequence to enhance constraint satisfaction.

This methodological separation of action generation by the diffusion policy and state prediction by the world model offers several advantages. It allows the proposed approach to work with existing action-only diffusion policies without requiring architectural changes. Furthermore, it decouples the horizon over which the diffusion policy predicts actions from the horizon over which STL satisfaction is evaluated, providing flexibility for different task requirements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed temporal logic guidance method for action-only diffusion policies was evaluated on the Can Transport task, a standard benchmark from the Robomimic suite. In this task, a robot is required to grasp a can and subsequently place it into a designated box. The underlying robot policy utilized was a diffusion policy trained on a diverse set of human demonstrations, designed to capture a variety of task execution behaviors.

The specific constraint imposed on the robot's behavior was to maintain the can in an upright orientation throughout the task. This constraint was formally expressed using an STL specification: G(R_zz > cos(5°)), where R_zz represents the alignment of the can's orientation with the world's z-axis (upright direction), and 5° defines a tolerance threshold for deviation from perfectly upright.

Initial observations of the unguided base diffusion policy revealed that while it successfully performed the task, it exhibited multiple behavioral modes for the same initial state. A notable finding was that a substantial portion of these behaviors involved trajectories where the can's uprightness constraint was violated, indicating the policy's tendency to select undesirable modes despite learning from diverse demonstrations. Concurrently, the separately trained world model demonstrated accurate prediction of these multi-step rollouts over the action horizon, affirming its capability to provide reliable state forecasts necessary for constraint evaluation.

Qualitative results, illustrated in Figure 2(b) of the paper, visually demonstrated the effectiveness of the guidance method. When the base policy generated an undesirable rollout that violated the uprightness constraint, the application of the proposed guidance mechanism successfully steered the robot's trajectory towards behaviors that satisfied the constraint, maintaining the can within the specified 5° tilt threshold.

For quantitative evaluation, the method's performance was assessed across 50 rollouts and compared against two baselines:
1. **Base Policy**: The unguided diffusion policy.
2. **Sample & Rank**: A baseline method that samples multiple action sequences from the diffusion policy and selects the one with the highest STL robustness score. This method uses the same robustness objective as the proposed approach.

The evaluation metrics included:
- **Average Tilt (degrees)**: A lower value indicates better adherence to the uprightness constraint.
- **Task Success (%)**: The percentage of trials where the robot successfully completed the can transport task.
- **Violations (%)**: The percentage of trials where the uprightness constraint was violated.

The results, summarized in Table I, are as follows:

| Method | Avg. Tilt (°) | Succ. (%) | Viol. (%) |
| :---------------- | :------------ | :-------- | :-------- |
| Base Policy | 8.51 | 100.0 | 84.0 |
| Sample & Rank | 8.42 | 98.0 | 82.0 |
| Guidance (Ours) | **1.93** | **100.0** | **4.0** |

Analysis of these results indicates that the proposed guidance method significantly improved constraint satisfaction. The average tilt was reduced from 8.51° (Base Policy) and 8.42° (Sample & Rank) to a substantially lower 1.93° with guidance. Crucially, the percentage of constraint violations decreased from 84.0% for the base policy and 82.0% for Sample & Rank to a mere 4.0% when the guidance was applied. This improvement in constraint adherence was achieved while maintaining a 100% task success rate, matching that of the unguided base policy and outperforming the Sample & Rank baseline which showed a slight drop to 98.0% success.

The comparative performance suggests that the gradient-based guidance mechanism is more effective at steering the policy towards compliant behaviors than a simple sampling-based selection approach. The authors posit that this gradient-based steering may be capable of recovering or reinforcing desired behavior modes that might be underrepresented or less frequently sampled within the learned distribution of the diffusion policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of robot learning and control, particularly at the intersection of diffusion policies and formal methods. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several key aspects:

Firstly, the most direct impact is the **extension of Signal Temporal Logic (STL) guidance to action-only diffusion policies**. Prior STL guidance methods for diffusion models typically required the policy to generate both actions and predicted states, increasing their complexity and limiting compatibility with the growing number of modern diffusion policies designed to output only action sequences. By introducing a separate, differentiable world model, this work effectively decouples action generation from state prediction, allowing formal temporal logic constraints to be applied to a broader range of robotic systems without requiring fundamental architectural changes or retraining of the core policy. This enhances the practical applicability of formal methods in robot control.

Secondly, the approach **facilitates more adaptive and responsive human-robot interaction**. Robots operating in human environments must often conform to user-specific preferences or dynamic safety requirements without the burden of continuous retraining. The ability to inject STL guidance at inference time means that a single, pre-trained diffusion policy can be dynamically steered to satisfy diverse, context-dependent constraints (e.g., "keep the can upright," "avoid splashing"). This capability reduces operational costs, increases the versatility of deployed robots, and improves the user experience by allowing for real-time behavioral adjustments based on human intent.

Thirdly, the demonstrated improvement in **constraint adherence while maintaining task performance** contributes to enhanced robot safety and reliability. In the evaluated Can Transport task, the method drastically reduced constraint violations from over 80% to 4% without compromising task success. This capacity to steer behavior towards formally specified safety or operational constraints is critical for deploying robots in sensitive or collaborative environments where unpredictable or non-compliant actions could lead to undesirable outcomes. The approach offers a mechanism to instill verifiable safety guarantees into the inherently probabilistic nature of diffusion policies.

Looking forward, the research lays groundwork for addressing **more complex and long-horizon temporal constraints**. The authors highlight that the decoupled nature of the world model allows for differing prediction horizons between action generation and STL evaluation. This architectural flexibility could be leveraged in future work to learn world models that predict at a higher level of abstraction (e.g., automaton states relevant to the STL specification), rather than raw environment states. Such an advancement would be crucial for tackling arbitrarily complex task specifications and extending the guidance over significantly longer operational timelines, which remains a challenge for most existing STL guidance methods. This potential for scalable, abstract prediction could unlock novel capabilities for robust, formally guided autonomous systems.

In summary, this work provides a practical and effective method for integrating formal temporal logic guidance with contemporary action-only robot diffusion policies. It contributes to greater adaptability, safety, and reliability in robot operations, with clear implications for advancing human-robot interaction and extending the reach of formal verification techniques in complex robotic tasks.
