# Imagine-RL: Residual-Confidence-Guided Cross-Attention for World-Model-Augmented VLA Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Imagine-RL augments noise-space reinforcement learning post-training of a frozen flow-matching VLA with action-conditioned visual-torque imagination. A frozen latent world model predicts compact future visual and torque features per candidate action chunk; a cross-attention critic queries observed history and predicted futures, down-weighting future tokens by prediction-residual confidence. Across four real contact-rich tasks with 100 RL trajectories, average success rises 23.6% over DSRL and 60% over VLA baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action policies remain vision-centric, so contact phenomena like resistance, slippage and jamming stay only indirectly observable during contact-rich manipulation.
- Force-aware VLAs feed torque into action generation only, never into value estimation, so critics cannot judge whether a candidate action will succeed.
- Latent-noise RL critics score only current observation and action; world-model approaches supply visual futures alone, with pixel-heavy reconstruction and no force-conditioned imagination for value learning.

## Method

- **VTLWM**: a frozen JEPA-style visual-torque latent world model extending ==LeWorldModel==, autoregressively predicting 10 future latent visual and normalized-torque states from 15-step history, no pixel reconstruction.
- **Residual-confidence-guided cross-attention critic**: an image-state-action query attends over history and predicted-future tokens; previous-window prediction residuals become additive ==log-confidence attention biases== suppressing unreliable future tokens.
- ==Three-stage SAC loop==: action critic trained with Bellman targets on world-model features, distilled into a noise critic, which trains the latent-noise actor while VLA and VTLWM stay frozen.

## Results

- Over **50** trials per task on four real dual-arm contact-rich tasks, average success rate improved **23.6%** relative over DSRL and **60%** over both base and torque-aware VLA.
- World model prediction held up: visual latent cosine similarity averaged **0.913**, torque MAE **0.0441** (**1.00%** of the plus-minus 4.3799 range) across the four tasks.
- Critic discrimination margin between successful and failed trajectories rose from **0.975** to **1.228** (**26.0%** relative); press-button gained **46%** success with **100** online trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Value estimation, not just action generation, benefits from force sensing: critics scoring predicted contact consequences separate visually similar success and failure cases.
> - Prediction residuals from a previous window are a cheap self-supervised reliability signal, usable as attention bias to discount a drifting world model's outputs.
> - Latent-space imagination suffices for critic supervision; pixel reconstruction and backbone fine-tuning are unnecessary, though autoregressive rollout error still limits usable horizon.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Contact-rich robot actions can look alike in a camera image yet have different physical consequences. A press may or may not engage a button, and torque can reveal contact that vision alone misses. The practical bottleneck is the expense of collecting real-robot experience to improve a capable but imperfect pretrained policy.

Imagine-RL lets an action critic consult both what the robot has observed and what a model predicts a candidate action will cause. It also discounts predicted details that have proved unreliable. In ordinary terms, the critic gets a cautious look ahead before the robot's policy is updated. Across four real-robot tasks, success is higher than with DSRL after the reported interaction budget.

Success rates (%) on Socket Plug-in, Press-button, USB Plug-in, and Peg-in-hole for π₀, TA-VLA, DSRL, and Ours. Ours is highest in all four groups. On USB Plug-in, DSRL is already high, so the visible difference there is small. Rates are from 50 trials per task, after 50 episodes of warm-up and 50 episodes of RL training.

### 2. From latent noise to imagined consequences

A VLA is a vision-language-action policy. Here, the frozen policy maps an observation and a latent-noise input to an action chunk. A lightweight learned noise-space actor selects the input to that frozen VLA. It does not directly learn robot actions or fine-tune the VLA.

This setup comes from DSRL, which already adapts a frozen generative robot policy by learning in its latent-noise space. [[2506.15799|Wagenmaker et al., "Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning"]] DSRL's critic is world-model-free.

Imagine-RL adds a separate action critic. For each candidate chunk, this critic queries a frozen visual–torque latent world model (VTLWM) for predicted consequences. It then uses those predictions, together with observed evidence, to estimate action value. The model predicts compact future representations rather than reconstructing pixels. The VLA and world model remain frozen during RL.

Training runs through a chain of three components:

1. The action critic evaluates a decoded candidate chunk.
1. A noise critic learns to match the action critic's values for VLA outputs generated from sampled noise.
1. The noise-space actor is updated to maximize the noise critic.

This is how predictive action values supervise the actor without making it or the VLA plan in pixels.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The four dual-arm contact-rich tasks are Socket Plug-in, Press-button, USB Plug-in, and Peg-in-hole. On these tasks, the reported average task success rises by **23.6% relative to DSRL**, which is not a 23.6-percentage-point increase. The four-task mean is 68% for Imagine-RL versus 55% for DSRL. These results follow 50 warm-up and 50 additional interaction trajectories per task, with each method evaluated for 50 trials per task.

Imagine-RL and DSRL share the pretrained VLA backbone and the same online-interaction amount. The paper also reports a 60% average relative improvement over π₀ and TA-VLA. Here, "efficient" means a reported interaction budget. It does not mean less elapsed time or fewer attempts.

The second comparison tests the critics directly. For each task, each independently trained critic scores the same executed action chunks under the same reward setting. These chunks come from a held-out set of 10 successful and 10 failed trajectories. The margin is the mean standardized Q for successful trajectories minus the mean for failed ones. Across tasks, the mean margin is 0.975 for DSRL and 1.228 for Imagine-RL, a 26.0% relative increase. This measures value separation, not task success.

Normalized Q-values (within-critic z-score) for DSRL and Imagine-RL on successful and failed held-out trajectories in each task. In every task, the Imagine-RL success/failure margin is larger. The difference is smallest for Press-button. Dots are final retained query-step values.

The paper does not report an ablation that isolates residual-based confidence weighting from the other added world-model features. So the comparisons do not establish that the confidence gate specifically caused the success or discrimination gains. They support the added critic's predictive-feature approach as a whole.

### 4. The boundary

Imagine-RL's contribution is to treat imagined visual–torque futures as evidence for evaluating a candidate action, not as pixel-level plans executed by the robot. This is demonstrated on four real-robot tasks. The authors note that autoregressive prediction quality declines with rollout length because of compounding error.
