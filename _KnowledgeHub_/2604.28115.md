# FreeOcc: Training-Free Embodied Open-Vocabulary Occupancy Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> FreeOcc introduces a training-free framework for open-vocabulary occupancy prediction, incrementally constructing 3D semantic maps from monocular or RGB-D inputs without requiring 3D annotations or ground-truth poses. The system demonstrated over 2x improvement in occupancy IoU and mIoU compared to self-supervised baselines on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet and exhibited robust zero-shot generalization to unseen environments on the ReplicaOcc benchmark, where learning-based methods failed.

## Problem

- Existing embodied occupancy prediction methods rely on costly voxel-level 3D annotations and accurate camera poses, limiting scalability and generalization to novel environments.
- Learning-based approaches tend to overfit training data, leading to poor zero-shot performance when deployed in previously unseen environments or with different sensor configurations.
- Current 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) based SLAM systems often prioritize photometric consistency for view synthesis, leading to geometric inaccuracies and ambiguities that are suboptimal for dense volumetric occupancy reasoning.

## Method

- **FreeOcc** employs an online, training-free framework that incrementally builds open-vocabulary 3D occupancy maps from monocular or RGB-D image streams.
- The system utilizes a four-layer pipeline that integrates a SLAM backbone for global geometry, a ==geometrically consistent 3D Gaussian construction==, open-vocabulary semantic association via pre-trained ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)==, and a ==Gaussian-to-occupancy projection==.
- A ==geometrically anchored Gaussian update strategy== fixes Gaussian centers to SLAM points and uses geometry-aware initialization to ensure multi-view geometric consistency, addressing 3DGS-SLAM ambiguities.

## Results

- Achieved over **2x** improvement in IoU (**34.40**) and mIoU (**15.84**) on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet using RGB-D input, compared to self-supervised baselines (e.g., GaussianOcc: **10.17** IoU, **4.34** mIoU), without any task-specific training.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization on the new ReplicaOcc benchmark, achieving **55.65** IoU and **20.90** mIoU with RGB-D, while all learning-based baselines largely failed to generalize with near-zero scores.
- The geometrically consistent Gaussian update strategy significantly enhanced the fidelity of 3D Gaussian construction, yielding the highest geometric IoU with average improvements of **76.1%** (monocular) and **54.0%** (RGB-D) over other 3DGS-SLAM backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By fixing Gaussian centers to SLAM-derived points and employing geometry-aware initialization, 3D Gaussian representations can achieve superior geometric consistency, which is crucial for accurate volumetric occupancy mapping.
> - Off-the-shelf vision-language models can be effectively integrated into a 3D mapping pipeline to provide language-aligned, open-vocabulary semantic understanding without requiring explicit 3D semantic annotations.
> - A training-free, component-based approach combining robust SLAM, geometrically-anchored 3D Gaussians, and VLM semantics can achieve strong zero-shot generalization to novel environments, surpassing learning-based methods prone to overfitting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability for autonomous agents to construct and comprehend their surroundings from egocentric observations is fundamental for lifelong autonomy and effective interaction within open environments. This necessitates scene representations that are dense, semantically complete, and globally spatially consistent, extending beyond mere geometric reconstruction.

Historically, point clouds derived from depth sensing, structure-from-motion, and SLAM have served as a core representation in robotic perception. However, their unstructured nature and irregular sampling limit their utility for downstream reasoning tasks like collision checking and motion planning. Recent advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provide a compact, continuous, and differentiable representation by augmenting 3D samples with anisotropic extent and opacity, improving upon point clouds for differentiable rendering. Nevertheless, 3DGS is typically optimized under photometric supervision, which can lead to geometric inaccuracies, depth distortions, and view-inconsistent artifacts. Furthermore, extracting clear geometric boundaries from 3DGS often relies on heuristic density thresholds, resulting in ambiguous object extents.

In contrast, occupancy maps discretize space into occupied, free, and unknown regions, offering explicit geometric boundaries that are crucial for direct use in collision checking and motion planning. Motivated by the complementary strengths of 3DGS and occupancy maps, recent research has explored combining 3D Gaussian primitives with voxelized occupancy representations, leading to the embodied occupancy prediction task. This task involves estimating semantic occupancy volumes from Gaussians constructed from egocentric observations.

Existing methods for embodied occupancy prediction have demonstrated progress in geometric and semantic accuracy, including real-time inference capabilities. However, a significant limitation is their reliance on extensive, voxel-level 3D annotations and the assumption of accurate camera poses during inference. Such supervision is costly to obtain and results in models that often generalize poorly to novel environments, unseen viewpoints, or different sensor configurations. While self-supervised approaches aim to reduce annotation requirements and incorporate open-vocabulary semantics using vision-language models (VLMs), they typically still depend on accurate camera poses during both training and inference and exhibit overfitting to specific scenes.

FreeOcc addresses these limitations by introducing a training-free framework for open-vocabulary occupancy prediction. It aims to eliminate the need for occupancy, pose, and semantic annotations, as well as any dedicated learning stage. By integrating SLAM-derived geometry with 3D Gaussian representations and VLM-based semantics, FreeOcc seeks to enable robust, generalizable, and incremental mapping that bridges continuous surface modeling with discrete volumetric reasoning, directly supporting embodied agents in unconstrained real-world settings. This positions FreeOcc as an effort to reduce dependency on data-intensive training and improve real-world deployability for semantic scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FreeOcc is to overcome the limitations of existing occupancy prediction methods by developing a framework that is training-free, open-vocabulary, and robustly generalizable to novel environments without requiring explicit 3D annotations or ground-truth camera poses.

The specific motivations and objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Eliminate Annotation Dependency and Training Stages**: Existing learning-based methods for occupancy prediction, both fully supervised and self-supervised, necessitate substantial 3D annotations (voxel-level occupancy, semantics, or ground-truth camera poses) and extensive training. This process is resource-intensive and limits scalability. FreeOcc aims to bypass these requirements entirely by being a training-free paradigm, directly processing sensor inputs for occupancy prediction.
- **Improve Generalization to Novel Environments**: Learning-based approaches tend to overfit the training data distribution, leading to a significant degradation in performance when deployed in previously unseen environments, with different visual characteristics, camera intrinsics, or metric scales. A key motivation for FreeOcc is to achieve strong zero-shot generalization capabilities across diverse indoor scenes, addressing this limitation.
- **Address Geometric Ambiguity in 3DGS-SLAM**: Current 3DGS-SLAM systems often decouple the optimization of Gaussian scene representations from the underlying point-based SLAM map. Gaussian parameters are primarily updated for photometric rendering consistency, while geometric consistency is enforced by the SLAM backend. This decoupling can lead to geometric ambiguity, resulting in spatially inconsistent 3D geometry that is suboptimal for dense volumetric occupancy reasoning. FreeOcc aims to resolve this by introducing a geometrically consistent Gaussian update strategy.
- **Enable Open-Vocabulary Semantic Understanding**: Traditional occupancy prediction methods typically operate with a closed set of predefined semantic categories, requiring specific semantic annotations. For robots operating in dynamic and diverse real-world environments, the ability to query objects with arbitrary language prompts is crucial. FreeOcc is motivated to integrate off-the-shelf vision-language models to facilitate open-vocabulary semantic association with 3D geometry, allowing for flexible, language-driven scene understanding without explicit semantic labels.
- **Support Incremental and Online Operation**: Embodied agents require systems that can incrementally construct and refine scene representations from a continuous stream of egocentric observations. FreeOcc is designed to operate in an online, streaming fashion, building globally consistent occupancy maps as new sensor data arrives, suitable for active exploration and interaction.
- **Provide Flexible Input Modalities**: Recognizing that robotic systems can be equipped with various sensors, FreeOcc is motivated to support both monocular RGB and RGB-D input sequences, offering flexibility in deployment across different sensing platforms.
- **Establish a Benchmark for Generalization**: To rigorously evaluate the cross-dataset generalization capabilities of occupancy prediction methods, the authors introduce ReplicaOcc, a new test-only benchmark for indoor open-vocabulary occupancy prediction, motivated by the need for more comprehensive generalization assessment than existing single-dataset evaluations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FreeOcc is designed as an online, training-free system that incrementally constructs an open-vocabulary 3D occupancy map from monocular or RGB-D image streams. It employs a multi-level scene representation maintained and refined continuously through a four-layer pipeline:

**A. Layer 1: SLAM Backbone (Point Cloud Map)**
The initial stage of FreeOcc involves processing incoming observations (monocular or RGB-D image sequences) to estimate camera poses and reconstruct sparse 3D geometry. The framework is compatible with various SLAM systems; for this work, DROID-SLAM is utilized due to its global geometric consistency and robustness, particularly with monocular input. DROID-SLAM performs joint optimization over long temporal windows, providing a stable set of globally consistent camera poses and accumulated 3D points. These outputs serve as a fundamental, unified coordinate frame and spatial reference for subsequent mapping stages, mitigating geometric drift in long-horizon operations.

**B. Layer 2: Geometrically Consistent 3D Gaussian Construction (3D Gaussian Map)**
This layer addresses the geometric ambiguity observed in existing 3DGS-SLAM systems, where Gaussian optimization primarily focuses on photometric consistency for view synthesis, potentially at the expense of precise geometry. FreeOcc proposes a "geometrically anchored" Gaussian update strategy.
The scene is represented by a set of 3D Gaussian primitives, each defined by its 3D mean (μ), anisotropic scale (s), rotation (r), opacity (o), color (c), and a language-aligned open-vocabulary feature (f).
To maintain geometric consistency, Gaussian centers (μ) are fixed to the 3D points provided by the SLAM backbone (P_t). The optimization then focuses on other Gaussian parameters (s, r, o, c, f).
A geometry-aware initialization is applied to the anisotropic scale. For each pixel ray, a ray-aligned anisotropic scale is initialized where the Gaussian extents perpendicular (s⊥) and parallel (s∥) to the viewing ray are controlled, with s∥ being a user-controlled elongation ratio (γ) times s⊥. This initialization models Gaussians as thin ellipsoids aligned with sensor rays, providing a prior that reduces ambiguity during optimization.
The optimization objective minimizes the rendering loss (L2 norm of RGB and depth differences between rendered and observed images) while constraining Gaussian means to the SLAM points, thus ensuring multi-view geometric consistency rather than solely view synthesis quality.

**C. Layer 3: Open-Vocabulary Semantic Association (Semantic Map)**
To enable open-vocabulary 3D querying without fixed label spaces, this module leverages pre-trained open-vocabulary segmentation models (e.g.,). These models generate dense, language-aligned embeddings (z(u)) for each pixel in an input image.
These 2D pixel-wise embeddings are then lifted into 3D space using the depth information provided by the SLAM module. Each lifted 3D point's embedding is associated with its corresponding geometrically anchored Gaussian in the existing 3D Gaussian map. This process enriches each Gaussian primitive with a language-aligned feature, creating Language-Embedded (LE) Gaussians. This layer allows for language-driven semantic querying of the 3D map without requiring voxel-level semantic supervision.

**D. Layer 4: Gaussian-to-Occupancy Projection (Occupancy Map)**
The final stage converts the continuous LE-Gaussian representation into a dense, discrete volumetric occupancy map. This process involves two main steps:
1. **Geometric Occupancy Determination**: For any query 3D location (x) within the voxel grid, FreeOcc retrieves its neighboring LE-Gaussian primitives. Each neighboring Gaussian (G_k) contributes a spatial support (α_k(x)) based on its 3D mean and covariance. These individual contributions are aggregated using probabilistic exclusion to compute a combined occupancy probability (α(x)) for the voxel at location x, indicating whether it is occupied or free.
2. **Semantic Propagation and Open-Vocabulary Querying**: The per-Gaussian language-aligned features (f_k) are propagated to the voxel grid. This is achieved by computing the posterior responsibility of each Gaussian for the voxel location, akin to a local Gaussian mixture model. The voxel's aggregate language feature (f(x)) is then calculated as a posterior expectation of the features from contributing Gaussians. This results in a language-embedded occupancy (LE-occupancy) map. For an arbitrary text query (e.g., "chair," "lamp"), a text encoder (e.g., CLIP) generates a text embedding. The semantic score for a voxel with respect to a queried category is then determined by computing the cosine similarity between the voxel's normalized language feature (f̂(x)) and the normalized text embedding (t̂_c) of the query. Semantics are typically reported only for voxels identified as occupied.

This four-layer architecture, executed in a fully streaming fashion, enables FreeOcc to incrementally build a comprehensive, open-vocabulary 3D occupancy map from raw sensor inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FreeOcc's performance was evaluated on three datasets: EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet, the newly introduced ReplicaOcc benchmark, and EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet-mini for ablation studies. Performance was measured using Intersection over Union (IoU) and mean IoU (mIoU) for global scene occupancy.

**A. Occupancy Prediction Evaluation on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet:**
On EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet, FreeOcc was compared against fully supervised methods and self-supervised learning methods (GaussianOcc, GaussTR), the latter also requiring ground-truth camera poses.
- FreeOcc (monocular input) achieved 31.29 IoU and 13.86 mIoU.
- FreeOcc (RGB-D input) achieved 34.40 IoU and 15.84 mIoU.
- These results represent over 2x improvement in both IoU and mIoU compared to self-supervised baselines (GaussianOcc: 10.17 IoU, 4.34 mIoU; GaussTR: 15.63 IoU, 4.95 mIoU). This was achieved without any task-specific training or the need for ground-truth camera poses at inference.
- While supervised methods generally achieved higher scores (e.g., RoboOcc: 53.3 IoU, 44.05 mIoU), FreeOcc operates without any 3D annotations, highlighting its annotation-free nature. The authors noted that ground-truth labels in EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet can sometimes be coarse or deviate from visual evidence, potentially affecting comparative scores for open-vocabulary models.

**B. Zero-shot Generalization on ReplicaOcc:**
The ReplicaOcc benchmark was introduced to specifically evaluate zero-shot generalization to unseen environments and label spaces. Models trained on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet were directly transferred to ReplicaOcc without fine-tuning.
- FreeOcc (monocular input) demonstrated strong zero-shot performance with 46.81 IoU and 16.93 mIoU.
- FreeOcc (RGB-D input) further improved to 55.65 IoU and 20.90 mIoU.
- In contrast, all learning-based baselines (both fully supervised and self-supervised) largely failed to generalize, producing near-zero per-class scores and mIoU. This failure was attributed to domain gaps in appearance, camera parameters, and metric scale, leading to overfitting of the training distribution by learning-based methods. These results indicate FreeOcc's ability to maintain robust geometric and semantic reasoning across different environments without retraining.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
1. **Comparison with 3DGS-SLAM backbones**: FreeOcc was compared against state-of-the-art 3DGS-SLAM systems (e.g., Photo-SLAM, MonoGS, DROID-Splat) in terms of geometric IoU. FreeOcc achieved the highest geometric IoU in both RGB and RGB-D settings, with average improvements of 76.1% (monocular) and 54.0% (RGB-D) over the next-best method. This indicates that FreeOcc's geometry-consistent Gaussian update strategy enhances the fidelity of 3D Gaussian construction for occupancy mapping.
2. **Component-wise ablation**:
 - Removing both Geometrically Anchored Gaussian Updates (GAGU) and Geometry-aware initialization (G-ini) significantly reduced IoU/mIoU and FPS.
 - Introducing GAGU alone improved IoU/mIoU and increased efficiency by 1.5x to 2.8x.
 - Adding G-ini further enhanced IoU/mIoU with minimal runtime impact. These findings confirm that GAGU enforces long-term geometric consistency and accelerates updates, while G-ini contributes to more accurate occupancy reconstruction through improved initialization.

**D. Exploratory Experiments:**
- **Influence of individual network components**: Replacing the default SLAM backbone with MASt3R-SLAM or VGGT-SLAM generally improved IoU and mIoU, demonstrating the modularity of FreeOcc and its potential to benefit from stronger geometric estimation. Using alternative open-vocabulary semantic segmentation models (SEEM, DINOv2) maintained comparable IoU but resulted in lower mIoU, suggesting semantic occupancy quality is sensitive to the VLM component.
- **Performance gap analysis**: Using ground-truth poses significantly improved IoU (from 34.40 to 45.06) and mIoU (from 15.84 to 21.34) in the RGB-D setting, indicating the impact of pose accuracy. Replacing the open-vocabulary module with a closed-set segmentation model also improved mIoU (to 20.42), implying better consistency with fixed benchmark taxonomies. Combining ground-truth poses with closed-set semantics further boosted mIoU to 27.39. These analyses suggest that the remaining performance gap to fully supervised methods is primarily due to pose alignment and semantic category assignment specific to fixed-label benchmarks.
- **Quantitative open-vocabulary validation**: On ReplicaOcc, mIoU decreased as the vocabulary expanded (Top-10: 31.06 mIoU, Top-40: 12.01 mIoU), which is expected as lower-frequency categories often correspond to smaller or visually ambiguous objects.
- **Desktop widgets**: The study noted that reconstructing fine-grained objects on a desk (e.g., small cups) required a significantly smaller voxel size (0.005 m vs. standard 0.08 m), indicating potential for future work on adaptive dynamic resolution occupancy grids.

**E. Qualitative Results:**
Qualitative visualizations corroborated the quantitative findings. FreeOcc consistently reconstructed coherent geometric structures and meaningful semantic occupancy across datasets, unlike learning-based methods that often produced incomplete or empty maps on ReplicaOcc. The system also demonstrated successful localization and retrieval of objects through open-vocabulary queries (e.g., "basket," "clock," specific colored cups). Furthermore, visualizations showcased the incremental construction of multi-layer maps in both benchmark and real-world indoor/outdoor environments, with well-aligned semantic and occupancy maps grounded in the underlying point cloud and Gaussian representations. Real-world deployment using a live RGB-D stream with a VLM-generated semantic cues was also demonstrated.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in FreeOcc holds several significances and potential impacts on the fields of embodied AI, robotics, and 3D scene understanding:

- **Novelty of Training-Free Paradigm**: FreeOcc introduces the first training-free framework for open-vocabulary occupancy prediction. This paradigm shift significantly reduces the dependency on large-scale 3D annotations and resource-intensive training pipelines, which are major bottlenecks for current learning-based methods.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities**: A central contribution is the demonstration of robust zero-shot generalization to novel, unseen environments. Unlike existing supervised and self-supervised learning methods that typically overfit training distributions and fail to transfer, FreeOcc maintains consistent performance across different datasets and real-world scenarios. This addresses a critical limitation for deploying embodied agents in diverse, unconstrained environments.
- **Practicality for Real-World Robotics**: By operating incrementally and online from standard monocular or RGB-D video streams without requiring ground-truth camera poses or prior training, FreeOcc offers a practical solution for real-world robotic deployment. The ability to directly process raw sensor data and construct rich 3D scene representations makes it suitable for applications in navigation, manipulation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Open-Vocabulary Semantic Understanding**: The integration of off-the-shelf vision-language models enables language-driven querying of the 3D environment with arbitrary text prompts. This open-vocabulary capability allows robots to understand and interact with objects beyond a predefined, closed set of categories, providing a more flexible and human-interpretable scene understanding.
- **Improved Geometric Fidelity**: The proposed geometrically consistent 3D Gaussian update strategy enhances the quality of the underlying 3D Gaussian map, addressing ambiguities inherent in photometric-only optimization. This leads to more spatially consistent and accurate geometric boundaries, which are crucial for reliable occupancy prediction and downstream tasks like collision avoidance.
- **Foundational for Future Research**: FreeOcc's modular architecture, separating SLAM, Gaussian mapping, semantic association, and occupancy projection, allows for future advancements in individual components to directly benefit the overall system. This provides a strong foundation for further research into:
 - Integrating geometric and semantic cues from the occupancy map directly into the SLAM backend to improve long-term consistency.
 - Developing more robust and temporally consistent vision-language models for semantic association.
 - Implementing adaptive dynamic resolution occupancy grids to efficiently represent both large environments and fine-grained objects.
- **Scalability and Accessibility**: By removing the need for extensive 3D labeling, FreeOcc makes advanced 3D scene understanding more accessible and scalable. It allows researchers and practitioners to focus on system integration and application rather than data collection and model training.

In summary, FreeOcc represents a significant step towards scalable, annotation-free, and generalizable occupancy prediction for embodied AI. Its training-free nature and strong zero-shot generalization capabilities have the potential to enable more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems in complex real-world settings.
