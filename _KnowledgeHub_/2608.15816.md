# ViTaR: Visuo-Tactile Residual Adaptation for Foundation VLA Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ViTaR augments a frozen VLA with bounded residual corrections gated by tactile evidence, treating touch as an execution modulator rather than an action generator. Effect-Guided Modeling ranks candidate residuals via within-state preference learning; Residual Action Modulation selects and continuously scales them. On UniVTAC, ViTaR hits 61.3% average success, a 30.6pp gain over the frozen base.

## Problem

- Existing tactile-fusion VLA methods grant touch unbounded influence over action generation, risking catastrophic forgetting of pretrained visual-semantic priors.
- Reinforcement-based tactile adaptation requires exploratory rollouts at near-failure contact states where safety margins are thinnest.
- Prior residual-policy and intervention frameworks lack contact feedback and assume corrections are independent of local physical state.

## Method

- **Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM)** ranks residual options against the base action via weighted ==Bradley-Terry preference learning== over restored-state branch rollouts, avoiding globally calibrated rewards.
- ==Adaptability and Effect Scoring== predicts a per-state adaptability score and relative effect scores from marker-derived contact descriptors and base-action encoding, usable at inference time.
- **Residual Action Modulation (RAM)** selects a discrete action choice (retain base or pick a residual direction) then predicts a continuous tactile-conditioned gain to scale the chosen residual.

## Results

- ViTaR reaches **61.3%** average success across seven UniVTAC tasks versus 30.7% for frozen OpenVLA-OFT base and 40.0% for Tactile-VLA, improving on all seven tasks.
- Physical-robot tasks show **48.3%** average success, beating OpenVLA-OFT by **30.0pp**, Tactile-VLA by **15.0pp**, and ACT by **21.7pp** across insertion, sliding, and grasping.
- Ablations: full ViTaR beats PPO-option (36.0%) and SAC-residual (32.0%) with lower unsafe rate (**0.04** vs 0.25/0.31); EGM ordering accuracy rises from 0.63/0.60 to 0.83/0.88, giving **23.3pp** end-to-end gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraining a new sensory modality to a bounded, structured correction role rather than unconstrained action generation preserves pretrained priors while still enabling adaptation.
> - Within-state preference comparison sidesteps the need for globally calibrated rewards across heterogeneous contact conditions, a generalizable fix for cross-state reward miscalibration.
> - Decoupling whether to intervene (contact descriptor) from how much to intervene (tactile gain) lets each signal be optimized independently, improving both safety and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents an advancement in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to interpret broad visual-semantic instructions for diverse tasks. These models acquire generalizable priors from large-scale pretraining datasets, allowing them to perform a wide range of tasks based on visual and linguistic cues. However, a limitation of current VLA models is their performance in contact-rich manipulation scenarios, such as insertion, connector mating, and force-sensitive grasping. These tasks rely on precise local physical interactions that external cameras often cannot fully observe. Consequently, a VLA policy, once deployed, may generate the same action regardless of the precise contact state (e.g., contact established, slipping, or lost), leading to execution failures.

Existing approaches to integrate tactile feedback into robotic policies can be broadly categorized. Some methods fuse tactile information directly into the VLA model's internal representations, for example, through deep token fusion, temporally decoupled injection, feature-level modulation, or residual tactile representations. While these methods aim to provide contact awareness, they risk catastrophic forgetting of the broad visual-semantic priors that make VLAs generalizable, as tactile input is granted unbounded influence over action generation. Other approaches use online reinforcement learning (RL) to adapt to contact bottlenecks. This typically involves exploratory rollouts in near-failure contact states, which can pose safety concerns and require substantial real-world interaction. These methods also tend to allow tactile feedback to override any aspect of the action, potentially conflicting with the pre-established VLA priors.

This research positions itself by identifying a structural commitment in prior work: granting tactile feedback an unconstrained role in action generation. Instead, ViTaR proposes a paradigm shift where tactile feedback modulates execution rather than participating in the primary action generation process. This approach is rooted in the observation that a frozen VLA often provides the semantically correct general action direction but lacks the fine-grained calibration necessary for execution under specific contact conditions. This work builds upon residual policy learning, which augments a fixed base policy with a learned correction term, and preference-based learning, which acquires relative quality judgments without requiring globally calibrated scalar rewards. However, existing residual methods typically operate without contact feedback and assume state-independent corrections, while preference learning usually involves cross-episode trajectory comparisons for global reward models. ViTaR extends these paradigms by conditioning corrections on real-time contact evidence and grounding the selection in outcome-based preference supervision at a local, within-state granularity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact-rich manipulation tasks by effectively incorporating tactile feedback. This is motivated by the inherent limitation of current VLA models, which, despite encoding broad visual-semantic priors, are often "blind" to local contact dynamics. This blindness results in identical actions being generated irrespective of whether contact is precisely established, lost, or destabilized, leading to failures in tasks requiring fine physical interaction.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing Contact Blindness without Catastrophic Forgetting:** A core motivation is to overcome the VLA models' inability to resolve local physical interactions that external cameras cannot observe, without compromising their pre-trained, generalizable visual-semantic knowledge. Existing tactile integration methods often modify VLA internals, which can lead to catastrophic forgetting of previously learned capabilities.
- **Avoiding Risks of Online Reinforcement Learning:** Current reinforcement learning-based adaptation methods for tactile feedback typically require extensive exploratory rollouts, often in near-failure contact states. This presents safety risks and demands significant data collection in real-world scenarios. The objective is to achieve contact-aware adaptation without relying on such online, risk-prone exploration.
- **Reframing Tactile Feedback's Role:** The authors argue that a frozen VLA generally provides the correct semantic direction for an action, but lacks the necessary calibration for execution in specific contact conditions. Therefore, a key objective is to reframe tactile feedback not as a primary action-generating perceptual input, but as an *execution modulator*. This means tactile feedback should select and scale bounded residual corrections on top of the VLA's output, rather than generating entirely new actions or overriding the VLA's core policy.
- **Developing a Structured Correction Mechanism:** To realize the execution modulation paradigm, the work aims to develop a mechanism that can apply contact-aware residual corrections while preserving the base VLA action space. This involves defining a small, task-aligned set of structured delta actions (residuals) that can be added to the VLA's base output.
- **Establishing a Local Learning Signal for Corrections:** A further objective is to create a robust learning signal that can effectively rank these potential corrections based on their local effects. This signal needs to operate without relying on globally calibrated rewards, which are often difficult to obtain or compare across heterogeneous contact states. The approach involves using within-state preference comparison, where the effects of different corrections are ranked from the same restored contact state. This allows for recovery of relative local effects without requiring cross-state calibration.
- **Ensuring Transferability to Real-World Scenarios:** The research aims to validate that the proposed bounded tactile modulation approach is effective and can transfer to physical robots, handling real sensor noise and dynamic conditions across diverse contact modalities.

In summary, the motivation stems from a recognized bottleneck in VLA deployment for contact-rich tasks, and the objectives focus on developing a novel, safe, and effective method for tactile integration that complements, rather than competes with, the generalizable priors of foundation VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViTaR (Visuo-Tactile Residual Adaptation) augments a frozen pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy with two learned components: Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM) and Residual Action Modulation (RAM). The core idea is that the VLA provides the semantic action, and tactile feedback modulates its execution via bounded residual corrections.

**Problem Formulation:**
The executed action $A_{exec_t}$ at time step $t$ is formulated as the sum of a base action chunk $A_{ref_t}$ from the frozen VLA policy $\pi_{VLA}(o_t, \ell)$ and a contact-aware residual correction $\Delta A_t$. The residual correction is selected from a small set of structured 7D delta actions $d(u) \in \mathcal{R}$ and applied uniformly across an $H$-step action chunk with a continuous scaling factor $\alpha$. Thus, $\Delta A_t = (\alpha d(u), \ldots, \alpha d(u))$.

**Overview of ViTaR:**
ViTaR decomposes adaptation into two stages, both supervised by short-horizon branch rollouts from common decision points:
1. **Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM):** Assesses whether the current contact state is amenable to improvement and ranks available residuals by their expected local effect through pairwise outcome comparisons. EGM predicts an adaptability score $g_i$ and relative effect scores $e_i(c)$ for each action choice $c$.
2. **Residual Action Modulation (RAM):** Converts EGM's evidence into a discrete action choice (retain base action or select a residual) and predicts a tactile-conditioned gain $\alpha_i$ for the selected residual.

**Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM):**
EGM's purpose is to learn state-conditioned effect evidence for action choices within the same local state.

- **Branch-Outcome Preference Learning:**
 - **Branches:** Short-horizon rollouts initiated from restored local states, each executing a single action choice $c$. Choices include retaining the base action ($\emptyset$) or applying a structured residual $u \in \mathcal{R}$.
 - **Local Outcome Score $q_i(c)$:** For each branch, a scalar score is computed from task outcome, marker-based contact retention, and restoration quality. This score is meaningful only within a single decision group.
 - **Preference Comparison:** Residuals are compared to the base action within the same decision point by calculating $\Delta q_i(u) = q_i(u) - q_i(\emptyset)$.
 - **Partitioning Residual Options:** Residuals are categorized into positive ($U^+_i$, if $\Delta q_i(u) > \delta$), neutral ($U^0_i$, if $|\Delta q_i(u)| \le \delta$), and negative ($U^-_i$, if $\Delta q_i(u) < -\delta$) sets, using a threshold $\delta=0.05$. This induces preference relations (positive $\succ$ base $\succ$ negative, positive $\succ$ neutral).
 - **Weighted Bradley-Terry Objective ($L_{rank}$):** A scalar comparator $e_i(c)$ is fitted using a weighted Bradley-Terry objective. Weights $w_{i,c^+_i,c^-_i}$ are based on the score gap and the quality of restored branches (represented by $\kappa_i(c)$), favoring large outcome gaps from reliably restored branches. This objective allows EGM to learn local effect orderings without needing a globally consistent score scale.
- **Adaptability and Effect Scoring:**
 - EGM predicts an adaptability score $g_i = G_\theta(x_i)$ and effect scores $e_i(c) = E_\theta(x_i, c)$, where $x_i$ is the state representation (marker-derived contact descriptor, visual-proprioceptive observations, language embedding, execution history, base-action encoding). All components of $x_i$ are available at inference time.
 - **Adaptability Target:** A binary adaptability target $y^ {adapt}_i$ is defined: 1 if $U^+_i \ne \emptyset$, 0 otherwise.
 - **EGM Objective ($L_{EGM}$):** Combines the ranking loss $L_{rank}$ and a binary cross-entropy loss $L_{adapt}$ for adaptability prediction, weighted by $\lambda_{adapt}$. EGM is trained to convergence and then frozen.

**Residual Action Modulation (RAM):**
RAM uses the frozen EGM's output to make deployment-time decisions.

- **Preference-Guided Residual Selection:**
 - **Ordinal Ranks:** EGM's effect scores are normalized into ordinal ranks $r^{EGM}_i(c) \in$ to preserve within-state orderings.
 - **Action Choice Labels ($c^{tar}_i$):** Labels are constructed by selecting the base action if no positive residual exists, or the best-performing positive residual (highest $\Delta q_i(u)$) otherwise.
 - **Selector $\Pi_\psi$:** A network predicts the probability $p_i(a)$ of selecting each action choice $a \in \{\emptyset\} \cup \mathcal{R}_i$, conditioned on $x_i$, $\mathcal{R}_i$, $g_i$, and $r^{EGM}_i$. It is trained via cross-entropy against $c^{tar}_i$.
- **Tactile-Conditioned Residual Scaling:**
 - **Gain Prediction:** For the selected residual direction $u^*_i$, a scaling network $M_\eta$ predicts a continuous execution gain $\alpha_i = \sigma(M_\eta(x_i, u^*_i, z_i))$, where $z_i$ is a bilateral tactile summary. $\alpha_i$ only modulates the gain; it does not alter the base action or residual direction.
 - **Auxiliary Gain Target (${\alpha^{tar}_i}$):** Since there's no ground truth for optimal scale, the ordinal rank evidence is repurposed: 0 if $c^{tar}_i = \emptyset$, otherwise $r^{EGM}_i(c^{tar}_i)$.
 - **RAM Objective ($L_{RAM}$):** Combines the selector's cross-entropy loss ($L_{selector}$) and a weighted binary cross-entropy loss for the auxiliary gain target ($L_{scale}$), balanced by $\lambda_\alpha$.

**Structured Residual Options:**
The set of candidate residuals $\mathcal{R}$ is constructed by analyzing frozen VLA action chunks over training trajectories, identifying predominant signed translational, rotational, or gripper components. This creates a small, task-aligned set of plausible local correction directions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ViTaR was conducted on the UniVTAC benchmark, spanning seven contact-rich tasks, and on three physical-robot tasks. The findings are summarized below:

**UniVTAC Success Rates:**
- ViTaR achieved an average success rate of **61.3%** across the seven UniVTAC tasks.
- This represents a **30.6 percentage-point (pp)** improvement over its frozen VLA base policy, OpenVLA-OFT (30.7%).
- ViTaR also surpassed purpose-built tactile baselines and other foundation policy baselines, including ACT (32.6%), VITaL (42.7%), ACT + UniVTAC (51.4%), Tactile-VLA (40.0%), and $\pi_{0.5}$ (48.3%).
- Improvements were observed across all seven tasks relative to OpenVLA-OFT, with significant gains on Lift Bottle, Put Bottle, Insert HDMI, and Grasp Classify.
- While ViTaR demonstrated broad improvement, it did not individually dominate every task. For instance, $\pi_{0.5}$ achieved a higher success rate on Lift Can, and Tactile-VLA achieved a higher rate on Lift Bottle.

**Physical-Robot Success Rates:**
- On three physical contact-rich tasks (Insert Hole, Lift Bottle, Wiping the Board), ViTaR achieved a **48.3% average success rate**.
- This represents a **30.0 pp improvement** over OpenVLA-OFT, a 15.0 pp improvement over Tactile-VLA, and a 21.7 pp improvement over ACT.
- Gains were observed in all three physical tasks, which involved distinct contact behaviors (precision alignment, sustained sliding, stable grasping), indicating the generality of the bounded tactile modulation approach across different contact modalities.
- The absolute success rate on physical robots was bounded by the base policy's semantic action quality, with improvements over the base consistent with simulated margins.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Comparison with Direct Residual RL (Table 2):**
 - ViTaR achieved 61.3% average success with a low unsafe/early-termination rate (0.04).
 - Direct residual RL methods, PPO-option (selecting discrete residuals) and SAC-residual (learning continuous residuals), achieved significantly lower success rates (36.0% and 32.0%, respectively) and higher unsafe/early-termination rates (0.25 and 0.31).
 - This suggests that effect-guided residual selection is more effective than the tested direct RL formulations under matched conditions.

- **Contact Information and Continuous Scaling (Table 3):**
 - Fixing the residual scale to a constant reduced success from 61.3% to 45.0%.
 - Removing the marker-derived contact descriptor ($m_i$, for selection) lowered paired recovery from 0.79 to 0.46.
 - Removing the tactile summary ($z_i$, for continuous scaling) lowered success to 46.0%.
 - Removing both contact cues yielded 36.0% success and 0.19 unsafe/early termination.
 - These results indicate that $m_i$ is critical for deciding *whether* to intervene, and $z_i$ is critical for determining *how much* to apply, demonstrating complementary roles.

- **Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM) (Table 4):**
 - Progressively adding EGM's design choices (pairwise ranking, outcome-gap weighting, restoration-aware weighting) improved preference ordering accuracy (P>B / B>N from 0.63/0.60 to 0.83/0.88) and reduced the False Positive Rate (FPR) at 80% recall (from 0.40 to 0.12).
 - This translated to a 23.3 pp end-to-end success gain (38.0% to 61.3%), supporting the effectiveness of local preference learning for generating effect evidence.

- **Residual Action Modulation (RAM) (Table 5):**
 - The full joint selector in RAM, conditioned on both EGM rankings and branch-outcome evidence, achieved the highest held-out top-1 agreement (0.79), paired recovery (0.79), and average success (61.3%).
 - Using EGM scores directly (EGM-Greedy) or restricting supervision to a single evidence source (Outcome-only, Effect-only) degraded performance, suggesting that both evidence sources contribute non-redundantly.

- **Multiscale Tactile Scaling Evaluation (Supplementary Table 6):**
 - ViTaR scaling achieved 0.68 exact-scale accuracy and 0.94 within-1-bin accuracy, reducing error to 0.08 and local regret to 0.04.
 - Relative to a fixed 1.0 scale, this reduced regret by 0.11 and improved branch success by 13.2 pp (48.7% to 61.9%), while lowering the unsafe rate from 0.12 to 0.04.
 - Removing the fine tactile summary $z_i$ increased regret to 0.10 and reduced success to 50.8%, confirming $z_i$'s role in continuous scaling.

- **Restoration Sensitivity and Branch Stability (Supplementary Tables 7a, 8):**
 - The default restoration-quality treatment (strict-pass branches + downweighted approximate restorations) achieved a balance of strong ordering quality (P>B 0.83, B>N 0.88), low FPR (0.12), and high end-to-end success (61.3%).
 - Assigning full weight to imperfectly restored branches reduced ordering accuracy and success, demonstrating the importance of reliability weighting.

- **Structured Residual Options (Supplementary Table 13):**
 - Using a structured, task-aligned 7D residual dictionary increased positive-option coverage from 0.41 to 0.72 and mean best gain from 0.13 to 0.34, compared to a matched random 7D set.
 - This resulted in significant improvements in paired recovery (0.40 to 0.79), preservation (0.65 to 0.92), and average success (31.0% to 61.3%), indicating the benefit of a well-defined residual set.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViTaR framework presents a method for integrating tactile feedback into pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with implications for robotic manipulation in contact-rich environments.

The primary significance of this work lies in its proposed paradigm shift: reframing tactile feedback as an execution modulator rather than an unbounded action generator. This approach addresses a known limitation of VLA models, their "blindness" to local contact dynamics, without necessitating internal modifications to the VLA architecture or risking catastrophic forgetting of its pre-trained visual-semantic priors. By adding bounded residual corrections, ViTaR aims to preserve the generality and capabilities of foundation VLAs while improving their precision in physical interaction.

Specific impacts include:

- **Enhanced Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks:** The reported 30.6 percentage-point improvement over the frozen VLA base on the UniVTAC benchmark, along with consistent gains on physical-robot tasks (insertion, sliding, grasping), demonstrates that contact-aware residual modulation can substantially improve success rates in tasks requiring fine physical interaction. This could enable more reliable deployment of VLA models in industrial, service, and domestic robotics applications where contact is unavoidable.
- **A Novel Approach to Learning Local Corrections:** The introduction of Effect-Guided Modeling (EGM), which uses within-state pairwise preference learning to rank candidate corrections, offers a method for acquiring local effect evidence without requiring globally calibrated rewards. This addresses a challenge in reward design for complex, heterogeneous contact states and could inform future research in learning from comparative feedback.
- **Effective Tactile Integration:** The methodology distinguishes between deciding *whether* to intervene (using marker-derived contact descriptors) and *how much* to apply (using a tactile summary for continuous gain scaling). This decomposition and the ablations supporting the complementary roles of these components provide insights into effective designs for incorporating tactile information.
- **Safer Adaptation:** By constraining tactile influence to bounded residual corrections and avoiding methods that require extensive online reinforcement learning in risky failure states, ViTaR offers a potentially safer path to adapting generalist policies for precision manipulation. The reduced unsafe/early-termination rates observed in comparisons with direct residual RL methods support this aspect.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The framework opens avenues for future research, such as extending the paradigm to multi-finger dexterous manipulation with heterogeneous tactile sensors and scaling the branch-comparison protocol to longer-horizon tasks. The work also contributes to the broader understanding of how large-scale pretraining and specialized feedback can be combined effectively in embodied AI.

Overall, ViTaR contributes a structured and validated approach to bridge the gap between high-level visual-language understanding and low-level physical interaction in robotic systems, offering a path toward more capable and robust robotic manipulation.
