# Embodied3DBench: Benchmarking Low-Level Embodied Spatial Intelligence of Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Embodied3DBench is a new benchmark assessing low-level embodied spatial intelligence in Vision-Language Models through 12 diverse tasks focused on precise 3D geometry and interaction-oriented perception. The evaluation revealed current VLMs struggle with metric-sensitive 3D reasoning and physical interaction priors, while fine-tuning on synthetic data and View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought improved these capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) show proficiency in high-level spatial reasoning but lack low-level spatial intelligence essential for embodied interaction, such as precise multi-view localization and geometry-aware reasoning.
- A systematic benchmark tailored for evaluating fine-grained, metric-sensitive 3D structural understanding and interaction-oriented perception in VLMs was missing.
- There was a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D data from real environments and challenges in annotating fine-grained spatial information required for low-level embodied tasks.

## Method

- Developed **Embodied3DBench**, a robot-centric benchmark with 6 core task categories and 12 sub-tasks (2D and 3D) to evaluate spatial structural understanding and interaction-oriented perception.
- Created an automated, high-fidelity simulation data generation pipeline, leveraging Isaac Sim, to synthesize over 21,000 QA pairs for the benchmark and a 1.3 million QA pair training dataset.
- Introduced View-Augmented ==Chain-of-Thought== (VA-CoT) to enhance 3D spatial perception in VLMs by integrating supplementary novel views into their reasoning process.

## Results

- Evaluation of 13 state-of-the-art VLMs on Embodied3DBench showed no single model achieved robust performance across both accurate metric grounding and pre-execution interaction prediction, with proprietary models like GPT-5 achieving only mid-range accuracy in 3D object bounding box tasks.
- Integrating View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought improved GPT-5 by **+2.7** points on fine-grained localization (Part Bounding Box, Object Point), while fine-tuning Qwen3-VL-4B on the 1.3M synthesized QA pairs gave **+40.5** on 3D Grounding, **+49.9** on 3D Grasp Point, and **+32.4** average on 3D tasks.
- Preliminary evaluation on the LIBERO benchmark indicated a correlation between Embodied3DBench performance and success rates in downstream robot manipulation tasks, suggesting its utility as an indicator for VLA applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit limitations in precise geometric encoding and metric-sensitive 3D reasoning, demonstrating a gap between high-level spatial understanding and the requirements for physical embodied interaction.
> - Interaction-oriented perception tasks, such as predicting grasp points and trajectories, represent a primary bottleneck for existing multimodal systems, proving more challenging than static structural understanding.
> - Large-scale fine-tuning on synthetically generated data and incorporating view-augmented reasoning paradigms can substantially improve VLMs' low-level embodied spatial intelligence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has made progress in tasks requiring high-level spatial intelligence. These models can interpret scene layouts, identify coarse object-to-object relationships (e.g., "left of," "in front of"), and engage in semantic reasoning within 2D and some 3D contexts. This has contributed to advancements in areas such as autonomous driving, AR/VR scene understanding, and embodied navigation. Existing benchmarks for spatial reasoning primarily focus on these high-level and often coarse-grained relationships, relying largely on internet-scale image datasets that may lack precise 3D grounding and kinematic context.

However, transitioning from passive visual observation to active embodied interaction, particularly in robotics, necessitates a different set of capabilities referred to as low-level spatial intelligence. This includes precise multi-view localization, geometry- and topology-aware reasoning in three-dimensional space, and the inference of physical interaction priors like optimal grasp points, functional affordances, and actionable spatial trajectories. Unlike high-level reasoning, which describes general object relationships, low-level reasoning requires integrating fine-grained spatial perception with an understanding of physical and kinematic constraints.

Previous efforts to bridge this gap have involved extending evaluations to 3D spaces for indoor navigation and tabletop interactions. Benchmarks such as SAT, RoboSpatial, and EmbSpatial-Bench emphasize egocentric spatial reasoning and free-space awareness. While these contribute to 3D spatial awareness, they often operate at scene or object levels of abstraction, not fully addressing the fine-grained, geometry-critical structural understanding essential for actionable interaction. Similarly, some methods leverage part-level inference for downstream execution, but a systematic evaluation of large models' native capabilities in low-level geometric and interaction reasoning has been largely unexplored.

Embodied3DBench addresses this identified gap by providing a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate low-level embodied spatial intelligence. It moves beyond high-level spatial understanding and object-level reasoning to focus on the precise, metric-sensitive, and interaction-oriented perceptual capabilities required for physical robot interactions in 3D environments. This work aims to establish a foundational evaluation framework and a scalable data solution for developing multimodal systems capable of robust pre-execution intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically evaluate the low-level spatial intelligence of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in embodied 3D environments. The authors aim to determine if these models possess the foundational perceptual capabilities necessary for complex physical interactions, which are critical for applications in embodied AI and robotics.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in the current state of VLM development:

- **Gap between High-level and Low-level Spatial Intelligence:** While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in high-level spatial reasoning—such as interpreting scene layouts, identifying coarse object relations (e.g., "left/right"), and understanding semantic context—they exhibit a significant deficiency in low-level spatial intelligence. This lower-level intelligence involves precise multi-view localization, geometry- and topology-aware reasoning in 3D, and inferring physical interaction priors. For instance, knowing a mug is "in front of" a kettle is high-level, but planning to grasp it requires estimating its exact 6D pose, handle position, and a collision-free trajectory. This highlights that high-level understanding is insufficient for the "doing" aspect of embodied agents.
- **Scarcity of Dedicated Benchmarks:** The development of low-level spatial intelligence has progressed slowly due to a lack of dedicated benchmarks. Existing benchmarks often focus on high-level reasoning or lack the fine-grained, 3D, and interaction-oriented details required for robust evaluation of embodied perception.
- **Data Limitations and Annotation Challenges:** Two major factors contributing to the gap are the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality 3D data from real environments and the inherent difficulty in annotating fine-grained spatial information (e.g., precise object geometry, poses, affordances, or actionable trajectories) from standard RGB images without corresponding 3D measurements. These constraints hinder both systematic benchmarking and model development.

To address these motivations, the research establishes a secondary objective: to provide a scalable data solution to train models in these critical low-level capabilities. By synthesizing a large-scale training dataset alongside the evaluation benchmark, the authors aim to actively bridge the capability gap identified through their evaluation framework. Ultimately, the work seeks to set a clear target for the development of interaction-aware multimodal systems by providing both an evaluation tool and a means to improve model performance in foundational embodied perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of Embodied3DBench, a robot-centric benchmark for evaluating low-level embodied spatial intelligence, and the creation of a large-scale training dataset.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design and Task Definition

Embodied3DBench comprises 6 core task categories, further divided into 12 sub-tasks, spanning two main groups:

- **Spatial Structural Understanding Tasks:** These tasks evaluate the fundamental comprehension of geometric and topological relationships in 3D space.
 - **Grounding:**
 - *Pointing:* Locate and mark target objects or part-level positions in 2D images based on semantic descriptions (e.g., "Locate the silver handle of the pot.").
 - *Bounding Box:* Enclose a target object within an axis-aligned bounding box, inferring its center coordinates, dimensions, and rotation in 2D and 3D.
 - **Spatial Relation:**
 - *Relative Distance Estimation:* Quantify the metric distance between objects.
 - *Relative Direction Determination:* Identify positional and orientational relationships (e.g., "Where is the book in relation to the toy? (A) Left (B) Right"). Both 2D and 3D variants are included, with 3D requiring metric predictions.
 - **Multi-view Correspondence:** Identify feature correspondences across different viewpoints. Given a point's coordinates in one view, determine its visibility and coordinates in another view.

- **Interaction-Oriented Perception Tasks:** These tasks evaluate the ability to infer physical interaction priors crucial for robot manipulation.
 - **Affordance Prediction:** Predict functional attributes and regions for task execution.
 - *Functional Point:* Key physical location for direct interaction.
 - *Functional Vector:* Spatial direction of motion for effective interaction.
 - **Trajectory Prediction:** Predict a continuous spatial motion path for an end-effector during interaction, serving as a kinematic prior.
 - **Grasp Point Prediction:** Identify the optimal region on an object for a robot to establish stable control for grasping.

All tasks include both 2D and 3D variants, with the 3D versions requiring metric predictions. The benchmark consists of over 21,000 high-quality question-answer (QA) pairs.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline

The benchmark and training data are constructed using a high-fidelity simulation data generation pipeline:

1. **Embodied Interaction Data Synthesis:**
 - **Asset Collection:** High-quality 3D scene and object scan data are collected (e.g., from Omni6DPose). Objects undergo annotation with interaction modes and component-level semantic segmentation, followed by manual quality inspection.
 - **Task Definition:** VLMs define simulated tasks into sub-phases based on required object interactions, specifying spatial constraints and kinematic parameters.
 - **Hi-Fi Data Generation:** Interaction trajectories are rendered using Isaac Sim, generating per-frame RGB-D images, component-level segmentation maps, and precise 6D pose data for the camera, objects, and agent.
 - **Embodied Data Quality Inspection:** Data is validated for visual occlusion, task completion logic, and trajectory plausibility, with invalid episodes filtered out.

2. **QA Pair Generation:**
 - **Filtering Rules:** To ensure geometric unambiguousness, two strict rules are applied: 'Invisibility Exclusion' (removing frames where objects are outside the field of view) and 'Occlusion Exclusion' (filtering frames with heavy occlusion based on pixel area and occlusion ratio).
 - **Ground Truth Extraction:** For visible frames, task-specific geometric rules are applied to extract ground-truth spatial priors:
 - **Affordance:** 3D functional points/vectors are transformed to camera space or projected onto the 2D plane.
 - **Object and Part Pointing:** 3D target points are converted to 2D precise positioning coordinates.
 - **Bounding Box:** Oriented 3D bounding boxes are calculated in camera space, while 2D bounding boxes are derived from segmentation masks.
 - **Grasp Point:** The 6D pose of the end-effector at the moment of the 'close gripper' command in simulation is extracted.
 - **Multi-View Correspondence:** Farthest Point Sampling selects candidate points; visibility across views is recorded and cross-referenced to extract coordinates.
 - **Trajectory:** End-effector motion trajectories are uniformly sampled, and these 3D waypoints are projected to generate both 2D and 3D metric trajectory priors.
 - **Spatial Relation:** Directions (up/down, left/right) are derived from projected pixel coordinates, while front/back is labeled based on non-overlapping 3D bounding boxes and average depth values.

3. **Human-in-the-Loop Verification:** A three-step hierarchical visual inspection platform ensures data reliability. Human annotators verify semantic labels and functional descriptions for 3D assets, filter video samples based on scene diversity and task validity, and examine generated QA pairs for absolute geometric alignment and unambiguous spatial grounding.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Statistics & Dataset Scaling

The pipeline generated 21,000 high-quality, fine-grained QA pairs for the Embodied3DBench evaluation. Additionally, the generation pipeline was scaled to synthesize a training dataset comprising 1.3 million QA pairs.

#### 3.4. View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought (VA-CoT)

To investigate methods for enhancing 3D spatial perception, a View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought (VA-CoT) paradigm was introduced. This extends conventional CoT by integrating supplementary novel views of the scene. These extra perspectives act as explicit spatial cues, intended to help models align object geometry across viewpoints, reason about hidden or occluded regions, and consolidate 3D structural relationships during their step-by-step reasoning.

#### 3.5. Experimental Settings and Evaluation Metrics

- **Models Evaluated:** 13 state-of-the-art VLMs, including both proprietary (e.g., Gemini series, GPT-5, GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4, Doubao series) and open-source models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5, Qwen3-VL-4B), were tested.
- **Protocol:** All models were evaluated under a zero-shot protocol.
- **Metrics:** Task-specific metrics were designed:
 - **Bounding Box:** Intersection-over-Union (IoU) for 3D boxes.
 - **Coordinate Point:** L2 distance (pixel-level for 2D, meters for 3D).
 - **Directional Vector:** Cosine similarity.
 - **Spatial Trajectory:** Hausdorff Distance.
 - **Categorical Choice:** Classification accuracy.
 - **Metric Distance:** Absolute error in meters.
- **Normalization:** Raw metric scores were mapped to a normalized score `s ∈` using non-linear functions (x^α and e^(-αx)).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 13 state-of-the-art VLMs on Embodied3DBench revealed several insights regarding low-level embodied spatial intelligence.

#### 4.1. General Performance and Geometric Encoding Deficiencies

Current VLMs exhibit limitations in precise geometric encoding. Performance declined significantly in tasks requiring absolute metric localization compared to coarse semantic descriptions. For instance, in 3D Object Bounding Box and Object Point tasks, GPT-5, a prominent proprietary reasoning model, achieved only mid-range accuracy. This suggests that while models demonstrate high-level spatial understanding, their internal representations may lack the geometric precision required for embodied structural understanding.

#### 4.2. Asymmetric Performance Across Perception Paradigms

Model proficiency was unevenly distributed across different task types. GPT-5 achieved the highest overall average score, driven by strong zero-shot reasoning in multi-view correspondence and affordance tasks. Gemini-2.5-Pro demonstrated competitive performance in geometry-grounded interaction tasks, such as inferring 3D affordance vectors and actionable trajectories. Interestingly, some open-source models displayed specialized strengths; Qwen3-VL-4B notably outperformed all proprietary models in 2D perception and showed competitive 3D perception capabilities, which the authors attribute to its pre-training phase.

#### 4.3. Interaction-Oriented Perception as a Primary Bottleneck

The benchmark indicated that inferring kinematic priors, such as grasp points and trajectory planning, presented a greater challenge for current VLMs than static structural understanding. No single system demonstrated uniformly robust capabilities across accurate metric grounding and pre-execution interaction prediction. This suggests a critical vulnerability in existing multimodal intelligence concerning interaction-oriented perception.

#### 4.4. In-Depth Error Analysis (GPT-5)

A detailed error analysis of GPT-5, chosen for its leading performance, identified specific failure modes:
- **Semantic Grounding Errors (36.3%):** These represented the largest proportion of errors, occurring when the model understood the global scene context but failed to accurately bind semantic instructions to precise geometric features, such as misidentifying functional sub-parts.
- **3D Metric Accuracy Limitations (28.6%):** This category accounted for a substantial portion of errors, involving inaccuracies in predicting exact 3D coordinates. This implies difficulties in implicit understanding of camera intrinsics and depth scale, which are fundamental for actionable interaction priors.
- **2D Metric Accuracy Limitations (19.8%):** Errors where bounding boxes correctly captured object scale but were spatially offset in the image plane.
- **Context-Dependent Spatial Reasoning Limitations (11.0%):** Failures occurring when correct grounding relied heavily on relative topological cues, indicating struggles in dynamically updating spatial representations under multiple relational constraints.

#### 4.5. Effect of View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought (VA-CoT)

Integrating View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought (VA-CoT) into GPT-5 demonstrated uniform improvements across 3D spatial tasks. The most notable gains were observed in fine-grained structural localization tasks, such as Part Bounding Box (+2.7 points) and Object Point (+2.7 points). Moderate enhancements were also seen in interaction-oriented tasks (e.g., Affordance Position: +1.1, Affordance Orientation: +1.9). This suggests that providing structured, cross-view geometric evidence can aid models in aligning semantic cues with precise 3D coordinates and improve kinematic prior predictions.

#### 4.6. Impact of Large-Scale Fine-Tuning

Fine-tuning the open-source architecture Qwen3-VL-4B on the synthesized 1.3 million embodied QA pairs resulted in substantial performance improvements. For example, 3D Grounding scores increased by +40.5 points, and 3D Grasp Point prediction improved by +49.9 points. Overall, 2D tasks saw an average improvement of +12.5 points, and 3D tasks saw an average improvement of +32.4 points. This outcome supports the effectiveness of the automated generation pipeline in providing scalable and high-quality data for training models in low-level spatial intelligence.

#### 4.7. Connection to Downstream Tasks (LIBERO Benchmark)

A preliminary evaluation on the LIBERO benchmark indicated a correlation between Embodied3DBench performance and utility in downstream embodied tasks. Qwen3-VL-4B, which achieved higher scores in the benchmark's spatial structural and interaction-oriented perception tasks, exhibited faster convergence and higher final success rates in the LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Goal suites when used as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy backbone. This suggests that the foundational spatial priors evaluated by Embodied3DBench may serve as an indicator of a model's effectiveness in VLA applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and Vision-Language Models by addressing a identified gap in the evaluation and development of low-level spatial intelligence.

**Fills a Critical Evaluation Gap:** Embodied3DBench provides a systematic evaluation framework tailored to low-level embodied spatial intelligence, a domain previously underexplored by existing benchmarks. It offers a structured way to assess capabilities such as precise geometric encoding, multi-view consistency, and interaction-oriented perception, which are distinct from high-level semantic reasoning.

**Identifies Key Limitations of Current VLMs:** The extensive evaluation of 13 state-of-the-art models reveals that despite advancements in high-level spatial understanding, current VLMs demonstrate fragility in metric-sensitive 3D geometric reasoning and in inferring robust, 3D-aware interaction priors. This finding highlights a fundamental bottleneck in current multimodal intelligence and indicates that models struggle with the transition from visual "seeing" to practical "doing" in physical environments.

**Offers a Scalable Data Solution:** The development of an automated, high-fidelity simulation data generation pipeline, leading to a 1.3 million QA pair training dataset, provides a scalable resource for addressing the identified capability gap. This large-scale dataset can facilitate the training of future models to acquire the necessary low-level spatial intelligence, circumventing the challenges associated with manual annotation of complex 3D interaction data.

**Guides Future Research and Development:** By establishing a clear benchmark and revealing specific areas of weakness (e.g., 3D metric accuracy, semantic-to-geometric binding), Embodied3DBench sets a defined target for future research. It encourages the development of multimodal systems with enhanced pre-execution perceptual capabilities, moving beyond coarse-grained spatial descriptions toward precise, actionable intelligence for robotics. The positive impact of View-Augmented Chain-of-Thought also suggests promising directions for designing more effective prompting and reasoning paradigms for spatial tasks.

**Potential for Advancing Embodied AI Applications:** The demonstrated correlation between benchmark performance and success in downstream robot manipulation tasks (LIBERO) suggests that improving models' scores on Embodied3DBench could directly translate to more capable and efficient embodied AI agents. This work lays foundational groundwork for developing more robust robots capable of complex, fine-grained interactions in unstructured real-world environments. The prospect of leveraging the massive training dataset for large-scale Vision-Language-Action pre-training indicates a path towards fundamentally integrating strong 3D spatial priors into foundational models, potentially accelerating progress in generalized robotic manipulation and autonomous systems.
