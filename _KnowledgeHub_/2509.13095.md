# Empowering Multi-Robot Cooperation via Sequential World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework termed Sequential World Model (SeqWM) integrates the sequential paradigm into multi-robot Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, structurally decomposing joint dynamics into autoregressive agent-wise models. This approach reduces modeling complexity, achieves higher asymptotic returns and faster convergence in simulations, and successfully enables sophisticated cooperative behaviors on physical multi-quadruped robots.

## Problem

- Extending Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) to multi-robot systems is challenging due to high-dimensional observation/action spaces and complex joint dynamics.
- Decentralized MBRL methods often fail to capture sufficient inter-agent coupling, limiting effective coordination.
- Centralized MBRL approaches face prohibitive computational and modeling costs, hindering their deployment in multi-robot systems with many agents.

## Method

- The ==Sequential World Model== (**SeqWM**) framework integrates the sequential paradigm into MBRL, reinterpreting joint dynamics as an autoregressive sequence modeling problem.
- Each agent is equipped with an independent world model that predicts its future trajectory (latent state and reward) conditioned on its own information and the predictions of preceding agents, enabling explicit intention sharing.
- A sequential multi-agent planner, based on Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), leverages these learned world models for multi-step lookahead and action optimization, incorporating real-world deployment enhancements like action smoothing and a communication cache.

## Results

- SeqWM achieved higher asymptotic returns and faster convergence rates compared to model-free (HASAC, MAPPO) and model-based (MARIE) baselines in simulated Bi-DexHands and multi-quadruped tasks, often reaching near-optimal performance within 2-4 million environment steps.
- The framework was successfully deployed on physical Unitree Go2-W quadruped robots, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer and enabling sophisticated cooperative behaviors like coordinated pushing, priority management, and adaptive role allocation.
- SeqWM maintains a per-step inference time of **12.8 ms** on an RTX A6000 GPU, suitable for real-time robotic control, with an optional early-stopping heuristic reducing time by **57.3%** for a **5.9%** performance trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing joint dynamics into autoregressive, agent-wise world models effectively manages the complexity of multi-robot systems within an MBRL framework while maintaining inter-agent dependencies.
> - Explicitly sharing predicted trajectories (intentions) sequentially between agents facilitates advanced cooperative behaviors such as predictive adaptation, temporal alignment, and dynamic role division.
> - Simple concatenation for message fusion proved more effective than complex neural architectures, preserving complete communication content and stable gradient propagation for inter-agent messages.
