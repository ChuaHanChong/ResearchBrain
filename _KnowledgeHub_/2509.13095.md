# Empowering Multi-Robot Cooperation via Sequential World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework termed Sequential World Model (SeqWM) integrates the sequential paradigm into multi-robot Model-Based Reinforcement Learning, structurally decomposing joint dynamics into autoregressive agent-wise models. This approach reduces modeling complexity, achieves higher asymptotic returns and faster convergence in simulations, and successfully enables sophisticated cooperative behaviors on physical multi-quadruped robots.

## Problem

- Extending Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) to multi-robot systems is challenging due to high-dimensional observation/action spaces and complex joint dynamics.
- Decentralized MBRL methods often fail to capture sufficient inter-agent coupling, limiting effective coordination.
- Centralized MBRL approaches face prohibitive computational and modeling costs, hindering their deployment in multi-robot systems with many agents.

## Method

- The **Sequential World Model (SeqWM)** framework integrates the sequential paradigm into MBRL, reinterpreting joint dynamics as an ==autoregressive sequence modeling== problem.
- Each agent is equipped with an independent world model that predicts its future trajectory (latent state and reward) conditioned on its own information and the predictions of preceding agents, enabling ==explicit intention sharing==.
- A sequential multi-agent planner, based on ==Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)==, leverages these learned world models for multi-step lookahead and action optimization, incorporating real-world deployment enhancements like action smoothing and a communication cache.

## Results

- SeqWM achieved higher asymptotic returns and faster convergence rates compared to model-free (HASAC, MAPPO) and model-based (MARIE) baselines in simulated Bi-DexHands and multi-quadruped tasks, often reaching near-optimal performance within **2-4 million** environment steps.
- The framework was successfully deployed on physical Unitree Go2-W quadruped robots, demonstrating robust sim-to-real transfer and enabling sophisticated cooperative behaviors like coordinated pushing, priority management, and adaptive role allocation.
- SeqWM maintains a per-step inference time of **12.8ms** on an RTX A6000 GPU, suitable for real-time robotic control, with an optional early-stopping heuristic reducing time by **57.3%** for a **5.9%** performance trade-off.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing joint dynamics into autoregressive, agent-wise world models effectively manages the complexity of multi-robot systems within an MBRL framework while maintaining inter-agent dependencies.
> - Explicitly sharing predicted trajectories (intentions) sequentially between agents facilitates advanced cooperative behaviors such as predictive adaptation, temporal alignment, and dynamic role division.
> - Simple concatenation for message fusion proved more effective than complex neural architectures, preserving complete communication content and stable gradient propagation for inter-agent messages.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of multi-robot cooperation, specifically addressing challenges in extending model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) to complex multi-robot systems. MBRL has demonstrated utility in robotics due to its sample efficiency and planning capabilities for single robots. However, its application to multi-robot cooperation presents difficulties primarily due to the complexity of modeling joint dynamics in high-dimensional observation and action spaces.

Prior approaches to multi-robot MBRL have generally fallen into two categories:
- **Decentralized methods:** These involve independent world models for each agent. While reducing individual agent complexity, they often fail to capture the coupling between agents, thereby limiting effective coordination.
- **Centralized methods:** These assume full observability and operate in the joint state-action space, attempting to model the entire system's dynamics. While capable of capturing inter-agent coupling, they face significant computational and modeling complexity, particularly in systems with many agents or high-dimensional control, which hinders their deployment in real-world scenarios. Examples include methods like CoDreamer, which uses transformers or GNNs for full state-action integration, and MARIE, which explores decentralized dynamics but still requires communication for aggregation.

In recent years, a "distributed sequential paradigm" has emerged as an alternative. This paradigm reformulates multi-agent decision-making as an autoregressive process where agents communicate and act in a predefined order, with each agent's policy conditioned on messages and actions from its predecessors. This approach has shown benefits such as more consistent joint reasoning, finer-grained credit assignment, reduced reliance on communication synchronization, and improved robustness against communication failures. Existing works in this paradigm, such as MAT, PMAT, HARL, and SeqComm, have explored its application in various aspects of multi-agent reinforcement learning.

This work integrates the sequential paradigm into MBRL, positioning itself as a method that structurally decomposes dynamics modeling and action planning. By doing so, it aims to reduce the modeling complexity inherent in centralized MBRL while leveraging the coordination benefits of sequential decision-making, thereby bridging the gap between theoretical MBRL advancements and practical deployment in physical multi-robot systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable effective multi-robot cooperation by developing a novel MBRL framework that can handle the complexity of joint dynamics in physical robot systems. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Address the Challenge of Joint Dynamics Complexity:** The central motivation is to overcome the difficulties associated with modeling the complex joint dynamics of multiple interacting robots. Traditional MBRL, when extended naively, either struggles with coordination (decentralized) or faces prohibitive computational and modeling costs (centralized) in high-dimensional multi-robot environments.
2. **Integrate the Sequential Paradigm into MBRL:** The authors are motivated by the observed advantages of the distributed sequential paradigm in multi-agent decision-making. By incorporating this paradigm into MBRL, they aim to structurally decompose the problem of dynamics modeling and action planning, allowing for a more manageable and scalable approach to multi-robot control.
3. **Reduce Modeling Complexity:** A specific objective is to lower the complexity of dynamics modeling. SeqWM aims to achieve this by employing independent, autoregressive agent-wise world models. Each agent's model predicts its future trajectory based on its own information and the predictions of its predecessors, rather than attempting to model the entire joint dynamics simultaneously.
4. **Enable Explicit Intention Sharing:** The framework is designed to facilitate the emergence of advanced cooperative behaviors by enabling explicit intention sharing. Through the sequential prediction and communication of future trajectories and actions, agents can anticipate and adapt to their partners' plans, fostering more sophisticated coordination.
5. **Achieve High Performance and Sample Efficiency:** The research seeks to develop a method that not only enables cooperation but also achieves high overall performance and exhibits superior sample efficiency compared to existing model-based and model-free baselines in multi-robot tasks. This is a core benefit of MBRL that the authors aim to preserve and enhance in the multi-agent context.
6. **Validate Real-World Applicability:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the practical effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework through deployment on physical multi-robot systems. This step aims to confirm that the benefits observed in simulation, such as reduced modeling complexity and advanced cooperative behaviors, translate reliably to real-world scenarios.

In summary, the work is motivated by the limitations of existing MBRL approaches in multi-robot settings and aims to introduce a sequential modeling and planning framework that is both computationally tractable and capable of fostering sophisticated cooperative behaviors, ultimately enabling more robust and efficient multi-robot deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Sequential World Model (SeqWM), integrates the sequential paradigm into Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) to address the challenges of multi-robot cooperation. The approach is structured into two main components: Sequential World Modelling and Sequential Planning.

#### 3.1. Sequential World Modelling

SeqWM reinterprets joint dynamics as a sequence modeling problem, where each agent's observation-action pair is considered a token. Instead of centralized prediction, SeqWM adopts an autoregressive paradigm for predicting future outcomes. Agent 1 first predicts its next-step observation and reward and passes this information to agent 2. Subsequently, each agent *i* conditions its prediction on its own observation-action pair and the predictions of all its predecessors (agents 1 to *i-1*). This sequential decomposition aims to reduce overall modeling complexity.

The core of Sequential World Modelling involves an independent world model for each agent, without parameter sharing, to facilitate distributed deployment. Each agent's world model consists of several MLP-based modules:
- **Encoder (E_i):** Maps an agent's raw observation (o_i_t) to a compact latent state (z_i_t).
- **Dynamics (D_i):** Predicts the next latent state (ˆz_i_t+1) based on the current latent state (z_i_t), action (a_i_t), and a received message (e_i_t) from predecessors.
- **Reward (R_i):** Predicts the immediate reward (ˆr_t+1) using the same inputs as the dynamics module.
- **Critic (Q_i):** Estimates the Q-value (ˆq_i_t) based on the latent state, action, and message.
- **Actor (π_i,Act):** Generates initial action estimates (ˆa_i_t) based on the latent state and message.
- **Communication (ϕ_i):** The communication function for passing messages between agents is implemented via a concatenation operator (⊕). For agent *i*, the message e_i_t aggregates the predicted latent states and actions from all its predecessors.

**Learning Objective:**
The world models are trained in a self-supervised framework. The loss function L_i(θ) for each agent's model combines:
- **Dynamics Loss:** L2 norm between the predicted latent state (ˆz_i_t+1) and the target latent state (sg(z_i_t+1)). The stop-gradient (sg) operator is used on the target to prevent cyclic gradient flow.
- **Reward Loss:** Soft Cross-Entropy between the predicted reward (ˆr_i_t) and the ground-truth reward (r_t).
- **Q-Loss:** Soft Cross-Entropy between the predicted Q-value (ˆq_i_t) and the TD target (G_t).
The parameters of the encoder, dynamics, reward, and critic are updated through this combined loss. The actor is trained separately using the Heterogeneous-Agent Soft Actor-Critic (HASAC) algorithm, minimizing a loss that balances Q-value and entropy.

**Training Scheme and Robustness:**
A sequential update scheme is adopted during training: when agent *v_i+1* is trained, its inputs are conditioned on predictions from the first *i* agents' most recently updated models. This preserves sequential dependency and enhances stability.
To improve robustness, random permutation of agent order and random masking of communication (allowing agents to skip communication with a certain probability) are employed during training, simulating uncertainties and communication failures.

#### 3.2. Sequential Planning

While the actor provides initial action estimates, the primary decision-making relies on a sequential multi-agent planner based on Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI). This planner leverages the learned world models for multi-step lookahead and action optimization.

The planning process for each agent *v_i* at timestep *t* proceeds as follows:
1. **Communication (S1):** Agent *v_i* receives a message *e_i_t* which is a concatenation of the predicted latent states (ˆz_j_t) and planned actions (a_j_t) from all preceding agents (*j < i*). Agent 1 receives an empty message. A masking-based concatenation scheme is used for efficiency.
2. **Action Sampling (S2):** *N* candidate action sequences (of horizon *H*) are sampled. A portion (*N_p*) are sampled from a diagonal Gaussian distribution (initialized by the actor), and another portion (*N_a*) are sampled directly from the actor module.
3. **World Model Prediction (S3):** For each sampled action sequence, the agent's local world model performs *H*-step latent rollouts to predict future latent states and rewards.
4. **Value Evaluation (S4):** Each predicted trajectory is assigned a value (V_i_Γ) calculated as the sum of predicted rewards over the horizon plus a terminal value estimated by the critic at the end of the horizon.
5. **Action Optimization (S5):** The top *M* trajectories with the highest values form an "elite set." The action distribution (mean and standard deviation) is then updated towards the statistics of these elite trajectories using a weighted average. This process is iterated *K* times to refine the action distribution.

**Enhancements for Real-World Deployment:**
- **Low-pass Action Smoothing:** During action sampling, noise is low-pass filtered (using a Butterworth filter) before being added to the action mean. This suppresses high-frequency fluctuations in control inputs, promoting smoother and more stable robot movements.
- **Heuristic Early-Stopping:** To manage computational costs on physical platforms, planning iterations can be terminated early if the KL divergence between consecutive action distributions falls below a predefined threshold, indicating convergence.
- **Communication Cache:** A cache stores predicted messages from previous agents. This allows agents to retrieve cached messages when real-time communication fails or for asynchronous execution, enabling "serial-blocking-free execution" where the overall decision latency is largely independent of the number of agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SeqWM was evaluated in simulated environments and deployed on physical multi-robot systems, demonstrating its performance, emergent cooperative behaviors, scalability, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparisons

- **Bi-DexHands Environment:** On tasks requiring high-dimensional manipulation (e.g., Over, CatchAbreast, CatchOver2Underarm, BottleCap, Pen, Scissors), SeqWM consistently achieved higher asymptotic returns and exhibited faster convergence rates compared to baseline methods. Baselines included model-free (HASAC, MAPPO) and model-based (MARIE) approaches, as well as another sequential decision-making method (MAT). For example, in tasks like *Over* and *Scissors*, SeqWM reached near-optimal performance within 2–4 million environment steps, while baselines either required significantly more interactions or did not achieve comparable performance.
- **Multi-Quadruped Environment:** In tasks requiring multi-robot coordination (e.g., Gate, PushBox, Shepherd), SeqWM rapidly achieved near-100% success rates, demonstrating superior sample efficiency. This performance was attributed to the sequential structure enabling agents to plan conditioned on predecessors' intentions.

#### 4.2. Empowered Cooperative Behaviors

SeqWM facilitated the emergence of advanced cooperative behaviors:
- **Predictive Adaptation:** In *Catch-Over2Underarm* (Bi-DexHands), the catching hand anticipated the object's trajectory and landing point based on the throwing hand's predictions, proactively adjusting its posture for a reliable grasp.
- **Temporal Alignment:** In *Pen* (Bi-DexHands), the two hands achieved simultaneous grasping of the pen body and cap by exchanging predictions of future actions, leading to efficient task completion.
- **Role Division:** In *PushBox* (Multi-Quad), robots divided roles, with one providing primary pushing force and the other making lateral adjustments for directional control, demonstrating fine-grained force coordination. In a 5-robot *Gate* task, agents dynamically exhibited a "prediction–waiting–passing–yielding" rhythm, with some robots decelerating to avoid congestion and allowing others to pass.

#### 4.3. Scalability to More Agents

The system's scalability was evaluated on a 5-agent *Gate* task. SeqWM demonstrated effective coordination, with agents exhibiting predictive adaptation (some decelerating to avoid congestion) and temporal alignment (wave-like alternation of x-commands reflecting passing order). This showed that the framework can manage coordination challenges with an increased number of agents.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment

SeqWM was successfully deployed on physical Unitree Go2-W quadruped robots for *PushBox*, *Gate*, and *Shepherd* tasks.
- **PushBox:** Robots coordinated pushing forces and directions, closely matching simulated behaviors, confirming successful sim-to-real transfer.
- **Gate:** Robots demonstrated priority management and yielding behaviors, with one slowing down for the other to pass and then veering aside, indicating effective trajectory prediction and intention sharing.
- **Shepherd:** Robots exhibited predictive coordination and adaptive role allocation, influencing the "sheep" robot's movement and adapting their actions to guide it to the target area.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Sequential Sample Generation:** Replacing SeqWM's sequential world model with centralized and decentralized architectures showed that sequential and centralized models achieved similarly low dynamics and reward prediction errors, both significantly outperforming the decentralized model. This supports the effectiveness of sequential prediction in capturing inter-agent dependencies.
- **Communication Function:** Comparing different communication fusion mechanisms (MLP, cross-attention, RNN vs. concat) revealed that simple concatenation achieved the highest and most stable performance. This was attributed to its preservation of complete communication content and stable gradient propagation. RNN-based fusion underperformed even the no-communication baseline.
- **Sequential Intention Sharing:** Experiments with DecWM (decentralized world model without explicit inter-agent trajectory passing) and SeqFree (no world model, only single-step messages) showed that SeqWM achieved the best performance. This indicates that both the multi-step trajectory prediction capability of the world model and the explicit sequential communication of these "intentions" are critical for advanced cooperation.

#### 4.6. Inference Time

On a single RTX A6000 GPU, SeqWM achieved a per-step execution time of 12.8 ms with default settings, indicating suitability for real-time robotic tasks. An early-stopping heuristic reduced execution time by approximately 57.3% with a performance loss of around 5.9%, offering a trade-off for computational constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents SeqWM as a contribution to the field of multi-robot cooperation, particularly for real-world applications of model-based reinforcement learning.

**Key Contributions:**
The primary contribution is the integration of the sequential paradigm into multi-robot MBRL, leading to a structural decomposition of joint dynamics into autoregressive, agent-wise models. This design aims to mitigate the high modeling complexity typically associated with centralized multi-robot MBRL.

**Impact on Multi-Robot Cooperation:**
- **Reduced Complexity:** By decomposing joint dynamics, SeqWM offers a principled approach to managing the complexity of high-dimensional multi-robot systems, potentially making MBRL more tractable for a wider range of cooperative tasks.
- **Enhanced Coordination:** The explicit intention sharing mechanism, enabled by sequentially predicted trajectories, facilitates sophisticated cooperative behaviors such as predictive adaptation, temporal alignment, and dynamic role division.
- **Improved Efficiency:** The framework demonstrates superior sample efficiency and overall performance compared to existing baselines, which is critical for learning complex behaviors in resource-constrained real-world robotics.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on physical quadruped robots validates the method's effectiveness and robustness in real-world environments, indicating its potential for practical implementation beyond simulation. This suggests a pathway for bridging the sim-to-real gap for complex multi-robot tasks.

**Broader Implications:**
SeqWM proposes that the sequential paradigm can serve as an efficient and scalable principle for structuring multi-agent cooperation, offering a balance between performance, efficiency, and real-world applicability. This may guide future research in designing multi-agent learning systems.

**Potential Future Work and Extensions:**
The authors identify several avenues for future research, leveraging the framework's characteristics:
- **Heterogeneous Robot Teams:** The independent agent-wise world models could naturally extend to teams composed of diverse robots with different dynamics and sensing modalities (e.g., quadruped robots, manipulators, aerial robots).
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The explicit trajectory rollouts, serving as interpretable intention signals, could be shared with humans. This could foster transparent collaboration, mutual understanding, and trust in human-robot teams, moving towards more intuitive human-robot interaction.

The identified limitations include the current focus on fully cooperative tasks with shared rewards, suggesting future exploration in competitive or mixed-motive settings. Additionally, the reliance on a fixed or random agent order presents a limitation for scenarios requiring dynamic adjustment of agent sequences.
