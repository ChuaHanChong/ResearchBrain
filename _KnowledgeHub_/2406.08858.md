# OmniH2O: Universal and Dexterous Human-to-Humanoid Whole-Body Teleoperation and Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The OmniH2O system presents a universal framework for human-to-humanoid teleoperation and learning, enabling dexterous whole-body control of humanoid robots. The system integrates various real-world input modalities and facilitates the learning of autonomous policies from human demonstrations, demonstrating robustness across diverse tasks and environments.

## Problem

- Controlling humanoid robots is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom and inherent instability.
- Coordinating dexterous manipulation and stable locomotion simultaneously in humanoid robots remains difficult.
- A lack of universal frameworks for real-time human-to-humanoid teleoperation and learning from diverse input modalities.

## Method

- **OmniH2O** provides a universal framework incorporating human motion retargeting, a two-phase sim-to-real training pipeline, and universal real-world teleoperation.
- It uses kinematic pose as an intermediate representation to bridge various human input modalities (VR, RGB cameras, verbal instructions) to robot actuation.
- A two-phase training pipeline, involving reinforcement learning for a privileged teacher policy and knowledge distillation to a real-world deployable student policy, enhances robustness and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The system demonstrated robust whole-body control capabilities, including recovery from physical disturbances and stable navigation across various outdoor terrains.
- OmniH2O effectively supports multiple control interfaces, including VR-based, RGB camera-based, and verbal instruction, enabling real-time teleoperation.
- Autonomous policies learned from the OmniH2O-6 dataset successfully performed six complex whole-body tasks, such as 'Catch-Release' and 'Rock-Paper-Scissors', with high reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using kinematic pose as an intermediate representation provides a universal bridge between various human input modalities and robot actuation.
> - A two-phase training pipeline, combining privileged reinforcement learning with teacher-student distillation and domain randomization, effectively enables robust sim-to-real transfer for complex humanoid control.
> - Specialized motion data augmentation, including sequences with fixed lower body positions, enhances stability during dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots hold enormous potential as general-purpose platforms for performing a wide range of tasks in human environments. However, controlling humanoid robots remains challenging due to their high degrees of freedom (DoF) and inherent instability. The primary challenge lies in creating control systems that can coordinate both dexterous manipulation and stable locomotion simultaneously.

The OmniH2O system addresses this challenge by providing a universal framework for human-to-humanoid teleoperation and learning. It enables real-time control of humanoid robots through various input modalities, including VR headsets, RGB cameras, and verbal instructions. The system facilitates dexterous whole-body control, allowing the humanoid to perform complex tasks that require coordinated movement of both upper and lower body parts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

What sets OmniH2O apart from existing approaches is its combination of versatile input interfaces, robust whole-body control, and ability to learn autonomous policies from human demonstrations. By using kinematic pose as an intermediate representation, the system creates a universal bridge between human instructions and robot actuation, making it compatible with various real-world input sources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. System Architecture

The OmniH2O system consists of three primary components:

1. **Human Motion Retargeting**: Transforms human motion data into feasible motion goals for the humanoid robot.
2. **Sim-to-Real Training**: Employs a two-phase approach combining reinforcement learning and teacher-student distillation.
3. **Universal Real-world Teleoperation and Learning**: Enables various control interfaces and facilitates autonomous policy learning.

The system architecture is designed to bridge the gap between simulation and reality. It uses a physics simulation to train a robust control policy that can be transferred to real-world humanoid robots. The simulation environment allows for extensive training with various scenarios, disturbances, and terrains before deployment in the real world.

#### 3.2. Motion Retargeting and Augmentation

A key component of OmniH2O is its motion retargeting and augmentation pipeline. The system starts with large-scale human motion datasets (such as AMASS) and transforms them into feasible motion goals for the humanoid robot. This process involves:

1. **Retargeting**: Adapting human motion to the robot's kinematic structure, accounting for differences in joint configurations and limb proportions.
2. **Feasibility Filtering**: Ensuring that retargeted motions are physically feasible for the robot to execute.
3. **Motion Augmentation**: Enhancing the motion dataset with variations that improve the policy's robustness.

The researchers implemented a specialized motion data augmentation technique that includes sequences with fixed lower body positions. This approach enhances stability during dexterous manipulation tasks by allowing the robot to maintain balance while performing complex upper body movements.

#### 3.3. Phase 1: Reinforcement Learning

OmniH2O employs a two-phase training pipeline to develop robust control policies that can be transferred from simulation to the real world:

In the first phase, a privileged teacher policy is trained using reinforcement learning in simulation. This policy has access to comprehensive state information that isn't available in the real world (such as precise center of mass positions and velocities).

The reward function for the RL training includes components for:
- Motion tracking accuracy
- Energy efficiency
- Balance maintenance
- Foot contact consistency

Mathematically, the reward function can be expressed as:

```
R = w_track * R_track + w_energy * R_energy + w_balance * R_balance + w_contact * R_contact
```

Where `w_*` are weight coefficients and `R_*` are individual reward components.

#### 3.4. Phase 2: Teacher-Student Distillation

In the second phase, the knowledge from the privileged teacher policy is distilled to a student policy with limited state space to match real-world deployments. This approach addresses the reality gap between simulation and the real world.

The student policy only uses observations available on the real robot:
- Joint positions and velocities
- Body orientation from IMU sensors
- Root odometry from visual-inertial odometry (VIO)

Domain randomization techniques are applied during training to enhance the policy's robustness to real-world variations in physical parameters, sensor noise, and environmental conditions.

#### 3.5. Universal Control Interface

OmniH2O features a versatile control interface that supports various input modalities:

1. **VR-based Teleoperation**: Allows operators to control the humanoid robot using VR headsets, providing intuitive and immersive control.
2. **RGB Camera-based Control**: Extracts human pose from standard RGB camera feeds to control the robot.
3. **Verbal Instruction**: Processes natural language commands to generate motion goals.

The system integrates with frontier models like GPT-4o, enabling the humanoid robot to respond to high-level instructions and visual input. This integration demonstrates how large language models can be leveraged to enhance robot autonomy.

#### 3.6. Autonomous Policy Learning

Beyond teleoperation, OmniH2O facilitates autonomous policy learning from human demonstrations. The researchers collected a novel dataset, OmniH2O-6, containing teleoperated demonstrations of six complex whole-body tasks:

1. Catch-Release: Catching and releasing objects
2. Squat: Performing squatting motions
3. Hammer-Catch: Using a hammer and catching objects
4. Rock-Paper-Scissors: Playing the game with humans
5. Boxing: Performing boxing movements
6. Basket-Pick-Place: Picking up and placing objects in baskets

Using this dataset, they trained autonomous policies using imitation learning techniques, particularly a diffusion policy approach. The resulting autonomous agents can perform these tasks without human intervention, demonstrating the system's ability to learn from demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The OmniH2O system was extensively evaluated in both simulated and real-world environments. Key findings include:

#### 4.1. Robustness to Disturbances and Terrains

The system demonstrated remarkable robustness against physical disturbances and on various outdoor terrains.

#### 4.2. Comparison of Control Interfaces

Experiments confirmed that the system effectively supports multiple control interfaces, with each offering unique advantages:
- VR provides the most immersive and precise control
- RGB camera-based control offers accessibility without special equipment
- Verbal instructions enable hands-free operation for simple tasks

#### 4.3. Motion Data Augmentation Impact

The experiments verified the importance of motion data augmentation for policy robustness:

#### 4.4. Autonomous Task Execution

The autonomous policies learned from the OmniH2O-6 dataset successfully performed all six tasks with high reliability.

#### 4.5. Visual-Inertial Odometry Evaluation

The researchers evaluated their neural velocity estimator against traditional visual-inertial odometry (VIO) methods, showing comparable performance with better stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its capabilities, OmniH2O has several limitations that present opportunities for future research:

1. **Reliance on Root Odometry**: The system depends on visual-inertial odometry (VIO) for root position tracking, which can introduce errors in real-world deployment. Future work could focus on developing more robust odometry methods or policies that are less sensitive to odometry errors.

2. **Safety Considerations**: While the system shows robustness to disturbances, additional safety mechanisms could be implemented for extreme scenarios to prevent falls or collisions.

3. **Complex Terrain Navigation**: The current system demonstrates outdoor walking but struggles with more complex terrains like stairs. Enhancing the system to handle diverse and challenging terrains would increase its practical utility.

4. **Sensor Integration**: Incorporating additional sensing modalities such as LiDAR or tactile feedback could improve the robot's environmental awareness and manipulation capabilities.

5. **Autonomous Learning Efficiency**: The autonomous policy learning could be improved to reduce the amount of demonstration data needed and increase the complexity of learnable tasks.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

OmniH2O represents a significant advancement in humanoid robotics, providing a universal framework for human-to-humanoid teleoperation and learning. By combining versatile control interfaces, robust whole-body control, and autonomous policy learning, the system enables humanoid robots to perform complex tasks that require coordinated movement of both upper and lower body parts.

The system's effectiveness is demonstrated through various real-world experiments, showing its capability to handle dexterous manipulation, stable locomotion, and human interaction. The OmniH2O-6 dataset and the autonomous policies learned from it showcase the system's potential for scalable skill acquisition from human demonstrations.

By releasing their dataset and code, the researchers aim to accelerate research in humanoid robotics and enable the development of more capable and versatile humanoid systems. OmniH2O represents a step towards realizing the vision of general-purpose humanoid robots that can seamlessly integrate into human environments and perform a wide range of tasks.
