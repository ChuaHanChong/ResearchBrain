# Open-Reasoner-Zero: An Open Source Approach to Scaling Up Reinforcement Learning on the Base Model

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from StepFun and Tsinghua University presents Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ), the first comprehensive open-source implementation for scaling reinforcement learning directly on base large language models for reasoning tasks. ORZ demonstrates that a minimalist PPO setup, utilizing specific GAE parameters and no KL regularization, can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance while requiring significantly fewer training steps than comparable methods.

## Problem

- Previous work demonstrated a 'scaling phenomenon' in RL training for LLMs on reasoning tasks, where performance consistently improved with more computation, but the precise methodologies and open-source implementations were lacking.
- Most RLHF pipelines for LLMs begin with instruction-tuned models, leaving an open question about the feasibility and effectiveness of directly applying large-scale RL to base models for diverse reasoning skills.
- The complexity and instability often associated with large-scale reinforcement learning, particularly the tuning of regularization terms like KL divergence, posed a challenge for broader accessibility and research.

## Method

- Introduces ==Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ)==, the first fully open-source implementation of large-scale reasoning-oriented RL training applied directly to base LLMs, bypassing supervised fine-tuning.
- Employs a vanilla ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== algorithm with specific ==Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)== parameters (lambda=1, gamma=1) and a minimalist, binary (0 or 1) rule-based reward function.
- Critically, all KL-based regularization techniques (KL shaped rewards, KL loss) are explicitly removed, demonstrating that a simpler, unregularized setup can be highly effective and stable for scaling reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ)-32B surpasses DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B on challenging benchmarks like AIME2024 (**48.1%** vs **47.0%**), MATH500 (**92.2%** vs **91.6%**), and GPQA Diamond (**55.5%** vs **55.0%**), achieving this with only **1/10** of the training steps.
- The methodology consistently replicates the scaling phenomenon across various model sizes (**0.5B** to **32B**), showing steady growth in training reward and response length, validating the robustness of the minimalist approach.
- Ablation studies confirm PPO's superior stability over GRPO and validate the benefits of specific GAE parameters (lambda=1, gamma=1) and the complete removal of KL regularization for optimal training stability, performance, and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A minimalist PPO setup with a learned critic is surprisingly effective and stable for large-scale RL on base LLMs, especially when designed without KL regularization, simplifying training and improving efficiency.
> - Specific GAE parameters (lambda=1, gamma=1) are optimal for reasoning tasks, simplifying advantage calculation and stabilizing training by valuing long-term rewards and minimizing bias.
> - The learned critic in PPO plays a crucial role in assigning accurate token-level credit, effectively identifying and penalizing undesirable patterns like repetition, which contributes to superior training stability over GRPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the forefront of research into scaling reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models, specifically focusing on complex reasoning tasks. It operates within a nascent but rapidly evolving paradigm often referred to as "Reasoner-Zero" training.

**Background and Motivation:** Recent breakthroughs, notably OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek's R1-Zero, have demonstrated a remarkable scaling phenomenon in RL training for LLMs: as computational resources and training time increase, the models exhibit consistent and steady improvements in benchmark performance and response length on reasoning tasks, without signs of saturation. This indicated a highly promising avenue for developing more capable and robust reasoning agents. However, the exact methodologies and underlying insights for achieving such scale and stability, especially for DeepSeek-R1-Zero, were not fully disclosed.

**The "Reasoner-Zero" Paradigm:** The paper defines "Reasoner-Zero" training as the direct application of large-scale RL to *base models* (i.e., models that have not undergone prior supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or instruction tuning). This contrasts with many RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) pipelines that typically start from instruction-tuned models. The ambition is to teach LLMs diverse reasoning skills (arithmetic, logic, coding, common-sense reasoning, etc.) directly via verifiable rewards.

**Addressing the Gap: Open-Source and Simplicity:** While previous works demonstrated the *existence* of this scaling behavior, they often lacked comprehensive public details or open-source implementations. Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) steps into this gap by providing the *first fully open-source implementation* of large-scale reasoning-oriented RL training directly on base LLMs. A key differentiator is its demonstration that a surprisingly minimalist approach—vanilla Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with specific Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) parameters (𝜆=1, 𝛾=1), straightforward rule-based rewards, and crucially, *without* KL regularization—is sufficient to achieve and even surpass state-of-the-art performance. This challenges the common assumption that complex regularization or intricate reward shaping is necessary for stable and effective large-scale RL.

**Competitive Landscape and Differentiation:**
- **DeepSeek-R1-Zero:** The most direct competitor and inspiration. ORZ directly compares its performance and efficiency, demonstrating superior results with significantly fewer training steps using the *same base model* (Qwen2.5-32B). ORZ provides a much more detailed and open study of its training strategy compared to DeepSeek's brief outline.
- **DAPO and VAPO:** Concurrent efforts in Reasoner-Zero training. ORZ distinguishes itself by achieving comparable or superior performance (e.g., outperforming DAPO on MATH500 and GPQA Diamond) with fewer iterations and a simpler algorithm design, avoiding the value function learning challenges others might face.
- **Reasoning-Enhanced Models:** The paper also acknowledges a related line of work where models are first "reasoning-enhanced" (e.g., via SFT or distillation from stronger reasoning models) before RL training. ORZ shows its methodology is effective even in this two-stage approach (e.g., on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B), indicating the generalizability of its RL recipe.

By open-sourcing its code, data, and models, ORZ aims to democratize access to advanced RL techniques, foster reproducibility, and serve as a robust and accessible baseline for future research, contributing significantly to the broader goal of building more advanced reasoning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ)**, which is presented as the first comprehensive open-source implementation of large-scale reasoning-oriented RL training applied directly to base large language models (LLMs).

The key motivations driving this work are multifaceted:

1. **Exploring and Replicating the "Scaling Phenomenon":** Inspired by the observations from OpenAI's o1 and DeepSeek's R1-Zero, the authors are motivated to explore the phenomenon where increased training computation leads to consistent and robust improvements in both benchmark performance and response length in LLMs for reasoning tasks. They aim to replicate this scaling behavior with their own open-source framework.
2. **Democratizing Advanced RL Techniques:** A core motivation is to make large-scale RL training for reasoning tasks accessible to the broader research community. By providing an open-source implementation, including code, training parameters, curated data, and model weights, the authors seek to lower the barrier to entry, facilitate reproducibility, and encourage further exploration and innovation in this field.
3. **Investigating Minimalist RL Designs:** The authors hypothesize that a simpler, more robust RL setup might be sufficient for scaling reasoning capabilities. They are motivated to empirically test if a vanilla PPO algorithm with specific GAE parameters, straightforward rule-based rewards, and *without* KL regularization can achieve stable and scalable training. This aim addresses the complexity often associated with RL training, especially the tuning of regularization terms.
4. **Providing In-Depth Insights into Training Stability:** Unlike prior work that briefly outlined their pipelines, the authors are motivated to offer a comprehensive study of their training strategy. This includes in-depth insights into overcoming training instability, particularly from the perspective of value estimation in RL, and how their design choices contribute to robustness. They aim to analyze training dynamics, the effectiveness of value and advantage estimation, and conduct extensive ablation studies to solidify these insights.
5. **Setting a New Open-Source Standard:** By releasing comprehensive resources (code, data, and models across various sizes, including critic model weights), the authors aim to establish a strong, verified, and accessible baseline for future research in large-scale RL for reasoning, pushing for greater transparency and collaborative progress in the AI community.

In essence, the paper seeks to prove that "simpler is better" in large-scale RL for reasoning, while simultaneously making the entire pipeline transparent and available to foster wider research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) employs a carefully designed, yet minimalist, reinforcement learning approach to scale reasoning capabilities directly from base language models.

#### 3.1. Core RL Algorithm and Key Design Principles

The foundation of ORZ is the **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** algorithm. The authors explicitly choose PPO over Generalized Policy Rejection Optimization (GRPO), which was used in DeepSeek-R1-Zero. This decision is based on several key design principles derived from extensive empirical studies:

- **PPO over GRPO:** The crucial advantage of PPO is its use of a learned **critic (value network)**. This critic enables accurate token-level value estimation, which is vital for effective **credit assignment**. The critic can identify and devalue detrimental patterns, such as repetitive text generation, which GRPO struggles with due to its lack of a dedicated value network. This leads to more robust advantage estimates and significantly enhances training stability, preventing the training collapse observed in vanilla GRPO.
- **Simplified GAE Parameters (𝛾=1, 𝜆=1):** The authors found that setting the discount factor (𝛾) and the GAE parameter (𝜆) to 1.0 is optimal for reasoning tasks.
 - **𝛾=1:** Ensures that the model considers long-term dependencies and does not prematurely terminate generation, as all future rewards are equally weighted.
 - **𝜆=1:** In large-scale training with substantial data, variance concerns in advantage estimation are mitigated. A 𝜆=1 setting minimizes bias, which is preferred.
 - **Combined Effect:** This specific setting dramatically simplifies the GAE advantage calculation to ˆ𝐴𝑡 = 𝑅 − 𝑉𝜙(𝑠𝑡) and the value target to 𝑉target𝑡 = 𝑅 (the single terminal reward). This simplification contributes to both stability and computational efficiency.
- **Removal of KL Regularization:** Unlike common RLHF practices and some Reasoner models, ORZ explicitly removes all KL-based regularization techniques (KL shaped rewards, KL loss). This choice offers several benefits:
 - **Simplification:** Eliminates a complex and often challenging-to-tune design space.
 - **Efficiency:** Reduces computational overhead and memory usage by removing the need for a reference model and its log probability calculations.
 - **Exploration:** Allows the policy to deviate more freely from the base model distribution, potentially encouraging broader exploration of the action space.
- **Minimalist Rule-Based Reward Function:** A simple binary (0 or 1) reward function is used, based solely on the correctness of the final answer. No complex format-specific rewards (e.g., for enforcing structured thought processes like `<think>...</think>`) are employed. The authors found that even unaligned base models quickly adopt desired output formats when incentivized by this simple outcome-based reward, suggesting complex reward engineering is unnecessary.

#### 3.2. Detailed Settings and Data Curation

- **Base Models:** Training is performed directly on **Qwen2.5-{7B, 32B} base models**, bypassing any initial SFT or distillation stages. Experiments also extended to Qwen2.5-{0.5B, 1.5B} to demonstrate scalability across model sizes.
- **Data Curation:**
 - **Scale and Diversity:** The training dataset comprises tens of thousands of carefully curated math and general reasoning question-answer pairs.
 - **Sources:** Public datasets like AIME (up to 2023), MATH, Numina-Math collection, Tulu3 MATH, OpenR1-Math-220k, and AoPS forum. Additionally, programmatic synthesis is used for logical puzzles, multi-step reasoning, and counterfactual scenarios.
 - **Filtering:** Problems difficult to evaluate with a rule-based reward (e.g., proofs) are excluded. LLM-based filtering is applied to remove samples with extreme pass rates, ensuring a balanced dataset.
 - **Language Specificity:** Ablations showed English-only data yielded superior training stability and performance compared to a mixed English and Chinese dataset. This highlights the importance of data quality and consistency.
- **Prompt Design:** A specific prompt template (provided in Appendix Table 5) is used to elicit inference computation and structured reasoning from the model.
- **Training Configuration (Appendix B):**
 - **Network Initialization:** Policy and critic networks are initialized with Qwen-2.5 base model weights. The value head is randomly initialized. Policy and critic do not share weights.
 - **Optimizer:** AdamW with specific learning rates (1e-6 for policy, 5e-6 for critic), constant learning rate with linear warm-up.
 - **Batching & Rollouts:** Each generation step uses 128 unique prompts. The policy generates 64 responses per prompt (rollout size) with temperature and top-p both set to 1.0.
 - **Optimization Steps:** Strict on-policy optimization for the policy (1 generation = 1 policy optimization step). Critic network processes experiences in 12 mini-batches, performing 12 optimization steps per iteration.
 - **Stabilization:** Batch-level advantage normalization is applied. Importantly, no KL-related regularization terms or entropy bonuses are used, underscoring the stability of the vanilla PPO setup.
 - **Annealing Stage (32B model):** A final training stage on 13k "difficult" prompts (where the model achieved <4/64 correct answers in early training) is used for 100 additional steps with a linear learning rate decay. This targeted training aims to enhance capability on challenging tasks.
 - **Distilled Models:** The ORZ recipe is also applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B by initializing from its weights and training on the 13k difficult prompts.

This comprehensive yet simplified methodology allows ORZ to achieve robust, scalable, and efficient training for reasoning-oriented LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) models yield several significant findings, demonstrating the effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness of their minimalist approach to large-scale RL for reasoning.

1. **Superior Performance and Efficiency:**
 - **Outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Zero:** ORZ-32B, using the same Qwen2.5-32B base model as DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B, achieves **superior performance** across challenging benchmarks including AIME2024, MATH500, and GPQA Diamond (e.g., ORZ-32B: 48.1% on AIME2024 vs. DeepSeek: 47.0%; ORZ: 92.2% on MATH500 vs. DeepSeek: 91.6%; ORZ: 55.5% on GPQA Diamond vs. DeepSeek: 55.0%).
 - **Remarkable Training Efficiency:** Crucially, ORZ-32B achieves these results requiring **only 1/10 of the training steps** compared to the DeepSeek-R1-Zero pipeline (Figure 1). This highlights a substantial improvement in training efficiency.
 - **Comparison with DAPO:** ORZ-32B also achieves comparable AIME performance to DAPO-Qwen-32B but *remarkably outperforms DAPO* on MATH500 and GPQA Diamond (Table 1), suggesting advantages in data curation and handling diverse answer formats.

2. **Validation of the Scaling Phenomenon:**
 - **Consistent Scalability:** Across various model sizes (0.5B, 1.5B, 7B, 32B), ORZ demonstrates **consistent and steady growth** in both training reward and response length as training progresses (Figure 2). This successfully replicates the scaling phenomenon observed in other state-of-the-art models.
 - **Robustness to Fluctuations:** Interestingly, the response length curve for ORZ-32B shows fluctuations, but these do not negatively impact training stability or the continuous growth of reward, indicating the robustness of the minimalist method.

3. **Ablation Studies Validate Key Design Choices:**
 - **PPO vs. GRPO Stability:** Detailed analysis shows PPO’s **superior training stability** compared to GRPO. GRPO empirically demonstrates severe instability, with rewards destabilizing and responses degenerating into repetitive or incoherent text. PPO, in contrast, maintains stable rewards and low truncation/repeat scores (Figure 7).
 - **Role of Critic and Advantage Estimation:** The learned critic in PPO effectively identifies and devalues repetitive patterns, assigning **lower values** to states containing repetitions. Quantitatively, PPO's advantage assignments are consistently *more negative* for repetitive tokens compared to GRPO, demonstrating its superior ability to **penalize undesirable patterns** and foster a more precise learning signal (Figure 5).
 - **Optimal GAE Parameters (𝜆=1, 𝛾=1):** Setting GAE 𝜆=1.0 (with 𝛾=1.0) leads to **superior training stability and final performance** compared to 𝜆=0.95, resulting in faster and steadier reward progression and better response length dynamics (Figure 3 Left).
 - **Benefits of Removing KL Regularization:** Omitting KL loss and penalty mechanisms (W/O. KL) yields **optimal training stability, performance, and response length scaling** (Figure 3 Mid). It also simplifies training and reduces computational overhead.
 - **Importance of Data Scale:** Training with the larger, curated ORZ 57k dataset leads to **sustained improvements** in training reward and response length, whereas a smaller academic dataset (MATH train 7.5k) causes performance to plateau early (Figure 3 Right).
 - **Data Quality:** English-only datasets resulted in superior training stability and final model performance compared to datasets combining English and Chinese data (Figure 8), underscoring the importance of data curation.

4. **Strong Generalization Capabilities:**
 - ORZ-32B models demonstrate **strong generalization** on MMLU and MMLU_PRO, significantly outperforming Qwen2.5-Instruct-32B through *pure scaled-up RL training* on reasoning-oriented tasks, without any additional instruction tuning (Table 2). This indicates that reasoning capabilities learned through RL can transfer well to broader knowledge and multi-task benchmarks.

5. **Applicability to Smaller Models and Distilled Models:**
 - The ORZ training methodology consistently improves reasoning capabilities even for substantially **smaller models** (0.5B, 1.5B, 7B) (Table 4, Figure 6).
 - The ORZ recipe is also highly effective when applied to **distilled models**, with ORZ-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B outperforming the larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, showcasing further gains (Table 3).

In summary, the main findings strongly validate the ORZ approach as an exceptionally efficient, stable, and effective method for training reasoning-oriented LLMs directly from base models, challenging conventional wisdom regarding the necessity of complex RL components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Open-Reasoner-Zero (ORZ) represents a significant contribution to the field of large language model development, particularly in the realm of complex reasoning and reinforcement learning. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Democratization of Advanced RL Research:** ORZ provides the *first comprehensive open-source implementation* of large-scale Reasoner-Zero training. By releasing all training resources—source code, detailed configurations, a meticulously curated dataset, and various model weights (including critic weights)—the authors dramatically lower the barrier to entry for researchers. This fosters reproducibility, enables broader community participation, and accelerates the pace of research in an area previously dominated by well-resourced, often closed-source, industry labs. This open approach is crucial for collective scientific advancement.

2. **Validation of Minimalist and Efficient RL:** The paper's most profound technical insight is demonstrating that a **vanilla PPO setup with specific GAE parameters (𝜆=1, 𝛾=1) and simple rule-based rewards, critically *without* any KL regularization**, is not only sufficient but *superior* for scaling reasoning capabilities. This challenges the prevailing belief that complex reward shaping, intricate regularization, or more advanced RL algorithms are always necessary. The finding that simpler methods can be more efficient (1/10 the training steps of DeepSeek-R1-Zero) could lead to:
 - **Reduced Computational Costs:** Making high-performance reasoning LLMs more accessible for development and deployment, not just for elite labs.
 - **Simplified Research:** Researchers can focus more on fundamental LLM capabilities and less on tuning complex RL hyperparameters.
 - **New Benchmarks:** Establishing this minimalist approach as a robust and accessible baseline for future comparative studies.

3. **Deepened Understanding of RL Dynamics:** The in-depth analysis of training dynamics, particularly the role of the learned critic in PPO for accurate credit assignment and devaluing repetitive patterns, provides invaluable insights. This quantitative and qualitative analysis helps explain *why* PPO is more stable than GRPO in this context, offering practical guidance for addressing common RL training challenges like instability and degenerate outputs. This contributes to the theoretical understanding of RL applied to LLMs.

4. **Advancing LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By demonstrating significant performance gains on challenging reasoning benchmarks (AIME, MATH500, GPQA Diamond) and strong generalization to MMLU/MMLU_PRO, ORZ pushes the state-of-the-art for reasoning LLMs. The ability to directly instill complex reasoning skills into base models without prior supervised fine-tuning showcases the power of RL for this specific task. Furthermore, showing effectiveness on smaller models (0.5B, 1.5B) and distilled models expands the applicability of the method across a spectrum of model sizes and initial conditions.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines future directions, encompassing data scaling, model scaling (including multimodality and sequence length), test-time scaling (multi-turn interactions, value model assessment, multi-agent scenarios), and scenario scaling (generalizing reasoning to diverse domains like creative writing or scientific discovery). This forward-looking perspective, coupled with the open-sourced resources, positions ORZ as a foundational framework for a sustained research agenda aimed at building comprehensive reasoning agents, embracing the "bitter lesson" that what truly matters in AI is what scales effectively.
