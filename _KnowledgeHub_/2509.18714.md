# A Generalized Bisimulation Metric of State Similarity between Markov Decision Processes: From Theoretical Propositions to Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Southeast University and Purple Mountain Laboratories developed a Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM) for quantifying state similarity between distinct Markov Decision Processes. This metric is rigorously proven to possess fundamental metric properties and enables the derivation of strictly tighter theoretical bounds for policy transfer regret, state aggregation error, and sampling-based estimation error, along with a closed-form sample complexity expression, compared to prior bisimulation-based methods.

## Problem

- The traditional bisimulation metric (BSM) quantifies state similarity only within a single Markov Decision Process (MDP), limiting its direct application to theoretical analyses involving multiple MDPs.
- Prior attempts to extend BSM for multi-MDP comparisons often resulted in high computational costs, loose error bounds, and the loss of essential pseudometric properties like symmetry and the triangle inequality.
- There was a lack of rigorous theoretical guarantees for the long-term impacts of policy transfer and no explicit sample complexity expressions for estimating state similarities in multi-MDP contexts.

## Method

- Formally defined **GBSM** as a recursive function using the ==1-Wasserstein== distance between transition probabilities and the absolute difference in rewards, allowing comparison between a state in one MDP and a state in another.
- Rigorous proofs established that GBSM possesses fundamental metric properties: symmetry (d(s,M1), (s',M2)) = d((s',M2), (s,M1)), an inter-MDP triangle inequality, and a distance bound on identical state spaces.
- Applied the established GBSM properties to derive new, tighter theoretical bounds for policy transfer regret, value function approximation (VFA) and state similarity approximation (SSA) in aggregated MDPs, and sampling-based estimation with an explicit sample complexity expression.

## Results

- GBSM was formally proven to exist as a unique fixed point and demonstrated to provide an upper bound on the difference between optimal value functions: |V*_1(s) - V*_2(s')| ≤ d_1-2(s, s').
- Derived a policy transfer regret bound with a computational complexity of O(|S1|*|S2|), which is an improvement over the O(|S1+S2|^2) complexity of methods relying on disjoint union state spaces.
- Achieved strictly tighter theoretical error bounds for Value Function Approximation (VFA) in aggregated MDPs (˜σ1/(1-γ)) and State Similarity Approximation (SSA) aggregation (2˜σ1/(1-γ)) compared to existing BSM-based bounds, and provided a closed-form sample complexity expression for SSA estimation.
- Numerical experiments on random Garnet MDPs consistently validated that GBSM-based bounds were strictly tighter across all tested applications (policy transfer regret, SSA, VFA) compared to previous BSM-based bounds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - By defining GBSM using the 1-Wasserstein distance, the core metric properties (symmetry, triangle inequality) are preserved when comparing states across distinct MDPs, addressing a key limitation of prior multi-MDP adaptations.
> - GBSM provides a direct upper bound for the difference between optimal value functions of states in different MDPs, establishing a strong theoretical link between state similarity and value function disparities.
> - The derivation of an explicit, closed-form sample complexity for GBSM estimation offers practical guidance on data requirements, moving beyond previous asymptotic results for achieving desired accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) constitute a foundational framework in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for modeling sequential decision-making. A significant tool for analyzing MDPs is the bisimulation metric (BSM), introduced by Ferns et al. (2004). BSM quantifies state similarity within a single MDP in a policy-independent manner, providing theoretical guarantees that states closer under this metric exhibit more similar optimal value functions. This property has led to its successful application across various RL domains, including state aggregation, representation learning, policy exploration, goal-conditioned RL, and safe RL.

However, the inherent definition of BSM limits its direct application to theoretical analyses involving multiple MDPs. Prior attempts to extend BSM to multi-MDP scenarios, such as in policy transfer, have encountered notable challenges. For instance, Phillips (2006) proposed constructing a disjoint union of source and target MDP state spaces to enable inter-MDP comparisons, but this approach fixed the total variation distance between their transition probabilities to one, hindering further analytical simplifications and incurring high computational costs, especially in deep RL. Furthermore, approximation methods for continuous or large-space discrete MDPs, as explored by Ferns et al. (2011), yielded loose error bounds and lacked explicit sample complexity guarantees. Analyses based on BSM for value function approximation in aggregated MDPs also produced loose bounds, particularly with large discount factors (Zhang et al., 2021).

Several works have sought to adapt BSM for comparisons between multiple MDPs. Castro and Precup (2010) utilized a modified BSM for policy transfer analysis, but the absence of rigorous metric properties constrained their theoretical performance bounds to only reflect one-step action effects rather than long-term policy impacts. Song et al. (2016) empirically validated a modified BSM for assessing MDP similarities and improving policy transfer, yet their study did not provide in-depth theoretical analysis. A comprehensive survey by García et al. (2022) acknowledged the effectiveness of modified BSMs but highlighted the scarcity of theoretical guarantees in existing methodologies. This context identifies a gap in the theoretical understanding and rigorous establishment of metric properties for state similarity measures between distinct MDPs, which this paper aims to address.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two critical questions regarding the generalization of the bisimulation metric (BSM) to scenarios involving multiple Markov Decision Processes (MDPs):
- **Q1: Do generalized BSM variants possess metric properties?** Previous adaptations of BSM for multi-MDP comparisons often lost the pseudometric properties (symmetry, triangle inequality, indiscernibility of identicals) that are fundamental to BSM within a single MDP. The researchers aimed to determine if a modified BSM could be formally defined to retain analogous metric properties when comparing states across different MDPs.
- **Q2: How can these properties advance multi-MDP theoretical analysis?** If such metric properties could be rigorously established, the motivation was to explore how they could facilitate more precise and comprehensive theoretical analyses in multi-MDP applications, specifically addressing the limitations observed in prior BSM-based studies.

Arising from these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Formal Definition of GBSM**: To formally establish a generalized bisimulation metric (GBSM) for quantifying state similarity between pairs of MDPs.
2. **Rigorous Proof of Metric Properties**: To rigorously prove that this newly defined GBSM possesses three fundamental metric properties: GBSM symmetry, an inter-MDP triangle inequality, and a distance bound on identical state spaces. These properties are intended to align with and extend the pseudometric properties of BSM.
3. **Application to Multi-MDP Analyses**: To demonstrate the utility of these established GBSM properties by applying them to critical multi-MDP problems:
 - **Policy Transfer**: Deriving performance bounds for policies transferred between MDPs.
 - **State Aggregation**: Establishing tighter error bounds for state similarity approximation (SSA) and value function approximation (VFA) in aggregated MDPs.
 - **Sampling-based Estimation**: Providing a more accurate estimation error bound and, specifically, a closed-form sample complexity for approximating state similarities.
4. **Empirical Validation**: To conduct numerical experiments that validate the derived theoretical findings and illustrate the effectiveness of GBSM in practical multi-MDP scenarios.
5. **Extendability**: To demonstrate that the GBSM framework can be extended to handle variations like different action spaces (lax GBSM) and non-optimal policies (on-policy GBSM).

Through these objectives, the research aims to introduce a valuable theoretical tool that enhances the analytical rigor and practical applicability of bisimulation-based methods in complex RL settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a formal mathematical framework to define and analyze the Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM), followed by its application to specific multi-MDP problems and empirical validation.

**Formal Definition and Properties of GBSM:**
The core of the methodology is the formal definition of GBSM, `d((s, M1), (s', M2))`, between a state `s` in MDP `M1` and a state `s'` in MDP `M2`. It is defined recursively as:
`d((s, M1), (s', M2)) = max_a |R1(s, a) - R2(s', a)| + γW1(P1(·|s, a), P2(·|s', a); d_1-2)`,
where `W1` denotes the 1-Wasserstein distance, `γ` is the discount factor, and `d_1-2` is the GBSM itself, serving as the cost function for the Wasserstein distance. The existence and convergence of this metric are established using the Knaster-Tarski fixed-point theorem, proving that the iterative application of the GBSM definition converges to a unique fixed point.

Key theoretical propositions for GBSM are then rigorously proven:
- **Optimal Value Difference Bound (Theorem 3.3)**: It is shown that GBSM provides an upper bound for the difference between the optimal value functions of states in different MDPs: `|V*_1(s) - V*_2(s')| ≤ d_1-2(s, s')`. The proof employs induction on the value iteration steps and utilizes the dual linear programming formulation of the Wasserstein distance.
- **GBSM Symmetry (Theorem 3.4)**: This property, `d_1-2(s, s') = d_2-1(s', s)`, demonstrates that the state similarity measure is symmetric, regardless of the direction of comparison between MDPs. The proof is inductive.
- **Inter-MDP Triangle Inequality (Theorem 3.5)**: For three MDPs, `M1, M2, M3`, the GBSM satisfies `d_1-2(s, s') ≤ d_1-3(s, s'') + d_3-2(s'', s')`. This is a crucial property for metric spaces. Its proof first establishes a transitive property for the Wasserstein distance using the Gluing Lemma, which is then extended to the GBSM through induction.
- **Distance Bound on Identical State Spaces (Theorem 3.6)**: When `M1` and `M2` share the same state space `S`, an upper bound for `max_s d_1-2(s, s)` is derived based on the differences in reward functions and the total variation distance between transition probabilities. This property confirms that if `M1 = M2`, then `d_1-1(s, s) = 0`, satisfying the indiscernibility of identicals.

**Applications in Multi-MDP Analysis:**
The established GBSM properties are applied to three fundamental RL problems:
- **Policy Transfer (Theorem 4.1)**: A regret bound for transferring a policy from a source MDP `M1` to a target MDP `M2` is derived. This bound quantifies the suboptimality of the transferred policy in `M2` in terms of GBSM distances and the policy's suboptimality in `M1`. This approach avoids the computational overhead of constructing disjoint union state spaces, leading to reduced complexity.
- **Approximation Methods**:
 - **State Aggregation (Theorems 4.4 and 4.5)**: GBSM is used to derive tighter error bounds for both Value Function Approximation (VFA) and State Similarity Approximation (SSA) when states are aggregated. The VFA bound `˜σ1/(1-γ)` and SSA aggregation bound `2˜σ1/(1-γ)` are proven to be tighter than existing BSM-based bounds.
 - **Sampling-based Estimation (Theorem 4.6)**: GBSM enables the derivation of a tighter SSA estimation error bound and, notably, a closed-form sample complexity expression. The sample complexity `K` required to achieve a certain error `ϵ` with probability `1-α` is explicitly bounded using Hoeffding's inequality and the GBSM distance bound on identical state spaces.

**Extensions to BSM Variants:**
The methodology also demonstrates the generalizability of GBSM by extending it to:
- **Lax GBSM**: To handle MDPs with different action spaces, leveraging the Hausdorff metric.
- **On-policy GBSM**: To compute state similarities under non-optimal policies, adapting the reward and transition probability definitions. For both variants, the core metric properties and tighter bounds are shown to be preserved.

**Numerical Validation:**
The theoretical claims are empirically validated using random Garnet MDPs. Experiments compare the GBSM-based bounds against existing BSM-based bounds and ground-truth errors across various discount factors for policy transfer regret, SSA (aggregation and estimation), and VFA error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research successfully introduces and rigorously validates the Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM), establishing its fundamental properties and demonstrating its enhanced utility in multi-MDP analysis compared to the standard BSM.

**Formal Definition and Metric Properties:**
- **Existence and Convergence**: The paper formally defines GBSM between states of two distinct MDPs and rigorously proves its existence and uniform convergence to a unique fixed point, leveraging the Knaster-Tarski fixed-point theorem.
- **Optimal Value Difference Bound**: GBSM is demonstrated to provide an upper bound on the difference between the optimal value functions of any two states from different MDPs, i.e., `|V*_1(s) - V*_2(s')| ≤ d_1-2(s, s')`.
- **Fundamental Metric Properties**: Three core metric properties are rigorously established for GBSM:
 - **Symmetry**: `d_1-2(s, s') = d_2-1(s', s)`, indicating that the similarity measure is reciprocal between MDPs.
 - **Inter-MDP Triangle Inequality**: `d_1-2(s, s') ≤ d_1-3(s, s'') + d_3-2(s'', s')`, allowing for transitive comparisons across multiple MDPs.
 - **Distance Bound on Identical State Spaces**: When two MDPs share the same state space, GBSM provides a bound that simplifies to `d_1-1(s,s) = 0` if the MDPs are identical, confirming the indiscernibility of identical states.

**Tighter Bounds in Multi-MDP Applications:**
- **Policy Transfer Performance**: A novel regret bound for policies transferred between a source and a target MDP is derived. This GBSM-based bound is mathematically explicit and avoids the computational and analytical limitations of prior approaches that relied on constructing a disjoint union of state spaces. The computational complexity for calculating GBSM is `O(|S1|*|S2|)`, which is an improvement over `O(|S1+S2|^2)` associated with the disjoint union method.
- **State Aggregation Error**:
 - For **Value Function Approximation (VFA)**, GBSM yields a tighter error bound (`˜σ1/(1-γ)`) for the difference between optimal value functions of an original MDP and its aggregated counterpart, demonstrating an improvement over the existing BSM-based bound `2˜σ1/(1-γ)`.
 - For **State Similarity Approximation (SSA) aggregation**, GBSM provides a tighter aggregation error bound (`2˜σ1/(1-γ)`) compared to the `2˜σ1(2+γ)/(1−γ)` bound derived from BSM in previous work.
- **Sampling-based Estimation Error**:
 - For **SSA estimation**, GBSM enables the derivation of a tighter estimation error bound.
 - Crucially, it provides an **explicit and closed-form sample complexity** (`K ≥ -ln(α/2) * (γ^2 * R_bar^2 * |S·|^2) / (2ϵ^2 * (1-γ)^4)`) required to achieve a specified level of accuracy. This represents a significant advancement over previous asymptotic results that lacked explicit expressions for sample complexity.

**Extendability to BSM Variants:**
The framework's adaptability is shown by extending GBSM to:
- **Lax GBSM**: For comparing MDPs with potentially different action spaces, while maintaining core metric properties and providing similar transfer bounds.
- **On-policy GBSM**: For analyzing state similarities under non-optimal policies, where it also preserves metric properties and offers tighter VFA bounds compared to existing on-policy BSM results.

**Numerical Validation**:
Empirical experiments conducted on random Garnet MDPs consistently validate the theoretical claims. The numerical results demonstrate that the GBSM-based bounds are strictly tighter than corresponding BSM-based bounds in all tested applications: policy transfer regret, SSA via aggregation, SSA via estimation, and VFA. Specifically, the GBSM-based bound for policy transfer effectively bounds the ground-truth regret, whereas an existing empirical metric from prior work fails to do so.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the theoretical foundations of Reinforcement Learning by establishing a Generalized Bisimulation Metric (GBSM) for comparing states across distinct Markov Decision Processes. This advancement addresses a long-standing limitation of the traditional bisimulation metric (BSM), which was primarily defined for a single MDP.

The rigorous establishment of GBSM's fundamental metric properties – symmetry, inter-MDP triangle inequality, and a distance bound on identical state spaces – provides a robust mathematical framework for multi-MDP analysis. These properties enhance the analytical capabilities in complex RL settings, enabling more precise theoretical guarantees than previously possible.

The direct impact of this research is observed in the derivation of strictly tighter theoretical bounds across several critical RL applications:
- **Policy Transfer**: By offering tighter regret bounds and a more computationally efficient comparison method (reducing complexity from quadratic in combined state space size to a product of individual state space sizes), GBSM can facilitate more effective and reliable transfer learning between simulated and real-world environments, or between related tasks. This could lead to faster adaptation and better performance in target domains.
- **State Aggregation and Abstraction**: The tighter error bounds for Value Function Approximation (VFA) and State Similarity Approximation (SSA) imply that more accurate and efficient state abstractions can be developed. This is crucial for handling large or continuous state spaces, enabling the application of RL to more complex problems with reduced computational burden while maintaining performance guarantees.
- **Sampling-based Estimation**: The derivation of an explicit, closed-form sample complexity for GBSM estimation is a notable advancement. This provides practitioners with concrete guidelines on the amount of data required to achieve a desired accuracy, which is invaluable in data-scarce or expensive-to-sample environments. It can lead to more data-efficient RL algorithms and robust empirical evaluations.

Beyond these direct applications, the extendability of the GBSM framework to variants like lax GBSM (for different action spaces) and on-policy GBSM (for non-optimal policies) broadens its potential applicability across a wider spectrum of RL problems. This work provides a versatile tool that can serve as a foundation for future research in areas such as multi-task RL, where GBSM could aid in coordinating policy optimization, clustering similar tasks, and mitigating gradient interference. It also opens avenues for exploring average-reward MDPs, a noted limitation, and for applications in continuous control and deep representation learning where robust inter-MDP similarity measures are essential. Overall, the GBSM represents a significant theoretical and practical advancement for understanding and managing complexity in multi-MDP reinforcement learning.
