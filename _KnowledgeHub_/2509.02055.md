# Align-Then-stEer: Adapting the Vision-Language Action Models through Unified Latent Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called "Align-Then-stEer (ATE)" enables pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to adapt to new robotic embodiments and diverse tasks using limited data. This is achieved through a two-stage process that establishes a unified latent space for action representation and then steers the generative VLA policies, yielding notable success rate improvements and enhanced robustness in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face a bottleneck in efficiently adapting to new robotic embodiments or distinct downstream tasks.
- A substantial "action distribution mismatch" occurs between pre-training and adaptation data due to varying robot kinematics, degrees of freedom, and task-specific action requirements.
- Current adaptation techniques often demand extensive and costly data collection and fine-tuning, limiting the practical scalability and deployment of VLA models.

## Method

- The "Align" stage constructs a unified latent space using two InfoVAEs: one for pre-training actions and a second for adaptation actions, embedding the latter into modes of the former via reverse KL divergence.
- The "Steer" stage integrates classifier guidance, derived from this unified latent space, to explicitly direct the generative process of ==diffusion==- or flow-based VLAs during fine-tuning towards the target action distribution.
- The framework is designed as a model-agnostic, plug-and-play solution that integrates with various diffusion/flow-based VLAs without architectural modifications and incurs negligible computational overhead.

## Results

- ATE achieved an average multi-task success rate gain of +**9.8%** for RDT-1B and +**8.7%** for π0 in simulation, and a **32%** success rate gain on a real-world dual-arm robot compared to direct fine-tuning baselines.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced generalization across various real-world perturbations including varying illumination, spatial shifts, visual distractors, and human disturbances, exhibiting increased robustness.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the two-stage Info-VAE training scheme, which aligns adaptation actions into pre-trained latent manifold modes, is critical for achieving higher final success rates and efficient adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging action distribution discrepancies through a hierarchically structured, unified latent space is crucial for data-efficient cross-embodiment and cross-task VLA adaptation.
> - Employing reverse KL divergence during latent space construction effectively embeds adaptation actions into the modes of broader pre-training distributions, leveraging prior knowledge and preventing distribution collapse.
> - Classifier guidance, when applied within this unified latent space, provides an efficient mechanism to steer VLA policy generation towards desired behaviors using minimal adaptation data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has witnessed a significant paradigm shift with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Drawing inspiration from the successes of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Visual-Language Models (VLMs), VLAs aim to equip robots with general-purpose manipulation capabilities, enabling them to interpret language instructions, perceive environments, and execute complex tasks. Models like Octo, RT-1/2, and OpenVLA have established the foundation for this approach, primarily through extensive pre-training on large, diverse datasets of robotic demonstrations. More recently, a new generation of VLA models, such as RDT-1B and π0, has leveraged diffusion and flow-matching architectures to enhance performance, particularly in modeling multi-modal action distributions and generating high-precision continuous action sequences.

However, a critical bottleneck in the widespread deployment of these powerful pre-trained VLA models lies in their adaptation to downstream tasks. The primary challenge arises when the robot's embodiment (e.g., number of arms, type of end-effector) or the specific task differs significantly from the data seen during the pre-training phase. This discrepancy leads to a substantial mismatch in action distributions, typically necessitating extensive and costly data collection and fine-tuning for each new robotic platform or task.

Existing adaptation techniques attempt to mitigate this cost through various means, such as reducing parameter-level training, exploring different architectural designs, or constructing latent action representations. Some methods employ kinematics retargeting or physically interpretable action spaces, while others focus on optimizing training efficiency (e.g., caching visual tokens, LoRA-based updates). However, many of these approaches often struggle to effectively bridge the significant domain gap caused by embodiment mismatches, are restricted to specific VLA architectures (e.g., auto-regressive models), or involve complex system constructions.

"Align-Then-stEer (ATE)" directly addresses this central challenge by introducing a novel, data-efficient, and plug-and-play adaptation framework specifically tailored for *diffusion- or flow-based VLAs*. Unlike previous work that might focus on universal latent representations without explicitly resolving distribution discrepancies, ATE tackles the *action distribution mismatch* head-on. By creating a unified latent space that actively embeds adaptation actions into the modes of pre-training distributions and then steering the policy generation process, ATE provides a more robust and efficient solution for cross-embodiment and cross-task generalization. This work significantly advances the practicality of VLA models by reducing the prohibitive adaptation costs, thereby bridging the gap between powerful pre-trained models and real-world robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged "primary bottleneck" in deploying pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their inability to efficiently adapt to new robotic embodiments or distinct downstream tasks. While large-scale pre-training endows VLAs with remarkable general-purpose capabilities, the inherent differences in robot kinematics, degrees of freedom (DoFs), and task-specific action requirements often result in a significant "action distribution mismatch" between the pre-training and adaptation phases. This mismatch currently demands extensive and costly data collection and computational resources for effective fine-tuning, severely limiting the practicality and scalability of VLA models in diverse real-world applications.

Against this backdrop, the paper poses a precise central question: **"how can we achieve VLA adaptation for both cross-embodiment and cross-task scenarios under a limited data regime?"**

To answer this question, the key objectives of the "Align-Then-stEer (ATE)" framework are:

1. **To bridge the action-distribution gap:** Develop a novel alignment strategy that creates a unified latent space capable of compactly representing actions from fundamentally disparate pre-training and adaptation embodiments and tasks.
2. **To enable data-efficient adaptation:** Introduce a mechanism that explicitly steers the generative process of diffusion- or flow-based VLAs during fine-tuning, pushing their output distribution towards the target domain using limited adaptation data.
3. **To maintain architectural independence:** Design a "model-agnostic and plug-and-play" framework that seamlessly integrates with various diffusion- or flow-based VLAs without requiring modifications to their original architecture, incurring only negligible computational overhead.
4. **To demonstrate practical effectiveness:** Validate the proposed method across diverse embodiments and tasks in both simulated and real-world manipulation settings, showcasing consistent improvements in success rates and generalization over direct fine-tuning baselines.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is to present a general and lightweight solution that greatly enhances the practicality of deploying VLA models to new robotic platforms and tasks, making advanced robotic manipulation more accessible and scalable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Align-Then-stEer (ATE)" framework is a two-stage strategy designed to achieve data-efficient and robust adaptation of pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Alignment through Unified Action Latent Space

The first stage, "Align," focuses on constructing a unified latent space to bridge the significant domain gap between the action distributions encountered during pre-training and the specific actions required for adaptation. This is achieved using two distinct Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), specifically an InfoVAE variant, known for its ability to learn robust and informative latent representations.

1. **Learning Pre-training Action Latent (V_pretrain):** An InfoVAE (`V_pretrain := {E_ϕ, D_ϕ}`) is trained on the large, diverse, cross-embodiment pre-training dataset (`D_pretrain`). The encoder `E_ϕ` compresses action chunks (sequences of actions of length H) into a low-dimensional Gaussian latent distribution `q_ϕ(z|a_t:t+H-1)`. The decoder `D_ϕ` reconstructs the action chunk from the sampled latent vector `z`. The training objective maximizes log-likelihood, regularizes towards a fixed unit Gaussian prior `p(z)` via KL divergence, and includes a mutual information maximization term (InfoVAE objective) to ensure informative latents and prevent posterior collapse. This process yields a learned pre-training action latent distribution `q_ϕ(z)`.
2. **Embedding Adaptation Action Latent into Pre-training Action Latent Space (V_adaptation):** A separate InfoVAE (`V_adaptation := {E_ψ, D_ψ}`) is then trained on the *limited* adaptation dataset (`D_adaptation`) from the target embodiment. Crucially, its learning objective incorporates a KL regularization term (`D_KL(q_ψ(z|a_t:t+L-1)∥q_ϕ(z))`) towards the *previously learned pre-training action latent distribution* `q_ϕ(z)`. The use of *reverse KL divergence* (`D_KL(q_ψ∥q_ϕ)` rather than `D_KL(q_ϕ∥q_ψ)`) is a key design choice. The mode-seeking behavior of reverse KL divergence ensures that the learned adaptation latent distribution `q_ψ(z)` is compelled to embed itself into a specific mode of the broader pre-trained action latent distribution `q_ϕ(z)`. This effectively creates a unified and hierarchically structured latent space, bridging the embodiment and task gap while leveraging the rich prior knowledge from pre-training.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Steering Efficient Adaptation with Latent Guidance

The second stage, "Steer," integrates a classifier guidance mechanism to explicitly direct the fine-tuning process of pre-trained diffusion- or flow-based VLAs towards the target action distribution. This guidance operates within the unified latent space established in Stage 1.

1. **Classifier Guidance Formulation:** The core idea is to define a guidance function `g` that measures how closely an intermediate, noisy action chunk (`a^k_t:t+h` or `a^τ_t:t+h`) generated by the VLA aligns with the ground truth action chunk (`a^0_t:t+h`) from the adaptation data, all within the unified latent space. This function is formulated using the adaptation VAE's encoder `E_ψ`:
 `g = -∇_(a^k_t:t+h) ||E_ψ(a^k_t:t+h) - E_ψ(a^0_t:t+h)||^2`
 This gradient `g` indicates the direction in which the noisy action should be moved to better match the target action's latent representation.
2. **Integration into VLA Training Objective:**
 - **Diffusion-based VLAs:** The guidance `g` is used to modify the noise prediction `ϵ_θ` (the VLA's output) during training. The new objective `L(θ)` incorporates `+ √(1-α_k) ⋅ λ ⋅ g`, where `λ` is a guidance scale. This effectively steers the denoising process towards actions whose latents are closer to the target action's latent.
 - **Flow-based VLAs:** Similarly, for flow-based models that predict a velocity field `v_θ`, the objective `L(θ)` is modified by adding `((1-τ)/τ) ⋅ λ ⋅ g` to the predicted velocity.
3. **Benefits:** This classifier guidance calibrates the VLA's generative process at every noise level, ensuring that the policy's output consistently aligns with the target distribution. It achieves efficient adaptation with limited data, prevents the VLA from deviating too far from the learned structured latent manifold, and implicitly preserves valuable visuomotor prior knowledge from pre-training. Critically, this process requires no modification to the VLA's architecture and introduces only negligible computational overhead.

The entire framework is plug-and-play, with the Info-VAEs (Stage 1) trained once to establish the latent space, and their encoders then frozen to provide guidance during the VLA fine-tuning (Stage 2).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the ATE framework was rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, addressing key questions regarding its efficiency, generalization, and the criticality of its core components.

#### 4.1. Efficiency in Cross-Task and Cross-Embodiment Adaptation (Q1)

1. **Simulation Results (RoboTwin 1.0 & ManiSkill3):**
 - **RoboTwin 1.0:** ATE significantly boosted performance across 17 diverse manipulation tasks. For RDT-1B, ATE achieved an average multi-task success rate gain of +9.8% (from 31.8% to 41.6%). For π0, the average gain was +8.7% (from 36.1% to 44.8%). ATE demonstrated substantial improvements, with gains exceeding +30% on challenging tasks like "Empty Cup Place" for RDT-1B. It also showed improved sample efficiency, reaching higher success rates earlier in training compared to baselines.
 - **ManiSkill3:** On contact-rich single-arm tasks, ATE also exhibited consistent improvements. For "Push Cube," RDT-1B + ATE achieved a +13.2% gain (from 65.2% to 78.4%), and for "Pick Cube," a +7.2% gain (from 7.6% to 14.8%).
2. **Real-World Results (Dual-arm RealMan Robot):**
 - On a dual-arm RealMan robot, ATE achieved a "striking 32% success rate gain" compared to direct fine-tuning of π0. Across four long-horizon, bimanual tasks (Cook Bun, Pick Bun, Make Sandwich, Use Toaster), ATE’s average success rate at 120k training steps reached 58.1%, significantly outperforming the baseline’s 16.7%. Learning was also substantially accelerated, with tasks like "Cook Bun" reaching 100% success with ATE at 90k steps, versus 15% for the baseline.
 - **Qualitative & Quantitative Motion Quality:** Beyond success rates, ATE generated "smoother trajectories" and exhibited "more stable force control." Detailed 6-axis force data (provided in Appendix G) for the "Cook Bun" task showed that ATE-trained policies applied less excessive and unstable force compared to the baseline, mitigating undesirable effects like steamer deformation. This implies a safer and more robust physical interaction.
3. **Tool Use (Appendix B):** An additional real-world experiment on the "Make Yogurt Bowl" task, requiring bimanual tool use (spatula and spoon), showed ATE outperforming the baseline (25% vs. 15% success rate), further demonstrating its ability to handle complex, coordinated manipulation.

#### 4.2. Generalization Ability Analysis (Q2)

ATE was rigorously tested against various real-world perturbations using the dual-arm RealMan robot, demonstrating enhanced robustness:

- **Varying Illumination:** ATE consistently outperformed the baseline under low, high, and flickering illumination conditions, showing resilience to lighting changes not encountered during training.
- **Spatial Generalization:** When objects were displaced beyond typical training variations (larger radius of ~6.5 cm), ATE maintained commendable success rates (e.g., 60% for Pick Bun), highlighting its ability to generalize to new object positions.
- **Visual Distractors:** ATE effectively ignored irrelevant objects (fruits, puzzle pieces) placed as distractors, focusing on task-relevant items and achieving higher success rates (e.g., 80% for Pick Bun) compared to the baseline.
- **Human Disturbance:** ATE policies demonstrated robustness to sudden external interventions (e.g., removing an object from the gripper), showcasing an ability to recover and adapt behavior, achieving success rates up to 60% for "Make Sandwich" where the baseline largely failed.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Unified Action Latent Space (Q3)

An ablation study confirmed the critical role of ATE's two-stage Info-VAE training scheme (aligning adaptation actions into modes of the pre-trained latent manifold). Comparing it against a single-stage VAE trained only on task-specific data (using both π0 and Diffusion Policy backbones), the two-stage approach consistently yielded "higher final success rates, especially on long-horizon or challenging tasks." This finding directly validates that the proposed aligned and structured action latent space is crucial for efficient adaptation, enabling downstream policies to rapidly leverage prior knowledge and generalize across tasks and robot platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Align-Then-stEer (ATE)" framework presents a highly significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for the practical deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

1. **Addressing a Core Bottleneck in Robotics:** The most immediate and profound impact of ATE is its solution to the "primary bottleneck" of VLA model adaptation. By enabling data-efficient and robust adaptation to new robot embodiments and diverse tasks, ATE drastically reduces the prohibitive data collection and computational costs traditionally associated with fine-tuning generalist robot policies. This makes the deployment of powerful pre-trained VLAs to novel real-world scenarios far more feasible and scalable.

2. **Enhancing Practicality and Accessibility:** ATE's "plug-and-play" and "model-agnostic" nature for diffusion- and flow-based VLAs is a major strength. It seamlessly integrates into existing VLA training pipelines without requiring architectural modifications, incurring only negligible computational overhead. This ease of integration lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, accelerating the development and adoption of advanced robotic systems in various industrial and service sectors.

3. **Superior Performance and Robustness:** The demonstrated significant improvements in multi-task success rates (up to 9.8% in simulation and a striking 32% in real-world cross-embodiment settings) are compelling. Furthermore, ATE's enhanced generalization abilities across varied illumination, spatial shifts, visual distractors, and even human disturbances highlight its capacity to build robust and reliable robot policies that can operate effectively in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.

4. **Safer and Smoother Manipulation:** The qualitative and quantitative evidence of ATE leading to smoother trajectories and more stable force control is an important, albeit secondary, benefit. Safer physical interaction is paramount for human-robot collaboration and delicate object handling, and ATE's implicit guidance towards such behavior through its constrained latent space is a valuable contribution to robot safety and performance.

5. **Foundation for Future Generalist Robotics:** The "alignment-and-guidance paradigm" introduced by ATE could serve as a foundational approach for the next generation of generalist robot policies. By systematically bridging action distribution gaps and leveraging structured latent spaces, ATE lays the groundwork for fine-tuning highly capable foundation models to a vast array of specialized tasks and platforms with minimal effort. This accelerates the vision of truly adaptive and versatile robotic assistants capable of learning new skills on demand.

In conclusion, ATE makes substantial strides towards making VLA models practical for real-world deployment by providing an elegant, efficient, and robust solution to the critical challenge of cross-embodiment and cross-task adaptation. Its broad applicability and significant performance gains position it to have a lasting impact on the field of robot learning and the broader adoption of advanced robotics.
