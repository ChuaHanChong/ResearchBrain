# VLA-0: Building State-of-the-Art VLAs with Zero Modification

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers introduce VLA-0, a Vision-Language-Action model that achieves state-of-the-art robotic manipulation by directly representing robot actions as numerical text strings and fine-tuning an unmodified Vision-Language Model. This minimalist design outperforms more complex or extensively pretrained alternatives in both simulation and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models introduce complexity through architectural modifications, specialized action heads, or vocabulary alterations, leading to trade-offs like restricted action resolution or compromised language understanding.
- The purest form of representing robot actions directly as text using a VLM's native capabilities for low-level control has largely been unexplored, despite its potential for simplicity and arbitrary action resolution.
- Achieving high-fidelity action generation for embodied agents while preserving the foundational knowledge of powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models remains a challenge.

## Method

- **VLA-0** utilizes an unmodified, off-the-shelf ==Qwen-VL-2.5-3B== Vision-Language Model, avoiding new tokens, vocabulary changes, or additional neural network layers.
- Robot actions are represented as continuous values normalized to an integer range and generated directly as ==space-separated numerical text strings== by the VLM's native ==text generation== capability.
- The approach incorporates a novel ==masked action augmentation== during training and an ==action ensembling== technique during inference to enhance robustness and performance.

## Results

- VLA-0 achieves an average success rate of **94.7%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming all existing non-pretrained VLA methods and securing an average rank of **1.0**.
- It demonstrates highly competitive performance against models pretrained on vast robotic datasets, achieving the second-best average rank (**2.8**) among all evaluated models.
- In real-world evaluations on an SO-100 robot, VLA-0 surpasses a strong pretrained baseline (SmolVLA) by an average of **12.5 points** in success rate across **four** manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly representing continuous robot actions as numerical text strings and leveraging a VLM's native text generation, without any architectural modifications, can achieve state-of-the-art performance.
> - Preserving the integrity of the underlying Vision-Language Model by avoiding architectural changes can unlock latent capabilities for effective action generation and potentially enhance generalization.
> - Simple training and inference strategies, such as masked action augmentation and action ensembling, are crucial for extracting high performance from this minimalist VLA design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a critical frontier in artificial intelligence, aiming to extend the capabilities of successful Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to embodied agents. VLAs seek to enable robots to understand visual inputs, comprehend human language instructions, and execute physical actions in the real world. This paper explicitly positions itself within this rapidly evolving landscape by addressing a fundamental question: what is the most effective and efficient way to build such models?

Prior research in VLAs has largely coalesced around three main strategies, each with its own trade-offs:

- **Discrete Token VLAs:** Pioneered by models like RT-2 and OpenVLA, this approach discretizes continuous robot actions into a finite set of bins, assigning a unique token from the VLM's existing or extended vocabulary to each bin. The VLM then predicts these action tokens using a standard language modeling objective. While straightforward, this method faces limitations: it restricts the resolution of the action space (requiring thousands of bins for fine-grained control, which strains the vocabulary) and can "corrupt" the VLM's pre-trained language understanding by repurposing semantic tokens for motor commands.
- **Generative Action Head VLAs:** Models such as π0 and SmolVLA fall into this category. They avoid altering the VLM's vocabulary by attaching a separate "action generation head" on top of the VLM. The VLM fine-tunes to output a latent vector, which this auxiliary head (often a diffusion process or flow-matching network) decodes into continuous actions. This improves action fidelity but introduces additional neural networks, increasing complexity, potentially degrading the VLM’s language grounding capabilities, and compromising overall system generalization due to the non-pretrained nature of the action head.
- **Custom Architecture VLAs:** This category includes approaches like OpenVLA-OFT and π-FAST, which involve more substantial architectural modifications or specialized tokenization schemes tailored for action prediction. While effective, these methods typically entail significant engineering effort, additional parameters, and custom training pipelines.

VLA-0 directly challenges the prevailing assumption that these complex modifications or additional architectural components are necessary for state-of-the-art VLA performance. The authors contend that the "simplest strategy of representing actions directly as text has remained largely unexplored" in its purest form. While some prior works, like LLARVA and HAMSTER, have explored generating action plans or trajectories as text, they often involve multi-stage or hierarchical processes. VLA-0 distinguishes itself by demonstrating that direct, end-to-end generation of low-level robot actions as numerical strings, without any modifications to the base VLM, can be surprisingly powerful and even outperform more intricate designs.

Furthermore, this work draws a distinction from traditional robot learning policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, RVT, Act3D) that train policies from scratch on in-domain data. VLA-0, like other VLA models, leverages the powerful pre-trained representations of large Vision-Language Models, demonstrating that properly harnessing these foundation models can yield superior performance even on in-domain robotic tasks. By advocating for "zero modification," VLA-0 contributes to the growing interest in efficient fine-tuning and adaptation strategies for large pre-trained models, minimizing the need for extensive domain-specific engineering.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "VLA-0: Building State-of-the-Art VLAs with Zero Modification" is to **rigorously investigate the efficacy of the simplest possible approach to constructing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: directly representing robot actions as text and leveraging a pre-trained VLM's native text generation capabilities without any architectural or vocabulary modifications.**

The motivations driving this investigation are multi-faceted:

- **Questioning Prevailing Complexity:** The authors observe that current VLA approaches, despite their successes, invariably introduce complexity—either by modifying the VLM's vocabulary with action tokens, attaching new generative action heads, or implementing custom architectures. Each of these strategies comes with inherent trade-offs, such as restricted action resolution, compromised language understanding, increased model complexity, or degraded generalization. The core motivation is to ask: "Have we ruled out predicting actions as text? Why not represent actions (e.g., coordinates, joint angles) as numerical strings and generate them using the VLM’s native text generation capability?"
- **Preserving VLM Integrity:** A key motivation is to maintain the "integrity" of the underlying Vision-Language Model. By avoiding new tokens, vocabulary alterations, or additional neural network layers, VLA-0 aims to leverage the VLM's extensive pre-trained knowledge in both vision and language without interfering with its foundational capabilities. This includes preserving the VLM's original language understanding and grounding, which might be critical for open-world generalization.
- **Arbitrary Action Resolution:** Unlike discrete token VLAs that are limited by vocabulary size, representing actions as numerical text strings allows for arbitrary resolution in the action space. This means precise, fine-grained control can be achieved without the need for thousands of specialized tokens.
- **Efficiency and Simplicity:** The authors are motivated by the hypothesis that a simpler architectural design, if properly executed, could be as, or even more, effective than complex alternatives. This simplicity would reduce development overhead, potentially speed up training and inference, and make VLA development more accessible.
- **Unlocking Latent Capabilities:** The work is driven by the belief that existing VLMs might already possess the latent capabilities for effective action generation, which simply need to be "unlocked" with the right prompting and training strategies, rather than requiring extensive re-engineering. The paper aims to discover and distill the specific techniques necessary to achieve high performance with this minimalist design.

In essence, the paper seeks to demonstrate that "zero modification" is not a compromise, but a surprisingly powerful strategy that can lead to state-of-the-art results, challenging the established norms in VLA model development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-0's methodology is characterized by its adherence to the principle of "zero modification" to the underlying Vision-Language Model (VLM), coupled with a carefully designed training and inference recipe.

**A. Foundation: The Unmodified VLM**
- **Base Model:** VLA-0 is built upon the Qwen-VL-2.5-3B model, a publicly available, 3-billion-parameter VLM. The choice is motivated by its competitive performance for its size, computational efficiency, and open-weight nature, which fosters accessibility and reproducibility.
- **Core Principle:** Crucially, VLA-0 introduces no new tokens, does not alter the VLM's existing vocabulary, and adds no new neural network layers or specialized architectural components. The VLM's architecture remains untouched.

**B. Input and Prompting**
- **Input Structure:** VLA-0 inherits the standard input format of the base VLM, comprising:
 - **System Prompt:** A meta-instruction guiding the VLM's behavior. For VLA-0, this prompt explicitly instructs the VLM to analyze images and predict a sequence of actions as space-separated integers. An example provided is: "Analyze the input image and predict robot actions for the next H timesteps. Each action has D dimensions. Output a single sequence of H × D integers (0 - B each), representing the H timesteps sequentially. Provide only space-separated numbers. Nothing else."
 - **Images:** Visual observations from the robot's cameras (e.g., third-person and wrist cameras for simulation, left and right cameras for real-world). The paper notes that both separate and tiled image inputs yield similar performance.
 - **Task Instruction:** A natural language command specifying the desired robotic task (e.g., "put the banana on the plate").

**C. Action Decoding**
- **Textual Representation:** Continuous robot actions (e.g., joint angles, end-effector coordinates) are normalized to a fixed integer range (e.g.,). The VLM is then tasked with generating these integers as a text string, with individual action dimensions and timesteps separated by spaces.
- **Arbitrary Resolution:** This approach inherently allows for arbitrary action resolution simply by adjusting the maximum integer range (B in the system prompt) without needing to modify the VLM's vocabulary or introduce new tokens.

**D. Training Recipe**
- **Full Fine-tuning:** VLA-0 undergoes full fine-tuning of the base Qwen-VL-2.5-3B VLM. This means all parameters of the VLM are updated during training.
- **Loss Function:** A standard cross-entropy loss is used, optimizing the VLM to accurately generate the target action strings from its vocabulary.
- **Optimizer and Hyperparameters:** Adam optimizer, 64 training epochs, batch size of 192, and a learning rate of 5e-6.
- **Computational Resources:** Training requires approximately 32 hours on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Masked Action Augmentation:** A novel training augmentation is introduced. During training, random characters within the target action string are masked out. This forces the VLM to infer missing action values from the visual observations and task instructions, rather than solely relying on the autoregressive property of text generation (i.e., simply auto-completing a numerical sequence). This promotes deeper multimodal reasoning.

**E. Inference Recipe**
- **Ensemble Prediction:** VLA-0 incorporates an action ensembling technique, inspired by the Action-Chunking Transformer (ACT). At each inference step, the VLM predicts a sequence of `n` future actions. For the current timestep `t`, multiple predictions for the same action become available (one predicted at `t`, another at `t-1` as the second action in its sequence, and so on). These `n` predictions are then averaged to produce a more stable and robust final action for timestep `t`. This technique significantly enhances performance.

**F. Evaluation Setup**
- **Simulation:** The LIBERO benchmark is used, which is a standard for VLA model evaluation. It includes four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), each testing different capabilities, with 10 tasks per suite and 50 episodes per task. Success rate is the primary metric.
- **Real-World:** Evaluation is conducted on an SO-100 robot using the LeRobot framework. Four distinct manipulation tasks (reorient block, push apple, pick/place banana, pick/place cupcake) are used, with 100 training demonstrations per task. Policies are evaluated across varied initial conditions to assess robustness.
- **Baselines:** VLA-0 is compared against a comprehensive set of state-of-the-art VLA models from all three categories mentioned earlier, both with and without large-scale action pretraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core findings of VLA-0 are presented as surprisingly powerful and counter-intuitive, challenging the prevailing assumptions in VLA design.

**A. Simulation Results (LIBERO Benchmark)**

- **Outperformance without Large-Scale Pretraining:** VLA-0 achieves state-of-the-art results among models that *do not* utilize large-scale robotic action pretraining. It outperforms all existing methods trained on the same robotic data, including prominent models like π0.5-KI, OpenVLA-OFT, and SmolVLA.
 - VLA-0 achieves an average success rate of **94.7%**, significantly higher than the second-best method (π0.5-KI at 93.3%) in this category, securing an average rank of **1.0**. This demonstrates its superior performance across all four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long).
- **Competitive Against Pretrained Models:** Even more remarkably, VLA-0, *without any large-scale robotic action pretraining*, demonstrates highly competitive performance against models that *have* been pretrained on vast robotic datasets.
 - It surpasses popular pretrained methods such as π0.5-KI, π0, GR00T-N1, MolmoAct, Octo, and OpenVLA.
 - Overall, VLA-0 achieves the **second-best average rank (2.8)** among *all* evaluated models (including those pretrained), trailing only OpenVLA-OFT (average rank 1.5), which is a custom VLA model with significant architectural modifications. This indicates that the simple, zero-modification strategy can effectively contend with the best models leveraging extensive pretraining.

**B. Real-World Evaluation (SO-100 Robot)**

- **Superior Real-World Performance:** The strong performance observed in simulation translates effectively to the real world. VLA-0 is evaluated on four real-world manipulation tasks using an SO-100 robot.
 - VLA-0 outperforms SmolVLA, a strong baseline that was specifically pretrained on the large-scale SO-100 dataset, by an average of **12.5 points** in success rate. This finding further validates the robustness and practical utility of the VLA-0 approach on physical hardware.
- **Inference Speed:** The system achieves an inference speed of 4 Hz on a desktop with a 5090 GPU, using a standard PyTorch implementation. The authors suggest this could be further improved with optimization techniques.

**C. Ablation Studies (LIBERO Benchmark)**

A series of ablation studies highlight the importance of key components in VLA-0's recipe:

- **Action Ensembling:** This technique is a critical component, contributing a **2.0 point increase** in the overall average success rate. Disabling it significantly degrades performance.
- **Masked Action Augmentation:** The proposed training augmentation provides a consistent, albeit modest, benefit, improving the average success rate by **1.2 points**. It helps the VLM robustly infer actions from multimodal inputs.
- **Action Resolution:** An action resolution of 1000 (normalizing continuous values to) is found to be optimal for the LIBERO benchmark. Decreasing resolution to 250 leads to a **1.5 point drop** in success rate, while increasing it to 4000 yields no additional performance gains, suggesting a sweet spot for resolution.
- **Image Tiling:** The method of providing multiple image observations (as separate inputs or tiled into a single composite image) has **no discernible impact** on performance.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that a minimalist, zero-modification VLA design, when coupled with a carefully engineered training and inference pipeline, can achieve state-of-the-art performance in robotic manipulation, often surpassing more complex or extensively pretrained alternatives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "VLA-0: Building State-of-the-Art VLAs with Zero Modification" carry significant implications for the field of robotic learning and the broader development of generalist AI agents.

- **Re-evaluating VLA Design Paradigms:** The most profound impact of VLA-0 is its challenge to the prevailing belief that complex architectural modifications, specialized action heads, or vocabulary alterations are prerequisites for high-performing VLA models. By demonstrating state-of-the-art results with "zero modification," the paper advocates for a paradigm shift, encouraging researchers to explore the latent capabilities of existing VLMs through careful prompting and training strategies before resorting to architectural changes. This could lead to a simplification of future VLA research efforts.
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Efficiency:** The simplicity of VLA-0's design has direct benefits for accessibility and efficiency. It leverages open-weight, relatively smaller VLMs (like Qwen-VL-2.5-3B) and avoids the need for specialized hardware or extensive computational resources beyond those required for VLM fine-tuning. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning, enabling more researchers and smaller labs to contribute without requiring massive budgets for developing or training proprietary, complex models from scratch. Reduced architectural complexity also translates to potentially faster iteration cycles and less engineering overhead.
- **Preserving VLM's Foundational Knowledge:** By not modifying the VLM's core architecture or vocabulary, VLA-0 implicitly preserves the vast pre-trained knowledge encapsulated within the VLM's vision and language components. This is crucial because altering tokens or adding new heads might degrade the VLM's ability to understand novel instructions or generalize to unseen visual scenarios. VLA-0 suggests that this preservation could be a key factor in its surprising performance and potential for broader generalization.
- **Highlighting the Power of Textual Action Representation:** The work underscores the surprising effectiveness of representing continuous actions directly as numerical text strings. This approach provides arbitrary action resolution without compromising the VLM's language capabilities, addressing a fundamental trade-off faced by discrete token-based VLAs. It further demonstrates that large language models are capable of generating precise numerical sequences, extending their utility beyond semantic text generation.
- **Guiding Future Research and Optimization:** VLA-0 identifies key components (action ensembling, masked action augmentation) that are crucial for unlocking high performance from this simple design. This provides clear directions for future research into optimizing prompting strategies, data augmentation techniques, and inference algorithms for VLA models. The paper also explicitly outlines limitations, such as the need to explore VLA-0 with large-scale action data and optimize inference speed (e.g., via distillation or quantization), paving the way for practical real-world deployments.
- **NVIDIA's Role in Robotics:** From NVIDIA's perspective, this research solidifies its position as a leader in foundational AI for robotics. It showcases how NVIDIA's expertise in large models and high-performance computing can yield innovative and efficient solutions for complex challenges in robot manipulation, further integrating AI and robotics within their ecosystem.

In conclusion, VLA-0 represents a significant contribution by demonstrating that simplicity, when carefully engineered, can outperform complexity in the domain of Vision-Language-Action models. It provides a robust, efficient, and potentially more generalizable framework for developing robots that understand and act in the world.
