# Interactive Sketchpad: A Multimodal Tutoring System for Collaborative, Visual Problem-Solving

> [!summary] Summary
> MIT researchers developed Interactive Sketchpad, a multimodal tutoring system utilizing GPT-4o and code execution to provide dynamic, interactive visualizations and hint-based guidance for visual problem-solving. The system significantly improved accuracy on graph algorithms and mathematical functions (e.g., 100% on Maxflow from IsoBench dataset) and received high user satisfaction for enhancing conceptual understanding in various math problems.

## Problem

- Existing intelligent tutoring systems are predominantly text-based, struggling to support tasks requiring visual or spatial reasoning.
- Text-only feedback often falls short in subjects like geometry or calculus where diagrams and sketches are critical for comprehension and problem-solving.
- Traditional AI tutors can hinder active learning by providing direct answers, rather than fostering deeper conceptual understanding through interactive engagement.

## Method

- Interactive Sketchpad uses a pre-trained Large Multimodal Model (LMM), GPT-4o, enhanced with ==code execution via OpenAI Code Interpreter== to generate precise and dynamic visualizations.
- The system provides ==step-by-step textual hints==, fine-tuned on human demonstrations, alongside interactive visual aids displayed on a ==shared whiteboard==.
- Students can input text queries and interact directly with the whiteboard using sketches and annotations, which the LMM processes to provide context-aware feedback.

## Results

- Interactive Sketchpad achieved **100%** accuracy on 'Maxflow', outperforming GPT-4o (**25.0%** on Maxflow) and Visual Sketchpad (**66.3%** on Maxflow).
- User studies showed high satisfaction with an average Likert score of **4.71/5** for Interface Intuitiveness and Accuracy of Visuals, and **4.57/5** for Effectiveness of Hints.
- Qualitative feedback indicated that generated visualizations and the hint-based, iterative guidance significantly improved conceptual understanding and problem-solving engagement for students.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging LMMs with external code execution for visualization generation dramatically improves accuracy and robustness in visual problem-solving compared to relying solely on internal model reasoning.
> - A multimodal approach combining subtle, hint-based textual guidance with dynamic, interactive visual aids fosters deeper conceptual understanding and active learning.
> - An interactive whiteboard serves as a natural shared workspace, facilitating seamless human-AI collaboration for solving visually complex problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the confluence of several rapidly evolving fields: intelligent tutoring systems (ITS), multimodal machine learning (LMMs), and human-computer interaction for education.

Historically, intelligent tutoring systems have aimed to personalize learning experiences, but many, as highlighted by the authors, have remained largely text-based. While effective in certain domains, text-only feedback often falls short in tasks requiring visual or spatial reasoning, such as geometry, calculus, or physics, where diagrams, graphs, and sketches are indispensable for comprehension and problem-solving. Cognitive science has long affirmed the critical role of visualizations in reducing cognitive load, improving memory retention, and enhancing understanding of abstract concepts. Interactive Sketchpad directly addresses this limitation by moving beyond textual feedback to incorporate dynamic, visual aids.

The advent of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), such as GPT-4o, represents a paradigm shift. Previous research in multimodal interaction and visual reasoning, like VisProg, ViperGPT, and Visual Sketchpad, has explored using code generation for LMMs to interpret or generate visual content. These systems primarily focused on improving the *model's* visual reasoning capabilities or performing visual tasks. Interactive Sketchpad builds upon this foundation but crucially reorients the application: instead of merely enhancing AI performance on visual tasks, it leverages LMMs to *assist human learners* interactively. This differentiates it from prior work by centering on human-AI collaboration within an educational context, specifically for tutoring.

Furthermore, the paper engages with the broader discourse on AI for education. While LMMs offer immense potential for automating educational tasks and providing personalized assistance, concerns have been raised about the negative impact of direct answer generation on student learning outcomes. Interactive Sketchpad aligns with research advocating for moderated LMM responses that provide hints rather than full solutions, thereby encouraging active learning and critical thinking. By combining textual hints with automatically generated, interactive visual aids, this work pushes the boundaries of how AI can foster deeper conceptual understanding and engagement in an educational setting, bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application through multimodal interaction. It also extends concepts like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which guides LLMs through intermediate steps, to a visual chain-of-thought where visual aids are progressively built and interacted with, creating a natural, collaborative reasoning loop between student and AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Interactive Sketchpad stems from the recognized gap in existing intelligent tutoring systems: their overwhelming reliance on text-based feedback, which often fails to support problems requiring visual and spatial reasoning. Humans instinctively use sketches and diagrams to simplify complex ideas, from drawing auxiliary lines in geometry to visualizing graphs in calculus. The paper argues that current educational technologies, predominantly text-only, are unintuitive and insufficient for these visually-oriented tasks, hindering effective learning and conceptual understanding.

Leveraging recent advancements in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), the core objective of Interactive Sketchpad is to **transform educational tutoring by integrating language-based explanations with dynamic, interactive visualizations**. This system aims to create a natural, multimodal learning experience that significantly enhances students' engagement, comprehension, and problem-solving accuracy.

Specifically, the key objectives are to:
- **Enable Multimodal Interaction:** Develop a system that can process both textual and visual inputs from students and provide feedback in both modalities.
- **Generate Accurate and Robust Visual Aids:** Utilize code execution to automatically generate precise and relevant diagrams and sketches that complement textual explanations.
- **Provide Step-by-Step Guidance:** Offer subtle hints and explanations iteratively, encouraging students to actively participate in problem-solving rather than passively receiving answers.
- **Improve Conceptual Understanding:** Facilitate deeper learning by allowing students to interact directly with visual representations of problems, thereby making abstract concepts more concrete and intuitive.
- **Foster Human-AI Collaboration:** Create a seamless interaction loop where students and the LMM can collaboratively build upon shared visual and textual information, mimicking a natural tutoring dialogue.

In essence, the system seeks to democratize access to personalized, visually-rich tutoring experiences that mirror effective human teaching methodologies, addressing the limitations of prior AI-driven educational tools that might inadvertently deter active learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Interactive Sketchpad employs a sophisticated multimodal interaction loop built upon a pre-trained Large Multimodal Model (LMM), specifically **GPT-4o**, and enhanced with code execution capabilities. The system operates through four interconnected components:

1. **Problem Analysis:** The process begins with the LMM (GPT-4o) evaluating the student's query and the problem statement to determine if a visualization would be beneficial. This initial step leverages the LMM's understanding to identify tasks where visual aids are particularly helpful.

2. **Visualization Generation:** If a visualization is deemed necessary, the system generates a Python program designed to create the appropriate visual representation. This is achieved by prompting the LMM to write Python code that utilizes common libraries like `matplotlib` for plotting. The generated code is then executed via the OpenAI Code Interpreter tool. A crucial aspect here is the robustness mechanism: if the code fails to run, the LMM iterates by attempting to generate and execute different code until a successful visualization is produced. This ensures the accuracy and reliability of the generated diagrams, a significant improvement over purely generative visual models that might produce inaccurate or ambiguous images.

3. **Hint Generation:** Concurrently with or subsequent to visualization, the GPT-4o model generates textual hints. To ensure these hints are effective and promote active learning, the LMM is fine-tuned on human demonstrations of "good hints." These demonstrations model a conversational flow between a student and a tutor, where hints are subtle, step-by-step, interleaved with images, and avoid revealing the final answer directly. In subsequent interactions, the model considers the entire conversation history, a screenshot of the user's interactive whiteboard (if available), and the current query to provide context-aware and personalized guidance. Moreover, to minimize calculation errors, the LMM is given access to the OpenAI Code Interpreter for performing mathematical computations (e.g., numerical integration, arithmetic), integrating computational accuracy into the reasoning process.

4. **Interactive Whiteboard:** This is a central component for human-AI collaboration. The generated hints and visualizations are displayed in a chatbot interface, but if a visualization is created, it also appears on a dedicated interactive whiteboard. Students can freely annotate, sketch, or write on this whiteboard using input devices like an iPad and Apple Pencil. They can then capture and send snapshots of their annotated work, along with additional text, back to the LMM. This annotated input becomes part of the LMM's context, allowing the system to understand the student's thought process, provide feedback on their visual work, and continue the iterative reasoning cycle. This shared visual workspace fosters a natural and seamless interaction between the human learner and the AI tutor.

The user interface comprises a traditional chatbot interface for textual and image input/output, and a specialized interactive whiteboard interface for visual collaboration.

For experimental validation, the authors conducted:
- **Performance Comparisons (RQ0):** Interactive Sketchpad was evaluated against GPT-4o and Visual Sketchpad on the IsoBench dataset for graph algorithms and mathematical functions. The goal was to assess its direct problem-solving accuracy, particularly highlighting the benefit of code execution.
- **User Studies (RQ1 & RQ2):** University undergraduate students (initially 7 participants) interacted with Interactive Sketchpad and a text-only system (ChatGPT) to solve various math problems (geometry, calculus, trigonometry) sourced from SAT, IsoBench, Geometry3k, and other studies. The studies aimed to evaluate improvements in conceptual understanding and the effectiveness of step-by-step hint-based guidance versus direct answers. Metrics included qualitative feedback (user comments) and quantitative ratings (Likert scale) on interface intuitiveness, system responsiveness, visual quality, and hint effectiveness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments and user studies conducted on Interactive Sketchpad yielded compelling results that validate its design and effectiveness as a multimodal tutoring system.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparisons (RQ0: System Accuracy)

On the IsoBench dataset, which evaluates capabilities in graph algorithms and mathematical functions, Interactive Sketchpad consistently outperformed both GPT-4o and Visual Sketchpad. For example, it achieved 100% accuracy on "Maxflow" compared to 25.0% for GPT-4o and 66.3% for Visual Sketchpad, and significantly higher scores across other categories like "Isomorphism," "Connectivity," "Convexity," and "Parity." This strong performance highlights the effectiveness of Interactive Sketchpad's approach, particularly its integration of code execution for precise calculations and robust visualization generation, which reduces errors that could otherwise confuse the student. The authors emphasize that removing the constraint of relying solely on internal visual reasoning (as in some other models) in favor of external code execution for calculations dramatically improves problem-solving accuracy.

#### 4.2. User Studies (Qualitative Results)

Qualitative feedback from university students was overwhelmingly positive, underscoring the system's ability to enhance the learning experience:
- **Visualization Quality:** Participants highly praised the quality and utility of the generated visualizations, noting that "The visual illustrations help a lot. The intuitive drawing makes the interaction feel more natural" and provided "conceptual understanding outside of just equations."
- **Interactive Experience:** Users appreciated the system's iterative, hint-based approach, preferring it to direct answers. Comments such as "It was nice that it didn’t give me the final answer right away, and instead gave hints/prompts to try" and "I like that it guides you through the problem-solving approach without jumping straight to the answer, like ChatGPT" confirmed that the system effectively fostered active engagement.
- **Learning Experience:** The multimodal approach significantly improved learning. Students found the visualizations "particularly helpful for solving the integral," making "the math feel more intuitive/meaningful." The ability to interact directly on the sketchpad was seen as a natural way to develop problem-solving strategies and gain insights.

#### 4.3. User Studies (Quantitative Results)

Quantitative feedback, gathered through a questionnaire using a 5-point Likert scale, reinforced the positive qualitative findings:
- **High Usability and Visual Quality:** The system received very high average scores for Interface Intuitiveness (4.71/5) and Accuracy of Visuals (4.71/5). Clarity of Visuals also scored highly (4.57/5). These scores indicate that users found the system easy to navigate and highly valued the precision and design of the visual aids.
- **Effective Hints:** The Effectiveness of Hints was rated at 4.57/5, confirming that the tailored, step-by-step guidance was well-received and beneficial for learning.
- **System Responsiveness:** While still favorable, System Responsiveness received a slightly lower average score of 4.43/5. This suggests a potential area for future improvement in optimizing the system's speed to reduce waiting times for hints and visualizations.

In summary, the results demonstrate that Interactive Sketchpad significantly improves task comprehension, problem-solving accuracy, and student engagement. It successfully addresses the limitations of text-based tutoring by providing a dynamic, multimodal learning environment that encourages active participation and deeper conceptual understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Interactive Sketchpad represents a significant advancement in educational technology and human-AI interaction, holding substantial potential to transform learning experiences, particularly in STEM fields.

**1. Revolutionizing Educational Tutoring:** By effectively bridging the gap between traditional text-based learning and intuitive visual reasoning, Interactive Sketchpad offers a new paradigm for intelligent tutoring systems. It moves beyond passive information delivery to active, collaborative problem-solving, which is crucial for deeper learning and knowledge retention. This multimodal approach could make complex subjects like mathematics and physics more accessible and engaging for a broader range of learners.

**2. Enhancing Conceptual Understanding and Problem-Solving:** The core strength of the system lies in its ability to provide accurate, dynamic visualizations alongside textual hints. This directly addresses the cognitive benefits of visual aids, helping students grasp abstract concepts, reduce cognitive load, and develop robust problem-solving strategies. The iterative nature of the system, coupled with the interactive whiteboard, encourages students to internalize problem-solving steps rather than merely memorizing solutions.

**3. Advancing Human-AI Collaboration:** Interactive Sketchpad pushes the boundaries of how humans and AI can collaborate. By establishing a natural, shared workspace (the interactive whiteboard) where both modalities—text and visuals—are seamlessly integrated, the system demonstrates a sophisticated level of human-AI partnership. This framework can inspire the development of more human-centered AI tools across various domains, where AI acts as an intelligent co-creator and guide rather than just a task automation agent.

**4. Mitigating Negative Impacts of Generative AI in Education:** The paper consciously addresses the documented concern that direct answer provision by large language models can hinder student learning. By focusing on subtle, step-by-step hints and encouraging active problem-solving, Interactive Sketchpad offers a responsible and pedagogically sound model for integrating powerful generative AI into educational settings, promoting true learning over rote answer recall.

**5. Foundation for Future Research and Applications:** The work opens several avenues for future research. Expanding the system to other STEM disciplines (e.g., physics force diagrams, chemistry molecular structures) and evaluating its effectiveness with a larger, more diverse user base are logical next steps. Furthermore, assessing the long-term impact on knowledge retention and skill development will be crucial for establishing its enduring educational value. Improvements in visualization accuracy through AI-powered verification mechanisms could further enhance the system's reliability and user trust.

In conclusion, Interactive Sketchpad demonstrates a powerful and effective approach to leveraging cutting-edge LMMs for personalized, multimodal education. Its emphasis on interactive, visual guidance and collaborative problem-solving positions it as a significant contribution to the fields of AI for education and human-computer interaction, with the potential to genuinely transform how students engage with and master complex subjects.
