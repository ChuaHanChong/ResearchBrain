# EgoExo-WM: Unlocking Exo Video for Ego World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The EgoExo-WM framework demonstrates a method to enable egocentric world models to learn from abundant exocentric video by synthesizing action-aligned egocentric views. This approach reduces L2 embedding error by over 50% on certain datasets and improves goal-directed planning for 3D human motion compared to models trained solely on egocentric data.

## Problem

- Egocentric video data is difficult and expensive to collect at scale, limiting the diversity of activities for world model training.
- Egocentric views often suffer from partial observability, obscuring critical body parts like hands, which hinders precise 3D human motion extraction for defining an action space.
- Abundant exocentric (third-person) video data lacks direct alignment with an agent's first-person perception and action space, making it unsuitable for direct egocentric world model training.

## Method

- The **EgoExo-WM** framework trains egocentric world models using a ==human motion-conditioned architecture== that learns jointly from real egocentric data and synthetic egocentric data derived from exocentric videos.
- ==EgoX-Body== is developed as an extension to synthesize visually plausible and action-aligned egocentric videos from exocentric sources by explicitly incorporating ==3D human kinematic priors== and egocentric hand overlays.
- A ==69-parameter 3D human motion vector==, encompassing root translation and SMPL joint rotations, is used as an explicit and interpretable action representation, complemented by an auxiliary ==wrist-position consistency loss== for hand interaction fidelity.

## Results

- EgoExo-WM consistently outperformed baselines in open-loop future visual predictions, reducing average L2 embedding error by over **50%** on HOMAGE and LEMMA datasets and achieving higher wrist PCK@20 for action consistency.
- The framework enhanced goal-directed planning, yielding the lowest Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) for both whole-body and wrist motion across all evaluated datasets in model-predictive control tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the performance gains are directly attributable to the action-aligned egocentric conversion from exocentric video, as models trained with raw exocentric data or only egocentric data showed inferior results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abundant exocentric video can be effectively utilized to scale egocentric world model training, provided the data is appropriately transformed into an action-aligned egocentric format.
> - Explicitly incorporating 3D human kinematic priors during exocentric-to-egocentric video conversion is crucial for synthesizing high-quality, action-consistent egocentric data, overcoming limitations of prior view synthesis methods.
> - Training a world model with this expanded, action-aligned data, leveraging 3D human motion, substantially improves both future visual prediction and goal-directed planning for embodied agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development and scalability of egocentric world models. Egocentric world models aim to provide agents with a first-person predictive understanding of how future states evolve under their own embodied actions. Such models are considered foundational for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and assistive technologies, where anticipating the consequences of human motion and interaction is crucial.

The existing landscape for training egocentric world models faces two primary limitations:
1. **Data Scarcity**: Egocentric video data, which requires individuals to wear cameras while performing diverse activities, is challenging and expensive to collect at scale. Existing egocentric datasets are significantly smaller than the vast quantities of exocentric (third-person) video available online.
2. **Partial Observability**: Egocentric views inherently provide a limited perspective of the body, often occluding hands and other body parts during interactions. This makes it difficult to extract the precise 3D human motion needed to define the action space for world models.

In contrast, exocentric video is abundant and typically captures full-body motion effectively. However, exocentric video lacks direct alignment with an agent's first-person action space.

Prior research has explored various aspects relevant to this work:
- **World Models**: General world models have been used for generating synthetic training data, improving policies, and learning action-conditioned representations for planning.
- **Egocentric World Models**: Recent efforts have focused on learning predictive dynamics from wearable camera videos, often conditioning on explicit body, hand, or head motion. These are limited by the scarcity of data with low-level action annotations and the restricted body pose visibility. Some approaches attempt to bypass explicit actions by learning latent representations or conditioning on text, but these can compromise the interpretability and precision required for fine-grained control.
- **Exocentric-to-Egocentric (Exo-to-Ego) Generation**: Methods exist to synthesize egocentric observations from exocentric videos. These are challenging due to significant viewpoint differences and occlusions. While some employ geometric representations to improve cross-view consistency, they often emphasize scene-level alignment, with human information transfer typically relying on egocentric hand priors rather than full-body dynamics. Existing frameworks like EgoX have shown promise but can exhibit action misalignment, such as incorrect hand-object interactions or hallucinated arm movements, due to a lack of explicit human priors.
- **Human Pose Estimation**: Significant progress has been made in recovering 2D or 3D human structure from monocular video, providing a compact, semantically meaningful representation of human motion useful for downstream tasks like motion prediction and robot learning.

This paper addresses the core challenge of scaling egocentric world modeling by bridging the gap between abundant exocentric video and the specific needs of egocentric models. It aims to leverage the strengths of exocentric data (abundance, full-body visibility) while overcoming its misalignment with egocentric perspectives and integrating a precise 3D human motion action space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the scalability and performance of egocentric world models by enabling them to learn from the vast and diverse repository of exocentric video data. This is motivated by the inherent limitations of purely egocentric training data.

The key specific objectives and motivations are:

1. **Overcome Egocentric Data Scarcity**: Egocentric datasets are difficult and costly to collect, limiting the diversity of activities, environments, and human interactions available for training. The motivation is to "unlock" internet-scale exocentric videos as a supplementary data source, drastically expanding the potential training pool for egocentric world models.
2. **Address Partial Observability of Human Actions in Egocentric Views**: Egocentric videos often obscure the human body, particularly hands, which are critical for defining the action space of embodied agents. Exocentric videos, in contrast, frequently provide a clearer, full-body view. The objective is to extract a comprehensive and accurate 3D human motion representation from exocentric video to serve as the action space for egocentric world models.
3. **Bridge the Viewpoint Discrepancy through Action-Aligned Synthesis**: Simply using raw exocentric video is insufficient as it does not align with an egocentric agent's perception or action space. The motivation is to develop a robust method (EgoX-Body) that can transform exocentric video into visually plausible and, crucially, *action-aligned* egocentric video. This involves incorporating human kinematic priors to ensure that the synthesized egocentric views accurately reflect the underlying human motion observed in the exocentric source, mitigating issues like action misalignment seen in prior view synthesis methods.
4. **Improve Egocentric World Model Prediction Quality**: By training egocentric world models with a combination of real egocentric data and a broad range of high-quality, action-aligned synthetic egocentric data, the aim is to improve the models' ability to accurately predict future egocentric visual states given a sequence of 3D human actions. This enables the models to learn more generalized and robust predictive dynamics.
5. **Enable Robust Downstream Planning and Control**: A well-trained egocentric world model should not only predict but also facilitate goal-conditioned planning. The motivation is to demonstrate that the proposed framework can support model-predictive control (MPC)-style planning, allowing the agent to infer and select sequences of 3D human motions that are predicted to achieve a specific visual goal state. This is critical for applications like robot manipulation and augmented reality guidance, where precise, interpretable action sequences are required.
6. **Provide a Scalable and Interpretable Action Representation**: Rather than relying on difficult-to-interpret latent actions or coarse textual descriptions, the use of 3D human motion as the explicit action space offers a physically grounded and interpretable representation. This facilitates more precise embodied control and makes the world model's internal dynamics more transparent.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the vision of agents learning about the world through observation, similar to how humans learn, by mentally transforming observed behavior into anticipated first-person experiences to predict how actions would reshape the visual scene.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EgoExo-WM framework is designed to leverage exocentric videos for training egocentric world models by unifying exocentric observation, world model learning, and egocentric video synthesis through 3D human motion.

#### 3.1. World Model Training

The EgoExo-WM formulates a human motion-conditioned egocentric world model that predicts future egocentric observations given past states and a 3D human motion action.

- **Action Representation**: An action `a_t` is defined as a structured vector of 69 parameters. This vector captures the change in root translation (3 dimensions) and the relative rotations of 22 SMPL body joints, with each joint rotation parameterized by Euler angles (22 * 3 = 66 dimensions). This representation enables a physically grounded and interpretable action space.
- **Visual Latent Space**: To optimize for computational efficiency and semantic preservation, the world model operates in a visual latent space. A pretrained DINOv3-L encoder `E` maps egocentric observations `x_t` to visual latents `z_t = E(x_t)`. The world model `f_θ` predicts the next visual latent `z_t+1` given a context window of `H` past visual latents and the current 3D human motion action.
- **Training Objective**: The model is supervised using a combination of two loss components:
 1. **Latent Loss (`L_latent`)**: An L2 distance between the predicted next visual latent `z_t+1` and the ground-truth latent `E(x_t+1)`. This ensures accurate prediction of the future egocentric visual state.
 2. **Wrist-Position Consistency Loss (`L_wrist`)**: An auxiliary objective designed to maintain physically plausible hand motion, crucial for egocentric interactions. A lightweight Transformer head `h_ϕ` (frozen after pretraining) predicts a wrist heatmap `V_t+1` from the predicted latent state. This heatmap is supervised against ground-truth wrist pseudo-labels extracted from the corresponding frames. The total loss is `L = L_latent + λ * L_wrist`, where `λ` is a scaling factor (set to 1).
- **Joint Training**: The world model is trained jointly on real egocentric videos (e.g., Nymeria dataset, which includes synchronized full-body motion capture) and synthetic egocentric data generated from exocentric videos. Both data types are converted into a shared observation-action format, allowing the world model to learn from a broader range of activities, scenes, and interactions.

#### 3.2. Exo-to-Ego Data Generation (EgoX-Body)

To convert exocentric video into action-aligned egocentric training data, the authors developed EgoX-Body, an extension of the EgoX framework. This module explicitly incorporates human kinematic structure to ground the generation process.

- **Exocentric-Side Kinematic Conditioning**: Human skeletons (SAM 3D Body representation) are overlaid onto the exocentric conditioning frames. This provides a structured, canonical representation of the actor's physical movement, which is less entangled with viewpoint or background variations.
- **Egocentric-Side Kinematic Conditioning**: An egocentric hand-skeleton overlay is introduced as a conditioning signal. This overlay is generated by projecting the 3D hand positions from the estimated body pose into the rendered egocentric view. This direct kinematic cue for hands helps to generate consistent hand motion and interaction-relevant details in the synthesized egocentric video, addressing a limitation of prior methods.
- **Inference Pipeline (Figure 4)**:
 1. **3D Human Motion and Scene Reconstruction**: From an input exocentric video, 3D full-body pose is estimated using SAM-Body4D, and a 4D scene reconstruction is obtained using ViPE. The pose is converted to SMPL-X for pipeline compatibility.
 2. **Egocentric Prior Rendering**: The SMPL-X skeleton is rendered into an egocentric view using a virtual pinhole camera anchored to the head (midpoint of eye joints, slightly pushed forward). This rendered skeleton forms the egocentric prior.
 3. **Hand Overlay Creation**: SMPL-X wrist and finger joints are used to draw a hand skeleton overlay, distinguishing left (red) and right (blue) hands.
 4. **Diffusion Model Input**: Two latent inputs are prepared for a video diffusion model:
 - The clean exocentric video latent concatenated with noise.
 - The exocentric video with the body skeleton overlay, concatenated with the egocentric hand prior.
 5. **Egocentric Video Generation**: These combined latent inputs are fed into a video diffusion model to generate the egocentric video sequence.
- **Practical Modifications**: For robustness and efficiency, EgoX-Body includes 250 additional training samples from the H2o dataset (focused on frontal human-camera interaction), removes the Geometry Guided Attention module from EgoX, reduces the generation resolution, and saves videos at 16 Hz. After generation, videos are further processed: center-cropped, resized to 224x224, and downsampled to 8 Hz for world model training, with corresponding 3D human motion trajectories.
- **Filtering Generated Videos**: An automatic quality filter is applied to remove unsuitable generated clips (e.g., black/white frames, blurred footage, motion artifacts) based on metrics like black/white pixel fraction, median Laplacian variance (blur), and median mean-absolute-difference between frames (motion).

#### 3.3. Planning with EgoExo-WM

The learned world model facilitates goal-directed planning using a Model-Predictive Control (MPC)-style procedure.

- **Action Proposal**: Given the current egocentric observation, UniEgoMotion acts as an action proposal model, sampling `N` physically plausible candidate 3D human motion sequences over a defined horizon `H`.
- **World Model Rollout**: For each candidate action sequence, the learned EgoExo-WM autoregressively rolls out future egocentric latent states.
- **Goal Matching**: The predicted final latent state of each rollout is compared against a target latent state `z_g` (encoded from a desired goal image) using an L2 distance metric `C^(i)`.
- **Action Selection**: The candidate action sequence that results in a predicted future state closest to the goal (i.e., minimum `C^(i)`) is selected as the optimal plan. This allows the agent to infer a sequence of body poses needed to achieve a visual goal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate EgoExo-WM's performance in both open-loop future prediction and downstream planning.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data**: The world model was trained on 200 hours of real egocentric Nymeria data (with paired 3D human motion) combined with 10 hours of converted exocentric video. The exocentric sources included ~5 hours from HowTo100M, ~1 hour from CrossTask, and ~4 hours from 100 Days of Hands.
- **Evaluation Data**: Performance was assessed on four distinct held-out datasets: Home Action Genome (HOMAGE), LEMMA (each with 150 clips), and Ego-Exo4D (125 clips each from Bike and Cooking subsets).
- **Baselines**:
 - **PEVA**: Diffusion-based, human-motion-conditioned egocentric video prediction model (L, XL, XXL variants).
 - **EgoControl***: Diffusion-based, human-motion-conditioned egocentric video prediction model (reimplemented).
 - **UniEgoMotion**: Used as an action generation baseline and as the action sampler for planning.
- **Ablations**:
 - **Ego-WM**: Our model's architecture trained only on Nymeria egocentric data.
 - **Naive EgoExo-WM**: Our model's architecture trained on Nymeria plus 10 hours of *raw* exocentric video (without EgoX-Body conversion).
 - **EgoExo-WM**: The full proposed method.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Open-loop rollout**: L2 distance in DINOv3-L feature space for visual prediction quality, and wrist PCK@20 (Percentage of Correct Keypoints within 20 pixels) for human-action consistency.
 - **Planning**: Whole-body and Wrist Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) in 3D human motion.

#### 4.2. Direct Evaluation of World Model Accuracy (Table 1)

- **Superior Prediction Performance**: EgoExo-WM consistently outperformed all PEVA variants and the adapted EgoControl baseline across all four evaluation datasets in open-loop 2-second rollouts. It achieved lower L2 embedding error, indicating more accurate visual future predictions.
- **Significant L2 Error Reduction**: On HOMAGE and LEMMA, EgoExo-WM reduced the average L2 error by more than 50% compared to the strongest PEVA baseline. This is attributed to the expanded distribution of human motions, object interactions, and environments introduced by the converted exocentric data, which are particularly relevant for household and object-centric activities.
- **Improved Action Consistency**: EgoExo-WM also demonstrated higher wrist PCK@20 values across all datasets, indicating better preservation of fine-grained hand motion and interaction cues, which is a direct benefit of the auxiliary wrist-consistency loss.
- **Impact of Exo-to-Ego Conversion**:
 - **Ego-WM** (egocentric-only) performed worse than EgoExo-WM, highlighting the benefit of incorporating exocentric data.
 - **Naive EgoExo-WM** (raw exocentric video) also performed worse than EgoExo-WM. This crucial ablation demonstrated that the improvements are not solely from adding exocentric data, but specifically from converting it into an egocentric, action-aligned format suitable for world model training.
- **Dataset Specificity**: While gains were clear, improvements on EgoExo4D-Bike and EgoExo4D-Cooking were smaller. This suggests that the diversity of activities in the *currently converted* exocentric data might not fully cover all aspects of these datasets (e.g., cooking is partly covered by Nymeria, biking less so in the selected exocentric clips).

#### 4.3. Impact for Model-Predictive Control Planning (Table 2)

- **Enhanced Goal-Directed Planning**: World-model-based ranking of action sequences generally improved planning performance. Both UniEgoMotion + Ego-WM and UniEgoMotion + EgoExo-WM achieved lower whole-body and wrist MPJPE compared to using UniEgoMotion alone. This indicates that predictive models can provide effective guidance for selecting human motion.
- **EgoExo-WM's Superior Planning**: EgoExo-WM consistently achieved the lowest MPJPE for both whole-body and wrist motion across all four evaluation datasets. This suggests that the predictions from EgoExo-WM are more informative for choosing goal-directed motions.
- **Broadened Motion Coverage**: The improved planning performance is attributed to the diverse converted exocentric videos, which provide a wider range of environments, body motions, and object-centric actions. This broader coverage enables EgoExo-WM to evaluate candidate action sequences more accurately, leading to better whole-body and fine-grained wrist motion planning.
- **Qualitative Evidence**: Qualitative examples (Figure 5 and Supplementary Video) illustrate that EgoExo-WM selects trajectories that align more closely with ground-truth behavior for tasks such as moving towards a sink or pouring cereal, compared to the Ego-WM baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoExo-WM framework presents a method for addressing critical bottlenecks in the development of egocentric world models, offering a pathway for scalable and effective learning.

- **Scalable Egocentric World Model Training**: The primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable strategy to leverage the vast and readily available resources of exocentric video for training egocentric world models. This overcomes the limitations of scarce and difficult-to-collect egocentric datasets, enabling the creation of more robust and generalizable predictive models.
- **Improved Predictive Accuracy and Action Consistency**: The integration of action-aligned synthetic egocentric data significantly enhances the world model's ability to predict future egocentric visual states and maintain consistency in human motion, particularly for hands and body. This improved predictive quality is fundamental for reliable agent behavior.
- **Enhanced Planning Capabilities**: By providing a more accurate and diverse understanding of action-observation dynamics, EgoExo-WM improves the effectiveness of goal-directed planning. This allows agents to reason about and select complex sequences of 3D human motions to achieve desired visual outcomes, moving beyond simple action execution to truly anticipatory behavior.
- **Robust Action Representation**: The use of 3D human motion as an explicit and structured action space offers an interpretable and physically grounded representation, which is valuable for understanding and controlling embodied agents.
- **Broadening Applications of Embodied AI**: The ability to learn from heterogeneous data sources opens avenues for practical applications in various domains:
 - **Robotics**: Robots could learn complex manipulation tasks and navigation strategies by observing human demonstrations from multiple perspectives, leading to more adaptable and intelligent robotic systems.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) Guidance**: AR systems could provide real-time coaching or assistance by predicting user actions and simulating the consequences of different human motions, offering intuitive guidance for tasks.
 - **Assistive Technologies**: Future assistive devices could benefit from models that predict human intent and interaction dynamics, providing proactive support in daily activities.

**Limitations**

The research acknowledges several limitations:
- **Temporal Scale**: The current framework operates with relatively short video clips (49 frames, 2-second planning horizon). Extending planning capabilities to longer horizons, where compounding prediction errors become more pronounced, remains a challenge for future work.
- **Complex Human-Object Interactions**: The exo-to-ego conversion process, particularly EgoX-Body, faces difficulties with highly complex human-object interactions. These include situations with significant occlusion, requirements for precise contact, and intricate small-object manipulation.
- **Degenerate Predictions**: Some generated egocentric predictions can exhibit visual artifacts, occasionally degenerating into mostly black or white frames, which would diminish their utility as training data. While filtering is applied, these issues indicate areas for further refinement in the synthesis process.

Despite these limitations, EgoExo-WM represents a step toward learning scalable, first-person predictive models from diverse, abundant third-person human video, fostering advancements in embodied intelligence.
