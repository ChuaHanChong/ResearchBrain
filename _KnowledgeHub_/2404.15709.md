# ViViDex: Learning Vision-based Dexterous Manipulation from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> ViViDex from Inria, ENS, CNRS, and PSL Research University enables multi-fingered robot hands to learn vision-based dexterous manipulation from only 1-3 human video demonstrations, achieving 99% success for seen objects and 50% for unseen objects in simulation without requiring privileged object information.

## Problem

- Noisy human hand and object trajectories extracted from videos are not directly usable for robust robot control.
- Existing dexterous manipulation methods often rely on privileged information like ground-truth object states or CAD models, limiting real-world applicability.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) is data-inefficient and requires complex, hand-crafted reward functions, while robot demonstrations are difficult and time-consuming to scale.

## Method

- A three-module framework: Reference Trajectory Extraction from human videos, Trajectory-guided State-based Policy Learning via RL, and Unified Vision-based Policy Learning.
- A novel two-stage (pre-grasp and manipulation) trajectory-guided reward function is introduced to refine noisy human trajectories into physically plausible robot actions during RL training.
- For the vision-based policy, 3D point clouds are transformed into task-relevant coordinate systems (target-centered and hand-centered) and combined with robot proprioceptive states to train a policy using either ==Behavior Cloning== or a 3D Diffusion Policy.

## Results

- Achieved **100%** success rate on the relocate task for seen objects in simulation, drastically outperforming state-of-the-art DexMV (**100%** SR3 vs. **79%** SR10) while using only 1-3 human videos per object, compared to ~100.
- The unified visual policy, utilizing 3D Diffusion Policy and coordinate transformations, achieved **99%** average SR3 for seen objects and **50%** average SR3 for unseen objects in simulation.
- Demonstrated successful real-world deployment on an Allegro hand, achieving an **80%** success rate for seen objects and **68%** for unseen objects, validating the framework's robustness in physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Carefully designed, trajectory-guided reward functions that constrain both hand and object motions are crucial for refining noisy human video demonstrations into robust, physically plausible robot behaviors.
> - Transforming 3D point clouds into hand-centered and target-centered coordinate systems significantly improves the visual policy's understanding of intricate hand-object interactions and task goals.
> - Using a 3D Diffusion Policy for visuomotor learning, combined with refined trajectories and enhanced visual representations, leads to superior generalization and robustness compared to standard Behavior Cloning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, especially dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands, is a long-standing grand challenge in robotics. Humans manipulate objects effortlessly using visual perception, a feat that remains difficult for robots due to the complexity of translating visual signals into high-dimensional control commands. ViViDex addresses this challenge by focusing on learning-based approaches, specifically leveraging **imitation learning from human video demonstrations**, a growing area of research aimed at overcoming the limitations of traditional methods.

Historically, dexterous manipulation research has involved:
- **Trajectory Optimization:** Requires accurate dynamic models, which are often hard to obtain in practice.
- **Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While showing significant progress (e.g., in-hand rotation, solving Rubik's cube), RL suffers from high data inefficiency, demands intricate reward engineering, and can lead to "unnatural" behaviors with high rewards.
- **Imitation Learning from Robot Demonstrations:** Using teleoperation or expert policies improves efficiency but requires substantial human effort to collect diverse, high-quality robot demonstrations, making it difficult to scale.

The recent abundance of human videos depicting hand-object interactions (e.g., DexYCB dataset) has sparked a trend to use these videos for teaching robots. Prior work in this area typically falls into a few categories:
- **Direct Trajectory Extraction and RL Acceleration:** Methods like DexMV extract noisy robot and object poses from human videos and use them to accelerate RL training (e.g., via Demonstration Augmented Policy Gradient, DAPG). A major limitation here is the *noise* in extracted trajectories and the reliance on *privileged object information* (ground-truth object CAD models or states) during policy learning, hindering real-world deployment. These methods often require *hundreds of human videos* for a single object and still need task-specific reward engineering.
- **Learning Reward Functions from Videos:** Approaches like DVD aim to learn reward functions directly from human videos, bypassing manual reward design.
- **Learning Generic Visual Representations:** Works like R3M pre-train visual representations from large-scale human video data, then apply these to control policies.

ViViDex positions itself as a significant advancement within the "direct trajectory extraction and RL acceleration" paradigm, but critically addresses its core weaknesses. It aims to develop a **unified vision-based policy** that can handle *multi-fingered robot hands* and *diverse objects in varying poses*, without relying on privileged object information. Its key differentiators are:
1. **Robust Trajectory Refinement:** It proposes a novel RL-based framework to refine noisy human video trajectories into physically plausible robot control trajectories.
2. **Vision-Only Policy:** The final policy operates solely on visual (3D point clouds) and robot proprioceptive states, eliminating the need for ground-truth object states.
3. **Efficiency and Generalization:** It achieves state-of-the-art performance with *significantly fewer human videos* and demonstrates strong generalization to *unseen objects*, both in simulation and on a real robot.

By tackling the noise in human video data, removing reliance on privileged information, and drastically reducing data requirements, ViViDex pushes the frontier towards more scalable, practical, and generalizable learning of dexterous manipulation skills from readily available human demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **learn a unified vision-based policy for multi-fingered robot hands to manipulate a variety of objects in diverse poses**. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing approaches to dexterous manipulation:

1. **Overcoming Noise in Human Video Trajectories:** Prior work utilizing human videos for policy learning, while promising, has been hampered by the inherent noise and inaccuracies in the estimated human hand and object trajectories. These noisy trajectories are often not directly usable for robot control and limit the performance gains. ViViDex aims to develop a method to **refine these noisy human video trajectories into physically plausible ones** suitable for robotic execution.

2. **Eliminating Reliance on Privileged Object Information:** Many existing methods for dexterous manipulation, even those using human videos, still require privileged information such as ground-truth object CAD models or precise object states (position, orientation). Obtaining such information reliably from visual sensor data in real-world scenarios is non-trivial and often impractical, severely limiting the applicability of these policies. A core motivation for ViViDex is to learn a **unified visual policy that operates entirely without any privileged object information**, relying solely on raw visual inputs (3D point clouds) and robot proprioceptive states.

3. **Addressing Data Inefficiency and Reward Engineering in RL:** Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches are known for their high data requirements and the difficulty of designing effective, hand-crafted reward functions for complex dexterous manipulation tasks. Furthermore, RL can sometimes converge to unnatural or suboptimal behaviors. ViViDex seeks to leverage the rich, natural interaction examples from human videos to **guide RL training more efficiently and effectively**, reducing the need for extensive reward engineering and promoting human-like manipulation strategies.

4. **Improving Scalability and Generalization:** Collecting diverse robot demonstrations through teleoperation is labor-intensive and challenging to scale for a wide range of objects and tasks. Human videos, being abundant, offer a scalable alternative. However, previous methods struggled with generalization, often requiring many videos per object. ViViDex aims to achieve **significant generalization with fewer human video demonstrations**, enabling the policy to handle novel object poses and even *unseen objects* effectively.

To achieve these motivations, the specific objectives of ViViDex are:
- To propose a novel framework that integrates trajectory extraction, RL-based refinement, and vision-based policy distillation.
- To design a novel trajectory-guided reward function for RL that ensures both visual naturalness and physical plausibility in the refined trajectories.
- To introduce effective data augmentation strategies during RL to enhance policy generalization.
- To develop an improved visual representation for point clouds by transforming them into task-relevant coordinate systems (e.g., hand-centered, target-centered).
- To evaluate and demonstrate the effectiveness of using both behavior cloning and 3D diffusion policy for learning the vision-based policy.
- To validate the proposed approach through extensive experiments in simulation and on a real robot, demonstrating superior performance and generalization compared to state-of-the-art methods, especially regarding data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViViDex proposes a three-module framework (illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper) to learn vision-based dexterous manipulation from human videos: (1) Reference Trajectory Extraction, (2) Trajectory-guided State-based Policy Learning, and (3) Unified Vision-based Policy Learning.

#### 3.1. A. Reference Trajectory Extraction

The first step involves obtaining initial, albeit noisy, human hand and object trajectories from video demonstrations. The authors use existing pose estimation techniques (e.g., MANO for hand shapes and poses) to get 3D joint locations for the human hand.
- **Motion Retargeting:** To translate human movements to a robot hand, an optimization problem is formulated. This minimizes the squared difference between the 3D locations of the robot's finger tips (calculated via forward kinematics from robot joint angles `q_r`) and the corresponding human hand positions (`ψ_h`). A regularization term is included to prevent sudden changes in robot pose, ensuring smooth movements. This process generates "reference trajectories" that are visually plausible from the human video but not yet physically plausible for robot execution due to kinematic and dynamic differences. The NLopt solver is used for this optimization.

#### 3.2. B. Trajectory-guided State-based Policy Learning

This module refines the reference trajectories to be physically plausible and executable by a robot. This is achieved by training a state-based policy using Reinforcement Learning (RL). The policy network uses an actor-critic MLP architecture, taking both robot proprioceptive states (joint angles, velocities) and ground-truth object states (position, orientation) as input, and outputting robot control commands.

- **Novel Trajectory-guided Reward Functions:** A key innovation is a two-stage reward function that leverages the extracted human trajectories:
 - **Pre-grasp Stage:** The robot needs to approach the object without contact. The reward `R_p` is designed to encourage the robot's finger tips to follow the human finger tip positions in the reference trajectory, using an exponential decay function. This ensures a natural and stable initial approach.
 - **Manipulation Stage:** After a successful pre-grasp, the robot manipulates the object to a desired target configuration. The reward `R_m` combines several terms:
 - `R_h_m`: Constrains robot hand motions to be similar to the human reference.
 - `R_o_m`: Constrains object motions (position and orientation) to be close to the reference trajectory.
 - `1_cont`: Rewards the number of finger tips in contact with the object, encouraging stable grasping.
 - `1_lift`: Assigns bonus points when the object is lifted off the table, crucial for manipulation tasks.
 This comprehensive reward system reduces the need for task-specific reward engineering and guides the RL agent towards human-like, physically plausible interactions.

- **Reference Trajectory Augmentation:** To improve generalization beyond the exact conditions of the human video, the reference trajectories are augmented during RL training. This involves randomly varying initial object positions and rotations (around the z-axis). Furthermore, target object positions and corresponding hand motions are interpolated to generate diverse goal configurations. This makes the state-based policy robust to different initial and target conditions.

#### 3.3. C. The Vision-based Policy

The final and most critical module trains a unified vision-based policy that removes the reliance on privileged object state information, accepting only robot proprioceptive states and 3D scene point clouds as input.

- **Data Generation for Visual Policy:** The refined, physically plausible trajectories generated by the optimized state-based policies (from module B) are rolled out. During these rollouts, 3D point clouds are rendered from a simulated depth camera for each timestep, alongside the robot states and corresponding control actions. This creates a dataset of high-quality, vision-action pairs.

- **Coordinate Transformation for Visual Representation:** To enhance the visual policy's understanding of the scene, particularly hand-object interactions and target locations, the input 3D point clouds (`PC_w`, represented in world coordinates) undergo coordinate transformations:
 - **Target-centered:** The point cloud is transformed into a coordinate system centered at the desired target position. This makes the visual features explicitly "aware" of the task goal.
 - **Hand-centered (Palm and Finger Tips):** Point clouds are also transformed into coordinate systems centered at the robot's palm and individual finger tips. This is crucial for capturing dense, fine-grained features related to hand-object contact and manipulation.
 The transformed point clouds are concatenated (`PC ∈ R^N × 3(j+3)`, where `j` is the number of finger tips) and then fed into a PointNet for feature extraction.

- **Visual Policy Training:** The extracted visual features, combined with robot proprioceptive states, are used to train the visual policy using two alternative approaches:
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A straightforward approach where the model directly learns to map the input (visual features + robot states) to the expert actions (robot control commands) using an ℓ2 loss.
 - **3D Diffusion Policy:** A more advanced technique where the extracted 3D features from PointNet serve as a global condition for a denoising model. This model learns to recover actions from Gaussian noise, often leading to more robust and generalized policies compared to standard BC.

By combining trajectory refinement with intelligent visual representations and advanced policy learning techniques, ViViDex aims to bridge the gap between human demonstrations and real-world robot dexterity, making policies more robust and data-efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ViViDex demonstrates significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods across multiple dexterous manipulation tasks, both in simulation and on a real robot. The key findings are structured around the evaluation of state-based and visual policies, as well as real-world deployment.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** Relocate (move object to target), Pour (pour particles from mug), Place Inside (place banana into mug).
- **Objects:** Evaluated on 5 "seen" YCB objects (mustard bottle, tomato soup can, sugar box, large clamp, mug) and 10 "unseen" YCB objects for generalization tests.
- **Robot Hands:** Adroit (for SOTA comparison, in MuJoCo) and Allegro (for main experiments and real robot, in SAPIEN).
- **Data Efficiency:** Crucially, ViViDex uses significantly fewer human videos (1-3 per object/task) compared to prior work (e.g., DexMV uses ~100 videos).

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation of the State-based Policy (Relocate Task)

1. **Ablation Studies on Reward Functions (Table I, Figure 3):**
 - The proposed trajectory-guided reward function is critical. Policies lacking the hand reward in the pre-grasp stage (R1) result in unstable grasps, failing to approach objects plausibly. Policies without hand motion constraints during manipulation (R2) lead to unnatural robot actions.
 - **ViViDex's full reward (R3), which combines hand rewards in both pre-grasp and manipulation stages, significantly outperforms R1 and R2 across all metrics (object/hand error, success rates), producing robust and realistic grasps.** This validates the novel reward design.

2. **State-of-the-Art Comparison (Table II):**
 - **ViViDex (S6, S7) achieves an outstanding 100% success rate (both SR10 and the more rigorous SR3) on the relocate task for all five seen objects using only *one human video per object*.**
 - This is a drastic improvement over DexMV (S1-S5). For instance, DexMV's best variant (S4, using 97 videos) achieved an average SR10 of 79%, struggling with objects like the sugar box. Even after re-training DexMV with 20 videos (S5) for fair comparison, its performance was significantly lower (average SR10 of 44%, SR3 of 24%).
 - ViViDex's hand reward in the pre-grasp stage prevents issues like knocking over thin objects (e.g., sugar box), a common problem for previous methods.
 - The policy's robustness is further shown by a minimal performance drop even without rotation augmentation during training (S8), demonstrating its inherent ability to generalize to varied object rotations.

#### 4.2. B. Evaluation of the Visual Policy (Relocate Task)

1. **Learning from a Single Video per Object (Table III):**
 - Visual policies trained on data rolled out from ViViDex's state-based policies perform almost as well as the state-based policies themselves, indicating effective knowledge distillation.
 - **Point cloud density matters:** Increasing point count from 512 (V1) to 2048 (V2) improves average SR3 from 86% to 96%.
 - **3D Diffusion Policy (V3) outperforms Behavior Cloning (BC) in robustness and achieves higher success rates (99% avg SR3)**, showing its advantage for visuomotor policy learning.

2. **Learning a Unified Multi-object Visual Policy (Table IV):**
 - **Data Quantity:** Increasing the number of human videos per object (from 1 to 3) for training a unified policy significantly improves performance for seen objects (V4-V6, avg SR3 rises from 34% to 81%) and unseen objects (avg from 31% to 38%).
 - **Coordinate Transformation is Crucial:**
 - Transforming point clouds to a **target-centered coordinate system (V7)** dramatically boosts performance for seen objects (from 81% to 95% avg SR3), making the model more target-aware.
 - Further transforming point clouds to **hand-centered coordinate systems (V8)**, capturing fine-grained hand-object interaction features, leads to even better performance (97% avg SR3 for seen, 41% for unseen).
 - **3D Diffusion Policy Generalization (V9):** Training with the 3D diffusion policy consistently yields the best results. It achieves an impressive **99% avg SR3 for seen objects** and significantly improves generalization to **unseen objects (50% avg SR3)** compared to BC (41%). This highlights the diffusion policy's superior robustness and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.3. C. Evaluation on Pour and Place Inside Tasks (Table V)

- ViViDex's state-based policy (L5) again drastically outperforms DexMV (L1-L4) on these more complex tasks.
- Using only *one human video per task*, ViViDex achieves 97% success rate for Pour and 68% for Place Inside, compared to DexMV's best results of 27% and 31% respectively (which used over 90 videos).
- The high-quality trajectories generated by the state-based policy enable the visual policy (L6) to achieve identical high performance, further reinforcing the effectiveness of the entire framework.

#### 4.4. D. Real-World Experiments (Table VI, Figure 4)

- ViViDex is successfully deployed on a real UR5 arm with an Allegro hand and a RealSense D435 RGB-D camera.
- **Real-world policies demonstrate good performance for both seen and unseen objects.** A unified visual policy trained with the diffusion method (R3) achieves 80% success rate for seen objects and 68% for unseen objects.
- This real-world validation is critical, showing that the learned policies are robust enough to handle the noise and complexities of physical environments.

In summary, ViViDex sets a new benchmark for learning dexterous manipulation from human videos. Its key finding is the ability to achieve unprecedented levels of performance and generalization with **significantly less data (1-3 human videos vs. ~100)**, by effectively refining noisy human demonstrations and employing sophisticated visual representations and policy learning techniques.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViViDex represents a substantial leap forward in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation and learning from human demonstrations, with significant implications and potential impact:

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap for Human Video Learning:** The most profound impact of ViViDex is its ability to effectively bridge the gap between noisy, visually-derived human demonstrations and robust, physically plausible robot control. By introducing a novel trajectory-guided RL reward and intelligent visual representations, ViViDex successfully extracts the essence of human dexterity while accounting for the inherent inaccuracies in motion estimation from videos.

2. **Elimination of Privileged Information:** A critical advancement is the final visual policy's independence from privileged object information (e.g., ground-truth object poses or CAD models). This is a game-changer for real-world deployment, where such information is often unavailable or difficult to obtain reliably. By relying solely on 3D point clouds and robot proprioceptive states, ViViDex makes learned policies far more practical and deployable in unstructured, dynamic environments.

3. **Unprecedented Data Efficiency:** ViViDex shatters previous benchmarks for data efficiency. Achieving state-of-the-art performance on complex manipulation tasks with only **1 to 3 human videos per object/task**, compared to the dozens or hundreds previously required, dramatically reduces the data collection bottleneck. This makes the approach highly scalable and accessible, opening the door for broader adoption in research and industry.

4. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization to diverse initial object poses, different object instances, and even *unseen objects*, both in simulation and on a real robot. The trajectory augmentation for RL and the coordinate transformation for point cloud representations are key contributors to this. The superior performance of the 3D diffusion policy for the visual component further boosts this generalization, showing its potential for learning more robust and adaptable visuomotor skills.

5. **Simplified Reward Engineering:** The trajectory-guided reward function significantly simplifies the traditionally arduous process of reward engineering in RL. By distilling task objectives and natural interaction patterns directly from human demonstrations, the method allows for more intuitive and effective policy training across various manipulation tasks.

6. **Empowering Multi-fingered Robot Hands:** The work directly addresses the challenges of controlling high-dimensional, multi-fingered robot hands, which are crucial for fine motor skills but notoriously difficult to program or train. ViViDex offers a powerful paradigm for teaching these complex manipulators new skills.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Skill Acquisition:** Researchers and engineers can rapidly teach new dexterous manipulation skills to robots by simply providing a few human video demonstrations, rather than extensive teleoperation or complex hand-coded rules.
- **Wider Adoption of Dexterous Robots:** Lowering the entry barrier for programming dexterous skills could accelerate the adoption of advanced robot hands in fields like manufacturing, logistics, service robotics, and even surgical assistance.
- **Leveraging Internet-Scale Data:** The ability to learn from "in-the-wild" human videos, which are abundant on platforms like YouTube, could unlock a massive, untapped resource for robot learning. This is explicitly mentioned as a future work direction, indicating the potential for ever-more general and versatile robotic agents.
- **Advancement in Learning-from-Demonstration:** ViViDex provides a strong blueprint for designing robust and data-efficient learning-from-demonstration systems, particularly for tasks involving fine-grained motor control and interaction.

In conclusion, ViViDex makes significant contributions by providing a practical, data-efficient, and generalizable framework for learning dexterous manipulation from human videos, critically moving towards real-world applicability by eliminating privileged information and showcasing impressive performance on physical robots.
