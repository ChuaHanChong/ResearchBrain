# ASID: Active Exploration for System Identification in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ASID framework, developed at the University of Washington, introduces an active exploration method to autonomously refine physics simulator parameters using minimal real-world data. This enables zero-shot transfer of policies learned in the updated simulator to real-world robotic manipulation tasks, achieving success rates of 6 out of 9 for rod balancing and 7 out of 10 for shuffleboard after collecting only a single real-world exploration episode.

## Problem

- The significant sim-to-real gap causes policies learned in simulation to fail on real robots due to discrepancies in physics and dynamics.
- Learning robust robotic manipulation policies in the real world is sample-inefficient and costly, making model-free reinforcement learning impractical.
- Manually tuning simulation parameters to accurately reflect real-world physical properties and kinematics requires extensive human effort and expertise.

## Method

- A three-stage "sim-to-real-to-sim-to-real" pipeline begins by training an active exploration policy in simulation, guided by Fisher information maximization and domain randomization, to efficiently collect informative real-world data.
- The exploration policy is deployed for a single episode in the real world to gather a trajectory that is then used with black-box optimization (e.g., CEM) to refine the simulator's physical and kinematic parameters.
- A task-specific policy is subsequently trained entirely within the refined, high-fidelity simulator and deployed to the real robot in a zero-shot manner, eliminating further real-world fine-tuning.

## Results

- The Fisher information-guided exploration policy demonstrated superior coverage and interaction with unknown parameters in simulation, like achieving uniform coverage of friction zones and 80% interaction with articulated objects, outperforming naive and disagreement-based baselines.
- In simulated downstream tasks, ASID achieved near-perfect rod balancing (0.00° to 0.72° tilt) and a 28% sphere striking success rate, significantly outperforming domain randomization and other baselines.
- On real-world robotic tasks with a Franka Emika Panda, ASID enabled zero-shot transfer with minimal data (a single exploration episode), achieving 6 out of 9 successes for rod balancing and 7 out of 10 for shuffleboard, while domain randomization baselines largely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning an effective exploration strategy, which is less sensitive to initial parameter inaccuracies, can be decoupled from learning a precise task-solving policy.
> - Utilizing the Fisher information matrix effectively quantifies the informativeness of trajectories for system identification, enabling targeted and efficient data collection.
> - Optimization-based system identification methods are more robust and stable than learned estimation modules, particularly when faced with limited real-world data.
