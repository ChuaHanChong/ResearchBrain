# Stable Transformer-Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control: A Contraction Analysis Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Cambridge, University College London, and LAAS-CNRS developed a Transformer-Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, proving Transformer networks can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and ensuring closed-loop contraction stability for nonlinear systems. This framework achieved robust convergence and flight stability in drone control under severe disturbances, outperforming unregularized baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional learning control methods for nonlinear systems lack rigorous stability guarantees, limiting their use in safety-critical applications.
- Existing Actor-Critic MPC implementations often rely on Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), which struggle to capture temporal correlations and process historical data effectively in dynamic environments.
- A gap exists in establishing fundamental robustness notions, such as incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and contraction theory, for Transformer networks when integrated into feedback control systems.

## Method

- A novel ==Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC== architecture is proposed where a Transformer network predicts dynamic cost function coefficients for the MPC based on historical state and reference trajectories.
- The Transformer's internal stability is rigorously analyzed, providing mathematical proof that the architecture can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) by bounding its constituent layers and multi-head attention mechanism.
- Riemannian contraction theory is employed to guarantee closed-loop stability for the interconnected physical plant and predictive Transformer network, enforcing a strict small-gain condition as a structural regularization loss during training.

## Results

- The stable Transformer-MPC demonstrated robust convergence to target locations and maintained flight stability in a 3D drone model under severe observation noise and process disturbances.
- An unregularized vanilla Actor-Critic MPC baseline consistently failed to reject the injected noise, leading to significant positional divergence and catastrophic flight instability.
- The stable network achieved a **0%** collision rate in obstacle avoidance scenarios under maximal disturbance, compared to a **30%** collision rate for the vanilla approach, validating its improved safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer architectures can be mathematically constrained to satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS), offering a theoretical foundation for their deployment in safety-critical control systems.
> - A sequence of contraction metrics can be derived for the MPC, enabling a global uniform contraction rate for the nominal unperturbed closed-loop system.
> - The coupled stability of the physical plant and the Transformer network can be ensured by satisfying a strict small-gain condition, which consolidates the metric's condition number as a localized boundary penalty during training.
