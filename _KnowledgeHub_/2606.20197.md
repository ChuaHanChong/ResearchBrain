# Stable Transformer-Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control: A Contraction Analysis Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Cambridge, University College London, and LAAS-CNRS developed a Transformer-Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, proving Transformer networks can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and ensuring closed-loop contraction stability for nonlinear systems. This framework achieved robust convergence and flight stability in drone control under severe disturbances, outperforming unregularized baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional learning control methods for nonlinear systems lack rigorous stability guarantees, limiting their use in safety-critical applications.
- Existing Actor-Critic MPC implementations often rely on Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), which struggle to capture temporal correlations and process historical data effectively in dynamic environments.
- A gap exists in establishing fundamental robustness notions, such as incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and contraction theory, for Transformer networks when integrated into feedback control systems.

## Method

- A novel ==Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC== architecture is proposed where a Transformer network predicts dynamic cost function coefficients for the MPC based on historical state and reference trajectories.
- The Transformer's internal stability is rigorously analyzed, providing mathematical proof that the architecture can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) by bounding its constituent layers and multi-head attention mechanism.
- Riemannian contraction theory is employed to guarantee closed-loop stability for the interconnected physical plant and predictive Transformer network, enforcing a strict small-gain condition as a structural regularization loss during training.

## Results

- The stable Transformer-MPC demonstrated robust convergence to target locations and maintained flight stability in a 3D drone model under severe observation noise and process disturbances.
- An unregularized vanilla Actor-Critic MPC baseline consistently failed to reject the injected noise, leading to significant positional divergence and catastrophic flight instability.
- The stable network achieved a **0%** collision rate in obstacle avoidance scenarios under maximal disturbance, compared to a **30%** collision rate for the vanilla approach, validating its improved safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer architectures can be mathematically constrained to satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS), offering a theoretical foundation for their deployment in safety-critical control systems.
> - A sequence of contraction metrics can be derived for the MPC, enabling a global uniform contraction rate for the nominal unperturbed closed-loop system.
> - The coupled stability of the physical plant and the Transformer network can be ensured by satisfying a strict small-gain condition, which consolidates the metric's condition number as a localized boundary penalty during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses fundamental challenges in synthesizing control strategies for nonlinear systems, particularly concerning the stability guarantees of learning-based controllers. Traditional pure learning methodologies, while effective in approximating dynamic programming, typically lack rigorous stability proofs. This deficiency has led to the development of hybrid optimization-learning frameworks, which integrate learning components into Model Predictive Control (MPC) to enhance computational tractability while striving to maintain certifiable properties. Establishing theoretical guarantees for these hybrid systems is an active area of research.

Previous efforts in this domain have explored concepts such as learned neural dynamics and contraction theory. Contraction theory, in particular, offers a framework for guaranteeing robust closed-loop stability without relying on restrictive terminal conditions, a common requirement in traditional MPC.

Within the context of MPC, non-convex optimization often necessitates constraint relaxation. Actor-Critic MPC generalizes the idea of learning reference trajectories by using a neural network to dynamically adjust the linear and quadratic coefficients of the MPC cost function. This approach, enabled by differentiable MPC formulations, allows the network to shape the local optimization landscape. However, the existing literature on Actor-Critic MPC is noted for a scarcity of formal guarantees, with current implementations typically relying on simpler Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).

MLPs are inherently limited in their ability to capture temporal consistencies, which are crucial in environments characterized by time-correlated disturbances. This limitation motivates the exploration of sequence models like Transformers, which can predict context-aware MPC weights from state trajectories. The integration of Transformers into optimization frameworks is also relevant for emerging applications, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics. While some ODE-based analyses have demonstrated asymptotic stability for Transformers, these often rely on restrictive unit-norm projections. A recognized gap remains in establishing fundamental robustness notions, such as incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and contraction, for Transformer-based feedback systems. This paper aims to bridge this gap by providing formal stability guarantees for a Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized challenges in guaranteeing closed-loop stability for sequence-based learning models integrated into Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MPC) pipelines. While Actor-Critic MPC offers an effective approach to complex, non-convex control problems, the lack of formal robustness guarantees for its learning components, particularly when dealing with temporal dependencies, is a significant limitation. Existing implementations typically use Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs), which are not well-suited for capturing time-correlated dynamics or historical context.

The paper identifies a critical gap in the theoretical understanding of Transformer networks within control systems, specifically concerning their fundamental robustness properties like incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) and contraction. Prior analyses of Transformers have often relied on restrictive assumptions, leaving the question of their rigorous stability in feedback systems open.

To address these challenges and gaps, the paper sets forth two intertwined key objectives:

1. **To formally prove that Transformer architectures can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS).** This involves developing mathematical constraints for Transformer networks to ensure their state deviations remain bounded with respect to initial state differences and input variations, specifically under the induced infinity norm. This objective aims to provide a theoretical foundation for using Transformers in safety-critical control applications.

2. **To propose a novel hybrid Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC architecture with formal robustness guarantees.** This involves integrating the δISS properties of the Transformer network with Riemannian contraction theory to analyze the interconnected dynamics between the physical plant and the predictive neural network. The ultimate goal is to enforce these theoretical bounds as a training regularizer, leading to a control policy that is certifiably robust while maintaining high performance. This objective seeks to move beyond empirical success by providing a principled method for designing stable learning-based controllers.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute to the development of robust and certifiable learning-based control systems, particularly for nonlinear dynamics where traditional methods or less-constrained learning approaches might fail.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper integrates a Transformer network into an Actor-Critic Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, with a focus on providing formal stability guarantees through contraction analysis.

**System Dynamics and MPC Formulation:**
The paper considers a discrete-time nonlinear system represented by $x_{k+1} = \Phi(x_k, u_k) + d_k(x_k)$, where $x_k$ is the state, $u_k$ is the control input, and $d_k(x_k)$ is an additive disturbance, assumed to be uniformly bounded. The state and action spaces are also assumed to be uniformly bounded (Assumption 1).

The MPC problem is formulated over a prediction horizon $T$ to minimize a cost function $J_Y$. This cost function includes quadratic state-action penalties ($Q_{i|k}$) and linear penalties ($P_{i|k}$), along with softplus-relaxed generalized boundary constraints $h(\omega_{i|k})$. The optimization problem is:
$min_{\omega} J_Y = \sum_{i=0}^{T} [ \omega_{i|k}^{\top} Q_{i|k}(x_k, x_d_k)\omega_{i|k} + P_{i|k}(x_k, x_d_k)\omega_{i|k} + \gamma So(h(\omega_{i|k}))_i ]$
subject to $x_{i+1|k} = \Phi(x_{i|k}, u_{i|k})$, $h(\omega_{i|k}) \le 0$, $u_{i|k} \in U$, $x_{i|k} \in X$.
The cost matrices $Q_{i|k}$ and $P_{i|k}$ are dynamically predicted by a neural network. To ensure positive semi-definiteness, $Q$ is structurally constrained using the softplus operator.

**Actor-Critic Architecture with Transformer:**
Unlike standard Actor-Critic MPC, which uses an MLP to predict $Q$ and $P$ from the current state, this approach employs a history-aware neural architecture. This network predicts the ordered set $Y = \{(Q_{i|k}, P_{i|k})\}_{i=0}^T$ from $L$-step past states ($x_{k-L:k}$) and references ($x_{d_{k-L:k}}$). An initial MLP layer maps these physical histories into latent sequence variables, which serve as inputs to a core Transformer block. The Transformer's output is then processed by a final MLP layer to yield $Y$.

**Differentiable MPC and KKT Analysis:**
Training of the network requires backpropagation of gradients through the MPC solution. Differentiable MPC frameworks are utilized for this purpose. Smooth gradients are ensured by relaxing inequality constraints with a smooth Softplus penalty. The Lagrangian of the MPC problem is formulated, and the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT) is applied to the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) residual to define the local sensitivity of the optimal control solution with respect to the cost matrices $Q$ and $P$. Assumption 2 (Strong Convexity and Regularity) ensures the KKT matrix remains non-singular.

**δISS Analysis of the Transformer Network:**
A crucial part of the methodology is proving the δISS property for the Transformer block. The Transformer is interpreted as a discrete dynamical system:
$z_{k+1} = \alpha_2 g(\alpha_1 z_k + A(z_k, p_k)) + f(g(\alpha_1 z_k + A(z_k, p_k)))$
where $z_k$ is the sequence, $p_k$ is the input sequence, $g$ is layer normalization, $f$ is an MLP, and $A(\cdot, \cdot)$ is the multi-head attention mechanism.
Under Assumption 3 (uniform boundedness of $p$), Theorem 1 establishes a sufficient condition for the system to be δISS, specifically $A_{\delta} < 1$, where $A_{\delta}$ is a derived Lipschitz bound. The proof involves bounding the infinity norm of the state deviation $\delta z_{k+1}$ using Lipschitz constants for layer normalization and MLP functions, and carefully bounding the multi-head attention deviation. This analysis provides an incremental state bound, confirming δISS according to Definition 1.

**Closed-Loop Stability via Contraction Analysis:**
To guarantee closed-loop stability for the interconnected system, Riemannian contraction theory is employed.
1. **Contraction Metric Sequence:** The methodology constructs a sequence of contraction matrices $M$ along the predicted trajectory. This sequence is anchored by an infinite-horizon terminal contraction metric $M_{T|k}$, derived from the steady-state Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation (DARE) using the Transformer's terminal predicted weights. Time-varying contraction matrices $M_{i|k}$ are then computed backward along the trajectory by solving the Dynamic Difference Riccati Equation (DRE). The root matrix $M_{0|k}$ serves as the Riemannian metric for the current physical step.
2. **Bounded Metric Shift:** Lemma 2 establishes that the temporal shift of the contraction metric (from $M_{1|k}$ to $M_{0|k+1}$) is strictly upper-bounded by the physical state differential, considering the Transformer's δISS property and the DRE's Lipschitz continuity.
3. **Global Uniform Contraction Rate:** Lemma 3 proves that the nominal unperturbed closed-loop MPC system is globally exponentially contractive in the $M$-weighted Riemannian manifold, with a strict, uniform contraction rate $\rho_{mpc} < 1$. This is derived from the algebraic identity of the DRE and the strong convexity assumption.
4. **Interconnected Stability:** The paper then evaluates the coupled sensitivity mapping between the neural parameter space and the physical Riemannian manifold. A mixed induced operator norm $S_Y$ is defined to quantify this sensitivity. By leveraging Assumption 2, $S_Y$ is bounded.
 Finally, the true Riemannian trajectory transition is bounded, incorporating the mixed-norm sensitivity. This leads to a set of coupled differential transition bounds for the physical state distance ($d_M$) and the Transformer's internal state deviation ($\|\delta z_k\|_{\infty}$). Theorem 2 provides the core result for interconnected closed-loop contraction: the system is contractive if $(1 - \rho_{mpc})(1 - A_{\delta}) - \gamma_z \gamma_x > 0$, where $A_{\delta}$ is the Transformer's δISS bound and $\rho_{mpc}$ is the MPC contraction rate. This represents a small-gain condition for the fully coupled loop, ensuring stability.

**Training Regularization:**
The derived theoretical bounds, specifically the small-gain condition $(1 - \rho_{mpc})(1 - A_{\delta}) - \gamma_z \gamma_x > 0$, are integrated as a structural regularization loss during the training of the Transformer-Actor-Critic network. This enforces the certifiable robustness property.

**Validation Setup:**
The methodology is validated on a nonlinear 3D drone model. The drone state includes position, velocity, orientation quaternion, and angular velocity. The control input consists of total thrust and body torque. A second-order discretization is applied for dynamics. The differentiable MPC is used with a horizon $T=10$. The system is subjected to uniform observation noise and process disturbances to test robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC architecture with contraction analysis-based regularization was conducted on a nonlinear 3D drone model executing target-reaching and obstacle-avoidance maneuvers. The key findings are as follows:

1. **Robustness under Disturbances:** The stable network, trained with the proposed regularization, demonstrated robust convergence to the target location in both unconstrained and obstacle-cluttered environments. This was observed despite the injection of significant uniform observation noise ($n_k \sim U(-0.4, 0.4)^{12}$) and uniform process disturbances ($d_k \sim U(-0.5, 0.5)^{12}$). In contrast, the unregularized "vanilla" Actor-Critic MPC network, used as a baseline, consistently failed to reject the noise, leading to positional divergence and flight instability.

2. **Empirical Validation of δISS:** The stable network exhibited tightly bundled trajectory rollouts when initialized from identical starting conditions (Figure 2d). This characteristic, where trajectories starting close together remain close, serves as empirical evidence supporting the theoretical δISS property proven for the Transformer network in Section III. It demonstrates that the maximum state deviation remained strictly bounded and proportional to the exogenous disturbances, as predicted by the theory.

3. **Safety in Obstacle Avoidance:** In the obstacle avoidance scenario, the theoretical robustness translated into improved safety performance. Under maximal disturbance conditions, the stable network achieved a 0% collision rate, successfully navigating the environment without incident. The unregularized vanilla approach, however, resulted in a 30% collision rate, indicating its susceptibility to disturbances when navigating constrained spaces.

4. **Trade-off in Transient Agility:** Analysis of the attitude tracking error revealed a nuanced trade-off. While the stable network maintained a bounded orientation error (standard deviation approximately 0.12 rad), the unregularized network generally achieved tighter nominal attitude tracking before its eventual divergence in the obstacle scenario. This suggests that the strict enforcement of rigid global contraction bounds during training might marginally restrict the network's flexibility to execute aggressive transient rotational maneuvers.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the theoretical framework, specifically the δISS proof for Transformers and the coupled small-gain condition, when enforced as a training regularizer, yields a control policy that is certifiably robust against significant disturbances and enhances safety in complex environments compared to an unregularized baseline. The observed trade-off in transient agility also highlights a direction for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions to the field of learning-based control, particularly in establishing theoretical foundations for the stability of complex neural network architectures within control loops.

**Theoretical Advancements:**
1. **δISS for Transformers:** A primary contribution is the formal proof that Transformer networks can satisfy global incremental Input-to-State Stability (δISS) with respect to the induced infinity norm. This is a crucial theoretical development, providing mathematical guarantees for a widely adopted, powerful sequence modeling architecture within dynamic systems. It addresses a recognized gap in the understanding of Transformer stability beyond asymptotic or restrictive conditions.
2. **Coupled Small-Gain Condition:** The paper successfully integrates the δISS properties of Transformers with Riemannian contraction theory. By analyzing the interconnected dynamics of the physical plant and the predictive neural network, it derives a coupled small-gain condition that guarantees uniform closed-loop contraction for the entire system. This framework offers a rigorous method for analyzing and ensuring the stability of hybrid optimization-learning control systems.
3. **Certifiable Robustness via Regularization:** The derived theoretical bounds are leveraged as a training regularizer for the Transformer-Actor-Critic MPC. This approach allows for the synthesis of control policies that are not only performant but also certifiably robust, moving beyond purely empirical validation of stability.

**Practical Impact and Implications:**
1. **Enhanced Robustness and Safety:** The empirical validation on a 3D drone model clearly demonstrates that the proposed framework yields control policies significantly more robust to severe observation noise and process disturbances compared to unregularized baselines. This enhanced robustness directly translates to increased safety, as shown by the 0% collision rate in obstacle avoidance scenarios under maximal disturbance. This is particularly relevant for safety-critical applications in robotics and autonomous systems where reliable performance under uncertainty is paramount.
2. **Advancing Learning-Based Control:** By providing formal stability guarantees for Transformer-based MPC, the research contributes to building confidence in using advanced deep learning models for control tasks. This could encourage wider adoption of learning-based controllers in domains that traditionally require high levels of certification and reliability.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified trade-off between strict global stability bounds and transient rotational agility suggests promising avenues for future work. Exploring state-dependent or localized contraction metrics could potentially mitigate this trade-off, leading to controllers that are both robust and highly agile. This also opens possibilities for integrating other forms of advanced neural networks with rigorous stability analysis.
4. **Relevance to VLA Models:** The integration of Transformers into optimization frameworks is noted as highly relevant for future applications, particularly in the context of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The stability guarantees developed here could extend to these more complex, multi-modal control systems, providing a foundation for their safe and reliable deployment.

In summary, this work represents a significant step towards developing a new generation of learning-based controllers that combine the adaptive capabilities of neural networks with formal guarantees of stability and robustness, addressing a critical need in complex autonomous systems.
