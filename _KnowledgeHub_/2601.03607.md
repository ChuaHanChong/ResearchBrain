# Locomotion Beyond Feet

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers developed a hierarchical, vision-based control system that enables a humanoid robot to perform whole-body locomotion, actively using hands and knees, to navigate challenging terrains like low-clearance spaces and steep stairs. This approach successfully transferred simulated motion-tracking policies to a physical robot without real-world fine-tuning, demonstrating a suite of contact-rich skills.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots predominantly rely on leg-based locomotion, making them struggle with terrains requiring whole-body contact for stability, such as crawling or climbing.
- Implementing versatile whole-body locomotion for humanoids faces significant challenges in developing sophisticated contact-rich motion planning and robust control mechanisms.
- Current methods often lack generalization across diverse motor skills and varying terrains, limiting the robot's adaptability in unstructured real-world environments.

## Method

- The **Locomotion Beyond Feet** system employs a hierarchical, vision-based policy framework that integrates physics-grounded keyframe motions, ==DeepMimic==-based reinforcement learning, and a depth-conditioned visual skill classifier.
- Human expertise is encoded through interactively designed, physics-grounded keyframes within a custom GUI, defining plausible whole-body movements directly in the robot's state space.
- ==Reinforcement learning== policies are trained using ==PPO== to robustly track these keyframe motions and adapt to perturbations, with extensive domain randomization facilitating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The ToddlerBot successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real execution of diverse whole-body skills, including crawling under chairs, climbing knee-high walls/platforms, and ascending/descending steep stairs.
- A depth-conditioned visual skill classifier achieved **93.9%** accuracy on real-world data, enabling autonomous and robust skill selection across various complex obstacle courses.
- The reinforcement learning policies generalized effectively to variations in obstacle heights (e.g., **9-14 cm** walls) and maintained robust performance across complex sequences of obstacles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining human-designed, physics-grounded keyframes with reinforcement learning policies creates an effective method for generating robust, contact-rich whole-body locomotion skills for humanoid robots.
> - A hierarchical architecture, decoupling low-frequency visual perception and skill planning from high-frequency proprioceptive control, provides a modular and robust system for real-time execution.
> - Achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, multi-contact movements is feasible through rigorous domain randomization during RL training and directly designing keyframes in the robot's state space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot locomotion has seen significant advancements, particularly with the rise of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. However, "Locomotion Beyond Feet" addresses a crucial gap in the existing literature: the prevalent focus on **leg-based gaits** for humanoid robots, often neglecting the natural human strategy of leveraging **whole-body contacts** (hands, knees, elbows, torso) for stability and support in challenging environments.

Historically, humanoid locomotion research has drawn heavily from quadrupedal strategies, which excel at terrain traversal through coordinated leg motion. Recent humanoid approaches (e.g., Zhuang et al., Allshire et al. referenced in the paper) have largely adopted these quadruped-inspired, leg-centric methods, with minimal arm involvement. While effective for basic walking and even some parkour-like behaviors, these methods often struggle or become infeasible on terrains requiring low clearance (e.g., crawling under objects), high obstacles (e.g., climbing over walls or onto platforms), or steep inclinations (e.g., stairs), where the support polygon provided by only feet is inherently limited.

This paper explicitly positions itself against this trend by advocating for and demonstrating **whole-body locomotion**. It draws inspiration from biomechanical studies on natural bipeds, which show that additional contacts significantly enlarge the support polygon, enabling greater stability and safer movement in complex scenarios.

Furthermore, the paper distinguishes its approach within the realm of **motion generation techniques**:
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL has enabled robust locomotion, pure RL approaches can be complex to train for diverse, contact-rich behaviors and may struggle with generalization without extensive domain randomization or explicit guidance. This work combines RL with human expertise.
- **Keyframe Motion:** Traditional keyframe animation provides an intuitive way to encode human knowledge but often lacks physical realism or robustness to perturbations. The paper enhances this by introducing **physics-grounded keyframe animation** using tools like MuJoCo, ensuring initial physical plausibility.
- **Motion Capture (Mocap) Retargeting:** A common method involves retargeting human motion capture data to robots. The paper criticizes this for the "embodiment gap" (differences between human and robot kinematics/dynamics) and the lack of inherent dynamics information, which makes sim-to-real transfer challenging and iteration cycles slow. "Locomotion Beyond Feet" bypasses these issues by directly designing keyframes in the robot's state space, allowing for motions optimized for the robot's specific capabilities.

In summary, this work contributes to the broader landscape by pushing the boundaries of humanoid locomotion beyond simple bipedal gaits. It offers a practical and robust framework that marries the intuitive design benefits of human-encoded motor skills (via physics-grounded keyframes) with the adaptive robustness of reinforcement learning, all within a hierarchical, vision-guided system, addressing the critical need for versatile, whole-body movement in real-world, challenging environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Locomotion Beyond Feet" is to **enable comprehensive whole-body humanoid locomotion across extremely challenging and diverse terrains** that are typically infeasible or difficult for leg-only locomotion methods. This involves actively engaging all available body parts—hands, knees, elbows, and torso—to establish additional contacts for enhanced stability and support.

The specific challenging terrains highlighted as target scenarios include:
- Low-clearance spaces under chairs.
- Knee-high walls.
- Knee-high platforms (for climbing onto and down from).
- Steep ascending and descending stairs.

The motivation for this work stems from two core observations and challenges in current humanoid robotics:

1. **Natural Bipedal Behavior vs. Robotic Implementation:** Humans and other natural bipeds frequently rely on hands, knees, and elbows for stability and support in complex environments, such as crawling or climbing. However, most existing humanoid locomotion methods disproportionately focus on leg-based gaits, overlooking these crucial whole-body strategies. This leads to a significant gap in the robots' ability to navigate real-world, unstructured environments.
2. **Addressing Key Challenges in Whole-Body Locomotion:** Implementing whole-body locomotion for humanoids introduces two significant technical challenges that the paper aims to resolve:
 - **Contact-rich Motion Planning and Robust Control:** Navigating complex environments with multiple contact points (feet, hands, knees) requires sophisticated planning and control to maintain balance and achieve desired movements. This is a substantially harder problem than maintaining balance with only feet.
 - **Generalization Across Diverse Motor Skills and Terrains:** Different terrains demand fundamentally different motor skills (e.g., walking, crawling, climbing). Developing a system that can seamlessly execute and transition between these diverse skills, and generalize to variations in terrain, is a major hurdle.

By tackling these motivations, the authors aim to advance humanoid robotics towards more versatile, adaptive, and human-like capabilities in navigating real-world obstacles, thereby expanding their potential applications beyond controlled environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Locomotion Beyond Feet" system employs a hierarchical, vision-based policy framework, integrating four key components to achieve robust whole-body humanoid locomotion:

#### 3.1. Physics-Grounded Keyframe Motion Generation

- **Concept:** This approach combines the intuitive human control of keyframe animation with physical plausibility. Users interactively specify robot poses, execution order, and arrival times within a custom GUI tool integrated with the MuJoCo physics engine.
- **Advantages:** This method allows encoding human knowledge of motor skills and critical contact states directly into the robot's state space, bypassing the "embodiment gap" of motion capture retargeting. The physical plausibility of keyframes can be immediately verified in simulation and validated via open-loop execution on hardware, significantly accelerating design iterations compared to extensive reward shaping for RL.
- **Streamlining Design:** The GUI includes utilities for joint mirroring, foot alignment, and visualization of the center of mass, collisions, and contacts to ease the manual tuning process. Simple motions can be fully designed to be physically valid for direct replay, while complex motions ensure static stability at individual keyframes to maintain overall plausibility.

#### 3.2. DeepMimic-based Motion Tracking Policies (Reinforcement Learning)

- **Concept:** To overcome the open-loop nature of keyframe animation, which lacks adaptability to perturbations, RL is used to train robust motion-tracking policies. These policies learn to execute the physics-grounded keyframe motions while adapting to uncertainties in the environment and robot state.
- **Policy Types:** Three categories of skills are trained:
 - **Locomotion Skills:** Continuous, periodic behaviors (e.g., walking, crawling) that can be executed indefinitely. The walking skill is command-conditioned for reactive velocity control, while others track whole-body motion.
 - **Transition Skills:** Handle changes between canonical poses (e.g., standing to crawling, prone to standing).
 - **Terrain Skills:** Address specific obstacles (e.g., climbing a wall, platform, stairs).
- **Training Details:** Policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a reward function comprising imitation, regularization, and survival terms. The imitation reward enforces tracking of the keyframe-generated reference motions (pose, velocity, end-effector, CoM). Exceptions include walking (velocity tracking) and "rotating on a platform" (heading tracking for in-place rotation with whole-body contact). Domain randomization (ground friction, motor parameters, initial states, pushes, IMU noise, delays) is extensively used to facilitate robust sim-to-real transfer.
- **Canonical Poses:** All policies are trained to start and end in one of four canonical poses (standing, crawling, prone, supine) to ensure smooth transitions between skills.

#### 3.3. Depth-Conditioned Visual Skill Classifier

- **Concept:** A vision-based component that autonomously selects the appropriate skill based on terrain perception.
- **Data Collection:** Real-world training data is collected from the robot's dual-fisheye cameras. RGB images are rectified and processed offline using Foundation Stereo to generate depth maps. Obstacles are randomized, and camera poses are varied to create diverse data. Ground-truth skill labels are assigned based on well-defined transition moments.
- **Classifier Training:** A ResNet classifier is trained on these depth map-skill pairs. Class imbalance is handled by weighting transition and terrain skills.
- **Deployment:** During real-world deployment, depth maps are generated in real-time from RGB inputs using the same Foundation Stereo pipeline and fed into the classifier, which runs at a lower frequency (3.1 Hz) compared to the low-level control policies.

#### 3.4. Hierarchical Policy Execution Framework

- **Concept:** Decouples vision-based planning (low frequency) from proprioception-based control (high frequency) to enhance modularity and robustness.
- **Skill Planning (Algorithm 1):** The visual classifier's predictions are temporally stabilized using an exponential moving average. The system switches to a new transition or terrain policy only when the smoothed confidence for a new skill surpasses a predefined threshold.
- **Failure Recovery:** The framework incorporates a robust failure recovery mechanism. IMU readings are continuously monitored, and if the robot's torso pitch deviates significantly, indicating a fall, a recovery skill (e.g., getting up from prone/supine) is triggered.
- **Skill Chaining:** Some skills are chained (e.g., rotating on a platform followed by climbing down). During skill execution, classifier outputs are ignored; upon completion, the system reverts to locomotion control until the next confident transition is detected. This robust state machine design ensures smooth and reliable execution of complex motion sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling experimental results, demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of "Locomotion Beyond Feet" across various challenging scenarios.

#### 4.1. Robust Whole-Body Locomotion on Challenging Terrains

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The motion-tracking policies, trained in simulation, successfully transferred to the physical ToddlerBot without any real-world fine-tuning.
- **Diverse Skills Demonstrated:** Real-world experiments showcased successful execution of a comprehensive suite of skills, including:
 - Crawling under low-clearance spaces (chairs).
 - Climbing over knee-high walls.
 - Climbing onto and down from knee-high platforms.
 - Climbing up and down steep stairs.
 - Transition skills like getting up from supine and prone positions.
- **Active Whole-Body Engagement:** The robot actively used hands, knees, elbows, and other body parts for stability and support, confirming the "locomotion beyond feet" paradigm.

#### 4.2. Depth Estimation Performance

- **Foundation Stereo for Real-Time Depth:** The Foundation Stereo model was successfully employed to generate depth maps from real-world dual-fisheye RGB images.
- **Efficiency:** By downsampling RGB resolution and using TensorRT with float16 precision, a 6x speedup (from 0.5 Hz to 3.1 Hz) was achieved on a Jetson Orin NX, making real-time deployment feasible without sacrificing skill classification accuracy.
- **Accuracy:** Qualitative comparison with ground-truth simulation depth showed effective capture of terrain features. Quantitatively, a pixel-wise mean absolute error (MAE) of 59 mm (480x640 resolution) and 62 mm (192x256 resolution) within a specific region, and Chamfer Distances of 17mm and 19mm respectively, were reported, deemed sufficient for the downstream visual classifier.

#### 4.3. Visual Skill Classifier Accuracy

- **Real-World Data Training:** The skill classifier was trained exclusively on 27,987 real-world depth map-skill pairs, demonstrating that data collection and labeling required minimal overhead (approx. 18 minutes of recording).
- **High Classification Accuracy:** The classifier achieved an overall classification accuracy of **93.9%** on a held-out real-world test set.
- **Robustness to Ambiguity:** Misclassifications primarily occurred during transitional moments between skills where geometric cues were inherently ambiguous, rather than arbitrary errors. These "natural ambiguities" were effectively handled by the downstream hierarchical planner's temporal smoothing and confidence-based selection mechanisms.

#### 4.4. System Robustness and Generalization

- **Generalization to Varying Obstacle Sizes:** Proprioceptive-only terrain skills (climbing a wall, platform) trained for fixed configurations (e.g., a 12 cm wall, 11 cm platform) generalized robustly to a range of heights (e.g., 9 cm to 14 cm walls, 8.5 cm to 13 cm platforms). This highlights the effectiveness of domain randomization during RL training.
- **Generalization to Diverse Terrain Sequences:** The hierarchical framework successfully navigated five different, complex obstacle courses with varying orders and counts of obstacles (e.g., two chairs in a row) in a zero-shot manner, demonstrating the adaptability and robustness of the skill planner.

In essence, the findings validate the paper's core hypothesis: combining human knowledge (keyframes) with RL, coupled with a vision-based hierarchical planning system, enables humanoids to perform complex, contact-rich locomotion behaviors on challenging and varied real-world terrains that go far beyond typical bipedal walking.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Locomotion Beyond Feet" presents a significant step forward for humanoid robotics, with substantial implications for future research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Enabling Versatile Humanoid Autonomy

The most immediate impact is the demonstrated capability of humanoids to autonomously traverse highly challenging and diverse terrains that were previously difficult or impossible with conventional leg-based methods. This includes navigating cluttered indoor environments (under chairs), overcoming significant vertical obstacles (walls, platforms), and handling complex inclinations (stairs). This greatly enhances the practical utility and versatility of humanoid robots, moving them closer to operating effectively in unstructured human environments.

#### 5.2. Bridging Human Expertise and Robotic Learning

The methodology successfully integrates human intuition and expertise (via physics-grounded keyframes) with the adaptive power of reinforcement learning. This hybrid approach offers a powerful paradigm for developing complex robotic skills, overcoming limitations of purely learned or purely human-programmed systems. It suggests a more efficient pathway to skill acquisition for new, complex behaviors, potentially reducing the extensive manual effort or data requirements of other methods.

#### 5.3. Modular and Robust System Architecture

The hierarchical framework, separating low-frequency vision-based planning from high-frequency proprioceptive control, provides a robust and scalable architecture. This modularity simplifies development, debugging, and potentially the integration of new skills and perception modalities. The explicit inclusion of failure recovery mechanisms further enhances the system's resilience in real-world deployments.

#### 5.4. Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer

The paper demonstrates effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, contact-rich whole-body motions. This is a critical challenge in robotics, and the rigorous domain randomization combined with physics-grounded keyframe references provides valuable insights and techniques for achieving robust transfer, even for highly dynamic and contact-rich tasks.

#### 5.5. Open-Source Contributions

The explicit mention that "All work is open-sourced" (along with the use of the open-source ToddlerBot platform) signifies a major contribution to the robotics community. This will accelerate research by providing readily available tools, policies, and a platform for others to build upon, test, and compare against.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

While impactful, the work also opens avenues for future research, as acknowledged by the authors:
- **Automated Keyframe Design:** Reducing the manual effort and domain expertise required for keyframe design through optimization or generative models could further scale the approach.
- **Improved Motion Naturalness:** Exploring more sophisticated interpolation methods beyond linear interpolation could enhance the fluidity and naturalness of the robot's movements.
- **Enhanced Contact Modeling:** Addressing the occasional failures on hardware due to contact modeling approximations in simulation is crucial for tasks involving extreme contact.

In conclusion, "Locomotion Beyond Feet" represents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by providing a practical, robust, and extensible framework for whole-body locomotion on exceptionally challenging terrains. Its methodology, combining human expertise with deep reinforcement learning and vision-based planning, paves the way for humanoids that can navigate and interact with the physical world in increasingly sophisticated and human-like ways.
