# SeaCache: Spectral-Evolution-Aware Cache for Accelerating Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sungkyunkwan University and NAVER Cloud developed SeaCache, a training-free caching policy that leverages the spectral evolution of diffusion models during inference. This method, which employs a Spectral-Evolution-Aware (SEA) filter, improves latency-quality trade-offs by accurately identifying redundant computations, yielding better perceptual fidelity and reconstruction metrics (e.g., up to an 8 dB PSNR increase for video models) with reduced inference time and TFLOPs.

## Problem

- Diffusion model inference is inherently slow, requiring numerous sequential denoising steps, which limits their applicability in latency-sensitive systems.
- Existing dynamic caching strategies rely on raw feature differences, overlooking the 'spectral evolution' where low-frequency structures appear early and high-frequency details are refined later in the denoising process.
- Raw feature differences conflate content-carrying signals with stochastic noise, leading to suboptimal cache reuse decisions and potential quality degradation.

## Method

- Derived a timestep-dependent Spectral-Evolution-Aware (SEA) filter based on ==Wiener filtering== theory and a power-law spectrum prior, which adapts to the changing frequency content during diffusion.
- Proposed **SeaCache**, a training-free, plug-and-play dynamic caching policy that utilizes the relative L1 distance between consecutive SEA-filtered input features as its redundancy metric.
- Integrated a gain normalization step for the SEA filter to ensure consistent feature magnitudes across timesteps, preventing bias in the cache decision metric.

## Results

- SeaCache consistently achieved superior latency-quality trade-offs, improving PSNR, LPIPS, and SSIM across text-to-image (FLUX.1-dev) and text-to-video (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1 **1.3B**) models while reducing inference latency and TFLOPs.
- For text-to-video generation, SeaCache yielded substantial PSNR improvements (e.g., an 8 dB increase for HunyuanVideo) over baselines and maintained higher perceptual quality, as indicated by VBench and CycleReward scores.
- The SEA filter introduced negligible runtime overhead (<**1%** of total sampling time) and demonstrated plug-and-play compatibility, enhancing existing caching methods like DiCache without requiring model retraining or architectural changes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models exhibit a predictable 'spectral evolution' where low-frequency content is established early and high-frequency details are added later, a property that can be mathematically modeled and leveraged for caching.
> - A specially designed, timestep-dependent spectral filter can effectively separate content-relevant signals from noise in intermediate features, providing a more accurate measure of redundancy for caching.
> - SEA-filtered input features serve as a reliable, training-free proxy for spectrally-aligned output changes, enabling effective spectrum-aware caching without requiring costly full denoiser evaluations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative models, particularly diffusion models and rectified flow (RF) models, have demonstrated the capability to produce high-quality images and videos. However, their inference process is often slow due to the inherently sequential and iterative nature of denoising, which typically requires tens to hundreds of steps. This latency can hinder their application in user-facing systems.

The broader research landscape for accelerating these models can be categorized into several directions:
- **Step Reduction:** Methods that aim to reduce the total number of sampling steps required. This includes improved ODE/SDE solvers (e.g., DPM-Solver) and distillation techniques (e.g., latent consistency models, progressive distillation), which often involve additional training or modifications to the original model.
- **Per-Step Cost Reduction:** Approaches that decrease the computational cost of each individual denoising step. Examples include quantization (reducing bit-precision), efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., FlashAttention, sparse attention), and token reduction strategies (e.g., token pruning, token merging). These methods also frequently require additional training, specialized engineering, or model modifications.
- **Caching-based Acceleration:** A complementary approach that exploits redundancy between consecutive timesteps in the iterative denoising process. The core idea is to reuse intermediate features from previous computations, thereby reducing the number of full denoiser forward passes.

Early caching methods often employed static schedules, reusing features at fixed intervals along the sampling trajectory, which offered predictable speedups but lacked adaptability to diverse content. More recent advancements have introduced dynamic schedules, where reuse decisions are made based on metrics like the distance between current and cached features. These dynamic approaches aim to reduce the error introduced by caching and adapt to the evolving signal.

This paper addresses a specific limitation within the dynamic caching paradigm. Existing dynamic caching strategies typically rely on raw feature differences to measure redundancy. The authors argue that this approach overlooks a fundamental characteristic of diffusion models: "spectral evolution." Prior research has shown that diffusion models establish low-frequency structures in early timesteps and progressively refine high-frequency details in later stages of the denoising process. Measuring redundancy using raw feature differences can entangle content-relevant components (signal) with high-frequency stochastic variation (noise), leading to suboptimal cache decisions. This work aims to explicitly integrate the understanding of spectral evolution into the cache scheduling mechanism to improve efficiency and quality.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Address the limitations of existing caching strategies:** Previous dynamic caching methods for diffusion models typically rely on raw feature differences between adjacent timesteps. This design fails to account for the spectral evolution observed in denoising processes, where low-frequency structural information appears early and high-frequency details are refined later. The raw feature differences can conflate content-carrying signals with stochastic noise, making cache reuse decisions less effective.
2. **Incorporate spectral evolution into cache scheduling:** The authors hypothesize that a cache metric explicitly sensitive to the spectral evolution of the generated content would lead to more judicious reuse decisions. By focusing on changes in the content-relevant signal while de-emphasizing high-frequency noise, the metric could better identify timesteps where computation can be skipped without significant quality degradation.
3. **Develop a Spectral-Evolution-Aware (SEA) filter:** The goal is to mathematically derive a timestep-dependent filter that, when applied to intermediate features, transforms them into a "spectrally aligned representation." This representation would facilitate a more accurate assessment of redundancy between timesteps by separating signal from noise. The filter should reflect the changing effective frequency band throughout the denoising trajectory.
4. **Propose a training-free, plug-and-play caching policy (SeaCache):** The proposed method should integrate seamlessly with existing diffusion models and caching policies without requiring architectural modifications, additional training, or data-specific tuning. This ensures broad applicability and ease of adoption.
5. **Achieve improved latency-quality trade-offs:** The ultimate motivation is to accelerate diffusion model inference significantly while preserving, or even enhancing, the perceptual fidelity of the generated outputs compared to existing caching techniques.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that despite the success of dynamic caching in reducing inference latency, the underlying mechanism for measuring feature redundancy has not fully leveraged the inherent properties of diffusion models. The "oracle experiment" conducted by the authors further motivated their approach by demonstrating that cache decisions based on spectrally filtered outputs (which are not practical for actual caching) yield higher perceptual quality than those based on raw outputs, indicating the potential benefit of a spectrum-aware metric. By developing an input-side proxy for this spectrally-aware output difference, the authors aim to translate this theoretical advantage into a practical and efficient caching solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of SeaCache is centered on introducing a Spectral-Evolution-Aware (SEA) filter into the dynamic cache scheduling process. The approach is structured in three stages:

#### 3.1. Spectral-Evolution-Aware Filter Derivation

The authors formalize the concept of spectral evolution by considering the optimal linear denoiser in the frequency domain.
- **Linear Denoising Model:** The process starts by modeling the noisy latent `x_t` as a linear mixture of the clean latent `x_0` and Gaussian noise `ε`, represented as `x_t = a_t x_0 + b_t ε`. This model is applicable to both DPMs and RF models.
- **Optimal Linear Filter:** Under the assumption of wide-sense stationary signals and independent noise, the optimal linear filter `h_t` for estimating `x_0` from `x_t` (minimizing mean squared error) is derived using Wiener filtering theory. Its frequency response, denoted `G_t(f)`, takes a Wiener-like form: `G_t(f) = (a_t S_x(f)) / (a_t^2 S_x(f) + b_t^2)`, where `S_x(f)` is the power spectrum of `x_0`.
- **Power-Law Spectrum Prior:** To make the filter tractable, a natural image power-law spectrum `S_x(f) ∝ |f|^(-β)` is assumed for `x_0`, where `f` is frequency and `β` is an exponent (e.g., `β=2` for images, `β=3` for videos).
- **Spectral Evolution:** The derived `G_t(f)` is timestep-dependent. As `t` decreases from `T` to `0` (reverse diffusion), `a_t` increases, causing the effective passband of `G_t(f)` to widen. This demonstrates that early steps primarily pass low frequencies, while later steps gradually include higher frequencies, consistent with the observed spectral evolution. This means the filter emphasizes content-carrying low frequencies early on and incorporates more high-frequency details later.
- **Feature Filtering:** The SEA filter is applied in the frequency domain. Given an input feature `I_t`, it is transformed via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), element-wise multiplied by `G_t(f)`, and then converted back to the spatial domain using Inverse FFT (iFFT) to produce `P(G_t, I_t)`. This operation essentially amplifies content-relevant signals and suppresses noise based on the timestep.
- **Gain Normalization:** To ensure that distances computed between filtered features are comparable across different timesteps, the raw filter response `G_t(f)` is normalized. A density-normalized filter `G_norm_t(f)` is created by scaling `G_t(f)` such that its mean gain over radial frequencies is constant (`ν_t G_t(f)`). This prevents the filtered feature magnitude from drifting and biasing the cache metric.

#### 3.2. Spectrum-Aware Dynamic Caching

- **Input Proxy for Output Change:** Directly using SEA-filtered outputs for cache decisions is impractical as it would require a full denoiser evaluation. Therefore, an input-side proxy is necessary. The authors evaluate several candidates for this proxy (raw input, raw output, polynomial-fitted input, and their SEA-filtered counterparts). Empirical analysis demonstrates that SEA-filtered input features `P(G_norm_t, I_t)` closely track SEA-filtered output features `P(G_norm_t, O_t)` across the entire generation trajectory. This finding supports the use of SEA-filtered inputs as a reliable, training-free proxy for spectrum-aware redundancy.
- **SeaCache Metric:** The per-step cache metric `e_∆_t` is defined as the relative `ℓ_1` distance between consecutive SEA-filtered input features: `e_∆_t = L1_rel(P(G_norm_t, I_t), P(G_norm_t+1, I_t+1))`.
- **Dynamic Caching Rule:** The standard accumulated distance rule from prior dynamic caching methods (e.g., TeaCache) is maintained. A cached output from step `t_a` is reused until the sum of `e_∆_s` from `s=t_a` to `t_b-1` exceeds a predefined threshold `δ`, at which point a refresh is triggered at `t_b`.
- **Plug-and-Play Nature:** SeaCache is designed to be a plug-and-play module. It requires no architectural changes or retraining of the diffusion model. It acts by replacing only the distance metric within existing dynamic caching policies. The method is network-agnostic and sampler-agnostic, as it only depends on the shared sampler schedule coefficients (`a_t`, `b_t`).

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Models:** Evaluated on text-to-image (FLUX.1-dev) and text-to-video (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1 1.3B) models, all sampled for 50 steps.
- **Baselines:** Compared against TeaCache, TaylorSeer, ∆-DiT, ToCa, and DiCache, using their official implementations and default settings. For fairness, the first few timesteps were explicitly refreshed for baselines, and control parameters (`δ` for TeaCache/SeaCache, `S` for TaylorSeer) were adjusted to match cache ratios.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Latency (s), TFLOPs, PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM (full-reference metrics against original model output). Perceptual quality was also assessed using CycleReward and VBench (for video).
- **Cache Budgets:** Experiments were conducted at approximately 50% and 30% refresh ratios (fraction of timesteps performing full denoiser evaluation).
- **Ablation Studies:** Variants of the SEA filter (complementary filter, no normalization, simple low-pass filter) were tested to confirm the effectiveness of design choices.
- **Compatibility:** The SEA filter was integrated into DiCache to demonstrate its plug-and-play nature with other caching methods.
- **Cache Ratio Visualization:** Analysis of refresh patterns across timesteps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several findings, both empirical and analytical, that support the effectiveness of SeaCache:

#### 4.1. Oracle Experiment Validation

An oracle experiment compared cache schedules derived from raw output differences versus SEA-filtered output differences. The results showed that the SEA-filtered criterion consistently achieved higher PSNR at matched refresh ratios. This indicated that a spectrum-aware distance measure better preserves the behavior of the original full-compute model, validating the core idea.

#### 4.2. Input Feature Proxy Reliability

The study found that SEA-filtered input features `P(G_norm_t, I_t)` closely tracked SEA-filtered output features `P(G_norm_t, O_t)` across the entire generation trajectory for both FLUX and Wan2.1 models. This empirical alignment suggests that SEA-filtered inputs serve as a reliable, training-free proxy for estimating output changes and redundancy, overcoming the impracticality of using outputs directly.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Performance Comparison

- **Text-to-Image (FLUX.1-dev):** SeaCache demonstrated superior latency-quality trade-offs. At a ~50% refresh ratio, it achieved lower latency and TFLOPs while significantly improving PSNR, LPIPS, and SSIM compared to TeaCache and TaylorSeer. This trend persisted at a more aggressive ~30% refresh ratio, where SeaCache maintained the best metrics despite higher acceleration. CycleReward scores also indicated that SeaCache achieved a lower average reward rank, suggesting better perceptual quality aligned with human preference.
- **Text-to-Video (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1 1.3B):** Similar performance improvements were observed for text-to-video generation. For HunyuanVideo, SeaCache achieved substantially higher PSNR (e.g., an 8 dB increase over baselines), lower LPIPS, and higher SSIM at both ~50% and ~30% refresh ratios, with reduced latency and TFLOPs. On Wan2.1 1.3B, SeaCache again showed consistently better metrics across the board, reducing latency and TFLOPs while maintaining or improving quality. VBench and CompressedVQA evaluations further confirmed SeaCache's superior performance across various video quality dimensions and full-reference metrics.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Performance Comparison

Qualitative comparisons visually supported the quantitative results.
- **Text-to-Image:** SeaCache consistently preserved semantic content, fine details, and text fidelity in generated images, whereas baselines often introduced artifacts, blurred details, or failed to render text accurately (e.g., the word "quantum" or number of objects).
- **Text-to-Video:** SeaCache maintained content consistency, object integrity, and overall visual quality in generated videos, avoiding noticeable distortions, blurriness, or altered elements that appeared in videos from baseline methods (e.g., sharp "STOP" sign, correct panda/coffee cup rendering, plausible Taichi motion).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the importance of each component of the SEA filter:
- The SEA filter (signal-focused) consistently outperformed its complementary form (noise-focused), indicating that tracking content redundancy is more effective.
- Normalization of the filter gain was crucial; removing it led to a noticeable drop in PSNR, as unnormalized feature magnitudes drifted across timesteps, biasing the cache metric.
- A static low-pass filter performed significantly worse, demonstrating that simple emphasis on low frequencies is insufficient, and the timestep-dependent spectral evolution captured by the SEA filter is vital.

#### 4.6. Adaptation to Other Cache Methods

The plug-and-play nature of SeaCache was demonstrated by integrating the SEA filter into DiCache. The "DiCache+Ours" variant achieved consistently higher PSNR for the same refresh ratio compared to the original DiCache, suggesting that the spectrum-aware distance can enhance other caching schemes regardless of their underlying mechanisms or feature levels.

#### 4.7. Adaptive Cache Schedule

Analysis of refresh patterns showed that SeaCache automatically concentrated most refreshes on early timesteps. This behavior aligns with the spectral-evolution prior, where early steps are critical for establishing global structure. In contrast, TeaCache distributed refreshes more uniformly. SeaCache's adaptive scheduling eliminates the need for manual tuning of early-step computation, as often required by other methods.

#### 4.8. Runtime Overhead

The additional computational cost introduced by the FFT, frequency-domain filtering, and iFFT operations for SEA filtering was measured to be negligible, typically less than 1% (e.g., 0.4-0.6%) of the total end-to-end sampling time across different models and resolutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SeaCache represents a notable advancement in the field of diffusion model acceleration through caching, with several significant implications:

1. **Advancement in Caching Mechanisms:** SeaCache introduces a novel and theoretically grounded approach to dynamic caching by explicitly leveraging the principle of spectral evolution in diffusion models. By deriving and applying a Spectral-Evolution-Aware (SEA) filter, the method moves beyond raw feature distance metrics, offering a more content-aware and noise-robust criterion for reuse decisions. This is the first caching policy to inject an explicit frequency prior into the reuse decision process.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The extensive experimental results demonstrate that SeaCache consistently achieves superior latency-quality trade-offs compared to prior caching baselines across diverse generative models (text-to-image and text-to-video) and various cache budgets. This includes significant improvements in reconstruction fidelity metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM) and perceptual quality benchmarks (CycleReward, VBench, CompressedVQA) while reducing inference latency and TFLOPs.

3. **Practicality and Accessibility:** A key strength of SeaCache is its design as a training-free, plug-and-play solution. It requires no architectural modifications or retraining of existing diffusion models, nor does it necessitate task- or data-specific tuning. Its network-agnostic and sampler-agnostic nature ensures broad compatibility, making it easy to integrate with current and future diffusion models and their inference pipelines. The negligible runtime overhead further enhances its practical applicability.

4. **Adaptive and Efficient Resource Utilization:** By automatically concentrating refreshes on critical early timesteps, SeaCache effectively utilizes the available cache budget. This adaptive scheduling removes the need for manual hyperparameters related to initial step computation, streamlining deployment and optimizing performance for varied inputs.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work highlights the importance of spectral properties in understanding and optimizing diffusion model inference. This can inspire future research directions, such as:
 - Developing more sophisticated content-aware or latent-space-specific spectral filters that relax the simplifying assumptions made in this work.
 - Integrating spectral evolution insights with other acceleration techniques (e.g., distillation, sparse attention) to achieve combined benefits.
 - Applying similar frequency-domain analysis to other aspects of generative model design and optimization.

Overall, SeaCache offers a robust, efficient, and broadly applicable method for accelerating diffusion models, potentially facilitating faster and higher-quality content generation in a wide range of user-facing and research applications.
