# Expanding Flow Maps

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows) and their distilled counterpart, Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs), which overcome the fixed-dimensionality limitation of current generative models by enabling dynamic expansion of the state space during inference. The framework successfully generates high-quality, variable-sized outputs across continuous molecular conformer generation, discrete molecular graph generation, and variable-length language modeling, often achieving competitive or superior results with fewer sampling steps.

## Problem

- Existing flow-based and score-based generative models are restricted to fixed-dimensional continuous state spaces or fixed-length discrete sequence spaces.
- This "fixed canvas" limitation prevents their direct application to many real-world generative tasks where output size is inherently variable (e.g., adaptive resolution images, arbitrary-length text, variable atom-count molecules).
- Many existing methods for variable-length generation do not leverage the computational efficiency of few-step generation offered by flow maps.

## Method

- Introduces **Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows)** as a ==piecewise-deterministic Markov process== that unifies smooth denoising (==transport==) with discontinuous dimension-expanding jumps (==expansion==).
- Derives **Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs)** by ==distilling== the EFlow process into efficient few-step generative models, factoring the map between any two timesteps into an =="expand operator"== and a =="transport map"==.
- Extends the framework to ==discrete simplex spaces==, employing learned ==insertion heads== to predict token/node insertions for variable-size graph and variable-length sequence generation.

## Results

- EFlow (**20** steps) and EFM+R (**14** steps) achieved performance competitive with or superior to state-of-the-art multi-step diffusion models in continuous molecular conformer generation, with EFM+R achieving the best metrics on GEOM-Drugs.
- EFlow and EFM demonstrated robust, variable-sized discrete molecular graph generation on QM9, outperforming a state-of-the-art discrete graph flow model (DeFoG) with significantly lower FCD, especially at reduced step budgets (e.g., EFM's 1-step FCD was nearly **5** times lower than CFM's).
- Expanding flows enabled variable-length language modeling on LM1B, improving upon fixed-length flow language models in multi-step scenarios and outperforming distilled diffusion baselines and categorical flow maps in few-step generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative flows can be generalized to increasing dimensionality through an "expanding interpolant" by augmenting the source state with conditional noise via an "expand operator" and assigning local time coordinates for denoising.
> - The process of combining continuous transport and discontinuous dimension expansion can be formally characterized as a Piecewise-Deterministic Markov Process (PDMP).
> - Few-step efficiency for variable-dimensional generation is achieved by distilling EFlows into EFMs, which are trained using consistency objectives adapted for expanding dimensions and interpreted as stochastic flow maps due to explicit noise injection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generative models has seen substantial advancements, particularly with flow-based and score-based methods. These models, including diffusion models, stochastic interpolants, and consistency models, facilitate rapid and controllable generation across continuous and discrete state spaces. A recent innovation in this area is the concept of "flow maps," which distill the multi-step integration process of continuous-time generative dynamics into a few-step (or even one-step) generative process. Flow maps learn to parameterize discrete jumps along the ODE trajectory between any two timesteps, typically trained via distillation from a teacher flow or self-distillation from data. This approach enables efficient sampling while retaining modeling flexibility.

However, a fundamental limitation across existing flow-based models, including consistency models and flow maps, is their constraint to a fixed "canvas." This means they operate on a continuous state space of a predetermined, fixed dimensionality or a discrete state space of a fixed sequence length. This restriction poses a challenge for numerous real-world generative tasks where the size of the output is inherently variable. Examples include images and 3D shapes with adaptive resolutions, audio and video of arbitrary durations, language generation with unknown sequence lengths, and multi-modal data that combines modalities of varying intrinsic dimensionalities.

This work addresses this specific limitation by introducing a framework that enables generative models to dynamically expand their state space during inference. It seeks to combine the efficiency of few-step generation offered by flow maps with the flexibility of adaptive dimensionality, thereby extending the applicability of these powerful generative paradigms to a wider array of complex, unstructured data generation problems that were previously difficult to tackle due to rigid size constraints. This positions the research as a significant step towards more flexible and universal generative modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question motivating this research is: "How can we enable few-step generation of both continuous and discrete data with adaptive dimensionality at inference?"

The key objectives and motivations behind the introduction of Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows) and Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs) are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing the Fixed-Dimensionality Constraint:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent limitation of current flow-based generative models and flow maps, which are restricted to fixed-dimensional continuous state spaces or fixed-length discrete sequence spaces. This "fixed canvas" prevents their direct application to many real-world scenarios where the output size is a variable quantity that needs to be modeled, not predetermined.
- **Enabling Adaptive Output Sizes:** The researchers aim to develop a generative framework that can accommodate and learn variable output sizes. This is crucial for tasks such as generating molecular structures with varying numbers of atoms, producing text sequences of arbitrary lengths, or creating images and 3D shapes with dynamic resolutions.
- **Maintaining Few-Step Efficiency:** While some existing methods have explored variable-length generation, they often fall outside the efficient, few-step paradigm of flow maps. A core objective is to integrate this adaptive dimensionality capability directly into the flow map framework, thereby retaining the significant computational advantages of generating samples in a minimal number of function evaluations.
- **Unifying Continuous and Discrete Generative Modeling:** The framework is designed to be generalizable across both continuous and discrete data modalities. This involves developing a consistent mathematical and algorithmic approach that can handle the specific challenges of dimension expansion in continuous spaces (e.g., adding new coordinates) and discrete spaces (e.g., inserting new tokens or nodes in sequences/graphs).
- **Principled Framework for State Expansion:** The work is motivated by a desire to provide a rigorous, principled formulation for generative flows between distributions of increasing dimensionality. This involves defining an "expanding interpolant" and formalizing the operations that allow a state to grow in dimension while progressing along the generative trajectory.
- **Specific Contributions (Objectives):**
 - **Introduce Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows):** Define a new class of generative models based on an expanding interpolant, formulated as a piecewise-deterministic Markov process (PDMP) that unifies smooth denoising (transport) with discontinuous jumps to higher dimensionalities (expansion).
 - **Derive Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs):** Build upon EFlows to create a few-step generative model. EFMs achieve this by factoring the map between any two timesteps into an "expand operator" (augments the state with conditional noise) and a "transport map" (pushes the augmented state forward).
 - **Extend to Discrete Spaces:** Develop specific instantiations of expanding generative flows and flow maps for discrete simplex spaces, enabling variable-size graph generation and variable-length sequence generation where standard fixed-dimensional methods are inapplicable.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to create a versatile and efficient generative modeling tool applicable to a broader spectrum of complex data generation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology introduces Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows) as a foundational concept, which are then distilled into the efficient Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs). The framework is designed to be general across continuous and discrete state spaces.

#### 3.1. Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows)

EFlows are constructed to enable flows between distributions of increasing dimensionality. This involves three core components:

- **Expanding Interpolant:** This generalizes the standard flow interpolant by allowing the state space dimension, `d(t)`, to be a non-decreasing function of time `t ∈`. Consequently, the marginal distribution `p_t` is defined on `R^d(t)`. The key challenge here is that for `s < t` with `d(s) < d(t)`, the source `x_s` and target `x_t` reside in different dimensional spaces, precluding a direct diffeomorphism or fixed-dimension velocity field.
- **Expand Operator (E_s,t):** To resolve the dimensionality mismatch, the expand operator augments the source state `x_s ∈ R^d(s)` with additional coordinates of noise, `ϵ ∼ p_ϵ|s,t ∈ P(R^(d(t)-d(s)))`, thereby lifting `x_s` to the target dimensionality `d(t)`. The operator `E_s,t(x_s, ϵ): R^d(s) × R^(d(t)-d(s)) → R^d(t)` defines the "placement scheme" for these new coordinates. This operator can be instantiated as:
 - **Concatenation:** Appends new coordinates to the end.
 - **Positional Insertion:** Interleaves new coordinates at specific positions.
 - **Child Expansion:** Attaches new coordinates to existing "parent" entries, initialized relative to them.
 The augmented noise and its placement can be deterministic or predicted by a parameterized network.
- **Local Time Coordinate:** Each newly inserted component `i` is assigned a `t_ins_i` (its insertion time). For any global time `t > t_ins_i`, its local time `t_i = (t - t_ins_i) / (1 - t_ins_i) ∈`. This ensures all inserted dimensions are denoised on a common local clock, reflecting the time elapsed since their insertion.
- **Transport Along the Expanding Interpolant:** After expansion, the standard velocity field `b_τ` of a continuous-time interpolant is integrated over `τ ∈ [s, t]` from the augmented state `x_ϵ_s` to `x_t`. Each coordinate `i` is integrated along its own local time `τ_i`. The velocity field `b_τ` is parameterized as `ˆb_τ` by minimizing the conditional flow-matching (CFM) objective and is set to zero for inactive (not yet inserted) coordinates.
- **Piecewise-Deterministic Markov Process (PDMP) Characterization:** EFlows are formally characterized as a PDMP on a state space `M = ⊔_t R^d(t)`. The generator of this PDMP includes a "transport" term (describing smooth denoising governed by `b_t`) and a "jump" kernel (describing instantaneous jumps to higher dimensions via `E_t` and `p_ϵ|t`, triggered by an insertion intensity `λ_t`). This unifies the two-stage process (expansion and transport) into a single well-defined stochastic process.

#### 3.2. Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs)

EFMs distill the multi-step EFlow process into efficient few-step generative models. An EFM `Φ_s,t` directly maps from `x_s ∈ R^d(s)` to `x_t ∈ R^d(t)` in a single learned step, jointly expanding dimensionality and advancing the flow in time.

- **Composition:** `Φ_s,t = X_s,t ◦ E_s,t`, where `X_s,t` is the transport map in the augmented `d(t)`-dimensional space, parameterized by a mean velocity `v_s,t` (for continuous) or mean denoiser `ψ_s,t` (for discrete).
- **Training Objective:** EFMs are trained using a combination of a diagonal objective and consistency objectives, analogous to fixed-dimensional flow maps.
 - **Diagonal Objective (L_diag):** Enforces `lim_{s→t} ∂_t Φ_s,t(x) = v_t,t(x) = b_t(x)`, ensuring that the EFM's instantaneous derivative matches the EFlow's velocity field. This is based on conditional flow-matching.
 - **Consistency Objectives (L_cons):** Derived from the Lagrangian, Eulerian, and Semigroup conditions. These conditions ensure that the learned flow map is self-consistent across different time intervals. The identities are adapted for expanding dimensions, notably in the Eulerian condition where the Jacobian `∇_x Φ_s,t` is rectangular.
- **Stochastic Flow Map Interpretation:** Due to the explicit conditional noise injection (`ϵ`) by the expand operator, EFMs define a stochastic flow. This means that for a given `x_s`, the EFM produces a distribution over `x_t`, capturing the full posterior over future states rather than a single deterministic trajectory.

#### 3.3. Discrete Expanding Flow Maps

The framework is extended to the discrete simplex space, specifically for variable-length sequence and graph generation.

- **Discrete Expanding Interpolant:** For sequences, each token `i` is assigned an independent insertion time `t_ins_i`. The intermediate sequence `x_t` comprises currently inserted tokens, where each `x_i_t_i` is a V-dimensional latent vector (on the simplex) denoised to its local time `t_i`.
- **Parameterizing Token/Node Insertions:** A learned "insertion head" predicts the (two-time) insertion expectation `I_s,t(x_s)[i]`, representing the expected number of tokens/nodes to insert into gap `i` over `(s, t]`. This is trained with an `L_insert` objective, typically a Poisson Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL).
- **Discrete Expand Operator:** Inserts latent tokens (e.g., initialized from Gaussian noise) according to the predicted gap lengths `ℓ_i`, sampled from a binomial distribution `ℓ_i ∼ Binomial(L - n_s, ˆI_s,t(x_s)[i]/(L-n_s))`. For graphs, this includes inserting new nodes and their associated edges.
- **Discrete EFM:** Composes the discrete expand operator with a mean denoiser `ψ_s,t` operating on the simplex. The `ψ_s,t` predicts denoised categorical distributions for each token/node/edge.
- **Discrete EFM Identities:** Analogous Lagrangian, Eulerian, and Semigroup conditions are derived for `ψ_s,t` in the discrete setting, leading to cross-entropy-based `L_DEFM` objectives for training `ˆψ` and `ˆE`.
- **Implementation Details:** This includes handling local time conditioning per position/node/edge, time reparameterization schemes to focus training on critical time intervals, adaptive loss weighting, and specific strategies for one-step sampling to mitigate mode collapse. For graph generation, it involves defining node and edge activation based on insertion times, managing node/edge interpolants, and compacting partially active graphs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of EFlows and EFMs was evaluated across three diverse generative tasks: molecular conformer generation (continuous), discrete molecular graph generation, and variable-length language modeling.

#### 4.1. Coarse-to-Fine Molecular Conformer Generation

- **Task:** Generating 3D atomic coordinates for molecules.
- **Datasets:** GEOM-QM9 and GEOM-Drugs.
- **Metrics:** Coverage (COV-R, COV-P) and Average Minimum RMSD (AMR-R, AMR-P) for recall (R) and precision (P), at specified RMSD thresholds. Ensemble property prediction (e.g., energy, HOMO-LUMO gap).
- **Findings:**
 - **EFlow (20 steps):** Achieved performance competitive with or superior to state-of-the-art multi-step diffusion baselines (e.g., GeoDiff, SubgDiff, MSGEN), which typically required 500-1000 denoising steps. For instance, on GEOM-QM9, EFlow achieved COV-R 88.51% and COV-P 53.07%.
 - **EFlow+R (30 steps, with refinement):** Consistently improved precision metrics, demonstrating refined local geometry. It attained the best AMR-P on GEOM-QM9 (0.4876 Å) and the best/second-best values on all four metrics for GEOM-Drugs.
 - **EFM (few steps, distilled):** Enabled generation in as few as 1, 2, or 4 steps. EFM+R (14 steps) achieved the single best score on all four GEOM-Drugs metrics, indicating high efficiency and quality.
 - **Chemical Validity:** EFlow+R showed comparable or better performance in reproducing molecular ensemble properties (e.g., median absolute errors for E_min, ∆ε_max) compared to multi-step baselines.
- **Conclusion:** EFlow and EFM provide a unified and efficient framework for conformer generation, scaling from multi-step to few-step regimes with competitive or superior performance.

#### 4.2. Discrete Molecular Graph Generation

- **Task:** Generating novel molecular graphs with variable numbers of nodes and edges.
- **Dataset:** QM9.
- **Metrics:** Validity, Uniqueness, and Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD).
- **Findings:**
 - **EFlow (multi-step):** Outperformed the state-of-the-art discrete graph flow model DeFoG, achieving a lower FCD (0.116 vs. 0.134 at 100 steps). EFlow demonstrated greater robustness to reduced step budgets; at 10 steps, EFlow maintained high validity (~98%) and uniqueness (~96%), whereas DeFoG's FCD and validity degraded significantly. At 4 steps, EFlow still produced 91.7% valid and 92.8% unique molecules, compared to DeFoG's 53.6% validity.
 - **EFM (few steps, distilled):**
 - **2 steps:** Achieved comparable validity and uniqueness to categorical flow maps (CFM) while yielding a lower FCD (0.40 vs. 0.49).
 - **1 step:** EFM's advantage widened, with FCD remaining strong at 0.44 and uniqueness at 97.7%, in contrast to CFM's FCD degrading to 2.14 and uniqueness to 91.8%. EFM's 1-step FCD was nearly 5 times lower than CFM's.
- **Conclusion:** Expanding flows offer a robust framework for variable-sized graph generation, performing effectively across multi-step and one-step sampling budgets.

#### 4.3. Variable-Length Language Modeling

- **Task:** Unconditional text generation with variable sequence lengths.
- **Dataset:** One Billion Word benchmark (LM1B).
- **Metrics:** Generative Perplexity (PPL) under GPT-2-Large and entropy of generated token distribution.
- **Findings:**
 - **EFlow (multi-step):** Consistently improved upon the fixed-length flow language model (FLM) across all evaluated step budgets (64 to 1024 steps), despite solving a strictly more expressive variable-length problem. Generative PPL decreased monotonically with sampling budget (e.g., 103.63 at 1024 steps), indicating that variable-length conditioning did not sacrifice sample quality. Sample entropy remained coherent.
 - **EFM (few steps, distilled):** Generated coherent text at 2 and 4 steps, outperforming all distilled diffusion baselines (Duo, MDLM) and categorical flow maps (CFM), while remaining competitive with FMLM.
 - **1 step:** EFM exhibited mode collapse in single-step sampling, attributed to the difficulty of predicting full-length expansion and applying the flow map over the entire interval from an entirely noisy sequence. Strategies like `t_denoise-min` were employed to mitigate this.
 - **Novelty:** This work represents the first demonstration of flow maps applied to variable-length discrete sequences.
- **Conclusion:** Expanding flows add variable-length flexibility to language generation without sacrificing sample quality in multi-step scenarios, and enable competitive few-step generation, expanding the design space for flow-map frameworks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Expanding Generative Flows (EFlows) and Expanding Flow Maps (EFMs) represents a significant advancement in the field of generative modeling, with several key implications and potential impacts:

- **Breaking the Fixed-Dimensionality Barrier:** The primary significance lies in overcoming the fundamental limitation of fixed-dimensional or fixed-length state spaces in existing flow-based and diffusion models. This innovation enables generative models to naturally handle data where output size is itself a learned and controllable variable, such as variable-resolution images, arbitrary-duration audio/video, and variable-length text or molecular structures. This broadens the applicability of these models to a wider array of real-world, inherently unstructured data.

- **Unified and Principled Framework:** EFlows and EFMs offer a unified theoretical and algorithmic framework for variable-dimensional generation across both continuous and discrete modalities. By formalizing the concept of an "expanding interpolant" and characterizing the process as a piecewise-deterministic Markov process, the work provides a rigorous foundation for future developments in this area. This unification simplifies the design and training of generative models for complex, multi-modal, and variable-sized data.

- **Enhanced Generative Efficiency:** EFMs demonstrate the capability to achieve high-quality generation in a few steps (as low as 1 to 4 function evaluations) even with adaptive dimensionality. This few-step efficiency, inherited from the flow map paradigm, significantly reduces the computational cost and inference time associated with generative tasks, making these models more practical for real-time applications and large-scale deployments.

- **Broad Applicability Across Domains:** The successful experimental validation across diverse domains—continuous molecular conformer generation, discrete molecular graph generation, and variable-length language modeling—underscores the versatility and generalizability of the proposed framework. This suggests that EFMs could be readily adapted to other domains requiring flexible output sizes, potentially impacting fields such as computational biology, materials science, computer vision (e.g., conditional image generation with variable canvas sizes), and natural language processing (e.g., long-form document generation).

- **Stochasticity and Posterior Modeling:** The interpretation of EFMs as stochastic flow maps, deriving randomness from explicit conditional noise injection, is valuable. This construction induces a distribution over trajectories, enabling the generation of multiple independent outputs from a single intermediate state, which is beneficial for tasks like posterior sampling and reward alignment that require diversity in generated samples.

- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens several avenues for future research. This includes exploring more sophisticated conditional noise distributions beyond simple Gaussian noise, investigating alternative interpolant definitions that are conditioned on insertion times or enforce structured insertion orderings, and potentially extending the framework to handle decreasing dimensionality along the interpolant, which could be relevant for tasks like hierarchical summarization or simplification.

In summary, Expanding Flow Maps contribute a robust and efficient solution to a longstanding limitation in generative modeling, paving the way for more flexible, adaptable, and computationally efficient generative AI systems capable of handling the complexity and variability of real-world data.
