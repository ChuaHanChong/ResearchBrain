# VTAP Gripper: Synergizing Fingertip Sensing and a Visuo-Tactile Active Palm for Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VTAP Gripper is a 13-DOF three-finger tactile-reactive gripper pairing Fin-Ray compliant fingers carrying 32x12 piezoresistive tactile arrays with a linearly actuated palm whose single camera switches between far-field vision and optical touch via LED-controlled mirror coating. A gesture-conditioned staged retargeting framework enables VR teleoperation, yielding 93.3% grasp success and singulation of 3 mm objects.

## Problem

- Sensing and actuation in existing grippers stay confined to fingers, neglecting palm-object contact that is critical for grasp stability and contact redistribution during in-hand manipulation.
- Most grippers use a single modality, so vision-only setups suffer occlusion and touch-only setups lack long-range localization; compactly fusing both plus fingertip touch remains unsolved.
- Non-anthropomorphic multi-finger grippers lack joint-to-joint human correspondence and use finger-centered base frames, so wrist-centered anatomy-based retargeting from dexterous-hand teleoperation fails.

## Method

- **Visuo-Tactile Active Palm** (VTAP): a 50 mm linear-actuator-driven palm whose camera sees through a ==mirror-paint silicone cartridge== when LEDs are off and images contact deformation when LEDs illuminate, no mechanical switching.
- Three kinematically identical 4-DOF ==Fin-Ray TPU fingers== with ==FlexiTac piezoresistive arrays== (32x12 taxels, 2x2 mm units), plus a ==tactile-reactive closure rule== thresholding contact-area change and summed taxel magnitude.
- ==Gesture-conditioned subspace retargeting==: hand gestures select cage/power/pinch priors fixing radial-ulnar and abduction DOFs, then ==Huber-robust optimization== over fingertip position, orientation, and velocity-smoothness losses in an intermediate frame.

## Results

- Tactile-reactive grasping of **five** YCB plus **four** everyday objects reached **93.3%** success over **10** trials each; only the heavy asymmetric drill failed often (**5/10**) due to pre-contact slip.
- Teleoperated syringe reorientation plus plunger depression succeeded in **13/20** trials (**65%**) averaging **33.4 +/- 5.43 s**; autonomous bi-modal peg-in-hole hit **70%** over **10** trials at **1 mm** tolerance.
- In-hand singulation isolated single items across mung beans, gummy bears, chocolate spheres, **7 mm** steel balls and cotton swabs, down to **3 mm** diameter; fingertip arrays give **~215%** more sensing area than GelSight Mini.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dexterity can come from coordinated finger-palm synergy rather than high-DOF anthropomorphic hands, trading actuator count for an actuated palm and richer contact sensing.
> - One camera behind a switchable mirror-coated elastomer yields both far-field vision and optical tactile sensing, avoiding stereo rigs or mechanical mode-switching hardware.
> - Retargeting heterogeneous embodiments works better by reducing configuration space with discrete gesture-selected grasp priors than by forcing direct anatomical joint correspondence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is undergoing a shift from structured industrial environments, characterized by predefined pick-and-place operations, to dynamic, semi-structured human environments. This transition necessitates robotic manipulators capable of performing complex post-grasp actions such as in-hand reorientation and precise alignment, beyond simple grasping.

Traditional robotic grippers exhibit limitations in this evolving landscape. Parallel grippers are mechanically simple and easy to control, but their limited reconfigurability restricts dexterity for complex tasks in human-centered settings. Anthropomorphic hands offer high dexterity but are often bulky, expensive, and challenging to control due to their many degrees of freedom (DOFs). Multi-finger grippers aim to strike a balance between actuation complexity and dexterity, particularly when augmented with tactile feedback.

This work addresses two primary challenges within the domain of multi-finger grippers:
1. **Neglect of Palm-Object Interaction and Lack of Active Palm Actuation:** Most existing robotic grippers primarily confine sensing and actuation to the fingers, often overlooking the critical role of palm-object contact in many grasping and manipulation tasks. Moreover, non-anthropomorphic multi-finger grippers rarely incorporate additional DOFs at the palm. An active palm, capable of controllable object manipulation, could enhance in-hand manipulation by facilitating coordinated contact redistribution across fingers and palm.
2. **Reliance on Single-Modality Sensing and Compact Multi-Modal Integration:** Many current grippers rely on a single sensing modality (either vision or tactile), which is often insufficient for comprehensive environmental perception in complex manipulation scenarios. Integrating tactile and visual sensing within a unified framework could mitigate occlusion issues and enhance perceptual robustness. However, compactly combining fingertip tactile sensing, palm tactile sensing, and visual perception into a single gripper design remains an engineering challenge.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the context of learning-from-demonstration methods, which rely on efficient data collection through teleoperation. For non-anthropomorphic multi-finger grippers, which offer a balance between structural simplicity and manipulation capability, developing effective retargeting strategies that bridge the embodiment gap between human hands and robot grippers is an underexplored problem. This work seeks to provide a practical solution for this class of grippers, distinct from simpler parallel grippers or more complex anthropomorphic hands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to develop a novel tactile-reactive robotic gripper and an accompanying teleoperation framework, aiming to advance dexterous in-hand manipulation capabilities.

The key objectives and their underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Objective 1: Design and develop a tactile-reactive gripper integrating an actuated bi-modal palm with vision and touch capabilities, alongside compliant, reconfigurable fingers equipped with tactile array sensors.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing robotic grippers often neglect the role of the palm in manipulation and primarily focus sensing and actuation on the fingers. This limits their capacity for complex in-hand tasks requiring continuous object rearrangement and fine control. Furthermore, reliance on a single sensing modality (either vision or tactile) often provides incomplete environmental perception, leading to robustness issues and challenges with occlusion. An actuated palm could enable controllable object manipulation and coordinate contact redistribution, which is essential for dexterous tasks. Integrating both vision and touch in a compact palm design could provide comprehensive, complementary feedback, mitigating the limitations of single-modality sensing and expanding the gripper's functional scope. The use of compliant fingers with tactile arrays is motivated by the need for robust grasping and fine contact-rich feedback.

- **Objective 2: Propose a staged, gesture-conditioned retargeting framework specifically tailored for teleoperating the proposed three-finger gripper.**
 - **Motivation:** Non-anthropomorphic multi-finger grippers, while offering a balance between simplicity and capability, suffer from a significant "embodiment gap" compared to human hands. Their distinct kinematic structures, joint ordering, and base frame definitions (e.g., three fingers arranged around a central palm rather than a human wrist) make direct human-to-robot mapping challenging. Existing teleoperation methods often focus on simple parallel grippers or complex anthropomorphic hands, leaving a gap for intermediate designs. Developing a specialized retargeting framework is motivated by the need to bridge this kinematic and geometric mismatch, ensuring stable and accurate teleoperation. This framework would also serve as an efficient means for collecting high-quality contact-rich data, which is crucial for training learning-based dexterous manipulation policies.

- **Objective 3: Conduct extensive experimental validation across a range of challenging tasks to demonstrate the performance advantages of the integrated system.**
 - **Motivation:** The theoretical and design merits of the VTAP gripper and its retargeting framework require empirical validation. Demonstrating the system's capabilities across diverse tasks—from reactive grasping of various objects (including fragile ones) to complex in-hand manipulations like reorientation, singulation, and precise assembly (e.g., peg-in-hole)—is crucial to verify its functionality and potential impact. This objective aims to show that high manipulation performance can be achieved through coordinated finger-palm interaction and multi-modal sensing, providing an alternative to complex, high-DOF anthropomorphic designs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the design of the VTAP gripper, its multi-modal perception system, and a specialized retargeting framework for teleoperation.

#### 3.1. A. Gripper Design

The VTAP gripper integrates an actuated palm module with three compliant, reconfigurable fingers.
- **Overall Structure:** The system features three fully actuated and kinematically identical fingers, plus a 1-DOF active palm, totaling 13 degrees of freedom.
- **Fingers:** Each finger is constructed from 3D-printed thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) with a Fin-Ray structure, providing passive compliance to adapt to object geometries. Each finger is driven by two servo motors:
 - A Dynamixel 2XC430-W250-T controls radial/ulnar deviation (q1) within ±60° and metacarpopha-langeal (MCP) flexion-extension (q2) from -25° to +90°.
 - A Dynamixel 2XL430-W250-T enables abduction/adduction (q3) within ±55° and proximal interphalangeal (PIP) flexion-extension (q4) from -60° to +90°.
- **Actuated Palm Module:** A linear actuator (Actuonix L8-P-50) is mounted within the gripper's base frame, providing a 50 mm vertical stroke to the palm. The palm itself integrates a vision-tactile bi-modal sensor.
- **Kinematic Modeling and Workspace:** A kinematic model of a single finger is derived as a rigid serial chain with four revolute joints. The reachable workspace of the gripper is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling, demonstrating a broad spatial coverage (approx. 469 mm along X, 477 mm along Y, and 311 mm along Z).

#### 3.2. B. Multi-Modal Perception

The gripper incorporates both fingertip and palm-based sensing modalities.
- **Fingertip Tactile Arrays:** Thin piezoresistive FlexiTac sensors are integrated into the Fin-Ray fingertips. These sensors offer a spatial resolution of 32 × 12 taxels over an active area of 66 × 25 mm², providing an effective resolution of approximately 2 × 2 mm² per sensing unit, by transducing pressure into resistance changes.
- **Visuo-Tactile Bi-modal Palm:** A compact USB camera (GC0307) with a 50° field of view is housed within a 50 mm cylindrical module. This module features a detachable sensing cartridge with a 40 mm diameter sensing area. A ring of multi-color LEDs (red, green, blue, white) provides internal illumination.
 - **Mechanism:** The transparent silicone outer layer of the sensing cartridge is coated with mirror-effect paint and covered by a thin protective silicone layer. When LEDs are off, the module is transparent, functioning as a conventional vision sensor for global scene perception. When LEDs are activated, the illumination is reflected toward the deformable surface, transforming the module into an optical tactile sensor that captures surface deformation. This design allows seamless, mechanical-reconfiguration-free switching between vision and tactile modes.

#### 3.3. C. Retargeting for Three-Finger Gripper

To enable dexterous teleoperation despite the non-anthropomorphic design, a staged control framework is developed.
- **Gesture-Conditioned Subspace Retargeting:**
 - **Addressing Structural Mismatch:** The framework utilizes gesture recognition (e.g., left-hand palm open-close gestures via VR) to select predefined grasp priors (cage, power, pinch). These priors constrain the radial-ulnar deviation (q1) of each finger, reducing the search space.
 - **Kinematic Ambiguity:** To avoid ambiguity, the adduction/abduction DOF (q3) is initially fixed, simplifying the kinematic search.
 - **Base Frame Alignment:** An intermediate coordinate frame (I) is introduced, derived from the VR-captured human wrist coordinate frame (W) via a constant SE(3) transformation, to mitigate discrepancies arising from different base frame definitions.
 - **Optimization-Based Retargeting:** For the remaining MCP (q2) and PIP (q4) DOFs of the thumb, index, and middle fingers, an optimization problem is solved at each timestep. The objective function (L) minimizes a weighted sum of:
 - **Fingertip position loss (L_pos):** Euclidean distance between robot fingertip positions (relative to gripper base O) and human fingertip positions (relative to intermediate base I).
 - **Fingertip orientation loss (L_rot):** Euclidean distance between robot fingertip orientation vectors (from contact plane center) and human PIP-to-fingertip vectors.
 - **Smoothness regularization (L_vel):** Minimizes changes in joint velocities.
 - A Huber-type robust penalty function is applied for numerical stability. The optimization is subject to joint limits.
- **Singulation Retargeting:** A specialized strategy is designed for in-hand singulation tasks:
 - **Mode Transition:** In the gesture-conditioned pinch mode, the system monitors the angle between the human thumbtip-MCP and index fingertip-PIP vectors, and the Euclidean distance between these fingertips. When these cues indicate a suitable state (vectors approaching parallel, distance below a threshold), the controller transitions to singulation mode.
 - **Geometric Mapping:** In singulation mode, human fingertip distance is linearly mapped to equal-magnitude but opposite-direction actuation of the two q3 joints (adduction/abduction) of the mapped robot fingers. The relative angle determines the sign of this motion. Additionally, MCP joint (q2) flexion is regulated via a linear mapping of the thumb-middle distance to allow fine adjustment along the flexion direction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VTAP gripper and its retargeting framework were subjected to a series of experiments evaluating grasping robustness, in-hand manipulation dexterity, and the functionality of the bi-modal palm.

#### 4.1. A. Tactile-Reactive Grasping and Manipulation

- **Grasping Performance:** The gripper was evaluated on five YCB objects and four everyday objects across three grasp configurations (cage, power, pinch). A tactile-reactive controller, using a threshold-based stopping criterion (Equation 7) based on contact area and signal intensity from fingertip tactile sensors, enabled adaptive grasping. The palm camera initially provided visual object orientation for pose alignment.
 - **Success Rate:** A high overall grasping success rate of 93.3% was achieved across 90 trials (10 trials per object). Table I indicates specific success rates, with the drill having the lowest (5/10) due to its weight and asymmetric mass distribution causing occasional slip.
 - **Fragile Object Handling:** Fingertip tactile sensors facilitated damage-free grasping of fragile objects.
 - **Large/Wide Objects:** The power mode and actuated palm supported stable handling of objects like a hammer.
- **In-Hand Object Reorientation:** The gripper demonstrated multi-axis in-hand re-orientation capabilities by manipulating a 40 mm diameter sphere. It achieved rotations of approximately ±15° about the x- and y-axes and ±20° about the z-axis through coordinated finger motions.

#### 4.2. B. Syringe Re-orientation and Plunger Actuation

This task validated the retargeting framework for a multi-step, laboratory-inspired manipulation.
- **Task Sequence:** The gripper performed a two-finger pinch grasp on a syringe (radial-ulnar DOF configured for pinch), using tactile arrays for contact feedback. Coordinated middle finger contraction reoriented the syringe downward. Finally, the actuated palm (commanded by little finger contraction via teleoperation) depressed the plunger.
- **Performance:** Over 20 teleoperation trials, the task achieved a success rate of 65% (13/20), with an average completion time of 33.4 ± 5.43 seconds.
- **Observed Issues:** Most failures were attributed to VR hand-tracking occlusion, where the headset's position for tracking little-finger motion inadvertently caused the thumb or index finger to lose tracking, leading to unintended grasp release.

#### 4.3. C. In-Hand Singulation Manipulation

This experiment demonstrated the gripper's ability to isolate a single object from a cluster using precise fingertip control.
- **Manipulation Process:** The gripper first grasped multiple objects. Then, coordinated thumb-index rolling motions from the human demonstrator were mapped to the gripper's adduction/abduction DOFs (q3) for controlled in-finger manipulation and singulation. Multi-modal feedback from tactile arrays (showing contact patterns) and the bi-modal palm (providing visual feedback on grasp state) supported the task.
- **Generality:** The task was successfully demonstrated on five diverse objects: mung beans, soft gummy bear candies, irregular chocolate spheres, 7 mm diameter steel balls, and cotton swabs, showcasing adaptability to varying size, shape, and stiffness.
- **Comparative Advantages:** The framework eliminated the need for teleoperated arm or external motion capture systems, manipulating objects as small as 3 mm in diameter. The fingertip tactile array provided an approximately 215% larger sensing area compared to compact vision-based tactile sensors like GelSight Mini, facilitating interaction with a broader range of object geometries.

#### 4.4. D. Multi-Modal Peg-in-hole

This fully autonomous task showcased the utility of the bi-modal actuated palm in an industrial-like precision assembly scenario.
- **Task Sequence:**
 1. The palm operated in vision mode to estimate the initial pose of a hexagonal prism (with an ArUco marker for coarse estimation) and guide the initial grasp.
 2. Upon contact, the palm switched to tactile mode (activating internal illumination) and pressed against the prism surface to initiate tactile-based hole localization.
 3. The end-effector moved downward while maintaining contact, using gradient-based edge extraction, contour filtering, and algebraic circle fitting on tactile images to accurately identify the center of a 15 mm diameter hole. Outlier rejection further refined the estimated circle parameters.
 4. The robotic arm then adjusted its pose based on the estimated hole center and successfully executed insertion onto a 14 mm peg, achieving a tolerance of 1 mm.
- **Performance:** The task achieved an overall success rate of 70% over 10 consecutive trials, demonstrating the precision and robustness of the bi-modal palm for localized perception and pose correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VTAP gripper and its associated retargeting framework present several significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, with implications for both hardware design and control strategies.

- **Reference Architecture for Dexterous Grippers:** The work establishes a practical reference architecture for designing dexterous grippers, particularly for non-anthropomorphic multi-finger systems. By demonstrating how structured finger-palm synergy and multi-modal perception can enable robust grasping and fine manipulation, it offers a design paradigm that balances complexity and capability.
- **Achieving Dexterity Without High-DOF Anthropomorphism:** A key finding is that high manipulation performance and dexterity do not necessarily require complex, high-degree-of-freedom anthropomorphic hands. The VTAP gripper demonstrates that comparable dexterity can be achieved through coordinated interaction between fingers and an active palm, leveraging intelligent sensing and control rather than simply increasing mechanical DOFs. This offers a more accessible and potentially robust alternative to fully anthropomorphic designs.
- **Enhanced Multi-Modal Perception:** The actuated bi-modal palm, seamlessly integrating vision and touch, provides complementary feedback crucial for tasks demanding comprehensive environmental understanding. Its ability to switch between long-range visual perception (for scene understanding and initial pose estimation) and contact-rich tactile feedback (for precise in-hand manipulation and object localization) mitigates occlusion issues and enhances perceptual robustness. This integrated sensing capability is valuable for complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Facilitating Scalable Data Collection for Learning-Based Approaches:** The proposed staged, gesture-conditioned retargeting framework addresses the embodiment gap inherent in controlling non-anthropomorphic grippers with human input. By enabling stable and accurate teleoperation, it offers an efficient method for collecting diverse, contact-rich demonstration data. This capability is critical for training and validating learning-based dexterous manipulation policies, accelerating progress in robot learning research, particularly in confined manipulation spaces where external cameras are prone to occlusion.
- **Practical Application in Diverse Tasks:** The validation across tasks like reactive grasping of fragile objects, syringe reorientation and plunger actuation, fine in-hand singulation of small items, and precise peg-in-hole insertion, highlights the system's versatility and potential for application in various real-world scenarios, from laboratory settings to industrial assembly.

The research suggests a pathway for future work, including the investigation of integrated finger-palm feedback control to further enhance contact-rich manipulation. The retargeting strategy is also positioned as a foundation for scalable data collection, underscoring its potential to support advancements in learning-based robotics.
