# An Empirical Study of Training Self-Supervised Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This empirical study from Facebook AI Research establishes robust training recipes for self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViT), identifying pervasive training instability as a major challenge and proposing a 'fixed random patch projection' trick for mitigation. The work demonstrates that vision-centric self-supervised methods, particularly MoCo v3, enable ViT models to scale effectively and achieve new state-of-the-art performance in visual representation learning.

## Problem

- Training recipes for Vision Transformers (ViT), especially in self-supervised settings, were immature and not well-understood compared to established CNN practices.
- Self-supervised ViT training often suffered from instability, leading to subtle but persistent accuracy degradations that were difficult to detect.
- A clear empirical understanding of how Vision Transformers perform under successful vision-specific self-supervised learning paradigms (like contrastive learning) was lacking, with initial efforts often mimicking NLP-inspired masked auto-encoding.

## Method

- The authors introduced ==MoCo v3==, an incremental evolution of the MoCo framework, simplifying it by leveraging large batch sizes and incorporating elements from other successful self-supervised methods.
- They conducted extensive empirical studies and systematic ablations of fundamental training components (batch size, learning rate, optimizer) to identify sources of instability in self-supervised ViT.
- A simple but effective =='fixed random patch projection' trick== was proposed to mitigate ==training instability== by freezing the initial patch projection layer, based on observations of gradient spikes.

## Results

- MoCo v3 consistently outperformed other self-supervised frameworks (SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV) when applied to ViT architectures, achieving **76.5%** linear probing accuracy for ViT-B/16.
- The 'fixed random patch projection' trick significantly improved stability and boosted linear probing accuracy by up to **1.7%** in MoCo v3 with ViT-B/16.
- Self-supervised ViT-H/14 achieved **78.1%** linear probing accuracy, scaling better than supervised ViTs and eventually surpassing state-of-the-art self-supervised ResNets (e.g., achieving **81.0%** with architectural modifications).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised Vision Transformer training is prone to subtle training instabilities, manifesting as accuracy dips and leading to partial performance degradation.
> - The initial patch projection layer is a key source of training instability; freezing this layer (using fixed random projections) significantly improves stability and accuracy across various self-supervised frameworks.
> - Self-supervised pre-training enables larger Vision Transformer models to scale effectively and avoid overfitting, unlike supervised ViTs which often show diminishing returns or degradation with increased size on ImageNet-1k.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is strategically positioned at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in deep learning:
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) in Computer Vision:** Over recent years, SSL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for pre-training visual representation models, aiming to reduce reliance on large, manually labeled datasets. Approaches like contrastive learning (SimCLR, MoCo) and non-contrastive methods (BYOL, SwAV) have achieved impressive results, often outperforming supervised pre-training on downstream tasks.
- **Vision Transformers (ViT):** Transformers, initially dominant in Natural Language Processing (NLP), were successfully adapted to computer vision with the introduction of Vision Transformers by Dosovitskiy et al.. ViT demonstrated that pure Transformer architectures, without convolutional inductive biases, could achieve state-of-the-art performance on image recognition tasks, especially with large datasets. This challenged the long-held supremacy of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in vision.
- **Bridging the NLP-Vision Pre-training Gap:** The paper explicitly highlights a methodological divergence: NLP primarily uses masked auto-encoding with Transformers (e.g., BERT, GPT), while vision largely relies on Siamese networks (e.g., MoCo) with CNN backbones (ResNets). Early attempts to self-supervise ViTs often mimicked NLP's masked auto-encoding. This paper, however, investigates the efficacy of vision's successful Siamese/contrastive paradigm for ViT, aiming to "complete the big picture of self-supervised learning in vision" and work "towards closing the gap of pre-training methodology between vision and language."

In this landscape, the paper serves as a crucial bridge. It takes the highly successful Transformer architecture from NLP, which has recently made inroads into vision (ViT), and subjects it to the rigorous testing of the most successful self-supervised learning paradigms *native to computer vision* (contrastive/Siamese methods). By meticulously dissecting the training dynamics and identifying key challenges (like instability), this work aims to establish robust, "must-know" baselines and training recipes for self-supervised ViT, which were previously underdeveloped compared to their CNN counterparts. It moves beyond simply applying NLP-inspired methods to ViT, instead exploring how established vision SSL techniques interact with this new, powerful architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to **empirically study and establish robust training recipes for self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViT)**, given the recent advancements in both ViT architectures and self-supervised learning techniques in computer vision.

The key motivations driving this research are:
1. **Address the Immaturity of ViT Training Recipes:** While training practices for standard convolutional networks are mature and well-understood, the optimal recipes for ViT, especially in the more challenging self-supervised settings, are still nascent. The authors explicitly state, "the recipes for ViT are yet to be built."
2. **Investigate Fundamental Training Components:** The paper aims to go "back to basics" by systematically investigating the effects of fundamental components such as batch size, learning rate, and optimizer on self-supervised ViT training.
3. **Identify and Mitigate Training Instability:** A crucial motivation is the observed instability during self-supervised ViT training. The authors found that this instability, unlike catastrophic divergence, often results in "partial failure" with subtle accuracy degradation (1-3%) that can be easily hidden. Revealing and addressing this "hidden degradation" is a significant driving factor.
4. **Benchmark ViT in Leading Self-Supervised Frameworks:** To provide a comprehensive understanding, the study benchmarks ViT models within MoCo v3 and other prominent self-supervised frameworks (SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV) to compare their efficacy.
5. **Explore Architectural Scaling and Ablations:** The authors are motivated to understand how ViT models of varying scales (Small, Base, Large, Huge) perform under self-supervised pre-training and to ablate key architectural choices (e.g., position embedding, class token) to understand their impact.
6. **Provide Useful Data Points and Experience:** Ultimately, the work aims to contribute a valuable empirical dataset and practical experience to the community, guiding future research in self-supervised Transformers towards bridging the methodological gap between vision and language pre-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a rigorous empirical approach, focusing on systematic ablations and comprehensive benchmarking rather than proposing a novel self-supervised learning algorithm.

1. **Framework Foundation: MoCo v3:** The study introduces "MoCo v3," an incremental evolution of the MoCo (Momentum Contrast) framework. MoCo v3 simplifies previous versions by abandoning the memory queue for negative samples, instead leveraging large batch sizes to provide negative keys. It incorporates elements from other successful SSL methods, such as an extra prediction head (inspired by BYOL) and a symmetrized loss, aiming for a better balance of simplicity, accuracy, and scalability. This framework serves as the primary testbed for ViT models.
2. **ViT Backbone Integration:** Vision Transformers (ViT-S, ViT-B, ViT-L, ViT-H) from Dosovitskiy et al. are used as backbones, closely following their original designs (e.g., patch size, LayerNorm).
3. **Investigating Training Stability:** This is a central part of the methodology.
 - **Monitoring:** The authors use densely sampled kNN accuracy curves (an approximate kNN monitor due to computational costs) during training to detect subtle "dips" and instabilities that traditional sparse monitoring might miss.
 - **Ablation of Basic Factors:** They systematically vary batch sizes (from 1K to 6K), learning rates, and optimizers (AdamW vs. LAMB) to observe their impact on stability and final accuracy.
 - **Proposed Stability Trick:** Based on an empirical observation that gradient spikes tend to occur earlier in the first layer (patch projection), they introduce a simple but effective trick: freezing the patch projection layer, effectively using fixed random projections instead of learning them. This is applied as a "stop-gradient" operation.
4. **Benchmarking Across SSL Frameworks:** Beyond MoCo v3, ViT models are also benchmarked in SimCLR, BYOL, and SwAV, ensuring a broad comparison of self-supervised paradigms. The fixed random patch projection trick is applied across all frameworks for fair comparison where applicable.
5. **Architectural Ablations:** Key ViT design choices are ablated within the MoCo v3 framework, including:
 - Type of position embedding (sine-cosine, learned, or none).
 - Role and necessity of the class token.
 - Presence of BatchNorm in the MLP heads.
 - Impact of the prediction head and momentum encoder.
6. **Scaling Experiments:** Larger ViT models (ViT-L, ViT-H) are trained to assess the scalability of self-supervised ViT and compare its performance against smaller models and state-of-the-art convolutional networks.
7. **Evaluation Protocols:**
 - **Linear Probing:** The primary evaluation metric, where a linear classifier is trained on frozen pre-trained features on ImageNet, is used to assess representation quality.
 - **End-to-End Fine-tuning:** Models are also fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k and four downstream transfer learning datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Oxford Flowers-102, Oxford-IIIT-Pets) to evaluate their transferability and performance in practical applications.
8. **Computational Resources:** Training is performed on Google Cloud Platform's TPU v3 infrastructure, enabling large-scale experiments with ViT-L and ViT-H models, which would be prohibitively slow on standard GPU setups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical study yielded several critical findings, revealing both strengths and challenges in training self-supervised Vision Transformers:

1. **Pervasive Training Instability in Self-Supervised ViT:** A major discovery is that self-supervised ViT training is highly prone to instability. Unlike catastrophic divergence, this instability often manifests as "dips" in the kNN accuracy curves during training, leading to a subtle but significant degradation in final linear probing accuracy (typically 1-3%). This "partial failure" is difficult to notice without careful monitoring. Factors like very large batch sizes and larger learning rates exacerbate this instability.

2. **Effective "Fixed Random Patch Projection" Trick:** Freezing the patch projection layer (using fixed random projections) significantly improves training stability and consistently boosts accuracy across MoCo v3, SimCLR, and BYOL. For instance, in MoCo v3 with ViT-B, it improved accuracy by up to 1.7% at higher learning rates. This suggests that the optimization landscape of the initial projection layer is particularly tricky and that strong initial inductive biases are not strictly necessary for this layer.

3. **MoCo v3 as a Strong Framework for ViT:** MoCo v3 consistently outperforms other major self-supervised frameworks (SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV) when applied to ViT architectures. For ViT-B/16, MoCo v3 achieved 76.5% linear probing accuracy, compared to 73.9% for SimCLR and BYOL. The relative performance of SSL frameworks was also observed to differ between ViT and ResNet backbones.

4. **Architectural Ablations Reveal Key Insights:**
 - **Position Embedding:** Surprisingly, removing position embeddings only leads to a small accuracy drop (1.6% for ViT-B/16, from 76.5% to 74.9%). This indicates that self-supervised ViT can learn strong representations that are largely permutation-invariant, but also implies that the model might not be fully exploiting positional information.
 - **Class Token:** The class token, typically used for classification, is not essential. Global average pooling can yield comparable results (e.g., 76.3% vs. 76.5%) if the final LayerNorm is also removed.
 - **BatchNorm in MLP Heads:** BatchNorm in the projection and prediction MLP heads is crucial for optimal performance, contributing a 2.1% accuracy boost, though the system can function without it (albeit with lower accuracy).
 - **Prediction Head and Momentum Encoder:** Both elements, inherited from other SSL advancements, demonstrably improve accuracy, adding 1% and 2.2% respectively.

5. **Self-Supervised ViT Scales Better than Supervised ViT:** Unlike ImageNet-supervised ViTs, which showed diminishing or even degrading returns with larger models (ViT-L, ViT-H) on ImageNet-1k, self-supervised ViTs with MoCo v3 consistently achieved higher accuracy with increased model size (e.g., ViT-H/14 reaching 78.1% in linear probing). This suggests that self-supervised pre-training is less prone to overfitting and better unlocks the capacity of very large ViT models.

6. **Competitive with State-of-the-Art ResNets and Masked Auto-Encoders:**
 - MoCo v3-based ViTs (e.g., ViT-B: 76.7%, ViT-L: 77.6%, ViT-H: 78.1%) significantly outperform prior self-supervised Transformer approaches based on masked auto-encoding (e.g., iGPT-XL: 72.0%).
 - With architectural modifications like replacing LayerNorm with BatchNorm in MLP blocks ("ViT-BN") and reducing patch size to 7x7 ("ViT-BN-L/7"), MoCo v3 achieves 81.0% linear probing accuracy, surpassing the previous bests from big ResNets (e.g., SimCLR v2 SK-ResNet152-3× at 79.8%). This demonstrates ViT's ability to learn strong representations with "fewer inductive biases".

7. **Strong Performance in End-to-End Fine-tuning and Transfer Learning:** MoCo v3 pre-training leads to robust end-to-end fine-tuning results on ImageNet-1k (e.g., 83.2% for ViT-B) and achieves better transfer learning accuracy on various downstream datasets compared to ImageNet-supervised ViTs, especially for larger models. It effectively mitigates overfitting issues that supervised ViT faces on smaller datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This empirical study holds significant implications for the future of computer vision research, particularly in the realm of self-supervised learning and Vision Transformers:

1. **Foundational Baseline for Self-Supervised ViT:** The paper establishes a critical and rigorously tested baseline for training self-supervised Vision Transformers. By meticulously studying fundamental training components and offering practical insights, it provides a "must-know" recipe that the community can reliably build upon, saving substantial research effort.

2. **Addressing a Hidden Challenge: Training Instability:** The identification and demonstration of training instability as a major, yet subtle, issue in self-supervised ViT is a crucial contribution. The proposed "fixed random patch projection" trick offers an immediate and effective mitigation strategy, enabling more stable and higher-performing models, thus preventing researchers from unknowingly working with sub-optimally trained ViTs. This finding has broader implications, as evidenced by the postscript noting similar instability in supervised BiT-ResNet.

3. **Validation of Contrastive Learning for ViT:** The finding that vision-centric self-supervised paradigms, specifically contrastive learning (MoCo v3), are highly effective for ViT, and even outperform NLP-inspired masked auto-encoding approaches, is pivotal. This suggests that the optimal self-supervised pre-training strategy for Transformers in vision may indeed differ from that in language, providing a clearer direction for future research in visual representation learning.

4. **Unlocking Scalability and Reducing Overfitting:** Demonstrating that self-supervised pre-training allows larger ViT models to scale effectively and achieve better accuracy (unlike supervised ViT which can overfit) is a powerful validation of SSL. This opens avenues for developing even larger and more powerful self-supervised Vision Transformers, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in vision with reduced reliance on massive labeled datasets.

5. **Bridging the Vision-Language Pre-training Gap:** By thoroughly investigating how successful vision-specific SSL methods interact with Transformer architectures, the work directly contributes to closing the methodological gap between vision and language pre-training. It suggests that while the architectures (Transformers) may converge, the optimal pretext tasks might retain domain-specific characteristics.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper not only provides solutions but also highlights remaining challenges and open questions, such as better understanding and fundamentally solving the instability issue, more effectively exploiting positional information in ViT, and scaling self-supervised pre-training to even larger datasets. These explicitly stated directions will guide future endeavors in the field.

In essence, this work provides a comprehensive empirical roadmap for self-supervised Vision Transformers, significantly advancing their understanding, stability, and performance. Its impact will likely be seen in the widespread adoption of its insights and baselines, accelerating progress in generalized visual representation learning.
