# An Empirical Study of Training Self-Supervised Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This empirical study from Facebook AI Research establishes robust training recipes for self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViT), identifying pervasive training instability as a major challenge and proposing a 'fixed random patch projection' trick for mitigation. The work demonstrates that vision-centric self-supervised methods, particularly MoCo v3, enable ViT models to scale effectively and achieve new state-of-the-art performance in visual representation learning.

## Problem

- Training recipes for Vision Transformers (ViT), especially in self-supervised settings, were immature and not well-understood compared to established CNN practices.
- Self-supervised ViT training often suffered from instability, leading to subtle but persistent accuracy degradations that were difficult to detect.
- A clear empirical understanding of how Vision Transformers perform under successful vision-specific self-supervised learning paradigms (like contrastive learning) was lacking, with initial efforts often mimicking NLP-inspired masked auto-encoding.

## Method

- The authors introduced ==MoCo v3==, an incremental evolution of the MoCo framework, simplifying it by leveraging large batch sizes and incorporating elements from other successful self-supervised methods.
- They conducted extensive empirical studies and systematic ablations of fundamental training components (batch size, learning rate, optimizer) to identify sources of instability in self-supervised ViT.
- A simple but effective =='fixed random patch projection' trick== was proposed to mitigate ==training instability== by freezing the initial patch projection layer, based on observations of gradient spikes.

## Results

- MoCo v3 consistently outperformed other self-supervised frameworks (SimCLR, BYOL, SwAV) when applied to ViT architectures, achieving **76.5%** linear probing accuracy for ViT-B/16.
- The 'fixed random patch projection' trick significantly improved stability and boosted linear probing accuracy by up to **1.7%** in MoCo v3 with ViT-B/16.
- Self-supervised ViT-H/14 achieved **78.1%** linear probing accuracy, scaling better than supervised ViTs and eventually surpassing state-of-the-art self-supervised ResNets (e.g., achieving **81.0%** with architectural modifications).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised Vision Transformer training is prone to subtle training instabilities, manifesting as accuracy dips and leading to partial performance degradation.
> - The initial patch projection layer is a key source of training instability; freezing this layer (using fixed random projections) significantly improves stability and accuracy across various self-supervised frameworks.
> - Self-supervised pre-training enables larger Vision Transformer models to scale effectively and avoid overfitting, unlike supervised ViTs which often show diminishing returns or degradation with increased size on ImageNet-1k.
