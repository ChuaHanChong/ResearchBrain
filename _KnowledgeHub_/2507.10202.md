# A Training-Free, Task-Agnostic Framework for Enhancing MLLM Performance on High-Resolution Images

> [!summary] Summary
> The Extract Candidate then Predict (ECP) framework enables existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to effectively process high-resolution images (e.g., 4K, 8K) by employing a training-free, two-stage coarse-to-fine approach. It demonstrates substantial accuracy improvements on tasks such as 4K GUI grounding and 4K/8K MLLM perception, making MLLMs viable for scenarios requiring fine-grained visual detail.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) perform poorly on tasks requiring fine-grained localization and reasoning within high-resolution images (e.g., 4K, 8K).
- This limitation stems from a train-test resolution discrepancy, where downsampling high-resolution images for MLLM input results in a significant loss of crucial fine-grained visual details.
- Prior approaches either demand computationally expensive re-training with specialized high-resolution datasets or are limited by manually crafted heuristics specific to particular tasks, lacking generalizability.

## Method

- The ==Extract Candidate then Predict (ECP)== framework is a ==training-free, two-stage approach== that utilizes existing pre-trained MLLMs.
- In the =='Extract Candidate' (EC) stage==, a downsampled version of the high-resolution image is fed to an MLLM to obtain a coarse localization cue (point or bounding box), from which a high-resolution candidate region is extracted.
- In the =='Predict' (P) stage==, the original high-resolution image is cropped to this candidate region, which is then provided to an MLLM (along with the instruction and optionally the downsampled full image) to generate the final, precise prediction.

## Results

- On the 4K GUI grounding ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, ECP improved OS-Atlas-7B's accuracy from **19.1%** to **40.4%**, an absolute increase of **21.3%**.
- For 4K and 8K MLLM perception on the HR-Bench, ECP boosted Qwen2-VL-7B's overall accuracy by **5.8%** on 4K (to **68.3%**) and **5.2%** on 8K (to **60.3%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that leveraging the MLLM's instruction-guided coarse localization in the first stage is critical, with random sampling for candidate region selection significantly degrading performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), even when processing downsampled images, possess an implicit coarse localization ability that can be effectively leveraged to identify instruction-relevant regions.
> - A training-free, two-stage coarse-to-fine processing strategy can successfully overcome the resolution discrepancy faced by MLLMs without requiring additional training, fine-tuning, or architectural modifications.
> - Developing task-agnostic frameworks allows for broad applicability across diverse vision-language tasks involving high-resolution imagery, enhancing practical utility and reducing deployment barriers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has witnessed a revolutionary transformation with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, subsequently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). MLLMs, which integrate vision and language processing, have demonstrated impressive capabilities in understanding, reasoning, and generation across a wide array of tasks, from image captioning to visual question answering. Many MLLM-powered applications are now emerging, promising to enhance human efficiency and transform industries.

Despite their remarkable successes, a critical limitation of current MLLMs lies in their struggle with tasks requiring fine-grained localization and reasoning within **high-resolution images**. This constraint fundamentally stems from the training paradigm of MLLMs. Most MLLMs are fine-tuned with a fixed, relatively low image resolution to align with the pre-trained image encoders they employ. Consequently, there are two primary issues when encountering high-resolution inputs:
1. **Train-Test Resolution Discrepancy:** Directly feeding high-resolution images into an MLLM, which was trained on lower resolutions, leads to poor generalization and degraded performance. The model is not accustomed to processing such detailed inputs.
2. **Loss of Fine-Grained Details:** Downsampling high-resolution images to match the MLLM's input requirements, while ensuring consistency, inevitably results in a significant loss of crucial visual information. This compromises the model's ability to discern small objects, intricate details, or subtle spatial relationships, which are often vital for precise tasks.

Prior research has attempted to address this "resolution curse" through two main avenues:
- **Specialized Modules and Re-training:** One approach involves designing and training specialized modules or architectures that can handle high-resolution inputs. Examples include LLaVA-UHD, LLaVA-HR, and INF-LLaVA, which introduce adaptive slicing, dual visual pathways, or dual-perspective cropping to process detailed images. While these methods show promise, they are inherently inefficient. They necessitate manual module design, extensive dataset construction (especially for high-resolution data), and significant computational resources for re-training or fine-tuning the entire model or new components. This limits their scalability and practical deployment.
- **Training-Free, Task-Specific Two-Stage Frameworks:** Another direction focuses on breaking down the problem into stages without requiring additional training. This often involves dividing high-resolution images into patches and then aggregating per-patch predictions, using methods like score averaging or tree-like structures. Some works, like iterative prompting, have explored coarse-to-fine prediction for specific tasks like GUI grounding. However, these methods are often limited by their reliance on *manually crafted heuristics tailored to specific tasks*. This task-specificity severely limits their generalizability, meaning a solution developed for one type of high-resolution task might not be easily applicable to another without significant redesign.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the absence of a **training-free, task-agnostic framework** that can effectively enhance MLLM performance on high-resolution images without the need for additional training or task-specific manual designs. The proposed ECP framework directly addresses this void, aiming to provide a broadly applicable and efficient solution that leverages the inherent capabilities of existing MLLMs. By focusing on a "divide and conquer" strategy that is generalizable, this work seeks to push the boundaries of MLLM applicability to real-world scenarios involving rich visual data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems directly from the aforementioned limitations of current MLLMs when confronted with high-resolution images. While MLLMs demonstrate remarkable understanding and reasoning on standard-resolution inputs, their performance severely degrades on high-resolution tasks requiring fine-grained localization and intricate detail comprehension. This degradation is attributed to the inherent training paradigm where MLLMs are fine-tuned with fixed, typically lower, image resolutions, leading to a mismatch when high-resolution inputs are provided. Downsampling, while maintaining resolution consistency, sacrifices critical visual fidelity.

The **key objective** of this work is to develop a novel, efficient, and broadly applicable solution to this challenge. Specifically, the authors aim to propose "Extract Candidate then Predict (ECP)," a framework designed to:

1. **Enhance MLLM Performance:** Substantially improve the accuracy and capability of existing MLLMs on tasks involving high-resolution images (e.g., 4K, 8K resolution).
2. **Maintain Fine-Grained Details:** Address the problem of information loss due to downsampling by allowing the MLLM to access and process high-resolution details when needed.
3. **Be Training-Free:** Eliminate the need for re-training or fine-tuning the base MLLM, or training new specialized modules, thereby reducing computational cost and deployment complexity. This is crucial for practical adoption and allows the framework to be immediately compatible with new MLLM architectures as they emerge.
4. **Be Task-Agnostic:** Provide a generalized approach that is not heuristically designed for a specific task (e.g., GUI grounding) but can be applied across diverse vision-language tasks without significant modifications. This is a crucial differentiator from prior training-free methods.

The core intuition motivating ECP is a critical empirical observation: even though MLLMs struggle with *precise* localization and reasoning on *high-resolution* images, their predictions on *downsampled* versions of these images still contain **implicit localization cues**. This suggests that MLLMs, despite losing fine-grained details during downsampling, retain a "coarse localization ability" – an approximate understanding of where the instruction-relevant information might reside. By leveraging this coarse understanding, the ECP framework aims to intelligently guide the MLLM's focus, allowing it to then re-examine specific high-resolution regions for accurate, detailed predictions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Extract Candidate then Predict (ECP)** framework is a novel two-stage approach designed to enhance MLLM performance on high-resolution images without requiring additional training or task-specific designs. Its core philosophy is a coarse-to-fine processing strategy, leveraging the MLLM's inherent, albeit coarse, localization abilities.

**Stage 1: Extract Candidate (EC)**

The first stage, **Extract Candidate (EC)**, is responsible for identifying an instruction-relevant region within the high-resolution image. The underlying assumption here is that while MLLMs may fail at precise localization on a downsampled image, they can still broadly understand *where* to focus.

1. **Input Processing:** A high-resolution RGB image (width W, height H) is first **downsampled** to a resolution that the pre-trained MLLM (denoted as F_EC) can readily process. This downsampled image, along with the user's text instruction, is fed into F_EC.
2. **Coarse Prediction:** F_EC, a standard pre-trained MLLM (e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B, OS-Atlas-7B), is prompted to output a coarse localization cue. This cue can be either a single point (O_point = (x, y)) or a bounding box (O_bbox = (x1, y1, x2, y2)).
3. **Representative Coordinate Calculation:** A "representative coordinate" (O_rep = (x_rep, y_rep)) is determined. If F_EC outputs a point, that point is used directly. If F_EC outputs a bounding box, its center coordinate is computed as O_rep. This step centralizes the coarse localization.
4. **Candidate Region Definition:** Based on O_rep, a fixed-size square bounding box (B_candidate = (x_left, y_top, x_right, y_bottom)) is defined. This box is centered around O_rep, with predefined dimensions (w, h), where w and h are smaller than the full image dimensions (e.g., 1024x1024 pixels in their experiments). To ensure the candidate region remains within the image boundaries, the coordinates are adjusted using `max(0, min(coord, Max_Dim - Size))`. This creates a smaller, high-resolution patch that is likely to contain the relevant information.

**Stage 2: Predict (P)**

The second stage, **Predict (P)**, generates the final, precise prediction by focusing on the high-resolution details within the candidate region identified in the first stage.

1. **High-Resolution Cropping:** The original high-resolution image is cropped to extract the patch corresponding to B_candidate. This cropped patch now contains the fine-grained visual details necessary for accurate predictions.
2. **Final Prediction:** This cropped, high-resolution patch, along with the original user instruction, is fed into another pre-trained MLLM (which can be the same MLLM used in EC, or a different one).
3. **Contextual Augmentation (Optional but Used):** For tasks where global context might still be important in addition to fine-grained details (e.g., MLLM perception tasks), the downsampled full image (from Stage 1) can also be provided to the MLLM in Stage 2, alongside the cropped patch and the instruction. This ensures that while fine details are captured, the model doesn't lose sight of the broader scene.

**Key Characteristics of ECP:**

- **Training-Free:** The framework exclusively uses existing, pre-trained MLLMs in both stages. No additional training, fine-tuning, or specialized datasets are required for ECP itself to function.
- **Task-Agnostic:** The design principles of EC (identifying a relevant region) and P (focusing on that region) are general enough to be applied across different vision-language tasks (e.g., grounding, perception, visual question answering) without task-specific heuristic modifications. The specific output format (point, bbox, text) is determined by the MLLM's capabilities and the task, not by ECP's structure.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Approach:** By first leveraging the MLLM's coarse localization ability on a downsampled image and then re-evaluating a high-resolution crop, ECP effectively addresses the resolution discrepancy while preserving fine-grained details.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ECP framework was rigorously validated on two challenging high-resolution vision-language tasks: 4K GUI grounding and 4K/8K MLLM perception. The results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness of ECP in significantly enhancing MLLM performance compared to conventional single-stage approaches.

#### 4.1. 4K GUI Grounding (ScreenSpot-Pro Benchmark)

- **Benchmark:** ScreenSpot-Pro, a 4K resolution GUI grounding benchmark covering diverse application categories (Dev, Cre, CAD, Sci, Office, OS). Metric: Accuracy (predicted point/bbox center within ground-truth bbox).
- **Baseline (Single-stage Framework):** General-purpose MLLMs like GPT-4o and Qwen2-VL-7B exhibited extremely poor performance, with accuracies of 0.8% and 1.5% respectively. Even OS-Atlas-7B, a GUI-specific MLLM, achieved only 19.1%. This strongly confirms that MLLMs struggle with fine-grained localization in high-resolution GUI environments.
- **ECP Framework Results:** ECP consistently and substantially boosted performance across all categories.
 - Using Qwen2-VL-7B in both EC and P stages, performance improved from 1.5% (single-stage) to **11.4%** overall, an absolute increase of **9.9%**.
 - When a more specialized MLLM, OS-Atlas-7B, was used in both stages, the accuracy soared from 19.1% to **40.4%** overall, an impressive absolute improvement of **21.3%**.
 - This highlights that ECP effectively mitigates the challenges posed by high-resolution images, making MLLMs viable for precise GUI interactions. The choice of a capable MLLM for the EC stage further amplifies the benefits.
- **Ablation Study (Random Sampling in EC):** Replacing the instruction-guided region selection in EC with random sampling drastically degraded performance. For instance, when using OS-Atlas-7B in P, the accuracy dropped from 40.4% (with OS-Atlas-7B in EC) to a mere 11.9% (with random sampling in EC). This significant drop of 28.5% unequivocally validates the core assumption: MLLMs, even on downsampled images, possess a valuable coarse localization ability that is critical for ECP's success.

#### 4.2. 4K, 8K MLLM Perception (HR-Bench Benchmark)

- **Benchmark:** HR-Bench, consisting of multiple-choice questions with 4K or 8K resolution images, categorized into Fine-grained Single-instance Perception (FSP) and Fine-grained Cross-instance Perception (FCP). Metric: Accuracy with Cyclic Permutation.
- **Baseline (Single-stage Framework):** Qwen2-VL-7B achieved 62.5% on HR-Bench 4K and 55.1% on HR-Bench 8K. GPT-4o performed similarly (59.0% on 4K, 55.5% on 8K).
- **ECP Framework Results:** ECP significantly improved overall accuracy.
 - For Qwen2-VL-7B (used in both EC and P), the overall accuracy increased by **5.8%** on HR-Bench 4K (from 62.5% to **68.3%**) and by **5.2%** on HR-Bench 8K (from 55.1% to **60.3%**).
 - A notable specific improvement was observed in the **FSP category** (single small object problems), where accuracy rose by +9.5% on 4K (71.5% to 81.0%) and +10.3% on 8K (61.5% to 71.8%). This strongly supports ECP's ability to enhance MLLM interpretation of small, fine-grained objects due to the localized high-resolution processing.
 - Even using a smaller model like Qwen2-VL-2B in EC and P still yielded improvements over the single-stage framework, suggesting resource-efficient deployment options.
- **Ablation Study (Random Sampling in EC):** Similar to GUI grounding, using random sampling in EC led to a substantial performance drop, often below the single-stage baseline. For Qwen2-VL-2B on HR-Bench 4K, accuracy decreased from 63.5% (ECP with Qwen2-VL-7B in EC) to 50.1% (random sampling). This further reinforces that the MLLM's capability to roughly identify meaningful regions is crucial for ECP's efficacy.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual examples (Figure 3 and 4) clearly illustrate ECP's advantage. While the single-stage framework failed to answer questions about small objects (e.g., phone number on a boat, text on a hang glider) or ground specific UI elements, ECP successfully located the relevant area and provided correct answers using the cropped, high-resolution patch.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that ECP effectively addresses the challenges of high-resolution image understanding for MLLMs. Its training-free and task-agnostic nature, combined with substantial performance gains, make it a highly practical and impactful contribution. The ablation studies consistently confirm the core hypothesis: leveraging the MLLM's coarse localization ability in the initial stage is key to unlocking fine-grained performance in the second stage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ECP framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models, particularly in addressing a critical limitation: their poor performance on high-resolution images. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the Resolution Gap for MLLMs:** ECP provides an effective and elegant solution to the long-standing "resolution curse" in MLLMs. By intelligently combining coarse localization with fine-grained detail analysis, it enables existing MLLMs to perform robustly on visual inputs ranging from 4K to 8K resolutions without requiring complex architectural changes or computationally expensive retraining. This expands the operational domain of MLLMs considerably.
2. **Training-Free and Task-Agnostic Paradigm Shift:** This is arguably the most impactful aspect of ECP. Unlike prior approaches that demand extensive retraining with specialized high-resolution datasets or intricate, task-specific heuristic designs, ECP is inherently training-free and task-agnostic. This means:
 - **Ease of Adoption:** Researchers and practitioners can immediately apply ECP to any pre-trained MLLM and any high-resolution vision-language task, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for utilizing MLLMs in high-detail scenarios.
 - **Cost-Effectiveness:** It bypasses the enormous computational resources typically associated with training large models, making high-resolution MLLM capabilities more accessible.
 - **Future-Proofing:** As new and more powerful MLLMs emerge, ECP can seamlessly integrate with them, ensuring its continued relevance without the need for constant re-development.
3. **Enhanced Practical Applicability:** The ability of MLLMs to handle high-resolution images with precision unlocks numerous real-world applications where fine-grained understanding is paramount:
 - **Professional Software Interaction:** ECP's demonstrated success in GUI grounding on 4K interfaces means MLLMs can become more effective AI agents for automating tasks in complex desktop applications (e.g., design software, scientific tools, enterprise systems), leading to increased productivity.
 - **Detailed Visual Analysis:** Industries relying on high-resolution imagery, such as medical diagnostics (analyzing scans), industrial inspection (detecting minute defects), satellite imagery analysis, and scientific visualization, can leverage ECP to extract more accurate and detailed insights from visual data.
 - **E-commerce and Product Details:** MLLMs can better understand and answer questions about intricate product features from high-resolution images, improving customer experience.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) and Robotics:** For applications requiring precise object recognition and interaction in complex environments, ECP can enhance the perception capabilities of AI systems.
4. **Inspiration for Future Research:** ECP's success in leveraging MLLM's implicit coarse localization ability highlights a promising avenue for future research. It encourages further exploration into how existing large models can be creatively repurposed and combined to overcome their limitations, rather than always resorting to building larger, more complex models from scratch. The authors themselves suggest exploring different image cropping strategies and broader MLLM ablations, pointing towards exciting directions for optimizing this paradigm.

In conclusion, ECP represents a highly significant contribution by providing an elegant, efficient, and broadly applicable framework that empowers current MLLMs to robustly tackle high-resolution visual tasks. Its training-free and task-agnostic nature democratizes access to advanced multimodal capabilities, with far-reaching potential across various scientific and industrial domains.
