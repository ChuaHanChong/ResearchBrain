# GenerativeMPC: VLM-RAG-guided Whole-Body MPC with Virtual Impedance for Bimanual Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology developed GenerativeMPC, a hierarchical cyber-physical framework that unifies semantic understanding from a VLM-RAG module with low-level physical control via whole-body MPC and impedance control for bimanual mobile manipulators. The system demonstrated dynamic adaptation of velocity limits, safety margins, and compliance parameters based on environmental context, achieving a 60% reduction in robot speed near humans and 17.5 mm position accuracy on a physical platform.

## Problem

- A fundamental division exists between high-level semantic reasoning (e.g., from VLMs) and low-level physical control (e.g., classical MPC) in robotic systems, preventing seamless integration.
- Existing robot control methods often use static, predefined parameters for physical interaction properties (stiffness, damping, safety margins), hindering dynamic adaptation to changing contexts or human presence.
- Robots currently lack robust memory mechanisms for experience-driven adaptation of control parameters, requiring continuous retraining or manual tuning of cognitive models for new scenarios.

## Method

- A three-layer hierarchical framework integrates a VLM-RAG module for task planning, a Whole-Body ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) for trajectory optimization, and a Unified Impedance-Admittance Controller for compliant execution.
- The VLM processes visual and linguistic context to generate structured JSON descriptions, which a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) module with ChromaDB uses to retrieve validated physical control parameters (velocity limits, safety margins, stiffness, damping) from an experience database.
- The MPC operates at 10 Hz to plan coordinated base and dual-arm trajectories with integrated obstacle avoidance, while the impedance-admittance controller executes these plans at 50 Hz, applying consistent spring-damper dynamics across both the mobile base and manipulator arms for compliant tracking.

## Results

- The robot demonstrated human-aware navigation, automatically reducing its linear velocity by **60%** and increasing base damping when humans were detected, achieving **17.5** mm position accuracy and **1.5**° heading error on hardware.
- In simulation, the system adapted to human hand presence during bimanual reaching by automatically retrieving parameters that reduced arm stiffness (K_i=**8.0**) and increased damping (D_i=**25.0**) and safety margin (**0.15** m), preventing collisions and maintaining safe interaction distances.
- The framework successfully operated on a physical bimanual mobile manipulator using onboard computation (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano), with observed sim-to-real performance degradation attributed primarily to communication latency rather than fundamental control issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Semantic context derived from visual-language models can be directly grounded into low-level physical control parameters, enabling dynamic and context-aware robot behaviors like speed reduction and compliance modulation during human interaction.
> - Integrating a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) module with an experience database allows robots to adapt control parameters based on past validated behaviors, creating a self-improving memory mechanism without continuous large cognitive model fine-tuning.
> - A unified impedance-admittance control formulation consistently applied to both the mobile base and manipulator arms provides a cohesive and robust approach to whole-body compliance, simplifying control design for high-DoF bimanual mobile manipulators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressing towards autonomous systems capable of operating in human-centric environments, requiring sophisticated capabilities in navigation, object manipulation, and human interaction. Bimanual mobile manipulators, which combine mobility with dual-arm dexterity, are considered suitable platforms for these complex tasks. However, current research exhibits a fundamental division between high-level semantic reasoning and low-level physical control.

Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and VLM-based planners have demonstrated capabilities in semantic scene understanding and generating high-level subgoals for robots. Examples include HYPERmotion for action primitive selection, VoxAct-B for bimanual role assignment, and larger models like MoManipVLA and FALCON for loco-manipulation. While these models excel at interpreting visual and linguistic context and translating it into kinematic plans, they typically do not address the modulation of a robot's physical interaction properties, such as stiffness, damping, or dynamic velocity constraints, based on semantic cues. They often focus on kinematic success rather than compliant physical behavior.

Concurrently, Whole-Body Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks have enabled real-time trajectory optimization for high-degree-of-freedom robotic systems, facilitating agile movements and collision-free locomotion and manipulation in platforms ranging from quadrupeds to mobile robots. However, a limitation of these classical MPC methods is that critical parameters like safety margins and velocity envelopes are typically static and predefined during system design. This fixed nature prevents robots from adapting their physical behavior dynamically in response to changing environmental contexts or the presence of humans.

The present work, GenerativeMPC, addresses the integration challenge by bridging this gap. It aims to unify semantic scene understanding, enabled by VLMs, with predictable, compliant physical control, provided by MPC and impedance control, for bimanual mobile manipulators. It builds upon the concept of VLM-driven impedance scheduling, previously explored for single-arm humanoid systems in SafeHumanoid, by extending it to a unified framework encompassing whole-body coordination, non-holonomic navigation, and dual-arm compliance. Furthermore, the paper introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) into robotic control loops, a concept primarily used in natural language processing to ground outputs in factual context, to provide an experience-driven "memory" mechanism for parameter selection in robotics. This is intended to allow robots to retrieve validated control parameter sets for similar tasks without requiring continuous VLM fine-tuning, thereby advancing the field towards more adaptive and context-aware human-robot interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind GenerativeMPC stems from the challenges faced by autonomous robots in human-centered environments. These robots are required to navigate complex, unstructured spaces, interact with diverse objects that may demand varying levels of compliance, and interpret instructions from non-expert human users. For bimanual mobile manipulators, which possess high degrees of freedom and capabilities for intricate tasks, the simultaneous need for rich semantic understanding and precise, safe physical interaction presents a significant hurdle. Current robotic systems typically handle these two aspects—semantic reasoning and physical control—through separate, often incompatible, subsystems. This architectural fragmentation prevents the realization of a truly unified cybernetic loop where high-level cognitive insights directly and seamlessly influence the robot's physical actions and reflexes.

The key objectives of this research are to:

1. **Bridge Semantic Understanding and Physical Control:** To develop a hierarchical cyber-physical framework that explicitly connects semantic scene understanding, derived from visual and linguistic context, with the generation of physical control parameters for bimanual mobile manipulators. This includes dynamic velocity limits and safety margins for a Whole-Body Model Predictive Controller (MPC), as well as virtual stiffness and damping gains for a unified impedance-admittance controller.
2. **Enable Context-Aware Compliance:** To allow the robot to adjust its physical compliance—its "feel" of interaction, such as how stiff or yielding it should be—based on the real-time context, particularly during human-robot interaction. This aims to facilitate safer and more socially-aware navigation and manipulation.
3. **Implement Experience-Driven Adaptation:** To integrate a memory mechanism using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) module with a vector database. This mechanism is intended to retrieve validated control parameter sets from past experiences for semantically similar conditions, thereby ensuring consistent parameter grounding and reducing the need for continuous retraining or manual tuning of cognitive models.
4. **Achieve Unified Whole-Body Coordination:** To develop a holistic control formulation that jointly handles whole-body coordination, non-holonomic navigation, and dual-arm compliance within a single, consistent framework. This involves applying a consistent spring-damper formulation across both the mobile base and the manipulator arms, with obstacle avoidance embedded within the MPC's cost function.
5. **Ground Large-Scale Cognitive Models:** Ultimately, to advance the field of human-centric cybernetics by demonstrating the practical grounding of large-scale cognitive models (VLMs) into predictable, high-frequency physical control loops, moving beyond abstract symbolic planning to direct modulation of physical behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenerativeMPC is structured as a three-layer hierarchical cyber-physical framework designed for bimanual mobile manipulation. It integrates semantic reasoning with compliant physical control through a continuous feedback loop.

**A. Hardware Platform**
The physical platform consists of a 22 kg differential-drive mobile base with a wheel radius of 0.085 m and a wheelbase of 0.455 m. It is equipped with two 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) manipulators, each actuated by Feetech STS3215 servos. Sensing capabilities include a Velodyne VLP-16 LiDAR for obstacle detection and a RealSense D435 RGB-D camera for visual input to the VLM. All computational processes are handled onboard by an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano with 16 GB of RAM, running under ROS 2 Humble.

**B. Simulation and Sim-to-Real**
Robot models were developed in SolidWorks and exported into URDF (Unified Robot Description Format), then converted to MuJoCo MJCF and IsaacSim USD formats, maintaining consistent link geometry, inertial properties, and joint limits. Controller development and human-aware navigation experiments were conducted in a MuJoCo warehouse environment featuring various obstacles and human-shaped mannequins. IsaacSim was utilized for photorealistic sensor validation and manipulation tasks. In simulation, the MPC, impedance controller, and physics engine operate within a single 50 Hz callback to minimize latency. For real-robot deployment, the architecture transitions to ROS 2 nodes, which introduced a 30-60 ms Modbus RTU communication latency. This latency necessitated a systematic reduction in control gains for the hardware implementation compared to simulation parameters.

**C. Three-Layer Architecture**

1. **Layer 1: VLM-RAG Task Planning**
 This is the highest layer, responsible for translating high-level task instructions and visual context into low-level control parameters.
 - **VLM Module:** An InternVL3-1B model, INT4-quantized and deployed at the edge, processes camera images and natural language instructions. It generates a structured JSON description that includes the task type, detected objects, human presence, and their proximity.
 - **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with ChromaDB:** The generated JSON description is embedded into a vector and matched via cosine similarity against an experience-driven vector database (ChromaDB). This database stores validated parameter sets from previous robot executions under semantically similar conditions.
 - **Output:** The RAG module retrieves and outputs two distinct sets of parameters:
 - Velocity limits (v\_max, ω\_max) and safety margin (d\_safe) for the Whole-Body MPC.
 - Stiffness (K\_i) and damping (D\_i) gains for the unified impedance-admittance controller.
 - **Experience-Driven Adaptation:** After each completed task episode, the resulting control parameters and contextual information are written back to ChromaDB, enriching the database and enabling self-improvement without requiring explicit VLM fine-tuning. For example, detecting a human within 1.5 meters triggers a conservative profile (e.g., v\_max = 0.10 m/s, K\_i = 8.0).

2. **Layer 2: Whole-Body Model Predictive Control (MPC)**
 Operating at 10 Hz with a prediction horizon of N=7 steps (∆t = 0.15 s), this layer plans coordinated trajectories for the mobile base and both manipulator end-effectors.
 - **State and Control Representation:**
 - **State (x ∈ R^9):** Comprises the base pose (x, y, θ) and the 3D positions of both left (p_L) and right (p_R) end-effectors in the base frame.
 - **Control (u ∈ R^8):** Consists of the base's linear (v) and angular (ω) velocities, and the velocities of the left (˙p_L) and right (˙p_R) end-effectors.
 - **Dynamics Model:** The base is modeled as a unicycle, and end-effector positions are updated based on their velocities.
 - **Cost Function (J):** The MPC minimizes a sum of costs over the prediction horizon:
 - `J_terminal`: Penalizes residual base pose and end-effector errors at the end of the horizon.
 - `J_running`: Promotes continuous progress towards the goal and alignment of the base's heading.
 - `J_obs`: Implements obstacle avoidance using a soft Artificial Potential Fields (APF) approach (Khatib APF). The potential field is active within a radius ρ_0 = 0.5 m and has a repulsive gain η = 50. This term fades to zero near the goal to prevent local minima.
 - `J_ctrl`: Penalizes the magnitude of control efforts (linear, angular, and end-effector velocities).
 - `J_smooth`: Penalizes step-to-step changes in control inputs to ensure smooth trajectories.
 - **Solver:** The optimization problem is solved using Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP), warm-started with the previous solution to improve computational efficiency.

3. **Layer 3: Unified Impedance-Admittance Control**
 This lowest layer executes the trajectories generated by the MPC at 50 Hz, ensuring compliant tracking for both the mobile base and the manipulator arms.
 - **Virtual Impedance Force:** For both the base and end-effectors, a virtual impedance force (F_virt) is calculated based on the error between the MPC's desired state (x_d, ˙x_d) and the measured state (x, ˙x), using stiffness (K_i) and damping (D_i) gains provided by the VLM-RAG layer.
 - **Admittance Filter:** This virtual force is then passed through an admittance filter to generate corrected velocities (˙x_c) and positions (x_c), which are subsequently integrated.
 - **Base and Arm Tracking:**
 - **Base:** The corrected velocity is projected into the robot's frame to determine linear (v_c) and angular (ω_c) velocities, which are then converted into left and right wheel speeds. Heading regulation incorporates a proportional gain (k_θ) with a blending scheme near the goal.
 - **Arms:** The impedance-admittance formulation is applied independently to each arm's end-effector in 3D task space. Corrected end-effector velocities are mapped to joint velocities using damped least-squares Inverse Kinematics (IK).
 - **Self-Collision Avoidance (Arms):** Prior to IK, if the distance between the corrected end-effector positions falls below the safety margin (d_safe) provided by the VLM-RAG, their references are symmetrically separated along their connecting vector to prevent self-collision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GenerativeMPC framework was evaluated through experimental results in both simulated environments (MuJoCo and IsaacSim) and on a physical bimanual mobile manipulation platform.

**A. Simulation Setup**
Experiments were conducted in a MuJoCo warehouse scene, which included shelving units, cylindrical obstacles, and human-shaped mannequins for testing human-aware navigation. IsaacSim provided a photorealistic environment for validating the manipulation pipeline. The VLM-RAG module was initially seeded with five demonstration episodes covering free navigation, navigation near humans, and bimanual reaching tasks to establish an initial experience database.

**B. Task 1: Human-Aware Navigation**
In this task, the robot was instructed to navigate to a target pose (3.0, 2.0, 0°) while two human mannequins were positioned along its path.
- **VLM-RAG Adaptation:** Upon detecting the proximity of humans, the VLM-RAG module retrieved a conservative control profile. This resulted in a 60% reduction of the base's maximum linear velocity (from a nominal 0.25 m/s to 0.10 m/s) and an increase in damping (D_i from 15.0 to 25.0) for the base's impedance controller. This adaptation occurred automatically without manual gain scheduling.
- **Simulation Performance:** The MPC successfully guided the robot to the goal, achieving a final position error of 7.8 mm and a heading error of 1.4° at 71.7 seconds. A transient heading spike of 176° was observed near the goal as the MPC aligned the base to the target orientation, which was subsequently handled smoothly by the admittance controller.
- **Hardware Validation:** On the physical platform, the robot navigated from (0, 0) to (2.0, -1.0) m in a shared workspace with a human present. The Velodyne VLP-16 LiDAR provided real-time obstacle point clouds, which were integrated into the MPC's APF cost for online obstacle avoidance. The controller achieved a position accuracy of 17.5 mm and a heading error of 1.5°. This degradation in position accuracy compared to simulation (17.5 mm vs. 7.8 mm) was attributed to the 30-60 ms Modbus RTU communication latency encountered on the hardware, which necessitated a systematic reduction in control gains (as documented in Table I). The 60% speed reduction near humans was also maintained in hardware.

**C. Task 2: Human Hand Avoidance (Simulation)**
Following base convergence, the robot was tasked with bimanual reaching. This task specifically evaluated the system's ability to adapt to a human entering the manipulation workspace.
- **VLM-RAG Adaptation:** When a human hand was detected entering the robot's workspace, the VLM-RAG module automatically retrieved a compliant control profile. This profile included reduced stiffness (K_i = 8.0), increased damping (D_i = 25.0), and an increased safety margin (d_safe = 0.15 m). These parameters resulted in slowed arm motion and increased compliance.
- **Simulation Performance:** The system successfully maintained a minimum end-effector distance of 0.15 m from the human hand throughout the interaction. Once the human hand departed the workspace, the nominal control parameters were automatically restored. End-effector errors remained below 2 mm after convergence, and no singularity events were observed across the tested configurations.
- **Hardware Arm Manipulation:** While the full human hand avoidance task was predominantly demonstrated in simulation, the hardware evaluation for arm manipulation indicated end-effector errors of 5-12 mm. This suggests a sim-to-real gap for arm precision, likely influenced by the same communication latency observed for the base, although explicit human-in-the-loop arm interaction on hardware for this specific task was described as "proposed" rather than fully validated.

**D. Performance Summary**
A summary of key performance metrics across simulation and hardware indicated consistent behavioral adaptation:
- **Position Error:** 7.8 mm (Sim) vs. 17.5 mm (Hardware).
- **Heading Error:** 1.4° (Sim) vs. 1.5° (Hardware).
- **Speed Reduction (near humans):** 60% in both simulation and hardware.
- **Arm End-Effector Error:** <2 mm (Sim) vs. 5-12 mm (Hardware).

The researchers concluded that the observed sim-to-real performance degradation was primarily attributable to communication latency rather than fundamental controller design issues, based on the required gain reductions for hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GenerativeMPC represents an advancement in the field of human-centric cybernetics by establishing a framework that bridges high-level semantic scene understanding with predictable, high-frequency physical control loops. This work directly addresses a significant limitation in autonomous robotics: the fragmentation between cognitive reasoning and physical interaction capabilities.

The primary significance lies in its **unified cyber-physical framework**, which integrates a VLM-RAG module with whole-body MPC and a unified virtual impedance-admittance controller for bimanual mobile manipulation. This constitutes a novel integration that allows for a holistic approach to robot control, encompassing navigation, dual-arm manipulation, and compliance within a single coherent system.

A key contribution is the **semantic-to-physical parameter grounding**. The system dynamically derives task-appropriate physical constraints—including velocity limits, safety margins, stiffness, and damping gains—directly from camera imagery and natural language instructions. This eliminates the need for manual gain scheduling or controller retraining for different contexts, enabling robots to exhibit context-aware and socially intelligent behaviors. For instance, the automatic 60% speed reduction near humans and adaptive compliance during interaction are direct demonstrations of this capability, contributing to safer human-robot collaboration.

The **holistic compliance formulation** is another significant aspect, applying a consistent spring-damper model to both the mobile base for navigation and the manipulator arms for interaction. This mathematically consistent approach simplifies control design and provides a uniform method for managing safe and compliant cohabitation in shared spaces.

Furthermore, the introduction of an **experience-driven adaptation mechanism** via ChromaDB for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses a "memory gap" in robotics. By storing and retrieving proven parameter sets from prior task executions, the robot can self-improve and maintain consistent, validated behavior without continuous VLM fine-tuning. This mechanism contributes to more robust and adaptive robotic systems that can leverage past experiences effectively.

The potential impact of GenerativeMPC is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The ability to dynamically adjust physical behavior based on human presence and environmental context can lead to safer, more intuitive, and socially acceptable human-robot interactions in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic environments.
- **Increased Robot Autonomy:** By directly grounding high-level cognitive understanding into low-level physical control, robots can become more autonomous and capable of operating in unstructured, dynamic human environments without constant human oversight or pre-programming for every scenario.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for future research in several areas, including full bimanual manipulation validation on hardware, expansion of experiential databases for broader task generalization, and efforts to reduce VLM inference latency for even faster and more reactive semantic-to-physical parameter adaptation. The identified sim-to-real gap due to communication latency also highlights specific areas for hardware and middleware optimization.

In summary, GenerativeMPC represents a step towards creating more intelligent, adaptable, and safe robotic systems that can seamlessly integrate semantic reasoning with physical interaction in complex human-centric environments.
