# Off-Policy Value-Based Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ReVal introduces an off-policy value-based reinforcement learning framework for large language models that interprets LLM logits as Q-values and utilizes a replay buffer for efficient experience reuse. This approach achieved an average of 4.3x speedup in convergence and an 18% reduction in wall-clock training time on mathematical reasoning tasks while outperforming on-policy baselines.

## Problem

- On-policy reinforcement learning methods for large language models (LLMs) suffer from sample inefficiency, requiring frequent, costly data generation rounds, especially for long and complex reasoning tasks.
- Directly applying traditional value-based off-policy RL to LLMs is challenging due to the vast token vocabulary (action space), sparse rewards, and the computational/memory overhead of separate Q-networks.
- Existing value-based interpretations for LLMs, such as TBRM, did not satisfy "Calibrated Initialization," causing unstable policy drift when reward signals were absent.

## Method

- **ReVal**, an ==off-policy value-based RL== framework, directly interprets an LLM's logits as its ==Q-function==, eliminating the need for a separate value network and maintaining a single-model architecture.
- A new ==Bellman objective== is introduced that ensures ==Calibrated Initialization==, preventing policy drift in the absence of rewards, and effectively integrates multi-temporal feedback.
- A ==FIFO replay buffer== is used to decouple data collection from policy updates, enabling efficient reuse of historical trajectories for multiple gradient updates per collected sample.

## Results

- ReVal achieved an average of **4.3x** speedup in convergence compared to the on-policy GRPO baseline, demonstrating enhanced sample efficiency, particularly for harder tasks.
- The framework outperformed GRPO and TBRM on various mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with up to a **4.5%** improvement on out-of-domain tasks like GPQA for the DPSK-R1-Distill-**1.5B** model.
- In limited-rollout scenarios, ReVal reduced the number of data generation rounds by **19%** and total wall-clock training time by **18%**, translating into tangible cost savings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy learning with a replay buffer dramatically improves sample efficiency and optimization speed, especially critical for complex LLM reasoning tasks where generating valid trajectories is expensive.
> - Interpreting LLM logits as Q-values allows for a low-cost, single-model value-based RL approach that avoids the overhead of separate value networks while leveraging pre-trained knowledge.
> - Ensuring "Calibrated Initialization" in the Bellman objective prevents spurious policy drift when reward signals are absent, contributing to stable and effective learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language model (LLM) post-training has increasingly adopted reinforcement learning (RL), particularly reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), to enhance reasoning abilities. Early approaches, such as those based on actor-critic policy optimization like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), leveraged the natural parameterization of autoregressive LLMs as token-level policies. As LLMs scaled, computational efficiency became a significant concern, leading to the development of actor-only RL methods like ReMax, GRPO, and DAPO, which reduced memory usage and training time.

However, these dominant methods remain fundamentally on-policy. On-policy algorithms require data to be sampled from the current policy for each update, rendering collected trajectories "stale" after limited use. This inherent sample inefficiency poses a challenge, especially as LLM development moves towards agentic settings involving long and variable horizons, where generating trajectories is computationally expensive. The cost of data collection often surpasses the cost of parameter updates in such scenarios.

This limitation motivates a shift towards off-policy RL, a paradigm common in classical RL that allows for efficient reuse of historical experience via replay buffers and Bellman learning. Applying traditional value-based methods to LLMs, however, presents challenges. Conventional Q-functions are typically explicit value models that might be incompatible with LLMs due to their vast token vocabulary (action space) and sparse rewards, or they could introduce additional memory and computational overhead, undermining the efficiency gains of actor-only methods.

A recent theoretical development by Li et al. (2025a) provided a pathway by demonstrating that the logits of a pre-trained LLM can be interpreted as parameterizing action values of an endogenous reward. This insight suggests that policy and value functions can be unified within a single LLM, paving the way for low-cost, single-model, off-policy RL. This work, ReVal, builds directly on this perspective, aiming to address the sample inefficiency of on-policy methods by developing a value-based, off-policy framework for LLM post-training that leverages the LLM's own logits as Q-values and incorporates a replay buffer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and evaluate an off-policy value-based reinforcement learning framework, ReVal, for fine-tuning Large Language Models. This framework aims to significantly improve data utilization efficiency compared to prevailing on-policy methods, especially for long-horizon and agentic tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and observations in the current landscape of RL for LLMs:

- **Sample Inefficiency of On-Policy Methods:** Dominant RL algorithms for LLMs, such as GRPO, are largely on-policy. They update the model with each batch of collected data typically only once, then discard it and collect fresh samples. For tasks involving long and complex reasoning paths, generating full trajectories is resource-intensive and time-consuming. This "one-shot" data usage results in poor sample efficiency, leading to a high number of required data generation rounds and a substantial portion of the total training time being spent on data collection (where $T_{generation}$ is often much larger than $T_{update}$). The paper empirically demonstrates this inefficiency, showing that on-policy GRPO requires significantly more optimization steps for harder tasks.

- **Need for Experience Reuse:** To mitigate the high cost of data generation, there is a fundamental need for RL algorithms that can effectively reuse past experiences. Off-policy methods, by decoupling data collection from policy updates and employing mechanisms like replay buffers, are designed for this purpose. This reuse can accelerate convergence and reduce the total number of costly data generation rounds, thereby lowering overall wall-clock training time.

- **Challenges with Traditional Value-Based RL for LLMs:** While off-policy methods are typically value-based, conventional Q-learning paradigms face difficulties when directly applied to LLMs. These include the enormous action space (token vocabulary), sparse rewards in reasoning tasks, and the potential need for an additional, separate value model. Introducing a separate value model would increase computational and memory overhead, negating the "low-cost" advantage sought in actor-only methods like ReMax.

- **Limitations of Existing Value-Based Interpretations for LLMs:** Recent work by Li et al. (2025a) and TBRM (Yuan et al., 2025) proposed interpreting LLM logits as Q-values, enabling a single-model approach. However, the paper identifies a limitation in TBRM: it does not satisfy "Calibrated Initialization." This property dictates that if no reward signal is present, the optimal policy should revert to the reference policy. TBRM's objective, when rewards are zero, leads to spurious policy drift, implying instability in the absence of explicit reward guidance.

- **Necessity for Multi-Temporal Signal Integration:** Effective value learning requires combining different temporal scales of feedback. Stepwise signals can promote internal consistency, while trajectory-level signals provide overall outcome correctness. Relying solely on one type of signal can be suboptimal; stepwise feedback alone might not efficiently reflect trajectory outcomes, while trajectory-level Bellman learning might suffer from mis-calibration. Therefore, a method that integrates both types of signals for stable optimization is desirable.

Based on these motivations, ReVal aims to propose a novel objective that ensures calibrated initialization and integrates multi-temporal signals, combined with a replay-buffer-based training mechanism to facilitate off-policy experience reuse, all while maintaining a single-model, low-cost architecture by interpreting LLM logits as Q-values.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, ReVal (Reinforcement Learning with Value), is an off-policy value-based RL framework designed for LLM post-training. It addresses the sample inefficiency of on-policy methods and the limitations of previous value-based approaches by unifying policy and value within a single LLM and leveraging a replay buffer for efficient data reuse.

#### 3.1. Q-Function Parameterization and LLM Integration

ReVal builds upon the insight that LLM logits can serve as a Q-function. Specifically, it adopts the parameterization $Q_\theta(s_h, a_h) := \text{logit}_\theta(s_h, a_h)$, where the LLM's own logits are directly used as soft Q-values. This eliminates the need for a separate value network, maintaining the low-cost property of actor-only methods and leveraging the pre-trained knowledge embedded in the LLM. The underlying theoretical basis for this is that LLMs implicitly learn a soft Q-function through next-token prediction during pre-training.

#### 3.2. ReVal Training Objective

The paper first highlights a limitation of a prior value-based method, TBRM, specifically its failure to satisfy "Calibrated Initialization." Calibrated Initialization requires that if the rule-based reward $r_{rule}$ is zero, the optimal policy under the KL-regularized RL objective should be the reference policy ($\pi^* = \pi_{ref}$). TBRM's objective (Equation 2 in the paper) causes spurious policy drift when $r_{rule}=0$, as it forces the log-likelihood ratio to cancel out the value term $V_\theta(s_1)$ rather than just minimizing KL divergence.

To address this, ReVal introduces a modified reward function and a new Bellman objective:
1. **Reward Shaping:** A modified reward function $R_\beta(s_h, a_h)$ is defined, incorporating the scaled environment reward ($r_{rule}(s_h, a_h)/\beta$), a log-probability term from the reference policy ($\log \pi_{ref}(a_h | s_h)$), and a state-dependent offset ($V_\theta(s_h) - V_{ref}(s_h)$). This reward shaping term is designed to not affect the optimal policy while facilitating stable learning.
2. **Bellman Operator:** Based on this modified reward, a Bellman operator $(T_\beta Q)(s_h, a_h)$ is defined.
3. **ReVal Loss Function:** The core of ReVal's optimization is the minimization of a trajectory-level Bellman residual loss:
 $L_{ReVal}(\theta) = \frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{\tau \in D} \left( V_\theta(s_1) - V_{ref}(s_1) + \log \pi_\theta(\tau) - \frac{r_{rule}(\tau)}{\beta} - \log \pi_{ref}(\tau) \right)^2$
 This formulation ensures that ReVal satisfies Calibrated Initialization. When $r_{rule}=0$ and $\pi_\theta = \pi_{ref}$, $L_{ReVal}(\theta)$ becomes 0, indicating stability at initialization. The loss function encourages internal consistency through the stepwise Q-value updates and trajectory-level correctness through the reward term.

#### 3.3. Replay Buffer for Off-Policy Learning

A central component of ReVal is the integration of a replay buffer, which enables off-policy learning. This mechanism decouples data collection from policy updates, allowing for the reuse of historical trajectories.
1. **FIFO Replay Buffer:** ReVal uses a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) replay buffer of size $M$.
2. **Training Loop:** In each iteration:
 - New trajectories are collected using the current policy $\pi_\theta$.
 - These new trajectories are added to the replay buffer. If the buffer capacity $M$ is exceeded, the oldest samples are evicted.
 - An off-policy batch is sampled uniformly from the replay buffer.
 - The LLM's parameters $\theta$ are updated via gradient descent using the $L_{ReVal}$ objective on this sampled off-policy batch.
 - This process allows for multiple gradient updates ($K$ updates per iteration) using the same collected data, unlike on-policy methods which typically perform one update and discard. For instance, with $B$ new trajectories and $K$ updates per iteration, a single trajectory is expected to contribute to approximately $K$ gradient updates. The paper uses $M=5120$, $B=1024$, and $K=2$, yielding an expected reuse of 2 gradient updates per trajectory.

#### 3.4. Key Factors and Hyperparameters

- **KL Regularization and Periodic Reset:** The KL-divergence term $\log (\pi_\theta(y|x)/\pi_{ref}(y|x))$ in the residual error $\delta(x, y)$ can grow, weakening the gradient signal. To mitigate this, ReVal periodically resets the reference model $\pi_{ref}$ to the current policy $\pi_\theta$, effectively relaxing the KL constraint and allowing for continued policy improvement.
- **Hyperparameter $\beta$:** This parameter scales the reward signal ($r_{rule}/\beta$), controlling its relative importance. An appropriate $\beta$ value is crucial for training stability and performance, as it balances the influence of the reward and the KL regularization terms. The optimal $\beta$ is noted to be correlated with response length.
- **Negative Samples Utilization:** The paper investigates different reward designs beyond simple 0/1 rewards to better leverage informative signals from incorrect responses. Normalized advantage (similar to GRPO) and a ±1 reward scheme are explored.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ReVal was conducted on standard mathematical reasoning benchmarks using two LLM models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B (DPSK-R1-Distill-1.5B) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B. Baselines included GRPO (an on-policy policy optimization method) and TBRM (a value-based but on-policy method).

#### 4.1. Importance of Off-Policy Data Reuse for Optimization Speed

- Experiments using a one-shot task learning setting with varying task difficulties demonstrated that ReVal, through frequent data reuse (e.g., up to 8 updates per generation round), achieved substantial speedups in convergence compared to GRPO.
- ReVal yielded an average of 4.3× speedup across tasks. Specifically, it achieved 3.6× speedup for hard tasks, 4.1× for medium tasks, and 5.2× for easy tasks to reach a score of 95.0.
- The advantage of multiple optimization updates became more pronounced as task difficulty increased, highlighting the value of off-policy data for scarce valid samples.

#### 4.2. Main Performance Results

- **DPSK-R1-Distill-1.5B:** ReVal generally outperformed both GRPO and TBRM across almost all mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC, MATH, MINERVA, Olympiad, GPQA).
 - It achieved a 2.7% improvement in AIME24 and a 4.5% improvement in the out-of-domain benchmark GPQA over GRPO.
 - ReVal maintained superior performance throughout the entire training process, as shown by the training curves.
- **Qwen2.5-Math-7B:** On this model, ReVal achieved performance comparable to or exceeding baselines, showing an overall improvement of 1.2% and a 4.2% improvement on out-of-domain benchmarks. This model required additional enhancements such as reward normalization and periodic reference policy resets for stable learning, potentially due to shorter output lengths affecting learning dynamics.

#### 4.3. Performance Under Limited Rollouts

- In a limited-rollout setting (N=1, meaning only one trajectory generated per prompt), ReVal demonstrated a pronounced advantage, particularly on challenging benchmarks like AIME and GPQA.
 - ReVal surpassed GRPO by 4.8% on AIME and 4.6% on GPQA.
- ReVal (with 8 update steps per generation round) reduced the average number of generation rounds required to reach state-of-the-art performance from 580 (GRPO) to 470, a 19% reduction.
- This also translated into a practical reduction in total wall-clock training time, from 7.5 hours for GRPO to 6.2 hours for ReVal, an 18% decrease, indicating that off-policy reuse can lead to tangible cost savings when trajectory generation is expensive.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Key Factors

- **KL Regularization and Periodic Reference Policy Reset:** The study confirmed that the KL regularization term can weaken gradient signals over time. Periodically resetting the reference policy (e.g., every 200 steps) was effective in relaxing this constraint, preventing performance saturation and enabling continued improvement.
- **Hyperparameter $\beta$:** The choice of $\beta$ was found to significantly influence both KL divergence and training dynamics. A larger $\beta$ resulted in a stronger KL penalty, keeping the policy closer to the reference model but potentially leading to vanishing gradients. A smaller $\beta$ allowed for more aggressive exploration and deviation, but excessive deviation could lead to training instability or collapse. The optimal $\beta$ was observed to depend on response length.
- **Utilization of Negative Samples:** Experiments with different reward designs showed that using a *normalized advantage* (similar to GRPO's approach) yielded the best performance. A simple ±1 reward scheme, which explicitly penalizes incorrect answers, resulted in performance degradation. This suggests that the way negative signals are incorporated is critical and that normalization helps stabilize learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, ReVal, introduces an off-policy value-based reinforcement learning framework for Large Language Models that addresses a key limitation of prevailing on-policy methods: data utilization efficiency. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Validation of Value-Based RL for LLMs:** ReVal empirically demonstrates that value-based RL is a practical and effective alternative to policy-based methods for LLM training. By leveraging the interpretation of LLM logits as Q-values, it successfully integrates value-based learning into the single-model, low-cost paradigm preferred for LLM fine-tuning, overcoming prior compatibility challenges.

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Faster Convergence:** The core contribution of ReVal is its ability to reuse historical trajectories through a replay buffer. This off-policy capability leads to significantly improved sample efficiency, resulting in faster convergence to high performance. This is particularly impactful for tasks with long horizons or in agentic settings where generating new trajectories is expensive.

- **Reduction in Training Costs:** Beyond sample efficiency, ReVal offers a tangible reduction in wall-clock training time. By requiring fewer expensive data generation rounds, it makes RL-based LLM post-training more economically viable, especially in scenarios where generation costs dominate. This efficiency can accelerate research and development cycles for LLMs.

- **Stable and Robust Optimization:** The proposed objective function, which combines stepwise and trajectory-level signals and ensures "Calibrated Initialization," contributes to more stable and effective policy improvement. Addressing the spurious policy drift observed in prior value-based methods ensures that learning is grounded and responsive to true reward signals.

- **Improved Generalization:** ReVal demonstrated strong performance on out-of-domain benchmarks, suggesting that its efficient learning process can lead to better generalization capabilities in LLMs, which is crucial for real-world applications.

- **Guidance for LLM RL Practitioners:** The detailed ablation studies on factors such as KL regularization, the hyperparameter $\beta$, and reward design provide practical insights and guidance for tuning and stabilizing value-based RL algorithms in the LLM context. This knowledge can inform future developments and implementations.

- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for further exploration in off-policy LLM RL, particularly regarding more advanced replay buffer sampling strategies (e.g., prioritized experience replay) and a deeper understanding of the differential learning requirements of various data samples in a value-based context.

In summary, ReVal offers a methodological advancement that makes RL for LLMs more efficient and scalable, directly addressing a critical bottleneck in their development and fine-tuning.
