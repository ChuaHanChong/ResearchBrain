# Learning Nonlinear Causal Reductions to Explain Reinforcement Learning Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Max Planck Institute developed Nonlinear Targeted Causal Reduction (nTCR), a method for explaining complex Reinforcement Learning policies by learning interpretable high-level causal models from low-level interactions. It provides theoretical guarantees for exact causal reductions and identifies policy biases and failure modes, such as a surprising clockwise swing preference in a pendulum task and critical ball trajectory dependencies in robot table tennis.

## Problem

- The black-box nature of complex Reinforcement Learning policies makes their decision-making opaque to humans, hindering debugging and trust in real-world applications.
- Existing Causal Model Reduction methods are limited by their reliance on linear mappings and Gaussian approximations, failing to capture the inherently nonlinear relationships prevalent in real-world RL systems.
- Standard performance metrics for RL agents offer limited insight into *why* specific actions or sequences of actions lead to success or failure, complicating policy improvement.

## Method

- Formulates RL policy explanation as a **Causal Model Reduction** (CMR) problem using ==Structural Causal Models== (SCMs) and identifying causal influences through shift interventions during policy execution.
- Introduces Nonlinear Targeted Causal Reduction (nTCR) which extends the TCR framework to nonlinear settings by incorporating a normality regularization term to ensure interpretable, Gaussian high-level causal representations.
- Employs a structured and interpretable nonlinear function class, based on Gaussian kernel basis functions over time-series features, to map low-level observations and interventions to high-level causes.

## Results

- nTCR achieved near-zero consistency loss and accurately identified ground-truth causal maps on synthetic data, validating its theoretical properties and implementation.
- In a pendulum swing-up task, nTCR uncovered a non-monotonic policy bias (a preference for clockwise motion) and specific failure modes, which linear causal reduction methods failed to detect.
- For a robot table tennis task, the method revealed detailed, state-dependent insights into critical robot joint movements and specific ball trajectories that posed greater challenges (e.g., balls closer to the net).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enforcing normality (Gaussian distribution) on the learned high-level causes in nTCR is crucial for both theoretical consistency and practical interpretability, especially in high-noise environments.
> - The use of a structured, nonlinear function class allows nTCR to capture complex, non-monotonic causal relationships in RL policies while maintaining the ability to pinpoint influential features and time steps.
> - Theoretical guarantees are provided for the existence and uniqueness of exact nonlinear causal transformations for a specific class of additive noise SCMs, offering a rigorous foundation for the learned explanations.
