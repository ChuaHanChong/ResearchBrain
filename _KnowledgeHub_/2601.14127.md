# The Side Effects of Being Smart: Safety Risks in MLLMs' Multi-Image Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces MIR-SafetyBench, the first benchmark for evaluating safety risks in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) stemming from complex multi-image reasoning, revealing widespread vulnerabilities where models' enhanced capabilities correlate with increased susceptibility to malicious queries. It also demonstrates that many "safe" responses are due to misunderstanding rather than true safety alignment.

## Problem

- Current MLLM safety evaluations primarily focus on "content-based safety," assessing explicit harmful content in single inputs.
- There is a lack of systematic evaluation for "reasoning-based safety," where harm emerges from an MLLM's complex multi-image reasoning and contextual understanding.
- Existing multi-image understanding benchmarks do not explicitly center on safety implications, leaving a critical gap in understanding advanced MLLM vulnerabilities.

## Method

- Developed ==MIR-SafetyBench==, the first comprehensive benchmark for multi-image reasoning safety, featuring instances generated through an iterative AI-driven pipeline across 9 multi-image relation types.
- Conducted extensive evaluations on ==19 MLLMs==, measuring ==Attack Success Rate (ASR)== and qualitatively analyzing the nature of "safe" responses.
- Probed internal model states using ==attention entropy== to diagnose underlying mechanisms of safety failures in multi-image contexts.

## Results

- Most evaluated MLLMs were highly susceptible to multi-image relational reasoning attacks, with ASRs reaching **87.63%**, indicating widespread vulnerability beyond content-based safety.
- A controlled comparison demonstrated that all tested models became significantly more vulnerable when malicious intent was embedded in multi-image prompts compared to single-image scenarios.
- Internal analysis showed that unsafe generations in multi-image tasks exhibited more concentrated attention, suggesting models might under-allocate resources to safety constraints under high cognitive load.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Increased multi-image reasoning capabilities in MLLMs can lead to an expanded attack surface, termed "the side effects of being smart," though frontier models show exceptions.
> - Many "safe" model responses are superficial, often resulting from "Harmless Misunderstanding" or evasive tactics, rather than genuine safety alignment.
> - Unsafe multi-image generations are associated with lower attention entropy, suggesting that cognitive load from complex reasoning might lead models to prioritize task completion over safety.
