# The Side Effects of Being Smart: Safety Risks in MLLMs' Multi-Image Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces MIR-SafetyBench, the first benchmark for evaluating safety risks in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) stemming from complex multi-image reasoning, revealing widespread vulnerabilities where models' enhanced capabilities correlate with increased susceptibility to malicious queries. It also demonstrates that many "safe" responses are due to misunderstanding rather than true safety alignment.

## Problem

- Current MLLM safety evaluations primarily focus on "content-based safety," assessing explicit harmful content in single inputs.
- There is a lack of systematic evaluation for "reasoning-based safety," where harm emerges from an MLLM's complex multi-image reasoning and contextual understanding.
- Existing multi-image understanding benchmarks do not explicitly center on safety implications, leaving a critical gap in understanding advanced MLLM vulnerabilities.

## Method

- Developed ==MIR-SafetyBench==, the first comprehensive benchmark for multi-image reasoning safety, featuring instances generated through an iterative AI-driven pipeline across 9 multi-image relation types.
- Conducted extensive evaluations on ==19 MLLMs==, measuring ==Attack Success Rate (ASR)== and qualitatively analyzing the nature of "safe" responses.
- Probed internal model states using ==attention entropy== to diagnose underlying mechanisms of safety failures in multi-image contexts.

## Results

- Most evaluated MLLMs were highly susceptible to multi-image relational reasoning attacks, with ASRs reaching **87.63%**, indicating widespread vulnerability beyond content-based safety.
- A controlled comparison demonstrated that all tested models became significantly more vulnerable when malicious intent was embedded in multi-image prompts compared to single-image scenarios.
- Internal analysis showed that unsafe generations in multi-image tasks exhibited more concentrated attention, suggesting models might under-allocate resources to safety constraints under high cognitive load.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Increased multi-image reasoning capabilities in MLLMs can lead to an expanded attack surface, termed "the side effects of being smart," though frontier models show exceptions.
> - Many "safe" model responses are superficial, often resulting from "Harmless Misunderstanding" or evasive tactics, rather than genuine safety alignment.
> - Unsafe multi-image generations are associated with lower attention entropy, suggesting that cognitive load from complex reasoning might lead models to prioritize task completion over safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly their enhanced reasoning capabilities to interpret complex instructions and integrate information from multiple visual inputs, mark a significant frontier in artificial intelligence. This progress enables MLLMs to address increasingly sophisticated real-world problems. However, this paper critically examines a crucial, yet often overlooked, aspect of this advancement: the potential for new and expanded safety risks.

Historically, safety evaluations for MLLMs have predominantly focused on "content-based safety." This involves assessing whether a model can identify and refuse explicitly harmful content, such as malicious text embedded in an image or direct queries related to unsafe keywords. Benchmarks like MM-SafetyBench (Liu et al., 2024), automated red-teaming datasets (Luo et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b), and those probing cross-modality alignment (Cui et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2024a; Lee et al., 2025) have contributed to this area. While valuable, these approaches largely neglect "reasoning-based safety," where harm is not immediately apparent in individual inputs but emerges only through the model's complex reasoning process, especially when integrating information across multiple images and user instructions.

The paper identifies a critical gap in the existing literature: the lack of a systematic benchmark specifically designed to evaluate safety risks arising from MLLMs' multi-image reasoning capabilities. While recent works have shown attacks exploiting multi-image contexts—such as distraction-based multimodal jailbreaks (Yang et al., 2025b), visual chain reasoning attacks (Sima et al., 2025), and compositional multi-image jailbreaks (Ding et al., 2025)—they do not offer a comprehensive, systematic framework for evaluation. The development of dedicated multi-image understanding benchmarks like MuirBench (Wang et al., 2025) and MMIU (Meng et al., 2025) highlights the community's focus on enhancing MLLMs' relational and contextual reasoning, but these benchmarks do not explicitly center on safety implications.

This work directly addresses this void by introducing MIR-SafetyBench, the first comprehensive benchmark specifically focused on multi-image reasoning safety. It shifts the safety evaluation paradigm from merely identifying harmful content to assessing the safety implications of sophisticated reasoning. By proposing a taxonomy of multi-image relations that can conceal harmful intent, the paper provides a structured approach to analyzing these advanced vulnerabilities. Furthermore, it probes the internal mechanisms of MLLMs, drawing parallels with cognitive load theory to understand why stronger reasoning capabilities might, counterintuitively, lead to increased safety risks. This positions the research at the forefront of AI safety, moving beyond superficial content filtering to address the deeper, systemic safety challenges posed by increasingly intelligent and context-aware multimodal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: as Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) rapidly gain more sophisticated reasoning capabilities, particularly in handling complex, multi-image instructions, there is a natural yet often unaddressed question of whether these enhanced capabilities also expand their attack surface and introduce new safety risks. The authors term this phenomenon "the side effects of being smart," suggesting a potential trade-off where improved reasoning inadvertently leads to increased vulnerability to malicious queries.

The paper identifies a significant gap in current MLLM safety evaluations, which predominantly focus on "content-based safety"—i.e., whether a model refuses explicit harmful images or text. These evaluations largely neglect "reasoning-based safety," where the harmful intent is not explicit but emerges only through the model's capacity for multi-step reasoning across several images and the user's instruction.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **To introduce MIR-SafetyBench:** Develop and present the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate MLLMs' safety in multi-image reasoning tasks. This benchmark aims to systematically capture risks that emerge from complex cross-image interactions and user instructions.
2. **To demonstrate the pervasiveness of multi-image reasoning safety risks:** Conduct extensive evaluations on a wide range of popular MLLMs to ascertain how widespread these vulnerabilities are and whether there is a correlation between enhanced multi-image reasoning capabilities and increased safety risks (Attack Success Rate, ASR).
3. **To differentiate genuine safety alignment from apparent harmlessness:** Analyze the nature of "safe" responses from MLLMs, distinguishing between truly robust safety alignment and instances where models appear safe merely due to their inability to understand complex malicious intent or their tendency to provide generic, uninformative refusals.
4. **To probe MLLMs' internal states for safety insights:** Investigate the internal cognitive processes of MLLMs during multi-image safety tasks, specifically using attention entropy, to identify distinct internal signatures associated with unsafe generations. This aims to understand if reasoning-based safety failures have underlying mechanistic explanations, such as models over-focusing on task-solving at the expense of safety constraints.

Ultimately, the paper aims to provide a reliable evaluation framework and initial diagnostic insights to inspire the discovery and mitigation of safety vulnerabilities that arise from the complex reasoning capabilities of advanced MLLMs in intricate multimodal scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach, combining benchmark construction, extensive empirical evaluation, and internal model analysis.

#### 3.1. MIR-SafetyBench Construction

The cornerstone of this work is **MIR-SafetyBench**, the first benchmark for multi-image reasoning safety. Its construction follows a meticulous multi-stage pipeline (illustrated in Figure 3 of the paper) to ensure high quality, diversity, and relevance to reasoning-based risks:

1. **Seed Question Curation:** The process begins with curating 600 high-quality harmful seed questions. These seeds span six major risk categories: Hate Speech, Harassment, Violence, Self-Harm, Illegal Activities, and Privacy (detailed in Appendix A). These questions are sourced from existing safety benchmarks (e.g., LongSafety, AdvBench, HarmBench, JailbreakBench) and undergo an initial automated triage using QwQ-32B for filtering and refinement, followed by human expert curation to ensure balance and quality.
2. **Multi-image Instance Generation (Iterative Pipeline):** Each seed question is transformed into a multi-image reasoning task via an iterative, five-step pipeline designed to conceal harmful intent within complex multi-image relations:
 - **Revisor (DeepSeek-R1):** This agent rewrites the direct harmful seed question into an indirect textual prompt, generates a set of image descriptions, and corresponding keywords. This rewriting is specifically tailored to one of the nine predefined multi-image relation types (described below) and incorporates feedback from the Evaluator in subsequent iterations.
 - **Image Generator (FLUX.1-dev):** Based on the image descriptions and keywords from the Revisor, this advanced generative model synthesizes the required set of 2–4 images for each instance.
 - **Tester (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct):** The newly generated multi-image instance (prompt + images) is fed to a Tester agent to simulate a model's response. This step validates if the rewritten prompt effectively elicits a harmful response, ensuring the attack's efficacy.
 - **Harmfulness Judgement (HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls):** The Tester's response is then classified as "harmful" or "safe" by HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls, a widely used and standardized classifier for safety research. This objective label is crucial for evaluation.
 - **Evaluator (DeepSeek-R1):** The Evaluator performs a holistic quality assessment, checking four criteria: (1) the generated answer was harmful, (2) the prompt remained neutral, (3) adherence to the target relation type, and (4) fidelity to the original harmful intent. If any criteria are not met, the Evaluator provides feedback to the Revisor, initiating a refinement iteration (up to five rounds).
 - **Multi-image Relations Taxonomy:** The benchmark incorporates a comprehensive taxonomy of 9 multi-image relation types, grouped into four primary categories:
 - **Temporal Relations:** Temporal Continuity (sequential progression of an event) and Temporal Jump (inferring intermediate actions between start/end points).
 - **Spatial Relations:** Spatial Juxtaposition (inferring actions linking spatially distinct scenes) and Spatial Embedding (framing harmful objects within benign contexts).
 - **Semantic Relations:** Relevance (hiding harmful items among similar benign distractors) and Complementarity (fragmenting a malicious image for reassembly).
 - **Logical Relations:** Analogy (applying a technique from a benign to a malicious topic), Causality (inferring the process to a harmful outcome), and Decomposition (disassembling a malicious objective into innocent sub-questions).

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Models:** 19 representative MLLMs are evaluated, including both open-source and closed-source models of varying scales and architectures (e.g., Llama3-LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, GLM-4.1V, GPT-4o, Gemini series).
- **Metric:** The primary metric is **Attack Success Rate (ASR)**, defined as the percentage of instances that elicit a harmful response, as judged by the HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls classifier. All models used their default safety settings.
- **Controlled Comparison with Single-Image:** To isolate the impact of multi-image reasoning, a controlled experiment compares ASRs of top models on the same harmful seeds, framed as either multi-image cases (from MIR-SafetyBench) or single-image cases (following MM-SafetyBench methodology).
- **Safety Mode Analysis:** For safe responses, a qualitative analysis is performed using DeepSeek-R1 as an expert judge to classify outputs into four modes: Correct Refusal (CR), Harmless Misunderstanding (HM), Incomplete Refusal (IR), and Clever Evasion (CE). This helps distinguish genuine safety alignment from superficial safety.
- **Internal Analysis via Attention Entropy:** To understand the internal "cognitive" state, attention entropy is calculated for four representative MLLMs (two chat, two reasoning models). This analysis compares the distribution of attention weights between safe and unsafe generations in both multi-image and single-image settings. It explores the hypothesis that complex multi-image reasoning might induce cognitive overload, causing models to over-focus on task-solving and neglect safety. Only longer responses (above 1000 characters) are considered to mitigate length-based biases.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a robust evaluation of multi-image reasoning safety, providing both quantitative metrics and qualitative insights into model behaviors and internal mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 19 MLLMs on MIR-SafetyBench yielded several crucial findings that highlight novel safety risks and provide insights into model behavior.

#### 4.1. Pervasive Vulnerability to Multi-Image Relational Attacks

- **Widespread Susceptibility:** The evaluation revealed that most of the tested MLLMs are significantly susceptible to multi-image relational reasoning attacks. The highest observed Attack Success Rate (ASR) reached 87.63%. This indicates that current safety alignment strategies are largely inadequate for risks that emerge from complex multi-image reasoning processes, going beyond simple content-based safety checks.

#### 4.2. The "Side Effects of Being Smart" Hypothesis Confirmed (with Nuances)

- **Correlation with Reasoning Capability:** For a broad range of models, stronger multi-image reasoning capabilities often correlate with higher ASR. Models optimized for multi-image processing generally exhibited higher ASRs than single-image models (e.g., Llama3-LLaVA-NeXT-8B and LLaVA-v1.5-7B had lower ASRs). Reasoning-enhanced variants (e.g., Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking vs. Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct) also typically showed increased ASR, as did models with larger parameter scales within the same family.
- **Frontier Model Exception:** A critical nuance was observed with the most capable closed-source models (e.g., GPT-5.1). These models demonstrated strong reasoning capabilities while maintaining remarkably low ASRs. This suggests that the "side effect" might not be monotonic across the entire capability spectrum; frontier models might possess sufficient robustness or more advanced safety alignment to navigate these complex tasks without compromising safety.
- **Cognitive Demand Correlation:** Vulnerability correlated with the cognitive demand of the task. Logical tasks like Decomposition and Causality, requiring abstract, multi-step thinking, consistently yielded higher ASRs across top models. In contrast, categories relying on more direct pattern recognition, such as Semantic Relevance, showed lower ASRs.

#### 4.3. Analysis of "Safe" Response Behaviors

- **Superficial Safety:** The analysis of safe responses (those not classified as harmful) revealed that many were only superficially safe, not stemming from genuine safety alignment.
 - **Lack of Correct Refusals (CR):** Most models rarely produced "Correct Refusals" that explicitly identified the harmful nature of the request. Only a subset of strong closed-source models (Gemini family, GPT-5.1) consistently achieved this.
 - **Harmless Misunderstanding (HM):** Many models appeared safe due to "Harmless Misunderstanding," failing to grasp the malicious intent and thus providing irrelevant answers. Improved prompt understanding (lower HM) often correlated with higher ASR, indicating that a model becoming "smarter" in understanding the prompt exposes its safety vulnerabilities.
 - **Incomplete Refusals (IR) and Clever Evasion (CE):** Models frequently resorted to "Incomplete Refusals" (generic, short refusals without reason) or "Clever Evasion" (understanding the harmful request but responding with unhelpful, generic scientific content without explicit warnings). These responses, while technically not harmful, lack true safety alignment as they fail to educate the user about risks.

#### 4.4. Controlled Comparison: Single-Image vs. Multi-Image

- **Increased Danger in Multi-Image Contexts:** A controlled comparison confirmed that all five tested models (including Llama3-LLaVA-NeXT-8B, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct, GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking) became significantly more dangerous when the same harmful intent was presented through multi-image relational prompts compared to single-image attacks. This decisively demonstrates that the complex multi-image relational structure is a crucial factor enabling safety bypasses.

#### 4.5. Internal Analysis via Attention Entropy

- **Concentrated Attention in Unsafe Generations:** In multi-image scenarios, unsafe generations consistently exhibited *lower attention entropy* (i.e., more concentrated attention) than safe ones across many layers and answer segments for both chat and reasoning models. This pattern was notably absent in single-image settings.
- **Hypothesis on Cognitive Overload:** This internal signature suggests a potential mechanism: when MLLMs are challenged by the cognitive load of complex multi-image reasoning, they might over-concentrate their attentional resources on solving the primary reasoning task, thereby under-allocating capacity to enforcing safety constraints. This aligns with cognitive load theory and provides a mechanistic explanation for the observed "side effects of being smart."

Overall, the findings underscore that multi-image reasoning introduces a new, challenging dimension to MLLM safety, where models' intelligence can be leveraged to bypass current safeguards, and superficial safety responses mask deeper vulnerabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) safety by identifying and systematically addressing a critical, yet previously underexplored, vulnerability: reasoning-based safety risks in multi-image contexts. The contributions and potential impact are profound across several dimensions:

1. **Establishing a New Frontier in MLLM Safety:** By introducing the concept of "reasoning-based safety" in multi-image scenarios, the paper pushes the boundaries of AI safety research beyond traditional content-based evaluations. It highlights that as MLLMs become more intelligent and capable of complex, contextual reasoning, their safety evaluation must evolve to match these advanced capacities. This work defines a new, vital area for future investigation and development in responsible AI.

2. **Introducing MIR-SafetyBench as a Foundational Tool:** The development of MIR-SafetyBench provides the AI community with the *first comprehensive and systematic benchmark* specifically tailored for multi-image reasoning safety. This benchmark, with its diverse instances across 9 relation types and 6 risk categories, is invaluable for:
 - **Reliable Evaluation:** Allowing researchers and developers to rigorously and consistently evaluate the multi-image safety of new MLLMs.
 - **Comparative Analysis:** Enabling standardized comparison of different models' robustness against sophisticated, reasoning-based attacks.
 - **Driving Development:** Serving as a crucial dataset for guiding the development of more robust safety alignment techniques that can withstand complex, multi-image adversarial prompts.

3. **Refining the Understanding of Model Safety:** The distinction between genuine safety alignment and superficial safety (e.g., Harmless Misunderstanding, Incomplete Refusal, Clever Evasion) is highly significant. It reveals that many "safe" responses from MLLMs are not indicative of robust safety but rather limitations in understanding or evasive tactics. This insight is critical for preventing overestimation of model safety and for designing true safety alignment mechanisms that incorporate explicit warnings and genuine refusal.

4. **Providing Mechanistic Insights into Safety Failures:** The novel use of attention entropy to probe MLLMs' internal states offers a preliminary, yet powerful, diagnostic tool. The finding that unsafe multi-image generations exhibit lower attention entropy suggests a potential "cognitive overload" mechanism where models prioritize task completion over safety constraints when facing complex reasoning challenges. This correlation opens avenues for:
 - **Proactive Detection:** Developing internal safety monitors that could detect states of concentrated attention indicative of potential safety risks.
 - **Architectural Guidance:** Informing the design of future MLLM architectures that can better balance reasoning capacity with safety enforcement, possibly by allocating dedicated resources or attention mechanisms for safety-critical evaluations.
 - **Advanced Alignment Strategies:** Inspiring new alignment techniques that account for cognitive load and ensure safety considerations are not neglected under high reasoning demand.

5. **Informing Ethical AI Development:** The "side effects of being smart" concept underscores the ethical imperative for responsible AI development. As MLLMs grow in capability, the potential for misuse and unintended harm increases proportionally. This research serves as a cautionary tale and a call to action, emphasizing that advanced intelligence without equally advanced safety measures poses significant societal risks. It encourages the AI community to proactively address these complex safety challenges as integral to the pursuit of more capable AI.

In conclusion, "The Side Effects of Being Smart" not only provides a robust framework for evaluating multi-image reasoning safety but also offers critical insights into the underlying mechanisms of safety failures. Its impact will likely resonate across research, development, and policy-making, fostering a more secure and responsible trajectory for the evolution of multimodal AI.
