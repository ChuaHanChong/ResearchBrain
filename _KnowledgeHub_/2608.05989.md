# Observation-Grounded Self-Predictive Reinforcement Learning for Visual Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Observation-Grounded Self-Predictive Representations (OG-SPR), developed by researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and Purdue University, introduces a model-free reinforcement learning algorithm that integrates multi-step latent self-prediction with next-observation prediction and short-term value prediction using lightweight adapters to learn dynamics-aware representations. The algorithm achieved a mean DrQv2-normalized score approximately 1.8 to 1.9 times higher than strong baselines on the DeepMind Control Suite under a 500k environment step budget.

## Problem

- Learning sample-efficient policies from high-dimensional pixel inputs for visual continuous control tasks remains a significant challenge in reinforcement learning.
- Existing dynamics-based representation learning methods either lack explicit grounding in observation-level dynamics (self-predictive) or do not enforce long-horizon temporal predictability (observation-predictive), leading to uneven performance.
- Directly imposing multi-step latent self-prediction on a shared representation used for policy and value learning can over-constrain it, hindering its ability to serve all tasks effectively.

## Method

- **OG-SPR** combines ==multi-step latent self-prediction== and ==next-observation prediction== objectives within an off-policy actor-critic framework to learn robust, data-efficient representations.
- It introduces two ==lightweight adapters== to mediate the self-prediction signal, preventing the direct imposition of the objective from over-constraining the shared representation.
- ==Short-term value prediction== is incorporated as an auxiliary task to provide lower-variance training signals and stabilize representation learning, especially with noisy value estimates.

## Results

- OG-SPR achieved the highest aggregate performance on 28 DeepMind Control Suite tasks, demonstrating a mean DrQv2-normalized score approximately **1.8x** that of MR.Q and **1.9x** that of AnonMethod.
- The method showed substantial performance gains in challenging domains, with improvements ranging from **20.9%** to **63.8%** in `dog` tasks and **61.7%** to **333.7%** in `humanoid` tasks over baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three auxiliary objectives (observation prediction, self-prediction, and short-term value prediction) contribute positively, and the two-adapter design is critical for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent self-prediction and observation prediction offer complementary inductive biases; combining them effectively leverages their individual strengths for more informative and robust representations.
> - Strategically designed lightweight adapters are crucial for integrating multiple auxiliary tasks, enabling shared representations to benefit from diverse signals without conflicting or becoming over-constrained.
> - Short-term value prediction acts as a powerful regularization, providing stable signals that significantly enhance the learning and quality of dynamics-aware representations in visual continuous control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of sample-efficient policy learning from pixel inputs in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly for visual continuous control tasks. This area has seen significant advancements, with recent model-free methods like DrQ and DrQ-v2 demonstrating potential, yet sample efficiency remains a bottleneck.

The current research landscape for data-efficient visual RL includes two prominent dynamics-based representation learning paradigms:
1. **Self-predictive methods**: These approaches learn representations by predicting future latent embeddings over multiple time steps, drawing inspiration from self-supervised learning. Examples include TD7 and MR.Q. They encourage latent representations to be temporally predictive over extended horizons.
2. **Observation-predictive methods**: These methods train representations by predicting future observations, thereby grounding the learned representations in observation-level dynamics. Examples include OFENet and AnonMethod. They explicitly align latent transitions with observation-level dynamics through future observation reconstruction.

Both categories have achieved performance competitive with or surpassing model-based RL methods (e.g., DreamerV3, TD-MPC2) in terms of data efficiency. However, the paper identifies that state-of-the-art methods from both lines often struggle in challenging visual control domains when training data is limited. The authors posit that relying on either predictive objective alone may be insufficient. Self-prediction lacks explicit grounding in observation-level dynamics, while observation prediction does not directly enforce long-horizon temporal predictability in latent space.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these two distinct lines of research, which have largely evolved separately. It aims to bridge this gap by exploring the complementary strengths of both latent self-prediction and observation prediction to enhance data efficiency in model-free visual RL, particularly for continuous control tasks. The paper also differentiates itself from model-based RL by not using a learned dynamics model for planning or value estimation, but rather for learning dynamics-aware representations within a model-free framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a model-free visual reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves improved data efficiency in continuous control tasks by learning representations that are simultaneously temporally predictive in latent space and grounded in observation-level dynamics.

The motivation for this objective stems from an identified limitation in current state-of-the-art dynamics-based representation learning methods. The authors observe that both self-predictive and observation-predictive approaches, while powerful, exhibit an uneven performance across challenging visual control tasks, especially under limited data budgets.

Specifically, the paper articulates the following motivations for combining these two predictive objectives:
- **Complementary Inductive Biases**:
 - **Latent Self-Prediction**: This objective encourages the learned latent representations to be predictive of future latent states over multiple time steps. This provides a long-horizon temporal regularization for the latent space. However, it does not inherently guarantee that these latent transitions accurately reflect the underlying observation-level dynamics.
 - **Next-Observation Prediction**: This objective grounds the learned representations directly in the observation space by requiring accurate reconstruction of future observations. This ensures that the latent dynamics are aligned with the perceived changes in the environment. However, it does not directly regularize the temporal predictability of latent representations over extended horizons.
- **Addressing Insufficiency of Single Objective**: The authors hypothesize that neither of these objectives alone provides a comprehensive inductive bias for learning robust, data-efficient representations across diverse and challenging continuous control tasks. Combining them is expected to leverage their individual strengths to form more informative and robust representations.

A secondary objective and motivation involves addressing a potential issue when integrating these objectives. The authors hypothesize that directly imposing a multi-step latent self-prediction objective on a shared representation, which is also used for policy and value learning, might over-constrain this representation. This could hinder its ability to simultaneously serve all required tasks effectively. To mitigate this, a specific architectural design featuring lightweight adapters is introduced.

Furthermore, the paper incorporates short-term value prediction as an auxiliary task. This is motivated by its ability to provide lower-variance training signals compared to bootstrapped value targets, thus stabilizing representation learning, particularly when value estimates are noisy.

In summary, the core motivation is to synthesize the benefits of both latent temporal predictability and observation-level grounding in representation learning to overcome the limitations of single-objective approaches, while also addressing architectural considerations for effective integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Observation-Grounded Self-Predictive Representations (OG-SPR), is built upon an off-policy actor-critic reinforcement learning framework. It integrates three auxiliary objectives designed to learn robust, dynamics-aware representations for visual continuous control.

**Overall Architecture:**
The agent processes an image-based state `s` through an online encoder `f`. The output of `f` is then passed through **Adapter 1** (`u1`), yielding a low-dimensional latent state representation `h`. This latent state `h` is then combined with an action `a` and fed into a state-action encoder `g` to produce a state-action representation `z`.
- `h = u1(f(s))`
- `z = g(h, a)`

The policy network utilizes `h` (with gradients detached), while the state-action representation `z` is employed for long-term value learning and the three auxiliary tasks.

**Core Components:**

1. **Value Learning**:
 - OG-SPR builds on the TD3 algorithm, utilizing two separate long-term value predictors (`Q1`, `Q2`) with identical architectures.
 - These predictors take `z` as input.
 - Target networks (`f'`, `u1'`, `g'`, `Q1'`, `Q2'`) are maintained for stable bootstrapping and are periodically synchronized with their online counterparts.
 - Smoothed target actions `a_π'` are generated by a target policy network `π'` with clipped Gaussian noise.
 - An n-step return mechanism is used to construct the value target `y`, which sums discounted rewards over `n-1` steps and bootstraps from the minimum target Q-value at `s_t+n`.
 - The Huber loss is used for value learning to mitigate bias from prioritized sampling.

2. **Policy Learning**:
 - The policy network `π` is optimized using the deterministic policy gradient.
 - An L2 regularization term is applied to the pre-activation policy outputs (`a_π`) to assist in avoiding local minima in sparse reward/value settings.
 - The policy network receives `h` (derived from `s` via `f` and `u1`) as input, but gradients from the policy loss are not propagated to `f` and `u1`.

**Auxiliary Tasks (Dynamics-Oriented Representation Learning):**

1. **Multi-step Latent Self-Prediction**:
 - This objective encourages temporal predictability in the latent space.
 - Starting from the current latent state `h_t`, future latent states (`h_t+1`..., `h_t+K`) are recursively predicted. The prediction process involves applying the state-action encoder `g` followed by **Adapter 2** (`u2`) with the corresponding actions from the sampled sequence.
 - `h_t+1_hat = u2(g(h_t, a_t))`
 - `h_t+i+1_hat = u2(g(h_t+i_hat, a_t+i))` for `i = 1, ..., K-1`.
 - **Adapter 2 (`u2`)** is crucial: it maps state-action representations `z` back to the latent state space defined by Adapter 1. This design choice prevents the self-prediction objective from being directly imposed on the shared state-action representation `z`, thereby reducing potential over-constraining. Both adapters `u1` and `u2` are designed to have consistent output spaces (same activation function).
 - Targets for latent self-prediction (`h_t+k_prime`) are computed using target networks (`f'`, `u1'`).
 - The loss is calculated using Mean Squared Error (MSE) over the prediction horizon `K`.

2. **Next-Observation Prediction**:
 - This objective grounds the representations in observation-level dynamics.
 - A decoder (`Dec`) takes the state-action representation `z_t` as input and reconstructs the next observation `o_t+1`.
 - The loss is computed using MSE.
 - For data augmentation (random shift), the same spatial shift is applied to both the input observation and its target for `L_Rec` to maintain spatial alignment, while independent shifts are applied for self-prediction targets.

3. **Short-Term Value Prediction**:
 - A predictor `q` takes `z_t` as input and forecasts the cumulative discounted reward over the next `n` steps (`R_t`).
 - This provides a lower-variance signal for representation learning and helps stabilize value estimation.
 - The loss is a cross-entropy (CE) loss between the predicted logits and a two-hot encoding of the target `R_t`, using a non-uniform reward support (symexp transform for bins).

**Combined Loss and Optimization:**
The overall training objective is a combined loss `L` that sums the value learning loss (`L_Value`) and the three auxiliary losses (`L_Rec`, `L_n-step`, `L_Self-predictive`), each weighted by a hyperparameter (`λ_Rec`, `λ_n-step`, `λ_Self-predictive`).
- `L = L_Value + λ_Rec * L_Rec + λ_n-step * L_n-step + λ_Self-predictive * L_Self-predictive`
All components of the value network (encoder, adapters, state-action encoder, predictors, decoder) are jointly optimized using `L`. Policy network parameters are updated separately based on `L_Policy`. The AdamW optimizer is used, and a LAP replay buffer facilitates prioritized sampling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of OG-SPR was conducted primarily on 28 visual continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite (DMControl) under a limited data budget of 500k environment steps. Additional evaluations were performed on Atari100k for discrete-action domains.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on DMControl

- **Aggregate Performance**: OG-SPR achieved the highest aggregate performance across the 28 DMControl tasks, as measured by both raw scores and DrQv2-normalized scores. Its mean DrQv2-normalized score was approximately 1.8 times that of MR.Q and 1.9 times that of AnonMethod, indicating substantial relative improvement over strong baselines.
- **Challenging Domains**: OG-SPR demonstrated particularly pronounced gains in challenging domains. For instance, in the `dog` domain, it achieved 20.9–63.8% improvements, and in the `humanoid` domain, improvements ranged from 61.7–333.7% over the stronger of MR.Q and AnonMethod.
- **Extended Training (Humanoid)**: Even with a relaxed training budget of 2M environment steps in the `humanoid` domain, OG-SPR maintained substantial gains over all baselines, indicating its scalability to longer training horizons in complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Computational Efficiency

- OG-SPR exhibited an increased training cost compared to its individual components. It required approximately 18.3% more training time than MR.Q.
- Despite this, OG-SPR trained substantially faster than DreamerV3 (PyTorch implementation), positioning it as a more computationally efficient alternative to some model-based methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation studies examined the contribution of each auxiliary objective and the adapter design:
- **Auxiliary Objective Contributions**: Removing any of the auxiliary objectives (observation prediction `L_Rec`, self-prediction `L_Self-predictive`, or short-term value prediction `L_n-step`) resulted in performance degradation. The most significant drop was observed when removing the self-predictive objective (OG-SPR - SP). This suggests that all three components contribute positively to the overall performance.
- **Adapter Design Importance**:
 - **Shared Adapter**: Using a single adapter for both mapping encoder features to latent state and re-mapping state-action representations to latent space (reducing flexibility) led to underperformance compared to the full OG-SPR.
 - **No Adapter**: Directly imposing the self-prediction objective on the shared representation (removing both adapters) further degraded performance, indicating that this over-constrains the shared representation.
 - **Adapter 2 Only**: Removing Adapter 1 (as in MR.Q's design) also resulted in worse performance, attributed to inconsistent output spaces between the state encoder and Adapter 2.
 These results collectively support the effectiveness of using two separate, lightweight adapters to mediate the self-prediction signal and prevent over-constraining the shared representation.

#### 4.4. Representation Analysis (Linear Probing)

An analysis of the learned representations through linear probing revealed distinct characteristics:
- **OG-SPR - SP (Observation Prediction only)**: This variant generally achieved lower `L_next-state` (loss for predicting next proprioceptive state from state-action representation). This suggests that observation prediction helps state-action representations capture more accurate dynamics information, grounding them in environmental changes.
- **OG-SPR - OP (Self-Prediction only)**: This variant tended to achieve lower `L_state` (loss for predicting current proprioceptive state from latent state representation). This indicates that self-prediction encourages state representations that are more informative for recovering the underlying proprioceptive state, emphasizing inherent state properties.
- **OG-SPR (Complete)**: The full OG-SPR demonstrated a balance, with its `L_state` close to that of OG-SPR - OP and its `L_next-state` often lying between OG-SPR - OP and OG-SPR - SP. This indicates that OG-SPR effectively combines the strengths of both objectives, leading to representations that extract useful information from current visual inputs and capture accurate dynamics information.

#### 4.5. Evaluation on Discrete-Action Tasks (Atari100k)

- When adapted with minimal modifications to the discrete-action Atari100k benchmark, OG-SPR remained competitive. It ranked first or second on three out of four aggregate metrics (Mean/IQM for Human-Normalized and SPR-Normalized scores).
- However, the performance gains were less pronounced compared to those observed in continuous control tasks. The authors suggest this may be due to the minimal adaptation strategy, implying that more extensive algorithmic modifications (similar to the Dreamer family's adaptations for discrete-action domains) might be required for robust cross-domain performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Observation-Grounded Self-Predictive Representations (OG-SPR), a model-free visual RL algorithm that aims to advance sample efficiency in continuous control. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Advancement in Data-Efficient Visual RL**: The paper demonstrates that combining multi-step latent self-prediction and next-observation prediction within an actor-critic framework can lead to improved data efficiency. This integrative approach addresses a gap in the existing literature where these two dynamics-based representation learning paradigms have largely been developed independently.
- **Architectural Innovation**: The introduction of lightweight adapters (Adapter 1 and Adapter 2) for latent self-prediction is a specific architectural contribution. This design allows the shared representation to benefit from temporal predictive signals without being overly constrained by direct imposition of the self-prediction objective, which is a novel mechanism for managing auxiliary task interactions.
- **Enhanced Performance in Challenging Domains**: The empirical results on the DeepMind Control Suite, particularly the substantial gains in complex `dog` and `humanoid` tasks under limited data, suggest that OG-SPR is effective in scenarios where previous state-of-the-art methods struggle. This indicates a step towards more robust and generalizable visual control agents.
- **Deeper Understanding of Representation Learning**: The representation analysis through linear probing provides insights into how different predictive objectives shape the information encoded in latent states and state-action representations. It confirms the complementary nature of self-prediction (for state informativeness) and observation prediction (for dynamics grounding), reinforcing the rationale for their combination.
- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The work explicitly states its role as an "initial step" towards integrating self-predictive and observation-predictive methods. This can inspire further exploration into how these and other representation learning techniques can be synergistically combined to improve model-free visual RL. Potential future directions include developing robust cross-domain mechanisms and adaptive weighting strategies for auxiliary losses, addressing the limitations identified in the paper.
- **Practical Implications**: For applications requiring learning from pixels with limited interaction data, such as robotics or autonomous systems where real-world data collection is expensive or time-consuming, OG-SPR offers a method for potentially achieving higher performance with less data.
