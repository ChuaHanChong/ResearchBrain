# Anticipating Visual Representations from Unlabeled Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Massachusetts Institute of Technology and University of Maryland, Baltimore County developed a self-supervised method to anticipate future high-level visual concepts, such as human actions and objects, by predicting abstract visual representations from unlabeled video. Their deep regression network, which explicitly models multi-modal futures, achieved up to a 19% relative gain in action forecasting accuracy and a 30% relative gain in object anticipation mean average precision over static baselines.

## Problem

- Anticipating future actions and objects in computer vision traditionally requires either low-level pixel/motion prediction or high-level semantic prediction with costly, extensive supervised labels.
- Existing methods struggle to bridge the gap between leveraging abundant unlabeled video data for low-level dynamics and predicting abstract semantic concepts without high annotation costs.
- The inherent uncertainty of future events is often not explicitly modeled by standard predictive methods, leading to single, potentially inaccurate, predictions.

## Method

- A self-supervised ==deep regression network== is trained on large amounts of unlabeled video to predict future high-level visual representations (specifically, ==fc7 features== from **AlexNet**).
- The network architecture incorporates multiple output heads (K distinct predictions) to explicitly model the multi-modal, uncertain nature of future events, trained using an ==alternating optimization strategy== similar to **EM**.
- Once future representations are predicted, standard recognition algorithms are applied, often after adapting a classifier to these predicted representations using a small set of labeled examples.

## Results

- The model achieved **43.3%** accuracy in forecasting human actions one second before they began, a **19%** relative improvement over static baselines on the TV Human Interactions dataset.
- It demonstrated a mean average precision (AP) of **10.1%** for anticipating household objects five seconds before their appearance, representing a **30%** relative gain compared to static baselines on the Activities of the Daily Living dataset.
- Using multiple output heads (**K=3**) consistently outperformed single-output models (**K=1**) for both action and object anticipation, confirming the benefit of addressing future uncertainty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting abstract visual representations (like fc7 features) from unlabeled video effectively bridges the gap between low-level temporal dynamics and high-level semantic understanding, avoiding the need for direct pixel prediction or extensive semantic labeling.
> - Explicitly modeling the multi-modal nature of future outcomes with multiple network outputs improves prediction accuracy by allowing the model to capture diverse possibilities rather than converging to a single average prediction.
> - Leveraging the temporal ordering in readily available, large-scale unlabeled video data (e.g., YouTube videos) provides a scalable and cost-effective way to train predictive models for future semantic concepts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of anticipating future actions and objects in computer vision. This task is situated within several active research areas, including video prediction, action recognition, object detection, and unsupervised or self-supervised learning from large-scale visual data.

Traditional approaches to predicting future visual events have largely fallen into two categories: those that predict low-level visual information (such as pixels or optical flow) from unlabeled video, and those that predict high-level semantic concepts (such as actions or object categories) but typically require significant amounts of supervised, labeled data. Prior work leveraging unlabeled video has focused on tasks like animating object trajectories, predicting motions, or generating future pixels. While these methods capitalize on the temporal structure inherent in videos, they often do not directly address the prediction of semantic concepts. Conversely, methods that predict actions or human paths have typically relied on labeled datasets, which are expensive and time-consuming to acquire at scale, thus limiting their generalizability and robustness.

The present research seeks to bridge this gap by developing a method that leverages the abundance of unlabeled video data to anticipate high-level semantic concepts. Instead of predicting raw pixels, which can be computationally intensive and semantically ambiguous, or relying on costly manual annotations for direct concept prediction, the authors propose predicting *visual representations* of future frames. These representations, such as the `fc7` layer of a deep convolutional neural network (CNN), are known to encode rich semantic information suitable for recognition tasks. This strategy allows the system to utilize the "free" temporal ordering of unlabeled video frames while also operating at a higher level of abstraction than pixel-based prediction.

The paper builds upon advancements in deep learning, particularly in learning rich visual representations from large image datasets (e.g., AlexNet, Places-CNN). It also draws inspiration from the broader field of unsupervised learning in vision, where methods have been developed to discover actions or objects, reconstruct storylines, or learn high-level features from large, unlabeled image and video collections. However, unlike prior work in representation learning that focuses on *learning* a visual representation, this work aims to *anticipate* a future representation. By combining these aspects, the research endeavors to offer a scalable and data-efficient approach to concept anticipation, moving beyond the limitations of purely low-level or heavily supervised methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable machines to anticipate human actions and objects before they commence or appear. This capability is identified as a difficult but crucial problem in computer vision, with potential applications across various domains.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key challenges and perceived opportunities:

- **Difficulty of Anticipation:** Anticipating future events requires a vast amount of accumulated world knowledge, which humans acquire over their lifetimes. Imbuing machines with similar predictive capabilities necessitates access to and learning from extensive data that captures the dynamics and common occurrences of the visual world.
- **Cost of Supervised Learning:** Many existing methods for semantic concept prediction rely on manually labeled data. Acquiring ground truth annotations for high-level concepts (e.g., actions, specific objects) is an expensive and time-consuming process, limiting the scalability and scope of models trained this way. The cost barrier prohibits the creation of sufficiently large and diverse datasets to capture the complexity of real-world future events.
- **Limitations of Low-Level Prediction:** While research has explored predicting future pixels or motions from unlabeled video, these low-level predictions do not directly translate into high-level semantic understanding. Predicting pixels can be inherently ambiguous and might not capture the underlying conceptual progression necessary for tasks like action forecasting.
- **Abundance of Unlabeled Video:** A significant motivation is the recognition of readily available, massive amounts of unlabeled video data online. This resource offers a rich, unexploited signal for learning temporal dynamics and contextual relationships. Unlike static images, videos inherently contain temporal ordering, which is valuable for forecasting tasks without explicit supervision.
- **Advantages of Visual Representations:** The authors propose that visual representations (e.g., deep network features) are a promising prediction target for several reasons:
 - **Semantic Encoding:** They encode images at a higher semantic level than raw pixels, containing information sufficient for recognizing concepts.
 - **Automatic Computability:** They can be automatically computed from video frames without manual annotation, making them suitable for large-scale unlabeled video training.
 - **Prediction Robustness:** Representations may be empirically easier to predict than pixels, as distance metrics in representation space tend to be more robust.
- **Leveraging Deep Learning:** The availability of large amounts of unlabeled video data motivates the use of deep networks. Deep networks are well-suited for handling massive datasets due to their high capacity and efficient training algorithms (e.g., stochastic gradient descent), allowing them to learn complex patterns from continuous streams of visual information.

In summary, the core objective is to develop a scalable, self-supervised framework that leverages the temporal structure of unlabeled video to learn predictive models of future semantic concepts, bypassing the limitations of purely supervised methods or low-level pixel prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on training a deep regression network using unlabeled video to predict future visual representations, which are subsequently used for high-level concept anticipation.

#### 3.1. Self-supervised Learning Framework

The fundamental idea is to train a function `g(x_t)` that predicts the visual representation `φ(x_t+Δ)` of a frame `x_t+Δ` in the future, given a current frame `x_t`. The model learns this predictive function `g` parameterized by `ω` by minimizing the squared Euclidean distance between its prediction and the actual future representation over a large collection of unlabeled videos:
`ω* = argmin_ω Σ_{i,t} ||g(x^i_t; ω) - φ(x^i_{t+Δ})||^2_2`
For visual representation `φ(.)`, the authors select the `fc7` layer output of AlexNet, which is recognized for its effectiveness in various recognition tasks. This choice is based on the representation's ability to capture semantic information while being automatically computable.

#### 3.2. Deep Regression Network Architecture

The predictive function `g(.)` is implemented as a deep regression network. The architecture consists of five convolutional layers followed by five fully connected layers. This design is inspired by the successful AlexNet architecture but is adapted for regression by utilizing a Euclidean loss function and incorporating three additional fully connected layers. The final layer of the network outputs the predicted future representation vector.

#### 3.3. Multi-Modal Outputs for Uncertainty

Recognizing that the future is inherently uncertain and can have multiple plausible outcomes (multi-modality), the network architecture is extended to produce `K` distinct predictions for the future representation. This addresses a limitation of standard least-squares regression, which tends to predict the mean of possible outcomes, potentially resulting in an unlikely or off-manifold representation. The system effectively trains a mixture of `K` networks, where each network aims to predict one of the possible modes in the future.

#### 3.4. Learning with Latent Variables

Training the multi-modal network poses two challenges:
1. Unlabeled videos typically show only one actual future event, making it unclear which of the `K` mixture components that event corresponds to.
2. The assignment of each training frame to one of the `K` mixtures is unknown.
To address this, the mixture assignment for each frame `(x^i_t, φ(x^i_{t+Δ}))` is treated as a latent variable `z^i_t ∈ {1, ..., K}`. The training proceeds using an alternating optimization strategy, similar to an Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm:
1. **Update Weights (E-step):** With the latent assignments `z^i_t` fixed, the network weights `ω` are updated using backpropagation to minimize the Euclidean loss for each assigned mixture component: `ω* = argmin_ω Σ_{i,t} ||g_{z^i_t}(x^i_t; ω) - φ(x^i_{t+Δ})||^2_2`.
2. **Re-estimate Assignments (M-step):** With the network weights `ω` fixed, each latent variable `z^i_t` is re-estimated by assigning it to the network `k` whose prediction `g_k(x^i_t; ω)` is closest (in Euclidean distance) to the actual future representation `φ(x^i_{t+Δ})`: `z^i_t = argmin_{k∈{1,...,K}} ||g_k(x^i_t; ω) - φ(x^i_{t+Δ})||^2_2`.
These two steps are alternated until convergence.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Parameter Sharing:** To manage the number of parameters, the first five convolutional layers and the first two fully connected layers are shared across all `K` networks. The last three fully connected layers use an interleaving strategy where each hidden unit is randomly committed to a network with a probability `p=1/2`.
- **Training Setup:** The networks are trained using stochastic gradient descent on a Tesla K40 GPU within the Caffe framework. Initial weights for the first seven layers are taken from a pre-trained Places-CNN network, while subsequent layers are initialized with Gaussian white noise. Dropout is applied to fully connected layers, and fixed learning rate and momentum are used.
- **Unlabeled Data:** The system is trained on over 600 hours of unlabeled video downloaded from YouTube (primarily television shows) and, in some experiments, videos from the THUMOS challenge.

#### 3.6. Predicting Categories

After training the regression network to predict future representations, semantic category labels (actions or objects) are attached using a relatively small set of labeled examples from the target task. Standard recognition algorithms are applied to the predicted future representations. Two strategies are explored for training the final classifier:
1. Training the classifier on ground truth `fc7` features from frames containing the category of interest and applying it to the *predicted* `fc7` representations.
2. Training the classifier directly on the *predicted* `fc7` representations, allowing it to adapt to potential structured errors in the regression.
During inference, the `K` predicted future representations each yield a distribution over categories. These distributions are then marginalized to determine the most likely future category.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework on two distinct tasks: forecasting human actions and anticipating household objects.

#### 4.1. Unlabeled Video Repository

The primary unlabeled training dataset comprised over 600 hours of publicly available television shows from YouTube. This dataset contained a wide variety of everyday human actions and object interactions. An additional dataset from the THUMOS challenge (400 hours of sports and tutorial videos) was used for diagnostic purposes to assess robustness to distribution shifts.

#### 4.2. Forecasting Actions

- **Dataset:** The TV Human Interactions dataset was used, containing 300 videos of four actions (handshake, high five, hug, kissing). Predictions were made one second before the action began.
- **Performance:** The proposed model, "Deep K=3, Adapted" (trained on YouTube videos with 3 output heads and classifier adaptation), achieved a classification accuracy of 43.3%. This represented a relative gain of approximately 19% over the "SVM Static" baseline (36.2%), which trains a classifier on frames before the action without leveraging unlabeled video for representation prediction. Other baselines, including those using nearest neighbors or linear regression on unlabeled data, or sequential models like MMED, performed lower.
- **Effect of Multiple Outputs:** The "Deep K=3, Adapted" model (43.3%) outperformed the "Deep K=1, Adapted" model (40.0%), suggesting that modeling the multi-modal nature of the future with multiple outputs is beneficial.
- **Representation Choice:** Using ActionBank as the predicted representation instead of `fc7` resulted in lower performance.
- **Training Data Source:** Training the network on the THUMOS dataset instead of YouTube videos still yielded competitive performance (43.6% with adaptation), indicating some robustness to dataset bias, although adaptation was more critical in this scenario.
- **Prediction Interval:** The relative gain of the proposed method tended to increase as the prediction time frame before the action lengthened, suggesting its utility for earlier anticipation.
- **Human Performance:** Human volunteers achieved 71.7% accuracy individually, and 85.8% accuracy via majority vote, indicating that human prediction is strong but not perfect, supporting the notion of inherent uncertainty in future prediction.
- **Qualitative Results:** Examples demonstrated successful anticipation of actions like kissing, hugging, or high-fives. Errors included confusion between similar actions (e.g., handshake vs. high five) or failures due to unexpected scene changes (e.g., a third person entering the scene).

#### 4.3. Forecasting Objects

- **Dataset:** The Activities of the Daily Living dataset, consisting of egocentric videos, was used. Three-fourths of the dataset served as an unlabeled repository for self-supervised learning, and the remaining one-fourth was used for evaluation (leave-one-out cross-validation). Objects were anticipated five seconds before their appearance.
- **Performance:** The "Deep K=3, Adapted" model achieved a mean average precision (AP) of 10.1% across various object categories. This represents a relative gain of approximately 30% over the "SVM Static" baseline (6.4%). The model demonstrated improved prediction performance for many individual categories.
- **Effect of Multiple Outputs:** Similar to action forecasting, the multi-output network (K=3) generally outperformed the single-output network (K=1), reaffirming the importance of handling uncertainty.
- **Baselines:** Scene features were also used as a baseline, performing at 8.2% mean AP, but the proposed method still showed an improvement.

Overall, the findings indicate that training deep networks to predict visual representations from large-scale unlabeled videos can significantly improve the ability to forecast high-level semantic concepts, such as human actions and objects, before they occur. The multi-modal output capability also contributed to improved performance by addressing the inherent uncertainty of future events.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in computer vision by demonstrating a scalable and self-supervised method for anticipating high-level semantic concepts from unlabeled video data. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Semantic Gap:** The work effectively bridges the gap between low-level pixel/motion prediction from unlabeled video and high-level semantic concept prediction that traditionally requires extensive supervision. By predicting abstract visual representations rather than raw pixels or directly annotated labels, the method leverages the strengths of both paradigms.
- **Leveraging Unlabeled Data:** The core innovation is the ability to acquire "world knowledge" about temporal dynamics and likely future events from the vast and ever-growing supply of unlabeled video. This bypasses the substantial cost and effort associated with manual data annotation, making the approach highly scalable and applicable to diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Enhanced Predictive Capabilities:** The demonstrated improvements in anticipating actions and objects, both quantitatively and qualitatively, indicate a step forward in endowing machines with a predictive understanding of their environment. This predictive capability moves beyond mere recognition of current events to proactive reasoning about future states.
- **Broad Applications:** The ability to forecast future events has wide-ranging implications across various domains:
 - **Robotics:** Robots could anticipate human actions for safer and more effective human-robot interaction, enabling better planning, proactive assistance, and collision avoidance.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Self-driving cars or drones could better predict pedestrian or vehicle behavior, enhancing safety and navigation.
 - **Surveillance and Security:** Predictive models could identify unusual or potentially threatening activities before they fully unfold, allowing for earlier intervention.
 - **Recommendation Systems:** Anticipating user actions or needs could lead to more accurate and timely suggestions for products, services, or content.
 - **Healthcare:** Monitoring patient activities in assisted living environments could provide early warnings for falls or other adverse events.
- **Generalizability of Approach:** The methodology is presented as general enough to predict various visual representations, not just `fc7` features. This flexibility allows for adaptation to different types of downstream recognition tasks and potentially more advanced representations as they emerge.
- **Addressing Uncertainty:** The explicit modeling of multi-modal futures is a crucial aspect, acknowledging that the future is not deterministic. This provides a more realistic and robust predictive framework, offering a distribution of possibilities rather than a single, potentially erroneous, prediction.

In conclusion, this research contributes a novel framework that significantly advances the capability of machines to anticipate future semantic events by efficiently learning from abundant unlabeled video. This could lead to more intelligent, proactive, and context-aware artificial intelligence systems with substantial real-world impact.
