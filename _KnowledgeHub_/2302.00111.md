# Learning Universal Policies via Text-Guided Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniPi proposes a universal policy representation as text-conditioned video generation, enabling agents to learn from internet-scale data and generalize across diverse tasks and environments. It demonstrates superior combinatorial generalization and multi-environment transfer in robot manipulation, achieving up to 60.1% accuracy on novel language prompts and effectively transferring knowledge to real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Difficulty in sharing knowledge and generalizing policies across diverse environments with unique state and action spaces.
- Challenge of designing precise, quantifiable reward functions for varied and complex real-world tasks.
- Limitations in leveraging vast, unstructured internet-scale video and text data for learning general-purpose decision-making agents.

## Method

- Formulates sequential decision-making as a ==Unified Predictive Decision Process (UPDP)== that uses images as a universal observation/action interface and text as a universal task specifier.
- Employs a universal video-based planner, instantiated as a ==text-to-video diffusion model==, to synthesize future visual trajectories (plans) given an initial observation and textual task description.
- Utilizes a separate, smaller ==task-specific inverse dynamics model== to translate the synthesized video plans into a sequence of executable actions for a given robot or environment.

## Results

- Achieved superior combinatorial generalization, outperforming baselines (e.g., Diffuser) significantly on novel language prompts for robot manipulation (e.g., **60.1%** vs. **12.5%** accuracy on novel "Place" tasks).
- Demonstrated strong multi-environment transfer, generalizing to unseen CLIPort tasks with substantial improvements over baselines (e.g., **51.6%** vs. **14.8%** on "Place Bowl").
- Pretraining on internet-scale video data improved the quality of generated video plans for real robots and enabled generalization to novel real-world commands and scenes, resulting in a **77.1%** success rate on robotic tasks with pretraining vs. **72.6%** without.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Casting sequential decision-making as text-conditioned video generation provides a universal policy representation that bypasses environment-specific state/action spaces and explicit reward functions.
> - Large-scale video diffusion models can effectively serve as universal planners, synthesizing coherent visual trajectories for diverse tasks and environments.
> - Decoupling a universal visual planner from a task-specific action adaptation module enables leveraging vast unstructured internet data for general planning and smaller datasets for precise action grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Learning Universal Policies via Text-Guided Video Generation" addresses one of the grand challenges in artificial intelligence: the creation of general-purpose agents capable of solving a wide variety of tasks across diverse environments. This ambition mirrors recent successes in natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV), where large pre-trained models (e.g., GPT, PaLM in NLP; CLIP, Flamingo, Diffusion Models in CV) have demonstrated remarkable zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities.

Historically, reinforcement learning (RL) has focused on training agents for specific tasks within isolated environments. However, the inherent diversity of environments—each with distinct state and action spaces (e.g., continuous joint controls in MuJoCo vs. discrete actions in Atari)—has largely prevented knowledge sharing and generalization across them. Traditional Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), while powerful for formalizing sequential decision-making, struggle with this diversity due to their reliance on environment-specific state representations, explicit real-valued reward functions, and dynamics models. Efforts to bridge this gap include:

- **Universal Tokenization:** Approaches like Google DeepMind's "Generalist Agent" (Reed et al., 2022) attempt to unify diverse environments using universal token sets in a sequence modeling framework. While promising, this can be complex to implement and may not fully leverage the rich, pre-existing knowledge embedded in vision and language models.
- **World Models:** Other lines of research focus on learning comprehensive "world models" that capture environmental dynamics (Hafner et al., 2020; Janner et al., 2021). While these models enable planning, they often require data in a strict state-action-reward format, limiting their ability to exploit vast, unlabeled internet datasets like YouTube videos. Furthermore, they separate world modeling from policy learning, potentially introducing a mismatch.
- **Language-Conditioned Policies:** A growing body of work trains policies directly conditioned on natural language instructions (Brohan et al., 2022; Ahn et al., 2022). However, these methods typically operate within the action space of a *specific* robot or environment, limiting their cross-morphology or multi-environment generalization.

UniPi distinguishes itself by proposing a radical shift in perspective: casting the sequential decision-making problem as a **text-conditioned video generation problem**. This formulation offers a novel solution to the problem of environmental diversity and reward specification by leveraging two naturally universal interfaces:
1. **Video (image sequences):** As a universal interface for conveying observation and action behavior across *any* visual environment, irrespective of underlying state or action spaces.
2. **Text:** As a universal interface for expressing complex and combinatorial task descriptions, bypassing the need for explicit reward function design.

This approach effectively bypasses the limitations of traditional MDPs by creating a **Unified Predictive Decision Process (UPDP)**. Furthermore, it capitalizes on the recent breakthroughs in **video diffusion models** (Ho et al., 2022; Villegas et al., 2022). By leveraging these powerful generative models, UniPi aims to unlock knowledge transfer from internet-scale text-video datasets, which are not typically structured for RL, directly into capable decision-making policies. While diffusion models have been applied to decision-making before (Janner et al., 2022; Ajay et al., 2022), UniPi's distinct contribution lies in using *text-conditioned video generation* as the *universal policy itself*, enabling generalization across tasks, environments, and even knowledge transfer from human-generated internet data to robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance towards the creation of a **general-purpose artificial agent** capable of solving a wide and diverse array of tasks across various environments. This overarching goal is motivated by fundamental challenges that limit the scalability and applicability of current reinforcement learning and robotics paradigms.

The key motivations and objectives addressed by UniPi are:

1. **Overcoming Environmental Diversity:** Traditional decision-making agents are typically trained for specific environments, each with its unique state and action spaces (e.g., robot joint angles vs. pixel-based game states). This heterogeneity makes it exceedingly difficult to share knowledge, learn generalizable skills, or transfer policies across different domains. UniPi aims to resolve this by proposing a universal representation for both observations and actions.
2. **Bypassing Reward Function Design:** In many real-world applications, designing precise, quantifiable reward functions for diverse tasks is arduous, impractical, or even impossible. UniPi seeks to eliminate this bottleneck by leveraging natural language as the primary means of task specification, allowing for high-level, human-interpretable goal setting without the need for handcrafted rewards.
3. **Enabling Combinatorial Generalization:** The ability to combine known concepts and skills in novel ways (combinatorial generalization) is a hallmark of human intelligence but remains a significant challenge for AI. By grounding policies in the compositional nature of language and the visual expressiveness of video, UniPi aims to synthesize new behaviors from novel combinations of goals and environmental elements.
4. **Facilitating Multi-Task Learning:** To be truly general-purpose, an agent must learn from and perform across a multitude of tasks. The paper aims to demonstrate UniPi's capacity to learn general policies that can be applied to many different tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.
5. **Leveraging Internet-Scale Knowledge:** A vast amount of human knowledge and behavior is encoded in internet videos and associated text. A crucial objective is to tap into this immense, readily available resource to pre-train policies, enabling knowledge transfer that goes beyond typical curated robotics datasets and allowing for the generation of realistic plans for real robots from high-level instructions.
6. **Developing a Unified Planning Mechanism:** UniPi aims to present a unified planning strategy that outputs visually interpretable plans (videos) from which control actions can be extracted. This design choice aims to implicitly perform hierarchical planning and allow for flexible, steerable plan generation at test time, offering a more intuitive and inspectable planning process compared to direct action prediction.

In essence, UniPi's core motivation is to move away from environment-specific, reward-driven policy learning towards a more universal, language-guided, and visually grounded approach, paving the way for truly generalist AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of UniPi revolves around the novel formulation of sequential decision-making as a **Unified Predictive Decision Process (UPDP)**, an alternative to the traditional Markov Decision Process (MDP). This abstraction leverages images as a universal observation/action interface and text as a universal task specifier.

#### 3.1. Unified Predictive Decision Process (UPDP) Formulation

A UPDP is defined as a tuple G = ⟨X, C, H, ρ⟩:
- **X:** The observation space of images.
- **C:** The space of textual task descriptions.
- **H:** A finite horizon length.
- **ρ(·|x₀, c):** A conditional video generator that outputs an H-step image sequence (τ = [x₁..., x_H]) given an initial frame (x₀) and a task description (c). This generator effectively *plans* a visual trajectory to achieve the task.

Following the video generation, a **trajectory-task conditioned policy π(·|{x_h}ₕ=₀ʰ, c)** infers an H-step action sequence from the generated video and the task description. This setup allows for:
- **Bypassing Reward Design:** The textual description `c` implicitly defines the goal, removing the need for explicit reward functions.
- **Universal Interfaces:** `X` (images) and `C` (text) are inherently universal and human-interpretable, enabling knowledge sharing across diverse environments.
- **Separation of Planning and Control:** `ρ` (the video planner) is environment and agent agnostic, generating visual plans. `π` (the action adaptation module) is task-specific, translating visual plans into concrete actions for a given robot/environment.

#### 3.2. Instantiation with Diffusion Models

UniPi leverages the recent advancements in **diffusion models** to implement the conditional video generator ρ. Diffusion models are powerful generative models capable of capturing complex conditional distributions. For UniPi, the model is trained to learn the conditional denoiser `s(τ_k, k|c, x₀)`, where `τ_k` is a noisy video and `k` is the diffusion timestep. Classifier-free guidance is used to control the strength of text and first-frame conditioning during sampling.

#### 3.3. UniPi Architecture: Universal Video-Based Planner and Task-Specific Action Adaptation

UniPi consists of two main components:

**1. Universal Video-Based Planner (ρ):** This component is a text-to-video diffusion model that synthesizes future image frames (the plan) given an initial observation (`x₀`) and a textual task description (`c`).
 - **Conditional Video Synthesis:** Unlike unconstrained text-to-video models, UniPi explicitly trains the video diffusion model by providing the first frame of each video (`x₀`) as an explicit conditioning context during training. This ensures the generated video plan accurately starts from the current observed state.
 - **Trajectory Consistency through Tiling:** To maintain environmental consistency and avoid visual "hallucinations" or changes inconsistent with the starting state, the observed initial image (`x₀`) is tiled across time and provided as additional context during the denoising process for each frame. This acts as a strong signal to maintain the underlying environment state.
 - **Hierarchical Planning:** The video generation process inherently supports hierarchical planning. UniPi first generates videos at a coarse temporal resolution (sparsely sampled "abstractions") and then refines them using temporal super-resolution to produce a dense, detailed plan. This coarse-to-fine approach improves temporal consistency.
 - **Flexible Behavioral Modulation:** The planning process can be adapted at test time by composing external constraints or priors (e.g., guiding towards an intermediate image). This is implemented by leveraging the flexibility of diffusion sampling.
 - **Language Encoding:** Pretrained T5 language embeddings are used to encode the textual instructions, ensuring rich semantic understanding.

**2. Task-Specific Action Adaptation (π):** Once a video plan ({x_h}ₕ=₀ʰ) is synthesized by the planner, a separate, smaller model translates these visual frames into a sequence of executable actions.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model:** A compact inverse dynamics model is trained to predict robot actions given a sequence of input images. This model is trained independently of the video planner, potentially on a smaller, potentially suboptimal dataset from a simulator or real robot.
 - **Action Execution:** For computational efficiency, an open-loop control strategy is employed in experiments, where an entire sequence of actions is inferred from the initial video plan and then executed sequentially.

#### 3.4. Training and Data

- **Video Diffusion Model Training:** Trained on a large dataset of videos with associated text, including internet-scale data (14M video-text pairs, 60M image-text pairs from Imagen Video, LAION-400M) and specific robotic datasets (e.g., Bridge dataset with 7.2k video-text pairs for real-world transfer).
- **Inverse Dynamics Model Training:** Trained on a smaller subset of videos that include action annotations, often generated by a scripted agent in simulation.

This decoupled architecture allows UniPi to leverage massive, uncurated internet data for the complex video generation task, while relying on smaller, specific datasets for action grounding, effectively bypassing the need for explicit reward functions and unified state/action spaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of UniPi focused on its ability to enable effective, generalizable decision-making across three key scenarios: combinatorial generalization, multi-environment transfer, and real-world transfer.

#### 4.1. Combinatorial Policy Synthesis (Generalization to Novel Language)

- **Setup:** Evaluated on robot manipulation tasks involving placing colored blocks in specific geometric relations ("put a red block right of a cyan block"). The language instructions were split into seen (70%) and novel (30%) combinations. Actions were predicted in the continuous robotic joint space.
- **Baselines:** Compared against Transformer-based Behavior Cloning (BC) agents (state-conditioned and image-conditioned), Trajectory Transformer (TT) for sequence prediction, and Diffusion models that directly predict actions (Diffuser).
- **Findings:**
 - **Superior Generalization:** UniPi significantly outperformed all baselines in task completion accuracy, especially on *novel* combinations of language prompts for both "Place" and "Relation" tasks (e.g., 60.1% vs. 12.5% for Diffuser on novel Place; 46.1% vs. 9.6% for Diffuser on novel Relation). This demonstrates its strong combinatorial generalization capability, synthesizing complex behaviors not directly seen during training.
 - **Ablation Study:** Ablations showed that all key architectural components—first-frame conditioning, trajectory consistency through tiling, and hierarchical temporal super-resolution—were crucial for UniPi's performance, progressively improving task completion accuracy.
 - **Adaptability:** UniPi demonstrated the ability to adapt its planning at test time by guiding the diffusion sampling process towards intermediate images, enabling specific block manipulation.
 - **Visual Fidelity:** Generated video plans for unseen language goals were visually coherent and aligned well with executed robot actions.

#### 4.2. Multi-Environment Transfer (Generalization to Unseen Tasks/Environments)

- **Setup:** Evaluated on the CLIPort suite of language-guided manipulation tasks. UniPi was trained on demonstrations from 10 diverse training tasks and tested on 3 entirely different, unseen test tasks (e.g., "put-block-in-bowl", "packing-unseen-google-objects").
- **Baselines:** Same as combinatorial tasks (Transformer BC, Transformer TT, Diffuser).
- **Findings:**
 - **Strong Multi-Task Generalization:** UniPi again substantially outperformed all baselines across the unseen test tasks (e.g., 51.6% vs. 14.8% for Diffuser on "Place Bowl"; 75.5% vs. 15.9% for Diffuser on "Pack Object"). This highlights its ability to learn universal skills from a diverse set of tasks and generalize to new ones without fine-tuning.
 - **Unified Representation Effectiveness:** The results validate the premise that video and text serve as effective universal interfaces, enabling knowledge transfer across different manipulation scenarios.
 - **High Fidelity Plans:** UniPi successfully generated high-resolution video plans for diverse, unseen language prompts across various environments.

#### 4.3. Real World Transfer (Leveraging Internet-Scale Knowledge)

- **Setup:** Investigated the impact of pretraining on a large internet-scale video-text dataset (Imagen Video data) before fine-tuning on a smaller real-world robotic dataset (Bridge dataset).
- **Metrics:** Standard video generation metrics (CLIP score, FID, FVD) and a novel "success classifier" metric trained on the last frame of generated videos to infer task completion.
- **Findings:**
 - **Benefit of Internet Pretraining:** Pretraining on non-robot, internet-scale data significantly improved the quality of generated video plans for real robots, leading to lower FID and FVD scores (better realism and temporal consistency) and a higher success rate (77.1% vs. 72.6% without pretraining).
 - **Generalization to Novel Real-World Tasks:** Pretraining enabled UniPi to generalize to novel task commands and scenes *not present* in the Bridge dataset's training split. Models trained from scratch often failed or generated incorrect plans for these novel tasks.
 - **Robustness:** UniPi showed relative robustness to background changes and visual distractions, indicating a focus on task-relevant dynamics.
 - **High Fidelity Real Robot Motion:** UniPi successfully synthesized realistic robot motion videos from language instructions, demonstrating the practical applicability of the approach.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate UniPi's superior generalization capabilities across different axes: novel language combinations, unseen environments, and real-world scenarios by effectively leveraging large-scale internet data. The formulation of policy as text-conditioned video generation proves to be a powerful paradigm for learning general-purpose agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Learning Universal Policies via Text-Guided Video Generation" represents a significant step towards the development of truly general-purpose artificial intelligence agents, particularly in embodied AI and robotics. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **A Novel Paradigm for Policy Representation:** By casting sequential decision-making as text-conditioned video generation, UniPi offers a fresh perspective that deviates from traditional reinforcement learning paradigms. This "policy-as-video" formulation naturally addresses several long-standing challenges in RL, such as the need for explicit reward functions and the difficulty of handling diverse state and action spaces across environments.
2. **Unlocking Knowledge Transfer from Unstructured Data:** The ability to effectively leverage massive, uncurated internet-scale video and text data for policy learning is a groundbreaking contribution. This moves beyond reliance on meticulously collected, task-specific, or reward-annotated datasets, opening up a vast new source of "demonstrations" for learning complex behaviors. This "data-rich" approach, analogous to the success of large language models, has the potential to accelerate progress in robotics where data collection is often a bottleneck.
3. **Superior Generalization Capabilities:** The experimental results convincingly demonstrate UniPi's strong generalization across multiple dimensions:
 - **Combinatorial Generalization:** The ability to synthesize novel behaviors from unseen combinations of language instructions is crucial for flexible and adaptive agents.
 - **Multi-Task Learning:** Learning a single policy that performs well across diverse environments and tasks, including unseen ones, is a hallmark of intelligent systems.
 - **Real-World Transfer:** Bridging the gap between internet data and real robot control, leading to more robust and capable real-world robotic systems. This is particularly impactful for reducing sim-to-real transfer challenges.
4. **Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** Video plans are inherently human-interpretable. This allows for easy verification of planned trajectories, diagnosis of failures, and a more intuitive understanding of the agent's decision-making process, a critical feature for building trustworthy and reliable AI systems.
5. **Decoupled and Flexible Architecture:** The separation of the universal video planner from the task-specific inverse dynamics model offers architectural flexibility. The planner can be broadly pre-trained, while the smaller inverse dynamics model can be efficiently fine-tuned for specific robotic platforms or action spaces. This modularity enhances scalability and adaptability.
6. **Advancing Towards Generalist Agents:** This work contributes directly to the vision of "generalist agents" – AI systems that can acquire and apply a wide range of skills. By demonstrating a path to unified learning across heterogeneous environments using universal interfaces, UniPi provides a blueprint for future research in general artificial intelligence.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Robotics:** Revolutionize how robots learn new skills, potentially leading to more versatile and adaptable robots that can perform complex, high-level tasks based on natural language instructions without extensive re-programming or task-specific training.
- **Embodied AI:** Provide a robust framework for agents operating in diverse simulated or real-world environments, enabling them to understand and execute tasks specified in natural language.
- **AI Foundation Models:** Further solidifies the role of large-scale generative models (like diffusion models) as powerful "foundation models" not just for content creation, but for enabling intelligent decision-making and control.

**Limitations and Future Work:** While highly promising, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the computational slowness of current video diffusion processes (addressable by distillation or faster samplers) and potential challenges in partially observable environments (hallucinations). Integrating UniPi with large language models (LLMs) for deeper semantic understanding and leveraging structured knowledge about the world are highlighted as exciting future directions.

Overall, UniPi marks a significant conceptual and empirical advance, demonstrating that universal policies can be learned through the lens of text-guided video generation, paving the way for more general, adaptable, and human-interpretable AI systems.
