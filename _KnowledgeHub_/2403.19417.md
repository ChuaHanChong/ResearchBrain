# OAKINK2: A Dataset of Bimanual Hands-Object Manipulation in Complex Task Completion

> [!summary] Summary
> OAKINK2 from Shanghai Jiao Tong University provides a large-scale, real-world dataset of bimanual human-object manipulation, featuring 4.01 million frames with detailed 3D pose and a 3-level hierarchical task annotation. This resource supports the development of robotic systems capable of understanding and executing complex daily tasks through a demonstrated framework for task interpretation and motion generation.

## Problem

- Robotic systems lack a human-level understanding of how to appropriately interact with objects in sequence to achieve desired outcomes in complex daily tasks.
- Existing datasets for robotic manipulation often focus on isolated actions or simple interactions, failing to capture the full complexity of multi-step, bimanual tasks involving multiple objects.
- A gap exists in bridging high-level task objectives with the low-level motions required for dexterous manipulation, especially for bimanual actions.

## Method

- Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed OAKINK2, a comprehensive dataset of real-world bimanual human-object manipulation in diverse environments like kitchens and study rooms.
- The dataset introduces a 3-level hierarchical task representation (affordance, primitive, and complex tasks) to systematically organize and annotate manipulation activities.
- A Complex Task Completion (CTC) framework and a Task-aware Motion Fulfillment (TaMF) model were developed, leveraging LLMs to interpret tasks and generate bimanual hand motions conditioned on object trajectories.

## Results

- OAKINK2 comprises 627 sequences and approximately 4.01 million frames of bimanual human-object manipulation, featuring 100 diverse household objects and precise 3D pose information for hands, body, and objects.
- The Complex Task Completion (CTC) framework, enabled by OAKINK2, demonstrates the ability to decompose complex tasks into primitive actions and generate corresponding bimanual hand motions.
- Benchmarks for hand mesh reconstruction and Task-aware Motion Fulfillment (TaMF) models were established, providing standardized metrics for evaluating performance in hand pose estimation and motion synthesis tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical task representation, spanning affordances, primitive tasks, and complex tasks, provides a structured framework for decomposing and understanding intricate manipulation activities.
> - Collecting large-scale, real-world bimanual human demonstration data, complete with multi-modal information and precise pose tracking, is essential for advancing robotic dexterity and generalization.
> - Integrating large language models with motion generation allows for interpreting high-level task instructions and synthesizing appropriate low-level hand movements for complex manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The ability to manipulate objects with dexterity and purpose is a fundamental aspect of human intelligence that remains challenging for robotic systems. While substantial progress has been made in robotic manipulation, current approaches still lack a human-level understanding of how to appropriately interact with objects in sequence to achieve desired outcomes.

OAKINK2 is a dataset developed by researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University that aims to address these limitations by providing a comprehensive resource for studying bimanual hands-object manipulation in complex task scenarios. Unlike previous datasets that may focus on isolated actions or simple interactions, OAKINK2 captures the full complexity of multi-step tasks that humans perform routinely but robots find challenging.

### 2. Dataset Overview

OAKINK2 is a large-scale, real-world dataset containing 627 sequences with approximately 4.01 million frames of bimanual object manipulation. The dataset captures humans performing a variety of complex daily tasks requiring the coordination of both hands to manipulate multiple objects.

Key features of the dataset include:

- Multi-view image streams from 4 RGB cameras
- 3D pose information for human body, hands, and objects captured by 12 Optitrack Prime 13W infrared cameras
- Precise annotations including object affordances, primitive tasks, complex tasks, and expert commentary
- A diverse object repository with approximately 100 household objects

The dataset captures interactions in three primary environments:
1. Kitchen table settings with cooking and food preparation tasks
2. Study room table settings with office-related tasks
3. Chemistry lab settings with experimental procedures

This diversity allows for the study of manipulation skills across different contexts and domains, making it particularly valuable for developing generalizable robotics capabilities.

### 3. Hierarchical Task Representation

A core contribution of OAKINK2 is its introduction of a structured 3-level abstraction for organizing manipulation tasks:

1. **Affordance Level (Level 1)**: Captures the potential functionalities of objects, such as `<contain, something>`, `<secure, something>`, or `<heat, something>`. These represent the basic ways humans can interact with an object.

2. **Primitive Task Level (Level 2)**: Represents basic manipulation actions like "unscrew container lid" or "pour out content." These are the fundamental building blocks of more complex activities.

3. **Complex Task Level (Level 3)**: Describes complete activities composed of multiple primitive tasks, such as "prepare a bowl of hot sweet fruit tea." This level includes dependency graphs showing the relationships between primitive tasks.

This hierarchical structure provides a comprehensive framework for understanding and decomposing complex manipulation tasks into manageable components. It creates a bridge between high-level task objectives and the low-level motions required to accomplish them.

### 4. Data Collection Process

The data collection for OAKINK2 was conducted in a controlled laboratory environment using a sophisticated capture platform. The setup included:

- 12 Optitrack Prime 13W infrared cameras for accurate motion tracking
- 4 RGB cameras to capture visual information from multiple angles
- A table surface where manipulation tasks were performed
- Tracked markers on the human body, hands, and objects

Participants performed a wide range of tasks following specific protocols. For complex tasks, they were given verbal instructions describing the goal (e.g., "prepare a bowl of hot sweet fruit tea"), but were not provided with step-by-step directions on how to accomplish it. This approach allowed for natural decision-making and execution strategies.

The tasks were designed to cover a variety of manipulation skills, including:
- Cutting and slicing
- Pouring and mixing
- Screwing and unscrewing
- Pressing buttons and operating controls
- Assembling and arranging items

### 5. Motion Capture and Annotation

The motion capture system tracked the 3D positions of markers attached to the participants' bodies, hands, and objects. This raw data was then processed to reconstruct:

1. Full body pose using the SMPL-X body mesh model
2. Detailed hand poses for both hands
3. Object 6D poses throughout the manipulation sequence

Each sequence in the dataset was annotated with:

- Object affordances identifying what functions each object can perform
- Primitive task labels for individual manipulation actions
- Complex task descriptions linking the primitive tasks together
- Expert commentary providing insights on task execution

The dataset includes a comprehensive collection of primitive task types, as shown above. This rich taxonomy of manipulation actions covers the fundamental building blocks needed to perform a wide range of complex tasks.

### 6. Complex Task Completion Framework

Based on the OAKINK2 dataset, the authors developed a Complex Task Completion (CTC) framework that aims to generate appropriate hand motions for executing complex manipulation tasks. The framework consists of three main components:

1. **LLM-based Task Interpreter**: Uses a large language model to decompose complex task objectives into sequences of primitive tasks.

2. **Object Trajectories Retrieval**: Obtains object motion trajectories needed for each primitive task.

3. **Motion Fulfillment Model**: Generates bimanual hand motions that can execute the primitive tasks while handling the objects appropriately.

The framework is demonstrated through examples like the one above, where the complex task of preparing a sweet pear soup is broken down into primitive tasks including cutting, uncapping, scooping, pouring, and unscrewing. For each primitive task, the system identifies the current state of the environment, determines the appropriate action, and generates the corresponding hand motions.

### 7. Task Aware Motion Fulfillment

A key contribution of the research is the introduction of Task-aware Motion Fulfillment (TaMF), a new task focusing on the generation of hand motion sequences that fulfill given object trajectories conditioned on textual task descriptions.

The TaMF model uses a transformer-based architecture to encode both the task description and the object trajectory information. It then generates appropriate hand motions that can accomplish the specified manipulation task. The model is trained on the rich motion data from the OAKINK2 dataset, learning to associate textual task descriptions with the corresponding hand-object interactions.

This approach allows the system to:
- Understand the semantic meaning of tasks
- Generate contextually appropriate hand poses and motions
- Adapt to different objects and manipulation requirements
- Coordinate bimanual actions for complex manipulations

### 8. Evaluation and Benchmarks

The authors established benchmarks for hand mesh reconstruction using the OAKINK2 dataset in both single-view and multi-view settings. These benchmarks provide a standard for evaluating the performance of algorithms on tasks related to:

- Hand pose estimation during object manipulation
- Hand-object interaction modeling
- Motion synthesis for manipulation tasks

Additionally, the paper introduces evaluation metrics for the Task-aware Motion Fulfillment (TaMF) model, assessing how well the generated hand motions:
- Match the ground truth motions (using metrics like Mean Per Joint Position Error)
- Achieve the intended manipulation objectives
- Maintain physical plausibility and contact relationships

These benchmarks and evaluations provide a foundation for future research on bimanual manipulation, offering standardized metrics for comparing different approaches.

### 9. Applications and Future Directions

The OAKINK2 dataset and associated framework open up several promising directions for future research:

1. **Large-scale Language-Manipulation Pre-training**: The dataset can support the development of models that integrate language understanding with manipulation skills, similar to how vision-language models have advanced in recent years.

2. **End-to-End Text-to-Manipulation Generation**: The hierarchical representation of tasks could enable systems that can directly generate appropriate manipulation sequences from natural language instructions.

3. **Embodiment Transfer**: The captured demonstrations could be retargeted to different embodiments, such as robotic hands or alternative manipulation platforms with different physical characteristics.

4. **Simulation Integration**: The interaction scenarios can be transferred to simulation environments to support embodied learning for object manipulation without the need for physical robots.

The framework shown above illustrates how the system can take a complex task objective, decompose it into primitive actions using an LLM, retrieve appropriate object trajectories, and generate the hand motions needed to execute the task. This approach provides a blueprint for developing more capable and flexible robotic manipulation systems.

### 10. Conclusion

OAKINK2 represents a significant advancement in the study of complex manipulation tasks by providing a comprehensive dataset that captures the intricacies of bimanual object interactions in real-world settings. The 3-level abstraction introduced in the dataset offers a structured way to understand and decompose complex tasks, facilitating the development of more capable robotic systems.

The dataset's rich annotations and multi-modal data (including 3D poses, RGB images, and textual descriptions) make it a valuable resource for researchers working on various aspects of robotic manipulation, from low-level motion generation to high-level task planning. The accompanying Complex Task Completion framework and Task-aware Motion Fulfillment model demonstrate how the dataset can be leveraged to develop systems capable of understanding and executing complex manipulation tasks.

By bridging the gap between language understanding, task planning, and motion generation, OAKINK2 contributes to the advancement of robotics research toward human-level manipulation capabilities. The dataset's focus on bimanual manipulation of everyday objects in complex task scenarios addresses a critical gap in existing datasets and provides a foundation for developing more versatile and capable robotic systems.
