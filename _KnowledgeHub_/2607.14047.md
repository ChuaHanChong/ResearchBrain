# Zero2Skill: Bootstrapping Robot Skills through Autonomous Data Collection, Training, and Deployment

> [!summary] Summary
> Zero2Skill introduces a human-robot symbiotic system that significantly reduces human effort for robotic manipulation data collection, achieving a six-fold reduction in human working time and 10.42 trajectories-per-human-minute. The system leverages language-guided persistent corrections to improve perception and execution, resulting in collected data that enables policies to perform comparably to those trained on human-teleoperated data.

## Problem

- High-volume, high-quality real-world demonstration data is a bottleneck for developing advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics.
- Existing data collection methods are either labor-intensive (requiring continuous human teleoperation) or suffer from silent degradation and recurring long-tail failures in autonomous modes.
- Human interventions in current interactive learning systems are often transient, meaning corrections apply only to the immediate episode and recurring problems require repeated human oversight.

## Method

- An autonomous ==collect-and-reset loop== is implemented, where ==VLM-based verification== rigorously checks both collection and reset phases, halting for human intervention upon persistent failures.
- A ==language-guided intervention== mechanism allows remote operators to provide free-form natural language feedback to correct perception (verifier) or execution strategies when the system alerts.
- A ==Corrective Memory== stores persistent, structured rules derived from natural language feedback, enabling the system to adapt its behavior in future similar situations without requiring VLA model retraining.

## Results

- Zero2Skill reduced human working time by approximately **six-fold** (from **30.0** to **4.8 minutes**) for **50** episodes compared to full human teleoperation, achieving a **10.42** Trajectories-per-Human-Minute (TpHM).
- Language-guided corrections significantly improved VLM verifier accuracy (e.g., from **40.0%** to **100.0%** for 'Basket desktop clearing') and robot execution success rates (e.g., from **12.5%** to **47.5%** for grasp depth).
- Policies fine-tuned on Zero2Skill-collected data achieved an **80.0%** success rate, matching policies trained on full human teleoperation data and outperforming a scripted collector (**55.0%**), validating the utility of the autonomously collected and certified demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly retaining and applying human-provided natural language corrections through a 'Corrective Memory' allows for persistent knowledge accumulation, significantly reducing repeated human interventions over time.
> - Adaptive VLM-based verification, which can be refined by language, is crucial for maintaining high data quality and ensuring that autonomously collected trajectories align with human intent.
> - A symbiotic human-robot approach, where humans provide high-level, persistent corrections and robots autonomously collect data, can achieve efficiency gains without compromising the quality of training data for downstream policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant advancement in general-purpose robotic manipulation, offering the capability to translate raw perception and language instructions into motor commands. The performance of these models is significantly dependent on the volume and quality of real-world demonstration data. Consequently, data acquisition has become a primary bottleneck in further progress in this field.

Existing approaches to data collection span a spectrum from fully manual teleoperation to entirely autonomous pipelines. Teleoperation systems, such as ALOHA and UMI, yield high-fidelity trajectories but demand continuous human attention, making data collection costly and time-consuming. At the other extreme, fully autonomous systems like RoboClaw and RADAR employ self-resetting mechanisms, VLM-based verification, or heuristic scripts to minimize human involvement. However, these systems often struggle with long-tail physical failures (e.g., perception drift, object displacement, strategy deadlocks) and can degrade silently over extended operation, with autonomous data sometimes yielding less improvement than human demonstrations.

Between these two extremes are interactive approaches that aim to optimize human effort. Methods like ThriftyDAgger and Fleet-DAgger route operator attention to uncertain states or manage multi-robot fleets. Language-guided correction methods (e.g., YAY Robot, DROC, IROSA) enable operators to refine robot behavior through natural-language feedback during single-task execution. A common limitation across many of these interactive and autonomous systems is the transient nature of human feedback; corrections typically apply only to the immediate episode or are implicitly learned into policy updates, rather than being retained as explicit, reusable rules. This means that recurring failure modes often require repeated human interventions.

Zero2Skill addresses this specific gap by proposing a human-robot symbiotic system where natural-language corrections are explicitly retained and reused across subsequent rounds. This approach aims to reduce human oversight costs by addressing distinct problems once, rather than repeatedly intervening for recurring failures. It leverages modern LLM-based agentic systems, which can interpret open-ended language, orchestrate tools, and maintain persistent memory, to enable the storage and application of corrective knowledge without requiring retraining of the underlying VLA model for every adjustment. This work positions itself as a step towards a multi-round data flywheel, where accumulated operational knowledge in language enhances system autonomy and improves data quality for subsequent policy training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Zero2Skill is to establish a human-robot symbiotic agentic system that significantly reduces the human effort required for real-world robotic manipulation data collection while maintaining, or even enhancing, the quality of the collected data for training VLA models. This objective is motivated by several critical challenges in current data acquisition pipelines:

1. **Data Acquisition Bottleneck:** As VLA models grow in capacity and complexity, the availability of high-volume, high-quality real-world demonstration data has become the limiting factor for further progress. Existing methods either demand continuous human teleoperation (high-cost) or operate autonomously with susceptibility to silent degradation from long-tail failures (potential low-quality data).
2. **Inefficient Human Intervention in Long-Horizon Tasks:** In extended data collection sessions, the same failure modes (e.g., incorrect success criteria, recurring perception errors, suboptimal grasp strategies) tend to recur across multiple episodes. Current interactive systems often treat each human intervention as specific to the current episode, meaning a correction must be re-issued every time a known problem reappears. This leads to a human oversight cost that scales with the number of episodes rather than the number of distinct problems, making long-term autonomous operation inefficient for human operators.
3. **Lack of Persistent Corrective Knowledge:** Existing frameworks largely lack a mechanism to explicitly retain and generalize human-provided corrective knowledge. Interventions typically result in demonstrations for policy updates or ephemeral adjustments for the current task. The system does not "learn" from human feedback in a way that directly modifies its operational logic for future, similar situations without extensive retraining.
4. **Desire for Human-Robot Symbiosis:** The authors envision a system where humans and robots collaborate, with the robot progressively improving its autonomy as human guidance accumulates. This symbiosis aims for a strict division of labor: humans provide high-level, natural-language corrective knowledge, and the robot integrates this knowledge to become more capable over time, reducing the need for constant human supervision.
5. **Closing the Loop to Deployment:** The ultimate goal is not just efficient data collection, but ensuring that the collected data is robust enough to train high-performing policies that can be deployed effectively in real-world scenarios without external support. This requires a feedback loop where verified data leads to stronger policies, which in turn enable more autonomous and reliable data collection in subsequent iterations (a "data flywheel").

Zero2Skill aims to address these motivations by introducing a verification-gated decision process for autonomous collection, coupled with a persistent Corrective Memory that stores natural-language-guided adjustments. This approach seeks to enable the system to adapt its behavior within a session without model retraining and to improve its underlying policy between sessions through certified data, thereby creating a more efficient and scalable paradigm for robotic skill bootstrapping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Zero2Skill operates as a human-robot symbiotic agentic system, built upon the OpenClaw platform, which provides skill, tool-calling, and persistent memory interfaces. The methodology centers on an autonomous collect-and-reset loop augmented by a language-guided intervention mechanism and a Corrective Memory.

#### 3.1. System Architecture and Workflow

1. **Task Analysis and Planning:**
 - The process begins with a natural-language instruction (e.g., "learn to tidy the tabletop").
 - An embodied agent observes the environment, extracts scene and object information, and uses a task description and tool library to determine manipulation goals and tool-call arguments.
 - The agent generates a comprehensive plan comprising:
 - A **collection routine** (per-episode action chain and tool calls).
 - A **collection success criterion** (expressed as a VLM verification prompt).
 - A **reset routine** (to return the scene to its initial state).
 - A **reset success criterion** (also a VLM verification prompt).
 - The operator reviews and confirms this initial plan.

2. **Autonomous Collection-Reset Loop (Algorithm 1):**
 - After plan confirmation, Zero2Skill enters an autonomous loop designed to gather a target number of trajectories ($K$).
 - Each iteration involves alternating a collection phase and a reset phase, each driven by its own command ($c_{col}$, $c_{res}$) and checked by a VLM-based verification prompt ($p_{col}$, $p_{res}$).
 - **Phase Execution:** The robot executes the command, waits for the scene to settle, and then queries a VLM (Seed1.8) on a fresh image. The VLM is instructed to provide an explicit "yes" or "no" verdict with a justification.
 - **Retry Mechanism:** A phase passes on a "yes" verdict. If the verdict is "no" or "unknown" (e.g., due to an infrastructure error), the phase is retried up to a predefined limit ($N$, default 3).
 - **Human Intervention Boundary:** If a phase exhausts its retries, the system halts, enters an "Alert" state, and notifies the remote operator. This explicit retry budget and verification strictness define the boundary between autonomous operation and human assistance.
 - **Separate Reset Verification:** The reset phase is independently verified. This is crucial because a failed reset can silently alter the scene, leading to data degradation if undetected. Verifying reset allows the system to detect and correct scene drift.

3. **Language Intervention and Corrective Memory:**
 - When in the "Alert" state, the operator provides free-form natural-language feedback. An LLM parser (DeepSeek-V3.2) interprets this utterance, considering the current state and existing memory.
 - **Correction Types:**
 - **Persistent Corrections:** These apply to all subsequent instances of the same problem. Examples include adjusting VLM verification criteria (e.g., "count an object as inside if any part of it is inside") or modifying execution strategies (e.g., "for flat, low-profile objects, grasp one centimeter below the height proposed by the policy"). These are translated into structured rules and stored in **Corrective Memory**.
 - **Transient Corrections:** These apply only to the current round (e.g., "nudge it slightly to the left this time") and are not stored.
 - **Corrective Memory:** This is a lightweight, structured Markdown file. Each entry includes a **trigger** (when it applies), a **correction** (the adjustment), a **scope** (bounds the trigger), and the **source utterance**. At the start of each round, the system consults this memory and applies relevant rules. This mechanism is model-agnostic, modifying system behavior directly via text without retraining VLA weights.

4. **Trajectory Labeling and Quality Certification:**
 - Each episode is saved with logs and multi-view video. It receives two labels: an **execution label** (whether the action chain completed) and an **outcome label** (VLM verdict: success, failure, unknown).
 - Infrastructure errors (e.g., dropped connection, timeout) result in an "unknown" label, preventing spurious failures from tainting the data.
 - **Offline Re-judging:** Since final observations are retained, episodes can be re-judged offline when verification prompts are corrected via language, ensuring data consistency under the latest criteria.
 - **Quality Statistics:** The system computes per-episode quality statistics (e.g., motion smoothness, image sharpness) and provides a report. Episodes are rated as "good," "marginal," or "bad" based on sanity rules and outlier detection (independent of task success).
 - **Training Data Preparation:** Only episodes that completed, passed the outcome rule, and were not flagged as "bad" recordings are consolidated and merged into the training dataset format.

5. **Policy Learning and Data Flywheel:**
 - Trajectories that pass verification are used to fine-tune the underlying VLA policy (e.g., $\pi_{0.5}$). The quality of this training set directly incorporates all persistent language-guided corrections.
 - The resulting policy is then evaluated in a deployment setting without agent or verifier support.
 - This process is designed to form a self-reinforcing, multi-round loop: a stronger policy from one session can serve as a collector for the next, succeeding more often unaided, and persistent corrections continually reduce human involvement. The current work evaluates one complete cycle (collect, correct, consolidate, fine-tune, deploy).

#### 3.2. Tools and Components (Appendix A)

- **Skills:** `self-learning` (orchestrator), `1-analyze-task` (plan generation, loop execution), `understand-three-view-images` (observation, VLM judging), `grasp-tool` (pick-and-place, recorder).
- **Grasp Tool:** Uses a language-conditioned pick-and-place skill. It involves:
 - **Perception:** RGB-D camera (head-mounted), point cloud projection, SAM3 for segmentation based on text prompts.
 - **Grasp Planning:** AnyGrasp for 6-DoF grasp prediction with collision detection, followed by filtering (mask projection, NMS, canonicalization, approach orientation re-ranking).
 - **Execution:** Pinocchio IK for joint angles, fixed phase chain (home, pre-grasp, approach, lift, transport, lower, release, retract, home).
- **Observation Tool:** Manages camera views (head, arm cameras) and VLM querying. Saved views are sent to Seed1.8 for scene description or success/failure judging.
- **Collection Recorder:** Records camera streams and arm joint states to HDF5 during arm motion. It generates logs, videos, and per-episode statistics. It also supports offline re-judging.
- **Data Curation and Quality Platform:** Computes various per-episode statistics (e.g., RMS jerk, smoothness, image aesthetics, brightness) and rates recording quality. A consolidation tool prepares filtered, verified trajectories for training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate Zero2Skill's effectiveness in reducing human effort, its ability to leverage language-guided corrections, and the quality of the data it collects for downstream policy learning.

#### 4.1. Experiment 1: Collection Efficiency & Human Cost

This experiment compared Zero2Skill against full human teleoperation and a scripted autonomous collector for a "Desktop Clearing" task involving 50 attempted episodes.
- **Human Working Time Reduction:** Zero2Skill reduced human working time to **4.8 minutes**, a approximately six-fold reduction compared to **30.0 minutes** for full human teleoperation. The scripted baseline required **31.0 minutes** of human working time, as it needed continuous monitoring.
- **Episode Collection Success Rate:** Both Zero2Skill and full human teleoperation achieved a **100.0%** episode collection success rate, successfully producing 50 valid trajectories. The scripted baseline achieved **70.0%** (35 successful out of 50 attempted episodes).
- **Wall-Clock Time:** Zero2Skill required **53.0 minutes** of total wall-clock time, longer than human teleoperation (35.0 minutes) and the scripted baseline (31.0 minutes), but with significantly less active human involvement.
- **Trajectories-per-Human-Minute (TpHM):** Zero2Skill achieved a TpHM of **10.42**, substantially higher than full human teleoperation (1.67) and the scripted baseline (1.13), indicating greater efficiency in obtaining valid data per unit of human effort.

#### 4.2. Experiment 2: Language-Guided Correction

This experiment evaluated the efficacy of language-guided corrections at both the perception (verifier) and action (execution) levels.

- **Verifier-Side Correction:**
 - Language corrections to VLM judging criteria significantly improved verifier accuracy across four task settings (Table 2).
 - For "Basket desktop clearing," collection verifier accuracy improved from **40.0% to 100.0%** (4/10 to 10/10) by accepting partial containment.
 - For "Basket desktop reset," reset verifier accuracy improved from **0.0% to 100.0%** (0/10 to 10/10) by relaxing the reset criterion.
 - For "Two-basket object sorting" (category-wise collection), accuracy improved from **20.0% to 100.0%** (2/10 to 10/10).
 - For the harder "Box fruit clearing" (ambiguous collection), accuracy improved from **0.0% to 40.0%** (0/10 to 4/10), indicating that some ambiguity may require iterative correction.

- **Execution-Side Correction: Segmentation and Grasp Depth:**
 - Cumulative language-guided corrections improved the **single-attempt collection success rate** across eight object categories (Figure 4).
 - Starting from the agent's initial plan (12.5% success), re-prompting SAM3 improved the average to **25.0%** (+12.5 points).
 - Further adjusting the approach depth offset cumulatively raised the average to **47.5%** (+22.5 points from SAM re-prompting, +35.0 points overall).
 - The depth offset improved every object category, suggesting a systematic error, while SAM re-prompting primarily impacted four categories.

- **Execution-Side Correction: Arm Selection:**
 - Language guidance to correct a proximity bias in arm selection increased the average single-attempt success rate from **20.0% to 50.0%** (+30.0 points) across most objects (Figure 5b).
 - An exception was noted for "Carambola," where the corrected rule led to a decrease in success, indicating that persistent rules are most beneficial when they align with the actual failure cause for specific objects.

- **Corrections in the Collection Loop (Cumulative Effect):**
 - Table 3 showed that as language interventions accumulated during live collection, the cumulative collection success rate generally improved or remained stable above the pre-intervention level in three out of four experimental groups.
 - For instance, "Banana" group improved from 57.14% to 61.11%, "Banana & Grape" from 60.87% to 66.67%.
 - One group ("Banana & Chili") saw an initial drop followed by partial recovery (from 80.00% to 70.59%, then 76.47%), illustrating that a stored correction's value depends on its alignment with the actual failure cause, which can be complex in harder scenes.

#### 4.3. Experiment 3: Data Quality via Downstream Policy

This experiment assessed whether data collected by Zero2Skill was sufficient to train competitive policies, using policy success rate as a measure of data quality. Policies were fine-tuned on 50 valid demonstrations from different collection sources and evaluated on 20 blind trials without agent, verifier, or human support.

- **Policy Success Rate:**
 - A policy fine-tuned on Zero2Skill-collected data achieved an **80.0%** policy success rate (Table 5).
 - This matched the policy success rate of **80.0%** achieved by a policy trained on full human teleoperation data.
 - In contrast, a policy trained on data from the scripted collector reached **55.0%**.
- **Data Quality Diagnostics (Table 4):**
 - Both Zero2Skill and human teleoperation data showed comparable illumination (Brightness: 130 vs. 133).
 - Zero2Skill data exhibited higher Aesthetics (61.2 vs. 55.2) and lower RMS jerk (191 vs. 267), suggesting smoother average joint motion and subjectively better image quality.
 - However, Zero2Skill data had lower Smoothness (64.2 vs. 78.1) with a wider spread, indicating more thresholded discontinuities or non-linear episode-level effects despite the lower average jerk.
 - These diagnostics suggest comparable observability and usability of the data, with a noted continuity gap in agent-collected motion.

In summary, Zero2Skill demonstrated significant reductions in human working time while achieving an episode collection success rate comparable to full human teleoperation. Its language-guided correction mechanism effectively improved both perception-side judgments and execution-side strategies. Crucially, policies trained on Zero2Skill-collected data performed comparably to those trained on human teleoperation data, validating the utility of its autonomously collected and language-certified demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Zero2Skill presents a novel approach to scaling real-world robotic manipulation data collection by integrating human oversight with persistent, language-guided system improvements. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Reduction in Human Effort for Data Collection:** The most direct impact is the demonstrated six-fold reduction in human working time compared to full teleoperation, while maintaining a 100% episode collection success rate. This addresses the primary bottleneck in VLA model development by making data acquisition substantially more efficient and less labor-intensive. This efficiency is quantified by the new metric, Trajectories-per-Human-Minute (TpHM), which jointly measures throughput and human effort.
2. **Persistent Knowledge Accumulation:** Unlike prior interactive learning methods where corrections are often transient, Zero2Skill's use of Corrective Memory allows human-provided natural-language feedback to be stored as reusable rules. This means that a problem corrected once does not require re-intervention, enabling the system to become progressively more autonomous over time as operational knowledge accumulates. This shift from transient to persistent corrections could fundamentally change how human-robot collaboration in data collection is conceptualized.
3. **Enhanced Data Quality through Adaptive Verification:** The system's ability to correct VLM verifier criteria via natural language ensures that the definition of "success" aligns with human intent, even if the initial automatically generated prompts are flawed. This adaptive verification, coupled with robust data quality checks and offline re-judging, ensures that the collected trajectories are consistently high-quality, which is crucial for training robust downstream policies.
4. **Enabling Competitive Policy Learning with Less Human Supervision:** The finding that policies fine-tuned on Zero2Skill-collected data achieve comparable success rates to those trained on full human teleoperation data demonstrates that the autonomously collected, language-certified demonstrations are as effective for learning. This validates the core premise that reduced human effort does not necessitate a compromise in the utility of the training data.
5. **Foundation for a Multi-Round Data Flywheel:** Zero2Skill is designed as a foundational step towards a self-reinforcing data flywheel. Stronger policies trained on improved data can act as more capable collectors, further reducing the need for human intervention in subsequent rounds. This continuous improvement loop holds the potential for truly scalable and sustained robotic skill development.
6. **General-Purpose Language Interface:** The single, free-form language feedback channel for correcting both perception-level judgments and execution-level strategies offers a flexible and intuitive interface for human operators. Its generalizability, limited by the LLM parser's capacity rather than predefined parameters, opens avenues for addressing a wider range of failure modes.

While the current system has limitations, such as its suitability for complex dexterous tasks and the unvalidated multi-round flywheel, Zero2Skill's contribution to human-supervised, scalable robot data collection, where operational knowledge is retained and applied through language, marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotics.
