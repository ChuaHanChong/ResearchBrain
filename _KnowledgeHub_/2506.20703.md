# Generative Blocks World: Moving Things Around in Pictures

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative Blocks World introduces a method for semantic image editing that leverages a 3D primitive-based scene representation to manipulate images. It achieves superior geometric adherence (AbsRel_src of 0.072 vs. LooseControl's 0.143) and texture preservation (PSNR of 18.7 vs. LooseControl's 6.65) for random camera moves, allowing precise control over object manipulation and camera movements.

## Problem

- Existing 2D image editing methods often struggle with 3D properties like perspective, occlusion, and shape constancy, leading to geometrically inconsistent results.
- Prior 3D-aware editing approaches frequently face challenges in computational complexity, reconstruction quality, or granular control over scene details.
- Generative image editing methods often struggle to preserve the texture and identity of objects after transformations, leading to altered appearances.

## Method

- **Generative Blocks World** extracts a sparse set of ==3D convex polytopes== from an input image, using a ==ResNet-18== encoder and training on depth maps.
- It integrates these 3D primitives with a ==depth-conditioned rectified flow model== (FLUX.1 Depth) for image synthesis, allowing precise geometric control.
- A novel ==projection-based texture hint== mechanism, combined with a confidence mask, is used to preserve object identity and texture fidelity during edits and camera movements.

## Results

- The approach demonstrates superior geometric adherence, achieving AbsRel_src of **0.072** and AbsRel_dst of **0.076** for random camera moves, significantly outperforming LooseControl's **0.143** and **0.146**.
- It achieves enhanced texture preservation with PSNR of **18.7** and SSIM of **0.874**, compared to LooseControl's **6.65** and **0.670**, due to the projection-based texture hint.
- The method supports flexible, multi-scale editing by adjusting the number of primitives (K=4 for coarse edits, K=60 for fine-grained), and handles coherent camera movements while preserving scene content.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing scenes as editable 3D convex polytopes provides an intuitive and geometrically consistent interface for complex image manipulations, including camera movements.
> - Projecting source image textures using 3D point correspondences is crucial for maintaining object identity and visual fidelity when generating edited scenes.
> - Powerful 2D diffusion models can be effectively conditioned on abstract 3D primitive geometry without requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning of the generator itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the domain of image editing, specifically focusing on paradigms that incorporate 3D scene understanding for more robust and geometrically consistent manipulations. Existing image editing techniques often rely on 2D interfaces, such as dragging features or using masks, which can struggle with 3D properties like perspective, occlusion, and shape constancy. While some recent methods attempt to integrate 3D priors (e.g., NeRFs for multi-view consistency or explicit mesh reconstruction), they frequently face challenges related to computational complexity, reconstruction quality, or limited control over granular details.

The concept of representing scenes as assemblies of simple geometric primitives (a "Blocks World" analogy) has historical roots in computer vision and graphics, dating back to Roberts (1963) and Biederman (1987). This work revisits and modernizes this approach by combining advanced neural models for convex decomposition with state-of-the-art diffusion-based image generation. It differentiates itself from contemporary primitive-based methods like LooseControl (Bhat et al., 2024) and Build-A-Scene (Eldesokey & Wonka, 2025) by offering a more accurate and variable-scale primitive representation that does not require domain-specific fine-tuning of the image generator. It also moves beyond single-object 3D editing approaches by enabling scene-level manipulations, including camera movements, while aiming to preserve texture and object identity more effectively than key-value transfer methods or 2D point-based dragging systems like DragGAN (Pan et al., 2023).

The work integrates several contemporary techniques:
- **Primitive Decomposition:** It builds upon recent advancements in fitting convex polytopes to real-world RGB-D images (Vavilala & Forsyth, 2023; Vavilala et al., 2025a), improving their robustness to noise.
- **Conditioned Image Synthesis:** It leverages rectified flow models, specifically the FLUX model (Esser et al., 2024; Labs, 2024), which can be conditioned on depth maps, to synthesize images.
- **Texture Preservation:** It introduces a novel projection-based texture hint mechanism to maintain visual fidelity during edits, addressing a common challenge in generative editing.

By offering an intuitive 3D-aware interface through editable primitives, the paper seeks to provide a more controllable, consistent, and flexible image editing paradigm compared to existing 2D and 3D-aware methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Generative Blocks World is to enable intuitive, 3D-aware semantic image editing by manipulating simple geometric abstractions of a scene. The core motivation stems from limitations observed in current image editing methods, particularly those that do not explicitly account for 3D scene geometry.

Specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Overcoming Limitations of 2D Editing Interfaces:** Traditional 2D image editing methods, such as dragging features, often lead to geometrically inconsistent results when attempting 3D-aware transformations (e.g., changes in perspective, scale, or occlusion). The paper aims to provide an interface that intrinsically respects these 3D properties.
- **Ensuring 3D Consistency:** Explicit 3D representations are proposed to maintain:
 - **Shape Constancy:** Objects should deform and scale appropriately when moved in a perspective view, maintaining their perceived shape and size.
 - **Contact Consistency:** Objects should maintain expected spatial relationships (e.g., remaining on a surface) unless explicitly manipulated otherwise.
 - **Shape Completion:** Implicit 3D information (like object backs) should be leveraged to handle occlusions and disocclusions naturally when objects are moved.
- **Achieving Compact and Accurate Scene Representation:** A key challenge in 3D-aware editing is finding a scene representation that is both compact enough for intuitive interaction and accurate enough to produce realistic edited images. The paper aims to utilize "generative blocks world" primitives—convex 3D polytopes—as this representation.
- **Enabling Multi-Scale Editing:** The method seeks to support various levels of detail in scene manipulation, allowing users to move entire objects or fine-tune smaller parts by adjusting the number of primitives used to represent the scene.
- **Preserving Texture and Object Identity:** A significant challenge in generative image editing is maintaining the texture and identity of objects after transformation. The paper aims to develop a texture hint generation mechanism that leverages the 3D primitive representation to project source textures onto the edited scene, thereby improving identity preservation during the image generation process.
- **Integrating with State-of-the-Art Image Generators:** The method intends to integrate the 3D primitive control with existing, powerful depth-conditioned image synthesis models (e.g., FLUX) without requiring extensive retraining or fine-tuning of the generator itself.
- **Demonstrating Superior Performance:** The paper aims to show, through extensive evaluation, that its approach offers improved geometric control, texture retention, and edit flexibility compared to existing baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Generative Blocks World operates through a multi-stage pipeline designed to fuse abstract 3D geometric control with realistic image synthesis. The process consists of four main stages: (i) primitive extraction, (ii) initial image generation, (iii) user editing of primitives or camera, and (iv) generating a new image conditioned on the updated primitives and a texture hint.

#### 3.1. Convex Decomposition for Primitive Extraction

The method begins by extracting a sparse set of 3D convex polytopes from an input image.
- **Primitive Vocabulary:** The primitives are blended 3D convex polytopes, as defined in CVXNet (Deng et al., 2020). Each polytope is represented by a collection of half-planes.
- **Signed Distance Function (SDF):** The SDF of each convex primitive is approximated using a differentiable LogSumExp function, which is then converted into a smooth indicator function (Sigmoid(−σΦ(x))) to classify points as inside or outside the shape. This differentiability facilitates gradient-based learning.
- **Model Architecture:** A ResNet-18 encoder processes RGB-D input, followed by three fully-connected layers that decode into the parameters of the primitives.
- **Training:** Different networks are trained for varying primitive counts (K, e.g., 4 to 72 parts). Training supervision comes from depth maps (e.g., from DepthAnythingv2 Yang et al., 2024), which are lifted to 3D point clouds. The models are trained on 1.8M images from LAION, with optimization focused on aligning primitives to observed geometry.
- **Rendering Primitives:** The extracted primitives are rendered into a depth map, which serves as a geometric condition for the subsequent image generation model. This primitive-derived depth map provides a structured representation that is editable while abstracting away fine details for the image synthesizer.

#### 3.2. Depth-Conditioned Inpainting in Rectified Flow Transformers

The pipeline uses a pretrained Rectified Flow (RF) model, specifically FLUX.1 Depth, for image synthesis.
- **Spatial Conditioning:** The FLUX model is conditioned on the depth map derived from the 3D primitives. This is achieved by concatenating the latent representation of the depth map with the image latents as input to the network, providing tight geometric control. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) layers can also be applied atop a frozen base RF model, offering an additional `lora_weight` parameter to tune the influence of the depth map on synthesis.
- **Role of Hint and Mask:** A key contribution is the use of a "hint" image ($x_{hint}$) and a confidence mask ($m$) to guide the generation process during specific denoising timesteps ($t_{end} \le t \le t_{start}$). The hint image, encoded into latents, is noised to match the current timestep's noise level and then combined with the current latents as $x_t = (1-m) \cdot x_{hint,t} + m \cdot x_t$. The mask $m$ indicates regions where the hint is trustworthy (typically outside depth discontinuities), allowing the diffusion model to synthesize new content in uncertain areas.

#### 3.3. Texture Hint Generation for Camera and Object Edits

To preserve texture and object identity during edits, a texture hint is generated.
- **Point Cloud Correspondences:** A method is developed to establish 3D point correspondences between the original and edited scenes. This involves processing point clouds at ray-primitive intersection points, a `convex_map` indicating primitive IDs, per-primitive transforms (scale, rotate, translate), and a `max_distance` threshold. This procedure accommodates both primitive manipulations and camera movements.
- **Texture Hint Creation:** Using the correspondence map, pixels from the source RGB image are projected onto the new view to create the texture hint image ($x_{hint}$). This hint reflects the warped appearance of objects after 3D transformations.
- **Confidence Mask Generation:** The confidence mask $m$ is derived from the correspondence process, assigning lower confidence near depth discontinuities or regions where new geometry is exposed (e.g., object backsides becoming visible). Low-confidence pixels are dilated to allow the diffusion model more freedom in those areas.
- **Hint Inpainting:** Low-confidence regions in the hint image are inpainted using a Voronoi-based nearest-neighbor approach to ensure a continuous input to the diffusion model, rather than leaving black pixels.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

The method is evaluated using metrics for both geometric and texture consistency:
- **Geometric Consistency:** Assessed by calculating the Absolute Relative Error (AbsRel) between the depth map rendered from the *requested* primitives (treated as ground truth) and the *estimated* depth map of the synthesized image (using DepthAnythingv2).
- **Texture Consistency:** Measured by reprojecting the edited synthesized image back to the original camera viewpoint using the point cloud correspondence. PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS are then computed between this reprojected image and the original source image, with the confidence mask filtering out pixels not visible in both views.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the capabilities of Generative Blocks World.

#### 4.1. Geometric Control and Fidelity

- **Quantitative Performance:** Table 1 shows that Generative Blocks World significantly outperforms LooseControl (Bhat et al., 2024) in geometric adherence. Our method achieves AbsRel_src of 0.072 and AbsRel_dst of 0.076, compared to LooseControl's 0.143 and 0.146, respectively, for random camera moves. This indicates tighter control over the synthesized geometry.
- **Depth Map Accuracy:** Table 2 illustrates that the primitive generator produces highly accurate depth maps. Even with a small number of primitives (K=4), the AbsRel error is 0.0376, decreasing to 0.0194 for K=60, suggesting that the primitive representation can closely approximate real scene geometry.
- **Qualitative Control:** Figures 1, 3, and 5 demonstrate precise control over object translation, scaling, deletion, and camera movements. The synthesized images accurately reflect the modified primitive geometry. For example, moving a cat left/right or scaling its head (Figure 1) or performing sequential edits on a soda can (Figure 3) results in geometrically consistent changes.

#### 4.2. Texture Preservation and Object Identity

- **Quantitative Performance:** Table 1 also highlights superior texture preservation. Generative Blocks World achieves PSNR of 18.7 and SSIM of 0.874, while LooseControl yields 6.65 and 0.670, respectively. Table 4, an ablation study on the DragBench dataset, confirms the critical role of the texture hint mechanism, showing PSNR of 14.24 and SSIM of 0.658 with the hint, compared to 8.42 and 0.465 without it.
- **Ablation of Texture Hint:** Figure 6 visually confirms that the projection-based texture hints are crucial for preserving object identity. Without these hints (or using only source depth), object textures are altered. While combining with StableFlow (Avrahami et al., 2025) key-value injection can sometimes improve fine details, the projection-based hint is presented as the primary driver for texture fidelity.
- **Camera Moves:** Figure 5 illustrates a key advantage over LooseControl. When the camera is moved, Generative Blocks World consistently shows the same scene from a new view, preserving details like the number of apples or the water level in a glass. LooseControl, in contrast, often hallucinates or alters objects during camera movements.

#### 4.3. Edit Flexibility and Generalization

- **Multi-Scale Editing:** Figures 7 and 10 demonstrate the ability to perform edits at varying levels of detail by adjusting the number of primitives (K). Coarse edits (K=6, 8) affect larger scene parts, while fine-grained edits (K=24, 60) allow manipulation of specific components.
- **Compositional Generalization:** The method supports various text prompts, allowing for diverse scene content while respecting the underlying primitive edits. Figures 7 and 10 showcase how the same primitive edit (e.g., scaling a primitive) leads to consistent geometric changes across different scenes and object types.
- **Comparison with Other Methods:**
 - **Drag Diffusion (Shi et al., 2024):** Figure 4 demonstrates that 2D point-based dragging struggles with 3D consistency, leading to shape distortions and texture loss. Our method, operating on 3D primitives, maintains object geometry, scale, and texture more effectively.
 - **Production Systems (Reve, ChatGPT, Gemini):** Figure 9 indicates that commercial systems either require manual perspective estimation for 2D boxes or lack precise interaction mechanisms, often failing to maintain object details or generate accurate camera moves.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Influence

- **Hint Timesteps ($t_{start}, t_{end}$):** Figure 18 and 19 show that the range of timesteps during which the hint is applied influences the balance between texture consistency and artifact generation. A wide range (e.g., $t_{start}=1000, t_{end}=0$) generally maximizes quantitative texture metrics but can introduce artifacts, while a more constrained range (e.g., $t_{start}=1000, t_{end}=300$) provides a good qualitative balance.
- **LoRA Weight:** Figure 20 and 21 illustrate a trade-off: increasing the LoRA weight strengthens depth adherence (improving geometric faithfulness) but can degrade texture similarity to the hint image. This parameter provides a controllable knob to balance geometric accuracy and texture preservation.
- **Confidence Mask Dilation:** Figure 23 and 24 suggest that moderate dilation of the confidence mask (e.g., 9-12 pixels) around low-confidence regions helps reduce edge artifacts and improves the harmonization of moved objects with the scene, even if it slightly affects texture consistency metrics.

#### 4.5. Limitations

The paper identifies several limitations:
- Difficulty with highly non-convex shapes.
- Inability to resolve or sharpen depth-of-field blurring.
- Potential for failure with significant object rotations, leading to distortions or hallucinations (Figure 8).
- Lack of accounting for view-dependent lighting effects and temporal consistency for video.
- Conflicts with text prompts (e.g., removing an object mentioned in the prompt) can lead to undesirable artifacts (Figure 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Generative Blocks World offers a structured and geometrically informed approach to image editing, with several implications for research and applications.

**Research Significance:**
- **Bridging 2D and 3D Generative Models:** The work demonstrates a practical and effective method for integrating explicit 3D representations with powerful 2D diffusion models, moving beyond implicit 3D priors. This could influence future architectures and pipelines for 3D-aware generation.
- **Advancement in Primitive-Based Representations:** It revitalizes and validates the utility of part-based, primitive representations for complex in-the-wild scenes, showcasing their benefits for interactive editing. The ability to vary the level of detail (primitive count) addresses a critical need for flexible control.
- **Novel Texture Preservation Mechanism:** The proposed projection-based texture hint generation, combined with a confidence mask, provides a method for maintaining object identity and texture fidelity during significant geometric transformations, which has been a challenge for other generative editing techniques.
- **Robust Camera Movement Support:** The method's ability to handle camera movements while preserving scene content and texture marks an advancement in coherent novel view synthesis for edited scenes.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Image and Video Editing Tools:** The intuitive 3D interaction paradigm could lead to more precise and consistent tools for professional and casual image editors. Users could easily reposition, scale, or delete objects while maintaining realistic perspective and texture, potentially extending to video editing with further development.
- **Content Creation:** Artists and designers could leverage this method for rapid prototyping and authoring of new scenes, as it enables the assembly and manipulation of primitives to define new layouts before generating high-fidelity images.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** By providing robust 3D scene understanding and manipulation, the approach could contribute to more interactive and realistic virtual environment generation and editing, where users might manipulate virtual objects with intuitive 3D controls.
- **Robotics and Simulation:** Accurate 3D scene representations and the ability to simulate object movements could be beneficial for training autonomous agents or creating realistic simulation environments.

While limitations such as handling complex non-convex shapes, lighting effects, and temporal consistency require further research, Generative Blocks World presents a step towards more controllable and physically consistent generative image editing. The explicit 3D primitives provide a transparent and editable scene understanding that enhances the capabilities of modern diffusion models.
