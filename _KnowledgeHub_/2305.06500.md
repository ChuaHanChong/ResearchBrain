# InstructBLIP: Towards General-purpose Vision-Language Models with Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> InstructBLIP advances general-purpose vision-language models by systematically applying instruction tuning, building on the BLIP-2 architecture. This work introduces an instruction-aware visual feature extraction mechanism and leverages a diverse, instruction-formatted dataset, leading to state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across various unseen vision-language tasks and achieving significant generalization improvements.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language models (VLMs) struggle to generalize broadly to diverse, unseen tasks and instructions, unlike instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) in NLP.
- Previous VLM approaches often use instruction-agnostic visual feature extraction, which limits their ability to adapt visual understanding to specific task instructions.
- Training VLMs on image caption data is too limited for broad generalization, while existing multitask learning approaches for VL tasks often fail to yield strong zero-shot performance.

## Method

- The model systematically applies instruction tuning to vision-language tasks by finetuning only the ==Query Transformer (Q-Former)== component of the BLIP-2 architecture, keeping the image encoder and LLM frozen.
- It introduces an =="instruction-aware" Q-Former== that integrates textual instructions to guide visual feature extraction, allowing the model to dynamically adapt its visual understanding based on the task.
- InstructBLIP curates ==26 diverse vision-language datasets== into an instruction-tuning format, using 10-15 distinct natural language templates per task and employing a balanced sampling strategy during training.

## Results

- InstructBLIP achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across **13 held-out datasets**, outperforming its BLIP-2 backbone by an average relative improvement of **15.0%** (FlanT5-XL).
- It demonstrates strong generalization to entirely unseen task categories, such as video question answering, showing up to a **47.1%** relative improvement on MSRVTT-QA despite no prior temporal video training.
- The smallest InstructBLIP FlanT5-XL model (**4 billion parameters**) outperforms the significantly larger Flamingo-80B model across all six shared evaluation datasets by an average of **24.8%** relative improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction-aware visual feature extraction, where the Q-Former adapts its visual understanding based on the textual instruction, is crucial for improving model performance and generalization, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained reasoning.
> - A balanced sampling strategy during instruction tuning is essential for effective and stable training across diverse vision-language datasets, preventing overfitting to smaller datasets and underfitting to larger ones.
> - Instruction tuning, rather than just multitask learning with unified formatting, is the primary driver for enhancing a vision-language model's zero-shot generalization capabilities to unseen tasks and categories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has a long-standing goal of creating a single model capable of performing a wide array of tasks specified by a user. In Natural Language Processing (NLP), this aspiration has seen remarkable progress with the advent of large language models (LLMs) and, more recently, instruction tuning. Instruction tuning involves finetuning LLMs on diverse tasks described by natural language instructions, enabling them to follow arbitrary, even unseen, instructions effectively. This approach has led to the development of "general-purpose" language models like FlanT5 and Vicuna, which exhibit broad competence across various NLP tasks.

However, extending this success to vision-language models (VLMs) presents a greater challenge. The integration of visual inputs introduces significantly richer input distributions and an even wider diversity of tasks compared to text-only domains. While vision-language pre-training (VLP) has been extensively studied—leading to models that can perform tasks like image captioning and visual question answering (VQA)—vision-language instruction tuning has remained relatively underexplored. Previous VLM approaches often fell into two categories:
1. **Multitask Learning:** Formulating various VL tasks into a unified input-output format. While versatile, the authors empirically find that this approach, without explicit instructions, struggles to generalize to unseen datasets and tasks.
2. **LLM Extension with Visual Components:** Adapting pre-trained LLMs to visual inputs, often by training visual components with image-caption data (e.g., BLIP-2, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA). While showing preliminary instruction-following capabilities, these methods are often limited by the scope of their training data, primarily focusing on visual descriptions and failing to generalize broadly to tasks requiring deeper visual reasoning or external knowledge.

InstructBLIP emerges as a pivotal work in this landscape by systematically and comprehensively studying vision-language instruction tuning. It aims to bridge the gap between the generalizability achieved by instruction-tuned LLMs and the multimodal challenges of VL tasks. By building upon the robust BLIP-2 architecture and meticulously curating a diverse set of instruction-formatted multimodal datasets, InstructBLIP directly addresses the limitations of previous VLM training paradigms. It proposes a novel "instruction-aware" mechanism for visual feature extraction, allowing the model to adapt its visual understanding based on the given textual instruction, a critical step towards true instruction following in multimodal contexts. This work positions itself as a significant advancement towards unified, general-purpose multimodal AI agents that can seamlessly understand and respond to diverse visual and linguistic queries.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of InstructBLIP is to develop **general-purpose vision-language models** that can solve a wide range of visual-language tasks through a unified natural language interface. This ambitious goal is motivated by several key challenges and observations in the current state of multimodal AI:

- **Bridging the Generalization Gap from NLP to VL:** Instruction tuning has proven highly effective in enabling LLMs to generalize to new, unseen NLP tasks. The primary motivation is to replicate and extend this success to the more complex domain of vision-language, where models traditionally struggle with broad generalization due to the immense diversity of visual inputs and corresponding tasks.
- **Addressing Data Limitations and Task Diversity:** Previous VLM training often relies on image caption data, which is too limited to foster broad generalization for tasks requiring more than simple visual descriptions. Vision-language tasks are inherently more diverse (e.g., VQA, visual reasoning, classification, dialog) and demand varied types of visual understanding and reasoning. InstructBLIP aims to tackle this by curating a significantly larger and more diverse instruction-formatted dataset.
- **Improving Instruction Following and Task Adaptation:** Existing zero-shot image-to-text generation methods, including the foundational BLIP-2, often employ an "instruction-agnostic" approach to visual feature extraction. This means the visual features fed into the LLM are static, regardless of the specific task instruction. The motivation for InstructBLIP is to introduce "instruction-aware" visual feature extraction, allowing the model to dynamically adapt its visual understanding based on the task instruction, leading to more relevant and informative visual representations.
- **Overcoming Multitask Learning Limitations:** While multitask learning unifies task formats, it has not consistently led to strong zero-shot generalization in VL domains. The authors hypothesize that explicit natural language instructions, rather than just unified formatting, are crucial for generalization. InstructBLIP seeks to validate this hypothesis through systematic comparison.
- **Enhancing Finetuning Efficiency and Performance:** Beyond zero-shot capabilities, the goal is also to develop a model that serves as a superior initialization for downstream task-specific finetuning. By keeping the image encoder frozen and primarily finetuning the Q-Former, InstructBLIP aims to achieve this with significantly reduced computational cost and trainable parameters compared to previous approaches that might finetune the entire visual backbone.

In essence, InstructBLIP is motivated by the vision of a truly versatile multimodal AI assistant that can understand complex visual scenes, engage in multi-turn conversations, perform various reasoning tasks, and generate rich, context-aware responses, all by simply being told what to do through natural language instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InstructBLIP's methodology is characterized by a systematic approach to vision-language instruction tuning, building upon the strengths of its BLIP-2 predecessor while introducing novel architectural and data sampling strategies.

#### 3.1. Foundation: BLIP-2 Architecture

InstructBLIP leverages the modular architecture of BLIP-2. This architecture consists of three main components:
- **Frozen Image Encoder:** A pre-trained ViT-g/14 image encoder (EVA) extracts initial visual features. This component remains frozen throughout the instruction tuning process.
- **Frozen Large Language Model (LLM):** The LLM is responsible for generating text responses. InstructBLIP experiments with both encoder-decoder LLMs (FlanT5-XL, FlanT5-XXL) and decoder-only LLMs (Vicuna-7B, Vicuna-13B). These LLMs are kept frozen during instruction tuning.
- **Query Transformer (Q-Former):** This is the crucial component bridging the image encoder and the LLM. It extracts a set of learnable query embeddings from the frozen image encoder's output through cross-attention. These encoded visual vectors are then linearly projected and fed into the LLM as "soft prompts."

#### 3.2. Training Strategy: Finetuning the Q-Former

The core training strategy involves **finetuning only the Q-Former**, while keeping both the image encoder and the LLM frozen. This approach significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters (from ~1.2B to 188M for FlanT5-XXL), making the instruction tuning process highly efficient. The Q-Former is initialized from a BLIP-2 pre-trained checkpoint, which itself undergoes a two-stage pre-training: vision-language representation learning with a frozen image encoder, followed by adapting Q-Former outputs as soft visual prompts for text generation with a frozen LLM. During instruction tuning, the model is trained with the standard language modeling loss to directly generate responses given the instruction.

#### 3.3. Data Curation: Diverse Instruction-Tuning Datasets

To ensure comprehensive coverage and diversity, InstructBLIP gathers **26 publicly available vision-language datasets**, transformed into the instruction tuning format. These datasets span **11 distinct task categories**, including image captioning, VQA, visual reasoning, knowledge-grounded QA, visual conversational QA, image classification, and others.
- **Instruction Templating:** For each task (except LLaVA-Instruct-150K, which is already in instruction format), 10 to 15 distinct natural language instruction templates are meticulously crafted. These templates articulate the task and objective (e.g., "Write a short description for the image," "Question: {} A:"). Terms like "short" or "briefly" are strategically used to prevent overfitting to lengthy outputs.
- **Held-in vs. Held-out Split:** The 26 datasets are divided into 13 "held-in" datasets for instruction tuning (training) and 13 "held-out" datasets for zero-shot evaluation. Crucially, four entire task categories (visual reasoning, video question answering, visual conversational QA, and image classification) are withheld for zero-shot evaluation at the task level, providing a strong test of generalization to truly unseen tasks. Strict measures are taken to avoid data contamination between held-in training and held-out evaluation sets.
- **OCR Tokens:** For datasets involving scene text, OCR tokens are explicitly added to the instruction as supplementary information, improving the model's ability to reason with text in images.

#### 3.4. Novel Architectural Enhancement: Instruction-Aware Visual Feature Extraction

A key innovation is the **instruction-aware Q-Former module**. Unlike BLIP-2's instruction-agnostic approach, InstructBLIP feeds the textual instruction tokens not only to the frozen LLM but also to the Q-Former itself. The instruction interacts with the Q-Former's learnable query embeddings through self-attention layers. This enables the Q-Former to extract visual features that are specifically tailored and relevant to the given instruction, allowing the LLM to receive visual information optimally conducive to following the instruction.

#### 3.5. Data Sampling Strategy: Balanced Training

Given the varied sizes of the 26 training datasets, a simple uniform mixing could lead to overfitting smaller datasets and underfitting larger ones. InstructBLIP addresses this with a **balanced sampling strategy**: datasets are sampled with probabilities proportional to the square root of their sizes. Additionally, manual adjustments are made to the weights of certain datasets (e.g., lowering A-OKVQA, increasing OKVQA) to account for inherent differences in task difficulty and desired training intensity, ensuring more synchronized learning progress across diverse datasets.

#### 3.6. Inference Methods

During inference, two main generation approaches are used:
- **Direct Generation:** For open-ended tasks like image captioning and open-ended VQA, the model directly generates responses, which are then compared to ground truth.
- **Vocabulary Ranking:** For classification and multi-choice VQA tasks, a vocabulary ranking method is employed. The model is prompted to generate answers, but its vocabulary is restricted to a list of candidates. Log-likelihoods are calculated for each candidate, and the one with the highest value is selected. For binary classification, additional verbalizers (e.g., "yes," "true" for positive) are used.
- **Video QA:** For video tasks, four uniformly-sampled frames per video are processed individually by the image encoder and Q-Former. Their extracted visual features are then concatenated before being fed into the LLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InstructBLIP demonstrates significant advancements in general-purpose vision-language models, validated through extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Zero-shot Performance

- **Outperformance over Baselines:** InstructBLIP achieves new state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across all 13 held-out datasets. It consistently and substantially outperforms its backbone, BLIP-2, across all LLM sizes (FlanT5-XL/XXL, Vicuna-7B/13B). For example, InstructBLIP FlanT5-XL yields an average relative improvement of 15.0% over BLIP-2 FlanT5-XL.
- **Generalization to Unseen Task Categories:** The instruction tuning effectively boosts zero-shot generalization on entirely unseen task categories, such as video question answering (MSVD QA, MSRVTT QA, iVQA). InstructBLIP shows up to a 47.1% relative improvement on MSRVTT-QA despite never being trained with temporal video data.
- **Efficiency vs. Performance:** Remarkably, the smallest InstructBLIP FlanT5-XL model (4 billion parameters) outperforms the significantly larger Flamingo-80B model across all six shared evaluation datasets, with an average relative improvement of 24.8%. This highlights InstructBLIP's superior efficiency and effectiveness due to its instruction tuning paradigm.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Instruction Tuning Techniques

The study rigorously investigates the impact of its core innovations:
- **Instruction-aware Visual Features:** Removing the instruction-aware mechanism from the Q-Former leads to a significant performance drop across all datasets. This drop is particularly severe for tasks requiring spatial or temporal visual reasoning (e.g., ScienceQA, iVQA), confirming that guiding visual feature extraction with instructions is crucial for attending to relevant image regions.
- **Balanced Dataset Sampling Strategy:** The absence of the balanced sampling strategy results in unstable and uneven training progress across datasets, leading to suboptimal overall performance. This validates the importance of a carefully designed sampling strategy for large-scale, diverse instruction tuning.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative examples showcase InstructBLIP's sophisticated capabilities:
- **Complex Visual Reasoning:** The model can infer plausible scenarios from visual cues (e.g., deducing a hurricane from debris and palm trees), demonstrating advanced reasoning beyond simple descriptions.
- **Knowledge-Grounded Image Description:** InstructBLIP can connect visual input with external textual knowledge (e.g., introducing a famous painting with historical context), enriching its responses.
- **Multi-turn Visual Conversation:** The model effectively engages in multi-turn dialogues, considering dialog history for coherent new responses.
- **Adaptive Response Length:** Compared to concurrent multimodal models (GPT-4, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4), InstructBLIP often provides more visually-grounded, logically coherent, and appropriately concise responses, adapting its length to the user's intent rather than defaulting to long-form output.

#### 4.4. Instruction Tuning vs. Multitask Learning

A direct comparison reveals the distinct advantage of instruction tuning:
- While instruction tuning and multitask learning achieve similar performance on held-in (seen) datasets, only instruction tuning yields a significant improvement in zero-shot generalization on unseen held-out datasets. Multitask learning, without explicit instructions, performs on par with the original BLIP-2. This strongly supports the conclusion that **instruction tuning is the primary driver for enhancing the model's zero-shot generalization ability.**

#### 4.5. Finetuning InstructBLIP on Downstream Tasks

InstructBLIP models serve as excellent initialization points for downstream task-specific finetuning:
- **Improved Finetuning Performance:** InstructBLIP leads to better finetuning performance on all tested datasets (ScienceQA, OCR-VQA, A-OKVQA, OKVQA) compared to BLIP-2. It establishes new state-of-the-art finetuning results on ScienceQA (IMG), OCR-VQA, and A-OKVQA.
- **Efficiency:** Maintaining a frozen visual encoder and a fixed image resolution (224x224) during finetuning significantly reduces trainable parameters (from 1.2B to 188M), making the process highly efficient.
- **LLM Specificity:** FlanT5-based InstructBLIP models perform better on multi-choice tasks, aligning with FlanT5's instruction tuning on NLP benchmarks with many QA and classification datasets. Vicuna-based InstructBLIP models excel at open-ended generation tasks, reflecting Vicuna's finetuning on open-ended instruction-following data. This highlights how the characteristics of the frozen LLM influence the final model's strengths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InstructBLIP represents a substantial leap forward in the development of general-purpose Vision-Language Models, with significant implications for the broader AI landscape:

- **Pioneering General-purpose Multimodal AI:** By rigorously demonstrating state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization across a wide array of unseen vision-language tasks and even entire task categories, InstructBLIP provides compelling evidence that instruction tuning is a highly effective paradigm for building truly general-purpose multimodal AI. This moves the field closer to the aspiration of a single model that can flexibly solve arbitrary tasks specified by the user in a multimodal context.
- **Unlocking New Research Avenues:** The comprehensive and systematic study of vision-language instruction tuning, coupled with the introduction of novel techniques like instruction-aware visual feature extraction and balanced data sampling, provides a robust framework and valuable insights for future research. It highlights critical components and strategies for effectively training and generalizing multimodal models.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** By successfully training and finetuning models with frozen image encoders and LLMs, InstructBLIP significantly reduces the computational resources and trainable parameters required. This efficiency makes powerful VLM development more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners, accelerating innovation. The fact that InstructBLIP FlanT5-XL, a 4B parameter model, outperforms a much larger 80B parameter model (Flamingo-80B) underscores this efficiency.
- **Enhanced Downstream Task Performance:** InstructBLIP's effectiveness as a superior initialization for downstream task finetuning means that specialized applications can leverage these broadly competent models to achieve state-of-the-art results with fewer adjustments and less data. This reduces the burden of training task-specific models from scratch.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to open-source all InstructBLIP models is a critical contribution to the AI community. It fosters transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative development, enabling researchers worldwide to build upon this foundation, test new hypotheses, and develop novel applications.
- **Catalyst for Advanced Human-AI Interaction:** The qualitative demonstrations of complex visual reasoning, knowledge-grounded responses, and multi-turn conversational abilities suggest InstructBLIP's potential to power more natural and intelligent human-AI interactions across diverse applications, from intelligent assistants to advanced content understanding systems.

While InstructBLIP inherits potential shortcomings from its foundational LLMs (e.g., hallucination, bias), the authors explicitly acknowledge these and recommend prior safety assessments for any downstream applications. Overall, InstructBLIP sets a new benchmark for generalizability in vision-language models and lays a strong foundation for future advancements in multimodal AI.
