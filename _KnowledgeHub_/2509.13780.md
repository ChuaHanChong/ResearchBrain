# Behavior Foundation Model for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and collaborating universities developed a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) for humanoid robots, which learns a unified distribution of behaviors to achieve zero-shot generalization across various control modes and accelerates the acquisition of new skills. The model demonstrates superior performance over general baselines and enables advanced behavioral composition and modulation on a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body control (WBC) frameworks for humanoids are task-specific, demanding extensive reward engineering for each new skill.
- Traditional WBC systems exhibit poor generalization across diverse tasks and control modes, limiting their real-world applicability and adaptability.
- Acquiring novel and challenging humanoid skills is typically slow and inefficient, often requiring training from scratch for each new behavior.

## Method

- A ==Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)==, based on a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), learns a unified distribution of "behaviors" (robot states and actions) independent of specific control modes.
- A "proxy agent" trained with goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning and motion imitation generates a diverse, high-quality behavioral dataset from human motions.
- Online distillation (DAgger) pre-trains the BFM, enabling it to generalize across arbitrary control modes through a flexible bit-wise binary mask strategy applied to its unified low-level control interface.

## Results

- The BFM consistently outperforms general baselines like HOVER across whole-body motion tracking, VR teleoperation, and locomotion, achieving performance comparable to or better than task-specific specialist policies.
- The model's structured latent space successfully enables behavior composition (e.g., performing a Roundhouse Kick) and modulation (e.g., improving balance for a Butterfly Kick).
- New and challenging skills, such as a Side Salto, are acquired significantly faster and more accurately by training a residual decoder on top of the frozen BFM compared to learning an RL policy from scratch.
- The BFM successfully deploys on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, showcasing robust sim-to-real transfer and practical applicability for diverse zero-shot behaviors and efficiently learned new skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Defining all humanoid control objectives as the generation of "behaviors" allows for a unified learning framework that decouples skills from their specific control modes.
> - A structured latent space within a generative model facilitates advanced manipulation, such as composing new behaviors by interpolating between latent variables and modulating behavior characteristics through extrapolation.
> - A pre-trained behavioral foundation model acts as a powerful prior, significantly accelerating the learning of novel robotic skills by allowing only a small residual decoder to be trained for new tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of the Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) for humanoid robots represents a crucial advancement within the broader landscape of robotics, particularly in the domain of whole-body control (WBC) and general-purpose AI. Existing WBC frameworks for humanoids, while achieving remarkable progress in specialized tasks like locomotion, teleoperation, or motion tracking, suffer from significant limitations. They are typically task-specific, demanding extensive and labor-intensive reward engineering for each new skill. More critically, they exhibit poor generalization across diverse tasks and control modes, meaning a policy trained for walking cannot be readily adapted for dancing or manipulation, and vice-versa. This rigidity severely restricts their deployment in complex, real-world scenarios where adaptability to arbitrary commands and situations is paramount.

This paper explicitly positions its work within the context of **Foundation Models**, a paradigm that has revolutionized fields like Natural Language Processing (e.g., GPT, BERT) and computer vision. These models, pre-trained on vast and diverse datasets, learn broad, reusable knowledge that can then be fine-tuned or adapted for a multitude of downstream tasks with minimal effort. The authors identify a similar opportunity in humanoid robotics: by treating all control objectives as ultimately generating "behaviors," they propose a unified formulation that transcends task-specific control modes.

In relation to prior work, the paper discusses various approaches to humanoid WBC, categorizing them by control modes ranging from abstract (natural language) to concrete (motion tracking). It acknowledges recent attempts to unify control modes, such as HOVER, which uses a masking strategy for multi-modal control. However, the authors differentiate BFM by addressing HOVER's limitations, specifically its prioritization of a fixed set of control modes and its two-stage masking strategy. BFM, in contrast, aims to support *arbitrary* control modes through direct application of a sparsity mask. Furthermore, the paper distinguishes BFM from other behavior foundation models like MaskedMimic by specifically targeting real-world humanoid robots and providing a deeper analysis of its structured latent space, which is crucial for advanced applications like behavior composition and modulation.

Thus, BFM aims to bridge the gap between highly specialized robotic controllers and the vision of truly general-purpose humanoid robots. It proposes a fundamental shift from learning isolated tasks to learning a comprehensive understanding of *how* to behave, setting the stage for more versatile, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) stems from the inherent limitations of conventional whole-body control (WBC) systems for humanoid robots. As highlighted by the authors, these systems are predominantly task-specific, necessitating arduous reward engineering and often failing to generalize effectively across different skills or control modes. This lack of versatility hinders their real-world applicability and scalability.

The key insight driving this research is the observation that, despite the diversity of control modes (e.g., velocity commands, teleoperation signals, reference motions), the ultimate output of any WBC system for humanoids is a "behavior"—a sequence of proprioceptive states and actions. By reframing the problem from learning isolated tasks to learning a unified distribution of behaviors, the authors seek to decouple behaviors from their specific control modes. This perspective enables the application of the "foundation model" paradigm, which has proven highly successful in other AI domains.

Based on this foundational motivation, the paper outlines several key objectives for the Behavior Foundation Model:

1. **Develop a Unified Behavioral Representation:** To establish a common abstraction for all humanoid control tasks by defining them as the generation of "behaviors" (trajectories of real-world proprioceptive states and actions), independent of the specific control mode or goal state.
2. **Achieve Zero-Shot Generalization Across Diverse Control Modes:** To enable the BFM to be directly steered by a wide range of control modes, accommodating arbitrary combinations without requiring retraining for each new mode or task. This aims to overcome the rigid specialization of existing WBC systems.
3. **Facilitate Efficient Acquisition of Novel Behaviors:** To allow humanoid robots to learn new and challenging skills rapidly and effectively, leveraging the extensive behavioral knowledge encoded within the pre-trained BFM, rather than starting from scratch.
4. **Demonstrate Versatility and Robustness on Real Humanoid Platforms:** To validate the BFM's capabilities not only in simulation but also through successful deployment on a physical humanoid robot, proving its practical applicability and sim-to-real transfer potential.
5. **Explore the Utility of a Structured Latent Space:** To investigate how the generative model's latent space can be leveraged for advanced applications such as behavior composition (combining elements from different behaviors) and modulation (adjusting the style or intensity of a behavior).

In essence, the overarching objective is to lay the groundwork for a new generation of general-purpose humanoid robots that are highly adaptable, learn efficiently, and can operate flexibly across a broad spectrum of real-world tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) proposes a sophisticated, multi-stage methodology to achieve versatile and generalizable humanoid control. The approach is primarily rooted in generative modeling and online distillation, leveraging large-scale behavioral data.

#### 3.1. Unified Behavioral Formulation

The authors first redefine humanoid control. Instead of viewing tasks as distinct entities, they conceptualize them as the generation of "behaviors," which are essentially trajectories over the robot's real-world proprioceptive states and actions. Goal states or control modes are treated as external specifications that *drive* the generation of these behaviors, rather than being intrinsic to the behavior definition itself. This decoupling is critical for creating a unified learning framework.

#### 3.2. Proxy Agent Training via Motion Imitation

To acquire a diverse and high-quality behavioral dataset, the methodology employs a "proxy agent" (π_proxy). This agent is trained through **motion imitation** in simulation using a goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework (Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO).
- **Dataset Source:** The publicly available AMASS dataset of human motions is used, which is parameterized by the SMPL model. A two-stage retargeting process adapts these human motions to the humanoid robot's embodiment. Notably, the raw retargeted data is used directly for BFM pretraining, bypassing further filtering by a motion imitator, which simplifies the pipeline.
- **State Space:** The proxy agent's state space includes privileged proprioceptive states (rigid-body position, joint position, orientation, linear/joint/angular velocities, previous action) and goal states derived from future reference poses.
- **Reward Design and Domain Randomization:** A composite reward function is used, encompassing task rewards for motion imitation, regularization terms, and penalties, with curriculum learning to progressively shape behaviors. Extensive domain randomization (e.g., varying CoM offsets, link masses, friction, PD gains, and applying external perturbations) is applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transferability and robustness.
- **Training Enhancements:** Techniques like Reference State Initialization (RSI), simplified early termination conditions (based on a single tracking tolerance), and hard negative mining (dynamically adjusting sampling probability for failed motions) are employed to improve training efficiency and ensure comprehensive dataset coverage. The proxy agent, once trained, can generate large quantities of on-policy behavioral data.

#### 3.3. BFM Pretraining with Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) and Online Distillation

The core of the methodology is the BFM itself, designed as a generative model that learns the underlying distribution of demonstrated behaviors.
- **Generative Objective:** The BFM aims to maximize the expected log-likelihood of actions given real-world proprioceptive states. This is reformulated using Jensen's Inequality to maximize a tractable lower bound, incorporating goal states drawn from a distribution specific to the current proprioception.
- **Real-world Proprioception Design:** The input `s_p,real_t` for the BFM is carefully constructed to include historical information (stacked joint positions, velocities, root angular velocity, projected gravity, and previous actions over 25 steps), making it robust for real-world deployment.
- **Control Interface and Mask Strategy:** To support arbitrary control modes, a unified low-level control interface is designed, encompassing root control, kinematic position control, and joint angle control. Crucially, diverse control modes are activated by applying **bit-wise binary masks** to this interface. Unlike prior works that use fixed or two-stage mask strategies, BFM samples each mask element independently from a Bernoulli distribution B(0.5), allowing for highly flexible and arbitrary mode combinations. A mask curriculum (cold-start) is used to stabilize early training.
- **CVAE Architecture:** A Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) is chosen to model the conditional probability of actions. The CVAE's Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) is the optimization objective.
 - To encourage the latent space `z` to encode richer behavioral knowledge, the goal state `s_g,real_t` is *removed from the input of the decoder*.
 - The encoder `q(z|s_p,sim_t, s_g,sim_t)` is designed as a residual to the prior `P(z|s_p,real_t, s_g,real_t)` and incorporates the current mask `m_t` as an input.
- **Online Distillation (DAgger Framework):** The BFM is pre-trained using the DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) framework. In each episode, the BFM executes actions in simulation, and at each timestep, the proxy agent (acting as an expert) provides a reference action. The BFM's parameters are then updated using a loss function that combines an L2 norm (between BFM's action and the proxy's action) and a KL-Divergence term (for latent space regularization).

#### 3.4. BFM Application Strategies

- **Direct Steering:** BFM can be directly applied to WBC tasks like motion tracking, VR teleoperation, and locomotion by simply crafting the appropriate mask for the control interface.
- **Behavior Composition and Modulation:** The structured latent space of the CVAE allows for advanced manipulation:
 - **Composition:** Linear interpolation in the latent space between latent variables derived from different control modes can generate novel, complex behaviors (e.g., combining turning and leg-raising for a Roundhouse Kick).
 - **Modulation:** Linear extrapolation in the latent space (analogous to Classifier-free Guidance) can modulate behavior characteristics, such as improving balance during a challenging maneuver (e.g., Butterfly Kick).
- **Efficient Behavior Acquisition:** For truly novel or challenging skills not perfectly captured by the pre-trained BFM, the model's parameters are frozen. A small **residual decoder** is then trained on top of the BFM, learning a `Δa_t` (residual action) to be added to the BFM's output. This leverages the BFM's extensive behavioral knowledge, significantly accelerating the learning of new skills.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning data generation, pretraining with a powerful generative model, and flexible application strategies, underpins the BFM's ability to achieve versatility and efficient learning in humanoid robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents extensive experimental results, both in simulation (IsaacGym, Mujoco) and on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, to validate the effectiveness and versatility of the Behavior Foundation Model (BFM). The findings unequivocally support the authors' claims regarding generalization, adaptability, and efficient skill acquisition.

#### 4.1. Cross-Task Generalization and Versatile Steering

The BFM's ability to generalize across diverse Whole-Body Control (WBC) tasks and control modes is a primary finding.
- **Superior Performance:** BFM consistently outperforms HOVER, a prominent general baseline, across three distinct tasks: whole-body motion tracking, VR teleoperation, and locomotion. This superior performance is observed across all evaluated datasets (AMASS Train, AMASS Test, 100STYLE) and metrics (Mean Per-Keypoint Error, Mean Per-Joint Error, root linear/angular velocity tracking errors).
- **Competitive with Specialists:** Crucially, BFM demonstrates performance that is "as good as, if not better than" task-specific specialist policies. This highlights that BFM can achieve high fidelity in a generalizable framework, overcoming the typical trade-off between generality and performance.
- **Validation of Training Paradigm:** The BFM, trained with online distillation from a proxy agent, significantly outperforms RL policies trained from scratch using the same architecture and hyperparameters. This confirms the efficacy of the proposed pretraining paradigm in acquiring broad behavioral knowledge.

#### 4.2. Structured Latent Space for Behavior Composition and Modulation

The use of a CVAE for BFM allows for a structured latent space, which provides powerful capabilities for manipulating behaviors.
- **Meaningful Latent Structure:** t-SNE visualization of latent variables (from different fundamental behaviors like standing, walking, sidestepping) reveals clear directionality, symmetry, and pre-clustering. This indicates that the CVAE successfully learns a semantically meaningful and structured latent manifold.
- **Behavior Composition:** Linear interpolation between latent variables derived from different control modes (e.g., root-only control vs. keypoint-only control for a Roundhouse Kick) successfully generates novel and complex behaviors. For instance, interpolating between a "turning movement without raising a leg" and "raising a leg without turning" enables the robot to perform a complete Roundhouse Kick, demonstrating the BFM's ability to compose new skills from learned components.
- **Behavior Modulation:** Linear extrapolation in the latent space, akin to Classifier-free Guidance, allows for modulating behavior characteristics. For example, applying this technique enables the humanoid to maintain balance during a challenging Butterfly Kick, where it previously failed. Quantitatively, this modulation also yields improvements in motion tracking performance for specific lambda values.

#### 4.3. Efficient Acquisition of Novel Behaviors

The pre-trained BFM serves as a robust foundation for learning new skills rapidly.
- **Accelerated Learning with Residuals:** By freezing the pre-trained BFM and training only a small residual decoder for novel behaviors, the system achieves significantly faster and more accurate learning compared to training an RL policy from scratch. This is vividly demonstrated with challenging motions like a Side Salto, where the BFM-aided residual learning avoids inefficient exploration at early stages and yields superior tracking results. The pre-encoded behavioral knowledge effectively provides a powerful warm-start.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment

The research validates the BFM's robustness and transferability by deploying it on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot. This critical step demonstrates the practical viability of the model beyond simulation environments, showcasing its potential for real-world applications. The supplementary videos further illustrate the robot performing diverse behaviors in zero-shot fashion and acquiring new skills efficiently.

In summary, the main findings establish BFM as a highly versatile and robust foundation model for humanoid control. It not only achieves state-of-the-art generalization and performance across various tasks but also unlocks advanced capabilities like behavior composition, modulation, and efficient acquisition of novel skills, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable humanoid robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) for humanoid robots presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence, with far-reaching potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in Humanoid Control

The most profound significance of BFM is its introduction of a novel paradigm for humanoid control. By shifting focus from isolated, task-specific learning to **unified, holistic behavior learning** via a generative foundation model, it redefines how robotic skills are acquired and generalized. This conceptual shift, inspired by the success of foundation models in other AI domains, offers a pathway towards more general-purpose and adaptable humanoid robots.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability

BFM directly addresses a critical bottleneck in current humanoid robotics: the lack of generalization across diverse tasks and control modes. Its ability to operate in a zero-shot manner across motion tracking, teleoperation, and locomotion, and to support *arbitrary* combinations of control modes, is a substantial leap forward. This enhanced versatility means humanoids can respond flexibly to unforeseen commands and adapt to complex, dynamic environments without constant, laborious reprogramming.

#### 5.3. Accelerated and Efficient Skill Acquisition

The BFM's capacity to facilitate the efficient acquisition of novel and challenging behaviors through residual learning is highly impactful. By leveraging a broad base of pre-trained behavioral knowledge, the time and computational resources required to teach a robot new skills are dramatically reduced. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing complex robotic applications and opens up possibilities for robots to learn and evolve their capabilities more autonomously.

#### 5.4. Unlocking Advanced Behavioral Manipulation

The demonstration of behavior composition and modulation through the structured latent space of the CVAE is a crucial innovation. This allows for creating entirely new behaviors by interpolating between existing ones and finely tuning aspects of a behavior (e.g., balancing during a complex maneuver). Such capabilities are vital for enabling robots to exhibit nuanced, context-aware, and sophisticated actions, moving beyond rigid, pre-programmed movements.

#### 5.5. Paving the Way for General-Purpose Humanoid Robots

BFM represents a promising step toward the long-term goal of developing truly general-purpose humanoid robots. Robots equipped with such a foundation model could potentially interact with the world and learn new skills in a manner akin to humans, adapting to a vast array of tasks and environments. This has implications for diverse sectors, including manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, personal assistance, and exploration, where flexible and intelligent physical agents are highly desirable.

#### 5.6. Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer

The successful deployment of BFM on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrates robust sim-to-real transfer, a perennial challenge in robotics. The methodologies employed, such as extensive domain randomization and careful state space design, contribute to building more reliable and deployable robotic policies.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

The work also naturally outlines future research avenues, such as extending the simplified control interface to support an even broader range of control modes, including more abstract ones like natural language. It encourages further exploration into large-scale, diverse behavioral datasets for humanoid robots and the development of more advanced foundation model architectures tailored for physical interaction.

In conclusion, the Behavior Foundation Model moves beyond incremental improvements in task-specific control, offering a holistic and generalizable framework for humanoid robot intelligence. Its impact could reshape how humanoid robots are designed, trained, and deployed, accelerating their integration into real-world applications and fostering the development of truly autonomous and intelligent robotic companions.
