# Behavior Foundation Model for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and collaborating universities developed a Behavior Foundation Model (BFM) for humanoid robots, which learns a unified distribution of behaviors to achieve zero-shot generalization across various control modes and accelerates the acquisition of new skills. The model demonstrates superior performance over general baselines and enables advanced behavioral composition and modulation on a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body control (WBC) frameworks for humanoids are task-specific, demanding extensive reward engineering for each new skill.
- Traditional WBC systems exhibit poor generalization across diverse tasks and control modes, limiting their real-world applicability and adaptability.
- Acquiring novel and challenging humanoid skills is typically slow and inefficient, often requiring training from scratch for each new behavior.

## Method

- A ==Behavior Foundation Model (BFM)==, based on a Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE), learns a unified distribution of "behaviors" (robot states and actions) independent of specific control modes.
- A "proxy agent" trained with goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning and motion imitation generates a diverse, high-quality behavioral dataset from human motions.
- Online distillation (DAgger) pre-trains the BFM, enabling it to generalize across arbitrary control modes through a flexible bit-wise binary mask strategy applied to its unified low-level control interface.

## Results

- The BFM consistently outperforms general baselines like HOVER across whole-body motion tracking, VR teleoperation, and locomotion, achieving performance comparable to or better than task-specific specialist policies.
- The model's structured latent space successfully enables behavior composition (e.g., performing a Roundhouse Kick) and modulation (e.g., improving balance for a Butterfly Kick).
- New and challenging skills, such as a Side Salto, are acquired significantly faster and more accurately by training a residual decoder on top of the frozen BFM compared to learning an RL policy from scratch.
- The BFM successfully deploys on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, showcasing robust sim-to-real transfer and practical applicability for diverse zero-shot behaviors and efficiently learned new skills.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Defining all humanoid control objectives as the generation of "behaviors" allows for a unified learning framework that decouples skills from their specific control modes.
> - A structured latent space within a generative model facilitates advanced manipulation, such as composing new behaviors by interpolating between latent variables and modulating behavior characteristics through extrapolation.
> - A pre-trained behavioral foundation model acts as a powerful prior, significantly accelerating the learning of novel robotic skills by allowing only a small residual decoder to be trained for new tasks.
