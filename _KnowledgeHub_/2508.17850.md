# GEPO: Group Expectation Policy Optimization for Stable Heterogeneous Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Pengcheng Laboratory's GEPO algorithm, integrated into the HeteroRL framework, enables stable and efficient reinforcement learning for large language models in decentralized, high-latency, and heterogeneous computing environments. It achieves this by introducing group-level importance weighting, which dramatically reduces the variance of importance weights and prevents training collapse.

## Problem

- Traditional synchronous Reinforcement Learning (RL) frameworks are incompatible with decentralized, geographically distributed environments for Large Language Model (LLM) training, leading to severe inefficiencies.
- High network latency in distributed settings causes 'policy staleness' and large KL divergence, resulting in an explosion of importance weight variance and training instability or reward collapse.
- Existing asynchronous RL methods, relying primarily on importance weight clipping or truncation, are insufficient to ensure stability under significant latency and policy divergence in truly heterogeneous setups.

## Method

- The ==HeteroRL== framework is introduced, an asynchronous and geographically distributed architecture that decouples rollout sampling and parameter learning to enhance efficiency and tolerate high latency across heterogeneous hardware.
- The **GEPO** (Group Expectation Policy Optimization) algorithm replaces traditional token-level or sequence-level importance weights with group-level importance weights, estimated through a Group Expectation Importance Weight (GEIW) mechanism.
- GEPO's GEIW uses a weighted expectation over a group of responses, providing a more numerically stable denominator for importance weights that significantly reduces variance under high KL divergence, leading to more stable gradient estimation.

## Results

- GEPO consistently outperformed all baselines in online RL settings, with the Qwen3-**8B** model achieving an average score of 65.0 on MATH benchmarks, surpassing GRPO by 3.2 points and GSPO by 4.1 points.
- In high-latency heterogeneous environments, GEPO demonstrated decisive stability, reducing 'best-to-last' performance degradation by **85%** compared to GSPO (from 12.0 to 1.8 average points degradation).
- Empirical evidence confirmed GEPO's significantly lower importance weight variance and smoother, bounded gradient norms compared to baselines, directly validating its theoretical claims of exponential variance reduction under high KL divergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary cause of instability in asynchronous RL under high latency is the variance explosion of importance weights due to high KL divergence between the behavior and target policies.
> - Using a coarser granularity for importance weights (group-level compared to token or sequence-level) can significantly reduce gradient variance and stabilize training in high KL divergence regimes.
> - Localized reward computation, where groups of generations are scored within the same process or node, substantially reduces communication overhead and mitigates sensitivity to network latency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of increasingly large AI models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), has pushed the limits of single-datacenter computing, necessitating a shift towards decentralized training across geographically distributed and heterogeneous nodes connected via the Internet. This paradigm shift introduces unprecedented challenges for traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, which are crucial for advanced LLM post-training techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and complex task reasoning.

**The Core Problem:** Traditional RL frameworks are architecturally incompatible with decentralized environments. Their tight coupling between rollout sampling (data generation) and parameter learning (model updates) demands strict synchronization, which becomes untenable under high network latency and computational heterogeneity inherent in real-world distributed settings. This incompatibility manifests as two critical bottlenecks:
1. **Efficiency:** Synchronous frameworks force computational resources to idle while waiting for the slowest processes, severely constraining overall efficiency.
2. **Stability:** Inevitable network latency creates "policy staleness," a temporal gap between the sampler's (behavior policy) and the learner's (target policy) versions. This high KL divergence between policies causes the variance of importance weights to explode, leading to training instability or reward collapse.

**Existing Solutions and Their Limitations:** The paper contextualizes its contribution against existing asynchronous and off-policy RL work for LLMs:
- **Asynchronous RL Frameworks (e.g., AREAL, Prime Intellect, LlamaRL):** While these works decouple generation and training, they largely *assume centralized, low-latency infrastructure* and support only *limited degrees of asynchrony*. They do not fully address the challenges of *geographically distributed, high-latency, and heterogeneous* environments.
- **Asynchronous RL Algorithms (e.g., Decoupled PPO, Truncated IS/IMPALA, CISPO, TOPR):** These methods typically employ gradient truncation or importance weight truncation (clipping/bounding) to manage variance in off-policy learning. However, the paper argues and empirically demonstrates that these approaches, even with KL regularization, are *insufficient* to ensure stability under significant latency and policy divergence. They might preserve gradient contributions from some samples but often fail to robustly handle the extreme variance explosion.

**GEPO's Unique Position:** This work directly tackles the *root cause* of instability in these challenging environments: the variance explosion of importance weights due to high KL divergence induced by latency. Instead of merely clipping or truncating, GEPO fundamentally improves the quality of gradient estimation by adopting a novel "group expectation importance weighting" mechanism. By proposing both the **HeteroRL framework** (a system-level solution) and the **GEPO algorithm** (an algorithmic solution), the paper offers a comprehensive, integrated approach designed specifically for truly heterogeneous, high-latency, and decentralized compute networks, a critical frontier for scalable AI training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable **stable and efficient reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) in decentralized, geographically distributed, and resource-heterogeneous computing environments.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational and Power Constraints:** The sheer scale of modern LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Llama 3) is rapidly outstripping the capacity of single-datacenter computing. Decentralized training across distributed nodes is becoming a necessity to meet these growing computational demands and overcome power limitations.
2. **Overcoming RL's Architectural Incompatibility:** Traditional synchronous RL methods, which are vital for enhancing LLM capabilities through post-training (e.g., mathematical reasoning, alignment), are fundamentally incompatible with decentralized setups. Their requirement for tight coupling between data sampling and parameter updates leads to severe inefficiencies and instability when faced with network delays and heterogeneous resources.
3. **Mitigating Policy Staleness and Training Instability:** In asynchronous distributed environments, network latency inevitably creates a temporal gap between the policy used by samplers to generate data and the latest policy being updated by the learner. This "policy staleness" leads to high KL divergence between policies, causing the variance of importance weights to explode, which is a major source of training instability and reward collapse in conventional RL algorithms.
4. **Enabling Efficient Resource Utilization:** Synchronous RL frameworks waste computational resources by forcing faster nodes to wait for slower ones. A decentralized, asynchronous design is needed to maximize the utilization of heterogeneous hardware (e.g., mixing NVIDIA and Ascend chips) and prevent resource idling.
5. **Establishing a Practical Foundation for Distributed AI:** The research aims to provide both a robust algorithmic solution and a practical system-level framework to make large-scale, delay-tolerant decentralized RL a reality, establishing a foundation for future distributed AI training in real-world heterogeneous compute networks.

In essence, the authors are motivated to bridge the gap between the increasing demand for massive LLM training and the limitations of current RL approaches in distributed, real-world computing infrastructures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **HeteroRL**, a novel heterogeneous RL architecture, and its core algorithmic component, **Group Expectation Policy Optimization (GEPO)**, to address the challenges of decentralized LLM training.

#### 3.1. HeteroRL Framework

HeteroRL is designed as an asynchronous, geographically distributed, and resource-heterogeneous framework.
- **Decoupling:** The framework fundamentally decouples the two computationally intensive phases of the RL pipeline: **rollout sampling** (data generation) and **parameter learning** (model updates). Sampler nodes continuously generate reasoning trajectories, while a learner node asynchronously consumes this data to update model parameters.
- **Asynchronous Operation:** Neither component waits for the other. Communication is infrequent and designed to tolerate high latency, with model checkpoints and rollout batches exchanged over the Internet.
- **Heterogeneous Environment:** The framework explicitly supports physically or logically independent nodes with potentially heterogeneous hardware (e.g., NVIDIA and Ascend chips). The experimental setup uses a star-shaped topology with one learner and four sampler nodes.
- **Latency Modeling:** Network delays between sampler and learner nodes are explicitly modeled using stochastic distributions (log-normal, Weibull, exponential) to simulate real-world conditions. A sliding window mechanism ensures the learner processes data within a fixed time window (e.g., max 1800 seconds).
- **Engineering Optimization (Localized Reward Computation):** To further reduce communication overhead, a key optimization ensures that each group of generations is entirely generated and scored within the *same process or node*. This allows for local computation of group statistics (mean, standard deviation for baselining) without requiring costly cross-process `all_gather` operations, making the system less sensitive to communication latency.

#### 3.2. GEPO Algorithm: Group Expectation Policy Optimization

GEPO is introduced to address the training instability arising from KL divergence under high-latency conditions by stabilizing importance weights.
- **Problem Identification:** The paper's analysis reveals that high latency significantly increases KL divergence between sampler and learner policies, leading to an explosion in the variance of importance weights.
- **Paradigm Shift: Group-Level Importance Weighting:** GEPO's core innovation is to replace the traditional token-level or sequence-level importance weights with **group-level importance weights**. This is motivated by the insight that a coarser granularity of importance weights (group > sequence > token) can significantly reduce gradient variance.
- **Group Expectation Importance Weight (GEIW):**
 - For a given input `x`, a group of `G` responses `{y1..., yG}` is sampled from the stale policy `q`.
 - Instead of using `q(y|x)` in the denominator of the importance weight (as in standard IS), GEPO uses its *group-wise expected value* under the current prompt `x`, denoted as `E_q[q(y|x)]`.
 - This expected value is estimated using a *weighted expectation*: `sum(q(yi|x)^2) / sum(q(yi|x))`, where `q(yi|x)` is the probability of the i-th response in the group.
 - The GEIW importance weight is then `w_GEIW(y|x) = p(y|x) / E_q[q(y|x)]`.
- **Key Advantages of GEIW:**
 1. **Numerical Stability and Gradient Effectiveness:** The denominator `E_q[q(y|x)]` is decoupled from any single `q(y|x)`, preventing extreme weight values when individual proposal probabilities approach zero. This avoids the issue of clipped gradients being zeroed out, leading to more effective updates.
 2. **Biased Yet Low-Variance:** While introducing a small bias, GEIW leverages within-group statistical information to provide a more reliable scale estimate. It is proven to significantly reduce variance under high KL divergence, leading to more stable gradient directions and improved training convergence.
- **Theoretical Guarantees (Theorem 1 and 3):** The paper provides a mathematical proof (Appendix A) demonstrating that GEPO exponentially reduces the variance of importance weights under high KL divergence compared to standard importance sampling (`Var_std - Var_new >= exp(D_KL(p||q)) - C`). An extended theorem shows this property holds even for advantage-weighted importance sampling.
- **Gradient Comparison:** GEPO's use of a common denominator `E_q[q(y|x)]` across the entire group (vs. `q(y_i|x)` for GSPO or `q(y_i,t|x)` for GRPO) highlights how coarser granularity enhances robustness and stabilizes training.

The combined approach of HeteroRL for system design and GEPO for algorithmic stability offers a comprehensive solution for large-scale, delay-tolerant decentralized RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly support the effectiveness and stability of the HeteroRL framework with GEPO.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance and Stability Across Settings

- **Online RL (Zero-Delay):** GEPO consistently outperforms all baselines (BNPO, Dr.GRPO, GRPO, GSPO) on Qwen3-1.7B/8B models across MATH benchmarks (AMC2023, AIME2024, AIME2025, MATH500). For Qwen3-8B, GEPO achieved an average score of 65.0, surpassing GRPO (61.8) and GSPO (60.5) by 3.2 and 4.1 points, respectively. This demonstrates that GEPO's group-level importance weighting improves gradient quality even without network latency.
- **Heterogeneous RL (High-Delay):** GEPO's stability advantage becomes "decisive." While GEPO and GSPO both improve over GRPO in "best performance," GEPO significantly outperforms GSPO in both accuracy (+7.2%) and stability. GEPO reduces the "best-to-last" performance degradation by an remarkable 85% compared to GSPO (∆=1.8 vs. 12.0 average). This highlights GEPO's ability to maintain near-peak performance throughout training, even under high latency, where other methods (like GSPO) show dramatic collapses in their "last scores."

#### 4.2. Mechanism of Stability

- **Importance Weight Variance and Gradient Norms (Figure 4):** GEPO exhibits significantly lower variance in importance weights compared to GRPO and GSPO, which show sharp spikes and fluctuations. This translates to smoother and bounded gradient norms for GEPO, indicating stable learning progress. In contrast, GRPO and GSPO exhibit large oscillations in gradient norms, leading to periods of stagnation or degradation in training reward.
- **Theoretical Validation:** This empirical observation directly validates Theorem 1 and 3, which state that GEPO exponentially reduces the variance of importance weights under high KL divergence.

#### 4.3. Impact of Latency

- **Correlation of Latency, KL Divergence, and Variance (Figure 5 & 7):** The study establishes a strong positive correlation between increased latency steps and higher KL divergence between sampler and learner, leading to increased variance of importance weights, and ultimately, higher estimation error of the advantage function. Correlation coefficients range from 0.76 to 0.96.
- **Latency-Induced Instability (Figure 6):** Experiments comparing 8-step and 64-step maximum delays demonstrate that higher latency causes spikes in KL divergence and sharp drops in evaluation accuracy, confirming that latency is a significant factor in training instability. Even GEPO experiences a performance dip under the highest latency, underscoring the inherent challenges of this setting, but it remains far more robust than baselines.

#### 4.4. Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Robustness

- **Group Size:** A group size of 8 provides optimal stability with consistently high final performance in heterogeneous RL settings.
- **KL Regularization:** A β_KL coefficient of 0.005 is critical for stability; values that are too low lead to training collapse, while values that are too high limit peak performance.
- **Latency Distribution:** The log-normal distribution for simulating latency yielded the best results, though GEPO showed robustness across Weibull and exponential distributions.
- **Sampling Strategies:** A temperature of 0.4 enhances stability, and `top-p=1.0` combined with `top-k=10` offers the best balance between peak and final performance. These findings validate the design choices made for the main experiments.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Asynchronous RL Baselines

- **Insufficiency of Clipping:** Asynchronous baselines like Truncated IS, TOPR, and CISPO, even when augmented with KL regularization, exhibit severe instability or collapse under high-latency conditions. This strongly suggests that mere clipping of importance weights is insufficient to ensure training stability in truly heterogeneous and high-latency environments, reinforcing GEPO's fundamental algorithmic advantage.

#### 4.6. Ablation and Think vs. Non-think Modes

- **Ablation Study (Table 13):** Confirmed that group-level importance weighting dramatically outperforms token-level (GRPO) and sequence-level (GSPO) IWs in terms of stability and final performance.
- **Think vs. Non-think Mode:** While "think mode" (internal monologue for reasoning) can offer marginal peak performance gains, it often introduces instability and higher computational overhead due to longer, potentially "overlength" rollouts. GEPO, particularly in "non-think mode," provides unmatched stability and efficiency, making it ideal for latency-sensitive or real-time deployments where interpretability from reasoning chains is not a primary concern.

In summary, GEPO, as part of the HeteroRL framework, demonstrates superior performance and significantly enhanced stability under both ideal and challenging high-latency, heterogeneous conditions. Its core mechanism of group expectation importance weighting effectively mitigates the variance explosion problem, making it a robust solution for the next generation of decentralized LLM training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model training and distributed artificial intelligence, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Scalable LLM Training:** The work directly addresses the critical bottleneck of scaling LLM training beyond the limits of single datacenters. By providing a robust framework and algorithm for decentralized training across geographically distributed and heterogeneous compute networks, HeteroRL with GEPO makes the development of even larger and more capable AI models feasible. This is crucial for future AI research and application, as model sizes continue to grow.

2. **Fundamental Algorithmic Contribution to RL:** GEPO offers a principled algorithmic solution to a long-standing challenge in off-policy and asynchronous Reinforcement Learning: the explosion of importance weight variance under high policy divergence. Its theoretical guarantee of exponentially reducing variance, especially in high KL divergence regimes, is a fundamental contribution to RL theory and practice. This goes beyond existing clipping or truncation methods, offering a more robust and effective approach to gradient estimation in dynamic environments.

3. **Practicality for Real-World Deployments:** The framework's explicit design for high-latency, heterogeneous environments makes it highly relevant for real-world scenarios where compute resources are geographically dispersed, network conditions are unpredictable, and hardware capabilities vary (e.g., mixing different GPU types or even custom AI accelerators like Ascend chips). This unlocks the potential for a broader range of organizations and communities to participate in and benefit from advanced AI model training.

4. **Improved Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** By decoupling sampling and learning, HeteroRL eliminates synchronization bottlenecks and reduces resource idling. The engineering optimization of localized reward computation further enhances efficiency by minimizing cross-node communication. This means more effective utilization of expensive distributed computational resources, leading to faster training times and lower operational costs.

5. **Enhanced Stability and Reliability of RL for LLMs:** The demonstrated superior stability of GEPO, particularly its ability to maintain high performance and prevent reward collapse under significant network delays, is critical for the reliable application of RL techniques (like RLHF) to LLMs. This reliability ensures that the training process converges consistently to high-quality policies, which is paramount for sensitive applications.

6. **Paving the Way for Future Distributed AI Research:** This work establishes a practical foundation for delay-tolerant decentralized RL, opening up new avenues for research into distributed AI systems. Future work, such as "defensive sampling" and "smooth denominator mechanisms," can build upon GEPO's insights to further refine bias-variance trade-offs and enhance robustness.

7. **Democratization of Advanced AI Training:** By making large-scale LLM training more accessible and robust across diverse, distributed infrastructures, this research could potentially democratize access to cutting-edge AI capabilities. Smaller organizations or research groups without access to single, massive supercomputing clusters might be able to pool distributed resources effectively.

In conclusion, "GEPO: Group Expectation Policy Optimization for Stable Heterogeneous Reinforcement Learning" addresses a crucial and emerging challenge in the era of large AI models. Its blend of a robust system architecture and a theoretically grounded, empirically proven algorithm offers a significant leap forward, making scalable, stable, and efficient RL training a reality for decentralized and heterogeneous computing environments.
