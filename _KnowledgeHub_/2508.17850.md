# GEPO: Group Expectation Policy Optimization for Stable Heterogeneous Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Pengcheng Laboratory's GEPO algorithm, integrated into the HeteroRL framework, enables stable and efficient reinforcement learning for large language models in decentralized, high-latency, and heterogeneous computing environments. It achieves this by introducing group-level importance weighting, which dramatically reduces the variance of importance weights and prevents training collapse.

## Problem

- Traditional synchronous Reinforcement Learning (RL) frameworks are incompatible with decentralized, geographically distributed environments for Large Language Model (LLM) training, leading to severe inefficiencies.
- High network latency in distributed settings causes 'policy staleness' and large KL divergence, resulting in an explosion of importance weight variance and training instability or reward collapse.
- Existing asynchronous RL methods, relying primarily on importance weight clipping or truncation, are insufficient to ensure stability under significant latency and policy divergence in truly heterogeneous setups.

## Method

- The ==HeteroRL== framework is introduced, an asynchronous and geographically distributed architecture that decouples rollout sampling and parameter learning to enhance efficiency and tolerate high latency across heterogeneous hardware.
- The **GEPO** (Group Expectation Policy Optimization) algorithm replaces traditional token-level or sequence-level importance weights with group-level importance weights, estimated through a Group Expectation Importance Weight (GEIW) mechanism.
- GEPO's GEIW uses a weighted expectation over a group of responses, providing a more numerically stable denominator for importance weights that significantly reduces variance under high KL divergence, leading to more stable gradient estimation.

## Results

- GEPO consistently outperformed all baselines in online RL settings, with the Qwen3-**8B** model achieving an average score of 65.0 on MATH benchmarks, surpassing GRPO by 3.2 points and GSPO by 4.1 points.
- In high-latency heterogeneous environments, GEPO demonstrated decisive stability, reducing 'best-to-last' performance degradation by **85%** compared to GSPO (from 12.0 to 1.8 average points degradation).
- Empirical evidence confirmed GEPO's significantly lower importance weight variance and smoother, bounded gradient norms compared to baselines, directly validating its theoretical claims of exponential variance reduction under high KL divergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary cause of instability in asynchronous RL under high latency is the variance explosion of importance weights due to high KL divergence between the behavior and target policies.
> - Using a coarser granularity for importance weights (group-level compared to token or sequence-level) can significantly reduce gradient variance and stabilize training in high KL divergence regimes.
> - Localized reward computation, where groups of generations are scored within the same process or node, substantially reduces communication overhead and mitigates sensitivity to network latency.
