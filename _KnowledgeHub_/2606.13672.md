# $\texttt{WEAVER}$, Better, Faster, Longer: An Effective World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A team from Mila, Carnegie Mellon, and McGill developed WEAVER, a world model for robotic manipulation designed to concurrently achieve high visual fidelity, long-horizon temporal consistency, and efficient inference. This model outperforms leading multi-view manipulation world models, demonstrating over 8x faster inference while providing superior fidelity, and facilitates real-world policy evaluation, improvement, and test-time planning.

## Problem

- Current robot world models face a trade-off among fidelity (physical accuracy), consistency (long-term coherence), and efficiency (inference speed).
- Models like Ctrl-World designed for robotic manipulation often exhibit slow inference, precluding real-time planning and computationally intensive policy improvement.
- The demanding nature of robotic manipulation requires accurate physical state predictions, multi-view handling, and inference of occluded objects, which existing models struggle to deliver jointly.

## Method

- **WEAVER** utilizes a ==latent dynamics== model with a pretrained Stable Diffusion 3 VAE encoder/decoder to predict future multi-view images and proprioceptive states.
- It employs sparse memory and short-term history conditioning within its 2D transformer-based dynamics model to enhance long-horizon temporal consistency.
- The model achieves high efficiency through KV caching, a progressive noise schedule, and an optional Rectified Flow post-training step for faster inference at test time.

## Results

- WEAVER achieves superior visual fidelity (lower FID/FVD) and consistency over long horizons, while demonstrating over **8x** faster inference speeds compared to the Ctrl-World model.
- Finetuned WEAVER (WEAVER-FT) shows a Pearson correlation of **0.870** with real-world task success rates for policy evaluation and boosts policy improvement by **38%** in real-world success over the base policy when using synthetic data.
- The model enables practical test-time planning with a 5-**10x** speedup in the planning pipeline, leading to an average **15%** real-world success rate improvement for manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging pretrained foundation models (like Stable Diffusion VAE) within a world model architecture allows for high visual fidelity and robust generalization in robotic contexts.
> - Incorporating multi-view image prediction alongside proprioceptive state prediction is critical for accurate simulation of complex robotic manipulation tasks, especially with occlusions and contact.
> - Efficient inference techniques such as KV caching and rectified flow post-training can significantly accelerate world model operations, enabling real-time test-time planning without sacrificing prediction quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models (WMs), or learned simulators, represent a significant area of interest in artificial intelligence, particularly in robotics. These models hold the promise of enabling robots to evaluate and improve policies and perform test-time planning with reduced reliance on costly and potentially hazardous real-world interactions. The development of effective WMs is considered foundational for scaling robotic learning and deployment.

For a WM to effectively support these downstream robotic capabilities, three core desiderata have been identified:
1. **Fidelity:** The ability to produce physically accurate predictions that correlate with real-world outcomes.
2. **Consistency:** The capacity to maintain coherent predictions over extended prediction horizons, crucial for multi-stage tasks.
3. **Efficiency:** The speed at which predictions are generated, which is critical for real-time planning and computationally intensive policy improvement.

Prior research has made progress in various aspects of world modeling. Video generation models, for instance, have demonstrated high fidelity but often at the expense of efficiency. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)-style WMs learn latent states, but these may not be directly decodable into the visual observations necessary for evaluating arbitrary visuomotor robot policies. Models like Dreamer-v4 learn encoders from scratch, which can impact robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios. For robotic manipulation specifically, challenges arise from the need to handle multiple camera views, infer occluded objects from historical data, and ensure predictions reflect physical reality rather than mere visual aesthetics. State-of-the-art manipulation WMs, such as Ctrl-World, have achieved fidelity but typically operate at speeds slower than real-time, which limits their applicability in test-time planning and makes policy improvement computationally demanding.

WEAVER aims to address these identified limitations by developing a world model that concurrently satisfies all three desiderata—fidelity, consistency, and efficiency—within the context of complex robotic manipulation tasks, thereby building upon and differentiating itself from existing approaches in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop WEAVER (World Estimation Across Views for Embodied Reasoning), a world model architecture designed to simultaneously achieve high fidelity, long-horizon consistency, and efficient generation of simulated trajectories for robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from the identified shortcomings of existing world models in meeting these three critical desiderata in a unified manner, which consequently limits their practical applicability in robotics.

Specifically, the motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Enabling Downstream Robotic Capabilities:** World models are posited to unlock significant advances in robotic policy evaluation, policy improvement, and test-time planning. However, existing models often fall short in one or more of the necessary attributes to fully realize these applications. High-fidelity predictions are essential for accurately assessing policy performance against real-world outcomes. Long-horizon consistency is crucial for simulating multi-stage tasks and understanding the cumulative effects of actions over time, which is particularly relevant for complex manipulation sequences involving occlusions or dynamic interactions. Efficient generation is paramount for real-time planning, where rapid assessment of multiple action trajectories is required, and for computationally feasible policy improvement processes that involve large-scale synthetic data generation.

- **Addressing Trade-offs in Existing World Models:**
 - **Fidelity vs. Efficiency:** Current video generation models often produce high-fidelity visual outputs but are computationally expensive, making them impractical for real-time robotic applications.
 - **Latent State Decodability:** Some models, such as JEPA-style WMs, predict latent states that may not be directly interpretable or decodable into the visual images required to evaluate arbitrary visuomotor robot policies. WEAVER aims to produce predictions that are directly usable for policy evaluation.
 - **Out-of-Distribution Robustness:** Approaches like Dreamer-v4, which learn encoders from scratch, may exhibit reduced robustness when encountering visual inputs that deviate from their training distribution. The research seeks to improve this aspect by leveraging pre-trained components.
 - **Specific Challenges in Robotic Manipulation:** Robotic manipulation tasks introduce particular complexities not always addressed by general video prediction models. These include the necessity of handling multiple camera views (e.g., wrist and external cameras) to resolve partial observability and capture fine-grained gripper-object interactions. Inferring occluded objects from historical data and ensuring that predicted world states reflect physical accuracy, rather than just visual plausibility, are also critical. Prior state-of-the-art models for manipulation, such as Ctrl-World, have achieved high fidelity but often operate at speeds too slow for test-time planning or efficient policy improvement. WEAVER is motivated to overcome this efficiency bottleneck.

By addressing these motivations, WEAVER aims to provide a robust and versatile world model that can be flexibly applied across evaluation, improvement, and planning, thereby pushing the capabilities of learned simulators in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WEAVER (World Estimation Across Views for Embodied Reasoning) is a world model architecture that integrates several design decisions from prior work in video generation and latent world models to concurrently achieve high fidelity, long-horizon consistency, and efficient generation.

**Setup: Robot and Policy**
The system considers long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks guided by natural language instructions. The robot's state includes proprioceptive information (e.g., joint angles) and multiple RGB camera views (wrist and third-person cameras). An observation $o_t$ at timestep $t$ comprises multi-view images $I_t$ and proprioceptive state $q_t$. The robot's base policy $\pi_\theta$ generates an $h$-step future action chunk $a_t := a_{t:t+h}$, which is then executed within the world model or environment.

**World Model Architecture**
WEAVER maps an observation $o_t$ into a latent state $z_t$ using a pretrained encoder $E_\psi$. The world model predicts $h$ future latents $\hat{z}_t$ conditioned on a sparse memory $z^{mem}_t$ (every $k$-th prior latent), a short-term history $z^{hist}_t$ (the $m$ most recent latents), and the $h$-step action plan $a_t$. A reward model $R$ simultaneously predicts future reward values $\hat{r}_t$ aligned with the language instruction $\ell$. A pretrained decoder $D_\eta$ converts predicted latents back into future observations $\hat{o}_t$, which are then fed back to the policy to generate subsequent action chunks.

**Key Design Decisions for High Fidelity, Temporally Consistent World Model Generation**

1. **Multi-View Camera Prediction:** WEAVER predicts observations from both external and wrist cameras. This multi-view approach enhances temporal consistency by providing richer information to handle occlusions and capture finer gripper-object interactions during manipulation. Each view is encoded into patch tokens using the Stable Diffusion 3 VAE encoder, and the proprioceptive state is projected and concatenated to these tokens to form $z_t$.

2. **Proprioceptive State Prediction:** Explicitly predicting the robot’s future proprioceptive states (e.g., joint configurations) is incorporated. This is critical for accurately modeling contact-rich and deformable object manipulation, where precise arm positions and gripper widths influence task outcomes.

3. **Sparse Memory and Short-Term History:** To maintain long-horizon consistency, the model conditions its predictions on two forms of past information:
 - **Sparse Memory ($z^{mem}_t$):** Captures long-term context by including every $k$-th encoded observation.
 - **Short-Term History ($z^{hist}_t$):** Includes the last two frames to capture immediate consequences of actions. This helps the model understand scene changes and invariances despite occlusions and viewpoint shifts.

4. **Latent Dynamics Model:** The core dynamics model $f_\phi$ is an efficient 2D transformer architecture, similar to Dreamer-v4, designed to balance fidelity and efficiency. It consists of `L` dynamics blocks with spatial and causal temporal attention. The model autoregressively generates an $h$-step latent chunk, conditioned on latent tokens, action tokens, and flow timestep embeddings. For training stability, it incorporates RMSNorm, RoPE, QKNorm, and SwiGLU feed-forward layers.

5. **Training Objective:** The latent dynamics model is trained using a flow-matching loss to predict future latents. This involves minimizing the mean squared error between the model's predicted velocity field and the actual velocity connecting a noisy latent (interpolated between a ground-truth future latent and Gaussian noise) to the ground-truth latent.
 - **Diffusion Forcing:** To improve long-horizon consistency, Diffusion Forcing is adopted, which involves training with independently sampled noise levels across future timesteps.
 - **SPRINT Blocks:** For efficiency, SPRINT blocks are utilized, aggressively dropping patch tokens in the latents.

**Accelerating World Model Inference Speed**
To achieve efficient generation, WEAVER addresses both the forward pass computation and iterative denoising steps inherent in diffusion models:
1. **KV Caching:** Key-value caching is employed for memory and history tokens across denoising steps, reducing redundant computations in the forward pass.
2. **Progressive Noise Schedule:** A cosine schedule replaces linear noise schedules for denoising, which is reported to result in higher fidelity generation.
3. **Rectified Flow Post-training:** The model is post-trained with a rectified flow objective. This distillation step allows for high-quality generation with fewer forward passes, significantly reducing inference time.

**Accurate and Efficient Value Estimation from the World Model**
1. **Reward Model:** A lightweight reward head, $R$, is trained to operate directly on latent states and language instructions. This head distills scores from an off-the-shelf reward model, eliminating the need to decode latents into images and query external Visual Language Model (VLM) judges, thereby improving efficiency.

2. **Critic:** A critic network, $V$, is learned to estimate values beyond the imagined horizon, supporting truncated-horizon rollouts. It shares a similar latent-space design as the reward model and is trained with an MSE objective to predict bootstrapped $\lambda$-returns.

**Experimental Setup**
- **Base Policy & Hardware:** The $\pi^{0.5}$ Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy, trained on the DROID dataset, serves as the base. Hardware includes a Franka Emika Panda manipulator and Zed 2i/Mini cameras. The WM uses the right external camera view and the wrist camera.
- **Datasets & Tasks:** The world model is pre-trained on the DROID dataset and then fine-tuned on real-world data ($D^{FT}_{real}$) collected from 50 rollouts per task across five robotic manipulation tasks: Stack Bowls, PnP Bag, PnP Marker, PnP Towel, and Pour Beans. These tasks cover rigid, deformable, and dynamic manipulation.
- **World Model Training:** WEAVER is a 928M parameter model, pre-trained for 1M steps and fine-tuned for 16k steps with a lower learning rate. Rewards for training are obtained from Robometer, a progress-reward system. Actions are represented as joint position differences, with an action adapter converting joint velocities from the policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings demonstrating WEAVER's effectiveness across fidelity, consistency, and efficiency, and its application in policy evaluation, improvement, and test-time planning.

#### 4.1. WEAVER's Performance as a World Model

- **Perceptually High Fidelity Generations and Pareto-Dominance:** WEAVER consistently outperforms Ctrl-World, a leading multi-view manipulation world model, across FID and FVD metrics on both the DROID validation set and an out-of-distribution (OOD) task dataset. Crucially, WEAVER achieves these results with significantly lower inference times. For example, at 16 Number of Function Evaluations (NFE), WEAVER's inference time is 4.78s compared to Ctrl-World's 14.65s (Table 1). As NFE decreases, Ctrl-World's generation quality degrades more significantly than WEAVER's, indicating WEAVER's robustness under tighter inference budgets. The results indicate that WEAVER Pareto-dominates Ctrl-World in terms of generation quality versus inference budget (Figure 5).

- **Higher Quality at Long Horizon:** When evaluating perceptual quality over long-horizon predictions (10s sequences), WEAVER consistently maintains lower FID values compared to Ctrl-World, even when inference budgets are reduced (Figure 3). This indicates superior temporal consistency over extended interactions, a critical aspect for complex manipulation tasks.

- **Latent Reward Prediction Accuracy:** WEAVER's internal latent reward model demonstrates alignment with ground-truth Robometer rewards. Qualitative examples show that the predicted rewards from WEAVER's imaginations correctly reflect key task events like grasping and stacking (Figure 4, Left). The computed advantage values based on these latent rewards are also capable of distinguishing between different outcomes of action samples (Figure 4, Right), indicating its suitability for policy filtering and planning.

#### 4.2. WEAVER Enables Effective Evaluation, Improvement, and Planning

- **Policy Evaluation that Tightly Correlates with Reality:**
 - Pre-trained world models (Ctrl-World and WEAVER) tend to underestimate policy performance, particularly on challenging tasks like pouring beans or deformable object manipulation.
 - WEAVER, when fine-tuned on real-world task data (WEAVER-FT), significantly improves evaluation accuracy. It achieves a Pearson Correlation coefficient of $\rho = 0.87$ with real-world success rates and a low Maximum Matrix Ranking Violation (MMRV) of 0.035 (Table 8). This correlation is significantly higher than pre-trained variants and Ctrl-World.
 - Qualitative examples confirm that WEAVER-FT more accurately predicts the outcomes of tasks such as placing a towel in a basket or pouring beans, including details like beans scattering, which pretrained models often miss (Figure 6).

- **Policy Improvement Without Real Interactions:**
 - Finetuning the $\pi^{0.5}$ base policy using synthetic data generated by WEAVER results in substantial improvements in real-world success rates. Finetuning with 1,000 synthetic segments achieves an average success rate improvement of 38% over the base $\pi^{0.5}$ policy, closely matching the performance achieved by finetuning with comparable amounts of real data (Figure 7, Left).
 - Combining filtered real and synthetic data yields further performance gains, increasing the average success rate by 11% over real-data finetuning alone.
 - Scaling the amount of synthetic data from 1,000 to 5,000 segments on the "Pour Beans" task consistently improves policy performance, eventually exceeding that obtained from finetuning with real data alone (Figure 7, Right).
 - Qualitative results illustrate that policies finetuned with synthetic data exhibit more precise grasping, improved placement, and smoother, more stable execution, addressing issues like imprecise motions seen in the base policy (Figure 8).

- **Test-Time Planning by Balancing Inference Speed and Quality:**
 - Using WEAVER for test-time planning with a "best-of-N" approach (sampling 4 action chunks and selecting the one with the highest advantage) improves the average success rate by 15% over the base policy across five tasks (Figure 9). This gain is more pronounced for tasks where the base policy is weaker.
 - WEAVER's efficiency is critical here. The full planning latency, including policy sampling and world model imagination (for a horizon of 12 with a batch size of 4), is approximately 1.45s (Table 7).
 - Compared to Ctrl-World, WEAVER demonstrates a significant speedup in dynamics prediction, being approximately 20 times faster (e.g., 1.4547s vs. 29.4244s for a horizon of 15 and batch size 4). This acceleration renders world-model-based test-time planning practical for real-time robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of WEAVER represents a step forward in the field of robotic world modeling by addressing the long-standing challenge of simultaneously achieving high fidelity, long-horizon consistency, and efficient generation. This unified approach has several significant implications and potential impacts.

### 6. Significance

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** WEAVER demonstrates performance on par with or exceeding prior world models, particularly in terms of fidelity and long-horizon consistency, while also being substantially more efficient. Its Pareto-dominance over methods like Ctrl-World in the quality-speed trade-off indicates a more practical and versatile solution for robotic applications.
- **Enabling Downstream Applications:** By fulfilling the three core desiderata, WEAVER effectively unlocks critical downstream capabilities for robotic learning and control. It provides a robust platform for policy evaluation, a scalable mechanism for policy improvement, and a foundation for real-time test-time planning.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Reduced Reliance on Real-World Interaction:** WEAVER's ability to accurately evaluate policies and generate high-quality synthetic data for policy improvement can significantly reduce the need for costly, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe real-world robot rollouts. This lowers the barrier to entry for developing and refining robotic manipulation skills.
- **Accelerated Policy Development:** The capacity to generate vast amounts of effective synthetic data for policy distillation means that robotic policies can be improved more rapidly and systematically, even with limited real-world interaction. The demonstration of consistent performance scaling with synthetic data size supports this impact.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Robustness:** The integration of WEAVER into test-time planning enables robots to perform online lookahead and select optimal actions, leading to more robust and adaptable behaviors in dynamic and uncertain environments. The significant speedup over prior methods makes this real-time planning practical.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** WEAVER's architecture, which fuses successful components from video generation and latent world models, provides a robust baseline and direction for future research in generalist robot world models and embodied AI.

**Limitations and Considerations for Future Impact:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations that frame the scope of WEAVER's current impact and point to avenues for future work. These include:
- **Partial Observability:** Reliance on visual observations means task-relevant information (e.g., contact forces, occluded geometry) may be hidden, suggesting a need for multimodal sensing (e.g., tactile feedback).
- **Complex Dynamics:** Deformable and dynamic interactions (e.g., pouring granular materials) remain challenging to model accurately, indicating potential benefits from integrating physics priors or specialized neural-physics simulators.
- **Limited Test-Time Planning Horizon:** Despite speed improvements, current latency restricts online planning to short horizons, limiting reasoning about long-term consequences.
- **Data Coverage:** The model's pre-training on DROID might limit generalization to diverse robot embodiments or underrepresented task dynamics, highlighting the need for broader data sources.
- **Noisy Reward Supervision:** The reliance on off-the-shelf reward models like Robometer introduces noise that can impact policy evaluation and improvement. Developing more accurate and calibrated reward models is critical.

Responsible deployment of such advanced world models in safety-critical domains like assistive robotics will require careful validation, uncertainty estimation, and safeguards against potential errors or biases in learned predictions.
