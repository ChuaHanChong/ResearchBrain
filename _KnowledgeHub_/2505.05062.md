# ULFine: Unbiased Lightweight Fine-tuning for Foundation-Model-Assisted Long-Tailed Semi-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for long-tailed semi-supervised learning leverages foundation models through prototype adaptive fitting and dual logit fusion, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmark datasets while effectively mitigating minority bottleneck and majority overconfidence issues common in imbalanced data scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional long-tailed semi-supervised learning methods struggle with class imbalance and limited labeled data
- Direct application of foundation models leads to "minority bottleneck" and "majority overconfidence" issues that hurt performance on tail classes

## Method

- ==Prototype Adaptive Fitting (PAF)== adjusts textual prototypes based on ==confidence-aware pseudo-labeling==
- ==Dual Logit Fusion (DLF)== combines unbiased textual prototypes with ==linear probing== to reduce classifier bias
- ==Lightweight fine-tuning strategy== preserves foundation model's knowledge while enabling task-specific adaptation

## Results

- Outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on CIFAR10-LT, CIFAR100-LT, STL10-LT and ImageNet-127 benchmarks
- Achieves more balanced performance across head and tail classes compared to baseline approaches
- Reduces training costs significantly compared to training from scratch while maintaining superior accuracy
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of both PAF and DLF components

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full fine-tuning of foundation models can degrade performance by disrupting intra-class distance distributions
> - Confidence-aware prototype adaptation helps address majority class overconfidence
> - Combining multiple prediction sources (textual + linear) improves tail class performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a confluence of several active areas within machine learning:

- **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):** SSL aims to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data to train robust models, especially when labeled data is scarce and expensive to acquire.
- **Long-Tailed Learning (LTL):** LTL deals with datasets where some classes have significantly more examples than others. This imbalance can lead to biased models that perform poorly on under-represented ("tail") classes.
- **Foundation Models:** These are large, pre-trained models (e.g., CLIP) trained on massive datasets, exhibiting strong generalization capabilities that can be adapted to various downstream tasks.

The paper identifies a gap in the existing literature: the limited exploration of foundation models' potential for enhancing Long-Tailed Semi-Supervised Learning (LTSSL). While foundation models have shown promise in supervised LTL and other downstream tasks, their application to the more complex LTSSL scenario remained relatively unexplored.

Previous LTSSL methods often rely on training from scratch, which can limit generalization and struggle to effectively address biases in pseudo-labels and classifiers. The authors argue that foundation models, with their pre-existing knowledge and generalization abilities, offer a promising avenue for improving LTSSL performance.

This work fits into a growing trend of adapting and fine-tuning foundation models for specialized tasks. It builds upon existing work in supervised LTL with foundation models while extending the focus to the more challenging semi-supervised setting. The paper also acknowledges and addresses the potential pitfalls of directly applying foundation models, such as the "minority bottleneck" and "majority overconfidence" issues, showcasing a nuanced understanding of the problem space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's key objectives are:

- **Explore the Impact of Foundation Models on LTSSL:** The primary objective is to investigate how foundation models, particularly CLIP, can be effectively utilized in LTSSL tasks.
- **Analyze the Effects of Different Fine-Tuning Strategies:** The study aims to understand the impact of various fine-tuning strategies (Linear Probing, Lightweight Fine-Tuning, and Full Fine-Tuning) when applied to foundation models in the context of LTSSL.
- **Identify and Address Challenges in Applying Foundation Models to LTSSL:** The research seeks to uncover specific problems that arise when directly using foundation models in LTSSL, such as the "minority bottleneck" and "majority overconfidence" phenomena.
- **Propose a Novel and Effective LTSSL Method:** The ultimate goal is to develop a new method (ULFine) that leverages foundation models while mitigating the identified challenges, leading to improved performance in LTSSL tasks.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Results:** The study aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed ULFine method by achieving superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art LTSSL algorithms on benchmark datasets.

The motivation behind this research stems from the practical relevance of LTSSL in real-world applications. Many real-world datasets exhibit long-tailed distributions, and labeled data is often limited. By developing effective LTSSL methods that leverage foundation models, the authors aim to improve the performance of machine learning models in these challenging but realistic scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a combination of empirical analysis and algorithmic development:

- **Empirical Analysis of Existing Methods:** The authors begin by conducting experiments using existing foundation models (CLIP) with different fine-tuning strategies (Linear Probing, Lightweight Fine-Tuning, Full Fine-Tuning) on LTSSL datasets. This analysis reveals the "minority bottleneck" and "majority overconfidence" issues.
- **Problem Formulation:** Based on the empirical analysis, the authors formally define the challenges of applying foundation models to LTSSL.
- **Development of ULFine:** The authors propose the ULFine method, which consists of two main components:
 - **Prototype Adaptive Fitting (PAF):** This component aims to address the "majority overconfidence" problem by adaptively adjusting textual prototypes based on confidence-aware pseudo-labeling distributions and introducing orthogonality constraints.
 - **Dual Logit Fusion (DLF):** This component mitigates the "minority bottleneck" and classifier bias by fusing logits from unbiased textual prototypes and linear probing.
- **Experimental Evaluation:** The proposed ULFine method is rigorously evaluated on several benchmark LTSSL datasets, including CIFAR10-LT, CIFAR100-LT, STL10-LT, and ImageNet-127.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies are conducted to assess the contribution of each component of ULFine.
- **Visualization and Statistical Analysis:** The authors use visualization techniques (e.g., per-class accuracy plots, similarity matrix visualization) and statistical analysis (e.g., confidence level analysis of false pseudo-labels) to gain deeper insights into the behavior of the proposed method and the challenges it addresses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of the paper are:

- **Foundation Models Can Improve LTSSL but Require Careful Fine-Tuning:** Linear Probing and Lightweight Fine-Tuning can boost overall performance compared to training from scratch, but Full Fine-Tuning can degrade performance due to destroying intra-class distance distribution.
- **"Minority Bottleneck" and "Majority Overconfidence" Issues:** Direct application of Linear Probing and Lightweight Fine-Tuning can lead to a "minority bottleneck," where tail classes are neglected, and "majority overconfidence," where the model is overly confident in incorrect pseudo-labels.
- **ULFine Effectively Mitigates the Identified Challenges:** The proposed ULFine method effectively alleviates the "minority bottleneck" and "majority overconfidence" problems, leading to more balanced and accurate classifiers.
- **ULFine Achieves State-of-the-Art Performance:** ULFine significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art LTSSL methods on multiple benchmark datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving LTSSL performance.
- **ULFine Improves Training Efficiency:** ULFine significantly reduces training costs compared to training from scratch while achieving superior performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this work are:

- **Advancement of LTSSL Research:** This research contributes to the advancement of LTSSL by providing a novel and effective method that leverages foundation models.
- **Improved Performance in Real-World Applications:** The ULFine method has the potential to improve the performance of machine learning models in real-world applications where long-tailed data distributions and limited labeled data are common.
- **Practical and Efficient Solution:** The proposed method is not only effective but also relatively simple and efficient, making it practical for real-world deployment.
- **Insights into Foundation Model Adaptation:** The paper provides valuable insights into the challenges and best practices for adapting foundation models to specialized tasks like LTSSL.
- **Potential for Broader Application:** The techniques developed in this research, such as confidence-aware adaptive fitting and dual logit fusion, may be applicable to other related problems in machine learning.

In conclusion, this paper presents a well-researched and significant contribution to the field of LTSSL. The authors identify a critical gap in the existing literature, provide a thorough analysis of the challenges involved, and propose an effective and efficient solution. The ULFine method has the potential to significantly improve the performance of machine learning models in real-world scenarios, making this research valuable to both the academic community and industry practitioners.
