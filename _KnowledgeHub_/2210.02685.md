# A Real2Sim2Real Method for Robust Object Grasping with Neural Surface Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> A geometric Real2Sim2Real pipeline for 6-DoF grasping in clutter. Depth and segmentation maps give object-level point clouds, a trained ConvONet reconstructs meshes, and inverting ConvONet's centering/scaling normalization places meshes in a simulator without pose estimation. The replica auto-labels grasps that train Contact-GraspNet and VGN, matching or beating models pre-trained on 17.7M grasps.

## Problem

- Sim2Real pipelines need hand-curated meshes, calibrated dynamics, and manual pose placement, which demands expertise and does not scale to many scenes and objects.
- Prior Real2Sim2Real work targets dynamics parameter estimation, leaving automatic geometric scene construction from raw camera observations largely unaddressed for manipulation.
- Existing reconstruction methods either retrieve mismatched CAD models from a database, or need manual mesh placement and affix objects to the support surface, blocking pick-and-place.

## Method

- Reconstruction framework: fuse depth maps into object-level point clouds via segmentation, apply ==statistical outlier removal==, then reconstruct meshes with ==ConvONet== plus ==Multiresolution Isosurface Extraction==.
- Pose-estimation-free placement: invert ==ConvONet's== centering and scaling preprocessing into a similarity transform that returns the mesh to the world frame, followed by ==approximate convex decomposition== for fast collision checks.
- Training modifications: label a grasp successful only when its mean success rate over repeated stochastic-planner trials exceeds 0.7, and train ==VGN== with negative labels on all unannotated input voxels.

## Results

- Contact-GraspNet trained on reconstructions scored **0.76** grasp success versus **0.66** for the retrieval-based reconstruction baseline, and matched **0.77** from ShapeNet ground-truth training.
- VGN trained on reconstructions reached **0.34** success against **0.10** for the pre-trained released model; the all-voxel negative labeling ablation lifted success from **34.0** to **60.0**.
- On a real xArm gripper, the pipeline reached **0.93** grasp success while Contact-GraspNet pre-trained on ACRONYM's **17.7M** Panda grasps scored **0.0**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction quality should be judged by downstream task success, not Chamfer distance; not every reconstruction method that looks accurate trains a performant policy.
> - Normalization preprocessing already encodes object pose, so inverting it sidesteps explicit pose estimation, a reusable trick for populating simulators.
> - Grasp networks pre-trained on huge datasets overfit to the training gripper; small scene-specific relabeling transfers to new hardware far more cheaply than redoing the dataset.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research situates itself within the evolving field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in the Sim2Real paradigm and advancing the emerging technique of Real2Sim2Real.

The Sim2Real approach, which involves learning robotic skills in simulation and transferring them to the real world, has driven progress in manipulation research. Its effectiveness stems from the ability to generate a high volume of interaction samples cheaply in simulators, a necessity for many modern learning approaches such as reinforcement learning and simulation-based grasp pose auto-labeling. However, a significant bottleneck in Sim2Real is the manual effort required to create digital replicas of real-world scenes. This process involves manually curating object meshes, calibrating dynamic parameters, and accurately placing objects, which is costly, time-consuming, and requires specialized domain expertise, limiting scalability.

To mitigate this bottleneck, recent research has explored Real2Sim2Real, aiming to automate the scene creation and calibration process. Prior work in Real2Sim2Real typically focused on *dynamics Real2Sim2Real*, which involves estimating simulation dynamic parameters. This paper distinguishes itself by concentrating on *geometric Real2Sim2Real*. The objective of geometric Real2Sim2Real is to automatically construct object geometry from camera observations, place these geometries at physically plausible poses in a simulator, and then demonstrate that these reconstructed scenes can effectively train performant neural networks for robotic tasks.

The work leverages recent advancements in learning-based 3D modeling, particularly state-of-the-art neural surface reconstruction methods (e.g., Convolutional Occupancy Networks or ConvONet). These methods can convert 3D sensor data into detailed meshes and have exhibited strong cross-scene generalizability. While autonomous driving research has benefited from automatic environment reconstruction, robotic manipulation necessitates finer geometric details and accurate physical properties for effective simulation.

The paper differentiates its geometric Real2Sim2Real approach from other reconstruction methods. It contrasts with retrieval-based methods that retrieve objects from an external database, which may not accurately approximate the real object's shape. It also differs from generative models that might require manual placement of reconstructed meshes or affixing objects to surfaces, practices unsuitable for manipulation tasks like pick-and-place where object-support separation is necessary. A key contribution of this work is demonstrating that the generated reconstructions are suitable for *training manipulation networks*, moving beyond mere geometric approximation.

By choosing 6-DoF object clutter grasping as a case study, a fundamental primitive for diverse manipulation tasks, the research aims to validate its compact framework and provide evidence for its applicability to more complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether advanced implicit 3D reconstruction methods can be integrated into a Real2Sim2Real pipeline to generate high-fidelity digital replicas. These replicas are intended to enable automated grasp labeling and the subsequent training of robust manipulation models, specifically focusing on 6-DoF object grasping in cluttered environments.

The motivation for this study stems from several factors:

- **Addressing the Sim2Real Bottleneck:** A major limitation in current Sim2Real approaches is the labor-intensive and expert-dependent process of manually building digital replicas for simulation. This manual curation of 3D assets, calibration of physical properties, and scene construction is costly and hinders the scalability of robotic learning to diverse and large-scale scenes. Automating this "Real2Sim" step is a central motivation.
- **Leveraging Advances in 3D Learning:** Recent breakthroughs in neural surface reconstruction offer the capability to convert 3D sensor inputs into detailed and geometrically accurate meshes with demonstrated cross-scene generalizability. The authors hypothesize that the quality of these generated 3D meshes is sufficient to support physical simulation and the learning of manipulation algorithms, thereby warranting an investigation into their practical application in robotics.
- **Requirement for Fine-Grained Geometry in Manipulation:** While automatic reconstruction has benefited fields like autonomous driving, robotic manipulation tasks, particularly grasping, demand precise geometric details for successful interaction. This research aims to determine if modern neural reconstruction algorithms can provide the necessary fidelity for such tasks.
- **Focus on a Foundational Manipulation Task:** The paper selects 6-DoF object clutter grasping as its case study. Grasping is considered an essential primitive for nearly all manipulation tasks. Demonstrating that the proposed Real2Sim2Real pipeline can train robust grasping networks provides strong evidence for its potential applicability to a wider array of complex manipulation skills.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency and Adaptability:** Traditional methods for training grasping networks often require immense datasets (e.g., millions of simulated grasps). The research is motivated by the potential to achieve high sample efficiency by training networks on labels generated from custom-reconstructed scenes. Furthermore, it seeks to demonstrate that this framework can facilitate adapting grasping capabilities to new robot systems or grippers without the need for extensive relabeling of large datasets.
- **Decoupling Complex Problems:** The underlying hypothesis is that robust performance can be achieved by decoupling scene modeling (geometric reconstruction) from grasp sampling into distinct sub-problems, each addressable with high-quality, state-of-the-art learning algorithms and physical simulation techniques.

In essence, the research aims to validate a fully automated pipeline for generating simulation environments from real-world observations, thereby simplifying the creation of training data for robot manipulation and accelerating the development and deployment of robotic skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is structured as a Real2Sim2Real pipeline, integrating neural surface reconstruction with grasp learning for robust object grasping. The approach addresses both the creation of a digital replica from real-world observations (Real2Sim) and the training and evaluation of grasping networks using this replica (Sim2Real).

#### 3.1. A. Real2Sim Reconstruction Framework

The initial phase focuses on constructing a digital replica of a real-world scene.
1. **Input Data Acquisition:** Posed depth images and corresponding semantic segmentation masks of test scenes are captured using RGB-D cameras positioned above a tabletop.
2. **Point Cloud Processing:**
 - Depth maps are converted into point clouds, which are then fused.
 - Semantic segmentation masks are used to extract object-level point clouds, isolating points belonging to individual object instances.
 - **Outlier Removal:** To enhance robustness against sensor noise, a statistical point cloud outlier removal technique is applied to each object-level point cloud. This step is crucial as normalization parameters used in the subsequent reconstruction are sensitive to noise.
3. **Neural Surface Reconstruction:**
 - A trained Convolutional Occupancy Network (ConvONet) is utilized to reconstruct an implicit representation of each object's surface. ConvONet typically requires normalized point clouds as input (centered and scaled to a canonical frame).
 - **Mesh Extraction:** From the implicit representation, the object mesh is extracted using the Multiresolution Isosurface Extraction (MISE) procedure.
4. **Automatic Mesh Placement:**
 - A significant aspect of the method is placing the reconstructed meshes into the simulated scene without explicit object pose estimation. This is achieved by applying the inverse of the centering and scaling operations (used during ConvONet's input normalization) to the reconstructed mesh. This transformation implicitly positions and scales the mesh correctly within the world frame, with the object's orientation preserved from the point cloud.
5. **Mesh Simplification:** To optimize for simulation performance, particularly collision detection, approximate convex decomposition is applied to the extracted meshes. This simplifies complex geometries often produced by MISE.
6. **Digital Replica Creation:** The posed and simplified object meshes are then imported into a physical simulator (SAPIEN, powered by PhysX) to create a functional digital replica of the original real-world scene.

#### 3.2. B. Sim2Real Grasp Learning and Evaluation

The second phase involves using the generated digital replicas to train and evaluate grasping networks.
1. **Grasp Label Generation:**
 - Within the simulated digital replica, 6-DoF grasp pose labels are automatically generated for a specified gripper (e.g., an xArm 6 parallel gripper).
 - **Robust Labeling:** To account for stochasticity in motion planning, a grasp pose is evaluated multiple times from the same initial conditions. A grasp is labeled as successful only if its mean success rate across these trials exceeds a predefined threshold (set at 0.7). This contrasts with methods relying on single-trial evaluations.
2. **Grasping Network Training:** The generated grasp labels are used to train two distinct state-of-the-art grasping networks:
 - **Contact-GraspNet (CGN):** Takes a point cloud as input and predicts a grasp pose and success probability for each point.
 - **Volumetric Grasping Network (VGN):** Accepts a TSDF (Truncated Signed Distance Function) representation and directly predicts grasp poses and qualities for 3D voxels.
 - **Training Modifications for VGN:** To prevent VGN from predicting high success probabilities in empty regions, the loss and gradients are computed through all input voxels. Voxels without explicit grasp annotations are assigned negative grasp labels.
3. **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Scenes:** Experiments are conducted on 30 synthetic tabletop scenes and 5 real-world scenes. The objects for training the reconstruction network and for the test scenes are disjoint.
 - **Noise Modeling:** Simulated depth images incorporate realistic noise using the SimKinect algorithm.
 - **Baselines:**
 - A pre-trained CGN model (trained on 17.7 million grasps from the ACRONYM dataset) serves as a strong baseline for performance comparison.
 - A retrieval-based scene reconstruction method is used as a baseline to evaluate the effectiveness of neural surface reconstruction compared to database retrieval.
4. **Evaluation:**
 - The primary metric for evaluating the pipeline's performance is the grasp success rate in both simulated and real-world execution. This emphasizes functional utility over mere geometric approximation metrics like Chamfer distance.
 - **Generalization Studies:** Additional experiments investigate generalization:
 - **Rearrange Setting:** CGN models trained on the reconstruction of a scene are evaluated on the same scene with objects rearranged, assessing generalization to novel configurations of seen objects.
 - **Training on Disjoint Scenes:** CGN models are trained on reconstructions from one set of scenes and evaluated on entirely different test scenes, examining generalization to unseen objects and environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness and advantages of the proposed geometric Real2Sim2Real pipeline for robust object grasping.

#### 4.1. A. Real-world 6-DOF Grasping in Clutter

- The pipeline enabled robust real-world grasping. A CGN model trained with grasp pose labels generated from reconstructed scenes using an xArm gripper achieved a grasp success rate of 0.93 (with a standard deviation of 0.25).
- In contrast, a CGN model pre-trained on a massive dataset (17.7 million simulated grasps from Acronym) using a Panda gripper failed to generalize to the xArm gripper, resulting in a 0.0 (0.0) grasp success rate. This indicates that the pre-trained model over-fitted to the specific gripper geometry and highlights the pipeline's utility in adapting to new robot systems without extensive relabeling.

#### 4.2. B. Simulated Experiments: Comparison Against Baselines

- **Quality of Reconstructed Scenes (CGN):** CGN models trained from scratch using the authors' reconstructed scenes achieved a grasp success rate of 0.76 (0.15). This performance was comparable to a publicly available CGN model pre-trained using 17.7 million simulated grasps from ground truth ShapeNet models and scenes, which yielded 0.77 (0.16). This result suggests that the reconstructed scenes provide sufficient quality to train performant grasping networks, even with a significantly smaller, custom-generated dataset.
- **Quality of Reconstructed Scenes (VGN):** VGN models trained from scratch using the reconstructed scenes achieved a grasp success rate of 0.34 (0.14). This was more favorable than the performance of the pre-trained VGN model released by its authors, which yielded 0.1 (0.04). The paper notes that CGN and VGN performances are not directly comparable due to differences in motion planning protocols during evaluation. VGN's grasp success varied by object category: bottle (0.22), can (0.31), bowl (0.42), and mug (0.41), with high standard deviations indicating varying object difficulty.
- **Comparison to Retrieval-Based Method:** The proposed neural surface reconstruction method outperformed a retrieval-based scene reconstruction baseline. CGN trained using the authors' reconstruction framework consistently achieved higher grasp success (0.76 (0.15) after 5000 epochs) compared to CGN trained with scenes reconstructed by the retrieval-based method (e.g., 0.66 (0.1) after 5000 epochs, peaking at 0.66). This indicates that the fidelity of the digital replica's geometry, as provided by neural surface reconstruction, contributes to improved performance of trained grasping networks.

#### 4.3. C. Training Modifications to CGN and VGN

- **CGN Grasp Labeling:** For CGN, generating grasp labels by executing each grasp multiple times and using a mean success rate threshold (0.7) for labeling improved performance (0.76 (0.15)) compared to using a single execution trial (0.716 (0.09)). This modification accounted for the stochastic nature of motion planning.
- **VGN Loss Computation:** For VGN, computing loss through all input voxels and assigning negative grasp labels to voxels without annotations significantly improved grasp success (from 34.0% to 60.0% on ground truth scenes). This modification helped prevent the network from assigning high success probabilities to regions without objects.

#### 4.4. D. Generalization Performance to Unseen Scenes

- **Rearrange Setting:** When CGN models, trained on the reconstruction of a specific scene, were evaluated on the same objects in rearranged configurations, they demonstrated generalization with only a minor drop in performance.
- **Training with Disjoint Scenes:** The results suggest that increasing the number of training reconstructed scenes improved generalization performance. Models trained on a larger set of reconstructed scenes performed better when evaluated on entirely different test scenes (whose reconstructions were not used during training). This indicates that while overfitting to individual scene reconstructions can yield high performance on those specific scenes, a broader dataset of reconstructed scenes contributes to better generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by proposing and validating a Real2Sim2Real pipeline that leverages neural surface reconstruction for robust object grasping. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Automation of Sim2Real Pipeline for Geometric Fidelity:** The work provides a method for automatically constructing high-quality geometric digital replicas from real-world camera observations. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotic manipulation research, which historically relies on manual, costly, and time-consuming scene creation in simulators. By automating the Real2Sim step, the pipeline reduces the need for human expertise in creating simulation environments.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency for Robotic Learning:** The study demonstrates that robust grasping networks can be trained effectively with high sample efficiency using grasp labels automatically generated from the reconstructed scenes. Achieving performance comparable to models trained on datasets orders of magnitude larger suggests a pathway to significantly reducing the data requirements for training advanced manipulation skills.
- **Increased Adaptability to New Robot Systems:** The ability to retrain existing grasping networks with simulated grasp sampling using the pipeline enables straightforward adaptation to new robot systems or grippers. This eliminates the need for extensive real-world data collection and relabeling for each new hardware configuration, thus accelerating the deployment of manipulation capabilities in diverse robotic setups.
- **Validation of Advanced 3D Vision for Robotics:** The research offers empirical evidence that state-of-the-art neural surface reconstruction methods (e.g., ConvONet) can produce 3D object geometries with sufficient detail and accuracy to support physical simulation and learning of complex manipulation algorithms. This bridges advanced computer vision techniques with practical robotics applications.
- **Decoupling of Complex Sub-problems:** The pipeline effectively decouples the challenges of accurate scene modeling from the problem of efficient grasp sampling. This allows for the application of specialized, high-quality modern algorithms to each sub-problem, contributing to the overall robustness and performance of the system.
- **Foundation for More Complex Manipulation Tasks:** By demonstrating successful 6-DoF object grasping in clutter, a fundamental manipulation primitive, the work establishes a foundation for future research into more intricate manipulation tasks that rely on precise object geometry and dynamic interactions in simulation.
- **Reduced Manual Effort and Domain Expertise:** The methodology's automatic nature for geometric reconstruction and object placement, notably by leveraging inverse normalization operations for implicit pose estimation, significantly reduces the manual effort and specialized domain expertise previously required for setting up simulated robotic learning environments. This potentially makes robotic manipulation research and development more accessible and scalable.
