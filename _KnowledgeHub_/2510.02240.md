# RewardMap: Tackling Sparse Rewards in Fine-grained Visual Reasoning via Multi-Stage Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Westlake University and collaborators developed REWARDMAP, a multi-stage reinforcement learning framework that enhances Multi-modal Large Language Models' performance on fine-grained visual reasoning tasks by tackling sparse rewards. The framework consistently outperformed all open-source baselines on the REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS benchmarks, achieving an average 3.47% improvement across six external generalization benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations in precise spatial and logical reasoning over high-resolution, information-dense images like transit maps.
- Applying standard reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-grained visual reasoning tasks is hindered by sparse rewards, leading to unstable optimization and ineffective exploration.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often proves insufficient for long-chain, multi-step decision-making required in complex visual reasoning, potentially leading to overfitting.

## Method

- The authors constructed REASONMAP-**PLUS**, an extended dataset with five new question categories and dense reward signals to provide a natural difficulty continuum for effective cold-start training.
- They developed REWARDMAP, a multi-stage RL framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a difficulty-aware reward design.
- The reward design includes format, correctness, and a novel 'detail reward' that provides partial credit for correctly identified components, all scaled by weights based on map and question difficulty.
- A multi-stage GRPO-based curriculum progressively schedules training data, moving from basic visual understanding (REASONMAP-PLUS) to complex visual reasoning (REASONMAP) tasks.

## Results

- REWARDMAP consistently outperformed all open-source baselines and reference models on the REASONMAP benchmark for route planning (e.g., **31.51%** S./**31.77%** L. Weighted Acc. vs. Qwen2.5-VL-**72B**-Instruct's **26.65%** S./**24.22%** L.) and REASONMAP-PLUS for visual understanding (**74.25%** Weighted Acc.).
- Models trained with REWARDMAP demonstrated improved generalization, achieving an average accuracy increase of **3.47%** across six diverse external benchmarks, with a notable **13.51%** gain on SpatialEval.
- Qualitative comparisons showed REWARDMAP successfully reduced visual confusions and hallucinations, consistently producing correct target routes on complex maps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-stage curriculum combined with a detailed, difficulty-aware reward function effectively mitigates the sparse reward problem in reinforcement learning for fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.
> - Providing intermediate 'detail rewards' for partially correct answers is crucial for stable optimization and learning in tasks requiring long reasoning chains.
> - Training MLLMs through a structured progression from fundamental perceptual skills to complex reasoning tasks enhances both domain-specific performance and general visual understanding and reasoning capabilities across diverse benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and their application to complex visual reasoning tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained understanding of structured visual inputs like transit maps. The paper addresses a known limitation of current MLLMs, which, despite advancements in vision-language pre-training and chain-of-thought prompting, often struggle with precise spatial and logical reasoning over high-resolution, information-dense images.

The recently introduced REASONMAP benchmark (Feng et al., 2025b) highlighted this gap, underscoring that even advanced MLLMs exhibit deficiencies in tasks such as route planning on transit maps, which combine visual understanding with spatial reasoning. This research builds directly on REASONMAP by proposing methods to enhance MLLM capabilities in this challenging domain.

A central challenge in improving MLLMs for such tasks using reinforcement learning (RL) is the issue of sparse rewards. Standard RL methods often struggle when successful outcomes are rare and only evident at the end of long reasoning chains, leading to unstable optimization and ineffective exploration. While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) offers dense supervision, it may not adequately prepare models for complex, multi-step decision-making inherent in visual reasoning. This paper aims to bridge this gap by developing an RL framework specifically designed to mitigate sparse rewards and provide more structured supervision for fine-grained visual reasoning. It contributes to the broader effort to move beyond static pre-training and SFT, leveraging dynamic RL paradigms to learn robust reasoning policies.

The work also relates to spatial reasoning on maps, traditionally handled by multi-stage computer vision systems involving OCR and graph algorithms. MLLMs offer an end-to-end alternative, but prior research indicates their struggles with fundamental spatial tasks. This paper attempts to advance MLLMs in this specific, practically relevant application area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Address the challenge of sparse rewards and unstable optimization** when applying reinforcement learning to fine-grained visual reasoning tasks in structured visual domains, particularly transit maps.
2. **Enhance the fine-grained visual understanding and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs** on complex visual inputs.

These objectives are motivated by several observations:
- **Persistent Performance Gap in Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning:** Despite advancements in MLLMs, benchmarks like REASONMAP reveal that state-of-the-art models consistently struggle with tasks requiring high-fidelity visual and topological understanding in structured visual inputs (e.g., transit maps). These tasks are critical for practical applications like navigation and transportation, and their difficulty exposes fundamental reasoning gaps in current MLLMs.
- **Limitations of Standard RL in Complex Visual Reasoning:** Direct application of standard RL methods to tasks like route planning on transit maps is hindered by sparse reward signals. Success is typically judged only at the final answer of a potentially long reasoning chain, leading to difficulties in providing meaningful gradient updates, destabilizing optimization, and impeding effective exploration.
- **Insufficiency of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Long-Chain Reasoning:** While SFT offers dense supervision, it may lead to overfitting and cognitive rigidity and often falls short in equipping models for the dynamic, long-chain decision-making required for visual reasoning tasks. There is a need for methods that allow models to learn a reasoning policy through exploration and reward, aligning reward signals directly with task objectives from the outset.
- **Lack of Structured Data for Cold-Start Training:** To effectively train RL agents for complex visual tasks, a curriculum that progressively introduces difficulty, starting with simpler perceptual skills, is beneficial. Existing benchmarks may not offer the necessary intermediate, dense reward signals for effective cold-start training.

To address these motivations, the authors aim to construct an extended dataset with dense reward signals and propose a multi-stage RL framework with a detailed, difficulty-aware reward design to facilitate more effective and stable learning for fine-grained visual reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology consists of two main components: the construction of an extended dataset called REASONMAP-PLUS, and the development of a multi-stage reinforcement learning framework named REWARDMAP.

#### 3.1. REASONMAP-PLUS Construction

REASONMAP-PLUS is an extension of the existing REASONMAP dataset, designed to provide dense reward signals and a natural difficulty continuum for effective cold-start training. Its construction pipeline involves three stages:
1. **Data Collection and Preprocessing:** This stage reuses high-resolution transit maps and annotated line-stop information ("Metro Data") from the original REASONMAP. This data serves as the foundation for generating new questions and answers.
2. **Design-Guided Construction of Question–Answer Pairs:** This is the core stage for creating REASONMAP-PLUS. The authors extend the planning questions in REASONMAP to five new categories that focus on basic visual understanding:
 - **Global Counting:** Asks for the total number of lines in a map, assessing global fine-grained visual understanding.
 - **Local Counting (two variants):** Inquires about intermediate stops between two specified stops or the number of lines passing through a specified stop, evaluating local fine-grained visual understanding.
 - **True or False (two variants):** Probes spatial relations between two stops or between a stop and a line, assessing fine-grained visual understanding with binary judgment.
 These questions are constructed using predesigned templates, and answers are automatically derived from the "Metro Data" to form question-answer pairs. Yes/no answers for True/False questions are balanced to prevent models from exploiting label frequency.
3. **Quality Control:** Automatically generated question-answer pairs are manually reviewed for correctness. The question distribution is also adjusted to maintain diversity and a balanced difficulty across the dataset.

**Difficulty Annotation:** Map difficulty (easy, medium, hard) is adopted from REASONMAP. Question difficulty in REASONMAP-PLUS is defined by the corresponding map difficulty, as these questions primarily test basic visual understanding rather than complex reasoning.

#### 3.2. REWARDMAP Framework

REWARDMAP is a multi-stage RL framework built upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), designed to enhance MLLMs' visual understanding and reasoning capabilities by mitigating sparse rewards.

**Target Tasks:** The framework targets two tasks: fine-grained visual understanding on REASONMAP-PLUS and fine-grained visual reasoning (route planning) on REASONMAP. Both are framed as Visual Question Answering (VQA).

**Baseline (GRPO with basic rewards):** The paper highlights that direct GRPO application with only format and correctness rewards on complex REASONMAP tasks suffers from sparse rewards, leading to near-zero or highly skewed advantage estimates, which results in low-signal or high-variance gradients and slow convergence.

**REWARDMAP Components:**
1. **Difficulty-Aware Reward Design:** The reward function `R` is structured as:
 `R = W_difficulty * (R_format + R_correctness + α * R_detail)`
 - **Format Reward (R_format):** Grants credit for adhering to task-specific output conventions (e.g., `\boxed{}` for REASONMAP-PLUS, specific formats for REASONMAP).
 - **Correctness Reward (R_correctness):** Uses exact-match scoring for REASONMAP-PLUS and the official evaluation algorithm for REASONMAP's planning tasks.
 - **Detail Reward (R_detail):** Introduced to alleviate reward sparsity for complex planning tasks. It provides partial credit for correctly identified components of an answer, such as origin/destination stops, route names, transfer stations, and the number of route segments. A weighting coefficient `α` (set to 0.5) controls its influence.
 - **Difficulty-Aware Weighting (W_difficulty):** Scales the total reward based on problem difficulty. It combines two factors:
 - `W_map`: Assigned based on map difficulty (easy, medium, hard) with predefined weights (e.g., γ_e, γ_m, γ_h).
 - `W_question`: Assigned based on the transfer count for route planning questions (e.g., β_0 for zero transfers, β_1 for one or more transfers).

2. **Multi-Stage GRPO-based Reinforcement Learning:** This component designs a curriculum to progressively schedule training data, ensuring a smoother optimization process and stable reward propagation. It adheres to two principles:
 - **Global Curriculum Principle:** Tasks are partitioned into distinct stages based on question type (binary judgment → counting → planning) and target task (fine-grained visual understanding → fine-grained visual reasoning). This ensures fundamental perceptual skills are acquired before complex reasoning.
 - **Local Stochasticity Principle:** Within each stage, randomness is introduced by shuffling training samples, preventing overfitting to a rigid curriculum and enhancing robustness.

This staged approach, combined with the detailed reward design, transforms curriculum training into a structured integration of reward shaping and task scheduling, intended to enable effective RL for sparse-reward visual reasoning problems. Training is conducted using AdamW optimizer with LoRA applied to language blocks and the text-vision projector.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research involved extensive experiments on REASONMAP, REASONMAP-PLUS, and six additional benchmarks, utilizing Qwen2.5-VL models as the base.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setups

- **Training Data:** REASONMAP (R_train, 696 samples) and REASONMAP-PLUS (RP_plus_train, 2,570 samples).
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-VL models (3B, 7B, 32B, 72B), Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct/Thinking, Seed1.5-VL, GPT-4o, GPT-5.
- **RL Baselines:** GRPO, REINFORCE++, and ReMax. SFT and SFT->RL baselines were also used.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Weighted Accuracy and Weighted Map Score for REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS. For other benchmarks, standard accuracy metrics.
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - REASONMAP (test set for fine-grained visual reasoning/spatial reasoning).
 - REASONMAP-PLUS (test set for fine-grained visual understanding).
 - Six generalization benchmarks: SEED-Bench-2-Plus (Map split), SpatialEval, V*Bench, HRBench, ChartQA, and MMStar (covering spatial reasoning, fine-grained visual reasoning, and general tasks).

#### 4.2. Results on REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS

- REWARDMAP consistently outperformed all baselines and open-source reference models across different question templates in REASONMAP and question types of REASONMAP-PLUS.
- On REASONMAP, REWARDMAP (31.51% S./31.77% L. Weighted Acc.) substantially surpassed the best open-source result (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, 26.65% S./24.22% L.) and approached the performance of the closed-source model Seed1.5-VL (34.20% S./38.02% L.).
- On REASONMAP-PLUS, REWARDMAP (74.25% Weighted Acc.) exceeded all open-source models and even outperformed Seed1.5-VL (73.58%).

#### 4.3. Results on Other Benchmarks (Generalization Ability)

- Models trained with REWARDMAP demonstrated consistent improvements across all six generalization benchmarks, achieving an average improvement of 3.47% compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline.
- The most substantial gain was observed on SpatialEval, with a 13.51% increase.
- Specific task-wise improvements included:
 - SEED-Bench-2-Plus: Improvement on the map split.
 - SpatialEval: Accuracy rose significantly on `mazenav` (from 19.60% to 57.20%) and moderately on `spatialreal` (from 70.37% to 72.59%).
 - HRBench: Performance increased for both single-image reasoning and cross-image alignment.
 - MMStar: Steady gains across fine-grained perception, instance reasoning, and mathematical reasoning.
- These results suggest that REWARDMAP enhances general visual perception and reasoning capabilities, strengthening visual grounding and robustness in diverse spatial and reasoning tasks.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons revealed that reference models and baseline RL models frequently suffered from visual confusion (e.g., mistaking routes or stops) or hallucination (e.g., repeating routes). In contrast, REWARDMAP consistently produced correct target routes even on visually complex maps, indicating its effectiveness in reducing visual confusions and hallucinations and improving visual grounding.

#### 4.5. Diagnostic Experiments

- **Ablation on Reward Design and Multi-Stage Design:** Both the reward design and multi-stage design components individually contributed to performance gains on REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS. Their combination yielded the best results, confirming their effectiveness and complementarity.
- **Ablation on REASONMAP-PLUS:** Cold-start training with REASONMAP-PLUS consistently improved performance on both REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS, regardless of whether SFT or RL was used for initial training.
- **Ablation on Granularity of Multi-Stage Design:** A coarse-grained multi-stage strategy led to performance degradation compared to the proposed finer-grained design, reinforcing the effectiveness of the multi-stage approach.
- **Ablation on Hyperparameter α:** The detail reward weighting coefficient (α) was found to be beneficial, with slight performance drops when α was very small, but performance remaining stable across a reasonable range (0.5 to 0.7), suggesting insensitivity within this interval.
- **Ablation on Difficulty-Aware Weighting Scheme:** Performance decreased slightly when difficulty weights (γ_e/m/h and β_0/1) were nearly uniform, but remained stable once clear distinctions were introduced. This supports the effectiveness of the difficulty-aware weighting and its insensitivity within a reasonable range.
- **Effectiveness in Tackling Sparse Reward:** Comparison of training reward trajectories showed that REWARDMAP alleviated reward sparsity compared to a baseline RL model trained solely on REASONMAP, confirming its ability to address this core problem.
- **Effectiveness Across Model Scales and Architectures:** REWARDMAP achieved promising results when applied to Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (a smaller model scale) and substantial performance gains with Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct (a different model architecture), demonstrating its robustness and generalizability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a principled approach to overcoming the sparse reward challenge in applying reinforcement learning to fine-grained visual reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Advancement in Fine-Grained Visual Reasoning:** The introduction of REWARDMAP, with its multi-stage RL framework and difficulty-aware reward design, directly addresses a critical limitation of MLLMs in structured visual domains. The consistent performance gains on the REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS benchmarks indicate a notable step forward in enabling MLLMs to perform complex tasks like route planning on transit maps with higher accuracy and reduced visual confusion and hallucinations.

2. **Mitigation of Sparse Rewards in RL:** The core contribution of REWARDMAP lies in its effective strategy to alleviate sparse reward signals. The detail reward provides crucial intermediate supervision for complex tasks, while the multi-stage curriculum systematically progresses from simpler perceptual tasks to more complex reasoning, providing a more stable and effective cold-start training process than conventional SFT or direct RL. This methodology offers a template for applying RL to other tasks characterized by long reasoning chains and sparse final rewards.

3. **Enhanced Generalization of MLLMs:** The demonstrated improvements across a diverse set of six external benchmarks (covering spatial reasoning, fine-grained visual reasoning, and general tasks) beyond transit maps suggest that REWARDMAP enhances the fundamental visual understanding and reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. This indicates that the learned skills are not overfitted to the specific transit map domain but generalize to broader visual intelligence challenges, potentially leading to more robust and versatile MLLMs.

4. **Novel Dataset for Structured Visual Reasoning:** REASONMAP-PLUS, by extending REASONMAP with dense reward signals and a difficulty continuum, provides a valuable resource for future research. It facilitates the development and evaluation of new methods for training MLLMs on a progression of visual understanding tasks, from basic perception to more intricate reasoning.

5. **Practical Implications:** The improved capabilities in interpreting transit maps have direct practical implications for real-world navigation and transportation systems, potentially enabling more accurate and reliable automated route planning and information extraction from visual representations.

6. **Broader Applicability:** The authors suggest that the framework is not limited to transit maps and can be extended to other structured visual domains (e.g., charts, diagrams), which is supported by observed gains on benchmarks like ChartQA. This opens avenues for applying REWARDMAP's principles to a wider range of technical and analytical visual reasoning tasks.

In summary, this work provides a robust methodological contribution for enhancing MLLMs in fine-grained visual reasoning by tackling the critical challenge of sparse rewards in reinforcement learning, with demonstrated improvements in both domain-specific tasks and broader generalization capabilities.
