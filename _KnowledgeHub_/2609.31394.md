# InternW0-$Δ$: A World Action Model Bridging Predictive Dynamics and Actions with 20K+ Hours of Open Data

> [!summary] Summary
> InternW0-Δ is a World Action Model coupling a pretrained Wan2.2 video expert and an action expert through a directed Mixture-of-Transformers, with frozen-VLM scene semantics, sparse anchor/recent/current memory, training-only Causal Imprint future-change supervision, and Track4World 4D distillation. Pretrained on 23K hours of curated robot, UMI, egocentric, and Ego2Robot data, it acts without future-video rollout and leads LIBERO-Plus (92.8%), RoboTwin 2.0, EBench, and RoboDojo.

## Problem

- Video-pretrained World Action Models predict future observations well, but predictive ability does not directly yield action-relevant representations capturing task-relevant changes, object geometry, motion, and instruction grounding.
- Many WAMs need costly future-video rollout at inference; Fast-WAM drops test-time imagination but collapses under distribution shift, reaching only 1.9% on RoboTwin Clean2Random.
- Robot, UMI, and egocentric human data differ in embodiment, control space, camera setup, and timing, and no large open unified corpus exists for WAM pretraining.

## Method

- ==Directed World-Action Mixture-of-Transformers==: 30 blocks couple a **Wan2.2-TI2V-5B** ==video expert== with an **ActionDiT** ==action expert==, while a frozen **RynnBrain** VLM supplies ==task-conditioned scene semantics== and ==sparse anchor/recent/current memory== adds history.
- **Causal Imprint**: tokens trained with a ==latent-difference loss== on future video-latent changes plus ==alignment to future video-expert features==, feeding the action expert without ever attending to realized future observations.
- Training-only ==4D-aware distillation== from a frozen **Track4World** teacher into the video expert, plus a 23K-hour corpus unified under a ==canonical 80-D state-action representation== with quality filtering and temporal alignment.

## Results

- LIBERO-Plus zero-shot robustness reaches **92.8%**, beating Qwen-RobotManip-Context by **1.4 points** and the strongest prior WAM Being-H0.7 by **8.0**; RoboTwin 2.0 Clean2Random reaches **71.9%** versus OpenWAM-α **48.7%**.
- RoboDojo average success **23.9%** versus OpenWAM-α **11.9%** and GPT-6 Astra **22.5%**; EBench top score **66.0**; pretraining lifts real-robot toast bread from **20%** to **95%** and Luminol from **0%** to **95%**.
- Cumulative LIBERO-Plus ablation from **49.6%** to **78.4%**: sparse memory **+3.9**, RynnBrain over Qwen3.5 VLM **+8.7**, Causal Imprint with alignment **+7.4**, Track4World distillation **+1.9 points**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Future prediction can shape policy representations purely as training-time supervision; aligning to future video features mattered far more than raw latent-difference prediction, and no inference-time imagination is needed.
> - VLM choice is a first-order lever for WAM action experts: swapping one 2B VLM for an embodied-reasoning one gave the largest single ablation gain.
> - Human data helps in proportion to its alignment with robot action spaces: UMI and Ego2Robot beat raw egocentric video, while injecting distilled 4D features into actions slightly hurt.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

InternW0-∆ is situated at the intersection of vision-language-action (VLA) models, video world models, robot foundation models, and cross-embodiment imitation learning. VLA systems such as RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5, and GR00T use pretrained visual and language representations to predict robot actions. These systems have improved instruction following and semantic generalization, but they do not necessarily model how the physical scene changes as a result of interaction.

World Action Models (WAMs) extend this formulation by jointly learning visual dynamics and action generation. Recent systems, including DreamZero, Motus, Fast-WAM, OpenWAM, 4D-WAM, and JEPA-WAM, investigate whether video prediction or predictive latent representations can improve robotic control. A central design question is whether future visual observations must be generated during action inference. Future-video sampling may provide predictive information, but it introduces additional computation and latency and can create a mismatch between imagined and observed trajectories.

InternW0-∆ addresses this issue by retaining future-video supervision during training while removing future-video generation from the online action pathway. It combines a pretrained video generator, a frozen vision-language model, geometric and motion distillation, and a separately parameterized action model. The work also responds to a second trend in robot learning: scaling heterogeneous data across robot demonstrations, human egocentric video, UMI demonstrations, and synthetic human-to-robot conversions. Its contribution is therefore both architectural and infrastructural.

### 2. Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to develop a general-purpose manipulation model that can use predictive visual dynamics for action generation without sampling future videos during deployment. The authors identify several limitations in conventional action policies and WAMs.

First, predicting future images does not automatically produce representations that are useful for control. Action generation requires identifying which scene changes matter for the task, determining how objects and robot components are moving, and connecting those changes to the current instruction and robot state. Second, a language encoder alone cannot identify the current scene configuration. The same instruction may require different actions depending on object identity, pose, spatial arrangement, or task progress. Third, heterogeneous robot datasets use incompatible action dimensions, coordinate systems, camera configurations, and control conventions, making direct joint training difficult.

The paper therefore pursues four linked goals:

1. To transfer predictive information from a pretrained video model into an action policy through an action-relevant representation called **Causal Imprint**.
2. To inject geometric and motion information using a training-only 4D representation distillation objective.
3. To create a unified data and training recipe spanning more than 20,000 processed hours of robot and human demonstrations.
4. To demonstrate adaptation across simulation benchmarks and different real-robot embodiments using a shared pretrained checkpoint.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

InternW0-∆ uses a directed Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) containing two modality-specific experts: a pretrained video expert and an action expert. The video expert is initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, while the ActionDiT action expert is randomly initialized. Both streams participate in masked joint attention but preserve separate parameters and modality-specific processing.

The video expert receives a sparse memory consisting of three observations: an episode-anchor frame, a recent frame preceding the previous action chunk, and the current frame. This design provides both long-range task context and recent interaction history without processing a dense video history. A frozen Wan VAE encodes the multi-camera observations into visual latents.

The action expert receives the current proprioceptive state and features from the frozen RynnBrain1.1-2B vision-language model. The VLM processes the current camera views together with the task instruction, providing scene-grounded semantic information. The video expert separately retains the T5 language-conditioning pathway inherited from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B. Thus, T5 supplies language conditioning to the video stream, while the VLM supplies observation-conditioned semantics to the action stream.

#### 3.2. Causal Imprint

Causal Imprint consists of learnable tokens inserted into the video stream. These tokens attend to recent and current observations and are intended to encode changes likely to be relevant to future interaction. During training, they are supervised using future video latents, but they cannot directly attend to realized future frames. Their representations are passed to the action expert through the directed attention structure.

Two losses train Causal Imprint. The first regresses adjacent differences between clean future video latents, encouraging the tokens to represent changes along the future trajectory. The second aligns intermediate Causal Imprint features with detached features from a later future-video slice. The latter transfers semantic and temporal information from the video expert without creating a forward path from future observations to actions.

#### 3.3. 4D-Aware Distillation

A frozen Track4World model produces a 1,430-dimensional descriptor containing geometry, two-dimensional and three-dimensional motion, camera motion, and visibility information. These descriptors are computed from action-aligned ground-truth video windows and cached offline.

A student branch processes only anchor, recent, and current observations from the video expert. Sixteen learnable queries aggregate these features through a Transformer decoder, and the resulting descriptor is trained to match the cached teacher descriptor using mean-squared error. Both teacher and student branches are removed from the deployed action pathway. Consequently, the distillation adds training supervision without adding policy inference cost.

#### 3.4. Canonical Data Representation and Curation

All data are converted to a canonical 80-dimensional state-action representation. The representation includes slots for arm joints, end-effector pose, gripper state, dexterous hand configuration, torso, head, lift, and mobile-base motion. Joint actions are represented as absolute targets, whereas end-effector actions contain relative translation and rotation changes.

The processed corpus contains 11,302 hours of robot demonstrations, 2,075 hours of UMI data, 4,061 hours of egocentric human video, and 5,634 hours of Ego2Robot data, totaling 23,072 hours. Robot data are filtered using signal anomaly checks, state-action consistency tests, static-boundary trimming, visual quality checks, action-magnitude limits, instruction screening, VLM-based video-instruction verification, and manual inspection.

Human demonstrations are converted into robot-compatible data by mapping hand trajectories to end-effector targets, solving inverse kinematics and base placement, checking joint limits and collisions, removing human arms from video, and compositing rendered robot embodiments into the scene. The human trajectories are also slowed by a factor of two to approximate robot motion speeds.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

Pretraining uses a mixture of 80% robot data, 10% Ego2Robot data, 8% UMI data, and 2% raw egocentric data. Training samples contain 33 video frames and a 32-step action chunk. The total pretraining run uses 245,000 optimization steps on 256 NVIDIA A800 GPUs.

The joint objective combines video flow matching, action flow matching, Causal Imprint regression, future-feature alignment, and 4D distillation. After pretraining, the model is post-trained separately for target embodiments and tasks.

During inference, the model performs one video-expert prefill using the sparse observation memory and Causal Imprint tokens. The resulting key-value features are cached. The action expert then performs iterative action-only flow denoising, without future-video sampling or decoding. For asynchronous deployment, committed actions from the previous chunk are supplied as a clean prefix, allowing the model to generate a compatible continuation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternW0-∆ reports strong results across four simulation benchmarks. On LIBERO-Plus, it achieves 92.8% overall success, including 90.6% under camera perturbations, 91.1% under robot perturbations, and 92.9% under language perturbations. On RoboTwin 2.0, a model trained only on clean demonstrations reaches 90.0% on Clean2Clean and 71.9% on Clean2Random, for an overall score of 81.0%. This indicates improved robustness to changes in background, lighting, clutter, and table height.

On EBench, InternW0-∆ obtains 49.2% overall success and a score of 66.0. Its Long Horizon category results are 49.4% success and a score of 76.5. On RoboDojo, it reaches an average success rate of 23.91% and score of 30.77 across generalization, precision, memory, long-horizon, and open-vocabulary categories.

The ablation studies identify contributions from several components. Sparse memory improves LIBERO-Plus performance from 49.59% to 53.47%. Adding the RynnBrain1.1-2B VLM raises it to 69.08%. Causal Imprint increases performance to 70.80%, while its future-feature alignment objective raises it further to 76.45%. Adding 4D distillation produces 78.37% in the cumulative ablation. Track4World distillation performs better than the alternative teacher features tested.

Data ablations show that robot pretraining provides the most consistent benefit in the evaluated gripper-focused benchmarks. On LIBERO-Plus, robot-only pretraining reaches 83.73%, compared with 80.45% for raw egocentric data, 81.86% for Ego2Robot data, and 83.24% for UMI data. On RoboTwin Clean2Random, robot-only pretraining reaches 32.34%, while UMI reaches 13.90% and Ego2Robot reaches 6.66%.

The model is also evaluated on four real-robot configurations involving grippers and dexterous hands. On two AC-One tasks, pretraining raises success from 20% to 95% for toast bread and from 0% to 95% for the Luminol reaction, based on 20 trials per condition. The reported deployment latency is 152.8 milliseconds on an RTX 5090 after process isolation, feature caching, compilation, context caching, grouped execution, and CUDA-graph replay. The paper also reports qualitative adaptation to different target water levels and different robot action interfaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work contributes a concrete design for separating predictive supervision from online control. Causal Imprint provides a mechanism for using future information during training without requiring future imagination during deployment, while 4D distillation introduces geometric structure without increasing inference complexity. These ideas are relevant to robot policies that require both temporal reasoning and low-latency execution.

The data contribution is also significant for reproducibility. A canonical action interface, documented filtering procedures, human-to-robot conversion tools, cached features, training recipes, checkpoints, and deployment utilities could lower the cost of comparing architectures and adding new embodiments.

However, the reported results should be interpreted within the paper’s evaluation scope. The contribution of raw egocentric data is not studied across many scaling ratios or dexterous tasks, and the agent-assisted experiments are limited to five RoboDojo tasks. The paper also does not fully establish how performance changes with data quality, embodiment balance, or alternative video backbones. Further evaluation on physical long-horizon tasks, unseen objects, safety constraints, and independently collected datasets would help determine the generality of the approach.
