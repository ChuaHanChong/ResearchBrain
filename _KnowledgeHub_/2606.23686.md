# LIBERO-Safety: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Physical and Semantic Safety in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LIBERO-Safety introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on physical and semantic safety, featuring a scalable data generation pipeline and a dataset of nearly 20,000 human-screened demonstrations. Its cross-paradigm evaluation identifies key vulnerabilities in existing VLA models, revealing a tension between generalization and safety adherence in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- VLA models lack robust operational safety when deployed in real-world environments involving human-robot interaction, dynamic obstacles, and unstructured physical conditions.
- Existing VLA benchmarks primarily focus on task-level execution in static settings, failing to comprehensively address multi-dimensional physical and semantic safety risks.
- The scalability of data generation for safety-critical scenarios is limited by reliance on time-consuming human teleoperation, hindering the training of robust foundation models.

## Method

- Developed **LIBERO-Safety**, a comprehensive benchmark with a parametric environment (UBDDL) and a safety-centric task taxonomy for procedurally generating diverse physical and semantic safety challenges.
- Introduced a ==keypose-driven data generation== pipeline to efficiently synthesize nearly 20,000 large-scale, kinematically feasible, and strictly collision-free expert demonstrations.
- Curated a high-fidelity dataset with extensive visual and physical domain randomization for training and rigorously evaluating VLA models in zero-shot and out-of-distribution safety scenarios.

## Results

- Evaluated VLA models exhibited significant performance degradation in L2 (out-of-distribution) scenarios, with OpenVLA showing near-total collapse in success rates across physical suites.
- Scaling demonstration data simultaneously increased task success rates and reduced collision rates for models like "pi"**0.5** and OpenVLA-OFT, demonstrating the benefit of diverse trajectories for both performance and safety.
- VLA models maintained task-level resilience under visual stochasticity but showed compromised physical safety compliance under global spatial reconfigurations (e.g., object placement), revealing limitations in spatial comprehension and OOD collision handling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard large-scale pre-training alone is insufficient for robust reactive physical safety in hazardous environments, necessitating explicit safety alignment mechanisms for VLA models.
> - High-diversity training data, through comprehensive state-space coverage, mitigates trajectory overfitting and facilitates emergent spatial reasoning, leading to improved task performance and safety adherence.
> - Explicit reasoning frameworks are crucial for robust semantic safety alignment, enabling VLA models to effectively process nuanced or adversarial natural language instructions and assess risks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant direction in general-purpose robotic intelligence, having demonstrated advancements in manipulation capabilities, task success, generalization, and transfer across embodiments through progress in data scaling, model architectures, and policy optimization. As these systems transition from controlled laboratory settings to real-world deployment, they encounter operational environments characterized by close human-robot interaction, dynamic obstacles, and unstructured physical conditions. These real-world contexts introduce safety-critical requirements, including motion-level reliability and consistent constraint satisfaction, which current VLA policies have not robustly addressed.

Existing safety-oriented approaches for robotics remain fragmented, often evaluated under inconsistent protocols. While foundational benchmarks such as the LIBERO series have propelled task-level execution, they typically rely on static and deterministic environments, thereby omitting the physical risks inherent in real-world deployment scenarios. More recent benchmarks, such as VLA-Arena, have incorporated dynamic elements and basic safety constraints for multimodal robustness evaluation. However, these benchmarks exhibit two primary limitations: their reliance on human teleoperation introduces a scalability bottleneck for generating the large datasets required to train robust foundation models, and their safety evaluations are largely restricted to simple, inanimate tabletop obstacles, neglecting the multi-dimensional risks present in complex human-robot co-habitation or precise hand-object maneuvering.

This research introduces LIBERO-Safety as a framework designed to address these limitations. It aims to bridge the gap by providing a comprehensive and systematic benchmark for evaluating VLA models across a spectrum of physical and semantic safety challenges, ranging from static spatial clutter to dynamic human-centric interactions. The work differentiates itself by incorporating multi-dimensional hazard scenarios, addressing data generation scalability through a novel pipeline, and evaluating both physical execution safety and semantic reasoning for malicious instructions, including proximal avoidance with complex hand-object configurations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish LIBERO-Safety, a comprehensive benchmark for systematically evaluating the physical and semantic safety boundaries of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Unverified Operational Safety in Real-World Contexts:** Despite advancements in VLA models' manipulation capabilities, their operational safety under strict real-world constraints remains largely unverified. Current VLA policies often fall short of satisfying safety-critical requirements robustly and consistently in environments involving human-robot interaction, dynamic obstacles, and unstructured physical conditions. Establishing robust safety properties is identified as a prerequisite for the practical deployment of VLAs in everyday environments.

2. **Scalability Bottlenecks in Data Generation:** Existing benchmarks for VLA evaluation heavily rely on human teleoperation for data acquisition. This method is prohibitively time-consuming and labor-intensive, creating a significant scalability bottleneck that hinders the creation of large, diverse datasets necessary for training and evaluating robust, general-purpose embodied foundation models. A more efficient data generation method is required to support extensive safety evaluations.

3. **Limited Scope of Existing Safety Evaluations:** Current benchmarks primarily focus on task proficiency or basic robustness, often overlooking the multi-dimensional risks associated with real-world deployment. Safety evaluations are often confined to simple, inanimate obstacles and do not comprehensively cover complex scenarios such as proximal avoidance around human hands or intricate hand-object configurations. Furthermore, there is a recognized need to assess a model's semantic reasoning capacity to refuse malicious or hazardous instructions, a dimension often neglected in existing physical safety benchmarks.

4. **Lack of a Unified and Systematic Safety Evaluation Framework:** The fragmented nature of existing safety-oriented approaches and their inconsistent evaluation protocols hinder systematic comparison and reliable quantification of safety guarantees. A unified framework is needed to provide a consistent and comprehensive assessment of VLA models' safety performance.

To address these motivations, the research outlines four core contributions as its key objectives:
- To introduce a **Parametric Safety Benchmark and Taxonomy** using the Unified Behavior Domain Definition Language (UBDDL) for procedural generation of safety-critical scenarios with environmental stochasticity and explicit constraints.
- To develop a **Keypose-Driven Data Generation Pipeline** to overcome teleoperation inefficiency, enabling rapid synthesis of large-scale, kinematically feasible, and collision-free expert demonstrations.
- To curate a **Large-Scale Safety Dataset** of nearly 20,000 human-screened, collision-free demonstrations with extensive domain randomization, leveraging the developed data generation pipeline.
- To conduct a **Cross-Paradigm Evaluation** of multiple VLA and embodied foundation models, aiming to identify fundamental bottlenecks, such as the generalization-safety tension, and provide insights into failure modes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development and application of LIBERO-Safety, a comprehensive benchmark framework designed to evaluate VLA models across physical and semantic safety dimensions. This framework comprises four core components: a parametric environment definition, a safety-centric task taxonomy, a keypose-driven data generation pipeline, and a large-scale safety dataset.

#### 3.1. Parametric Environment Definition

The benchmark builds upon the LIBERO-Plus framework and introduces the **Unified Behavior Domain Definition Language (UBDDL)**, an extension of the standard BDDL. UBDDL integrates high-fidelity stochasticity, dynamic interactive entities, and explicit safety constraints into task definitions. A parameterized safety task is formally defined as a tuple T = ⟨G, C_safety, S_init, P_env⟩, where:
- **G** represents symbolic goal conditions.
- **C_safety** encompasses state-dependent Boolean predicates enforcing continuous physical bounds, such as strict collision-free margins.
- **S_init** specifies initial configurations for the robot and objects, including dynamic entity trajectories.
- **P_env** includes environmental parameters such as camera extrinsics, visual variations, and sensor noise.

UBDDL acts as a procedural generation engine, instantiating 7,603 unique scenes with 953 distinct objects and 462 hand-object pairs. This includes comprehensive scene perturbations (sensor noise like motion blur, Gaussian defocus, zoom blur, fog, glass distortion; camera viewpoint randomization including orbital and in-place rotations; procedural scene generation with material randomization and lighting variations; and perturbed robot initialization from 500 distinct kinematic poses), dynamic object definitions (linear and circular kinematics for moving entities), and explicit safety constraints (kinematic spatial constraints for collision avoidance and dynamic wrench constraints for limiting contact forces).

#### 3.2. Safety-Centric Task Taxonomy

A hierarchical safety taxonomy operationalizes the evaluation across five distinct task suites, each scaled across three difficulty levels (L0-L2), totaling 75 unique benchmark tasks. This design systematically increases physical execution complexities and semantic safety comprehension requirements.
- **Affordance-Aware Grasping (AAG):** Assesses comprehension of object affordances and synthesis of geometry-aware grasps, progressing from canonical poses (L0) to randomized spatial translations (L1) and out-of-distribution (OOD) orientations (L2).
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Evaluates collaborative safety with human proxies, scaling from nominal conditions (L0) to dynamic spatial perturbations of the proxy (L1) and robust action despite diverse paraphrased natural language instructions (L2).
- **Tabletop Spatial Avoidance (TSA):** Measures collision-averse trajectories amidst tabletop clutter, advancing from familiar static obstacles (L0) to real-time reactive avoidance against dynamic elements (L1) and zero-shot visual generalization to OOD static objects (L2).
- **Free-Space Hand-Object Avoidance (FSHOA):** Strictly evaluates 3D geometry-aware collision avoidance between the end-effector and complex human hand-object configurations (generated using MANO and GrabNet models), progressing through static (L0), dynamic (L1), and novel/OOD configurations (L2).
- **Semantic Safety Reasoning (SSR):** Systematically evaluates risk assessment and physical grounding by requiring identification and mitigation of unsafe natural language instructions (generated using Qwen3-8B), from direct refusal of malicious instructions (L0) to prediction of physics-based hazards (L1) and evasion of subtle semantic traps (L2).

#### 3.3. Keypose-Driven Data Generation Pipeline

To overcome the scalability limitations of human teleoperation, a keypose-driven pipeline is developed. Human operators define a sparse set of object-centric keyposes (e.g., pre-grasp, grasp), which are then augmented with five distinct spatial variants. These keyposes are initially defined in the target object's canonical frame, promoting data scalability across diverse spatial layouts. The CuRobo motion planner then generates diverse, kinematically feasible, and collision-free trajectories by transforming keyposes and environmental obstacles into the robot's base frame and solving for collision-free paths. This method significantly reduces human effort (to 1.8 minutes per task compared to 7.4 minutes for teleoperation) and ensures algorithmic trajectory consistency and collision guarantees.

#### 3.4. Large-Scale Safety Dataset

Leveraging the keypose-driven pipeline, a dataset of 19,664 human-screened, strictly collision-free demonstrations is curated across 40 distinct L0 and L1 tasks from the four physical safety categories. The Semantic Reasoning suite and all L2 tasks are explicitly omitted from the training data to maintain the rigor of zero-shot evaluations for cognitive capabilities and OOD physical robustness. The dataset incorporates extensive visual and physical domain randomization to foster robust generalization.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics

Ten representative VLA architectures, categorized into four paradigms (Standard VLA, World Model-based VLA, Dual-System VLA, and Embodied Foundation Models), are evaluated.
- **Embodied Physical Safety Track:** Models are fine-tuned via Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using Behavior Cloning (BC) on the curated dataset. Evaluation involves 10 independent trials per task, averaged across three random seeds. A safety constraint violation immediately terminates an episode. Primary metric is **Success Rate (SR)** (goal completion without violations). Supplementary metrics include **Collision Rate (CR)**, **Execution Time**, and **Log-Dimensionless Jerk (LDLJ)** for trajectory smoothness.
- **Semantic Safety Reasoning Track:** Embodied foundation models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting, assessing their high-level cognitive alignment. Models process an observation and a natural language instruction to determine if an action is safe. Primary metric is **Refusal Rate (RR)**, quantifying successful rejection of hazardous instructions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The systematic cross-paradigm evaluation across LIBERO-Safety's multi-level task taxonomy revealed specific vulnerabilities and insights into current VLA models.

- **Key Finding 1: Standard large-scale pre-training is insufficient for reactive safety.**
 Most evaluated models showed significant performance degradation, particularly in L2 scenarios with out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions, where success rates for several architectures neared collapse. For example, the foundational OpenVLA model achieved minimal success across nearly all physical suites. This indicates that generic large-scale pre-training, without explicit safety alignment, does not reliably ensure robust and reactive physical interaction in hazardous environments.

- **Key Finding 2: Joint training on Internet-scale data and sub-task planning facilitates superior cross-suite generalization.**
 Among the standard VLAs, π0.5 demonstrated the highest overall success rate across various suites and difficulty levels. This robustness is attributed to its training strategy, which combines Internet-scale pre-training with diverse sub-task planning. While strong in capabilities, π0.5 still exhibited variance in performance, suggesting a sensitivity to specific environmental initializations that could affect reactive safety.

- **Key Finding 3: High-diversity training data mitigates trajectory overfitting and facilitates emergent spatial reasoning.**
 An ablation study within the Tabletop Spatial Avoidance suite showed that when physical obstacles were removed from the workspace (an OOD condition), π0.5 dynamically generated direct, kinematically optimal trajectories instead of deterministically replicating the non-linear avoidance paths from the training demonstrations. This zero-shot spatial generalization is linked to the scale and structural diversity of the generated dataset, which likely prevented the policy from overfitting to spurious kinematic correlations.

- **Key Finding 4: Explicit reasoning frameworks are essential for robust semantic safety alignment.**
 The semantic safety reasoning track highlighted cognitive limitations in standard embodied foundation models. RoboBrain2.0 achieved an 80% refusal rate at L0 but saw a substantial drop in performance on more complex prompts (L1 and L2). Conversely, RynnBrain-CoP demonstrated greater cognitive resilience in higher difficulty tiers. This suggests that integrating advanced reasoning paradigms, such as Chain-of-Point (CoP), is important for maintaining high-level safety alignment when processing nuanced or adversarial natural language commands.

#### 4.1. Analysis of Generalization and Safety

The research further investigated the trade-off between task execution robustness and safety adherence.

- **Key Finding 5: Scaling demonstration data yields joint improvements in task performance and safety adherence.**
 Evaluating π0.5 with increased demonstration data (from 50 to 500 demonstrations per task) resulted in a 2.4% increase in success rate (SR) and a reduction in collision rate (CR) from 12.7% to 10%. Additionally, there were improvements in Log-Dimensionless Jerk (LDLJ) scores and reduced execution times. This indicates that comprehensive state-space coverage from diverse trajectories contributes to robust visual-spatial grounding and fewer constraint violations.

- **Key Finding 6: VLA models exhibit task-level resilience but compromise safety under global spatial reconfigurations.**
 When π0.5 was tested under various environmental perturbations (noise, initial state, viewpoint, scene variations, unseen objects) within the Tabletop Spatial Avoidance L0 tier, the SR remained relatively stable. Local environmental variations, such as the introduction of unseen objects, caused only a marginal increase in CR. However, object placement perturbations (Layout) significantly degraded physical safety compliance, raising the CR to 6.3% while also reducing the SR. This indicates a fundamental bottleneck in the spatial comprehension of current VLA models, emphasizing the necessity of explicitly evaluating collision metrics under OOD conditions.

#### 4.2. Failure Case Analysis

A qualitative analysis of failed trials revealed that task incompletion frequently occurred without physical safety violations, primarily due to two factors:

- **Key Finding 7: Sub-optimal trajectory synthesis drives task failures without constraint violations.**
 Models often generated paths that deviated from optimal task execution, resulting in overly conservative halting or erratic oscillatory maneuvers due to a lack of long-horizon temporal consistency. These behaviors led to kinematically restricted configurations or temporal overflows, causing task incompletion without direct safety constraint breaches.

- **Key Finding 8: Semantic misalignment induces stable but task-irrelevant interactions.**
 VLAs showed vulnerabilities in fine-grained semantic grounding, frequently misdirecting collision-free grasps toward semantic distractors, particularly when multiple objects shared visual similarities. This indicated that physical safety alone is insufficient without high semantic fidelity and robust relational reasoning to bind linguistic commands to correct physical targets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LIBERO-Safety establishes a comprehensive foundation for evaluating and developing safer and more reliable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** The benchmark directly addresses the identified fragmentation and limitations of existing safety evaluation protocols for VLAs. By offering a unified, systematic, and parametric framework, it provides a consistent basis for comparing different VLA approaches in safety-critical scenarios, which was previously lacking.

2. **Enabling Robust VLA Development:** The research provides valuable infrastructure, including a scalable data generation pipeline and a robust, large-scale dataset of collision-free demonstrations. These resources can facilitate the training and fine-tuning of VLA models to acquire more safety-aware manipulation skills, moving beyond purely task-oriented objectives.

3. **Deep Insights into VLA Vulnerabilities:** The systematic cross-paradigm evaluation uncovers fundamental bottlenecks in current embodied manipulation, such as the generalization-safety tension, sub-optimal trajectory synthesis, and semantic misalignment. These detailed insights into failure modes are crucial for guiding future research directions, allowing developers to target specific weaknesses in VLA architectures and training methodologies.

4. **Advancing Real-World Robot Deployment:** By explicitly evaluating physical safety challenges (e.g., human-robot interaction, free-space hand-object avoidance) and semantic safety reasoning (e.g., refusing malicious instructions), LIBERO-Safety aims to ensure that VLAs can operate reliably and safely in complex, dynamic, and human-centric environments. This is a prerequisite for the broader adoption of general-purpose robots in everyday settings.

5. **Foundation for Future Safety Research:** The work explicitly outlines its limitations, such as simulation fidelity, the sim-to-real gap, and the current focus on safe demonstrations rather than hard-negative unsafe trajectories. These acknowledged limitations pave the way for future research, including:
 - **Real-world Validation:** The benchmark serves as a structured evaluation and data-generation foundation for real-world validation of safety properties.
 - **Intrinsic Safety Enforcement:** It prompts the development of intrinsically safety-aligned VLA models, exploring hierarchical architectures to bridge semantic risk assessment and low-level motor control.
 - **Uncertainty-Aware Safety Filters:** The proposed chunk-level Control Barrier Function (CBF) post-processing, demonstrated to improve real-world safety, highlights a practical direction for developing robust uncertainty-aware safety filters capable of detecting OOD visual states and triggering verified safe fallback maneuvers.
 - **Dense Unsafe-Event Annotation:** The absence of hard-negative unsafe trajectories in the current training corpus indicates a future direction for generating and utilizing such data to enhance model robustness against risky situations.

In summary, LIBERO-Safety contributes a critical tool for the robotics community, shifting the focus towards quantifiable safety alongside task performance. By providing a structured evaluation environment and insights into current model limitations, it has the potential to accelerate the development of VLA models that are not only capable but also reliably safe for real-world interaction.
