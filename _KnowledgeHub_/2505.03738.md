# AMO: Adaptive Motion Optimization for Hyper-Dexterous Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego developed Adaptive Motion Optimization (AMO), a framework that integrates trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning to enable hyper-dexterous whole-body control for humanoid robots. It achieved expanded operational workspace, allowing tasks like picking objects from the ground, and demonstrated robust real-world performance on a Unitree G1 robot with high generalization to out-of-distribution commands.

## Problem

- Humanoid whole-body control faces challenges due to high degrees of freedom, complex nonlinear dynamics, and the need for real-time adaptation in unstructured environments.
- Traditional motion imitation using human motion capture (MoCap) data suffers from an 'embodiment gap,' where human motions are not dynamically feasible for a specific robot, leading to instability or sub-optimal performance.
- Trajectory optimization methods, while dynamically sound, are computationally intensive for real-time applications and lack generalization to novel or out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) scenarios, limiting hyper-dexterous movements.

## Method

- ==The Adaptive Motion Optimization== (**AMO**) module pre-trains a neural network to map upper-body commands to dynamically feasible lower-body joint angle references, generated by combining human MoCap data with a dynamics-aware trajectory optimizer.
- A lower policy is trained using a teacher-student reinforcement learning framework to robustly track these AMO-generated lower-body references and base velocity commands.
- The framework integrates teleoperation via weighted inverse kinematics for human control and leverages imitation learning (using ACT) from collected demonstrations to train autonomous upper policies for complex tasks.

## Results

- The system achieved superior tracking accuracy for torso orientation, base height, and linear velocity in simulation compared to baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of the AMO module.
- AMO expanded the robot's operational workspace, enabling a significantly larger range of torso motion (e.g., bending completely flat) and exhibited remarkable adaptability in tracking out-of-distribution commands (e.g., torso yaw up to 1772 radians).
- Successfully demonstrated hyper-dexterous teleoperation and autonomous execution of complex loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., paper bag picking, trash bottle throwing) on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving high success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning effectively bridges the 'embodiment gap' in motion imitation, generating dynamically feasible reference motions for high-DoF robots.
> - Training an RL policy on a synthetic, dynamics-aware dataset enables robust generalization to out-of-distribution commands and expanded operational workspaces previously unachievable by human motion capture or pure trajectory optimization.
> - A hierarchical control architecture, combining a pre-trained module for lower-body references with an adaptive RL policy, allows for real-time, adaptive whole-body control and efficient data collection for autonomous task learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid whole-body control is a grand challenge in robotics, primarily due to the high degrees of freedom (DoF), complex nonlinear dynamics, and frequent contact interactions with the environment. Historically, this field has been dominated by **traditional model-based optimal control methods**. While precise, these methods demand accurate models, high computational power, and often rely on reduced-order models (e.g., simplified bipedal walking models like ZMP or LIPM) that limit the full utilization of the robot's DoFs, especially for hyper-dexterous movements.

More recently, **reinforcement learning (RL) combined with sim-to-real transfer** has emerged as a promising paradigm for robust real-time control of high-DoF robots. However, pure RL often requires extensive manual reward shaping and struggles to learn complex, coordinated behaviors from scratch. To mitigate this, many researchers have turned to **motion imitation frameworks**. These approaches typically leverage human motion capture (MoCap) data to provide reference trajectories for RL policies.
- **MoCap-driven methods** (e.g.,) are excellent for generating kinematically viable motions but often suffer from an "embodiment gap." The human motions, while natural for humans, might not be dynamically feasible or optimal for a specific robot platform, leading to instability or sub-optimal performance when deployed in the real world. They also tend to be biased towards locomotion patterns (walking, turning) and often lack complex arm-torso coordination.
- An alternative, **trajectory optimization (TO)-based methods** (e.g.,), synthesize dynamically feasible reference motions. However, these methods are computationally intensive for real-time applications and often rely on a limited set of motion primitives, hindering generalization to novel or out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) scenarios. They also don't inherently provide a continuous mapping from commands to actions.

AMO directly addresses these limitations. It bridges the gap by **integrating sim-to-real RL with trajectory optimization** in a novel way. Instead of directly imitating human MoCap or relying solely on TO, AMO *synthesizes* a hybrid dataset that is *dynamically feasible* for the robot and then uses this dataset to train a generalizable RL policy. This allows AMO to combine the robustness of RL, the dynamic feasibility of TO, and the ability to generalize beyond discrete reference motions, thus enabling "hyper-dexterous" capabilities that previous methods could not achieve, particularly in real-time and O.O.D. scenarios. The paper positions AMO as enabling humanoid robots to perform tasks requiring a "large operational workspace," such as picking objects off the ground, which is a common challenge for humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable **hyper-dexterous whole-body movements for humanoid robots**, allowing them to perform tasks that require a large operational workspace, such as picking objects off the ground or reaching high shelves.

The motivation stems from the limitations of current whole-body control approaches:
- **High DoF and Nonlinear Dynamics:** Humanoid robots are inherently complex, making precise real-time control challenging for traditional model-based methods.
- **Computational Burden:** Optimal control requires significant computational power, often necessitating reduced-order models that sacrifice full DoF utilization.
- **Embodiment Gap in RL:** Motion imitation RL, while robust, often relies on human motion capture (MoCap) data, which is kinematically viable but may not be dynamically feasible or efficient for a specific robot. This leads to a disconnect between simulated training and real-world deployment.
- **Limited Dexterity and Generalization:** Existing methods are often constrained to simplified locomotion or manipulation patterns. MoCap datasets lack rich coordinated arm-torso movements for hyper-dexterity. Trajectory optimization, while dynamically sound, is computationally slow and doesn't generalize well to unstructured or out-of-distribution inputs.
- **Real-time Adaptation:** There is a critical need for policies that can rapidly adapt to unpredictable inputs, such as those from human teleoperation, or unexpected environmental perturbations.

To address these, AMO aims to develop a framework that:
- Integrates sim-to-real RL with trajectory optimization for **real-time, adaptive whole-body control**.
- Mitigates distribution bias in motion imitation RL by constructing a **hybrid AMO dataset** of dynamically feasible motions.
- Trains a network capable of **robust, on-demand adaptation to potentially O.O.D. commands**.
- Ultimately, facilitates **autonomous task execution via imitation learning** based on the robust teleoperation system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AMO is presented as a hierarchical framework with four key synergistic stages (illustrated in Figure 2):

**A. Adaptive Motion Optimization (AMO) Module Pre-Training (Hybrid Motion Synthesis):**
This is the core innovation for generating dynamically feasible reference motions.
1. **Hybrid Motion Synthesis:** The authors formulate hybrid upper-body command sets by fusing:
 - Arm trajectories sampled from the **AMASS human motion capture dataset** (for kinematic realism of arms).
 - Probabilistically sampled **torso orientations (RPY) and heights** (to achieve hyper-dexterity and explore large workspace).
2. **Dynamics-Aware Trajectory Optimization (TO):** These hybrid commands drive a model-based trajectory optimizer (BoxFDDP through Crocoddyl) to produce whole-body reference motions. This TO aims to:
 - Track the specified arm trajectories, torso RPY, and base height.
 - Maintain a stable Center of Mass (CoM).
 - Adhere to wrench cone constraints (ensuring stable ground contact).
 - Consider both feet in contact with the ground (double-support stance).
3. **AMO Dataset Creation:** The output of this TO, which are dynamically feasible whole-body joint angles (specifically, lower-body joint angle references), forms the AMO dataset. This is the "first humanoid motion repository explicitly designed for dexterous loco-manipulation."
4. **AMO Module Training:** A three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) is trained on this dataset. This "AMO module" ($\phi$) learns a continuous mapping from upper-body commands (upper joint angles, torso RPY, height) to dynamically feasible lower-body joint angle references ($q_{ref}^{lower}$). This module is frozen during subsequent policy training.

**B. Lower Policy Training:**
This policy learns to execute the dynamically feasible lower-body motions generated by the AMO module.
1. **Teacher-Student Framework:** The lower policy is trained in massively parallel simulation (IsaacGym) using a teacher-student distillation approach.
2. **Teacher Policy (PPO):** An RL teacher policy is trained using off-the-shelf PPO. It observes *privileged information* (e.g., ground-truth base velocity, torso orientation, height, CoM, contact signals) in addition to proprioceptive data and commands. This allows the teacher to learn robust control.
3. **Student Policy:** The teacher's learned policy is then distilled into a student policy via supervised learning. The student policy only observes information available in the real world (proprioception, and a 25-step history of proprioceptive observations for state estimation), making it suitable for real-robot deployment. The lower policy is responsible for tracking base velocity commands ($v$) and the AMO-generated lower-body joint references ($q_{ref}^{lower}$).

**C. Teleoperation Upper Policy Implementation:**
This component allows a human operator to control the robot's upper body and provide high-level whole-body commands.
1. **Multi-Target Weighted Inverse Kinematics (IK):** The system uses an extended multi-target weighted IK (Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm based on Pink) to compute desired upper-body joint angles (head, left/right arms) and intermediate control signals (torso RPY and base height).
2. **Input:** The IK receives sparse 6D targets (position and orientation) for the head, left wrist, and right wrist from a VR teleoperation system.
3. **Prioritization:** The IK prioritizes upper-body actuators for simpler tasks but generates RPY and height commands for the lower policy when whole-body movement is required (e.g., bending over).
4. **Hand Retargeting:** Dexterous hand movements are generated via a separate retargeting module based on Dex-retargeting, which minimizes distances between human and robot hand keypoints.

**D. Autonomous Upper Policy Training:**
This stage enables the robot to perform tasks autonomously.
1. **Demonstration Collection:** Human operators teleoperate the robot using the goal-conditioned teleoperation policy, recording observations (stereo images, proprioception) and actions as demonstrations.
2. **Imitation Learning:** The autonomous upper policy is learned via imitation learning using **ACT (Action Chunking Transformer)** as the policy backbone.
3. **Visual Encoder:** A DinoV2 visual encoder processes stereo images, concatenated with a state token derived from upper-body proprioception and the last lower-policy commands.
4. **Output:** The policy outputs all upper-body joint angles (head, dual-arm, dual-hand) and the intermediate control signals for the lower policy (velocity, torso RPY, height).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in both simulation (IsaacGym) and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot (modified with two Dex3-1 dexterous hands and a custom active head).

**A. Simulation Results (Tracking Performance):**
AMO was compared against several baselines (w/o AMO, w/o priv, w rand arms) in terms of tracking errors for torso yaw (Ey), pitch (Ep), roll (Er), base height (Eh), and base linear velocity (Ev).
- **Torso Orientation Tracking:** AMO achieved superior accuracy in torso roll and pitch, particularly in pitch where other baselines struggled significantly. While "w rand arms" showed the lowest yaw error, AMO still provided critical reference for stable yaw control, outperforming "w/o AMO".
- **Height Tracking:** AMO demonstrated the lowest height tracking error. The "w/o AMO" baseline showed a significantly higher error, indicating its inability to track height commands without AMO's generated references.
- **Linear Velocity Tracking:** Despite the AMO module being designed for a double-support stance (not accounting for foot swing), AMO maintained stable locomotion with low tracking error, highlighting its robustness.

**B. Simulation Results (Workspace and O.O.D. Generalization):**
AMO was compared to "w/o AMO," "Waist Tracking" (direct waist motor control), and "ExBody2" in terms of maximum torso control ranges and O.O.D. performance.
- **Expanded Workspace:** AMO achieved a "significantly larger range of torso motion," especially in pitch, allowing the robot to "bend its upper body completely flat." This is a major advantage over methods limited by human motion datasets (ExBody2, which had minimal control) or mechanical joint limits (Waist Tracking). AMO also demonstrated adaptive behaviors, such as leveraging leg motors to adjust lower posture for stability during torso roll.
- **O.O.D. Performance:** AMO exhibited "remarkable adaptability" in tracking out-of-distribution commands. It successfully tracked torso yaw up to ±2 radians (trained ±1.57) and height as low as 0.4m (trained 0.5-0.8m). In contrast, "w/o AMO" struggled significantly with O.O.D. commands, failing to track before reaching training ranges or failing entirely (e.g., height). This demonstrates strong generalization capabilities for both the AMO module and the RL policy.

**C. Real-World Performance (Teleoperation and Autonomous Tasks):**
- **Hyper-Dexterous Teleoperation:** AMO enabled "hyper-dexterous manipulation tasks" via teleoperation, as shown in Figure 1 and supplementary videos, highlighting its capability in torso orientation and base height control.
- **Autonomous Loco-Manipulation Tasks:** The system successfully learned and executed challenging autonomous tasks via imitation learning from teleoperated demonstrations:
 - **Paper Bag Picking:** Required precise loco-manipulation without an end-effector. The system achieved near-perfect success rates with stereo input and longer action chunk sizes (9/10 placing success with full setup).
 - **Trash Bottle Throwing:** Demanded extensive torso movements (90-degree waist turn). Achieved 10/10 placing success rate.
 - **Basket Picking (Case Study):** A complex long-horizon task requiring the robot to crouch low, adjust torso, grasp baskets, stand up, walk, and place on a high shelf. Joint angle visualizations (knee and waist motors) confirmed that the learned policy effectively coordinated whole-body actuators for these complex movements (crouching, tilting, walking, reaching).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Adaptive Motion Optimization (AMO) framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid whole-body control, offering a novel pathway that transcends the limitations of conventional approaches.

**Key Significance Points:**
- **Enabling Hyper-Dexterity:** AMO significantly expands the operational workspace and dexterity of humanoid robots, allowing them to perform tasks previously considered extremely challenging, such as picking objects directly from the ground or reaching far targets, by fully leveraging their high DoF.
- **Bridging Key Gaps:** It effectively addresses the "embodiment gap" inherent in MoCap-driven RL by generating dynamically feasible reference motions through trajectory optimization. Simultaneously, it overcomes the computational and generalization limitations of pure TO by integrating it into an RL policy that learns continuous mappings and adapts in real-time.
- **Robustness to O.O.D. Commands:** The demonstrated ability to generalize to out-of-distribution commands (e.g., larger torso rotations, lower heights than seen in training) is crucial for real-world deployment, especially in dynamic, unstructured environments or with human teleoperators providing unpredictable inputs.
- **Real-world Applicability:** The successful deployment on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, along with real-time teleoperation and autonomous task execution, underscores the practical viability and robustness of the system. The capability to run the full system onboard a Jetson Orin NX at 50Hz inference frequency further highlights its deployment readiness.
- **Foundation for Autonomous Learning:** The robust teleoperation system serves as an effective data collection tool, enabling the training of autonomous policies via imitation learning for complex, long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Humanoid Capabilities:** AMO could unlock a new generation of humanoid robot applications in diverse fields, including logistics, domestic assistance, hazardous environments, and even entertainment, where complex, adaptive, and dexterous manipulation is required.
- **Advancement in Robot Learning:** The novel approach of generating a dynamically feasible, hybrid dataset for RL training could inspire future research in robot learning, particularly for complex robotic systems where synthesizing realistic and executable reference motions is critical.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** The robust teleoperation capabilities facilitate more intuitive and effective human control of humanoids, potentially lowering the barrier to entry for operators and enabling complex tasks to be demonstrated for autonomous learning.

**Acknowledged Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge a key limitation: the **decoupled nature** of their approach. While innovative, the separation of arm control from the robot's base state means that the arms are not currently utilized for balance. In highly dynamic scenarios, humans naturally use their arms to maintain stability. The authors suggest that incorporating base state information into upper-limb joint-angle acquisition could enhance overall stability and adaptability, indicating a clear direction for future work. This transparency regarding limitations adds to the credibility of the research.

In conclusion, AMO presents a powerful and pragmatic solution to a long-standing challenge in humanoid robotics, marking a significant step towards truly hyper-dexterous and adaptable whole-body control in real-world scenarios.
