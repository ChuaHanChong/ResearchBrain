# AMO: Adaptive Motion Optimization for Hyper-Dexterous Humanoid Whole-Body Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego developed Adaptive Motion Optimization (AMO), a framework that integrates trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning to enable hyper-dexterous whole-body control for humanoid robots. It achieved expanded operational workspace, allowing tasks like picking objects from the ground, and demonstrated robust real-world performance on a Unitree G1 robot with high generalization to out-of-distribution commands.

## Problem

- Humanoid whole-body control faces challenges due to high degrees of freedom, complex nonlinear dynamics, and the need for real-time adaptation in unstructured environments.
- Traditional motion imitation using human motion capture (MoCap) data suffers from an 'embodiment gap,' where human motions are not dynamically feasible for a specific robot, leading to instability or sub-optimal performance.
- Trajectory optimization methods, while dynamically sound, are computationally intensive for real-time applications and lack generalization to novel or out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) scenarios, limiting hyper-dexterous movements.

## Method

- ==The Adaptive Motion Optimization== (**AMO**) module pre-trains a neural network to map upper-body commands to dynamically feasible lower-body joint angle references, generated by combining human MoCap data with a dynamics-aware trajectory optimizer.
- A lower policy is trained using a teacher-student reinforcement learning framework to robustly track these AMO-generated lower-body references and base velocity commands.
- The framework integrates teleoperation via weighted inverse kinematics for human control and leverages imitation learning (using ACT) from collected demonstrations to train autonomous upper policies for complex tasks.

## Results

- The system achieved superior tracking accuracy for torso orientation, base height, and linear velocity in simulation compared to baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of the AMO module.
- AMO expanded the robot's operational workspace, enabling a significantly larger range of torso motion (e.g., bending completely flat) and exhibited remarkable adaptability in tracking out-of-distribution commands (e.g., torso yaw up to 1772 radians).
- Successfully demonstrated hyper-dexterous teleoperation and autonomous execution of complex loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., paper bag picking, trash bottle throwing) on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving high success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating trajectory optimization with reinforcement learning effectively bridges the 'embodiment gap' in motion imitation, generating dynamically feasible reference motions for high-DoF robots.
> - Training an RL policy on a synthetic, dynamics-aware dataset enables robust generalization to out-of-distribution commands and expanded operational workspaces previously unachievable by human motion capture or pure trajectory optimization.
> - A hierarchical control architecture, combining a pre-trained module for lower-body references with an adaptive RL policy, allows for real-time, adaptive whole-body control and efficient data collection for autonomous task learning.
