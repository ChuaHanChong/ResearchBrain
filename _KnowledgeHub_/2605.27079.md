# Trust Region Q Adjoint Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST AI and Seoul National University developed Trust Region Q-Adjoint Matching (TRQAM), an off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that stabilizes the fine-tuning of pretrained generative flow policies. TRQAM achieves a 68% overall success rate on 50 OGBench offline RL tasks, a 33-point improvement over QAM, by adaptively controlling the deviation from the prior through an internalized trust-region parameter.

## Problem

- Gradient-based fine-tuning of pretrained generative flow policies is computationally expensive and suffers from instability due to their multi-step, implicitly defined nature.
- Off-policy RL methods, especially those leveraging critic guidance like Q-Adjoint Matching (QAM), are fragile to approximation errors in the learned critic, which can be exponentially amplified and lead to destructive policy drift.
- Existing heuristic stabilizers, such as gradient clipping, are often insufficient to prevent model collapse and maintain task performance when fine-tuning generative policies.

## Method

- **TRQAM** introduces a re-parameterized Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) where a trust-region parameter `λ` explicitly scales the ==diffusion== coefficient, allowing for intrinsic control over policy deviation.
- The method theoretically establishes that this `λ` parameter directly corresponds to the inverse of the path-space Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the controlled and base trajectory distributions.
- An adaptive update rule for `λ` is implemented using projected dual descent, dynamically adjusting the trust region to ensure the fine-tuned policy stays within a prescribed KL divergence bound from the pretrained prior.

## Results

- TRQAM achieved an overall offline RL success rate of **68%** across 50 OGBench tasks, marking a 33-point improvement over QAM (**35%**) and a 22-point improvement over DSRL (**46%**).
- The adaptive `λ` mechanism consistently and tightly tracked the prescribed target KL bound (`ε_KL`), preventing the adjoint loss from diverging and model performance from collapsing, unlike fixed-λ QAM or external KL regularization.
- TRQAM demonstrated predictable sensitivity to the `ε_KL` hyperparameter, allowing smooth adjustment of task success rates and enabling the use of time-varying `ε_KL` schedules for more efficient online adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Small approximation errors in the learned critic can be exponentially amplified, causing significant policy deviations when using fixed-temperature adjoint matching for policy improvement.
> - Scaling the diffusion coefficient of an SDE by `√λ` fundamentally redefines the generative dynamics such that the path-space KL divergence is an exact, closed-form function of `λ`, thereby offering a principled trust-region mechanism.
> - Internalizing the trust-region parameter `λ` within the SDE's sampling dynamics provides superior and more robust control over the realized KL divergence compared to using `λ` as an external regularization penalty in the loss function.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) often benefits from leveraging pretrained policies, particularly those capable of modeling diverse and complex action distributions. Flow matching policies, and more broadly diffusion policies, have emerged as a method for generating high-capacity behavior beyond traditional unimodal Gaussian policies. These pretrained flow policies can capture useful skills, behavioral constraints, and broad coverage over plausible actions, making them attractive priors for downstream off-policy RL fine-tuning.

However, fine-tuning these generative policies with gradient-based RL methods presents several challenges. Flow policies are implicitly defined through multi-step denoising processes. Direct backpropagation through this multi-step sampling chain is computationally expensive and can be unstable. Existing approaches to address this include:
- **Residual-style methods:** These freeze the pretrained policy and learn an additive residual to its actions. Their limitation is that corrections occur only at the action level, neglecting the underlying multi-step generative dynamics.
- **Noise-space RL methods:** These also freeze the pretrained flow policy but run actor-critic algorithms over its input noise. Their expressivity is bounded by the capabilities of the fixed flow policy.

More recently, Q-learning with Adjoint Matching (QAM) introduced a reformulation of this fine-tuning problem into a memoryless stochastic optimal control (SOC) problem. QAM uses a learned critic to guide the sampling process towards higher-value actions via adjoint matching, thereby sidestepping the multi-step sampling instability. While QAM addressed the instability arising from differentiating through the sampling chain, it introduced another challenge inherent to critic-guided improvement: instability due to approximation errors in the learned critic. In off-policy RL, an imperfect critic can lead to errors that are amplified through temporal difference (TD) bootstrapping and replay, potentially causing systematic overestimation and model collapse, where the policy deviates destructively from the pretrained prior. QAM attempted to mitigate this with gradient clipping, but empirical observations suggest this is often insufficient.

This paper positions its contribution, Trust Region Q-Adjoint Matching (TRQAM), as a solution to this critic-induced instability in the context of off-policy fine-tuning of pretrained flow policies. It draws inspiration from trust-region principles, which have been successfully applied in on-policy RL (e.g., TRPO, PPO) to ensure policy updates remain within a bounded deviation from previous policies, thus improving stability. TRQAM aims to extend this stabilization to the off-policy setting for complex generative policies by adaptively controlling the deviation from the pretrained prior at a fundamental level within the SOC dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a stable and effective off-policy fine-tuning algorithm for pretrained flow policies, addressing the limitations of existing methods, particularly the fragility observed in critic-guided approaches like Q-learning with Adjoint Matching (QAM).

The motivation stems from several interconnected problems:

1. **Instability in Fine-tuning Flow Policies:** Pretrained flow policies are capable of generating complex action distributions, making them valuable priors for RL. However, their multi-step, implicit definition makes direct gradient-based fine-tuning computationally expensive and unstable. QAM mitigated the multi-step sampling instability by reformulating it as a memoryless Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) problem and using adjoint matching.

2. **Critic-Induced Instability in Off-Policy RL:** Even with QAM's advancements, a fundamental fragility remains: errors in the learned critic function. In off-policy RL, critics are inevitably imperfect due to factors like function approximation errors, compounding errors through TD bootstrapping, and distributional shift between the behavior policy and the target policy. These small critic errors can be exponentially amplified when the critics are ill-conditioned, leading to significant policy deviations from the pretrained prior and often resulting in model collapse, where task performance degrades severely. The paper formalizes this fragility with a lemma showing exponential amplification of critic errors for policies exponentially tilted from a base policy.

3. **Limitations of Heuristic Stabilizers:** Prior work, including QAM, acknowledged this instability and applied heuristic remedies like gradient clipping. However, empirical observations presented in the paper indicate that such measures are often insufficient to prevent the adjoint loss from diverging and task performance from collapsing. This highlights the need for a more principled and robust stabilization mechanism.

4. **Desire for Controlled Deviation:** The central challenge, therefore, is to improve downstream task performance by leveraging the learned critic's guidance without catastrophically drifting away from the useful pretrained prior. This necessitates a mechanism to adaptively control the deviation of the fine-tuned policy from the pretrained policy, analogous to the trust-region principles that have proven effective in on-policy RL for maintaining stability. The goal is to balance the exploitation of critic signals with the preservation of the pretrained policy's beneficial properties.

In summary, the key objective is to introduce an adaptive trust-region control into the SOC dynamics for flow policies, specifically by internalizing a trust-region parameter `λ`, to achieve stable off-policy fine-tuning that reliably improves performance while preventing destructive policy drift caused by amplified critic errors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TRQAM proposes a stable off-policy fine-tuning algorithm for pretrained flow policies by integrating an adaptive trust-region mechanism directly into the Stochastic Optimal Control (SOC) sampling dynamics. The methodology addresses the fragility of critic-guided updates through three main components: a re-parameterized SDE with an explicit trust-region parameter, a theoretical link between this parameter and path-space KL divergence, and an adaptive update rule for the parameter via projected dual descent.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Re-parameterized SDE

The work builds upon the SOC framework for fine-tuning flow policies. A base flow policy is represented by a Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE):
`dX_base_τ = b(X_base_τ, τ) dτ + √λ σ(τ) dB_τ`
A controlled SDE, which generates the fine-tuned policy, introduces a drift perturbation `u`:
`dX_u_τ = [b(X_u_τ, τ) + σ(τ) u(X_u_τ, τ)] dτ + √λ σ(τ) dB_τ`
A key methodological choice is the parameterization of the diffusion coefficient. Following prior work, the diffusion coefficient `σ(τ)` is chosen based on a memoryless optimal transport (OT) schedule, `p(2(1-τ)/τ)`. Crucially, TRQAM explicitly separates a scalar `√λ` from this diffusion coefficient. This `√λ`-scaling of the diffusion coefficient is central to the theoretical developments.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Foundations of Trust-Region Control

1. **Exponential Amplification of Critic Errors (Lemma 1):** The paper first formalizes the problem. It proves that for policies exponentially tilted from a base policy (which includes QAM's terminal policy form), the total variation distance between a policy derived from a true Q-function and one from an imperfect Q-function (`eQ`) is bounded exponentially by `βε`, where `β` is the inverse temperature and `ε` is the critic error. This demonstrates that fixed-temperature adjoint matching can amplify small critic errors into large policy deviations.

2. **λ as a Trust-Region Parameter (Theorem 1):** The core theoretical contribution is proving that by scaling the diffusion coefficient by `√λ`, the path-space Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the controlled (`P_u`) and base (`P_base`) trajectory distributions becomes an explicit closed-form function of `λ`:
 `D_KL(P_u || P_base) = E_X~P_u [ (1/(2λ)) ∫_0^1 ||u(X_u_τ, τ)||^2 dτ ]`
 This identity, derived using Girsanov's theorem, demonstrates that `λ` directly acts as an inverse coefficient to the quadratic control cost, making `1/λ` proportional to the path-space KL. Thus, `λ` becomes a principled trust-region parameter: increasing `λ` reduces the allowed deviation from `P_base`.

3. **Terminal KL Bounded by Path-Space KL (Proposition 1):** The paper further establishes that controlling the path-space KL is sufficient for controlling the terminal action distribution. It shows that the KL divergence between the fine-tuned and base terminal action distributions (`π_θ` and `π_base`) is upper-bounded by the path-space KL divergence. This provides a link between controlling trajectory-level deviation and controlling policy-level deviation.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Control of λ via Projected Dual Descent

To adapt `λ` and enforce a target KL bound (`ε_KL`), TRQAM employs a primal-dual optimization approach:

1. **Path-Space KL Estimator:** A tractable estimator for the path-space KL divergence is derived for the discretized memoryless OT sampler. Since the discretized transitions are Gaussian with shared covariance, per-step KL divergences can be computed in closed form. Summing these along a trajectory and averaging over a batch provides an estimate (`D_n`). Exponential Moving Average (EMA) smoothing is used to reduce variance.

2. **Dual Descent Update:** `λ` is treated as the dual variable for a KL-constrained improvement problem (`max_u E[Q_π(X_1)] s.t. D_KL(P_u || P_base) ≤ ε_KL`). It is updated using projected dual descent:
 `λ_n+1 = max{0, λ_n + η_λ (D_n - ε_KL)}`
 This update rule dynamically adjusts `λ`: if the estimated KL (`D_n`) exceeds the target `ε_KL`, `λ` increases (making the control more conservative); if it falls below, `λ` decreases (allowing more aggressive improvement).

#### 3.4. Internal vs. External KL Regularization

A critical distinction is drawn between internalizing `λ` within the SOC sampling dynamics (TRQAM's approach) and treating it as an external regularization weight in the loss function.

- **Internal (TRQAM):** `λ` directly scales the diffusion coefficient (`√λ σ(τ)`) in the SDE. This means adjusting `λ` structurally reshapes the entire controlled SDE and its resulting trajectory distribution. By Theorem 1, the realized path-space KL is an *exact function* of `λ`, so the dual update directly enforces the trust region through the sampling dynamics themselves.
- **External:** `λ` would only be an additive penalty term (`λ ⋅ D_n(θ)`) in the policy loss. In this scenario, `λ` merely competes with other loss terms (e.g., critic guidance). Strong critic signals can override this penalty, allowing the realized KL to drift significantly beyond `ε_KL`, thus failing to enforce the target bound effectively.

#### 3.5. Overall Algorithm (TRQAM)

The TRQAM algorithm iteratively performs:
1. Critic update (standard TD backup).
2. Policy update using adjoint matching, where trajectories are sampled from the `√λ`-scaled SDE.
3. Estimation of the current path-space KL.
4. Dual descent update of `λ` based on the estimated KL and the target `ε_KL`.

This iterative process ensures that the policy improves while consistently staying within a prescribed deviation from the pretrained prior, as enforced by the `λ`-controlled SOC dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TRQAM's performance in off-policy fine-tuning of pretrained flow policies, primarily on the OGBench and Robomimic benchmarks, comparing it against six existing methods.

1. **Superior Performance in Offline RL and Offline-to-Online RL:**
 - **OGBench Tasks:** TRQAM achieved an overall offline RL success rate of 68% across 50 OGBench tasks, significantly outperforming previous state-of-the-art baselines. This includes a 33-point improvement over QAM (35%), a 23-point improvement over its stronger variant QAM-E (45%), and a 22-point improvement over DSRL (46%). These gains were particularly notable in long-horizon and combinatorial task suites.
 - **Offline-to-Online Transition:** The performance lead of TRQAM was sustained through the offline-to-online fine-tuning phase, demonstrating its robustness and continued effectiveness when transitioning to online data collection.

2. **Effective Utilization of Pretrained Policies:**
 - Experiments on `humanoidmaze-medium-task1` showed that TRQAM substantially benefits from a pretrained flow policy, achieving high success rates much faster than when initialized from scratch. In contrast, QAM and QAM-E exhibited minimal or no benefit from the same pretrained initialization, with their pretrained and scratch performance curves remaining close. This indicates TRQAM's ability to leverage the pretrained prior effectively for stable improvement.

3. **Validation of Adaptive KL Control:**
 - **Adaptive vs. Constant λ:** A comparison between constant-λ QAM and adaptive-λ variants (TRQAM and QAM + External KL) demonstrated that adaptive control of `λ` is crucial. Fixed-temperature QAM exhibited diverging adjoint loss (reaching values above 10^20) and a collapse in task success (e.g., on Robomimic-can), even with gradient clipping. Both adaptive variants, however, remained stable and achieved better performance, consistent with the theoretical prediction (Lemma 1) about the exponential amplification of critic errors under fixed temperatures.
 - **Internal vs. External KL Regularization:** The study highlighted the importance of `λ` being internalized within the SOC sampling dynamics. On Robomimic tasks, TRQAM (internal KL) consistently and tightly tracked the prescribed target KL bound (`ε_KL`) throughout training. In contrast, QAM + External KL, which uses `λ` as an additive loss penalty, allowed the realized KL to drift significantly above the target `ε_KL`, leading to degraded task success rates. This empirical result supports the theoretical argument that an external penalty can be overridden by strong critic guidance, while an internalized `λ` structurally enforces the constraint.

4. **Predictable Sensitivity to KL Budget (ε_KL):**
 - A sensitivity analysis across various OGBench tasks showed that task success rates change smoothly with the `ε_KL` hyperparameter. While tight budgets were generally found to be optimal across most tasks, the best setting for `ε_KL` varied systematically with task structure (e.g., larger budgets were beneficial for tasks with larger state spaces like `puzzle-4x4`). This indicates that `ε_KL` acts as a predictable control knob, facilitating adaptation to new environments based on their structural properties.

5. **Handling Time-Varying KL Budgets:**
 - TRQAM demonstrated the capability to track time-varying `ε_KL` schedules without instability. For instance, on `antmaze-giant`, switching from a tight `ε_KL` in the offline phase to a looser one in the online phase (to encourage exploration) resulted in faster online adaptation and higher success rates. The realized KL adapted almost immediately to the new target, confirming the stability of the dual update under dynamic constraints.

In summary, the main findings substantiate that TRQAM provides a robust and theoretically grounded solution to a critical instability problem in modern off-policy RL. It achieves this by directly embedding trust-region control within the generative policy's sampling dynamics, leading to substantial performance gains and greater training stability compared to prior methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Trust Region Q-Adjoint Matching (TRQAM) represents a significant methodological contribution to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in the domain of off-policy fine-tuning of generative policies. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Stability in Off-Policy RL with Generative Policies:** TRQAM provides a principled and effective solution to the problem of instability arising from amplified critic errors in off-policy fine-tuning of expressive flow policies. This addresses a fundamental fragility that limited the practical application and performance of previous methods like QAM, which struggled with model collapse despite using heuristic stabilizers. By preventing destructive drift from the pretrained prior, TRQAM makes fine-tuning more reliable.

2. **Theoretical Advancements in Trust-Region Control for SDEs:** The research establishes novel theoretical connections:
 - **Formalization of Critic Error Amplification:** Lemma 1 formally quantifies how small critic errors can be exponentially amplified into large policy deviations, providing a clear theoretical underpinning for the observed instabilities.
 - **λ as an Intrinsic SDE Parameter:** Theorem 1's proof, utilizing Girsanov's theorem, demonstrates that scaling the diffusion coefficient of an SDE by `√λ` makes the path-space KL divergence an exact function of `λ`. This is a critical insight that elevates `λ` from a mere regularization weight to an intrinsic parameter directly governing the deviation of the controlled dynamics from the base.
 - **Path-Space KL as a Proxy for Policy KL:** Proposition 1 shows that path-space KL bounds terminal-policy KL, justifying the focus on controlling trajectory-level deviations. These theoretical contributions provide a stronger foundation for designing stable RL algorithms that interact with SDE-based policies.

3. **Algorithmic Innovation through Internalized Trust-Region:** The distinction between internalizing `λ` within the SOC sampling dynamics (TRQAM) versus applying it as an external loss penalty is a key algorithmic innovation. The empirical evidence strongly supports that internalizing `λ` leads to tighter control over the KL divergence and greater stability. This design principle could influence the development of other RL algorithms that require constrained policy updates, particularly those interacting with complex generative models.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** TRQAM consistently outperforms prior methods on challenging benchmarks such as OGBench (50 tasks) and Robomimic. The substantial improvements in success rates (e.g., 68% vs. 46% for the strongest baseline on OGBench offline RL) demonstrate its practical efficacy and robustness across diverse tasks, including those requiring long-horizon planning and combinatorial reasoning.

5. **Predictable and Controllable Policy Deviation:** The ability to precisely control the deviation from the pretrained policy via the `ε_KL` parameter, and for this control to be predictably tied to task structure, offers practitioners a powerful tool. This predictability simplifies hyperparameter tuning for new environments and enables tailored fine-tuning strategies (e.g., time-varying `ε_KL` schedules for different training phases).

6. **Broader Impact on Generative Models in RL:** The increasing use of high-capacity generative models (like flow matching and diffusion models) for policy representation in robotics and other embodied AI tasks necessitates robust fine-tuning mechanisms. TRQAM's contributions in stabilizing the fine-tuning process for such policies could accelerate their adoption and improve their reliability in real-world deployments. It offers an analogous trust-region stabilization for off-policy fine-tuning, akin to how TRPO and PPO provided stability for on-policy RL.

**Potential Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges a limitation regarding the computational cost associated with computing the vector-Jacobian product (VJP) through the velocity field at each step of the backward ODE, which scales with model size. Future research could explore methods to optimize this computational aspect without compromising stability or performance.

In conclusion, TRQAM provides a robust, theoretically-backed, and empirically superior method for off-policy fine-tuning of pretrained flow policies, addressing a critical instability challenge and paving the way for more reliable and effective deployment of expressive generative policies in complex reinforcement learning scenarios.
