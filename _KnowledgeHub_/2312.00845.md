# VMC: Video Motion Customization using Temporal Attention Adaption for Text-to-Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework enables precise adaptation of text-to-video diffusion models to distill and reproduce custom motion patterns from a single input video, using a one-shot tuning approach on temporal attention layers. It achieves superior motion preservation and visual diversity over current baselines, demonstrating computational efficiency with training completing in under 5 minutes on a single GPU.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Video (T2V) models lack the ability to perform specific customization tasks, such as replicating precise motions from an input video or generating diverse visual variations of the same motion.
- Existing generative model customization methods, when extended to video, often lead to entanglement of appearance and motion data, hindering the ability to generate new contexts while preserving specific motion.
- Many customization approaches are computationally intensive, requiring significant time and memory resources for fine-tuning large video diffusion models.

## Method

- **VMC** introduces a one-shot tuning approach that specifically adapts only the ==temporal attention== layers within the key-frame generation module of pre-trained cascaded ==video diffusion== models.
- A novel motion distillation objective is proposed, focusing on aligning ground-truth motion vectors (residual vectors between consecutive latent frames) with their denoised estimates, optimized using a cosine similarity-based loss.
- The framework employs 'appearance-invariant prompts' during training to filter out appearance and background-related noise, compelling the temporal attention layers to focus solely on motion information.

## Results

- VMC qualitatively demonstrated superior performance in applying complex motions to new subjects and environments, including challenging compositional changes, compared to existing baselines like VideoComposer, Gen-1, and Tune-A-Video.
- Quantitatively, VMC achieved higher average CLIP-based textual alignment (**0.801**) and temporal consistency (**0.959**) compared to baselines, and significantly outperformed them in a user study across motion preservation (**4.42**), appearance diversity (**4.54**), text alignment (**4.56**), and temporal consistency (**4.57**).
- The framework exhibited high computational efficiency, requiring only 15GB of vRAM and completing training in approximately 5 minutes for 400 steps on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Motion patterns can be effectively disentangled from appearance and background by precisely targeting and adapting only the temporal attention layers in a T2V diffusion model using a dedicated motion distillation objective.
> - Utilizing simplified, 'unfaithful' text prompts during training (appearance-invariant prompts) significantly aids in isolating motion learning by preventing entanglement with visual attributes and contextual details.
> - Focusing fine-tuning on a minimal subset of parameters, specifically temporal attention layers, enables computationally efficient motion customization while preserving the model's spatial synthesis capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has witnessed significant advancements with the advent of diffusion models, particularly in Text-to-Image (T2I) generation. This success has subsequently been extended to Text-to-Video (T2V) generation through cascaded diffusion pipelines and architectural modifications like the integration of temporal attention blocks and 3D convolution layers. However, current T2V models generally lack the ability to perform specific customization tasks, such as replicating precise motions from a given input video or generating diverse visual variations of the same motion in different contexts.

Existing research in generative model customization primarily focuses on appearance customization, where models are fine-tuned to generate images of specific subjects in various contexts. These methods, often relying on minimizing the L2-distance between predicted and ground-truth noise, can lead to an entanglement of appearance and motion data when directly applied to video. This entanglement makes it challenging to disentangle the motion pattern from the subject's appearance or background, limiting the model's flexibility in generating new contexts while preserving a specific motion. Furthermore, many customization approaches, while effective, can be computationally intensive, requiring substantial time and memory resources for fine-tuning.

This work addresses the challenge of "Motion Customization" within this landscape. It aims to develop a method that can adapt pre-trained Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) to produce videos characterized by a specific motion pattern, enabling variations in visual attributes and backgrounds, without the aforementioned entanglement or high computational cost. The proposed Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework contributes a novel, efficient approach by targeting specific architectural components (temporal attention layers) and introducing a dedicated motion distillation objective, thereby differentiating itself from general fine-tuning or depth-map-reliant baselines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the VMC framework is to enable accurate and diverse video generation with custom motion patterns using pre-trained Text-to-Video diffusion models. This overarching goal can be broken down into several specific aims:

1. **Motion Distillation:** To effectively extract and isolate the underlying motion patterns from a single input video, treating these patterns as a reusable template. The challenge here is to separate motion information from visual attributes such as the subject's appearance, background, and other irrelevant visual details.
2. **Appearance-Invariant Customization:** To adapt a pre-trained VDM so that it can generate new videos where a specific distilled motion is accurately reproduced, but applied to entirely different subjects, contexts, or backgrounds. This requires preventing the entanglement of appearance and motion, which often occurs when traditional image customization methods are extended to video.
3. **Visual Diversity:** To enable the generation of a wide variety of videos sharing the same motion, but with distinct visual styles, subjects, and environments, thereby expanding the creative applications of T2V models.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** To achieve the above objectives with minimal computational overhead, specifically in terms of training time and memory (vRAM) requirements. Existing customization methods can be resource-intensive, making a lightweight and fast approach desirable for practical implementation.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in current Text-to-Video diffusion models:
- **Inadequate Motion Reproduction:** While T2V models can generate plausible videos, they often struggle to precisely replicate a specific motion from a target video.
- **Lack of Visual Diversity with Fixed Motion:** Models typically find it difficult to generate diverse visual outputs (e.g., different subjects or backgrounds) while strictly adhering to a given motion pattern.
- **Appearance-Motion Entanglement:** Straightforward fine-tuning of T2V models for customization often leads to an undesirable entanglement where appearance data becomes inseparable from motion data, hindering the ability to swap out subjects or backgrounds.
- **Resource Intensiveness:** Many proposed customization techniques, especially those that involve extensive model fine-tuning, demand significant computational resources, limiting their accessibility and scalability.

By addressing these challenges, VMC aims to provide a practical and effective solution for motion-driven video customization, offering a more flexible and efficient paradigm for generating tailored video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video Motion Customization (VMC) framework is a one-shot tuning approach designed to adapt temporal attention layers within video diffusion models. It leverages a cascaded video diffusion model architecture, specifically using Show-1 as its backbone, for efficient motion distillation and video generation.

**Framework Overview:**
VMC operates by fine-tuning only a specific subset of parameters within the pre-trained video diffusion model. This targeted approach is applied to the key-frame generation module, which is responsible for producing low-resolution video keyframes. Critically, the subsequent frame interpolation and spatial super-resolution modules of the cascaded VDM pipeline are kept frozen during the fine-tuning process. This selective adaptation strategy contributes to computational and memory efficiency.

**Temporal Attention Adaptation (Motion Distillation Objective):**
The core of VMC lies in its novel motion distillation objective, which focuses on learning motion trajectories.
1. **Motion Vector Definition:** The method introduces the concept of "residual vectors between consecutive (latent) frames" as motion vectors. For a given noisy video latent vector `v_1:N_t` at timestep `t`, the frame residual vector `δv_n_t` is defined as the difference `v_n+c_t - v_n_t`, where `c` is the frame stride (fixed at 1). Similarly, an epsilon residual vector `δϵ_n_t` is defined. These motion vectors are hypothesized to encode information about motion patterns.
2. **Denoised Motion Vector Estimation:** The framework leverages Tweedie’s formula to derive a denoised estimate of the motion vector, `δv_n_0_hat(t)`, which is expressed in terms of the noisy motion vector `δv_n_t` and the predicted epsilon residual `δϵ_n_θ,t`.
3. **Optimization Objective:** The goal is to fine-tune the model parameters `θ` by aligning the ground-truth motion vector `δv_n_0` with its denoised estimate `δv_n_0_hat(t)`. This alignment is shown to be equivalent to matching the ground-truth epsilon residual `δϵ_n_t` with the predicted epsilon residual `δϵ_n_θ,t`.
4. **Loss Function:** While an L2-distance loss (`ℓ_2`) could be used for this matching, the authors found that a cosine similarity-based loss (`ℓ_cos`) function, defined as `1 - (x·y) / (||x|| ||y||)`, yields empirically better distillation performance. Therefore, the optimization aims to maximize the residual cosine similarity between `δϵ_n_t` and `δϵ_n_θ,t`.
5. **Targeted Parameter Tuning:** Only the temporal attention layers (`θ_TA`) within the key-frame generation U-Net are fine-tuned. Specifically, the Query, Key, and Value projection matrices (`W_Q`, `W_K`, `W_V`) of these layers are updated. This preserves the model's spatial synthesis capabilities while adapting its temporal understanding.

**Appearance-Invariant Prompts:**
To further enhance motion distillation by filtering out appearance and background-related noise, VMC employs "appearance-invariant prompts" during the training phase. Unlike traditional customization methods that use highly descriptive or "faithful" text prompts, VMC uses simplified, "unfaithful" prompts. For example, a prompt like "A cat is roaring on the grass under the tree" would be reduced to "A cat is roaring." This encourages the temporal attention layers to focus solely on the motion information, ignoring contextual or appearance details that could interfere with the motion learning process.

**Inference Pipeline:**
After fine-tuning, the inference process unfolds in a cascaded manner:
1. **Latent Inversion:** The input video is first inverted into latents using DDIM inversion.
2. **Keyframe Generation:** These inverted latents are fed into the temporally fine-tuned keyframe generation model, which produces short, low-resolution keyframes.
3. **Temporal Interpolation:** The generated keyframes undergo temporal extension by the *frozen* frame interpolation model.
4. **Spatial Super-resolution:** Finally, the interpolated frames are spatially enlarged by the *frozen* spatial super-resolution model to achieve the target high resolution.

**Implementation Details:**
The framework utilizes the Show-1 VDM backbone with its publicly available pre-trained weights. Training is conducted efficiently on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPU, requiring 15GB of vRAM and completing within approximately 5 minutes for 400 training steps. Video generation (29 frames at 576x320 resolution) takes about 12 minutes during inference. The AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of 0.01 and a learning rate of 0.0001 is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VMC framework was evaluated against several contemporary baselines across a diverse dataset of 24 videos encompassing various motion types and contexts.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visual comparisons demonstrate that VMC exhibits superior performance in motion-driven customization compared to baselines such as VideoComposer (VC), Gen-1, Tune-A-Video (TAV), and Control-A-Video (CAV). The baselines often struggle with accurately adapting the input video's motion to new contexts, frequently failing to incorporate the specified background (e.g., "underwater" or "on the sand") or deviating from the target subject's shape (e.g., generating a "shark-shaped airplane"). In contrast, VMC successfully applies complex motions to new subjects and environments, including challenging compositional changes like transforming "Two sharks are moving" into "Two airplanes are moving in the sky," while maintaining the original motion pattern.

**Quantitative Results:**
Quantitative evaluation was performed using both automatic metrics (CLIP-based) and a user study.

- **Automatic Metrics:**
 - **Textual Alignment:** VMC achieved an average CLIP cosine similarity of 0.801 between the target prompt and generated frames, slightly outperforming VC (0.798) and significantly surpassing Gen-1 (0.780), TAV (0.758), and CAV (0.764).
 - **Temporal Consistency:** VMC demonstrated an average CLIP image feature cosine similarity of 0.959 among all pairs of video frames, indicating high frame consistency. This also surpassed VC (0.958), Gen-1 (0.957), TAV (0.947), and CAV (0.952).

- **User Study:** A survey involving 27 participants assessed four key aspects on a 1-5 rating scale:
 - **Motion Preservation:** VMC scored 4.42, significantly higher than VC (3.45), Gen-1 (3.46), TAV (3.50), and CAV (2.75).
 - **Appearance Diversity:** VMC achieved a score of 4.54, again outperforming VC (3.43), Gen-1 (3.17), TAV (2.88), and CAV (2.45).
 - **Text Alignment:** VMC received a score of 4.56, surpassing VC (2.96), Gen-1 (2.87), TAV (2.67), and CAV (2.07).
 - **Temporal Consistency:** VMC scored 4.57, exceeding VC (3.03), Gen-1 (2.73), TAV (2.80), and CAV (2.00).

These results indicate that VMC significantly outperforms the baselines across all evaluated quantitative metrics, both automatic and human-perceptual.

**Ablation Studies:**
The paper included ablation studies to evaluate specific components of the VMC framework.

- **Comparison of Attention Layers for Fine-tuning:** A comparison between fine-tuning only temporal attention layers (VMC's approach) and fine-tuning self- and cross-attention layers revealed that both approaches can learn motion effectively. However, fine-tuning temporal attention layers resulted in smoother frame transitions, suggesting its effectiveness in motion distillation. This supports the hypothesis that motion information is specifically and accurately embedded within these layers when the proposed objective is applied.
- **Choice of Loss Functions:** The study compared the `ℓ_2` distance and `ℓ_cos` loss functions for the `δϵ`-matching objective. Both demonstrated compatibility with the objective, but `ℓ_cos` showed marginal superiority, leading to its adoption in the main experiments.
- **Importance of Temporal Attention Adaptation:** An ablation where temporal attention adaptation was omitted (i.e., U-Net modules remained frozen during training) demonstrated that motion distillation failed. While DDIM inversion could guide generations to mimic input motion to some extent, it led to entangled changes in appearance and motion. This confirmed the necessity of the explicit motion distillation objective and temporal attention adaptation for achieving consistent and appearance-independent motion transfer.

**Additional Results:**
- **Video Style Transfer:** VMC demonstrated the ability to perform video style transfer by incorporating style prompts after applying appearance-invariant prompting. The framework successfully injected target styles while preserving the distilled motion from an input video.
- **Learning Backward Motion:** VMC was tested on a challenging scenario involving learning "backward motion" from reversed video sequences, a motion highly improbable in standard training datasets. The framework successfully learned and generated diverse videos with subjects performing "a bird walking backward" motion, showcasing its proficiency in distilling and leveraging unusual motion patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VMC framework represents a significant contribution to the field of Text-to-Video generation and customization. Its core significance lies in addressing the limitations of existing methods concerning motion-specific video generation and computational efficiency.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Effective Motion Disentanglement:** VMC successfully disentangles motion patterns from subject appearance and background context. This is achieved through a novel motion distillation objective that leverages residual vectors between consecutive latent frames and the use of appearance-invariant prompts. This disentanglement is crucial for flexible content creation, as it allows users to port a specific motion to diverse subjects and environments without visual entanglement.
2. **High-Quality Motion Customization:** The framework demonstrates a robust ability to accurately reproduce intricate motion patterns from a single input video and apply them to newly generated videos with varied visual attributes and contexts. This includes challenging compositional customizations and even uncommon motions like "backward walking," which are unlikely to be present in large-scale training datasets.
3. **Computational Efficiency:** By focusing the fine-tuning process exclusively on the temporal attention layers within the key-frame generation module and keeping other parts of the cascaded VDM frozen, VMC achieves significantly reduced training time (under 5 minutes) and lower memory requirements (15GB vRAM). This efficiency makes motion customization more accessible and practical for researchers and developers with limited computational resources.
4. **Pioneering Targeted Fine-tuning:** VMC introduces and validates a pioneering approach of solely fine-tuning temporal attention layers in VDMs for successful motion customization, without needing to optimize spatial self or cross-attention layers. This suggests a refined understanding of how motion information is processed and encoded within diffusion models.
5. **Enhanced Generative Control:** The ability to distill and apply specific motion patterns provides a higher level of control over T2V generation. This expands the creative possibilities for video content creation, animation, special effects, and virtual reality applications, where precise motion control is often desired.

**Potential Broader Impact:**

- **Democratization of Video Production:** By simplifying and expediting the process of customizing video motion, VMC could lower the barrier to entry for generating high-quality animated content, enabling individuals and small teams to create sophisticated videos without extensive animation expertise.
- **Research in Motion Understanding:** The findings regarding the effectiveness of targeting temporal attention layers for motion distillation could inform future research into how video diffusion models represent and process motion, potentially leading to more advanced and controllable video synthesis architectures.
- **Ethical Considerations:** As with any advanced generative AI, the capabilities of VMC carry ethical implications. The authors acknowledge the potential for misuse, such as the creation of deceptive content, and the possibility of biases from foundational training datasets. Continued vigilance and development of ethical guidelines will be necessary as such technologies become more widespread.

In summary, VMC contributes a practical, efficient, and effective method for motion customization in text-to-video diffusion models, addressing key challenges and opening new avenues for controllable and diverse video generation.
