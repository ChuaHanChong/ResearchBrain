# cuRoboV2: Dynamics-Aware Motion Generation with Depth-Fused Distance Fields for High-DoF Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA's cuRoboV2 framework enables dynamics-aware motion generation for high-DoF robots, unifying planning, perception, and control components on the GPU. It achieved a 99.7% success rate under 3kg payload, offered 7-10x faster ESDF generation than baselines, and delivered higher-quality reference motions for humanoid locomotion policy training.

## Problem

- Motion planners frequently generate kinematically valid but physically unexecutable trajectories by neglecting robot dynamics and torque limits, especially with non-convex collision constraints.
- Reactive control struggles to balance rapid, high-fidelity visual perception with strict safety guarantees in dynamic environments, with current methods being either too slow or lacking robustness.
- Existing motion generation and control methods often fail to scale effectively to high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) robots like humanoids, facing computational and convergence challenges.

## Method

- Employs a B-spline trajectory optimization formulation, where control points inherently generate C2-continuous paths, facilitating enforcement of torque limits and handling of static or non-static boundary conditions.
- Develops a GPU-native perception pipeline that fuses diverse sensor inputs into a block-sparse TSDF and generates a dense ESDF on demand, offering millimeter-resolution and full workspace coverage with high speed and memory efficiency.
- Implements scalable, GPU-native computational components for high-DoF robots, including adaptive kinematics, a map-reduce strategy for self-collision, and differentiable inverse dynamics for torque-limited planning.

## Results

- Achieved a **99.7%** success rate for dynamics-aware motion planning with a 3kg payload on a Franka Panda, significantly outperforming baselines (**71.5%****-77.1%**) while generating C2-continuous, lower-energy trajectories.
- Demonstrated 7-**10x** speedup and 7-**8x** less GPU memory usage for ESDF generation compared to nvblox, producing millimeter-resolution, full-workspace distance fields in **1.69** ms with high collision recall.
- Enabled **99.6%** success for self-collision-free inverse kinematics on a 48-DoF humanoid (versus **0%** for baselines) and improved humanoid motion retargeting, yielding superior locomotion policies with lower error and higher alive rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing joint trajectories as B-splines simplifies enforcing C2 continuity and torque limits, making trajectories inherently smoother and dynamically feasible within optimization.
> - A block-sparse TSDF paired with on-demand ESDF generation effectively provides millimeter-resolution, full-workspace distance fields for collision avoidance with high speed and low memory usage.
> - Scaling motion generation to high-DoF robots requires GPU-native, optimized components for kinematics, self-collision (using map-reduce), and differentiable inverse dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Effective robot autonomy necessitates motion generation capabilities that are safe, feasible, and reactive within dynamic environments. Existing research in this domain is often compartmentalized, leading to several limitations that cuRoboV2 aims to address.

In **global motion planning**, conventional fast planners frequently disregard robot dynamics, producing trajectories that are physically unexecutable due to unmet torque limits. While dynamic trajectory optimization methods respect these limits, they typically struggle to scale to the non-convex constraints arising from mesh or depth-based collision avoidance. Previous B-spline formulations for manipulators were restricted to convex obstacles and did not integrate depth-image-based collision or torque limits.

**Reactive motion generation** faces a trade-off between safety and high-fidelity perception. Model-based approaches (e.g., Riemannian motion policies, MPC) offer safety guarantees but are often too slow to process raw depth data, confining them to simplified geometric primitives. Conversely, learning-based methods can process visual observations rapidly but lack the strict collision guarantees and generalization capabilities required for safe deployment and new environments. Existing GPU-accelerated methods like `cuRobo` and `PyRoki` primarily target single-arm robots, exhibiting performance degradation and convergence issues when scaled to higher degrees of freedom (DoF).

For **visual representations**, traditional methods like KinectFusion generate TSDFs but lack direct collision queries. GPU-accelerated libraries such as `nvblox` provide ESDFs for gradient-based planning but compute distances only within sparsely allocated blocks, leading to incomplete workspace coverage and high memory overhead at fine resolutions. CPU-based methods are not suitable for real-time applications.

In **human-to-humanoid motion retargeting**, transferring human motion to humanoids is crucial for locomotion policy training. Current optimization-based frameworks (`GMR`, `mink`, `PyRoki`) often produce motions containing self-penetrations, joint-limit violations, or fail to converge on high-DoF robots. These infeasible reference motions can destabilize downstream policy training.

cuRoboV2 addresses these fragmentation points by proposing a unified framework that integrates dynamics-aware planning, high-fidelity perception, and scalable computation for high-DoF robots, thereby aiming to bridge the identified gaps in feasibility, perception-reactivity, and scalability.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for cuRoboV2 stems from three fundamental barriers identified in achieving unified, high-performance robot autonomy:

1. **The Feasibility Gap:** Motion planners often prioritize speed, resulting in piecewise-linear joint paths that neglect robot dynamics and torque limits, particularly under heavy payloads. This leads to physically unexecutable motions, necessitating post-processing that can compromise safety guarantees. Conversely, existing dynamic trajectory optimization methods, while respecting limits, encounter difficulties in scaling to the non-convex constraints of modern collision avoidance representations. The objective is to develop a method that inherently generates dynamically feasible, smooth trajectories.

2. **The Perception-Reactivity Trade-off:** Reactive control in dynamic environments requires rapid processing of visual data while maintaining safety guarantees. Current analytic controllers are typically too slow for raw depth data, confining them to simplified geometric primitives. Learning-based approaches, while faster for visual input, generally do not offer strict collision guarantees or generalize well to novel environments, hindering safe deployment. The objective is to enable real-time, high-fidelity perception with strict collision guarantees across diverse inputs.

3. **The Scalability Wall:** Methods effective for single-arm robotic platforms frequently prove inadequate for high-DoF systems, such as bimanual manipulators or humanoids. These higher-dimensional spaces present challenges for state-of-the-art planners, particularly in achieving collision-free inverse kinematics (IK) in cluttered environments. The objective is to develop computational building blocks that scale efficiently to complex, high-DoF robots.

To overcome these barriers, cuRoboV2 sets forth three core algorithmic objectives:
1. **Introduce a B-Spline Optimization Formulation:** This aims to inherently enforce smoothness and enable the solver to satisfy torque limits for global planning and manage non-static boundary conditions for reactive control, providing a unified motion representation.
2. **Develop a GPU-native Perception Pipeline:** The goal is to fuse diverse sensor inputs (depth, meshes, cuboids) into a millimeter-resolution block-sparse TSDF and, on demand, generate a dense ESDF covering the full workspace. This is designed to be significantly faster and more memory-efficient than existing methods, offering high collision recall and enabling millisecond-rate updates for reactive control.
3. **Implement Scalable Kinematics, Dynamics & Self-Collision:** This objective involves creating GPU-native components, including topology-aware kinematics, differentiable inverse dynamics, and map-reduce self-collision. These are engineered to scale effectively to high-DoF humanoids, accelerate optimization iterations, and ensure convergence while enforcing torque limits.

The overarching motivation is to deliver a unified, dynamics-aware motion generation framework that functions across a range of robot complexities, from single-arm manipulators to full humanoids, and across various tasks including planning, reactive control, and motion retargeting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

cuRoboV2 integrates three primary algorithmic innovations: B-spline trajectory optimization, a GPU-native perception pipeline for distance fields, and scalable whole-body computation for high-DoF robots.

**B-Spline Basis as Optimization Space:**
The framework models joint trajectories as uniform cubic B-splines, with control points serving as optimization variables. This parameterization inherently yields C2-continuous trajectories, promoting smoothness, and its local support property ensures that modifications to one knot affect only a limited number of neighboring segments. The state vector, including joint angles, velocities, accelerations, and jerk, is computed using a matrix polynomial that scales with time. Gradients with respect to control points are calculated efficiently via a highly parallel GPU reduction, summing contributions from relevant interpolation points. The B-spline formulation implicitly handles boundary conditions: static goals are satisfied by repeating the final knot, and non-static initial states are accommodated by introducing and computing fictitious knots.

**ESDF for Scene Collision Avoidance:**
The perception pipeline employs a two-stage approach:
1. **Block-Sparse TSDF Storage:** Millimeter-resolution geometry over meter-scale environments is managed using a block-sparse TSDF. The volume is divided into 8³-voxel blocks, with only blocks near observed surfaces allocated. A hash table, using Compare-And-Swap (CAS) for concurrent insertions, maps block coordinates to pooled indices. Each voxel stores two independent float16 signed-distance channels: one for depth observations and another for analytic primitives. The minimum of these is used for collision queries, contributing to memory efficiency (e.g., 11x less than an equivalent dense grid).
2. **Depth and Primitive Integration:** Depth images are integrated using a "Voxel-Project" strategy. This involves four phases: (1) per-pixel rays discover affected voxel blocks; (2) unique block keys are deduplicated; (3) blocks are allocated via CAS; and (4) in the voxel-centric integration, each voxel projects itself into the depth image, reads the corresponding depth, and writes its signed distance and weight without requiring atomic operations, ensuring coalesced memory writes. Cuboids and meshes are analytically stamped into the geometry channel. Frustum-aware decay, a two-tier multiplicative weight decay, applies to blocks (time decay universally, frustum decay additionally for in-frustum blocks), with low-weight blocks being recycled.
3. **On-Demand ESDF with Sign Recovery:** A dense ESDF is generated at a task-specific, often coarser, resolution from the TSDF for O(1) trilinear distance queries. This process involves three stages:
- **Site Seeding:** Surface sites (voxels near zero-crossings in the sparse TSDF) are identified using a "gather" strategy, where each ESDF cell probes the TSDF with a 7-point stencil. This avoids atomics and is compatible with CUDA graph capture, improving real-time latency.
- **Distance Propagation:** The Parallel Banding Algorithm (PBA+) computes exact nearest-site assignments. It decomposes the 3D Voronoi diagram into three independent axis-aligned passes, utilizing Maurer's parabola intersection test for efficient distance propagation. This fixed-kernel-count approach is CUDA-graph compatible.
- **Sign Recovery:** For voxels beyond the TSDF truncation band, sign is recovered by sampling an adjacent voxel in the static geometry channel, as depth-fused surfaces are open and their sign is only reliable near the surface.

**Scaling to High-DoF Robots:**
To manage complexity and computational demands of high-DoF systems, cuRoboV2 introduces:
1. **Kinematics Improvements:** An adaptive kernel dispatch for forward kinematics automatically switches between a fused single-kernel approach for simple robots (≤100 collision spheres) and a two-kernel split for complex robots (>100 spheres). The latter computes frame transforms in the first kernel and then sphere positions, tool poses, centers of mass, and Jacobians in parallel in the second. Parallel gradient backpropagation exploits kinematic sparsity using a precomputed topology cache for O(1) ancestor lookups, handling mimic joints implicitly. Sparse Jacobian computation for the Levenberg-Marquardt (LM) solver uses a two-stage filtering process (coarse precomputed check and fine ancestor check) to efficiently identify relevant kinematic chains. Collision-free IK combines LM for initial pose convergence and L-BFGS for refinement under collision constraints.
2. **Self-Collision via Map-Reduce:** Addressing the quadratic growth of collision pairs in high-DoF robots, a two-stage map-reduce strategy is employed. The first stage partitions pairs across GPU blocks, each identifying its most-penetrating pair. The second stage reduces these local maxima to find the global maximum, converting a memory-bound problem into a compute-bound one.
3. **Torque Limits via Differentiable Inverse Dynamics:** Torque limits are enforced directly within the optimization loop using a differentiable Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA). This implementation features robot-generic GPU kernels (supporting any URDF at runtime), compact spatial representations (factored Featherstone form, 12 floats vs. 72), a Vector-Jacobian Product (VJP)-based backward pass for O(N) gradient computation (avoiding O(N²) Jacobian materialization), and tree-level parallelism with warp-level synchronization to optimize GPU utilization.

This comprehensive approach allows cuRoboV2 to address multiple challenges within a unified, GPU-native framework, enabling dynamics-aware motion generation for complex robotic systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Dynamics-Aware Planning:**
On MotionBenchMaker and MπNets datasets (2600 Franka Panda problems), cuRoboV2 achieved 99.7% success under a 3 kg payload, significantly outperforming baselines which ranged from 71.5% to 77.1%. Even without payload, baselines (e.g., cuRobo) saw a drop from 99.8% kinematic success to 97.9% when dynamics were considered, while cuRoboV2 maintained 99.7%. The B-spline formulation also resulted in the lowest energy consumption (106 J vs. 116–160 J for baselines) and inherently produced C2-continuous (smooth) trajectories, contrasting with the non-smooth acceleration and jerk profiles of per-timestep optimization methods. The planning time for dynamics-aware motions was 48 ms.

**Runtime Breakdown:**
Enabling dynamics constraints added 9.38 ms to kernel execution time (+44%) and 7 ms to the end-to-end wall-clock planning time (from 35 ms to 42 ms). The Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA) kernels accounted for 29% of the full pipeline kernel time when dynamics were enabled, adding 31.6 µs to the per-iteration cost of trajectory optimization. This allowed for real-time motion generation at over 30 Hz.

**Inverse Dynamics Comparison:**
Benchmarking against `GRiD` and `Newton` on 7-DoF, 12-DoF, and 48-DoF robots showed that cuRoboV2 was 14-18x faster than `Newton` across configurations (e.g., 29.3 µs vs. 400.0 µs for a 7-DoF arm at batch size 256). Notably, `GRiD` (code-generated kernels) failed entirely on the 48-DoF humanoid due to shared memory limitations, whereas cuRoboV2 scaled effectively (96.2 µs), demonstrating its capability for high-DoF systems.

**High-DoF Inverse Kinematics:**
cuRoboV2 demonstrated significant speedups in kinematics and self-collision computation. Without self-collision, it was 3-5x faster than `cuRobo`, 16-19x faster than `Newton`, and 62-86x faster than `PyRoki` (batch size 1000). With self-collision enabled, `cuRoboV2` achieved a 61x speedup over `cuRobo` and 42x over `PyRoki` on a 48-DoF humanoid (162k collision pairs).
For standard IK (without collision avoidance), `cuRoboV2` achieved 100% success on the 48-DoF humanoid with 0.96 µm position error in 34 ms, while `PyRoki` achieved 49.8% success with 18 µm error in 1123 ms. For self-collision-free IK, `cuRoboV2` reached 99.6% success on the humanoid (2.4 µm error, 533 ms), whereas both `cuRobo` and `PyRoki` exhibited 0% success. This performance was attributed to `cuRoboV2`'s two-stage LM-L-BFGS optimization strategy.

**Humanoid Retargeting:**
In motion retargeting 70k frames of human motion to a Unitree G1 humanoid, `cuRoboV2-IK` achieved 89.5% constraint satisfaction (joint limits, self-collision, ground contact), surpassing `PyRoki` (61.2%), `mink` (54.5%), and `GMR` (40.6%). Visually, `cuRoboV2` resolved complex self-collisions (e.g., hands crossing during crawling) gracefully, where `GMR` produced penetrations and `PyRoki` failed near contact.
In subsequent locomotion policy training, `cuRoboV2-IK` yielded superior policies. For running motions, `PyRoki`'s policies suffered from high resets and low episode lengths (31%) due to constraint violations. For crawling, where self-collision is critical, `cuRoboV2` policies achieved the lowest Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of 151.4 ± 4.5 mm, exhibiting 12x lower cross-seed variance than `GMR` (178.0 ± 58.3 mm), whose reference motions contained interpenetrations. Overall, `cuRoboV2` produced policies with the lowest MPJPE (145.5 mm), highest alive rate (99.6%), and fewest resets (1.1).

**Fast and Safe ESDF at mm-Resolution:**
cuRoboV2's ESDF generation pipeline demonstrated 7-10x speedup and 7-8x less GPU memory usage compared to `nvblox` across various TSDF resolutions and workspace coverages. For instance, at 10 mm TSDF resolution with full workspace coverage, `cuRoboV2` generated a complete ESDF in 1.69 ms using 1.63 GB of memory, versus `nvblox`'s 12.68 ms and 11.87 GB, while maintaining comparable or higher collision recall (92.5% vs. 92.0%). The speedup primarily originated from the PBA+ distance propagation, which was 10x faster than `nvblox`'s iterative updates. `cuRoboV2` also supported heterogeneous TSDF/ESDF resolutions and generated dense ESDFs covering the full workspace, unlike `nvblox` which left unobserved regions blank. Integrating known geometry via ESDF reduced mean motion planning time by 19% (47 ms to 38 ms).

**Real-World Manipulation:**
The framework was successfully deployed on an I2RT YAM robot with a ZED Mini stereo camera. The full pipeline—depth integration, ESDF generation, and MPC trajectory optimization—operated within a single control loop, demonstrating real-time collision avoidance with live ESDF updates against dynamically observed obstacles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in cuRoboV2 offers several significant advancements with potential impacts on the field of robotics:

1. **Unified and Feasible Motion Generation:** By integrating B-spline trajectory optimization with dynamics-aware constraints and efficient collision checking, cuRoboV2 provides a unified framework capable of generating motions that are not only collision-free but also physically executable. This addresses a common limitation of many planning systems where kinematically valid paths often violate torque limits in real-world scenarios. The high success rates under payload demonstrate a practical advancement in robot autonomy.

2. **Scalability to High-DoF Systems:** The innovations in scalable kinematics, differentiable inverse dynamics, and map-reduce self-collision computation enable high-performance motion generation for complex, high-DoF robots such as bimanual manipulators and humanoids. This extends the applicability of advanced motion planning and control techniques to a wider range of robotic platforms where previous GPU implementations often failed or performed poorly. This is particularly impactful for tasks like humanoid locomotion and complex manipulation.

3. **Real-time High-Fidelity Perception:** The GPU-native TSDF/ESDF perception pipeline, with its substantial improvements in speed (up to 10x faster) and memory efficiency (up to 8x less) compared to existing state-of-the-art solutions like `nvblox`, enables millimeter-resolution, full-workspace distance queries in real-time. This capability is critical for reactive control and safe interaction in dynamic and unstructured environments, moving beyond reliance on simplified geometric primitives.

4. **Improved Downstream Policy Learning:** The demonstration of `cuRoboV2-IK` producing higher-quality, collision-free, and joint-limit-satisfying reference motions for humanoid retargeting has a direct positive impact on reinforcement learning-based locomotion policy training. Policies trained with `cuRoboV2` generated more accurate motions with significantly lower cross-seed variance and fewer resets, indicating more robust and reliable learning outcomes. This suggests that the quality of reference motion data directly influences the trainability and performance of learned robot behaviors.

5. **Validation of LLM-Assisted Development:** The paper quantifies the substantial contribution of LLM coding assistants (up to 73% of new modules authored in later phases) in developing complex, performance-critical modules, including CUDA kernels. This demonstrates a practical pathway for human-LLM collaboration in scientific software engineering, particularly when supported by well-structured, discoverable codebases following clear software engineering principles (e.g., typed interfaces, predictable naming, small modules, executable tests). This could accelerate the development cycle of advanced robotics software.

Overall, cuRoboV2 represents a comprehensive effort to bridge the gaps in feasibility, reactivity, and scalability in robot motion generation. Its advancements in GPU-native computation, dynamics-aware planning, high-fidelity perception, and demonstration of LLM-assisted development contribute to making robust and intelligent robot autonomy more achievable across a diverse range of robotic systems and tasks.
