# Kimina-Prover Preview: Towards Large Formal Reasoning Models with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A large language model-based theorem prover achieves 80.7% accuracy on the miniF2F formal mathematics benchmark through a novel combination of autoformalization and reinforcement learning, demonstrating clear performance scaling with model size and effective bridging between informal mathematical intuition and formal verification.

## Problem

- Traditional neural theorem provers rely on computationally expensive search algorithms that limit scalability
- Previous approaches show no clear performance improvements with increased model size
- Existing methods struggle to bridge the gap between informal mathematical reasoning and formal proofs

## Method

- **Kimina-Prover** develops a ==reasoning-driven exploration paradigm== that leverages the LLM's internal ==long chain-of-thought== reasoning capabilities instead of explicit search
- Combined ==autoformalization==, ==supervised fine-tuning==, and ==reinforcement learning== with a ==formal reasoning pattern== that interleaves informal thinking and Lean 4 proof steps
- Trained on ==Qwen2.5-72B== with a carefully controlled learning rate and ==KL divergence coefficient== for RL stability

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art **80.7% accuracy** on the miniF2F benchmark with **pass@8192**, open-sourcing distilled versions with **1.5B and 7B parameters**
- Performance scales effectively with computational budget at test time
- Successfully bridged the gap between informal and formal mathematics through a unified reasoning approach

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model demonstrates clear performance scaling with size, previously unobserved in formal mathematics
> - High sample efficiency achieved even with minimal sampling (pass@1)
> - Learned reasoning style bridges formal verification and informal mathematical intuition

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses the burgeoning field of neural theorem proving, which aims to automate or augment the process of proving mathematical theorems using machine learning techniques. It stands at the intersection of several key trends:

- **Large Language Models (LLMs) for Reasoning:** The paper acknowledges the increasing use of LLMs in formal reasoning tasks. The trend of using LLMs to generate individual proof steps or entire proof structures is a focus.
- **Formal Theorem Proving:** The work situates itself within the context of formal theorem proving using interactive proof assistants like Lean 4, highlighting the challenges of automating proof generation within these systems.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs:** The paper explicitly builds upon the recent success of RL in enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs.
- **Bridging Formal and Informal Reasoning:** The Kimina-Prover aims to integrate formal verification with informal mathematical intuition.

The paper distinguishes itself from previous work by:

- **Reasoning-Driven Exploration:** Departing from traditional search algorithms, the research pioneers the application of large-scale reinforcement learning to elicit long chain-of-thought reasoning in theorem proving.
- **Model Scaling in Formal Mathematics:** The research addresses the lack of observed improvements in performance corresponding to increases in model size.
- **Long-Form Reasoning:** The model is specifically optimized for long-form reasoning via reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the research is to develop a large language model capable of effectively performing formal theorem proving in the Lean 4 proof assistant. The motivation stems from several key challenges and limitations of existing approaches:

- **Computational Overhead of Search Algorithms:** Traditional neural theorem provers often rely on explicit search algorithms like BFS or MCTS, which introduce significant computational complexity and limit scalability.
- **Difficulty in Capturing Structured Reasoning:** Existing methods struggle to capture the deep, structured, and often non-linear reasoning required for complex formal proofs.
- **Lack of Model Scaling:** Previous neural theorem provers tailored for formal mathematics have generally not demonstrated clear improvements in performance corresponding to increases in model size.
- **Gap Between Formal and Informal Reasoning:** Transferring knowledge from general mathematical problem-solving to formal mathematical reasoning remains a challenging task.

The authors aim to overcome these limitations by developing a novel reasoning-driven exploration paradigm that leverages the LLM's own internal reasoning capabilities, enhanced through large-scale RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-stage methodology, combining autoformalization, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL) with a specific formal reasoning pattern:

- **Autoformalization:** To create a large and diverse training dataset, the authors train a model to automatically translate natural language problem statements into syntactically valid Lean 4 code.
- **Formal Reasoning Pattern:** This approach is used to allow the model to "think" in an environment that aligns with both informal and formal mathematical reasoning. Responses are formatted with special tokens that help organize the model's thoughts and final proof.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A minimal supervised fine-tuning run is performed to kickstart the model's ability to output formal proofs following the defined reasoning pattern. They incorporate informal mathematical thinking data from Kimi k1.5 into the SFT training.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The model is further trained using reinforcement learning to enhance its formal theorem-proving capabilities. The RL process iteratively refines the model's policy using a binary reward signal based on the correctness of generated proofs.
- **Training Details:** The RL training is conducted starting from Qwen2.5-72B, with a constant learning rate and a fixed KL divergence coefficient to ensure stability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key findings and results:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Kimina-Prover Preview achieves state-of-the-art performance on the miniF2F benchmark, reaching 80.7% with pass@8192, significantly surpassing prior state-of-the-art results.
- **Sample Efficiency:** Kimina-Prover demonstrates high sample efficiency, delivering strong results even with minimal sampling (pass@1) and scaling effectively with computational budget.
- **Model Scaling:** The research demonstrates clear performance scaling with model size, a trend previously unobserved for neural theorem provers in formal mathematics.
- **Emergent Reasoning Style:** The learned reasoning style, distinct from traditional search algorithms, shows potential to bridge the gap between formal verification and informal mathematical intuition.
- **Gap between informal Mathematics and Formal Mathematics:** The model shows potential in bridging this gap.
- **Test Time Scaling in Formal Reasoning:** The model demonstrated ability to improve with increased test-time budget.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research has significant implications for the field of automated theorem proving and the broader AI landscape:

- **Advancement of Automated Theorem Proving:** The development of Kimina-Prover represents a significant step towards more effective and scalable automated theorem proving systems.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities of LLMs:** The research demonstrates the potential of RL to elicit long chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs, enabling them to tackle complex formal reasoning tasks.
- **Bridging the Gap Between Formal and Informal Reasoning:** The learned reasoning style of Kimina-Prover has the potential to bridge the gap between formal verification and informal mathematical intuition, opening up new avenues for AI-assisted mathematical discovery.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The reasoning-driven exploration paradigm and RL-based training methodology developed in this research could be applicable to other domains that require complex reasoning and problem-solving, such as software verification, code synthesis, and scientific discovery.
- **Community Contribution:** The authors are open-sourcing distilled versions of Kimina-Prover with 1.5B and 7B parameters.

In summary, this research presents a novel and promising approach to automated theorem proving, leveraging the power of LLMs and reinforcement learning to achieve state-of-the-art performance and demonstrate the potential for more human-like reasoning in AI systems.
