# Count Counts: Motivating Exploration in LLM Reasoning with Count-based Intrinsic Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> MERCI introduces an RL algorithm that uses count-based intrinsic rewards to improve exploration in Large Language Models for complex reasoning tasks. It consistently outperforms existing baselines in mathematical reasoning and SQL generation, achieving up to 2.1% higher pass@k on difficult math problems and 1.3% higher pass@16 for out-of-domain SQL generation.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) trained with Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggle with sparse, outcome-based rewards in multi-step reasoning tasks, leading to repetitive or suboptimal solutions.
- Current 'shallow' exploration methods are inefficient for discovering long, precise reasoning paths, while existing 'deep exploration' techniques are not scalable to the vast state spaces of modern LLMs.
- The absence of directed exploration mechanisms prevents LLMs from effectively discovering optimal reasoning trajectories.

## Method

- **MERCI** augments LLM policy optimization with a principled intrinsic reward derived from a simplified Uncertainty Bellman Equation (UBE), leveraging the deterministic nature of LLM transitions.
- A lightweight Coin Flipping Network (CFN) is employed to estimate local reward uncertainty, serving as a scalable proxy for state novelty or pseudo-counts.
- The intrinsic reward is integrated into existing policy optimization frameworks with budget-aware filtering (percentile, spatial coherence, noise-suppression) to guide exploration effectively and stably.

## Results

- MERCI consistently improved performance over vanilla GRPO and DAPO baselines across mathematical reasoning (AIME, Minerva, MATH500) and SQL generation (Bird, Spider) benchmarks.
- DAPO w/ MERCI achieved an average pass@k of **69.0%** (vs. DAPO's **66.9%**) and mean@k of **44.9%** (vs. DAPO's **42.2%**) on mathematical reasoning, demonstrating better sample quality.
- Performance on out-of-domain SQL generation (Spider) also improved, with DAPO w/ MERCI reaching **88.5%** pass@16 compared to DAPO's **87.2%**, indicating enhanced generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM reasoning, where subsequent tokens are deterministically appended, can be modeled as an MDP with known and deterministic transitions, significantly simplifying the Uncertainty Bellman Equation for uncertainty estimation.
> - Local reward uncertainty, critical for intrinsic exploration, can be efficiently estimated using a lightweight Coin Flipping Network (CFN) as a scalable proxy for state novelty or pseudo-counts.
> - The cumulative intrinsic reward should be derived by summing local reward variances along a trajectory and then taking the square root, which is theoretically sound and superior to summing per-step standard deviations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. RL for LLM Reasoning: Challenges and Current State

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for augmenting the multi-step reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). By providing feedback on generated sequences, RL allows LLMs to learn complex behaviors such as competitive mathematics, code generation, and logical problem-solving, which are difficult to achieve with supervised learning alone. Foundational RL methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) have been adapted for LLMs, treating token positions as states for advantage estimation. More recently, advancements such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO) have refined this process by leveraging sequence-level reward normalization, eliminating the need for an explicit value function and streamlining training for long reasoning chains. These "value-free" methods improve training efficiency and local variability at the token level.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Existing Exploration Strategies

Despite these advances, a critical bottleneck in RL for LLMs remains the challenge of effective exploration, particularly in tasks characterized by sparse, outcome-based rewards. When feedback is only available at the end of a lengthy reasoning trajectory (e.g., getting a final answer correct), LLMs often struggle to discover optimal paths, instead converging on repetitive or suboptimal reasoning patterns.

Traditional "shallow" exploration methods, such as epsilon-greedy or Boltzmann exploration with entropy regularization, inject undirected noise to encourage policy diversity. While theoretically capable of visiting all states, they are notoriously inefficient for complex, long-horizon tasks, making the discovery of precise action sequences (like a correct mathematical derivation) vanishingly small.

"Deep exploration" strategies, founded on the principle of "optimism in the face of uncertainty," aim to provide more directed exploration by generating intrinsic rewards for novel or uncertain states. Canonical examples include pseudo-counts, Bootstrapped DQN, Random Network Distillation (RND), and the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM). However, these methods face significant scalability challenges when applied to the vast, high-dimensional state spaces of modern LLMs:
- **Deep Ensembles:** Prohibitively expensive, requiring multiple independent models.
- **Monte Carlo Dropout:** While cheaper, still introduces substantial inference overhead.
- **Pseudo-count techniques:** Often depend on normalized probability densities, precluding efficient batching.
- **Curiosity-driven methods:** Lack strong theoretical guarantees regarding the decay of exploration bonuses.
- **Uncertainty Bellman Equation (UBE) frameworks:** While principled, rely on estimating local uncertainty, which is traditionally difficult and often heuristic-driven for general MDPs.

MERCI directly addresses these limitations by providing a principled, scalable, and theoretically grounded approach to deep exploration for LLM reasoning. It stands out by simplifying the UBE framework under the unique assumption of deterministic LLM transitions, bridging the gap between model-aware RL theory and the typically model-free application of RL to LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **design an effective exploration strategy for LLM reasoning that can overcome the limitations of sparse outcome-based rewards and lead LLMs away from repetitive, suboptimal reasoning patterns.**

The motivation for MERCI stems from several critical observations and challenges:
- **Sparse Rewards in Complex Reasoning:** Many advanced LLM applications (e.g., math, code generation) provide feedback only upon task completion, making it difficult for standard RL to learn intermediate steps. This scarcity of signal leads to inefficient learning and poor exploration.
- **Limited and Undirected Exploration:** Current RL methods for LLMs often rely on local entropy regularization, which encourages local policy diversity but lacks "temporal consistency" or a "directed signal" across a full reasoning trajectory. This "shallow" exploration is insufficient for discovering long, precise solution paths.
- **Scalability Issues of Deep Exploration Methods:** While deep exploration is desirable, existing techniques for estimating epistemic uncertainty (e.g., deep ensembles, RND) do not scale well to the massive parameter counts and inference demands of modern LLMs. There is a fundamental mismatch between classical uncertainty quantification methods and the practical constraints of LLM training.
- **The Unique Nature of LLM Reasoning MDPs:** The authors identify a critical insight: in self-contained LLM reasoning tasks (like mathematical problem-solving), the LLM acts as its own world model with **known and deterministic transitions**. If an LLM is in state *s* (a token sequence) and selects action *a* (the next token), the subsequent state *s'* is deterministically *(s, a)*. This observation drastically simplifies the theoretical problem of uncertainty propagation.

By leveraging this unique property of LLM reasoning, MERCI aims to make principled, uncertainty-driven exploration tractable at scale, providing a "deep exploration" mechanism that is compatible with modern value-free RL frameworks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach of MERCI

MERCI (Motivating Exploration in LLM Reasoning with Count-based Intrinsic Rewards) is a novel RL algorithm that augments policy optimization with a principled intrinsic reward. Its methodology is built upon a theoretical simplification of the Uncertainty Bellman Equation (UBE) and a scalable pseudo-counting mechanism.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: The Uncertainty Bellman Equation with Known Transitions

The core theoretical contribution of MERCI lies in its simplification of the UBE, which traditionally quantifies the propagation of epistemic uncertainty (variance of Q-values) through an MDP. The critical insight is that for LLM reasoning, the underlying Markov Decision Process (MDP) has **known and deterministic transition dynamics**. This means that when an LLM generates a token (action) from a partial sequence (state), the next state is uniquely determined.
Formally, the general UBE accounts for uncertainty in both the reward function estimate ($\hat{r}$) and the transition function estimate ($\hat{P}$). However, with known and deterministic transitions, the epistemic uncertainty of $\hat{P}$ becomes zero. This dramatically simplifies the UBE to:

$U_h(s, a) \le V_t[\hat{r}_h(s)] + \sum_{s',a'} \pi_h_{s'a'} P_h_{s'sa'} U_{h+1}(s', a')$

Where $U_h(s, a)$ is the posterior variance of the Q-value, and $V_t[\hat{r}_h(s)]$ is the local reward uncertainty. This proposition states that the uncertainty of a state-action pair is bounded by the immediate reward uncertainty plus the expected uncertainty of the *unique subsequent state*. This elegant simplification reframes the intractable problem of estimating Q-value uncertainty into the more manageable task of estimating **local reward uncertainty**. The paper then posits that this local reward uncertainty is inversely proportional to the number of times a state has been visited ($V_t[\hat{r}_h(s)] \propto 1/N(s)$), linking it to count-based exploration.

#### 3.2. Estimating Reward Variance via Coin Flipping Network (CFN)

To practically estimate the local reward uncertainty (or state novelty), MERCI employs a lightweight **Coin Flipping Network (CFN)**. CFN is a computationally efficient method for pseudo-count-based exploration. It trains a neural network ($f_\phi$) to predict the average of random coin-flip vectors associated with each state. For each visit to a state $s_i$, a random vector $c_i \sim \{-1, 1\}^d$ is generated as a label. The CFN minimizes the mean-square error between its output and these random vectors. The key property is that the squared L2-norm of the CFN's output, scaled by $1/d$, approximates the inverse of the state's visitation count ($1/N(s)$), thereby providing an estimate for $V_t[\hat{r}_h(s)]$.
The CFN network is a separate instance of the LLM (initialized from the same Supervised Fine-Tuned checkpoint) with a small MLP head attached to its final hidden layer. It is trained in parallel with the policy network using its own supervised regression objective.

#### 3.3. Intrinsic Reward Calculation and Integration

A crucial aspect of MERCI is the **correct calculation of the intrinsic reward**. Drawing directly from Proposition 1, MERCI sums the *local reward variances* along a trajectory and then takes the square root of this sum to obtain the standard deviation of the cumulative Q-value posterior. This cumulative standard deviation serves as the intrinsic reward. The authors explicitly highlight that this is superior to the common but theoretically flawed heuristic of summing *per-step standard deviations*, which significantly overestimates uncertainty over long horizons.

This intrinsic reward is then integrated into existing policy optimization frameworks like GRPO or DAPO. The exploration bonus $B$ for a trajectory is computed by averaging the squared CFN outputs for the retained tokens and applying a square-root compression. This bonus is standardized within each group of trajectories and truncated to only positive values, preserving only exploratory incentives. Finally, this "exploration advantage" ($\hat{A}_{i}^{\text{exploration}}$) is scaled by an exploration coefficient $\gamma$ and added to the original outcome-based advantage ($\hat{A}_{i}^{\text{old}}$), with an additional clipping factor $\alpha$ to prevent the intrinsic term from overpowering the task reward.

#### 3.4. Budget-Aware Exploration Bonus Control

To prevent the intrinsic reward from introducing instability or promoting aimless exploration, MERCI incorporates three stages of "budget-aware exploration bonus control":
1. **Percentile Filtering:** Only a fixed fraction (e.g., top 30%) of tokens with the strongest bonus signals within each generated response are retained. This adapts to the gradual change in bonus magnitude over training.
2. **Spatial Coherence Filtering:** Isolated spikes in bonus values are discarded, retaining only clusters of adjacent tokens that consistently exhibit elevated bonuses. This ensures steadier and more meaningful updates.
3. **Noise-suppression Filtering:** Incentives attached to semantically irrelevant content (e.g., meaningless repetitions, gratuitous code blocks in a math problem) are removed. This step can be task-specific.

These filtering stages allocate a controlled exploration budget, ensuring that the intrinsic motivation guides exploration productively while preserving the primary task reward signal.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MERCI was evaluated on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME2024/2025, Minerva, MATH500, OlympiadBench, College Math) using a Qwen2.5-Math-7B backbone, and on SQL generation tasks (Bird, Spider) using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The results consistently demonstrate MERCI's effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Coin Flip Network Effectiveness

Initial evaluations confirmed that the CFN effectively estimates state novelty. Token sequences assigned higher uncertainty by the CFN predominantly corresponded to novel reasoning paths, Python code (often indicating exploration of computational tools), or specialized mathematical terminologies. This aligns with the hypothesis that novel tokens induce higher epistemic uncertainty, validating the CFN's role in identifying unexplored reasoning avenues.

#### 4.2. RL Training Performance (Mathematical Reasoning & SQL Generation)

**Mathematical Reasoning:**
- MERCI consistently improved performance over both vanilla GRPO and DAPO baselines across all mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- Gains were most significant on the highly challenging AIME suites, indicating MERCI's ability to facilitate exploration in difficult problem spaces.
- Improvements were observed in both `pass@k` (the proportion of problems solved by at least one of `k` samples) and `mean@k` (average score over `k` samples), suggesting better overall sample quality and enhanced exploration leading to more robust solutions. For instance, `DAPO w/ MERCI` achieved an average `pass@k` of 69.0% compared to `DAPO`'s 66.9%, and `mean@k` of 44.9% vs. 42.2%.

**SQL Generation:**
- Similar to mathematical reasoning, MERCI yielded performance gains on both in-domain (Bird) and out-of-domain (Spider) SQL generation tasks.
- Notably, MERCI showed larger improvements in out-of-domain generalization (Spider test set). This indicates that MERCI encourages LLMs to discover and utilize more generalizable SQL patterns, improving transferability to new database schemas. For `DAPO w/ MERCI`, `pass@16` on Spider increased to 88.5% from `DAPO`'s 87.2%.

**Qualitative Observations and Training Dynamics:**
- MERCI encourages richer and more varied chains of thought, effectively helping the policy escape local routines and discover better solutions.
- It improves sample quality, leading to uniform and stable gains across different metrics.
- The method prevents gratuitous chain elongation, concentrating probability mass on more diverse yet more reliable solutions expressed in shorter, more focused traces. This indicates more intelligent and efficient exploration, pruning task-irrelevant branches.
- Training dynamics showed that MERCI realized its exploratory potential earlier, leading to faster identification of good solutions and more stable improvements in `pass@k` compared to scaling vanilla GRPO.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Training Dynamics

Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of MERCI's design choices:
- **Bonus Filtering:** Both percentile and spatial coherence filtering were found to be essential. Without them, training became unstable and could even underperform the baseline. Noise-suppression filtering also contributed to robustness.
- **Correct Uncertainty Estimation:** The theoretically grounded approach of summing variances (and then taking the square root) for cumulative uncertainty yielded better performance than summing standard deviations, underscoring the importance of theoretical precision.
- **Trajectory-level Integration:** Integrating the bonus at the trajectory level, rather than token-level, was also crucial, indicating that a coherent, temporally consistent exploration signal is more effective for reasoning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MERCI algorithm represents a significant step forward in training LLMs for complex reasoning tasks, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Novel Theoretical Foundation for LLM Exploration:** MERCI provides the first derivation and application of a deep exploration algorithm for LLM reasoning directly from a principled simplification of the Uncertainty Bellman Equation. By recognizing the deterministic nature of LLM transitions, it transforms an intractable problem (Q-value uncertainty) into a manageable one (local reward uncertainty), bridging model-aware RL theory with practical LLM applications. This theoretical insight could inspire further research into the unique characteristics of LLM MDPs.
- **Practical and Scalable Exploration:** The use of the Coin Flipping Network (CFN) offers a computationally lightweight and theoretically grounded method for estimating state novelty and local reward uncertainty. CFN can be trained in parallel with the policy model, introducing minimal computational overhead, which is crucial for scaling to large LLMs. The budget-aware bonus control mechanisms (percentile, spatial coherence, noise-suppression filtering) further ensure the stability and efficacy of the intrinsic reward.
- **Enhanced Performance in Complex Reasoning:** MERCI demonstrates state-of-the-art performance improvements on a diverse set of challenging mathematical reasoning and SQL generation benchmarks. Its ability to encourage richer, more varied chains of thought helps LLMs escape local optima and discover more robust and accurate solutions. This is particularly impactful for tasks where sparse rewards make traditional RL challenging.
- **Mitigation of Premature Convergence and Repetitive Patterns:** By providing a directed exploration signal, MERCI effectively addresses the tendency of standard RL algorithms to converge on repetitive and suboptimal reasoning patterns. This leads to higher `pass@k` rates, indicating a greater diversity of successful solutions, and improved `mean@k` scores, suggesting better overall solution quality.
- **Improved Generalization:** The significant gains in out-of-domain SQL generation highlight MERCI's potential to foster more generalizable reasoning abilities, enabling LLMs to transfer learned patterns to novel problem structures more effectively.
- **Advancing RL for LLMs:** This work contributes to the growing body of research on making RL more robust, efficient, and theoretically sound for large-scale language models, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve in complex, multi-step reasoning. The framework could potentially be adapted or extended to other domains of LLM applications facing similar exploration challenges.

In conclusion, MERCI provides a well-founded and empirically strong solution to a critical problem in LLM reasoning, offering a pathway to develop more intelligent, adaptable, and performant language models for complex tasks.
