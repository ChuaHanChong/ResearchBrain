# Bring Reason to Vision: Understanding Perception and Reasoning through Model Merging

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from City University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, National University of Singapore, and Northwestern University enhances vision-language models' reasoning abilities by merging their language components with reasoning-specialized large language models, a training-free process that yielded up to a 3.6-point absolute improvement on MathVista and a 30% relative increase on MathVerse's Text-Dominant mode. It also leverages model merging as an interpretability tool to localize perception in early layers and reasoning in middle-to-late layers of VLMs.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often exhibit limitations in complex multimodal reasoning tasks, such as visual math problems, despite their strong perceptual abilities.
- The scarcity of high-quality multimodal reasoning data makes it challenging to train VLMs for advanced reasoning through traditional fine-tuning methods.
- A lack of understanding exists regarding where perception and reasoning capabilities are specifically encoded and interact within the architectural layers of VLMs.

## Method

- The authors propose a novel ==cross-modal model merging strategy== that combines the language model component of a VLM with task vectors from reasoning-specialized Large Language Models (LLMs).
- They utilize ==linear merging== (θ' = θ_base + λτ_vlm + (1-λ)τ_reason) as the primary training-free method, applied to VLMs like LLaVA-Next and InternVL2.
- An ==interpretability methodology==, involving 'masking out' specific VLM layers or modules with base model parameters or uniform noise, is used to analyze the internal localization of perception and reasoning.

## Results

- Model merging consistently improved VLM reasoning capabilities, achieving a **3.6-point** absolute improvement on MathVista's math subset and a **30%** relative increase (**6-point** absolute) on MathVerse's Text-Dominant tasks for LLaVA-Next-LLaMA3-8B.
- Merged models exhibited significantly longer and more detailed reasoning processes during inference, with prediction lengths for chain-of-thought intensive tasks sometimes exceeding **250%** of the original answer length.
- Interpretability analysis demonstrated that reasoning capabilities contribute across nearly all layers after merging, while perception abilities remained largely stable, confirming a targeted enhancement of reasoning with minimal disruption to perceptual foundations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning abilities from text-focused LLMs can be effectively transferred to VLMs through cross-modal model merging, providing a training-free and efficient method for capability enhancement.
> - Within VLMs, visual perception abilities and general world knowledge are primarily localized in the early layers, while mathematical and chain-of-thought reasoning skills are concentrated in the middle-to-late layers.
> - Model merging serves as an effective interpretability tool, revealing how different capabilities are distributed across a model's layers and how they are affected by the merging process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), and model merging techniques, addressing a critical challenge in multimodal AI: complex reasoning.

**Current State of VLMs and LLMs:** Vision-Language Models have achieved remarkable success in tasks requiring visual perception and linguistic integration, such as visual question answering (VQA) and image captioning. However, the paper highlights a significant limitation: VLMs often struggle with complex multimodal reasoning tasks, such as interpreting scientific charts or solving visual math problems. This is partly attributed to the scarcity of high-quality multimodal reasoning data and the inherent difficulty in combining distinct perceptual and reasoning abilities effectively. In contrast, Large Language Models have made extraordinary advancements in complex reasoning capabilities within the textual domain.

**Model Merging Landscape:** Model merging is an emerging "training-free" paradigm that involves combining parameters from multiple pre-trained or fine-tuned models to create a new model, often to achieve improved performance or multi-task capabilities without additional training. Previous work in model merging primarily focused on models of the "same kind" – for instance, merging different fine-tuned versions of an LLM or different vision models. These techniques often rely on the assumption that models fine-tuned from a shared initialization reside in a connected subspace of the parameter space, allowing for arithmetic operations on their task-specific parameter changes (task vectors).

**Novelty and Gap Addressed:** This paper distinguishes itself by proposing and thoroughly investigating **cross-modal model merging**. Specifically, it explores how to incorporate the sophisticated textual reasoning abilities of LLMs directly into VLMs by merging their respective language model components. This is a significant departure from prior art, as it challenges the assumption that merging is only effective for models of the same modality or architecture. The core innovation lies in bridging the "reasoning gap" of VLMs using a training-free method, thereby avoiding the high computational costs and data requirements of traditional fine-tuning.

Furthermore, beyond simply demonstrating performance improvements, the work leverages model merging as an **interpretability tool**. It delves into the internal mechanisms of VLMs to understand *where* perception and reasoning abilities are encoded within the model's layers and *how* merging affects this internal distribution. This interpretability aspect is crucial for advancing our fundamental understanding of multimodal AI systems, moving beyond black-box performance metrics.

The research also implicitly touches upon the limitations of current VLM training paradigms, suggesting that general image-text pair training might enhance perception at the expense of robust reasoning, thus motivating the need for targeted intervention like cross-modal merging.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhance Vision-Language Model Reasoning:** The central objective is to improve the ability of VLMs to perform complex multimodal reasoning tasks, particularly in mathematical and logical contexts. This is motivated by the observation that while VLMs excel at perception and basic language understanding, they lag significantly behind LLMs in advanced reasoning, and existing multimodal datasets for reasoning are scarce.
2. **Explore Cross-Modal Capability Transfer via Model Merging:** The authors aim to investigate whether the reasoning capabilities of LLMs can be effectively transferred to VLMs through model merging, a training-free approach. This addresses the unexplored potential of merging models *across different modalities*, challenging the conventional use of merging for same-modality models. The motivation here is to find an efficient, cost-effective way to imbue VLMs with advanced reasoning without extensive re-training.
3. **Understand the Internal Mechanisms of Perception and Reasoning in VLMs:** A crucial secondary objective is to use model merging as an interpretability tool to gain insights into how perception and reasoning abilities are encoded and interact within the layers of VLMs. Specifically, the authors seek to determine:
 - Where in the model's architecture (e.g., early vs. late layers, MLP vs. attention modules) are perception and reasoning capabilities predominantly located?
 - How does the process of merging influence the distribution of these abilities across the model's layers?
 - This motivation stems from the generally poor understanding of the internal workings of complex VLMs and the desire to disentangle distinct capabilities.
4. **Validate Model Merging as an Interpretability Tool:** Beyond just enhancing VLMs, the paper aims to demonstrate that model merging itself can serve as a powerful analytical tool for dissecting model capabilities and understanding their compositional nature.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the "reasoning gap" in VLMs efficiently and transparently, not only by achieving performance gains but also by shedding light on the underlying mechanisms that enable such multimodal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel methodology centered on cross-modal model merging, combined with an interpretability framework.

#### 3.1. Model Merging Strategy

The core of the approach involves merging the parameters of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with those of a specialized Large Language Model (LLM) that excels in reasoning.

- **Targeted Components:** A typical VLM consists of a vision tower (for visual processing), a language model (for reasoning and response generation), and a projector (to bridge visual and linguistic features). This work specifically targets the **language model component** of the VLM for merging, keeping the vision tower and projector unchanged. This ensures that the visual perception capabilities of the VLM are preserved while enhancing its reasoning engine.
- **Linear Merging:** For simplicity and robustness, the authors primarily adopt **linear merging**. This technique involves calculating "task vectors," which represent the changes made to a base model's parameters during fine-tuning for a specific task.
 - Given a base LLM (θ_base), a VLM (θ_vlm) fine-tuned from it, and a reasoning-specialized LLM (θ_reason) also fine-tuned from the same base, the task vectors are defined as:
 - τ_vlm = θ_vlm - θ_base (captures VLM-specific multimodal adaptations)
 - τ_reason = θ_reason - θ_base (captures reasoning enhancements)
 - The merged VLM (θ'_vlm) is then computed as:
 θ'_vlm = θ_base + λτ_vlm + (1 - λ)τ_reason
 where λ is a hyperparameter (tuned to 0.9 in main experiments) that balances the contribution of the original VLM's capabilities and the LLM's reasoning strength.
- **Other Merging Methods:** While linear merging is the primary method, the authors also experimented with other techniques like TIES merging, Dare merging (Dare-TIES, Dare-Linear), and Layer Swapping in an appendix, finding comparable performance, which supports the choice of linear merging for its simplicity.

#### 3.2. Experimental Setup

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) Used:**
 - LLaVA-Next-LLaMA3-8B (LLaMA-based, 8B parameters)
 - Idefics2-8B (Mistral-based, 8B parameters)
 - InternVL2-LLaMA3-76B (LLaMA-based, 76B parameters)
 - Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (in appendix)
 This selection spans different model sizes and base architectures to assess generalization.
- **Reasoning Task Vectors (Specialized LLMs) Used:**
 - Math-specific: Dart-Math (Uniform and Prop2diff variants), MAmmoTH-1, Magpie-v0.3, MetaMath, Deepseek-R1-Distill. These are LLMs fine-tuned on diverse mathematical reasoning datasets.
 - General/Logical Reasoning: MAmmoTH-2 (general domain), LogiCoT (logical chain-of-thought, in appendix).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** A comprehensive set of five multimodal benchmarks specifically designed to assess reasoning capabilities was used:
 - MathVista: Diverse benchmark with math-related reasoning and general VQA tasks, providing meta-information for fine-grained analysis.
 - MathVerse: Focuses on visual math problems, with categories like Text-Dominant, Vision-Only.
 - MathVision, DynaMath, MMStar: Additional benchmarks for mathematical and multimodal reasoning.
 - MM-Math: Also included in appendix for detailed results.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Accuracy (exact match) on the respective benchmarks. Significance tests (p < 0.05) were conducted to validate improvements.

#### 3.3. Interpretability Methodology ("Mask Out" Technique)

To understand the internal workings of VLMs and how merging affects them, the authors employ a "mask out" technique:

- **Layer-wise Analysis:** For specific layers or modules (MLP and attention), their parameters are replaced with:
 - **Parameters from a base model (e.g., LLaMA):** This reveals what capabilities were added by LLaVA's SFT training, as performance drops indicate the importance of the original LLaVA parameters.
 - **Parameters from the original LLaVA model (when analyzing merged model):** This helps pinpoint where the *infused reasoning abilities* from the LLM reside in the merged VLM. A drop in reasoning performance when original LLaVA parameters are used suggests the importance of the merged (LLM-derived) parameters in those layers.
 - **Uniform Distribution (1/N):** Replacing parameters with uniform noise effectively "disables" a module, allowing quantification of its absolute contribution to overall performance.
- **Hypothesis Testing:** By observing performance changes (drops or improvements) on "General VQA" (proxy for perception/knowledge) and "Math VQA" (proxy for perception/reasoning) tasks after masking, the authors infer the location and interplay of different abilities.
- **Inference Time Scaling Analysis:** Answer lengths of generated responses were analyzed before and after merging, particularly for chain-of-thought intensive tasks, to understand if merging influences the model's ability to elaborate its reasoning.

This multi-faceted approach, combining practical capability enhancement with deep interpretability, allows for a holistic understanding of cross-modal model merging.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, both in terms of enhancing VLM capabilities and providing novel interpretability insights:

#### 4.1. Enhancement of VLM Reasoning Capabilities

- **Consistent Improvements in Math Reasoning:** Model merging consistently improved the reasoning capabilities of VLMs across all tested math benchmarks. For example, merging LLaVA-Next-LLaMA3-8B with Dart-Prop (a math-specialized LLM) resulted in a **3.6-point absolute improvement** on MathVista's math-related subset and a **30% relative improvement (6-point absolute increase)** on MathVerse's Text-Dominant evaluation mode. These improvements were often statistically significant.
- **Generalization Across VLMs:** The benefits of merging were observed across different VLM sizes (8B to 76B) and base models (LLaMA-series, Mistral-series), demonstrating the generalization ability of the method. For instance, InternVL2-76B also saw approximately 1 absolute point improvement across benchmarks after merging with Dart.
- **Context-Dependent Gains:** While powerful, the gains were not uniform. VLMs already extensively fine-tuned on math-specific data (e.g., Idefics2-8B, Qwen2-VL) showed more modest or mixed improvements, suggesting potential overlap or saturation of reasoning capabilities.
- **Minimal Impact on Perception-Dominant Tasks:** Merging primarily enhanced reasoning with minimal impact on perception-dominant tasks. In some cases, slight performance degradation was observed for vision-intensive tasks (e.g., "figure question answering" in MathVista), indicating a trade-off or a shift in the model's focus.
- **Inference Time Scaling:** Merging with math models (like Dart) significantly increased the average prediction length for chain-of-thought-intensive tasks (e.g., geometry problem solving, algebraic reasoning), sometimes exceeding 250% of the original answer length. This suggests that the transferred reasoning ability manifests as a more detailed and step-by-step reasoning process during inference.
- **Generalization of Reasoning Domains:** Merging with an LLM fine-tuned on logical chain-of-thought data (LogiCoT) also improved multimodal math reasoning, suggesting that reasoning skills can generalize across different reasoning domains (e.g., logic to math) and modalities (text to vision-text).

#### 4.2. Interpretability Insights into VLM Internals

- **Localization of Perception Ability:** Through the "mask out" analysis, it was found that **image perceptual abilities and world knowledge are predominantly encoded in the early layers** of the VLM (LLaVA). Masking out early layers had a greater impact on general VQA tasks than masking later layers.
- **Localization of Reasoning Ability:** In contrast, **mathematical reasoning skills (chain-of-thought) are concentrated in the middle-to-late layers** of the VLM. Masking out these later layers in a merged VLM had a more pronounced negative impact on math-related tasks.
- **Effect of Merging on Layer-wise Abilities:**
 - After merging with reasoning models, **reasoning abilities are observed to contribute across nearly all layers** of the VLM. This implies a more distributed and integrated reasoning capability.
 - The **distribution of perception abilities across layers remains largely unchanged** after merging, reinforcing that merging specifically targets and enhances reasoning without significantly disrupting the model's core perceptual foundation.
- **Early-to-Middle Layers are Crucial:** Disabling modules by replacing them with uniform noise revealed that early-to-middle layers are indispensable for both general and math-related tasks in the base VLM, highlighting their foundational role in initial processing and feature extraction.
- **VLM's Initial Weak Math Reasoning:** The base LLaVA model showed a smaller performance drop for math-targeted VQA compared to general VQA when modules were disabled, indicating its initially weaker math reasoning ability and less sensitivity to parameter alterations in that domain, thereby justifying the need for external reasoning injection.
- **Comparable Performance of Merging Methods:** Different merging methods (linear, TIES, Dare, Layer Swapping) generally yielded comparable performance, supporting the use of simpler methods like linear averaging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the development, deployment, and understanding of multimodal AI systems:

1. **Efficient Capability Transfer:** The work demonstrates a powerful and **training-free pathway to transfer complex reasoning capabilities from specialized LLMs to VLMs**. This is a major breakthrough, as traditional methods would require extensive and costly multimodal fine-tuning, which is often hampered by data scarcity. This "free lunch" approach makes advanced multimodal reasoning more accessible and efficient.
2. **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning Systems:** By significantly boosting VLMs' performance on math and other complex reasoning tasks, this research paves the way for more intelligent multimodal systems. Such systems could be crucial for applications requiring deep understanding of visual data combined with logical inference, such as scientific document analysis, medical imaging interpretation, educational tools, and autonomous driving.
3. **Novel Interpretability Framework:** Leveraging model merging as an interpretability tool provides a new lens through which to understand the internal architecture and functional decomposition of complex neural networks. By localizing perception and reasoning abilities to specific layers, the authors offer concrete insights into how VLMs process different types of information. This contributes to the broader goal of making AI models more transparent and explainable.
4. **Guiding Future Model Design and Training:** The findings regarding the layer-wise distribution of abilities and how merging affects them can inform the design of future VLMs. For instance, architects might optimize early layers for robust perception and later layers for flexible reasoning, or tailor training regimes to address identified weaknesses. The observation that early layers are indispensable suggests their critical role in foundational feature extraction.
5. **Understanding Model Compositionality:** The research sheds light on the principle of model compositionality, suggesting that distinct capabilities (perception, reasoning, knowledge) can be somewhat disentangled and manipulated within the parameter space. This opens avenues for more modular and adaptable AI systems that can be customized by combining expert modules.
6. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** By leveraging pre-trained LLMs and their abundant reasoning data, the method indirectly mitigates the problem of multimodal reasoning data scarcity, which is a major bottleneck in developing highly capable VLMs.
7. **Economic and Environmental Impact:** A training-free method for capability enhancement significantly reduces the computational resources and energy consumption associated with large-scale model development, promoting more sustainable AI research.

In conclusion, "Bring Reason to Vision" not only presents a practical and effective method for enhancing VLM reasoning but also provides a deeper, empirical understanding of how perception and reasoning interact within these complex models. This dual contribution marks a significant step forward in the quest for more intelligent, transparent, and efficient multimodal AI.
