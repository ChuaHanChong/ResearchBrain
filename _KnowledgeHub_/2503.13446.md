# MoManipVLA: Transferring Vision-language-action Models for General Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from BUPT and NTU develop MoManipVLA, a framework that transfers pre-trained vision-language-action models from fixed-base to mobile manipulation tasks through bi-level trajectory optimization, achieving a 4.2% higher success rate on the OVMM benchmark while requiring only 50 expert episodes for real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Current mobile manipulation systems struggle to generalize across different tasks and environments
- VLA models excel at fixed-base manipulation but don't account for coordinated mobile base and arm movements

## Method

- Bi-level optimization framework that jointly plans mobile base and end-effector trajectories
- Motion planning objectives that maximize physical feasibility considering reachability, smoothness, and collision avoidance

## Results

- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods by 4.2% on OVMM benchmark
- Successfully transfers to real-world tasks with minimal additional training (50 episodes)
- Demonstrates generalization across diverse tasks like stacking blocks and opening drawers
- Validates effectiveness of each motion planning objective through ablation studies

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained VLA models can be effectively adapted to mobile manipulation through careful trajectory optimization
> - Separating base and end-effector planning into hierarchical optimization improves efficiency
> - Combining reachability, smoothness, and collision costs enables physically feasible trajectories

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work falls within the active and rapidly evolving areas of:

- **Mobile Manipulation:** This field combines the challenges of robot navigation and robotic arm manipulation, enabling robots to perform complex tasks across extended spaces. It is highly relevant to real-world applications such as household service, manufacturing, and logistics.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** These models have emerged as powerful tools for robotic control, leveraging pre-trained knowledge from large datasets to enable robots to understand human instructions and perform corresponding actions in diverse environments.
- **Transfer Learning:** A core concept in machine learning where knowledge gained from solving one problem is applied to a different but related problem. In this context, transfer learning enables the adaptation of VLA models trained on fixed-base manipulation to the more complex domain of mobile manipulation.
- **Trajectory Optimization:** This involves computing an optimal path for a robot to follow, considering various constraints such as obstacle avoidance, smoothness, and kinematic limits.

The paper addresses a key limitation in current mobile manipulation research: the difficulty in achieving generalization across different tasks and environments. Traditional methods often involve separate training of navigation and manipulation modules, leading to error accumulation. End-to-end methods, while promising, require extensive and costly data collection.

Existing VLA models excel in fixed-base manipulation but do not inherently account for the cooperative actions required between a mobile base and a robotic arm. This work aims to bridge this gap by providing a method to transfer the generalization capabilities of VLA models to mobile manipulation.

The paper builds upon existing work in modular mobile manipulation frameworks such as Home-Robot and OK-Robot, and is inspired by recent trajectory optimization methods like VoxPoser and ReKep, which leverage foundation models to enhance generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the paper are:

- To develop an efficient policy adaptation framework (MoManipVLA) that can transfer pre-trained VLA models from fixed-base manipulation to mobile manipulation.
- To enable high generalization ability across diverse tasks and environments in mobile manipulation.
- To design motion planning objectives that maximize the physical feasibility of robot trajectories, considering factors such as reachability, smoothness, and collision avoidance.
- To create an efficient bi-level optimization framework for trajectory generation that optimizes the movement of the mobile base and the end-effector trajectory.
- To validate the proposed method through experiments in both simulation (OVMM) and real-world environments.

The motivation behind this work stems from the limitations of current mobile manipulation approaches, which often struggle to generalize due to a lack of large-scale training data. VLA models offer a promising solution due to their inherent generalization capabilities learned from extensive datasets. However, these models are primarily designed for fixed-base manipulation tasks. The paper seeks to leverage the strengths of VLA models while addressing the specific challenges of mobile manipulation, thereby enabling robots to perform more complex and versatile tasks in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed MoManipVLA framework operates through the following steps:

1. **Waypoint Generation:** Pre-trained VLA models are used to predict end-effector waypoints based on visual observations and human instructions. These waypoints serve as intermediate goals for the robot to achieve during the manipulation task.
2. **Motion Planning Objectives:** Motion planning objectives are designed to evaluate the physical feasibility of trajectories for both the mobile base and the robot arm. These objectives include:
 - **Reachability:** Measures the ability of the robot arm to reach the desired end-effector poses, considering the position of the mobile base.
 - **Smoothness:** Ensures that the trajectory is smooth and continuous, avoiding sudden changes in joint angles and base movements.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Prevents collisions between the robot (arm and base) and the environment, using a distance field representation of the scene.
3. **Bi-Level Trajectory Optimization:** A bi-level optimization framework is used to efficiently plan the whole-body motion:
 - **Upper-Level Optimization:** Predicts waypoints for base movement to enhance the manipulator's policy space.
 - **Lower-Level Optimization:** Selects the optimal end-effector trajectory to complete the manipulation task, guided by the VLA model's predictions.

The framework uses a transformation function to convert end-effector waypoints from the base frame to the world frame. The overall objective is to minimize a cost function that combines the reachability, smoothness, and collision avoidance costs. The trajectory optimization is performed using a Dual Annealing search algorithm with a gradient-based local optimizer (SLSQP).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents the following key findings:

- MoManipVLA achieves a 4.2% higher success rate than state-of-the-art mobile manipulation techniques on the OVMM benchmark.
- The method requires only 50 expert episodes for real-world deployment, demonstrating the strong generalization ability of the pre-trained VLA models.
- Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of each cost term (reachability, smoothness, and collision avoidance) in the motion planning objective.
- Real-world experiments validate the effectiveness of the proposed method in performing tasks such as stacking blocks, opening drawers, and placing objects in bowls.
- The bi-level optimization framework improves the efficiency of trajectory generation compared to direct optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of this work lies in its ability to efficiently transfer the generalization capabilities of pre-trained VLA models to the domain of mobile manipulation. This has several potential impacts:

- **Improved Generalization in Mobile Manipulation:** By leveraging VLA models, the proposed method can achieve higher success rates and adapt to a wider range of tasks and environments compared to traditional approaches.
- **Reduced Data Collection Costs:** The transfer learning approach reduces the need for extensive data collection, making it more practical for real-world deployment.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The ability to understand human instructions and perform complex manipulation tasks autonomously can lead to more capable and versatile robots for various applications.
- **Advancement of VLA Models:** The work contributes to the development and application of VLA models in robotics, demonstrating their potential for enabling more sophisticated robot behaviors.
- **Real-World Applications:** The method can be applied to various real-world scenarios, including:
 - **Household Service Robots:** Assisting with everyday tasks such as tidying up, preparing meals, and providing assistance to elderly or disabled individuals.
 - **Manufacturing:** Performing complex assembly tasks, handling materials, and collaborating with human workers.
 - **Logistics:** Automating warehouse operations, sorting packages, and delivering goods.

The paper also identifies limitations of the proposed method, including its dependence on the performance of VLA models and its inability to generate effective motions for long-horizon tasks. Future work will focus on addressing these limitations and integrating task planning modules from foundation models.
