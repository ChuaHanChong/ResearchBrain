# DriveWAM: Video Generative Priors Enable Scalable World-Action Modeling for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DriveWAM introduces a world-action model for autonomous driving from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Voyager Research, Didi Chuxing, that leverages a pretrained video diffusion transformer for unified future video and ego-action generation. It integrates VLM-based semantic guidance and selective memory management, achieving a Predictive Driver Model Score of 90.1 on NAVSIM v1 and lower trajectory prediction errors on PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving often lack inherent temporal dynamics and motion priors due to reliance on static image-text pretraining.
- Existing world-action models struggle to integrate high-level semantic planning (e.g., route intent, right-of-way) into their action prediction capabilities.
- Autoregressive driving policies face challenges with unbounded memory growth or suboptimal information eviction when managing historical context for long-horizon rollouts.

## Method

- A pretrained ==video diffusion transformer== is adapted into an ==autoregressive video-action policy==, formulating driving as joint generation of future video latents and ego actions with a ==flow-matching objective==.
- A frozen VLM provides ==scene-evolving driving guidance== by generating chunk-specific semantic intent, which is injected via ==temporally localized cross-attention== to maintain causal consistency.
- A ==selective KV memory== mechanism maintains bounded, modality-aware memory pools for historical video and action tokens, using a relevance-redundancy criterion to retain critical information during long-horizon inference.

## Results

- Achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of **90.1** on NAVSIM v1, demonstrating competitive planning performance with high scores in safety and comfort metrics.
- Recorded lower trajectory prediction errors on the PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles benchmark, with an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of **0.47 meters** and Final Displacement Error (FDE) of **1.35 meters** at a 3-second horizon, outperforming baselines.
- Selective KV memory reduced KV memory usage by over **12 times** and attention GFLOPs by a similar factor for a **300-second** rollout, while largely closing the accuracy gap to full KV caching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repurposing pretrained video diffusion transformers enables robust world-action modeling for autonomous driving by leveraging inherent spatio-temporal generative priors for joint video and action prediction.
> - Integrating causally consistent, VLM-generated semantic guidance effectively bridges the gap between low-level dynamic prediction and high-level driving intentions, enhancing overall policy robustness.
> - Efficient and intelligent memory management (selective KV memory) allows autoregressive policies to maintain high performance in long-horizon scenarios while significantly reducing computational and memory overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of end-to-end autonomous driving systems is increasingly integrating pretrained foundation models as policy backbones. A significant line of research focuses on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which adapt large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate actions based on semantic understanding and instruction following. While VLA-based policies demonstrate proficiency in high-level scene understanding and semantic reasoning, they typically rely on models pretrained on static image-text data. This necessitates acquiring temporal dynamics, motion continuity, and scene evolution priors largely from downstream driving datasets, which can be a limitation for dynamic decision-making in driving.

Complementary to VLA approaches, video generative models offer a different foundational capability. These models are pretrained on extensive video datasets to capture object persistence, motion patterns, and scene evolution, rendering them inherently suitable for tasks involving dynamic environments. Some recent VLA-based driving methods have begun to incorporate future image or video generation to enhance spatio-temporal awareness, often using visual generation as an auxiliary or modular component atop a VLM-centric policy.

In parallel, the robotics community has explored "world-action (WA)" models, demonstrating that pretrained video foundation models can be more directly adapted for action prediction and planning. However, applying this paradigm to autonomous driving presents several challenges. These include adapting a video generation model for continuous ego-action control while preserving its generative prior, integrating high-level semantic planning (such as route intent and right-of-way) into models primarily focused on near-future dynamics, and managing long-horizon autoregressive rollouts with persistent historical context without unbounded memory growth or suboptimal cache eviction. Existing driving-oriented WA methods frequently employ separate planners, discrete video tokenizers, or custom generation architectures, leaving an open area for directly adapting modern video foundation models into comprehensive, semantically guided, and scalable end-to-end driving policies. DriveWAM addresses these identified gaps by proposing a unified architecture that leverages the strengths of video generative models while integrating high-level semantic guidance and efficient memory management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop DriveWAM, a driving world-action model that adapts a pretrained video foundation model into an end-to-end autonomous driving policy. The motivation stems from the recognition of limitations in existing approaches and the potential benefits of integrating video generative models more directly into driving policy backbones.

The key motivations and corresponding objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Leveraging Pretrained Video Generative Priors for Ego-Action Control:**
 - **Motivation:** Traditional VLA models, being VLM-centric, primarily derive from image-text pretraining, which provides strong semantic reasoning but may lack inherent temporal dynamics and motion priors crucial for driving. Video generative models, on the other hand, inherently capture these dynamic characteristics due to their large-scale video pretraining.
 - **Objective:** To adapt a pretrained video diffusion transformer into an autoregressive video-action policy. This involves preserving the video model's capacity for future visual generation while simultaneously coupling it to continuous action prediction. The goal is to formulate driving as joint autoregressive video-action generation, where future video latents are predicted, and then ego actions are decoded conditioned on these generated latents, effectively realizing an inverse-dynamics action generation scheme. This adaptation is achieved through a unified temporal token sequence and a joint flow-matching objective.

2. **Integrating High-Level Semantic Understanding into World-Action Models:**
 - **Motivation:** While video foundation models excel at predicting near-future dynamics and scene evolution, they typically lack the high-level semantic planning capabilities required for complex driving decisions, such as understanding route intent, right-of-way, or the relevance of specific traffic participants. Existing world-action methods often rely on simplistic navigation commands, leaving rich scene-level semantic reasoning largely unaddressed.
 - **Objective:** To introduce "scene-evolving driving guidance." This involves employing a frozen Vision-Language Model (VLM) to provide chunk-specific semantic intent dynamically. This VLM, constrained to use only causally available context (latest observation, recent ego motion, route command), generates concise guidance text for each prediction horizon. This guidance is then injected into the video-action generation process through temporally localized cross-attention, ensuring semantic relevance without breaking causal consistency or requiring the video model itself to perform complex VLM-level reasoning.

3. **Ensuring Scalable Long-Horizon Rollout with Bounded Memory:**
 - **Motivation:** Autoregressive policies require access to historical context for long-horizon rollouts. Storing the complete history (full KV caching) leads to memory costs that grow linearly with the rollout length, becoming impractical for extended driving scenarios. Simple sliding-window caching, while bounding memory, may discard older but still critical evidence, such as distant traffic or previously observed pedestrians.
 - **Objective:** To develop "selective KV memory." This mechanism aims to maintain bounded memory pools for both video and action KVs (keys and values) by applying a relevance-redundancy selection rule at inference time. This rule prioritizes retaining tokens that are highly relevant to current predictions and non-redundant with other cached tokens, thereby keeping a compact, prediction-relevant history without changing the training objective or model parameters, and overcoming the limitations of age-based eviction.

These objectives collectively aim to create a robust, efficient, and semantically aware end-to-end autonomous driving policy by effectively harnessing and adapting the strengths of large-scale video generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DriveWAM is a semantically guided world-action model that adapts a pretrained video foundation model for unified future world evolution and ego-action generation in autonomous driving. Its methodology is structured around three core components: autoregressive video-action generation, scene-evolving driving guidance, and selective KV memory.

#### 3.1. Autoregressive Video-Action Generation

The foundation of DriveWAM is its formulation of driving as autoregressive video-action generation, building upon a pretrained flow-matching video diffusion transformer.

- **Problem Formulation and Tokenization:** A driving clip is segmented into *K* consecutive chunks. At each decision step *k*, the model observes the history up to chunk *k* and predicts the subsequent video-action chunk (x_{k+1}, a_{k+1}), where x_{k+1} is the next video segment and a_{k+1} is the corresponding ego action. Causal conditions include historical context H_k (video and action tokens from previous chunks), ego state e_k (e.g., velocity, acceleration, curvature), and a textual guidance g_k.
 - Video chunks are encoded by a pretrained VAE, producing latents z_k.
 - Ego-action chunks (normalized ego-frame translation and yaw increments) are embedded by an MLP action encoder E_a, yielding u_k.
 - These video and action tokens are organized into a unified temporal token sequence, maintaining their temporal order. VAE latents are mapped to the transformer's hidden dimension for consistency.

- **World-Action Flow:** DriveWAM utilizes a pretrained flow-matching video diffusion transformer (Tω) to predict both the next video and action chunks.
 - **Video Prediction:** During training, a flow timestep τ is sampled. The clean latent z_{k+1} is noised to z_{k+1,τ}, and the video branch of the transformer predicts the velocity v_{z_{k+1,τ}} along the rectified-flow path, conditioned on H_k, e_k, g_k, and τ. This repurposes the video model as a policy prior while aligning its visual prediction objective with driving context.
 - **Action Generation (Inverse-Dynamics):** Actions are generated via an inverse-dynamics flow on the same diffusion transformer. The next action chunk is perturbed in the normalized action space and embedded (u_{k+1,τ}). The shared transformer then predicts the action velocity v_{a_{k+1,τ}}, conditioned on the future world latent (clean z_{k+1} during training, generated ˆz_{k+1} during inference) and the driving context. An MLP action decoder D_a reads out the final action. This design grounds action generation in the predicted world evolution. Noisy-history augmentation is used to mitigate train-test mismatch.

- **Training Objective:** A joint flow-matching objective is used for both branches:
 L = E_{k,τ} [||ˆv_{z_{k+1,τ}} - v_{z_{k+1,τ}}||²₂ + β_a ||ˆv_{a_{k+1,τ}} - v_{a_{k+1,τ}}||²₂]
 The video term preserves the spatio-temporal generative prior, while the action term trains the backbone to translate this prior into ego motion.

- **Full-Clip Training and Autoregressive Rollout:** For training efficiency, all chunks of a clip are processed in a single forward pass with a causal teacher-forcing mask. During inference, DriveWAM rolls out one chunk at a time, sampling future video latents, then actions, and updating history with real observations to keep predictions grounded.

#### 3.2. Scene-Evolving Driving Guidance

To address the video foundation model's lack of high-level semantic planning, DriveWAM introduces scene-evolving driving guidance using a frozen VLM.

- **Causal Guidance Generation:** At each decision step *k*, a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B model generates concise guidance text g_k. This VLM only receives causally available information: the latest observation x_k, recent ego trajectory a_k, and the route command c_k for the upcoming horizon. This ensures that guidance for (x_{k+1}, a_{k+1}) does not leak future information. Guidance texts are precomputed for training and queried once per decision step during inference.

- **Temporally Localized Guidance Injection:** To maintain causal consistency, guidance g_k is injected into the transformer through temporally localized cross-attention. A block-diagonal text mask ensures that video-action tokens of target chunk k+1 only attend to the guidance tokens of g_k, preventing cross-chunk leakage of semantic information.

#### 3.3. Selective KV Memory for Long-Horizon Rollout

To enable bounded long-horizon inference without discarding critical information, DriveWAM implements selective KV memory.

- **Modality-Aware Memory Pools:** Instead of a single global cache, history H_k is decomposed into two bounded, modality-specific pools: H^v_k for video tokens and H^a_k for action tokens, with capacities B_v and B_a respectively. This prevents visual tokens from dominating the cache and ensures retention of ego-motion context.

- **Relevance-Redundancy Retention:** When a memory pool exceeds its budget, cached tokens are ranked by a retention score (s^m_j) based on two criteria:
 - **Relevance (ρ^m_j):** Measured by the average attention mass assigned to token *j* from current query tokens.
 - **Redundancy (η^m_j):** Measured by the average cosine similarity of token *j*'s key to other cached keys in its pool.
 The retention score s^m_j = λρ^m_j - (1-λ)η^m_j balances these factors, where λ is a hyperparameter. Tokens with low scores (i.e., less relevant and more redundant) are evicted. This approach aims to filter out static background while retaining dynamic or geometrically significant cues.

- **Inference Procedure:** This memory strategy is applied exclusively during inference. After processing chunk k+1, newly generated KVs (∆H^m_{k+1}) are added, and the oldest tokens with the lowest retention scores are evicted from their respective pools to maintain the budget. This provides a compact, prediction-relevant history for long-horizon autoregressive attention.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DriveWAM was evaluated on two distinct benchmarks: NAVSIM and the PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles benchmark, complemented by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Planning Performance on NAVSIM

DriveWAM was compared against state-of-the-art end-to-end planners on NAVSIM v1, encompassing classical pipelines, VLA-based policies, and other WA-based methods. Using a single front-view camera, DriveWAM achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.1. This performance metric represents a composite measure of driving safety and comfort. Specifically, DriveWAM recorded high scores across sub-metrics: 98.3 for No at-fault Collisions (NC), 98.1 for Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), 95.2 for Time-To-Collision (TTC), 100.0 for Comfort (C.), and 84.3 for Ego Progress (EP). These results indicate that DriveWAM demonstrated competitive planning performance relative to other methods evaluated under comparable training conditions.

#### 4.2. Planning Performance on PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles

Evaluation on the large-scale PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles benchmark, using a single front-view camera, involved comparison with VaVAM and Alpamayo-1.5. DriveWAM achieved an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of 0.47 meters and a Final Displacement Error (FDE) of 1.35 meters at a 3-second prediction horizon. For a 4-second horizon, DriveWAM recorded an ADE of 0.83 meters and an FDE of 2.47 meters. These metrics indicate a lower trajectory prediction error compared to both VaVAM and Alpamayo-1.5, despite Alpamayo-1.5 being trained on a substantially larger dataset.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples from both NAVSIM and PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles benchmarks illustrated that the ego trajectories predicted by DriveWAM were consistent with the jointly generated future scenes. The generated future video frames and corresponding ego motions maintained photometric and geometric consistency across multiple timesteps.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Scene-Evolving Driving Guidance:** Replacing a fixed global prompt with chunk-specific VLM guidance consistently improved trajectory prediction accuracy across various training data scales. For instance, with 4k clips, ADE@4s decreased from 1.21 to 1.01, and with 100k clips, it decreased from 0.92 to 0.83. This indicates that high-level semantic reasoning provided by the VLM guidance offers a complementary benefit to the low-level video-action backbone.

- **Data Scaling:** A data scaling study from 4k to 100k training clips demonstrated that DriveWAM's performance consistently improved with increased training data. Both ADE@4s and FDE@4s showed significant reductions as the training set size grew, regardless of whether scene-evolving guidance was applied. This trend suggests the scalability of the video-action modeling approach.

- **Video Foundation Model Adaptation:** The study confirmed the importance of both pretrained video backbone initialization and joint video flow-matching supervision. Training the model entirely from scratch without pretrained initialization resulted in degraded ADE@4s/FDE@4s (1.10/3.26). Similarly, removing video supervision while retaining pretrained initialization also led to poorer performance (1.23/3.79). The full configuration, combining pretrained initialization with joint video-action flow-matching supervision, yielded the most favorable results (0.83/2.47).

- **Selective KV Memory:** Comparison of inference-time memory strategies showed that selective KV memory largely closed the accuracy gap to full KV caching while maintaining a bounded memory footprint. Selective KV memory achieved an ADE@4s of 0.89 and an FDE@4s of 2.52, close to full caching (0.83/2.47). In contrast, FIFO caching performed substantially worse (1.40/3.47). For a 300-second rollout, selective KV memory reduced KV memory usage by over 12 times (from 3.07 GB to 0.25 GB) and attention GFLOPs by a similar factor (from 17.37 to 1.44 GFLOPs per step) compared to full caching. This indicates an efficient trade-off between memory and performance for long-horizon inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DriveWAM introduces a framework that integrates a pretrained video foundation model directly into an end-to-end autonomous driving policy, addressing several challenges in the field. The work's significance lies in its methodological innovations and empirical demonstrations of performance and scalability.

### 6. Significance

1. **Unified World-Action Modeling:** DriveWAM demonstrates that repurposing a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a joint video-action policy can effectively leverage large-scale spatio-temporal priors. By formulating driving as autoregressive video-action generation under a unified flow-matching objective, the approach maintains the generative capabilities of the video model while adapting it for ego-action control. This contrasts with VLM-centric policies that primarily rely on static image-text pretraining.
2. **Enhanced Semantic Guidance:** The integration of scene-evolving driving guidance, through a frozen VLM providing chunk-specific semantic intent, addresses the limitation of video foundation models in high-level semantic reasoning. This causally consistent and temporally localized guidance ensures that the driving policy is informed by route intent, traffic rules, and scene-specific interactions, which are critical for robust decision-making.
3. **Scalable and Efficient Long-Horizon Planning:** The introduction of selective KV memory offers a practical solution for managing historical context during long-horizon autoregressive rollouts. By maintaining modality-aware, bounded memory pools based on a relevance-redundancy criterion, DriveWAM achieves inference efficiency comparable to sliding-window caches while largely preserving the performance of full history retention. This is crucial for real-world autonomous driving where continuous and extended operation is required.
4. **Demonstrated Performance and Scalability:** Experimental results on NAVSIM and the PhysicalAI-Autonomous-Vehicles benchmark indicate that DriveWAM achieves competitive planning performance, outperforming several existing methods in trajectory prediction accuracy. The data-scaling study further confirms that the world-action modeling paradigm exhibits consistent improvements with increasing data, suggesting its potential for further scaling with larger datasets.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Advancement in End-to-End Autonomous Driving:** DriveWAM contributes to the development of more capable and robust end-to-end autonomous driving systems by integrating the strengths of video generative models for dynamic scene understanding with high-level semantic reasoning. This could lead to policies that are not only aware of immediate dynamics but also adept at complex decision-making in diverse scenarios.
2. **Inspiration for Future Foundation Models:** The work highlights the utility of video generative models as foundational backbones for robotic control and autonomous driving, potentially motivating further research into pretraining such models specifically for sequential decision-making tasks. The modular approach of integrating a VLM for high-level guidance also suggests a promising direction for combining different types of foundation models.
3. **Practical Deployment of Autoregressive Policies:** The selective KV memory mechanism provides a practical method for deploying autoregressive policies with long historical contexts, mitigating the computational and memory challenges typically associated with such models. This could make advanced planning architectures more feasible for real-time applications in autonomous vehicles.
4. **Improved Safety and Comfort:** By generating coherent future world states and ego actions, and by integrating nuanced semantic guidance, DriveWAM has the potential to contribute to driving policies that are both safer (e.g., better collision avoidance, compliance with rules) and more comfortable (e.g., smoother trajectories, appropriate reactions to traffic).
