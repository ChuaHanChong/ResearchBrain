# SafeBimanual: Diffusion-based Trajectory Optimization for Safe Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University developed SafeBimanual, a framework for robust bimanual robotic manipulation that enhances physical safety through test-time trajectory optimization. This framework integrates a Vision-Language Model to dynamically schedule safety constraints, leading to a 13.7% absolute increase in success rate and an 18.8% absolute decrease in danger rate in simulations, and comparable real-world improvements on a humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion policies for bimanual manipulation often neglect physical safety, leading to potential collisions, object damage, or other unsafe interactions.
- Bimanual tasks require precise spatial and temporal coordination, where even minor misalignments can result in dangerous situations.
- Static, predefined safety rules are insufficient for the diverse and dynamic safety requirements of real-world bimanual tasks.

## Method

- A test-time ==trajectory optimization== framework, **SafeBimanual**, is introduced to integrate dynamic safety constraints into pre-trained ==diffusion-based== bimanual manipulation policies.
- ==Differentiable safety cost functions==, derived from an 'Unsafe Bimanual Manipulation Taxonomy', are formulated to penalize unsafe interactions like object collision, gripper poking, and behavior misalignment.
- An adaptive ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== with ==Chain-of-Thought== reasoning dynamically schedules relevant safety costs during the final denoising steps of trajectory generation, based on inferred unsafe patterns for each task stage.

## Results

- In simulation, SafeBimanual improved the average success rate by **13.7%** and reduced the danger rate by **18.8%** across eight bimanual tasks when applied to state-of-the-art diffusion policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the VLM-based adaptive scheduler with Chain-of-Thought reasoning achieved superior performance compared to fixed-weight or non-CoT scheduling.
- Deployed on a Galaxea-R1 humanoid robot, the framework demonstrated a **32.5%** increase in success rate and a **30.0%** reduction in unsafe interactions in real-world bimanual tasks, including long-horizon scenarios like 'Prepare-Breakfast'.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic taxonomy of unsafe bimanual interaction patterns, derived from demonstration analysis, provides a structured basis for designing comprehensive safety cost functions.
> - Dynamically scheduling safety constraints using VLM-based reasoning is crucial for adapting to diverse task-specific safety needs and preventing conflicting constraints, outperforming static or non-adaptive approaches.
> - Injecting energy-based safety guidance during the later stages of the diffusion denoising process effectively refines trajectories for safety while preserving the exploration capabilities of the base policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research navigates several prominent and rapidly evolving areas within robotics and artificial intelligence: bimanual manipulation, diffusion models for policy learning, and trajectory optimization. The paper effectively bridges these fields to address a critical, yet often overlooked, challenge: ensuring physical safety in complex robotic tasks.

**Bimanual Manipulation:** Dual-arm robotic systems are highly desirable for tasks requiring complex coordination, stability, and handling of large or awkward objects, ranging from household services to manufacturing and surgical operations. However, the inherent high degrees of freedom (DOFs) present significant challenges for policy learning, leading to a vast and complex action space. Prior work has explored hierarchical policy learning to decompose complex tasks and simplify the learning problem. "SafeBimanual" builds upon this foundation by recognizing that while previous methods enhance task completion, they often fail to explicitly consider the physical constraints inherent in dual-arm interaction, leading to unsafe behaviors.

**Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Inspired by their success in generative AI (e.g., image synthesis), diffusion models have recently gained traction in robotics for policy learning. They excel at modeling high-dimensional, multimodal action distributions and demonstrate excellent scalability, leading to improved stability, efficiency, and success rates in robotic tasks. These models learn to generate actions by iteratively denoising from a Gaussian noise distribution. This paper acknowledges the power of diffusion policies but identifies a critical limitation in bimanual contexts: existing diffusion policies often ignore spatial and temporal coordination safety, resulting in collisions, object tearing, or misaligned actions.

**Trajectory Optimization:** This field is fundamental to robotic motion planning, aiming to generate feasible and efficient robot movements. Traditionally, trajectory optimization relies on hand-crafted geometric or contact constraints to ensure collision-free paths. While effective for specific, well-defined scenarios, these methods struggle with scalability and adaptability in high-DOF systems and unstructured environments. More recently, data-driven approaches have emerged, learning objectives and constraints from demonstrations. "SafeBimanual" significantly contributes to this area by integrating insights from large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), similar to works like VoxPoser and ReKeP. These models infer affordances and constraints, enhancing generalization and planning efficiency. The novel contribution here is leveraging a VLM to *dynamically schedule* safety constraints for trajectory optimization within the diffusion denoising process itself, a paradigm shift from static, pre-defined constraints.

In summary, "SafeBimanual" positions itself at the intersection of these advancements, specifically targeting the safety-critical gap in state-of-the-art diffusion-based bimanual manipulation policies. It leverages the strengths of diffusion models for robust action generation while introducing a novel, adaptable safety layer informed by a VLM, moving towards more reliable and deployable robotic systems in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the physical safety of bimanual robotic manipulation policies** by integrating a test-time trajectory optimization framework that imposes dynamic safety constraints. The authors aim to transform existing, pre-trained diffusion-based bimanual manipulation policies into "safe" policies, thereby avoiding dangerous robot behaviors and simultaneously improving task success rates.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical oversight in current bimanual manipulation research, particularly concerning diffusion-based policy learning:

1. **Safety Neglect in Current Diffusion Policies:** While diffusion policies have demonstrated promising performance in modeling complex action distributions for bimanual tasks, they inherently lack mechanisms to account for physical safety constraints. This omission can lead to hazardous outcomes, such as collisions between robot arms, damage to manipulated objects (e.g., tearing), or unintended interactions with the environment. In real-world deployments, such unsafe behaviors are unacceptable and can cause significant damage to robots, objects, or even human operators, hindering wider adoption.

2. **Complexity of Bimanual Coordination:** Bimanual tasks demand precise spatial and temporal coordination between two arms. Even slight misalignments in trajectories or actions can cascade into dangerous situations. Existing methods struggle to account for the diverse and context-dependent safety requirements that arise in different bimanual cooperation patterns (e.g., symmetric tasks like lifting a large object vs. asymmetric tasks like pouring liquid).

3. **Need for Dynamic and Adaptive Safety:** A static set of safety rules is insufficient for the varied and dynamic nature of real-world bimanual tasks. Optimal safety constraints often change depending on the current stage of manipulation, the objects involved, and the specific cooperation pattern. There is a strong need for a system that can dynamically identify potential unsafe interactions and apply appropriate, adaptive safety measures.

4. **Practical Value for Real-World Deployment:** For robots to be truly valuable and trustworthy in household services, manufacturing, or surgical operations, they must operate safely. By explicitly addressing physical safety, "SafeBimanual" aims to increase the robustness and reliability of bimanual manipulation systems, thus bridging the gap between promising research results and practical, safe deployment. The goal is to create a "plug-and-play" module that can efficiently adapt to diverse safety constraints without extensive re-training of the base policy.

In essence, the authors are motivated to make powerful, high-dimensional robotic policies, particularly those enabled by diffusion models, suitable for practical applications by embedding a crucial layer of physical intelligence related to safety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SafeBimanual introduces a novel test-time trajectory optimization framework designed to integrate safety constraints into pre-trained diffusion-based bimanual manipulation policies. The methodology is structured around three core components: guided sampling within the diffusion denoising process, a comprehensive set of differentiable safety cost functions, and an adaptive Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based scheduler for dynamic constraint activation.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement and Context

The problem is framed around existing diffusion policies that generate action sequences $A_t = (a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+n})$ given an observation $O_t$. The key challenge is that directly sampling from the denoising distribution $p(A_t | O_t)$ often overlooks physical safety. The authors propose decomposing bimanual tasks into $K$ sequential stages, $s_t$, and for each stage, inferring potential unsafe patterns quantified by differentiable safety costs $C(A_t, P, s_t)$, where $P$ represents relational keypoints. The goal is to steer the trajectory towards a physically safer version, $A_{safe_t}$.

#### 3.2. Guided Sampling in Denoising Process

SafeBimanual extends the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework by incorporating task-aware safety constraints through energy-based guidance, similar to classifier-guided diffusion. Instead of directly evaluating costs on noisy samples, the framework first estimates the corresponding clean action chunk $A_{0|k}$ using the noise prediction network $\epsilon_\theta$. A scheduled cost, $C_{sched}(A_{0|k}, P, s_t)$, is then computed. The gradient of this cost function is injected into the denoising update rule (Equation 2), effectively steering the generated action sequence towards a safer trajectory. The strength of this safety guidance is controlled by a parameter $\rho_k$. Crucially, guidance is only applied during the final $M$ denoising steps ($k \le M = 0.3K$) to balance exploration (maintaining trajectory diversity in early steps) and safety refinement (in later steps when noise levels are low).

#### 3.3. Safety Cost Formulation

To systematically address unsafe bimanual interactions, the authors first developed an "Unsafe Bimanual Manipulation Taxonomy." This taxonomy was derived from analyzing 1,320 demonstrations across 65 tasks and 7 benchmarks, revealing that over 96.2% of unsafe interactions fall into five categories:
- **Object unsafe interaction:** Object-object collision, behavior misalignment.
- **Gripper unsafe behavior:** Gripper poking, tearing, gripper-gripper collision.
Based on this taxonomy, five differentiable safety cost functions, $C_1$ to $C_5$, are formulated, leveraging keypoints $P = \{k_l^t, k_r^t\}$ that identify high-risk interaction regions for both arms and manipulated objects. These keypoints are computed using differentiable forward kinematics, transforming object keypoints and gripper tip positions into the action space.

- **$C_1$: Objects Collision Cost:** Penalizes proximity between manipulated objects' keypoints, $k_l^t, k_r^t$.
- **$C_2$: Behavior Alignment Cost:** Enforces task-level spatial alignment (e.g., axis alignment and vertical offset for pouring) between keypoints of different objects.
- **$C_3$: Grippers Poking Cost:** Penalizes deviations between the gripper's approach direction and the vector from the keypoint to the gripper tip, preventing unintended surface contact.
- **$C_4$: Grippers Tearing Cost:** Penalizes deviations from an initial grasp width between gripper tips, preventing tearing of rigidly held objects.
- **$C_5$: Grippers Collision Cost:** Penalizes proximity between the two gripper tips, maintaining minimum clearance to avoid self-collision.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Safety Cost Scheduler

A key innovation is the dynamic scheduling of these safety constraints using a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o, employed in a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) process. This scheduler operates in two stages:
1. **Identify Unsafe Pattern:** Given the current observation ($O_t$), relational keypoints ($P$), and task stage ($s_t$), the VLM infers the most probable unsafe interaction pattern from the predefined taxonomy. This grounds the reasoning in real-world semantics.
2. **Schedule Cost Terms with Keypoints:** Based on the inferred unsafe pattern, the VLM dynamically selects a subset of relevant safety cost terms ($C_i$) via a binary mask $\alpha$ and identifies the corresponding relational keypoints. This ensures that only necessary costs are activated, forming $C_{sched}$.

For keypoint proposal and tracking, the framework uses a combination of techniques: semantically meaningful object keypoints from ReKeP, tracked temporally with CoTracker3 for 2D consistency, and back-projected to 3D. Gripper tip positions are derived from forward kinematics, and object pose centers are estimated via Omni6DPose. The system assumes a static-offset for occluded 3D keypoints from the object's frame, ensuring temporal consistency.

The overall pipeline integrates these components: the task is decomposed into stages; at each stage, the VLM identifies the unsafe pattern and schedules appropriate cost functions; these costs guide the diffusion denoising process during the final steps of trajectory generation, leading to safe and effective bimanual manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SafeBimanual was rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating significant improvements in safety and task success rates.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (RoboTwin Benchmark)

Experiments were conducted on eight diverse bimanual manipulation tasks within the RoboTwin simulator, covering a wide range of dual-arm coordination patterns and unsafe interactions. SafeBimanual was applied as a plug-and-play module to three state-of-the-art diffusion-based policies: 2D Diffusion Policy (DP), 3D Diffusion Policy (DP3), and RDT-1b (a diffusion-based bimanual foundation model).

- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - **Success Rate (SR):** SafeBimanual consistently improved the average success rate across all baselines and tasks. For instance, it increased the average SR from 39.6% to 50.7%, representing a **13.7% absolute increase**.
 - **Danger Rate (DR):** The framework significantly reduced the danger rate, which measures the proportion of episodes with at least one unsafe interaction. It achieved an average DR reduction from 43.9% to 25.0%, an **18.8% absolute decrease**.
 - **Task-Specific Performance:** SafeBimanual showed greater improvements in tasks involving multiple unsafe modes (e.g., Dual Shoes Place, Dual Bottles Pick (Hard), Pour Water), where failures often stemmed from gripper poking, object collisions, or behavioral misalignment. For tasks primarily involving semantic errors (e.g., Blocks Stack (Easy)), while danger rate was still reduced, the impact on success rate was limited, confirming that the method specifically addresses safety-critical failures.

- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations (Figure 3, Figure 7) confirmed that SafeBimanual successfully avoided various unsafe interaction patterns, including collisions between objects/grippers, misalignment, and gripper poking. It achieved this while maintaining coordinated dual-arm behavior, showcasing its ability to generate physically safe and task-effective trajectories.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were performed by applying SafeBimanual to a Diffusion Policy baseline to assess the contribution of its key components: individual safety cost terms and the adaptive scheduling mechanism.

- **Safety Cost Ablation:** Removing any single cost term consistently led to an increase in the overall Danger Rate. The "Objects Collision Cost" ($C_1$) had the most significant impact, reflecting the prevalence of object-object collisions across tasks. The "Grippers Tearing Cost" ($C_4$) had a smaller impact, as tearing is specific to tasks where grippers manipulate a single object in a particular way. This validates the importance and modularity of each defined safety constraint.
- **Cost Scheduling Ablation:** Comparing different scheduling strategies:
 - A fixed-weight baseline (activating all costs equally) performed poorly (SR 19.4%, DR 42.5%), indicating that non-adaptive constraints can hinder task execution by introducing conflicts.
 - A VLM-based scheduler without Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning showed improvement but was still suboptimal (SR 25.6%, DR 36.9%).
 - The proposed CoT-VLM scheduler demonstrated superior performance (SR 35.5%, DR 19.8%), highlighting its ability to dynamically select stage-appropriate cost combinations and adapt to task-specific safety needs.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

SafeBimanual was deployed on a Galaxea-R1 humanoid robot for four real-world bimanual tasks, including a challenging long-horizon "Prepare-Breakfast" benchmark.

- **Quantitative Improvements:** The framework improved success rates and reduced unsafe interactions significantly in real-world settings. Compared to original policies, SafeBimanual achieved a **32.5% increase in success rate** and a **30.0% reduction in unsafe interactions**.
- **Long-Horizon Task Performance:** For the "Prepare-Breakfast" task, fine-tuning iDP3 with SafeBimanual enhanced object-level generalization and reduced compounding errors over extended sequences, demonstrating its practical value for complex, multi-stage tasks.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Real-world visualizations (Figure 3, Figure 8) further verified SafeBimanual's capability to prevent common real-world unsafe behaviors like spilling (Pour Water), object tearing (Pass Banana), and knocking over objects (Place Fruits), leading to more robust and reliable robot operation.

In summary, the findings robustly support SafeBimanual's effectiveness as a safety-enhancing framework for diffusion-based bimanual manipulation, validating its approach across diverse tasks and environments, and highlighting the critical role of dynamic, VLM-scheduled safety constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "SafeBimanual" framework represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Safe Real-World Robotic Deployment:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is addressing the critical need for physical safety in complex robotic operations. By actively mitigating unsafe behaviors like collisions, object tearing, and misalignments, SafeBimanual brings diffusion-based bimanual manipulation policies closer to practical, reliable, and trustworthy deployment in sensitive environments such as homes, hospitals (surgical assistance), and factories. This greatly reduces the risk of damage to expensive robots, valuable objects, and, most importantly, potential harm to human co-workers or users.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Policy Learning and Safety Constraints:** The paper successfully integrates state-of-the-art policy learning (diffusion models) with dynamic safety reasoning. This is a crucial step beyond traditional policy learning, which often focuses solely on task success, and highlights the importance of incorporating real-world physical constraints into the learning and execution loop.

3. **Novelty of VLM-Based Adaptive Safety Scheduling:** The use of a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for dynamically scheduling safety constraints is a groundbreaking contribution. This intelligent, context-aware mechanism allows the robot to infer potential unsafe interaction patterns from visual observations and high-level task instructions, and then activate the most appropriate cost functions. This adaptive approach overcomes the limitations of hand-crafted or statically applied safety rules, making the system highly versatile and generalizable across diverse and dynamic tasks. The Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning further enhances the VLM's ability to provide robust and interpretable safety guidance.

4. **Plug-and-Play Compatibility:** SafeBimanual is designed as a "test-time trajectory optimization framework," meaning it can be used as a plug-and-play module with *any* pre-trained diffusion-based bimanual manipulation policy. This dramatically reduces the need for re-training complex, high-dimensional policies, making it highly efficient and accessible for researchers and practitioners alike. This modularity will accelerate the integration of safety measures into existing and future policy learning systems.

5. **Comprehensive Unsafe Interaction Taxonomy:** The development of a systematic taxonomy of unsafe bimanual manipulation patterns is a valuable contribution in itself. This taxonomy provides a structured basis for understanding and categorizing failure modes, which can inform future research in safety-aware robotics, beyond just diffusion policies.

6. **Empirical Validation across Simulation and Real-World:** The rigorous experimental validation in both simulated (RoboTwin) and real-world (Galaxea-R1 humanoid robot) settings, including challenging long-horizon tasks, underscores the robustness and practical value of the proposed method. The consistent improvements in success rate and drastic reductions in danger rate provide strong evidence for its effectiveness.

Despite its current limitations, such as not guaranteeing complete safety in extreme environments, its focus on physical interactions over semantic task failures, and reliance on visual keypoint tracking, SafeBimanual lays a strong foundation. Its approach is likely to inspire future work on robust, safe, and adaptive robotic control, especially as VLMs and foundation models continue to advance, further enhancing their reasoning capabilities and leading to even more sophisticated safety scheduling. The framework marks a significant step towards truly deployable and intelligent bimanual robotic systems.
