# What Matters in Humanoid General Motion Tracking? An Empirical Study

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Inria, Université de Lorraine, and CNRS empirically investigated the impact of common modeling and training factors in humanoid general motion tracking pipelines. Their YAHMP framework demonstrated zero-shot transferability to the Unitree G1 robot, clarifying how design choices influence tracking accuracy, actuation effort, and physical interaction capabilities.

## Problem

- A systematic understanding of how individual design choices within humanoid motion tracking pipelines affect general tracking performance is limited.
- Ablation studies are often confined to specific methods, and comparisons between methods evaluate entire systems rather than isolated components.
- Difficulty in identifying design factors that are consistently beneficial versus those unique to a particular pipeline.

## Method

- Developed and open-sourced **YAHMP** (Yet Another Humanoid Motion tracking Policy), a modular framework for training and evaluating whole-body motion tracking policies on the Unitree G1 robot.
- Conducted a controlled empirical study systematically varying key modeling factors (e.g., ==motion-command representation==, ==observation history==, ==action representation==, ==actuation profile==) and training factors (e.g., ==hand-force randomization==, ==teacher-student training==).
- Evaluated policies in simulation using comprehensive tracking and effort metrics, and performed zero-shot deployment on the physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Results

- All YAHMP policies successfully completed the entire test set of **1,024** motions in simulation without falling, demonstrating robustness across variants.
- Policies trained with hand-force randomization showed significantly less elbow deviation (e.g., **6.6°** vs **15.5°** at a **4 kg** load) and withstood heavier loads (up to **6 kg**) in real-robot interaction experiments.
- Nominal YAHMP policies achieved zero-shot transfer to the Unitree G1 robot, tracking diverse whole-body motions and maintaining balance under external perturbations, including on soft terrain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit reference joint velocities and sufficient observation history are crucial for robust and accurate motion tracking, with their absence significantly degrading performance (e.g., 7-50% increased base errors).
> - Mechanics-based actuation profiles achieve comparable tracking quality with lower peak torques and reduced oscillations compared to stiffer, fixed-scale approaches.
> - Hand-force randomization primarily enhances the robot's ability to sustain external loads and perform forceful interactions, rather than improving free-space motion tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research positions itself within the field of example-guided reinforcement learning (RL) for robotics, specifically addressing complex motor skills in humanoids. Initial work in this domain, exemplified by DeepMimic, demonstrated the use of reference motions as a training signal for simulated characters. Subsequent efforts extended this concept to humanoid and legged robots, enabling them to learn specific locomotion behaviors by imitating human or animal references. These early applications primarily focused on replicating single or limited sets of related motions.

More recently, the research landscape has shifted towards "general motion tracking", which involves training deployable controllers capable of following a broad and diverse distribution of whole-body references. For humanoid robots, this encompasses a wide range of behaviors, including locomotion, turns, gestures, dancing, crouching, and interaction with objects and the environment. Concurrently, whole-body imitation has found application as a low-level tracking layer within teleoperation architectures. The development of such systems relies on large-scale human motion and interaction datasets, coupled with retargeting methods that adapt human motion data to specific robot morphologies.

While existing literature offers several complete pipelines for humanoid motion tracking, a systematic understanding of how individual design choices within these pipelines affect general tracking performance remains limited. Ablation studies are typically confined to specific methods, and comparisons between methods often evaluate entire systems rather than isolated components. This hinders the identification of design factors that are consistently beneficial across different implementations versus those that are unique to a particular pipeline. This paper aims to address this gap by conducting a controlled empirical study of frequently recurring modeling and training factors to provide practical guidance for system design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to empirically investigate and evaluate the impact of common modeling and training factors used in contemporary humanoid motion-imitation pipelines. The motivation stems from the observation that while numerous full-system pipelines exist, there is a scarcity of systematic analysis on the individual contributions of their constituent design choices. This lack of controlled assessment makes it challenging for researchers and practitioners to ascertain which specific settings are generally effective and transferable, as opposed to those that are highly context-dependent.

To achieve this overarching goal, the authors set forth several detailed objectives:
- **Controlled and Reproducible Study:** To conduct an empirical study where individual design choices can be systematically varied and assessed within a standardized and reproducible framework.
- **Development of YAHMP:** To develop and open-source YAHMP (Yet Another Humanoid Motion tracking Policy), a modular framework specifically designed for training, evaluating, and deploying whole-body motion tracking policies on the Unitree G1 robot. This framework serves as the platform for the controlled experiments.
- **Factor Identification and Analysis:** To identify and evaluate key factors that frequently appear in recent whole-body tracking pipelines but lack systematic analysis. These factors include:
 - **Motion-command representation:** Investigating which reference quantities (e.g., joint positions, joint velocities, base velocities, key-body targets) are most effective when provided to the actor policy.
 - **Observation history:** Assessing the utility of incorporating past state and command information to aid the policy in handling partial observability and short-term dynamics.
 - **Action representation:** Comparing policies that output residual offsets around reference joint positions versus those that output offsets around a fixed default posture.
 - **Actuation profile:** Examining the combined effects of PD gains and action scales on how policy outputs are converted into motor torques.
 - **Hand-force randomization:** Studying the effect of applying random external forces to the robot's hands during training to enhance physical interaction capabilities beyond free-space tracking.
 - **Training approach:** Comparing the performance and complexity of standard single-stage Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) against teacher-student training paradigms.
- **Comparative Evaluation:** To define a nominal configuration within YAHMP and systematically compare variants by altering one modeling or training component at a time. This includes evaluating these variants against an external baseline (TWIST2), retrained under the same conditions.
- **Differentiation of Effects:** To differentiate between design choices that primarily influence tracking accuracy and those that predominantly affect factors such as actuation effort, training complexity, or the robot's capacity for physical interaction.
- **Real-World Deployment Demonstration:** To perform zero-shot deployment of selected YAHMP policies on the physical Unitree G1 robot to demonstrate their capability for diverse whole-body motion tracking, balance maintenance under external perturbations, and forceful interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research utilizes YAHMP (Yet Another Humanoid Motion tracking Policy), an open-source, modular framework built upon mjlab, specifically designed for the Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The framework enables the systematic variation of individual design choices within a consistent training and evaluation environment.

**Problem Formulation:** Whole-body motion tracking is formulated as a goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) problem. At each timestep, the policy receives an observation (`o_t`) encompassing the current robot state and a motion reference, and outputs an action (`a_t`). This action is then translated into joint-position targets for the robot's low-level PD controllers. The Unitree G1 robot has 29 joints. Reference motions are derived from retargeting human motion-capture data from AMASS and OMOMO datasets to the G1's morphology. The policy is trained to maximize a reward function that encourages tracking of the reference motion while penalizing excessive contacts, actions, and joint-limit violations.

**Observations:** The observations provided to the policy are constructed from configurable components:
- **Proprioception:** Includes base angular velocity, gravity direction in the base frame, joint positions (relative to a nominal configuration), joint velocities, and the previous policy action.
- **Motion Command:**
 - **Nominal:** Comprises reference joint positions (`q̂_t`), reference joint velocities (`q̇̂_t`), planar base velocity, yaw rate, world-frame base height, and reference roll and pitch.
 - **Pos-ref-only Variant:** Excludes reference joint velocities (`q̇̂_t`) from the motion command.
- **History Buffer:** Concatenates past proprioception and motion command vectors. The nominal setting uses a history length of 10 steps (History-10). Variants include No history (0 steps) and History-20 (20 steps).
- **Privileged Observations:** Utilized during training for the critic or teacher policy, these include noiseless proprioceptive data, base linear velocity, reference-relative base pose, body poses and orientations in the world frame, foot contact state, and ground-friction coefficients.

**Actions:** Policy outputs (`a_t`) are interpreted as offsets to generate joint-position targets:
- **Nominal (Residual Actions):** `q_target_t = q̂_t + α ⊙ a_t`. The policy outputs residual adjustments around the reference joint configuration.
- **No Residual Variant:** `q_target_t = q_nom + α ⊙ a_t`. Actions are applied around a fixed default posture.
These joint targets are then tracked by PD controllers that generate torques, which are saturated at the actuator limits.

**Actuation Profile:** This combines joint PD gains and action scales:
- **Nominal (Mechanics-based):** PD gains (`k_p,j`, `k_d,j`) are derived from reflected actuator inertia, with a critical damping ratio (`ζ = 2`) and a natural frequency (`ω_0 = 2π ⋅ 10 rad/s`). Joint-wise action scales (`α_j`) are set based on actuator effort limits, such that equal policy outputs correspond to equal fractions of torque limits.
- **Stiffer fixed-scale Variant:** Employs manually specified, generally higher PD gains and a constant action scale across all joints.

**Actor-Critic Architecture:** Both actor and critic networks use a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with hidden dimensions (512, 512, 256, 128), featuring observation normalization, layer normalization in the final hidden layer, and ELU activations. When a history buffer is present, a 1D temporal convolutional network encodes it into a 64-dimensional embedding, which is then concatenated with current proprioception and motion command.

**Domain Randomization:**
- **Nominal:** Includes randomization of foot-ground friction, torso center of mass, uniform additive proprioceptive noise, and intermittent base-velocity pushes.
- **Hand-force Randomization Variant:** Applies external forces to the hands during training. Force events are sampled for side, application point (around the wrist), duration, and cooldown, with forces scaled to remain within joint-torque limits.

**Reward, Reset, and Motion References:** All variants share the same reward function. Episodes terminate upon reference motion completion or if robot bodies deviate excessively. At reset, a motion clip and an initial phase are sampled. The sampling distribution is adaptively updated to replay poorly tracked clips/phases more frequently, while maintaining uniform coverage.

**Policy Training and Dataset:**
- **Dataset:** 12,175 retargeted motions from AMASS and OMOMO, filtered for suitability. 11,151 motions are used for training, and 1,024 for testing. The test set is diverse and distinct from training data.
- **Nominal Training:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic setup. The actor uses only deployable observations, while the critic also receives privileged observations.
- **Teacher-student Variant:** Involves two stages: first, a teacher policy is trained with PPO using privileged observations; then, a student policy is trained with PPO using only deployable observations, regularized by a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence penalty towards the frozen teacher's distribution.
- **Computational Resources:** Training for all YAHMP variants utilizes 8,192 parallel environments over 20,000 PPO iterations on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090, requiring approximately 25 hours per run.

**Evaluation:**
- **Simulation Evaluation:** Policies are evaluated on the 1,024 test motions in MuJoCo (0.005 s timestep, 50 Hz control frequency). Metrics include base-position and orientation errors, mean key-body position and orientation errors (over nine key bodies), and joint-position and velocity errors. Torque metrics (mean and maximum absolute) are also reported. Success is defined as completing the motion without falling.
- **External Baseline:** TWIST2 is retrained on the same training set and evaluated using the same metrics.
- **Real-Robot Evaluation:** Selected policies are deployed zero-shot on the Unitree G1. This includes qualitative assessment of general motion tracking, balance under manual external perturbations, and robustness to unseen terrain (soft mattress). Quantitative studies focus on the impact of hand-force randomization on sustaining external loads (measuring elbow deviation) and the effects of different actuation profiles on stability and torque.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**Simulation Evaluation:** All policies successfully completed the entire test set of 1,024 motions without falling, indicating robustness across variants. The evaluation primarily focused on tracking accuracy and effort metrics.

- **Motion-Command Representation:** Removing reference joint velocities (`Pos-ref-only`) consistently degraded tracking performance across all metrics. Base errors increased by 7–14%, key-body errors by approximately 9%, joint-position error by 8%, and joint-velocity error by 15%. This suggests that explicit joint velocity information is valuable even with observation history.
- **Observation History:** The absence of an observation history (`No history`) resulted in significant increases across all tracking errors: base errors rose by 38–50%, key-body errors by 10–11%, joint-position error by 10%, and joint-velocity error by 17%. Increasing history length from 10 to 20 steps (`History-20`) offered mixed results, slightly reducing base errors (6–7%) but marginally worsening key-body and joint-space tracking (approximately 4% increase in errors). History-10 was identified as offering a balance between temporal context and input compactness.
- **Action Representation:** Using actions around a fixed default posture (`No residual`) led to modest increases in key-body pose errors (5–6%) and joint-position error (5%). Base tracking effects were mixed, with a 1% increase in base-position error and a 5% decrease in base-orientation error. Residual actions generally provided a slight improvement in key-body and joint-position tracking.
- **Actuation Profile:** The `Stiffer fixed-scale` profile did not demonstrate a clear tracking advantage, showing mixed improvements and degradations across different tracking metrics. However, it significantly increased maximum torque, by 13% across all joints and 41% specifically on the upper body, compared to the `Mechanics-based` nominal profile, while mean torques showed smaller changes. The mechanics-based profile achieved comparable tracking with lower peak torques.
- **Hand-Force Randomization:** In unperturbed simulation, hand-force randomization had a mild effect on tracking performance. Its primary impact was observed during real-robot interaction experiments.
- **Teacher-Student Training:** This more complex training approach provided only minor and mixed improvements over the nominal PPO policy. Base-position, key-body orientation, joint-position, and joint-velocity errors slightly decreased (3–8%), while base-orientation error increased by 8%.
- **Comparison with TWIST2:** When retrained and evaluated under the same protocol, TWIST2 achieved the lowest key-body position error (10% lower than nominal YAHMP). However, it exhibited substantially higher errors in other metrics: base errors increased by 32–36%, key-body orientation error by 51%, joint-position error by 38%, and joint-velocity error by 81%.

**Real-Robot Evaluation:**
- **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** The nominal YAHMP policy demonstrated zero-shot transferability to the Unitree G1 robot. It successfully tracked diverse whole-body motions, including locomotion, crouching, dancing, and loco-manipulation styles, without any real-robot fine-tuning or dedicated sim-to-real pipelines. The robot maintained balance under manual pushes and drags and was able to perform double-support motions (e.g., squats) on unseen soft mattress terrain, though stable locomotion on such terrain would likely require dedicated training.
- **Forceful Interactions via Hand-Force Randomization:** Policies trained with hand-force randomization exhibited a superior ability to sustain external loads. In a hand-load experiment, the randomized policy showed significantly less elbow deviation (e.g., 6.6° vs 15.5° at a 4 kg load) and could withstand heavier loads (up to 6 kg) compared to the non-randomized counterpart. This confirms that hand-force randomization primarily enhances interaction capabilities rather than free-space tracking. A qualitative demonstration involved the policy successfully replaying a full-body lifting motion with a 1.5 kg box.
- **Actuation Profile Effects in Sim-to-Real:** The `Stiffer fixed-scale` policy deployed successfully, but qualitative observations revealed increased oscillations, particularly in ankle joints, during motions like crouching, compared to the `Mechanics-based` policy. Quantitatively, the stiffer profile led to larger maximum torque peaks (75.2 N m vs 54.5 N m) for the same motion phase, while the mean absolute torque remained largely unchanged.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by providing a systematic and empirical investigation into the practical design choices for general motion tracking pipelines. The findings offer practical guidance for researchers and developers in designing more effective and robust humanoid controllers.

The key aspects of significance and potential impact include:

- **Empirical Design Guidance:** The study systematically evaluates common modeling and training factors, offering insights into their individual effects on tracking performance, actuation effort, training complexity, and physical interaction capabilities. This moves beyond anecdotal evidence or full-system comparisons, providing a more granular understanding that can inform future pipeline designs.
- **Open-Source Framework (YAHMP):** The release of YAHMP provides a standardized, modular, and open-source platform for training, evaluating, and deploying motion tracking policies on the Unitree G1. This framework can facilitate reproducible research, lower the barrier to entry for new researchers, and potentially accelerate innovation in humanoid control by offering a shared, configurable foundation.
- **Demonstrated Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot deployment of YAHMP policies on the real Unitree G1, without specific hardware tuning or additional real-robot training, demonstrates the viability of simulation-trained policies for real-world humanoid tasks. This reduces the time and resources typically required for robot deployment, advancing the practical applicability of RL-based control.
- **Clarification of Factor Importance:**
 - The research highlights the importance of including **reference joint velocities** and **observation history** in motion commands, as their absence significantly degrades tracking. This suggests that temporal context and explicit velocity information are crucial for accurate reproduction of dynamic motions.
 - It indicates that **residual action representations** offer a modest improvement in certain tracking metrics, suggesting their utility but not as a universally decisive factor.
 - The study advocates for **mechanics-based actuation profiles**, demonstrating that they can achieve comparable tracking performance while mitigating excessive torque peaks and oscillations observed with stiffer, fixed-scale approaches. This has implications for robot longevity and energy efficiency.
 - Crucially, the findings reveal that **hand-force randomization** is a specialized training technique that significantly enhances the robot's ability to exert and sustain meaningful interaction forces, a capability distinct from free-space motion tracking. This is important for developing humanoids capable of complex manipulation and interaction with the environment.
 - The comparison of training approaches suggests that a **standard PPO pipeline** can yield robust performance, with the more complex **teacher-student approach** offering only minor, mixed gains in this particular setup. This can inform decisions regarding training complexity versus performance returns.

- **Foundation for Advanced Robotics:** The established general motion tracking policies serve as a foundational layer for more complex humanoid applications. The authors propose integrating YAHMP with teleoperation and learning-from-demonstration systems, enabling the development of coordinated manipulation skills and autonomous robot stacks that can execute tasks based on user instructions. This trajectory suggests a path toward humanoids performing intricate tasks in unstructured environments.
