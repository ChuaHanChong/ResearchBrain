# Rethinking the Practicality of Vision-language-action Model: A Comprehensive Benchmark and An Improved Baseline

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) and collaborators introduce CEBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model practicality across diverse robotic embodiments, and LLaVA-VLA, a lightweight, pre-training-free model capable of cross-embodiment mobile manipulation. LLaVA-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on single-arm tasks (e.g., 50.6% success on CALVIN's 5/5 sub-task), demonstrates robust generalization under domain randomization, and is the first end-to-end VLA model to successfully perform real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models possess excessive parameter scales, making them computationally intensive and difficult to deploy on resource-constrained platforms.
- Current VLAs rely on vast, large-scale robotic datasets for pre-training, leading to prohibitive computational resources and high training costs.
- Most existing VLAs are tailored for fixed-base manipulation, limiting their versatility and applicability to more complex scenarios like mobile manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **CEBench**, a ==cross-embodiment benchmark== for evaluating VLA practicality, including single-arm, bimanual, and mobile bimanual manipulation in simulated and real-world environments with ==domain randomization==.
- Proposed **LLaVA-VLA**, an improved baseline model designed to be lightweight (0.5B parameters) and ==pre-training-free==, built upon a compact ==LLaVA-OneVision== backbone.
- Implemented a ==unified hybrid action space== combining direction and value tokens to seamlessly handle both navigation and manipulation for cross-embodiment tasks.

## Results

- LLaVA-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark, surpassing larger models like OpenVLA 7B and RoboFlamingo 3B with a **50.6%** success rate on the final 5/5 sub-task.
- The model maintains a robust **28.6%** success rate in simulated bimanual DR tasks and **30.7%** in real-world DR bimanual tasks, outperforming diffusion and VAE-based baselines.
- LLaVA-VLA is the first end-to-end VLA model to handle real-world mobile manipulation, achieving **4/10** success on tasks like "Move and fetch bottles" with its hybrid action space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lightweight VLA models (0.5B parameters) can achieve performance comparable to or better than larger models (3B-7B) without the need for extensive, costly cross-embodiment pre-training.
> - Combining multi-view images (e.g., vertically concatenated first- and third-person views) with tokenized proprioceptive signals significantly enhances 3D spatial perception and state understanding in VLAs.
> - A two-stage training paradigm of post-training on in-domain multi-task data followed by fine-tuning is sufficient for establishing vision-to-action mapping and achieving high performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has seen rapid advancements, positioning them as a promising paradigm for generalist robotic agents capable of understanding complex language instructions and executing visuomotor control. Pioneering works like RT-1, RoboFlamingo, and OpenVLA have showcased the potential of end-to-end learning to interpret multimodal sensory data and generate robotic actions. These models integrate large language models (LLMs) and vision language models (VLMs) with action generation capabilities, aiming for broad generalization across diverse tasks.

Despite these breakthroughs, current VLA models face significant practical hurdles that impede their widespread adoption and deployment. The authors identify three critical limitations:
1. **Excessive Parameter Scales:** Many state-of-the-art VLAs possess billions of parameters, rendering them computationally intensive and challenging to deploy on resource-constrained platforms such as mobile robots or consumer-grade GPUs.
2. **Prohibitive Pre-training Requirements:** The reliance on vast, large-scale robotic datasets for pre-training demands substantial computational resources and incurs high training costs, making development inaccessible for many researchers and smaller labs.
3. **Limited Applicability to Diverse Embodiments:** Most existing VLAs are tailored for fixed-base manipulation tasks, limiting their versatility and preventing their application to more complex scenarios like mobile manipulation, where navigation and manipulation must be seamlessly integrated.

Prior research has attempted to address some of these issues. Efforts like TinyVLA, MiniVLA, NORA, and SmolVLA have focused on developing lightweight VLA architectures and exploring pre-training-free designs. However, as the authors highlight, these approaches often lack a systematic examination of the practicality of their designs and generally remain incapable of performing mobile manipulation tasks.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by proposing a holistic solution. It introduces **CEBench**, a comprehensive, cross-embodiment benchmark designed specifically to evaluate VLA practicality under diverse, real-world relevant conditions, including domain randomization and mobile manipulation. Furthermore, it presents **LLaVA-VLA**, an improved baseline model engineered to be lightweight, pre-training-free, and inherently capable of cross-embodiment mobile manipulation. By systematically studying design choices and offering empirical insights, this work aims to bridge the gap between powerful, but resource-intensive, VLA models and their practical, democratized deployment, thereby significantly advancing the accessibility and real-world utility of generalist robotic agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **improve the practicality of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** by making them more efficient, generalized, and versatile for real-world deployment. The authors are driven by the recognition that despite their impressive capabilities, existing VLAs are largely impractical for widespread adoption due to their heavy computational demands, high training costs, and limited applicability to diverse robotic embodiments, particularly mobile platforms.

To achieve this overarching goal, the paper outlines several key specific objectives:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive and Practical Robotic Benchmark (CEBench):** The first objective is to construct a new benchmark that addresses the shortcomings of existing evaluation methods. CEBench is designed to provide a "comprehensive and unbiased evaluation across embodiments, potential domain randomization, and mobile manipulation needs." This involves creating a dataset and evaluation framework that spans single-arm, bimanual, and mobile bimanual manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, with explicit consideration for visual and environmental variations (domain randomization).
2. **Conduct a Systematic Study on VLA Practicality:** The authors aim to empirically investigate three critical aspects influencing the practicality of VLAs:
 - **Q1: Lightweight Designs:** To what extent does model performance depend on parameter scale, and what techniques enable smaller models to rival larger counterparts?
 - **Q2: Training Curriculum:** Is large-scale cross-embodiment pre-training truly necessary for small models in specific scenarios?
 - **Q3: Unified Action Space:** How can a hybrid action space be defined to support cross-embodiment manipulation, including both fixed-base and mobile tasks?
 This systematic study seeks to provide valuable insights and empirical findings to guide the design of more practical VLA models.
3. **Propose an Improved, Lightweight, and Practical VLA Baseline (LLaVA-VLA):** Informed by the findings from their systematic study, the ultimate objective is to introduce a new VLA model, LLaVA-VLA. This baseline is designed to be lightweight (deployable on consumer-grade GPUs), pre-training-free (eliminating reliance on costly large-scale datasets), and capable of cross-embodiment tasks, particularly mobile manipulation. The goal is to demonstrate that high performance and generalization can be achieved without the "billions of parameters" or "extensive pre-training" currently common in the field.

In essence, the motivation stems from a desire to democratize VLA technology, making advanced robotic control more accessible, robust, and applicable to a wider range of real-world scenarios, thereby bridging the gap between theoretical advancements and practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is two-fold: first, the development of a novel comprehensive benchmark (CEBench), and second, the systematic investigation of VLA design choices leading to an improved lightweight baseline (LLaVA-VLA).

#### 3.1. CEBench: A Cross-Embodiment Benchmark

CEBench is introduced to address the limitations of existing benchmarks, which often lack comprehensive evaluation across diverse embodiments, domain randomization, and mobile manipulation tasks.
- **System Setup:**
 - **Single-arm manipulation:** Utilizes the established CALVIN benchmark, featuring a Franka Panda arm and a table environment for long-horizon, language-conditioned tasks.
 - **Bimanual manipulation:** Employs the RoboTwin benchmark, built on the Sapien simulator, specifically designed for evaluating positional generalization and visual robustness.
 - **Bimanual mobile manipulation:** Involves a real-world setup using the Cobot-Magic dual-arm mobile robot, equipped with four Piper robotic arms, capturing multi-view RGB images.
- **Datasets:**
 - **CALVIN:** Leverages official datasets for consistency.
 - **RoboTwin:** A large-scale simulated dataset was constructed, comprising 14.4k trajectories across 36 tasks, automatically generated under simplified, clutter-free scenarios.
 - **Real-world:** 1.6k expert-curated, high-quality real-world trajectories were collected across 8 tasks. These tasks range from basic pick-and-place (e.g., "Stack bowls") to complex bimanual interactions (e.g., "Lift pot") and crucially, include two mobile manipulation tasks ("Move and fetch bottles," "Move and open the drawer").
- **Evaluation and Metrics:**
 - **CALVIN:** Standard evaluation metrics include success rates for sub-tasks and average success length (ABC→D split, unseen scene).
 - **RoboTwin:** Evaluated on 8 representative tasks under both "seen" and "unseen" settings with extensive **domain randomization (DR)**, incorporating clutter, random lighting, diverse textures, and variable table heights.
 - **Real-world:** All collected tasks are evaluated, with DR settings simulated by varying tabletop colors/textures and introducing distractor objects.

#### 3.2. LLaVA-VLA: Model Architecture and Training Paradigm

LLaVA-VLA is designed as a lightweight, pre-training-free, and cross-embodiment VLA model, informed by the systematic study of three research questions (Q1-Q3).

- **Core Architecture:** LLaVA-VLA is built upon a compact **LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B** pre-trained VLM backbone.
 - **Input:** It takes concatenated multi-view images (combining first- and third-person perspectives) augmented with proprioceptive signals.
 - **Output:** It generates action chunks through an action tokenizer.
 - **Hybrid Action Space:** Crucially, it integrates a unified action space to seamlessly handle both navigation and manipulation. Navigation actions are represented by a direction token (forward, turn left, turn right, stop) followed by a value token (distance/angle). Manipulation actions are appended as value tokens when the direction token is 'stop'. A special `<Navigation>` token is used to prompt mobile manipulation tasks.

- **Systematic Study and Key Findings (F1-F8):** The authors empirically explore design choices that contribute to LLaVA-VLA's practicality:
 - **Q1: Lightweight Designs:**
 - **F1 (Model Scale):** Performance is not strictly proportional to parameter scale; small VLAs can achieve comparable performance to larger ones.
 - **F2 (Multi-view Images):** Multi-view images are critical for 3D spatial perception. Vertically concatenating first- and third-person views into a single composite image is an effective strategy to reduce tokens while preserving information.
 - **F3 (Proprioception):** Proprioception is vital for understanding physical states and maintaining action continuity. Tokenizing proprioception (via a proprioception tokenizer) is more effective than encoding it through linear layers (MLPs).
 - **F4 (Action Chunking):** Predicting action chunks strengthens the model's planning capability and improves action stability and temporal coherence.
 - **Q2: Training Curriculum:**
 - **F5 (Cross-embodiment Pre-training):** Large-scale cross-embodiment pre-training is not essential. A two-stage paradigm of **post-training on in-domain multi-task data** (to establish vision-to-action mapping) followed by **fine-tuning on task-specific data** is sufficient and effective, eliminating reliance on costly pre-training.
 - **Q3: Unified Action Space:**
 - **F6 (Discrete vs. Continuous Action Space):** Continuous action spaces (e.g., using diffusion heads) are not indispensable. Discrete actions, combined with action chunking, can achieve comparable performance while preserving the autoregressive nature of the model, beneficial for VLM/LLM integration.
 - **F7 (Action Granularity):** Fine-grained action tokenization does not necessarily lead to higher performance. Coarser discretization (e.g., 256 bins) can yield better efficiency and generalization than overly fine-grained options (e.g., 1024 bins).
 - **F8 (Unified Action Space Design):** A unified action space for mobile manipulation is best realized by combining direction tokens and value tokens, allowing the model to flexibly switch between navigation and manipulation in a stable manner.

- **Training Setup:** All post-training is performed on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, while fine-tuning is conducted on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU, demonstrating its deployability on consumer-grade hardware. Training involves a single epoch of post-training for CALVIN (approx. 6 hours) and 2 epochs of post-training followed by 8 epochs of fine-tuning for bimanual tasks in RoboTwin and the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LLaVA-VLA on the newly proposed CEBench benchmark yields several significant findings, demonstrating its practicality, generalization capabilities, and versatility across diverse robotic tasks and embodiments.

#### 4.1. Performance on Single-arm Manipulation (CALVIN Benchmark)

- LLaVA-VLA, with its compact 0.5B parameters and without large-scale robot-dataset pre-training, achieves state-of-the-art performance on CALVIN.
- It surpasses larger VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA 7B, RoboFlamingo 3B) in success rates across all sub-tasks and achieves the best average completed trajectory length (3.68). For instance, on the final 5/5 sub-task, LLaVA-VLA achieves 50.6% success, outperforming OpenVLA (43.5%) and RoboFlamingo (23.5%).
- It also outperforms 3D-aware baselines and models relying on large-scale video pre-training, demonstrating strong spatial reasoning and efficient learning from limited data. This supports **Finding 1 (F1)** that performance is not strictly proportional to parameter scale.

#### 4.2. Performance on Bimanual Manipulation (RoboTwin Benchmark)

- In simulation, LLaVA-VLA achieves a 40.3% success rate on seen tasks and a 28.6% success rate on domain-randomized (DR) tasks.
- It significantly outperforms diffusion-based (Diffusion Policy: 34.5% seen, 0.6% DR) and VAE-based (ACT: 7.1% seen, 0.5% DR) baselines, especially under DR settings.
- Crucially, LLaVA-VLA achieves higher success rates in the DR environment (28.6%) compared to larger VLAs like RDT (11.4% DR), indicating superior visual generalization and spatial comprehension capabilities under varying conditions. This reinforces **F1** and highlights the effectiveness of the proposed lightweight design choices.

#### 4.3. Performance on Bimanual Manipulation in the Real World

- For fixed-base bimanual tasks, LLaVA-VLA consistently outperforms ACT and TinyVLA across 6 real-world tasks. Its average success rate is 44.2% on seen tasks and 30.7% on DR tasks.
- Under unseen scenarios with background variations and distractor objects (DR settings), ACT and TinyVLA suffer dramatic performance drops, nearing zero. In contrast, LLaVA-VLA maintains a robust 30.7% success rate, showcasing its strong robustness and visual generalization in practical, real-world conditions.

#### 4.4. Performance on Mobile Manipulation Tasks in the Real World

- LLaVA-VLA is demonstrated as the **first end-to-end VLA model capable of handling mobile manipulation tasks**.
- In tasks like "Move and fetch bottles" and "Move and open the drawer," LLaVA-VLA achieves a 4/10 success rate with its proposed "Direction + Value Token" hybrid action space.
- The baseline using ACT (adapted for mobile ALOHA) struggles, achieving only 2/10 and 1/10 successes respectively, primarily due to low navigation accuracy and lack of multi-task learning capabilities. This outcome directly validates **Finding 8 (F8)** regarding the effectiveness of the unified action space.

#### 4.5. Key Insights from Systematic Study (Q1-Q3)

- **F1 (Model Scale) & F5 (Training Curriculum):** Lightweight models (0.5B) can match/surpass larger (7B) models, and costly large-scale pre-training is not essential if post-training is performed on high-quality, in-domain multi-task data followed by fine-tuning.
- **F2 (Multi-view Images) & F3 (Proprioception):** Vertically concatenating multi-view images and tokenizing proprioception are critical design choices that significantly boost performance by providing better 3D perception and state understanding.
- **F4 (Action Chunking):** An action chunking size of 5 yields optimal average trajectory length, indicating improved planning and action stability.
- **F6 (Discrete vs. Continuous Actions) & F7 (Action Granularity):** Discrete action spaces with chunking achieve comparable performance to continuous diffusion heads while preserving autoregressivity. Furthermore, a coarser granularity (256 bins) for action tokenization is more effective than finer granularity (1024 bins), balancing precision and generalization.
- **F8 (Unified Action Space):** The proposed hybrid action space (direction + value tokens) proves stable and effective for unifying navigation and manipulation, enabling flexible switching between these modalities.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that LLaVA-VLA successfully addresses the practicality concerns of VLAs, delivering strong performance, remarkable generalization under domain randomization, and pioneering mobile manipulation capabilities, all within a lightweight and pre-training-free framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step towards democratizing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, making them more accessible, efficient, and applicable for real-world robotic systems. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Democratization of VLAs:** By demonstrating that lightweight (0.5B parameters) and pre-training-free models can match or surpass larger, pre-trained counterparts, LLaVA-VLA drastically lowers the barrier to entry for VLA research and deployment. This enables researchers and developers with consumer-grade GPUs to develop and deploy advanced robotic agents, fostering innovation across a broader community. This directly addresses the critical issue of prohibitive computational costs and reliance on vast, expensive datasets.

2. **Enhanced Real-World Applicability and Robustness:**
 - **Mobile Manipulation Breakthrough:** LLaVA-VLA is established as the **first end-to-end VLA model capable of mobile manipulation**. This is a major leap, extending VLA capabilities beyond fixed-base manipulation to embodied systems that require both navigation and dexterous interaction. This opens up new avenues for applications in logistics, assistance robotics, and service robots within dynamic environments.
 - **Superior Generalization under Domain Randomization:** The model's strong performance under domain randomization (both in simulation and the real world) highlights its robustness to unseen visual variations. This is crucial for deploying robots in unpredictable real-world settings where exact replication of training conditions is impossible.

3. **Foundational Contributions to VLA Design:**
 - **CEBench: A New Standard for Practicality Evaluation:** The introduction of CEBench provides a much-needed, comprehensive benchmark that incorporates cross-embodiment tasks, domain randomization, and mobile manipulation. This will serve as a standardized and rigorous testbed for future VLA models, driving research towards more practical and deployable solutions.
 - **Empirical Roadmap for Lightweight VLAs:** The systematic study yielding **eight key findings (F1-F8)** offers invaluable empirical insights into the optimal design choices for lightweight, practical VLAs. These insights — covering model scale, multi-view perception, proprioception handling, action chunking, training curricula, and unified action spaces — provide a clear roadmap for future research, accelerating the development of more efficient and effective VLA architectures.

4. **Reduced Training Costs and Environmental Impact:** By proving that large-scale cross-embodiment pre-training is not essential and advocating for a two-stage post-training and fine-tuning paradigm, the paper offers a more resource-efficient training strategy. This not only saves computational costs but also aligns with efforts to reduce the carbon footprint of AI research.

5. **Accelerating Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to open-source all datasets, codes, and checkpoints upon acceptance is a vital contribution. This will allow other researchers to reproduce the results, build upon LLaVA-VLA, and further explore the design space of practical VLAs, fostering collaborative progress in the field.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the practicality of VLA models by addressing core limitations in computational demands, training complexity, and functional versatility. By providing both a rigorous benchmark and an effective, lightweight baseline, it lays a strong foundation for the next generation of generalist robotic agents that are truly ready for real-world deployment.
