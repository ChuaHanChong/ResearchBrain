# MotionPro: A Precise Motion Controller for Image-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionPro, developed by researchers at the University of Science and Technology of China and HiDream.ai Inc., introduces a precise motion controller for image-to-video generation by using region-wise trajectories and motion masks as control signals. The framework, integrated into a Stable Video Diffusion model via adaptive feature modulation, achieves superior motion-trajectory alignment, evidenced by an FVD of 59.88 on WebVid-10M and lower Mean Distance scores on the new MC-Bench dataset.

## Problem

- Existing controllable video diffusion models struggle to achieve fine-grained motion control due to coarse approximations and instability of pixel-level trajectories.
- Ambiguity in distinguishing between object movement and camera movement from a single trajectory input often leads to motion misinterpretation.
- A lack of dedicated benchmarks exists for evaluating fine-grained and object-level motion control under realistic user interaction scenarios.

## Method

- A novel motion control paradigm is introduced using region-wise trajectories to capture and leverage accurate, fine-grained local motion details.
- A motion mask is proposed as a complementary control signal to explicitly emphasize the motion area, disambiguating between object and camera movements.
- These control signals are integrated into a ==Stable Video Diffusion== (SVD) model through adaptive feature modulation and ==LoRA== integration within attention modules for enhanced motion-trajectory alignment.

## Results

- MotionPro achieved an FVD of **59.88** on WebVid-**10M**, outperforming MOFA-Video (**87.70**) and DragNUWA (**96.65**), indicating closer alignment with ground-truth video distributions.
- On the MC-Bench dataset, MotionPro demonstrated lower Mean Distance values for both fine-grained (MD-Vid **8.34**) and object-level (MD-Vid **8.59**) motion control, signifying improved trajectory alignment.
- Human evaluations showed MotionPro was preferred for motion quality, temporal coherence, and trajectory alignment across fine-grained and object-level tasks, with preference ratios ranging from 53.12% to 75.00% over baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining region-level trajectories with a motion mask provides a robust and precise way to control both the granularity and category of motion in image-to-video generation.
> - Adaptive feature modulation effectively integrates spatial-temporal motion conditions into the diffusion model's latent space, guiding denoising towards precise alignment without strict pixel-level correspondence.
> - The new MC-Bench benchmark, comprising 1.1K user-annotated image-trajectory pairs, fills a gap in evaluation by reflecting real-world user intentions for motion control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced significant advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion models for both text-to-video (T2V) and image-to-video (I2V) synthesis. While these models can generate visually coherent content, precisely controlling the motion within the synthesized videos remains a challenge.

Early efforts in controllable video diffusion models utilized conditions such as depth, edge maps, or optical flow. However, providing these detailed signals can be impractical for general users. This led to a focus on more user-friendly conditions, including bounding boxes and sparse trajectories. Methods like DragNUWA and DragAnything have explored guiding video denoising with Gaussian-filtered point-wise trajectories. This approach aims to mitigate pixel-level trajectory instability by extending flow through large Gaussian kernels. However, this Gaussian filtering can introduce inaccuracies in fine-grained motion details, limiting precise control. Additionally, relying solely on trajectory information often fails to explicitly differentiate between object movement and camera movement, potentially leading to motion misinterpretation. For instance, a trajectory on a static object could be interpreted as either the object moving or the camera moving relative to a static object.

More recent works, such as Motion-I2V and MOFA-Video, have introduced two-stage motion control frameworks that first densify sparse input trajectories and then use these dense trajectories to regulate video denoising. MOFA-Video also incorporates a movement region mask, but its application is primarily for flow masking as a post-processing step rather than an integrated conditional input for motion-region-aware video generation. This approach can result in less natural motions and localized distortions.

MotionPro positions itself within this landscape by addressing the identified limitations of existing trajectory-based control methods. It aims to achieve more precise and unambiguous motion control in I2V generation by proposing a novel formulation of control signals: region-wise trajectories and a motion mask. This work seeks to improve both the accuracy of motion expression and the naturalness of generated motion by integrating these signals directly into the video denoising process, rather than relying on coarse approximations or post-processing. Furthermore, the paper contributes a new benchmark, MC-Bench, specifically designed to evaluate fine-grained and object-level motion control, acknowledging the limitations of existing datasets in representing typical user interaction scenarios. The proposed methodology builds upon advanced I2V diffusion models, specifically Stable Video Diffusion (SVD), by adapting its architecture for enhanced motion control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a precise motion controller for image-to-video (I2V) generation that facilitates interactive and fine-grained motion control using user-friendly input conditions. The work aims to overcome several limitations observed in existing controllable I2V synthesis approaches.

The motivation for MotionPro stems from two main issues identified in current trajectory-guided video generation methods:

1. **Lack of Fine-grained Motion Control:** Existing methods, such as those relying on Gaussian-filtered trajectories, typically extend sparse trajectories using large Gaussian kernels. While this helps smooth out pixel-level instability, it can lead to a loss of fine-grained motion details. This coarse control prevents the generation of natural and accurate subtle movements within a video. For example, a slight head turn might appear unnatural if the motion control signal is too diffuse. The paper motivates the need for a mechanism that can preserve and utilize precise local motion information.

2. **Ambiguity in Motion Category Identification:** A single trajectory as a conditional input often fails to clearly distinguish between an object moving within a static scene and the camera moving relative to a static object. This ambiguity can lead to misinterpretation during video generation, where the model might synthesize a camera movement instead of an intended object movement, or vice versa. For instance, a trajectory on a planet could imply either the planet itself rising or the camera being pulled downwards. The research identifies the need for an additional signal that can explicitly define the target motion category (object or camera movement) and emphasize the areas of movement.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of MotionPro are:

- To develop a novel motion control paradigm that leverages **region-level trajectories** to capture and utilize accurate motion details, thereby enabling fine-grained motion control.
- To introduce a **motion mask** as a complementary control signal to globally emphasize the motion area, explicitly identifying the target motion category and preventing motion misinterpretation.
- To integrate these proposed control signals (region-wise trajectory and motion mask) into the video denoising process of a diffusion model through an effective **feature modulation mechanism**, ensuring natural motion synthesis and precise alignment with user-provided trajectories.
- To improve **motion-trajectory alignment** by fine-tuning attention modules within the base diffusion model using techniques like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA).
- To construct a **dedicated benchmark, MC-Bench**, comprising user-annotated image-trajectory pairs, for a robust evaluation of both fine-grained and object-level I2V motion control under realistic user interaction scenarios, as existing datasets often rely on automatically sampled trajectories that do not fully reflect practical use cases.

By achieving these objectives, MotionPro aims to provide a more precise, intuitive, and robust interactive motion control framework for I2V generation, enhancing both the quality and controllability of synthesized videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionPro is designed as a precise motion controller for controllable Image-to-Video (I2V) generation, built upon a pre-trained video diffusion model, Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). The framework introduces novel motion conditions—region-wise trajectory and a motion mask—and integrates them into the denoising process through adaptive feature modulation and LoRA integration.

The overall architecture of MotionPro comprises two main pipelines: training and inference, with a core component dedicated to motion condition generation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)

MotionPro utilizes SVD as its base architecture. SVD is an I2V generation model that operates by denoising latent video codes. Given an input video `x_0`, VAE extracts clean video latent codes `z_0`. Gaussian noise `n` is added to `z_0` to create `z`. A 3D-UNet `F_θ` then predicts `z_0` from `z`, noise level `σ`, and a reference image `c_I`. The reference image information is incorporated via channel-wise concatenation with noisy video latents and cross-attention with the image CLIP embedding. The model is trained using denoising score matching (DSM). MotionPro extends this by adding region-wise trajectory and motion mask as additional control signals.

#### 3.2. Motion Condition Generation

This is a critical component that generates the control signals for the diffusion model.

- **Region-wise Trajectory:**
 - **Optical Flow and Visibility Mask Estimation:** For an input video clip `x_0` (L frames), a dense optical tracking model (DOT, specifically DOT) is used to estimate per-frame optical flow maps `f_i` and corresponding visibility masks `M_i` between the first frame and the `i`-th frame.
 - **Global Visibility Mask:** A global visibility mask `M_g` is computed by taking the intersection of all `M_i` across the temporal dimension. This mask identifies pixels with visible optical flow throughout the video.
 - **Masked Flow Maps:** The estimated flow maps `f_i` are multiplied element-wise by the global visibility mask `M_g` to obtain masked flow maps `f_m`.
 - **Region Sampling:** The masked flow maps `f_m` are spatially divided into multiple local regions of size `k × k`. Region-wise trajectories `T_s` are sampled from these regions using a region selection mask `M_sel`. `M_sel` is uniformly sampled from {0,1} with a random mask ratio `r_m` (in the range `[r_min, 1.0]`) to simulate diverse real-world motion scenarios and enhance network robustness. A padding function `Pad(·)` is applied to fill the mask value into the `k × k` region around each selected position. This process yields precise, localized trajectories.

- **Motion Mask:**
 - **Average Flow Magnitude:** The average flow magnitude `f_avg` is calculated across the temporal dimension for all estimated flow maps.
 - **Binary Mask Creation:** A binary motion mask `M_mot` is constructed by setting pixels to True where `f_avg` exceeds a threshold (e.g., 1). This mask captures the holistic dynamic regions of movement.
 - **Temporal Alignment:** `M_mot` is repeated L times to create a sequence `M_mot` that matches the temporal length of the input video for subsequent control learning. This mask aims to specify the target motion category (object or camera).

#### 3.3. Motion Control Learning

The generated motion conditions are integrated into the SVD architecture.

- **Adaptive Feature Modulation:**
 - The region-wise trajectory `T_s` and motion mask `M_mot` are concatenated along the channel dimension to form the input condition.
 - A lightweight motion encoder, composed of convolutional layers, processes this concatenated input to generate multi-scale feature maps `l_s`.
 - At each scale `s` of the 3D-UNet, the corresponding feature map `l_s` from the motion encoder is used to predict a scale `γ_s` and a bias `β_s` via a spatial-temporal convolutional layer.
 - These `γ_s` and `β_s` values then modulate the normalized video latent feature `h_s` from the 3D-UNet: `h'_s = GN(h_s) ⋅ γ_s + β_s + h_s`, where `GN(·)` is group normalization. A skip connection `+ h_s` is included. Zero initialization is applied to the temporal convolutional layers for `γ_s` and `β_s` to ensure stable model optimization at the outset. This adaptive modulation allows the motion conditions to dynamically influence the video latent representations.

- **LoRA Integration:**
 - To preserve the pre-trained motion prior within SVD and enhance the alignment between synthesized motion and input trajectories, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) layers are integrated into all attention modules (self-attention, cross-attention, temporal attention) of the spatial-temporal transformer blocks within the 3D-UNet. LoRA modifies the original weights `W` by adding a low-rank update `∆W = AB^T`, where `A` and `B` are trainable matrices.

#### 3.4. Inference Pipeline

MotionPro is designed for user interaction during inference.
- Users upload a reference image, brush a motion region, and draw a desired movement trajectory.
- The brushed region directly forms the motion mask.
- The user-drawn trajectory, typically pixel-level, is padded within a `k × k` region around the pixel position to become the region-wise trajectory, mirroring the training paradigm.
- These two control signals guide the iterative denoising process in MotionPro, leading to controlled I2V generation.

#### 3.5. Benchmark Construction: MC-Bench

Recognizing the limitations of existing datasets for evaluating practical motion control, the authors constructed MC-Bench. This benchmark comprises 1.1K user-annotated image-trajectory pairs, including diverse visual content and complicated trajectories, specifically tailored for evaluating both fine-grained and object-level I2V motion control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MotionPro was evaluated extensively on two benchmarks: WebVid-10M for fine-grained motion control and the newly introduced MC-Bench for both fine-grained and object-level motion control. The evaluation metrics included Frechet Video Distance (FVD), Frechet Image Distance (FID), Frame Consistency (Frame Consis.), Mean Distance (MD-Img), and Mean Distance for Video (MD-Vid).

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Fine-grained Motion Control

- **WebVid-10M:** MotionPro exhibited performance across all metrics. It achieved an FVD of 59.88, a significant reduction compared to MOFA-Video (87.70) and DragNUWA (96.65). FID values were 10.40 for MotionPro, lower than MOFA-Video (12.18) and DragNUWA (13.19). Frame Consistency was comparable to MOFA-Video (0.9895 vs. 0.9894) and slightly higher than DragNUWA (0.9888). These results suggest a closer alignment between generated videos and ground-truth distributions.
- **MC-Bench (Fine-grained):** MotionPro achieved lower Mean Distance values, with MD-Img at 10.56 and MD-Vid at 8.34. These were lower than MOFA-Video (MD-Img: 13.94, MD-Vid: 10.50) and DragDiffusion (MD-Img: 14.70, MD-Vid: 13.84). Frame Consistency was 0.9962 for MotionPro, slightly lower than MOFA-Video (0.9972) but higher than DragDiffusion (0.9947). The lower MD values indicate better alignment between the generated motion and the input trajectory. The paper notes that MOFA-Video tends to produce videos with less motion dynamics (average flow magnitude 4.95) compared to MotionPro (average flow magnitude 8.95), suggesting MotionPro generates more variable motion while maintaining alignment.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fine-grained):** Visual examples on MC-Bench demonstrated that MotionPro generates more natural movements and better aligns with input trajectories. Baselines like DragNUWA occasionally exhibited motion misinterpretation (e.g., generating camera movement instead of object movement). MOFA-Video sometimes showed unnatural object movement with localized distortions, which the authors attribute to its region mask being used only for post-processing rather than integrated conditional guidance. MotionPro, by integrating the motion mask within the 3D-UNet, reportedly achieves more rational fine-grained movements.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Object-level Motion Control

- **MC-Bench (Object-level):** MotionPro achieved the lowest MD-Img (10.48) and MD-Vid (8.59) among the compared methods, outperforming DragAnything (MD-Img: 12.30, MD-Vid: 11.37) and MOFA-Video (MD-Img: 15.56, MD-Vid: 12.04). Frame Consistency was 0.9943, comparable to MOFA-Video (0.9951) and higher than DragAnything (0.9917). These quantitative results support the effectiveness of MotionPro's approach for precise object-level motion control.
- **Qualitative Observations (Object-level):** Visual comparisons indicated that MotionPro generated videos that precisely matched the input trajectory and maintained natural object-level motion dynamics. It was observed to handle complicated trajectories (e.g., round trips) and counterintuitive movements (e.g., objects moving backward). MOFA-Video continued to show local part distortion and limited motion dynamics. DragAnything, while aligning pixel movement, sometimes misinterpreted trajectories as camera motion rather than object motion. MotionPro's use of the region mask to specify motion category appeared to mitigate such misinterpretations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on MotionPro

- **Local Region Size (k):** Varying the local region size `k` for trajectory design on MC-Bench showed that `k=8` resulted in optimal performance for MD-Vid. Smaller `k` (e.g., 1 or 2) weakened control signals, leading to inferior trajectory matching. Larger `k` (e.g., 16) extended trajectories over larger regions, diminishing fine-grained control accuracy. Frame Consistency remained largely stable across different `k` values.
- **Minimal Mask Ratio (r_min):** The minimal mask ratio `r_min` in trajectory selection was investigated. An `r_min` of 0.95 yielded favorable motion control performance. Very small `r_min` (more sampled trajectories during training) could create a mismatch with real-world inference scenarios (few trajectories), while very large `r_min` (scarce trajectory signals) made network optimization difficult.
- **Multi-scale Feature Injection:** Comparison of feature injection strategies (concatenation, summation, and adaptive feature modulation) demonstrated that adaptive feature modulation in MotionPro achieved superior MD-Vid performance. This is attributed to its ability to indirectly regulate features without strict spatial-temporal alignment requirements, enhancing information extraction from control signals.
- **Control Signals:** Disabling either the region-wise trajectory (replacing with random trajectory) or the motion mask (using all-one masks) resulted in degraded FVD scores on WebVid-10M (73.7 and 66.2, respectively, compared to 59.88 for full MotionPro), indicating the importance of both components.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation

A human evaluation conducted on MC-Bench across 200 randomly sampled videos showed that MotionPro was preferred over baselines in terms of motion quality, temporal coherence, and trajectory alignment for both fine-grained and object-level motion control. MotionPro received user preference ratios ranging from 53.12% to 75.00% across different criteria, consistently outperforming competitors.

#### 4.5. Robustness and Applications

The motion mask was demonstrated to be robust to diverse shapes at inference, attributed to its training with estimated (not ground-truth precise) flow maps. MotionPro also supports applications such as simultaneous object and camera motion control (by using all-ones mask and multiple trajectories) and camera control with camera pose sequences (by converting poses to sparse trajectories). Runtime performance of MotionPro (17 seconds for a 16-frame video) was comparable to or faster than most baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "MotionPro: A Precise Motion Controller for Image-to-Video Generation" contributes to the field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of controllable video synthesis, through several key advancements and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Technical Advancement in Motion Control

MotionPro introduces a novel approach to motion condition formulation by combining **region-wise trajectories** and a **motion mask**. This dual-signal strategy directly addresses the limitations of prior methods that relied on coarse, Gaussian-filtered trajectories and struggled with motion category disambiguation. The region-wise trajectories provide fine-grained local motion details, while the motion mask explicitly delineates the dynamic regions, allowing the model to distinguish between object and camera movements more effectively. The integration of these signals via **adaptive feature modulation** into the diffusion model's U-Net, complemented by **LoRA integration** for attention modules, represents a refined method for guiding video denoising towards precise motion-trajectory alignment.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Precision and Naturalness in Video Generation

The quantitative results, particularly the improved FVD and lower Mean Distance values on WebVid-10M and MC-Bench, indicate that MotionPro achieves superior alignment between generated motion and user-provided trajectories. Qualitatively, the generated videos exhibit more natural movement dynamics and reduced artifacts such as local part distortions or incorrect motion interpretations seen in other models. This enhanced precision allows for a broader range of complex and subtle movements to be accurately controlled.

#### 5.3. Improved User Experience and Practicality

By allowing users to define motion through intuitive brushed regions and drawn trajectories, MotionPro offers a more user-friendly interface for interactive video generation. This approach is more accessible than requiring complex inputs like depth maps or dense optical flow fields. The ability to control both fine-grained and object-level motion, along with its demonstrated robustness to variations in mask shapes, enhances the practical applicability of the system.

#### 5.4. Introduction of a Specialized Benchmark (MC-Bench)

The creation and release of MC-Bench, a benchmark with 1.1K user-annotated image-trajectory pairs, is a significant contribution to the research community. This dataset addresses a crucial gap by providing evaluation scenarios that more closely reflect real-world user intentions for both fine-grained and object-level motion control, moving beyond automatically sampled trajectories. MC-Bench can serve as a valuable tool for future research and development in controllable I2V generation, enabling more relevant and robust comparative studies.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact and Applications

The capabilities demonstrated by MotionPro have the potential to impact various fields:
- **Content Creation:** Artists and designers could leverage MotionPro to animate static images or existing video frames with precise, custom movements, accelerating the production of animations, visual effects, and marketing materials.
- **Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality:** More realistic and controllable dynamic content could enhance immersive experiences.
- **Simulation and Robotics:** Generating videos with specific object or camera movements could aid in synthetic data generation for training and testing.
- **Education:** Creating illustrative animations of physical phenomena or processes.

The exploration of applications like simultaneous object/camera control and camera control from pose sequences further highlights the versatility and potential extendability of the MotionPro framework, suggesting its principles could be adapted for diverse video manipulation tasks beyond simple trajectory following. Overall, MotionPro represents a notable step towards making high-quality, precisely controllable video generation more accessible and robust.
