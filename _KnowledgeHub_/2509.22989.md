# Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign established a theory-driven framework using Bayesian persuasion to evaluate and enhance the strategic persuasive capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Their work demonstrated that frontier LLMs exhibit sophisticated strategic information disclosure, and reinforcement learning effectively improved persuasion gains in smaller models, generalizing across different LLM receivers.

## Problem

- Existing methods for evaluating LLM persuasive capabilities are often subjective, resource-intensive, and lack a unified theoretical foundation, leading to fragmented findings.
- The inherent heterogeneity of persuasion across contexts makes systematic study and generalization of LLM persuasive power challenging.
- There is a need for a scalable and principled framework to understand, evaluate, and improve LLMs' strategic persuasion, moving beyond ad-hoc empirical observations.

## Method

- Introduced a theory-driven framework, grounded in ==Bayesian persuasion==, to measure LLM persuasive capabilities by modeling persuasion as strategic information transmission.
- Constructed a scalable benchmark by repurposing human-human persuasion datasets into controlled multi-agent environments where LLMs act as rational senders and receivers.
- Developed a reinforcement learning framework (PPO, GRPO) to train smaller LLMs for strategic persuasion, optimizing for the sender's expected utility (persuasion gains).

## Results

- Larger, frontier LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4o) consistently achieved higher persuasion gains, with DeepSeek-R1 showing average gains of **0.23** in static and 1.27 in dynamic settings on a 7-point Likert scale.
- RL-trained small LLMs (Llama-3.2-**3B**-Instruct) achieved significantly higher persuasion gains compared to their untuned versions, with some results comparable to larger, untuned models, and these gains transferred to different receiver architectures.
- A human study validated the plausibility of LLMs as rational receivers, with human annotators confirming that LLM belief updates were directionally appropriate (**77.18%** accuracy in Turn 1, increasing to **85.23%** in Turn 3) and proportionally plausible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier LLMs exhibit sophisticated strategic behaviors in information disclosure, adapting their signaling strategies over time in dynamic settings, consistent with theoretical predictions from dynamic Bayesian persuasion.
> - Reinforcement learning can significantly enhance the strategic persuasive capabilities of even smaller LLMs, and these learned improvements generalize effectively to different, unseen LLM receiver architectures.
> - Persuasion is most effective when the receiver's prior beliefs are at intermediate levels, aligning with Bayesian persuasion theory, as these are the contexts where information has the greatest potential to shift decisions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the emerging capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in persuasion, a domain with significant societal implications. Persuasion has long been recognized as a fundamental aspect of economic and political systems, influencing individual beliefs and behaviors across various sectors such as advertising, voting, security, and public health. Historical discourse on persuasion has debated its consequences, ranging from potential manipulation by elites to its role in providing useful, efficiency-enhancing information.

Recent studies have demonstrated LLMs' capacity to generate persuasive arguments comparable to human-written content. Evaluations, such as OpenAI's classification of GPT-4o's persuasive abilities as a "medium" risk, highlight both the potential benefits (e.g., in public health messaging, marketing) and substantial risks (e.g., influencing political views) associated with these capabilities.

However, the systematic study of LLMs' persuasive capabilities is challenging. Empirical evidence in human-human persuasion reveals heterogeneous effects across different domains and contexts, making generalization difficult. Prior research on LLM persuasiveness has relied on various evaluation setups and metrics, often leading to fragmented and conceptually unclear findings. Human evaluation, while valuable, is subjective and resource-intensive, while existing model-based evaluations have shown limited correlation with human judgments.

To address these challenges, the paper situates its work within the theoretical framework of game theory, specifically Bayesian persuasion. This paradigm, established by Kamenica & Gentzkow (2011), offers a rigorous foundation for conceptualizing persuasion as strategic information transmission between informed senders and rational receivers. Bayesian persuasion provides a systematic framework to analyze how an informed sender can shape a receiver's decisions through strategic information provision, even when the receiver anticipates the sender's motives. This theoretical lens has been successfully applied to analyze selective information disclosure in various domains.

This paper bridges the gap between the empirical observations of LLM persuasive power and the rigorous theoretical foundations of strategic information design. By adopting a theory-driven approach, it aims to provide a scalable and principled framework for understanding, evaluating, and enhancing the strategic persuasive capabilities of LLMs, moving beyond ad-hoc empirical observations to a more systematic scientific understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a systematic and principled framework for studying the persuasive capabilities of Large Language Models. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Challenge of Systematic Evaluation:** The effectiveness of persuasion varies significantly across domains and contexts, even among humans. This heterogeneity makes it inherently challenging to systematically evaluate the persuasive capabilities of LLMs. Existing empirical methods for evaluating LLM persuasiveness, often relying on human or automatic assessments, frequently suffer from subjectivity, high cost, and a lack of conceptual clarity or generalizability. These limitations result in disparate findings that are difficult to compare or integrate into a cohesive understanding.

2. **Need for Conceptual Clarity and Operational Rigor:** Previous empirical studies on LLM persuasion have utilized diverse settings and metrics, leading to fragmented and sometimes inconsistent findings. There is a need for a unified theoretical foundation that can provide both conceptual clarity and operational rigor to the study of LLM-driven persuasion.

3. **Leveraging Game Theory:** The authors motivate their approach by drawing on established foundations in game theory, particularly Bayesian persuasion theory. This theory conceptualizes persuasion as strategic information transmission between agents, offering a robust framework for identifying when and how an informed sender can influence a rational receiver's decisions. The authors contend that such a theory-driven approach can provide the necessary clarity and rigor missing in current LLM persuasion research.

4. **Understanding LLMs as Strategic Agents:** The paper aims to investigate whether LLMs can not only generate persuasive text but also exhibit sophisticated strategic behaviors in information revelation that align with theoretical predictions from game theory. This includes examining their ability to adapt disclosure strategies in dynamic environments.

5. **Developing Scalable Evaluation and Training Methods:** Given the cost and subjectivity of human evaluation, a key motivation is to develop scalable methods for both evaluating and improving LLMs' persuasive capabilities. This involves repurposing existing datasets into controlled multi-agent interaction environments and exploring reinforcement learning techniques for training LLMs in strategic persuasion.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Introduce a theory-driven framework, grounded in Bayesian persuasion, for measuring LLM persuasive capabilities.
- Instantiate this framework into scalable evaluation and training environments using repurposed human-human persuasion datasets.
- Evaluate the strategic persuasion capabilities of frontier LLMs within these environments.
- Demonstrate whether reinforcement learning can effectively enhance the strategic persuasion capabilities of LLMs, including smaller models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a theory-driven approach, grounded in Bayesian persuasion, to establish a framework for evaluating and training LLMs as strategic persuaders.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Background

The foundation of the methodology is **Bayesian Persuasion Theory** (Kamenica & Gentzkow, 2011), which models persuasion as strategic information transmission.
- **Two-Player Setting:** Involves a Sender (who wants to influence) and a Receiver (who makes decisions based on beliefs).
- **Components:** A finite state space (Ω), a finite action space (A), and utility functions for both Receiver (u) and Sender (v). A common prior belief (μ0) over the state of nature is known.
- **Process:** The Sender commits to a signaling scheme (π: Ω → ∆(S)), which generates a signal (s) based on the true state (ω). The Receiver observes the signal, updates their belief to a posterior (μs) using Bayes' rule, and then chooses an action (a*) to maximize their expected utility. The Sender's objective is to maximize their expected utility given the Receiver's best-response action.
- **Sender's Optimization:** The Sender chooses a Bayes-plausible distribution over posterior beliefs (τ) that maximizes their expected payoff, effectively "concavifying" their payoff function over the belief simplex. This framework predicts that optimal persuasion often involves carefully designed partial transparency.
- **Dynamic Bayesian Persuasion:** The paper briefly introduces extensions to dynamic environments (Ely, 2017), where the state evolves stochastically, and the Sender commits to a sequence of signal policies over time. The Receiver is assumed to be myopic, and the optimal mechanism often involves delayed signal policies (further details in Appendix A).

#### 3.2. Evaluation Approach

The persuasive capabilities of LLMs are conceptualized as the Sender's ability to strategically reveal information to align the Receiver's decisions with the Sender's objectives. Two key metrics are used:
- **Persuasion Gains:** Measured as the Sender's expected utility benefit.
 - `Δˆv(μ)`: The Sender's payoff when inducing posterior `μ` relative to the payoff under the prior `μ0`.
 - `ΔV(μ0)`: The optimal persuasion gain, representing the maximum expected payoff achievable by the Sender relative to the payoff under the prior.
- **Persuasion Signals:** Measured to assess adaptive information disclosure in dynamic environments. Conditional mutual information `I(mt; ωt | Ht-1)` quantifies how much state-relevant information an LLM reveals given past interactions. In experiments, semantic similarity is used as a proxy for this measure.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction

To operationalize the framework, a benchmark focused on opinion change tasks is constructed:
- **Task Formulation:** The Sender aims to shift the Receiver's stance on a claim. The Receiver's action space is a set of discrete stance categories (e.g., a 7-point Likert scale). The Receiver minimizes an expected loss function `ℓ(a, ω)`, while the Sender's utility `v(a, ω)` is a simple score-mapping that assigns higher scores to actions favoring the target position. Both static (single-round) and dynamic (multi-round) persuasion settings are considered.
- **Dataset Processing:** Established human-human persuasion datasets are repurposed: Anthropic, DDO, Perspectrum, and CMV. For datasets without explicit claims, LLMs (e.g., Llama3.3-70B-Instruct) are used to summarize the focal claims (Appendix D). A subset of 375 instances is used for evaluation.
- **Environment Construction (LLMs as Receivers):** LLMs are used as proxies for the rational Receivers. This is supported by prior research indicating LLMs can approximate Bayesian inference (Zhang et al., 2025a; Agarwal et al., 2026; Chlon et al., 2025). Prompts for LLM Senders and Receivers are provided (Appendix C).
- **Human Study Validation:** A human study with 45 participants and 149 transcripts (DeepSeek-R1 Sender, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct Receiver, 3 rounds) was conducted to empirically assess the plausibility of LLMs as Receiver proxies. Participants evaluated the direction and proportion of Receiver LLM belief updates.

#### 3.4. Training Language Models in Strategic Persuasion

The paper introduces a reinforcement learning (RL) framework to train LLMs for strategic persuasion:
- **Setup:** Both Sender and Receiver are LLMs. The Sender LLM (policy `πθ`) is prompted with the prior, utilities, action space, and true state to generate messages. The Receiver LLM (fixed policy `ρϕ`) responds with an action.
- **Reward Function:** The reward `r(ω, m, a)` is defined directly as persuasion gains: `v(a, ω) - ˆv(μ0)`, where positive rewards indicate successful persuasion.
- **Objective:** Maximize the expected persuasion reward `J(θ)` over a distribution of persuasion contexts.
- **Algorithms:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are used.
- **Models:** Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct is trained, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is used as the fixed Receiver during training.
- **Evaluation:** Generalization of learned persuasive capabilities is tested against multiple Receiver architectures (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the persuasive capabilities of LLMs and the effectiveness of reinforcement learning in enhancing them.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Strategic Persuasion with Language Models

- **Model Performance:** Larger, frontier models demonstrated higher persuasion gains compared to smaller models in both static (single-round) and dynamic (multi-round) persuasion settings. DeepSeek-R1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and GPT-4o consistently achieved higher scores. For instance, DeepSeek-R1 showed average gains of 0.23 in static settings and 1.27 in dynamic settings on a 7-point Likert scale. These translate to approximately 3.29% and 18.14% of the full Sender's expected utility scale, respectively.
- **Impact of Dynamic Settings:** Persuasion gains were modest in static contexts but significantly increased in dynamic settings. This indicates that the ability to adaptively deploy strategies across multiple rounds of interaction substantially enhances an LLM's persuasive power.

#### 4.2. Training Language Models to be Strategic Persuaders

- **RL Effectiveness:** Small LLMs (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) trained using reinforcement learning (PPO and GRPO) achieved significantly higher persuasion gains compared to their untuned base versions. These improved gains were, in some cases, comparable to those of larger, untuned models.
- **Generalization Across Receivers:** The improvements in persuasive capabilities from RL training transferred to different Receiver architectures (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), even though training was conducted against a single Receiver (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). This suggests that the models learned general principles of information design rather than merely exploiting specific architectural vulnerabilities of the training Receiver.
- **Limitations:** Despite significant improvements, the persuasion gains achieved by RL-trained smaller models remained lower than those of frontier models, indicating inherent differences in their base capabilities.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Persuasive Behavior

- **Effects of Prior Beliefs:** Empirical results aligned with Bayesian persuasion theory, showing that persuasion was most effective when the Receiver's prior beliefs were at intermediate levels. Medium to high prior confidence in the claim correlated with larger persuasion gains and higher final scores, as small shifts could alter the Receiver's action.
- **Adaptive Information Disclosure:** Larger models exhibited progressively lower semantic similarity in their messages as persuasion sequences unfolded. This behavior suggests an ability to diversify signaling strategies and adapt information disclosure over time, consistent with theoretical predictions for optimal signaling strategies in dynamic Bayesian persuasion.
- **Variation Across Receivers:** The DeepSeek-R1 Sender model achieved substantial persuasion gains across various Receiver models, though the magnitude of opinion change varied. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 was the most persuadable Receiver, while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct was the least persuadable, indicating that architectural differences among LLMs influence their susceptibility to persuasion. Dynamic persuasion consistently outperformed static persuasion for all Receiver types.
- **Analysis of Persuasion Strategies:** Zero-shot classification using LLMs revealed that the most common persuasion strategies employed by Sender models (both small and large) were "evidence," "credibility," and "impact." This indicates a primary reliance on information-related strategies within the experimental setup.

#### 4.4. Human Study Results

- **Plausibility of LLM Receivers:** A human study involving 45 participants evaluating 149 transcripts confirmed that LLM Receivers generally updated their beliefs in a directionally appropriate and proportionally plausible manner. Annotators rated overall transcript quality highly and expressed strong confidence in their assessments.
- **Reasonableness of Belief Updates:** Belief-direction accuracy was consistently high (77.18% in Turn 1, increasing to 85.23% in Turn 3), and proportional-update ratings were significantly above the neutral benchmark.
- **Qualitative Insights:** Annotator feedback indicated that perceived inconsistencies or limitations in LLM belief updates stemmed more from mismatched internal world models or cautious updating tendencies rather than fundamental contradictions of Bayesian principles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the scientific understanding of strategic behavior in large language models and has several potential impacts:

- **Advancement of LLM Persuasion Research:** The paper introduces a novel, theory-driven framework grounded in Bayesian persuasion for evaluating and training LLMs. This framework provides conceptual clarity and operational rigor, addressing limitations in previous empirical studies that often lacked a unified theoretical foundation. It establishes a more systematic and principled approach to understanding LLM persuasive capabilities.
- **Scalable Evaluation and Training:** By repurposing existing human-human persuasion datasets into controlled multi-agent interaction environments, the research offers a scalable benchmark for both evaluation and training. This move away from purely human-based evaluation methods reduces cost and subjectivity, enabling more extensive and consistent study of LLM persuasion.
- **Insights into LLM Strategic Reasoning:** The findings demonstrate that frontier LLMs can exhibit sophisticated strategic persuasion behaviors, such as adaptive information disclosure aligning with theoretical characterizations from dynamic Bayesian persuasion. This provides empirical evidence that LLM capabilities extend to complex strategic interactions beyond mere text generation.
- **Enhancing LLM Capabilities through RL:** The successful application of reinforcement learning to significantly improve the persuasive gains of even smaller LLMs highlights a promising avenue for post-training algorithms. The observed transferability of these improvements to different Receiver architectures suggests that LLMs can learn generalized principles of information design, potentially leading to more robust and versatile strategic AI agents.
- **Informing Responsible AI Development and Governance:** The research directly addresses the dual-use risks associated with powerful persuasive AI technologies. By providing a scientific understanding of how LLMs engage in strategic information disclosure, this work can inform the development of ethical frameworks, alignment techniques, and regulatory oversight for persuasive AI systems. The focus on truthful, welfare-improving persuasion, consistent with Bayesian persuasion theory, frames the work as foundational for mitigating potential harms and ensuring responsible deployment.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This paper represents an initial step towards a scientific understanding of strategic behavior in LLMs. The framework and findings can facilitate future research into more complex persuasion scenarios, including those with multiple senders or receivers, and different models of persuasion (belief-based vs. preference-based), ultimately contributing to a more comprehensive understanding of LLM interactions with humans and other AI agents.
