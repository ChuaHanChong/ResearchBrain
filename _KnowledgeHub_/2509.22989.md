# Towards Strategic Persuasion with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign established a theory-driven framework using Bayesian persuasion to evaluate and enhance the strategic persuasive capabilities of large language models (LLMs). Their work demonstrated that frontier LLMs exhibit sophisticated strategic information disclosure, and reinforcement learning effectively improved persuasion gains in smaller models, generalizing across different LLM receivers.

## Problem

- Existing methods for evaluating LLM persuasive capabilities are often subjective, resource-intensive, and lack a unified theoretical foundation, leading to fragmented findings.
- The inherent heterogeneity of persuasion across contexts makes systematic study and generalization of LLM persuasive power challenging.
- There is a need for a scalable and principled framework to understand, evaluate, and improve LLMs' strategic persuasion, moving beyond ad-hoc empirical observations.

## Method

- Introduced a theory-driven framework, grounded in ==Bayesian persuasion==, to measure LLM persuasive capabilities by modeling persuasion as strategic information transmission.
- Constructed a scalable benchmark by repurposing human-human persuasion datasets into controlled multi-agent environments where LLMs act as rational senders and receivers.
- Developed a reinforcement learning framework (PPO, GRPO) to train smaller LLMs for strategic persuasion, optimizing for the sender's expected utility (persuasion gains).

## Results

- Larger, frontier LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4o) consistently achieved higher persuasion gains, with DeepSeek-R1 showing average gains of **0.23** in static and 1.27 in dynamic settings on a 7-point Likert scale.
- RL-trained small LLMs (Llama-3.2-**3B**-Instruct) achieved significantly higher persuasion gains compared to their untuned versions, with some results comparable to larger, untuned models, and these gains transferred to different receiver architectures.
- A human study validated the plausibility of LLMs as rational receivers, with human annotators confirming that LLM belief updates were directionally appropriate (**77.18%** accuracy in Turn 1, increasing to **85.23%** in Turn 3) and proportionally plausible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier LLMs exhibit sophisticated strategic behaviors in information disclosure, adapting their signaling strategies over time in dynamic settings, consistent with theoretical predictions from dynamic Bayesian persuasion.
> - Reinforcement learning can significantly enhance the strategic persuasive capabilities of even smaller LLMs, and these learned improvements generalize effectively to different, unseen LLM receiver architectures.
> - Persuasion is most effective when the receiver's prior beliefs are at intermediate levels, aligning with Bayesian persuasion theory, as these are the contexts where information has the greatest potential to shift decisions.
