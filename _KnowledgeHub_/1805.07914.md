# Imitating Latent Policies from Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> ILPO enables agents to learn effective policies directly from state observations with minimal environment interaction by inferring latent actions offline and then quickly mapping them to real actions. This approach demonstrates superior sample efficiency compared to Behavioral Cloning from Observation across various discrete action environments.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods like Behavioral Cloning require synchronized state-action pairs, which are often unavailable in real-world observational data.
- Many existing Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO) approaches necessitate extensive and potentially risky or costly environment interaction to learn intermediate models (e.g., inverse dynamics or reward functions).
- Artificial agents struggle to learn complex behaviors from mere observation, unlike humans who frequently mimic skills without explicit action instruction.

## Method

- The approach, Imitating Latent Policies from Observation (**ILPO**), operates in two steps: first, offline learning of latent policies and dynamics from expert state observations.
- A neural network concurrently learns a latent forward dynamics model to predict next states for discrete latent actions and a latent policy to assign probabilities to these latent actions given the current state.
- Second, a minimal amount of online environment interaction is used to collect (state, action, next state) triplets, enabling a separate remapping network to align the learned latent actions with the agent's real-world action space.

## Results

- ILPO consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency in the online interaction phase compared to Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO) across all tested environments.
- In classic control environments (Cartpole, Acrobot), ILPO achieved performance comparable to expert behavior and behavioral cloning with fewer than 100 steps of environment interaction during remapping.
- For the high-dimensional CoinRun environment, ILPO significantly outperformed BCO, validating its relative efficiency even in complex visual domains, though not reaching expert performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex behaviors can be decoupled into abstract "latent actions" learned offline solely from state observations, which are then grounded to real actions with minimal online interaction.
> - A specialized loss function for the latent forward dynamics model enables it to predict distinct future states for each latent action, effectively discovering meaningful behavioral modes without explicit labels.
> - The two-stage architecture significantly improves sample efficiency by performing the computationally intensive learning of abstract behaviors offline, reserving online interaction only for action grounding.
