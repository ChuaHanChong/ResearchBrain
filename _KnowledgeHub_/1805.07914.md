# Imitating Latent Policies from Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> ILPO enables agents to learn effective policies directly from state observations with minimal environment interaction by inferring latent actions offline and then quickly mapping them to real actions. This approach demonstrates superior sample efficiency compared to Behavioral Cloning from Observation across various discrete action environments.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning methods like Behavioral Cloning require synchronized state-action pairs, which are often unavailable in real-world observational data.
- Many existing Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO) approaches necessitate extensive and potentially risky or costly environment interaction to learn intermediate models (e.g., inverse dynamics or reward functions).
- Artificial agents struggle to learn complex behaviors from mere observation, unlike humans who frequently mimic skills without explicit action instruction.

## Method

- The approach, Imitating Latent Policies from Observation (**ILPO**), operates in two steps: first, offline learning of latent policies and dynamics from expert state observations.
- A neural network concurrently learns a latent forward dynamics model to predict next states for discrete latent actions and a latent policy to assign probabilities to these latent actions given the current state.
- Second, a minimal amount of online environment interaction is used to collect (state, action, next state) triplets, enabling a separate remapping network to align the learned latent actions with the agent's real-world action space.

## Results

- ILPO consistently demonstrated superior sample efficiency in the online interaction phase compared to Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO) across all tested environments.
- In classic control environments (Cartpole, Acrobot), ILPO achieved performance comparable to expert behavior and behavioral cloning with fewer than 100 steps of environment interaction during remapping.
- For the high-dimensional CoinRun environment, ILPO significantly outperformed BCO, validating its relative efficiency even in complex visual domains, though not reaching expert performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex behaviors can be decoupled into abstract "latent actions" learned offline solely from state observations, which are then grounded to real actions with minimal online interaction.
> - A specialized loss function for the latent forward dynamics model enables it to predict distinct future states for each latent action, effectively discovering meaningful behavioral modes without explicit labels.
> - The two-stage architecture significantly improves sample efficiency by performing the computationally intensive learning of abstract behaviors offline, reserving online interaction only for action grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates within the domain of **Imitation Learning (IL)**, a subfield of machine learning where an agent learns a policy by observing expert demonstrations. More specifically, it addresses a crucial challenge within IL: **Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO)**, also known as Learning from Demonstration without access to expert actions.

Traditionally, imitation learning methods, such as **Behavioral Cloning (BC)** (Pomerleau, 1989), rely on paired state-action demonstrations. An expert provides observations of states (`s_t`) and the corresponding actions (`a_t`) taken in those states. The learning agent then trains a policy (`π(a|s)`) using supervised learning. While effective, this approach is limited by the availability of explicit action labels, which are often difficult, expensive, or impossible to obtain, especially from real-world human demonstrations (e.g., observing someone play a video game or perform a physical task).

The challenge of ILfO has given rise to several approaches:
- **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)** (Ng & Russell, 2000; Abbeel & Ng, 2004) aims to infer the expert's underlying reward function from state-action trajectories. While powerful, many IRL methods still implicitly or explicitly rely on state-action pairs or require extensive environment interaction to learn a reward function that can then be used for reinforcement learning.
- **Direct Policy Optimization methods** like Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) (Ho & Ermon, 2016) and State Aware Imitation Learning (SAIL) (Schroecker & Isbell, 2017) learn to match expert state-action visitation frequencies. While efficient in expert data, these methods often require significant interaction with the environment for training, a common characteristic of many modern reinforcement learning-based approaches.
- **Imitation from Observation (ILfO) specific methods**: Recent work, as cited by the authors (Liu et al., 2017; Sermanet et al., 2016, 2017; Stadie et al., 2017; Aytar et al., 2018), has explored learning from observation only, often by training features for a reward signal that is subsequently used for reinforcement learning. This intermediary step, however, still necessitates numerous environment samples for the subsequent RL phase.
- **Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO)** (Torabi et al., 2018a), a direct competitor to ILPO, addresses ILfO by first learning an *inverse dynamics model* within the agent's environment. This model infers actions from state transitions and is then used to label expert observations, which are subsequently fed into a standard behavioral cloning pipeline. The main drawback highlighted by Edwards et al. is that learning a robust inverse dynamics model online often requires a "substantial number of interactions with the environment."

Edwards et al.'s work positions itself as a more **sample-efficient alternative** to existing ILfO methods, particularly BCO. It tackles the core problem of reducing the reliance on extensive, potentially risky, or costly environment interactions. Instead of learning a full inverse dynamics model online, ILPO proposes a two-stage process:
1. **Offline learning of a latent policy**: This stage leverages only expert state observations to infer a policy over abstract "latent actions" that best describe observed transitions. This can be done efficiently without any environment interaction.
2. **Minimal online action remapping**: Only a few interactions are then needed to "ground" these learned latent actions to the agent's real-world action space.

This two-stage approach signifies a notable shift in the ILfO paradigm, prioritizing offline learning from passive observation before requiring active environmental engagement. By decoupling the learning of abstract behavior from the grounding to specific actions, ILPO aims to achieve a higher degree of data efficiency and safety in real-world applications where expert actions are unavailable and interaction is costly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "Imitating Latent Policies from Observation" stems from the observation that **humans frequently learn complex behaviors through mere observation**, without explicit instruction on the underlying actions or intentions. Examples include mimicking gestures, learning a new game by watching someone play, or understanding complex motor skills by observing a master. This natural human capability contrasts sharply with the demands of most artificial intelligence learning paradigms, which often require precise action labels or extensive trial-and-error in the environment.

The paper identifies key limitations in existing imitation learning (IL) approaches that hinder the development of general artificial agents capable of such mimicry:
- **Requirement for Expert Actions**: Traditional behavioral cloning (BC) necessitates synchronized state-action pairs, which are often unavailable in real-world observational data (e.g., YouTube videos, surveillance footage). This severely limits the amount of readily usable demonstration data.
- **Extensive Environment Interaction**: While recent Imitation Learning from Observation (ILfO) methods overcome the need for expert actions, many still rely on learning an intermediate model (e.g., inverse dynamics or a reward function) through significant, often unguided, interaction within the agent's environment. This online exploration can be "risky in many real-world scenarios and costly to obtain," particularly for robots in complex or dangerous environments.

Given these limitations, the **key objectives** of this research are:
1. **To develop a novel imitation learning approach that can infer policies directly from state observations**, without requiring explicit access to expert actions.
2. **To minimize the necessity for extensive interactions within the environment** during the learning process, thereby making the approach more sample-efficient, safer, and more practical for real-world deployment.
3. **To characterize the causal effects of latent actions on observations** and simultaneously predict their likelihood. This involves hypothesizing that predictable, though unknown, causes (latent actions) can describe the observed state transitions.
4. **To outline an action alignment procedure** that leverages a *small amount* of environment interactions to map these inferred latent actions to the agent's real-world action space.
5. **To demonstrate that this approach can effectively imitate observed behavior** and outperform existing methods that rely more heavily on online environment interaction, specifically Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO).

The authors use a compelling analogy to clarify their approach: "learning to play a video game by observing a friend play first, and then attempting to play it ourselves. By observing, we can learn the goal of the game and the types of actions we should be taking, but some interaction may be required to learn the correct mapping of controls on the joystick." This analogy perfectly encapsulates the two-step process of ILPO: first understanding the underlying "moves" (latent policies) from observation, and then quickly "grounding" those moves to the physical controls (action remapping) with minimal hands-on experience.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, **Imitating Latent Policies from Observation (ILPO)**, is a two-step process designed to learn an effective policy from expert state observations with minimal environment interaction.

**Step 1: Learning Latent Policies (Offline)**

This initial phase aims to extract a robust understanding of the expert's behavior from observed state trajectories, even without knowing the expert's actions. It operates entirely offline, using only `{state, next state}` pairs from the expert demonstrations.

1. **Assumption of Latent Actions**: ILPO assumes that transitions between states can be described by a discrete set of latent actions, `z ∈ Z`. Initially, the size of this latent action space, `|Z|`, is set equal to the known number of real-world actions, `|A|`, as an educated guess.
2. **Latent Policy Network**: The core of this step is a neural network architecture comprising two interdependent components:
 - **Latent Forward Dynamics Model (G)**: This generative model, `G_θ(E_p(s_t), z)`, learns to predict the next state `s_{t+1}` (or more precisely, the state difference `Δ_t = s_{t+1} - s_t`) given the current state `s_t` (encoded by embedding `E_p`) and a latent action `z`. The model is trained to predict distinct future states for each latent action. The loss function, `L_min = min_z ||Δ_t - G_θ(E_p(s_t), z)||^2`, is crucial. Instead of penalizing all predictions, it only penalizes the prediction from the latent action `z` that yields the closest next state to the observed `s_{t+1}`. This encourages each `G_θ(s_t, z)` to specialize in predicting a distinct "mode" or cluster of transitions, corresponding to a particular latent action.
 - **Latent Policy (π_ω)**: Concurrently, a policy `π_ω(z|E_p(s_t))` is learned. This represents the probability that a specific latent action `z` would be observed given the current state `s_t`. This policy is trained by minimizing `L_exp = ||s_{t+1} - E_{π_ω}[s_{t+1}|s_t]||^2`, where `E_{π_ω}[s_{t+1}|s_t] = Σ_z π_ω(z|s_t)G_θ(E_p(s_t), z)`. This loss encourages the latent policy to assign higher probabilities to latent actions that lead to the *most likely observed* next state. In essence, it learns which latent "cause" is most probable for a given observation.
 - **Combined Training**: Both components are trained jointly using `L_policy = L_min + L_exp`. The embeddings `E_p` are also learned concurrently.

**Step 2: Action Remapping (Online, Minimal Interaction)**

After establishing a latent policy offline, the agent needs to "ground" these abstract latent actions to its concrete, real-world action space. This phase requires a limited amount of interaction with the environment.

1. **Collecting Experience**: The agent interacts with its environment to gather a small dataset of `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` triples, where `a_t` is a real action taken by the agent and `s_t, s_{t+1}` are the resulting states. This interaction can be guided by an `ε`-greedy strategy, exploring real actions based on the current (initially untrained) remapped policy `π_ξ`.
2. **Inferring Latent Action**: For each collected `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` triplet, ILPO uses the *already trained* latent forward dynamics model `G_θ` from Step 1 to infer the most likely latent action `z_t` that would have caused the observed state transition `(s_t, s_{t+1})`. This is done by finding `z_t = argmin_z ||E_p(s_{t+1}) - E_p(G_θ(E_p(s_t), z))||^2` (using embedding distance for robustness, especially in visual domains). This step essentially asks: "Which of my learned latent 'causes' best explains what just happened in the environment when I took action `a_t`?"
3. **Training Action Remapping Network (π_ξ)**: Once `z_t` is identified for a given `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})` triplet, the real action `a_t` serves as a label for the inferred latent action `z_t` at state `s_t`. A separate remapping network `π_ξ(a|z, E_a(s_t))` (with its own embedding `E_a`) is trained using a standard cross-entropy loss, `L_map`, to learn the mapping from latent actions to real actions, conditioned on the state. The state dependence `s_t` accounts for potential context-dependent mappings (e.g., "move up" might look different if the agent is upside down).

**Policy Execution (Inference)**

To imitate the expert's behavior during deployment, the agent follows a two-step inference process:
1. Given a current state `s_t`, first determine the most probable latent action based on the learned latent policy: `z* = argmax_z π_ω(z|s_t)`.
2. Then, use the remapping network to translate this latent action into a real-world action: `a* = argmax_a π_ξ(a|z*, s_t)`.

This modular approach ensures that the computationally intensive and data-heavy dynamics learning happens offline using readily available observation data, while the online interaction is reserved only for the critical, but data-efficient, task of aligning abstract behaviors with concrete controls.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluate ILPO across four distinct environments, comparing its performance against a random policy, Behavioral Cloning (BC - given ground truth actions), and the most relevant baseline, Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO). The key metrics are reward obtained and the number of environment interactions required to achieve performance.

#### 4.1. Classic Control Environments (Cartpole, Acrobot, Mountain Car)

- **Setup**: These environments are low-dimensional (state spaces of 2-6 dimensions) with discrete action spaces (2-3 actions). ILPO used 50,000 expert state observations for offline training.
- **Cartpole & Acrobot**: ILPO demonstrated strong performance, achieving results comparable to both the expert and BC (which has access to ground truth actions) within **fewer than 100 steps** of environment interaction during the action remapping phase. Crucially, ILPO **significantly outperformed BCO** in these environments, highlighting its superior sample efficiency. This suggests that pre-training latent policies offline is more efficient than learning inverse dynamics online.
- **Mountain Car**: Neither ILPO nor BCO managed to perform as well as the expert. However, ILPO still consistently **outperformed BCO**, indicating a relative advantage even in scenarios where full expert performance wasn't reached by observation-only methods. The paper notes that these environments require stochastic demonstrations for optimal performance, though ILPO still functions with deterministic ones.
- **Effect of Latent Action Space Size (|Z|)**: The experiments showed that setting `|Z| = |A|` (the true number of actions) is generally a good initial guess. While other sizes can still lead to learning, a `|Z| = 1` performs poorly, as expected, because it cannot disentangle distinct actions. This validates the design choice of allowing multiple latent actions.

#### 4.2. CoinRun Environment (High-dimensional Visual Domain)

- **Setup**: CoinRun is a more complex platform game with 128x128x3 pixel image inputs and 7 discrete actions. The expert demonstrations consisted of 1000 episodes. ILPO used `|Z|=5` latent actions, which was found to perform better, possibly due to certain actions being less frequent in the expert data.
- **Results**: In both easy and hard CoinRun levels, ILPO **did not achieve expert-level performance**, but it **performed significantly better than BCO**. This demonstrates that ILPO's sample efficiency advantage extends to higher-dimensional, visually complex tasks, even though the overall learning challenge is greater.
- **Learning Efficiency**: While requiring more steps than classic control, ILPO still showed its relative efficiency over BCO. The authors note that BCO's training was "very slow" due to needing to train both the policy and inverse dynamics model at each iteration on image data. This underscores the practical advantage of ILPO's decoupled training.
- **Initialization Sensitivity**: The paper mentions that some random seeds led to poor initialization, preventing the agent from learning effectively, while good initializations sometimes allowed for strong zero-shot performance. This suggests that while powerful, the method can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning and initial conditions in complex environments.
- **Qualitative Results**: Figure 6 illustrates ILPO's ability to predict distinct next-state images corresponding to different actions, confirming that the latent dynamics model successfully captures multiple modes of transition.

**Overall Summary of Findings**:
ILPO consistently demonstrates **superior sample efficiency** in the online interaction phase compared to BCO, its direct competitor in the ILfO space. This efficiency is most pronounced in classic control tasks where ILPO can match expert performance with very few interactions. While not always reaching expert performance in more complex visual domains like CoinRun, it maintains a significant advantage over BCO, making it a more practical approach for scenarios where online data collection is costly or risky. The two-stage architecture, which pre-trains latent policies offline, is validated as a more efficient strategy for learning from observation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Imitating Latent Policies from Observation" holds significant implications for the field of imitation learning and practical AI applications, primarily by addressing critical bottlenecks in data acquisition and safety.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Reduced Reliance on Expert Actions and Online Interaction**: The most significant contribution of ILPO is its ability to learn effective policies from *only state observations* with *minimal environment interaction*. This directly tackles two major limitations of traditional imitation learning:
 - **Action Label Availability**: Expert actions are often unavailable in real-world demonstration data (e.g., from videos, surveillance, or natural human behavior). ILPO bypasses this requirement.
 - **Cost and Risk of Online Exploration**: Many ILfO methods necessitate extensive and potentially risky or costly online environment interaction to learn necessary intermediate models (like inverse dynamics). By performing the bulk of learning (latent policy inference) offline using readily available observation data, ILPO drastically reduces this burden. This is crucial for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and other domains where real-world interaction is expensive, time-consuming, or dangerous.

2. **Improved Sample Efficiency for ILfO**: The experimental results clearly show that ILPO outperforms Behavioral Cloning from Observation (BCO) in terms of sample efficiency (fewer online interactions needed). This is a direct consequence of ILPO's decoupled training strategy: learning abstract latent dynamics and policies offline, and reserving online interaction only for the comparatively simpler task of grounding these latent concepts to real actions.

3. **Novel Approach to Latent Action Discovery**: The method for discovering and utilizing latent actions by penalizing only the closest predicted next state in a multi-modal distribution (`min_z ||Δ_t - G_θ(E_p(s_t), z)||^2`) is innovative. This technique allows the model to automatically cluster and associate distinct observed state transitions with abstract "causes" without explicit labels, which is a powerful form of unsupervised learning within the context of sequential data.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**

- **Real-world Robotics and Autonomous Systems**: The reduced need for expert actions and safe online exploration makes ILPO highly relevant for training robots from human demonstrations where direct control data is scarce (e.g., surgical robots, manufacturing arms). Similarly, for autonomous vehicles, learning from dashboard camera footage (observations) without access to expert driver's exact control inputs becomes more feasible.
- **Learning from Diverse Data Sources**: The approach opens up opportunities for learning from vast amounts of observational data available online (e.g., YouTube videos of tasks, sports, or gameplay) that lack explicit action annotations.
- **Foundation for More Complex Systems**: The authors suggest that ILPO could serve as a valuable "pre-training step" for more sophisticated imitation learning approaches. By providing a good initial policy, it could accelerate the learning process for methods that might later refine the policy with additional data or via reinforcement learning.
- **Extensibility to Broader Scenarios**: The paper acknowledges current limitations (discrete actions, deterministic transitions) and suggests future work. Extending ILPO to continuous action spaces and non-deterministic environments would significantly broaden its applicability.
- **Cross-embodiment and Transfer Learning**: A particularly exciting future direction highlighted is the potential to learn from agents with different embodiments or to transfer learned latent policies across different environments. Since the latent policy is abstract, it might represent a high-level "intent" or "behavior" that could be remapped to different physical or virtual agents. This could facilitate more generalizable AI that can adapt to new platforms.

In conclusion, "Imitating Latent Policies from Observation" represents a substantial step towards more practical and data-efficient imitation learning. By pioneering a two-stage approach that prioritizes offline inference of abstract behaviors and minimizes online interaction for action grounding, ILPO offers a compelling solution for scenarios where expert actions are unavailable and environment interaction is a scarce or risky resource, pushing the boundaries of how artificial agents can learn through observation.
