# Rethinking Video Generation Model for the Embodied World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and ByteDance Seed introduce RBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating robot-oriented video generation, and RoVid-X, a large-scale robotic video dataset comprising 4 million annotated clips. This work establishes a robust framework for developing and assessing video generation models for embodied AI, revealing current models' deficiencies in physical realism while demonstrating RoVid-X's effectiveness in improving performance.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks for robotics primarily assess visual quality, failing to evaluate physical plausibility, task completion, and action-goal alignment critical for embodied AI.
- A significant scarcity of diverse, scalable, and high-quality robotic training data hinders the generalization capabilities of video foundation models in embodied AI applications.
- This narrow evaluation focus leads to overly optimistic assessments, where visually appealing but physically unrealistic or functionally incorrect robot behaviors can still receive high scores.

## Method

- Introduce RBench, a comprehensive, fine-grained benchmark for robot-oriented video generation, evaluating physical realism and task correctness across diverse robot types and five task domains.
- Develop **RoVid-X**, a large-scale open-source robotic video dataset with 4 million annotated clips, collected via a four-stage pipeline to address data scarcity and imbue physical interaction priors.
- Utilize a novel suite of automatic metrics in RBench, combining MLLM-based evaluations for physical-semantic plausibility and task adherence with computational indicators for motion and stability.

## Results

- RBench evaluations revealed significant shortcomings in current models' ability to generate physically realistic robot behaviors, with consumer-oriented models performing sub-optimally compared to commercial ones.
- A strong correlation of 0.96 (Spearman rank) was found between RBench's automated metrics and human preference studies, validating the benchmark's reliability.
- Finetuning existing open-source models with a subset of the RoVid-X dataset resulted in stable and significant performance improvements across all five task domains and four distinct robot embodiments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current video generation models exhibit significant deficiencies in generating physically realistic robot behaviors and struggle with high-level logic or fine-grained manipulation tasks.
> - The field is transitioning from focusing on visual fidelity to 'Physical AI,' where advanced models begin to function as 'World Simulators' by understanding and simulating physical interactions.
> - Iterative model scaling and large-scale pretraining are critical for acquiring physical reasoning and 'World Knowledge,' as narrow domain-specific data alone does not fully compensate.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the critical intersection of advanced **video generation models** and **embodied artificial intelligence (AI)**.

Recent years have witnessed remarkable breakthroughs in video generation, largely propelled by the advent of diffusion models. These models have expanded their capabilities from basic video editing and synthesis to more complex applications such as multi-subject generation, motion control, 3D scene reconstruction, and even autonomous driving simulation. A growing consensus in the field, as highlighted by a recent study, suggests that video models are evolving into "unified foundation models" for machine vision, mirroring the transformative role of Large Language Models (LLMs) in natural language processing.

Simultaneously, the integration of video models into robot learning is gaining significant traction. They are increasingly utilized for action prediction, world modeling, and synthesizing robotic trajectories, offering a scalable alternative to the labor-intensive and data-scarce process of human teleoperation for robot training. This development is crucial for advancing embodied agents, as video generation models can provide rich information within the perception-reasoning-action loop, paving the way for more generalizable intelligent systems capable of interacting with the physical world.

However, despite these advancements, several critical gaps persist in the broader research landscape that this paper endeavors to address:

1. **Inadequate Evaluation Benchmarks for Robotics:** Current evaluation practices for video generation, when applied to robotics, are largely underdeveloped. They primarily rely on perceptual metrics that assess visual quality (e.g., clarity, motion smoothness) but fail to capture crucial aspects unique to robotic interactions, such as task completion, action-goal alignment, and physical plausibility. This often leads to overly optimistic evaluations, where visually appealing but physically unrealistic or functionally incorrect robot behaviors receive high scores. Existing physics-based benchmarks also tend to lack task-specific datasets and criteria tailored for complex robotic scenarios.
2. **Scarcity of High-Quality Robotic Training Data:** Unlike computer vision and natural language processing, which benefit from vast web-scale datasets, the field of robotic learning has been constrained by a significant shortage of diverse, scalable, and high-quality interaction data. Existing robotic datasets often suffer from limited scale, narrow distributions across environments, object sets, task spectrums, and robot morphologies (e.g., confined to specific robot types, low-resolution recordings, or limited task ranges). This data scarcity severely hampers the generalization capabilities of video foundation models when applied to embodied AI.

This work directly addresses these two fundamental challenges. By introducing **RBench**, a comprehensive, fine-grained benchmark, the authors aim to provide a standardized lens for rigorously evaluating robot-oriented video generation, moving beyond mere visual fidelity to assess physical realism and task-level correctness. Concurrently, they introduce **RoVid-X**, the largest open-source robotic dataset for video generation, specifically designed to mitigate the data scarcity issue by offering a high-quality, diverse, and physically annotated resource. Collectively, this research seeks to create a synergistic ecosystem of evaluation and data, establishing a robust foundation that can rigorously assess and scalably train video models, thereby accelerating the evolution of embodied AI towards general intelligence in the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **rethink the role and evaluation of video generation models for embodied AI**, establishing a more robust framework for their development and assessment in the physical world. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **To bridge the gap between general video generation and embodied robotics:** While video generation models have shown remarkable progress in general scenarios, their application to robotics introduces unique challenges related to physical realism, task-level correctness, and precise interaction. The existing tools and datasets are not adequately tailored to these demands.
2. **To address the limitations of current evaluation methodologies:** The paper identifies that contemporary video generation benchmarks for robotics primarily focus on perceptual quality, often neglecting critical aspects like physical plausibility, structural consistency, and action completeness. This leads to an incomplete and often misleading assessment of model performance, failing to identify crucial deficiencies in generating realistic robotic behaviors. A key motivation is to move beyond superficial visual metrics to deeper, task-specific, and physics-grounded evaluations.
3. **To establish a comprehensive and standardized benchmark (RBench):** The motivation here is to create the first of its kind—a comprehensive and reproducible benchmark that can fairly compare and drive progress in robot-oriented video generation. This involves defining diverse task domains and robot embodiments, alongside fine-grained, reproducible sub-metrics that accurately reflect the complexities of embodied interactions.
4. **To identify and highlight deficiencies in current models:** By systematically evaluating a wide range of state-of-the-art video generation models against rigorous, physically-grounded criteria, the authors aim to quantitatively and qualitatively expose their limitations in synthesizing physically realistic robot behaviors. This diagnostic capability is crucial for guiding future research efforts.
5. **To overcome the critical shortage of high-quality training data (RoVid-X):** Recognizing that advanced models require extensive and diverse data, the authors are motivated to address the long-standing challenge of data scarcity in robot learning. Existing datasets are often limited in scale, diversity, and detailed annotations, hindering the generalization capabilities of video foundation models. The objective is to create a large-scale, high-quality, and richly annotated dataset specifically designed to imbue video generation models with physical interaction priors and task semantic diversity.
6. **To foster the development of "World Simulators" and Physical AI:** Ultimately, the motivation is to accelerate the transition of video generation models from mere aesthetic creators to capable "World Simulators" that can accurately understand, predict, and generate complex physical interactions. This is a crucial step towards developing truly generalizable embodied AI systems that can reason and act intelligently in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a synergistic ecosystem composed of a novel benchmark, RBench, and a large-scale dataset, RoVid-X, to tackle the challenges in robot-oriented video generation.

#### 3.1. RBench: A Comprehensive Robotics Benchmark

RBench is designed to provide a systematic and fine-grained evaluation framework for video generation models in robotic scenarios, moving beyond traditional perceptual metrics.

- **Benchmark Construction:**
 - **Diversity:** RBench comprises 650 image-text pairs, meticulously curated to cover a wide range of scenarios.
 - **Task Categories:** Five representative task domains are included:
 1. **Common Manipulation:** Basic object interactions (grasping, placing, pushing, rotating) across single-arm, dual-arm, and humanoid robots.
 2. **Long-horizon Planning:** Multi-stage sequential actions requiring complex planning (e.g., opening a refrigerator, retrieving an object, closing the door).
 3. **Multi-entity Collaboration:** Interactions between a primary robot entity and a secondary entity (human, animal, or another robot), focusing on coordinated behaviors.
 4. **Spatial Relationship:** Understanding and expressing spatial relations (e.g., above/below, left/right) between robots and objects.
 5. **Visual Reasoning:** Complex scenes requiring visual-semantic understanding (e.g., color recognition, numerical ordering, attribute matching, geometric properties).
 - **Embodiment Types:** Four mainstream robot types are evaluated:
 1. **Dual-arm robots:** Emphasizes coordinated bimanual manipulation.
 2. **Humanoid robots:** Prioritizes tool use and natural full-body postures, leveraging human activity priors.
 3. **Single-arm robots:** Focuses on precise object interactions.
 4. **Quadruped robots:** Tests terrain adaptation and motion continuity.
 - **Data Sourcing & Curation:** Each sample image is a keyframe from high-quality videos (public datasets or online sources), manually verified. New task prompts are designed to avoid training data overlap and ensure logical alignment. Metadata (object, embodiment, camera viewpoint) is recorded for detailed analysis.

- **Automatic Metrics:** RBench employs a set of automatic evaluation metrics, largely building upon Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like Qwen3-VL and GPT-5 as zero-shot evaluators, complemented by low-level computational indicators.
 - **Task Completion (MLLM-based):**
 - **Physical-Semantic Plausibility:** Assesses adherence to physical laws and common sense. Detects violations like floating/penetration of objects/robots, spontaneous emergence/disappearance of entities, non-contact attachment, and incorrect grasping. Evaluated via VQA-style protocol on temporal grids of sampled frames.
 - **Task-Adherence Consistency:** Evaluates if the generated video aligns with the prompt's intent and action sequence. Checks for missing actions, incorrect action order, semantic drift, and non-responsiveness. Uses an MLLM-based VQA checklist.
 - **Visual Quality (Hybrid MLLM/Computational):**
 - **Motion Amplitude (Computational):** Measures the actual motion of the robotic subject, distinguishing it from camera motion. Uses GroundingDINO for localization, GroundedSAM for masks, and CoTracker for keypoint tracking. A "Motion Amplitude Score (MAS)" is computed to penalize smooth but inactive videos.
 - **Robot-Subject Stability (MLLM-based):** Assesses the temporal stability of robot morphology and target object attributes. Detects failures like robot shape distortion (e.g., arm disappearing, morphing), extra/missing manipulators, and object misidentification or attribute drift (color, position, impossible deformation). Uses a contrastive VQA setup comparing reference and generated frames.
 - **Motion Smoothness (Computational):** Quantifies temporal continuity and natural dynamics, detecting jitter, blur, or aliasing. Measures frame-to-frame quality stability using the Q-Align aesthetic score and an adaptive threshold based on subject motion.
 - **Score Aggregation:** Normalized scores from these fine-grained metrics are consolidated into two higher-level indicators: "Task Completion" and "Visual Quality." Penalty terms are applied for insufficient motion amplitude or visual instability.

#### 3.2. RoVid-X: A Large-Scale High-Quality Robotic Video Dataset

RoVid-X is constructed through a refined four-stage end-to-end data pipeline, designed to provide diverse, scalable, and comprehensively annotated training data for embodied robotic video generation.

- **Data Sources:** Integrates over 20 open-source robotic datasets and publicly available internet video platforms. All data is licensed for use.
- **Four-Stage Pipeline:**
 1. **Robot Video Collection:** Raw robotic videos are collected. GPT-5 automatically filters content for relevance, identifying videos related to robotic tasks and actions based on visual content and subtitles. Approximately 3 million raw clips are identified.
 2. **Video Quality Filtering:** Rigorous filtering removes low-quality or irrelevant clips. Scene segmentation identifies and removes non-robot scenes. A multi-dimensional video quality scoring system (clarity, dynamic effects, aesthetics, OCR) is applied to ensure high standards.
 3. **Task Segmentation and Captioning:** A video understanding model, guided by a specialized prompt template, automatically analyzes robot actions. Videos are segmented by task timestamps, and short subtitles are generated for each segment, describing the robot's actions (subject, object, operation details).
 4. **Physical Property Annotation:** Videos are enhanced with physical attributes:
 - **Resolution Improvement:** FlashVSR upscales video resolution.
 - **Optical Flow:** AllTracker annotates unified optical flow for subjects, ensuring consistent tracking.
 - **Depth Maps:** Video Depth Anything generates relative depth maps for accurate spatial and depth information.
- **Dataset Characteristics:** RoVid-X emerges as the largest open-source robotic dataset for video generation, containing 4 million annotated video clips, covering thousands of tasks, diverse robot morphologies, and comprehensive physical property annotations. It aims to embed physical interaction priors and task semantic diversity into video foundation models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation conducted using RBench on 25 representative video generation models, coupled with human preference studies and dataset validation, yielded several critical findings:

- **Significant Deficiencies in Physical Realism:** The evaluations unequivocally highlight that current general video foundation models still possess significant shortcomings in generating physically realistic robot behaviors. A persistent gap exists between their capabilities and the stringent requirements of embodied robotic tasks.
- **Transition from Visual Fidelity to Physical Intelligence:** The results indicate a pivotal paradigm shift in the video generation landscape. While traditional benchmarks focused on pixel-level quality, RBench reveals that top-tier commercial models (e.g., Wan 2.6, Seedance 1.5 Pro) are beginning to function as effective "World Simulators." This signifies a move towards "Physical AI," where models demonstrate an understanding and simulation of interaction-rich, physically challenging real-world scenarios.
- **Iterative Scaling Unlocks Physical Capabilities:** A strong correlation was observed between model iteration/scaling and improvements in physical reasoning capabilities. For instance, the Wan series demonstrated substantial performance leaps from Wan 2.1 to Wan 2.6, and Seedance from 1.0 to 1.5 Pro. These gains suggest that scaling laws and iterative optimization are refining models' understanding of physics, distinct motion patterns, and control logic, not just visual quality.
- **The Media-Simulation Gap in Consumer Models:** Surprisingly, widely recognized consumer-oriented models like the Sora series performed sub-optimally on RBench (Sora v2 Pro at Rank 17, Sora v1 at Rank 22). This "domain gap" suggests that models optimized for media consumption prioritize visual smoothness and cinematic appeal, often at the expense of physical fidelity and precise motion control crucial for embodied AI. Proficiency in creative video generation does not naturally translate to embodied tasks.
- **Commercial Models Lead in Performance:** Closed-source commercial models consistently occupy the top ranks in the benchmark, demonstrating a clear and significant advantage over their open-source counterparts. This capability gap underscores the need for more concerted efforts in the open-source community to scale physical training data and optimize architectures for embodied video tasks.
- **Dilemma of Specialization: Domain Data vs. World Knowledge:** While general foundation models lead, the robotics-specific model Cosmos 2.5 showed remarkable resilience, outperforming many larger open-source general video models. This confirms that training with physical data yields stable gains in robotic tasks. However, models fine-tuned on specific robot entities (e.g., Vidar, UnifoLM) struggled significantly, ranking at the bottom. This highlights a trade-off: domain-specific data is valuable for precision, but it cannot fully compensate for the "World Knowledge" acquired through large-scale pretraining.
- **Cognitive and Fine-grained Control Bottlenecks:** A consistent limitation across all model families is their struggle with tasks requiring high-level logic (e.g., Visual Reasoning) or fine-grained manipulation. Performance drops sharply for tasks like Visual Reasoning compared to execution-oriented tasks. Similarly, models score higher on coarse-grained locomotion (Quadruped, Humanoid) than on fine-grained manipulation, implying that mastering contact dynamics for object interaction is physically more challenging than generating rhythmic legged locomotion.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual examples demonstrate RBench's ability to capture subtle yet critical failure modes such as structural distortions (robot morphing), floating components, omissions of key actions, incorrect spatial relationships, and errors in visual reasoning (e.g., misidentifying objects or failing to follow complex instructions).
- **High Correlation with Human Preference:** A human preference study involving thirty participants yielded a Spearman rank correlation coefficient of 0.96 between RBench scores and human judgments. This strong agreement validates the effectiveness and human alignment of the automated metrics, establishing RBench as a reliable evaluation standard. Bland-Altman analysis further confirmed close absolute agreement after calibration.
- **RoVid-X Effectiveness Validation:** Finetuning existing open-source models (Wan2.1 14B and Wan2.2 5B) with a sampled subset of RoVid-X demonstrated stable and significant performance improvements across all five task domains and four distinct embodiments. This validates the quality and utility of the proposed dataset and its collection pipeline for enhancing robotic video generation capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research, through the introduction of RBench and RoVid-X, makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact on the fields of video generation, robotics, and embodied AI:

- **Standardizing Evaluation for Embodied AI:** RBench provides the first comprehensive, fine-grained, and human-aligned benchmark specifically tailored for robot-oriented video generation. This is critically significant because it moves beyond superficial perceptual metrics to rigorously assess physical plausibility, structural consistency, task-level correctness, and action completeness. This standardization will enable fair and meaningful comparisons between models, foster targeted research, and accelerate progress towards genuinely functional and realistic robotic video synthesis.
- **Guiding Future Research and Development:** The detailed quantitative and qualitative analyses, coupled with identified failure modes, offer invaluable insights into the limitations of current video generation models. Highlighting the "Physical AI" paradigm shift, the "Media-Simulation Gap," and cognitive/fine-grained control bottlenecks provides clear directions for researchers to focus their efforts, whether in improving model architectures, training strategies, or data collection.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics:** RoVid-X represents a monumental step in overcoming the long-standing challenge of data scarcity in robot learning. As the largest open-source robotic dataset specifically designed for video generation, with 4 million annotated clips and rich physical property annotations, it provides an unprecedented resource. This will enable researchers to train more robust, generalizable, and physically-aware video foundation models, democratizing access to high-quality data that was previously a major bottleneck.
- **Accelerating the Evolution of Embodied Intelligence:** The synergistic ecosystem of RBench for rigorous evaluation and RoVid-X for scalable training establishes a robust foundation crucial for advancing video generation models in robotics. This infrastructure is essential for the progression of embodied AI from theoretical concepts to practical, real-world applications. By improving the ability to generate realistic robotic interactions, this work will directly facilitate the development of more intelligent and adaptable robots.
- **Paving the Way for Actionable Robot Policies:** The outlined future work plans, particularly the intention to employ Inverse Dynamics Models (IDM) to recover executable actions from generated videos and enable closed-loop control experiments on real hardware, signifies a critical pathway towards bridging the gap between video generation and actionable robot policies. This could lead to a paradigm where robots learn complex skills by "imagining" or synthesizing scenarios, and then executing them, significantly enhancing autonomous learning and generalization.
- **Advancing World Models:** By focusing on physical realism and task consistency, this research contributes directly to the development of more accurate and capable "world models." These models are vital for robots to understand, predict, and plan their interactions within dynamic environments, ultimately fostering greater autonomy and intelligence in robotic systems.

In conclusion, this paper provides not only a critical diagnostic tool but also a foundational dataset, both of which are indispensable for pushing the boundaries of video generation towards the complex and demanding requirements of embodied AI. Its impact will be felt across academic research and industrial applications seeking to develop more intelligent, physically aware, and autonomous robotic systems.
