# FetchMan: Learning Visual Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Policies from Simulated Experiences

> [!summary] Summary
> FetchMan learns visual humanoid loco-manipulation entirely from procedurally-generated simulated scenes on a Unitree G1: behavior cloning on scripted demonstrations gives a usable prior, then Flow-GRPO refines it with a sparse success reward, breaking the imitation ceiling and deploying zero-shot to reach-and-pick real objects at 73.3% success.

## Problem

- Scaling real-world humanoid demonstrations is costlier than tabletop arms since failures need extra supervision for safe recovery and whole-body balance restoration.
- Prior vision-based humanoid loco-manipulation systems rely on narrow, heavily constrained visual environment structures and hand-crafted rewards, limiting scene generalization.
- Scripted simulated demonstrators expose hidden phase variables the policy cannot observe, causing discontinuous motion at navigation-reach-manipulation boundaries that imitation cannot learn from.

## Method

- ==Procedural data-generation pipeline== samples scene, target object, and contact-free start pose from over 150,000 MolmoSpaces houses, then rolls out a privileged scripted whole-body controller with domain randomization.
- **Two-stage policy training**: a ==flow-matching DiT== with frozen DINOv3 vision tokens and delta-action targets is behavior-cloned on scripted trajectories, decoupled from a fixed pre-trained SONIC lower-body locomotion controller.
- ==Flow-GRPO refinement== adds Gaussian noise to the flow integrator for closed-form per-step likelihoods, using group-relative rollouts from identical reset states under a single sparse success reward to surpass the demonstrator.

## Results

- Behavior cloning success rate climbs from **40%** to **67%** loco-manipulation SR as demonstrations scale from 5k to 50k, then plateaus at 150k, confirming an imitation ceiling.
- Flow-GRPO refinement lifts simulated loco-manipulation SR from **67%** to **83%** and real-world SR from **56.7%** to **73.3%**, while manipulation-only SR stays nearly flat (**72.7%** to **77.2%**).
- Ablations show frozen DINOv3 versus SigLIP drops real-world loco-manipulation SR from **73.3%** to **0%**, and absolute versus delta action targets also collapses real-world SR to **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Behavior cloning on synthetic demonstrations has an intrinsic ceiling when the demonstrator conditions on privileged hidden phase state the policy cannot observe, regardless of data scale.
> - Group-relative RL refinement needs a behavior-cloned prior strong enough to produce mixed success groups; refining from a weak checkpoint yields no improvement.
> - Vision-encoder choice and delta-action parameterization are critical, not incidental, design decisions for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in visual manipulation policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of developing visual loco-manipulation policies for humanoid robots that can generalize to novel scenes and objects. This area of robotics research faces significant hurdles, primarily related to data scarcity and the complexities of transferring behaviors learned in simulation to the real world (sim-to-real transfer).

Historically, simulation has become a standard environment for training humanoid locomotion policies due to the ease of data generation and interaction in controlled settings. These policies often operate with low-dimensional observations and limited contact interactions (e.g., feet-floor contact). However, extending this success to complex loco-manipulation tasks, which involve diverse objects, contact-rich behaviors, and whole-body coordination, has proven difficult. The two main reasons cited are the scarcity of diverse simulation assets and the increased difficulty in accurately modeling contact-rich interactions compared to locomotion.

Collecting real-world data for humanoid loco-manipulation is costly, time-consuming, and presents safety concerns. Each trajectory requires significant supervision for safe recovery and balance restoration, limiting the scale and diversity of data that can be acquired.

Prior work has explored several avenues to address these limitations:
- **Synthetic Data Generation:** Projects like MolmoSpaces and MolmoBot have advanced the generation of large-scale procedurally generated environments and demonstrated zero-shot transfer for tabletop manipulation using synthetic data and broad domain randomization.
- **Humanoid Systems in Simulation:** Other research has shown that vision-based whole-body behaviors for humanoids can be trained purely in simulation and deployed zero-shot to real hardware. However, these systems are often trained and evaluated within constrained visual environments and rely on complex, hand-crafted rewards, limiting their generalization to diverse real-world settings without per-environment tuning.
- **Learning from Human Data:** Approaches like Human-Plus, OmniH2O, GR00T-N1, Humanoid-VLA, and ψ0 utilize teleoperation or human motion data for training. While effective, they are bottlenecked by the expense and scale of real-world data collection, restricting coverage of diverse scenes, objects, and failure modes. These methods often require post-training adaptation during deployment.
- **Policy Refinement:** Techniques like DAgger and more recent flow-based methods like ReinFlow and Flow-GRPO aim to refine policies initialized by imitation learning using reinforcement learning (RL) to surpass demonstrator performance, particularly for policies with flow-based action heads.

This work integrates and extends these advancements by developing a scalable, end-to-end sim-to-real pipeline for visual humanoid loco-manipulation. It specifically addresses the limitation of behavior cloning on scripted synthetic data, where the "imitation gap" arises from the demonstrator's access to privileged information and discrete phase structures that are unobservable to the policy. By combining large-scale synthetic data generation, behavior cloning, and reinforcement learning refinement using Flow-GRPO, the paper aims to achieve robust generalization and zero-shot transfer for humanoids in complex, diverse environments, moving beyond fixed-environment structures and hand-crafted rewards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary goal of this research is to develop a visual humanoid loco-manipulation policy that can generalize to novel scenes and objects, learned entirely in simulation and deployable zero-shot on real hardware. This overarching objective is broken down into four specific research objectives and their motivations:

1. **To study and identify a scalable recipe for training visual humanoid loco-manipulation policies in simulation.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing methods struggle with the high data requirements for complex loco-manipulation tasks on humanoids. A key motivation is to leverage the scalability of simulation to generate vast amounts of data, similar to how locomotion policies are trained. This involves investigating effective ways to factorize locomotion and manipulation commands, specifically through the use of a fixed low-level whole-body controller, to simplify the learning problem.

2. **To identify and address the limitations of scripted demonstration generation, particularly regarding the "imitation gap."**
 - **Motivation:** Scripted demonstrators, while easy to scale in simulation, often impose a discrete phase structure (e.g., navigating, reaching, grasping) whose transitions are based on privileged information (like a hidden phase index or exact object pose) unavailable to the learned policy. This leads to discontinuous motion, imperfect imitation, and a performance ceiling for behavior cloning. The objective is to explicitly acknowledge this imitation gap and propose a method, Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to overcome it by allowing the policy to learn beyond the demonstrator’s behavior, facilitated by simulation’s ability to generate identical-state group rollouts.

3. **To release a reproducible simulation benchmark for humanoid loco-manipulation, named FetchMan-Bench.**
 - **Motivation:** A lack of standardized benchmarks for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks makes it challenging to compare different methods effectively. Creating FetchMan-Bench with fixed held-out scenes and scoring mechanisms aims to provide a consistent platform for evaluating and advancing research in this domain.

4. **To scale the identified recipe to train FETCHMAN, an environment-generalist visual loco-manipulation policy, and explore its extension to multi-object training.**
 - **Motivation:** The ultimate aim is to demonstrate a policy capable of robust performance in diverse, unseen real-world environments without requiring per-scene tuning or real-world data collection. This involves developing an end-to-end pipeline that leverages the strengths of behavior cloning for initial learning and reinforcement learning for refinement. Extending this recipe to multi-object training is motivated by the desire to take a foundational step toward developing more generalist robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a two-stage training pipeline within a large-scale simulation environment, followed by zero-shot deployment to a physical robot.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

- **Robot Embodiment:** The Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with Dex1-1 grippers is used.
- **Control Interface:** A 15-dimensional whole-body command is accepted at 10 Hz.
- **Observations (o_t):** Consist of:
 - `I_head_t`, `I_wrist_t`: Head-mounted fisheye and wrist-mounted RGB images (224x384x3).
 - `o_prop_t`: Proprioceptive vector including base height, roll, pitch, upper-body joint positions (waist and right arm), and gripper aperture.
- **Actions (a_t):** Composed of:
 - `v_t`: Base linear and yaw velocity command (3D).
 - `h_t`: Base-height target (1D).
 - `q_waist_t`, `q_arm_t`: Waist and right-arm joint targets (3D and 7D, respectively).
 - `g_t`: Right-gripper target (1D).
- **Hierarchical Control:** A pre-trained SONIC lower-body controller tracks base commands (v_t, h_t) at 50 Hz, handling balance and walking. Upper-body and gripper targets (q_waist_t, q_arm_t, g_t) are tracked directly by PD controllers. This hierarchical control reduces the command space from 29 joint torques to 15 meaningful commands, making learning tractable and mitigating sim-to-real shift by using the same stack in both simulation and hardware.

#### 3.2. FetchMan — Data Generation

Training data is generated by rolling out a scripted, privileged whole-body controller (π_ctrl) in procedurally generated indoor scenes. This controller has access to privileged simulator state unavailable to the learned policy.
- **Task Sampling:** Episodes are instantiated using MolmoSpaces to provide diverse indoor scenes. A task consists of a scene (s), a target object (m), and a robot start pose (x_0).
 - **Scenes:** Drawn from ProcTHOR-10k, Holodeck, and ProcTHOR-Objaverse splits. A 2D free-space occupancy grid (O_s) is precomputed for path planning.
 - **Target Objects:** Pickable, free-jointed assets from MolmoSpaces. A grasp set (G_m) is precomputed for each object, filtered for the Dex1-1 gripper and G1 Inverse Kinematics (IK) feasibility. Object pose and supporting surface height are resampled per episode.
 - **Robot Start Pose:** Sampled from free cells of O_s, with yaw oriented toward the target. Validated for contact-free whole-body pose and target visibility. 3D validation allows for a wider distribution of approach geometries.
- **Scripted Demonstrator (π_ctrl):** Operates on privileged information (object pose T_m, selected grasp T_g, occupancy grid O_s, robot's base pose x_t, and internal phase index k_t).
 - **Reaching:** Plans an A* path on an inflated O_s, reducing it to waypoints. Emits base velocity commands to navigate towards the next waypoint while facing the target.
 - **Manipulation:** Upon reaching a grasp standoff, π_ctrl advances through discrete phases: {reach, descend, close, lift}. IK is used to select a reachable grasp, jointly optimizing base height with arm joints to bring the target into the arm's workspace.
- **Episode Mixture:**
 - 80% "fetch" (reach+pick) episodes: Exercise the full navigation-to-grasp pipeline.
 - 20% "pick-only" episodes: Robot initialized at a grasp standoff; only manipulation phases executed. This re-balances data towards the terminal grasp and allows for randomization of initial upper-body configuration.
- **Domain Randomization (per episode):**
 - **Textures:** Scene surfaces (walls, floors, counters, tables, doors) draw from predefined material pools.
 - **Lighting:** Light source positions, colors, intensities, and shadows are resampled. Robot body color is tinted.
 - **Cameras:** Head fisheye and wrist cameras perturbed in intrinsics (FOV, distortion) and extrinsics (position, orientation).
 - **Surface and Object Pose:** Target's supporting surface height is reset within the G1's reachable workspace, propagating rigidly to other objects. Target's planar pose is jittered.
 - **Action Noise:** Small Gaussian noise added to commands before low-level controllers.

#### 3.3. FetchMan — Policy Learning

The unprivileged policy (π_θ) is trained in two stages:
- **Architecture:**
 - **Vision Encoder:** Frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone for head and wrist RGB streams, emitting patch-token grids.
 - **Proprioceptive Encoding:** Proprioceptive vector projected into a single state token.
 - **Action Head:** DiT-style transformer that cross-attends from noised action tokens to observation context, predicting a chunk of actions (a_t:t+H). H=16, first 8 actions executed at deployment.
 - **Text Conditioning (for multi-object variant):** Frozen dino.txt stack where object name is encoded into a text token, fused into patch tokens via cross-attention.
- **Stage 1: Imitation Pretraining:**
 - π_θ is behavior-cloned (BC) on the generated demonstration trajectories.
 - 11 dimensions (absolute joint/pose targets) are re-parameterized as deltas relative to an observation-read anchor at time t, while base velocity and gripper commands remain absolute. This delta parameterization aids sim-to-real transfer.
- **Stage 2: Flow-GRPO Refinement:**
 - The BC-initialized π_θ is fine-tuned with large-scale on-policy Reinforcement Learning using Flow-GRPO. DAgger is not used due to the demonstrator's privileged phase variable.
 - **Flow-GRPO Mechanism:** At inference, the deterministic Euler step of the flow integrator is replaced with a Gaussian transition (x_τ+Δτ ~ N(x_τ + ˆv_θ(x_τ, τ, o_t) Δτ, σ^2_τ |Δτ|)). This turns the ODE into a stochastic process with closed-form per-step likelihoods, enabling importance ratio calculation for RL.
 - **Grouped Rollouts:** G groups of M episodes are collected per update. Each group is reset to an identical task, and M rollouts are performed, differing only in the SDE noise injected by π_θ. This is efficient in simulation. (M=8, G=32 for single-object; G=64 for multi-object).
 - **Advantage Calculation:** For a group with returns {R_i}, the advantage is the standardized group-relative return A_i = (R_i - μ)/σ, without a learned value function. Groups with uniform success/failure are dropped.
 - **Actor Update:** PPO-style clipped objective over the per-flow-step importance ratio, with dual-clipping. An optional KL penalty to the frozen BC policy is used for multi-object policy.
 - **Reward:** A single sparse signal: 1 for successful target fetch, 0 otherwise. Length-normalization (L̄/L_i) of the actor loss ensures each episode contributes equally.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments address four research questions using the FetchMan-Bench simulation benchmark and zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree G1 robot. The reference model uses a frozen DINOv3 encoder, delta-action targets, and 150k demonstrations.

#### 4.1. Setup and Benchmark

- **Data Scale:** 150k bowl-pick demonstrations (approx. 650 hours of robot experience across 150k distinct scenes), generated in ~40 GPU-hours.
- **FetchMan-Bench:** A reproducible simulation benchmark with fixed held-out scenes, reporting:
 - **Manipulation SR:** Success rate for the terminal grasp phase (robot initialized at grasp standoff).
 - **Loco-manipulation SR:** Success rate for the full task (robot starts away, navigates, then grasps).
- **Evaluation:** On a fixed set of 100 unseen initializations in simulation; zero-shot deployment on the physical G1 with no real-world data.

#### 4.2. RQ1: Scaling Alone Saturates Below the Demonstrator

- **Result:** Behavior cloning performance (Loco-manipulation SR) increased from 40% (5k demonstrations) to 67% (50k demonstrations) but then saturated, showing no further measurable gain when scaling to 150k demonstrations.
- **Interpretation:** This plateau is attributed to the "imitation ceiling." The scripted demonstrator relies on a hidden phase index (k_t) that the policy cannot observe from its inputs (images and proprioception). Consequently, more data from the same demonstrator cannot bridge this structural information gap, limiting the policy's ability to learn nuanced phase transitions.

#### 4.3. RQ2: RL Refinement Recovers the Headroom

- **Result:** Applying Flow-GRPO refinement to the saturated 150k-demonstration BC checkpoint increased simulated Loco-manipulation SR from 67% to 83%.
- **Detailed Analysis (Table 1):**
 - **Simulation:** FetchMan (BC+RL) achieved 79.0% Manipulation SR and 83.0% Loco-manipulation SR, compared to FetchMan-BC's 75.0% and 67.0% respectively.
 - **Real World:** FetchMan (BC+RL) achieved 77.2% Manipulation SR and 73.3% Loco-manipulation SR, compared to FetchMan-BC's 72.7% and 56.7%.
- **Interpretation:** RL refinement successfully broke through the imitation ceiling. The gains concentrated almost entirely in the loco-manipulation regime, specifically improving the locomotion-to-manipulation handoff, which is the exact phase-transition behavior predicted to be difficult for behavior cloning from privileged-state-driven demonstrations.
- **Sensitivity to Initialization:** RL refinement from a weaker 5k-demonstration checkpoint (40% SR) did not improve comparably, oscillating around its starting success rate. This indicates that a strong prior from sufficient imitation pretraining is necessary for Flow-GRPO to effectively utilize its group-relative advantage signal, as a weak prior yields too many all-failure groups.

#### 4.4. RQ3: Zero-Shot Transfer and What Enables It

- **Result:** The FetchMan (BC+RL) model achieved 73.3% loco-manipulation SR on a real Unitree G1 robot without any real-world data or tuning.
- **Enabling Factors (Table 2 - Ablations):**
 - **Vision Encoder:** A frozen DINOv3 encoder was crucial. Replacing it with SigLIP resulted in substantially lower simulation performance (42% vs. 67%) and nearly collapsed real-world performance (8.3% manipulation, 0% loco-manipulation). DINOv3's geometric features are suggested to be more robust to the sim-to-real appearance gap.
 - **Action Parameterization:** Delta-action targets were essential. Using absolute targets led to significantly lower real-world performance (16.7% manipulation, 0% loco-manipulation), confirming that the near-zero-mean delta targets aid transfer.

#### 4.5. RQ4: Toward Object Generalism

- **Result:** The recipe was extended to multi-object training by regenerating 350k demonstrations across all pickable object categories and training a text-conditioned variant (dino.txt backbone). In simulation, the multi-object BC policy reached 40% loco-manipulation SR, which Flow-GRPO refined to 62%.
- **Interpretation:** While lower than the single-object performance (83%), this indicates that the recipe can extend to the multi-object setting. The reduced performance is partly attributed to the increased task difficulty and the relatively smaller, frozen dino.txt backbone compared to more advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Qualitative zero-shot evaluations on the real G1 showed successful fetches across several object categories, although with less robustness than the single-object policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "FetchMan" represents a significant step forward in developing generalist humanoid loco-manipulation policies. Its core contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Demonstration of Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation:** The most direct significance is the successful deployment of a complex, vision-based humanoid loco-manipulation policy trained entirely in simulation to a real Unitree G1 robot without any real-world data or per-scene tuning. Achieving 73.3% success in diverse, unseen real-world scenes for a full reach-and-pick task highlights the effectiveness of the proposed pipeline for bridging the sim-to-real gap in a challenging domain.
- **Overcoming the Imitation Gap with RL Refinement:** The paper clearly identifies and addresses the "imitation gap" inherent in behavior cloning from scripted, privileged demonstrators. The finding that behavior cloning alone saturates due to the demonstrator's unobservable phase structure, and that Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning effectively breaks through this ceiling, is a crucial methodological advancement. This two-stage approach provides a robust strategy for leveraging scalable synthetic data while mitigating its inherent limitations.
- **Introduction of a Scalable and Reproducible Pipeline:** The research establishes a scalable recipe for generating diverse synthetic data (150k scenes, 150k demonstrations) and training policies within this environment. The detailed methodology, including procedural scene generation, scripted demonstrator design, domain randomization, and hierarchical control, provides a blueprint for future work aiming for large-scale robot learning.
- **Release of FetchMan-Bench:** The introduction of FetchMan-Bench, a reproducible simulation benchmark, is an important contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized evaluation platform, enabling consistent comparison of different approaches to humanoid loco-manipulation and accelerating progress in the field.
- **Advancement Towards Object Generalism:** The successful extension of the recipe to multi-object training, even with a performance decrease compared to single-object tasks, demonstrates the potential for developing policies that can interact with a wide variety of objects based on visual and textual cues. This is a foundational step towards more generalist, versatile humanoid robots.
- **Implications for Robotics Training:** By demonstrating that complex humanoid behaviors can be learned primarily in simulation, this work suggests avenues for safer, more cost-effective, and more data-rich training paradigms. It could reduce the reliance on expensive and potentially hazardous real-world data collection, making advanced robotics research more accessible.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as a stateless policy and a fixed lower-body controller, the demonstrated capabilities for zero-shot generalization in diverse visual environments with a full humanoid body mark a notable progression in the field of embodied AI and robotic manipulation.
