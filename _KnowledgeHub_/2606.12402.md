# DIRECT: When and Where Should You Allocate Test-Time Compute in Embodied Planners?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Stanford University and the University of Waterloo introduce DIRECT, a framework that dynamically allocates test-time computational resources for Vision-Language Model (VLM) planners in embodied agents. This system achieves task success rates comparable to or better than fixed high-compute strategies, while substantially reducing average latency and computational cost by adapting VLM configuration based on multimodal scene and instruction context.

## Problem

- Deploying large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as high-level planners in embodied agents incurs high test-time compute costs and latency, limiting real-world viability.
- Naive scaling of test-time compute (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, larger models) often yields diminishing or uneven returns in downstream task success, leading to wasted resources.
- Existing dynamic routing methods for language models primarily rely on text features, which are insufficient to capture the complex multimodal context relevant for embodied AI task difficulty.

## Method

- **DIRECT** (Dynamic ==Inference Router== for Embodied Compute Tradeoffs) is proposed, a lightweight, multimodal context-aware routing framework that dynamically selects optimal VLM planner configurations.
- The framework collects quality scores and inference costs for various VLM planners across training tasks, using synthetic data generation to support hardware deployment.
- A small router, conditioned on encoded multimodal scene and instruction context, learns to map tasks to specific planner configurations by maximizing a utility function that balances quality and cost.

## Results

- DIRECT achieved comparable performance to 'Thinking' models while reducing latency by approximately **30%** in CoT routing on VLABench, and for multi-step tasks on a Franka arm, it reached **95.24%** success at 6.85 seconds average latency compared to **90.48%** success at 19.58 seconds for the 'Thinking' model.
- Routing over model sizes improved performance by 5.1 points over the most expensive **32B** model while reducing average latency by 32.4 seconds on VLABench, by selectively using smaller, faster models when sufficient.
- On physical hardware, DIRECT achieved **100%** success on memory-stressing tasks, matching the oracle, while processing roughly half the number of frames (15.6 vs. 32) compared to an always-memory-enabled planner.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diagnostic analyses reveal that different axes of test-time compute (reasoning depth, model size, memory history) yield non-uniform and context-dependent gains, challenging the effectiveness of uniform application.
> - A lightweight, multimodal router can effectively infer task-specific demands, enabling adaptive allocation of compute resources to match VLM planner capabilities to task complexity.
> - Optimal VLM compute configurations vary significantly by task; no single planner or scaling strategy is universally efficient across diverse embodied planning challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the fields of embodied artificial intelligence, large vision-language models (VLMs), and adaptive inference. It specifically addresses challenges associated with deploying advanced VLM-based high-level planners in robotic systems.

Vision-Language Models are increasingly employed as high-level planners that decompose abstract instructions into executable sub-skills for embodied agents. This hierarchical approach, where a VLM generates semantic subtasks for a low-level policy, offers semantic flexibility for open-world robotic interaction. Previous efforts in this domain typically commit to a single high-level VLM with fixed capabilities and computational costs. This work proposes treating the high-level planner as a dynamic variable, adapting its configuration based on task requirements.

The concept of dynamic model routing has emerged in natural language processing to optimize inference by directing queries to the most efficient model within an ensemble. Frameworks such as FrugalGPT and RouteLLM, which predict performance from linguistic features, have demonstrated the utility of this approach for language models. However, these methods primarily route based on text. Recent multimodal extensions indicate that text features alone may be insufficient to capture the complexity relevant for routing in diverse contexts. In embodied AI, task difficulty involves additional dimensions, including physical scene understanding and trajectory feasibility. While some robotic systems, such as DA-SIP, apply dynamic routing to low-level diffusion controllers to select integration-step budgets, this paper extends multimodal routing to the *high-level planning stack*. This application context involves a broader ecosystem of interchangeable VLM planners that span orders of magnitude in compute cost and capability, necessitating a more comprehensive routing strategy.

Furthermore, the paper examines the strategy of scaling test-time compute to improve model capabilities. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, deliberate search, and parallel sampling can enhance performance, sometimes outperforming larger models. However, these methods often incur significant latency, token usage, and computational costs. Empirical observations indicate that longer reasoning chains can sometimes be detrimental or lead to early saturation of performance gains. In embodied settings, while grounded multi-step reasoning can improve VLA success, its latency can be prohibitive for real-world robot operation. Prior work often applies these test-time compute strategies uniformly. This research challenges this uniform application, investigating *when* and *where* specific types of compute yield meaningful gains. It proposes identifying high-signal cases using perception-aware classifiers that condition on multimodal scene and instruction context.

In summary, this work addresses a gap by introducing a framework for dynamically allocating high-level VLM planning compute based on multimodal contextual cues, aiming to optimize the trade-off between planning success and computational cost in embodied systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from an observed inefficiency in the current deployment paradigm of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as high-level planners for embodied agents. While scaling test-time compute is a common strategy to enhance VLM capabilities, the authors identify significant drawbacks: increased latency, token usage, and FLOPs. Critically, these computational expenditures often result in uneven, and frequently diminishing, returns in downstream task success. This limits the practical deployment of embodied agents in real-world scenarios where responsiveness and resource efficiency are paramount.

The core argument put forth in the paper is that effective management of test-time compute – specifically, deciding *when* and *where* to allocate it – is fundamental to bridging the gap between frontier VLM performance and viable real-world robotic applications. The authors highlight the growing friction between VLM capabilities and practical viability, noting that state-of-the-art VLM latencies can extend to tens of seconds. An illustrative example provided is a "Thinking" VLM on a Franka arm, which, despite being 3.8 times slower than its non-thinking counterpart, may achieve the same success rate, indicating wasted compute.

Against this backdrop, the research sets out to achieve three key objectives:

1. **Diagnostic Analyses:** Conduct comprehensive diagnostic investigations to understand the interplay between task difficulty, planner capability, and the various dimensions of test-time compute in embodied settings. This involves systematically analyzing how different scaling approaches—such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning depth, VLM model size, and memory history mechanisms—impact planning quality and computational cost. The motivation is to empirically demonstrate that test-time compute is not a uniform lever, and different approaches confer distinct capabilities.
2. **Framework Development (DIRECT):** Propose and develop DIRECT (Dynamic Inference Router for Embodied Compute Tradeoffs), a novel routing framework. This framework is designed to dynamically allocate test-time compute to VLM planners by conditioning on multimodal scene and instruction context. The objective is for DIRECT to infer the specific demands of each task and match them to the VLM planner configuration that offers the most favorable quality-cost trade-off from a predefined pool of candidates.
3. **Experimental Validation:** Validate the effectiveness of DIRECT through extensive experiments. This validation encompasses both simulation environments (VLABench and RoboMME) and physical hardware (a Franka robotic arm in a DROID setup). The primary aim is to demonstrate that DIRECT can either match or exceed the success rate of a stronger, more computationally expensive VLM, while simultaneously achieving a significantly lower average latency and overall computational cost. This objective seeks to prove DIRECT's ability to provide frontier-level embodied planning performance more efficiently.

These objectives collectively aim to move beyond naive scaling of VLM compute by providing a principled, context-aware approach to resource allocation in embodied planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research can be divided into two main phases: an investigative phase to understand test-time compute tradeoffs and the development and validation of the DIRECT routing framework.

#### 3.1. Investigating How Test-Time Compute Impacts Embodied Planning (Section 3)

The initial phase involved diagnostic analyses across three axes of test-time compute to demonstrate its non-uniform impact on embodied planning:

- **Reasoning Depth (Chain-of-Thought, CoT):** The authors compared the performance of "No-Thinking" (instruct-style) and "Thinking" (CoT-enabled) VLM planners using Qwen3-VL 8B Instruct and Qwen3-VL 8B Thinking on the VLABench benchmark. The objective was to quantify the gains in planning quality versus the increase in token generation latency. Findings indicated that while CoT reasoning generally improved average performance, the No-Thinking model matched or surpassed the Thinking model on 44% of tasks, with significantly faster inference. This suggested that uniform application of CoT could be wasteful.
- **Model Size:** To understand the effect of planner scale, Qwen3-VL Instruct variants ranging from 2B to 235B parameters were evaluated on VLABench. The study aimed to observe how performance (score and latency) scaled with model size. The results showed a non-monotonic relationship between model size and both score and latency. Analysis by skill category revealed that larger models primarily broadened the range of skills a planner could reliably command, implying that uniform deployment of large models might overspend compute on tasks not requiring those specific skills.
- **Memory Architectures:** The impact of different memory architectures on long-horizon tasks was investigated using RoboMME. Various memory-augmented VLA (MME-VLA) variants, including FrameSamp, TokenDrop (perceptual memory), SimpleSG, GroundSG (symbolic memory), and MemER (retrieval-based memory), were assessed. The goal was to understand how these architectures trade accuracy against computational overhead and how their optimal choice shifts with task difficulty. It was observed that lightweight schemes (e.g., FrameSamp) were more efficient for easy tasks, while more complex memory architectures (e.g., MemER, GroundSG) excelled on tasks requiring distant history.

These investigations collectively informed the necessity of an adaptive allocation framework, confirming that specific task demands dictate the optimal compute configuration.

#### 3.2. Proposed Embodied Routing Framework: DIRECT (Section 4)

Based on the diagnostic insights, the DIRECT framework was developed to dynamically allocate VLM planning compute.

- **Formalization:** Embodied test-time compute allocation was framed as a selection problem. Given a pool M = {m_1..., m_K} of VLM embodied planners, each taking a task input x = (I, ℓ) (scene observation and language instruction) and outputting a sequence of sub-skills (a_1..., a_T) for a low-level policy, DIRECT aims to select the optimal m_k.
- **Data Collection:** For training the router, two key quantities were measured for every training task x_i and planner m_k:
 - **Quality Score (q_i,k):** The task success rate (or progress score) from end-to-end execution of m_k's skill sequence.
 - **Inference Cost (c_i,k):** Latency (for autoregressive components like reasoning depth) or FLOPs (for parallel structures like memory architectures).
 These measurements formed quality (Q) and cost (C) matrices over the task-planner grid.
- **Synthetic Data Generation for Hardware:** To overcome the impracticality of exhaustive physical robot rollouts for router training, a synthetic data pipeline was established. This involved sampling scenes offline, using a large VLM to generate candidate instructions with reference skill decompositions, running each planner on these synthetic tasks to record sequences and latencies, and then using an LLM judge to score the quality of the planner's output against the reference. The resulting router was deployed zero-shot on physical hardware.
- **Router Architectures:** DIRECT employs a lightweight router whose inference cost is negligible (20-50 ms) compared to VLM planners (>1 second). The router input ϕ(x) is generated by encoding the scene (using a frozen SigLIP-family vision encoder) and the instruction (using a frozen BGE-M3 text encoder), then concatenating these embeddings. Various parameter-efficient router architectures were ablated, including linear models, k-nearest-neighbors (KNN), pairwise preference aggregated KNN (PRkNN), k-means, one-versus-rest (OVR), and two-layer multilayer perceptrons (MLP).
- **Routing Objective:** The router learns to map ϕ(x) to a planner index k̂ by maximizing a utility function U(q, c), which combines quality and cost objectives. While the framework is agnostic to the specific form of U, regression heads predicting ˆq_i,k and ˆc_i,k (predicted quality and cost) were generally found to be effective, with utility functions like U_reg(ˆq_i,k, ĉ_i,k) = 1/ĉ_i,k * 1[max_k' ˆq_i,k' - ˆq_i,k < α] used for decision-making.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Router configurations were compared using a harmonic-mean efficiency score (η), which balances scaled quality (q_s) and cost savings (1-c_s), weighted by a parameter β.
- **Deployment:** At inference, the router processes a new task x=(I,ℓ), computes ϕ(x), and predicts k̂. For multi-stage tasks, the router is re-invoked independently for each step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DIRECT was conducted across three axes of test-time compute (CoT reasoning depth, model size, and memory history) on VLABench, RoboMME, and a physical Franka arm in a DROID setup, involving over 270,000 simulated routing decisions and 245 hardware trajectories.

#### 4.1. Routing on the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Test-Time Compute Axis

- **Simulation Results (VLABench):** DIRECT consistently achieved the highest routing efficiency (η) across homogeneous (e.g., GLM-4.6 Instruct vs. Thinking, Qwen3-VL 8B Instruct vs. Thinking) and heterogeneous model pairs. For open-weight homogeneous pairs, DIRECT recovered performance comparable to the expensive "Thinking" models while reducing latency by approximately 30%, with η values around 75%. Notably, for closed-weight pairs, DIRECT's success score sometimes surpassed both the cheap and expensive static choices, indicating its ability to leverage complementary strengths. These results suggested that only a subset of VLABench tasks benefit from CoT, and DIRECT could identify these specific tasks from multimodal context.
- **Physical Validation (Franka DROID):** Using Qwen3.5-9B (Instruct vs. Thinking) on tasks requiring common sense, spatial reasoning, and perceptual understanding, DIRECT demonstrated a practical advantage. While the non-thinking planner was fast but less successful, CoT reasoning significantly increased success at a 20x latency cost. DIRECT effectively escalated to the thinking model only when beneficial, achieving close to the Oracle's success and latency, and significantly outperforming fixed baselines and OOD-based routing. On a multi-step grocery-bagging task, DIRECT's step-wise routing attained 95.24% success at 6.85 seconds average latency, matching the Thinking model's success (90.48%) at a much lower cost (19.58 seconds).

#### 4.2. Routing on the Model Size Test-Time Compute Axis

- **Simulation Results (VLABench):** Routing over Qwen3-VL Instruct variants (2B to 235B) revealed that individual model performance was non-monotonic with scale. DIRECT's cumulative routing strategy (e.g., combining 2B+4B+8B+32B) transformed this erratic curve into monotonic improvement. It recovered each model’s distinct skill coverage without inheriting its weaknesses. For instance, the cumulative router improved performance by 5.1 points over the most expensive 32B model, while reducing average latency by 32.4 seconds, by falling back on smaller, faster models when their capabilities sufficed.
- **Physical Validation (Franka DROID):** Using Qwen3.5-VL 9B and 27B Instruct on household skills, the 27B planner generally improved command success but not uniformly across all skills. Significant gains were observed for complex skills like "Fold" and "Close," while basic skills like "Put/Place" showed minimal change. This validated the simulation finding that model size primarily broadens the breadth of skills a planner can reliably command, rather than uniformly enhancing all capabilities.

#### 4.3. Routing on the Memory Context Test-Time Compute Axis

- **Simulation Results (RoboMME):** DIRECT's routed Pareto frontiers, across various memory architectures (FrameSamp, TokenDrop, SimpleSG, GroundSG, MemER) and difficulty suites, consistently achieved a success-cost point superior to the most performant individual architecture, often at a fraction of the cost of the most expensive specialist (MemER). This indicated that no single memory model was optimal across all task contexts, and DIRECT effectively identified task-specific optimal memory configurations.
- **Physical Validation (Franka DROID):** On tasks stressing recall (Unobservable Goal vs. Observable Goal), DIRECT demonstrated its ability to allocate memory-oriented compute selectively. Routing between a memory-free and a memory planner, DIRECT achieved 100% success—matching the oracle—while processing roughly half the number of frames (15.6 vs. 32) compared to the always-memory planner. It outperformed both the memory-free variant and other baselines, confirming that memory is only worth its cost when tasks genuinely depend on distant history.

In summary, DIRECT consistently matched or exceeded the performance of stronger, more expensive VLM planners, while substantially reducing average computational costs, across diverse embodied planning tasks and scaling dimensions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the field of embodied artificial intelligence and the practical deployment of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

First, the work directly addresses a critical challenge in VLM deployment: the growing friction between advanced VLM capabilities and their real-world viability due to high test-time compute costs and latency. By demonstrating that naive scaling of compute is often wasteful and yields diminishing returns, the paper highlights the necessity of intelligent resource allocation.

Second, the introduction of DIRECT, a lightweight, multimodal context-aware routing framework, provides a practical solution for this challenge. DIRECT's ability to dynamically allocate compute per task, by inferring task demands from scene and instruction context, allows for the recovery of near-frontier VLM performance at substantially lower costs. For instance, achieving similar success rates at up to 65% lower average latency, or by processing half the amount of historical visual information, translates directly into more efficient, responsive, and thus more viable robotic systems. This increased efficiency can broaden the range of applications for VLM-powered embodied agents, making them suitable for real-time human-robot interaction or resource-constrained environments.

Third, the diagnostic analyses conducted across reasoning depth, model size, and memory history provide valuable empirical insights into the nature of VLM capabilities. The finding that different scaling axes yield qualitatively distinct and non-uniform gains challenges the previous assumption of uniform benefits from increased compute. This understanding is crucial for guiding future VLM architecture design, training strategies, and prompting techniques, encouraging more targeted and efficient development efforts rather than brute-force scaling.

Fourth, the validation on physical robot hardware (Franka DROID) solidifies the real-world applicability of DIRECT. The framework's success in zero-shot manipulation and long-horizon chaining tasks, including a multi-step grocery bagging scenario, demonstrates its robustness beyond simulation environments. This hardware validation is particularly important for fostering confidence in deploying such adaptive systems in actual robotic deployments.

Finally, the methodology suggests that effective routers can be built from lightweight, embedding-based features, keeping the router's own overhead negligible compared to the VLM planners it manages. This low overhead ensures that the routing mechanism itself does not become a new bottleneck. While the current framework operates over a fixed pool of planners and is trained offline, it lays foundational work for future research into adaptive pool management and more complex cross-stage dependencies in multi-stage tasks, further refining the learning theory of embodied routing.

In essence, this research positions the high-level VLM planner as a dynamic, adaptable component, offering a practical pathway toward realizing frontier-level embodied planning capabilities with significantly improved cost-efficiency, thereby accelerating the deployment of advanced robotic systems.
