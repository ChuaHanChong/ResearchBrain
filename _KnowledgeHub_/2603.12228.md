# Neural Thickets: Diverse Task Experts Are Dense Around Pretrained Weights

> [!summary] Summary
> MIT CSAIL researchers Yulu Gan and Phillip Isola propose that large pretrained neural networks exist in a "thicket" regime, where their weight space is dense with diverse task-specific experts accessible via simple random perturbations. Their "RandOpt" algorithm, which randomly samples and ensembles these perturbations, achieved performance competitive with or superior to established gradient-based post-training methods on various LLM and VLM tasks.

## Problem

- Prevailing assumptions suggest that effective adaptation to downstream tasks after pretraining requires structured, iterative optimization methods.
- A limited understanding of the structural properties of the multi-task loss landscape around large pretrained weights, specifically regarding solution density and diversity.
- The conventional view of a pretrained model as merely a single optimal starting point, potentially overlooking latent capabilities accessible through simpler means.

## Method

- Conducted empirical analysis on Qwen2.5 (0.5B-32B) and other models by applying ==Gaussian noise perturbations== to pretrained weights to measure solution density and diversity across tasks.
- Developed ==RandOpt==, a fully parallel, gradient-free post-training algorithm that samples numerous random parameter perturbations and ensembles predictions from the top-K performing models.
- Performed controlled experiments in a minimal 1D signal setting to simulate and understand the emergence of the =="thicket regime"== under different pretraining scenarios.

## Results

- RandOpt achieved performance competitive with or superior to PPO, GRPO, and ES across various LLM and VLM tasks, for example, obtaining **85.0%** accuracy on Countdown with OLMo3-7B-Instruct, surpassing ES (**71.0%**).
- Solution density around pretrained weights scales monotonically with model size, with a higher fraction of random perturbations leading to performance gains in larger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B), and this local neighborhood is populated by diverse specialists, as indicated by increasing "Spectral Discordance" with model scale and distinct clusters in performance spectra.
- Ensembling predictions from multiple selected perturbations (K > 1) is crucial for RandOpt's strong performance, significantly outperforming selection of only the single best perturbation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained weight spaces form a "thicket" where the neighborhood around weights is significantly dense with diverse task-improving solutions, contrasting with a "needle in a haystack" landscape for untrained models.
> - Both the density of task-improving solutions and their diversity (tendency for specialization rather than generalization) exhibit monotonic scaling laws, increasing with model size.
> - Simple random search and ensembling (RandOpt) can effectively discover and leverage these latent task experts, suggesting that complex iterative optimization is not always necessary for post-training large models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work contributes to the understanding of the loss landscape of deep neural networks, particularly in the context of large pretrained models and their subsequent adaptation to downstream tasks. Traditionally, the loss landscape for untrained models is often conceptualized as a "needle in a haystack" problem, where good solutions are sparse and difficult to find, necessitating sophisticated optimization methods like gradient descent. This perspective has been reinforced by concepts like the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, which suggests that effective initializations are rare.

The paper posits that the landscape significantly changes after extensive pretraining. It aligns with and expands upon observations from the field of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), such as LoRA, which demonstrate that effective adaptation often occurs within low-dimensional subspaces of the enormous parameter space. This suggests that task-relevant directions are efficiently accessible. The work also connects to meta-learning, where algorithms like MAML aim to find initializations from which rapid adaptation is possible. The authors suggest that pretraining implicitly achieves a "MAML-like init."

Furthermore, the paper engages with the literature on randomized search and evolutionary methods in machine learning, which have shown competitive performance in certain settings. While previous works (e.g., those by Schmidhuber et al. and Oller et al.) explored random guessing for simple problems or sequential random search for control, this paper specifically investigates the efficacy of fully parallel random guessing for post-training large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) in contemporary, complex tasks. It contrasts with standard iterative optimization algorithms like PPO, GRPO, and ES by proposing a non-iterative, parallel approach.

By characterizing the pretrained weight space as a "thicket" dense with diverse task-specific experts, this research challenges the singular view of a "pretrained model" as merely a starting point and instead proposes viewing it as implicitly defining a distribution of specialized models. This provides a novel interpretation of the "flat minima" phenomenon, suggesting that while the aggregate pretraining loss surface might be flat, the underlying multi-task landscape can be highly structured with task-specific peaks and valleys.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the structural properties of the multi-task loss landscape around pretrained weights in large neural networks. The authors aim to challenge the conventional view that pretraining produces a single, optimal starting point for subsequent iterative adaptation. Instead, they propose viewing the outcome of pretraining as implicitly defining a *distribution* over parameter vectors, a subset of which already contains effective task-specific experts.

The key motivations driving this investigation are:

- **Re-evaluating Post-training Assumptions:** The prevailing assumption is that finding effective adaptations for downstream tasks requires structured, iterative optimization methods (e.g., gradient descent or reinforcement learning algorithms like PPO). The authors are motivated by the counter-intuitive observation that, in large pretrained models, simple random parameter perturbations can yield substantial performance improvements, suggesting that the landscape around pretrained weights might be more hospitable than previously thought for "guess and check" methods.
- **Characterizing the Post-Pretraining Loss Landscape:** A central motivation is to understand *how* the loss landscape changes after pretraining to enable the efficacy of random search. Specifically, they aim to measure:
 - **Solution Density:** The proportion of parameter perturbations in a local neighborhood that improve performance on a given task. They hypothesize that this density increases with model scale.
 - **Solution Diversity:** Whether the task-improving perturbations are generalists (improving overall performance) or specialists (improving one task while potentially degrading others). They hypothesize that diverse specialists populate the neighborhood.
- **Developing a Simple, Parallel Post-Training Algorithm:** Based on the hypothesized density and diversity of solutions, the authors are motivated to explore a minimal, fully parallel post-training method. This method, termed RandOpt, aims to leverage these landscape properties by randomly sampling perturbations and ensembling the best-performing ones, thereby providing a practical probe for their theoretical claims about the thicket regime.
- **Understanding Scaling Effects:** The research is motivated to understand how solution density and diversity scale with model size, linking the emergent "thicket regime" to the increasing scale and performance of foundation models.

In essence, the work seeks to shift the perspective from pretraining yielding a single best model to pretraining yielding a richly structured region of weight space, dense with a variety of specialized solutions that can be accessed with surprisingly simple, non-iterative search methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach involving empirical analysis of large language and vision-language models, controlled experiments in a minimal 1D signal setting, and the development and evaluation of a novel post-training algorithm.

#### 3.1. Structure of the Multi-task Loss Landscape Around Pretrained Weights

- **Model Families and Tasks:** The analysis uses Qwen2.5 models (0.5B to 32B parameters), including instruction-tuned variants, across a diverse set of tasks: mathematical reasoning (Countdown, GSM8K, MATH-500, OlympiadBench), code generation (MBPP), creative writing (ROCStories), and chemistry (USPTO).
- **Perturbation Method:** Models are perturbed by adding Gaussian noise (ϵ ∼ N(0, σ^2 I)) to their pretrained weights, with a fixed noise scale (σ = 0.005 for initial exploration).
- **Solution Density Measurement:**
 - **Definition:** Solution Density δ(m) is defined as the probability that a random perturbation ϵ improves the base model’s score by a margin m: P[s(θ + ϵ) ≥ s(θ) + m].
 - **Measurement:** Quantified by sampling 1000 random perturbations and observing the fraction that meet performance improvement thresholds (e.g., +5% accuracy).
 - **Visualization:** Accuracy landscapes are visualized in 2D using random projection of perturbed models, with colors indicating relative accuracy change.
- **Solution Diversity Measurement:**
 - **Definition:** Spectral Discordance (D) is defined as 1 - (average off-diagonal Pearson correlation of a percentile-rank matrix P). P contains percentile ranks of N seeds across M tasks. D approaches 1 for orthogonal task rankings (specialists) and 0 for parallel rankings (generalists).
 - **Measurement:** Evaluated 500 random weight perturbations across seven diverse tasks for models of different sizes.
 - **Analysis:** Visualized using performance spectra (plotting percentile ranks of individual perturbations across tasks) and PCA projection of performance vectors, followed by K-means clustering. An RGB visualization maps different task accuracies to color channels to show correlation.

#### 3.2. Minimal Setting Experiment (1D Signals)

- **Model:** A multilayer perceptron (MLP) acts as a next-value predictor for 1D signals.
- **Training Distribution:** A mixture of random function types (sinusoidal, linear, harmonic, sigmoidal, sawtooth, square waves).
- **Pretraining Scenarios:**
 1. No pretraining (Xavier/Kaiming initialization).
 2. Pretraining on all mixed signal types.
 3. Pretraining only on linear signals.
- **Probing:** N=1000 random Gaussian parameter perturbations (ϵ ∼ N(0, σ)) are applied. The autoregressive rollouts of perturbed models are compared to a target linear test signal.
- **Evaluation:** Focus on approximation (fitting the same function used in post-training) and generalization (fitting new functions of the post-trained type).

#### 3.3. RandOpt Algorithm

- **Mechanism:** A fully parallel, gradient-free post-training method.
- **Training (Random Guessing & Checking):**
 1. Sample N random seeds and corresponding noise scales (σ uniform from a set Σ).
 2. Generate N perturbed parameter vectors: θ_i = θ + σ_i * ϵ(s_i).
 3. Evaluate each f_θ_i on a small training/validation set D_train to obtain a performance score v_i.
 4. Select the top-K performing models based on v_i (I_top = arg topK(v_i)).
- **Inference (Ensembling of Predictions):**
 1. For a test input x, generate predictions from the selected top-K models.
 2. Aggregate individual predictions via majority voting (for discrete tasks) or mean ensembling (for continuous tasks, as shown in 1D signal experiments).
- **Computational Budget:** FLOPs are carefully normalized across RandOpt and baseline methods (PPO, GRPO, ES) for fair comparison.

#### 3.4. Evaluation of RandOpt

- **LLMs:** Evaluated on Qwen, Llama, and OLMo3 models (0.5B-8B, base and instruct variants) across the same seven task categories used for landscape analysis. Compared against PPO, GRPO, ES, Test-Time Majority Vote (TT-MV), and Best-of-N, all with equivalent training FLOPs.
- **VLMs:** Tested on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct on the GQA dataset, perturbing only the language model component.
- **Scaling Properties:** Investigated how RandOpt performance varies with population size N, ensemble size K, and the scale of the base model.
- **Distillation:** Explored distilling the top-K ensemble into a single model via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on "hard samples" to reduce inference-time cost.
- **Analysis of Thicket Types:** Decomposed accuracy gains on GSM8K for Qwen2.5-3B-Inst into "reasoning thicket" (base wrong, adapted model correct) and "format thicket" (base correct answer, but wrong format, adapted model fixes format).
- **Generative Models (Proof of Concept):** Explored "color thickets" in Stable Diffusion XL, selecting models based on color-specific text prompts and using mean ensembling during denoising.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the nature of pretrained weight spaces and the efficacy of the RandOpt algorithm:

- **Scaling Law of Solution Density:** In large models (e.g., Qwen2.5-32B), the Gaussian neighborhood around pretrained weights is significantly denser with task-improving solutions compared to smaller models. This density exhibits a monotonic scaling law, increasing with model size, meaning a higher fraction of random perturbations lead to performance gains (Figures 2, 3a, 12). For small, untrained models, the landscape resembles a "needle in a haystack," while large pretrained models operate in a "thicket regime."
- **Scaling Law of Solution Diversity:** The local neighborhood is populated by diverse specialists rather than generalists. Individual perturbations tend to improve performance on specific tasks while potentially degrading others. The "Spectral Discordance" measure, quantifying this specialization, increases monotonically with model scale (Figure 3b). Visualizations through performance spectra and PCA projections further confirm the existence of distinct clusters of task experts within the perturbed parameter space (Figure 4).
- **Thicket Emergence in Minimal Settings:** The "thicket regime" is not exclusive to LLMs; it can be reproduced in simpler autoregressive models for 1D signals. Thickets emerge when the base model is pretrained on a diverse set of signal types. No pretraining results in a "needle in a haystack" regime, while pretraining on only one signal type leads to a "plateau regime" where the base model is already optimal (Figure 5, Table 5, 6).
- **RandOpt Performance:** Despite its simplicity and O(1) training steps (in terms of wall-clock time), RandOpt, using a population size N and ensembling K=50 top perturbations, achieves accuracy competitive with or superior to established post-training methods (PPO, GRPO, ES) and inference-time baselines (TT-MV, Best-of-N) across a wide range of LLM and VLM tasks and model scales (Figure 6, Table 4). For example, on OLMo3-7B-Instruct, RandOpt achieved 85.0% on Countdown, outperforming ES (71.0%) and GRPO (68.5%).
- **Crucial Role of Ensembling:** Ensembling predictions from multiple selected perturbations (K > 1) is critical for RandOpt's strong performance. RandOpt with K=1 (taking only the single best perturbation) shows substantial improvement over the base model but is generally less effective than ensembling (Figure 1c, 11).
- **Scaling Properties of RandOpt:**
 - Performance generally improves monotonically with increasing population size N (search budget), particularly at sufficiently low selection ratios (K/N).
 - A strong, sufficiently large pretrained base model is essential for RandOpt to be effective. For very small models (e.g., GPT-2 0.1B), RandOpt offers negligible gains, and it completely fails when applied to untrained models ("RandOpt from scratch") (Figure 8).
- **Distillation Efficacy:** Distilling the knowledge from the RandOpt ensemble (top-K models) into a single model via supervised fine-tuning on hard examples can achieve performance comparable to the ensemble, significantly reducing inference-time computational cost (Table 2). This process required approximately 2% of the initial training cost.
- **Types of Thickets:** Analysis on GSM8K reveals that performance gains from RandOpt are attributable to two types of "thickets": "reasoning thickets" (where the adapted model correctly solves problems the base model failed) and "format thickets" (where the adapted model fixes output formatting issues that caused the base model's correct answer to be marked incorrect) (Figure 9). Both contribute substantially to the overall accuracy improvement.
- **Generalizability to Generative Models:** The concept of "thickets" extends beyond language models, demonstrated with "color thickets" in a diffusion model (Stable Diffusion XL). Perturbations can lead to specialists in generating specific visual attributes (e.g., color palettes), which can then be ensembled (Table 7, 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the understanding and practice of deep learning:

- **Rethinking Pretraining and Model Representation:** The central finding that pretrained weights reside in a "thicket" dense with diverse task experts challenges the conventional view of a pretrained model as a singular entity. It suggests that pretraining implicitly creates a rich, multi-faceted parameter distribution capable of supporting varied task-specific behaviors. This perspective encourages viewing pretrained models not just as "starting points," but as comprehensive "representations" that already contain the building blocks for diverse adaptations.
- **Simplified and Scalable Post-Training:** The efficacy of RandOpt demonstrates that complex, iterative, and gradient-based fine-tuning methods might not always be necessary for effective post-training, especially for large, well-pretrained models. RandOpt's O(1) training steps and fully parallel nature offer a highly efficient alternative in terms of wall-clock time and communication bandwidth, making it particularly attractive for settings with abundant parallel compute, federated learning, or latency-critical applications. This could democratize access to advanced model adaptation for researchers and practitioners without extensive experience in complex RL algorithms.
- **Deeper Understanding of Loss Landscapes:** The characterization of solution density and diversity as scaling laws provides new insights into how pretraining fundamentally reshapes the multi-task loss landscape. It suggests that "flat minima" in aggregate loss might conceal significant task-specific structure, where pretrained models are "jacks of all trades, masters of none" that can be specialized by minor perturbations.
- **Unlocking Latent Capabilities:** The success of RandOpt implies that large pretrained models possess a vast array of latent capabilities and specialized behaviors that are not fully expressed by the base model but can be readily accessed through simple random exploration. This opens avenues for efficiently discovering and harnessing these hidden capabilities without extensive, targeted training.
- **Implications for "Pretraining Is All You Need":** The work reinforces the growing sentiment that once a sufficiently powerful pretrained representation is obtained, further adaptation becomes remarkably facile. It suggests that the primary challenge shifts from learning fundamental representations to efficiently selecting and combining already latent specialized skills.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The findings invite further investigation into the precise mechanisms by which pretraining cultivates these "thickets," the limits of their generalizability, and methods for extending RandOpt to more complex and structured prediction tasks (e.g., novel architecture search in the thicket). The demonstration of "color thickets" in diffusion models suggests broad applicability across different modalities.
