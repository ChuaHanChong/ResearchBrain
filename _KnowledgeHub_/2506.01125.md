# iRonCub 3: The Jet-Powered Flying Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed iRonCub 3, a jet-powered humanoid robot, which achieved its first controlled liftoff. This work validates the concept of aerial humanoid mobility, demonstrating the robot becoming airborne for brief periods while highlighting critical challenges in jet dynamics modeling and sensor robustness.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots are primarily restricted to terrestrial locomotion, limiting their operational scope in three-dimensional environments.
- Integrating full-body aerial mobility into a humanoid form introduces complex issues in robot control, state estimation, and system integration.
- Current aerial manipulators or hybrid aerial-terrestrial robots often lack the comprehensive physical interaction capabilities inherent to humanoids.

## Method

- The **iRonCub 3** robot was developed, based on the iCub3 humanoid platform, and augmented with four ==JetCat P250 Pro== turbines on custom forearms and a jetpack.
- A software architecture was implemented, featuring Unscented Kalman Filters for thrust and base pose estimation, and a Linear Parameter Varying Model Predictive Control for flight.
- A dedicated outdoor experimental facility was established, including safety protocols and heat management, for operating the jet-powered humanoid robot.

## Results

- Simulation results validated the control framework's ability to track desired center of mass position and orientation during takeoff and trajectory following.
- Experimental liftoff of the physical iRonCub 3 was achieved, with the robot briefly becoming airborne while maintaining roll, pitch, and yaw angles within approximately 5 degrees.
- Observed deviations, such as a horizontal drift of up to 60 cm, were attributed to the sim-to-real gap caused by simplified jet dynamics models, vibration-induced sensor noise, and inaccuracies in force-torque sensor calibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The successful first controlled liftoff of iRonCub 3 confirms the fundamental feasibility of integrating jet propulsion with a full-body humanoid robot.
> - Achieving stable and repeatable aerial maneuvers requires precise modeling of highly dynamic jet propulsion and robust sensor performance amidst significant vibration.
> - Comprehensive mechanical and software integration is critical for developing multi-modal humanoid robots capable of operating in complex 3D environments.
