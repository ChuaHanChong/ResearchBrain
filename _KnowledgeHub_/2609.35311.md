# RoGSW4RLD: Feed-Forward 4D Gaussian Lifting for Robot World Model Rollouts

> [!summary] Summary
> RoGSW4RLD is a feed-forward framework that lifts synchronized multi-camera robot videos, recorded or generated by action-conditioned world models, into one time-queryable metric 4D Gaussian field. Stage 1 jointly fuses views with robot mesh anchoring and forward-kinematics-conditioned deformation; Stage 2 refines geometry and appearance via rendering feedback while preserving displacements. On 256 held-out DROID episodes it beats camera-wise merging by 2.15 dB novel-view PSNR and 61% lower robot displacement error.

## Problem

- Action-conditioned multi-camera video world models like Cosmos 3 output disparate per-camera video collections, not a shared metric scene queryable across viewpoints and time.
- Reconstructing each camera stream independently and merging via calibration enforces no cross-view consistency during geometry and motion estimation, leaving duplicated or conflicting surfaces.
- Mixing moving wrist cameras with fixed external views entangles ego-motion with scene dynamics, and existing feed-forward 4D reconstructors ignore the robot's known geometry and kinematics.

## Method

- Kinematically conditioned joint lifting (Stage 1): a ==MoVieS/VGGT== backbone with ==LoRA adapters== processes all views and times jointly, predicting metric depth, Gaussian attributes, and query-time 3D displacements.
- Robot ==mesh source anchoring== plus FK-conditioned deformation: rasterized articulated-mesh depth replaces predicted depth on robot pixels, while a zero-initialized residual branch injects ==forward-kinematics== displacements into the learned motion.
- Displacement-preserving refinement (Stage 2): a ==ReSplat-style recurrent updater== pools contribution-weighted rendering-error feedback and applies time-shared position corrections, keeping each Gaussian's center trajectory between query times exactly unchanged.

## Results

- On recorded DROID inputs the full model beats MoVieS: novel-view PSNR **16.08** vs **13.93 dB**, depth AbsRel **0.197** vs **0.373**, robot displacement error **8.6** vs **22.2 mm**.
- Gains persist on Cosmos **3** rollouts: novel-view PSNR **15.52** vs **13.69 dB**, AbsRel **0.215** vs **0.404**; Stage **1** alone takes **0.59** s per clip versus **9.08** s with four refinement updates.
- Ablations: blocking cross-camera attention leaves novel AbsRel near **0.391** despite refinement; removing FK conditioning raises robot error from **8.64** to **30.15 mm**; removing mesh anchoring raises robot depth error from **13.7** to **37.6 mm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video world model outputs can be spatialized after the fact: a frozen generator plus a robot-aware feed-forward lifter yields queryable metric 4D scenes without retraining the generator.
> - Joint cross-view field formation beats post-hoc calibrated merging; better source-view photometric fitting cannot recover held-out geometry lost by reconstructing cameras independently.
> - Known robot priors split cleanly: mesh anchoring fixes where the robot is, kinematic conditioning fixes how it moves; analytic FK is equally accurate, so learning mainly unifies the representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Robot manipulation scenes are often recorded by several synchronized cameras. A wrist camera moves with the arm while external cameras stay fixed. Separate camera videos do not, by themselves, form one spatial scene. They mix robot motion with scene motion, so reconstructing each feed separately and aligning the results afterward can leave duplicated or conflicting geometry. RoGSW4RLD instead reconstructs the camera feeds jointly and uses known robot shape and kinematics to anchor the robot in the shared scene. On held-out views, this joint approach produced better reconstructions than the per-camera baseline it was compared with.

(a) The PointWorld-DROID setup: a Franka Panda arm, a moving wrist-mounted stereo camera, and fixed external cameras. (b) Even after alignment, the independent reconstruction still shows inconsistencies, while the joint metric reconstruction (Stage 1) appears more coherent. (c) For the joint result, the caption identifies reduced duplication and misalignment in renderings and merged geometry.

### 2. One shared scene that moves over time

The output is a *4D Gaussian field*: a collection of 3D Gaussian primitives whose positions and appearance vary over time. Rendering the field produces an image from a selected camera at a selected time. The field is *metric*, meaning its coordinates have a fixed real-world scale. The input can include video predicted by an existing world model. The approach requires no per-scene optimization or retraining of the underlying world model.

Stage 1 processes the synchronized views together. It places visible robot surfaces using the articulated robot mesh and calibrated cameras. It also feeds supplied joint states and forward kinematics (FK) as cues to a learned motion predictor; FK computes how robot links move from joint positions. The mesh anchors supported robot surfaces at source times. The learned model still predicts their query-time displacement. The video supplies the cues for object and other scene dynamics.

Stage 2 adapts the recurrent refiner of [[2510.08575|ReSplat]]. It compares rendered source views with observations and updates appearance and placement, four times at inference. Each Gaussian receives the same position correction at every query time, which preserves its Stage 1 center displacement. Rendered motion can still change, because visibility and compositing weights can change.

Wrist and external images, robot joint and gripper states, camera parameters, and the robot mesh enter joint lifting. A mesh source-anchor path and an FK-conditioned learned-deformation path build the Stage 1 field. Source-view feedback then drives a recurrent updater, repeated four times, which applies one positional correction to a Gaussian across query times.

### 3. Joint geometry, beyond better input fitting

The evaluation used 256 held-out DROID episodes. Three left-eye cameras served as inputs, and paired right-eye cameras served as held-out views. Five times were selected from a 17-frame interval. Three metrics were used:

- **Novel-view PSNR** measures image similarity on the held-out views.
- **AbsRel** measures normalized depth error against stereo depth estimates. These estimates are not sensor ground truth.
- **Robot EPE** measures error in estimated robot-track displacement, not absolute position.

On recorded observations, Stage 1+2 outperformed [MoVieS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Lin_MoVieS_Motion-Aware_4D_Dynamic_View_Synthesis_in_One_Second_CVPR_2026_paper.html), which reconstructs camera streams separately:

- **Novel-view PSNR:** 16.08 dB versus 13.93 dB.
- **AbsRel:** 0.197 versus 0.373.
- **Robot EPE:** 8.6 mm versus 22.2 mm.

MoVieS received one source-stereo scale scalar per camera; RoGSW4RLD received no test-time stereo depth. Architectures, training, and robot inputs differ, so this is a system-level comparison.

The comparison was repeated on Cosmos 3 rollouts, which are world-model predictions. RoGSW4RLD reached 15.52 dB and 0.215 AbsRel, against 13.69 dB and 0.404 for MoVieS. These rollouts are scored against recorded futures, so the scores combine generation and reconstruction error.

A sharper test kept the jointly trained checkpoint frozen and blocked cross-camera interaction at inference. This test differs from three independently trained monocular models. In this camera-wise variant, Stage 2 lifted source PSNR from 19.64 to 23.71 dB. Its novel-view AbsRel nevertheless stayed at 0.391–0.392, while joint Stage 1 reached 0.200.

Inference-time interventions without retraining on 256 episodes. On recorded observations, camera-wise Stage 2 reaches 23.71 source PSNR and 0.392 novel AbsRel. Joint Stage 1 reaches 21.92 and 0.200, so the camera-wise variant's better source fitting does not yield the held-out geometry of joint Stage 1.

### 4. From video to scene

Combining views while the field is being formed matters because the target is a shared metric scene that can be queried across time and viewpoint. RoGSW4RLD reconstructs supplied video and does not test downstream planning or control. Its evaluation used calibrated cameras, known robot geometry, supplied trajectories, and short intervals. Wider baselines, new robot morphologies, and longer rollouts remain untested.
