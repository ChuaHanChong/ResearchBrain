# MiniLLM: Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MiniLLM introduces a knowledge distillation method for generative large language models, leveraging reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence and on-policy optimization to compress white-box teacher models. Student models distilled with MiniLLM achieve superior performance, higher precision in generated text, and improved calibration across various instruction-following tasks, facilitating more efficient and reliable LLM deployment.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) incur substantial computational and memory costs, impeding their broad deployment and fine-tuning, especially in resource-constrained settings.
- Standard Knowledge Distillation (KD) methods, particularly those based on forward Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD), prove ineffective for compressing generative LLMs, often leading student models to produce low-quality or inaccurate text.
- Forward KLD encourages student models to cover all modes of a complex teacher distribution, potentially causing them to assign excessive probability to less probable or 'void' output regions.

## Method

- The methodology replaces standard forward KLD with ==reverse KLD (KL[q||p])== to encourage mode-seeking behavior, thereby focusing the student model on the major, high-quality modes of the teacher's distribution.
- An ==on-policy optimization strategy==, similar to Reinforcement Learning, is implemented to train the student model by sampling from its own distribution and deriving gradients using the Policy Gradient Theorem.
- The approach incorporates specific techniques to stabilize on-policy training, including ==single-step decomposition== for variance reduction, teacher-mixed sampling to prevent 'reward hacking', and length normalization to mitigate bias towards shorter sentences.

## Results

- MINILLM consistently outperforms all tested baselines (SFT w/o KD, KD, SeqKD) across diverse evaluation datasets and model families (GPT-2, OPT, LLaMA), demonstrating superior overall generation quality and generalizability.
- The approach achieves higher precision and quality in generated responses, as evidenced by improved **Rouge-L scores** and higher **GPT-4 feedback scores**, with some student models even surpassing teacher models on Rouge-L for specific tasks.
- Student models distilled with MINILLM exhibit **reduced exposure bias** and better calibration compared to other KD methods, particularly for long-text generation and classification tasks, enhancing model reliability and trustworthiness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KL[q||p]) is inherently more suitable for generative Knowledge Distillation than forward KLD (KL[p||q]), as it guides smaller student models to prioritize high-quality, major modes of the teacher's distribution.
> - On-policy optimization effectively addresses the challenges of training generative student models by allowing them to learn from their own generated samples, which significantly mitigates exposure bias.
> - Specific algorithmic improvements, such as single-step decomposition for gradient calculation, teacher-mixed sampling during generation, and length normalization, are crucial for robustly applying policy gradient methods to generative Knowledge Distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Knowledge Distillation (KD), addressing a critical challenge in the deployment and accessibility of powerful, yet computationally intensive, LLMs.

**Large Language Models (LLMs):** The rapid advancement of LLMs (e.g., GPT series, PaLM, LLaMA) has revolutionized various NLP tasks, demonstrating emergent abilities and strong performance across diverse applications. However, their immense size necessitates substantial computational resources for training, inference, and deployment, limiting their widespread practical use, especially on edge devices or in scenarios with strict latency and cost constraints. This creates a significant demand for model compression techniques.

**Knowledge Distillation (KD):** Knowledge Distillation, first formally introduced by Hinton et al. (2015), is a prominent model compression technique where a smaller "student" model is trained to mimic the behavior of a larger, more powerful "teacher" model. KD aims to transfer the rich, generalized knowledge from the teacher to the student, allowing the student to achieve comparable performance with fewer parameters and lower computational overhead.

**Existing KD for NLP:**
- **Traditional NLP KD:** Historically, KD in NLP has been widely applied to smaller language *understanding* models (e.g., BERT, Transformers for classification tasks). Methods often involve mimicking teacher's output logits (word-level KD), hidden states, or attention distributions.
- **Black-Box KD for LLMs:** More recently, with the advent of proprietary LLM APIs (like ChatGPT), a category known as "black-box KD" has emerged. Here, researchers train smaller models by generating data from the black-box teacher (e.g., instruction-response pairs) and then fine-tuning the student on this generated dataset (often referred to as sequence-level KD or instruction tuning). Examples include Stanford Alpaca and Vicuna. This approach is effective but limited by the inability to access the teacher's internal probabilities or hidden states, potentially leading to suboptimal knowledge transfer.
- **White-Box KD for LLMs (The Gap):** The authors identify a significant gap: while white-box KD (where the teacher's internal workings, like output distributions, are accessible) is well-studied for smaller language *understanding* models, its application to large, *generative* language models, particularly in a white-box setting, is "still under-explored." This gap has become even more pertinent with the rise of open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, OPT), which allow full access to their internal states, enabling richer distillation signals.

**Contribution of MiniLLM:** This work directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel white-box KD approach specifically designed for generative LLMs. It challenges the traditional reliance on forward Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) in generative KD, arguing its sub-optimality, and introduces reverse KLD along with a sophisticated on-policy optimization framework. This positions MiniLLM as a foundational contribution to improving the efficiency and quality of distilled generative LLMs in an era of increasing open-source model availability. The paper also tackles known issues in generative model training like exposure bias and calibration, further solidifying its relevance in the current landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of the "MiniLLM" paper are clearly articulated.

### 3. Overarching Objective

To develop an effective knowledge distillation (KD) approach that can compress Large Language Models (LLMs) into smaller, more efficient models while preserving or even enhancing their performance in generative tasks, particularly in an instruction-following setting. The focus is specifically on *white-box* KD, where the internal output distribution of the teacher LLM is accessible.

### 4. Addressing the Limitations of Standard KD Objectives for Generative LLMs

The central motivation stems from the authors' observation that traditional KD objectives, which typically minimize the forward Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KL[p||q]), are sub-optimal for generative LLMs.
- **Complex Output Space:** For open-ended text generation, the output space is vast and complex, meaning the teacher's probability distribution (p) can have many more "modes" (plausible outputs) than a smaller student model (q) can possibly represent due to its limited capacity.
- **Zero-Forcing Behavior:** Minimizing forward KLD forces the student to "cover all modes" of the teacher distribution. This often leads the student to assign unreasonably high probabilities to low-probability or "void" regions of the teacher's distribution. In practice, this results in the generation of low-quality, less precise, or even nonsensical text. This is analogous to "zero-forcing" in generative modeling, where the student tries to match every aspect of the teacher, even the less important ones.

### 5. Enhancing Generative Quality and Reliability

The authors are motivated to produce student models that generate:
- **More precise responses:** Higher overlap with ground-truth answers.
- **Higher overall quality:** Judged favorably by automatic metrics (like GPT-4 feedback) and human evaluators.
- **Lower exposure bias:** Overcoming the training-inference mismatch common in generative models, which causes errors to accumulate during free-run generation.
- **Better calibration:** Ensuring that the model's predicted probabilities accurately reflect the true likelihood of events, which is crucial for reliability.
- **Improved long-text generation performance:** Standard methods tend to favor shorter, less complex outputs.

### 6. Capitalizing on Open-Source LLMs

With the increasing availability of open-source LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, OPT), the opportunity for white-box KD, which utilizes the teacher's internal distribution, becomes more valuable. The research aims to provide a method that fully leverages these detailed signals for more effective distillation.

### 7. Scalability and Generalization

The objective also includes developing a method that is scalable across various model families and sizes (from 120M to 13B parameters), demonstrating its broad applicability and robustness in the evolving landscape of LLM development.

In essence, MiniLLM aims to develop a principled, white-box KD method for generative LLMs that overcomes the limitations of current approaches by adopting a more suitable divergence metric and robust optimization strategies, ultimately leading to smaller, more performant, and reliable student models.

### 8. Methodology and Approach

The core of MiniLLM's methodology revolves around a novel knowledge distillation objective based on reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KLD) and an advanced on-policy optimization framework.

#### 8.1. Knowledge Distillation with Reverse KLD

- **Problem with Forward KLD (KL[p||q]):** For generative tasks, the teacher distribution `p(y|x)` can be highly complex with many modes. A student model `q_θ(y|x)` with limited capacity struggles to cover all these modes. Minimizing `KL[p||q_θ]` (forward KLD) forces the student to cover all modes of the teacher, often leading it to assign high probabilities to low-probability or "void" regions of `p`. This results in less precise and sometimes nonsensical generations.
- **Proposed Solution: Reverse KLD (KL[q||p]):** MiniLLM proposes minimizing the reverse KLD, `KL[q_θ||p] = - E_{x~p_x, y~q_θ} [log(p(y|x) / q_θ(y|x))]`.
- **Advantages of Reverse KLD:**
 - **Mode-Seeking Behavior:** Unlike forward KLD, minimizing reverse KLD encourages the student `q_θ` to focus on the *major* modes of the teacher `p`. It allows `q_θ` to assign low probabilities to regions where `p` is also low, preventing the student from "overestimating" unlikely teacher outputs.
 - **Generation Correctness:** For text generation, this means the student avoids learning too many long-tail variants from the teacher and instead concentrates on generating correct and faithful responses, which is critical for practical applications requiring truthfulness and reliability.
 - **Feasibility:** It's more achievable for a low-capacity student to accurately represent the major modes of `p` rather than trying to cover all of them.

#### 8.2. On-Policy Distillation and Optimization Strategies

To optimize the reverse KLD objective, the authors derive its gradient using the Policy Gradient Theorem, akin to Reinforcement Learning (RL). This leads to an on-policy optimization approach where the student learns from samples generated by its own current policy. The gradient derivation is:
`∇L(θ) = - E_{x~p_x, y~q_θ(·|x)} Σ_{t=1}^T (R_t - 1)∇log q_θ(y_t|y_<t, x)`, where `R_t` measures the quality of generation from step `t` onwards.

However, standard policy gradient methods suffer from well-known issues in generative modeling:
- **High Variance:** Gradient estimates can be noisy, slowing down training.
- **Reward Hacking:** The student might discover degenerate sequences that coincidentally receive high "rewards" (high `p(y|x)/q_θ(y|x)` ratio) but are not truly high-quality.
- **Length Bias:** The accumulated reward `R_t` can favor shorter sentences, leading to models that output truncated responses.

To mitigate these problems, MiniLLM introduces three key strategies:

1. **Single-Step Decomposition:**
 - The gradient `∇L(θ)` is decomposed into two parts: `(∇L)_Single` (focusing on immediate single-step generation quality) and `(∇L)_Long` (focusing on longer-term accumulated rewards).
 - `(∇L)_Single` directly computes the expected value of `r_t = log(p(y_t|y_<t,x) / q_θ(y_t|y_<t,x))` over the vocabulary, which is more precise and efficient than Monte-Carlo sampling, thereby reducing variance and accelerating convergence.

2. **Teacher-Mixed Sampling:**
 - To combat reward hacking and improve the quality of samples used for training, the student samples tokens `y_t` at each step from a mixed distribution: `εp(y_t|y_<t, x) = α * p(y_t|y_<t, x) + (1 - α) * q_θ(y_t|y_<t, x)`.
 - `α` is a mixing coefficient. Sampling from this mixed distribution prevents the student from generating overly degenerate sequences by leveraging the teacher's guidance.
 - Importance sampling weights `w_t` are used to correct for the difference between `q_θ` and `εp`, although an approximation `w_t ≈ q_θ(y_t|y_<t,x) / εp(y_t|y_<t,x)` is used to reduce variance.

3. **Length Normalization:**
 - To counteract the bias towards short sentences, the accumulated reward `R_t+1` is normalized by the remaining sequence length: `R_Norm_t+1 = (1 / (T - t - 1)) * Σ_{t'=t+1}^T log(p(y_t'|y_<t',x) / q_θ(y_t'|y_<t',x))`. This ensures that longer sequences are not unfairly penalized.

#### 8.3. Training Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

The overall training process for MiniLLM involves two phases, analogous to RLHF pipelines:
1. **Initialization/Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The student model is first fine-tuned on a conditional generation dataset `D` using ground-truth responses (standard teacher-forcing). The checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is selected as the starting point.
2. **On-Policy Distillation:** The model from Phase 1 undergoes continuous training using the optimized reverse KLD objective.
 - Mini-batches of prompts are sampled from `D`.
 - Responses are generated using the teacher-mixed sampling distribution (`εp`).
 - Gradients are computed using the single-step decomposed objective, with length-normalized `R_Norm_t+1` for the long-term part. Clipping is applied for stability.
 - An additional **language modeling loss (L_PT)** is included, computed on a large pre-training corpus (`D_PT`), to preserve the student model's general language understanding and generation capabilities learned during pre-training.
 - Model parameters are updated using a combination of all three gradients: `∇L(θ) = (∇L)_Single + (∇L)_Norm_Long + ∇L_PT`.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a theoretically sound objective (reverse KLD) with practical and robust optimization techniques, distinguishes MiniLLM from prior KD approaches for generative tasks.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted in the instruction-following setting demonstrate the consistent superiority of MiniLLM over existing knowledge distillation baselines.

#### 9.1. Superior Overall Performance

- **Consistent Outperformance:** MiniLLM consistently outperforms all baselines (SFT without KD, token-level KD, and sequence-level KD) across various generative tasks, evaluation datasets (DollyEval, SelfInst, VicunaEval, S-NI, UnNI), model families (GPT-2, OPT, LLaMA), and sizes (120M to 13B parameters).
- **Higher Quality Responses:** Measured by GPT-4 feedback scores, MiniLLM generated responses receive higher overall quality ratings.
- **More Precise Responses:** Achieves higher Rouge-L scores, indicating greater overlap and precision compared to ground-truth responses.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** MiniLLM shows better performance on datasets other than the training data (Dolly), suggesting improved generalization capabilities.
- **Human Preference:** Human evaluation results on the SelfInst dataset confirm that MiniLLM's outputs are preferred over baselines, performing comparably to the teacher model.

#### 9.2. Excellent Scalability

- **Across Model Sizes and Families:** The performance improvements of MiniLLM are consistent when distilling from different teacher models (GPT-2-1.5B, GPT-J-6B, OPT-13B, LLaMA-13B) into student models ranging from 120M to 13B parameters, demonstrating the method's scalability and broad applicability.
- **Teacher Model Scaling:** Experiments show that MiniLLM effectively distills knowledge from larger teacher models, with student performance positively correlating with teacher model size, unlike some previous observations where larger teachers didn't always guarantee better students.

#### 9.3. Reduced Exposure Bias

- MiniLLM significantly lowers the "Excess Accumulated Error" (ExAccErr), a metric measuring the error due to training-decoding discrepancy (exposure bias).
- Unlike baselines, where ExAccErr continuously grows with generation length, MiniLLM maintains a much lower and stable error, especially in long-text generation. This indicates that the on-policy optimization helps alleviate the training-inference mismatch.

#### 9.4. Improved Calibration

- On text classification tasks (SST2 and BoolQ), MiniLLM student models are significantly better calibrated than other KD baselines, narrowing the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) gap with the teacher model.
- This suggests that focusing on major modes with reverse KLD leads to more reliable probability predictions, preventing the student from confidently assigning high probabilities to unlikely outputs.

#### 9.5. Enhanced Long Response Generation

- When evaluating performance based on the length of ground-truth responses, MiniLLM shows a distinct advantage for prompts requiring longer responses (≥ 6 tokens).
- This is attributed to the mode-seeking behavior of reverse KLD, which is particularly beneficial when the teacher's distribution is complex and multi-modal, as is often the case for longer, open-ended generations.

#### 9.6. Preserved Generation Diversity

- While reverse KLD is sometimes associated with mode collapse, MiniLLM maintains generation diversity, as indicated by similar distinct 4-gram proportions and language modeling losses on test sets compared to baseline models. This suggests that the proposed optimization strategies effectively mitigate potential diversity loss.

#### 9.7. Efficacy of Optimization Strategies (Ablation Studies)

- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of each proposed optimization strategy:
 - **Teacher-Mixed Sampling:** Crucial for stabilizing training and preventing "reward hacking," where models generate degenerate or repetitive responses.
 - **Length Normalization:** Essential for preventing the model from generating overly short responses.
 - **Single-Step Decomposition:** Effectively reduces training variance, leading to faster convergence and higher performance.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that MiniLLM's novel approach—minimizing reverse KLD with robust on-policy optimization—leads to distilled LLMs that are not only smaller and more efficient but also generate higher-quality, more precise, better-calibrated, and less biased responses, particularly for complex generative tasks.

### 10. Significance and Potential Impact

The "MiniLLM" paper introduces a significant advancement in the field of knowledge distillation for large language models, with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

#### 10.1. Theoretical and Methodological Contribution

- **Novel Application of Reverse KLD:** The most significant contribution is the principled adoption of reverse Kullback-Leibler Divergence (KL[q||p]) as the primary objective for distilling generative LLMs in a white-box setting. This challenges the long-standing reliance on forward KLD (KL[p||q]), which the authors convincingly argue is sub-optimal for complex, multi-modal generative tasks. This shift provides a more theoretically sound foundation for generative KD.
- **Robust On-Policy Optimization Framework:** The paper develops a sophisticated optimization approach, extending policy gradient methods with tailored strategies (single-step decomposition, teacher-mixed sampling, length normalization). These innovations effectively address common challenges in generative model training—such as high variance, reward hacking, and length bias—within the KD context, making the reverse KLD objective practically feasible and stable.
- **Addressing Key Generative Model Issues:** By tackling exposure bias and calibration directly within the KD framework, MiniLLM contributes to the broader understanding of how to train more robust and reliable generative models, beyond just compression.

#### 10.2. Practical Impact and Deployment

- **Democratizing LLMs:** MiniLLM enables the creation of smaller, more efficient LLMs that retain high generative quality, precision, and alignment. This directly addresses the immense computational demands of state-of-the-art LLMs, making them more accessible and deployable on resource-constrained hardware (e.g., mobile devices, embedded systems, cost-sensitive cloud environments). This democratizes access to advanced LLM capabilities for a wider range of users and applications.
- **Faster Inference and Lower Costs:** Smaller models translate to faster inference times and lower operational costs, which are critical factors for industrial deployment of LLM-powered applications.
- **Leveraging Open-Source Models:** The white-box nature of MiniLLM makes it particularly impactful in the context of emerging open-source LLMs (LLaMA, OPT, etc.). Researchers and developers can now effectively distill these powerful open models into custom-sized versions for specific needs without losing crucial generative capabilities.

#### 10.3. Advancing Research Directions

- **New Avenues for White-Box KD:** The work opens new research avenues for exploring white-box knowledge distillation specifically for generative models, encouraging further investigation into optimal divergence metrics and advanced optimization techniques.
- **Improved Generative Model Training:** The insights gained regarding reverse KLD, exposure bias, and calibration can inform the training of generative models more broadly, beyond just distillation.
- **Foundation for Hybrid Compression:** MiniLLM could serve as a foundational method that can be combined with other model compression techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization) to achieve even greater efficiency.
- **Reproducibility and Community Engagement:** The authors' commitment to releasing code, data, and model checkpoints fosters reproducibility and encourages further research and development by the community, accelerating progress in the field.

In conclusion, "MiniLLM" offers a principled and empirically validated solution for the efficient distillation of large generative language models. By addressing critical limitations of prior methods and proposing a robust new framework, it significantly contributes to making the power of LLMs more widely available and practically usable, while also providing valuable insights for future research in generative AI.
