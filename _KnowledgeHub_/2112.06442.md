# Contact-Rich Manipulation of a Flexible Object based on Deep Predictive Learning using Vision and Tactility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hitachi and Waseda University introduced a deep predictive learning system that integrates vision and tactility, alongside a point-based attention mechanism and Softmax Transformation, for contact-rich flexible object manipulation. The system achieved a 93.3% success rate in an unzipping task and maintained lower mechanical loads on the robot, showing improved performance in visually occluded conditions.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of flexible objects is challenging due to their complex, dynamic deformation and the difficulty in predicting their responses to actions.
- Vision-only manipulation systems struggle with contact-rich tasks due to visual occlusion by the robot's gripper or the object itself, and are sensitive to dynamic background changes.
- Traditional physical model-based approaches for deformable objects are computationally expensive, complex to build, and find it difficult to define abstract manipulation goals.

## Method

- A ==deep predictive learning== model was developed, integrating visual images, tactile sensor data processed by ==CNNs==, and robot joint angles, to predict future states and actions.
- A self-organized ==point-based attention mechanism== extracts important visual features and positional information, making the system robust to occlusions and dynamic visual changes.
- ==Softmax Transformation (SMT)== is applied to joint angle prediction to enhance learning efficiency and enable the generation of finer, more precise robot movements.

## Results

- The proposed full model achieved a **93.3%** overall task success rate in unzipping a fabric bag, significantly outperforming a vision-only baseline model (**16.7%**).
- In highly occluded scenarios (e.g., perpendicular puller), the tactile-enhanced model achieved an **86.7%** success rate, while vision-only models failed in all trials (**0%**).
- The model utilizing tactility consistently exhibited lower maximum and average loads on the robot's fingertips, demonstrating smoother, more compliant, and efficient manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactility with vision in a deep predictive learning framework is critical for robustly handling contact-rich tasks where visual information alone is insufficient or misleading.
> - Softmax Transformation significantly improves learning and enables finer, more adaptive robot motions for continuous control of flexible objects.
> - Using CNNs for processing high-dimensional tactile sensor data is more effective than fully connected layers, providing robustness to positional shifts of the object relative to the sensor.
