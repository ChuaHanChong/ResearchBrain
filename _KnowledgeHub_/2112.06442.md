# Contact-Rich Manipulation of a Flexible Object based on Deep Predictive Learning using Vision and Tactility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hitachi and Waseda University introduced a deep predictive learning system that integrates vision and tactility, alongside a point-based attention mechanism and Softmax Transformation, for contact-rich flexible object manipulation. The system achieved a 93.3% success rate in an unzipping task and maintained lower mechanical loads on the robot, showing improved performance in visually occluded conditions.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of flexible objects is challenging due to their complex, dynamic deformation and the difficulty in predicting their responses to actions.
- Vision-only manipulation systems struggle with contact-rich tasks due to visual occlusion by the robot's gripper or the object itself, and are sensitive to dynamic background changes.
- Traditional physical model-based approaches for deformable objects are computationally expensive, complex to build, and find it difficult to define abstract manipulation goals.

## Method

- A ==deep predictive learning== model was developed, integrating visual images, tactile sensor data processed by ==CNNs==, and robot joint angles, to predict future states and actions.
- A self-organized ==point-based attention mechanism== extracts important visual features and positional information, making the system robust to occlusions and dynamic visual changes.
- ==Softmax Transformation (SMT)== is applied to joint angle prediction to enhance learning efficiency and enable the generation of finer, more precise robot movements.

## Results

- The proposed full model achieved a **93.3%** overall task success rate in unzipping a fabric bag, significantly outperforming a vision-only baseline model (**16.7%**).
- In highly occluded scenarios (e.g., perpendicular puller), the tactile-enhanced model achieved an **86.7%** success rate, while vision-only models failed in all trials (**0%**).
- The model utilizing tactility consistently exhibited lower maximum and average loads on the robot's fingertips, demonstrating smoother, more compliant, and efficient manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactility with vision in a deep predictive learning framework is critical for robustly handling contact-rich tasks where visual information alone is insufficient or misleading.
> - Softmax Transformation significantly improves learning and enables finer, more adaptive robot motions for continuous control of flexible objects.
> - Using CNNs for processing high-dimensional tactile sensor data is more effective than fully connected layers, providing robustness to positional shifts of the object relative to the sensor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of flexible objects presents a significant challenge due to their complex and dynamic deformation properties. Traditional approaches often rely on physical models of the deformable objects, which are resource-intensive to build and struggle with defining abstract goals or complex dynamics. Consequently, research has increasingly shifted towards data-driven methods, particularly those leveraging deep learning.

Prior work in flexible object manipulation using deep learning has predominantly utilized vision-based approaches. Deep predictive learning, where models concurrently predict future motions and visual states, and reinforcement learning have shown promise in tasks like folding cloth or tying ropes. However, these vision-centric methods encounter limitations when visual information is incomplete or obscured, a common occurrence in contact-rich manipulation scenarios. Occlusion by the robot's own gripper or the manipulated object itself can render crucial visual cues unavailable, making it difficult for the robot to infer the object's true state or the impact of its actions. Furthermore, dynamic changes in the visual background during manipulation can degrade the performance of models trained only on specific visual contexts.

To address these limitations, researchers have explored incorporating other sensory modalities. Tactile sensing, in particular, offers complementary information about contact forces, pressure distribution, and object texture, which remains available even under visual occlusion. While tactile sensors have seen considerable application in fine-grained manipulation tasks performed by robot hands, such as object recognition or in-hand re-orientation, their integration into larger robot arm systems for complex, contact-rich tasks has been less explored. Existing applications of tactility with robot arms often involve simpler tasks or are primarily validated in simulation, with fewer demonstrations on real-world systems for intricate flexible object manipulation.

This research integrates an attention mechanism, deep predictive learning, and multimodal sensing (vision and tactility) within a robot arm control system. By combining these elements, the work aims to overcome the challenges of occlusion and dynamic visual changes inherent in contact-rich flexible object manipulation. It seeks to bridge the gap between vision-only deep learning approaches and the practical demands of tasks where visual information is insufficient, demonstrating the utility of tactility in a full robot arm system for a complex manipulation task. The introduction of point-based attention mechanisms within the deep predictive learning framework and the application of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for tactile feature extraction are specific architectural choices intended to enhance robustness and performance in these challenging conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve contact-rich manipulation of flexible objects using a robot, specifically targeting tasks where visual information alone is insufficient for effective control. The authors selected the task of unzipping a fabric bag as a representative validation task, illustrating common challenges encountered in such manipulations.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robotic manipulation capabilities and existing research methodologies:

1. **Inherent Difficulty of Flexible Object Manipulation:** People perform daily tasks involving flexible objects with ease, but these tasks (e.g., unzipping a bag, folding clothes) are challenging for robots due to the complex and constantly changing state of flexible materials. Predicting how these objects deform in response to robot actions is difficult.
2. **Limitations of Vision-Only Approaches:** Many deep learning-based robotic manipulation systems rely primarily on vision. However, in contact-rich tasks, the robot's own gripper or the object itself can occlude critical parts of the target object, hiding its state (e.g., the direction and amount of bag deformation behind the zipper puller). This lack of comprehensive visual information makes it challenging to generate appropriate robot actions. Vision-only systems can also be sensitive to changes in background or object positions not present during training.
3. **Limitations of Physical Model-Based Approaches:** Building accurate physical models for flexible objects is computationally expensive and complex due to their intricate dynamics. Furthermore, for abstract goals like "unzipping," defining ideal trajectories based on a physical model can be difficult.
4. **Need for Multi-modal Sensing:** To overcome the deficiencies of vision in occluded or contact-rich scenarios, supplementary sensory information is required. Tactile sensing offers direct feedback on contact forces and pressure, providing information that vision cannot. While tactile sensing has been explored for in-hand manipulation, its application to larger robot arm systems for complex, dynamic tasks has been limited.
5. **Enhancing Robustness and Finer Movements:** The authors aim to develop a system robust to dynamic visual changes and occlusions. They also seek to enable the robot to generate finer, more nuanced movements that can adapt to the continuous deformation of flexible objects, which is difficult with simple, pre-programmed actions.
6. **Generalizability:** By focusing on the unzipping task, which shares common problems with many other flexible object manipulations (occlusion, dynamic state changes), the research aims to develop a generalizable model applicable beyond this specific task.

To address these motivations, the research proposes a deep predictive learning model that integrates vision and tactility, utilizing a point-based attention mechanism to focus on relevant visual features and softmax transformation for fine motion prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a deep predictive learning-based robot control system designed for contact-rich flexible object manipulation, integrating vision, tactility, and motion. The model takes a camera image (i\_t), tactility data (p\_t), and robot joint angles (a\_t) at time *t* as input, and predicts the camera image ($\hat{i}_{t+1}$), tactility data ($\hat{p}_{t+1}$), and joint angle after softmax transformation ($\hat{S}(a_{t+1})$) for the next time step (*t* + 1).

The model consists of three main architectural features:

#### 3.1. Point-Based Attention Mechanism

- **Purpose:** To extract important image features and positional information, making the system robust to dynamic visual changes and occlusions.
- **Mechanism:** This mechanism employs Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and a soft argmax function to extract specific positional coordinates in the image, referred to as "attention points." These points are intended to highlight regions important for the task.
- The architecture includes a recurrent block, an attention point prediction block, and a joint angle prediction block, all utilizing Fully Connected (FC) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layers. These components predict the next state's attention points, joint angles, and tactile features from the current state.
- An image prediction block generates the predicted next image ($\hat{i}_{t+1}$) by using the predicted attention points to create heat maps. These heat maps then weight feature maps obtained from the image feature extraction block, allowing the model to focus on relevant visual information for prediction.
- The attention points are determined in a self-organized manner during training by minimizing the prediction error of both the image and joint angles, without requiring explicit labeling of attention points in the training data.

#### 3.2. Tactility Data Processing using CNNs

- **Tactile Sensor:** The system utilizes XELA Robotics XR1944 tactile sensors, attached to the two fingers of a gripper. Each sensor contains 16 elements, providing 4x4x3 values (pressure and shear forces in three axial directions).
- **Purpose:** To process the high-dimensional tactile data and enhance the system's robustness to positional shifts of the object (e.g., the puller) relative to the sensor.
- **Mechanism:** The tactile sensor output is treated as a 4x4x6 image (H=4, W=4, C=6, combining 3-axis pressure and shear data for each finger), and CNNs are used to extract relevant features. This approach leverages CNNs' inherent robustness to positional shifts in spatial data.

#### 3.3. Joint Prediction using Softmax Transformation (SMT)

- **Purpose:** To improve learning efficiency and enable the generation of finer, more precise robot movements.
- **Mechanism:** SMT transforms continuous joint angle values into a sparse representation across *J* neurons (set to 1000 in this study). This discrete representation is then predicted by a 1-dimensional softmax layer. During robot execution, the predicted sparse output is converted back into continuous joint commands using a weighted sum. The parameter $\sigma$ (set to 0.1) controls the distribution of the transformation.

#### 3.4. Loss Function

The total loss function (g) is a sum of four components across a sequence length *T*:
- **Image Prediction Error ($g_i$):** Mean squared error between the predicted ($\hat{i}_{t+1}$) and actual ($i_{t+1}$) next-state images.
- **Tactility Prediction Error ($g_p$):** Mean squared error between the predicted ($\hat{p}_{t+1}$) and actual ($p_{t+1}$) next-state tactility data.
- **Joint Angle Prediction Error ($g_a$):** Cross-entropy loss between the predicted ($\hat{S}(a_{t+1})$) and actual ($S(a_{t+1})$) next-state joint angles (after SMT).
- **Attention Point Error ($g_f$):** Euclidean distance error between the attention points from the encoder output ($\hat{f}_t$) and the decoder input ($\hat{f}_{t+1}$). This term encourages stability in attention point prediction over time.
The overall loss is a weighted sum, with $\alpha$ controlling the weight of $g_f$, dynamically adjusted during training.

#### 3.5. Training Setup

- **Data Acquisition:** 36 distinct patterns of human-operated unzipping motions were acquired through direct teaching. During data collection, the fabric bag was randomly deformed, and the operator performed the unzipping while minimizing force.
- **Data Format:** Joint angle, camera image (64x64 RGB), and tactile sensor data were collected for 10 seconds at 20 Hz.
- **Parameters:** Models were trained for 10,000 epochs with a batch size of 18. The Adam optimizer was used with standard parameters ($\alpha=0.001, \beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$). TensorFlow was the software library, and the ABCI system of AIST provided the computational resources (two nodes with 16GB GPU memory each).

#### 3.6. Hardware

- **Robot System:** Two KUKA LBR iiwa 14 R820 7-DoF robot arms.
- **Gripper:** Robotiq 2F-85 Adaptive Gripper.
- **Camera:** Buffalo BSW500M Series RGB camera, mounted on the gripper.
- **Tactile Sensors:** Two XELA Robotics XR1944 sensors, one on each finger of the gripper.
- **Object:** A commercially available Captain Stag tote bag with a 3D-printed puller for easier grasping. One arm fixed the upper part of the bag, while the controlled arm manipulated the puller.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

The effectiveness of the proposed model was evaluated by comparing the task success rates of five different models, focusing on the contributions of SMT, tactility, and CNNs for tactile processing. Initial bag states varied to test robustness to different deformation and puller orientations:
- **(i) No visible deformation, puller horizontal:** Expected to be relatively easy.
- **(ii) No visible deformation, puller perpendicular:** Vision might suggest a straight pull, but tactility is crucial to avoid jamming.
- **(iii) Visible bag deformation:** Requires adaptive motion matching zipper direction.
Each model was tested with 30 trials per bag state. Task success was defined as completing the unzipping within 10 seconds. Additionally, the loads on the robot fingertips were measured to assess the smoothness and efficiency of manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed model components: Softmax Transformation (SMT), tactility, and the use of CNNs for tactile feature extraction. Five different models were compared, and their performance was assessed based on task success rates and mechanical loads.

#### 4.1. Task Success Rate Analysis (Table I)

- **Effectiveness of SMT:**
 - Model (A) (Base, without SMT and tactility) achieved an overall success rate of 16.7%.
 - Model (B) (with SMT, without tactility) achieved an overall success rate of 56.7%. The substantial increase in success rate (from 16.7% to 56.7%) for Model (B) over Model (A) indicates that SMT contributes to improved learning and finer motion generation.
 - A similar trend was observed when comparing (C) (with tactility, without SMT) at 41.1% and (D) (with SMT and tactility (CNN)) at 93.3%. This further supports the effectiveness of SMT.

- **Effectiveness of Tactility:**
 - Model (B) (vision with SMT, 56.7% overall) and Model (D) (vision, SMT, tactility (CNN), 93.3% overall) were compared. The higher success rate of Model (D) demonstrates the effectiveness of incorporating tactile information.
 - Notably, for bag state (ii) where the puller was perpendicular to the zip teeth and visual information was misleading, vision-only models (A) and (B) both failed in all 30 trials (0% success rate). In contrast, Model (D) achieved an 86.7% success rate in this challenging scenario, highlighting tactility's critical role in compensating for visual ambiguities and occlusion.

- **Effectiveness of CNNs for Tactile Processing:**
 - Model (D) (with SMT, tactility via CNNs) achieved 93.3% overall success.
 - Model (E) (with SMT, tactility via FC layers) achieved 61.1% overall success. The significantly higher success rate of Model (D) over Model (E) confirms that using CNNs for processing tactile data is more effective than using fully connected layers, likely due to CNNs' robustness to positional shifts in the tactile input.

In summary, the proposed model (D), which integrates SMT, tactility with CNN-based processing, and the attention mechanism, achieved the highest overall success rate of 93.3%, demonstrating the combined effectiveness of these components.

#### 4.2. Attention Mechanism Functionality (Fig. 5)

Visual analysis of the unzipping task using Model (D) demonstrated the proper functioning of the attention mechanism:
- The attention points (marked by ©) generated by the system were consistently located on task-relevant areas, specifically the gripper and the zip teeth.
- The predicted attention points (marked by ×) were observed to be appropriately predicted ahead of the current attention points, aligning with the expected direction of motion.
- In a scenario (bag state (iii)) where the puller and zip teeth were initially misaligned, the robot demonstrated adaptive behavior. At 2.5 seconds, the robot's wrist rotated to align the puller with the zip teeth direction, a motion generated based on the attention points on the zip teeth. This indicates that the attention mechanism correctly identified the critical visual cues (zip teeth direction) and guided the robot's adaptive motion to successfully complete the unzipping.

#### 4.3. Tactility Contribution to Action Generation (Table II)

To further understand the contribution of tactility, the maximum and average loads on the robot's fingertips were measured for successful trials in bag states (i) and (iii), comparing Model (B) (without tactility) and Model (D) (with tactility).
- **Reduced Loads:** For both bag states, Model (D) (with tactility) consistently exhibited lower maximum and average loads on the fingertips compared to Model (B) (without tactility).
 - Bag state (i): Max load 12.5 N (D) vs. 15.8 N (B); Avg load 7.8 N (D) vs. 8.0 N (B).
 - Bag state (iii): Max load 14.7 N (D) vs. 22.5 N (B); Avg load 8.0 N (D) vs. 10.1 N (B).
- **Adaptive Manipulation:** The difference in load reduction was more pronounced in bag state (iii), which involved significant bag deformation. This suggests that the tactile feedback enabled the robot to generate smoother, more adaptive motions that responded to the bag's deformation, thereby reducing stress on the zipper and preventing excessive force application. These results imply that tactility facilitates more compliant and efficient interaction with flexible objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates a significant step towards enabling robots to perform complex, contact-rich manipulation of flexible objects, a domain where traditional robotic systems and vision-only deep learning approaches face substantial challenges.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Overcoming Vision-Centric Limitations:** The primary contribution lies in addressing the limitations of vision-alone systems in contact-rich tasks, particularly concerning occlusion and dynamic visual changes. By effectively integrating tactility with vision in a deep predictive learning framework, the proposed method provides robots with a more comprehensive understanding of the object's state, even when visual information is incomplete or misleading. This directly expands the range of tasks robots can reliably perform.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The combination of a self-organized point-based attention mechanism, CNNs for tactile feature extraction, and Softmax Transformation (SMT) for motion prediction contributes to a more robust and adaptive control system. The attention mechanism helps the robot focus on relevant task features, while tactility allows it to respond to physical contact, deformation, and potential jamming without relying solely on visual cues. The SMT enables the generation of finer, more precise movements, which are crucial for delicate manipulation of flexible materials.
3. **Foundation for General-Purpose Flexible Object Manipulation:** Although validated using an unzipping task, the authors state that the model is designed to be task-agnostic. The principles of integrating multimodal sensing, predictive learning, and attention mechanisms for dynamic object states could be generalized to a broader spectrum of flexible object manipulation tasks, such as folding laundry, dressing assistance, or industrial handling of textiles.
4. **Reduced Mechanical Stress:** The finding that the tactile-enhanced model (D) generates motions with lower maximum and average loads on the fingertips implies more compliant and safer robot-object interaction. This is critical for tasks involving fragile materials or where damage prevention is paramount, leading to increased reliability and reduced wear on both the robot and the manipulated object.
5. **Advancement in Multi-modal Deep Learning for Robotics:** This work contributes to the growing field of multi-modal learning in robotics by providing a practical demonstration of how vision and touch can be synergistically combined within a deep predictive learning architecture to achieve superior performance in complex, real-world tasks. It highlights the benefits of specialized network components, like CNNs for tactile data, for optimal integration of different sensory inputs.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust and effective method for contact-rich flexible object manipulation, addressing critical challenges in robot autonomy. Its potential impact extends to various industrial and service robotics applications, where dexterous handling of deformable materials is required, thereby contributing to the broader development of intelligent robotic systems capable of operating in complex and dynamic environments.
