# Symskill: Symbol and Skill Co-Invention for Data-Efficient and Reactive Long-Horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SymSkill jointly learns relative-pose predicates, operators, and SE(3) dynamical-system skills from unsegmented, unlabeled demonstrations, using a VLM offline to pick reference objects. A symbolic planner composes and reorders skills online for real-time failure recovery. It reaches 85% success on 12 RoboCasa single-step tasks and composes 12-step real Franka tasks from 5 minutes of play data.

## Problem

- Imitation-learning policies are reactive but monolithic, so they cannot decide which reusable skill applies when scenes change, lacking compositional generalization across multi-step tasks.
- Classical task-and-motion planning gives compositionality but takes tens to hundreds of seconds per plan in contact-rich sims, blocking real-time recovery in dynamic environments.
- Prior symbol/skill co-invention methods hand-engineer predicates or rely on slow propose-and-down-select optimization that degrades with more objects, demonstrations, or non-prehensile, suboptimal data.

## Method

- ==Premotion/motion segmentation== with reference-frame selection: velocity change-points split demonstrations into gripper-only and gripper+object segments, expressed in relative frames chosen via a VLM queried on segment frames.
- Relative-pose predicate learning: ==Gaussian distributions== fit over end-effector-to-object and object-to-reference-object poses in each segment yield ==Mahalanobis-distance-threshold predicates==, avoiding brittle single-endpoint or search-based selection.
- Operator and ==SE(3) LPV-DS== skill learning: recurring predicate-transition groups define typed operators with pre/effect/maintain conditions, each paired with a stable SE(3) LPV-DS policy plus online ==A* replanning==, obstacle-avoidance modulation, and failure-triggered attractor resampling.

## Results

- On RoboCasa, SymSkill averages **85.0%** success across **12** single-step tasks from only **5-10** demonstrations, versus **65.0%** without online monitoring/replanning and **3.3%** for a diffusion-policy variant.
- A re-implemented NSIL baseline frequently selects spurious, non-semantic relative-pose predicates on OpenSingleDoor and PnPCounterToCab, failing to recover reusable predicates under low data and non-prehensile contact.
- Without any additional data, SymSkill composes learned skills into a new **6-skill** StoreCheese task, and on a real Franka robot learns **11 operators** from **5 minutes** of play data to execute a **12-step** goal-specified task with recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric relative-pose clustering is a strong structural prior that replaces expensive predicate search, exploiting that most manipulation interactions recur in a small set of relative configurations.
> - Decoupling symbolic replanning from motion execution lets a system get TAMP-style compositional recovery without paying TAMP's planning latency, since replanning stays cheap and only the skill layer needs stability guarantees.
> - Learned symbolic preconditions can encode dataset-specific quirks rather than generic priors, so predicate invention from limited play data risks overfitting to incidental patterns in the collected demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces challenges in enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks, particularly in dynamic environments. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into Imitation Learning (IL) and Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), each with distinct advantages and limitations that SymSkill aims to address.

Imitation Learning methods, such as Diffusion Policy and other visuomotor policies, excel at reproducing skills from large, high-quality datasets. These approaches offer reactivity and can handle continuous state spaces. However, they typically learn monolithic policies that lack compositional generalization, meaning they struggle to adapt to novel sequences of sub-tasks or reuse skills in varied environmental contexts. This limitation makes them less effective for multi-step tasks where environmental conditions or task goals can change.

Conversely, classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) frameworks provide compositional generalization by decomposing problems into symbolic planning over predicates and operators, combined with continuous motion generation. This hierarchical structure allows for robust reasoning about task dependencies and sequencing. Nevertheless, TAMP faces practical scalability issues:
1. **Hand-engineering**: Symbolic predicates and low-level skills are often manually defined and tuned for specific environments, a labor-intensive process that hinders scalability and generalization.
2. **High Latency**: TAMP can require significant computation time (tens to hundreds of seconds) to solve complex problems in realistic environments. This high latency prevents real-time failure recovery and interaction in dynamic settings, where rapid replanning is often necessary.

To bridge this gap, research into Symbol and Skill Co-Invention methods, seeks to integrate the benefits of IL and TAMP by learning reusable symbols and skills from robot demonstrations. These methods typically involve defining candidates for predicates and then selecting optimal ones through optimization procedures. However, prior co-invention approaches encounter difficulties:
- **Predicate Granularity**: There is a trade-off in defining operators—they can be too general for effective planning or too fine-grained for robust skill learning from limited data.
- **Optimization Complexity**: Propose-and-down-select optimization for predicate selection can become computationally expensive with increasing numbers of objects and demonstrations, potentially failing to identify semantically meaningful predicates. For example, NSIL relies on detecting low-relative-velocity regions as predicate candidates, which can be sensitive to suboptimal demonstrations or distractor objects. LAMP uses relational critical regions but still relies on motion planning for skills, incurring high planning times. NOD-TAMP uses neural descriptor features for predicates but also requires significant planning time.

SymSkill distinguishes itself by addressing these limitations through a unified framework that jointly learns predicates, operators, and skills from unlabeled and unsegmented demonstrations. It leverages structural priors and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in a lightweight offline role for semantic grounding, reducing reliance on extensive data or online VLM inference. The approach prioritizes data efficiency and real-time reactive capabilities, aiming to achieve compositional generalization and robust recovery without the drawbacks of prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SymSkill is to enable robots to perform complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks reliably and reactively in dynamic, real-world environments. This requires a system that can combine the compositional reasoning of symbolic planning with the continuous control and adaptability of learning-based approaches, while overcoming their individual limitations.

The key objectives of this research are detailed as follows:

1. **Joint Learning of Symbols and Skills from Minimal Data**: A central objective is to develop a framework that can automatically discover and learn symbolic abstractions (predicates and operators) and goal-oriented, low-level skills from robot demonstrations that are unlabeled and unsegmented. A specific goal is to achieve this with high data efficiency, requiring as few as 5 demonstrations per task, thereby reducing the burden of data collection and labeling.

2. **Achieving Compositional Generalization**: The framework aims to enable robots to compose learned skills into multi-step plans to achieve long-horizon goals. This compositionality should allow the robot to generalize across different goals and object configurations without needing additional task-specific demonstrations.

3. **Enabling Real-Time Online Execution and Failure Recovery**: A critical objective is to support reactive planning and execution. This includes the ability to recover from unexpected failures, disturbances (e.g., human interference, environmental changes), and planning errors at both the low-level motion control and high-level symbolic planning stages, all within real-time constraints. This directly addresses the high latency issue of traditional TAMP.

4. **Ensuring Robust and Stable Skill Execution**: The learned low-level skills should be inherently stable and robust to continuous-state perturbations. This involves using control policies that can gracefully handle variations in initial conditions and external forces, preventing task failures due to minor deviations.

5. **Leveraging Semantic Priors with VLMs**: The research aims to explore the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in an offline capacity to identify task-relevant reference objects. This approach is intended to provide semantic grounding for predicate and skill learning without incurring the computational cost of online VLM inference for control.

By addressing these objectives, SymSkill seeks to provide a unified, data-efficient, and reactive solution for complex robotic manipulation, bridging the gap between current imitation learning and task and motion planning paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SymSkill operates through a two-phase architecture: an offline learning phase that discovers and learns symbolic and skill components, and an online execution phase that utilizes these components for real-time task completion and failure recovery.

**Offline Pipeline: Symbol and Skill Co-Invention**

1. **Demonstration Segmentation and Reference-Frame Selection**:
 - **Input**: Unlabeled and unsegmented robot demonstrations, each comprising continuous robot state data (end-effector pose, object poses) and object types.
 - **Segmentation**: The system first computes linear and angular velocities for all objects. Change points in velocities are used to segment each demonstration into two types of sequential segments:
 - **Premotion segments**: These capture the end-effector's motion towards an object before contact. The object being approached or that will be manipulated ($o_{int}$) serves as the reference frame for all end-effector trajectories in this segment.
 - **Motion segments**: These capture periods where the end-effector and the manipulated object ($o_{int}$) move concurrently. To establish a meaningful reference, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Gemini-2.5-Pro, is queried offline with a structured prompt on evenly spaced frames from the segment. The VLM identifies a *stationary reference object* ($o_{ref}$) in the scene relevant to $o_{int}$'s motion (e.g., a cabinet for a door). Trajectories in this segment are then expressed relative to $o_{ref}$.
 - **Assumption**: Objects of the same type are manipulated similarly, sharing common trajectory structures.

2. **Relative Pose Predicate Learning**:
 - **Goal**: To automatically invent semantically meaningful symbolic predicates representing relative object poses.
 - **Process**: For both premotion and motion segments, the system collects relevant relative poses: {$o_{int}T_{ee}$} for premotion, and {$o_{ref}T_{o_{int}}$} for motion.
 - **Distribution Fitting**: Normal distributions (Gaussians) are fit over the collected relative positions and orientations (using log-quaternions for orientation). This process creates ellipsoidal regions in SE(3) space.
 - **Predicate Definition**: A predicate (e.g., $o_{int}\psi_{ee}$ or $o_{ref}\psi_{o_{int}}$) is defined as true if the Mahalanobis distances of a given relative pose to the learned Gaussian means (for both position and orientation) fall below predefined thresholds. These learned distributions not only define predicates but also serve as samplers for goal-pose resampling during online recovery.

3. **Operator Learning using Learned Predicates**:
 - **Goal**: To derive symbolic operators that describe state transitions based on the learned predicates.
 - **Process**: All demonstration trajectories are re-evaluated using the newly learned relative-pose predicates. Each segment boundary yields an abstract state (set of true ground atoms).
 - **Transition Identification**: Recurring transition groups are identified across these abstract state sequences by grouping segments that exhibit identical *add effects* (predicates becoming true), *delete effects* (predicates becoming false), and *preconditions* (predicates true before the transition).
 - **Maintenance Conditions**: Each operator is augmented with *maintenance conditions*, which are continuous-state predicates that must hold throughout the execution interval of the corresponding skill. This allows for real-time monitoring.
 - **Output**: Each learned operator $\alpha$ is a tuple: $\langle \text{params, pre, eff, maintain, skill} \rangle$.

4. **SE(3) Skill Learning**:
 - **Goal**: To learn robust, goal-oriented low-level motion policies for each discovered operator.
 - **Approach**: SymSkill employs Dynamical System (DS)-based motion policies, specifically SE(3) Linear Parameter Varying DS (LPV-DS) combined with convex policy learning. This method is selected for its stability, robustness to uncertainty, and data efficiency.
 - **Policy Formulation**: Each policy $f_\alpha$ is a concatenated function for position and orientation control, representing a convergent feedback field.
 - **Data Usage**: For premotion operators, skills are learned using the {$o_{int}T_{ee}$} demonstration data. For motion operators, skills are learned using the {$o_{ref}T_{ee}$} data (the end-effector's pose relative to the stationary reference object), which was found to be more effective for non-prehensile motions.
 - **Execution**: The output 6D end-effector twist from these policies is tracked by a task-space passive impedance controller, ensuring compliant and stable execution.

**Online Pipeline: Execution, Monitoring, and Adaptation**

1. **Symbolic Planning**:
 - Given an initial continuous state $x_0$ and a symbolic goal state $s_g$ (a conjunction of learned predicates), $x_0$ is abstracted into $s_0$.
 - An A* search algorithm is then used with the learned operators ($\Omega$) to generate a plan skeleton, which is a sequence of operators $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n$.

2. **Skill Execution**:
 - The robot sequentially executes the low-level DS skill associated with each operator in the plan. Due to the inherent stability of DS policies, they converge to a target state; execution advances to the next operator when the policy output velocity approaches zero.

3. **Real-Time Monitoring and Adaptation**: SymSkill continuously monitors execution and adapts to failures using several mechanisms:
 - **Maintain Condition Monitoring**: The system checks if the maintenance conditions of the currently executing operator remain true.
 - **Effect Satisfaction Checking**: At the completion of each skill, it verifies if the expected effects of the operator are satisfied.
 - **Obstacle Avoidance**: During skill execution, other objects in the scene (excluding the one being manipulated) are modeled as ellipsoids. The DS policy is locally modulated to incorporate obstacle avoidance behaviors.
 - **Replanning and Resampling**: If a failure is detected (e.g., maintain condition lost, effect not satisfied), two recovery mechanisms are triggered:
 - **Goal Pose Resampling**: The policy's goal attractor can be transformed by sampling a new target pose from the normal distribution of the corresponding learned predicate (e.g., $o_{int}\psi_{ee}$ or $o_{ref}\psi_{o_{int}}$). This allows for adjusting the skill's target without fundamentally changing the operator.
 - **Symbolic Replanning**: After resampling the goal pose or if the error is more fundamental, the system initiates a new A* search from the current symbolic state, potentially generating a revised plan. This multi-level recovery enables the robot to autonomously recover from disturbances like a dropped object or an external human interference.

This integrated approach aims to provide a robust, data-efficient, and reactive solution for long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of SymSkill was evaluated through experiments in both simulation (RoboCasa) and on a physical Franka Panda robot. The results highlight its data efficiency, robustness, and reactive capabilities compared to alternative methods.

**Simulation Results (RoboCasa)**

1. **Single-Step Task Performance**: SymSkill was tested on 12 single-step manipulation tasks using 5-10 demonstrations per task, sourced from the RoboCasa dataset.
 - It achieved an average success rate of 85.0%.
 - Ablation studies demonstrated the contribution of online monitoring and replanning: SymSkill without these reactive components ("Proposed w/o monitoring") achieved an average success rate of 65.0%, indicating that real-time failure recovery significantly enhances performance.
 - Comparison with Diffusion Policy (DP): When SymSkill's low-level SE(3) LPV-DS policies were replaced with a U-Net-based Diffusion Policy ("Proposed w/ DP"), the average success rate dropped to 3.3%. This result underscores the data-efficiency and stability advantages of the DS-based skill learning, especially with limited data and under perturbations. DP struggled with generalization to initial poses outside the narrow demonstration funnels and failed to achieve necessary precision for task completion, even with data augmentation.
 - Predicate Learning: SymSkill accurately segmented trajectories, identified motion objects, and the VLM correctly determined reference objects, leading to accurate predicate learning in RoboCasa tasks.
 - Failure Modes: Primary failures occurred in Pick-and-Place (PnP) tasks when randomly generated container heights led to arm-object collisions.

2. **Multi-Step Task Performance (Zero-Shot Compositionality)**:
 - A new 6-step task, "StoreCheese" (open cabinet, pick cheese, place on counter, close cabinet), was designed in RoboCasa.
 - SymSkill successfully completed this task by composing previously learned predicates, operators, and skills *without any additional demonstrations* for the multi-step task itself.
 - The system accurately planned the sequence of operations and recovered from symbolic errors multiple times during execution, demonstrating its compositional generalization and real-time recovery capabilities.

3. **Comparison with NSIL (Re-implementation)**:
 - A qualitative comparison with a re-implementation of NSIL on "OpenSingleDoor" and "PnPCounterToCab" tasks revealed NSIL's limitations in low-data, multi-object settings.
 - NSIL often generated spurious relative-pose predicates, failed to capture semantically meaningful relationships, and was sensitive to suboptimal demonstrations or noisy non-prehensile interactions. This resulted in a failure to recover reusable predicates and learn effective low-level policies, highlighting SymSkill's more robust predicate discovery and skill learning.

**Real-World Results (Franka Panda Robot)**

1. **Learning from Play Data**: SymSkill was deployed on a Franka Panda robot and learned from approximately 5 minutes of unlabeled, unsegmented "play data." This dataset involved a variety of objects (e.g., block, banana, plates, dishrack, lid, pan) and diverse manipulation sequences.
2. **Operator Discovery**: From this minimal real-world data, SymSkill successfully identified 11 semantically meaningful and logical operators (e.g., "Pick lid from cabinet," "Place thing → cookware"), as detailed in Table III of the paper. This included discovering non-obvious but consistent preconditions based on the specific dataset (e.g., "Thing-in-container" as a precondition for picking from a red plate).
3. **VLM Efficacy**: The offline VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) correctly identified reference objects for motion segments with minimal prompt engineering, facilitating accurate predicate learning.
4. **Reactive Execution**: The Franka robot executed multi-step tasks to achieve user-specified symbolic goals in real-time (e.g., "RelPose(banana, plate)").
5. **Robustness to Disturbances**: SymSkill demonstrated robust recovery from various disturbances during real-world execution:
 - **Symbolic-level recovery**: Closing a lid by a human triggered symbolic replanning.
 - **Motion-level robustness**: Moving a plate did not require explicit recovery due to the inherent stability of DS skills.
 - **Obstacle avoidance**: Adding an obstacle was handled by the local modulation of the DS policy.
 These demonstrations indicate SymSkill's ability to maintain task completion in dynamic and interactive environments.

Overall, the findings indicate that SymSkill offers a data-efficient, robust, and reactive framework for long-horizon manipulation. It successfully learns compositional symbolic representations and stable motion skills from limited, unsegmented demonstrations and effectively recovers from various failures and disturbances in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SymSkill presents a unified framework for robotic manipulation that integrates symbolic planning with skill learning, demonstrating several significant advancements and offering potential impacts on the field:

1. **Data Efficiency**: A core contribution is the ability to jointly learn predicates, operators, and skills from a limited number of unlabeled and unsegmented robot demonstrations (as few as 5 per task or 5 minutes of real-world play data). This significantly reduces the data collection and annotation burden, which is a common bottleneck for data-intensive imitation learning methods and the manual engineering of TAMP systems. This data efficiency is crucial for rapid deployment of robots in new environments or for new tasks.

2. **Reactive Long-Horizon Manipulation**: SymSkill effectively combines the compositional generalization capabilities of Task and Motion Planning with the real-time reactivity and failure recovery typical of learning-based approaches. By enabling fast symbolic replanning and robust motion-level adaptation (through stable DS skills and dynamic obstacle avoidance), the framework allows robots to operate in dynamic environments and recover from unexpected disturbances or errors, a capability often lacking in traditional TAMP due to its high planning latency.

3. **Unified Neuro-Symbolic Approach**: The research provides a concrete demonstration of a practical neuro-symbolic system where neural components (SE(3) LPV-DS for skills, VLM for semantic grounding) and symbolic components (predicates, operators, A* planner) are seamlessly integrated. This contributes to the broader field of hybrid AI, offering a blueprint for combining the strengths of different AI paradigms to tackle complex robotic problems.

4. **Robustness and Stability**: The use of Dynamical System (DS)-based skills, specifically SE(3) LPV-DS, confers inherent stability and robustness to perturbations. This ensures that the robot can consistently achieve goal states even under continuous-state variations or minor external forces, enhancing the reliability of autonomous operations. The multi-level failure recovery mechanisms (goal pose resampling, symbolic replanning, obstacle avoidance) further augment this robustness.

5. **Generalizability**: By learning object-centric, relative-pose predicates and skills, SymSkill fosters generalization. The learned components are not tied to specific world coordinates but to relationships between objects, enabling the system to apply learned knowledge to different instances of objects or varied scene configurations. The VLM-based semantic grounding further supports this by identifying task-relevant objects independent of their precise visual appearance.

The potential impact of SymSkill extends to various applications and future research directions:

- **Accelerated Robot Deployment**: The data-efficient learning capabilities could significantly reduce the time and expertise required to program robots for new manipulation tasks, accelerating their deployment in industries (e.g., manufacturing, logistics) and service sectors (e.g., domestic assistance).
- **Enhanced Autonomy**: Robots equipped with SymSkill's capabilities would be more autonomous, capable of handling unforeseen events and recovering from errors without constant human supervision, leading to more resilient and reliable robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Generalist Robots**: This work contributes foundational elements towards the development of "generalist robots" that can learn a broad repertoire of skills and symbols and compose them flexibly to adapt to diverse, complex tasks in unstructured real-world environments.
- **Advancement in VLM Integration**: The practical, offline use of VLMs for semantic grounding demonstrates a viable strategy for incorporating large language and vision models into robotics without incurring the computational overhead for online control, potentially inspiring similar lightweight integrations in other robotic systems.
- **Open-Source Contribution**: The provision of an open-source implementation facilitates reproducibility and enables the broader research community to build upon this work, accelerating further developments in data-efficient and reactive manipulation.

In conclusion, SymSkill offers a significant step towards more capable and autonomous manipulation robots by addressing critical challenges in data efficiency, reactivity, and generalization through a unified neuro-symbolic approach.
