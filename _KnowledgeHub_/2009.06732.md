# Efficient Transformers: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey from Google Research systematically categorizes the diverse landscape of efficient Transformer models, presenting a novel taxonomy based on their underlying technical innovations. It critically assesses the practical utility of these models, highlighting that theoretical efficiency gains often do not translate directly to real-world performance improvements.

## Problem

- The self-attention mechanism in standard Transformer models has a quadratic computational and memory complexity (O(N^2)) with respect to sequence length N, limiting scalability for long sequences.
- A rapid proliferation of 'X-former' variants has led to a fragmented research landscape, making it challenging for researchers to keep pace with innovations and systematically compare different models.
- There was a lack of clarity on what 'efficiency' entails in this context and a need for standardized evaluation methods across various models and applications.

## Method

- The authors propose a comprehensive ==taxonomy categorizing efficient Transformer models== into ==nine distinct classes== based on their technical innovations and primary use cases.
- They provide detailed explanations and draw connections between representative models within each category, clarifying their core ideas, mathematical formulations, and complexity analyses.
- The survey analyzes broader design trends in efficient Transformer research, discusses the challenges in the current evaluation landscape, and outlines future research directions.

## Results

- A nine-category taxonomy for efficient Transformer models was established, classifying approaches based on technical innovations like Fixed Patterns, Learnable Patterns, Low-Rank Methods, and Kernels.
- Detailed characterizations of numerous efficient Transformer models are provided, outlining their specific mechanisms, computational/memory complexities, and practical considerations.
- The survey identified chronological design trends in efficient Transformer development, progressing from simpler fixed patterns to more complex kernel methods, learnable patterns, and multi-stage or sparse approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The 'efficiency misnomer' reveals that theoretical linear complexity often does not translate to practical speedups or memory gains due to implementation complexity, causal masking requirements, or hardware-specific optimizations.
> - The research landscape for efficient Transformers is fragmented, lacking standardized benchmarks and consistent evaluation protocols, which hinders direct comparison and broader adoption.
> - An ideal efficient Transformer must balance theoretical computational gains with practical throughput, ease of implementation, and universality across diverse tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The Transformer architecture, introduced in 2017, rapidly became the dominant paradigm in sequence modeling, particularly in Natural Language Processing (NLP), and later extended successfully to computer vision, speech, and reinforcement learning. Its core innovation, the self-attention mechanism, allows models to weigh the importance of different parts of an input sequence, enabling powerful long-range dependency modeling.

However, the immense success of the original Transformer came with a significant limitation: the self-attention mechanism has a computational and memory complexity that scales quadratically with the input sequence length (O(N^2)). This quadratic dependency makes it prohibitively expensive to process very long sequences—such as entire documents, high-resolution images, or long video streams—and limits the scalability of Transformer models in many real-world applications.

This inherent scalability bottleneck spurred a "dizzying number" of research efforts to develop more "efficient" Transformer variants, often dubbed "X-formers" (e.g., Reformer, Linformer, Performer, Longformer). As these variants proliferated, the research landscape became fragmented, making it challenging for researchers and practitioners to keep abreast of the innovations, understand their underlying principles, and compare their efficacy.

"Efficient Transformers: A Survey" directly addresses this critical need for consolidation and organization. It positions itself as a timely and essential resource to navigate this complex and rapidly expanding field. By providing a structured taxonomy, detailed explanations of various models, and an analysis of design trends and evaluation challenges, the survey serves to:
- **Consolidate Knowledge:** It brings together diverse approaches to Transformer efficiency from scattered research papers across different domains.
- **Provide a Unified Framework:** It introduces a coherent classification system for these models, allowing for a systematic understanding of their core techniques.
- **Highlight Key Challenges:** It emphasizes the fundamental problem of quadratic complexity and the various ways researchers have attempted to mitigate it.
- **Inform Future Research:** By outlining current trends and limitations, the survey implicitly guides future research towards more promising or overlooked areas.

In essence, this survey moves beyond simply reporting individual research breakthroughs to providing a meta-analysis of an entire, burgeoning sub-field, critically assessing its progress and identifying its remaining hurdles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Efficient Transformers: A Survey" is to provide a comprehensive, organized, and critical overview of the vast and rapidly growing body of research on efficient Transformer models. The authors aim to characterize a "large and thoughtful selection" of these models, offering clarity amidst the "flurry" of new architectures.

The motivation behind this undertaking stems from several pressing issues within the deep learning community:

1. **Overwhelming Rate of Innovation:** The field of Transformer research has seen an "overwhelming influx" of new models, particularly those focused on efficiency. The authors note that "nearly a dozen new efficiency-focused models proposed in just the past 6 months" (as of the first draft circa August 2020), making it "challenging to keep pace with the rate of innovation" for researchers and practitioners. A survey is thus deemed "beneficial for the community and quite timely."
2. **Addressing the Quadratic Complexity of Self-Attention:** The foundational motivation for all efficient Transformer research is the quadratic time and memory complexity of the self-attention mechanism (O(N^2) for sequence length N). This inherent scalability limitation restricts the application of Transformers to long sequences (e.g., long documents, high-resolution images, videos), which are increasingly common in real-world data. The survey is motivated to categorize and explain how different models tackle this specific problem.
3. **Defining and Interpreting "Efficiency":** The paper aims to clarify what "efficiency" entails in the context of Transformers. It highlights that efficiency can refer to various aspects, including memory footprint (crucial for limited accelerator memory) and computational costs (FLOPs, important for training, inference, and on-device applications). The survey explicitly focuses on how models perform with "large inputs."
4. **Consolidating Cross-Domain Advancements:** Transformers are now applied across diverse domains like language, vision, speech, and reinforcement learning. The survey intends to unify the literature, reviewing models with applications in both language and vision to provide a holistic perspective.
5. **Establishing a Taxonomy:** A significant objective is to propose a structured taxonomy of efficient Transformer models. This classification, based on core technical innovations and primary use cases, is intended to help researchers understand the underlying principles and relationships between different "X-former" variants.
6. **Detailed Explanation and Connection Drawing:** Beyond mere listing, the authors aim to provide "a detailed walk-through of many of these models and draw connections between them," helping readers grasp the nuances, pros, cons, and specific mechanisms of prominent efficient Transformers.

In essence, the survey is a critical response to the challenges posed by the rapid and somewhat uncoordinated development of efficient Transformer models, aiming to impose order, provide clarity, and guide future research directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic and comprehensive methodology to survey the landscape of efficient Transformer models:

1. **Comprehensive Literature Collection:** The survey begins by collecting a "large and thoughtful selection" of recent "efficiency-flavored 'X-former' models." This involves identifying models that specifically aim to improve the computational and memory efficiency of the original Transformer architecture. The paper reflects ongoing engagement by including "Author notes on the updated version (December 2021)" and "Author notes on the updated version (March 2022)," indicating a continuous effort to incorporate the latest developments.

2. **Foundation and Background:** Before diving into efficient variants, the authors provide a concise background section on the standard Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017). This covers the multi-head self-attention mechanism, position-wise feed-forward layers, and a crucial discussion on the compute cost of Transformers, particularly the quadratic complexity of attention. This ensures that readers, regardless of their prior familiarity, have a common understanding of the baseline and the problem being addressed.

3. **Taxonomy Development:** A core methodological contribution is the proposal of a novel **taxonomy** for efficient Transformer models. This classification system categorizes models based on their "technical innovation and primary use case." The proposed categories are:
 - **Fixed Patterns (FP):** Sparsifying attention to predefined patterns (e.g., local windows, strided patterns, compressed patterns).
 - **Combination of Patterns (CP):** Combining two or more distinct fixed access patterns to improve coverage.
 - **Learnable Patterns (LP):** Learning the attention sparsity pattern in a data-driven fashion (e.g., clustering or sorting tokens).
 - **Neural Memory:** Leveraging learnable side memory modules (e.g., inducing points, global tokens) to access multiple tokens at once.
 - **Low-Rank Methods:** Exploiting low-rank approximations of the attention matrix.
 - **Kernels:** Viewing attention through kernelization to avoid explicit O(N^2) computation.
 - **Recurrence:** Connecting adjacent blocks via recurrence mechanisms.
 - **Downsampling:** Reducing sequence resolution to decrease computational costs.
 - **Sparse Models and Conditional Computation:** Sparsely activating a subset of model parameters, often through Mixture-of-Experts (MoE).
 The authors acknowledge that these categories are "broad characterization" and "no sharp boundary," as models often combine multiple techniques.

4. **Detailed Model Walkthroughs:** The paper provides in-depth explanations of representative models within each category (or chronologically where logical). For each model, the authors detail:
 - The core idea and modifications introduced.
 - Mathematical formulations where relevant (e.g., for self-attention variations).
 - Their specific computational and memory complexity.
 - Noted pros, cons, and restrictions (e.g., inability for causal masking, implementation complexity).
 This detailed analysis allows for a nuanced understanding of individual approaches and their trade-offs.

5. **Discussion and Analysis of Trends:** The survey goes beyond mere description by including a critical discussion section that analyzes:
 - **Evaluation Landscape:** It highlights the current fragmentation in benchmarks and hyperparameter settings, making side-by-side comparisons difficult. It also notes the split between encoder-only and generative tasks, and the emergence of unified benchmarks like Long Range Arena (LRA).
 - **Model Design Trends:** It traces the evolution of design choices over time, showing a progression from simpler fixed patterns to more complex learnable, kernel-based, or multi-stage approaches.
 - **Orthogonal Efficiency Efforts:** It briefly summarizes other important efficiency initiatives that are not directly related to self-attention optimization (e.g., weight sharing, quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, NAS, alternative architectures).

6. **Retrospective and Future Directions:** The V2 update incorporates a retrospective analysis of the field's evolution since the initial draft, critically assessing the practical adoption of efficient Transformers. It addresses the "efficiency misnomer" – the common misunderstanding that theoretical linear complexity always translates to practical speedups. Finally, it outlines characteristics of an "ideal xformer" and poses forward-looking questions about the future of Transformer efficiency.

Through this multi-faceted approach, the authors provide a structured, analytical, and forward-looking survey that serves as a valuable guide for the research community.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core "findings" of this survey are presented through its comprehensive categorization of efficient Transformer models, its detailed analysis of their mechanisms, and its critical assessment of the overall research landscape.

#### 4.1. Taxonomy of Efficient Transformers

The most significant "result" is the proposed taxonomy, which successfully categorizes the diverse range of efficient Transformer models into nine distinct classes based on their technical innovations:
- **Fixed Patterns (FP):** Models like Memory Compressed Transformer, Image Transformer, Longformer, and BigBird, which restrict attention to local windows or fixed strided patterns, reducing complexity from O(N^2) to O(N*k) or O(N*logN).
- **Combination of Patterns (CP):** Models such as Sparse Transformer and Axial Transformer that combine multiple fixed patterns (e.g., local and strided) to improve receptive field while maintaining efficiency.
- **Learnable Patterns (LP):** Approaches like Reformer, Routing Transformer, and Sinkhorn Transformer that dynamically learn attention patterns through clustering or sorting, enabling more optimal global views with efficiency.
- **Neural Memory:** Models such as Set Transformer, ETC, and Longformer (with global tokens) that introduce a learnable, fixed-size memory that can attend to or be attended by the entire sequence, compressing information.
- **Low-Rank Methods:** Exemplified by Linformer and Factorized Synthesizers, these methods leverage low-rank approximations of the attention matrix to reduce complexity, often by projecting key/value dimensions to a lower rank.
- **Kernels:** Models like Performer and Linear Transformer that reformulate the attention mechanism using kernel functions, avoiding the explicit computation of the O(N^2) attention matrix.
- **Recurrence:** Transformer-XL and Compressive Transformers use segment-level recurrence to handle long sequences by passing hidden states or compressed memories between blocks.
- **Downsampling:** Newer models like Funnel Transformer, Perceiver, Swin Transformer, and TokenLearner reduce the sequence resolution at various layers to cut down computation.
- **Sparse Models and Conditional Computation:** Models like Switch Transformer, ST-MoE, and GShard, which achieve efficiency by sparsely activating subsets of parameters (e.g., in feed-forward networks) often using Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures.

#### 4.2. Detailed Model Characterization

For each discussed model, the survey consistently provides:
- **Mechanism Description:** A clear explanation of *how* the model achieves efficiency (e.g., LSH for Reformer, k-means for Routing Transformer, strided convolutions for Memory Compressed Transformer).
- **Complexity Analysis:** Explicit statements of their computational and memory complexity (e.g., O(NlogN) for Reformer, O(N) for Linformer/Performer).
- **Practical Considerations:** Discussions on implementation challenges (e.g., custom GPU kernels for Sparse Transformer), limitations (e.g., inability for causal masking in ETC/BigBird), and real-world performance implications (e.g., slower parallel training for causal kernel models).

#### 4.3. Insights into Design Trends

The survey identifies a chronological evolution in efficient Transformer design:
- Early efforts focused on simple **fixed patterns** (local, strided).
- This was followed by **combined patterns** and early forms of **neural memory**.
- A subsequent wave saw the emergence of **learnable patterns** (clustering/sorting).
- More recently, **low-rank approximations** and **kernel methods** have gained prominence.
- The latest trend involves **two-stage or multi-level attention mechanisms** combining different techniques, and the rise of **sparse models** using conditional computation.

#### 4.4. Critical Evaluation of the Landscape

The survey's most critical "finding" relates to the state of evaluation and the practical utility of these models:
- **Fragmented Evaluation:** There's a lack of standardized benchmarks and consistent evaluation protocols across different papers, making direct "apples-to-apples" comparison difficult. Models often choose specific benchmarks (generative vs. encoder-only) where they perform well.
- **The "Efficiency Misnomer":** A key insight from the updated version is that theoretical linear complexity often *does not* translate to practical speed or memory gains in real-world scenarios, especially for shorter sequences. Many efficient attention models have "extraordinarily painful requirements to achieve causal masking (or TPU packing)" and "often have to substantially trade-off throughput for linear complexity." This highlights a significant gap between theoretical guarantees and practical deployment.
- **Lack of a De Facto Standard:** Despite the proliferation of "X-former" variants, the original quadratic Transformer remains dominant in many applications. Most efficient variants "have never been battle-tested against common paradigms like pretrain-and-finetuning only up till recently" and often suffer from high intrinsic implementation complexity (e.g., custom CUDA kernels for Longformer, making them prohibitive on TPUs).

In summary, the survey provides a clear map of the diverse approaches to Transformer efficiency, details their mechanisms and complexities, and critically assesses the practical challenges and current limitations of these advancements in the broader research ecosystem.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Efficient Transformers: A Survey" holds significant importance for the deep learning community and promises a substantial impact across several dimensions:

1. **Indispensable Reference for Researchers:** By meticulously collecting, categorizing, and explaining a vast array of efficient Transformer models, the survey provides an invaluable, single-source reference. This drastically reduces the time and effort researchers need to invest in understanding the state-of-the-art, identify relevant prior work, and avoid redundant efforts. Its organized structure and clear explanations make it accessible for both newcomers and seasoned practitioners.

2. **Structuring a Fragmented Field:** The proposed taxonomy is a critical contribution. It brings much-needed order to a rapidly expanding and often chaotic research landscape. By identifying fundamental principles and grouping models based on core techniques, it helps researchers conceptualize the design space for efficient Transformers, fostering a more systematic approach to future innovation. This structural clarity can lead to more focused and impactful research directions.

3. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The "Retrospective on the Past Year and Future Research Directions" section is particularly impactful. By openly discussing the "efficiency misnomer" – where theoretical complexity benefits don't always translate to real-world speed – and the challenges in practical adoption (e.g., implementation complexity, causal masking, TPU packing), the authors provide candid feedback to the community. This encourages future work to not only optimize theoretical complexity but also prioritize practical throughput, ease of implementation, and universality across tasks. It sets a high bar for an "ideal xformer," pushing the field towards more robust and deployable solutions. The question, "will that new xformer still be a Transformer?" hints at the potential for entirely new architectural paradigms to emerge as truly efficient alternatives.

4. **Highlighting Evaluation Gaps:** The discussion on the fragmented evaluation landscape is a call to action. By explicitly pointing out the lack of standardized benchmarks and the inconsistent evaluation protocols, the survey advocates for more rigorous and comparable experimental setups (like the Long Range Arena benchmark, which one of the authors co-authored). This can drive the community towards more unified and fair assessments of model performance and efficiency.

5. **Industry Relevance:** Coming from Google Research, the survey inherently reflects the practical challenges faced by large-scale model developers and deployers. The emphasis on factors like "TPU-packed" models, inference-time efficiency, and the "efficiency misnomer" directly addresses real-world constraints in deploying AI systems. This perspective is invaluable for industry practitioners seeking to optimize their models for production environments.

6. **Comprehensive View of Efficiency:** By including a section on "Orthogonal Efficiency Efforts" (e.g., weight sharing, quantization, pruning, knowledge distillation, NAS, alternative architectures), the survey broadens the definition of "efficiency" beyond just the attention mechanism. This holistic view informs researchers about various avenues for optimization, promoting a multi-pronged approach to making deep learning models more resource-frugal.

In conclusion, "Efficient Transformers: A Survey" serves as a landmark paper that consolidates, clarifies, and critically assesses a vital sub-field of deep learning. Its significance lies in its capacity to empower researchers, streamline research efforts, and ultimately guide the development of truly efficient and practical Transformer architectures for the next generation of AI applications.
