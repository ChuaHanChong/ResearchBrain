# Whole-Body Planning for Humanoids Navigating Confined Spaces via Self-Collision Avoidance References

> [!summary] Summary
> A three-stage whole-body planning framework for humanoids in confined spaces. It lifts particle-based convex kinematic relaxations to rigid-body volumes, embeds differentiable collision detection for self-collision avoidance, then transcribes the guides into a full-order dynamics trajectory optimizer, and trains a residual RL tracking policy. On a Unitree G1 it solves 12-18 s multi-contact tasks at confinement ratios below 1.5 where spline baselines fail entirely.

## Problem

- Reduced-order trajectory optimizers ignore link collisions, so their spline guides on particle abstractions give poor local minima inside narrow non-convex confined passages.
- Imitation and data-driven methods need physically feasible reference datasets, which are scarce for confined spaces because traversal demands unnatural joint configurations and exact retargeting.
- End-to-end reinforcement learning exploring self- and environment-collision awareness by reward alone is sample inefficient, prone to local minima and dynamic instability.

## Method

- Stage 2 ==differentiable self-collision avoidance==: a ==cone-program== minimum-distance test over capsule and sphere proxies, with gradients from the ==implicit function theorem== on KKT conditions, constrains Bezier control points.
- Stage 3 full-order dynamics trajectory optimization with ==constrained SQP==, using a two-pass strategy: first pass tracks Stage 2 guides for dynamic feasibility, second warm-starts hard signed-distance collision constraints.
- A ==residual RL== tracking policy trained with ==PPO== and ==asymmetric actor-critic==, outputting joint-target residuals over the plan under ==domain randomization== of mass, friction, and base and hand pushes.

## Results

- Full pipeline succeeds **10/10** on tilted stairs and both unobstructed-hole sequences, and **7/10** and **6/10** on obstructed-hole sequences, with solve times of roughly **125-332** seconds.
- Both baselines score **0/10** on all hole sequences; the knee-free spline gets **9/10** on tilted stairs and linear frame interpolation **7/10** at **632** seconds mean solve time.
- Removing the Stage 2 self-collision guides drops obstructed-hole success to **1/10** and **0/10**; the residual policy exceeds **95%** traversal after **2e8** environment steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planning over reachable rigid-body volumes rather than point particles is what unlocks tight passages; guide fidelity, not solver strength, decides feasibility.
> - Writing collision detection as a cone program yields analytic gradients through the primal-dual solution, avoiding both sphere padding and finite-difference derivative estimates.
> - Warm-starting hard collision constraints from a dynamically feasible but collision-relaxed pass escapes the infeasible-start trap that stalls gradient-based whole-body optimizers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged locomotion for humanoid robots has seen considerable advancements, with capabilities ranging from walking on flat ground to agile locomotion over significant obstacles. This progress is attributed to developments in both learning-based approaches, which facilitate dynamic motion execution, and classical planning techniques utilizing convex optimization and differentiable geometry to generate collision-free trajectories. These foundations enable the synthesis of kinematically and dynamically consistent motions, particularly in environments with geometric constraints.

However, navigating highly confined spaces presents specific challenges that existing methods often do not fully address. These environments feature configuration spaces that are narrow and non-convex, where minor posture deviations can lead to environmental collisions and potential robot destabilization. Long-horizon planning is crucial in these settings, as short-horizon strategies can result in kinematic or dynamic dead-ends due to insufficient foresight regarding future footholds or upper-body postures.

Current data-driven and imitation-based frameworks are limited by the scarcity of high-quality demonstration data for confined space navigation. Such data would require precise motion retargeting that strictly adheres to the robot's kinematic reachability and torque limits, which is difficult to obtain for the unnatural joint configurations involved. Conversely, classical trajectory optimization formulations often simplify collision models to maintain computational efficiency, rely on overly generalized bounding volumes, or necessitate specific initializations to achieve feasible solutions. These simplifications become problematic in dense obstacle scenes.

This research addresses these limitations by developing a framework that synthesizes morphology-aware guides for whole-body planning. The framework aims to leverage differentiable collision detection to generate plans directly over kinematically reachable rigid-body volumes. This approach is positioned to provide the non-myopic seeding required for high-fidelity reference trajectories, which can then enable effective reinforcement learning policy training for online execution, bridging a gap between planning and control for humanoids in complex, restricted environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a whole-body planning (WBP) and control framework specifically designed for humanoid robots navigating highly confined and geometrically complex environments. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties current planning paradigms face in such settings.

Traditional trajectory optimizers, which often abstract the robot as particles or rely on simplified spline representations, frequently encounter poor local minima when attempting to navigate large collision spaces in confined environments. This necessitates a more detailed and geometrically informed approach. The authors identify the need for a planning mechanism that directly accounts for the rigid-body volumes of the robot throughout its motion, rather than approximating them.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated by the authors are:

1. **Geometry-Aware Kinematic Reference Generation:** To extend existing constrained particle planning strategies to constrained rigid bodies, facilitating the discovery of kinematically feasible seeds for diverse locomotion styles. This is intended to guide self-collision avoidance (SCA) motions within confined spaces.
2. **Dynamically Consistent Confined-Space Planning:** To develop a morphology-aware optimization pipeline that translates complex kinematic collision-free references into dynamically consistent whole-body trajectories suitable for humanoids. This involves ensuring that the generated motions respect the robot's physical limits and environmental interactions.
3. **Closed-Loop Whole-Body Tracking Policy:** To create a fast-converging learned policy capable of tracking the generated WBPs in simulation. This policy is designed to be robust against sensor noise, modeling uncertainties, and dynamics randomization, thereby enabling reliable online execution.
4. **Long-Horizon Benchmark in Standardized Confined Spaces:** To validate the proposed framework on a physical humanoid platform (Unitree G1) in challenging, long-horizon tasks (12–18 seconds) across confined environments that meet or exceed NIST emergency response standards. This objective aims to demonstrate the framework's capability to generate stable plans where standard baseline planners typically fail.

In essence, the research is motivated by the limitations of current humanoid planning systems in tight spaces and seeks to overcome these by integrating a novel, geometry-aware planning mechanism with dynamic optimization and robust control, ultimately enabling humanoids to operate effectively in hazardous and complex restricted environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for whole-body planning and control in confined spaces comprises a three-stage planning pipeline followed by a reinforcement learning-based motion controller.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework Structure

The planning process is sequential:
- **Stage 1:** Generates initial smooth, environment-collision-free paths for task-space frames, ensuring kinematic reachability.
- **Stage 2:** Refines these paths to incorporate self-collision avoidance (SCA) using proxy geometries.
- **Stage 3:** Uses the refined paths as guides for a full-order trajectory optimization (TO) problem to find dynamically feasible torque profiles.
- **Motion Controller:** A residual reinforcement learning (RL) policy tracks the computed plans online.

#### 3.2. Kinematic Planning Avoiding Environment Collisions (Stage 1)

Stage 1, building on prior work, focuses on generating smooth, collision-free task-space reference paths for key robot parts such as the torso, feet, and palms, while enforcing kinematic reachability. The environment is represented using primitive convex volumes, specifically IRIS regions, which are assigned to each planning frame. A search-based planner then determines the sequence of region traversals, with phase durations allocated proportionally to path lengths within each region.

Task-space trajectories are parameterized as B´ezier curves, with optimal control points obtained by solving a Second-Order Cone Program (SOCP). The objective function minimizes path length and includes a logarithmic penalty on knee-to-ankle distance variance (RigL) as a convex relaxation of the rigid link constraint. Constraints include the contact schedule, reachability, and collision-free bounds within the IRIS regions. Non-contacting environmental surfaces are padded by link radii to generate a smooth kinematic reference.

#### 3.3. Differentiable Self-Collision Avoidance (Stage 2)

This stage refines the kinematic paths generated in Stage 1 to account for the actual rigid-body volumes of the robot and avoid self-collisions. The approach leverages a differentiable collision framework. The minimum distance between primitive pairs of robot bodies is computed by solving a SOCP. This formulation represents two convex shapes, S1 and S2, expanded by a factor α. If α* > 1, the bodies are not in collision; if α* ≤ 1, they are colliding.

A key benefit of this formulation is that the gradient of the minimum distance with respect to body positions can be directly obtained from the Lagrangian and its primal-dual solution. This allows for the use of a single rigid body primitive for collision checks between pairs, bypassing the need for multiple bounding spheres or numerical differentiation.

The humanoid robot's geometry is approximated using a capsule for the torso and spheres for the palms, knees, and ankles for collision detection in Stage 2. These primitive shapes are defined by mathematical inequalities involving their origins (r1, r2), rotations (Q), radii (R), lengths (L), and a slack variable (γ).

The rigid body collision detection is integrated as a constraint (α(β) ≥ 1) into the SOCP of Stage 1, where β represents the B´ezier control points. The necessary gradients of this new constraint with respect to β are computed using the Implicit Function Theorem (IFT) on the KKT conditions of the collision detection SOCP. While enforcing constraints at discrete B´ezier control points does not guarantee continuous collision avoidance, the convex hull property of B´ezier curves ensures the trajectory remains close to these points, providing an efficient guide for the subsequent high-fidelity refinement. The implementation uses CasADi for automatic differentiation and warm-starts the SOCP solver with the primal-dual solution from Stage 1.

#### 3.4. Full-Order Dynamics Trajectory Optimization (Stage 3)

After kinematic paths are adjusted for self-collision and environment collision, Stage 3 computes a dynamically feasible plan. This involves solving a whole-body trajectory optimization problem, where the state vector includes generalized configuration (q) and velocity (v), and the control input (u) comprises actuated joint torques.

The optimization objective (Equation 7) includes running and terminal costs:
- **Tracking Cost:** Minimizes the squared difference between the robot's task-space forward kinematics (FK) and the desired positions of task frames obtained from the Stage 2 B´ezier curves (`x_f,des_k`). This guides the optimizer along the refined kinematic paths.
- **State Regulation:** Minimizes the difference between the current state and a reference state (`x_ref_k`).
- **Torque Regularization:** Minimizes the magnitude of joint torques.

Constraints imposed to ensure physically possible motions include:
- **Dynamics:** Discrete dynamics (`x_k+1 = f_d_i(x_k, u_k)`).
- **Joint Limits:** Box constraints for joint positions and velocities (`X_lim`).
- **Torque Limits:** Box constraints for joint torques (`U`).
- **Friction:** Wrench cone constraints for foot contacts and friction cone constraints for hand point contacts, based on segmented environment surfaces.
- **Collision Avoidance:** A more refined collision model of the robot (Fig. 3b) is used, with hard inequality constraints based on signed distance (`d(A, B)`) and its derivative (Equation 8), ensuring strict environment and self-collision avoidance.

The problem is solved using a Constrained SQP solver. Torque, environment, and SCA constraints are enforced as hard constraints, while friction constraints are implemented as soft constraints with a Barrier function.

#### 3.5. Pipeline for Fully SCA Motions (Integration)

To achieve robust convergence for the high-fidelity Stage 3 problem, a two-pass optimization strategy is employed.
1. **First Pass:** Solves for dynamically consistent joint torques and contact forces, tracking Stage 2 guides, but *initially neglects hard collision constraints*. This prioritizes satisfying nonlinear dynamics and physical limits while remaining near the collision-free space.
2. **Second Pass:** Uses the resulting trajectory from the first pass as a warm-start for a final optimization, where *collision avoidance is enforced as hard inequality constraints* using the refined SCA model. This sequential approach enhances numerical stability by starting the final pass within a dynamically plausible and nearly collision-free basin of attraction.

Stage 3 initialization involves solving Inverse Kinematics for target end-effector poses from the contact sequence to obtain nominal joint configurations and computing quasi-static torques under friction constraints to provide a physically grounded seed.

#### 3.6. Motion Tracking Controller

A residual reinforcement learning (RL) policy, based on an asymmetric actor-critic framework, is developed to track the generated plans in real-time under potential real-world perturbations in the MuJoCo environment.

- **Policy Design:** The policy `πθ(at | ot)` maps noisy local observations (`o_t`) to residual joint targets (`δa_t`), which are then executed via low-level PD control (`q_target = q_ref + δa_t`). The critic network evaluates based on the full, uncorrupted state (`s_t`).
- **Observations:** The observation vector `o_t` includes tracking errors relative to the reference guide (base pose/velocity, center-of-mass, joint position/velocity, end-effector errors), noisy proprioceptive data (base velocities, projected gravity, joint states – see Table I), previous residual actions, torque history, local obstacle distances, phase progress, and look-ahead windows for the planned contact schedule, forces, surface normals, and end-effector references. The critic receives privileged ground-truth contact forces and locations during training.
- **Rewards:** Reward terms (Table II) are designed to maximize tracking performance for joints, CoM, and end-effectors, promote contact quality, and penalize residual action magnitudes and rates.
- **Domain Randomization & Terminations:** To enhance robustness and address the sim-to-real gap, episodes undergo extensive domain randomization (Table I), including ground friction, mass scaling (pelvis, torso, hip-pitch, knee links), and continuous perturbations at the base and hands. Initial robot states are randomized. Episodes terminate on timeout, root falling, or excessive tilt.
- **Training:** The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in the Unitree RL mjlab environment, utilizing Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) for the actor and critic networks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework on a 27-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot across three confined testbeds: an Unobstructed Hole, an Obstructed Hole, and Tilted Stairs. These environments are stated to exceed NIST emergency response standards and feature confinement ratios (Cr) below 1.5, indicating restricted maneuverability. The tasks involve long-horizon plans (12–18 seconds) with complex foot and hand contacts.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance in Confined Spaces

- **Success Rates and Solve Times:** The framework demonstrated high success rates in generating feasible plans for the Tilted Stairs and Unobstructed Hole environments (10/10 trials), with average solve times between approximately two and three minutes. For the more challenging Obstructed Hole, success rates were 7/10 for the Left Step sequence and 6/10 for the Right Step sequence, with average compute times ranging from three to six minutes. One outlier trial in the Right Step sequence extended solve time to 854 seconds.
- **Stage 2 Compute Times:** The kinematic guides from Stage 2 were computed within an average of 70 to 128 seconds across the environments.
- **Motion Characteristics:** The generated motions exhibited dexterity and smoothness, operating close to the robot's joint limits and maintaining proximity to other links and the environment. Small penetrations (up to a couple of centimeters) were allowed, which the real-time controller is expected to correct.

#### 4.2. Baseline Comparisons

Two baseline planning approaches were evaluated against the proposed framework:
- **Spline-Guided Planners (excluding knee trajectories):** These planners, which mimic reduced-order models neglecting detailed link collisions, achieved a 9/10 success rate in the Tilted Stairs. However, they failed completely (0/10) in both the Unobstructed and Obstructed Hole environments, indicating their inadequacy for tighter scenarios that require precise knee and foot trajectory coordination, especially when end-effectors are not directly "in sight" from one contact to the next.
- **Linear Frame Interpolation:** This simplistic method involved linear interpolation between contact states for all planning frames, including knees. It achieved a 7/10 success rate in the Tilted Stairs but also failed in the Hole environments (0/10). This method also incurred higher compute times, suggesting the optimizer had to work harder to find feasible motions from poor-quality references that often passed through the robot's own body.

These comparisons establish that ignoring detailed geometry (like knee trajectories) or using overly simplistic reference paths significantly hinders the ability to plan successful whole-body motions in highly confined spaces.

#### 4.3. Pipeline Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of specific stages:
- **Without Stage 2 (Volume-Aware SCA Planning):** This ablation, which skips the differentiable self-collision avoidance, still performed well in the Tilted Stairs and Unobstructed Hole (10/10 success). However, its performance significantly dropped in the Obstructed Hole, achieving only 1/10 for the Left Step sequence and 0/10 for the Right Step sequence. This outcome indicates that the volume-aware SCA guides from Stage 2 are critical for providing accurate collision-free references in highly constrained environments.
- **Without Dynamics Feasibility Pass in Stage 3:** Bypassing the initial dynamics feasibility pass within Stage 3 yielded comparable success rates in the challenging Obstructed Hole. However, it resulted in a slight reduction in reliability for the Unobstructed Hole (7/10 success). This suggests that while not strictly necessary for convergence in all cases, the dynamics feasibility pass generally improves the reliability of the initial guess for the final, fully constrained WBP.

#### 4.4. Closed-Loop Controller in Physics Simulation

- **Policy Training:** The feasible plans generated by the framework, along with their respective domains of initial base positions, were successfully used to train a residual reinforcement learning policy. The policy trained to convergence within 2 x 10^8 environment steps (or 2,000 iterations), which the authors attribute to the high quality of the generated plans.
- **Robustness and Success Rate:** The trained policy achieved a traversal success rate exceeding 95% while operating under full domain randomization (including ground friction, mass scaling, and continuous perturbations) and external push disturbances.
- **Motion Execution:** The resulting motions in simulation were smooth and confident, demonstrating the policy's ability to traverse the confined spaces effectively and leverage different knee styling derived from the planning process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a whole-body planning framework that addresses a notable challenge in humanoid robotics: stable and feasible locomotion in highly confined environments. Its primary significance lies in moving beyond traditional particle-based abstractions for kinematic path planning by directly considering rigid-body volumes and integrating differentiable collision avoidance.

The framework's ability to synthesize "volume-informed guides" is a key aspect, enabling a full-order trajectory optimizer to converge in geometrically restricted spaces (with confinement ratios below 1.5) where conventional planners fail. This capacity to discover diverse and kinematically/dynamically feasible locomotion strategies, without relying on manual initial guesses, could significantly advance the autonomy of humanoids in complex settings.

The demonstrated success in generating long-horizon plans (12-18 seconds) for a Unitree G1 humanoid across environments exceeding NIST emergency response standards is a practical validation of the framework's capabilities. Furthermore, the use of these optimized plans as high-quality references for training a robust residual reinforcement learning policy, which then successfully tracks these plans under extensive domain randomization in physics simulation, suggests a viable path toward real-world deployment and robust online execution.

Potential impact areas include enhancing robot capabilities for disaster response, inspection in hazardous or tight industrial settings, and exploration in environments unsuitable for human access. The methodological contributions in geometry-aware kinematic reference generation and dynamically consistent planning could also inform future developments in multi-contact locomotion and loco-manipulation for complex robots.

Future work outlined by the authors includes hardware validation on a physical robot, integrating the planner into efficient contact exploration pipelines for general confined locomotion policies, and extending the strategy to handle loco-manipulation tasks involving large objects in restricted spaces. These future directions indicate the potential for further expanding the framework's applicability and bridging the sim-to-real gap.
