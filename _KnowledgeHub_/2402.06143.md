# Reinforcement Learning for Blind Stair Climbing with Legged and Wheeled-Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a reinforcement learning approach that enables diverse robot platforms, including quadrupeds, bipeds, and wheeled-legged systems, to climb stairs using only proprioceptive feedback and a terrain mode signal. This method demonstrates successful sim-to-real transfer, allowing robots like the Ascento to climb 15cm steps without external perception.

## Problem

- Robotic stair navigation is challenging due to complex dynamics and contact interactions, often requiring extensive model tuning or external perception.
- Traditional control methods and existing RL approaches typically rely on external sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR), increasing computational overhead and potential failure modes.
- There is a need for robust, sensor-independent stair-climbing capabilities to improve practical robot deployment.

## Method

- An asymmetric actor-critic ==reinforcement learning== architecture is employed, where the critic uses privileged information during training, while the deployed actor relies only on proprioceptive data.
- Stair climbing is formulated as a position-based task, allowing the robot to discover flexible and efficient movement strategies for reaching target locations.
- A boolean mode switch is introduced to activate specialized stair-climbing behaviors, enabling seamless transitions between flat ground and stair navigation without complex perception.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful stair climbing on four diverse robot platforms: Unitree Go1 (quadruped), Cassie (bipedal), Ascento (wheeled-legged), and ANYmal on Wheels.
- The Ascento wheeled-legged robot achieved successful ascent of 15cm steps, a previously difficult task for the platform using traditional methods.
- Learned policies exhibited robust transfer from simulation to real-world hardware, validating the efficacy of the training methodology.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing privileged information to the critic during training is essential for learning effective and terrain-cautious stair-climbing policies.
> - A simple boolean mode switch allows robots, especially bipeds, to develop distinct and specialized movement strategies for navigating stairs.
> - Comprehensive domain randomization in the Isaac Gym simulator facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer of learned locomotion policies to diverse physical robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Navigating stairs represents one of the most significant mobility challenges for robots operating in human-designed environments. This paper presents a reinforcement learning approach that enables various robot platforms—including quadrupeds, bipeds, and wheeled-legged systems—to climb stairs without relying on external sensors or complex perception systems. The research demonstrates how a single control framework can be adapted across different robot morphologies, making stair climbing more accessible and practical for real-world deployment.

The work addresses a critical gap in robotic mobility by developing controllers that can operate "blindly"—using only internal sensors and pre-programmed terrain awareness signals rather than requiring complex visual or spatial mapping systems. This approach significantly reduces the computational and hardware requirements for stair-climbing robots while improving their reliability in diverse environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Stair climbing presents unique challenges that distinguish it from flat-ground locomotion. Traditional robotic control methods, such as Model Predictive Control (MPC), often struggle with the complex dynamics and contact patterns required for effective stair navigation. These approaches typically require accurate dynamic models and extensive tuning for different robot platforms and stair configurations.

Previous reinforcement learning approaches to legged locomotion have shown promise but often rely on external sensors like cameras, LiDAR, or GPS systems for terrain perception. While effective, these solutions introduce additional complexity, computational overhead, and potential failure modes that limit their practical deployment. The need for robust, sensor-independent stair climbing capabilities has driven the development of this research.

The paper tackles these challenges by formulating stair climbing as a reinforcement learning problem that can be solved using only proprioceptive feedback (internal joint positions, velocities, and orientations) combined with a simple boolean signal indicating when stair-climbing behavior should be activated. This approach eliminates the need for external perception while maintaining the adaptability and robustness that make reinforcement learning attractive for complex locomotion tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core innovation of this approach lies in its asymmetric actor-critic architecture, which strategically separates the information available during training from what is required during deployment. This design allows the system to learn from rich environmental information while maintaining simplicity in real-world operation.

During training, the critic network receives privileged information including:
- Linear velocity and base height measurements
- Relative goal position data
- Detailed terrain height measurements
- Contact forces and friction coefficients

This additional information helps the critic provide better value estimates and guidance during the learning process. The critic essentially acts as a knowledgeable teacher that has access to ground truth about the environment and can guide the actor toward more effective behaviors.

In contrast, the actor network—which is the only component used during actual robot deployment—operates with a much more limited observation space:
- Joint positions and velocities
- Projected gravity vector
- Angular velocity
- 2D goal direction and height
- Heading error
- Terrain boolean (stair-climbing mode activation)

This asymmetric design enables the system to learn complex behaviors while ensuring that the deployed controller remains practical and doesn't depend on sensors or information that may not be available in real-world scenarios.

#### 3.1. Position-Based Task Formulation

Unlike traditional velocity-based control approaches that require robots to track specific velocity profiles, this research employs a position-based task formulation. The robot is given a goal location to reach within a specified time window, providing the reinforcement learning algorithm with greater freedom to explore different strategies for climbing stairs.

This formulation proves particularly beneficial for stair climbing because it allows the robot to discover natural movement patterns rather than forcing it to follow predetermined trajectories. The position-based approach enables the robot to:

- Adjust its speed dynamically based on terrain difficulty
- Develop unique strategies for different step heights and configurations
- Learn recovery behaviors when encountering unexpected obstacles
- Optimize energy efficiency by finding the most effective climbing patterns

The reward structure encourages the robot to reach its goal efficiently while maintaining stability and avoiding falls. This creates a learning environment where the robot can develop robust, adaptive behaviors that generalize well to different stair configurations and real-world variations.

#### 3.2. Boolean Mode Switch for Terrain Adaptation

A key innovation in this work is the introduction of a boolean observation that activates or deactivates stair-climbing mode. This simple but effective mechanism allows the robot to learn specialized behaviors for stair climbing while maintaining excellent performance on regular terrain.

The boolean mode switch serves several important functions:

**Behavior Specialization**: When the stair-climbing mode is activated, the robot can employ more aggressive or specialized movement patterns that might be unnecessary or inefficient on flat ground. This includes higher leg lifts, different gait patterns, and more cautious forward progress.

**Seamless Transition**: The boolean signal allows for smooth transitions between different locomotion modes without requiring separate controllers or complex mode-switching logic. The same neural network policy handles both terrain types but adapts its behavior based on the mode signal.

**Simplified Deployment**: In real-world applications, the boolean signal can be triggered by simple sensors, pre-mapped environments, or even manual activation, eliminating the need for complex terrain classification systems.

The research shows that this approach is particularly beneficial for bipedal robots, which often require more distinct movement strategies for different terrain types compared to quadrupedal systems. The boolean mode switch enables these robots to learn specialized stair-climbing skills while maintaining a robust general locomotion policy.

#### 3.3. Training Environment and Domain Randomization

The training process utilizes the Isaac Gym simulator, which provides high-throughput, GPU-accelerated physics simulation. This enables rapid training across multiple robot instances simultaneously, significantly reducing the time required to develop effective policies.

Domain randomization plays a crucial role in ensuring that the learned policies transfer effectively to real-world conditions. The training environment incorporates several randomization strategies:

**Physical Parameter Variation**: The simulator randomly varies friction coefficients, robot mass properties, and joint dynamics to ensure the learned policy is robust to modeling uncertainties and hardware variations.

**Environmental Disturbances**: Random forces are applied to the robot during training to simulate unexpected disturbances like wind, contact with objects, or uneven terrain that might not be perfectly modeled.

**Control Loop Delays**: The training incorporates realistic delays in the control loop to match the timing characteristics of real robotic systems, ensuring that the learned policy accounts for the latency present in actual hardware.

**Terrain Variation**: The training environments include stairs of different heights, widths, and configurations, as well as combinations of flat terrain and obstacles to develop a versatile locomotion policy.

This comprehensive approach to domain randomization helps bridge the challenging sim-to-real gap that often prevents reinforcement learning policies from working effectively on physical robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates successful stair climbing across four distinct robot platforms:

**Unitree Go1 Quadruped**: The policy enables this commercially available quadruped to climb stairs reliably using natural gaits that adapt to step height and configuration.

**Cassie Bipedal Robot**: Despite the increased complexity of bipedal locomotion, the learned policy successfully enables stair climbing while maintaining balance and forward progress.

**Ascento Wheeled-Legged Robot**: The policy allows this balancing robot to climb 15cm steps, a capability that was previously unachievable with traditional control methods.

**ANYmal on Wheels**: The wheeled-legged quadruped demonstrates the versatility of the approach across different hybrid locomotion systems.

The experimental results reveal several key insights:

**Privileged Information Importance**: The research confirms that privileged information provided to the critic during training is essential for learning effective stair-climbing behaviors. This information helps guide the actor toward terrain-cautious strategies that might not emerge naturally from sparse reward signals alone.

**Boolean Mode Effectiveness**: The boolean observation proves particularly valuable for bipedal robots, enabling them to develop distinct movement strategies for stairs while maintaining robust general locomotion capabilities.

**Transfer Success**: The policies successfully transfer from simulation to real hardware, demonstrating the effectiveness of the domain randomization approach and the robustness of the learned behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The successful transfer to real-world robots represents a significant achievement in practical robotics. The Ascento robot's ability to climb 15cm steps marks a substantial improvement in its mobility capabilities, enabling it to navigate environments that were previously inaccessible.

The practical impact of this work extends beyond the specific robots tested:

**Deployment Simplicity**: The lack of dependence on external sensors makes the system easy to integrate into existing robot control stacks without requiring additional hardware or complex sensor fusion algorithms.

**Cost Effectiveness**: By eliminating the need for expensive sensors like LiDAR or high-resolution cameras, the approach makes stair-climbing capabilities more accessible to a broader range of robotic applications.

**Reliability**: The sensor-independent approach reduces potential failure modes and makes the system more robust to environmental conditions that might affect external sensors.

**Scalability**: The method's success across multiple robot platforms suggests that it can be adapted to other robotic systems with relatively straightforward modifications to the observation and action spaces.

#### 5.1. Significance and Future Directions

This research makes several important contributions to the field of robotic locomotion and reinforcement learning. The demonstration that complex stair-climbing behaviors can be learned without external sensors challenges the prevailing assumption that sophisticated perception systems are necessary for navigating complex terrain.

The asymmetric actor-critic architecture provides a template for other applications where privileged information can be used during training to improve learning efficiency while maintaining practical deployment constraints. This approach could be valuable for various robotics tasks beyond locomotion, including manipulation and navigation in structured environments.

The position-based task formulation offers advantages over traditional velocity-based approaches and could inspire new methods for training locomotion policies that provide greater behavioral flexibility and adaptability.

Future research directions emerging from this work include:

- Extending the approach to more complex terrain types and obstacle combinations
- Investigating how the boolean mode switch concept could be generalized to support multiple terrain-specific behaviors
- Exploring the integration of minimal sensing (such as simple contact sensors) to enhance performance while maintaining the simplicity of the approach
- Developing methods for automatic terrain classification that could trigger the boolean mode switch without human intervention

The success of this research in enabling practical stair climbing across diverse robot platforms represents a meaningful step toward more capable and deployable robotic systems that can effectively navigate human environments. By demonstrating that complex locomotion behaviors can be achieved with minimal sensing requirements, this work opens new possibilities for practical robot deployment in real-world scenarios.
