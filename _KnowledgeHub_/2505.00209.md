# Direct Motion Models for Assessing Generated Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind introduce TRAJAN, a trajectory autoencoder framework that evaluates temporal coherence in generated videos through point track analysis, demonstrating stronger correlation with human judgments of motion consistency and realism compared to existing metrics like FVD while enabling spatiotemporal localization of generative inconsistencies.

## Problem

- Existing video quality metrics like FVD are primarily sensitive to frame-level content rather than temporal coherence
- Current evaluation methods struggle to identify and localize specific motion inconsistencies in generated videos
- Existing evaluation methods lack a unified framework for assessing videos across distribution-level comparisons, pairwise comparisons, and individual videos without a reference

## Method

- Developed **TRAJAN**, a trajectory autoencoder that analyzes point tracks extracted using **BootsTAPIR**
- Created a unified framework for evaluating videos at distribution, pair, and individual levels using the encoded motion features
- Conducted a detailed human study rating videos on interaction quality, realism, appearance/motion consistency, and object/camera speed to validate correlation with TRAJAN's automated scores

## Results

- TRAJAN shows higher sensitivity to temporal distortions compared to existing metrics and successfully identifies motion artifacts in synthetic videos that other metrics miss
- Better correlation with human ratings for realism, appearance, and motion consistency
- Enables precise localization of temporal inconsistencies in generated content

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Point track analysis enables better separation of motion assessment from appearance/content evaluation
> - Reconstruction errors in the trajectory autoencoder correlate well with human perception of motion plausibility
> - Spatiotemporal localization of inconsistencies provides interpretable feedback for improving generative models

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Metrics:** The paper directly acknowledges a major limitation in the field of video generation: the difficulty of accurately evaluating the temporal coherence and realism of generated videos. Current metrics like Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) are more sensitive to frame-level content and less effective at capturing motion inconsistencies.
- **Generative Video Model Evaluation:** The work contributes to the growing body of research dedicated to evaluating generative video models. As these models advance, there is a need for robust and reliable metrics that can assess the quality of the generated content.
- **Connection to Point Tracking Research:** This research relies heavily on point tracking models (BootsTAPIR), reflecting a broader trend of leveraging such techniques for video analysis and understanding. By focusing on point tracks, the authors aim to disentangle motion from content, allowing for a more direct assessment of temporal consistency.
- **Relation to Video Understanding and Representation:** The paper relates to research on video representation learning. Auto-encoding the motion features, the method, TRAJAN, attempts to create meaningful representation of videos.
- **Relation to Video Physics:** The paper is highly relatable to the evaluation of a physically accurate generated video, such as VideoPhy, showing that it can evaluate the level of consistency between video frames.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The primary objective is to develop a new metric, TRAJAN, that better measures the plausibility of object interactions and motion in generated videos.
- **Motivation:** The motivation stems from the inadequacy of existing metrics in capturing temporal distortions and motion inconsistencies in synthetic data. The authors seek to create a metric that is more sensitive to these aspects of video quality and can better predict human evaluations of temporal consistency and realism.
- **Unifying Framework for Video Evaluation:** The paper aims to create a unified approach for evaluating videos in various scenarios: comparing distributions of videos, comparing pairs of videos, and evaluating individual videos without a reference.
- **Improved Interpretability:** A further motivation is to provide spatiotemporal localization of generative video inconsistencies, enhancing the interpretability of errors in generated videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **TRAJAN: Trajectory Autoencoder:** The core of the proposed approach is a novel trajectory autoencoder (TRAJAN) architecture. TRAJAN is trained to encode and reconstruct point tracks extracted from videos.
- **Point Tracking with BootsTAPIR:** The method uses the BootsTAPIR model to estimate point tracks in videos. These point tracks represent the low-level motion features.
- **Autoencoding Motion Features:** TRAJAN autoencodes the point track features, creating a dense, high-level motion representation in its latent space.
- **Different Evaluation Scenarios:** The TRAJAN latent space is used to compare distributions of videos (using Fréchet distance), pairs of videos (using L2 distance), and individual videos (using reconstruction error).
- **Human Evaluation:** The authors conduct a detailed human study to assess the correlation between TRAJAN's output and human perception of video quality, with focus on: Quality of Interactions, The Realism of the objects and behavior, Consistency of Appearance and Motion, and the Speed of objects and the camera.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** The proposed method is compared to a wide range of alternative metrics, including FVD-based methods (I3D, VideoMAE), motion-based methods (motion histograms, optical flow), and metrics designed for text-to-video alignment (CLIPScore).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Sensitivity to Temporal Distortions:** TRAJAN is shown to be markedly more sensitive to temporal distortions in synthetic data than existing metrics.
- **Motion Similarity Capture:** When comparing pairs of videos, TRAJAN embeddings better capture similarities in motion, even when appearance-based metrics suggest they are different.
- **Prediction of Human Ratings:** The reconstruction score from TRAJAN is more predictive of human ratings for realism, appearance, and motion consistency than a wide range of alternatives.
- **Spatiotemporal Localization of Errors:** The method can spatiotemporally localize generative video inconsistencies, providing insights into the specific errors made by generative models.
- **Correlation with Human Judgements:** TRAJAN correlates with human judgements, and is better correlated for realism of object interactions than most methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Improved Video Quality Assessment:** TRAJAN offers a more accurate and reliable way to evaluate the quality of generated videos, particularly in terms of motion and temporal coherence.
- **Guidance for Generative Model Development:** The method provides valuable feedback for improving generative video models. By identifying specific areas of inconsistency, it can help developers focus their efforts on enhancing motion realism and temporal stability.
- **Unifying Evaluation Framework:** TRAJAN provides a unifying framework for evaluating videos, enabling assessment at different levels of granularity (distribution, pair, individual).
- **Broader Applicability:** The proposed approach could potentially be extended to other video analysis tasks, such as video editing, anomaly detection, and action recognition.
- **Potential Impact for Content Creation:** By improving the quality of generated videos, TRAJAN could have a significant impact on content creation, allowing for the generation of more realistic and engaging video content.

In conclusion, the research presents a significant advance in video quality assessment. TRAJAN addresses a critical gap in existing metrics by focusing on motion and temporal coherence, providing a more accurate and interpretable way to evaluate generated videos. This work has the potential to impact the development of generative video models and contribute to broader advances in video analysis and content creation.
