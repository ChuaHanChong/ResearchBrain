# in2IN: Leveraging individual Information to Generate Human INteractions

> [!summary] Summary
> in2IN is a Siamese Transformer diffusion model for human-human interaction generation that conditions self-attention on individual motion descriptions and cross-attention on interaction descriptions, paired with multi-weight classifier-free guidance and DualMDM composition; it sets new state of the art on InterHuman while boosting intra-personal diversity.

## Problem

- Existing interaction datasets like InterHuman lack detailed individual textual descriptions, limiting fine-grained control over each person's motion.
- Prior interaction-generation methods replicate training-set motions, showing limited intra-personal diversity and no per-person control capability.
- Standard classifier-free guidance handles only single conditions well, making it hard to independently weight interaction versus individual conditioning signals.

## Method

- **in2IN** ==Siamese diffusion architecture==: two parameter-sharing Transformer copies condition self-attention on individual descriptions and cross-attention on interaction descriptions to model intra- and inter-personal dynamics separately.
- ==Multi-weight classifier-free guidance== extends CFG with three independent weights (interaction, individual, combined) via quadruple sampling, giving separate control over each condition's influence.
- **DualMDM** ==model-composition== technique blends in2IN's interaction output with a single-person motion prior using a time-varying scheduler (exponential, linear, constant) instead of a fixed blending weight.

## Results

- in2IN achieves state-of-the-art R-Precision Top-1 of **0.455** and FID of **5.177** on InterHuman, surpassing MoMat-MoGen (**0.449** R-Precision, **5.674** FID).
- Grid search finds optimal CFG weights of w_c=**3**, w_I=**3**, w_i=**1**, individually ablated weights of **4** for w_c and w_I and **2** for w_i.
- Exponential DualMDM scheduler (lambda=**0.00875**) yields EID of **1.492** versus **1.238** baseline, balancing individual diversity gain against interaction-quality (FID) degradation better than constant scheduling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating self-attention and cross-attention conditioning by scope (individual versus interaction) lets one architecture control fine-grained and holistic motion semantics independently.
> - A fixed blending weight for model composition is suboptimal; the ideal balance between two combined generative priors shifts across the denoising trajectory.
> - LLM-generated auxiliary annotations can substitute for missing fine-grained labels in a dataset, though at risk of hallucination and label-motion mismatch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research is situated within the field of Human Motion Generation, specifically focusing on the text-conditioned synthesis of multi-human motion interactions. Recent advancements in generative AI have propelled this field, yet the scarcity of annotated motion datasets, due to expensive recording setups and actors, presents a notable challenge. Natural language text is increasingly recognized as a versatile conditioning tool, particularly with the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs), enabling detailed specification of movements across various body parts, velocities, contexts, and emotional states.

The generation of realistic individual human motion from text is a complex task due to the intricacies of intra-personal dynamics and the alignment between textual descriptions and specific movements. This complexity is further amplified when modeling human-human interactions, where inter-personal dynamics introduce additional challenges. Existing human-human interaction datasets, such as InterHuman, while comprehensive in the range of interactions, often lack sufficient diversity in individual motions and detailed textual descriptions for each interactant's specific actions. This limitation contributes to current generation methods tending to replicate training data, resulting in restricted intra-personal diversity and limited individual control capabilities within generated interactions.

Diffusion models have demonstrated effectiveness in various generative tasks, including text-to-motion synthesis, offering capabilities for generating realistic samples with high diversity and fine-grained control. Previous works in text-driven human interaction generation, such as ComMDM, InterGen, and MoMat-MoGen, have explored different architectures, often involving cooperative denoisers or shared attention mechanisms to model inter-person relationships. However, these methods have encountered issues such as overfitting due to data scarcity or limitations in controlling individual nuances within an interaction.

Furthermore, the concept of motion composition, which involves combining multiple motions, has primarily focused on single-person scenarios (temporal or spatial composition). While DiffusionBlending proposed a generic technique for combining two single-person diffusion models using a fixed blending weight, the current work posits that a more dynamic approach to blending is necessary for complex scenarios involving human-human interactions and individual priors. This paper aims to address these identified gaps by introducing methodologies that enhance both the control and diversity of individual dynamics within generated human-human interactions, leveraging individual-specific textual conditioning and a dynamic model composition technique.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the generation of human-human motion interactions conditioned on textual descriptions. The authors identify two main difficulties in this domain: accurately modeling the high-dimensional inter-personal dynamics and effectively capturing the intra-personal diversity of interactions.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current methodologies and available datasets:
- **Limited Intra-Personal Diversity:** Existing methods for generating human-human interactions tend to produce motions with limited diversity in the individual actions performed by each person. This is largely attributed to the constraints of current interaction datasets and conditioning strategies, which often lack detailed individual-level annotations. For example, a "salute" interaction might involve waving a left or right hand, varying speeds, or even a bow, but current systems struggle to produce or control such variations effectively.
- **Lack of Individual Control:** A consequence of limited diversity is the inability to exert fine-grained control over the specific motions of each individual within an interaction. Users cannot easily specify that one person should "wave their right hand" while the other "bows forward" as part of a "salute" interaction.
- **Dataset Deficiencies:** Datasets like InterHuman are extensive for interactions but do not provide sufficient individual diversity or detailed textual descriptions of the individual motions occurring within the interaction.

To address these challenges, the paper outlines three key contributions and their underlying objectives:

1. **Introduce in2IN, a novel diffusion model architecture:**
 - **Objective:** To generate human-human motion by conditioning not only on a general textual description of the overall interaction (inter-personal dynamics) but also on specific textual descriptions for each individual's actions (intra-personal dynamics).
 - **Motivation:** This finer-grained conditioning is hypothesized to enable more precise interaction generation, leading to improved performance on existing datasets like InterHuman. To facilitate this, a secondary objective is to extend existing datasets (specifically InterHuman) with automatically generated individual descriptions using Large Language Models.

2. **Propose a multi-weight Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) technique:**
 - **Objective:** To allow independent weighting of the influence of each distinct condition (overall interaction, individual person 1, individual person 2) during the diffusion sampling process.
 - **Motivation:** This aims to provide users with greater control over the balance between general interaction coherence and specific individual behaviors in the generated motions.

3. **Introduce DualMDM, a new motion composition technique:**
 - **Objective:** To further enhance individual diversity and control by combining the outputs of an interaction generation model (like in2IN) with a single-person motion prior pre-trained on a diverse individual motion dataset (e.g., HumanML3D).
 - **Motivation:** This technique seeks to overcome the inherent limitations of interaction datasets regarding individual diversity by injecting knowledge from broader single-person motion datasets. It also aims to improve upon prior composition methods by using a dynamic blending weight scheduler, positing that the optimal balance between interaction and individual priors varies throughout the denoising process.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond mere interaction replication to achieve a generative system capable of producing diverse and precisely controllable human-human interactions, addressing the dual challenges of inter-personal coherence and intra-personal variability.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The research introduces two primary methodological contributions: in2IN, an interaction-aware diffusion model, and DualMDM, a motion composition technique.

#### 3.1. in2IN: Interaction Diffusion Model

The in2IN architecture is a Transformer-based diffusion model designed to generate denoised motion for two interacting individuals. It is built upon the principle of commutative property in interactions, meaning that the interaction {x_a, x_b} is considered equivalent to {x_b, x_a}. This is implemented using a Siamese configuration, where two copies of the diffusion model share parameters. Each copy processes the noisy motion input (x_t_a or x_t_b) of one individual and predicts its denoised version (x_0_a or x_0_b) directly, allowing the use of kinematic loss functions.

The model architecture incorporates:
- **Multi-head self-attention module:** This module models the intra-personal dynamics of each individual's motion. It is conditioned using the specific textual description of that individual's motion.
- **Multi-head cross-attention module:** This module models the inter-personal dynamics by combining the output of the self-attention module with the motion of the other individual in the interaction. It is conditioned on the overall interaction textual description.
- **Adaptive normalization layers:** These layers are used to inject the various textual conditions into the respective attention modules.
This conditioning strategy ensures that the self-attention focuses on individual actions, while the cross-attention focuses on the broader interaction context.

#### 3.2. Multi-Weight Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG)

To manage the multiple conditioning inputs (overall interaction, individual person 1, individual person 2), the authors propose an extension of Classifier-Free Guidance. This multi-weight CFG allows independent weighting of each condition's influence during the generation process. The sampling function `G_I(x_t, t, c)` is formulated to combine an unconditional model output `G(x_t, t, ∅)` with weighted differences between the conditioned outputs `G(x_t, t, c)`, `G(x_t, t, c_I)`, `G(x_t, t, c_i)` and the unconditional output. This method enables granular control but requires four separate samplings from the denoiser per step, as opposed to the two in standard CFG.

#### 3.3. DualMDM: Model Composition

DualMDM is a motion composition technique designed to enhance the intra-personal diversity and control within generated interactions. It extends the DiffusionBlending method by combining outputs from two different diffusion models: an interaction diffusion model (e.g., in2IN) and an individual motion prior trained on single-person motions. The key innovation is replacing the fixed blending weight `w` with a dynamic weight scheduler `w(t)`, which varies based on the current denoising step `t`. The proposed formula is `G_I,i(x_t, t, c) = G_I(x_t, t, c) + w(t) · (G_i(x_t, t, c_i) - G_I(x_t, t, c))`. This allows the influence of the individual prior to change throughout the denoising process. Various weight schedulers (constant, linear, exponential, inverse exponential) were explored.

#### 3.4. Datasets and Preprocessing

- **InterHuman:** This dataset is the primary source for human-human interactions. Motions are represented by joint positions and velocities in the world frame, 6D local rotations in the root frame, and binary foot-ground contact features for 22 joints.
- **LLM-generated individual descriptions:** Since InterHuman lacks specific individual motion descriptions, GPT-3.5 turbo was used to automatically generate these from the existing interaction descriptions. A specific prompt structure was employed for this task. The authors acknowledge potential risks of hallucination or mismatch with this automated approach.
- **HumanML3D:** This dataset, containing a wider range of annotated individual motions, was used to train the single-person motion prior. Its motion representation was converted to match the world-frame representation used in InterHuman for compatibility.

#### 3.5. Individual Motion Prior

A single-human baseline model, based on a modified in2IN architecture (with cross-attention modules removed and only individual conditioning retained), was trained on the converted HumanML3D dataset. This prior uses only L2 loss during training.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The in2IN models consist of 8 multi-head attention layers with a latent dimension of 1024 and 8 heads. A frozen CLIP-ViTL/14 model served as the text encoder. The diffusion process used 1,000 timesteps with a cosine noise schedule, and DDIM sampling with 50 timesteps during inference. Multi-weight CFG was enabled by randomly setting 10% of CLIP embeddings to zero during training. Models were trained for 2,000 epochs with AdamW optimizer, a batch size of 64, and 16-bit mixed precision. Loss functions included L2 loss and kinematic losses from MDM (foot contact, velocity) and InterGen (bone length, masked joint distance map, relative orientation), combined with InterGen's kinematic loss scheduler.

#### 3.7. Evaluation Metrics

Standard metrics from were used:
- **R-Precision & Multimodal-Dist:** Assess semantic closeness between generated motions and input prompts.
- **FID:** Measures dissimilarity between distributions of generated and ground-truth motions.
- **Diversity:** Quantifies the range of variation in generated motions.
- **MultiModality:** Calculates the average variance for motions from a single text prompt.
A new metric, **Extrinsic Individual Diversity (EID)**, was introduced to quantify the influence of individual descriptions on generating varied intra-personal dynamics. EID is calculated as the Wasserstein distance between a distribution of motions generated with ground-truth individual descriptions (D_GT) and a distribution generated with randomly replaced individual descriptions (D_rand). For DualMDM evaluation, D_GT used in2IN, and D_rand used in2IN combined with DualMDM.

### 4. Main findings and results

#### 4.1. in2IN: Interaction Generation

Quantitative evaluation on the InterHuman dataset demonstrated that the in2IN architecture achieved state-of-the-art performance compared to previous methods. It attained a R-Precision (Top 1) of 0.455 and an FID score of 3.790, surpassing or matching the current state-of-the-art MoMat-MoGen. The MultiModality score for in2IN was 1.061, which was lower than most previous methods. This reduction in MultiModality is interpreted as a consequence of the additional individual information used by in2IN, which narrows the spectrum of valid motions that fulfill the interaction description while maintaining fidelity. A variant of in2IN, denoted `in2IN*` (conditioned only on the interaction description during sampling), showed slightly reduced performance (R-Precision of 0.425 and FID of 3.803), indicating the contribution of individual descriptions.

#### 4.2. Multi-Weight Classifier-Free Guidance Analysis

An ablation study on the individual weights of the multi-weight CFG showed that optimal individual weights for `w_c` and `w_I` were around 4, while for `w_i` it was 2. Exceeding these values, particularly for `w_i`, resulted in decreased performance. Through a grid search on a validation subset, the optimal combination of weights was determined to be `w_c=3`, `w_I=3`, and `w_i=1`.

#### 4.3. DualMDM: Individual Diversity

Evaluation of the DualMDM technique with different weight schedulers demonstrated a trade-off between Extrinsic Individual Diversity (EID) and interaction quality (R-Precision and FID). Schedulers that assigned higher weight to the individual motion prior generally resulted in higher EID but lower R-Precision and higher FID.
- A constant scheduler with λ=0 (effectively only using the interaction model) served as a baseline, yielding the highest interaction quality (R-Precision of 0.687, FID of 5.177) and the lowest EID (1.238).
- The exponential weight scheduler with λ=0.00875 was identified as providing a balanced trade-off, achieving an R-Precision (Top 3) of 0.574, an FID of 22.86, and an EID of 1.492.
The authors hypothesize that the exponential schedule's effectiveness is due to the greater importance of intra-personal motion relationships during early denoising stages, while inter-personal interaction dynamics become more critical in later stages. Gradually decreasing the individual prior's influence is posited to allow the interaction model to recover and maintain inter-personal coherence during later denoising steps.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative comparisons indicated that the in2IN model generated more realistic interactions that were better aligned with textual descriptions compared to InterGen. For the DualMDM method, the exponential scheduler provided more coherent results. It preserved the semantics of the interaction while generating individual motions that matched the individual descriptions, demonstrating improved fine-grained control. In contrast, constant schedulers, particularly when assigning high weights to the individual prior, were observed to degrade the inter-personal dynamics, affecting trajectories and orientations. A limitation noted was that the optimal λ value for the exponential scheduler could vary depending on the specific characteristics of the interaction and individual motions being combined.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research introduces methodologies that contribute to the field of text-driven human motion generation, particularly for multi-person interactions. The contributions of in2IN and DualMDM offer specific advancements in controlling and diversifying generated human motions.

**Enhanced Control and Fidelity in Interaction Generation:** The in2IN model represents an advancement by integrating individual-specific textual descriptions alongside overall interaction descriptions. This dual-level conditioning, coupled with the multi-weight Classifier-Free Guidance, allows for a more granular control over the generated motions. The achievement of state-of-the-art performance on the InterHuman dataset suggests that leveraging individual information can improve the semantic alignment and realism of generated interactions. This capability for fine-grained control over both inter- and intra-personal dynamics could enable creators to specify complex scenarios where each participant's actions are precisely defined within a coherent interaction.

**Increased Intra-Personal Diversity through Model Composition:** The DualMDM technique addresses a recognized limitation in existing interaction datasets and generation methods: the lack of diversity in individual behaviors within an interaction. By dynamically combining an interaction model with a single-person motion prior, DualMDM can inject a broader range of individual movements while aiming to preserve the overall interaction's coherence. The proposed dynamic weight scheduler, particularly the exponential function, offers a flexible approach to balancing the influence of different generative priors throughout the denoising process. This methodology could be significant for generating a wider array of human behaviors for similar interaction prompts, moving beyond merely replicating training data.

**Broadening Application Scope:** The ability to generate highly realistic, diverse, and controllable human-human interactions has implications for several applied domains:
- **Robotics:** For simulating human-robot or multi-robot interactions with greater realism and variability, which could aid in training and validation of autonomous systems.
- **Gaming and Animation:** Automating the creation of complex and nuanced character interactions, potentially reducing manual animation efforts and increasing the dynamic range of virtual characters in video games and animated content.
- **Metaverse and Virtual Reality:** Populating virtual environments with more believable and context-aware non-player characters, enhancing user immersion and interaction quality.

**Methodological Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies areas for further development, such as improving the fidelity of LLM-generated individual descriptions and developing adaptive blending strategies for DualMDM that do not require manual parameter tuning. These suggestions point towards future research directions aimed at creating more robust, autonomous, and user-friendly generative systems for complex human behaviors. The exploration of dynamic weighting for model composition also establishes a precedent for combining diverse generative models in a nuanced, context-dependent manner.
