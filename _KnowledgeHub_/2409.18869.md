# Emu3: Next-Token Prediction is All You Need

> [!summary] Summary
> The Emu3 model from BAAI demonstrates that next-token prediction, a method typically used for language models, can effectively handle multiple modalities, including text, images, and video. It achieves competitive performance in tasks such as image and video generation and vision-language understanding through a unified transformer decoder architecture and a dedicated vision tokenizer.

## Problem

- Multimodal AI historically relies on fragmented architectures, with separate models dominating different domains (e.g., diffusion models for image generation, transformers for text, compositional approaches for multimodal understanding).
- Applying the next-token prediction paradigm to non-textual modalities like images and video has been challenging, often requiring hybrid approaches or multiple training objectives.
- Previous multimodal systems often suffer from architectural complexity due to the need to combine disparate components for different modalities.

## Method

- ==Emu3== employs a single transformer decoder architecture, adapted with an expanded embedding layer and an extended context length (up to **131,072** tokens).
- A dedicated ==vision tokenizer== (based on SBER-MoVQGAN) converts images and videos into discrete tokens, allowing them to be processed alongside text tokens in the unified framework.
- The model is trained in two stages using high-quality text, image, and video data, followed by post-training refinements like ==Quality Fine-Tuning== and ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==.

## Results

- Emu3 achieves competitive performance in text-to-image generation, outperforming SDXL and achieving comparable results to DALL-E 3 and Midjourney v5.2 in human evaluations.
- The model demonstrates strong video capabilities, scoring **81.0** on the VBench benchmark compared to OpenSora 1.2's **79.8**.
- Emu3 performs competitively across **12** vision-language benchmarks, averaging **62.1**, comparable to LLaVA-1.6's **61.8**, using a unified next-token prediction approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Next-token prediction, the paradigm powering large language models, can be effectively extended to handle multiple modalities including text, images, and video.
> - By converting images and videos into discrete tokens, a single transformer decoder architecture can achieve competitive performance across diverse tasks without specialized components or multiple training objectives.
> - The unified token-based approach offers architectural simplicity, improved scaling properties, and potential for emergent cross-modal capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

The field of artificial intelligence has seen remarkable progress in recent years, with separate architectures dominating different domains: diffusion models for image generation, transformer-based language models for text, and compositional approaches like CLIP+LLMs for multimodal understanding. The Emu3 model, developed by the BAAI (Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence) team, proposes a unifying approach that challenges this fragmentation by demonstrating that next-token prediction—the paradigm that powers large language models—can effectively handle multiple modalities including text, images, and video.

#### 1.2. Understanding Next-Token Prediction

Next-token prediction is the fundamental training objective behind large language models like GPT. The model is trained to predict the next token in a sequence given all previous tokens. This approach has proven remarkably effective for text, enabling models to generate coherent paragraphs, solve complex reasoning tasks, and even exhibit emergent capabilities beyond their explicit training.

However, applying this paradigm to non-textual modalities like images and video has been challenging. Previous approaches either:

1. Used diffusion models for generation (like Stable Diffusion)
2. Created compositional systems combining separate vision encoders with LLMs (like LLaVA)
3. Employed hybrid approaches with multiple training objectives

Emu3 demonstrates that with appropriate tokenization of visual content, next-token prediction can be extended to handle multiple modalities effectively. This represents a significant simplification in multimodal AI architecture and training.

The core insight is that by converting images and videos into discrete tokens—similar to how text is tokenized into words or subwords—these modalities can be processed within the same framework. This unified approach offers several advantages:

- Architectural simplicity (single model, single objective)
- Better scaling properties
- Potential for emergent cross-modal capabilities
- Reduced computational complexity compared to diffusion models

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Proposed Approach

This paper presents a significant step toward general-purpose AI by showing that a single model with a single training objective can compete with specialized architectures across diverse tasks. Unlike previous multimodal systems that rely on diffusion models or compositional approaches with separate components, Emu3 achieves impressive results through autoregressive prediction alone, treating various modalities as sequences of tokens processed by a transformer decoder.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. The Emu3 Architecture

Emu3 employs a transformer decoder architecture similar to Llama-2, but with important adaptations for multimodal processing:

1. **Expanded embedding layer** to accommodate vision tokens in addition to text tokens
2. **Vision tokenizer** to convert images and videos into discrete tokens
3. **Extended context length** (up to 131,072 tokens) to handle the token-heavy nature of visual content
4. **Architectural components** including RMSNorm for normalization, GQA for attention, SwiGLU activation, and rotary positional embeddings (RoPE)

The model processes multimodal inputs by first tokenizing them appropriately: text is tokenized using a standard text tokenizer, while images and videos are processed through the vision tokenizer. These tokens are then fed into the transformer decoder, which predicts the next token in the sequence regardless of modality.

For generation tasks, the model can generate text tokens that describe visual content (for vision-language understanding) or visual tokens that can be converted back into images or videos (for generation tasks).

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

Emu3's training follows a comprehensive approach encompassing diverse data and multiple training stages:

- **Language data**: High-quality Chinese and English text corpus
- **Image data**: Large-scale image-text datasets filtered based on resolution and aesthetic quality
- **Video data**: Diverse video collection preprocessed to split into scenes, remove excessive text, filter for motion, and assess aesthetic quality

All visual data underwent extensive captioning using fine-tuned Emu2 models and GPT-4V to provide high-quality text descriptions for training.

#### 3.3. Training Process

The training proceeded in two primary stages:

1. **Stage 1**: Training on text and image data with a context length of 5,120 tokens
2. **Stage 2**: Introducing video data and increasing the context length to 131,072 tokens

The training employed advanced techniques such as tensor parallelism, context parallelism, and data parallelism for efficient scaling across GPU clusters.

#### 3.4. Post-Training Refinement

For specific capabilities, additional fine-tuning was performed:

- **Quality Fine-Tuning (QFT)**: Using high-quality image and video data to improve generation quality
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: Aligning the model with human preferences for visual quality and prompt alignment
- **Specialized fine-tuning** for vision-language understanding tasks

The training approach demonstrates the versatility of next-token prediction as a foundation for multimodal capabilities, requiring only refinements rather than fundamental changes to the training paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Performance in Image Generation

Emu3 demonstrates competitive performance in text-to-image generation, a domain traditionally dominated by diffusion models. The model achieves this through autoregressive generation of image tokens, which are then decoded back into pixel space using the vision tokenizer.

Human evaluation studies show that Emu3 outperforms SDXL and achieves comparable results to DALL-E 3 and Midjourney v5.2. The model particularly excels at:

1. **Prompt alignment**: Accurately reflecting text instructions in generated images
2. **Visual quality**: Producing aesthetically pleasing and coherent visuals
3. **Stylistic range**: Generating diverse visual styles from photorealism to artistic renderings

The application of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) significantly improved both visual quality and prompt alignment, as shown in the human preference scores:

The model generates a wide range of image styles and content types, showcasing versatility comparable to specialized image generation systems.

#### 4.2. Video Capabilities

One of the most impressive aspects of Emu3 is its ability to generate coherent videos—a capability that has traditionally required specialized architectures. The model generates videos by predicting sequences of video tokens autoregressively, maintaining temporal consistency across frames.

Emu3 demonstrates several key video capabilities:

1. **Text-to-video generation**: Creating short (5-second) videos from text descriptions
2. **Future prediction**: Extending existing videos by predicting future frames
3. **Temporal consistency**: Maintaining coherent motion and scene elements across frames

On the VBench benchmark for video generation, Emu3 achieves competitive results against specialized video models, scoring 81.0 compared to 79.8 for OpenSora 1.2 and 75.7 for OpenSora 1.1.

The video capabilities demonstrate the extensibility of next-token prediction to the temporal domain, effectively treating video as a sequence of visual tokens with temporal relationships. This allows the model to learn patterns of motion and environment evolution without specialized architectures.

Examples showcasing the model's video generation abilities include a wide range of content types spanning human subjects, natural scenes, and dynamic events.

#### 4.3. Vision-Language Understanding

Beyond generation capabilities, Emu3 demonstrates strong performance in vision-language understanding tasks, where the model must comprehend visual inputs and respond with appropriate text.

The model achieves competitive results across 12 vision-language benchmarks, scoring 62.1 on average compared to 61.8 for LLaVA-1.6 and 52.8 for LLaVA-1.5. This is particularly notable given that Emu3 uses a unified next-token prediction approach rather than the encoder-decoder architecture typical in vision-language models.

Examples of Emu3's vision-language capabilities include:

1. **Image description**: Providing detailed descriptions of visual content
2. **Visual question answering**: Responding to specific questions about images
3. **Mathematical reasoning**: Solving equations presented visually

#### 4.4. The Vision Tokenizer

A critical component enabling Emu3's multimodal capabilities is its vision tokenizer, based on SBER-MoVQGAN. This tokenizer converts images and videos into discrete tokens that can be processed alongside text tokens in the transformer model.

The tokenizer achieves:
- 4x temporal compression for videos
- 8x8 spatial compression for images and video frames

This compression allows visual content to be represented efficiently as sequences of tokens while preserving essential details. The tokenizer's reconstruction capabilities demonstrate high fidelity:

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Significance and Impact

Emu3 represents a substantial advance in multimodal AI with several important implications:

1. **Paradigm Simplification**: Demonstrates that next-token prediction can serve as a unified training objective across modalities, potentially simplifying the development of general AI systems.

2. **Architectural Unification**: Shows that a single model architecture can effectively handle diverse tasks that traditionally required specialized approaches.

3. **Computational Efficiency**: Avoids the computational intensity of diffusion models while achieving competitive results.

4. **Scaling Potential**: The token-based approach has favorable scaling properties, suggesting that further improvements could come from simply scaling model size and training data.

5. **Cross-Modal Learning**: Enables the model to leverage knowledge across modalities, potentially fostering emergent capabilities through cross-modal transfer.

The competitive performance across image generation, video generation, and vision-language understanding tasks—domains traditionally requiring different specialized architectures—suggests that next-token prediction could indeed be a more fundamental paradigm for artificial intelligence than previously recognized.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

Emu3 provides compelling evidence that next-token prediction can serve as a foundation for general multimodal AI systems. By converting various modalities into token sequences and training a large transformer to predict them autoregressively, the model achieves competitive performance across diverse tasks without requiring specialized architectures or multiple training objectives.

The model demonstrates that:

1. Images and videos can be effectively processed as token sequences
2. A single training objective (next-token prediction) can yield strong performance across modalities
3. The architectural simplicity of transformers can be preserved while expanding to multimodal tasks

This unified approach to multimodal AI represents a significant step toward more general artificial intelligence systems that can seamlessly process and generate content across modalities. As the paper's title suggests, "next-token prediction" may indeed be "all you need" to build increasingly capable multimodal AI systems.

The open-sourcing of key techniques, particularly the vision tokenizer, will likely accelerate research in this direction, potentially leading to even more capable unified models in the future.
