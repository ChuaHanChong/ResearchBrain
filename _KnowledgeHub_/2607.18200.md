# Learning Adaptive Safety Margins for Visual Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for visual navigation introduces a learnable, context-conditioned safety critic that dynamically adjusts obstacle clearance based on environmental context. This approach leads to higher success rates and improved path efficiency compared to existing baselines across simulated environments and demonstrates robust performance in real-world cluttered settings with a physical robot.

## Problem

- Mobile robots often rely on fixed, hand-tuned safety margins, leading to overly conservative paths in open spaces or brittle, collision-prone behavior in cluttered areas.
- The required safety clearance is highly context-dependent, varying with local clutter, maneuvering demands, and the reliability of perception systems.
- Uncertainty in vision-based depth and egomotion estimates makes a fixed minimum measured clearance an unreliable proxy for safe and executable navigation.

## Method

- A **learnable safety critic** is developed to evaluate and rank candidate trajectories, replacing traditional fixed-margin approaches with an adaptive clearance preference.
- The critic's learned preference is decomposed into three components: a ==safety term== for collision avoidance, an ==efficiency term== for smooth, direct paths, and a ==balancing term== to stabilize the learned margin.
- A ==two-stage teacher-student training== process is used: a teacher critic is trained with privileged geometric information (==ESDFs==) in simulation, and its scoring is then distilled into a perception-only student selector for real-world deployment on RGB-D observations.

## Results

- The method achieved the highest success rate (SR) of **0.783** (a **7.3** percentage point improvement over NavDP) and success weighted by path length (SPL) of **0.611** on the HM3D dataset, with similar leading performance on MP3D and cross-dataset transfers.
- Successful sim-to-real transfer was demonstrated on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in real indoor scenes, achieving **100%** success in easy/medium environments and **80%** in a challenging cluttered lab, significantly outperforming all baselines without task-specific tuning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three components of the safety critic (safety, efficiency, and balancing terms) are essential for achieving optimal performance, effectively mitigating trade-offs between conservatism and efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single, globally fixed safety margin cannot effectively balance safety and efficiency across diverse and dynamic indoor navigation scenarios.
> - Decomposing the adaptive clearance preference into distinct safety, efficiency, and balancing terms allows for a more nuanced and context-aware navigation policy.
> - Leveraging privileged information during a teacher training phase in simulation, followed by distillation to a perception-only student, enables robust sim-to-real transfer without requiring online mapping or explicit geometric reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile robot navigation in indoor, human-centric environments presents challenges beyond mere geometric collision avoidance. A crucial aspect is maintaining sufficient clearance from obstacles, which is context-dependent. The required safety margin can vary based on local clutter density, specific maneuvering demands (e.g., sharp turns in narrow passages), and the momentary reliability of perception systems. In vision-based navigation, where depth and egomotion estimates are subject to noise and environmental factors, a fixed minimum measured clearance often serves as an unreliable proxy for executability.

Existing navigation systems, particularly those employing generate-and-select paradigms with diffusion-based planners, commonly utilize analytic critics that rely on hand-tuned, globally fixed safety margins. This approach leads to two primary failure modes:
1. **Overly conservative behavior:** In open spaces, large fixed margins result in unnecessary detours and prolonged navigation times, potentially leading to timeouts.
2. **Brittle behavior:** In dense clutter or under perception bias, overly permissive margins can lead to near-boundary shortcuts that result in collisions or near-misses.

The broader research landscape for visual navigation includes end-to-end deep reinforcement learning (RL) agents like DD-PPO, hybrid systems incorporating explicit spatial memory (e.g., Active Neural SLAM), and generative decision-making models that propose diverse action or trajectory sequences (e.g., Diffusion Policy, NavDP). Model-based approaches using high-quality distance fields (e.g., Voxblox, EGO-Planner) also exist. While these systems demonstrate various strengths in perception, planning, or action generation, they often converge on a common bottleneck: the final selection of a trajectory based on a simplistic, hand-crafted scoring rule that incorporates a fixed clearance margin. This paper addresses this specific limitation by proposing a learned, adaptive safety margin mechanism.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the limitations of fixed safety margins in robot navigation. The authors identify that a single, global clearance threshold cannot effectively balance safety and efficiency across diverse and dynamic indoor environments. This limitation is particularly pronounced in vision-based systems, where the uncertainty in perception measurements makes a fixed margin susceptible to miscalibration, leading to either overly cautious or dangerously aggressive robot behavior.

The key objectives of the research are:
1. **Develop a context-conditioned safety critic:** The central objective is to replace the traditional fixed-margin trajectory ranking mechanism with a learnable critic that can adapt its clearance preference based on the robot's current environmental context.
2. **Decompose the adaptive preference:** To achieve this adaptability, the authors aim to structure the critic's learnable clearance preference into three complementary terms:
 - A **safety term** to ensure waypoint-wise and transition-wise collision avoidance.
 - An **efficiency term** to encourage smooth, direct paths while avoiding unnecessary detours.
 - A **balancing term** to prevent the learned margin from becoming unrealistically conservative or permissive.
3. **Enable deployment without privileged information:** The system is designed to be trained using privileged geometric information (Euclidean Signed Distance Fields, ESDFs) in simulation, but then distilled into a perception-only selector that operates solely on egocentric RGB-D observations and candidate trajectories, making it suitable for real-world deployment.
4. **Demonstrate superior performance:** The authors aim to validate their proposed method by achieving higher success rates (SR) and success weighted by path length (SPL) compared to strong diffusion, optimization, and reinforcement learning baselines in both simulated environments (HM3D, MP3D) and real-world scenarios.
5. **Confirm sim-to-real transferability:** A specific goal is to demonstrate that the critic, trained purely in simulation, can effectively transfer to a physical robot (Unitree G1 humanoid) and navigate cluttered indoor scenes without requiring task-specific tuning.
6. **Analyze individual component contributions:** Through ablation studies, the research aims to quantify the contribution of each term within the proposed safety critic to its overall performance, thereby justifying the design choices.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed policy operates on a generate-select pipeline for PointGoal navigation, where a diffusion generator produces diverse trajectory candidates from RGB-D observations, and a learnable safety critic selects the most suitable one.

**A. Diffusion Trajectory Generator:**
The trajectory generator is based on existing diffusion models for navigation (e.g., NavDP, T-diff). It uses a shared transformer encoder to process RGB-D observations and an optional goal token, generating a compact context. A diffusion head then produces K (default 16) diverse candidate trajectories, represented as ego-motion offsets, without requiring privileged geometric inputs. The generator is trained to optimize a conditional U-Net denoiser using a DDPM scheduler and a stabilized SNR weighting, predicting injected Gaussian noise. During inference, these ego-motion offsets are transformed into world-frame waypoints, and control commands (speed and angular velocity) are derived from finite differences between waypoints. Critically, the generator's training and inference processes do not utilize ESDFs.

**B. Learnable Safety Critic:**
The core contribution is a learnable safety critic that evaluates and ranks the candidate trajectories. The critic's score, denoted `V_ours(τ)`, is a sum of three components: `V_safe(τ)`, `V_efficient(τ)`, and `V_balance(τ)`. The critic relies on ESDF distances for scoring during teacher training, where `d_j = ESDF(π(p_j))` is the distance between the j-th waypoint and the nearest obstacle.

A key element is the context-conditioned margin head `qη`, which predicts a time-varying safety budget `d_min,j` for each waypoint. This budget is defined as `d_min,j = d_safe + softplus(qη(f_j))`, where `d_safe` is a fixed physical safety floor (0.1m) and `f_j` is a lightweight context feature vector including local clearance, ESDF gradient magnitude, and normalized step index. This ensures `d_min,j ≥ d_safe` and adapts the clearance budget based on the specific trajectory and local geometry.

The three terms of the critic are:
1. **Safety Term (`V_safe(τ)`)**: This term (`Equation 6`) combines two safety mechanisms:
 - An unsafe-count component that penalizes waypoints `j` where the ESDF distance `d_j` falls below the learned adaptive budget `d_min,j`.
 - A Control Barrier Function (CBF) residual loss (`L_cbf(τ)`) that enforces transition-wise safety-set invariance. This term encourages trajectories to remain within a physically meaningful safe set defined by `h(p) = d(p) - d_safe ≥ 0`, using a parameter `ρ` to control conservativeness.
2. **Efficiency Term (`V_efficient(τ)`)**: This term (`Equation 8`) promotes efficient and smooth trajectories:
 - A smoothness penalty based on the discrete second difference of planar waypoint positions, discouraging abrupt changes in direction.
 - A safety-gated detour-ratio penalty (`L_gate_detour(τ)`). The base detour-ratio loss (`L_detour(τ)`) penalizes paths that are significantly longer than the direct chord length between start and end points. This penalty is "safety-gated" by `¯w(τ)`, an average per-step weight `w_j = σ(κ(d_j - d_min,j))`. The gate emphasizes the detour penalty when clearance is comfortably above the learned budget `d_min,j` and weakens it when the robot is operating close to the safety boundary, preventing efficiency from incentivizing risky shortcuts.
3. **Balancing Term (`V_balance(τ)`)**: This term (`Equation 9`) couples the learned budget `d_min,j` to the realized ESDF clearance `d_j`. It penalizes deviations between `d_j` and `d_min,j`, stabilizing the safety–efficiency trade-off. This term encourages `d_min,j` to be tight in narrow passages (to avoid rejecting feasible paths) and wider in open areas, while preventing the margin from collapsing below safe physical limits.

The unconstrained weight variables for the critic terms and the margin head `qη` are learned during training, with non-negative penalty weights enforced via softplus reparameterization.

**C. Selector Training (Two-stage Teacher–Student):**
The selector is trained in two stages:
1. **Teacher Training:** The safety critic (with its learnable penalty weights and margin head) is trained as a teacher. This stage uses ESDF-based supervision. A dataset of "expert" smooth paths (generated via A\* and spline interpolation) and "non-expert" challenging negative trajectories (A\* with randomized edge costs, rejected if `d < d_safe`) is constructed in 3D scenes with high-resolution ESDFs. The teacher is trained using a logistic adversarial loss (`L_scr`, Equation 12), where a calibrated discriminator head `D_ϕ(τ)` learns to assign higher probabilities to expert trajectories and lower probabilities to non-expert ones under matched start-goal conditions.
2. **Student Training:** The student selector is trained to imitate the teacher's scoring. It takes RGB-D observations and candidate trajectory tokens (ego-motion offsets) as input. The student's prediction `P(τ)` is trained to regress the teacher's scores `V_ϕ(τ)` using a mean squared error loss (`L_sel`, Equation 13). The training data for the student mixes expert sub-trajectories and diffusion-generated candidates. This distillation process allows the deployed selector to operate solely on perception data, without needing online ESDF reconstruction or map building.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world environments.

**Simulation Experiments:**
The method was benchmarked on PointGoal navigation tasks in HM3D and MP3D datasets, including cross-dataset transfer (HM3D trained, MP3D tested). It was compared against NavDP (diffusion generator with fixed critic), iPlanner (differentiable optimizer), ViPlanner (learned visual module), and PointNav (end-to-end RL).
- **HM3D:** The proposed method achieved the highest success rate (SR) of 0.783, surpassing NavDP by 7.3 percentage points and PointNav by 23.6 percentage points. It also attained the highest success weighted by path length (SPL) of 0.611.
- **MP3D:** The method maintained its leading performance with an SR of 0.680 (+5.3 over NavDP) and an SPL of 0.523.
- **Cross-dataset Transfer (HM3D→MP3D):** The method demonstrated robustness to domain shift, achieving the highest SR of 0.597 and SPL of 0.465 (+7.4 and +11.6 over NavDP, respectively).
These results indicate that the learnable critic enables both safer navigation (higher SR) and more efficient path execution (higher SPL) across varied and challenging simulated environments.

**Sensitivity to ESDF Construction and Inference Cost:**
- **ESDF Sensitivity (Table II, Panel A):** The performance of the system showed relative insensitivity to variations in ESDF construction hyperparameters (voxel size and truncation band) during training. Minor changes in these parameters resulted in small variations in SR and SPL, suggesting the learned critic is robust to reasonable ESDF quality fluctuations.
- **Inference Cost (Table II, Panel B):** The per-step inference cost for the generator and selector was measured on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 GPU. For the default K=16 candidate trajectories, the generator took approximately 56 ms/step, and the selector took 21 ms/step. These timings indicate that the system operates at a practical speed for real-time robotic deployment.

**Real-World Experiments:**
The policy was deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot with an Intel RealSense D435i camera in three real indoor scenes: an easy corridor, a medium apartment, and a hard cluttered laboratory, without any task-specific tuning.
- **Performance (Table III):** The method achieved perfect success rates (10/10) in the Corridor and Apartment environments and a high success rate (8/10) in the challenging Lab environment, outperforming all baselines (NavDP, ViPlanner, PointNav, EGO-Planner) significantly.
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 3):** Visualizations showed the policy maintained stable progress and consistent safety margins, generalizing well across varying scene difficulties.
- **Adaptability to Narrowness (Fig. 4):** Tests involving varying "Degree of Narrowness" (DoN) demonstrated the learned critic's ability to adapt. At DoN=0.75, the proposed policy successfully navigated passages by tightening its learned constraint `d_min` while maintaining clearance above the physical safety floor `d_safe`. In contrast, NavDP with a fixed `d_min` of 0.5m stalled. At DoN=0.90, both methods failed, with the proposed critic refusing to further relax `d_min` when clearance fell below a safe envelope, illustrating its balance between adaptability and a fundamental safety floor.

**Ablation Study: Safety-Critic Structure:**
An ablation study on HM3D examined the contribution of each critic component:
- **Removing `V_efficient` (Model B):** Led to a lower SPL (0.468) but a relatively higher SR (0.707) compared to removing `V_safe`. This indicated that without an explicit efficiency objective, the selector opted for overly conservative detours, causing timeouts but fewer direct failures.
- **Removing `V_balance` (Model C):** Resulted in a lower SR (0.633) but a higher SPL (0.585). This suggested that without anchoring the learned budget to realized clearance, the critic miscalibrated, leading to more direct but failure-prone selections in narrow areas.
- **Removing `V_safe` (Model A):** Achieved an SR of 0.690 and SPL of 0.572. While retaining the gate and coupling from other terms, it lacked explicit pointwise and transition-wise safety regularization, making it vulnerable in tight, near-contact situations.
The full model consistently achieved the best SR and SPL. Overlap analysis (Table V) confirmed that the full model successfully addressed a high proportion of episodes solved by ablated variants, while also solving unique, challenging episodes. These results confirm that all three terms—`V_safe`, `V_efficient`, and `V_balance`—are necessary for a well-calibrated selector that effectively balances safety and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research introduces a methodological advancement in visual navigation by addressing the limitations of fixed safety margins. By proposing a learnable, context-conditioned safety critic that adaptively adjusts clearance preferences, the work enhances robot robustness and efficiency in complex environments.

The decomposed structure of the critic—incorporating distinct safety, efficiency, and balancing terms—provides a principled framework for learning nuanced navigation behaviors. The two-stage teacher-student training procedure is significant as it leverages privileged information (ESDFs) during offline training to supervise a teacher model, which is then distilled into a perception-only student for real-time deployment without requiring online mapping or explicit geometric reconstruction. This approach streamlines the transition from simulation to reality.

The demonstrated performance gains in simulation (HM3D and MP3D) over strong baselines, coupled with successful sim-to-real transfer to a humanoid robot in cluttered real-world environments without task-specific tuning, highlight the practical utility and generalization capabilities of the proposed method. The ability of the critic to dynamically adjust its safety margin based on the "Degree of Narrowness" while adhering to a physical safety floor represents a key improvement for navigating diverse spatial constraints.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** It contributes to more reliable and efficient autonomous navigation for mobile robots in human-centric spaces, such as homes, hospitals, and industrial facilities, by reducing failures caused by miscalibrated safety margins.
- **Broader Adoption:** By improving the safety and efficiency of visual navigation systems, the method could facilitate the wider adoption of autonomous robots in various applications requiring robust interaction with complex, dynamic environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concept of learning adaptive safety margins, and the specific design of the multi-term critic, could serve as a foundation for further research in adaptive control, risk assessment, and decision-making in robotics, extending beyond visual navigation to other motion planning and control problems.
