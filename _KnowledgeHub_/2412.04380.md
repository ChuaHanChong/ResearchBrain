# EmbodiedOcc: Embodied 3D Occupancy Prediction for Vision-based Online Scene Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> EmbodiedOcc enables agents to build a global 3D occupancy map of unknown indoor environments online using only monocular RGB vision. This framework, developed at Tsinghua University, leverages 3D semantic Gaussians with a depth-aware refinement module and an explicit, confidence-based memory update mechanism, achieving 45.15% mIoU for local prediction and 42.53% mIoU for embodied global understanding on the EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet benchmark.

## Problem

- Embodied agents require a robust method for real-time, global 3D scene understanding in unknown indoor environments using low-cost, monocular vision.
- Existing 3D occupancy prediction methods are primarily offline, rely on RGB-D inputs, or are tailored for outdoor driving scenarios, lacking the ability for continuous online indoor exploration.
- Maintaining a consistent and progressively refined global scene memory from sequential monocular observations, especially when re-encountering areas, poses a significant challenge due to inherent depth ambiguity.

## Method

- **EmbodiedOcc** introduces a ==Gaussian-based framework== that uses ==3D semantic Gaussians== to represent and continuously update the global scene from streaming monocular RGB inputs.
- A ==local refinement module== processes individual RGB frames, incorporating a ==depth-aware branch== to infer structural information and integrating ==image-to-Gaussian and Gaussian-to-Gaussian== features.
- An explicit ==global Gaussian memory==, coupled with a ==confidence-based update mechanism==, integrates observations from multiple views, allowing for progressive refinement and consistent scene understanding over time.

## Results

- EmbodiedOcc achieved a **45.15% mIoU** for local 3D occupancy prediction on the Occ-ScanNet dataset, establishing state-of-the-art performance and significantly outperforming prior methods.
- The framework attained a **42.53% mIoU** for embodied global occupancy prediction on the newly introduced EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet benchmark, surpassing a strong splicing baseline (**40.74% mIoU**) and adapted driving scene methods.
- Continuous online updating demonstrated improved mIoU in 'Look-Back' evaluations (e.g., from **40.86%** to **41.17%** for K=8 frames), confirming the memory's ability to refine and maintain consistent knowledge over exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D Gaussian representations offer superior flexibility and efficiency for online, embodied 3D occupancy prediction compared to voxel-based memory, particularly in dynamic local-global interactions.
> - A dedicated depth-aware branch, leveraging monocular depth predictions, is crucial for effectively mitigating depth ambiguity and guiding accurate 3D Gaussian refinement in challenging monocular indoor settings.
> - A confidence-weighted update strategy for the global Gaussian memory ensures robust integration of new information while preserving accumulated knowledge, leading to continuous refinement and consistency across sequential observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical, yet underexplored, niche within the broader landscape of 3D scene understanding: **embodied 3D occupancy prediction for online exploration of unknown indoor scenes using only monocular RGB vision**.

The broader research landscape can be broken down as follows:

- **3D Occupancy Prediction:** This field aims to provide a comprehensive description of the surrounding environment, including both semantics (what objects are present) and structures (where objects are and what space is occupied).
 - **Offline Perception:** Most existing methods, such as MonoScene, ISO, and SCFusion, primarily focus on offline processing from a single view or a few views. These often take RGB-D inputs, or if monocular, are limited to local predictions within a fixed frustum. EmbodiedScan introduced an offline global prediction framework with sequential multi-modal inputs. These approaches, while advanced, are not suited for real-time, online exploration by an active agent.
 - **Outdoor (Driving) Scenes:** Significant progress has been made in 3D occupancy prediction for outdoor driving scenarios. Methods like TPVFormer and GaussianFormer (from the same research group) have achieved impressive results in understanding road layouts and vehicle positions. However, these methods are often optimized for specific outdoor structures, larger scales, and may not translate effectively to the fine-grained, diverse, and tightly arranged objects typical of indoor environments. Crucially, they typically operate on multi-view inputs or require specific scene priors, and their "online" aspect often refers to processing sequences for navigation, not necessarily continuous global scene memory updates from a single, embodied perspective.

- **Online 3D Scene Perception:** While some works, like Online3D, have explored online perception, they often rely on RGB-D inputs and focus on tasks like point segmentation or 3D detection rather than comprehensive 3D occupancy, which includes predicting both occupied and empty spaces. Real-time updates for embodied agents using only visual input remain a significant challenge.

- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** This recent technique has revolutionized neural rendering due to its speed and quality. Its success has led to explorations in various computer vision tasks, including scene editing, dynamic scenes, and SLAM. GaussianFormer first applied 3DGS to outdoor 3D occupancy prediction, demonstrating its potential as a scene representation. This paper builds directly on that idea but extends it to a far more challenging domain.

**The critical gap identified and addressed by this paper** is the lack of a framework that enables **embodied agents** to **progressively explore** and **online update** a **global 3D understanding** of an **unknown indoor scene** using **only monocular RGB inputs**. Current methods are either too localized, require additional sensor data (depth), are offline, or are ill-suited for the structural complexity and interaction patterns of indoor environments.

EmbodiedOcc positions itself at the intersection of these fields, combining the strengths of vision-based 3D occupancy, the efficiency of 3D Gaussian Splatting, and the demands of embodied AI. By formulating this novel task and providing a robust solution, it pioneers a new direction for enabling truly autonomous agents to build comprehensive mental models of their surroundings in real-time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the rapid advancements in embodied intelligence and active agents. These intelligent systems require sophisticated 3D scene perception capabilities to navigate, interact with, and understand their environments. The paper highlights several key motivations:

- **Mimicking Human Perception:** Humans effortlessly build a 3D understanding of unknown surroundings by progressively exploring and integrating visual information from various perspectives. Embodied AI agents aspire to replicate this fundamental human capability.
- **Low-Cost Sensors:** Vision-based perception using monocular RGB cameras is highly attractive due to their widespread availability and low cost, making them ideal for deployment on resource-constrained embodied agents. Relying solely on RGB avoids the need for expensive depth sensors (e.g., LiDAR, structured light) or complex multi-camera setups.
- **Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - Most current 3D occupancy prediction methods are designed for **offline processing** or focus on **local scene understanding** from a single or a few views, preventing continuous, global exploration.
 - Many rely on **RGB-D inputs**, which is not always practical for embodied agents.
 - Methods developed for **outdoor driving scenes** struggle with the intricate, fine-grained details and diverse object arrangements of indoor environments.
 - Existing **online perception** methods often target different tasks (e.g., object detection, point segmentation) or still require depth inputs.
- **Need for Global, Consistent Scene Understanding:** For indoor scenarios, a global understanding of the room is crucial, as agents often need to traverse and revisit areas. The perception system must maintain consistency and improve its understanding over multiple observations.

Based on these motivations, the paper sets forth the following key objectives:

1. **Formulate Embodied 3D Occupancy Prediction:** To formally define a new task where an embodied agent progressively explores an unknown 3D scene using only streaming monocular RGB inputs and continuously updates a global 3D occupancy map. This task emphasizes online, progressive, and vision-only perception.
2. **Develop a Robust Framework (EmbodiedOcc):** To propose a novel Gaussian-based framework capable of accomplishing this embodied 3D occupancy prediction task with high accuracy and efficiency.
3. **Establish an Explicit Global Memory:** To enable the agent to maintain an explicit and consistent global memory of the scene, allowing it to integrate new observations, refine existing knowledge, and improve understanding over time, similar to how humans learn from experience.
4. **Address Monocular Depth Ambiguity:** To effectively leverage monocular RGB inputs for 3D reconstruction, specifically by introducing mechanisms to infer and utilize structural information to mitigate the inherent depth ambiguity.
5. **Create a Standardized Benchmark:** To reorganize and release the "EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet" benchmark to facilitate the evaluation and comparison of future methods for the embodied 3D occupancy prediction task, fostering further research in this critical area.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EmbodiedOcc proposes a novel framework that uses 3D semantic Gaussians to represent the global scene and progressively updates this representation through continuous monocular RGB observations. The methodology is structured around two main components: a **Local Refinement Module** and an **Online Gaussian Memory Update mechanism**.

#### 3.1. Local Refinement Module

This module is responsible for processing a single monocular RGB input and refining the 3D Gaussians within the agent's current field of view (frustum).

- **Gaussian Initialization:** A set of 3D semantic Gaussians is uniformly initialized within the current camera frustum. Each Gaussian is represented by a vector containing its mean (position), scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and semantic logits. These vectors are lifted to high-dimensional feature vectors.
- **Depth-Aware Branch:** To address the monocular depth ambiguity, a critical challenge in indoor scenes, a dedicated depth-aware branch is designed.
 - It first employs a pre-trained depth prediction network (e.g., DepthAnything-V2) to generate a metric depth map from the input RGB image.
 - For each Gaussian, its mean is projected onto the image plane to obtain pixel coordinates. The predicted depth value at these coordinates, along with the Gaussian's z-component in camera coordinates, are fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP).
 - This MLP outputs a "depth-aware feature" which is then added to the Gaussian's original feature vector. This injects explicit structural information, guiding the refinement of Gaussian properties beyond just their means.
- **Feature Integration and Gaussian Refinement:**
 - **Gaussian-to-Gaussian Interaction:** Gaussian centers are voxelized, and 3D sparse convolutions are applied to allow interaction and information sharing among Gaussian features.
 - **Image-to-Gaussian Interaction:** Gaussian centers are projected onto multi-scale image feature maps extracted by an image encoder (e.g., EfficientNet-B7). Deformable cross-attention is used to integrate queried image features with the Gaussian feature vectors.
 - **Refinement:** The integrated features are used to predict update amounts (∆m, ∆s, ∆r, ∆o, ∆c) for each Gaussian's properties. These updates are applied iteratively over multiple refinement layers.
- **Gaussian-to-Voxel Splatting:** After refinement, the updated Gaussians within the frustum are converted into a local 3D occupancy prediction using a Gaussian-to-voxel splatting module, similar to GaussianFormer.

#### 3.2. Gaussian Memory Updated Online (Embodied Framework)

This component manages a global, explicit memory of 3D Gaussians for the entire scene and integrates observations from the Local Refinement Module.

- **Memory Initialization:** For a novel scene, the entire room is initialized with uniform 3D semantic Gaussians in world coordinates. Each Gaussian is assigned a `γ` tag, initially set to `0`, indicating that it has not yet been updated by an observation.
- **Memory Update Cycle:** At each time step `t`:
 1. **Load Gaussians:** A mask, derived from coordinate system transformation, selects all Gaussians from the global memory that fall within the current camera frustum. These Gaussians are transformed into camera coordinates.
 2. **Confidence Refinement:** This is a crucial aspect for maintaining consistency and integrating new information. For Gaussians loaded from memory:
 - If a Gaussian's `γ` tag is `1` (meaning it was previously updated), a confidence value `θ` (e.g., 0.5) is used. The predicted update `∆G` from the local module is scaled by `(1 - θ)` before being applied to the existing Gaussian properties. This means previously "well-updated" Gaussians are only moderately refined.
 - If a Gaussian's `γ` tag is `0` (never updated), `θ` is set to `0`, meaning the `∆G` from the current observation fully updates its properties.
 3. **Local Refinement:** The refined Gaussians are processed by the Local Refinement Module as described above.
 4. **Update Memory:** The locally refined Gaussians are transformed back into world coordinates, "detached" (to prevent gradient flow back through the entire memory in every step), and then used to update the corresponding Gaussians in the global memory. Their `γ` tags are set to `1`.
- **Stopping Mechanism:** A simple confidence ratio `α` (proportion of updated Gaussians in the global memory) is used to determine when a room has been sufficiently explored, allowing the agent to decide whether to continue exploration or move to an adjacent room.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The training is performed in two stages:
1. **Local Refinement Module Training:** Trained on the Occ-ScanNet dataset using a combination of focal loss, lovasz-softmax loss, and scene-class affinity losses (L_geo_scal and L_sem_scal), similar to previous works.
2. **EmbodiedOcc Framework Training:** The trained local module is then used to train the full embodied framework. The global memory is initialized for each scene, and the loss (calculated on the local occupancy within the frustum) is computed after each update. The goal is to ensure consistency and continuous improvement across sequential observations.

#### 3.4. EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet Benchmark

The authors reorganized the existing Occ-ScanNet and ScanNet datasets to create EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet. This dataset comprises sequences of 30 posed frames per scene, along with their corresponding global occupancy ground truth, tailored for evaluating embodied, online perception.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Occ-ScanNet and the newly introduced EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet benchmarks demonstrate the superior performance and effectiveness of the EmbodiedOcc framework.

- **Local Occupancy Prediction:**
 - EmbodiedOcc's local refinement module achieves state-of-the-art performance on the Occ-ScanNet dataset. It significantly outperforms existing methods such as MonoScene, ISO, and even adapted driving scene methods like TPVFormer, GaussianFormer, and SurroundOcc.
 - Specifically, EmbodiedOcc achieves an mIoU of **45.15%**, which is a substantial improvement over the next best method, SurroundOcc (30.83% mIoU). This strong local perception ability forms the foundation for its embodied performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of its Gaussian-based representation and depth-aware branch for fine-grained indoor structure modeling.

- **Embodied Occupancy Prediction:**
 - On the EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet dataset, designed for progressive online scene understanding, EmbodiedOcc again demonstrates superior performance in predicting the global occupancy of an explored scene.
 - EmbodiedOcc achieves an mIoU of **42.53%**, significantly outperforming the "SplicingOcc" baseline (40.74% mIoU), which simply stitches together local predictions from the best local module. This result validates the critical role of the Gaussian memory and its online update mechanism in integrating information from multiple views to build a coherent global scene understanding.
 - Adapted driving scene methods (TPVFormer, SurroundOcc, GaussianFormer) show poor embodied results (mIoU below 28%), indicating their inability to handle the continuity of observations and lack of a global memory tailored for this task.

- **Effect of Continuous Online Updating (Look-Back Evaluation):**
 - To assess the consistency and refinement capability of the memory, "Look-Back" experiments were performed, where the model revisited previously explored frames.
 - The results show that reprocessing past frames leads to improved mIoU compared to "First-Time" predictions (e.g., for K=8 frames, mIoU improved from 40.86% to 41.17%). This directly confirms that EmbodiedOcc effectively refines its knowledge and maintains consistency when re-encountering previously observed areas, rather than corrupting its accumulated understanding.

- **Analysis of Confidence Refinement:**
 - Ablation studies on the confidence value (`θ`) used in the memory update mechanism reveal that a moderate confidence (e.g., `θ=0.5` for previously updated Gaussians) yields the best embodied occupancy prediction. This highlights the importance of balancing the integration of new information from the current observation with the accumulated, stable knowledge in the global memory.

- **Analysis of Model Design (Gaussian vs. Voxel):**
 - A critical finding is the superiority of the explicit Gaussian memory over a voxel-based equivalent ("EmbodiedOcc-Voxel"). While the voxel version showed reasonable local performance, its embodied prediction significantly lagged (26.99% mIoU compared to 42.53% mIoU for Gaussian-based EmbodiedOcc). This validates the flexibility and efficiency of object-centric Gaussians for handling local-global interactions and maintaining a global scene representation in a dynamic, online setting.

- **Analysis of Depth-Aware Branch:**
 - The depth-aware branch is shown to be essential. Removing it drastically reduces both local and embodied prediction performance (e.g., embodied mIoU drops from 41.45% to 30.73%), emphasizing its critical role in mitigating depth ambiguity and guiding accurate Gaussian updates in monocular settings. The proposed depth-aware branch also outperforms a naive depth-based initialization method.

- **Gaussian Parameters and Runtime Analysis:**
 - The study confirms that appropriate Gaussian parameters (number, scale, interval) are crucial for optimal performance, balancing density and overlap to avoid holes or excessive blurring in the occupancy prediction.
 - Runtime analysis indicates that EmbodiedOcc is efficient for online operation, with the main bottlenecks being the image and depth backbone networks, suggesting potential for further speed improvements with optimized backbones.

- **Visualizations:**
 - Qualitative results visually confirm the model's ability to perform reasonable local perception and gradually achieve accurate and complete online global scene understanding, with Gaussians transitioning from random to ordered as exploration progresses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EmbodiedOcc represents a significant advancement in 3D scene understanding for embodied AI, with several key points of significance and potential impact:

- **Novel Task Formulation and Benchmark:** The paper formally defines and tackles the challenging "embodied 3D occupancy prediction" task, which is highly relevant for real-world autonomous agents. The introduction of the EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet benchmark provides a standardized platform for future research, enabling direct comparisons and fostering innovation in this critical area.
- **Pioneering Online, Vision-Only Global Perception:** EmbodiedOcc is among the first to successfully demonstrate online, progressive global 3D scene understanding using only monocular RGB inputs. This addresses a major limitation of prior work, which often relied on offline processing, multi-view setups, or additional sensor data (e.g., depth sensors).
- **Effective Integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting:** The work successfully extends the utility of 3D Gaussian Splatting from neural rendering and outdoor occupancy to the complex domain of online, embodied indoor scene understanding. The explicit Gaussian memory is a novel and effective way to leverage the properties of Gaussians for consistent local-global interaction.
- **Robustness and Consistency through Memory:** The explicit Gaussian memory with its confidence-based update mechanism is a key innovation. It allows the agent to continuously refine its understanding, maintain consistency even when revisiting previously explored areas, and resist catastrophic forgetting—a crucial requirement for long-term autonomy.
- **Addressing Monocular Challenges:** The introduction of the depth-aware branch effectively tackles the inherent depth ambiguity in monocular vision, providing vital structural cues that significantly enhance both local and global occupancy prediction accuracy.
- **Enabling More Capable Embodied AI:** By providing a comprehensive and real-time 3D understanding of unknown environments, EmbodiedOcc can have a profound impact on the development of more intelligent and autonomous embodied agents, including:
 - **Robotics:** Enhanced navigation capabilities in unknown indoor environments, enabling robots to build and update maps in real-time. Improved object manipulation and interaction through a better understanding of object semantics and 3D spatial relationships.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** More robust and consistent scene understanding for dynamic environmental mapping, enabling seamless integration of virtual content into real-world spaces.
 - **Human-Robot Interaction:** Agents with a more complete understanding of their surroundings can interact more naturally and safely with humans.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, including:
 - Exploring more sophisticated memory architectures and update strategies.
 - Integrating active perception strategies to guide exploration efficiently.
 - Extending the framework to dynamic indoor environments with moving objects.
 - Investigating tighter integration of monocular depth prediction with Gaussian refinement.
 - Scaling the approach to even larger environments and longer-term scene memory.

In conclusion, EmbodiedOcc marks a significant step forward in vision-based 3D scene understanding for embodied agents. By pioneering a new task, developing a highly effective Gaussian-based framework, and providing a robust benchmark, this research paves the way for truly adaptive and intelligent systems capable of perceiving and interacting with complex, unknown environments using minimal sensor inputs.
