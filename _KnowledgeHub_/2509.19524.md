# Score the Steps, Not Just the Goal: VLM-Based Subgoal Evaluation for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces StepEval, a framework that automates the granular evaluation of multi-step robotic manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as judges. This work promotes a shift from single binary task success rates to diagnostic vectors of per-subgoal success rates, aiming to provide detailed insights and accelerate policy development.

## Problem

- Robot learning often relies on a single binary success rate for complex, multi-step manipulation tasks, providing insufficient diagnostic information.
- Manually labeling subgoal successes for detailed evaluation is labor-intensive, subjective, and impractical for large-scale experiments.
- Lack of standardized, granular evaluation hinders effective debugging, iterative policy improvement, and meaningful comparison of robotic policies.

## Method

- Proposes **StepEval**, a modular and ==cost-aware framework== that employs off-the-shelf ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== as black-box, automated judges.
- Utilizes VLMs to infer ==subgoal outcomes== directly from recorded visual trajectories (images or videos) in a ==zero-shot or few-shot== manner.
- Outlines a ==prompting strategy== where VLMs receive task subgoals and visual context, generating a ==binary success vector== for all subgoals in a trajectory.

## Results

- Conception and a detailed architectural blueprint for the StepEval framework, designed for extensibility and community contributions.
- Formal definition of a trajectory-level vector of per-subgoal success rates as the primary output for robotic manipulation task evaluation.
- A four-step plug-and-play workflow that enables researchers to integrate automated VLM-based subgoal evaluation into their existing robot learning pipelines with minimal effort.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "subgoal-first" evaluation, providing a vector of per-subgoal success rates, offers superior diagnostic insights into policy performance compared to a single task-level metric.
> - Existing Vision-Language Models can serve as effective, model-agnostic, and post-hoc judges for robotic subgoal completion without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
> - The framework benefits from separating primary evaluation metrics (subgoal success rates) from optional diagnostic metrics (cost, latency, judge accuracy) used for framework optimization.
