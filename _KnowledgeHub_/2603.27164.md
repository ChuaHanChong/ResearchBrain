# daVinci-LLM:Towards the Science of Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> The daVinci-LLM project from SII, SJTU, and GAIR developed a 3-billion-parameter language model using a fully-open, scientific approach to pretraining, achieving an overall average score of 51.72 which is comparable to 7-billion-parameter models like OLMo-3, and demonstrating superior performance in reasoning tasks such as MATH (62.80). This was accomplished through a hierarchical 'Data Darwinism' framework for data processing and a two-stage adaptive training curriculum.

## Problem

- The fundamental pretraining phase for large language models remains significantly under-explored due to a 'structural paradox' where well-resourced commercial entities lack transparency, while academic efforts with research freedom face computational limitations.
- Existing LLM development paradigms lack systematic, evidence-based methodologies for pretraining data processing, training dynamics, and design rationales, hindering scientific understanding and reproducibility.
- Post-training techniques are limited in overcoming foundational capability ceilings set during pretraining, underscoring the need for deeper scientific investigation into this initial phase.

## Method

- A fully-open research paradigm combines industrial-scale computational resources with complete research freedom to systematically investigate the foundational pretraining phase of large language models.
- A 3.09 billion-parameter **Qwen2**-based transformer model is trained using a ==Data Darwinism framework== (L0-L9 taxonomy) for ==hierarchical data curation== and a ==two-stage adaptive curriculum== spanning 8 trillion tokens.
- Comprehensive disclosure of model architecture, data processing pipelines, training processes, and over 200 controlled ablations are provided to advance pretraining as a scientific discipline.

## Results

- **daVinci-LLM-3B** achieved an overall average score of **51.72**, making it comparable to the 7-billion-parameter **OLMo-3** model and outperforming other parameter-matched baselines such as LLaMa-3.2-3B (**37.58**) and Qwen-2.5-3B (**51.44**).
- The model demonstrated particularly strong performance in reasoning-intensive domains, scoring **62.80** on MATH (over **23 points** higher than OLMo-3 7B) and **55.99** in average code generation, comparable to the larger OLMo-3 7B.
- Ablation studies confirmed that L4 generative refinement improved MATH by **7.00 points**, and L5 cognitive completion with synthetic QA data effectively steered targeted capability development in code and science reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical data processing, from model-based filtering (L3) to generative refinement (L4) and cognitive completion (L5), systematically enhances model capabilities, with L5 synthesis acting as a high-precision tool for targeted development.
> - Adaptive training curricula are essential, as general knowledge saturates faster than reasoning abilities, necessitating shifts from rebalancing existing text data to introducing fundamentally new data formats like structured QA.
> - Data mixture design benefits from internal balance between reasoning domains and progressive intensification of structured QA, preventing over-specialization while enabling further capability gains across training stages.
