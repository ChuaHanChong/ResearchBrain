# AutoQD: Automatic Discovery of Diverse Behaviors with Quality-Diversity Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper "AUTOQD" from the University of Southern California introduces an iterative method for automatically generating behavioral descriptors in Quality-Diversity optimization, which enables the discovery of diverse, high-performing policies in continuous control tasks. This approach leverages policy occupancy measures and random Fourier features to create stable behavioral representations, demonstrating superior performance in policy discovery and adaptability to environmental changes across various Gymnasium environments.

## Problem

- Traditional Quality-Diversity (QD) optimization relies on manually designed behavioral descriptors (BDs), requiring extensive domain expertise and limiting the scope of discoverable behaviors.
- Developing effective BDs becomes increasingly challenging with task complexity, creating a bottleneck for applying QD algorithms.
- Prior unsupervised behavior discovery methods often lack theoretical grounding for their representations or struggle with scalability when generating a large number of diverse policies.

## Method

- **AutoQD** efficiently embeds policy occupancy measures into a finite-dimensional vector space using Random Fourier Features (RFFs), where Euclidean distances reliably approximate Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between behaviors.
- An iterative algorithm alternates between discovering diverse policies using a QD optimizer (CMA-MAE) and refining low-dimensional behavioral descriptors via Calibrated Weighted PCA (cwPCA) applied to the archive of policy embeddings.
- The cwPCA dynamically weights policies by their fitness, ensuring descriptors focus on variations among high-quality behaviors, and calibrates projections to a fixed range for grid-based archives.

## Results

- AutoQD consistently achieved superior Ground-Truth QD Scores compared to five baselines in environments such as Ant, Swimmer, and BipedalWalker, often producing the most diverse policy populations.
- Generated policy populations demonstrated enhanced adaptability to environmental dynamics in BipedalWalker, achieving the highest Area Under the Curve for both friction (**1429.66**) and mass (**295.65**) changes.
- The automatically learned behavioral descriptors exhibited high stability, with variance often orders of magnitude lower than autoencoder-based methods, and maintained competitive performance even with as few as two rollouts per policy for BD estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policy occupancy measures provide a complete and theoretically sound characterization of policy behavior in fully-observable Markov Decision Processes.
> - Random Fourier Features offer a computationally tractable way to approximate kernel-based distances (MMD) between high-dimensional occupancy measures, enabling verifiable policy embeddings.
> - An adaptive and iterative refinement of behavioral descriptors, learned from the evolving archive of policies, facilitates open-ended discovery of meaningful diversity without requiring predefined human specifications.
