# VLMPC: Vision-Language Model Predictive Control for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VLMPC integrates vision-language models with model predictive control to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. The framework from Shandong University demonstrates improved success rates on standard benchmarks and can handle novel objects in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Traditional MPC implementations often lack robust environmental perception and struggle with complex real-world dynamics.
- Many VLM-based methods for robotics make decisions based on current observations without forecasting future states.
- Existing manipulation approaches frequently rely on predefined task primitives, limiting their adaptability to open-set challenges and novel objects.

## Method

- The **VLMPC** framework integrates conditional action sampling, action-conditioned video prediction, and a hierarchical cost function within an iterative ==MPC== loop.
- Conditional action sampling uses a VLM to generate candidate action sequences, refined by historical action information to balance VLM reasoning with past robot actions.
- An action-conditioned video prediction model (DMVFN-Act) forecasts future video frames for each candidate action sequence, allowing the robot to visualize potential outcomes.

## Results

- VLMPC achieved an average success rate of **78%** across tasks on the RoboDesk benchmark, outperforming the state-of-the-art VP² method (**64%**).
- It demonstrated improved performance in complex, multi-step tasks such as "push flat" (**41%** vs. **13%** for VP²) and "open drawer" (**59%** vs. **25%** for VP²).
- Real-world experiments with a UR5 robotic arm showed capabilities in multi-step task planning, interference avoidance, and handling novel objects not seen during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining vision-language models (VLMs) for reasoning with Model Predictive Control (MPC) for forward-looking planning enables robots to adapt to unstructured environments.
> - A hierarchical cost function, integrating pixel-level differences and VLM-assisted semantic understanding, allows for robust evaluation of predicted actions.
> - Integrating historical action information with VLM suggestions helps mitigate potential VLM hallucinations during action sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in unstructured environments remains a significant challenge in robotics research. While robots excel in controlled settings with predefined tasks, they often struggle when faced with novel objects or complex scenarios not encountered during training. The paper "VLMPC: Vision-Language Model Predictive Control for Robotic Manipulation" introduces an innovative approach that combines the reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) with the forward-looking planning of Model Predictive Control (MPC).

Developed by researchers from Shandong University, VLMPC addresses a fundamental limitation in robotic manipulation: the ability to reason about and interact with objects in open-world environments. By leveraging the perceptual understanding of VLMs and integrating it with predictive control, the framework enables robots to perform complex manipulation tasks with greater adaptability and robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Traditional approaches to robotic manipulation face several limitations:

1. **Lack of Environmental Perception**: Standard MPC implementations rely on predefined dynamics models that often fail to account for the complexity of real-world environments.

2. **Limited Forward Planning**: Many VLM-based methods in robotics make decisions based only on current observations without forecasting future states.

3. **Predefined Primitives**: Existing approaches often require manual design of task-specific primitives, limiting adaptability to new scenarios.

4. **Open-Set Challenges**: Robots frequently encounter objects and environments not seen during training, requiring flexible reasoning capabilities.

VLMPC draws inspiration from human problem-solving, where we "look before we leap" - considering future consequences before taking action. By integrating VLMs' perceptual and reasoning capabilities with MPC's forward-looking planning, VLMPC aims to bridge these gaps and enable more adaptive, generalizable robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLMPC framework consists of three key components:

1. **Conditional Action Sampling**: Generates candidate action sequences based on VLM analysis of the current scene and goal.

2. **Action-Conditioned Video Prediction**: Predicts future video frames resulting from candidate actions.

3. **Hierarchical Cost Function**: Evaluates predicted outcomes using both pixel-level metrics and VLM-assisted semantic understanding.

These components work together in an iterative loop, allowing the robot to continuously replan as it receives new observations. The framework can accept either a goal image or a language instruction as input, providing flexibility in how tasks are specified.

#### 3.1. Conditional Action Sampling

The conditional action sampling module generates candidate action sequences by leveraging the perceptual and reasoning capabilities of VLMs. When presented with a current observation and a goal (either as an image or language instruction), the VLM analyzes the scene and generates a distribution of possible actions.

The module operates as follows:

1. The VLM receives the current observation and goal, producing a suggested action distribution.

2. To mitigate potential VLM hallucinations, the framework incorporates historical information from previous actions through global mean pooling.

3. The final action distribution is derived by combining the VLM's suggestion with historical information:

 ```
   μt = α · μt^VLM + (1-α) · μt^sub
   ```

 where μt^VLM is the VLM's suggested action, μt^sub is derived from historical actions, and α is a weighting factor.

4. Multiple candidate action sequences are sampled from this distribution for evaluation.

This approach balances the VLM's reasoning capabilities with the robot's action history, reducing the impact of potential hallucinations while maintaining adaptability.

#### 3.2. Action-Conditioned Video Prediction

To evaluate the potential outcomes of candidate actions, VLMPC employs an action-conditioned video prediction model called DMVFN-Act (Dynamic Multi-Scale Voxel Flow Network with Action conditioning). This model is a modified version of DMVFN, enhanced to incorporate robotic actions as input.

The video prediction process works as follows:

1. The model takes as input:
 - Past two observation frames (Ot-1, Ot)
 - A candidate action sequence (at+1^n, at+2^n..., at+T^n)

2. For each action in the sequence, the model predicts the next observation frame.

3. This prediction continues iteratively, using previously predicted frames as input for subsequent predictions.

The result is a sequence of predicted future frames (Ôt+1^n, Ôt+2^n..., Ôt+T^n) for each candidate action sequence. These predictions allow the robot to "visualize" the potential consequences of different actions before execution.

#### 3.3. Hierarchical Cost Function

VLMPC evaluates the candidate action sequences using a hierarchical cost function that combines low-level pixel metrics with high-level semantic understanding. This dual approach allows for both precise alignment with goal states and reasoning about objects and their relationships.

The hierarchical cost function consists of two components:

1. **Pixel Distance Cost**: Measures the pixel-level difference between predicted future frames and the goal image.

2. **VLM-Assisted Cost**: Utilizes a VLM to identify sub-goals and interference objects in the predicted frames.

The VLM-assisted cost encourages the robot to:
- Move toward identified sub-goals
- Avoid collisions with interference objects
- Maintain appropriate gripper states (open/closed) based on the task

A "VLM Switcher" dynamically selects which cost components to use based on the current scenario, enabling the framework to adapt to different tasks. The mathematical representation of the cost function is:

```
J(St^n) = λ1 · Jpixel(St^n) + λ2 · JVLM(St^n)
```

where λ1 and λ2 are weighting factors determined by the VLM Switcher.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VLMPC was evaluated on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks and demonstrated superior performance compared to existing methods. On the RoboDesk benchmark, which includes tasks such as pressing buttons, opening drawers, and pushing objects, VLMPC consistently outperformed the state-of-the-art VP² method.

Key findings from the benchmark evaluation include:

- VLMPC achieved an average success rate of 78% across all tasks, compared to 64% for VP².
- The most significant improvements were observed in complex tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, such as "push flat" (41% vs. 13%) and "open drawer" (59% vs. 25%).
- Even in simpler tasks like button pressing, VLMPC showed modest but consistent improvements over the baseline.

These results demonstrate the effectiveness of integrating VLM reasoning with MPC planning, particularly for tasks requiring complex manipulation sequences and environmental understanding.

#### 4.1. Real-World Applications

Beyond simulation benchmarks, VLMPC was tested in real-world scenarios using a Universal Robots UR5 robotic arm. The framework demonstrated the ability to perform various manipulation tasks with novel objects not seen during training.

Notable real-world capabilities include:

1. **Multi-step Task Planning**: Successfully breaking down complex tasks into logical sequences of sub-goals.

2. **Interference Avoidance**: Identifying and avoiding potential collisions with obstacles in the workspace.

3. **Novel Object Handling**: Generalizing manipulation strategies to previously unseen objects based on visual reasoning.

4. **Adaptive Behavior**: Dynamically adjusting plans when faced with unexpected situations or environmental changes.

The real-world experiments validate VLMPC's practical applicability beyond controlled environments, demonstrating its potential for deployment in various domains including manufacturing, healthcare, and household robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLMPC represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by bridging the gap between VLMs' perception capabilities and MPC's forward-looking planning. By enabling robots to reason about novel objects and environments while planning multiple steps ahead, the framework addresses key limitations in existing approaches.

The experimental results demonstrate VLMPC's superior performance on benchmark tasks and its effectiveness in real-world applications with unseen objects. The framework's ability to break down complex tasks into meaningful sub-goals, avoid obstacles, and adapt to new scenarios highlights its potential for advancing robotic manipulation in unstructured environments.

As foundation models continue to evolve, approaches like VLMPC that effectively integrate these models into control frameworks will play an increasingly important role in developing more capable, adaptable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its promising results, VLMPC faces several limitations and areas for improvement:

1. **Video Prediction Accuracy**: The mismatch between predicted video frames and actual outcomes can impact planning effectiveness, especially for longer-horizon tasks.

2. **Computational Efficiency**: The integration of VLMs within the MPC loop introduces significant computational overhead, potentially limiting real-time performance.

3. **Constrained Motion Planning**: Tasks requiring highly precise movements in constrained spaces remain challenging for the current implementation.

Future research directions include:

- Improving the video prediction model's accuracy for longer prediction horizons
- Optimizing the VLM integration for reduced computational load
- Extending the framework to handle more complex manipulation constraints
- Incorporating diverse training data to enhance generalization capabilities
