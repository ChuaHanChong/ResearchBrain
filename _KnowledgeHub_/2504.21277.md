# Reinforced MLLM: A Survey on RL-Based Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper provides the first comprehensive survey on the application of Reinforcement Learning to enhance reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models. It systematically reviews current algorithmic designs, reward mechanisms, and applications, highlighting how RL enables more robust and adaptive reasoning while identifying key challenges such as text-centric biases in multimodal reasoning paths and the need for more sophisticated reward designs.

## Problem

- Existing surveys on MLLM reasoning or RL in LLMs did not specifically address the rapidly growing intersection of RL and reasoning in multimodal contexts.
- MLLMs struggled to achieve robust and sophisticated reasoning across diverse modalities (vision, audio, video) due to text-centric biases and limited, simple rule-based reward functions.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods for MLLMs exhibited limitations in generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data and could suffer from catastrophic forgetting.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive and systematic review of the field of ==RL-based reasoning in MLLMs==, categorizing algorithmic designs, reward mechanisms, and applications.
- It analyzes core RL algorithmic designs in LLMs/MLLMs, classifying them into ==value-model-free and value-model-based methods== and discussing their respective strengths and weaknesses.
- The survey examines algorithmic frameworks and ==reward function designs for multimodal perception==, detailing strategies for integrating multiple modalities and surveying relevant benchmark datasets.

## Results

- The survey found that RL effectively activates powerful reasoning capabilities in MLLMs, evidenced by the successful transfer of the R1 training paradigm to multimodal tasks.
- Value-model-free RL methods like GRPO dominate current RL-based MLLM reasoning approaches due to their simplicity and robustness, while value-model-based methods are gaining traction with new optimizations.
- Current reward mechanisms are largely outcome-supervised, with a growing trend towards cross-modal interaction and process-supervised rewards, yet significant challenges remain in addressing sparse rewards and promoting truly multimodal reasoning trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) is crucial for cultivating abstract, adaptive, and robust reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), complementing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) which primarily imbues specific knowledge.
> - The 'R1 training paradigm', originating from LLMs, has become a foundational approach for enhancing reasoning in MLLMs, primarily relying on verifiable outcome-based rewards.
> - Current RL-based MLLM reasoning methods largely generate text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and struggle with sparse rewards, leading to inefficient reasoning trajectories and underutilization of true cross-modal interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper positions itself within the burgeoning and highly dynamic intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on how RL can enhance the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-3 and LLaMA has revolutionized AI, showcasing unprecedented capabilities in language understanding and generation, particularly through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. MLLMs represent the next frontier, extending LLMs to process and reason across diverse modalities such as vision, audio, and video. While MLLMs can handle various inputs, robust and sophisticated reasoning across these modalities remains a significant challenge, especially for complex, multi-step tasks.

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has played a pivotal role in aligning LLMs with human preferences, significantly improving their coherence and utility (e.g., in ChatGPT). More recently, research such as DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K1.5 has demonstrated that RL can directly incentivize and "activate" complex reasoning capabilities in LLMs, often outperforming traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in generalization and self-correction. This paradigm, termed "R1 training paradigm" in the paper, allows LLMs to develop sophisticated reasoning without extensive, labor-intensive CoT annotations.

The core contribution of this survey is to bridge a significant gap in the existing literature: while many surveys cover MLLM reasoning or RLHF in LLMs, none specifically address the specialized and rapidly growing area of **RL-based reasoning for MLLMs**. The paper highlights that current RL-based methods in MLLMs often suffer from limitations inherited from their text-centric LLM predecessors, neglecting the unique challenges and opportunities presented by true multimodal interaction. For instance, most generated reasoning trajectories remain text-based, overlooking the critical role of visual or other modalities in multimodal scenarios. Moreover, current RL-based reasoning often relies on simple rule-based reward functions that require verifiable answers, limiting applicability to broader general-scenario problems.

By systematically reviewing algorithmic designs, reward mechanisms, and applications within this specific niche, the paper provides a much-needed structured guide. It articulates the transition from LLM-centric RL paradigms (like PPO, GRPO) to MLLM-specific adaptations, emphasizing the unique considerations for multimodal reasoning, such as cross-modal alignment and dynamic understanding. The work underscores the principle that "SFT for memory, RL for generalization," asserting that RL is essential for cultivating adaptive and robust reasoning in complex, uncertain multimodal environments. This survey therefore serves as a foundational reference for researchers aiming to push the boundaries of MLLM capabilities through advanced RL techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Reinforced MLLM: A Survey on RL-Based Reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models" is to provide a **comprehensive, systematic, and structured guide** to the rapidly advancing field of RL-based reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

The authors are motivated by several key observations and challenges in the current research landscape:
1. **Rapid Progress and Fragmentation:** The application of reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance MLLM reasoning is a rapidly advancing area, leading to a proliferation of methods and findings. Without a structured overview, researchers may struggle to identify effective approaches and understand the overarching trends.
2. **Gap in Existing Surveys:** While surveys exist for MLLM reasoning and RL in LLMs, there is a distinct lack of dedicated reviews specifically addressing the intersection of RL and reasoning in multimodal contexts. This survey aims to fill this critical void.
3. **Challenges in Multimodal Reasoning:** MLLMs extend LLMs to handle diverse modalities, but enabling robust reasoning across these varied inputs (vision, audio, video) remains challenging. Issues include:
 - **Text-centric Bias:** Many current RL-based reasoning methods for MLLMs are primarily adapted from NLP, resulting in reasoning trajectories that are predominantly textual, neglecting the crucial role and unique characteristics of other modalities in multimodal scenarios (MM-CoT).
 - **Limited Reward Mechanisms:** Current RL-based reasoning methods often rely on simple, rule-based reward functions that require objectively verifiable answers (e.g., correct/incorrect), overlooking broader, general-scenario problems where such explicit ground truth is unavailable or difficult to define. This leads to issues like sparse rewards and insufficient guidance for intermediate reasoning steps.
 - **Inefficient Reasoning Trajectories:** The lack of effective reasoning path rewards can lead to MLLMs generating excessive redundant tokens or "overthinking" in their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) outputs, hindering efficiency and interpretability.
4. **Need for Generalization:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods, while effective for specific tasks and precise knowledge injection, tend to cause "catastrophic forgetting" and exhibit limited generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) data. RL, in contrast, shows promise for fostering more adaptive and robust reasoning.

In light of these motivations, the authors set out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Systematic Review:** Provide a structured analysis of technological advancements, methodologies, practical applications, and future directions in RL-based reasoning for MLLMs.
- **Categorization of RL Designs:** Analyze core RL algorithmic designs in LLMs/MLLMs, including value-model-free and value-model-based methods, discussing their strengths, weaknesses, and optimization opportunities.
- **Analysis of Reasoning Methods:** Examine algorithmic frameworks, the design of reward functions (accuracy-oriented and structure-oriented), and strategies for integrating multiple modalities, highlighting their contributions to multimodal reasoning challenges.
- **Benchmark Overview:** Survey relevant benchmark datasets and evaluation protocols for reasoning tasks, including their construction and domain coverage (math, science, spatial, interaction-based).
- **Identification of Limitations and Future Directions:** Highlight unresolved challenges (sparse rewards, inefficient trajectories, cross-modal coordination) and propose concrete future research avenues (hierarchical reward modeling, vision-guided CoT, lightweight RL frameworks) to foster innovation.

Ultimately, the paper aims to equip researchers with a comprehensive framework to navigate the complexities of RL-based multimodal reasoning, thereby promoting further innovation and progress in this dynamic field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the authors' "methodology" is centered on a structured and systematic review of existing literature, categorizing and analyzing the state-of-the-art in RL-based reasoning for MLLMs. Their approach involves:

1. **Defining the Scope:** The survey clearly defines its focus on the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically for enhancing reasoning capabilities. It distinguishes itself from broader surveys on MLLM reasoning or RL in text-only LLMs.

2. **Laying the Background:** The paper begins by establishing foundational knowledge.
 - **LLMs and CoT:** It introduces the rise of LLMs, their instruction-following and few-shot learning capabilities, and the importance of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for complex tasks.
 - **MLLMs and MM-CoT:** It then transitions to MLLMs, explaining how they extend LLMs to handle diverse modalities (vision, audio, video) and the concept of Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MM-CoT), differentiating between text-only and truly multimodal CoT.
 - **Reinforcement Learning Fundamentals:** A concise overview of RL is provided, focusing on key policy optimization methods (PPO, RLOO, GRPO), common reward mechanisms (Outcome Reward Mechanisms - ORM, and Process Reward Mechanisms - PRM), and crucial aspects of training efficiency (curriculum RL, data-efficient learning).

3. **Categorization and Analysis of RL Algorithms:**
 - **Core Designs:** The authors classify RL methods used in LLMs/MLLMs into two main paradigms:
 - **Value-Model-Free Methods:** These approaches (e.g., GRPO, DAPO, Dr.GRPO, CPPO, GPG) eliminate the need for a separate value model, often deriving advantages from trajectory-level rewards or group comparisons. The survey details specific innovations within these methods (e.g., asymmetric clipping, dynamic sampling, removal of normalization terms, completion pruning) aimed at improving diversity, efficiency, and stability.
 - **Value-Model-Based Methods:** Exemplified by PPO (e.g., ORZ, VC-PPO, VAPO), these methods learn a value model for precise, step-wise credit assignment. The survey discusses advancements like value pretraining, Decoupled-GAE, Length-Adaptive GAE, and positive example LM loss to address challenges in long-chain reasoning and sparse rewards.

4. **Deep Dive into RL-Based MLLM Reasoning:**
 - **RL Training Paradigm Transfer:** The paper traces the transfer of the successful DeepSeek R1 "R1 training paradigm" (RL with Verifiable Reward - RLVR) from LLMs to MLLMs. It differentiates between "R1-Zero" (large-scale RL on base models) and "R1" (incorporating cold-start SFT) and discusses its application to various modalities beyond images (video, audio).
 - **Reward Mechanism Design for Multimodal Perception:** This section is a crucial part of the survey, categorizing multimodal reward strategies:
 - **Outcome-supervised Reward Mechanisms:** These focus on the final correctness of the output and include task-oriented rewards (e.g., IoU for detection, pixel-level metrics for segmentation), cross-modal interaction rewards (e.g., combining visual and semantic accuracy, temporal consistency), and curriculum-based rewards that adapt to task difficulty.
 - **Process-supervised Reward Mechanisms:** These aim to provide finer-grained feedback on intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., evaluating logical coherence, completeness of reasoning chains), addressing the sparse reward problem.
 - **Training Efficiency and Stability:** The survey examines techniques specifically adapted for MLLMs, such as curriculum learning (progressively increasing task difficulty), sample efficiency optimization (prioritized sampling, high-quality samples, two-stage training), and strategies to mitigate catastrophic forgetting (KL regularization, rejected sample-based self-improvement).

5. **Applications and Benchmarks:**
 - **Applications:** The authors outline diverse application areas where RL-based MLLMs show promise, including Embodied AI (e.g., 3D scene understanding, video reasoning), MLLM Agentic Systems (e.g., GUI-based task execution), and Domain-Specific Applications (e.g., medical imaging, human-centered interaction).
 - **Datasets and Benchmarks:** A comprehensive overview of existing benchmarks is provided, categorized by reasoning over structured knowledge (mathematical, chart, scientific, code generation, algorithmic, socio-cultural), contextual and perceptual reasoning in real-world scenarios (action prediction, spatial reasoning), and generalization/multi-task reasoning (multi-image inductive reasoning, composite benchmarks). The performance of various RL-based MLLMs on these benchmarks is summarized.

6. **Identification of Limitations and Future Directions:** The survey concludes by critically assessing current limitations (sparse rewards, evaluation paradigms, lack of real-time adaptivity) and underlying challenges (multimodal alignment, non-Markovian dependencies, training-inference mismatch). Based on this analysis, concrete future research directions are proposed, such as unified hierarchical reward frameworks, cross-modal reward generalization, scalable optimization, interactive reinforcement, and embodied environments.

By employing this systematic approach, the authors provide a structured overview that not only summarizes existing work but also critically analyzes its strengths and weaknesses, highlighting key trends and charting a path for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents several key findings and observations regarding the state and trends of RL-based reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):

1. **RL as a Key Enabler for Reasoning Generalization:** The paper strongly asserts the principle "SFT for memory, RL for generalization." While supervised fine-tuning (SFT) excels at imbuing models with specific knowledge and precise cognitive templates, reinforcement learning (RL) is crucial for cultivating abstract, adaptive, and robust reasoning capabilities, especially in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. This is evidenced by models like DeepSeek R1, which demonstrates that RL can elicit complex reasoning processes without explicit CoT supervision.

2. **Influence of the R1 Training Paradigm:** The "R1 training paradigm," originating from DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K1.5 in LLMs, has become a foundational approach for enhancing reasoning in MLLMs. This paradigm primarily relies on verifiable outcome-based rewards (accuracy + format adherence) and is shown to successfully transfer characteristics like "Aha Moments" and self-reflection to multimodal tasks (e.g., MedVLM-R1, VisualThinker-R1-Zero, MM-Eureka).

3. **Dominance of Value-Model-Free RL:** A significant portion of current RL-based MLLM reasoning models, particularly those leveraging the R1 paradigm, rely on value-model-free methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (as seen in MedVLM-R1, Visual-RFT, Vision-R1, Seg-Zero, etc.). These methods are favored for their implementation simplicity and robustness, especially in complex tasks involving long reasoning chains, as they avoid the computational demands of training a separate critic model. However, the survey also notes their inherent limitations, such as entropy collapse and reward noise, which recent optimizations like DAPO and Dr.GRPO attempt to address.

4. **Emerging Value-Model-Based Optimizations:** While less prevalent, value-model-based approaches like PPO are gaining traction, especially with new optimizations (ORZ, VC-PPO, VAPO). These advancements aim to overcome PPO's challenges in long-chain reasoning by addressing value initialization bias, refining advantage estimation (Decoupled-GAE, Length-Adaptive GAE), and improving sample efficiency, leading to enhanced stability and performance.

5. **Evolution of Reward Mechanisms:**
 - **Outcome-Supervised Rewards Dominate:** Most current implementations continue to use outcome-supervised reward mechanisms, primarily binary correct/incorrect signals for the final answer, often combined with format adherence (Equation 17 from DeepSeek R1).
 - **Transition Towards Cross-Modal Interaction and Process Supervision:** There's a growing trend towards more sophisticated reward designs. Some methods (e.g., Visual-RFT, Seg-Zero, R1-SGG) incorporate task-specific visual metrics (IoU, L1 distance) or structured output validation. Crucially, methods like R1-VL and ChestX-Reasoner are moving towards "process-supervised" rewards that evaluate intermediate reasoning steps for logical coherence and factual accuracy, attempting to mitigate sparse reward signals and guide better reasoning trajectories.
 - **Multi-Tier and Curriculum-Based Rewards:** Several studies (e.g., MetaSpatial, GFlowVLM, Curr-ReFT, Embodied-R) integrate multi-tier, hierarchical, or curriculum-based reward structures to handle increasing complexity, incorporate physics-based constraints, or guide learning from simple to complex tasks.

6. **Challenges in Multimodal Integration and Reasoning Trajectories:**
 - **Text-Centric MM-CoT:** A significant finding is that current RL-based reasoning methods for MLLMs, despite processing multimodal inputs, predominantly generate text-only CoT. They often neglect the integration or generation of multimodal signals within the reasoning process itself, limiting true cross-modal reasoning.
 - **Overthinking and Redundancy:** The sparsity of rewards often leads to models generating excessively long or redundant reasoning paths, highlighting the "temporal credit assignment problem."
 - **Modality Bias:** The design of rewards and reasoning often leans towards linguistic biases, failing to fully leverage or accurately incorporate information from other modalities.

7. **Benchmark Limitations:** The performance summary (Table 7) reveals a concentration of evaluation in structured knowledge domains (e.g., mathematical, scientific, chart reasoning), where answers are verifiable. While progress is evident (e.g., VL-Rethinker-72B achieving 80.3 on MathVista), this focus leaves open questions about generalization to more open-ended, perceptually rich, and interactive real-world scenarios. Underrepresented areas include dynamic environments, spatial abstraction, and socio-cultural reasoning.

8. **Emerging Applications:** RL-based MLLMs are expanding into critical application areas like Embodied AI (e.g., 3D scene generation, video spatial reasoning), MLLM Agentic Systems (e.g., GUI control), and specialized domains like medical diagnosis and human-centered AI (emotion recognition, audio reasoning), demonstrating their potential beyond traditional VQA.

In essence, the survey concludes that while RL has demonstrably activated powerful reasoning capabilities in MLLMs and enabled significant progress, the field is still in its early stages regarding truly multimodal, adaptable, and efficient reasoning processes, particularly due to limitations in reward design and full cross-modal integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and has the potential for considerable impact across several dimensions of AI research and development:

1. **Consolidating a Nascent Field:** The most immediate impact of this survey is its role in consolidating and structuring knowledge in the rapidly evolving, yet previously fragmented, field of RL-based reasoning in MLLMs. By being the first dedicated review of this specific intersection, it provides a much-needed comprehensive reference point for researchers, helping them navigate the diverse methodologies, algorithms, and applications. This consolidation can prevent redundant efforts and foster more directed research.

2. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The paper's systematic identification of limitations, challenges, and concrete future directions is invaluable.
 - **Addressing Sparse Rewards:** By highlighting the pervasive issue of sparse and delayed reward signals, the survey calls for the development of more sophisticated, unified, and hierarchical reward frameworks that capture not just final outcomes but also the quality and fidelity of intermediate reasoning steps across modalities. This could lead to breakthroughs in more efficient and interpretable model training.
 - **Promoting True Multimodal CoT:** The critique that most RL-based MLLMs still largely rely on text-centric reasoning paths, neglecting intrinsic multimodal interactions, will likely spur research into truly vision-guided (or audio-guided) CoT mechanisms and dynamic cross-modal reasoning generation. This is crucial for unlocking the full potential of MLLMs in rich, real-world environments.
 - **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** By emphasizing RL's role in generalization over SFT's memory function, the survey encourages the development of more robust MLLMs that can perform effectively on out-of-distribution (OOD) data and adapt to unpredictable real-world scenarios. This includes exploring lightweight and scalable RL frameworks to make these models more deployable.
 - **Fostering Interactive and Embodied AI:** The call for integrating real-time user feedback and deploying MLLMs in spatially grounded, embodied environments (robotics, AR/VR) points towards a future where MLLMs are not just static knowledge systems but interactive, adaptive agents that can learn and refine their reasoning in dynamic settings.

3. **Facilitating Cross-Domain Knowledge Transfer:** By categorizing various RL algorithms (value-model-free vs. value-model-based) and their specific optimizations (e.g., DAPO, VC-PPO, VAPO), the survey enables researchers to better understand the trade-offs in efficiency, stability, and performance. This deep dive into algorithmic nuances can facilitate the transfer of successful RL techniques from one domain (e.g., LLMs) to another (e.g., MLLMs) and inspire novel hybrid approaches.

4. **Informing Benchmark Development:** The extensive overview of datasets and benchmarks provides a critical assessment of current evaluation paradigms, revealing a bias towards structured, verifiable tasks. This finding will likely motivate the creation of more diverse and challenging benchmarks that cover interactive, spatial, temporal, and socio-cultural reasoning, which are vital for assessing real-world MLLM capabilities.

5. **Accelerating Innovation and Application:** By providing a clear roadmap, the survey empowers researchers and practitioners to more effectively identify relevant methods and promising avenues. This can accelerate the development of next-generation MLLMs with enhanced reasoning, leading to more intelligent AI assistants, advanced embodied agents, and improved solutions in high-stakes domains like healthcare and human-computer interaction. The involvement of ByteDance highlights the direct industrial relevance and potential for real-world impact.

In conclusion, "Reinforced MLLM" is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a critical analysis that synthesizes current achievements, identifies core challenges, and strategically outlines a path for future innovation. Its impact will be felt in both academic research, by shaping grant proposals and PhD theses, and in industry, by guiding the development of more capable and versatile multimodal AI systems.
