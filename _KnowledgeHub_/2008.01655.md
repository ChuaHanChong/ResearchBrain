# Deep Visual Odometry with Adaptive Memory

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep visual odometry (VO) method integrates adaptive memory and a recurrent refining module to leverage global information and mitigate error accumulation, a common limitation in learning-based approaches. It demonstrates improved translational and rotational accuracy on KITTI and TUM-RGBD datasets, outperforming many learning-based methods and showing superior robustness in texture-less or abrupt motion scenarios compared to classic VO.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based visual odometry (VO) methods suffer from significant error accumulation over long sequences, primarily due to their difficulty in representing and leveraging global information.
- Many deep learning VO models often treat the problem as pure tracking, focusing on short-term frame-to-frame estimations without mechanisms to integrate long-term historical knowledge or refine past pose estimates.
- RNN-based approaches for VO have finite memory capacity, which limits their ability to retain long-term dependencies and global context necessary for robust and consistent pose estimation over extended periods.

## Method

- Developed an end-to-end Deep Visual Odometry framework featuring an Encoder for feature extraction, a Tracking module with ConvLSTM for relative pose estimation, an Adaptive Remembering module, and a Recurrent Refining module.
- Implemented an Adaptive Remembering module that constructs a hierarchical global map by storing selected ConvLSTM hidden states based on motion-based keyframe-like selection criteria, ensuring diverse and non-redundant contextual information.
- Designed a Recurrent Refining module utilizing two-level spatial-temporal attention to selectively extract relevant features from the global memory and current observations, which then refines absolute pose estimates by propagating corrected information through a dedicated ConvLSTM branch.

## Results

- Achieved superior performance on the KITTI dataset, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art supervised and unsupervised learning-based VO methods in terms of average translational and rotational RMSE drift.
- Demonstrated enhanced robustness on the TUM-RGBD dataset, performing comparably to ORB-SLAM2 and DSO in rich texture scenes, and exhibiting superior stability in challenging texture-less regions and scenarios with abrupt motions.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Remembering and Refining modules, along with the spatial-temporal attention mechanisms, are critical for the model's performance, particularly in mitigating error accumulation and improving accuracy in difficult conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating global information through an adaptive memory and a recurrent refinement mechanism is effective in significantly mitigating error accumulation in learning-based visual odometry.
> - A two-level spatial-temporal attention mechanism enhances the refinement process by selectively focusing on co-visible and geometrically stable features from both current observations and stored global memories.
> - Adaptive memory management, similar to keyframe selection in classic SLAM, is crucial for building a concise yet informative global map, balancing computational efficiency with the retention of long-term contextual information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Odometry (VO) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) are fundamental tasks in computer vision and robotics, crucial for applications such as autonomous driving, virtual/augmented reality, and robot navigation. These fields involve estimating camera poses from image sequences by exploiting consistency between consecutive frames.

Historically, VO and SLAM have been dominated by geometry-based methods, which are broadly categorized into indirect and direct approaches. Indirect methods, exemplified by ORB-SLAM2, rely on detecting and matching robust keypoints (e.g., ORB features) to build co-visibility graphs and optimize poses and 3D points through bundle adjustment. While robust to varying illuminations and viewpoint changes, they often entail high computational costs for feature extraction and struggle in texture-less environments. Direct methods, such as DSO, estimate camera poses by minimizing photometric errors directly from pixel intensities, particularly those with strong gradients. These methods tend to be less computationally intensive than indirect methods but are more sensitive to illumination variations and large viewpoint changes, and can struggle with insufficient structures or abrupt motions.

The advent of deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), has led to the emergence of end-to-end learning-based VO models. These models either learn depth and ego-motion jointly using CNNs or leverage RNNs to incorporate temporal information for pose estimation. While these learning-based methods have shown promise in estimating relative camera poses from image snippets, they commonly suffer from significant error accumulation over long sequences. This limitation arises primarily from their difficulty in effectively representing and leveraging global information, as RNNs have finite capacity for remembering long-term historical knowledge. Many existing methods primarily focus on short-term temporal dependencies or frame-to-frame estimations, neglecting the potential contribution of future observations to refine past results, a concept central to traditional SLAM backends.

This research positions itself within the supervised learning-based monocular VO paradigm, specifically addressing the challenge of error accumulation by integrating a mechanism for handling global information and long-term dependencies. The paper aims to bridge the gap between the tracking-focused nature of many deep learning VO methods and the global optimization capabilities of classic SLAM systems by introducing explicit memory and refinement modules within an end-to-end neural network architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel deep visual odometry (VO) method that effectively incorporates global information to mitigate the severe error accumulation observed in many existing learning-based approaches. The authors argue that current learning-based methods predominantly treat VO as a pure tracking problem, recovering camera poses from limited image snippets, which inherently leads to unbounded drift over extended sequences.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Addressing Error Accumulation**: Existing learning-based VO methods struggle with accumulated errors because they do not adequately represent or leverage global information. This is particularly true for unsupervised methods where single-reference frame depth maps have limited capacity to convey long-sequence information, and for RNN-based methods whose finite memory capacity limits their ability to retain long-term historical knowledge.
- **Integrating Global Context**: The research aims to embed global information into an end-to-end architecture to enable the system to maintain long-term dependencies. This is inspired by classic VO/SLAM systems that explicitly build and utilize global maps for robust localization and loop closure.
- **Moving Beyond Pure Tracking**: Current deep VO models often focus solely on estimating relative poses between consecutive frames. The authors contend that a comprehensive VO system should also allow for the refinement of previously estimated poses using new observations, a process akin to the backend optimization in traditional SLAM systems.
- **Adaptive Memory Management**: To effectively store and retrieve global information, the authors propose an adaptive memory mechanism that progresses from local to global information, similar to how keyframes are selected in traditional systems. This approach seeks to avoid redundancy while preserving sufficient and valuable information.
- **Leveraging Future Observations**: A critical motivation is to incorporate "future observations" (i.e., information from later frames in a sequence) to refine earlier pose estimates. This concept is often overlooked in existing learning-based methods but is essential for reducing accumulated errors over time.
- **Robustness in Challenging Scenarios**: The authors motivate the development of a system that performs robustly in challenging environments, such as texture-less regions and scenarios with abrupt motions, where classic geometry-based algorithms often fail due to their reliance on specific visual features or assumptions about motion continuity.

In summary, the core objective is to design a deep learning framework that can explicitly and adaptively manage spatio-temporal information over long sequences, using this global context to refine pose estimates and achieve performance comparable to, or exceeding, state-of-the-art classical and learning-based VO systems, particularly in difficult conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed "Deep Visual Odometry with Adaptive Memory," introduces a novel end-to-end VO framework that integrates global information by designing Remembering and Refining modules in addition to a standard tracking component. The architecture consists of an Encoder, a Tracking module, a Remembering module, and a Refining module.

#### 3.1. Encoder

The Encoder component utilizes a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based on the FlowNet (simple version) architecture. Its purpose is to extract high-level features from consecutive RGB image pairs. It processes two concatenated images ($I_{t-1}$ and $I_t$) and outputs a 1024-channel 2D feature-map $X_t$, embedding initial correspondences in the feature domain.

#### 3.2. Tracking Module

The Tracking module is responsible for aggregating temporal information and estimating relative camera motions between consecutive views.
- **Sequence Modeling**: It employs a Convolutional LSTM (ConvLSTM) unit to process the sequence of feature maps ($X_t$). Unlike standard LSTMs that operate on 1D vectors, ConvLSTM maintains the spatial structure of features, enhancing its capacity to retain more knowledge. The ConvLSTM unit processes the current feature map $X_t$ and the previous hidden state $H_{t-1}$ to produce an output $O_t$ and an updated hidden state $H_t$.
- **Relative Pose Estimation**: The output feature $O_t$ from the ConvLSTM is fed into an SE(3) layer, which then predicts the 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) relative motion $P_{t,t-1}$ between frames $t-1$ and $t$. This module primarily focuses on short-term temporal dependencies and local motion estimation.

#### 3.3. Remembering Module

This module aims to learn a neural analogue of a global map, similar to those used in classic VO/SLAM systems. It progressively and adaptively builds this map from local to global information.
- **Hierarchical Map Representation**: The system represents contexts at three levels:
 1. **First Level**: Features extracted by the encoder from paired images, representing initial feature-level correspondences.
 2. **Middle Level**: Hidden states ($H_t$) of the Tracking ConvLSTM, which aggregate partial historical information over a limited time span. These are used for relative pose estimation due to their robustness against visual ambiguities and geometric uncertainties.
 3. **Last Level (Memory)**: A buffer that stores selected hidden states from the middle level. This forms the "global map" of the entire sequence, designed to provide long-term contextual information.
- **Motion-based Selection**: To avoid redundancy and manage computational cost, not all hidden states are stored in the Memory. Instead, a keyframe-like selection strategy is employed based on the motion distance. A hidden state is added to the Memory only if the parallax (rotational or translational distance) between the current view and the latest view stored in the Memory exceeds predefined thresholds ($\theta_{Rot}$ and $\theta_{Trans}$). This ensures that the stored memories represent sufficiently distinct viewpoints, promoting co-visibility and global information retention.

#### 3.4. Refining Module

Once the Memory is constructed, the Refining module utilizes this global information to estimate and optimize the absolute pose of each view, treating it as a registration problem.
- **Recurrent Refining Process**: An additional recurrent branch, also implemented with a ConvLSTM ($U_A$), processes the refined outputs. This allows previously refined results ($H^A_{t-1}$) to influence and improve subsequent estimations ($H^A_t$), propagating corrected information through the sequence. The input to this branch, $X^A_t$, is a fused representation of current observations and selected memories.
- **Spatial-Temporal Attention**: A key component is a two-level attention mechanism that selects relevant features from the Memory for the current view. The last refined output ($O^A_{t-1}$) serves as guidance due to the continuity of camera motions.
 - **Temporal Attention**: It re-weights each element ($m_i$) in the Memory ($M$) based on its contribution to the current pose estimation, effectively computing a weighted sum of memory elements ($M'_t = \sum \alpha_i m_i$). The weights ($\alpha_i$) are calculated based on the cosine similarity between the guiding output $O^A_{t-1}$ and each memory element $m_i$.
 - **Spatial Attention**: Recognizing that memory elements are 3D tensors, this mechanism extends attention to the spatial domain. It re-weights each channel ($m_{ij}$) of a memory element based on its correlation with the guiding output $O^A_{t-1}$. This allows the model to focus on specific co-visible contents within the feature maps.
- **Absolute Pose Estimation**: The reorganized memories ($M'_t$) and the current observations (features $X_t$, after being filtered by attention guided by $O^A_{t-1}$) are concatenated and passed through convolutional layers for fusion, forming $X^A_t$. This fused feature is then fed into the refining ConvLSTM, and its output $O^A_t$ is used by an SE(3) layer to calculate the absolute pose $P_{0,t}$.

#### 3.5. Loss Function

The model is trained using a composite loss function that considers both local (relative) and global (absolute) pose errors.
- $L_{local}$: Measures the L2 error between predicted and ground-truth relative translations ($\hat{p}_{i-1,i}$) and rotations ($\hat{\phi}_{i-1,i}$).
- $L_{global}$: Measures the L2 error between predicted and ground-truth absolute translations ($\hat{p}_{0,i}$) and rotations ($\hat{\phi}_{0,i}$).
- $L_{total} = L_{local} + L_{global}$: The overall loss function. A fixed parameter $k$ balances translational and rotational errors.

During testing, only the refined absolute poses from the Refining module are used as final outputs, while relative poses aid in the motion-based selection within the Remembering module. The model processes sub-sequences of a fixed length (e.g., 11 frames) in a sliding window approach during both training and testing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations of the proposed deep visual odometry (VO) method on the KITTI and TUM-RGBD datasets, along with ablation studies and attention visualizations.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **KITTI Dataset**:
 - **Against Learning-based Methods**: The proposed method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art supervised learning-based VO approaches such as DeepVO, ESP-VO, CL-VO, and GFS-VO-RNN by significant margins in terms of average translational (t_rel) and rotational (r_rel) RMSE drift. This is attributed to the effective mitigation of error accumulation through global information. It also shows superior performance compared to unsupervised methods (e.g., SfmLearner, GeoNet, Vid2Depth), even those utilizing stereo images for scale recovery (e.g., UnDeepVO, Depth-VO-Feat).
 - **Against Classic Methods**: The model outperforms VISO2-M. While ORB-SLAM2, especially with loop closure (ORB-SLAM2 (LC)), achieves lower rotational errors due to explicit geometric constraints and bundle adjustment, the proposed method yields competitive results, particularly in terms of translational accuracy on longer sequences where ORB-SLAM2 can suffer from scale drift. The small performance gap with ORB-SLAM2 (LC) suggests that the novel framework effectively retains and utilizes global information. The model also demonstrates reduced accumulated errors on longer path lengths and higher speeds compared to other learning-based methods.
- **TUM-RGBD Dataset**:
 - **Against Classic Methods**: The model delivers comparable performance to ORB-SLAM2 and DSO in scenes with rich textures. However, it demonstrates superior robustness in challenging scenarios such as texture-less regions (e.g., fr3/nstr ntex near loop, fr3/str ntex far, fr2/large cabinet) and environments with abrupt motions (e.g., fr2/pioneer 360, fr2/pioneer slam3), where classic methods often fail or perform poorly due to their reliance on specific visual features or assumptions about motion continuity.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Effectiveness of Remembering and Refining**: A baseline model containing only the Tracking component (similar to DeepVO) exhibits poor performance in both regular and challenging conditions. The introduction of the Remembering and Refining modules significantly improves results, highlighting their importance in mitigating error accumulation by explicitly preserving global information and refining previous outputs.
- **Spatial-Temporal Attention**: Both temporal and spatial attention mechanisms within the Refining module contribute to the model's performance. The removal of either attention component leads to degraded results, particularly in challenging conditions, demonstrating their crucial role in distilling relevant features from memories and observations based on co-visibility.
- **Influence of Sequence Length**: The model's performance consistently improves as the input sequence length (number of frames) increases (e.g., from 5 to 11 frames). This indicates that the Remembering and Refining components effectively leverage longer-term dependencies and more global information to enhance pose estimation accuracy.

#### 4.3. Attention Visualization

Visualizations of the attention maps indicate that the model prioritizes static areas (e.g., roads) with more geometric priors while effectively disregarding unstable or dynamic elements (e.g., leaves, cars). This suggests an ability for relevant feature selection. The visualizations also show that previous, current, and future frames within a sub-sequence contribute to the pose calculation of a given frame, supporting the claim that information from the entire sequence is utilized for refinement.

#### 4.4. Running Time Analysis

The tracking module alone achieves a high frame rate (up to 76 fps) on a GPU. The full model, incorporating Remembering and Refining with an 11-frame memory size, operates at an acceptable speed of 17.8 ms/frame. This speed is faster than CPU-based classic methods and suitable for real-time applications, especially since the memory and computation costs can remain constant through a sliding window approach.

#### 4.5. Generalization

On unseen KITTI sequences (11-19, using stereo VISO2 as a reference), the model demonstrates improved generalization by significantly alleviating scale drift compared to GFS-VO and ORB-SLAM2, particularly in complex scenes and high-speed highway environments where ORB-SLAM2 struggled with initialization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in learning-based visual odometry by directly addressing a core limitation: the severe error accumulation stemming from the lack of effective global information integration. The proposed framework, with its Remembering and Refining modules, signifies a move beyond treating VO purely as a short-term tracking problem within deep learning paradigms.

The significance of this work includes:

- **Mitigation of Error Accumulation**: By explicitly designing an adaptive memory to store global information and a refinement module to leverage this memory along with future observations, the method effectively mitigates pose drift over long sequences, which is a persistent challenge for many learning-based VO systems.
- **Hierarchical Map Representation**: The concept of constructing a multi-level map from local feature correspondences to global sequence memory offers a structured approach for neural networks to process spatio-temporal information, drawing parallels with the front-end and back-end architectures of classic SLAM systems.
- **Adaptive Memory Management**: The motion-based key state selection strategy contributes to computational efficiency and memory management, ensuring that relevant, non-redundant global information is retained.
- **Enhanced Pose Refinement with Attention**: The introduction of spatial-temporal attention mechanisms for feature selection during refinement allows the model to selectively focus on co-visible and relevant visual cues, further improving the accuracy of absolute pose estimation. The recurrent nature of the refinement process also enables information propagation, leading to more consistent results over time.
- **Robustness in Challenging Environments**: The demonstrated superior performance in texture-less regions and scenarios with abrupt motions, where traditional geometry-based methods often fail, highlights the advantages of deep learning's ability to extract high-level, robust features in difficult conditions.
- **Competitive Performance**: Achieving state-of-the-art results against other learning-based methods and competitive performance against sophisticated classic approaches (like ORB-SLAM2 with loop closure) on benchmark datasets (KITTI, TUM-RGBD) validates the effectiveness of the proposed architecture.

The potential impact of this research is substantial across several domains:

- **Autonomous Navigation**: The enhanced robustness and accuracy in ego-motion estimation are directly beneficial for autonomous vehicles, drones, and robots, enabling more reliable localization and mapping, especially in complex or dynamic urban environments.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality**: More precise and drift-free camera tracking can lead to more immersive and stable AR/VR experiences, reducing motion sickness and improving the realism of virtual content overlay.
- **Robotics**: Robots can achieve more accurate self-localization and environmental understanding, leading to improved task execution, path planning, and interaction with their surroundings.
- **Advancement in Deep Learning for Sequential Data**: The methodology offers insights into how explicit memory and attention mechanisms can be integrated into recurrent neural networks to handle long-term dependencies in other sequential data processing tasks beyond VO.

This work contributes a robust and adaptable end-to-end deep learning framework that effectively bridges the gap between local tracking and global consistency in visual odometry, addressing a fundamental problem in the field and pushing the boundaries of what learning-based methods can achieve.
