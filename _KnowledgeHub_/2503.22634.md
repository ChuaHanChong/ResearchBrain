# Empirical Analysis of Sim-and-Real Cotraining of Diffusion Policies for Planar Pushing from Pixels

> [!summary] Summary
> An empirical study from MIT explored sim-and-real cotraining of diffusion policies for planar pushing, showing it substantially boosts robot policy performance with limited real data. The analysis highlighted the critical role of physical simulation accuracy and the benefit of policies distinguishing between real and simulated domains for optimal action prediction.

## Problem

- The prohibitive cost and time of collecting real-world robot data impede scaling imitation learning compared to other AI fields.
- Difficulty in effectively leveraging easily scalable simulated data with expensive real-world data to improve robot policy performance.
- Lack of foundational understanding of the underlying mechanisms and optimal strategies for sim-and-real cotraining in robotics.

## Method

- Empirically analyzed sim-and-real cotraining for ==Diffusion Policies== on a planar pushing task, jointly training policies on mixed real and simulated data.
- Conducted extensive experiments across various real-world data sizes, simulated data scales, and mixing ratios (alpha) using a KUKA LBR iiwa 7 robot and a 'sim-and-sim' setup.
- Systematically investigated the impact of different sim2real distribution shifts (visual, physics, task gaps) and the mechanisms of positive transfer through embedding analysis and scaling laws.

## Results

- Cotraining dramatically improved real-world policy performance, achieving 2-**7x** improvement with limited real data and increasing success rates from 10/20 to 19/20 for ∣DR∣=50|D_R|=50∣DR∣=50.
- Physical accuracy in simulation is paramount for contact-rich tasks, with even small physics gaps leading to significant performance degradation.
- Data mixing directly (cotraining) consistently outperformed pretraining-finetuning strategies, and optimal mixing ratios (alpha) were critical for maximizing performance, especially with scarce real data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policies learn to discern between simulated and real environments, and this domain awareness is crucial for effective action prediction, especially when underlying physics differ.
> - Simulated data facilitates positive transfer by improving dataset coverage, allowing policies to utilize sim-learned behaviors for states underrepresented in real data.
> - The scaling of performance with simulated data follows predictable power-law relationships, but scaling real data generally yields larger exponents, indicating its greater impact on the performance ceiling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed transformative breakthroughs in areas like natural language processing and computer vision, largely driven by the availability of massive datasets and powerful "foundation models." However, replicating this data-driven success in robotics has been challenging due to the prohibitive cost and time required for collecting real-world robot data for imitation learning. This paper addresses this fundamental challenge by investigating **sim-and-real cotraining**, an approach that leverages both easily scalable simulated data and expensive real-world data to improve robot policy performance.

This work is positioned at the intersection of several active research frontiers in robotics:
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** The paper uses Diffusion Policies, a state-of-the-art algorithm for visuomotor policy learning. The broader goal is to scale IL beyond the limitations of purely real-world data.
- **Simulation for Robotics:** There's a growing body of work on using simulation for data generation, augmentation, and for robust testing in robotics. This paper explores how to best integrate this synthetic data with real data.
- **Sim2Real Transfer vs. Sim-and-Real Cotraining:** Traditional sim2real pipelines typically involve training a policy entirely in simulation and then adapting it for the real world. In contrast, sim-and-real cotraining (also seen in tasks like assembly and kitchen tasks) involves simultaneously training a policy on data from both domains. This paper aims to understand the mechanisms of this "symbiotic" learning.
- **Multi-Domain Learning:** The problem of learning from multiple domains is prevalent across machine learning. In robotics, much focus has been on cotraining from various *real* domains. This paper carves out a specific niche by focusing on cotraining from *simulated* domains as a special case of cross-embodiment training, recognizing the unique challenges and opportunities that simulation presents.
- **Data-Centric AI for Robotics:** With the emergence of large consolidated real-world datasets like Open X-Embodiment and DROID, and advancements in synthetic data generation, understanding how to effectively combine these sources is critical for the future of generalist robots.

By focusing on the "canonical task of planar pushing from pixels," the authors strategically limit the scope of the task to allow for an exhaustive empirical analysis, providing generalizable insights into cotraining rather than a solution to a specific new task. This thoroughness is crucial for informing larger-scale, more complex robotics applications where extensive experimentation would be prohibitively expensive.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind this research stems from the significant challenge of scaling imitation learning in robotics. While data-driven approaches have revolutionized other AI fields, the scarcity and high cost of collecting real-world robotics data impede similar progress for robot control. Simulation offers a promising avenue for scalable, automated data generation.

The key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Elucidate Basic Principles of Sim-and-Real Cotraining:** To move beyond simply confirming that cotraining works, the authors aim to uncover the underlying mechanisms and factors that govern its effectiveness.
2. **Inform Simulation Design:** Understand which aspects of simulation (e.g., visual fidelity, physical accuracy) are most critical for generating synthetic data that positively impacts real-world policy performance.
3. **Guide Sim-and-Real Dataset Creation:** Provide insights into optimal strategies for combining real and simulated data, including effective mixing ratios and data scales.
4. **Optimize Policy Training:** Offer guidance on best practices for training policies using cotraining, including comparisons with alternative approaches like finetuning.
5. **Analyze Distribution Shifts (Sim2Real Gaps):** Systematically investigate how different types and magnitudes of discrepancies between simulated and real (or "target sim") environments affect cotraining performance.

In essence, the authors seek to provide a robust empirical foundation for designing effective hybrid data strategies for robot imitation learning, ultimately aiming to unlock massive improvements in robot capabilities by synergistically combining simulated and real-world experiences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employs a thorough empirical analysis focused on **planar pushing from pixels**, a canonical task involving a robot pushing an object to a target pose. This task was chosen for its ability to capture core robotics challenges (visuomotor control, contact, high-level reasoning) while being amenable to scalable data generation and evaluation.

**Policy Architecture:** The policies are **Diffusion Policies**, a state-of-the-art imitation learning algorithm that learns a denoiser for conditional action distributions. The vision backbone uses a ResNet18 and is trained end-to-end.

**Data Collection:**
- **Real Data ($D_R$):** Collected by a human teleoperator using a KUKA LBR iiwa 7 robot.
- **Simulated Data ($D_S$):** Generated by an optimization-based planner and replayed in Drake to render observations. A notable "action gap" exists, as the simulated planner is "near-optimal" while the human operator is not.
- **Observation Space:** Includes the pusher's pose and two RGB images from an overhead and a wrist camera.

**Cotraining Formulation:**
- The primary approach is **vanilla cotraining**, where policies are jointly trained on a mixture of real ($D_R$) and simulated ($D_S$) data.
- A mixed dataset $D_\alpha$ is created by sampling from $D_R$ with probability $\alpha$ and $D_S$ with probability $(1-\alpha)$, effectively reweighting the contributions of the real and simulated loss terms.

**Experimental Phases:**

1. **Real-World Experiments (Section IV):**
 - **Variables:** Explores combinations of real data size ($|D_R| \in \{10, 50, 150\}$), simulated data size ($|D_S| \in \{500, 2000\}$), and mixing ratio ($\alpha \in \{0, |D_R|/(|D_R|+|D_S|), 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1\}$).
 - **Evaluation:** Each policy is evaluated on 20 real-world trials, measuring binary success rate.
 - **Comparison:** Benchmarks against purely real-data baselines and compares cotraining with pretraining-finetuning strategies.

2. **Simulation Experiments (Sim-and-Sim Setup, Section V):**
 - **Purpose:** To scale experiments and precisely control sim2real gaps. A "target sim" environment is introduced as a surrogate for the real world, designed to mimic the sim2real gap (visual, physics, action gap) with the primary simulation environment.
 - **Scaling Asymptotes (Section V-B):** Extends real-world experiments with larger sweeps of target data size ($|D_T| \in \{10, 50, 150\}$) and simulated data size ($|D_S| \in \{100, 250, 500, 2000, 4000\}$). Evaluations are more robust, with 200 trials per policy.
 - **Distribution Shift Experiments (Section V-C):** Investigates 6 types of distribution shifts at varying intensities by modifying $D_S$. These shifts are categorized as visual (Color Mean, Color Randomization, Camera Shift), physics (Center of Mass Shift), and task (Goal Shift, Object Shift). The impact on performance and optimal $\alpha$ is analyzed. Specific experiments eliminate visual or physics gaps entirely.

**Analysis of Mechanisms (Section VI):**
- **Sim-and-Real Discernability:** Uses binary probes on policy embeddings to assess how well policies can distinguish between simulation and real environments. Also analyzes policy behavior (slider error over time) in different domains.
- **Positive Transfer:** Explores two mechanisms:
 - **Dataset Coverage:** Utilizes k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) analysis on learned observation-action embeddings to determine whether policy actions are primarily derived from $D_R$ or $D_S$ during rollouts.
 - **Power Laws:** Investigates the scaling of target domain test loss and action Mean Squared Error (MSE) with respect to $|D_S|$ and $|D_T|$, looking for power-law relationships.

**Ablations (Section VII):**
- **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Explores whether amplifying the action gap between domains during inference (using CFG) can improve performance.
- **Alternative Cotraining Losses:** Tests modified loss functions (Adversarial and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)) aimed at reducing the sim2real gap at the policy's representation level.

The study's strength lies in its meticulous experimental design, encompassing a vast number of real-world (50+ policies, 1000+ trials) and simulated (250+ policies, 50,000+ trials) evaluations, allowing for high-confidence conclusions on the fundamental aspects of sim-and-real cotraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical analysis yielded several key findings:

1. **Cotraining Dramatically Improves Performance with Limited Real Data:** Cotraining with simulated data significantly boosts real-world policy performance, particularly in low and medium real-data regimes (e.g., 2-7x improvement for $|D_R|=10$, and from 10/20 to 19/20 for $|D_R|=50$). This demonstrates its efficacy in overcoming real-world data scarcity. While less pronounced, improvements were observed even in high-data regimes in simulation.

2. **Scaling Simulated Data Helps, but Plateaus:** Performance gains scale with increasing simulated data ($|D_S|$), but eventually plateau without a corresponding increase in real-world data. Adding more real-world data raises this performance ceiling. The study estimates that cotraining with sim data can be equivalent to increasing the real-world dataset size by approximately 2-3x in their setup.

3. **Mixing Ratio (α) is Crucial:** Policy performance is sensitive to the mixing ratio $\alpha$, especially when real data is limited. Optimal $\alpha$ generally increases with the amount of real data. A surprising finding is that even a very small mixture of real data ($\alpha \searrow 0$) can drastically improve performance compared to training purely on sim data.

4. **Data Mixing Outperforms Finetuning:** Strategies that jointly mix sim and real data during training (cotraining) consistently outperform methods that pretrain on sim-only data and then finetune on real data. Finetuning cotrained models did not consistently improve performance in this single-task setting.

5. **Impact of Sim2Real Gaps:**
 - **Physics Gap is Highly Impactful:** For contact-rich tasks like planar pushing, improving the physical accuracy of simulators (reducing the "physics gap") has a **massive impact** on downstream policy performance. Even a small initial physics gap caused a significant performance drop, suggesting its critical importance.
 - **Visual Gap Nuances:** While improved rendering generally helps, the study found a paradox: **some visual gap can be beneficial**. Policies cotrained with perfectly rendered (no visual gap) sim data performed slightly worse. This is because high-performing policies learn to **distinguish simulated domains from real** if their underlying physics require different actions. If visual cues are identical, this distinction becomes harder, harming action prediction. Providing an explicit "one-hot encoding" for the environment can recover performance in the absence of a visual gap, confirming the need for domain discernability.
 - **Task Gaps** (e.g., goal shift, object shift) also significantly reduce performance.

6. **Mechanisms of Positive Transfer:**
 - **Domain Discernability:** Policies learn to identify their deployment domain. Binary probes on policy embeddings can accurately classify sim vs. real environments. This information is preserved until the final activation, indicating its importance for action prediction. Policies adapt their behavior to align more closely with the deployment domain's characteristics.
 - **Dataset Coverage:** Simulated data enhances performance by "filling gaps" in the real-world dataset. During real-world rollouts, policies primarily rely on behaviors learned from real data, but switch to sim-learned behaviors when encountering states not well-covered by the real dataset.
 - **Power Laws:** The study observed predictable power-law relationships between scaling simulated data ($|D_S|$) and the reduction in target domain test loss and action MSE. These laws suggest that scaling real-world data ($|D_T|$) has a greater impact (larger exponents) than scaling simulated data, reinforcing the intuition that real data remains more valuable.

7. **Ablation Results:**
 - **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** Artificially amplifying the action gap between sim and real using CFG during inference did not improve performance. Simply providing a one-hot encoding for the environment and sampling regularly was more effective.
 - **Alternative Loss Functions (Adversarial, MMD):** Attempts to reduce the sim2real gap at the representation level using adversarial or MMD losses did not reliably outperform vanilla cotraining. This is consistent with the finding that policies need to retain domain-specific information (discernability) when physics differ.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes significant contributions to the field of robot learning by providing a rigorous empirical foundation for understanding and applying sim-and-real cotraining. Its findings have both practical implications for practitioners and theoretical insights for researchers:

**Practical Significance & Best Practices:**
- **Scalability of Imitation Learning:** The work unequivocally confirms that cotraining is a powerful strategy to scale imitation learning, especially in data-scarce scenarios common in robotics. It offers a viable path to leverage large simulated datasets effectively.
- **Simulator Design Guidelines:** Crucially, it informs simulator design by highlighting the paramount importance of **physical accuracy** for contact-rich tasks. This suggests development efforts should prioritize realistic physics over potentially less critical visual fidelity.
- **Dataset Curation Strategies:** The sensitivity to the mixing ratio ($\alpha$) and the observation of performance plateaus provide practical guidance for how much simulated data to collect and how to balance it with real data. It emphasizes that while sim data provides initial performance boosts, real data is still essential to increase the overall performance ceiling.
- **Domain Distinguishability:** The counter-intuitive finding that some visual gap can be beneficial, or that explicit domain encoding (e.g., one-hot vector) is necessary when physics differ, offers direct guidance for how to handle visual discrepancies between sim and real environments. Practitioners should ensure policies can distinguish domains if the optimal actions differ.
- **Training Methodology:** The demonstration that direct data mixing (cotraining) generally outperforms sequential pretraining-finetuning pipelines is a valuable insight for structuring robot learning experiments.

**Theoretical Insights:**
- **Mechanisms of Transfer:** The analysis into dataset coverage and the discovery of power-law scaling for target domain loss provide deeper theoretical understanding of how simulated data contributes to real-world performance. These insights can motivate new theoretical models for hybrid data learning.
- **Role of Domain Discrepancy:** The nuanced exploration of sim2real gaps and the concept of "sim-and-real discernability" advance our understanding of how domain shifts should be managed. It suggests that uniform domain invariance might not always be the optimal strategy if underlying task dynamics differ.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By thoroughly exploring a single canonical task, the paper establishes a robust baseline and methodology for investigating cotraining. It opens avenues for future work to:
 - Extend these findings to massively multi-task scenarios and more complex robot manipulation problems.
 - Explore alternative imitation learning algorithms and data generation pipelines.
 - Investigate adaptive mixing strategies or more sophisticated representation learning techniques that can balance domain generalization with domain-specific adaptation.

In conclusion, this research provides a comprehensive empirical roadmap for leveraging hybrid simulated and real-world data in robotics. Its clear findings on the efficacy of cotraining, the critical role of physical fidelity, the surprising benefits of domain discernability, and the underlying mechanisms of positive transfer will undoubtedly inform and accelerate the development of more capable and robust robotic systems.
