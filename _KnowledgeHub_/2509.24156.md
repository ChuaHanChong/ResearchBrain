# Reasoning or Retrieval? A Study of Answer Attribution on Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Large Reasoning Models generate answers through a dynamic interplay of explicit reasoning and direct memory retrieval, a study found by investigating these mechanisms using a joint perturbation framework. A new method, Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning (FARL), was developed to enhance reasoning fidelity, leading to a 22.8% in-domain and 5.8% out-of-domain accuracy improvement over base models.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently produce final answers that contradict their own Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces, suggesting their conclusions may not solely rely on explicit reasoning.
- Existing research primarily examines either reasoning or internal knowledge retrieval in isolation, or focuses on retrieval without considering reasoning as a competing mechanism for answer generation.
- A lack of understanding exists regarding how deliberate CoT reasoning and direct internal memory retrieval jointly influence LRMs' answer generation and what factors determine the dominance of each.

## Method

- The research developed a ==joint perturbation== framework to manipulate and observe the influence of reasoning (via misleading CoT cues) and retrieval (via memory poisoning through Supervised Fine-Tuning) on LRM answers.
- Experiments were conducted across various open-weight LRMs, problem domains (e.g., MMLU, ARC), and reasoning elicitation methods (distillation-based vs. Reinforcement Learning-based) to identify influencing factors.
- A novel fine-tuning framework, Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning (FARL), was introduced to promote reasoning-dominant behavior by integrating memory unlearning (Negative Preference Optimization) with standard RL (GRPO).

## Results

- Individual perturbations confirmed that both reasoning and retrieval mechanisms could independently alter LRM answers, and when disparate targets were presented, a 'tug-of-war' phenomenon was observed.
- Distillation-based models exhibited higher retrieval dominance and were more prone to fabricating post-hoc explanations to justify retrieved answers, while RL-trained models showed stronger reasoning dominance.
- FARL achieved the highest accuracy improvements over base models (**22.8%** in-domain, **5.8%** out-of-domain) and improved CoT quality metrics, demonstrating enhanced reasoning-dominant behavior and robustness compared to standard RL and SFT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LRMs simultaneously employ both deliberate reasoning via CoT and direct retrieval from internal memory when generating answers, and these mechanisms can independently influence the final output.
> - The dominance of reasoning versus retrieval is influenced by factors such as the problem domain (e.g., math problems show more reasoning), model scale (larger models exhibit stronger reasoning dominance), and the reasoning elicitation method (RL-trained models show stronger reasoning than distillation-based models).
> - Attention heads in the middle layers of LRM architectures act as critical control loci, arbitrating between reasoning and retrieval pathways, a finding confirmed through causal intervention with activation patching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated advanced problem-solving capabilities, often facilitated by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This approach involves generating step-by-step reasoning traces before producing a final answer, which is generally believed to enhance performance, interpretability, and user trust. However, a growing body of research has highlighted inconsistencies between the final answers generated by LRMs and their preceding reasoning traces, suggesting that answers do not always directly emerge from the explicit reasoning process. Concurrently, studies on Large Language Models (LLMs) have investigated their internal knowledge retrieval mechanisms, showing how factual associations are localized and recalled.

Existing work on "reasoning-answer disconnects" has explored how contextual biases or a lack of causal dependence on CoT can influence final answers. Complementary research on "internal retrieval mechanisms" has focused on how LLMs access structured memories for factual queries, often identifying specific network components involved in recall. However, these studies have predominantly examined either reasoning or retrieval in isolation, or focused solely on retrieval without considering reasoning as a competing mechanism for answer generation.

This paper addresses a gap in understanding how these different capabilities — deliberate reasoning via CoT and direct retrieval from internal memory — jointly influence LRMs' answer generation. It seeks to identify the factors that determine the relative dominance of these mechanisms and explore methods to control their interplay. By investigating the simultaneous operation and interaction of reasoning and retrieval, this work aims to provide a mechanistic understanding of how LRMs arrive at their answers, building upon prior observations of reasoning-answer inconsistencies. Furthermore, it connects to ongoing discussions on reasoning elicitation methods, particularly the observed differences between distillation-based and reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, where distillation sometimes promotes memorization over genuine generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the observation that Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently produce final answers that contradict their own Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces. This inconsistency suggests that the models might not be solely relying on explicit reasoning for their conclusions. The authors hypothesize that this behavior arises from the simultaneous operation of two competing mechanisms for generating answers: deliberate reasoning via CoT and direct retrieval from internal memory.

To investigate this hypothesis and bridge the existing gap in understanding, the study sets out to answer three primary research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: Do LRMs employ reasoning and retrieval simultaneously to derive answers?** This objective aims to establish whether both internal memory and explicit reasoning processes contribute concurrently to the final answer generation in LRMs, rather than one mechanism exclusively dominating.
- **RQ2: What factors influence the dominance of one capability over the other?** This objective seeks to identify the conditions or characteristics that determine which mechanism (reasoning or retrieval) exerts a stronger influence on the LRM's final answer. This includes factors such as problem domains, model architectures and scales, and different fine-tuning approaches.
- **RQ3: How can we control the relative strength of these capabilities?** Building on the insights from RQ1 and RQ2, this objective focuses on developing and evaluating a novel method to manipulate the balance between reasoning and retrieval, aiming to promote reasoning-dominant behavior and enhance generalizable reasoning capabilities. This is particularly motivated by concerns that current reasoning fine-tuning paradigms might allow models to exploit retrieval as a shortcut, thereby undermining genuine reasoning development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a controlled experimental design centered on a **joint perturbation framework** to investigate the interplay between reasoning and retrieval in LRMs.

**Response Generation and Perturbations:**
Given a prompt `x`, an LRM `M` produces a CoT `z` and a final answer `y`.
1. **Perturbation to Reasoning:** To assess the influence of reasoning, a misleading cue `c` (suggesting a target answer `y_r` different from the original `y`) is appended to the LRM's original CoT `z`. The model then generates a new answer `y'` from the perturbed prompt `x∥z∥c`. A successful reasoning perturbation occurs if `y'` matches `y_r`.
2. **Perturbation to Retrieval:** To assess memory's influence, the model's internal memory is "poisoned" using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The model is explicitly trained to associate a prompt `x` with a chosen incorrect answer `y_t` (the highest logit answer, excluding the original correct one). This fine-tuning is designed to narrowly target the question-answer association. A successful retrieval perturbation occurs if the memory-poisoned model `M(·; θ')` produces `y_t` as `y'`, regardless of the generated CoT `z'`. The reliability of this SFT-based memory editing for efficacy, generalization, and locality was empirically validated.
3. **Combined Perturbation:** The interaction between reasoning and retrieval is studied by applying reasoning perturbation to a memory-poisoned model: `M(x∥z∥c; θ') = y'`. This is performed under two conditions: (i) `y_r = y_t` (aligned targets), and (ii) `y_r ≠ y_t` (disparate targets). This creates a "tug-of-war" to observe which pathway takes precedence.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **Datasets:** Standard multiple-choice QA datasets were used, including MMLU (grouped into eight broader domains like Math&Logic, SciEng, Humanities), ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, and GPQA. Experiments were limited to samples that the base models could originally answer correctly. A free-form QA dataset (GeneralThought) was also used to test generalizability.
- **Models:** A range of open-weight LRMs were evaluated, categorized by their reasoning elicitation methods:
 - **Distillation-based:** DeepSeek-R1 family (e.g., R1-Llama-8B, R1-Qwen-7B, R1-Qwen-32B).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based:** Qwen3 series (e.g., Qwen3-1B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B) and Phi4 series (Phi4-mini-reasoning, Phi4-reasoning).
- **Metrics:** Reasoning Perturbation Success Rate (R-PSR) and Retrieval Perturbation Success Rate (T-PSR) were defined to quantify the influence of each capability. For combined perturbations, these rates were adjusted based on target alignment. The Post-hoc Explanation Rate (PER) was introduced to measure instances where a model fabricates a CoT to justify a retrieved (poisoned) answer.

**Mechanistic Analysis:**
To understand the underlying mechanisms, the researchers conducted:
- **Attention Pattern Analysis:** Logistic regression classifiers were trained on activation vectors from attention heads across all layers to predict perturbation success labels. AUC scores identified heads most predictive of reasoning- or retrieval-dominant behavior.
- **Causal Intervention:** Activation patching was performed on top-performing attention heads (identified by AUC) to causally demonstrate their control over the selection between reasoning and retrieval pathways.
- **Logits Dynamics:** The logits of both the retrieval-led and reasoning-led answers were tracked step-wise throughout the reasoning process to observe their dynamic competition.

**FARL (Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning):**
To address RQ3, a novel fine-tuning framework, FARL, was introduced.
- **Motivation:** Acknowledging that RL can be more effective for genuine reasoning but susceptible to retrieval shortcuts, FARL aims to "purify" reward signals.
- **Methodology:** FARL integrates memory unlearning (using Negative Preference Optimization, NPO) with Reinforcement Learning (specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization, GRPO). The unlearning step after GRPO iterations continuously suppresses retrieval shortcuts, forcing the model to rely more on reasoning during RL training.
- **FARL Evaluation:** Compared against base models, SFT, and typical RL (GRPO). Metrics included perturbation rates (R-PSR, T-PSR), accuracy (ACC), mean token length (MTL) (both in-domain and out-of-domain), and CoT quality metrics (cycle, diameter, small world index of reasoning graphs). Training was conducted on the Math&Logic domain of MMLU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding the interaction of reasoning and retrieval in LRMs:

**RQ1: Simultaneous Operation of Reasoning and Retrieval:**
- Experiments with individual perturbations (reasoning or retrieval) consistently showed non-zero success rates (T-PSR and R-PSR) across models and datasets, indicating that both mechanisms can independently alter the models' final answers. This suggests that LRMs' answers emerge from the joint influence of both capabilities.
- When reasoning cues and poisoned memory targeted the same answer, the combined perturbation effect was amplified, resulting in a higher success rate than either individual perturbation. This indicates increased model confidence when both pathways converge.
- With disparate targets (reasoning cue suggesting `y_r`, poisoned memory suggesting `y_t`), a "tug-of-war" phenomenon was observed, where the final answer gravitated towards one of the competing suggestions, providing further evidence of simultaneous operation and interaction.

**RQ2: Factors Influencing Dominance:**
- **Problem Domains:** Mathematics and logic domains exhibited lower T-PSR and R-PSR compared to other domains. This suggests that for mathematical problems, models engage in more procedural computation (reasoning) rather than relying on memorized answers. The structured and verifiable nature of mathematical reasoning also made the models' internally generated rationales more robust against misleading cues.
- **Reasoning Elicitation Methods:** Distillation-based models demonstrated higher T-PSR and R-PSR, indicating greater retrieval dominance and lower confidence in their original CoT reasoning. In contrast, RL-trained models showed stronger reasoning dominance, suggesting that RL encourages the development of robust, generalizable reasoning capabilities.
- **Post-Hoc Explanation:** Distillation models exhibited a significantly higher Post-hoc Explanation Rate (PER), meaning they were more prone to fabricating rationales to justify retrieved (poisoned) answers.
- **Model Sizes:** Larger models showed significant negative correlations with PER, T-PSR, and R-PSR. This indicates that larger models are more resistant to misleading information in both memory and CoT, less likely to fabricate justifications, exhibit greater reasoning dominance, and maintain stronger confidence in their original reasoning. They generally demonstrate better generalization of reasoning principles.
- **Attention Patterns and Causal Intervention:** Probe analysis revealed that attention heads in middle layers (e.g., layers 12-16 for R1-Llama-8B, 15-20 for R1-Qwen-7B, 12-18 for Phi4-mini-reasoning) consistently achieved the highest AUC scores in predicting perturbation success. This suggests these mid-network layers are critical control loci for arbitrating between reasoning and retrieval pathways. Causal intervention experiments using activation patching confirmed that these high-AUC attention heads exert direct causal control over the selection of reasoning or retrieval pathways.
- **Logits Dynamics:** Tracking logits showed dynamic competition. In reasoning-dominant cases, the logit for the retrieval-led answer (`y_t`) declined, while the reasoning-led answer (`y`) logit increased. In retrieval-dominant cases, `y_t`'s logit remained consistently higher, indicating retrieval overriding reasoning.

**RQ3: Controlling Relative Strength with FARL:**
- **RL vs. SFT & Distillation:** RL (using GRPO) significantly reduced R-PSR (31.5%) and T-PSR (31.2%) compared to the base model, outperforming SFT. RL also achieved higher accuracy improvements both in-domain (19.8%) and out-of-domain (4.1%) over the base model. RL-trained models showed improved CoT quality metrics (increased cycle, diameter, and small world index), indicating stronger reflective reasoning and broader state exploration. SFT, conversely, sometimes reduced CoT quality.
- **FARL Performance:** FARL achieved further reductions in R-PSR (47.8% over base) and T-PSR (38.5% over base) compared to typical RL, demonstrating stronger reasoning-dominant behavior and enhanced CoT robustness. It delivered the highest accuracy improvements: 22.8% in-domain and 5.8% out-of-domain over the base model. FARL also yielded the most substantial improvements in CoT quality metrics, including an 84.0% increase in the small world index, suggesting highly efficient and integrated reasoning trajectories. The positive correlation between Mean Token Length (MTL) and accuracy indicated that stronger reasoning abilities explicitly produced longer reasoning traces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the understanding of Large Reasoning Models by proposing and providing evidence for a dual-mechanism hypothesis for answer generation, involving both deliberate reasoning and direct memory retrieval. This shifts the perspective from viewing reasoning as a singular process to acknowledging a dynamic interplay between different internal capabilities.

The study's significance lies in several aspects:
- **Mechanistic Understanding:** It offers a mechanistic study into how LRMs derive their final answers, identifying key factors—problem domains, model scales, and fine-tuning approaches—that influence the relative dominance of reasoning versus retrieval. This provides granular insights into LRM behavior beyond mere input-output observation.
- **Addressing Shortcut Learning:** The findings reveal a critical limitation in current reasoning fine-tuning paradigms, particularly that models can exploit the retrieval mechanism as a shortcut, potentially "hacking" reward signals and hindering genuine reasoning development. This highlights an area for improvement in model training.
- **Novel Elicitation Method:** The introduction of FARL (Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning) represents a novel approach to addressing this shortcut problem. By integrating memory unlearning with reinforcement learning, FARL demonstrates a method for actively suppressing retrieval shortcuts, thereby promoting reasoning-dominant behavior and enhancing generalizable reasoning capabilities.
- **Improved Reasoning and Generalization:** FARL's observed improvements in CoT robustness, in-domain accuracy, out-of-domain accuracy, and CoT quality metrics (cycle, diameter, small world index) suggest its potential to elicit more robust and generalized reasoning abilities in LRMs.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens promising avenues for future research, including exploring methods for condensing reasoning pathways without sacrificing accuracy, and validating these conclusions on a broader range of very large LRMs, given the computational constraints of the current study.

Overall, this research deepens the scientific understanding of LRM internal workings and proposes a practical solution to enhance their reasoning fidelity, with implications for developing more reliable and genuinely intelligent AI systems.
