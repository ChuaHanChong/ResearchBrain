# Reasoning or Retrieval? A Study of Answer Attribution on Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Large Reasoning Models generate answers through a dynamic interplay of explicit reasoning and direct memory retrieval, a study found by investigating these mechanisms using a joint perturbation framework. A new method, Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning (FARL), was developed to enhance reasoning fidelity, leading to a 22.8% in-domain and 5.8% out-of-domain accuracy improvement over base models.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently produce final answers that contradict their own Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces, suggesting their conclusions may not solely rely on explicit reasoning.
- Existing research primarily examines either reasoning or internal knowledge retrieval in isolation, or focuses on retrieval without considering reasoning as a competing mechanism for answer generation.
- A lack of understanding exists regarding how deliberate CoT reasoning and direct internal memory retrieval jointly influence LRMs' answer generation and what factors determine the dominance of each.

## Method

- The research developed a ==joint perturbation== framework to manipulate and observe the influence of reasoning (via misleading CoT cues) and retrieval (via memory poisoning through Supervised Fine-Tuning) on LRM answers.
- Experiments were conducted across various open-weight LRMs, problem domains (e.g., MMLU, ARC), and reasoning elicitation methods (distillation-based vs. Reinforcement Learning-based) to identify influencing factors.
- A novel fine-tuning framework, Forgetting-Augmented Reinforcement Learning (FARL), was introduced to promote reasoning-dominant behavior by integrating memory unlearning (Negative Preference Optimization) with standard RL (GRPO).

## Results

- Individual perturbations confirmed that both reasoning and retrieval mechanisms could independently alter LRM answers, and when disparate targets were presented, a 'tug-of-war' phenomenon was observed.
- Distillation-based models exhibited higher retrieval dominance and were more prone to fabricating post-hoc explanations to justify retrieved answers, while RL-trained models showed stronger reasoning dominance.
- FARL achieved the highest accuracy improvements over base models (**22.8%** in-domain, **5.8%** out-of-domain) and improved CoT quality metrics, demonstrating enhanced reasoning-dominant behavior and robustness compared to standard RL and SFT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LRMs simultaneously employ both deliberate reasoning via CoT and direct retrieval from internal memory when generating answers, and these mechanisms can independently influence the final output.
> - The dominance of reasoning versus retrieval is influenced by factors such as the problem domain (e.g., math problems show more reasoning), model scale (larger models exhibit stronger reasoning dominance), and the reasoning elicitation method (RL-trained models show stronger reasoning than distillation-based models).
> - Attention heads in the middle layers of LRM architectures act as critical control loci, arbitrating between reasoning and retrieval pathways, a finding confirmed through causal intervention with activation patching.
