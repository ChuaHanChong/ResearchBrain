# VLM-3R: Vision-Language Models Augmented with Instruction-Aligned 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM-3R enhances Vision-Language Models' spatial and spatio-temporal reasoning by implicitly integrating 3D reconstruction directly from monocular video input. The framework achieves leading performance on static 3D spatial reasoning benchmarks and a newly introduced temporal 3D reasoning benchmark, VSTemporalI-Bench.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) primarily rely on 2D visual encodings, which leads to difficulties in tasks requiring deep 3D spatial reasoning and geometric understanding.
- Prior 3D-aware LMMs either depend on specialized hardware (RGB-D sensors), limiting their scalability, or employ multi-stage explicit 3D reconstruction pipelines that are prone to errors and lack efficient 3D feature encoders.
- Existing spatial reasoning datasets are often small-scale, limiting the comprehensive training and evaluation of robust spatial intelligence in LMMs.

## Method

- Introduces ==VLM-3R==, a unified framework that leverages a pre-trained end-to-end ==3D reconstruction model (CUT3R)== to implicitly derive 3D feature and camera view tokens directly from monocular video.
- Employs a ==Spatial-Visual-View Fusion mechanism== to integrate these 3D tokens with 2D visual tokens and language instructions into the VLM's latent space, with only a few learnable layers efficiently fine-tuned.
- Curates large-scale, automatically generated instruction-aligned 3D spatial (over **200K** QA pairs) and spatio-temporal (==VSTemporalI-Bench==, ~**138.6K** QA pairs) datasets for training and evaluation.

## Results

- VLM-3R achieves state-of-the-art performance on the VSI-Bench for static spatial reasoning, outperforming open-source and proprietary models, particularly in tasks like Absolute Distance (**49.4%** MRA) and Object Size (**69.2%** MRA).
- The framework demonstrates leading performance on the newly introduced VSTemporalI-Bench, excelling in temporal 3D reasoning tasks such as Camera Displacement (**39.4%** MRA) and Camera-Object Relative Distance (**86.5%** accuracy).
- Ablation studies confirm the crucial role of both 3D geometry and camera tokens fusion, as their removal consistently degrades performance across various spatial reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit 3D reconstruction and latent 3D token integration from monocular video can effectively provide spatial context to VLMs without relying on external sensors or explicit, multi-stage pipelines.
> - Fusing 2D visual tokens with separate 3D geometric and camera view tokens through a cross-attention mechanism enables the VLM to learn a unified representation of visual appearance, camera perspective, and global 3D geometry.
> - Large-scale, automatically generated instruction-aligned QA datasets derived from 3D environments are critical for developing robust spatial and spatio-temporal reasoning in VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in endowing Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with a robust understanding of 3D spatial and temporal reasoning. While LMMs have demonstrated capabilities in open-ended dialogue and 2D image/video understanding, they often exhibit limitations in comprehending physical world interactions that require geometric reasoning, such as distance estimation or object layout in 3D space.

Existing approaches to integrate 3D understanding into VLMs generally fall into a few categories. Some methods rely on specialized depth sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras) to capture explicit 3D geometry during training and inference. Examples include LLaVA-3D, ROSS3D, and SpatialRGPT. However, this dependence restricts their scalability and applicability to environments equipped with such sensors, limiting the use of abundant monocular video data.

Another line of work employs multi-stage pipelines that use off-the-shelf 3D reconstruction or Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms to pre-construct explicit 3D maps, often as point clouds, which are then integrated into language models. While these methods provide geometric information, they can be slow, brittle in new environments, and often produce scale-ambiguous geometric representations, which can hinder accurate metric estimations.

Furthermore, several geometry-augmented VLM architectures, such as Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, and SpatialStack, integrate geometry-aware tokens. These models frequently utilize normalized depth representations, which lack global metric scale. This absence of absolute scale information makes accurate measurement-dependent reasoning difficult for tasks like estimating metric distance or object size.

VLM-3R positions itself within this landscape by proposing an end-to-end, spatially aware VLM system that directly interprets scene geometry, camera movement, and spatial relations from *monocular video*. It aims to overcome the limitations of prior work by eliminating the need for external depth sensors or pre-built 3D maps and by explicitly integrating metric-scale 3D structural information. This approach seeks to provide a more scalable and adaptable solution for equipping VLMs with human-like visual-spatial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the VLM-3R research is to develop an end-to-end Vision-Language Model that possesses deep spatial understanding directly from monocular video input. This involves interpreting scene geometry, understanding camera movement, and discerning spatial relationships without relying on external depth sensors or pre-constructed 3D maps.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and observations:

- **Gap in Human-like Visual-Spatial Intelligence:** Humans acquire robust visual-spatial intelligence through continuous interaction with the physical world, forming internal maps and recalling spatial layouts, object sizes, and potential actions. Current Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), despite advancements in open-ended dialogue and 2D vision, still exhibit deficiencies in core spatial skills that require geometric understanding, such as accurate distance estimation.
- **Limitations of Existing 3D-Aware Models:**
 - **Scalability Constraints:** Many existing 3D-aware LLMs and VLMs rely on specialized depth sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras) for 3D data acquisition. This hardware dependency limits their deployment to sensor-equipped environments and restricts their ability to leverage the vast amounts of available monocular video data.
 - **Pipeline Brittleness and Scale Ambiguity:** Other methods employ off-the-shelf reconstruction or SLAM algorithms to pre-construct explicit 3D maps (e.g., point clouds). These multi-stage pipelines can be slow to adapt to new spaces and may suffer from scale ambiguity if traditional reconstruction methods do not preserve real-world scene scale. This can lead to irreversible degradation in a VLM's spatial comprehension.
 - **Incomplete 3D Representations:** Some geometry-augmented VLMs utilize normalized depth, which lacks metric scale, making it difficult for models to perform accurate measurement-dependent reasoning.
- **Need for Temporal Reasoning:** While many benchmarks evaluate static scene understanding, there is a recognized need to assess and enhance a VLM's ability to reason about spatial configurations as they evolve over time, particularly those induced by camera motion in a static scene. This spatio-temporal understanding is crucial for embodied reasoning and interaction in dynamic environments.
- **Data Scarcity for Spatial Instruction Tuning:** Advancing robust spatial reasoning in VLMs requires large-scale, diverse, 3D-aware instruction-tuning data. Existing datasets, while valuable, are often insufficient in scale for comprehensive training.

To address these motivations, VLM-3R sets out to:
1. Develop an architecture that unifies image-level semantics with metric-scale 3D structural and camera motion information derived directly from monocular video.
2. Introduce a scalable and automated data creation pipeline for generating over 200K 3D reconstructive instruction-tuning QA pairs, including simulator-based route-planning instances.
3. Propose a new benchmark, VSTI-Bench, to specifically evaluate spatio-temporal reasoning capabilities of VLMs, with 138.6K QA pairs focused on evolving spatial relationships.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM-3R introduces an end-to-end framework that augments Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with 3D reconstructive instruction tuning, enabling spatial reasoning from monocular video. The methodology comprises a novel architecture for integrating 3D information, a scalable data curation pipeline, and a new benchmark for temporal reasoning.

#### 3.1. VLM-3R Architecture

The VLM-3R architecture processes monocular video frames along with language instructions. It extracts visual, geometric, and camera pose tokens from the video, which are then aligned with language representations through learnable layers and instruction tuning.

- **3D Reconstructive Tokenization:**
 - VLM-3R utilizes a pre-trained **CUT3R** model as its core geometry encoder. CUT3R is a feed-forward, dense 3D reconstruction model capable of deriving metric-scale 3D structures directly from monocular video.
 - For each input image `I_t` in a video sequence, `I_t` is first passed through an image encoder (`f_enc`) to extract feature tokens `F_t`.
 - These tokens `F_t`, along with a learnable pose query token `z` and the previous recurrent state `s_t-1`, are processed by a transformer decoder (`f_dec`). This yields an updated state `s_t`, context-aware image tokens `F'_t` (spatial tokens), and pose-related output tokens `z'_t` (view tokens).
 - Prediction heads then use `F'_t` and `z'_t` to output 3D point maps (`P_map_t`) and relative camera pose (ego-motion `T_t`). A key aspect is that CUT3R produces *metric-scale* outputs, which facilitates accurate spatial instruction alignment.
 - Instead of directly fusing explicit point clouds, which can be sparse and inconsistently sized, VLM-3R leverages the implicit latent representations: `F'_t` encodes scene context, and `z'_t` captures camera motion. These collectively serve as the rich 3D reconstructive tokens.
 - Input images are resized to a standard size (e.g., 432x432) to ensure consistency in token generation.
 - During subsequent VLM training, the weights of both the pre-trained visual encoder and the CUT3R-based spatial encoders (`f_enc`, `f_dec`) are frozen.

- **Spatial-Visual-View Fusion:**
 - The enriched spatial tokens (`F'_t`) and camera view tokens (`z'_t`) are concatenated to form a unified 3D representation, `Z_3D`.
 - This `Z_3D` representation is fused with the VLM’s native visual tokens (`H_v`, extracted from video frames by a pre-trained visual encoder, e.g., CLIP ViT) using a **cross-attention mechanism**. `H_v` serves as queries, while `Z_3D` provides keys and values.
 - The cross-attention output (`H_attn`) is then combined with the original visual tokens `H_v` via a residual connection (`H'_v = H_v + H_attn`) to preserve appearance information while injecting 3D priors.
 - The fused tokens `H'_v` are subsequently passed through a two-layer projector, aligning their dimensions with the input space of the LMM backbone (e.g., Qwen2).
 - Finally, these 3D-aware visual tokens are concatenated with the language instruction tokens (`H_instruct`) and fed into the LMM backbone.

- **Training Objective and Fine-tuning Strategy:**
 - VLM-3R employs the same learning objective as LLaVA-NeXT-Video.
 - For efficient adaptation, **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** is used for fine-tuning, with a rank of 128 and scale of 256.
 - Only parameters within the 3D fusion attention block and the projection layers are updated.
 - Training is performed for one epoch, leveraging NVIDIA H200 GPUs in a distributed setup.

#### 3.2. Scalable Spatial and Temporal Data Curation

To overcome limitations of existing, small-scale spatial QA datasets, VLM-3R develops a scalable, automated pipeline for data curation.

- **Multimodal Instruction Generation:**
 - **Data Sources and Scene Graph:** Diverse open-source 3D datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes) providing 3D geometry, semantics, and instance-level meta-information are consolidated. A detailed spatio-temporal scene graph is constructed, representing each frame as a temporal node and each object instance as a distinct node with attributes like global/local coordinates and semantic information.
 - **General Spatial QA:** Leveraging this scene graph, the pipeline automatically generates over 200K diverse spatial reasoning QA pairs for seven of the eight core VSI-Bench task types (e.g., Object Count, Absolute Distance, Relative Direction).
 - **Route Planning Data:** To scale the route planning task, the Habitat simulator is used with 3D scenes (converted from.ply to.glb using `assimp`). The simulator generates accurate navigation routes, including turns, between specified points. Nearest objects are identified along these paths, and this information is used to construct 4,225 QA pairs in the VSI-Bench format, providing high-level navigation descriptions.
 - **Data Leakage Prevention:** The pipeline adheres to standard train/test splits of the source datasets to prevent data leakage.

- **Vision-Spatial-Temporal Intelligence Benchmark (VSTI-Bench):**
 - **Motivation:** To address the gap in evaluating temporal reasoning from monocular video, VLM-3R introduces VSTI-Bench. This benchmark assesses a model's ability to reason about camera motion, camera-object interactions, and evolving spatial configurations over time.
 - **Task Categories:** VSTI-Bench comprises 138.6K QA pairs across five temporal tasks categorized into three primary types: Camera Dynamics (49.6%), Camera–Object Interactions (38.4%), and Object Relative Position (12.0%).
 - **Temporal QA Generation:** Similar to the general spatial QA, a spatio-temporal scene graph (from ScanNet, ScanNet++, ARKitScenes) is used. This graph explicitly models object attributes (position, size, rotation, labels) and precise camera viewpoint information (pose) at every time step. This structured understanding enables the computation of temporal changes (e.g., net camera displacement) and the automated formulation of diverse spatio-temporal QA pairs.
 - **Metrics:** Standard Accuracy (ACC) is used for Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) tasks. Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) is adopted for Numerical Answer (NA) tasks, capturing prediction proximity across multiple tolerance levels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLM-3R involved a comprehensive set of benchmarks designed to assess spatial, temporal, and general visual understanding.

- **Performance on VSI-Bench (3D Spatial Reasoning):**
 - VLM-3R (7B) achieved an average score of 60.9, outperforming other open-source VLMs, including fine-tuned LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (57.7).
 - Notable gains were observed on tasks requiring explicit geometric perception and metric-aware reasoning. For example, performance on "Absolute Distance" increased from 20.2 to 49.4, "Room Size" from 12.3 to 67.1, and "Relative Direction" from 42.4 to 80.5. These results indicate the effectiveness of the architecture and spatially oriented training data in enhancing spatial understanding.
 - Tasks like "Object Counting" showed less dependence on fine-grained 3D geometry, demonstrating similar performance across variants.

- **Performance on VSTI-Bench (Spatio-Temporal Reasoning):**
 - VLM-3R (7B) achieved an average score of 58.8, surpassing proprietary models like Gemini-2.5 Pro (42.4) and other open-source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B at 40.0).
 - The strong performance on VSTI-Bench, particularly in tasks related to "Camera-Object Relative Distance" (86.5), validates that the incorporation of view tokens from the geometry encoder assists in disentangling camera movement from object-centric spatial relationships.

- **Performance on ScanQA and SQA3D (3D QA Benchmarks):**
 - VLM-3R demonstrated competitive performance on 3D QA benchmarks (ScanQA and SQA3D) despite using only monocular video input, achieving CIDEr of 101.9 on ScanQA and EM-R of 63.4 on SQA3D.
 - It outperformed video-only LLMs and was competitive with 2.5D/3D models that rely on ground-truth depth inputs, indicating the effectiveness of its spatial–visual–language reasoning and generalization capabilities.

- **Generalization to Online Spatio-Temporal Understanding (OST-Bench):**
 - Despite being trained solely on static indoor scenes, VLM-3R exhibited generalization to the online embodied exploration setting of OST-Bench.
 - It outperformed its base model (LLaVA-Video-7B) across various categories, showing particular improvements in "Agent State," "Agent–Object Spatial Relationship," and "Estimation" tasks. This suggests robustness and cross-domain generalization.

- **General-Purpose Visual Understanding (Video-MME and VQAv2):**
 - VLM-3R maintained comparable performance to LLaVA-NeXT-Video on general video understanding (Video-MME overall) and image understanding (VQAv2).
 - Crucially, it improved "Spatial Perception" by +3.7% on Video-MME, indicating that the spatial–visual fusion strategy generalizes to 2D and video comprehension while specifically enhancing spatial perception.

- **Ablation Studies on VLM-3R Components (VSI-Bench):**
 - Removing spatial tokens led to a performance drop from 60.90 to 59.46, confirming their role in modeling 3D layout and depth.
 - Removing view tokens resulted in a decrease to 59.09, highlighting the importance of pose encoding for maintaining an egocentric spatial frame.
 - A 2D-only fusion variant, lacking geometric cues, yielded lower accuracy (58.12 vs. 60.90), underscoring the necessity of 3D spatial integration.
 - Directly fusing explicit point clouds performed worse (57.87 vs. 60.90) than token-level fusion, suggesting that the latter offers more stable cross-modal reasoning and avoids sparsity issues.
 - The full 3D fusion framework demonstrated a 3.2-point gain over a baseline LLaVA-NeXT-Video fine-tuned without context and geometry tokens, validating the proposed fusion approach.

- **Ablation on Pretrained Geometry Encoders (VSI-Bench, Supplementary Material):**
 - Comparison between CUT3R and VGGT as geometry encoders showed CUT3R achieving better overall performance (60.9% vs. 58.1%). CUT3R's metric-scale and temporal reasoning capabilities were more beneficial for tasks like "Appearance Order" and "Room Size" compared to VGGT's reliance on relative-scale priors.

- **Generalization to OpenEQA (Supplementary Material):**
 - On the OpenEQA benchmark, VLM-3R improved spatial question performance (from 49.95% to 51.60%) over its base model (LLaVA-NeXT-Video), with a modest decrease in non-spatial questions.
 - VLM-3R also demonstrated competitive zero-shot performance (61.7%) against other general-purpose multimodal models, including GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM-3R framework presents an advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models by endowing them with enhanced 3D spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities directly from monocular video. This work carries several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Increased Accessibility and Scalability of 3D Understanding:** By eliminating the reliance on explicit depth sensors or pre-computed 3D maps, VLM-3R addresses a key limitation of prior 3D-aware VLMs. This enables broader application of spatial intelligence in real-world scenarios where only monocular video streams are available, making 3D spatial assistance more scalable and practical across diverse environments.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The robust 3D spatial understanding, particularly with metric-scale awareness and the ability to disentangle camera motion, is crucial for developing more capable embodied agents and robots. These agents require accurate perception of object sizes, distances, layouts, and their temporal evolution to effectively navigate, interact with, and reason about physical environments. The strong generalization demonstrated on the OST-Bench, which simulates online embodied exploration, indicates VLM-3R's potential for real-world robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Temporal Reasoning:** The introduction of VSTI-Bench specifically addresses the often-overlooked aspect of temporal reasoning in VLMs. This benchmark, alongside VLM-3R's performance on it, highlights the importance of understanding dynamic changes in spatial relationships induced by camera movement. This contributes to a more complete spatio-temporal intelligence in AI models.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Multimodal Models:** The reconstructive instruction tuning approach, coupled with the Spatial-Visual-View fusion module, provides a novel method for seamlessly integrating rich 3D information into existing VLM architectures. This architectural design could serve as a foundation for developing more sophisticated multimodal models capable of comprehending complex 4D (3D + time) dynamics.
- **Contribution to Data Curation:** The scalable and automated data curation pipeline developed for VLM-3R, generating over 200K spatial QA pairs and the 138.6K QA pairs for VSTI-Bench, offers valuable resources to the research community. These datasets can accelerate future research and development in spatial and spatio-temporal reasoning, providing structured and diverse training and evaluation data.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** A more intuitive and accurate understanding of 3D space by AI models could lead to more natural and effective human-AI interactions, particularly in augmented reality, virtual reality, and assistive technologies that require spatial awareness.

While the current work primarily focuses on static indoor scenes, the framework lays the groundwork for future research into dynamic and extreme environments. Addressing challenges in large-scale 4D data collection and further improving end-to-end 3D reconstruction accuracy remain important areas for future exploration, building upon the foundations established by VLM-3R.
