# Cybo-Waiter: A Physical Agentic Framework for Humanoid Whole-Body Locomotion-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Cybo-Waiter introduces a physical agentic framework for humanoid robots to reliably execute complex, natural language-instructed tasks involving whole-body locomotion and manipulation in real-world environments. The system transforms high-level VLM plans into structured, verifiable programs with multi-object 3D geometric supervision and targeted recovery, leading to improved success rates on established multi-step and new long-horizon tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Robot agents, especially those leveraging VLMs/LLMs, exhibit brittleness in real-world scenarios due to underspecified plans, noisy perception, and implicit success detection.
- Humanoid robots present unique challenges with tightly coupled locomotion and manipulation, where minor errors can cascade into significant failures impacting balance and reachability.
- Existing humanoid systems often lack explicit verification and fine-grained diagnostics for execution, making failure attribution and effective recovery difficult.

## Method

- A training-light, hierarchical agent system that translates open-ended VLM plans into structured JSON subtasks, each with explicit predicate-based preconditions and success conditions.
- Multi-object 3D geometric grounding using RGB-D observations and SAM3 to robustly estimate object states, enhanced by temporal consistency filtering to mitigate perception noise.
- A geometry-grounded supervisor module that continuously monitors task progress, provides condition-level diagnostics, and triggers feedback-driven targeted recovery actions (e.g., re-observation, skill adaptation, re-grounding).

## Results

- Achieved comparable or improved success rates on Being-0-aligned multi-step manipulation tasks, including 10/10 for 'Grasp-bottle' and 9/10 for 'Place-basket', surpassing Being-0's 8/10 and 6/10 respectively.
- Outperformed an ablation (W/O Supervisor) on challenging long-horizon tasks (e.g., 'Tidy-desk' 7/10 vs. 5/10, 'Bring-me-a-drink' 9/10 vs. 7/10), demonstrating the supervisor's critical role.
- The framework demonstrated robust whole-body locomotion and manipulation on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in cluttered indoor settings, coordinating gait-conditioned RL for locomotion with MPC for manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly defining task preconditions and success conditions as verifiable predicates with temporal stability is crucial for robust robot execution in dynamic and noisy real-world environments.
> - Structured 3D geometric grounding of task-relevant entities, combined with object-centric representations, enables precise and verifiable state monitoring for complex loco-manipulation tasks.
> - Feedback-driven replanning, utilizing condition-level diagnostics, allows for targeted recovery actions that enhance system resilience by avoiding full task restarts and addressing specific failure modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Cybo-Waiter" operates within the burgeoning field of robot agents designed to interpret and execute open-ended natural language instructions in complex human environments. This domain is characterized by the need for robots to perform long-horizon, spatially extended tasks involving both navigation and manipulation, often under conditions of clutter and partial observability.

Current approaches often leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) or Large Language Models (LLMs) to construct modular pipelines comprising task planning, semantic grounding, and execution evaluation. These systems aim to decompose high-level instructions into executable steps, ground linguistic concepts to scene entities, and verify task completion online. Examples include language-based self-feedback for replanning and feasibility-aware skill selection. However, a common limitation across many existing frameworks is their brittleness in real-world scenarios. Model outputs can be underspecified for downstream controllers, success detection is susceptible to transient perception noise, and failures are often difficult to attribute and recover from effectively. While some research has explored explicit verification (e.g., logic-based plan checking) and proactive replanning (e.g., scene-graph-driven), these are frequently validated on mobile manipulators or wheeled platforms.

This paper positions itself by specifically addressing the unique challenges associated with **humanoid robots**. Humanoid whole-body execution introduces tighter coupling between locomotion, manipulation, stance, reachability, and balance, meaning small errors can cascade into significant failures. Existing humanoid systems largely pursue two directions: (i) hierarchical agent stacks focusing on online efficiency and modular skills, or VLM-based monitoring for skill sequencing; and (ii) learning-heavy vision-language-action policies trained at scale for broad generalization.

"Cybo-Waiter" differentiates itself by prioritizing a **verifiable and diagnosable execution loop** for humanoids. Instead of solely focusing on efficiency or broad generalization, this work emphasizes grounding all task-relevant entities in actionable 3D geometry, evaluating explicit success conditions with temporal stability against noisy perception, and triggering targeted recovery mechanisms. This approach aims to enhance dependability and robustness, which are often overlooked aspects in systems prioritizing rapid deployment or wide-ranging capabilities without explicit verification. By doing so, it contributes to developing more reliable and trustworthy humanoid agents capable of sustained operation in dynamic, real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Cybo-Waiter" research is to enable humanoid robots to reliably execute complex, long-horizon tasks specified by open-ended natural language instructions within real-world human environments. This involves orchestrating both locomotion and manipulation capabilities, which are inherently tightly coupled in humanoid platforms.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the current landscape of robot agents, particularly when applied to humanoids:

1. **Bridging the Gap between VLM Planning and Robust Execution:** While VLMs can generate high-level plans from natural language, these plans often lack the specificity and verifiability required for reliable real-world robot execution. VLM outputs can be underspecified, making it difficult for downstream controllers to interpret and execute actions precisely. There is a need to translate these abstract plans into structured, executable programs that include explicit conditions for success and failure.

2. **Addressing Brittleness from Noisy Perception and Implicit Success Detection:** Real-world sensory data (e.g., from RGB-D cameras) is inherently noisy and subject to partial observability and occlusions. Existing systems often rely on implicit or transient success detection, which can lead to premature transitions, misinterpretations of the scene state, and cascading failures. The researchers are motivated to develop mechanisms that ensure temporal stability in perception and robustly verify task progression despite sensor noise.

3. **Enabling Targeted Recovery and Replanning:** When failures occur, many systems either restart the entire task or lack fine-grained diagnostics to identify the root cause. This leads to inefficient and unreliable operation. A core motivation is to provide condition-level diagnostics that can pinpoint specific failing predicates or constraints, allowing for targeted recovery actions (e.g., re-observation, re-grounding, skill adaptation) rather than generic replanning or full task restarts. This increases the overall resilience of the robotic system.

4. **Accounting for Humanoid-Specific Constraints:** Humanoids present unique challenges where locomotion and manipulation are fundamentally interdependent through factors like balance, reachability, and stance. Minor errors in pose or distance can destabilize the robot or prevent successful manipulation. Existing frameworks often do not fully account for these whole-body constraints. The motivation here is to integrate these considerations into the planning, execution, and verification loops to ensure safe and feasible whole-body motion.

5. **Developing a Dependable Agent Framework:** Rather than prioritizing computational efficiency or broad generalization through extensive learning from large datasets, a key motivation for "Cybo-Waiter" is to focus on creating a **dependable, verifiable, and diagnosable agent**. This means building a system where the internal state, progress, and reasons for failure are transparent and explicitly monitored, facilitating development, debugging, and ultimately, more reliable autonomous operation in human environments.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to advance the state of humanoid robot autonomy, making these complex platforms more capable of performing practical tasks in unstructured settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Cybo-Waiter" framework is designed as a training-light, hierarchical agent system that transforms open-ended VLM plans into verifiable task programs and employs multi-object 3D geometric supervision to close the perception-action loop. The system integrates several modules to achieve robust humanoid whole-body locomotion-manipulation.

**A. Structured VLM Task Decomposition:**
The system decouples high-level semantic planning from perception and execution. A VLM-based planner takes a natural language instruction and an observation to generate a structured task plan. This plan is formalized as a typed JSON sequence of subtasks (`τ_i`). Each subtask explicitly defines:
- `type`: The subtask family (e.g., MANIPULATION, LOCOMOTION).
- `target`: The object to be manipulated, specified by a symbolic handle (`ref`), a language descriptor (`phrase`), and optional attributes or relations.
- `destination`: The goal region or reference object.
- `manipulation`: An action primitive with actuation settings (e.g., arm selection, MPC usage).
- `locomotion`: Parameters for locomotion.
- `preconditions` and `success_conditions`: Predicate assertions (e.g., VISIBLE, SUPPORTED_BY) with specified temporal stability (`stable_frames`), enabling deterministic monitoring.
- `failure_handlers`, `timeout_sec`, `max_retry`: Mechanisms for robust execution.

**B. Conditional Segmentation and Geometric Coordinate Estimation:**
This module grounds the task-conditioned plan in 3D geometry using RGB-D observations and SAM3 (Segment Anything Model 3).
1. **Task-conditioned Multi-object Segmentation:** For each task-relevant entity (target, destination, relation references, predicate references) specified in the active subtask, SAM3 is queried with language prompts (e.g., `target.phrase`). Attributes and relations refine the query. When multiple masks are detected, temporal consistency is prioritized to select the most stable candidate over time.
2. **Lifting Masks to 3D Geometric States:** Selected 2D masks are back-projected into 3D point clouds using calibrated RGB-D data. From these point clouds, compact geometric states are estimated for each object: centroid, spatial extent (bounding box), and confidence score. For tasks requiring orientation, a descriptor (e.g., dominant surface normal, principal axis via PCA) is also extracted. Temporal filtering and outlier rejection are applied to enhance robustness against sensor noise.
3. **Candidate Selection and Object-centric Representation:** The best instance for each entity is selected by a scoring function that combines semantic consistency, relational consistency, geometric feasibility, and temporal stability. The selected instances are stored in an object-centric workspace state (`W_t`), which also includes derived pairwise geometric relations (e.g., distances, height differences, alignment). This `W_t` serves as the basis for predicate evaluation.

**C. Supervisor: Task Monitoring with RGB-D and SAM3:**
The supervisor module is responsible for continuous verification of task progress and completion.
1. **Inputs and Outputs:** It consumes the active subtask, the grounded target handle, and the `W_t`. It outputs a discrete subtask status (in_progress, done, blocked, failed) along with an uncertainty flag, condition-wise flags, and continuous diagnostic metrics.
2. **Predicate Representation and Evaluation:** Preconditions (`P_i`) and success conditions (`G_i`) are defined as parameterized predicates. The supervisor evaluates a monitoring function (`ϕ`) for each predicate, which returns a scalar geometric measure from `W_t`. This measure is converted into an instantaneous boolean value (`eval`).
3. **Temporal Stability:** To mitigate transient noise, a predicate is considered satisfied (`sat`) only if its `eval` value holds true for a specified number of consecutive frames (`stable_frames`). Subtask readiness and completion are determined by the logical conjunction of all corresponding `sat` predicates.
4. **Diagnostics and Corrective Feedback:** Beyond discrete status, the supervisor provides a diagnostic vector (`d_t`) with continuous measures (e.g., object-support height gap, relative distance). These diagnostics localize constraint failures and quantify errors, enabling targeted corrections. The supervisor can also request re-observation or re-grounding if geometric estimates are unreliable.
5. **VLM-assisted Semantic Verification:** Optionally, a VLM can be invoked as an auxiliary semantic verifier when the supervisor indicates uncertainty or when non-geometric cues are crucial. This VLM output (boolean judgment and rationale) supplements geometric verification, taking precedence only if geometric evidence is missing or ambiguous.

**D. Feedback-Driven Replanning for Robust Task Execution:**
Replanning is a critical component for robustness, triggered by structured feedback from the supervisor.
1. **Replanning Triggers:** Replanning is initiated if a subtask is `blocked` (preconditions unsatisfied), `failed` (e.g., `timeout_sec` exceeded or safety predicates violated), or if the supervisor raises an `uncertain` flag due to low-confidence estimates.
2. **Structured Feedback:** The supervisor provides a feedback record (`F_t`) containing the subtask status, the set of failing predicate instances, and continuous diagnostics.
3. **Recovery Actions:** A set of reusable recovery operators are employed based on failure type: (i) Re-observation (adjust viewpoint), (ii) Re-grounding (re-run segmentation with updated prompts), (iii) Skill/parameter adaptation (refine tolerances, micro-adjust end-effector pose), and (iv) Task-level revision (insert corrective subtasks). These actions are managed by `failure_handlers`.
4. **Retry Budget and Plan Update:** Each subtask has a `max_retry` budget. If not exhausted, a recovery operator is applied, and the subtask is re-executed. If the budget is depleted, the VLM planner generates an updated plan conditioned on `F_t`.

**E. Integrated Execution Framework for Robust Humanoid Task Execution:**
This layer translates subtasks into low-level humanoid control.
1. **Execution Interface:** It takes the subtask schema and grounded target handle, then dispatches either locomotion or manipulation skills, returning execution status and telemetry.
2. **Locomotion:** A gait-conditioned reinforcement learning (RL) policy, trained on a multi-clip locomotion dataset, provides a library of 7 locomotion primitives (e.g., forward walk, side-walk, turn). This policy handles lower-body commands for balance and stepping while tracking desired base velocities.
3. **Manipulation:** For manipulation subtasks, upper-body Model Predictive Control (MPC) is used to generate constraint-aware end-effector trajectories, enabling arm motion. Concurrently, the lower body is controlled independently to maintain balance and support stance, allowing manipulation without destabilizing the robot.
4. **Cross-skill Coordination:** A lightweight coordinator switches between the RL locomotion skill and the upper-body manipulation controller based on subtask type and supervisor feedback. It triggers repositioning if geometric preconditions are not met or pauses manipulation for recovery if tracking errors or safety predicates are violated.

**Real-World Setup:** Experiments are conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot equipped with a Dex3-1 dexterous hand and a head-mounted RGB-D camera. Perception and decision modules run on a desktop workstation, while low-level controllers run on the robot's onboard computer. The environment consists of cluttered indoor office settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the "Cybo-Waiter" framework through real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, addressing both standard multi-step manipulation tasks and newly introduced long-horizon loco-manipulation scenarios.

**A. Results on Being-0-aligned Multi-step Tasks:**
The system was initially tested on a suite of multi-step tasks adopted from the Being-0 benchmark, with 10 trials per task. The results, summarized in Table I of the paper, indicate:
- **Fetch-bottle:** Achieved 9/10 successful trials, comparable to Being-0 (9/10).
- **Deliver-basket:** Achieved 8/10 successful trials, comparable to Being-0 (8/10).
- **Grasp-bottle:** Achieved 10/10 successful trials, an improvement over Being-0 (8/10).
- **Place-basket:** Achieved 9/10 successful trials, a notable improvement over Being-0 (6/10).
- **Place-coffee:** Achieved 8/10 successful trials, an improvement over Being-0 (6/10).

These results demonstrate that the proposed framework, through its combination of structured task programs, multi-object 3D grounding, and predicate-based supervision, enables the humanoid agent to achieve reliable completion in established manipulation routines. The improvements observed in tasks like "Grasp-bottle," "Place-basket," and "Place-coffee" suggest that the detailed verification and execution mechanisms contribute to enhanced robustness during critical manipulation phases.

**B. Results on Long-Horizon Tasks:**
Beyond the Being-0 aligned tasks, the study introduced additional long-horizon tasks designed to stress open-ended instruction following and multi-object reasoning in cluttered environments. For these tasks (Tidy-desk, Tabletop-sorting, Bring-me-a-drink), the full "Cybo-Waiter" system was compared against an ablation labeled "W/O Supervisor," where the geometry-grounded supervisor was disabled. The success counts over 10 trials are presented in Table II of the paper:
- **Tidy-desk:** The full Cybo-Waiter system achieved 7/10 successful trials, an improvement over W/O Supervisor (5/10).
- **Tabletop-sorting:** The full Cybo-Waiter system achieved 8/10 successful trials, an improvement over W/O Supervisor (6/10).
- **Bring-me-a-drink:** The full Cybo-Waiter system achieved 9/10 successful trials, an improvement over W/O Supervisor (7/10).

The consistent outperformance of the full system compared to the ablation directly highlights the importance of the geometry-grounded supervisor in executing long-horizon tasks. The supervisor's ability to evaluate predicate-based preconditions and success conditions over stable frames reduced premature transitions caused by transient perception noise. Furthermore, it enabled the detection of off-nominal states and triggered targeted recovery actions, preventing complete task restarts. The qualitative examples (Figs. 2 and 3) illustrate complex sequences of locomotion and manipulation. These findings indicate that explicit monitoring and structured feedback are critical for maintaining reliable task progression, particularly in scenarios requiring repeated object-level actions and extended navigation by a humanoid robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Cybo-Waiter" framework presents a contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the domain of reliable autonomous task execution in human environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Dependability for Humanoid Robots:** The work directly addresses the fragility of current robot agents when confronting real-world noise and the tight coupling of locomotion and manipulation inherent in humanoid platforms. By introducing a verifiable and diagnosable execution loop, the framework significantly enhances the robustness of humanoid robots, reducing premature task terminations and increasing their overall dependability for practical applications.

2. **Structured and Interpretable VLM-based Planning:** The approach of compiling VLM outputs into structured JSON subtasks with explicit predicate-based preconditions and success conditions provides a clearer, more interpretable interface between high-level language understanding and low-level robot control. This structure facilitates debugging, failure attribution, and targeted recovery, moving beyond opaque, end-to-end learning systems.

3. **Robust Multi-Object 3D Geometric Grounding:** The use of SAM3 and RGB-D for task-conditioned, multi-object 3D geometric grounding enables the robot to accurately localize and understand task-relevant entities in cluttered environments. This robust grounding, coupled with temporal stability filtering, is crucial for reliable interaction and predicate evaluation, overcoming challenges posed by noisy real-world perception.

4. **Effective Feedback-Driven Replanning and Recovery:** The geometry-grounded supervisor, providing condition-level diagnostics and structured feedback, enables targeted recovery actions. Instead of restarting entire tasks, the system can perform specific corrective actions like re-observation, re-grounding, or skill adaptation. This mechanism is vital for sustained autonomous operation and resilience in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

5. **Integrated Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation:** The framework effectively coordinates gait-conditioned reinforcement-learned locomotion with decoupled upper-body MPC-based manipulation. This integrated approach ensures that complex tasks requiring both navigation and precise object interaction can be performed while maintaining humanoid balance and stability, which is a fundamental challenge in humanoid control.

6. **Potential for Broader Application:** The principles of verifiable task programs, geometry-grounded supervision, and feedback-driven recovery could be extended beyond humanoids to other complex robotic systems, improving their reliability and safety. The training-light nature of the framework also suggests that it could be more readily deployable in scenarios where extensive data collection for large-scale learning is impractical.

In conclusion, "Cybo-Waiter" provides a principled framework for building more reliable and resilient humanoid agents. Its emphasis on explicit verification, diagnostic feedback, and targeted recovery represents a significant step towards enabling humanoids to perform complex, long-horizon tasks dependably in unstructured human environments.
