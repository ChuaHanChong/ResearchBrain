# UP-Fuse: Uncertainty-guided LiDAR-Camera Fusion for 3D Panoptic Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> UP-Fuse introduces an uncertainty-guided LiDAR-camera fusion framework for 3D panoptic segmentation, dynamically modulating visual feature influence based on predicted reliability to enhance robustness under sensor degradation and domain shifts. The framework achieves competitive performance on benchmarks like Panoptic nuScenes (80.7% PQ) and significantly outperforms baselines in maintaining accuracy during camera sensor dropout, calibration drift, and nighttime conditions.

## Problem

- Existing LiDAR-camera fusion methods for 3D panoptic segmentation exhibit significant performance degradation under adverse camera conditions, such as sensor failure, calibration errors, or visual domain shifts.
- Current fusion approaches primarily focus on feature relevance but lack explicit mechanisms to assess and account for the *reliability* or *validity* of features from different modalities.
- Converting 2D range-view predictions to dense 3D point cloud outputs often introduces spatial ambiguities and boundary discontinuities, compromising 3D segmentation accuracy.

## Method

- The **UP-Fuse** framework integrates an ==Uncertainty-Aware Fusion Module== that quantifies feature instability under diverse degradations to predict uncertainty, dynamically modulating cross-modal interaction.
- A ==Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder== is introduced to process fused range-view features and directly predict 3D panoptic segmentation masks, mitigating spatial ambiguities and boundary discontinuities inherent in 2D-to-3D lifting.
- A new multi-modal 3D panoptic benchmark for the Waymo Open Dataset was established to facilitate research in robust multimodal 3D perception.

## Results

- Achieved competitive Panoptic Quality (PQ) on standard benchmarks, including **80.7%** PQ on Panoptic nuScenes validation (**5.8%** gain over LiDAR-only) and **60.9%** PQ on the new Panoptic Waymo benchmark.
- Demonstrated superior robustness to camera sensor dropout, showing only a **1.2%** PQ reduction compared to its LiDAR-only baseline, while other fusion baselines experienced **4.2-5.0%** PQ drops below their LiDAR-only performance.
- Maintained robust performance under calibration drift (limiting PQ degradation to **4.4%** at 5° rotation compared to **8.3%** for IAL) and visual domain shifts (showing a **0.1%** PQ improvement relative to LiDAR-only in nighttime scenes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly quantifying and integrating feature reliability (uncertainty) in multimodal fusion is crucial for maintaining performance under diverse sensor degradations and visual domain shifts.
> - Dynamically attenuating the influence of unreliable visual cues through uncertainty modulation prevents them from negatively impacting the overall fused representation, leading to greater robustness.
> - A dedicated hybrid 2D-3D decoder can effectively bridge range-view processing and 3D point cloud output, directly addressing inherent projection ambiguities and improving segmentation accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Three-dimensional (3D) panoptic segmentation is a perception task that unifies both semantic and instance understanding of a scene. This capability is considered essential for autonomous systems, particularly in robotic perception and autonomous driving. LiDAR sensors provide precise geometric measurements, which are valuable for 3D scene understanding. However, LiDAR data are inherently sparse and lack the rich appearance cues (e.g., texture, color) that are often necessary for accurate segmentation of small, distant, or geometrically ambiguous objects.

To address the limitations of LiDAR-only systems, researchers have integrated dense, high-resolution camera data, leveraging the complementary information provided by visual sensors. This multimodal fusion has been shown to enhance 3D perception tasks, including panoptic segmentation. A significant challenge in multimodal fusion, however, lies in effectively combining these diverse data streams. Models must learn not only which information is relevant to fuse but also *when* to trust each modality, as sensor inputs can vary in reliability.

Existing LiDAR-camera fusion approaches for 3D panoptic segmentation generally improve performance over LiDAR-only methods by emphasizing relevance-based feature selection. However, these methods commonly exhibit a critical vulnerability: their performance degrades significantly under adverse conditions such as camera sensor failure, calibration errors, or visual domain shifts (e.g., adverse weather, nighttime). In such scenarios, camera inputs can become unreliable, leading to compromised perception system reliability, which is a concern for safety-critical applications.

This research addresses a limitation in the current landscape by proposing a fusion paradigm that explicitly considers the reliability, in addition to the relevance, of visual features. It aims to develop a system that can adaptively integrate modalities, attenuating the influence of unreliable camera data when necessary, thereby maintaining robust performance even when one modality is degraded or fails. This focus on uncertainty-awareness in multimodal fusion distinguishes it from approaches that primarily concentrate on feature alignment or content-aware integration without an explicit mechanism for reliability assessment under varying sensor conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the observation that while LiDAR-camera fusion generally enhances 3D panoptic segmentation, it introduces a critical failure mode: the perception system's reliability can be significantly compromised under adverse conditions affecting the camera sensor. These conditions include degradation (e.g., overexposure, underexposure, blur), calibration drift (misalignment between sensors), and complete sensor failure (dropout). Existing fusion approaches typically do not explicitly account for these scenarios, leading to substantial performance degradation or even failures that fall below LiDAR-only baselines when camera inputs become unreliable. The paper identifies a notable challenge in the current research landscape: existing fusion methods are not designed to discern the *validity* or *reliability* of features, in addition to their relevance, for adaptive and context-dependent integration across modalities.

To address this challenge, the key objectives of the UP-Fuse framework are:

1. **Develop an uncertainty-aware multimodal fusion framework (UP-Fuse) for 3D panoptic segmentation:** The overarching goal is to create a robust system that can maintain strong performance even under various camera sensor degradations, calibration drifts, and sensor failures.
2. **Introduce an Uncertainty-Aware Fusion Module:** This module is designed to jointly learn to evaluate both cross-modal relevance and visual reliability. It aims to dynamically modulate the interaction between LiDAR and camera features using predicted uncertainty maps. These maps are learned by quantifying representational divergence under diverse visual degradations, ensuring that only reliable visual cues influence the fused representation.
3. **Propose a Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder:** This decoder is intended to process the fused range-view features. Its objective is to mitigate spatial ambiguities and boundary discontinuities inherent to the 360° range-view projection and to directly predict 3D panoptic segmentation masks for the original point cloud.
4. **Establish a new multi-modal 3D panoptic benchmark for the Waymo Open Dataset:** Recognizing the need for challenging benchmarks with dense LiDAR and extensive camera coverage, the authors aim to generate panoptic annotations for the Waymo Open Dataset to facilitate further research in robust multimodal 3D perception.

These objectives collectively aim to improve the robustness and reliability of 3D panoptic segmentation in safety-critical robotic perception settings, such as autonomous driving, where maintaining performance under diverse and challenging real-world conditions is crucial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UP-Fuse framework integrates LiDAR and multi-view camera data into a unified 2D range-view representation for 3D panoptic segmentation. The architecture consists of three main components: a Range-View Feature Representation module, an Uncertainty-Aware Fusion Module, and a Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder.

#### 3.1. Range-View Feature Representation

1. **LiDAR Range-View Projection and Encoding:**
 - **Projection:** Raw 3D LiDAR point clouds, comprising Cartesian coordinates (x, y, z) and intensity (i), are projected into a dense 2D spherical range-view image ($L_{RV}$) of resolution H × W. Each 3D point is mapped to pixel coordinates (u, v) based on its horizontal azimuth angle and vertical elevation angle, normalized to the LiDAR sensor's field of view. The range ($r$) is also computed. The features assigned to each pixel in $L_{RV}$ are $[r, z, i]$. To manage occlusions, points are sorted by decreasing range, and only the nearest point (minimum $r$) is retained for each pixel. This projection provides a mapping from 3D to 2D ($P_{3D \to RV}$) and its inverse ($P_{RV \to 3D}$).
 - **Encoding:** The $L_{RV}$ image is processed by a 2D LiDAR encoder, specifically a Swin Transformer, to extract hierarchical feature maps at multiple scales (F$_{L,4}$, F$_{L,8}$, F$_{L,16}$, F$_{L,32}$) with corresponding output strides.

2. **Image Encoding and View Transformation:**
 - **Image Encoding:** Multiple calibrated camera views are individually processed by a frozen image encoder (Swin Transformer, pre-trained) to extract multi-scale image features (F$_{I,4}$, F$_{I,8}$, F$_{I,16}$, F$_{I,32}$).
 - **View Transformation:** To align image features with the LiDAR range-view, a dense correspondence is established. The LiDAR point cloud is first projected into each camera view to generate sparse depth maps, which are then densified using a depth completion method. Each pixel in the densified depth maps is back-projected to a 3D point in the LiDAR coordinate frame. This camera-derived 3D point cloud is then projected into the H × W range-view using the same formulation as for LiDAR, creating a mapping from camera pixels to range-view pixels. This mapping warps the multi-scale image features (F$_{I,s}$) into the range-view domain, producing RV-aligned camera features (F$_{C,s}$). Features from multiple pixels projecting to the same RV location are averaged.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Aware Fusion Module

This module combines RV-aligned camera features (F$_{C,s}$) and LiDAR features (F$_{L,s}$) at each scale (s) through uncertainty-guided cross-modal interaction.

1. **Uncertainty Quantification of Camera Features:**
 - Uncertainty is modeled as feature instability under input degradations. A lightweight 3-layer MLP ($U_{\theta,s}$) is trained to predict this instability.
 - During training, for each original image ($I_{orig}$), a corrupted counterpart ($I_{aug}$) is sampled using diverse non-spatial augmentations (e.g., brightness/contrast shifts, sensor dropout, histogram matching to external datasets like Dark Zurich). A probability of 0.1 allows for $I_{aug} = I_{orig}$ to provide zero-uncertainty samples.
 - Both $I_{orig}$ and $I_{aug}$ are processed by the frozen camera encoder and view transformer to obtain RV-aligned features (F$^{orig}_{C,s}$ and F$^{aug}_{C,s}$).
 - Ground-truth instability ($d_{gt,s}$) is defined as the L2 norm between F$^{orig}_{C,s}$ and F$^{aug}_{C,s}$ at each spatial location. The MLP $U_{\theta,s}$ predicts this instability ($d_{pred,s}$).
 - The network is optimized using a Huber loss between $d_{pred,s}$ and $d_{gt,s}$.
 - The predicted instability is transformed into an uncertainty score ($U_{C,s} \in$) using $1 - \exp(-d_{pred,s})$, which modulates cross-modal interaction.

2. **Uncertainty-Guided Fusion:**
 - Fusion is achieved using deformable attention, which aggregates spatially aligned visual information conditioned on LiDAR queries.
 - Crucially, the camera features used in the attention mechanism are modulated by the predicted uncertainty score: $\tilde{F}^{(l,p)}_{C,s} = (1 - U_{C,s}) \odot F^{(l,p)}_{C,s}$. This element-wise multiplication attenuates contributions from unreliable visual cues.
 - The attended camera features (F$_{A,s}$) are then fused with the LiDAR features (F$_{L,s}$) via residual addition: F$_{F,s}$ = F$_{L,s}$ + F$_{A,s}$. This process aims to preserve LiDAR's geometric accuracy while enriching it with semantically reliable visual context.

#### 3.3. Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder

This component bridges 2D range-view features and 3D point-cloud outputs, addressing issues like label ambiguity from 2D-to-3D lifting and fragmentation due to 360° range-view wrap-around.

1. **2D Pixel and Transformer Decoder:** Fused features (F$_{F,s}$) are processed by a multi-scale deformable attention-based pixel decoder, which outputs multi-scale feature maps ($M_s$) and a high-resolution mask feature map (F$_{mask}$). A Transformer decoder, based on the Mask2Former paradigm, uses learnable object queries to attend to $M_s$.

2. **3D-Aware Mask Head:**
 - This head generates a per-point feature vector for each point in the original 3D point cloud.
 - For each point $p_j$ with true 3D range $r_{j,true}$ and its corresponding RV pixel $(u_j, v_j)$, a K × K search window is defined in the range channel of $L_{RV}$.
 - Within this window, K pixels with the smallest range difference to $r_{j,true}$ are selected as 3D-consistent neighbors.
 - Their corresponding features from F$_{mask}$ are gathered, concatenated, and fused via a lightweight 2-layer MLP to produce a context-aware per-point feature vector (f$_{point,j}$). This process aims to mitigate label bleeding and instance fragmentation.

3. **Panoptic Prediction and Loss:** The final transformer decoder layer outputs refined query embeddings ($Q_{embed}$) and the per-point feature map ($F_{point}$). A class head predicts class logits from $Q_{embed}$. A mask head transforms $Q_{embed}$ into mask embeddings and computes 3D mask logits via a dot product with $F_{point}$. A set-based loss, combining classification, mask, and Dice losses, is computed on a subset of sampled points after bipartite matching between ground-truth instances and queries.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of UP-Fuse was conducted across Panoptic nuScenes, SemanticKITTI, and a newly introduced Panoptic Waymo benchmark. The findings highlight UP-Fuse's competitive performance, efficiency, and robustness under various degradation scenarios.

#### 4.1. Benchmarking Results

- **Panoptic nuScenes:** UP-Fuse (LiDAR-Camera, LC) achieved a Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 80.7% on the validation set, outperforming several multi-modal baselines, including LCPS (79.8% PQ) and Panoptic-FusionNet (77.2% PQ). Its LiDAR-only (L) variant achieved 74.9% PQ, indicating a 5.8% gain from fusion. While IAL-PieAug achieved a higher PQ (82.3%), UP-Fuse operated at 5.7 Frames Per Second (FPS), which is approximately 6 times faster than IAL (0.9 FPS) and substantially quicker than other fusion methods. This demonstrates a balance between accuracy and runtime efficiency in the unified 2D range-view space. The improvement was largely driven by thing classes (PQ$_{th}$ boost of 7.9%). On the test set, UP-Fuse achieved 81.1% PQ.
- **SemanticKITTI:** On the SemanticKITTI validation set, UP-Fuse (LC) attained 61.8% PQ, surpassing LCPS (59.0% PQ) and performing competitively, indicating effective use of visual cues even with limited camera coverage.
- **Panoptic Waymo:** On the new Panoptic Waymo validation set, UP-Fuse (LC) achieved 60.9% PQ, representing a 3.5% gain over its LiDAR-only baseline and outperforming other multi-modal methods like IAL (60.4% PQ).

#### 4.2. Robustness Analysis

- **Robustness to Sensor Dropout:** Under complete camera failure at inference time (L\*), UP-Fuse exhibited a PQ reduction of only 1.2% compared to its LiDAR-only baseline. In contrast, Panoptic-FusionNet, LCPS, and IAL experienced PQ drops of 5.0%, 4.2%, and 4.6% respectively, falling below their own LiDAR-only performances. This outcome suggests that UP-Fuse's uncertainty-driven fusion module effectively suppresses unavailable image features, relying more heavily on the LiDAR representation.
- **Robustness to Calibration Drift:** When simulating mechanical calibration drift with rotational perturbations up to 5°, UP-Fuse limited the performance degradation to 4.4% in PQ. Other baselines, such as IAL, showed larger drops of 8.3% at 5°. This indicates the adaptive behavior of UP-Fuse, where the uncertainty module progressively reduces the influence of unreliable image features as misalignment increases.
- **Robustness to Visual Domain Shift:** When models trained on daytime scenes were evaluated on held-out nighttime scenes (a natural domain shift), UP-Fuse maintained its performance, showing a 0.1% improvement in PQ relative to its LiDAR-only baseline. This indicates that its uncertainty-driven fusion module effectively identifies and attenuates unreliable visual features in degraded conditions. Baseline fusion methods, conversely, exhibited drops of 2.1% to 3.1% in PQ compared to their LiDAR-only baselines, suggesting they incorrectly fuse uninformative image features under domain shift.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Architectural Ablation:** The Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder contributed a 2.1% PQ gain over a 2D-3D post-processing method, confirming its effectiveness in mitigating projection ambiguities. Using dense depth in view transformation provided a 0.8% PQ gain over sparse depth. Deformable Cross-Modal Interaction (D-CMI) resulted in a 2.4% PQ boost compared to simple concatenation. Introducing sensor degradation augmentations initially led to a slight performance drop, which was recovered and surpassed by enabling the Uncertainty-Aware D-CMI, yielding 80.7% PQ. This highlights the necessity of uncertainty-awareness for effectively leveraging diverse augmentations.
- **Hybrid Decoder Design:** Placing the 3D-aware mask head in the final layer of each decoder block yielded optimal performance (80.7% PQ), allowing queries to refine in 2D before 3D lifting. A neighborhood size (K) of 5 for 3D-aware feature aggregation was identified as optimal.
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** The predicted feature instability ($d_{pred}$) showed positive correlation with the ground-truth feature deviation ($d_{gt}$) across various degradations. The correlation was highest for sensor dropout (Pearson correlation $\rho$ = 0.9595) and substantial for out-of-domain shifts ($\rho$ = 0.7802) and fog ($\rho$ = 0.7258). These correlations support the design choice of using feature-level uncertainty to guide fusion.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative comparisons with the IAL baseline on Panoptic nuScenes under sensor dropout, calibration drift, and nighttime domain shift illustrate that UP-Fuse preserves correct semantic labels and instance separation. IAL, in contrast, showed misclassifications (e.g., concrete barriers as man-made structures), incorrect merging of instances (e.g., two trucks into one), and mislabeling of object parts (e.g., vehicle tops as background) in these challenging scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of 3D perception by addressing a notable challenge in LiDAR-camera fusion: the vulnerability of perception systems to camera sensor degradation, calibration inaccuracies, and complete sensor failure. The introduction of UP-Fuse offers a novel approach that specifically focuses on reliability-aware fusion, extending beyond traditional relevance-based fusion strategies.

The key significance of this work includes:

1. **Enhanced Robustness for Safety-Critical Systems:** By explicitly quantifying and leveraging uncertainty in camera features, UP-Fuse demonstrates improved resilience to common adverse conditions. This robustness is critical for real-world robotic deployments, particularly in autonomous driving, where the reliable operation of perception systems under varying environmental and sensor conditions is paramount for safety.
2. **Adaptive Multi-modal Integration:** The uncertainty-guided fusion module represents an advancement in multimodal learning, enabling the network to dynamically modulate the influence of each modality based on its perceived reliability. This adaptive behavior allows the system to attenuate unreliable visual cues, thereby preventing them from negatively impacting the fused representation.
3. **Improved Efficiency and Accuracy Balance:** UP-Fuse achieves competitive panoptic segmentation accuracy while operating at a substantially higher frame rate compared to some state-of-the-art fusion methods. This efficiency, combined with its robustness, presents a practical solution for real-time applications in resource-constrained robotic platforms.
4. **Mitigation of Projection Ambiguities:** The proposed Hybrid 2D-3D Panoptic Decoder offers a refined method for converting 2D range-view predictions into dense 3D point-cloud outputs. By directly addressing spatial ambiguities and 360° discontinuities inherent in range-view representations, it enhances the accuracy of 3D segmentation.
5. **New Benchmark for Research:** The creation of the Panoptic Waymo benchmark, derived from the Waymo Open Dataset, provides a denser and more challenging testbed for multi-modal 3D panoptic segmentation. This benchmark can facilitate future research and comparisons in the domain of robust 3D perception.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to publicly releasing code and models contributes to research transparency, reproducibility, and accelerates further development in uncertainty-aware multimodal perception systems.

In summary, UP-Fuse's focus on integrating uncertainty-awareness into LiDAR-camera fusion represents a step towards more reliable and deployable perception systems in complex and dynamic environments, particularly for applications requiring high safety standards.
