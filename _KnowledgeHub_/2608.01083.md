# Sparse Meets Dense: Correspondence Guided Robotic Manipulation with Rigid-Deformable Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Peking University developed a hybrid correspondence framework that guides robotic manipulation during rigid-deformable object interactions by combining sparse task-specific keypoints with robust dense deformable object tracking. The approach achieved an average success rate of 73.5% in simulation and 65.0% in real-world tasks, outperforming existing methods and demonstrating generalization from a single demonstration.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with manipulation tasks involving interactions between rigid and deformable objects due to complex multi-point contacts and high-dimensional dynamics of deformable bodies.
- Traditional object-centric representations like 6D poses or sparse keypoints are insufficient to capture the rich contact information and full deformation behavior of deformable objects.
- Purely dense representations often require extensive annotated data, can be computationally intensive, and exhibit reduced interpretability, limiting their generalization and data efficiency.

## Method

- A ==hybrid correspondence== representation combines structure-, task-, and interaction-aware **sparse keypoints** with **dense correspondences** for accurate deformable object tracking.
- Utilizes Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to decompose complex tasks into sequential stages, enabling context-aware manipulation.
- Formulates manipulation as a ==closed-loop constraint optimization== problem using positional and directional constraints derived from the hybrid correspondences.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **73.5%** in simulation and **65.0%** in real-world rigid-deformable manipulation tasks across four scenarios, outperforming baselines (e.g., **58.5%** and **47.5%** for Annot. Rekep).
- Demonstrated generalization to novel object shapes and deformations from a single demonstration, achieving **65.2%** success for a complex task like 'insert hanger into shirt' compared to **15.6%** for a baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both the proposed sparse keypoints and dense correspondence tracking, with performance dropping from **71.4%** to **20.1%** without sparse keypoints for a key task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining sparse, task-specific interaction keypoints with robust dense tracking of deformable objects provides a more effective and data-efficient representation for rigid-deformable manipulation.
> - Distilling 2D dense correspondence knowledge into a 3D tracking module, then fine-tuning with limited 3D data, significantly improves accuracy and robustness for deformable objects, even under occlusion.
> - Framing rigid-deformable interactions as a constraint optimization problem using these hybrid correspondences enables precise and stable robotic actions from a single demonstration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a field with ongoing research, particularly concerning tasks involving deformable objects or interactions between rigid and deformable bodies. Such tasks, common in daily life (e.g., hanging clothes, assisting with dressing), present significant challenges for robotic systems. These challenges stem from the multi-point contacts involved and the complex, high-dimensional dynamics of deformable objects, which introduce unpredictability compared to single-object manipulation or rigid-rigid interactions.

Existing approaches to robotic manipulation often rely on object-centric representations such as 6D poses or structural keypoints. While effective for rigid objects, these representations are generally insufficient for complex rigid-deformable interactions. They struggle to capture the rich contact information and the high degrees of freedom (DoF) associated with deformable bodies.

Recent advancements for deformable objects have moved towards dense representations, including affordances or dense correspondences. However, these methods frequently necessitate extensive annotated data, may exhibit reduced interpretability with increasing object diversity, and can introduce redundancy when applied to rigid bodies.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the limitations of both sparse (object-centric) and dense representations in the context of rigid-deformable interactions. It proposes a hybrid approach that integrates the advantages of sparse, task-specific information with the robustness of dense, deformation-aware tracking. By developing a unified, generalizable, and data-efficient representation, the research aims to improve robotic capabilities for complex manipulation tasks that are crucial for household and service robotics. It builds upon ideas from structural representations for manipulation, constrained optimization in motion planning, and the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for task decomposition and constraint generation, while seeking to overcome their individual limitations in rigid-deformable scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research arises from the inherent difficulties robots encounter when performing manipulation tasks that involve interactions between rigid and deformable objects. These tasks are prevalent in human environments, making them essential for household robots, yet they pose significant challenges due to several factors:
- **Complex Multi-Point Contacts:** Interactions between rigid and deformable bodies often involve numerous points of contact, making precise control and prediction difficult.
- **High-Dimensional Dynamics:** Deformable objects exhibit complex and often unpredictable dynamics, with high degrees of freedom, which complicates accurate modeling and state tracking.
- **Insufficient Existing Representations:** Traditional object-centric representations, such as 6D poses or sparse structural keypoints, are limited. They do not effectively capture the contact-rich nature of rigid-deformable interactions nor the full deformation behavior of soft bodies. Conversely, purely dense representations, while capturing deformation, often require extensive data annotation and can be computationally intensive or redundant for rigid components.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a Hybrid Correspondence-Based Representation:** The central objective is to propose a representation that effectively models both the global structures of interacting objects and the local contact details critical for manipulation. This hybrid representation aims to combine:
 - **Structure-, Task-, and Interaction-Aware Sparse Keypoints:** To capture fine-grained, task-specific interaction information between objects. These keypoints are designed to be relevant to both global object structures and local contact regions, addressing the limitations of general structural keypoints.
 - **Dense Correspondences on Deformable Objects:** To enable accurate and robust tracking of the sparse keypoints on deformable bodies throughout complex manipulation sequences, particularly in dynamic and occluded environments. This component is motivated by the difficulty of tracking sparse features on high-dimensional, deforming objects.

2. **Enable Data-Efficient Generalization and One-Shot Transfer:** The research aims to develop a framework that can generalize to novel object shapes, deformations, and scenarios with minimal training data, ideally from a single demonstration. This addresses the practical challenge of acquiring extensive annotated data for every new task or object variation.

3. **Formulate Rigid-Deformable Interactions as Constraint Optimization Problems:** The work seeks to translate the insights from the hybrid correspondence representation into actionable constraints that can guide robotic motion planning effectively, thereby achieving precise and stable manipulation.

By achieving these objectives, the researchers aim to provide a more robust, generalizable, and data-efficient solution for robotic manipulation in environments characterized by rigid-deformable interactions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates a hybrid correspondence-based representation to address rigid-deformable interaction tasks. It comprises four main components: stage decomposition, sparse correspondence modeling, constraint optimization with dense correspondence tracking, and generalization to novel objects.

#### 3.1. Stage Decomposition

The system begins by utilizing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to decompose a complex robotic task, provided as a demonstration sequence and task description, into sequential stages. These stages are categorized into "auxiliary stages" (preparatory actions, e.g., grasping an object) and "interaction stages" (contact-rich rigid-deformable interactions, e.g., inserting a hanger into a shirt). For auxiliary stages, VLMs identify a specific manipulation point and associated constraints. For interaction stages, the subsequent components of the framework are primarily utilized.

#### 3.2. Sparse Correspondences Modeling Interactions

To capture both global structural alignment and local physical interaction details, the method introduces sparse keypoint correspondences ($C_{sparse}$).

#### 3.2.1. Structural Keypoints of Objects

- **Rigid Objects:** Candidate keypoints ($K_r$) are selected using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) on their point clouds ($PC_r$), focusing on stable geometric features.
- **Deformable Objects:** For deformable objects, which have more complex structures, keypoints ($K_d$) are extracted based on their underlying skeletons using UniGarmentManip. This approach aims to capture the object's topology and deformation behavior more effectively than purely geometric methods.

#### 3.2.2. Structural Keypoints of Interactions

To derive task-relevant, local interaction information, sparse correspondences are established based on a single demonstration:
1. **Relative Pose Annotation:** The relative pose between the rigid and deformable objects in their final interaction state (from the demonstration) is manually annotated. This transforms their point clouds ($PC_r$, $PC_d$) into a common 3D space.
2. **Mutual Nearest Neighbors:** For each keypoint in $K_r$, its nearest neighbor in $K_d$ is identified, and vice-versa. A pair is retained as a candidate correspondence only if they are mutual nearest neighbors.
3. **Distance Thresholding:** A distance threshold ($\tau$) is applied to filter out keypoint pairs that are not sufficiently close, ensuring that only those involved in the physical interaction are considered.
4. **Sampling for Efficiency:** Furthest Point Sampling (FPS) is applied to further reduce the number of sparse keypoint pairs (typically 3 to 6 pairs), aiming to preserve geometric and interaction information while minimizing computational cost for real-time optimization.

#### 3.3. Constraint Optimization for Manipulation Sequences

The extracted sparse keypoint correspondences are used to formulate constraints for guiding robotic motion planning. The task execution is framed as a closed-loop optimization problem to find the optimal end-effector poses.

#### 3.3.1. Optimization Constraints

Two types of constraints are defined:
- **Positional Constraint ($c_{pos}$):** This constraint minimizes the Euclidean distance between corresponding keypoint pairs ($k^r_i, k^d_i$) from $C_{sparse}$. It enforces spatial alignment between the rigid and deformable objects.
- **Directional Constraint ($c_{ori}$):** This constraint ensures correct relative orientation. Three representative keypoint pairs (selected via FPS if more than three exist) define two planes, one on the rigid object ($P_r$) and one on the deformable object ($P_d$). The constraint minimizes the angular deviation between the normal vectors of these two planes.

The end-effector pose ($P_{ee}$) is linked to the keypoint positions. For rigid object grasping, $k_r$ is directly computed via rigid transformation of $P_{ee}$. For deformable object interaction, $k_d$ is estimated based on the nearest keypoint to the end-effector.

#### 3.3.2. Constraint Optimization Guided Manipulation with Dense Correspondences

Accurate tracking of sparse keypoints on deformable objects is crucial for closed-loop planning but is challenging due to high-dimensional state space and severe occlusions. To address this, a dense correspondence module, derived from UniGarmentManip and extended to 3D, is employed.
1. **Distilling 2D Knowledge to 3D:** Given a 3D point cloud ($P_{3D}$), it is first projected to 2D ($P_{2D}$). A pretrained 2D UniGarmentManip model extracts 2D dense features ($F_{2D}$). A PointNet++ network ($\psi_{3D}$) is then trained to predict 3D features ($\hat{F}_{3D}$) from $P_{3D}$, supervised by a distillation loss ($L_{distill}$) minimizing the difference between $\hat{F}_{3D}$ and $F_{2D}$.
2. **Fine-tuning with 3D Ground Truth:** A small set of manually annotated 3D deformable object pairs with ground-truth correspondences ($C_{GT}$) is used to fine-tune the network. This incorporates 3D geometric cues via a contrastive loss ($L_{con}$), enhancing accuracy and robustness. The fine-tuning involves cross-deformation and cross-object correspondence losses, with a coarse-to-fine refinement strategy to handle challenging regions.
3. **Keypoint Tracking:** The sparse keypoints are anchored to their corresponding dense representations. The dense correspondence module then maps these keypoints to their updated positions as the deformable object deforms, enabling accurate and real-time constraint updates. For rigid bodies, a 3D tracker is used.

#### 3.4. Generalization to Novel Objects

To generalize to new objects with varying shapes and deformations, the dense correspondence module ($C_{dense}$) is utilized. Sparse keypoints ($C_{sparse}$) from a single demonstration instance are mapped to new object pairs by retrieving dense feature embeddings and performing a nearest-neighbor search in the dense feature space of the novel object. These transferred sparse keypoints ($C'_{sparse}$) then formulate new constraints for optimizing actions in novel scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and generalizability of the proposed framework were evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environment:** GarmentLab implemented on Isaac Sim 4.5.0.
- **Real-World Environment:** A Franka Panda robot and two RealMan RM75-6F arms, each equipped with a PsiBot G0-R dexterous hand. RealSense cameras were used for depth imaging.
- **Tasks:** Four representative rigid-deformable interaction tasks were constructed: (1) inserting a hanger into a shirt, (2) putting a racket into a bag, (3) putting a book into a backpack, and (4) scooping peanuts into a bag. Each task included various rigid and deformable object assets to test diversity and generalization.

#### 4.2. Baselines

The proposed method was compared against two baselines:
- **Annot. Rekep:** This method employs human-annotated relational keypoint constraints and hierarchical optimization for action generation. It relies on DINOv2 for keypoint tracking.
- **UniGarment:** This baseline adapts the UniGarment framework to guide constraint formulation by establishing dense correspondences between rigid and deformable objects based on their final relative positions in a demonstration.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Comparison (Table I)

The proposed method demonstrated improved performance across all tasks in both simulation and real-world settings when compared to the baselines.
- **Simulation Average Success Rate:** Ours: 73.5%, Annot. Rekep: 58.5%, UniGarment: 54.9%.
- **Real-World Average Success Rate:** Ours: 65.0%, Annot. Rekep: 47.5%, UniGarment: 50.0%.
The paper notes that Annot. Rekep's reliance on DINOv2 feature matching for tracking keypoints on soft bodies leads to inconsistencies under deformation, resulting in tracking errors and constraint failures. UniGarment, while using dense correspondences, experienced higher computational costs and inconsistencies in occluded or highly deformable regions, impacting overall accuracy.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table II, Figure 4)

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the necessity of the proposed components:
- **Ours w/o $C_{sparse}$ (DINOv2 keypoints):** Performance significantly dropped (e.g., from 71.4% to 20.1% for "Insert hanger into shirt"). This indicates the critical role of the proposed structure- and interaction-aware sparse keypoints.
- **Ours w/o $C_{dense}$ (DINOv2 pixel-wise feature tracking):** Performance decreased (e.g., from 71.4% to 56.4% for "Insert hanger into shirt"). This demonstrates the importance of dense correspondences for accurate deformable object keypoint tracking, particularly in dynamic and complex scenes, outperforming DINOv2 feature tracking and other 3D trackers like SpatialTracker.
- **Ours w/o $c_{pos}$ (no positional constraint):** Task success rates dropped to 0% for evaluated tasks, indicating that the positional constraint is fundamental for rigid-deformable interaction tasks.
- **Ours w/o $c_{ori}$ (no orientational constraint):** Performance decreased (e.g., from 71.4% to 28.3% for "Insert hanger into shirt"). This suggests the orientational constraint is generally important, although its omission might be tolerable in a few specific interactions where precise alignment is not strictly required.
- **Ours w/ 3D-Tracker:** Performance was similar to "Ours w/o $C_{dense}$" (56.4% for "Insert hanger into shirt"), reinforcing the finding that the proposed dense correspondence tracking is more robust for deformable objects than general 3D trackers.

#### 4.5. Generalization (Table III, Figure 5)

The framework demonstrated generalization capabilities across variations in object shapes, deformations, and poses. Using a single high-quality demonstration, sparse keypoints were extracted and mapped to novel objects via dense correspondences, allowing successful task execution. For "Insert hanger into shirt," generalization success was 65.2% for Ours compared to 15.6% for Rekep and 57.1% for UniGarment, indicating improved transferability.

#### 4.6. Real-World Evaluation (Table I, Appendix A)

The method's effectiveness was confirmed on physical robot setups, with success rates consistent with simulation results, demonstrating the sim-to-real transferability of the approach.

#### 4.7. Comparison with VLM-Generated Constraints (Table IV, Appendix C.3)

When comparing constraints generated solely by VLMs against those designed by the proposed method for precise rigid-deformable interactions, the VLM-generated constraints performed significantly worse (e.g., 25.6% vs 71.4% for "Insert hanger into shirt"). This indicates that current VLMs struggle with the precision required for complex rigid-deformable interaction constraints.

#### 4.8. System Error Breakdown (Figure 7, Appendix E)

An analysis of failure cases revealed that inaccurate 3D tracking of rigid objects (due to occlusion) and incorrect task decomposition or keypoint selection by VLMs were the most significant sources of errors. Errors from sparse and dense correspondences occurred less frequently but were critical when they did.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a notable challenge in robotic manipulation: effectively handling interactions between rigid and deformable objects. The proposed hybrid correspondence-based representation offers a systematic approach to model these complex scenarios, which are prevalent in domestic and service robotics.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to combine the advantages of sparse and dense representations. Sparse, task-specific keypoints efficiently encode critical interaction information, while dense correspondences provide robust and accurate tracking of deformable bodies through complex deformations and occlusions. This integration leads to improved performance and generalization capabilities compared to methods relying solely on sparse or dense representations.

The framework's demonstrated data efficiency, requiring only a single demonstration for generalization to novel object shapes and deformations, is a practical advantage. This feature could reduce the extensive data collection and annotation efforts typically associated with learning robotic manipulation skills, thereby accelerating the deployment of robots in diverse environments.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Household and Service Robots:** The ability to perform tasks like hanging clothes or assisting with dressing more reliably could significantly advance the utility and adoption of robots in daily life settings.
- **Improved Deformable Object Manipulation:** The robust keypoint tracking on deformable objects could benefit a wider range of deformable object manipulation tasks beyond rigid-deformable interactions.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The hybrid correspondence concept and the methodology for distilling 2D dense correspondence knowledge to 3D, combined with constraint optimization, provide a basis for exploring more intricate manipulation problems, including potentially extending to deformable-to-deformable interactions, which the authors identify as a future challenge.

While the method exhibits limitations, particularly with very complex deformable-to-deformable interactions and identified failure modes in rigid object tracking under occlusion or VLM-based task decomposition, it represents a step towards more capable and adaptive robotic systems for handling the complexities of the physical world.
