# Co-training with Ego-centric Video and Demonstration for Robot Navigation Task

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a framework to convert egocentric human walking videos into robot-compatible action datasets for mobile robot navigation. This method, when used for co-training with robot-collected data, enhances a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model's ability to navigate robustly in complex environments and generalize to unseen initial positions, improving success rates in language-conditioned tasks.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for mobile robot navigation requires large, high-quality datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to collect using real robots.
- Abundant egocentric human videos present a scalable data source, but direct transfer to robots is hindered by viewpoint changes and kinematic mismatches (e.g., 6-DoF human motion vs. 2-DoF robot motion).
- Existing methods for leveraging human data often struggle with the domain gap between human-centric observations and robot-centric control signals, leading to unstable or non-transferable behaviors.

## Method

- A multi-stage data processing pipeline was developed to convert 6-DoF estimated camera motion from egocentric human videos into smooth, 2-DoF planar robot-compatible action representations (forward and angular velocities).
- The pipeline includes visual motion estimation using SuperPoint and LightGlue, followed by kinematic projection, velocity clipping, median filtering, and ==Kalman== filtering to refine and smooth trajectories.
- Discretization and mode filtering are applied to generate consistent action commands compatible with a target mobile robot's controller, which are then used in conjunction with robot-collected data to train a VLA model.

## Results

- Models trained solely on human-derived data exhibited low success rates (**11.1%** without distractors, **0%** for unseen positions) due to unstable action generation and an inability to reliably perform tasks.
- The co-trained model achieved success rates comparable to or better than robot-only training in scenarios without distractors (e.g., **100%** for red apple/orange) and significantly outperformed robot-only training in the presence of distractors (up to **94.4%**).
- The combined approach demonstrated improved generalization to untrained initial positions, achieving a **75.0%** success rate overall, successfully navigating where the robot-only model completely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-derived data significantly contributes to a robot's ability to understand language and generate diverse motion patterns, improving generalization to varied environmental conditions.
> - Robot-collected data is crucial for bridging the domain gap, providing robot-specific visual observations and actuation dynamics necessary for stable and consistent action generation.
> - Joint training with both human-derived and robot-collected data effectively mitigates the limitations of each data source, enabling robust navigation with enhanced language-conditioning and generalization capabilities.
