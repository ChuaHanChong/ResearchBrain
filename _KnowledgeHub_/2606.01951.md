# Co-training with Ego-centric Video and Demonstration for Robot Navigation Task

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a framework to convert egocentric human walking videos into robot-compatible action datasets for mobile robot navigation. This method, when used for co-training with robot-collected data, enhances a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model's ability to navigate robustly in complex environments and generalize to unseen initial positions, improving success rates in language-conditioned tasks.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for mobile robot navigation requires large, high-quality datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to collect using real robots.
- Abundant egocentric human videos present a scalable data source, but direct transfer to robots is hindered by viewpoint changes and kinematic mismatches (e.g., 6-DoF human motion vs. 2-DoF robot motion).
- Existing methods for leveraging human data often struggle with the domain gap between human-centric observations and robot-centric control signals, leading to unstable or non-transferable behaviors.

## Method

- A multi-stage data processing pipeline was developed to convert 6-DoF estimated camera motion from egocentric human videos into smooth, 2-DoF planar robot-compatible action representations (forward and angular velocities).
- The pipeline includes visual motion estimation using SuperPoint and LightGlue, followed by kinematic projection, velocity clipping, median filtering, and ==Kalman== filtering to refine and smooth trajectories.
- Discretization and mode filtering are applied to generate consistent action commands compatible with a target mobile robot's controller, which are then used in conjunction with robot-collected data to train a VLA model.

## Results

- Models trained solely on human-derived data exhibited low success rates (**11.1%** without distractors, **0%** for unseen positions) due to unstable action generation and an inability to reliably perform tasks.
- The co-trained model achieved success rates comparable to or better than robot-only training in scenarios without distractors (e.g., **100%** for red apple/orange) and significantly outperformed robot-only training in the presence of distractors (up to **94.4%**).
- The combined approach demonstrated improved generalization to untrained initial positions, achieving a **75.0%** success rate overall, successfully navigating where the robot-only model completely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human-derived data significantly contributes to a robot's ability to understand language and generate diverse motion patterns, improving generalization to varied environmental conditions.
> - Robot-collected data is crucial for bridging the domain gap, providing robot-specific visual observations and actuation dynamics necessary for stable and consistent action generation.
> - Joint training with both human-derived and robot-collected data effectively mitigates the limitations of each data source, enabling robust navigation with enhanced language-conditioning and generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is progressively moving towards enabling service robots to operate autonomously in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Imitation learning, which allows robots to acquire behaviors from demonstrations, has emerged as a key paradigm for this purpose. Recently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated potential for language-conditioned robot control across diverse tasks. These models integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation into a unified framework.

A significant challenge in developing robust VLA models is the requirement for large-scale, high-quality training datasets. Collecting such data using real robots is a costly and time-consuming endeavor. This limitation has motivated researchers to explore alternative data sources, such as readily available egocentric human videos. Prior work has investigated the use of human videos to augment datasets for robot manipulation tasks. However, directly applying these approaches to mobile robot navigation presents specific difficulties due to the dynamic viewpoint changes inherent in human locomotion and the kinematic differences between human movement and mobile robot capabilities. The domain gap between human-centric observations and robot-centric control signals must be addressed for effective transfer.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing the data scarcity issue for mobile robot navigation. It distinguishes itself from existing foundation-model-based navigation systems, such as LM-Nav and VLMaps, which often focus on high-level semantic map construction and subgoal planning, delegating low-level trajectory generation to conventional navigation pipelines. In contrast, this research aims to directly learn robotic navigation behaviors, including local trajectory generation, from egocentric human videos, thereby reducing reliance on explicit subgoal planning.

Furthermore, while other imitation learning methods from human data have utilized inverse kinematics or learned human-centric latent spaces, they often struggle with direct transfer to robotic systems due to the inherent domain gap in viewpoints and motion characteristics. This paper proposes a method that explicitly converts human egocentric walking videos into robot-compatible action representations through kinematic projection and trajectory smoothing, thereby directly bridging the gap between human observation and robot action space. This direct conversion method offers an approach to leveraging the scalability of human video data while maintaining compatibility with robot kinematics and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent difficulties in training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for mobile robot navigation. VLA models hold promise for enabling robots to perform complex tasks through natural language instructions in unstructured environments. However, their performance is contingent upon the availability of substantial volumes of high-quality training data. The collection of such datasets using real robots is resource-intensive, involving considerable time, labor, and financial cost.

In contrast, egocentric human videos, which capture tasks from a first-person perspective, are becoming increasingly abundant and can be collected with relative ease and lower cost. This disparity in data availability motivates the exploration of human videos as a scalable alternative data source for robot learning.

The key challenges identified by the authors in directly leveraging egocentric human videos for mobile robot navigation are:
1. **Viewpoint Changes:** Human locomotion involves significant and often unstable viewpoint changes, including vertical oscillations and pitch rotations, which differ from the typically more stable camera perspectives of ground mobile robots.
2. **Kinematic Mismatch:** Human motion is 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) and often holonomic, whereas ground mobile robots typically operate under non-holonomic constraints, primarily executing planar motion (forward/backward translation and yaw rotation). Directly mapping human actions to robot kinematics is not straightforward.
3. **Domain Gap:** Discrepancies exist in visual observations (e.g., field of view, camera properties) and dynamic characteristics between human recordings and robot platforms.

To address these challenges, the overarching objective of this work is to develop a framework that effectively converts egocentric human walking videos into a usable dataset for mobile robot imitation learning.

More specifically, the key objectives are:
- To propose a methodology for estimating camera motion from egocentric human videos and transforming these 6-DoF trajectories into 2-DoF action representations that are kinematically compatible with ground mobile robots.
- To develop a pipeline that includes visual motion estimation, motion smoothing, kinematic projection, and action discretization to produce high-quality, robot-compatible image-action pairs from human videos.
- To demonstrate that by jointly training a VLA model on both this human-derived dataset and a smaller robot-collected dataset, the model can achieve improved language understanding and more robust action generation compared to training with either data source alone.
- To experimentally validate the effectiveness and scalability of using human egocentric videos as a data source for language-conditioned mobile robot navigation in real-world scenarios, including unseen environments and the presence of distractors.

The main contributions of this work are explicitly stated as:
- A framework for converting egocentric walking videos into mobile robot imitation learning datasets.
- A trajectory transformation method that generates robot-compatible action representations from human locomotion.
- Experimental validation showing improved language-conditioned navigation through joint training with human and robot data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework aims to generate datasets of image-action pairs for robot learning from egocentric human videos and subsequently use these datasets, in conjunction with robot-collected data, to train a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for navigation. The methodology consists of a multi-stage data processing pipeline followed by an imitation learning phase.

#### 3.1. A. Data Processing Pipeline

The human-derived dataset generation involves three main steps:

1. **Visual Motion Estimation from Human Videos (Sec. III-A):**
 - **Goal:** Estimate a 6-DoF camera trajectory from egocentric videos.
 - **Feature Extraction:** SuperPoint, a self-supervised convolutional network, is used to robustly detect keypoints and extract descriptors from consecutive video frames. This method is selected for its performance under conditions like motion blur common in egocentric videos.
 - **Feature Matching:** LightGlue, a Transformer-based feature matching algorithm, establishes correspondences between keypoints across frames. It employs self-attention (within-image relationships) and cross-attention (between-image relationships) for robust matching, even with noisy egocentric motion.
 - **Motion Estimation:** The matched feature correspondences are used to estimate the essential matrix (E) between consecutive frames. This matrix encodes the relative rotation and translation of the camera. The RANSAC algorithm is applied during estimation to mitigate outlier correspondences caused by dynamic objects or environmental noise. The relative camera poses are then accumulated to reconstruct the full 6-DoF camera trajectory.

2. **Motion Smoothing and Kinematic Projection (Sec. III-B):**
 - **Goal:** Convert the noisy 6-DoF camera motion into smooth, robot-compatible 2-DoF planar motion commands (forward velocity and angular velocity).
 - **Kinematic Projection:** The estimated relative pose (ΔT_k) between frames is projected onto an SE(2) representation, suitable for non-holonomic ground robots. Forward velocity (v_k) is derived by projecting the translation vector onto the robot's forward direction, and angular velocity (ω_k) is computed from the planar component of the rotation matrix. Due to monocular visual odometry's scale ambiguity, the translational component is interpreted as a normalized "strength of motion intention" rather than a metric velocity.
 - **Velocity Clipping:** The projected velocities (v_k, ω_k) are clipped within predefined physical limits (e.g., v_min=-0.3, v_max=0.3, ω_min=-0.5, ω_max=0.5) to ensure they are physically plausible for the target robot.
 - **Median Filtering:** A median filter with a window size of N=5 is applied to suppress impulsive outliers in the instantaneous velocity estimates, introducing a small phase delay.
 - **Kalman Filtering:** A Kalman filter, based on a constant-velocity model, further smooths the velocity trajectory by combining noisy observations with a motion prior. The state vector comprises forward and angular velocities, with empirically tuned process and observation noise parameters (Q=0.005, R=0.5) to favor temporal smoothness.

3. **Action Discretization for Target Robot Consistency (Sec. III-C):**
 - **Goal:** Convert the smoothed velocity signals into discrete navigation actions compatible with the target robot's controller.
 - **Forward Velocity Discretization:** The filtered forward velocity (v) is converted into discrete actions (0.2 m/s forward, -0.2 m/s backward, or 0 m/s stop) using asymmetric thresholds (τ_fwd=0.3, τ_bwd=-1.5). Backward motion requires a larger threshold, reflecting a stronger intentional action. The normalized intention is mapped to the robot's nominal speed (0.2 m/s).
 - **Rotational Velocity Discretization:** The rotational velocity (ω), which retains geometric consistency, is discretized into turning actions (+/- ω_turn, or 0 rad/s) using a threshold (τ_turn=0.003). This suppresses small fluctuations from body sway.
 - **Mode Filter:** A final post-processing step applies a mode filter to remove short-duration action reversals (fewer than 12 frames or ~200 ms), enforcing temporal consistency and generating control commands feasible for robot actuators.

#### 3.2. B. Imitation Learning with VLA Architecture (Sec. III-D)

The processed human-derived dataset is combined with a robot-collected dataset to train a VLA model for language-conditioned navigation.

- **VLA Architecture:** The model takes a visual observation, a language instruction, the robot state, a noisy action sequence, and a diffusion time step as inputs.
 - **Prompt Encoder:** Siglip2 is used to encode language instructions into language tokens.
 - **Visual Encoder:** A dual-encoder architecture consisting of Siglip2 and DINOv3 is employed. Siglip2 provides aligned representations with language, while DINOv3 captures fine-grained visual details. Both encoders are kept frozen to preserve pretrained representations. The features from both are linearly fused.
 - **Transformer-based Adapter:** Fused visual and language features are fed into a Transformer adapter. Cross-attention, with language features as queries and visual features as keys/values, produces vision-conditioned language representations.
 - **Action Generation:** An 8-layer Diffusion Transformer (DiT) predicts a sequence of future actions (32 steps). Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) is used for deterministic action generation from noise. The DiT incorporates pre-normalization and Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) for time-step embedding and uses cross-attention to condition generated actions on the encoder's visual and language features.

- **Dataset Composition:** Training is performed on a combined dataset:
 - **Human-derived dataset:** Generated using the described pipeline from egocentric walking videos. Provides scalable, low-cost data with diverse motion patterns for generalization.
 - **Robot-collected dataset:** Obtained from real-world robot executions. Mitigates the domain gap by exposing the model to robot-specific visual observations (e.g., camera field of view, viewpoint) and dynamics, ensuring action consistency with the real robot.

#### 3.3. C. Experimental Setup

- **Task:** Fruit-search navigation in an indoor laboratory environment using a CobotMagic dual-arm mobile manipulator. The robot must locate a specified fruit (red apple, green apple, or orange) and navigate to a position directly in front of it.
- **Datasets:**
 - **Robot-collected:** 150 trials (50 per goal location) on CobotMagic, fixed camera (480x640, 30 fps). Linear velocity 0.2 m/s, angular 0.4 rad/s. Target: red apple only. Initial positions: 4 predefined (S_A, S_B, S_C, S_D).
 - **Human-derived:** 240 videos collected with an iPhone (1080p, 60 fps) by a human performing the same task. Includes all three fruits. Two conditions: simple (target fruit only, 60 trials) and complex (distractors present, 20 trials). Start and goal positions uniformly distributed.
- **Training Details:** VLA model trained via imitation learning. SigLIP2 (base 256) and DINOv3 (vit base patch16 dinov3) are frozen pretrained encoders. Adapter and DiT are 8-layer Transformers (hidden dim 512, feedforward dim 2048). AdamW optimizer, learning rate 1e-4, cosine scheduling. Batch size 4, gradient accumulation over 32 steps for 400,000 iterations. Standard geometric and photometric data augmentations applied.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Configurations:** Robot-only training, human-only training, joint training (Ours).
 - **Metrics:** Task success rate (robot reaches within 1m of target and stays for >1s). Failures: collisions, stationary/rotational behavior >5s.
 - **Conditions:**
 - Trained initial positions (S_A-S_D), with and without distractor objects (NoD, Dist).
 - Untrained initial positions (S_E, S_F) to assess generalization, with red apple only (no distractors).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the stability of action generation, generalization to language prompts, and robustness to unseen initial positions across three training configurations: robot-only, human-only, and a combined approach (Ours).

#### 4.1. A. Evaluation from Trained Initial Positions

- **No Distractors (NoD) Setting:**
 - The model trained solely on human-derived data exhibited a low success rate of 11.1%. This indicated that direct training on human-derived data alone led to unstable action generation and an inability to reliably perform the task, which the authors attributed to the domain gaps in camera viewpoints and motion characteristics. For instance, it failed to execute sequences requiring both rotation and forward motion, instead often continuing to rotate.
 - The robot-only model achieved a high success rate of 88.9%.
 - The combined (Ours) model achieved success rates comparable to or better than the robot-only model, with 100.0% for red apple and orange, and 88.9% for green apple. This suggests that the combined training strategy effectively mitigates the issues associated with human-only data while maintaining performance. The combined model also demonstrated improved stability, with fewer collisions.

- **With Distractors (Dist) Setting:**
 - The success rate for both the human-only and robot-only models decreased. The robot-only model's performance dropped significantly to 22.2%, often misidentifying distractor objects as targets.
 - The combined model, despite a performance degradation compared to its NoD performance, still achieved significantly higher success rates than the other two configurations (88.9% for red/green apple, 94.4% for orange). This improvement was particularly evident in tasks requiring object differentiation based on language instructions, such as navigating towards an orange while red and green apples were present. This result indicates that incorporating human-derived data enhanced the model's ability to generate appropriate actions conditioned on specific language instructions, even in visually complex environments.

- **Trajectory Comparison:** Visual comparison of robot trajectories showed that the human-only model frequently failed by exhibiting erratic or repetitive motions, such as continuous rotation. In contrast, both the robot-only and combined models generated smooth and purposeful trajectories, indicating that the proposed method of incorporating human-derived data did not compromise the smoothness or stability of action generation.

#### 4.2. B. Evaluation from Untrained Initial Positions

- **Generalization to Unseen Positions (S_E, S_F):**
 - The human-only model recorded a 0% success rate across all untrained initial positions and goal locations. This finding further reinforced the conclusion that human-derived data alone is insufficient for robust generalization in robot navigation.
 - The robot-only model achieved some success when initialized at S_E (5/6 trials), but failed in all cases (0/6 trials) when initialized at S_F. Failures included collisions or an inability to identify a feasible forward direction, leading to the robot remaining stationary or rotating in place.
 - The combined (Ours) model achieved a total success rate of 75.0% on the unseen initial positions (9/12 trials). It successfully navigated from S_E (6/6 trials) and showed partial success from S_F (3/6 trials), where the robot-only model completely failed. This demonstrates that combining robot-collected and human-derived data improved both the stability of action generation and the exploration capabilities of the robot under novel initial conditions, enabling it to find feasible paths where other models could not.

#### 4.3. C. Discussion of Findings

The experimental results indicate that robot-collected and human-derived data fulfill distinct and complementary roles in the training process. Human-derived data appears to contribute to the model's capacity for diverse motion patterns and flexible responses to language instructions, which translates into improved generalization to varied environmental conditions. Robot-collected data, conversely, provides crucial robot-specific characteristics, such as precise camera viewpoints and actuation dynamics, which are essential for stable and consistent action generation.

While using human-derived data alone proved insufficient due to inherent domain gaps, the proposed approach effectively leveraged the benefits of this scalable data source by integrating it with robot-collected data. This dual-source training mitigated the domain gap, allowing the model to adapt to real-world robotic environments while retaining the broader generalizability offered by human demonstrations. The authors highlighted that human-derived data can be collected with significantly less cost and effort, making the combined approach practical for scalable dataset construction.

Limitations identified include the continued dependence on some robot-collected data to bridge the domain gap, the uninvestigated optimal mixing ratio of data, and the variability in quality of human egocentric videos. The current focus on a 2-DoF ground mobile robot also implies that the gap between human and robot kinematics is only partially addressed. However, the core method, based on visual odometry and essential matrix decomposition, is presented as potentially extensible to other robotic platforms by adapting the kinematic models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents a significant advancement in addressing the data scarcity problem for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in mobile robot navigation. Its potential impact spans several areas:

- **Scalable Data Generation for Robot Learning:** The most immediate impact is the provision of a scalable and cost-effective methodology for generating high-quality training datasets for mobile robots. By effectively converting readily available egocentric human videos into robot-compatible action representations, the framework reduces the reliance on laborious and expensive real-robot data collection. This democratizes access to large datasets, potentially accelerating research and development in robot learning.
- **Enhanced VLA Model Robustness and Generalization:** The co-training approach, combining human-derived and robot-collected data, leads to VLA models that exhibit improved language understanding and more robust action generation. The findings demonstrate better generalization to unseen environments and enhanced performance in cluttered scenarios with distractors. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world settings where they must interpret nuanced commands and navigate complex visual scenes.
- **Bridging the Human-Robot Domain Gap:** The detailed methodology for motion estimation, kinematic projection, smoothing, and action discretization effectively bridges the inherent domain gap between human locomotion characteristics and robot kinematic constraints. This systematic transformation allows human demonstrations, which are intuitive and varied, to be directly translated into practical robot control signals.
- **Practical Applicability in Service Robotics:** By enabling robots to robustly perform language-conditioned navigation tasks in diverse and previously unencountered environments, the work contributes to the development of more capable and versatile service robots. Such robots could assist in households, public spaces, or industrial settings, responding to natural language commands for tasks like fetching objects or navigating to specific locations.
- **Foundation for Future Robot Learning Architectures:** The core idea of leveraging visual odometry and essential matrix decomposition for motion estimation from egocentric videos is versatile. This suggests that the framework could be extended to other robotic platforms beyond 2-DoF ground robots, such as omnidirectional mobile robots or even humanoid robots, by adapting the kinematic projection module. This potential for generalization could pave the way for broader applications of human-derived data in various robot learning contexts.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective strategy for leveraging the abundance of human egocentric video data to train sophisticated VLA models for mobile robot navigation. It contributes to making robot learning more efficient, scalable, and adaptable to real-world complexities.
