# SAILViT: Towards Robust and Generalizable Visual Backbones for MLLMs via Gradual Feature Refinement

> [!summary] Summary
> SAILViT introduces a three-stage 'gradual feature refinement' training pipeline for Vision Transformers, yielding visual backbones that are more robust and generalizable for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This method systematically bridges modality semantic gaps and improves MLLM performance across various benchmarks, including multidisciplinary multimodal Q&A and logical reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing visual encoders in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often suffer from parameter initialization conflicts and semantic gaps when integrated with LLMs.
- Current visual backbone pre-training methods (e.g., multimodal contrastive learning, autoregressive modeling) struggle to incorporate comprehensive 'world knowledge' into visual features.
- Suboptimal visual backbones create performance bottlenecks in MLLMs, limiting their potential in complex visual understanding and reasoning tasks.

## Method

- SAILViT proposes a novel three-stage ==gradual feature refinement== training pipeline for Vision Transformers (ViTs), building on architectures like AIMv2.
- Stage 1 focuses on ==Coarse-grained Modality Alignment==, training only the MLP connector to establish initial links between visual and linguistic representations.
- Stage 2 performs 'Fine-grained Modality Alignment', optimizing both the visual backbone and connector, followed by Stage 3, ==World Knowledge Infusion==, which jointly trains the visual backbone, connector, and LLM on diverse, complex data.

## Results

- SAILViT-Large achieved average relative performance improvements of **2.3%** on InternLM2.5, **5.5%** on Qwen2.5, and **5.4%** on Qwen3 series compared to baselines on the OpenCompass benchmark.
- The method demonstrated strong generalizability, showing consistent performance gains across different LLM families and sizes, and proved effective when applied to other ViT architectures.
- On standard visual recognition tasks (ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-A/R/V2), SAILViT-Large and SAILViT-Huge improved average performance by **1.5%** and **2.11%** respectively, compared to their AIMv2 counterparts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive, hierarchical training process effectively aligns visual features with linguistic semantics and infuses world knowledge directly into the visual backbone.
> - Jointly training the visual backbone, connector, and LLM in the final 'World Knowledge Infusion' stage is crucial for comprehensive cross-modal reasoning and overall MLLM performance.
> - The 'gradual feature refinement' strategy yields visual backbones that not only enhance MLLMs but also extract more effective general-purpose visual features for traditional computer vision tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has become a frontier in artificial intelligence, aiming to equip large language models (LLMs) with visual comprehension capabilities. This field primarily relies on the "visual encoder + connector + LLM" paradigm, where Vision Transformers (ViTs) serve as the foundation for visual feature extraction.

Existing approaches to training visual encoders, such as ViTs and their variants, largely fall into two categories:
1. **Multimodal Contrastive Learning:** Methods like CLIP and SigLIP pre-train visual encoders on large-scale image-text pairs to capture discriminative features by aligning representations in a shared embedding space. While effective for basic perception, these methods often struggle with sophisticated spatial perception tasks, are sensitive to scaling limitations, and can be affected by noise in large-scale datasets.
2. **Autoregressive Modeling:** Approaches like InternViT and AIMv2 employ generative objectives, often combining image pixel reconstruction or text autoregressive loss, to provide more intensive supervision. However, a significant challenge with these pre-training strategies is ensuring "parameter alignment and consistent enhancement with LLMs for objective optimization" when integrated into MLLMs. The inherent semantic gap between independently pre-trained visual encoders and LLMs, as well as potential parameter initialization conflicts, often leads to performance bottlenecks during the subsequent co-training (instruction-tuning) phase of MLLMs. Furthermore, existing visual backbones are often limited by pre-training data that focuses on basic perception, lacking comprehensive world knowledge crucial for complex multimodal reasoning.

SAILViT directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel, three-stage "gradual feature refinement" training pipeline for visual backbones. Instead of relying solely on existing pre-trained ViTs, SAILViT aims to mitigate the aforementioned conflicts and discrepancies *before* the final MLLM training. This progressive learning approach stands in contrast to common practices that might freeze the visual backbone during MLLM fine-tuning or use less targeted alignment strategies. By focusing on deep feature alignment and world knowledge infusion within the visual backbone's pre-training itself, SAILViT seeks to provide a more "robust and generalizable" visual foundation that seamlessly integrates with and enhances LLMs, pushing the performance boundaries of MLLMs in complex multimodal interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel training methodology for Vision Transformers (ViTs) that yields robust and generalizable visual backbones, specifically designed to enhance the performance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in complex visual understanding and reasoning tasks.

The key motivations behind SAILViT are driven by critical shortcomings observed in current MLLM architectures:

- **Parameter Initialization Conflicts:** When independently pre-trained ViTs are integrated with LLMs through connectors (e.g., MLPs), their differing parameter initialization schemes and learned representation spaces can create conflicts, hindering optimal co-training.
- **Modality Semantic Gaps:** A significant semantic discrepancy often exists between visual features extracted by a ViT and the linguistic representations of an LLM. Current pre-training methods for ViTs do not adequately bridge this gap, especially when it comes to incorporating rich "world knowledge."
- **Performance Bottlenecks in MLLMs:** The combined effect of parameter conflicts and semantic gaps leads to suboptimal visual backbones, which in turn causes performance bottlenecks in MLLMs, preventing them from achieving their full potential in tasks requiring sophisticated multimodal interactions and reasoning.
- **Limited World Knowledge in Visual Encoders:** Existing ViT pre-training often focuses on basic visual perception (e.g., image-text pair alignment), neglecting the infusion of comprehensive world knowledge necessary for complex reasoning, question answering, and logical inference in MLLMs.

SAILViT aims to overcome these challenges by introducing a "gradual feature refinement" strategy. This approach is motivated by the hypothesis that a progressive, hierarchical learning process can better align visual features with linguistic semantics and infuse essential world knowledge directly into the visual backbone, thereby facilitating a more harmonious and effective co-training with LLMs for diverse downstream tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAILViT proposes a novel three-stage gradual feature refinement training procedure for its vision foundation model, building upon existing Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures (specifically, AIMv2 is used as the base). The core idea is to progressively bridge the modality semantic gap and mitigate parameter initialization conflicts between the visual backbone and the LLM, leading to more robust and generalized visual representations. The training follows a standard autoregressive objective, where the loss is constrained to text tokens for gradient propagation.

The three distinct training stages are:

#### 3.1. Coarse-grained Modality Alignment

- **Objective:** To establish an initial bridge between visual and linguistic representations and prepare the connector for more complex learning, ensuring training stability in subsequent stages.
- **Trainable Components:** Only the MLP-driven connector is trained. The visual backbone (ViT) and the LLM are kept frozen.
- **Data:** Approximately 8 million basic perception training samples, consisting of 4.9M high-quality caption data (from SAIL-Caption) and 3.1M OCR data (from IDL-WDS).
- **Training Details:** Learning rate of 2e-4, batch size of 1,920, for one epoch. This warm-up phase allows the connector to rapidly adapt to mapping features between different modalities.

#### 3.2. Fine-grained Modality Alignment

- **Objective:** To enhance the expressiveness and richness of visual element semantics by allowing the visual backbone to learn more informative representations, more closely aligned with real-world distributions.
- **Trainable Components:** Both the visual backbone (ViT) and the connector are simultaneously optimized. The LLM remains frozen.
- **Data:** Enriched and more complex data, including 6.7M caption data (from SAIL-Caption), additional OCR data (from DocStruct), and new video caption data (ShareGPTVideo, Charades, Mementos). The inclusion of video data helps the ViT capture dynamic and more diverse visual semantics.
- **Training Details:** Learning rate reduced to 2e-5, batch size of 512, for one epoch. The reduced learning rate is to prevent catastrophic forgetting as more parameters are unfrozen.

#### 3.3. World Knowledge Infusion

- **Objective:** To enable the visual backbone to incorporate comprehensive world knowledge and narrow parameter distribution gaps with the LLMs, strengthening logical reasoning efficacy in dynamic feature adaptation.
- **Trainable Components:** All parameters are unlocked: the visual backbone (ViT), the connector, and the LLM are jointly trained.
- **Data:** A large number of diverse mixed data sources, covering different disciplines, topics, and domains. This includes various Q&A datasets (e.g., Infinity-MM, ShareGPT4V, OKVQA), Math-related datasets, additional OCR datasets, and plain text data (Openhermes 2.5, Magpie) to maintain the LLM's original generative capacity. The total data for this stage is substantial, including 3.1M caption, 9.6M OCR, 7.1M OpenQA, 4.8M Text, 1.5M Math, 0.9M ShortQA, and 9M Mix Data.
- **Training Details:** Learning rate further reduced to 1e-5, batch size of 512, for one epoch. This full-parameter fine-tuning allows the entire MLLM to learn holistic cross-modal reasoning.

**Model Architecture and Implementation:**
SAILViT series includes two versions: SAILViT-Large (300M parameters) and SAILViT-Huge (600M parameters), both built on AIMv2. They accept 448x448 image inputs. For larger images, a tiling strategy with up to 8 sub-images is used. Visual tokens (1024 per image with 14-patch size) are compressed to 256 using pixel shuffle for efficiency. The SAILViT series is integrated with Qwen2.5-1.5B via a 2-layer MLP. Training is conducted on PyTorch using Ascend 910B2 NPUs, employing DeepSpeed ZeRO3 and gradient checkpointing for memory optimization, and AdamW optimizer with bf16 precision.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical analyses confirm the powerful robustness and generalizability of SAILViT across various dimensions, consistently outperforming existing visual backbones when integrated into MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Performance on OpenCompass Benchmark

- **Effectiveness with Different LLMs:** SAILViT demonstrates significant and consistent performance gains across different families of LLMs (InternLM2.5, Qwen2.5, and Qwen3 series). For instance, SAILViT-Large achieved average relative improvements of 2.3% on InternLM2.5, 5.5% on Qwen2.5, and 5.4% on Qwen3 series compared to baselines. The Huge version showed similar consistent gains.
- **Cross-task Performance:** SAILViT significantly boosts performance on various tasks, notably on HallusionBench, where SAILViT-Huge paired with InternLM2.5-1.8B increased performance from 29.84% (AIMv2-Huge) to 37.62%. SAILViT-Large with Qwen2.5-1.5B achieved a new SOTA, boosting performance by 7.2%. This suggests SAILViT's ability to mitigate multimodal hallucination by extracting more fine-grained and holistic visual semantics. It also showed benefits in multidisciplinary multimodal Q&A and logical reasoning.
- **Model Generalizability:** SAILViT shows consistent improvements across different sizes of LLMs within the same series (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B vs. 7B). A plausible explanation is better feature alignment proximity for models of similar scales involved in the gradual learning process.
- **Model Robustness:** The performance gains of SAILViT adapt well to different LLM series, even when the visual backbone's adaptation training was performed on a specific LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B). SAILViT-Large consistently outperformed AIMv2-Huge on average performance across different LLM series, indicating its ability to break through scaling law limitations to some extent through the gradual feature refinement strategy.

#### 4.2. Performance on OpenSource Benchmark

- This benchmark, more challenging with 11 additional public datasets, further validated SAILViT's superiority.
- SAILViT consistently improved the overall performance of MLLMs. For example, SAILViT-Large surpassed AIMv2-Large and InternViT-300M-v2.5 by at least 2% gain when paired with Qwen3 series models.
- SAILViT-Huge enhanced MLLM performance with a gain of 0.98%-2.67% on multidisciplinary multimodal tasks. These results reinforce its powerful generalizability and robustness.

#### 4.3. Scaling Law Analysis

- By varying the data magnitude in the "world knowledge infusion" stage, the study found that SAILViT-Large/Huge exhibits clear scaling law properties on both OpenCompass and OpenSource metrics.
- Increased data size in this stage leads to better performance, suggesting that visual features extracted by SAILViT become more compatible with LLM representations with more comprehensive knowledge infusion. This trend was also validated with smaller LLMs like Qwen2.5-0.5B.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Trainable Parameters:** Unlocking LLM parameters during the world knowledge infusion stage significantly improved performance, as it allows the ViT to be trained more adequately by guiding it to produce more adaptive feature semantics.
- **Scale of Integrated LLMs:** Training with a larger LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B vs. 0.5B) yielded better ViT results, indicating that more LLM parameters contribute to more accurate cross-modal associations.
- **Rationality of Training Strategy:** The "All In One" (AIO) multi-task uniform Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for world knowledge infusion achieved more competitive results than a "step-by-step" approach. AIO prevents learning bias and catastrophic forgetting by ensuring more integrated knowledge learning.
- **Effectiveness of Evaluation Regimes:** SAILViT maintained its performance superiority even when evaluated under different MLLM training/evaluation settings (e.g., LLaVA-1.5's regime, where ViT parameters are initially frozen). Unlocking all parameters during SFT further improved performance, confirming the depth of cross-modal feature alignment.
- **Scalability of Visual Backbones:** The gradual feature refinement strategy was successfully generalized to another visual backbone (InternViT-300M-v2.5). The resulting SAILViT-InternViT version consistently outperformed the original InternViT, proving the strategy's wide applicability.

#### 4.5. Comparison Results on Visual Recognition Tasks

- SAILViT's effectiveness extends beyond MLLM integration. When evaluated on standard visual recognition tasks (ImageNet-1k, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-V2), SAILViT-Large improved by 1.5% on average compared to AIMv2-Large, and SAILViT-Huge by 2.11%. SAILViT-Huge even showed comparable performance to InternViT-6B despite having fewer parameters.
- This indicates that the proposed three-stage progressive training approach leads to the extraction of more effective general-purpose visual features, not just for MLLMs.
- The scaling law was also observed here: increasing the training data magnitude in the world knowledge infusion phase led to better performance on visual recognition tasks, suggesting continued room for improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAILViT represents a significant advancement in the development of robust and generalizable visual backbones for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Its core contribution lies in the innovative "gradual feature refinement" training pipeline, which effectively addresses fundamental challenges in integrating visual encoders with LLMs.

### 6. Significance

- **Bridging Modality Gaps:** The staged training approach systematically tackles the parameter initialization conflicts and semantic discrepancies between visual features and LLM representations. By gradually aligning and enriching the visual backbone's understanding from coarse perception to fine-grained world knowledge, SAILViT provides a more harmonious foundation for MLLM co-training.
- **Enhanced MLLM Performance:** The consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse benchmarks (OpenCompass and OpenSource) and various LLM families/sizes demonstrate that SAILViT effectively "breaks through performance bottlenecks" in existing MLLMs. This implies that the quality of the visual backbone's internal representations and its compatibility with the LLM is crucial for overall MLLM efficacy.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** The extensive experimental validation, including cross-LLM series compatibility, scaling law analysis, and detailed ablation studies, solidifies SAILViT's claims of robustness and generalizability. Its ability to improve performance even under varying evaluation regimes and to be effectively applied to other ViT architectures highlights the versatility and foundational strength of the proposed training strategy.
- **Improved Visual Understanding:** Beyond MLLMs, SAILViT's superior performance on traditional visual recognition tasks indicates that its refined training process leads to generally more effective visual feature extraction, which can benefit a broader range of computer vision applications.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of SAILViT series models on Hugging Face fosters transparency, reproducibility, and accelerates further research and development in the multimodal AI community.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated MLLM Development:** By providing a readily adaptable and highly effective visual backbone, SAILViT can significantly streamline the development of future MLLMs, allowing researchers and practitioners to build more capable models with less effort dedicated to foundational visual encoder tuning.
- **More Capable Multimodal AI:** Enhanced MLLMs, powered by SAILViT, can lead to more sophisticated applications in areas such as:
 - **Content Understanding and Moderation:** Better analysis of images and videos for meaning, context, and policy adherence.
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** More accurate and nuanced visual question answering, enabling assistants to "see" and reason about the world more effectively.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved visual perception for decision-making and interaction in complex environments.
 - **Accessibility:** Enhanced tools for visually impaired individuals through advanced image and video description generation.
- **New Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for exploring how different granularities of feature alignment and knowledge infusion can be optimized, and how visual backbones can be made even more adaptive to diverse LLM architectures and downstream tasks. It also prompts further investigation into effective strategies for scaling multimodal models without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, including the need to evaluate SAILViT against ViTs with larger parameter scales (e.g., AIMv2-1B/3B, InternViT-6B) and under more complex MLLM training strategies beyond the ones tested. Future work will focus on:
- Compressing effective visual information into fewer tokens for longer visual inputs.
- Systematically exploring the upper bounds of parameter scaling for SAILViT by leveraging larger open-source ViTs.
- Investigating ViT architectures that support native image resolutions to avoid detail loss from tiling or downsampling.

In conclusion, SAILViT offers a robust and generalizable solution to a critical bottleneck in MLLM development. Its methodical approach to gradual feature refinement sets a new standard for training visual backbones, promising a significant uplift in the capabilities of multimodal AI systems.
