# Tree-based Dialogue Reinforced Policy Optimization for Red-Teaming Attacks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology, Oracle AI, and the University of Pennsylvania developed DIALTREE, an on-policy reinforcement learning framework integrating tree search for multi-turn red-teaming attacks against large language models. The framework achieved an 81.5% average Attack Success Rate across 12 target LLMs, improving upon prior methods by 44.2% ASR, and demonstrated robust cross-model transferability.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to generating harmful content, particularly when subjected to multi-turn conversational attacks.
- Current red-teaming methods often rely on single-turn attacks or fixed multi-turn templates, which inadequately explore the complex and adaptive space of adversarial dialogue strategies.
- Applying reinforcement learning to multi-turn red-teaming is difficult due to the exponential action space, non-verifiable rewards for jailbreaking success, and potential training instability from complex gradient updates.

## Method

- DIALTREE, an on-policy reinforcement learning framework, formulates ==multi-turn red-teaming== as a strategic dialogue between an attacker policy and a target LLM.
- The framework uses a dialogue tree rollout mechanism with pruning criteria (format validity, topic adherence, branch limiting) to efficiently explore the vast space of multi-turn attack trajectories.
- It employs a specialized binary reward function from a guardrail classifier and an adaptive masking technique during policy optimization to stabilize training and prevent "format unlearning".

## Results

- The DIALTREE framework achieved an **81.5%** average Attack Success Rate (ASR) across 12 diverse target LLMs, representing a **44.2%** ASR improvement over previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Attack policies trained with DIALTREE demonstrated strong cross-model transferability, successfully jailbreaking highly aligned models like Claude-4-Sonnet (**71%** ASR) and GPT-4o (**86%** ASR).
- Adaptive masking proved critical by preventing "format unlearning" and stabilizing training, while tree-based exploration provided a 9.8 ASR point gain, contributing to the framework's query efficiency and the autonomous discovery of novel attack strategies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing red-teaming as a strategic, multi-turn dialogue problem allows for more effective discovery of LLM vulnerabilities compared to single-turn approaches.
> - Structured exploration using dialogue tree rollouts with intelligent pruning is essential for efficiently navigating the vast conversational action space in multi-turn RL.
> - Adaptive masking of format tokens during policy optimization prevents "format unlearning," crucial for stable training in generative RL tasks that require structured outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to rapid advancements in artificial intelligence capabilities; however, their potential to generate harmful content when deliberately manipulated remains a significant concern within the field of AI safety. Red-teaming, the process of adversarially testing LLMs to identify safety vulnerabilities, is a crucial component of responsible AI development.

Initial red-teaming efforts predominantly focused on single-turn attacks, which involve a single adversarial prompt designed to elicit harmful responses. However, real-world interactions with LLMs are typically conversational and multi-turn, allowing attackers to adapt their strategies iteratively. Recent research has indicated that multi-turn attacks are generally more successful than single-turn methods, as they can gradually circumvent safety mechanisms, exploit contextual dependencies over time, and adjust tactics based on the model's intermediate responses.

Existing multi-turn red-teaming approaches often rely on manually crafted heuristics or predefined templates. While these methods can identify some vulnerabilities, they typically do not effectively explore the extensive space of potential multi-turn attack strategies. Consequently, they may fail to uncover novel attack trajectories that emerge from complex dialogue dynamics and strategic conversational planning. This limitation is particularly relevant given the observed higher vulnerability of LLMs to multi-turn attacks.

Within the broader landscape of applying reinforcement learning (RL) to LLMs, most successful applications have focused on tasks with verifiable rewards, such as mathematical reasoning or code generation, where ground-truth solutions provide clear feedback. Extending RL techniques to multi-turn conversational scenarios with non-verifiable rewards, like jailbreaking, presents unique challenges due to the reliance on imperfect proxy models for outcome assessment and the inherent complexity of dialogue exploration. This research contributes to addressing this gap by developing an RL-based framework specifically designed for systematic and adaptive exploration of multi-turn red-teaming in non-verifiable settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent vulnerability of large language models (LLMs) to adversarial manipulation, particularly within multi-turn conversational contexts. Despite advances in AI safety, LLMs can still be induced to produce harmful content, necessitating robust red-teaming methodologies.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address Limitations of Existing Red-Teaming:** Current red-teaming methods, whether manual or automated, primarily focus on single-turn attacks or employ fixed templates for multi-turn scenarios. These approaches are limited in their ability to explore the vast and dynamic space of possible multi-turn attacks. They often fail to capture the strategic, adaptive nature of real-world adversarial interactions where attackers iteratively refine their prompts based on an LLM’s responses.
- **Exploit Multi-Turn Vulnerabilities:** Empirical evidence suggests that LLMs exhibit significantly higher vulnerability to multi-turn attacks compared to single-turn attacks. This heightened susceptibility arises because multi-turn interactions allow attackers to gradually erode safety boundaries, leverage contextual information across turns, and adjust their tactics when initial attempts are unsuccessful. The research aims to develop a method that systematically exploits these known multi-turn vulnerabilities.
- **Formulate Red-Teaming as Strategic Reasoning:** The authors posit that effective multi-turn red-teaming should not merely involve repeated trials but rather strategic planning, where each conversational turn contributes to a long-term goal of eliciting harmful content. This perspective motivates framing red-teaming as a strategic reasoning problem within goal-oriented dialogues.
- **Overcome Challenges in Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL offers a paradigm for sequential decision-making and strategic planning, its application to multi-turn red-teaming presents specific challenges:
 - **High Exploration Complexity:** The action space in multi-turn dialogues expands exponentially, making exhaustive exploration impractical. This necessitates efficient exploration mechanisms.
 - **Non-Verifiable Rewards:** Unlike tasks like mathematical problem-solving or code generation, where outcomes can be definitively verified, the success of a jailbreak is assessed by imperfect proxy models (safety guardrails). This introduces challenges related to reward signal reliability.
 - **Training Instability:** The complex gradient updates inherent in multi-turn policy optimization can lead to unstable training dynamics, potentially hindering learning.
- **Develop an Autonomous and Adaptive Framework:** The core objective is to propose DIALTREE, an on-policy reinforcement learning framework integrated with tree search. This framework is designed to autonomously discover diverse and adaptive multi-turn attack strategies without relying on manually curated attack data or human-defined heuristics. The goal is to learn optimal dialogue policies that maximize attack success over multiple turns, systematically exploring novel attack trajectories.
- **Improve Attack Success Rate and Discover Novel Strategies:** Through this new framework, the researchers aim to achieve significantly higher attack success rates compared to existing state-of-the-art methods and to uncover previously unknown or less-explored attack strategies, thereby providing deeper insights into LLM safety weaknesses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces DIALTREE, an on-policy reinforcement learning (RL) framework for multi-turn red-teaming. The methodology formalizes red-teaming as a goal-oriented sequential decision-making problem and integrates several innovations to address the complexities of conversational attack strategy discovery.

**Problem Setup as Conversational Strategic Reasoning:**
The task is defined as a strategic conversation between an attacker model ($\pi_\theta$) and a target model ($\pi_{tgt}$). A red-teaming goal $g$ specifies the unsafe content to be elicited.
The state at turn $t$ is represented as $s_t = (g, [(c_1, q_1, r_1), \dots, (c_t, q_t, r_t)])$, comprising the goal and the dialogue history. The history at each turn $i$ includes a chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning step $c_i$, an attack query $q_i$, and the target's response $r_i$.
The attacker policy $\pi_\theta$ generates an action $(c_t, q_t)$ based on $s_{t-1}$. The target model $\pi_{tgt}$ responds to $q_t$ given the preceding dialogue history (but without access to the goal $g$ or attacker CoTs). The interaction proceeds until a successful jailbreak or a maximum turn limit ($T_{max}$) is reached.

**Training Paradigm:**
The attacker policy $\pi_\theta$ is trained in two stages:
1. **Stage 1 (Cold-Start SFT):** The attacker model is initialized via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a dataset of red-teaming conversations paired with CoTs. This stage aims to relax the model's initial safety constraints and teach it to generate outputs in the required structured format (CoT and attack query at each turn). The base model used for the attacker is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
2. **Stage 2 (RL with DIALTREE):** The SFT-initialized policy is further optimized using the DIALTREE RL algorithm. The objective is to maximize the expected reward from successful jailbreaks while regularizing against deviations from the reference SFT policy using a KL divergence term: $\max_{\pi_\theta} E_{g \sim D, y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|g;\pi_{tgt})} [r_\phi(g, y)] - \beta D_{KL}[\pi_\theta(y | g; \pi_{tgt}) \parallel \pi_{ref}(y | g; \pi_{tgt})]$. The target model during training is Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct.

**DIALTREE Innovations:**

1. **Dialogue Tree Rollout with Pruning:**
 - **Structured Exploration:** To navigate the expansive multi-turn dialogue space, DIALTREE employs a tree-based rollout mechanism. Starting from an initial goal $g$, the attacker policy interacts with the target across multiple branches.
 - **Branching:** At each turn $t$, for every active state $s_{t-1}$, the attacker samples $n$ distinct candidate actions $\{(c_{t,k}, q_{t,k})\}$. Each query $q_{t,k}$ is sent to the target to elicit a response $r_{t,k}$, forming a new state $s_{t,k}$.
 - **Pruning Criteria:** To maintain efficiency and focus on relevant trajectories, three criteria are applied:
 - **Format Validity:** Branches where the attacker produces malformed outputs (missing CoT or query) are discarded.
 - **Topic Adherence:** An on-topic classifier (a Natural Language Inference model) prunes branches where the conversation drifts from the original goal.
 - **Branch Limiting:** To prevent exponential growth, a maximum of $w$ nodes are retained per turn by random subsampling from those that pass format and topic filters.
 - **Trajectory Collection:** After the tree rollout completes (reaching $T_{max}$ or a jailbreak), dialogue trajectories are collected by tracing paths from the root to leaf nodes, excluding pruned branches. Each trajectory $\tau^{(i)}$ receives a scalar reward for subsequent policy optimization.

2. **Specialized Reward Function:**
 - **Feedback Mechanism:** The reward function guides the attacker policy to generate goal-relevant unsafe responses.
 - **Harmfulness Assessment:** HarmAug-Guard (a lightweight guardrail classifier, $r_\phi$) is used to compute a harmfulness score for each query-response pair $(q_t, r_t)$ relative to the goal $g$.
 - **Binary Reward:** A binary reward $R$ is assigned to a trajectory: $R=1$ if any turn $t$ in the trajectory elicits a harmful response (i.e., $r_\phi(g; q_t, r_t) > \eta$, where $\eta=0.5$), and $R=0$ otherwise.

3. **Adaptive Masking Technique:**
 - **Addressing Format Unlearning:** The authors identified "format unlearning" during the RL stage, where the model's ability to produce structured outputs deteriorates. This is hypothesized to occur because negative gradient updates for unsuccessful trajectories inadvertently penalize correctly formatted tokens.
 - **Conditional Masking:** An adaptive masking mechanism is introduced to selectively protect format tokens. For trajectories with a negative group-relative advantage ($A < 0$), the loss computation on format tokens ($V_{fmt}$) is masked. This preserves the acquired format adherence. For trajectories with non-negative advantage ($A \ge 0$), no masking is applied, allowing the policy to learn from both successful attack patterns and their associated formats.

**Dialogue GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):**
Instead of using a separate value function, DIALTREE utilizes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). For each goal, a group of $G$ trajectories is sampled via dialogue tree rollout. The policy $\pi_\theta$ is updated by maximizing a GRPO objective that incorporates the adaptive mask $M_t^{(i)}$ and group-relative advantages $\hat{A}_t^{(i)}$, along with the KL regularization term.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of DIALTREE in discovering multi-turn attack strategies and achieving high attack success rates across a diverse set of target LLMs.

**Overall Attack Performance:**
DIALTREE achieved an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 81.5% across 12 target LLMs. This performance represents an improvement of 44.2% ASR compared to the previously established state-of-the-art approaches. The method performed consistently well across both closed-source (average ASR: 82.7%) and open-source models (average ASR: 80.2%).

**Cross-Model Transferability:**
A significant finding is DIALTREE's strong transferability. Even though the attacker model was trained against a relatively small target model (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), it maintained high ASRs when attacking a broad range of models, including those generally considered strongly safety-aligned. For instance, DIALTREE achieved 71% ASR against Claude-4-Sonnet, a model that existing baselines struggled to jailbreak (most yielding below 10% ASR, with the best reaching 26%). Similarly, it achieved 86% ASR against GPT-4o. This indicates that the learned attack policies generalize beyond the specific training target.

**Contribution of the RL Stage:**
Ablation studies comparing the full DIALTREE method against its Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) initialized counterpart (SFT-only baseline) showed that the RL stage contributes significant improvements. The RL stage consistently led to an average increase of 44.5% ASR across all 12 target models, with particularly pronounced gains on highly safety-aligned models such as Claude-4-Sonnet (+69.5% ASR) and Grok-4 (+67.5% ASR). This suggests that while SFT provides a necessary initialization, the RL-driven exploration is critical for discovering more effective and generalizable attack strategies.

**Effectiveness of Adaptive Masking:**
Experiments evaluating different masking strategies highlighted the importance of adaptive masking. Without any masking or with static masking, the attacker policy exhibited severe "format unlearning," where the rate of malformed outputs rose sharply (approaching 100%) and overall reward collapsed. In contrast, the adaptive masking mechanism effectively preserved the format-following capabilities, keeping malformed trajectory rates below 50% throughout most of the training, while simultaneously facilitating a steady increase in reward. This result indicates that adaptive masking is crucial for stable and efficient multi-turn policy learning by preventing the degradation of structural output formats.

**Ablation Studies on DIALTREE Components:**
- **Tree Rollout and Pruning:** Disabling all pruning mechanisms resulted in a substantial performance decline (average 25% ASR drop), confirming that filtering low-quality and invalid dialogue paths is essential for efficient training. The tree rollout mechanism itself, even with pruning, demonstrated an average improvement of 9.8 ASR points over a standard GRPO approach without tree exploration, validating the benefits of structured dialogue exploration.
- **Tree Depth (Planning Horizon):** The ASR generally increased as the maximum conversation turns extended from 1 to 5, supporting the premise that multi-turn strategies become more effective with a longer planning horizon. A slight performance decrease was observed at 7 turns, which the authors attribute to sparse and delayed reward signals in very long horizons.
- **Branching Width:** Increasing the branching factor (n) from 1 (linear conversation) to 4 generally improved ASR. Analysis using Self-BLEU indicated that a branching factor of 4 offered sufficient diversity in generated trajectories, with diversity diminishing at higher factors (n=8) as semantically similar examples began to emerge.
- **Group Size:** Larger group sizes (up to G=32) consistently led to improved attacker performance and generalization, suggesting that more diverse trajectory pools provide richer feedback for policy updates.

**Attack Efficiency:**
DIALTREE demonstrated high query efficiency, achieving the highest ASR with the fewest average queries (2.79 queries per attack) compared to all baselines. This indicates that the method efficiently guides exploration towards promising attack trajectories rather than relying on extensive random sampling.

**Attack Success Evolution through Dialogue:**
Analysis of attack success rates across turns revealed that success generally increased through dialogue progression. The percentage of successful attacks (Score 5) steadily rose from 26.1% at the first turn to 60% by the fourth turn, then plateaued. This trend supports the hypothesis that multi-turn interactions enable attackers to progressively exploit vulnerabilities more effectively than single-turn attempts.

**Discovery of Novel Red-Teaming Strategies:**
Manual analysis of dialogues generated by DIALTREE identified several novel and diverse attack strategies that emerged autonomously through RL-based exploration. These include:
- **Pretexting:** Framing malicious requests within seemingly legitimate contexts (e.g., fictional stories, research).
- **Gradual Escalation:** Starting with benign requests and incrementally steering the conversation towards harmful specifics.
- **Persistent Gap-Filling (Jigsaw Attacks):** Iteratively requesting missing components of a harmful plan.
- **Policy-Laundering Keywords:** Embedding softening terms (e.g., "fictional," "educational") that the target model might mirror.
- **Cross-Lingual Evasion:** Interleaving multiple languages to bypass monolingual filters.
- **Sycophancy Exploitation:** Leveraging model agreeableness through expressions of disappointment or frustration.

**Robustness to External Guardrails:**
Experiments showed that DIALTREE is robust against additional external safety guardrails. When target models were paired with guardrails (e.g., Llama-Guard-3-8B), DIALTREE maintained relatively high attack success rates, demonstrating its ability to bypass both the model's inherent safety alignment and external defense mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DIALTREE, a novel framework that carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of AI safety and beyond.

**Advancement in Automated Red-Teaming:** DIALTREE represents an advancement in automated red-teaming by formalizing multi-turn adversarial testing as a goal-oriented sequential decision-making problem. This approach moves beyond traditional manual red-teaming or template-based automated methods, offering a more systematic and adaptive mechanism for uncovering LLM vulnerabilities. Its ability to autonomously discover diverse attack strategies without relying on manually curated data marks a step toward more comprehensive safety evaluations.

**Highlighting LLM Vulnerabilities to Multi-Turn Attacks:** The work empirically demonstrates that current LLMs, including highly safety-aligned proprietary models, remain significantly vulnerable to strategic, multi-turn attacks. The substantial performance gains of DIALTREE over previous state-of-the-art methods underscore that defenses designed for single-turn interactions are often insufficient against adaptive, conversational adversaries. This finding emphasizes a critical and largely unresolved challenge in AI safety.

**Methodological Contributions to RL for LLMs:** The paper extends the application of reinforcement learning to complex, non-verifiable conversational tasks, a domain where RL has been less explored compared to verifiable tasks like mathematical reasoning. The introduction of dialogue tree rollout with quality-aware pruning for efficient exploration, a specialized reward function for non-verifiable outcomes, and an adaptive masking technique to stabilize multi-turn policy optimization are notable methodological contributions. These innovations can inform future RL research in interactive and open-ended generation tasks.

**Practical Tool for Stress-Testing Safety:** DIALTREE provides a practical and efficient tool for stress-testing LLMs. Its high attack success rate, combined with query efficiency and cross-model transferability, suggests it could be integrated into LLM development pipelines for more thorough safety evaluations. By identifying vulnerabilities more effectively, it can contribute to the development of more robust and trustworthy AI systems.

**Actionable Insights for Defense Development:** The detailed analysis of novel attack strategies discovered by DIALTREE (e.g., pretexting, gradual escalation, cross-lingual evasion, sycophancy exploitation) offers concrete, actionable insights for the defense community. Understanding these emergent attack patterns can guide the development of more sophisticated, context-aware, and adaptive defense mechanisms that can specifically counteract multi-turn adversarial tactics.

**Broader Applicability to Strategic Reasoning:** Beyond red-teaming, the underlying framework of DIALTREE—which combines tree search with RL for strategic dialogue in non-verifiable settings—has potential for adaptation to a wider range of multi-turn strategic reasoning tasks. This could include applications in negotiation, debate, pedagogical interactions, or other goal-oriented conversational agents, where agents need to reason about sequential interactions to achieve complex objectives.

**Ethical Considerations and Responsible Disclosure:** The research acknowledges the dual-use nature of tools that can generate harmful content. The authors commit to responsible disclosure practices, emphasizing that understanding these weaknesses is essential for developing robust countermeasures. Their plan to provide controlled access to verified researchers highlights a commitment to mitigating potential misuse while advancing AI safety research.
