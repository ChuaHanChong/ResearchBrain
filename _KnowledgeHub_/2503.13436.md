# Unified Autoregressive Visual Generation and Understanding with Continuous Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's UniFluid unifies visual generation and understanding within a single autoregressive framework by leveraging continuous visual tokens and a pre-trained Gemma large language model backbone. The model demonstrates that joint training on both tasks improves image generation quality and achieves competitive performance across diverse visual understanding and editing applications.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal AI models often specialize in either visual generation or understanding, lacking a unified framework that can perform both tasks seamlessly.
- Many autoregressive image generation models rely on discrete visual tokens, which can lead to information loss and quantization artifacts.
- The challenge of integrating visual and textual modalities into a single, consistent autoregressive framework while overcoming the limitations of discrete tokenization for images.

## Method

- ==UniFluid== develops a unified autoregressive framework that processes both discrete text tokens and ==continuous visual tokens== within a single decoder-only transformer backbone.
- It employs a ==continuous Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== for image tokenization during generation and a pre-trained ==SigLIP encoder== for understanding, with task-specific prediction heads for text (classification) and images (diffusion).
- The framework uses a ==unified loss function== combining cross-entropy for text and a diffusion loss for continuous visual tokens, along with ==dynamic random-order generation== for image tokens during training.

## Results

- UniFluid, trained with a unified objective, achieved better image generation quality (e.g., FID **7.20** for 2B models) compared to a model trained solely for text-to-image (FID **7.88**).
- The hyperparameter lambda (λ) effectively controls the trade-off between generation and understanding performance; a smaller λ (e.g., 0.005) yields high-fidelity generation while retaining significant understanding ability.
- The model demonstrated strong transferability to various downstream tasks, achieving competitive results on 4 image captioning benchmarks and 20 visual question answering tasks, and successfully performing image editing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified training on both generation and understanding tasks can mutually benefit each other, leading to improved image generation quality compared to generation-only models.
> - A stronger pre-trained large language model backbone significantly enhances performance across both visual generation and understanding tasks within the unified framework.
> - Random-order image generation during training is crucial for achieving high-quality image synthesis, while raster-order processing may slightly benefit understanding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of UniFluid is situated at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), multimodal AI, and generative modeling.

- **Evolution from Text-Centric to Multimodal LLMs:** The paper correctly contextualizes its work within the broader trend of LLMs evolving from purely text-centric architectures (like BERT, GPT-3, PaLM) to multimodal systems. Early multimodal models like Flamingo demonstrated the potential of integrating visual inputs for unified reasoning. Subsequent works such as LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, and mPLUG-Owl further advanced visual understanding by aligning pre-trained image encoders with LLMs. UniFluid builds directly on this trajectory, particularly aligning with the PaliGemma series developed by Google, which has focused on versatile vision-language models for diverse downstream understanding tasks. UniFluid aims to take this a step further by not only understanding but also generating visual content within the *same* framework.

- **Autoregressive Models for Generation:** While diffusion models currently dominate high-fidelity image generation, autoregressive (AR) methods remain a compelling alternative due to their simplicity and alignment with LLM training paradigms. Previous AR image generation models often relied on discrete visual tokens, such as Parti and Muse, which tokenize images into a fixed vocabulary. A key distinguishing aspect of UniFluid's lineage is its focus on **continuous visual tokens**, an approach explored by works like MAR and Fluid. This continuous token approach avoids the potential information loss and quantization artifacts associated with discrete tokenization, maintaining a higher fidelity to the original image data. UniFluid leverages this continuous token paradigm for its generation capabilities.

- **Unified Multimodal Models (Generation + Understanding):** A growing research interest lies in creating single models that can perform *both* visual generation and understanding tasks. Existing approaches include:
 - **VQ-based Models:** Models like Chameleon, Emu, and Janus unify tasks by encoding visual inputs into discrete tokens and framing both generation and understanding as next-token prediction within this shared discrete space.
 - **Hybrid Training Targets:** Transfusion and Show-O integrate next-token prediction with diffusion objectives.
 - **Latent Feature Regression:** MetaMorph uses an LLM to regress visual features (e.g., SigLIP features) but requires a separate diffusion model for image decoding.

 UniFluid distinguishes itself from these by specifically focusing on **per-token autoregressive generation using continuous visual tokens** within a *single, unified decoder-only framework*. Unlike VQ-based methods, it is not limited by discrete token spaces. Unlike MetaMorph, it integrates the diffusion-based decoding more directly within its architecture by employing a per-token diffusion head for continuous visual token prediction, avoiding a completely separate external model. This emphasis on a "pure autoregressive framework" that can handle both continuous and discrete tokens (for image and text, respectively) within a shared embedding space represents a novel and direct approach to unifying vision-language tasks.

In essence, UniFluid contributes to the cutting edge of multimodal AI by proposing a highly integrated autoregressive framework that harnesses the power of advanced LLMs (Gemma) and the benefits of continuous visual token representation to simultaneously achieve strong performance in both visual generation and understanding tasks, while exploring the synergistic relationship between these two modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of UniFluid is to develop a **unified autoregressive framework for joint visual generation and understanding**, specifically by leveraging **continuous visual tokens**.

The core motivations driving this research are:

- **Leveraging Autoregressive Advantages:** The authors are motivated by the "advantages and strong scaling properties of autoregressive models, coupled with their simplicity." Autoregressive models, common in LLMs, predict the next token in a sequence based on preceding ones. Applying this paradigm to multimodal data offers a consistent and well-understood training and inference mechanism.
- **Overcoming Vector Quantization Limitations:** A significant motivation is to explore visual generation "without the limitations introduced by vector quantization (VQ)." VQ-based methods often quantize images into discrete tokens, which can lead to information loss or artifacts. By using continuous visual tokens, UniFluid aims to maintain higher fidelity and potentially enable richer representations.
- **Achieving Unified Capabilities within a Single Model:** The paper seeks to demonstrate that a single, unified model can effectively handle diverse visual understanding tasks (like captioning and question answering) and visual generation tasks (like text-to-image synthesis and image editing). This unification has "promising potential for leveraging knowledge and reasoning abilities that transfer across different vision and language tasks," leading to more robust and generalizable multimodal representations.
- **Exploring Mutual Benefits between Tasks:** A key hypothesis and motivation is to investigate whether visual generation and understanding tasks can "mutually benefit each other" within a single architecture. The authors aim to discover if joint training on both tasks can lead to improved performance compared to training separate, single-task models.
- **Building on Pre-trained LLMs:** The work is motivated by the success of large language models like Gemma. By building upon such powerful text-centric backbones and extending them to visual modalities, the goal is to "unlock powerful visual generation and understanding capabilities."

In essence, UniFluid seeks to advance multimodal AI by creating a more elegant, efficient, and potentially more capable system that can seamlessly switch between generating and comprehending visual information, all within a single, consistent autoregressive framework that sidesteps the constraints of discrete visual tokenization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniFluid employs a sophisticated yet unified autoregressive framework designed to process and generate both text and continuous image data.

- **Unified Autoregressive Modeling:**
 - The core principle is to treat both text and image data as sequences of tokens within a single autoregressive framework. The model factorizes the joint probability distribution of the sequence, predicting each token based on the preceding ones.
 - Crucially, this autoregressive formulation is applied to *both discrete text tokens and continuous visual tokens*, enabling a truly modality-agnostic approach where both are embedded into a shared space.
 - A single, **decoder-only transformer backbone** (specifically, the Gemma model series) serves as the core architecture, processing these multimodal sequences.

- **Architecture Components:**
 - **Text Tokenization:** Standard **SentencePiece tokenizer** is used to convert textual inputs into discrete tokens with a defined vocabulary.
 - **Image Tokenization (for Generation):** For image generation, images are encoded into continuous visual tokens using a **continuous Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**, as seen in prior works like Fluid. This avoids the need for vector quantization.
 - **Image Encoder (for Understanding):** For visual understanding tasks, a separate **pre-trained SigLIP image encoder** is used to extract high-level semantic features from input images. These SigLIP features are used as a prefix to the input sequence during understanding tasks.
 - **Prediction Heads:**
 - **Per-token Classification Head (for Text):** A standard classification head converts the transformer's output logits for text tokens into a categorical probability distribution, optimized with a **cross-entropy loss ($L_{Text}$)**.
 - **Per-token Diffusion Head (for Images):** A lightweight MLP acts as a diffusion head to model the per-token distribution of predicted continuous visual tokens. It uses a **diffusion process and loss function ($L_{Visual}$)** specifically tailored for continuous token prediction, similar to MAR and Fluid.

- **Positional Embeddings and Special Tokens:**
 - Standard 1D positional embeddings are used for text and overall sequence ordering.
 - To capture the 2D spatial structure inherent in images, **learnable 2D positional embeddings** are added to the image token embeddings.
 - A "Beginning of Image" (BOI) token is prepended to the image token sequence to signal the start of visual generation.
 - Inspired by RAR, a position embedding for the *next* predicted token is also added to each image token to support **random-order generation**.

- **Task-Specific Training Configurations and Attention Masks:**
 - **Image Understanding:** Input includes image embeddings (from SigLIP) and question tokens. A bidirectional attention mask is applied to these prefix tokens, while a causal attention mask is applied to the answer tokens, and $L_{Text}$ is calculated on the answer.
 - **Image Generation:** Text prompts are provided as conditioning. A bidirectional attention mask is applied to the text prompt tokens, allowing full attention within the prompt. A causal attention mask is applied to the generated image tokens, ensuring each token only attends to preceding image tokens. $L_{Visual}$ is calculated on the generated image tokens.

- **Unified Loss Function:** The total training loss is a weighted sum: $L = L_{Visual} + \lambda \cdot L_{Text}$, where $\lambda$ is a hyper-parameter controlling the balance between the visual and text loss contributions.

- **Training Details:**
 - Trained on the **WebLI dataset**, which contains high-quality image-text pairs and image question-answer pairs.
 - Optimization: AdamW with a learning rate of 1e-4, 1 million steps, and a warm-up period.
 - **Random Order Generation:** For image generation, the image token order is randomly permuted during the initial 300k iterations, then linearly anneals to raster order between 300k and 600k iterations, and finally sticks to raster order for the remaining 400k steps. This dynamic ordering strategy is crucial for image quality.
 - The **Gemma-2 model series** is used as the backbone transformer for most experiments.

- **Inference:**
 - **Text Decoding:** Categorical sampling for discrete text tokens, with greedy decoding or beam search (for specific captioning tasks).
 - **Image Decoding:** A diffusion sampling process is used to generate continuous visual tokens.
 - **Efficiency:** Key-Value (KV) caching is employed for both text and image token generation to accelerate inference.

This comprehensive approach allows UniFluid to leverage the strengths of autoregressive modeling and continuous representations, unifying diverse multimodal tasks within a single, efficient framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniFluid's experiments reveal several critical insights into training unified vision-language models with continuous tokens:

- **Unified Training Improves Generation Performance:**
 - A direct comparison showed that UniFluid, trained with a unified objective, achieved better image generation quality (lower FID and higher GenEval scores on MS-COCO) compared to a model trained *solely* for text-to-image (T2I only).
 - For 0.7B models, UniFluid achieved FID 8.39 (vs. 9.71 for T2I only). For 2B models, UniFluid achieved FID 7.20 (vs. 7.88 for T2I only).
 - This finding suggests that the visual understanding capability learned during unified training contributes positively to the visual generation quality, indicating a synergistic relationship where the tasks "mutually benefit each other."

- **Trade-off Between Generation and Understanding, Controllable by $\lambda$:**
 - The hyperparameter $\lambda$, which balances the contribution of visual loss ($L_{Visual}$) and text loss ($L_{Text}$), directly influences the performance trade-off between generation and understanding.
 - Increasing $\lambda$ (giving more weight to text/understanding loss) generally improves image understanding performance (higher Cap Avg, QA Avg, and COCO CIDEr) but concurrently diminishes image generation capabilities (sharply increasing FID score).
 - For instance, with $\lambda=0.005$, UniFluid maintains strong generation (FID 8.39) while achieving a Cap Avg of 120.25 and QA Avg of 60.29. As $\lambda$ increases to 2.0, Cap Avg reaches 125.80 (exceeding I2T-only baseline) and QA Avg reaches 65.88, but FID deteriorates significantly to 54.02.
 - The authors recommend a "smaller $\lambda$ value (e.g., 0.005)" for most scenarios, as it allows for high-fidelity image generation while preserving a significant portion of image understanding ability. A $\lambda=0.1$ was found to roughly equalize the scale of the two losses.

- **Stronger Pre-trained LLM Backbone is Crucial:**
 - Experiments comparing Gemma-1 2B and Gemma-2 2B as the backbone LLM revealed that a more powerful LLM significantly enhances performance across both modalities.
 - With Gemma-2, UniFluid achieved an FID of 7.20 (vs. 9.73 for Gemma-1) and a GenEval of 0.59 (vs. 0.52).
 - Image understanding also saw improvements: COCO CIDEr 40.91 (vs. 38.02), Cap Avg 116.13 (vs. 113.40), and QA Avg 62.10 (vs. 60.21) with Gemma-2.
 - This indicates that even if the LLM is pre-trained solely on text, its enhanced reasoning and representational capabilities transfer positively to multimodal tasks, demonstrating the importance of a strong foundational language model.

- **Random Order Generation Benefits Generation, Not Understanding:**
 - Comparing random-order image generation during training against raster-order revealed distinct impacts.
 - Random-order generation is critical for achieving high-quality image synthesis, yielding a lower FID (7.20 vs. 8.28 for raster-order) and similar GenEval (0.59). This confirms previous findings in autoregressive image generation (e.g., Fluid).
 - However, random-order generation did *not* necessarily improve visual understanding performance; raster-order surprisingly achieved slightly better results on MS-COCO CIDEr (45.57 vs. 40.91) and downstream captioning tasks (Cap Avg 116.55 vs. 116.13). This implies that while spatially unconstrained generation helps image quality, a more structured processing order might aid understanding tasks.

- **Strong Transferability to Downstream Tasks:**
 - UniFluid demonstrated strong generalization and transferability to various downstream tasks after fine-tuning.
 - **Image Captioning and Question Answering:** The fine-tuned model achieved competitive results across 4 captioning benchmarks (COCOcap, Screen2Words, TextCaps, WidgetCap) and 20 QA tasks (e.g., OKVQA, AOKVQA, GQA, TextVQA), showcasing its robust understanding capabilities.
 - **Image Editing:** UniFluid successfully adapted to image editing tasks involving interleaved image and text prompts, demonstrating abilities like object removal, insertion, and style/color changes, despite being a preliminary exploration.
 - **Aesthetic Fine-Tuning:** The model also showed the ability to generate high-quality, aesthetically pleasing images after dedicated fine-tuning.

In summary, UniFluid successfully validates its unified framework, showcasing that a single autoregressive model with continuous visual tokens can perform both generation and understanding tasks comparably to or better than single-task baselines. The research also meticulously identifies key architectural and training choices, such as the LLM backbone strength, loss balancing, and token ordering, that are crucial for optimizing performance across these diverse multimodal capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniFluid represents a notable stride in the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Novel Unified Autoregressive Framework:** UniFluid introduces a "pure autoregressive framework for joint visual generation and understanding" that specifically leverages **continuous visual tokens** and a pre-trained LLM backbone (Gemma). This approach is distinct from prevailing VQ-based methods or hybrid diffusion/AR models, presenting a more consistent and potentially simpler paradigm for multimodal learning within a single model. By avoiding discrete quantization, it aims to preserve more nuanced visual information.

- **Demonstrated Mutual Benefit Between Tasks:** The paper empirically validates the hypothesis that visual generation and understanding tasks can "mutually benefit each other." The finding that unified training improves image generation quality compared to a generation-only baseline, and that understanding capabilities are maintained or even enhanced, suggests a richer internal representation is learned when tasks are jointly optimized. This points towards more holistic and generalizable multimodal intelligence.

- **Efficiency and Scalability:** As an autoregressive model built on the Gemma series, UniFluid inherits the strong scaling properties and simplicity of LLMs. The use of KV caching further enhances inference efficiency for both image and text generation. This efficiency is critical for deploying large, capable multimodal models.

- **Insights into Architectural and Training Choices:** The systematic investigation into hyperparameters like $\lambda$, the choice of LLM backbone, and image token ordering provides valuable guidance for future research and development of unified multimodal models. The finding that a stronger unimodal LLM significantly boosts *both* visual generation and understanding, and that random-order generation is crucial for image quality, are important practical takeaways for the community.

- **Advancing Foundation Models:** UniFluid contributes directly to the vision of creating truly general-purpose AI foundation models. By showing that a single model can adeptly handle both generative and discriminative tasks across modalities, it moves closer to a unified AI agent capable of fluidly interacting with and creating diverse forms of information.

- **Potential for Enhanced Multimodal Applications:**
 - **More Capable AI Assistants:** A unified model could lead to more intelligent virtual assistants that can not only understand complex visual queries but also generate relevant images or edit existing ones based on natural language instructions.
 - **Advanced Content Creation:** UniFluid's capabilities in image generation and editing, especially with aesthetic fine-tuning, pave the way for more intuitive and powerful creative tools for designers, artists, and general users.
 - **Robust Understanding Systems:** The strong performance on various visual understanding benchmarks implies UniFluid's potential for applications requiring sophisticated visual reasoning, such as medical image analysis, autonomous driving, or accessibility tools.
 - **Interleaved Multi-Modal Reasoning:** The demonstrated ability to perform image editing with combined image and text prompts hints at future systems that can handle highly complex, interleaved multimodal inputs and outputs for diverse real-world tasks.

In conclusion, UniFluid is a significant step towards creating more unified, efficient, and versatile multimodal AI systems. By effectively integrating continuous visual tokens within a pure autoregressive framework based on powerful LLMs, it not only pushes the boundaries of visual generation and understanding but also provides crucial insights into the synergistic relationship between these fundamental AI capabilities. This work encourages further exploration of continuous representations in multimodal learning, promising more robust and generalizable AI.
