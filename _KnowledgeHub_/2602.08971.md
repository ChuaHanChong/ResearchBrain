# WorldArena: A Unified Benchmark for Evaluating Perception and Functional Utility of Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, WorldArena, unifies the evaluation of embodied world models by assessing both their perceptual fidelity and functional utility in robotic manipulation tasks. The benchmark, evaluating 14 models, identifies a "perception–functionality gap" where models with high visual quality often do not perform well in practical embodied applications.

## Problem

- Current evaluation protocols for embodied world models are fragmented, focusing predominantly on perceptual fidelity without a holistic view of their practical utility.
- Existing benchmarks fail to assess the critical functional roles of world models in downstream embodied tasks like synthetic data generation, policy evaluation, or action planning.
- Model coverage is insufficient, with many evaluations focusing on general video generation models while overlooking specialized robot- and action-conditioned world models.

## Method

![[2602.08971-method.png]]
*WorldArena's three embodied task evaluation tracks: world models as data engines (downstream policy success), policy evaluators (correlation with real-world results), and action planners (world-model-based policy success).*

- **WorldArena** introduces a ==unified benchmark== using the ==RobotTwin 2.0== dataset to comprehensively evaluate 14 representative embodied world models.
- A ==multi-faceted evaluation framework== combines 16 objective metrics across 6 perceptual quality dimensions with assessments of ==functional utility== in three embodied tasks.
- The framework incorporates ==subjective human evaluation== and proposes **EWMScore**, an interpretable, unified metric for generative performance.

## Results

- General video models like Veo 3.1 achieved the highest scores in visual quality and aesthetics, while action-conditioned embodied models (e.g., CtrlWorld) showed better physical adherence and higher human-rated win rates.
- The EWMScore correlated strongly with human judgments on perceptual quality (Pearson **r = 0.825**) but only moderately with data synthesis performance (**r = 0.600**) and weakly with action planning performance (**r = 0.360**).
- CtrlWorld exhibited a strong correlation (Pearson **r = 0.986**) as an embodied policy evaluator, but overall, synthetic data from world models provided limited gains for policy training compared to real data, and action planning capabilities were substantially lower than specialized VLA policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A significant "perception–functionality gap" exists, indicating that high visual quality in world models does not consistently translate to strong performance in embodied tasks.
> - General video models excel in perceptual realism but often lack physical adherence, while embodied models show better consistency in structure and interaction-related metrics.
> - Current world models struggle to serve as robust data engines, policy evaluators, or action planners, generally lagging behind real data or specialized policies in functional tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" has emerged as a cornerstone of embodied intelligence, enabling AI agents to predict future environmental states and consequences of their actions. These models are crucial for autonomous decision-making, planning, and efficient training of embodied agents in complex environments. However, as the paper highlights, the evaluation of these sophisticated models remains significantly fragmented and often incomplete.

Historically, the assessment of world models, particularly in the context of generative capabilities, has largely focused on **perceptual fidelity**. This includes metrics related to video generation quality, such as visual realism, motion smoothness, and content consistency. While important for generating plausible visual sequences, these metrics often fail to capture the **functional utility** of world models in supporting downstream embodied tasks like robotic manipulation or navigation. The paper explicitly argues that existing benchmarks treat world models primarily as advanced video generators, neglecting their critical roles as data synthesis engines, policy evaluators, or action planners within a closed-loop control system.

Furthermore, the existing landscape suffers from **insufficient model coverage**. Many general text-conditioned video generation models are evaluated, but robot-specialized or action-conditioned world models, which are more pertinent to embodied AI, often receive limited systematic attention. This creates a gap where the progress of truly embodied world models cannot be adequately measured or compared.

WorldArena directly addresses these limitations by introducing a **unified and comprehensive benchmark**. It significantly expands the scope of evaluation beyond mere perceptual quality to include three critical functional dimensions relevant to embodied AI. By systematically evaluating a broader range of both general and specialized world models, and integrating objective metrics with subjective human assessments, WorldArena provides a holistic framework that aims to bridge the gap between perceptual performance and functional utility. This work fits into the broader research landscape by advocating for a more rigorous and task-oriented evaluation paradigm, thereby guiding the development of world models towards greater practical applicability in embodied intelligence. It serves as a necessary step to move the field beyond aesthetically pleasing video generation towards models that are truly capable of supporting complex robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the WorldArena benchmark are clearly articulated in the paper:

1. **Address Fragmented Evaluation:** Current evaluation protocols for embodied world models are fragmented, focusing predominantly on perceptual fidelity (e.g., video generation quality) without a holistic view. WorldArena aims to provide a unified framework that systematically assesses both perceptual and functional dimensions.
2. **Evaluate Functional Utility:** A major motivation is to move beyond evaluating world models solely as video generators. The authors emphasize that these models serve critical functional roles in embodied intelligence, such as synthetic data generation for policy training, environment proxies for policy evaluation, and action planners for autonomous control. WorldArena is designed to explicitly evaluate these functional utilities.
3. **Expand Model Coverage:** Many existing benchmarks focus on general text-conditioned video generation models, overlooking a growing number of robot-specialized and action-conditioned world models. The benchmark aims to provide systematic evaluation for a wider spectrum of representative models, including those specifically designed for robotics.
4. **Reveal the Perception–Functionality Gap:** The authors hypothesize a significant disparity between high visual quality and actual performance in embodied tasks. A core objective is to empirically investigate and reveal this "perception–functionality gap," providing critical insights into the limitations of current world models.
5. **Propose a Holistic Evaluation Metric:** To simplify the interpretation of multi-dimensional performance, the paper aims to introduce a single, interpretable metric, EWMScore, which integrates various perceptual and functional assessments.
6. **Provide a Publicly Accessible Benchmark:** By releasing the WorldArena benchmark and a public leaderboard, the authors intend to provide a valuable tool for the research community to track progress and guide future development toward truly functional world models in embodied AI.

In essence, WorldArena is motivated by the need for a more comprehensive, relevant, and standardized evaluation methodology to accelerate progress in embodied AI by focusing on the practical utility of world models beyond their visual generative capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldArena proposes a multi-faceted evaluation framework comprising objective metrics, embodied task assessments, and subjective human evaluation, all synthesized into a unified score.

#### 3.1. Dataset and Tested Models

- **Dataset:** The benchmark focuses on **bimanual robotic manipulation** scenarios, utilizing the **RobotTwin 2.0 dataset** (Chen et al., 2025). This dataset covers 50 diverse robotic scenarios and 2500 videos, ensuring both scenario diversity and evaluation reliability.
- **Tested Models:** A comprehensive set of **14 representative world models** is evaluated. This includes:
 - **General-purpose video world models:** CogvideoX, Wan 2.2, Wan 2.6, Veo 3.1.
 - **Text-conditioned embodied world models:** Genie Envisioner, GigaWorld, TesserAct, Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (text), WoW, RoboMaster, Vidar.
 - **Action-conditioned embodied world models:** IRASim, Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (action), CtrlWorld.
 To ensure fair comparison, models with available training code were post-trained on the RoboTwin 2.0 dataset following their official implementations.

#### 3.2. Multi-faceted Video Quality Evaluation (Perceptual)

This component assesses a world model’s open-loop prediction ability across 6 sub-dimensions with 16 numerical metrics:
1. **Visual Quality:** Measures perceptual reliability, fidelity, and aesthetic appeal. Metrics include **Image Quality** (MUSIQ), **Aesthetic Quality** (LAION aesthetic predictor), and **JEPA Similarity** (V-JEPA encoder, MMD).
2. **Motion Quality:** Assesses the physical meaningfulness and temporal coherence of dynamics. Metrics include **Dynamic Degree** (RAFT optical flow, top 5% active pixels), **Flow Score** (average optical flow magnitude), and **Motion Smoothness** (VFI-Mamba, weighted by motion magnitude).
3. **Content Consistency:** Evaluates the stability of objects and scenes. Metrics include **Subject Consistency** (DINO features, cosine similarity), **Background Consistency** (CLIP features, cosine similarity), and **Photometric Consistency** (optical flow-based average end-point error). These are weighted by dynamic degree to prevent favoring static videos.
4. **Physics Adherence:** Determines if generated behaviors conform to real-world physical constraints. Metrics include **Interaction Quality** (Qwen3-VL-based judge, 1-5 Likert scale for contact, force, etc.) and **Trajectory Accuracy** (SAM3 for robot arm bounding boxes, normalized dynamic time warping distance).
5. **3D Accuracy:** Assesses preservation of real-world spatial structure. Metrics include **Depth Accuracy** (Depth-Anything, median-scaled absolute relative error) and **Perspectivity** (Qwen3-VL-based judge for 3D plausibility).
6. **Controllability:** Measures the model’s ability to respond to external instructions. Metrics include **Instruction Following** (Qwen3-VL-based judge, 1-5 Likert scale), **Semantic Alignment** (Qwen2.5-VL for video description, CLIP for cosine similarity), and **Action Following** (pairwise feature dissimilarity for instruction-conditioned video diversity).

#### 3.3. Embodied Task Evaluation (Functional Utility)

This component assesses closed-loop performance across three typical embodied downstream tasks:
1. **Embodied Data Engine:** Evaluates the world model's ability to generate synthetic video-action sequences to augment training data for downstream embodied policy models. The performance gain of a baseline policy (π$_{0.5}$) trained with synthetic data is measured (task success rate in RoboTwin simulator).
2. **Embodied Policy Evaluator:** Assesses the world model's capability as an environment proxy for evaluating policy performance. Policies with varying capabilities are rolled out in the action-controllable world model, and task success is judged by a VLM. The correlation between the world model's evaluation and the RoboTwin simulator's evaluation is measured.
3. **Embodied Action Planner:** Investigates the world model's ability to function as an end-to-end action planner. The world model, paired with an inverse dynamics model, takes text instructions and an initial frame to generate an action sequence. This sequence is executed in the RoboTwin simulator, and the task success rate is measured.

#### 3.4. Human Evaluation

To complement objective metrics, human annotations are collected for qualitative aspects:
1. **Likert Scale Scoring:** Annotators score videos on a 1-5 scale for **overall video quality, instruction following, and physical adherence**, normalized to a 0-100 range.
2. **Head-to-Head Comparison:** Annotators choose the superior video from a pair generated by two different models for the same prompt, yielding a **win-rate metric**. 70 annotators evaluated 3500 videos.

#### 3.5. EWMScore Metric

The **EWMScore** is a unified, objective metric derived from the 16 video quality metrics. Each metric is linearly normalized (based on empirical min/max bounds) to a range, and then the arithmetic mean across all normalized metrics is computed to provide a single, comprehensive index of an embodied world model’s generative performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation conducted using WorldArena revealed several critical insights into the capabilities and limitations of current embodied world models:

1. **The Perception–Functionality Gap:** A central finding is the **significant gap between perceptual fidelity and embodied task performance**. Models achieving high visual quality do not necessarily translate this into strong capabilities in downstream decision-making tasks. This highlights that photorealistic generation alone is insufficient for effective embodied intelligence.

2. **Performance Across Video Quality Dimensions:**
 - **General video models (e.g., Veo 3.1, Wan 2.6)** consistently achieve the highest scores in **visual quality and aesthetic scores**, demonstrating superior perceptual realism and semantic alignment. However, they often show limited improvements in embodied-specific metrics like physics adherence or 3D accuracy.
 - **Embodied world models** generally perform better on **structure- and interaction-related metrics**. For instance, CtrlWorld and TesserAct score highly in subject consistency, background stability, and trajectory accuracy, indicating better alignment with manipulation dynamics. WoW demonstrates strong action-following ability.
 - **Qualitative results** further suggest that visually strong models tend to suffer from semantic drift, while embodied world models produce more coherent and goal-consistent action sequences despite potentially lower overall visual fidelity.

3. **Performance in Embodied Tasks:**
 - **Embodied Data Engine:** Synthetic data from most world models provided **some performance gains** for downstream policy learning, but generally **lagged behind real data**. Only RoboMaster and WoW data surpassed real-data training on one specific task. This indicates that the quality of synthetic data from current world models is still insufficient for robust policy training.
 - **Embodied Policy Evaluator:** **CtrlWorld** exhibited a **strong correlation (Pearson r = 0.986)** with evaluation results from the RoboTwin simulator, suggesting it effectively captures meaningful environment dynamics. In contrast, Cosmos-Predict 2.5 showed a weaker correlation (r = 0.483). Both models, however, showed consistently higher success rates than the simulator, implying some overfitting to successful trajectories.
 - **Embodied Action Planner:** While several world models achieved non-trivial task success rates as action planners, their performance was **substantially lower than that of specialized VLA policies**. This indicates that current models struggle with reliable closed-loop task execution, particularly over long horizons, pointing to significant room for improvement in supporting autonomous control.

4. **Human Evaluation Insights:**
 - Commercial and large-scale general video models (e.g., Veo 3.1, Wan 2.6) received the highest human scores for overall quality, instruction following, and physical adherence, reaffirming their perceptual strength.
 - Among embodied models, **action-conditioned approaches (e.g., CtrlWorld)** showed notably **better physical adherence and higher win rates** than text-only counterparts, suggesting the importance of explicit action modeling.

5. **Inter-metric Analysis and EWMScore Validation:**
 - **EWMScore showed a strong correlation with human judgments (Pearson r = 0.825)**, indicating its reliability in capturing subjective perceptual assessments.
 - However, EWMScore exhibited **only moderate correlation with data synthesis performance (r = 0.600)** and a **weak correlation with action planning performance (r = 0.360)**. This crucial finding reinforces the perception–functionality gap: high perceptual realism (as captured by EWMScore) does not directly translate into proportional gains in functional embodied tasks. The limited correlation with action planning, in particular, suggests that current synthetic data lacks the robust predictive or decision-relevant signals needed for complex embodied reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldArena represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and the development of world models, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Standardized and Holistic Evaluation:** By introducing the first comprehensive benchmark that integrates both perceptual and functional evaluations, WorldArena provides a much-needed standardized framework for assessing embodied world models. This unification addresses the fragmentation in current evaluation practices, enabling fairer comparisons and clearer progress tracking across diverse models and applications.

2. **Guiding Research Towards Functional Utility:** The benchmark's emphasis on functional utility in embodied tasks (data engine, policy evaluator, action planner) directly shifts the research focus from mere visual fidelity towards practical applicability. The revelation of a significant "perception–functionality gap" is a critical insight that will guide future research to develop world models that are not only visually realistic but also functionally reliable for real-world robotic applications. This encourages the community to prioritize physically grounded, action-consistent dynamics over superficial perceptual quality.

3. **Comprehensive Model Coverage and Benchmarking:** By evaluating a wide array of 14 representative models, including both general-purpose and robot-specialized approaches, WorldArena provides a rich dataset for understanding the strengths and weaknesses of different world model architectures and conditioning mechanisms (text vs. action). This systematic benchmarking offers valuable insights and guidance for model designers and developers.

4. **Introduction of EWMScore:** The proposed EWMScore offers an interpretable, unified metric that correlates well with human judgment on perceptual quality. While it highlights the gap with functional utility, EWMScore itself serves as a robust objective measure for one critical dimension of world model performance, facilitating efficient and automated assessment.

5. **Accelerating Progress in Embodied AI:** By providing a public leaderboard and a robust evaluation framework, WorldArena is poised to become a central resource for the embodied AI community. It will foster healthy competition, accelerate model development, and provide clear benchmarks for advancements, ultimately contributing to the creation of more capable, intelligent, and autonomous embodied agents.

6. **Broader Implications for AI Evaluation:** The principles behind WorldArena—combining multi-dimensional objective metrics with task-specific functional evaluations and human judgment—could inspire similar holistic evaluation frameworks in other complex AI domains where perceptual output needs to translate into real-world utility.

In conclusion, WorldArena is more than just a collection of metrics; it is a conceptual shift in how embodied world models should be assessed. By exposing the limitations of current models and providing a roadmap for future development, it lays a crucial foundation for building truly intelligent and functional embodied AI systems.
