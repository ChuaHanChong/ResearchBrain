# ViTacGen: Robotic Pushing with Vision-to-Touch Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The ViTacGen framework enables robotic pushing by synthesizing tactile contact depth images from visual input, removing the need for physical tactile sensors during real-world deployment. This approach allows for robust sim-to-real transfer, achieving 80-86% success rates in various pushing tasks using only visual input and demonstrating 75.2% average success on novel objects.

## Problem

- High-resolution tactile sensors are expensive, fragile, difficult to calibrate, and suffer from manufacturing inconsistencies, hindering widespread robotic deployment.
- Vision-only robotic policies often lack the fine-grained contact dynamics needed for precise and robust manipulation tasks like pushing.
- Current robotic systems struggle to infer detailed tactile properties from visual cues, unlike human perception.

## Method

- A Vision-to-Touch Generation (VT-Gen) network synthesizes standardized tactile contact depth images directly from visual input sequences.
- An RL policy (VT-Con) integrates the generated tactile data, visual observations, and proprioception using attention-based feature fusion and contrastive learning (MoCo).
- The system is trained entirely in simulation with extensive domain randomization, enabling zero-shot transfer to real-world visual-only robotic platforms.

## Results

- The VT-Gen network effectively generates tactile contact depth images from visual inputs, demonstrating high perceptual quality (e.g., PSNR 20.25-30.75) at 305.90 FPS.
- ViTacGen, using generated tactile data, achieves 80-86% success rates in simulated pushing tasks, outperforming visual-only and naive visual-tactile fusion baselines.
- The framework demonstrates robust zero-shot transfer to real-world scenarios, achieving an 86% success rate for a mug pushing task (compared to 10% for a visual-only baseline) and generalizing to unseen objects with an average 75.2% success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality tactile information can be accurately synthesized from visual data, enabling tactile-informed policies without physical tactile sensors.
> - Effective multi-modal feature fusion through attention mechanisms and cross-modal alignment via contrastive learning significantly enhances robotic manipulation policy performance.
> - Utilizing standardized tactile representations and extensive domain randomization in simulation facilitates robust zero-shot transfer to real-world visual-only systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: robotic manipulation, tactile sensing, multi-modal perception, reinforcement learning, and sim-to-real transfer.

- **Robotic Pushing as a Fundamental Task:** Robotic pushing is recognized as a fundamental manipulation primitive, crucial for moving objects in cluttered environments or when grasping is infeasible. Early approaches often relied on purely visual data or simplified physics models. However, the paper highlights that vision-only methods often struggle to capture the *subtle contact dynamics* essential for precise and robust pushing, leading to suboptimal performance.
- **The Rise and Challenges of Tactile Sensing:** Tactile sensing has emerged as a vital component for intricate manipulation tasks, providing localized and precise feedback about contact forces, surface properties, and interaction dynamics. It complements vision by offering detailed information at the point of contact. However, the paper critically addresses the significant practical challenges of deploying high-quality tactile sensors in real-world scenarios: high cost, fragility, complex calibration, susceptibility to noise and wear, and manufacturing inconsistencies across different units. These limitations create a "perception gap" that restricts the widespread adoption of tactile-driven robotics.
- **Vision-Tactile Fusion and Cross-Modal Learning:** The research draws inspiration from human perception, where visual and tactile information are naturally integrated to form a comprehensive understanding of the environment. In robotics, the trend has moved from simple data-level fusion to more sophisticated *joint representation learning* using deep learning, attention mechanisms, and contrastive learning. This paper contributes to this trajectory by not just fusing modalities but *generating* one from the other. Previous works, including those by the authors, have explored visual-tactile fusion for tasks like texture recognition and grasp outcome prediction. The idea of "vision-to-touch generation" is a more recent and powerful paradigm within cross-modal generative modeling, directly enabling the inference of tactile properties without physical contact, thereby overcoming the aforementioned hardware limitations.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Complex Behaviors:** Data-driven reinforcement learning (RL) has become a dominant approach for teaching robots complex behaviors through trial and error. Algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), used in this paper, are favored for their sample efficiency and ability to handle high-dimensional observation spaces. The integration of generated tactile data within an RL framework for robotic pushing is a key contribution to making RL policies more robust and informed, especially when direct tactile sensing is unavailable.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer and Domain Randomization:** Training RL policies often requires vast amounts of data, which is impractical to collect in the real world. Simulators like Tactile Gym 2.0 (developed by some of the co-authors) are crucial for generating large datasets. However, transferring policies trained in simulation to the real world (sim-to-real) remains a challenge due to the "reality gap." This paper tackles this by leveraging a standardized tactile representation (contact depth images) and extensive visual domain randomization during simulation training, aiming for *zero-shot deployment* in real-world visual-only systems. This approach offers a practical solution to the sim-to-real problem for tactile-informed policies.

In summary, ViTacGen advances the state-of-the-art by presenting a novel solution to a long-standing practical problem in robotic manipulation: how to leverage the benefits of tactile feedback without the associated hardware and deployment costs. It synthesizes insights from human perception, cross-modal generative modeling, and reinforcement learning to create a robust and deployable system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ViTacGen stems from a critical perception gap in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks like pushing that demand subtle contact dynamics. While tactile sensing offers invaluable, detailed information about contact interactions, its practical deployment is severely hampered by a myriad of challenges.

**Primary Motivation/Problem Statement:**

- **Limitations of Real Tactile Sensors:** High-resolution tactile sensors are typically expensive, fragile, and require specialized setups and precise calibration. They are also prone to noise, wear, and physical damage, and suffer from manufacturing inconsistencies that can impair model performance when deployed across different units. These hardware and deployment hurdles make them inaccessible or impractical for many real-world robotic applications.
- **Inadequacy of Vision-Only Policies:** Conversely, robotic policies relying solely on visual input often lack the ability to capture the fine-grained contact forces and subtle dynamics between the end-effector and the object. This limitation results in suboptimal performance, accuracy, and robustness in tasks requiring precise interaction, such as pushing objects along complex trajectories.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Humans possess a remarkable ability to infer tactile states—such as an object's texture, hardness, or the forces being applied—from visual cues alone, even without direct physical contact. This biological capability provides a powerful inspiration for developing more versatile and robust artificial perception systems.

**Key Objectives of ViTacGen:**

In response to these motivations, the authors propose ViTacGen with the following key objectives:

1. **Eliminate Reliance on Physical Tactile Sensors:** The overarching goal is to design a robot manipulation framework for visual robotic pushing that completely removes the need for high-resolution, expensive, and fragile *real* tactile sensors during inference and deployment.
2. **Generate Standardized Tactile Information from Vision:** Develop a robust "vision-to-touch generation network" (VT-Gen) capable of synthesizing tactile contact depth images—chosen as a standardized, generalized tactile representation—directly and accurately from visual image sequences. This aims to bridge the information gap left by the absence of real tactile sensors.
3. **Learn Robust Policies through Multi-Modal Fusion and Contrastive Learning:** Integrate the generated tactile information with visual observations and robot proprioception within a reinforcement learning framework (VT-Con). This integration should utilize advanced techniques like attention-based feature fusion and contrastive learning to establish strong, meaningful relationships between visual and generated tactile modalities, thereby learning more effective and robust manipulation policies.
4. **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Deployment:** Ensure that the trained policies can be transferred seamlessly and effectively from simulation to real-world visual-only robotic systems without further training or adaptation. This addresses the practical deployment challenges and aims for high performance in unseen real-world scenarios.
5. **Address Manufacturing Variations:** By generating a standardized tactile representation (contact depth maps), the framework implicitly tackles the problem of manufacturing inconsistencies and variations across different physical tactile sensors, as the generated input is consistent regardless of real sensor specifics.

By achieving these objectives, ViTacGen aims to make tactile-informed robotic manipulation more accessible, cost-effective, robust, and widely deployable, significantly expanding the capabilities of robots in interaction-rich tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ViTacGen framework is meticulously designed with two core components and a two-stage training process, primarily leveraging simulation for data generation and policy learning, followed by zero-shot deployment in the real world.

**A. Overall Framework (ViTacGen) and Training Pipeline:**

ViTacGen consists of:
1. **VT-Gen (Vision-to-Touch Generation Network):** An encoder-decoder network responsible for synthesizing tactile contact depth images from visual input.
2. **VT-Con (Reinforcement Learning with Vision-to-Touch Generation):** An RL policy network that fuses visual and *generated* tactile data along with proprioception to learn manipulation behaviors.

The training pipeline is sequential and conducted entirely in simulation:
- **Stage 1: VT-Gen Training:** Paired visual and *ground truth tactile* data are collected from expert robot trajectories generated by a pre-trained RL network that uses both actual visual and tactile observations. This dataset is then used to train VT-Gen to predict tactile contact depth images from visual sequences.
- **Stage 2: VT-Con Training:** After VT-Gen is trained and its weights are *frozen*, it is integrated into the RL loop. VT-Con is then trained using visual observations, *generated tactile observations* from the frozen VT-Gen, and robot proprioception. This setup ensures that during policy learning, the system relies on generated tactile data, mirroring its eventual real-world visual-only deployment.

**B. VT-Gen: Encoder-Decoder Vision-to-Touch Generation**

- **Input:** A sequence of visual images $V = \{v_1, \ldots, v_N\}$, where N is the number of frames (empirically 3).
- **Architecture:** A coarse-to-refine encoder-decoder structure.
 - **Encoder:**
 - The visual sequence is first concatenated along the channel dimension.
 - A "coarse encoder" ($E_{coarse}$) extracts initial visual feature maps.
 - These features then pass through a "cross-modal attention" operation ($A_{cm}$), which incorporates a learnable positional embedding ($p$). This attention mechanism aims to process visual features more effectively for tactile inference.
 - A "refine encoder" ($E_{refine}$) further processes these features, followed by a series of residual blocks to enhance representation and preserve spatial information.
 - **Decoder:** A hierarchical decoder, composed of transposed convolutional layers and upsampling operations, reconstructs the final output.
- **Output:** A generated tactile contact depth image ($c_{gen}$). This image is then repeated N times to form a generated tactile sequence $C = \{c_1, \ldots, c_N\}$ to match the visual sequence length for VT-Con.
- **Loss Function:** VGG loss ($L_{vgg}$) is used, which measures the perceptual similarity between the generated contact depth image ($c_{gen}$) and the ground truth ($c_{gt}$) by comparing their high-level feature representations extracted from a pre-trained VGG network.

**C. VT-Con: RL with Visual-Tactile Contrastive Learning**

- **Inputs:**
 - Visual sequence $V = \{v_1, \ldots, v_N\}$.
 - Generated tactile sequence $C = \{c_1, \ldots, c_N\}$ (from VT-Gen).
 - Proprioception: Robot's Tool Center Point (TCP) coordinates.
- **Feature Extraction:**
 - Two structurally identical Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), $E_v$ and $E_c$, act as encoders for the visual and generated tactile modalities, respectively, producing feature maps $f_v$ and $f_c$.
- **Contrastive Learning between Vision and Touch:**
 - To improve the fusion of visual and tactile features, $E_v$ and $E_c$ are optimized using **Momentum Contrast (MoCo)**.
 - Two momentum-based encoders ($M_v, M_c$) (updated with a momentum coefficient $\eta$) generate momentum visual ($m_v$) and tactile ($m_c$) features.
 - The **InfoNCE loss** ($L_{con}$) is computed to align $f_v$ with $m_c$ and $f_c$ with $m_v$. This loss is incorporated into the actor's training within the RL process, optimizing the encoders $E_v$ and $E_c$ after each RL update.
- **Attention-based Visual-Tactile Feature Fusion:**
 - An attention-based fusion mechanism (similar to that in VT-Gen) is employed to integrate the visual ($f_v$) and tactile ($f_c$) feature maps, resulting in a fused feature map $f_{fuse}$.
- **Reinforcement Learning Policy:**
 - The flattened $f_{fuse}$ is concatenated with MLP-processed proprioception to form the complete observation vector for the RL algorithm.
 - **Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)** is chosen as the RL backbone due to its off-policy nature, entropy regularization for exploration, and sample efficiency.
 - **Reward Function:** A dense reward $R(s_t, a_t) = -d_{goal} - d_{TCP}$ is used, minimizing both the object-to-goal distance and the TCP-to-object distance.

**D. Tactile Representation and Simulation Environment:**

- **Standardized Tactile Representation:** The paper adopts **contact depth images** as the standardized tactile representation. This is derived from the difference between a current depth image and a reference depth image within a virtual optical tactile sensor, generalizing to various sensor shapes and approximating soft tip deformation.
- **Simulation Platform:** **Tactile Gym 2** is used, a PyBullet-based simulator known for efficient visual-tactile robot learning capabilities.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, extensive domain randomization is applied in simulation, including variations in object mass, surface friction, camera view, lighting, background, and object colors.

**E. Experimental Setup:**

- **Robot:** UR5e arm with a pushing end-effector.
- **Camera:** External Intel RealSense D435 RGB camera.
- **Objects:** YCB dataset objects (tea box, meat can, mug) for primary evaluation, and five novel "unseen objects" for zero-shot testing in the real world.
- **Training Details:** VT-Gen trained for 200 epochs on 1,000 manipulation sequences. VT-Con (SAC) trained for 1,000,000 timesteps. Adam optimizer with learning rate 1e-4.

This comprehensive methodology allows ViTacGen to learn robust manipulation policies by effectively synthesizing and integrating tactile information from purely visual inputs, paving the way for practical real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulation and real-world settings, consistently demonstrate the superior performance and practical viability of the ViTacGen framework.

**A. Effectiveness of Vision-to-Touch Generation (VT-Gen)**

- **High Generation Quality:** Quantitative evaluation of VT-Gen using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics (Table I) shows excellent performance in generating tactile contact depth maps from visual image sequences. For instance, PSNRs range from 20.25 to 30.75, SSIMs from 0.8222 to 0.9482, and LPIPS from 0.0101 to 0.0417, indicating high perceptual similarity and low error compared to ground truth tactile data.
- **Architectural Robustness:** The robust performance is attributed to the coarse-to-refine cross-modal based encoders, which effectively capture hierarchical and multi-scale visual features for tactile inference.
- **Real-time Operation:** VT-Gen boasts a modest model size (146.74 MiB) and an impressive inference speed (305.90 FPS), ensuring its suitability for real-time robotic control and practical deployment.

**B. Simulation Robotic Pushing Results**

- **Limitations of Single Modality and Naive Fusion:** As shown in Table II, single-modality baselines (visual-only, tactile-only) exhibit poor performance across all metrics (cumulative rewards, episode length, distance error, success rate). The "Visual & Tactile" baseline, which simply concatenates features without effective cross-modal alignment or contrastive learning, shows only marginal improvements, highlighting the inadequacy of naive fusion.
- **Superiority of VT-Con (with Actual Tactile):** The ViTacGen framework, when trained with actual visual and tactile data (i.e., using VT-Con without VT-Gen, for reference), demonstrates significant performance gains. It achieves 64.61% to 69.50% higher cumulative rewards, 12.92% to 23.30% shorter episode lengths, and 13.36 mm to 26.94 mm lower distance errors compared to the best baseline, with success rates reaching 83% to 94%. This validates the effectiveness of contrastive learning and attention-based fusion for multi-modal policy learning.
- **Effectiveness of ViTacGen (with Generated Tactile):** Crucially, when ViTacGen is deployed with *only visual input* and relies on the *generated tactile contact depth images* from VT-Gen, it still achieves very strong performance. It significantly outperforms *all* baselines (including the one with actual visual and tactile observations). ViTacGen (visual-only input) maintains high success rates of 80% to 86%, demonstrating that generated tactile information can effectively substitute real tactile sensors for robust manipulation. In some cases, it even surpassed the performance of the actual visual & tactile input version in metrics like episode length and distance error for certain objects.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Figure 4 visually confirms the robust and stable pushing performance across objects of various shapes and the high fidelity of the generated tactile contact depth maps compared to ground truth.

**C. Real World Robotic Pushing Demonstration**

- **Robust Zero-Shot Transfer:** ViTacGen exhibits robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to real-world scenarios (Table III, Figure 5). It dramatically outperforms the visual-only baseline in real-world settings. For example, for pushing a mug, ViTacGen achieves an 86% success rate compared to the baseline's 10%.
- **Tolerance to Sim-to-Real Discrepancy:** Although the tactile predictions generated from real-world visual input show some noise artifacts due to sim-to-real visual discrepancies, these perturbations do not significantly impede the RL algorithm's execution or the overall manipulation performance.
- **Generalizability to Unseen Objects:** Experiments on unseen real-world objects (Table IV) further confirm ViTacGen's strong generalizability and robustness. It achieves an average success rate of 75.2% with a 3.8 cm distance error across diverse objects (apple, coffee box, ceramic cup, olive jar, soup can), highlighting its ability to handle novel situations without specific training.

**D. Ablation Studies**

- **Fusion Module:** The ablation study on fusion modules (Table V) confirms that **attention-based fusion** outperforms simpler methods like addition and concatenation, leading to better rewards, shorter episode lengths, lower distance errors, and higher success rates.
- **Contrastive Learning Module:** Comparing contrastive learning methods (Table VI), **MoCo** demonstrates superior performance over SimCLR across all metrics, indicating its effectiveness in aligning visual and tactile modalities for this specific task.

In summary, the findings robustly validate ViTacGen's core hypothesis: high-quality vision-to-touch generation, combined with effective multi-modal fusion and contrastive learning, can enable tactile-informed robotic manipulation without direct reliance on physical tactile sensors, offering a practical and high-performing solution for real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViTacGen represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, addressing a long-standing practical hurdle in deploying tactile sensing and unlocking new possibilities for robust and accessible robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of Tactile-Informed Robotics:** The most immediate and profound impact is the elimination of the dependency on expensive, fragile, and calibration-intensive high-resolution real tactile sensors. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for incorporating tactile-like feedback into robotic systems, making advanced manipulation capabilities more accessible and cost-effective for researchers and industrial applications. This could lead to a wider adoption of robots in tasks previously deemed too delicate or complex for vision-only systems.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Precision in Manipulation:** By inferring and integrating contact depth information from vision, ViTacGen enables robots to perceive subtle contact dynamics that are critical for tasks like pushing, which vision-only policies often miss. This leads to more precise control, higher success rates, and greater robustness in interaction-rich manipulation tasks, even in scenarios with minor environmental variations.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Tactile Tasks:** The framework demonstrates effective zero-shot transfer from simulation to real-world visual-only systems. This is achieved by using contact depth as a standardized tactile representation and employing extensive domain randomization during simulation training, mitigating the "reality gap" that often plagues sim-to-real transfer for tactile sensing. This makes it easier and faster to develop and deploy tactile-informed policies.
- **Biologically Inspired and Computationally Efficient Perception:** Drawing inspiration from humans' ability to infer tactile states from vision, ViTacGen offers a biologically plausible approach to robotic perception. Furthermore, the high inference speed of VT-Gen (305.90 FPS) ensures that the generated tactile information is available in real-time, making the entire system practical for dynamic robotic operations.
- **Standardization for Tactile Representation:** The use of contact depth images as a standardized tactile representation effectively addresses the problem of manufacturing inconsistencies and variations between different physical tactile sensors. This simplifies data interpretation and model generalization, as the robot's "sense of touch" is consistent regardless of specific hardware.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** While ViTacGen focuses on contact depth, the authors acknowledge limitations in perceiving detailed physical properties like local force distributions, fine-grained surface geometry, or shear forces. This sets a clear direction for future research, where advanced vision-to-touch generation could explicitly predict these richer tactile properties, further enhancing policy performance. It also opens avenues for exploring inference of shear forces directly from visual observations of object motions.
- **Broader Applications Beyond Pushing:** The core concept of generating tactile information from vision could be extended to a wide range of other manipulation tasks, such as grasping, assembly, dexterous manipulation, and even tasks involving deformable objects, where fine-grained interaction understanding is crucial. This could enable robots to perform more human-like manipulations in unstructured environments.

In essence, ViTacGen moves beyond simply combining visual and tactile data; it proposes an ingenious way to *synthesize* the missing modality, thereby circumventing the practical barriers of one of robotics' most challenging sensory inputs. This innovation promises to accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems capable of more nuanced and robust interactions with the physical world.
