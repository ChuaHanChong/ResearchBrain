# X-Humanoid: Robotize Human Videos to Generate Humanoid Videos at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> X-Humanoid adapts a Wan 2.2 video diffusion transformer into a video-to-video model that translates third-person human videos into humanoid-robot videos, trained via LoRA finetuning on a synthesized Unreal Engine paired dataset. It releases a 60-hour, 3.6M-frame robotized Ego-Exo4D dataset for VLA and world-model training.

## Problem

- Prior robotizing methods only overlay rendered robot arms onto egocentric video, unable to handle full-body motion needed for third-person humanoid footage.
- Rule-based inpaint-and-overlay techniques produce occlusion artifacts and cannot cope with dynamic backgrounds or severe occlusions in third-person scenes.
- No paired human-humanoid video dataset exists to train a generative model for human-to-humanoid video translation, blocking a learned alternative to overlay methods.

## Method

- ==Video-to-video DiT architecture==: adapts Wan 2.2 by concatenating condition tokens (encoded human video) with generation tokens, sharing positional embeddings for spatio-temporal alignment.
- ==Unidirectional attention masking== in each DiT block prevents condition tokens from attending to generation tokens, so only generation tokens are denoised into the humanoid output.
- ==Unreal Engine synthetic data pipeline==: skeleton alignment, animation retargeting/baking, and paired camera-synchronized rendering yields 11,172 paired 1080p clips (2.8M frames) across 14 scenes.

## Results

- Outperforms Kling, MoCha, and Runway Aleph baselines on synthetic validation: PSNR **21.836dB**, SSIM **0.671**, MSE **459.302** versus baselines' best of **17.683dB**/**0.402**/**1295.640**.
- User study (**29** raters) on Ego-Exo4D: **69.0%** preferred motion consistency, **75.9%** background consistency, **62.1%** embodiment consistency, **62.1%** overall quality, all highest among methods.
- Ablations: **5B** model beats VACE-1.3B/14B and a 14B variant of itself (PSNR **21.836** vs **16.424-21.311**) while the **14B** model is over **10x** slower to train/infer, confirming **5B** as the compute-quality optimum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fixed conditioning text keyword tightly binds to a learned visual concept: dropping the finetuning prompt token collapses embodiment correctness, showing prompt-concept coupling in LoRA finetunes.
> - More finetuning steps can trade validation similarity for real-world generalization: overfitting to synthetic domain quietly degrades results on real videos despite improving held-out synthetic metrics.
> - Synthetic paired data quality directly imprints on generation surface details (e.g. matte vs reflective masks), so simulation asset choices leak into perceived realism, not just geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a fundamental and pressing challenge in the field of embodied artificial intelligence: **data scarcity**. The advancement of intelligent humanoid robots, particularly in the areas of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models, is severely constrained by the limited availability of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality robot-specific training data. Traditional methods of collecting robot data are prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and limited in scale and scene diversity.

The broader research landscape has explored several strategies to mitigate this data bottleneck. One promising avenue involves leveraging the vast repositories of existing human activity videos. However, this approach encounters a significant hurdle: the **visual embodiment gap**. Humans and robots possess distinct physical appearances, making direct application of human video data problematic for training robot policies that operate at the pixel level.

Prior attempts to bridge this gap primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Intermediate Representations:** Methods that extract abstract representations like human poses or trajectories from videos to guide robot execution, or use specific reward functions for pretraining visual encoders. While these approaches bypass the visual domain gap, they do not provide pixel-level robot data, which is often preferred for more straightforward training of VLA or world models.
2. **Limited "Robotization":** Earlier "robotization" techniques, such as those that overlay rendered robot arms onto egocentric human videos, have shown some benefits but suffer from crucial limitations. They often produce visual artifacts (e.g., incorrect occlusions) and are typically restricted to egocentric views. Critically, they struggle with the complexity of third-person (exocentric) videos, which involve intricate full-body motions, dynamic backgrounds, and severe occlusions, making them unsuitable for generating comprehensive humanoid data.

**X-Humanoid carves out a novel and significant niche** by directly tackling the visual embodiment gap at the pixel level for **third-person human videos** using **modern generative video models**. This work represents a significant departure from previous rule-based or overlay methods by leveraging the powerful capabilities of diffusion transformers to perform flexible, high-fidelity human-to-humanoid video editing. By doing so, it aims to produce visually consistent, pixel-level robot data that can directly serve as training input for robot policies and world models, promising more straightforward training and potentially higher performance bounds.

The paper further contributes to the research ecosystem by designing a scalable synthetic data creation pipeline. This pipeline generates the necessary paired human-humanoid videos, which are a prerequisite for training their generative model but did not previously exist at scale. The release of both this synthetic dataset and a large-scale "robotized" version of a prominent human activity dataset (Ego-Exo4D) directly enriches the public resources available for embodied AI research, establishing a new foundation for future developments in general-purpose humanoid autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind X-Humanoid stems from the critical bottleneck of **data scarcity** in embodied AI research. The development of advanced humanoid robots capable of understanding and interacting with the world through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models is severely hampered by the lack of sufficiently large, diverse, and realistic training data. Existing robot data collection is expensive and limited, while leveraging human videos is challenging due to the inherent "visual embodiment gap" and the limitations of current "robotization" techniques, particularly for complex third-person perspectives.

Against this backdrop, X-Humanoid sets out to achieve several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Generative Video Editing Approach:** The primary objective is to create a robust and high-fidelity method for "robotizing" third-person human videos. This involves transforming a human protagonist into a specific humanoid robot within the same scene, ensuring that the robot's motion is accurately consistent with the original human's action and strictly per-frame aligned, while preserving all other scene details.
2. **Bridge the Visual Embodiment Gap at the Pixel Level:** Unlike methods that rely on intermediate representations or simple overlays, X-Humanoid aims to generate pixel-level robot video data that is directly usable for training VLA and world models, thereby enabling more seamless and potentially more effective learning for humanoid robots.
3. **Create a Scalable Paired Human-Humanoid Dataset:** To train the proposed video-to-video generative model, a large-scale dataset of paired human and humanoid videos performing identical actions in identical environments is indispensable. A key objective is to design and implement a scalable pipeline to synthetically generate such a dataset, leveraging existing community assets and advanced rendering tools.
4. **Generate a Large-Scale Robotized Video Dataset:** A crucial objective is to apply the developed generative model to substantial real-world human video datasets, such as Ego-Exo4D, to produce and publicly release a new, large-scale dataset of "robotized" humanoid videos. This directly addresses the data scarcity problem by providing a valuable resource for the research community.
5. **Achieve Superior Performance:** The method aims to quantitatively and qualitatively outperform existing video editing baselines in terms of motion consistency, embodiment correctness (specifically for the Tesla Optimus humanoid), background preservation, and overall video quality when "robotizing" human videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The X-Humanoid methodology employs a novel paradigm for "robotizing" human videos, fundamentally based on adapting and finetuning a powerful video diffusion transformer. The approach is structured around three main pillars: data creation, model architecture adaptation, and specialized finetuning.

**Overall Paradigm (Figure 1):**
1. **Model Adaptation:** A Video Diffusion Transformer (DiT), specifically the Wan 2.2 model, is adapted into a video-to-video generation architecture.
2. **Finetuning on Synthetic Data:** This adapted model is then finetuned on a custom-designed, large-scale synthetic dataset of paired human and humanoid videos.
3. **Large-Scale Inference:** The finetuned model is applied to real-world human videos to generate a massive dataset of "robotized" humanoid videos, intended for downstream robotics applications like training VLA or world models.

**Detailed Methodology:**

1. **Creating Human-Humanoid Paired Videos (Section 3.2):**
 - **Motivation:** Training a video-to-video model for human-to-humanoid translation requires corresponding pairs of videos where a human and a humanoid perform the exact same actions in the same scene. Since no such large-scale dataset existed, the authors developed a scalable synthesis pipeline using Unreal Engine.
 - **Pipeline (Figure 2):**
 - **Step 1: Skeleton Alignment:** Diverse community-sourced 3D assets (characters, animations, environments) possess incompatible skeletons. This is resolved through manual IK Rig Retargeting to ensure motion compatibility.
 - **Step 2: Animation Transfer:** Once skeletons are aligned, a single animation (motion) can be transferred (or "baked") to both human and humanoid characters, ensuring identical actions.
 - **Step 3: Video Rendering:** Both animated human and humanoid characters are placed in the same virtual 3D scenes. Identical virtual camera setups and movements are used to record them, generating perfectly synchronized paired video clips.
 - **Diversity and Scale:** To enhance model robustness, the data includes varied camera paths, intentional occlusions, off-center characters, diverse exposure and color grading settings, and a range of focal lengths and apertures. The pipeline generated 11,172 pairs, totaling over 17 hours (more than 2.8 million frames) of 1080p 30fps video data, rendered in 10 days on a single NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU.

2. **Model Architecture (Section 3.3):**
 - **Base Model:** The powerful Wan 2.2 Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is chosen as the foundational video generation model.
 - **Video-in Video-out Adaptation (Figure 4):** The core innovation is to adapt Wan 2.2, originally designed for text-to-video generation, into a video-in video-out architecture.
 - **Condition and Generation Tokens:** The input human video is encoded into a sequence of "condition tokens." These are concatenated with "generation tokens" that the model will denoise to produce the output humanoid video.
 - **Spatio-temporal Correspondence:** Identical positional embeddings are used for both sets of tokens to maintain strict spatio-temporal alignment between the input and output.
 - **Unidirectional Masking:** Crucially, a unidirectional mask is applied within each DiT block's self-attention mechanism. This prevents the condition tokens (derived from the input human video) from attending to the generation tokens, thereby preserving the input information from being corrupted during the generation process. The model's output on conditional tokens is ignored.

3. **Finetuning (Section 3.4):**
 - **Objective:** The adapted model is finetuned using a flow-matching objective, consistent with Wan 2.2's original training paradigm.
 - **Loss Function:** A mean-squared error (MSE) loss is employed to train the model `v_theta` to predict a ground-truth velocity `v_t`. This velocity is the derivative of a linear path (`x_t = t * x_1 + (1 - t) * x_0`) interpolating from pure noise (`x_0`) to the clean latent tokens (`x_1`).
 - **Inputs for Finetuning:** The model takes condition tokens (`x_con_1`), generation tokens (`x_gen_t`), a fixed conditional text embedding ("Humanoid video"), and a timestep (`t`).
 - **Inference:** During inference, the model iteratively predicts the velocity, which is then integrated from `t = 0` to `1` to reconstruct the final, edited (robotized) video.
 - **Experimental Setup (Section 4.1):**
 - **Data Preparation:** Both synthetic and real videos (from Ego-Exo4D and the Internet) are uniformly processed: downsampled to 15 fps, resized to 864x400, and segmented into 90-frame (6-second) clips.
 - **Training Details:** Rank-96 LoRA finetuning was performed on a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B model for 500 steps, utilizing four NVIDIA H200 GPUs with Distributed Data Parallel (DDP). The AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-4 was used. This efficient process completed in 2.5 hours, leveraging only 6.4% of the newly synthesized paired data.

**Evaluation:**
- **Real-world (Ego-Exo4D):** User studies with 29 participants (with computer vision/robotics backgrounds) were conducted to assess motion consistency, background consistency, embodiment consistency (with the Tesla Optimus humanoid), and overall video quality. Preference rates for each method were reported.
- **Synthetic (Validation Set):** Quantitative metrics including PSNR, SSIM, and MSE were used to compare generated videos against ground-truth synthetic videos.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

X-Humanoid demonstrates significant advancements in "robotizing" human videos, outperforming existing baselines across both quantitative and qualitative evaluations.

**Superior Performance Against Baselines:**

1. **Quantitative Metrics on Synthetic Data (Table 1):** When evaluated against ground-truth synthetic videos, X-Humanoid significantly surpasses baselines (Kling, MoCha, Runway Aleph) in fidelity:
 - **PSNR:** 21.836 dB (vs. 16.952-17.683 dB).
 - **SSIM:** 0.671 (vs. 0.288-0.402).
 - **MSE:** 459.302 (vs. 1295.640-1534.145).
 These metrics confirm X-Humanoid's ability to generate videos that are substantially more similar to the desired output.

2. **User Preference on Real-world Ego-Exo4D Videos (Table 1):** User studies with expert participants highlight X-Humanoid's qualitative superiority on complex real-world data:
 - **Motion Consistency:** 69.0% user preference, significantly higher than MoCha (20.7%), Kling (17.2%), and Aleph (0.0%). This indicates that X-Humanoid effectively preserves the original human's actions in the robotized output.
 - **Background Consistency:** 75.9% user preference, demonstrating excellent preservation of environmental details compared to baselines.
 - **Embodiment Consistency:** 62.1% user preference for the generated humanoid's appearance matching the Tesla Optimus, outperforming Kling (37.9%) and other baselines. The only noted limitation was the rendering of a matte (non-reflective) face mask, faithfully replicated from the synthetic training data.
 - **Overall Video Quality:** 62.1% user preference, indicating a generally superior visual experience.

3. **Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 5 & 10):** Visual results corroborate the quantitative findings:
 - X-Humanoid successfully generates correct humanoid embodiment with motions synchronized with the original human, accurately reproducing details like shoulder and leg joints.
 - Baselines often fail in crucial aspects: MoCha maintains motion but produces incorrect embodiment details; Kling generates a more correct embodiment but suffers from poor motion consistency (unsynchronized actions, different positions/orientations) and undesired scene alterations; Runway Aleph generally fails on both embodiment and motion.

**Robustness on In-the-wild Internet Videos (Figure 8):**
X-Humanoid exhibits remarkable robustness, seamlessly replacing human protagonists in diverse Internet videos. It preserves original motion, fine details, camera cuts, aspect ratios, video quality, and even complex effects like motion blur. This showcases the power inherited from the base Wan 2.2 model and its potential for broad applicability.

**Ablation Studies:**

1. **Model Architecture (Table 2, 3 & Figure 6):** The Wan2.2-TI2V-5B model (used in X-Humanoid) was found to be the optimal choice. While VACE models failed to generate correct robot embodiments, the larger Wan2.2-T2V-A14B model, despite being more complex, yielded slightly worse quantitative performance and incurred over tenfold higher computational costs for both training and inference, making it impractical for large-scale robotization.

2. **Finetuning Steps (Table 4 & Figure 9 in Supplementary B):** An optimal finetuning duration of 500 steps was identified. Too few steps (e.g., 200) resulted in incorrect occlusions, making the robot appear "overlaid." Conversely, too many steps (e.g., 700+) led to overfitting to the synthetic data's domain, causing performance degradation on real videos (e.g., fusion of robot leg with a bicycle frame).

3. **Inference Prompt (Figure 7):** The specific finetuning prompt ("Humanoid video") was found to be most effective. Using alternative or incomplete prompts led to suboptimal performance or incorrect embodiments, highlighting the model's learned association between the "Humanoid" keyword and the target robot's appearance.

**Data Release:**
A significant practical outcome is the creation and public release of a 17+ hour, 1080p 30fps synthetic paired human-humanoid video dataset, alongside a massive 60+ hour (3.6 million frames) "robotized" version of the Ego-Exo4D dataset featuring the Tesla Optimus humanoid.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The X-Humanoid project offers profound significance and promises substantial impact on the fields of embodied AI, robotics, and generative video modeling.

1. **Alleviating Data Scarcity in Embodied AI:** This work directly tackles the most critical bottleneck hindering the progress of intelligent humanoid robots: the severe scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality training data for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and world models. By enabling the "robotization" of vast quantities of existing human activity videos, X-Humanoid provides a scalable and cost-effective solution to generate unprecedented volumes of robot-centric visual data. This could unlock new levels of performance and generalization for humanoid robots.

2. **Bridging the Visual Embodiment Gap at the Pixel Level:** X-Humanoid offers a novel and effective method to bridge the visual embodiment gap by directly generating pixel-level robot videos. Unlike prior approaches that rely on intermediate representations or limited overlays, this method provides a "straightforward training" path for robot policies and world models. By training on data that visually matches the robot's perception, learning systems can potentially achieve higher performance bounds and more robust generalization in real-world scenarios.

3. **Advancing Generative Video Modeling:** The research showcases a powerful application of modern generative video models (Diffusion Transformers) in a complex video editing task. Adapting a text-to-video model into a high-fidelity video-to-video architecture for precise character replacement, while maintaining spatio-temporal consistency and preserving scene details, pushes the boundaries of what these models can achieve. This demonstrates the versatility and potential of generative AI beyond mere content creation, extending its utility to data generation for scientific and engineering domains.

4. **Creation of Invaluable Public Resources:** The release of two significant datasets—a 17+ hour synthetic paired human-humanoid video dataset and a 60+ hour "robotized" Ego-Exo4D dataset featuring the Tesla Optimus humanoid—is a monumental contribution to the research community. These resources will foster reproducibility, accelerate research by providing readily available training data, and inspire new directions in humanoid robotics and embodied AI, reducing the barrier to entry for many researchers.

5. **Foundation for Future Robotics Research:** X-Humanoid establishes a robust foundation for numerous future research directions. This includes:
 - **More Generalizable Robots:** Training VLA and world models on diverse, large-scale robotized data will lead to robots that are more capable, adaptable, and generalizable across various tasks and environments.
 - **Enhanced World Models:** The generated data can be instrumental in training more accurate and predictive world models, allowing robots to better anticipate outcomes, plan complex actions, and engage in sophisticated reasoning.
 - **Advanced Video Editing Capabilities:** The techniques developed for this specific robotization task can be extended to other complex video editing challenges, potentially enabling the generation of synthetic data for a wide array of embodied agents or virtual characters.

6. **Addressing Limitations for Future Work:** The paper thoughtfully outlines limitations, such as handling multi-person scenes, requiring new LoRAs for each humanoid embodiment, and ensuring perfect detail preservation near the robot. These identified areas provide clear pathways for future research to further refine and expand the capabilities of robotization techniques.

In essence, X-Humanoid is not merely a technical achievement in video generation; it is a strategic intervention designed to catalyze progress in humanoid robotics by solving a fundamental data problem. Its impact will likely be felt through the development of more intelligent, capable, and autonomous embodied AI systems in the years to come.
