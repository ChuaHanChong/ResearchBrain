# eMEM: A Hybrid Spatio-Temporal Memory System For Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> eMEM is a hybrid spatio-temporal memory system for embodied agents that integrates LLM-agent memory's temporal consolidation with robotic spatial grounding. The system achieved an 80.8% overall weighted mean score on a novel diagnostic benchmark and demonstrated stable recall performance over simulated durations up to one year, indicating effective long-term memory retention.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-agent memory systems primarily focus on text or conversational contexts and lack native support for spatial coordinates, multi-modal perception, or spatial reasoning crucial for embodied agents.
- Robotics research provides advanced spatial representations, but these are primarily perceptual systems that do not track environmental changes over extended periods, consolidate experience, or support long-term episodic recall.
- Embodied agents require a memory system that is simultaneously searchable by meaning, space, and time to enable complex reasoning about physical environments and past experiences.

## Method

- **eMEM** (Embodied Memory) is a hybrid graph-based memory system designed to run in-process, integrating a working-memory buffer, a consolidation engine, and a 10-tool retrieval surface.
- It employs a biologically inspired tiered memory architecture (Working, Short-term, Long-term, Archived) and a two-phase consolidation pipeline to transform raw observations into compressed, higher-level summaries.
- The system utilizes a multi-index storage approach combining SQLite for relational data, HNSW for semantic search, and R-tree for 3D spatial queries, all unified under a graph data model.

## Results

- eMEM achieved an overall weighted mean score of 80.8 across 988 probes on the new eMEM-Bench v1 diagnostic benchmark.
- The system demonstrated a flat retention curve at ceiling (**100%** hit rate) for room-unique items from 1 hour to 1 year of simulated delay, indicating no measurable forgetting under long-term pressure.
- Ablation studies showed significant performance drops (30 percentage points on context-dependent retrieval and 29 percentage points on DRM lure rejection) for a pure Retrieval-Augmented Generation baseline, highlighting the contributions of eMEM's multi-layer storage and consolidation pipeline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating temporal and consolidation mechanisms from LLM-agent memory with spatial grounding from robotics is essential for effective memory systems in embodied agents.
> - A biologically-inspired tiered memory architecture and consolidation process can effectively manage memory growth and ensure long-term retention in continuously operating agents.
> - A hybrid multi-index architecture, encompassing relational, semantic, and spatial indexing, provides comprehensive retrieval capabilities necessary for complex spatio-temporal queries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of memory systems for artificial agents is a prominent area of research, particularly with the advent of large language models (LLMs). This work positions itself at the intersection of two primary research threads: LLM-agent memory systems and embodied spatial representations in robotics.

Existing LLM-agent memory architectures, such as Generative Agents, MemGPT, and A-MEM, typically treat memory as text streams or knowledge graphs. While these systems demonstrate sophisticated temporal and conversational memory capabilities, they generally lack native support for spatial coordinates, multi-modal perception layers, or the spatial reasoning required by embodied agents. For instance, they do not inherently handle queries like "where is the kitchen?" or "what objects are near the charging station?".

In parallel, robotics research has produced advanced spatial representations, including 3D scene graphs (e.g., Hydra, ConceptGraphs), visual-language maps, and semantic mapping techniques. These systems excel at understanding the current state of an environment and grounding perception in spatial reality. However, they are primarily perceptual systems that do not typically function as memory systems designed to track environmental changes over extended periods, consolidate experience into abstract knowledge, or support episodic recall over long horizons.

eMEM aims to bridge this gap by integrating the temporal and consolidation mechanisms characteristic of LLM-agent memory with the spatial grounding inherent in robotic scene understanding. It seeks to combine the strengths of both fields to create a memory system explicitly designed for embodied agents that operate in dynamic physical environments over extended durations, requiring memory that is simultaneously searchable by meaning, space, and and time. The paper also notes that many of eMEM's design choices are informed by computational counterparts of established findings in the neuroscience of memory, such as the Complementary Learning Systems theory and the role of the hippocampus in spatial memory.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a memory system tailored for embodied agents that interact with physical environments, addressing limitations observed in existing LLM-agent memory and robotic spatial representation systems.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the spatio-temporal memory gap for embodied agents:** Embodied agents, such as robots and autonomous vehicles, accumulate experiences that are inherently spatial and temporal. Current LLM-agent memory systems predominantly focus on text-based or conversational contexts and do not natively handle spatial coordinates, multi-modal perceptions, or the necessity to remember *where* and *when* events occurred. Robotic spatial representations, while spatially adept, typically lack mechanisms for temporal tracking, experience consolidation, or long-term episodic recall. eMEM seeks to provide a memory architecture that integrates semantic, spatial, and temporal dimensions seamlessly.

2. **Enable complex spatial and temporal reasoning:** An embodied agent needs to relate observations across different perceptual modalities, compress vast amounts of data into retrievable knowledge, and answer queries that involve combinations of meaning, location, and time. This necessitates a memory system capable of resolving concepts to locations, synthesizing cross-modal information, and tracking changes over extended periods.

3. **Implement a biologically inspired consolidation pipeline:** Drawing inspiration from biological memory systems, particularly the hippocampal-neocortical consolidation pathway, a key objective is to transform raw perceptual observations into compressed, higher-level summaries. This involves a tiered memory structure that supports rapid encoding of specific events and gradual extraction of regularities, aiding long-term retention and managing storage growth.

4. **Support persistent, long-duration operation:** Embodied agents operating continuously in physical environments require a memory system that is fully embedded, runs in-process, and can manage memory growth over arbitrarily long periods. This includes mechanisms for efficient storage, retrieval, and eventual archival of information.

5. **Provide a comprehensive tool interface for LLMs:** To facilitate effective interaction with LLM-based agents, the memory system needs to expose its retrieval capabilities through a structured tool-calling interface. This includes primitives for searching by meaning, location, and time, as well as higher-order tools that compose these primitives for more complex recall patterns.

6. **Develop a diagnostic benchmark for embodied memory:** A significant objective is the creation of a new evaluation framework, eMEM-Bench v1, designed specifically to assess embodied memory systems. This benchmark aims to move beyond surface-task evaluations by organizing probes around cognitive-psychology paradigms, allowing for a deeper understanding of which memory functions are working effectively or failing. This interpretability against human memory literature is a core motivation for the benchmark's design.

7. **Explore the benefits of a hybrid multi-index architecture:** The motivation includes demonstrating that a combination of established indexing techniques (relational, semantic, spatial) unified under a single graph model can effectively meet the diverse retrieval demands of embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

eMEM (Embodied Memory) is designed as a hybrid graph-based memory system that runs in-process alongside an embodied agent. Its architecture integrates multiple indexing and processing components.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview (Section 2)

The system comprises a `SPATIOTEMPORALMEMORY` facade that orchestrates a working-memory buffer, a consolidation engine, and a 10-tool retrieval surface. These components are backed by a `MEMORYSTORE` which combines SQLite, HNSW, and R-tree indices.

#### 3.1.1. Data Model (Figure 2)

eMEM represents memory as a graph with four node types and six edge types:
- **Observation nodes:** Represent single perceptual events (text, coordinates, timestamp, layer tag). These are analogous to hippocampal episodic traces.
- **Episode nodes:** Task containers that group observations and provide episodic context, supporting hierarchical nesting.
- **Gist nodes:** Consolidated summaries with spatial extent (center, radius) produced from clusters of observations, serving as neocortical schemata.
- **Entity nodes:** Persistent object concepts tracked across observations, forming a semantic memory store of objects.
These nodes are connected by `BELONGS_TO`, `FOLLOWS`, `SUBTASK_OF`, `SUMMARIZES`, `OBSERVED_IN`, and `COOCCURS_WITH` edge types.

#### 3.1.2. Memory Tiers (Figure 3)

Memory progresses through four tiers, inspired by biological consolidation:
- **Working tier:** Observations are held in an in-process deque buffer, auto-flushing based on batch size (default 5) or time interval (default 2s). Entity extraction is periodically triggered.
- **Short-term tier:** Flushed observations are stored across the three indices, becoming fully searchable while awaiting consolidation.
- **Long-term tier:** At episode end, observations are promoted, preserving text and embeddings. Raw observations remain searchable alongside their gists.
- **Archived tier:** After a configurable age (e.g., 1 hour), a `maintenance` sweep moves observations here, dropping raw text and embeddings, leaving only the gist searchable to manage storage growth.

#### 3.1.3. Three-Index Storage

eMEM uses complementary indices for different retrieval axes:
- **SQLite:** Stores structured data (observation text, timestamps, layer names, entity attributes, graph edges). Indexed on timestamp, layer, episode, tier, and entity name for relational queries.
- **HNSW (hnswlib):** Provides approximate nearest-neighbor semantic search over embedding vectors. A single index stores embeddings for observations, gists, and entities, with an ID mapping in SQLite. Cosine distance is used, with build parameters M=16, ef\_construction=200, and search ef=50.
- **R-tree (rtree):** Supports 3D spatial range and nearest-neighbor queries, rebuilt on startup from SQLite coordinates.
Queries often engage all three indices, e.g., semantic search (HNSW), spatial filtering (R-tree), and temporal/layer constraints (SQLite). Semantic search can optionally be biased towards recent observations using a recency weight.

#### 3.1.4. Multi-Layer Perception

Observations carry a `layer_name` tag identifying their perceptual source (e.g., VLM, object detector, place classifier, interoception). These are stored as independent observations at the same coordinates, allowing for emergent relationships between layers and flexible querying with optional layer filters.

#### 3.2. Consolidation Engine (Section 3)

The engine implements two-phase consolidation, analogous to biological systems.
- **Episode Consolidation:** At episode end, observations are sorted by timestamp and chunked based on temporal gaps (default 30 min). Each chunk is summarized into a gist by an LLM, supporting multi-layer synthesis. Entities are extracted and either merged with existing ones (based on semantic similarity and spatial proximity) or newly created.
- **Two-Phase Archival:** Phase one promotes observations to long-term memory (text, embeddings, spatial entries preserved). Phase two, a later `maintenance` sweep, moves observations older than `archive_after_seconds` (default 1 hour) to the archived tier, dropping text and embeddings, making only the gist searchable.
- **Time-Window Consolidation:** For non-episodic observations that age past a consolidation window, DBSCAN clustering groups observations by spatial proximity, and each cluster is consolidated into a gist independently.
- **Entity Tracking:** Entities are extracted during flush events and episode consolidation. Deduplication merges similar entities based on name similarity (cosine > 0.85) and spatial proximity (within 5m). `COOCCURS_WITH` edges track co-presence.

#### 3.3. LLM Tool Interface (Section 5)

eMEM exposes 10 tools to LLMs via JSON-schema descriptions, returning token-efficient formatted strings (Table 2):
- **Core Primitives:** `semantic_search`, `spatial_query`, `temporal_query`, `episode_summary`, `search_gists`, `entity_query`, `get_current_context`, `body_status`. These cover meaning, location, time, and agent state.
- **Higher-Order Tools:** `locate(concept)` resolves a semantic concept to a spatial position (centroid, radius, match count) by querying semantic memory. `recall(query)` chains `locate` with spatial aggregation, gathering gists and entities to produce a cross-modal summary grounded in spatial reality. This emphasizes emergent recall patterns from primitives.

#### 3.4. Systems Characterisation (Section 4)

Benchmarks were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX A5000 workstation using synthetic random-unit-vector embeddings, measuring ingest throughput, storage footprint, and R-tree rebuild times at N = 10^3, 10^4, and 10^5 observations.
- Storage grew predictably: ≈23 MiB at 10^4 and ≈223 MiB at 10^5 observations for 384-dimensional vectors.
- R-tree rebuild was sub-second at 10^4 and ≈5s at 10^5 (O(N log N) growth).
- Ingest throughput declined with N (∼2.6k obs/s at 10^3 to ∼0.6k obs/s at 10^5), tracking HNSW insertion cost.

#### 3.5. Neuroscience Foundations (Section 6)

eMEM's design is informed by concepts from memory neuroscience, including:
- **Complementary Learning Systems theory:** Observation nodes as hippocampal traces, gist nodes as neocortical abstractions.
- **Hippocampal spatial coordinate system:** Places emerge from data (clusters of observations), rather than being dedicated primitives.
- **Episodic vs. Semantic memory (Tulving):** Observations/episodes for episodic, gists/entities for semantic.
- **Memory reconsolidation:** Entity auto-merge updates existing records upon re-observation.
- **Context-dependent retrieval:** `get_current_context` leverages spatial proximity for recall.
- **Interoception:** Body states are treated as peer observation layers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of eMEM was conducted using eMEM-Bench v1, a novel benchmark specifically developed for embodied memory systems.

#### 4.1. eMEM-Bench v1 Design (Section 8)

- **Motivation:** Existing benchmarks (e.g., LoCoMo for text, OpenEQA for embodied QA) were deemed insufficient as they either lack spatial/multi-modal aspects or do not isolate specific memory functions. eMEM-Bench v1 aims to provide diagnostic resolution by aligning with cognitive-psychology paradigms, enabling interpretability against human memory research.
- **Harness:** A replay-based harness ingests pre-recorded trajectories into a fresh eMEM instance. A ReAct agent then uses eMEM's tool surface to answer probes, with predictions scored by an LLM judge (Gemma 3:27b). The agent model used was Qwen 3.6 (27B) for both agent and entity extraction, and embeddings were nomic-embed-text-v2-moe.
- **Scenes and Trajectories:** The benchmark uses 20 multi-room ProcTHOR-10K houses. Fixed teleport-and-rotate paths ensure every reachable position is visited. Three perception layers (VLM scene description, object detections, place classification) and synthetic interoception are recorded. `AdvanceClockPhase` allows simulated delays of up to one year, firing consolidation and archival processes to test long-term memory under system pressure.
- **Eight Paradigms:** The benchmark is organized around eight cognitive-psychology paradigms, each testing a distinct memory function with a known empirical signature and a natural embodied-agent analogue:
 - **Encoding and similarity:** DRM lures (false recognition of semantically related items), Pattern separation (encoding similar episodes distinctly), Pattern completion (recovering complete memory from partial cues).
 - **Source and context:** Source monitoring (attributing memory to its source, e.g., VLM vs. object detector), Context-dependent retrieval (recall is best in encoding context, e.g., current location).
 - **Temporal and interference:** Long-horizon interference (recall despite subsequent interfering material), Serial position (U-shaped recall over a sequence), Retention curve (recall as a function of delay).
- **Foil Methodology:** For yes/no probes (e.g., in the retention curve), foils (cross-scene absent objects) are included to ensure signal-detection discriminability and prevent yes-bias, confirming recall performance rather than mere instruction parroting.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results (Table 4)

- **Overall Performance:** eMEM achieved an overall weighted mean score of **80.8** across 988 probes. The "Perfect %" (share of probes scored 5 by the judge) was 78.5%, and "Tool Acc" (fraction of probes where an expected tool was invoked) was 56%.
- **Paradigm-Specific Scores:**
 - **Encoding and Similarity:** DRM scored 86.7 (highest in this family), Pattern Separation 71.7, and Pattern Completion 79.5. This indicates eMEM's consolidation pipeline effectively suppresses associated lures and facilitates discrimination of similar scenes.
 - **Source and Context:** Source monitoring scored 78.6, demonstrating the utility of per-layer tagging for attributing memory to its perceptual source. Context-dependent retrieval scored 63.8, identified as an area where agent-side tool selection improvements could contribute.
 - **Temporal and Interference:** Long-horizon interference scored 69.4, and Serial position 69.4. The Retention curve (foils) scored 89.1 (highest overall).
- **Retention Curve Analysis (Table 5):** The retention curve demonstrated a flat performance at ceiling (100% hit rate on seen items) from 1 hour to 1 year of simulated delay for room-unique items. This suggests no measurable forgetting under long-term pressure from consolidation and archival. The false-alarm rate remained constant at approximately 15.5% across all delays, attributed to item-level confusions (e.g., "key chain" vs. "keys") rather than time-dependent forgetting.

#### 4.3. Ablations (from Abstract)

The abstract reports that a pure Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) baseline ("flat_rag" ablation) experienced significant performance drops:
- 30 percentage points loss on context-dependent retrieval.
- 29 percentage points loss on DRM lure rejection.
These results isolate the contribution of eMEM's multi-layer storage and consolidation pipeline, respectively, demonstrating their importance for these specific memory functions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

eMEM represents a contribution to the field of AI memory systems, particularly for embodied agents. Its significance and potential impact stem from several aspects:

1. **Bridging a critical gap:** eMEM directly addresses the identified limitation of existing LLM-agent memory systems that lack spatial grounding and robotic spatial representations that lack robust temporal memory and consolidation mechanisms. By integrating these capabilities, it creates a more comprehensive and functional memory system for agents operating in physical environments.

2. **Enhanced capabilities for embodied agents:** The hybrid spatio-temporal memory system allows embodied agents (e.g., robots, drones, autonomous vehicles) to accumulate and retrieve experiences that are inherently linked to *where* and *when* they occurred. This enables more sophisticated reasoning about their environment, such as resolving concepts to locations, synthesizing cross-modal information over time, and tracking object persistence, which are crucial for long-duration, complex tasks.

3. **Biologically-inspired design for robustness:** The tiered consolidation pipeline and multi-index storage architecture, informed by neuroscience, provide a structured approach to managing information. This design contributes to the system's ability to exhibit stable recall performance over long simulated durations (up to one year with no measurable forgetting on room-unique items), addressing a key challenge for continuously operating physical agents.

4. **Diagnostic evaluation framework:** The introduction of eMEM-Bench v1 is a methodological contribution. By organizing evaluation around cognitive-psychology paradigms, the benchmark provides a higher level of diagnostic resolution than traditional surface-task benchmarks. This allows researchers to pinpoint specific memory functions that are robust or deficient, offering clear guidance for future system improvements and enabling interpretation of results within a broader scientific context.

5. **Practical implications for deployment:** The system is fully embedded and designed to run in-process, making it suitable for onboard deployment in resource-constrained robotic platforms. Its predictable storage growth and sub-second R-tree rebuild times (for typical operational scales) further enhance its practicality for real-world applications.

6. **Foundation for future research:** eMEM provides a robust foundation for future work in several areas. This includes enhancing agent-side reasoning for tool selection, enriching consolidation with novelty and reward modulation, integrating object-frame coordinates, and exploring the combination with latent-graph representations for schema transfer and disambiguation of perceptually similar contexts. Such advancements could lead to agents that acquire transferable knowledge across environments, rather than starting from scratch each time.
