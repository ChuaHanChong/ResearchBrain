# UWM-JEPA: Predictive World Models That Imagine in Belief Space

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced UWM-JEPA, a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture that uses density matrix latents and a unitary predictor to imagine future states in partially observable environments. This model maintains joint-state invariants during blind rollout, learns action-sensitive dynamics through counterfactual targets, and demonstrates improved short-horizon information retention compared to LSTM-JEPA baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) use vector-valued latent spaces, which struggle to represent uncertainty and multiple compatible hidden continuations in partially observable environments.
- Blind rollout, where world models predict future states without new observations, risks the dissipation of represented uncertainty and critical hidden information over time.
- Standard "teacher-forced" JEPA training objectives can result in predictors that are not sensitive to the impact of different actions, limiting their utility for decision-making and planning.

## Method

- The **UWM-JEPA** model utilizes a density matrix as its latent representation, allowing it to explicitly encode and evolve a "belief" over potential hidden continuations.
- A learned unitary transformation acts as the predictor, fundamentally designed to preserve the joint-state spectrum, purity, and von Neumann entropy of the density matrix during blind rollouts.
- Counterfactual targets, derived from an environment simulator with varied action sequences, are integrated into the ==JEPA== training to ensure the model's imagined future states are genuinely sensitive to specific actions.

## Results

- The unitary predictor in UWM-JEPA maintained its joint-state spectrum, purity, and von Neumann entropy with high precision (drifts below 2.4e-7) during blind rollout, confirming its non-dissipative property.
- With counterfactual training, UWM-JEPA-CF exhibited appropriate action sensitivity in perturbation tests and achieved 0.770 ± 0.011 accuracy on a hidden-velocity indicator task, whereas a matched LSTM-JEPA-CF performed at chance level.
- UWM-JEPA demonstrated superior short-horizon blind rollout imagination, with significantly less information loss (e.g., Delta R^2 of 0.023 ± 0.008 at k=1) compared to LSTM-JEPA baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Endowing a JEPA's latent space with an explicit belief structure (density matrix) and enforcing non-dissipative, unitary dynamics on its predictor enables robust imagination under partial observability.
> - Learning action-conditioned future forecasting within JEPAs requires a shift from standard "teacher-forced" objectives to counterfactual targets that explicitly expose the predictor to diverse action outcomes.
> - The improved performance of UWM-JEPA in blind rollout and action sensitivity is primarily due to its unique predictor dynamics and latent geometry, not merely a better initial observation encoder.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of world models, specifically focusing on Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). JEPAs are a class of self-supervised learning methods where an online encoder maps observed context to a latent representation, a predictor advances this latent representation forward, and a target encoder, often an exponentially moving average (EMA) of the online encoder, provides the future representation to be matched. This approach, exemplified by models like I-JEPA and V-JEPA, contrasts with observation-level reconstruction by separating representation learning from generative decoding.

A central challenge for JEPAs, and world models more generally, arises in partially observable environments. In such settings, the current observation history does not uniquely determine the hidden state, meaning the future is not a single point in representation space. Existing vector-valued latent spaces in JEPAs often lack the internal structure to explicitly represent and carry beliefs over multiple compatible hidden continuations during "blind rollout"—the process of imagining future states without new observations. This limitation impacts how JEPAs manage uncertainty and how they can be steered by counterfactual actions.

This work addresses this specific challenge by proposing a JEPA variant designed to represent latent states as belief distributions rather than deterministic points. By introducing a density-matrix latent and a unitary predictor, the authors aim to provide a structured mechanism for carrying uncertainty and hidden modes through imagined futures. This differentiates it from existing JEPA implementations and other non-contrastive self-supervised learning methods that typically rely on vector embeddings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the limitations of current JEPA-style world models when operating in partially observable environments.

1. **Addressing Uncertainty in Partially Observable Environments:** In environments where current observations do not fully reveal the underlying hidden state, the future is inherently ambiguous. Traditional vector-valued latents in JEPAs, designed to predict a single point in representation space, are not inherently equipped to represent this multiplicity of compatible hidden futures. The authors are motivated to develop a latent representation that can explicitly encode and evolve a "belief" over these hidden continuations, enabling more nuanced imagination.

2. **Preventing Information Dissipation During Blind Rollout:** When a world model generates future predictions without new sensory input (blind rollout), there is a risk that the represented uncertainty and relevant hidden information will dissipate over time. The paper aims to introduce a predictor mechanism that, by its fundamental mathematical properties, can preserve the structural integrity of the latent belief state during such imagined rollouts. This is motivated by the desire to maintain the richness of the belief state rather than averaging away or forgetting hidden modes.

3. **Enabling Action-Sensitive Prediction:** For world models to be useful in decision-making and planning, they must be able to accurately predict the consequences of different actions. The authors observe that standard "teacher-forced" JEPA training objectives can lead to action-agnostic predictors, as the target encoder has already observed the action's effect. A key motivation is to design a training methodology and predictor architecture that ensures the model's imagined future states are genuinely sensitive to counterfactual actions.

4. **Investigating the Role of Latent Geometry and Predictor Dynamics:** Beyond raw representation capacity, the authors are motivated to explore whether the *geometry* of the latent space and the *dynamics* of the predictor itself are crucial factors for imagination under partial observability. They hypothesize that giving the latent explicit structure (density matrix) and constraining its evolution (unitary dynamics) will yield advantages that cannot be explained solely by improvements in the initial context encoding.

5. **Developing a Concrete Alternative to Vector-Valued Latents:** The paper is motivated by the need to provide a practical and empirically testable alternative to the prevalent vector-valued latents in JEPAs. By implementing a density-matrix latent and a learned unitary predictor, they aim to offer a specific instantiation of a belief-state JEPA that can be rigorously evaluated against established baselines.

In essence, the overarching goal is to enhance the imaginative capabilities of JEPA world models in partially observable settings by endowing their latent spaces with explicit belief structure and enforcing non-dissipative dynamics on their predictors, thereby enabling more robust action-conditioned future forecasting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Unitary World Model JEPA (UWM-JEPA) modifies the predictor and latent representation within a standard JEPA training scaffold.

**Latent Representation:**
The core architectural change in UWM-JEPA is the use of a density matrix $\rho_t$ as the latent representation, rather than a vector. This density matrix, an element of $C^{d_{total} \times d_{total}}$, is Hermitian, positive semidefinite, and has a trace of 1, satisfying the properties of a quantum density matrix. The latent space is bipartite, $H_T = H_S \otimes H_E$, where $H_S$ is the system factor (read by the JEPA loss and probes) and $H_E$ is the environment factor. In the experiments, $d_{sys}=8$, $d_{env}=2$, and $d_{total}=16$.

**Encoder and Observation Update:**
The online encoder $E_\theta$ maps a partially observed sequence $o_{\le t}$ to $\rho_t$. The density matrix constraints are enforced by construction:
- The initial state is maximally mixed on the joint space.
- Each observation update $o_t$ is performed by sandwiching the current state with a positive system measurement operator $K(o_t) \otimes I_E$ (where $I_E$ is the identity on the environment factor) and then renormalizing the trace. This process is designed to preserve Hermiticity and positive semidefiniteness.

**Predictor Dynamics:**
The prediction operator is a learned unitary transformation. For a horizon $k$, UWM-JEPA rolls the latent forward using unitary conjugation: $P_k(\rho_t) = U_k \rho_t (U_k)^\dagger$. The unitary operator $U$ is parameterized as $U = \exp(-iH\Delta t)$, where $H$ is a learnable Hermitian operator (Hamiltonian). This unitary evolution ensures that the joint-state spectrum, purity, and von Neumann entropy of the density matrix are exactly preserved during blind rollout, a property termed "joint-state non-forgetting."

**Action Integration:**
Actions $a$ are incorporated into the predictor by making the Hamiltonian action-conditioned: $H(a) = H_0 + aH_1$, where $H_0$ and $H_1$ are learnable Hermitian parameters. For a sequence of actions $(a_t, a_{t+1}, \dots)$, the rollout applies the corresponding sequence of unitary operations.

**JEPA Training Protocol:**
UWM-JEPA utilizes a standard JEPA-native training scaffold:
- An EMA target encoder $E_\xi$ (where $\xi$ are parameters updated via EMA from $\theta$) produces the future target $\tilde{\rho}_{t+k}$ from the observed future $o_{\le t+k}$.
- A stop-gradient is applied to the target parameters $\xi$.
- The loss is evaluated after reducing the predicted and target density matrices to their system factors ($Tr_E \rho$) and passing them through a small projection head $g_\phi$. The objective is an $L_2$ matching loss: $L_{JEPA} = ||\hat{h}_{t+k} - sg(h^+_{t+k})||_F^2$.

**Counterfactual Targets for Action Binding:**
To enable action-sensitive prediction, the standard "teacher-forced" target is replaced by a "counterfactual target." Instead of using the observed future $o_{\le t+k}$, the counterfactual target $\tilde{\rho}^+_{t+k}$ is generated by rolling out an environment simulator from the current latent simulator state $s_t$ under a *freshly sampled counterfactual action sequence* $\tilde{a}_{t:t+k-1}$. The target encoder then processes the observed prefix concatenated with the observations from this simulated counterfactual future. This ensures that different action sequences lead to different targets, forcing the predictor to learn to use the action channel. This approach requires access to an environment simulator during training.

**Evaluation Metrics and Baselines:**
The model's performance is evaluated across several dimensions:
- **Action Sensitivity:**
 - **Action Perturbation Controls:** Hilbert-Schmidt distance between the predicted state and the correct counterfactual target under various test-time action conditions (correct, reversed, base dynamics, wrong, shuffled, negated).
 - **Hidden-Velocity Indicator Task:** A binary classification task to predict the sign of hidden velocity at a future step ($k=5$), where the target observation is masked. This assesses if the action-conditioned predictor produces a state from which hidden continuations are decodable.
- **Blind Rollout Imagination:**
 - **Future-Latent Retrieval:** Top-1 retrieval accuracy, measuring how well predicted future latents match target latents from the EMA encoder.
 - **Teacher-to-Blind Loss ($\Delta R^2$):** A linear probe is trained on target encoder latents ($R^2_{teacher}$) and then applied to latents after blind rollout ($R^2_{blind}$). $\Delta R^2 = R^2_{teacher} - R^2_{blind}$ quantifies the information lost.
- **Context Representation Quality:**
 - **Held-out Context Probe:** A linear probe measures the $R^2$ score on hidden velocity from the *encoder's* latent representation (without rollout), assessing the initial representation quality.

**Environments and Baselines:**
Experiments are conducted on a partially observable CartPole-style spring benchmark. Baselines include:
- Parameter-matched LSTM-JEPA (with plain and residual MLP predictors).
- Non-recurrent baselines: Orthogonal RNN, GRU, and MLP.
- Ablations and controls for LSTM-CF and supervised recurrent models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the UWM-JEPA model's performance and properties:

1. **Joint-State Non-Forgetting Property:** The unitary predictor in UWM-JEPA maintains the joint-state spectrum, purity, and von Neumann entropy of the density matrix exactly during blind rollout. Numerical checks over 30 rollout steps showed maximum drifts below $2.4 \times 10^{-7}$, confirming the preservation of these invariants to floating-point precision. This is a structural property of the predictor, independent of downstream probe performance.

2. **Counterfactual Targets are Essential for Action Sensitivity:**
 - When trained with a standard teacher-forced JEPA objective, the action channel in the Hamiltonian ($H_1$) collapses to near-zero magnitude, meaning the predictor effectively ignores actions ($\|H_1\| / \|H_0\| \approx 0.03$).
 - Switching to counterfactual targets (which require simulator access during training) restores the action channel's relevance, making $\|H_1\| / \|H_0\| = 1.00 \pm 0.15$. This indicates the action term becomes comparable to the base dynamics.
 - **Action Control Battery:** Under counterfactual training, UWM-JEPA-CF exhibits expected behavior: Hilbert-Schmidt distance to the target increases when actions are perturbed (e.g., wrong, shuffled, negated actions lead to larger distances compared to correct actions).
 - **Hidden-Velocity Indicator Task:** UWM-JEPA-CF achieved an accuracy of $0.770 \pm 0.011$ on a five-step forward prediction task requiring inference of hidden velocity from masked observations. Its performance degraded monotonically with action perturbations (e.g., $0.639 \pm 0.034$ for wrong actions), consistent with physical intuition. In contrast, a parameter-matched LSTM-JEPA-CF, trained under the same counterfactual objective, collapsed to majority-class accuracy ($0.530 \pm 0.000$) across all action conditions.

3. **Improved Short-Horizon Blind Rollout Imagination:**
 - **Information Retention ($\Delta R^2$):** UWM-JEPA demonstrated stronger short-horizon retention during blind rollout. At $k=1$ and $k=3$ (shorter than the trained horizon of $k=5$), UWM-JEPA lost less than ten points of $R^2$ relative to the target encoder ($\Delta R^2$ of $0.023 \pm 0.008$ and $0.069 \pm 0.026$, respectively).
 - In comparison, the matched LSTM-JEPA lost substantially more information at these horizons ($\Delta R^2$ of $0.413 \pm 0.046$ and $0.678 \pm 0.179$). The LSTM-JEPA re-aligned at the trained horizon $k=5$ ($\Delta R^2 = 0.013 \pm 0.062$), but its intermediate blind states were poorly aligned.
 - Beyond $k=10$, both models exhibited low or negative blind probe scores, indicating degradation at long extrapolation horizons.
 - **Future-Latent Retrieval:** UWM-JEPA's predictor showed improved future-latent retrieval accuracy compared to LSTM-JEPA models with plain and residual MLP predictors across various horizons.

4. **Context Representation is Not the Primary Factor:**
 - On a held-out linear probe designed to measure context encoding quality (predicting hidden velocity from the encoder's latent without rollout), UWM-JEPA ($R^2 = 0.901 \pm 0.032$) and the parameter-matched LSTM-JEPA ($R^2 = 0.894 \pm 0.021$) performed comparably, with no statistically significant difference ($p=0.70$).
 - This finding indicates that the observed behavioral advantages of UWM-JEPA in blind rollout and action sensitivity are attributable to its predictor dynamics and latent geometry, rather than a superior initial context encoding capacity.

5. **Training Stability:** UWM-JEPA was shown to be stable and avoid representational collapse across a sweep of six different anti-collapse regularization protocols (e.g., no explicit regularizer, contrastive, VICReg, Barlow Twins, log-determinant penalties). All tested variants yielded non-trivial held-out $R^2$ scores, confirming that the model did not collapse under these conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces UWM-JEPA, a novel approach to world modeling within the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancing World Models for Partial Observability:** The core contribution is the demonstration that explicitly structuring a JEPA's latent space as a density matrix and evolving it with a unitary predictor can effectively address challenges posed by partially observable environments. This provides a mechanism for JEPAs to "imagine" multiple compatible hidden futures and manage uncertainty during blind rollout, which is a critical capability for intelligent agents operating in real-world scenarios.

2. **Highlighting the Importance of Latent Geometry and Predictor Dynamics:** The finding that UWM-JEPA's advantages stem from its predictor dynamics and latent geometry, rather than superior initial context encoding, is significant. It suggests that for effective imagination and counterfactual reasoning in world models, merely having a powerful encoder is insufficient; the way latent representations are structured and propagated through time plays a crucial role. This could influence future research directions in designing more capable world model architectures.

3. **Enabling Action-Conditioned Imagination in JEPAs:** The work identifies and resolves a limitation of teacher-forced JEPA training, which can render action pathways inert. By demonstrating that counterfactual targets enable UWM-JEPA to learn action-sensitive dynamics and perform well on an action-conditioned hidden-velocity indicator task, the paper contributes to developing world models that can be effectively guided by actions for planning and decision-making.

4. **Providing a Structured Testbed for Belief-State World Models:** UWM-JEPA serves as a concrete, empirically validated testbed for the concept of belief-state world models within the JEPA framework. The exact non-dissipation property of the unitary predictor on the joint latent state offers a theoretical grounding for why uncertainty can be preserved, while empirical results quantify its operational memory.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Control and Planning:** World models that can reliably imagine consequences of actions under uncertainty are fundamental for robotics, enabling robust planning and decision-making in complex, partially observable environments.
- **More Robust Reinforcement Learning:** By providing richer, uncertainty-aware latent predictions, UWM-JEPA could improve the efficiency and effectiveness of model-based reinforcement learning agents.
- **New Directions in Self-Supervised Learning:** The architectural principles, particularly the use of density matrices and unitary dynamics, might inspire novel approaches in self-supervised learning for sequential data beyond the immediate context of world models.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, including the current requirement for simulator access to generate counterfactual targets, which restricts its applicability to settings with passive video or observation streams. The long-horizon imagination capabilities of UWM-JEPA are also limited, degrading beyond 10 steps. The current results are demonstrated in structured partially observable oscillator and CartPole-style settings, and generalization to complex, unstructured visual environments remains an open question. The model is presented as a structured testbed rather than a universally stronger encoder or a competitive general-purpose planner. These limitations outline clear avenues for future research to broaden the scope and improve the capabilities of this class of world models.
