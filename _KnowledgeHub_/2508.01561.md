# One Subgoal at a Time: Zero-Shot Generalization to Arbitrary Linear Temporal Logic Requirements in Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The GenZ-LTL framework enables reinforcement learning agents to generalize zero-shot to complex, unseen Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications by adopting a "one subgoal at a time" approach. This method is enhanced with Hamilton-Jacobi reachability for robust safety and a novel observation reduction technique, demonstrating superior success rates and near-zero violations across various tasks and complexities.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle to generalize policies to novel, complex, and temporally extended task objectives specified by LTL, particularly in a zero-shot manner.
- Existing LTL-guided RL approaches often lack robust and explicit mechanisms for enforcing safety constraints, leading to potential violations when task progression conflicts with safety.
- Scaling LTL-guided RL is challenging due to the combinatorial explosion of atomic propositions and subgoals, and current methods often fail to detect and adapt to inherently unsatisfiable subgoals.

## Method

- **GenZ-LTL** decomposes LTL specifications into ==reach-avoid subgoals== derived from Büchi automata, training a policy on individual subgoals in a "one subgoal at a time" fashion.
- A novel subgoal-induced observation reduction technique processes environment states into compact, fixed-size vectors, managing combinatorial complexity by focusing on task-relevant information.
- Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability theory is integrated into a safe RL formulation with a state-dependent Lagrangian multiplier to explicitly enforce hard safety constraints during policy learning.

## Results

- GenZ-LTL achieved consistently higher success rates and near-zero violation rates on both finite and infinite-horizon zero-shot LTL tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robustness to increasing subgoal chain lengths (up to **12**), environmental complexity, partial observability, and successfully adapted to unsatisfiable subgoals.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Hamilton-Jacobi reachability for explicit safety and the subgoal-induced observation reduction are critical for GenZ-LTL's enhanced generalization and safety performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Focusing on one immediate reach-avoid subgoal at a time, when combined with explicit safety and smart observation reduction, can lead to superior zero-shot generalization compared to conditioning on complex subgoal sequences.
> - Hamilton-Jacobi reachability provides a principled and robust method to enforce safety as a hard constraint within RL, effectively managing conflicts between task progression and safety requirements.
> - Reducing observations based on the current subgoal leverages an assumed observational symmetry across propositions, enabling effective scaling and generalization to a larger, unseen set of atomic propositions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a critical and active area within reinforcement learning (RL): the challenge of generalizing policies to satisfy complex, temporally extended task objectives and safety constraints, especially in a zero-shot manner to unseen specifications. This problem is particularly relevant for real-world applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and healthcare, where agents must adapt to dynamic environments and multifaceted instructions.

Traditionally, RL has struggled with task specification that goes beyond simple reward functions. Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) has emerged as a powerful formal language to precisely define system behaviors over time, offering a compositional syntax and well-defined semantics. Early efforts in LTL-guided RL focused on learning policies for a single, fixed specification. However, the contemporary research landscape increasingly demands zero-shot generalization to novel specifications, enabling agents to handle an arbitrary range of tasks without retraining.

Existing approaches to LTL-guided zero-shot generalization broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Subgoal Decomposition:** Methods that break down an LTL formula into a sequence of simpler subgoals. This paper aligns with this paradigm, but critically re-evaluates the conventional wisdom of conditioning on full subgoal sequences.
2. **Direct Specification/Automata Encoding:** Approaches that directly encode the LTL formula or its equivalent automaton (e.g., Büchi automaton or deterministic finite automaton) into a neural network representation, often using graph neural networks or specialized embeddings.

Beyond task generalization, the paper also intersects with the field of **Safe Reinforcement Learning**. Many existing LTL-guided RL methods incorporate safety implicitly through the LTL specification itself, assigning rewards for satisfaction and penalties for violation. However, the authors highlight that this approach is often insufficient for achieving robust safety, especially when task progression and safety requirements conflict. The safe RL landscape includes various techniques like constrained policy optimization, Gaussian processes, control barrier functions, and Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability, with the latter forming a core component of this paper's safety mechanism.

GenZ-LTL carves out a unique niche by proposing a subgoal decomposition strategy that deviates from prior work, particularly from methods like DeepLTL which condition on *subgoal sequences* to achieve non-myopic behavior. The authors argue and demonstrate that a "one subgoal at a time" approach, when augmented with two key innovations—a rigorous safe RL formulation using state-wise Hamilton-Jacobi reachability constraints and a novel subgoal-induced observation reduction technique—can achieve superior zero-shot generalization. This positions GenZ-LTL as an advancement that tackles the limitations of complexity, safety, and combinatorial explosion that plague many existing LTL-guided RL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning framework, GenZ-LTL, capable of *zero-shot generalization to arbitrary Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications*. This includes both finite-horizon tasks (where a task is satisfied after a finite number of steps) and infinite-horizon tasks (requiring satisfaction over an infinite trajectory).

The motivation stems from several critical limitations in current RL methodologies:
- **Handling Complex and Temporally Extended Objectives:** Traditional RL struggles with tasks that involve sequential objectives, conditional behaviors, and sustained properties over time, such as "reach A, then avoid B until C, and eventually reach D." LTL provides a precise language for these, but its integration into RL still faces significant hurdles.
- **Ensuring Robust Safety:** While LTL can specify safety constraints (e.g., "always avoid region X"), existing LTL-guided RL methods often treat safety implicitly through reward shaping. This approach is prone to failure when task progression objectives conflict with safety requirements, leading to violations. A more principled and robust mechanism for prioritizing safety is needed.
- **Addressing Unsatisfiable Subgoals:** In real-world environments, certain subgoals derived from an LTL specification might be impossible to satisfy due to environmental constraints. Current methods often lack a mechanism to detect and adapt to such situations, causing agents to get stuck.
- **Mitigating Combinatorial Complexity:** LTL specifications can involve many atomic propositions (e.g., "blue," "green," "magenta"). As the number of propositions increases, the number of possible subgoals and their combinations with environment states grows exponentially, making direct policy learning intractable and hindering generalization.

The authors hypothesize that by adopting a "one subgoal at a time" approach, seemingly myopic, but paired with advanced safe RL techniques and a clever observation reduction, they can overcome these limitations more effectively than methods that condition on longer sequences or full automata. This approach aims to simplify the learning problem by focusing on immediate, well-defined reach-avoid subgoals while preserving the overall LTL satisfaction through intelligent subgoal sequencing at test time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenZ-LTL operates on a principled decomposition of LTL specifications using Büchi automata, combined with a novel safe RL formulation and an observation reduction technique.

**Overall Framework:**
During training, GenZ-LTL samples individual reach-avoid subgoals. A subgoal-induced observation reduction technique processes the current environment state based on the sampled subgoal. A general goal-conditioned policy is then trained using this processed state to achieve the subgoal while respecting safety constraints. At test time, given a new LTL formula, the corresponding Büchi automaton guides the sequential selection of optimal subgoals using the learned value functions, and the policy executes actions accordingly.

**Key Technical Components:**

1. **Reach-Avoid Subgoal Construction from Büchi Automata:**
 - LTL formulas are converted into equivalent (non-deterministic) Büchi automata, which represent the temporal properties as state-transition graphs.
 - A Depth-First Search (DFS) algorithm is applied to the automaton's graph to identify "lasso structures"—paths that lead to an accepting state and cycle back to it, indicating an infinite satisfaction.
 - From these structures, two types of subgoals are extracted for any given automaton state `q`:
 - **Reach Subgoal (α+):** A set of atomic propositions that, if true in the current MDP state, would lead to a transition in the Büchi automaton that progresses towards satisfaction (e.g., moving to a new automaton state along an accepting path).
 - **Avoid Subgoal (A−):** A set of atomic propositions that, if true, would lead to a transition violating the LTL specification.
 - The agent's task is then to reach a state satisfying α+ while continuously avoiding any state satisfying propositions in A−.

2. **Subgoal-Induced Observation Reduction (Novel):**
 - This technique addresses the combinatorial explosion arising from the exponential number of possible atomic proposition assignments and subgoals.
 - It assumes that the MDP state observation `s` can be separated into a proposition-independent component (`s¬AP`, e.g., agent's own state) and a proposition-dependent component (`sAP`, e.g., sensor readings about regions).
 - Crucially, `sAP` is assumed to consist of individual observations (e.g., LiDAR beams to different colored regions) produced by *identical observation functions under an output transformation*. This implies a certain symmetry across how different atomic propositions are observed.
 - Instead of concatenating the raw observations for all propositions, for a given subgoal `σ = (α+, A−)`, the `sAP` is reduced to two fixed-size vectors:
 - A "reach observation" corresponding to `α+`.
 - A "fused avoid observation" that combines information from all `α ∈ A−` (e.g., element-wise minimum of LiDAR distances to any avoided region).
 - The policy then conditions on this compact, *subgoal-dependent reduced state* `sσ`, significantly lowering the input dimension and enabling generalization to unseen atomic propositions that adhere to the observational symmetry.

3. **Goal-Conditioned Policy Learning with Reachability Constraints (Novel Safe RL Formulation):**
 - GenZ-LTL treats avoid subgoals as *hard constraints*, meaning any violation renders the entire LTL specification unsatisfiable.
 - It integrates Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability theory into the RL objective to explicitly enforce safety. The agent learns a value function `Vr` for cumulative reward (task progression) and a *reachability value function* `Vh` that quantifies the worst-case constraint violation from a given state.
 - The optimization problem is formulated to maximize expected return *within the largest feasible set* (states from which safety can be guaranteed) while simultaneously minimizing `Vh` for states outside this set (where violations are inevitable).
 - This objective is transformed into a constrained policy optimization problem, solved using a PPO-like clipped surrogate objective and a state-dependent Lagrangian multiplier `λ`. This multiplier dynamically weighs the importance of safety violations, ensuring that safety is prioritized.

4. **Subgoal Switching Mechanism:**
 - To handle scenarios where a chosen subgoal might be unsatisfiable in the environment, GenZ-LTL introduces a timeout mechanism.
 - If the agent fails to satisfy the current subgoal within a predefined timeout threshold (based on typical completion time `μ_subgoal` during training), that subgoal is marked as unsatisfiable for the current context.
 - The agent then revisits the Büchi automaton, and the learned value functions (`Vr - λVh`) are used to select the next optimal *alternative feasible subgoal*, effectively allowing the agent to adapt and escape deadlocks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of GenZ-LTL is extensive, comparing it against state-of-the-art baselines (LTL2Action, GCRL-LTL, DeepLTL, RAD-embeddings) across various environments (LetterWorld, ZoneEnv, and its variants) and a wide range of LTL specifications.

**Key Findings:**

- **Superior Zero-Shot Generalization (Q1):**
 - **Finite-Horizon Tasks:** GenZ-LTL consistently achieved significantly higher success rates (ηs) and near-zero violation rates (ηv) compared to all baselines on complex, unseen LTL specifications. Baselines often struggled with "out-of-distribution" (OOD) specifications (e.g., longer subgoal sequences for DeepLTL, unseen automata for RAD-embeddings), whereas GenZ-LTL's one-subgoal-at-a-time approach was more robust.
 - **Infinite-Horizon Tasks:** GenZ-LTL outperformed DeepLTL and GCRL-LTL (RAD-embeddings is not applicable here) in terms of average visits to accepting states and violation rates. Its sequential subgoal handling naturally extended to infinite horizons, maintaining performance and safety over long trajectories.

- **Robustness to Increasing Complexity (Q2):**
 - **Subgoal Chaining:** As the length of required subgoal sequences increased (up to n=12), GenZ-LTL maintained high success and near-zero violation rates. Baselines showed a marked degradation in performance, highlighting their struggle with longer, OOD sequences.
 - **Environment Complexity:** Variations in zone size and the number of zones per atomic proposition tested environmental robustness. GenZ-LTL exhibited the highest success rates and lowest violation rates, demonstrating better compliance with safety constraints under environmental variations.
 - **Unsatisfiable Subgoals:** The timeout and subgoal-switching mechanism proved highly effective. While baselines often got stuck when faced with inherently unsatisfiable subgoals (e.g., non-overlapping regions required to be simultaneously true), GenZ-LTL successfully identified these and switched to alternative, satisfiable paths, ensuring task completion where possible.
 - **Partial Observability & Overlapping Areas:** GenZ-LTL outperformed DeepLTL in scenarios with limited agent observation or environments featuring overlapping regions, further validating the robustness of its single-subgoal focus.
 - **Increasing Atomic Propositions:** The subgoal-induced observation reduction enabled GenZ-LTL to generalize zero-shot to environments with a larger number of previously unseen atomic propositions, provided their observations followed the assumed symmetric structure. Baselines, reliant on fixed encodings, failed in such scenarios.
 - **Complex Dynamics (Ant Agent):** When tested with a more complex Ant robot in ZoneEnv, GenZ-LTL showed an even greater performance gap over DeepLTL, demonstrating its ability to handle more challenging agent dynamics and enforce hard constraints (like preventing falls) more effectively due to its explicit safe RL formulation.

- **Ablation Study (Q3): Impact of Key Innovations:**
 - The ablation study explicitly demonstrated the value of the two main innovations. Removing the subgoal-induced observation reduction (`Ours(w/o sσ)`) still showed safety improvements over DeepLTL due to the safe RL formulation, but with lower overall efficiency and success.
 - The full GenZ-LTL, incorporating both the safe RL approach and the observation reduction, achieved the best performance, confirming that both components are crucial for its superior generalization and safety. The observation reduction significantly improved performance by reducing the policy input dimension.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GenZ-LTL framework represents a significant step forward in multi-task reinforcement learning, particularly at the intersection of formal methods and safe AI. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Advancing Zero-Shot Generalization:** The most prominent impact is enabling RL policies to understand and satisfy *arbitrary LTL specifications* in a zero-shot manner. This capability is critical for deploying intelligent agents in dynamic, open-world environments where new tasks and constraints may emerge without prior training. By demonstrating that a "myopic" one-subgoal-at-a-time approach can be superior to conditioning on entire subgoal sequences when properly augmented, the paper challenges existing paradigms and offers a more scalable solution to generalization.

- **Robust and Explicit Safety Guarantees:** The integration of Hamilton-Jacobi reachability into the RL formulation provides a principled and explicit way to enforce safety constraints. Unlike methods that rely solely on reward shaping, GenZ-LTL prioritizes safety as a hard constraint, making its policies more dependable for safety-critical applications like autonomous systems and medical robotics. This addresses a major gap in LTL-guided RL, where the interplay between task progression and safety has historically been underexplored.

- **Tackling Combinatorial Complexity:** The novel subgoal-induced observation reduction technique offers a practical solution to the exponential growth of state-subgoal combinations, a notorious bottleneck in scaling LTL-guided RL to environments with many atomic propositions. By abstracting observations to task-relevant "reach" and "avoid" signals, the method drastically reduces the policy's input space, enhancing learning efficiency and enabling generalization to new atomic propositions, given certain observational symmetries.

- **Increased Robustness and Adaptability:** The inclusion of a timeout and subgoal-switching mechanism significantly improves the agent's robustness by allowing it to dynamically adapt to unsatisfiable subgoals or environmental changes. This prevents agents from getting stuck in futile attempts, making them more resilient in complex, unpredictable settings.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including exploring alternative techniques to exploit Boolean structure within subgoals (e.g., Boolean task algebra) to further improve sample efficiency and generalization. It also lays groundwork for more sophisticated perception modules that can leverage the observation reduction paradigm in real-world sensor data (e.g., semantic segmentation for camera/LiDAR data in autonomous driving).

**Potential Negative Societal Impacts:**
The authors acknowledge that the power of this framework, while enabling highly flexible and expressive task definitions, places significant responsibility on the user. Poorly designed or malicious LTL specifications could lead to unsafe or unintended agent behaviors. For instance, an LTL specification guiding an autonomous vehicle to prioritize speed over basic traffic laws could result in dangerous outcomes. Thus, careful design, verification, and ethical considerations for LTL specifications are paramount when applying such methods in real-world scenarios.
