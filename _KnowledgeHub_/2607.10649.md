# Coverage Path Planning: Classical Foundations, Recent Advances, and Future Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey systematically reviews 125 works on Coverage Path Planning (CPP), creating a problem-driven taxonomy that unifies classical methodologies with recent advancements across single-robot, multi-robot, 3D, constrained, learning-based, and visual coverage. It identifies key trends like subarea decomposition and the evolution to non-local online planning, alongside future challenges such as scalable online planning and integrated 3D visual coverage.

## Problem

- Generating trajectories for robots to completely cover a target workspace while optimizing objectives like path length, overlap, and energy consumption.
- The lack of a unified, up-to-date survey comprehensively connecting classical Coverage Path Planning (CPP) methodologies with recent advancements in multi-robot, 3D, constrained, learning-based, and visual coverage.
- Existing surveys were either foundational but outdated or specialized, failing to bridge the expanding scope of CPP research across diverse applications and robot types.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic review of 125 representative ==Coverage Path Planning (CPP)== research works, primarily from 2015-2026, to capture contemporary developments while acknowledging foundational concepts.
- Developed a novel, ==problem-driven taxonomy== categorizing CPP literature into six main areas: ==single-robot==, ==multi-robot==, ==3D==, ==constrained==, ==learning-based==, and ==visual CPP==.
- Analyzed each category by summarizing main problem formulations, representative algorithms, key ideas, and evaluating their strengths and limitations.

## Results

- The survey identified a progressive trend across **125 works** in CPP research from basic 2D single-robot coverage to increasingly complex, multi-faceted problems involving multiple robots, intricate 3D geometries, and advanced sensing or learning paradigms.
- It detailed how subarea decomposition remains a foundational principle while online single-robot methods have evolved to non-local approaches and multi-robot CPP shifted to subarea-level coordination for efficiency.
- It delineated key open challenges and future research directions, such as the need for scalable online planning in dynamic environments, effective multi-robot heterogeneity management, and the development of hybrid learning-classical CPP frameworks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Subarea decomposition serves as a fundamental principle across many CPP settings, effectively reducing planning complexity from single-robot to 3D and multi-robot scenarios.
> - Online single-robot CPP has evolved from local, myopic decision-making to non-local approaches that integrate higher-level decision structures for improved path quality and reduced fragmentation.
> - Multi-robot CPP research has progressed towards subarea-level coordination, which significantly improves workload balancing and reduces inter-robot interference compared to finer-grained coordination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Coverage path planning (CPP) constitutes a fundamental problem within robot motion planning, focusing on generating trajectories that ensure complete coverage of a target workspace while minimizing various task-specific objectives such as path length, overlap, number of turns, and energy consumption. Its applicability spans a wide array of domains, including household services (e.g., cleaning, lawn mowing), environmental monitoring (e.g., terrain mapping), industrial operations (e.g., inspection, spray-painting), agriculture (e.g., weeding), and hazardous operations (e.g., demining, oil spill cleaning). These applications frequently involve diverse robotic platforms, including unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs), unmanned underwater vehicles (UUVs), unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and industrial manipulators.

Historically, classical CPP research primarily concentrated on single-robot systems operating in two-dimensional environments. However, the field has undergone significant expansion, driven by advancements in robotics and the increasing complexity of real-world applications. Recent developments have extended CPP to multi-robot systems, complex three-dimensional environments, platforms with specific constraints (e.g., limited energy, curvature constraints), and tasks requiring visual sensing (e.g., inspection, reconstruction). A distinct methodological shift has also introduced learning-based approaches to CPP.

Prior surveys have addressed various facets of CPP. Early works, such as Choset (2001) and Galceran and Carreras (2013), provided foundational reviews of single-robot CPP in 2D, with some discussion of nascent multi-robot and 3D extensions. More recent surveys have adopted specialized scopes, focusing on specific applications or problem settings, such as the evaluation of indoor CPP algorithms (Bormann et al., 2018), UAV-specific CPP methods (Cabreira et al., 2019), or 2D offline CPP for precision agriculture (Höffmann et al., 2024).

This current survey positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing a perceived gap: the lack of a unified and up-to-date review that comprehensively connects the established classical CPP methodologies with the diverse array of recent advancements. It aims to bridge this divide by providing a structured overview that encompasses both foundational concepts and emerging trends across the broadened scope of CPP, thus offering a contemporary reference for researchers in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the significant evolution and expansion of the Coverage Path Planning (CPP) problem beyond its classical 2D, single-robot formulation. The increasing adoption of autonomous robots in complex real-world scenarios has led to new challenges and research directions, necessitating a current and comprehensive synthesis of the field.

The key objectives of this paper are detailed as follows:
- **Comprehensive Review**: To present a thorough and structured review of 125 representative research works on CPP. The selection of these works primarily focuses on publications between 2015 and 2026, ensuring the survey captures the most recent developments.
- **Connecting Classical with Modern**: To explicitly link the recent advancements in CPP with their classical foundations. This involves tracing the evolution of problems and solutions, illustrating how new methodologies build upon or diverge from established techniques.
- **Problem-Driven Taxonomy**: To organize the vast body of CPP literature into a novel, problem-driven taxonomy. This contrasts with purely algorithmic classifications by categorizing methods based on the specific challenges they address. The six main categories identified are: single-robot CPP, multi-robot CPP, 3D CPP, constrained CPP, learning-based CPP, and visual CPP.
- **Detailed Analysis per Category**: For each of these six categories, the survey aims to:
 - Summarize the main problem formulations and objectives addressed.
 - Identify and describe representative algorithms.
 - Explain the key ideas and underlying principles of these algorithms.
 - Evaluate their strengths and limitations, providing a balanced perspective on their applicability and performance.
- **Contextual Analysis**: To analyze how various contextual factors—including environmental knowledge (known vs. unknown), workspace geometry (2D vs. 3D), specific robot constraints (e.g., energy, curvature), sensing objectives (e.g., physical coverage vs. visual inspection), and coordination requirements (single vs. multiple robots)—shape and define the CPP problem.
- **Highlighting Emerging Directions**: To specifically highlight learning-based CPP as a promising, albeit relatively new, methodological direction, detailing how learning techniques are being integrated into CPP frameworks.
- **Identifying Future Research**: To discuss open challenges and suggest future research directions, thereby guiding subsequent investigations in the field. This includes areas such as scalable online planning, multi-robot coordination in complex scenarios, advancements in 3D and visual coverage, the development of unified platform-constrained and resource-aware coverage, and the further enhancement of coverage through learning-based approaches.

Through these objectives, the survey seeks to provide a unified and up-to-date reference that not only consolidates existing knowledge but also delineates the current state of the art and outlines the trajectory for future research in coverage path planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and comprehensive methodology to review the literature on Coverage Path Planning (CPP), focusing on establishing connections between classical foundations and recent advancements. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

- **Literature Selection and Timeframe**: The survey primarily focuses on 125 representative works published between 2015 and 2026. This timeframe ensures an emphasis on contemporary developments while also acknowledging foundational works published before 2015 to illustrate the evolution of the field.
- **Problem-Driven Taxonomy**: Instead of organizing the literature solely by algorithmic families, the authors employed a problem-driven taxonomy. This approach categorizes CPP methods based on the specific type of problem they address, reflecting the major directions in which CPP research has expanded. The six main categories established are:
 - **Single-Robot CPP**: Focuses on methods for individual robots in 2D environments.
 - **Multi-Robot CPP**: Addresses coordination and task allocation for multiple robots.
 - **3D CPP**: Explores coverage in complex three-dimensional geometries.
 - **Constrained CPP**: Examines planning under robot-specific limitations (e.g., energy, curvature, tether).
 - **Learning-Based CPP**: Investigates the integration of machine learning techniques into CPP.
 - **Visual CPP**: Deals with viewpoint planning for inspection and reconstruction tasks.
- **Categorization within Sections**: Each of the six main categories is further subdivided to provide granular analysis. For instance, single-robot CPP is differentiated into offline and online methods based on environmental knowledge. Online methods are then further classified into local, connectivity-aware local, and non-local approaches. Similarly, multi-robot CPP distinguishes between offline and online strategies, with subdivisions like spanning-tree-based and subarea-decomposition methods. 3D CPP is explored through layered-coverage, contour-aware, and decomposition-based methods.
- **Detailed Analysis Elements**: For each sub-category and representative algorithm, the authors consistently summarize:
 - **Main Planning Formulations**: The mathematical or conceptual framework underlying the problem.
 - **Representative Algorithms**: Specific algorithms developed to address the formulation.
 - **Key Ideas**: The core concepts or mechanisms central to the algorithm's operation.
 - **Strengths and Limitations**: A critical evaluation of the advantages and disadvantages, including factors like computational efficiency, applicability in different environments, and completeness guarantees.
- **Analytical Framework**: The survey analyzes how critical factors shape the CPP problem across different contexts. These factors include:
 - **Environmental Knowledge**: Distinguishing between methods for known (offline) and unknown/partially known (online) environments.
 - **Workspace Geometry**: Addressing the shift from 2D planar spaces to complex 3D terrains and surfaces.
 - **Robot Constraints**: Considering platform-specific limitations such as battery capacity, minimum turning radius, tether length, and reconfigurable morphology.
 - **Sensing Objectives**: Differentiating between physical area coverage and visual coverage (e.g., inspection, mapping).
 - **Coordination Requirements**: Examining the complexities introduced by multi-robot systems, including task allocation, conflict resolution, and workload balancing.
- **Visual Aids and Summaries**: The paper incorporates figures to illustrate conceptual timelines (e.g., timeline of online single-robot CPP) and algorithmic operations (e.g., subarea decomposition, potential fields). Tables are used to provide comparative summaries of key features for representative methods within specific categories, detailing their main idea, planning representation, allocation unit, optimization focus, and observed strengths and limitations.
- **Identification of Future Directions**: The concluding section synthesizes observed trends and explicitly outlines open challenges and promising avenues for future research, ensuring the survey serves as a guide for ongoing and prospective studies.

This comprehensive approach allows the survey to systematically map the CPP landscape, highlight areas of significant progress, and pinpoint remaining challenges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a detailed overview of Coverage Path Planning (CPP), synthesizing findings across six major categories and highlighting key trends and developments. The main findings and results can be summarized as follows:

- **Pervasive Role of Subarea Decomposition**: Subarea decomposition remains a foundational and central principle across various CPP settings. In offline single-robot, multi-robot, and 3D CPP, it effectively reduces planning complexity by breaking down large workspaces into manageable, simpler units. For online single-robot CPP, decomposition methods provide higher-level decision structures that facilitate non-local waypoint selection, mitigating issues like myopia, dead ends, and fragmentation. In multi-robot contexts, it enables spatial separation and distinct task assignments, thereby reducing inter-robot conflicts and coordination overhead.

- **Evolution of Single-Robot Online CPP**:
 - **Classical Local Methods**: Early online methods were predominantly local, offering computational efficiency and ease of implementation. However, they frequently led to myopic decisions, repetitive traversal, dead ends, and coverage holes due to their limited local information.
 - **Connectivity-Aware Methods**: An intermediate development introduced connectivity checks to local decision-making, aiming to prevent the fragmentation of uncovered areas and reduce redundant travel. While an improvement, these methods still faced limitations in complex scenarios where all local options might lead to fragmentation.
 - **Emergence of Non-Local Methods**: More recently, non-local methods have gained prominence. These approaches integrate higher-level decision structures (e.g., subarea graphs, sampling-based representations like Rapidly Covering Graphs, or path-repair mechanisms) to guide coverage. This provides non-myopic waypoint selection, reduces backtracking, and addresses fragmentation, albeit at a potentially higher computational cost associated with managing these complex structures.

- **Advancements in Multi-Robot CPP**: Multi-robot CPP is crucial for large-scale coverage, enhancing speed, resilience, and distributed execution.
 - **Shift from Grid-Level to Subarea-Level Coordination**: The field has progressed from fine-grained, grid-cell-level coordination, which is prone to conflicts and overlaps, towards coarser, subarea-level coordination. This hierarchical approach improves workload balancing, reduces inter-robot interference, and supports more efficient task reassignment and global tour updates as environments are discovered or robot states change.
 - **Offline Methods**: Spanning-tree-based methods provide systematic coverage but require optimization for workload and turn reduction. Subarea-decomposition methods offer greater flexibility by integrating various cost metrics but face computational challenges in joint optimization of decomposition, assignment, and transitions.
 - **Online Methods**: Grid-based methods are efficient but susceptible to conflicts. Subarea-decomposition methods improve workload management and conflict reduction through region-level coordination, though they demand more computation and communication.

- **Addressing 3D Complexity**: 3D CPP extends coverage to complex terrains and structures, requiring specialized approaches:
 - **Layered-Coverage**: Simplifies 3D problems by sequential 2D planning on planar layers, with performance depending on layer definition and inter-layer transitions.
 - **Contour-Aware Methods**: Designed for terrains with significant elevation changes, these methods generate paths conforming to contours to reduce energy-intensive vertical motions, necessitating accurate terrain models.
 - **Decomposition-Based Methods**: Operate directly on 3D surfaces, partitioning them based on geometric or kinematic properties. These methods handle complex surface characteristics but rely heavily on accurate models and can be computationally intensive due to factors like Inverse Kinematics (IK) analysis.

- **Platform-Specific Constrained CPP**: This area tailors CPP to specific robot limitations:
 - **Energy-Constrained CPP**: Focuses on complete coverage with return-and-recharge strategies or optimizing partial coverage within a fixed energy budget, sometimes incorporating mobile replenishment. Challenges involve scheduling and managing uncertainty.
 - **Curvature-Constrained CPP**: Addresses nonholonomic robots by incorporating Dubins or Reeds-Shepp paths, leading to executable trajectories but often increasing path length and complicating planning.
 - **Tethered CPP**: Manages cable length, entanglement, and topological constraints for tethered robots, extending operation time but limiting movement by base location and obstacles.
 - **Reconfigurable CPP**: Utilizes morphology adaptation to improve accessibility in narrow spaces and efficiency in open areas, requiring careful modeling of reconfiguration costs and feasibility.

- **Emergence of Learning-Based CPP**: This novel methodology integrates machine learning (e.g., supervised learning, reinforcement learning, GNNs) to enhance various CPP components, including path generation, subarea sequencing, and multi-robot task allocation and coordination. While showing potential for improved efficiency, adaptability, and scalability by learning heuristics and policies, these methods generally offer weaker guarantees on completeness, safety, generalization, and robustness compared to classical approaches.

- **Focus on Visual CPP**: Visual CPP shifts the objective from physical area traversal to sensing-driven tasks like inspection and reconstruction.
 - **Classical Methods**: Generate and sequence viewpoints (often via TSP) to achieve complete visual coverage, with later methods refining viewpoint quality and handling uncertainty. Computationally intensive for large or complex scenes.
 - **Hierarchical Methods**: Decompose environments into subareas, enabling global ordering and local planning, reducing complexity and improving scalability for large-scale visual tasks.

In summary, the survey identifies a progressive trend in CPP research: from basic 2D single-robot coverage to increasingly complex, multi-faceted problems involving multiple robots, intricate 3D geometries, diverse robot constraints, and advanced sensing or learning paradigms. This evolution reflects a continuous effort to enhance efficiency, completeness, and applicability in real-world robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey significantly contributes to the field of Coverage Path Planning (CPP) by providing a comprehensive, structured, and up-to-date analysis that bridges classical foundations with contemporary advancements. Its problem-driven taxonomy offers a novel framework for understanding the evolution and diversification of CPP research.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Unified Reference for Researchers**: The survey consolidates a vast and fragmented body of literature, primarily from 2015 to 2026, into a single, organized resource. This allows researchers, particularly those new to specific CPP sub-fields, to quickly grasp the state of the art, understand problem formulations, and identify key algorithms, their strengths, and limitations. It acts as a comprehensive starting point for literature reviews and project conceptualization.

- **Guidance for Future Research Directions**: By explicitly identifying open challenges and promising future directions, the survey directly influences the trajectory of future research. These suggested avenues include:
 - **Scalable Online Planning**: Encouraging the extension of advanced non-local online single-robot CPP methods to more complex multi-robot, 3D, and visual coverage scenarios, where real-time, adaptive decision-making is critical.
 - **Multi-Robot Heterogeneity and Communication Constraints**: Promoting research into multi-robot CPP methods that can effectively manage heterogeneous robot teams (differing capabilities, footprints, energy), operate under limited or intermittent communication, and ensure robust task allocation. This addresses a major gap between theoretical assumptions and real-world deployment.
 - **Integrated 3D and Visual Coverage**: Calling for a deeper integration of mapping, surface representation, uncertainty estimation, viewpoint selection, and path planning, especially for online 3D coverage and inspection. This would enable robots to build and use models from noisy sensor data in unknown environments.
 - **Hybrid Learning-Classical Frameworks**: Advocating for the development of hybrid approaches that combine the adaptability and efficiency of learning-based methods (for heuristics, policy generation, decision acceleration) with the interpretability, completeness, and safety guarantees offered by classical CPP techniques. This aims to overcome the current limitations of learning methods in providing strong guarantees.
 - **Embodied AI and Semantic Coverage**: Proposing an expansion of CPP beyond purely geometric considerations to task- and semantics-aware coverage. This involves integrating semantic mapping, vision-language models, active perception, and embodied decision-making, allowing robots to understand and respond to human instructions and semantic regions within environments.
 - **Dynamic Environments**: Highlighting the importance of extending online CPP to highly dynamic environments, incorporating advancements from motion planning research in areas like real-time replanning, uncertainty-aware prediction, and risk-aware decision-making to handle moving obstacles, humans, or other robots effectively.

- **Facilitating Cross-Pollination of Ideas**: The structured comparison of methods across different CPP categories can inspire researchers in one area to adapt or extend techniques developed in another. For instance, multi-robot coordination strategies might inform distributed 3D mapping, or learning-based approaches from single-robot contexts could be scaled for constrained multi-robot problems.

- **Informing Application Development**: The detailed analysis of strengths and limitations for various CPP methods provides valuable insights for engineers and practitioners developing robotic applications. It helps in selecting the most appropriate planning strategy based on the specific robot platform, environmental characteristics, and mission objectives, thereby improving the efficiency and reliability of robotic deployments in real-world settings.

In essence, this survey serves not only as a critical review of past and current research but also as a forward-looking document that identifies crucial scientific and engineering challenges, thereby shaping the agenda for future innovation in the broad and impactful field of coverage path planning.
