# SIMPACT: Simulation-Enabled Action Planning using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMPACT enables Vision-Language Models to perform zero-shot, physics-aware robotic manipulation by integrating them with automatically constructed multi-physics simulations. This framework iteratively refines action plans using simulated rollouts, leading to significantly improved success rates across a suite of seven challenging real-world tasks involving both rigid and deformable objects, outperforming baseline VLM-based and VLA methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack a grounded understanding of physical dynamics, struggling to predict action outcomes in dynamic environments due to their training on static data.
- Fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks, such as stacking or shaping deformable materials, require precise physical interaction and reasoning that current VLM-based planners cannot reliably achieve.
- Existing robotic manipulation approaches using VLMs either require extensive, task-specific training data or lack explicit modeling of temporal dynamics crucial for rich physical interactions.

## Method

- An automated pipeline constructs a ==multi-physics simulator== from a single RGB-D image and language instructions, inferring 3D geometries and ==physical parameters== (e.g., mass, elasticity) using VLMs and pretrained models.
- An ==iterative VLM-driven planning loop== proposes initial symbolic action sequences, evaluates their physical outcomes through ==simulated rollouts==, and refines plans based on observed successes or failures.
- The framework translates high-level ==symbolic actions== generated by the VLM into continuous ==6-DoF robot trajectories== for real-world execution on a Franka Emika robot arm.

## Results

- **SIMPACT** achieved substantially higher success rates (e.g., **80%** for 'Non-toppling push', **90%** for 'Shape rope') across **7** challenging real-world tasks, outperforming VLA (π0.5, **0%** success) and VLM-based baselines (VoxPoser, MOKA, limited success).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of VLM-guided action sampling, simulation rollout context, and the VLM optimizer for achieving high performance, with removing any component causing significant performance degradation.
- A high consistency (**89%** agreement) between simulation outcomes and real-world execution validated the simulator's reliability as a world model, with simulation construction averaging less than **2 minutes** and VLM planning about **3.7 minutes**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a test-time, simulation-in-the-loop world model with VLMs effectively bridges their high-level semantic reasoning with a grounded understanding of physical dynamics.
> - VLMs can successfully infer physical parameters and reason about action consequences by interpreting simulated outcomes, enabling physics-aware manipulation without explicit physics training.
> - Iterative refinement using the VLM as an optimizer, leveraging simulated failures as context, is crucial for achieving high success rates in complex, physically demanding tasks.
