# SIMPACT: Simulation-Enabled Action Planning using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SIMPACT enables Vision-Language Models to perform zero-shot, physics-aware robotic manipulation by integrating them with automatically constructed multi-physics simulations. This framework iteratively refines action plans using simulated rollouts, leading to significantly improved success rates across a suite of seven challenging real-world tasks involving both rigid and deformable objects, outperforming baseline VLM-based and VLA methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often lack a grounded understanding of physical dynamics, struggling to predict action outcomes in dynamic environments due to their training on static data.
- Fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks, such as stacking or shaping deformable materials, require precise physical interaction and reasoning that current VLM-based planners cannot reliably achieve.
- Existing robotic manipulation approaches using VLMs either require extensive, task-specific training data or lack explicit modeling of temporal dynamics crucial for rich physical interactions.

## Method

- An automated pipeline constructs a ==multi-physics simulator== from a single RGB-D image and language instructions, inferring 3D geometries and ==physical parameters== (e.g., mass, elasticity) using VLMs and pretrained models.
- An ==iterative VLM-driven planning loop== proposes initial symbolic action sequences, evaluates their physical outcomes through ==simulated rollouts==, and refines plans based on observed successes or failures.
- The framework translates high-level ==symbolic actions== generated by the VLM into continuous ==6-DoF robot trajectories== for real-world execution on a Franka Emika robot arm.

## Results

- **SIMPACT** achieved substantially higher success rates (e.g., **80%** for 'Non-toppling push', **90%** for 'Shape rope') across **7** challenging real-world tasks, outperforming VLA (π0.5, **0%** success) and VLM-based baselines (VoxPoser, MOKA, limited success).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of VLM-guided action sampling, simulation rollout context, and the VLM optimizer for achieving high performance, with removing any component causing significant performance degradation.
- A high consistency (**89%** agreement) between simulation outcomes and real-world execution validated the simulator's reliability as a world model, with simulation construction averaging less than **2 minutes** and VLM planning about **3.7 minutes**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a test-time, simulation-in-the-loop world model with VLMs effectively bridges their high-level semantic reasoning with a grounded understanding of physical dynamics.
> - VLMs can successfully infer physical parameters and reason about action consequences by interpreting simulated outcomes, enabling physics-aware manipulation without explicit physics training.
> - Iterative refinement using the VLM as an optimizer, leveraging simulated failures as context, is crucial for achieving high success rates in complex, physically demanding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively pursuing general-purpose robots capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured environments. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a promising approach for enabling robots to understand scenes and respond to human instructions, owing to their extensive training on internet-scale visual and language data. These models demonstrate capabilities in semantic reasoning, object understanding, and high-level task goal inference.

However, a recognized limitation of current VLMs in robotic applications is their lack of a grounded understanding of physical dynamics. VLMs are typically trained on static data, which lacks information about causal interactions, action-conditioned changes, or the temporal evolution of physical systems. Consequently, while VLMs can describe plausible actions in language, they often struggle to predict the physical outcomes of those actions in dynamic environments. This gap hinders their application in fine-grained manipulation tasks that require precise interaction with objects, such as stacking, pivoting, or shaping deformable materials, where small variations in contact or force can lead to significant changes in outcomes.

Existing approaches to robotic manipulation using VLMs include:
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models**: These models often fine-tune an action prediction head on large datasets of action-labeled robot data. While capable of zero-shot generalization to tasks similar to those in training, their performance on out-of-domain or complex physical interaction tasks can be limited due to the need for extensive, task-specific training data.
- **VLM-based methods with geometric representations**: Some works augment VLMs with 3D geometric representations, such as volumetric value maps, motion arrows, or keypoint affordances, to improve spatial reasoning. While these methods enhance 3D grounding, they frequently lack explicit modeling of temporal dynamics, which is crucial for tasks involving rich physical interactions.
- **Model-based planning in robotics**: This long-standing area focuses on synthesizing complex action sequences using predictive models of the environment. Recent advances incorporate neural dynamics models to learn physical dynamics from image or latent spaces. However, these methods often require training within specific problem domains and face challenges in open-world settings, where dynamic models must be built from real-world perception and plans generated from language instructions.

SIMPACT positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical challenge of integrating the high-level semantic reasoning of VLMs with a grounded understanding of physical dynamics. It aims to bridge the gap between VLM's abstract common sense and the concrete physics of the real world, without requiring additional VLM training or large-scale action-labeled datasets. By providing VLMs with a test-time, simulation-in-the-loop world model, SIMPACT enables physical reasoning for manipulation planning, differentiating it from approaches that rely solely on static geometric grounding or require domain-specific dynamics training. It proposes a novel approach to automatically construct multi-physics simulations from single-view RGB-D images, which is more efficient than some digital twin or real-to-sim-to-real methods that might require multiple views or extensive manual setup.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform physics-aware, fine-grained robotic manipulation in a zero-shot manner, meaning without task-specific training. The framework, named SIMPACT (SIMulation-enabled ACTion Planning), aims to equip VLMs with the capability to understand and reason about physical dynamics, a limitation of current VLMs trained on static internet data.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges:

- **VLM strengths and weaknesses:** VLMs possess advanced common-sense and semantic reasoning abilities, allowing them to interpret complex natural language instructions and understand diverse scenes. This makes them highly promising for general-purpose robot control in open-world environments. However, their training on static visual-language data means they inherently lack an understanding of how objects interact physically, how actions cause changes, or the temporal dynamics of a scene. They can describe what an action entails but struggle to predict its physical consequences.
- **Challenges in fine-grained manipulation:** Many real-world robotic tasks, such as precisely stacking objects, carefully pushing items without toppling, or shaping deformable materials, require a deep understanding of physical interactions. These tasks are sensitive to small variations in contact, force, and timing. Current VLM-based planners often propose action sequences that are semantically plausible but physically unsound, leading to failures in execution.
- **Need for physical grounding:** To overcome the VLM's lack of physical grounding, a mechanism is needed to provide them with the ability to anticipate how proposed actions will unfold in the physical world. This requires a dynamic model that can predict consequences and enable reasoning about contact dynamics and action outcomes.
- **Limitations of existing approaches:**
 - Traditional VLA models require extensive, action-labeled training data, which can be difficult to acquire for diverse, complex manipulation tasks, and often struggle with generalization to out-of-domain scenarios.
 - VLM-based methods that use 3D geometric representations improve spatial reasoning but often omit temporal dynamics, making them insufficient for tasks involving rich physical interactions.
 - Model-based planning frameworks, while powerful for long-horizon tasks, typically require domain-specific training for their dynamic models and struggle with open-world settings where models must be built from real-world perception and instructions provided in natural language.
- **Efficiency and generality:** The development of a system that can efficiently construct a physical simulator directly from a single-view RGB-D image and integrate it with VLM reasoning at test time, without additional VLM training, offers a path toward more generalizable embodied intelligence. This approach aims to leverage the VLM's existing broad knowledge rather than requiring it to learn physics from scratch or from specialized robot data.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to enable robots to execute precise, physics-aware actions based on high-level human instructions, thereby advancing the capabilities of general-purpose robots in unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SIMPACT framework enables zero-shot robotic manipulation action generation from a single RGB-D image and a natural language instruction, outputting a robot action sequence. The approach centers on constructing a physics simulator from real-world observations and integrating it into an iterative VLM-driven planning loop.

The overall framework operates in three main stages:

1. **Simulation Construction:** Given an initial RGB-D observation ($I_0$) and a language task description ($\ell_{task}$), an automated pipeline generates a multi-physics simulator (SIM). This simulator models rigid and deformable objects.
2. **VLM-driven Action Planning:** A manipulation planner integrates the simulator with a VLM. The VLM iteratively proposes actions, evaluates them through simulated rollouts, and refines its reasoning based on the observed outcomes.
3. **Real-world Execution:** Once a successful action sequence is validated in simulation, it is executed on the physical robot.

#### 3.1. Simulation Construction (Sec. 3.1)

The goal of this stage is to create a physics-based simulator that can accurately predict action consequences. The simulation is defined by a discrete-time state transition function: $s_t = \text{SIM}(s_{t-1}, a_t; \theta)$, where $s_t$ is the state, $a_t$ is the action, and $\theta$ contains time-invariant simulation parameters. The process involves reconstructing 3D geometries and inferring physical parameters:

- **Object Identification and Segmentation:** A VLM is prompted to generate object labels based on the user's instructions. A pretrained segmentation model, GroundedSAM2, then segments each identified object in the RGB-D image.
- **Physics Engine Selection:** The VLM automatically selects the appropriate physics engine based on inferred object characteristics:
 - **MuJoCo** for rigid bodies.
 - A variant of **projective dynamics** solver for stiff deformable objects.
 - The **Material Point Method (MPM)** solver for soft deformable objects (to handle topological changes).
- **Physical Parameter Inference ($\theta_{phys}$):** The VLM is prompted to infer physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction, elasticity) using its common-sense reasoning, based on the object's visual appearance and identified material. This is formulated as a question-answering framework where the VLM receives the scene RGB image as input (Supplementary Sec. A.1 provides detailed VLM prompts for this).

**Geometry Pipeline ($\theta_{geom}$):**

- **Mesh-based Rigid Body Simulation:**
 - From the segmented RGB image, complete triangle meshes ($\hat{M}_i$) for each object are reconstructed using a pretrained image-to-3D model.
 - Each mesh is scaled and centered according to the real-world bounding box derived from point cloud segmentation, yielding $M_i = \alpha_i (\hat{M}_i - \beta_i)$.
 - The 6DoF pose ($X_i$) for each object is estimated using FoundationPose, leveraging the triangle mesh and the initial RGB-D observation.
 - Physical parameters include mass, friction coefficients, and center of mass.
- **Particle-based Deformable Object Simulation:**
 - The segmented object mask from the depth image is back-projected to obtain 3D surface points.
 - To represent the full volume, points are uniformly sampled within the volume bounded by the object surface and the supporting table surface.
 - Physical parameters for deformable bodies include elasticity and plasticity parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, mass density, etc., also inferred by VLM).

#### 3.2. Action Planning via Simulation-enabled VLM (Sec. 3.2)

The action planning framework follows an iterative refinement process (Algorithm 1):

1. **Initial Action Sampling (SAMPLE):** An initial set of $K$ action sequences ($A$) is generated by the VLM. For each sequence, a simulation rollout ($s_i$) is performed, and the results are stored in $S$.
2. **Iterative Action Optimization (OPTIMIZE):** For $K_{max}$ iterations, the VLM acts as an optimizer. It takes the previous action sequences ($A$) and their simulated rollouts ($S$) as context to propose a new, refined action sequence ($a_k$).
3. **Success Evaluation (TASKSUCCESS):** The new action sequence ($a_k$) is rolled out in the simulator ($s_k$). The VLM then evaluates the final simulation state to determine if the task objective is achieved. If successful, the process terminates, and $a_k$ is executed on the real robot. Otherwise, $a_k$ and $s_k$ are added to the context for further optimization.

The VLM's role is instantiated by three modules:

- **VLMs for Action Proposal Generation (SAMPLE):**
 - **Contextual Representation:** A comprehensive context is created, including the initial RGB-D observation ($I_0$), robot proprioceptive state, and 6-DoF poses and geometric attributes of manipulated objects.
 - **Hierarchical Action Generation:** Instead of directly generating continuous 6-DoF end-effector poses, the VLM generates high-level symbolic action sequences (e.g., MOVE, GRASP, RELEASE, PUSH, LIFT, DESCEND, ROTATE). These symbolic actions are then parameterized by continuous control variables and translated into 6-DoF trajectories by a deterministic mapping (ACTION2POSE). The VLM is guided by a specific system prompt ($\ell_{sample}$) to produce diverse initial proposals (Supplementary Fig. 8).
- **VLMs for Action Optimization (OPTIMIZE):**
 - The VLM utilizes in-context learning, taking previous action sequences and their corresponding simulated rollouts as input (Equation 2).
 - For each rollout, an optimization context ($c_i$) is constructed by subsampling time steps and including rendered simulator observation images, numerical actions, and object states. This provides both visual and state-based evidence for the VLM to refine actions. The VLM is guided by an optimization prompt ($\ell_{opt}$) to reason from failures and refine plans (Supplementary Fig. 11).
- **VLMs for Success Evaluation (TASKSUCCESS):**
 - The VLM evaluates the final state of a simulated rollout ($s_k$) by examining a rendered image ($I_k^T$) and the simulator state ($s_k^T$).
 - It determines if the task is successfully completed based on the task description ($\ell_{task}$) and an evaluation prompt ($\ell_{eval}$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SIMPACT on seven challenging real-world manipulation tasks involving both rigid and deformable objects, requiring fine-grained, physics-aware planning.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Robot System:** Franka Research 3 robot arm with a parallel-jaw gripper; custom 3D-printed end-effector for Play-Doh tasks.
- **Perception:** Single Intel RealSense D435i RGB-D camera.
- **Tasks:** Seven tasks including "Non-toppling push," "Bowl stacking," "Pivoting," "Shape rope," "Shape playdoh," "Avoid obstacle," and "Sweeping." These tasks demand precise physical interaction and reasoning.
- **Metric:** Success rate over 10 trials per method.
- **Baselines:**
 - **π$_{0.5}$:** A Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model pretrained on a large robot manipulation dataset.
 - **VoxPoser:** A VLM-based method leveraging volumetric value maps for spatial affordances.
 - **MOKA:** A VLM-based method predicting keypoints and affordance regions.
- **Implementation Details:** Google Gemini 2.5 Pro as the default VLM; K=10 initial action proposals, $K_{max}$=13 (max 3 optimization iterations). MuJoCo, PyTorch-based projective dynamics solver, and Warp-based MPM simulator were used.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Overall Performance (Table 2):** SIMPACT consistently demonstrated a substantially higher success rate across all seven tasks compared to baseline methods. For instance, SIMPACT achieved 80% on "Non-toppling push" and "Avoid obstacle," 90% on "Shape rope," and 60% on "Bowl stacking." In contrast, π$_{0.5}$ achieved 0% on all tasks, and VLM-based methods like VoxPoser and MOKA showed limited success (e.g., VoxPoser 20% on "Bowl stacking" and "Sweeping," MOKA 20% on "Shape rope," but 0% on most others).
- The VLA model π$_{0.5}$ frequently failed due to poor generalization to out-of-domain, challenging tasks despite approaching target objects. VLM-based baselines like VoxPoser and MOKA struggled with the precision required for fine-grained tasks, often making small errors (e.g., incorrect push points, improper grasping) that led to failures (Figure 6). SIMPACT's integration of simulation-enabled reasoning allowed it to refine plans, anticipate consequences, and avoid unstable strategies (Figure 4).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Removing VLM Sampler:** Replacing the VLM-guided action sampling with uninformed Gaussian sampling resulted in a significant performance drop, often to 0% success. This highlights the importance of VLM-conditioned sampling in generating reasonable initial action proposals for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Removing Simulation Rollout Context:** Disabling simulation-based validation led to substantial performance degradation (e.g., 0% on "Bowl stacking" and "Pivoting," 30% on "Shape rope"). This indicates that VLM's language-based reasoning alone, without physical grounding, is insufficient for reliably inferring successful actions in physically complex scenarios.
- **Removing VLM Optimizer:** Disabling the iterative action refinement process (i.e., selecting the best initial proposal without further optimization) resulted in a performance decrease (e.g., 30% on "Non-toppling push," 40% on "Shape rope"). This suggests that initial VLM-generated samples are often insufficient and require iterative refinement to achieve task success.
- **Sampling Length Ablation (Table 4):**
 - Using only 3 initial VLM-sampled actions degraded performance (e.g., 50% for "Non-toppling push" vs. 80% with 10 samples), as limited rollouts provided insufficient information for optimization.
 - Increasing samples from 10 to 20 sometimes led to a performance drop (e.g., "Bowl stacking" from 60% to 20%), possibly due to reduced VLM reasoning efficacy with longer contexts.

#### 4.4. Additional Analysis

- **Failure Case Analysis (Figure 7, Figure 14):** Failures were categorized as perception (single-view 3D reconstruction errors, 10%), planning (most frequent, especially in complex tasks like pivoting, 50%), and execution (sim-to-real discrepancies, 40%).
- **Correlation Between Simulation and Real-World Performance (Figure 12):** A high consistency (89% agreement) was observed between simulation outcomes (success/failure) and real-world execution. This indicates that the physical simulation serves as a reliable world model for VLM-based planning. Discrepancies (11% sim-success/real-fail) were noted, particularly in tasks sensitive to physical modeling (e.g., pivoting, shape dough).
- **Computation Time (Table 5):** Simulation construction averaged less than two minutes. The VLM planning stage was the most time-consuming (approx. 3.7 minutes for sampling and optimization) due to VLM reasoning time and API latency. Simulation rollouts were efficient (less than one minute).
- **Robustness Validation (Figure 13, Table 6, Table 7):** The method demonstrated robustness to scene variations (distractors, object poses, colors) due to VLM's scene understanding. VLM-estimated physics parameters exhibited low variance within valid ranges. Robustness to minor inaccuracies in friction coefficients was also observed within a reasonable range.
- **CALVIN Benchmark (Table 8):** SIMPACT outperformed zero-shot imitation learning (HULC) and VLA (RoboFlamingo) baselines on the CALVIN Long-Horizon Multi-Task Language Control benchmark, achieving 87.5% success for 1-instruction chains and 20% for 5-instruction chains, demonstrating strong generalization in simulation.
- **Replanning (Figure 15):** An optional replanning mechanism, incorporating real-world feedback after execution failure, successfully recovered a portion of initially failed cases (e.g., 50% for pivoting, 100% for avoid obstacle), indicating potential for improved robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SIMPACT framework presents a significant advancement in integrating Vision-Language Models with physical reasoning for robotic manipulation, offering several implications for the field:

- **Bridging Semantic and Physical Reasoning:** The work directly addresses a fundamental limitation of current VLMs: their lack of grounded understanding of physical dynamics. By equipping VLMs with simulation-in-the-loop world modeling, SIMPACT enables them to move beyond purely semantic or geometric reasoning to incorporate physical consequences, contact dynamics, and action outcomes into their planning. This integration allows for more informed and robust decision-making in physically interactive tasks.
- **Enabling Zero-Shot, Physics-Aware Manipulation:** SIMPACT demonstrates the capability for zero-shot robotic manipulation in complex, fine-grained tasks without requiring task-specific training. This is a crucial step towards general-purpose robots that can adapt to novel situations and instructions without extensive data collection or fine-tuning for every new task. The ability to generalize across different rigid and deformable object properties and diverse manipulation strategies (pushing, grasping, pivoting, squeezing, sweeping) highlights its potential for real-world application in unstructured environments.
- **Novel Test-Time Reasoning Paradigm:** The proposed iterative planning framework, where simulation rollouts serve as contextual feedback for a VLM-based optimizer, introduces a new form of test-time reasoning in robotics. Unlike traditional model-predictive control or optimization methods, the VLM's rich prior knowledge and in-context learning capabilities enable it to reason about and learn from failure examples, leading to non-local action refinements. This paradigm leverages the VLM's intelligence to interpret and learn from simulated experiences on the fly.
- **Efficient Simulation Construction:** The automated pipeline for constructing multi-physics simulations from a single RGB-D observation, utilizing pretrained visual foundation models (segmentation, 3D generation, pose estimation) and VLM-based parameter inference, contributes to the practicality of this approach. This efficiency in simulator creation reduces the overhead typically associated with setting up complex physics environments for novel scenes.
- **Advancing Embodied Intelligence:** By embedding physics understanding into VLM reasoning, SIMPACT offers a promising direction towards developing more generalizable embodied intelligence. Robots equipped with such capabilities could operate more autonomously and reliably in dynamic, unpredictable real-world settings, performing tasks that require delicate interaction and prediction of physical consequences.
- **Impact on Future VLM and Robotics Research:** This work suggests that future VLMs could be designed with a deeper inherent understanding of physics, or that specialized architectures could be developed to better integrate dynamic world models. It also paves the way for further research into reducing the sim-to-real gap, enhancing the fidelity of automatically generated simulations, and incorporating real-time feedback for increased robustness in execution. The ability to plan for tasks involving deformable objects is particularly impactful, as these are often more challenging for traditional robot control methods.
