# Connecting Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture with Contrastive Self-supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces C-JEPA (Contrastive-JEPA), integrating Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) into the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA) to address issues of representation collapse and inaccurate mean patch learning. The combined framework achieves superior performance and faster convergence across various vision tasks, including a 1.0% increase in ImageNet-1K fine-tuning accuracy and a 0.8 AP_box improvement on COCO compared to I-JEPA.

## Problem

- The Exponential Moving Average (EMA) mechanism in I-JEPA is often insufficient to prevent "entire collapse" of learned representations, leading to trivial features.
- I-JEPA's prediction mechanism struggles to accurately learn the mean of patch representations within masked target blocks, impacting feature quality.

## Method

- Introduces **C-JEPA** by integrating ==VICReg=='s variance, invariance, and covariance regularization terms directly into the I-JEPA loss function.
- VICReg's variance and covariance terms explicitly encourage diverse and decorrelated features, effectively preventing model collapse.
- The invariance term of VICReg helps to accurately align the mean of patch representations across different augmented views, addressing a key I-JEPA limitation.

## Results

- C-JEPA achieves faster and better convergence compared to I-JEPA, consistently yielding higher performance metrics with fewer pre-training epochs.
- Demonstrates improved ImageNet-**1K** classification, achieving **73.7%** (+**0.8%**) linear probing and **84.5%** (+**1.0%**) fine-tuning top-1 accuracy for ViT-B/16 over I-JEPA.
- Outperforms I-JEPA across diverse downstream tasks, showing gains of **0.8** AP_box and **0.8** AP_mask on COCO, 1.1 mIoU on ADE20K, and 1.7 (J&F)_m on DAVIS video object segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit regularization strategies, such as VICReg's variance and covariance terms, are more robust than EMA in preventing collapse in joint-embedding predictive architectures.
> - Combining the strengths of predictive learning from masked images (JEPA) with robust collapse-prevention and feature diversity mechanisms (VICReg) leads to a more stable and effective self-supervised learning framework.
> - Theoretical analysis using the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework provides insights into the necessity of stop-gradient and predictor in JEPA, further justifying the need for explicit regularization for stable, non-collapsing learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of unsupervised visual representation learning, a crucial area of machine learning aiming to extract meaningful features from unlabeled image data. The broader landscape is characterized by two dominant paradigms: Masked Image Modeling (MIM) and Contrastive Self-supervised Learning (CSL).

**Masked Image Modeling (MIM)** approaches, exemplified by methods like MAE (Masked Autoencoders) and BEiT, learn representations by reconstructing masked portions of an input image based on the unmasked context. The Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), particularly its image-based variant I-JEPA, belongs to this category. I-JEPA distinguishes itself by learning embedding spaces directly rather than reconstructing pixels or tokens, predicting embeddings of masked target blocks from visible context blocks. Yann LeCun, a prominent figure in AI and an author on several foundational JEPA papers, has championed JEPA as a promising path towards more autonomous machine intelligence.

**Contrastive Self-supervised Learning (CSL)** methods, such as SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, and VICReg, learn representations by bringing augmented views of the same image closer in an embedding space while pushing apart representations of different images (or discouraging trivial solutions in non-contrastive variants). VICReg (Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization) specifically aims to prevent model collapse by enforcing variance in embeddings, invariance across augmented views, and decorrelation among features, without requiring negative pairs.

The existing I-JEPA framework, while successful, has faced identified limitations, particularly concerning its Exponential Moving Average (EMA) mechanism's inadequacy in preventing "entire collapse" (where the model learns trivial, non-informative representations) and its prediction mechanism's struggle to accurately learn the mean of patch representations. This paper strategically bridges the gap between these two paradigms. It takes the architectural strengths of JEPA (predictive learning from masked patches) and integrates them with the robust collapse-prevention and feature diversity mechanisms inherent in VICReg, a successful CSL technique. By doing so, C-JEPA attempts to create a more stable and effective self-supervised learning framework that inherits the best from both worlds, aiming to push the boundaries of unsupervised representation quality and learning efficiency. This integration represents a significant step towards developing more robust and versatile self-supervised models for complex visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research stem directly from two identified limitations of the Image-based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA), a prominent method in unsupervised visual representation learning:

1. **Addressing Inefficacy of EMA in Preventing Entire Collapse:** I-JEPA utilizes an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the online network's weights to update its target encoder. However, a critical limitation observed is that this EMA mechanism is often insufficient to prevent "entire collapse," a phenomenon where the model learns trivial representations (e.g., all embeddings become constant or identical), rendering the learned features useless. The motivation here is to introduce a more robust mechanism to ensure that the learned representations maintain sufficient variance and distinctiveness, thereby preventing this collapse.

2. **Improving Accuracy in Learning Mean of Patch Representations:** The I-JEPA prediction mechanism aims to accurately learn the mean of patch representations within masked target blocks. However, the existing approach has been found to be inadequate in consistently and accurately capturing these mean representations. This limitation impacts the quality and granularity of the learned features. The objective is to enhance the model's ability to learn more informative and invariant mean patch representations across different augmented views of the same image.

To address these challenges, the study introduces **C-JEPA (Contrastive-JEPA)**, with the overarching goal of significantly enhancing the stability and quality of visual representation learning. The specific motivation for integrating Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) is that VICReg's methodology is specifically designed to:
- Learn variance and covariance to effectively avert entire collapse by encouraging diverse and non-redundant features.
- Ensure invariance for the mean of augmented views, directly supporting the objective of learning accurate and consistent patch representations.

Ultimately, the key motivation is to develop a novel framework that not only mitigates the identified weaknesses of I-JEPA but also achieves faster and improved convergence in downstream tasks like linear probing and fine-tuning, thereby setting a new benchmark for unsupervised visual representation learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work is the novel integration of the Image-based Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (I-JEPA) with the Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) strategy, leading to the proposed **C-JEPA** framework. The approach can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Reinterpreting Existing Frameworks

The paper first revisits the foundational architectures:
- **I-JEPA:** Explained as a joint-embedding predictive architecture that uses a context encoder, a target encoder (updated via EMA), and a predictor. The objective is to minimize the L2 distance between predicted patch-level representations from the context encoder and target patch-level representations from the target encoder for masked blocks (Equation 1). The target encoder's gradient is usually stopped (`SG` operator) to prevent trivial solutions.
- **SimSiam:** A contrastive self-supervised learning method that minimizes the distance between two augmented views of the same image, processed by an online network and a target network (without EMA, but with a stop-gradient). Its objective (Equation 2) highlights the goal of learning invariance.
- **VICReg:** A regularization strategy designed to prevent collapse in Siamese networks by enforcing three terms:
 - **Variance regularization (v):** A hinge function on the standard deviation of embeddings to ensure features are not all identical (Equation 3).
 - **Covariance regularization (c):** Minimizes off-diagonal elements of the covariance matrix to encourage features to be decorrelated and non-redundant (Equation 4).
 - **Invariance term:** Minimizes the Euclidean distance between augmented views, similar to other contrastive methods.

#### 3.2. Connecting I-JEPA with SimSiam

The paper draws theoretical and empirical parallels between I-JEPA and SimSiam, noting their shared underlying principle of minimizing distances between representations. While I-JEPA focuses on predicting masked patches and SimSiam on augmented views, both aim for consistency. Empirical studies suggesting that linear predictors in related frameworks align with the correlation matrix of representations further strengthen this connection, implying SimSiam's approach could implicitly align with learning the mean of augmented views, a goal for I-JEPA's patch prediction.

#### 3.3. Connecting I-JEPA with VICReg (C-JEPA Framework)

This is the central methodological contribution. C-JEPA integrates VICReg's regularization terms directly into the I-JEPA loss function.
- **Loss Function:** The overall C-JEPA loss is defined as a weighted sum of the original JEPA loss and the VICReg loss (Equation 21 in Appendix C.1). The VICReg loss itself is a weighted sum of its similarity, standard deviation (variance), and covariance terms (Equation 22).
- **Preventing Collapse:** VICReg's variance and covariance terms are crucial here. The variance term prevents the model from mapping all inputs to the same constant vector, ensuring diversity. The covariance term encourages different dimensions of the embedding space to encode distinct information, reducing redundancy.
- **Improving Mean Representation Learning:** The invariance term of VICReg, which minimizes the distance between augmented views, helps align the mean of patch representations across different views, addressing I-JEPA's limitation in this regard.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis using the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) framework to characterize the learning dynamics. It shows that in a linearized I-JEPA model with a stop-gradient operator and a particular predictor structure, the representation dynamics converge (Equation 8). Crucially, it demonstrates that *without* the stop-gradient operator, the representations would collapse to zero (Equation 19 in Appendix B.1), and *without* the predictor, representations would not be updated (Equation 20 in Appendix B.2). This theoretical insight underscores the importance of the stop-gradient and predictor for stable learning in JEPA, and implicitly supports why VICReg's explicit regularization is necessary to achieve robust, non-collapsing learning in more complex scenarios.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Backbones:** Vision Transformers (ViT-T, ViT-S, ViT-B, ViT-L) are used as encoders.
- **Training:** AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule with warmup, and specific batch sizes and epochs are employed (detailed in Appendix C.3).
- **Masking Strategy:** Similar to I-JEPA, 4 possibly overlapping target block masks are used.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific coefficients (e.g., β_vicreg = 0.001, β_sim = 25, β_std = 25, β_cov = 1) are determined through ablation studies.

By integrating VICReg, C-JEPA aims to leverage the benefits of predictive learning from masked images while simultaneously ensuring the stability and diversity of the learned representations, overcoming the critical limitations of its predecessor.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of C-JEPA demonstrates significant improvements over I-JEPA and other state-of-the-art self-supervised learning methods across a range of vision tasks.

#### 4.1. Faster and Better Convergence (Figure 1 & General Trends)

A central finding is that C-JEPA achieves faster and better convergence compared to I-JEPA. This is visually represented in Figure 1 and substantiated by consistently higher performance metrics even with fewer pre-training epochs (e.g., 600 epochs for C-JEPA vs. 1600 for MAE/iBOT).

#### 4.2. ImageNet-1K Image Classification (Table 1 & 9)

- **ViT-B/16 (Table 1):** C-JEPA significantly outperforms I-JEPA, achieving 73.7% (+0.8%) in linear probing and 84.5% (+1.0%) in fine-tuning top-1 accuracy. It also surpasses other baselines like MAE (83.6%), BEiT (83.4%), and DINO (82.8%) in fine-tuning, demonstrating superior learned representations.
- **ViT-T/16 and ViT-S/16 (Table 9 in Appendix D):** Similar trends are observed for smaller models, with C-JEPA showing clear improvements in knn, linear probing, and fine-tuning accuracies over I-JEPA. For ViT-S/16, C-JEPA achieves 78.17% fine-tuning accuracy compared to I-JEPA's 76.87%.

#### 4.3. COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation (Table 1 & 10)

- **ViT-B/16 (Table 1):** C-JEPA yields notable gains of 0.8 AP_box (50.7% vs. 49.9%) and 0.8 AP_mask (45.3% vs. 44.5%) compared to I-JEPA. It also outperforms DINO and MAE in both metrics.
- **ViT-T/16 and ViT-S/16 (Table 10 in Appendix D):** Consistent improvements are seen across smaller models, with C-JEPA exhibiting better AP_box and AP_mask scores across various thresholds.

#### 4.4. ADE20K Semantic Segmentation (Table 1 & 11)

- **ViT-B/16 (Table 1):** C-JEPA surpasses I-JEPA by 1.1 mIoU (48.7% vs. 47.6%), indicating better capture of semantics for dense prediction tasks. It also outperforms MAE and data2vec.
- **ViT-T/16 and ViT-S/16 (Table 11 in Appendix D):** C-JEPA consistently achieves higher mIoU for both Semantic FPN and UPerNet architectures across smaller ViT models.

#### 4.5. Scaling up to Large Models (ViT-L/16) and Other Tasks (Table 2)

- **DAVIS Video Object Segmentation:** C-JEPA shows significant gains, achieving 58.3% (+1.7%) (J&F)_m compared to I-JEPA's 56.6%, indicating superior frozen representations for video tasks.
- **Clevr Object Counting and Depth Prediction:** C-JEPA achieves 86.8% (+1.2%) for Clevr/Count and 71.6% (+0.4%) for Clevr/Dist, further validating its ability to learn better representations for low-level tasks.
- **Linear Probing and Fine-tuning:** Even with ViT-L/16, C-JEPA maintains its advantage, achieving 78.1% (+0.6%) in linear probing and 86.2% (+0.9%) in fine-tuning compared to I-JEPA.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (Tables 3, 4, 12, 13)

- **Contribution of VICReg Components:** Ablation studies clearly demonstrate the efficacy of incorporating Variance/Covariance and Invariance terms from VICReg.
 - Adding Variance/Covariance (which addresses "collapse") and Invariance (which addresses "mean") leads to faster convergence and better overall performance (Tables 3 & 12 vs. baseline I-JEPA).
 - The full C-JEPA (I-JEPA + Variance/Covariance + Invariance) consistently yields the best results for both faster (Tables 3 & 12) and better convergence (Tables 4 & 13) across various metrics.
 - Specifically, comparing `✓(EMA for collapse) ✗ ✓(mean)` against `✓(mean) ✓(collapse) ✗` and `✓ ✓ ✓` highlights that VICReg's explicit Variance/Covariance terms (`✓(collapse)`) are more effective at preventing collapse than I-JEPA's EMA (`✓(EMA for collapse)`), leading to superior performance when combined with the invariance term.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Attention Visualizations (Figure 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8)

- Visualizations of learned attention maps show that C-JEPA produces more focused and contextually relevant attention compared to the base I-JEPA model. This visually confirms the theoretical improvements in maintaining stable and meaningful patch-level representations across diverse image contexts.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that integrating VICReg into I-JEPA (creating C-JEPA) effectively addresses the limitations of collapse and mean representation learning, leading to significantly enhanced stability, quality, and efficiency of learned visual representations across a wide array of computer vision tasks and model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of C-JEPA marks a significant advancement in the field of unsupervised visual representation learning by successfully addressing critical limitations of existing Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) like I-JEPA. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Enhanced Robustness and Stability in Self-Supervised Learning

C-JEPA directly tackles the issues of "entire collapse" and inaccurate learning of patch representation means, which have plagued previous JEPA models. By integrating VICReg's variance, invariance, and covariance regularization, C-JEPA provides a more robust and stable learning framework. This stability is crucial for training large models on vast, unlabeled datasets, where trivial solutions and feature redundancy can severely hinder performance. The empirical evidence of C-JEPA's improved stability and collapse prevention has a foundational impact on the reliability of self-supervised learning methods.

#### 5.2. Improved Performance Across Diverse Downstream Tasks

The consistent and significant performance gains demonstrated by C-JEPA across a wide array of tasks—including image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, semantic segmentation, video object segmentation, object counting, and depth prediction—underscore its versatility and effectiveness. These improvements are observed across different Vision Transformer (ViT) model scales, from tiny to large, indicating the scalability and generalizability of the proposed framework. This positions C-JEPA as a new benchmark for pre-trained representations, capable of yielding better results with potentially faster convergence compared to prior art.

#### 5.3. Bridging Paradigms for Synergistic Gains

The work successfully connects two prominent self-supervised learning paradigms: Masked Image Modeling (MIM) through JEPA, and Contrastive Learning/Regularization through VICReg. This integration demonstrates that combining strengths from different approaches can lead to synergistic gains that overcome individual limitations. This methodological innovation could inspire future research to explore similar hybrid architectures, further unlocking the potential of unsupervised learning.

#### 5.4. Practical Implications for Real-World Applications

In many real-world scenarios, labeled data is scarce or expensive to acquire. Unsupervised learning models like C-JEPA provide a powerful alternative for extracting high-quality features from unlabeled data, which can then be fine-tuned for specific downstream tasks with minimal labeled examples. C-JEPA's improved representations can translate into more accurate and efficient AI systems for applications ranging from autonomous driving and medical imaging to content recommendation and robotics.

#### 5.5. Setting a New Benchmark and Guiding Future Research

C-JEPA's superior performance sets a new benchmark in unsupervised visual representation learning. This will likely stimulate further research in several directions:
- **Further Refinements:** Investigating more sophisticated ways to integrate regularization techniques into predictive architectures.
- **Scalability:** Testing C-JEPA on even larger, more diverse datasets and with different backbone architectures.
- **Theoretical Understanding:** Deeper theoretical analysis into why certain combinations of objectives lead to better representation properties.
- **Adaptability:** Exploring the adaptability of C-JEPA to multimodal learning (e.g., video, text-image), 3D data, or other complex data types beyond standard images.

In conclusion, C-JEPA is a significant step forward that not only solves critical issues within the JEPA framework but also offers a robust, high-performing model with broad applicability and the potential to accelerate advancements across the field of computer vision and machine learning.
