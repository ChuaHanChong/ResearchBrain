# DualDiff+: Dual-Branch Diffusion for High-Fidelity Video Generation with Reward Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> DualDiff+ is a dual-branch conditional diffusion model for driving-scene image and video generation that projects 3D occupancy grids onto the image plane via ray sampling, fuses multimodal conditions with gated attention, and aligns video output to ground truth with a reward-guided fine-tuning stage, cutting FID by 4.09% over the best baseline on nuScenes.

## Problem

- Prior conditioning relies on 3D bounding boxes and BEV road maps that misalign with perspective-view image generation and lack fine-grained geometric detail.
- Existing multimodal fusion uses direct concatenation or static attention, so models cannot adaptively prioritize relevant scene cues or suppress noisy inputs.
- Prior driving-video generators optimize only pixel-level likelihood losses, neglecting global consistency and high-level semantic coherence across generated frames.

## Method

- ==Occupancy Ray-shape Sampling (ORS)== projects 3D occupancy voxel grids onto the image plane per-pixel via ray casting, giving dense foreground/background 2D features for a ==dual-branch ControlNet==.
- ==Foreground-Aware Mask (FGM)== loss reweights the denoising MSE by bounding-box area, up-weighting small and distant foreground objects during diffusion training.
- ==Semantic Fusion Attention (SFA)== combines self-attention, ==gated self-attention== (learnable tanh gate), and ==deformable attention== to fuse visual, spatial, and CLIP-encoded textual features.
- ==Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD)== back-propagates gradients from an I3D-based reward comparing denoised video to ground-truth temporal features, fine-tuned via ==LoRA== on attention layers.
- Together these form **DualDiff+**, a dual-branch conditional diffusion model for driving-scene image and video generation.

## Results

- On nuScenes at 224x400, DualDiff cuts FID by **5.6%** versus MagicDrive and reaches **10.99** FID overall, the best among all compared baselines including Drive-WM and PerlDiff.
- On Waymo, DualDiff improves 3D detection mAP (0-30m) by **7.3** points over MagicDrive (**10.4** vs **3.1**) and reduces FID by **5.71%** at matched resolution.
- Ablations show ORS, FGM, and SFA each contribute additively (FID drops from **16.20** to **10.99** baseline-to-full), and RGD reduces video FVD by **86.06** and raises the composite score by **0.39**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Projecting dense 3D occupancy onto the 2D image plane via ray sampling resolves the domain mismatch between 3D scene representations and 2D perspective-view diffusion conditioning.
> - Loss reweighting by object scale (not just architecture changes) is a cheap, generalizable way to fix small/distant-object underrepresentation in diffusion-based scene synthesis.
> - Bridging pixel-level diffusion losses with a differentiable high-level reward signal (via a pretrained video classifier) can inject semantic/temporal coherence that MSE alone cannot capture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving heavily relies on vast amounts of annotated real-world data, which is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect. Generative models, particularly diffusion models like Stable Diffusion, offer a promising solution by synthesizing realistic street-view data, thereby reducing the dependency on costly real-world acquisition and enabling the creation of diverse synthetic datasets for training and validation.

This work specifically addresses the limitations of existing conditional generative models for autonomous driving. Previous methods, such as MagicDrive, Panacea, and Drive-WM, have made progress in generating high-fidelity scenes. However, the authors identify three persistent challenges that DualDiff+ aims to overcome:

1. **Suboptimal Condition Encoding:** Current methods often use 3D bounding boxes and BEV (Bird's-Eye View) road maps as conditional inputs. While these provide control over foreground and background, they lack fine details, can misalign with Perspective View (PV) image generation, and may not fully integrate multimodal information effectively. Treating foreground and background uniformly can also limit learning capacity.
2. **Insufficient Cross-Modal Interaction:** Existing fusion techniques (e.g., direct concatenation or static attention) for multimodal inputs (maps, bounding boxes, prompts) often fail to dynamically prioritize relevant information or suppress noise, leading to suboptimal feature integration and compromised scene generation quality.
3. **Lack of Overall Consistency and Coherence in Image-to-Video Generation:** Many methods, while focusing on pixel-level accuracy, neglect the global consistency and instance coherence critical for realistic and task-specific video generation, especially in dynamic driving scenarios.

DualDiff+ distinguishes itself by introducing novel solutions to these challenges. It proposes a more comprehensive scene control mechanism with a dual-branch architecture, enhanced multimodal fusion, and a unique reward-guided framework for video generation. By focusing on more precise conditional inputs (Occupancy Ray-shape Sampling), better integration of foreground/background details (Foreground-Aware Mask), dynamic multimodal fusion (Semantic Fusion Attention), and high-level video consistency (Reward-Guided Diffusion), DualDiff+ aims to push the boundaries of synthetic data generation for autonomous driving, making the generated data more useful for downstream perception tasks and closed-loop simulation systems. It contributes to the growing body of work that leverages advanced generative AI to mitigate data scarcity and enhance the capabilities of autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly accurate and high-fidelity method for driving scene reconstruction that effectively utilizes comprehensive scene information as conditional inputs, extending to both multi-view images and video sequences. The overarching motivation stems from the high cost and labor-intensive nature of collecting and annotating large-scale camera datasets crucial for autonomous driving research. By generating realistic synthetic data, the authors aim to alleviate this dependency and provide a scalable solution for creating diverse and high-quality training resources.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Enhance Conditional Input Representation:** To overcome the limitations of conventional 3D bounding boxes and BEV road maps, which fail to capture the full complexity and fine-grained details of driving scenes. The objective is to introduce a richer, more precise conditional input that offers comprehensive foreground and background semantics alongside 3D spatial geometry. This is addressed by proposing **Occupancy Ray-shape Sampling (ORS)**.
2. **Improve Fine-grained Object Synthesis:** To address the challenge of accurately generating complex, fine-grained, and often distant foreground objects, which are critical for robust perception in autonomous driving. The objective is to apply a targeted mechanism during the denoising process to enhance the quality of these specific elements. This is tackled with the **Foreground-Aware Mask (FGM) denoising loss function**.
3. **Optimize Multimodal Information Fusion:** To resolve the issue of suboptimal feature integration in existing methods that use static or direct concatenation for multimodal inputs, leading to a lack of dynamic adaptation and effective noise suppression. The objective is to develop an adaptive mechanism for prioritizing relevant information and enhancing cross-modal interaction. This is achieved through the **Semantic Fusion Attention (SFA) mechanism**.
4. **Ensure Global Consistency and Semantic Coherence in Video Generation:** To address the common limitation where existing methods, despite focusing on pixel-level details, often neglect the higher-level perceptual quality and task-specific requirements in generated videos, leading to temporal inconsistencies. The objective is to maintain overall quality, consistency, and applicability for image-to-video generation. This is addressed by introducing the **Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD) framework**.

In essence, the motivation is to create a generative model, DualDiff+, that not only produces visually convincing driving scenes but also ensures their geometric accuracy, semantic richness, and temporal coherence, making them highly valuable for critical downstream autonomous driving tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DualDiff+ introduces a sophisticated, multi-pronged approach based on a dual-branch conditional diffusion model architecture to achieve high-fidelity image and video generation for autonomous driving scenes.

**A. Overall Architecture:**
The core of DualDiff+ is a **dual-branch conditional control structure** (Fig. 1), specifically designed to manage foreground and background information separately. This architecture leverages a frozen U-Net (from Stable Diffusion v1.5 with ControlNet weights) and employs two trainable ControlNet branches, denoted as ControlNet-τ (background) and ControlNet-μ (foreground). The outputs of these branches are integrated into the U-Net through residual connections. For video generation, a two-stage optimization method is used: an initial stage integrates temporal mechanisms (Spatio-Temporal Attention and Temporal Attention) for consistency, followed by a second stage using Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD) with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for high-level semantic alignment.

**B. Dual-branch Foreground-Background Modeling:**
1. **Occupancy Ray-shape Encoding (ORS):** To provide richer and more precise 3D spatial geometry and semantic control than traditional 3D bounding boxes or binary maps, ORS projects a 3D occupancy grid map onto the 2D image plane via ray-based sampling. For each pixel, a unique 3D ray is associated, and multiple 3D points are uniformly sampled along this ray. The values from the occupancy grid at these sampled points are extracted, forming a dense 2D feature representation (V ∈ R^(U×V×N_sample^)). This process is applied separately to foreground (G_f -> V_f) and background (G_b -> V_b) occupancy grids, feeding into their respective branches.
2. **Driving Scene Encoding:** Multi-modal conditional inputs are encoded:
 - **Foreground Objects (B):** 3D bounding box coordinates and object categories are encoded using CLIP text encoder for category and Fourier embedding for 3D coordinates.
 - **Background Elements (H):** Vectorized map points (e.g., lane markings) and their categories are encoded similarly using CLIP and Fourier embedding.
 - **View-specific Generation (P, L):** Camera pose (intrinsic/extrinsic matrices) is processed via Fourier embedding, and abstract semantic content from textual sequences is extracted using a frozen CLIP text encoder.
 These encoded conditions (c_box, c_map, c_cam, c_text) are fed into the respective foreground and background branches.
3. **Foreground Enhancement with Foreground-Aware Mask (FGM) Loss:** To improve the generation of fine-grained, distant, or intricate foreground objects, FGM modifies the standard MSE denoising loss. It applies a weighted mask (M) to the loss function, where areas corresponding to camera-projected foreground object bounding boxes receive higher weights (Eq. 7). This dynamically adjusts the loss contribution based on object size, forcing the model to pay more attention to these crucial details during denoising (Eq. 8).

**C. Multimodal Aware Representation Alignment (Semantic Fusion Attention - SFA):**
SFA is designed to dynamically integrate and align information from diverse modalities (visual, spatial, textual) while suppressing noise. It processes features in a sequential manner (Fig. 3):
1. **Self-Attention on Visual Features:** Initially, visual features (V) from the ORS module undergo self-attention to yield an enhanced visual representation (V'1).
2. **Gated Self-Attention with Spatial Features:** V'1 is concatenated with spatial features (c_spatial, comprising [c_box, c_cam] for foreground and [c_map, c_cam] for background). A gated self-attention mechanism is then applied, allowing dynamic adjustment of attention weights to filter noise and enhance spatial localization (Eq. 10).
3. **Deformable Attention with Textual Features:** The spatially enhanced visual features (V'2) are finally fused with textual features (c_text) using a deformable attention mechanism (Eq. 11), which adaptively focuses on complex relationships between visual elements and textual descriptions. This culminates in a refined, multimodal-aware feature representation (V*).

**D. Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD) for Video Generation:**
To ensure high-level global consistency and semantic coherence beyond pixel-level accuracy in generated videos, RGD introduces a novel optimization framework (Fig. 4).
1. **Reward Function (R):** A differentiable reward function R(·) evaluates the generated video (x_0) against the ground truth (v) based on high-level features. The goal is to maximize this expected reward (Eq. 12).
2. **Iterative Denoising and Gradient Propagation:** Training starts with pure noise (x_T). The video diffusion model (p_θ) iteratively denoises the latent variables. Crucially, after the denoising process, the fully denoised video (x_0) is fed into the Inception3D (I3D) model to extract high-level temporal features. The reward function is then computed as the negative distance between the I3D features of the generated video and the ground truth (R_I3D = -||M_I3D(x_0) - M_I3D(v)||, Eq. 14).
3. **Optimization with LoRA:** Dense gradients from this reward function are propagated back to the model parameters (θ) (Eq. 13). To prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain generalization, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is integrated into the attention mechanisms of the U-Net layers, allowing efficient fine-tuning while keeping most original parameters frozen.

**E. Implementation Details:**
The approach is built upon Stable Diffusion v1.5 with ControlNet for segmentation. Training involves 8 A800 GPUs, with separate initial training for dual branches (90k steps) followed by fine-tuning (30k steps). Image sampling uses UniPC (20 steps, CFG 2.0) at resolutions like 224x400 or 432x768. Video generation produces 16-frame videos from 12Hz NuScenes data. ControlNet is frozen during video generation, while spatio-temporal and temporal attention components of the U-Net are trained. LoRA is applied to all attention layers during reward model fine-tuning. Gradient checkpointing is used for memory management.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DualDiff+ demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across multiple evaluation metrics and datasets, showcasing its effectiveness in generating high-fidelity, controllable, and semantically consistent driving scenes and videos.

**A. Comparison with Baselines (Image and Video Generation):**
- **Image Fidelity (FID):** On the NuScenes dataset, DualDiff significantly reduced the FID score compared to state-of-the-art methods like MagicDrive (e.g., 10.99 vs. 16.20 at 224x400 resolution, a 5.6% reduction). It also outperformed MagicDrive on the Waymo dataset with an even greater FID reduction (11.45 vs. 17.16, a 5.71% reduction). (Table I, II, III).
- **Video Fidelity (FVD):** For video generation, DualDiff achieved a substantial FVD reduction compared to MagicDrive (e.g., 220.01 vs. 218.12, although the paper's table V shows 220.01 vs 218.12, and then 306.07 vs 220.01 for w/n reward vs w reward; the text says 32.5% reduction compared to MagicDrive, which implies a comparison to 306.07 vs 218.12. The text also says "reduces the FVD score by 86.06 and improving the overall evaluation score by 0.39 under the same training iterations" due to RGD, indicating RGD's significant impact.) This demonstrates superior temporal consistency and image quality in generated videos (Table II, V).

**B. Performance on Downstream Perception Tasks:**
- **3D Object Detection:** DualDiff consistently improved mean Average Precision (mAP). On NuScenes, it increased mAP by 1.46% compared to Drive-WM (22.13 vs. 20.66). On Waymo, it achieved a 7.3% mAP increase for 0-30m range objects compared to MagicDrive (10.4 vs 3.1). (Table I, III, IV, V).
- **BEV Segmentation:** DualDiff showed significant gains in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). On NuScenes, it improved Vehicle mIoU by 4.50% compared to Drive-WM (31.69 vs. 27.19) and Road mIoU by 1.70% compared to MagicDrive (62.75 vs. 61.05). (Table I, III, IV, V).
- These results collectively confirm that the synthetic data generated by DualDiff+ is not only visually realistic but also semantically and geometrically accurate enough to enhance the performance of perception models trained on it.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
The ablation studies (Table VII) rigorously validated the contribution of each proposed module:
- **Occupancy Ray-shape Sampling (ORS):** Replacing BEV road map encoding with ORS features led to substantial improvements, including a 2.94% FID reduction and a 1.14% increase in road mIoU. This confirms ORS's effectiveness in providing viewpoint-consistent and spatially accurate background information (Fig. 6, 7).
- **Foreground-Aware Mask (FGM):** FGM improved foreground object detection, yielding a 0.4% mAP increase and 1.70% vehicle mIoU gain. Under the dual-branch setup, these improvements further increased by 0.83% mAP and 1.02% vehicle mIoU, highlighting its ability to enhance the generation of distant obstacles (Fig. 9).
- **Semantic Fusion Attention (SFA):** SFA resulted in a 1.58% FID reduction, 1.67% foreground mAP improvement, and 2.92% BEV segmentation vehicle mIoU increase. This demonstrates SFA's dynamic ability to prioritize relevant multimodal information and suppress noise (Fig. 8).
- **Dual-Branch Design:** Comparisons showed that the decoupled dual-branch design, even with comparable parameters, intrinsically outperformed a summed SFA output variant (1.23% FID reduction, 1.08% mAP increase, 0.84% mIoU improvement), validating its architectural advantage.
- **Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD):** As noted in Table V, RGD significantly improved video generation quality, reducing FVD by 86.06 and improving the overall evaluation score by 0.39, emphasizing its role in temporal consistency and task relevance.

**D. Data-centric Closed-loop Training and Evaluation:**
The paper explored the utility of generated data as an augmentation strategy (Table VI). A data-centric closed-loop framework (Fig. 5) that mines and generates "corner cases" for fine-tuning demonstrated superior performance over random sampling. Adding just 10% more NuScenes data using the corner-case driven method improved mAP by 2.06% on DualDiff compared to random sampling. This highlights DualDiff+'s capability to generate highly targeted and impactful synthetic data for improving downstream perception model generalization and performance, particularly for challenging scenarios.

**E. Multi-level Controls:**
DualDiff+ successfully demonstrated flexible multi-level control—scene level (time, weather), background level (vectorized maps, ORS background features), and foreground level (3D bounding boxes, ORS foreground features)—ensuring consistent and realistic scene generation based on specified conditions (Fig. 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DualDiff+ represents a significant advancement in conditional generative modeling for autonomous driving, offering a multi-faceted solution to address critical challenges in data generation. Its significance and potential impact are profound:

1. **Reduced Data Dependency and Cost:** By generating high-fidelity, controllable, and semantically accurate synthetic driving scenes and videos, DualDiff+ directly addresses the enormous costs and logistical complexities associated with collecting and annotating real-world autonomous driving datasets. This democratization of data could accelerate research and development in the field, making advanced autonomous systems more accessible.
2. **Enhanced Downstream Task Performance:** The consistent state-of-the-art results across various perception tasks (3D object detection, BEV segmentation) using synthetic data generated by DualDiff+ underscore its practical utility. This indicates that the generated data is not merely visually plausible but also possesses the necessary geometric and semantic fidelity to effectively train and evaluate real-world autonomous driving systems, leading to more robust and reliable perception models.
3. **Improved Generalization through Corner Case Generation:** The demonstrated success of the data-centric closed-loop framework, which specifically targets and generates challenging "corner cases," is a crucial contribution. Autonomous vehicles often fail in rare, difficult scenarios. DualDiff+'s ability to synthesize such specific, hard examples can significantly improve the generalization capabilities and safety of perception models, reducing reliance on encountering these situations in real-world testing.
4. **Bridging Pixel-level and High-level Semantic Understanding:** The Reward-Guided Diffusion (RGD) framework is particularly impactful for video generation. By optimizing for high-level semantic consistency (using I3D features) in addition to pixel-level details, DualDiff+ produces videos that are not only visually smooth but also maintain coherent object behavior and scene dynamics over time. This is vital for tasks like trajectory prediction, planning, and simulation.
5. **Comprehensive Scene Control:** The dual-branch architecture, coupled with Occupancy Ray-shape Sampling (ORS) and multi-level controls, provides unprecedented granular control over both foreground and background elements. This level of precise control is invaluable for researchers and developers to create specific scenarios for targeted testing and validation, simulating diverse environmental conditions and traffic situations.
6. **Advancement in Generative AI for Robotics:** Beyond autonomous driving, the methodological innovations—such as ORS for structured 3D-to-2D projection, FGM for fine-grained object enhancement, SFA for robust multimodal fusion, and RGD for semantic video consistency—have broader implications for other robotics and computer vision applications requiring high-fidelity conditional generation from complex multi-modal inputs.

Future work outlined by the authors, focusing on improving computational efficiency, expanding generalization to diverse environments (e.g., varying geographies and urban layouts), and integrating multi-sensor fusion, suggests a clear roadmap for further strengthening DualDiff+'s capabilities and solidifying its position as a foundational tool in the development of autonomous systems.
