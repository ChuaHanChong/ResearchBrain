# InterDyn: Controllable Interactive Dynamics with Video Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> InterDyn introduces a framework that synthesizes realistic, controllable videos of interactive dynamics from an initial image and dynamic control signal, leveraging the implicit physics knowledge within large video diffusion models without explicit 3D reconstruction or physics simulations. It achieves high spatio-temporal coherence and realistic object motion for human-object interactions.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for dynamic interaction prediction relied on computationally expensive and error-prone "reconstruct-simulate-render" pipelines with explicit 3D geometry and physics simulators.
- Existing generative models often focused on synthesizing static state transitions, failing to capture continuous, temporally extended dynamics and force propagation between interacting objects.
- Generalizing physically plausible interaction generation from simplified synthetic environments to complex real-world scenarios remained a significant challenge due to the limitations of explicit modeling.

## Method

- **InterDyn** extends a frozen, pre-trained Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model with a trainable **ControlNet-like** branch, allowing fine-grained control over dynamic interactions.
- The control branch processes dynamic control signals, primarily binary masks (e.g., hand masks), and integrates them into the SVD's latent space via zero-initialized convolutions.
- The system is trained on datasets like **CLEVRER** (for object collision) and **Something-Something-v2** (for human-object interaction) to learn implicit physical dynamics.

## Results

- On the CLEVRER dataset, InterDyn successfully generates realistic force propagation and demonstrates counterfactual reasoning by producing distinct, physically plausible outcomes from altered control signals.
- In human-object interaction tasks (Something-Something-v2), InterDyn surpasses baselines like CosHand and DynamiCrafter in spatio-temporal dynamics (FVD, KVD) and achieves the highest Motion Fidelity, capturing continuous post-interaction effects.
- The framework generates diverse and complex interactions, including articulated object motion, fluid dynamics, and deformable object behaviors, while maintaining high visual quality and robustness to noisy control signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large video diffusion models, trained on extensive real-world video data, implicitly learn sophisticated "physics knowledge" and can function as neural renderers and implicit physics simulators.
> - Continuous, interactive dynamics, including force propagation and post-interaction effects, are crucial for realistic scene understanding and generation, moving beyond static state predictions.
> - The model demonstrates robustness to simple control signals like binary masks, indicating its capacity to infer complex interactive motions and details (e.g., individual fingers) from sparse input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

InterDyn operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence and computer vision: intuitive physics, generative modeling, controllable video synthesis, and human-object interaction (HOI).

Historically, predicting the dynamics of interacting objects in a visually realistic manner has been a grand challenge. **Intuitive physics** research aimed to equip AI systems with human-like physical reasoning. Early approaches relied on explicitly modeling 3D geometry from images, using physics simulators to predict future states, and then rendering the results. This "reconstruct-simulate-render" paradigm, exemplified by works like Wu et al., was computationally expensive, prone to errors from each sub-module, and struggled to generalize beyond simplified, synthetic environments. Subsequent efforts leveraged graph relational frameworks or incorporated prior physical knowledge (e.g., PDEs), but these too were often confined to toy settings.

The advent of **large-scale generative models**, particularly **diffusion models** (e.g., Stable Diffusion, SVD, Lumiere, Sora), has revolutionized image and video synthesis. These models, trained on massive datasets, demonstrate an astonishing ability to generate visually coherent and diverse content from minimal inputs like text prompts or single images. This success has led to a paradigm shift: instead of explicit, hand-engineered models of physics and rendering, researchers are exploring whether these large generative models implicitly learn such knowledge from data.

Within **controllable video generation**, the focus has moved beyond unconditional or simple text-to-video synthesis. Tools like ControlNet showed how to inject fine-grained control (e.g., edges, poses) into image diffusion models. Similar efforts are emerging for video, conditioning generation on trajectories, bounding boxes, or camera movements. However, a significant gap remained in generating *interactive dynamics* where a control signal for one object implicitly drives the motion of other objects in a physically plausible way.

Specifically in **human-object interaction (HOI)**, research has traditionally focused on 3D reconstruction of hands and objects, or synthesizing static hand poses and object meshes. More recently, generative models have been applied to HOI, for instance, inferring 2D hand positions or generating text-guided 3D HOI. A closely related work is CosHand, which predicts *static state transitions* – given a hand and an object, it generates a single future image based on a future hand mask.

InterDyn critically addresses the limitations of these prior approaches by asserting that static state transitions are insufficient for capturing the richness of interactive dynamics. Interactions are continuous processes, often involving forces that propagate, and consequences that extend beyond the immediate contact (e.g., an object rolling away after being pushed). The paper posits that large video generation models, having been exposed to vast amounts of real-world video, implicitly encode this "physics knowledge." InterDyn bridges these fields by developing a framework that leverages this implicit knowledge within a controllable video diffusion model to synthesize *continuous, interactive dynamics* without explicit 3D reconstruction or physics simulation, thus pushing the boundaries of physically plausible and controllable video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of InterDyn is to **synthesize realistic and controllable videos of interactive dynamics** from a single input image and a dynamic control signal, without relying on traditional 3D reconstruction and explicit physics simulations.

The core motivations behind this objective are:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Static State Transitions and Continuous Dynamics**: Existing generative approaches for interaction often focus on predicting a single future state (e.g., an image of a hand and object at a later time). The authors argue that this is insufficient because real-world interactions involve continuous processes where dynamics can extend long after the initial intervention, impacting multiple objects through force propagation. InterDyn aims to model these continuous, temporal dynamics.
2. **Leveraging Implicit Physics Knowledge in Large Generative Models**: The fundamental hypothesis is that large video generation models, trained on immense datasets of diverse videos, implicitly learn a sophisticated "physics knowledge" or "dynamics prior." This means they can act as both neural renderers and implicit physics simulators, having observed how objects interact in real-world scenarios. The goal is to harness this latent capability.
3. **Generalizing to Complex Real-World Environments**: Traditional approaches involving explicit 3D reconstruction and physics engines are notoriously difficult to generalize from simplified, synthetic environments to complex, varied real-world scenarios due to their reliance on precise models and computational intensity. InterDyn seeks a more robust, data-driven approach that can generalize better.
4. **Enabling Controllability for Interactive Processes**: While video generation has advanced, fine-grained control over specific object interactions, especially with respect to causing consequential dynamics, remains a challenge. InterDyn aims to provide an intuitive control mechanism (e.g., hand masks) that drives these interactive dynamics.
5. **Efficiency and Simplicity**: By sidestepping explicit 3D modeling and physics simulations, InterDyn seeks a simpler, potentially more efficient, and less error-prone method for generating physically plausible interactions.

In essence, InterDyn is motivated by the desire to endow AI systems with an intuitive understanding of physical interactions, akin to human intuition, but through the powerful, data-driven learning capabilities of modern video diffusion models rather than traditional symbolic or explicit physics engines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InterDyn's methodology revolves around extending a pre-trained, large-scale video diffusion model with a dedicated control mechanism.

**Core Framework: Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) with ControlNet-like Branch**
1. **Foundation Model**: The framework builds upon **Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)**, a powerful U-Net-based latent diffusion model. SVD extends Stable Diffusion 2.1 for video generation by incorporating temporal layers, allowing it to generate video sequences from a static input image. Crucially, InterDyn **freezes the weights of the SVD backbone**. This preserves SVD's extensive learned dynamics prior and generation capabilities, avoiding catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning.
2. **Control Mechanism (ControlNet-like Branch)**: To enable interactive control, InterDyn introduces an additional, **trainable control branch**, conceptually similar to ControlNet.
 - This branch is a copy of the SVD encoder.
 - It takes the control signal (e.g., a sequence of masks) as input.
 - The encoded control signal is then combined with the noisy input latent and passed to the SVD's frozen decoder via skip connections.
 - To ensure the control branch doesn't disrupt the pre-trained SVD weights initially, it's modulated by **zero-initialized convolutions**. This ensures that at the beginning of training, the control branch has no effect, gradually learning to incorporate the control signal.
 - The control branch is designed to be "temporal-aware," processing the control signal across frames, which helps in generating temporally consistent dynamics and robustness to noisy inputs.

**Control Signal**
- **Primary Signal**: Binary masks are chosen as the main control signal due to their accessibility and ease of generation (e.g., using Segment Anything 2 (SAM2)).
- **Flexibility**: The method is designed to be extendable to other types of control signals. Ablation studies (in the supplementary material) investigate the impact of different signals like hand joint skeletons or colored mesh renderings, as well as the presence of object contours within hand masks.

**Training and Inference**
1. **Training Procedure**: The control branch is fine-tuned while the SVD backbone remains frozen.
 - **Optimizer**: Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵.
 - **Noise Schedule**: EDM framework for noise distribution.
 - **Data Augmentation**: Random dropping of the input image (5% probability) for classifier-free guidance.
 - **Hardware**: Two 80GB H100 GPUs.
 - **Temporal Resolution**: Videos are subsampled to 7 FPS to balance short- and long-range dynamics.
2. **Inference**: Given an input image, a sequence of control signals (e.g., masks for the driving object's motion), and random Gaussian noise, InterDyn iteratively applies its denoising process over 50 timesteps (using the Euler scheduler) to generate the final video sequence depicting plausible interactive dynamics.

**Datasets**
1. **CLEVRER**: A synthetic dataset of colliding objects.
 - **Purpose**: Used to probe the model's implicit physical understanding in a controlled environment, specifically to test force propagation and counterfactual reasoning.
 - **Control Signal**: Semantic masks of objects entering the scene or driving motion. Stationary objects are left uncontrolled.
2. **Something-Something-v2 (SSV2)**: A large-scale real-world human action dataset.
 - **Purpose**: To evaluate performance on complex human-object interactions (HOI).
 - **Hand Mask Generation**: Binary hand masks are generated by prompting SAM2 with hand bounding boxes from the Something-Else dataset.
 - **Filtering**: Excluded "pretending" and "behind something" classes to avoid ambiguity or out-of-view objects. Trained at 256x256 and 256x384 resolutions.

**Evaluation Metrics**
- **Image Quality**: SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, FID, KID (frame-wise).
- **Spatio-Temporal Similarity**: FVD, KVD (to assess perceptual similarity of video distributions).
- **Motion Fidelity (MF)**: An adapted metric based on point-tracking trajectories. It uses CoTracker3 to track 100 points on an object in both ground truth and generated videos, then measures the correlation between these trajectories. This is crucial as explicit pixel alignment isn't guaranteed for uncontrolled objects.

This comprehensive methodology allows InterDyn to effectively learn and leverage implicit physical knowledge from video data, enabling controllable and temporally consistent generation of complex interactive dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InterDyn's performance was evaluated across synthetic and real-world scenarios, demonstrating its ability to generate plausible interactive dynamics.

#### 4.1. Probing Dynamics with Object Collision Events (CLEVRER Dataset)

- **Force Propagation**: InterDyn successfully generated realistic force propagation. When a controlled object collided with an uncontrolled object, and that uncontrolled object subsequently collided with others, InterDyn accurately depicted the sequential movements and trajectory changes of all affected objects (both directly and indirectly impacted). This indicates an implicit understanding of physical causality and force transfer.
- **Counterfactual Dynamics**: The model demonstrated the ability to produce distinct, physically plausible future scenarios for the same initial image simply by altering the driving control signal. For instance, removing a 'driving' brown cylinder in an experiment led to an uncontrolled red sphere continuing on its original trajectory, consistent with expectations. This highlights InterDyn's causal reasoning capabilities without explicit simulation.

These qualitative results on CLEVRER underscore InterDyn's capacity to model interactive dynamics within a simplified environment, effectively "probing" the physical understanding embedded in the video diffusion model.

#### 4.2. Generating Human-Object Interactions (Something-Something-v2 Dataset)

- **Quantitative Performance**: InterDyn (especially the 256x384 resolution variant, aligning with SVD's prior) achieved superior overall performance compared to baselines.
 - It significantly outperformed baselines like CosHand (both independent and auto-regressive variants), Seer, and DynamiCrafter on spatio-temporal dynamics metrics (FVD and KVD), indicating better temporal consistency and perceptual quality of the generated videos.
 - InterDyn also achieved the highest **Motion Fidelity (MF)** score, demonstrating its ability to generate realistic object motion and trajectories for uncontrolled objects following interaction.
 - While CosHand-Independent showed slightly better LPIPS for image quality, InterDyn still achieved very competitive results across SSIM and PSNR, indicating high visual quality. The minor trade-off in some image metrics is attributed to InterDyn's frozen SVD backbone, which prioritizes dynamics, versus CosHand's fine-tuning on SSV2 for static image transitions.
- **Temporal Coherence and Post-Interaction Dynamics**: A critical advantage of InterDyn was its ability to capture continuous dynamics, particularly **post-interaction effects**. Unlike CosHand, which struggled when objects continued moving after direct hand contact was lost (e.g., rolling, falling), InterDyn successfully depicted these nuanced, temporally coherent behaviors.
- **Robustness to Noisy Control Signals**: Despite using potentially coarse or noisy hand masks generated by SAM2 (especially in frames with motion blur), InterDyn was able to generate detailed and plausible hands, including individual fingers, alongside realistic object dynamics. This suggests the model's inherent capacity to interpret and infer details beyond the immediate control signal.
- **Diverse Qualitative Examples**: InterDyn showcased impressive versatility in handling various complex human-object interactions:
 - Generating articulated motion of objects.
 - Simulating fluid dynamics, such as water being poured into a glass with a rising water level.
 - Depicting falling objects that move out of frame and then realistically roll back in, including motion blur.
 - Accurately showing the compression and restoration of deformable objects like rubber or springs during squeezing actions.
 - Handling interactions with reflective objects and exhibiting an understanding of physical size and distance to the camera.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Control Signal)

- An ablation study on the DexYCB dataset explored different control signals (binary masks, joint skeletons, colored meshes) and the impact of object occlusion within hand masks.
- **Key Finding**: The study revealed that neither the type of control signal nor the presence of object contours in hand masks had a significant impact on image quality, spatio-temporal dynamics, or motion fidelity. This suggests that the video generation model's **implicit understanding of interactive dynamics** is robust enough to infer complex motions even from simple control signals like binary masks, reducing the need for highly detailed or explicit 3D controls.

#### 4.4. Limitations

- InterDyn performs best on relatively straightforward translational dynamics (moving objects up/down, towards/away from the camera, dropping, picking up).
- It struggles with more complex non-translational interactions such as throwing an object, burying an object, or poking a complex stack of objects, particularly for underrepresented classes in the training data.
- Performance is also noted to be weaker in scenarios where depth information is ambiguous in the initial input image.

Overall, the findings demonstrate InterDyn's strong capability in generating realistic and controllable interactive dynamics by implicitly leveraging the physics knowledge within large video diffusion models, significantly outperforming existing baselines in capturing continuous temporal processes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InterDyn represents a significant step forward in the field of physically plausible video generation and AI's understanding of dynamics, with potential impacts across several domains:

1. **Redefining "Physics Engines"**: The most significant impact is InterDyn's demonstration that large video generative models can function as **implicit physics engines** and neural renderers, effectively sidestepping the need for explicit 3D reconstruction and traditional physics simulations. This paradigm shift could simplify the development of AI systems that reason about and generate dynamic scenes, as it moves from complex, hand-engineered physical laws to learning these dynamics directly from observational data.
2. **Advancement in Controllable Video Synthesis**: InterDyn provides a highly effective framework for **controllable interactive dynamics**, moving beyond mere image/video generation to purposeful manipulation of scene elements. The ability to dictate the motion of a "driving object" and have the model realistically predict consequential dynamics of other objects marks a crucial advancement in generating temporally coherent and physically plausible visual content.
3. **Enhanced Human-Object Interaction (HOI) Modeling**: By accurately capturing continuous HOI dynamics, including post-interaction effects, InterDyn offers a more comprehensive approach than previous methods that focused on static state transitions. This could lead to more realistic and versatile applications in human-robot collaboration, virtual assistants, and human-computer interfaces.
4. **Applications in Robotics and Simulation**:
 - **Synthetic Data Generation**: InterDyn can generate vast amounts of realistic video data depicting object interactions, which is invaluable for training and testing robotic manipulation policies without requiring expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection.
 - **Virtual Prototyping and Testing**: Robots or virtual agents could be tested in diverse, physically plausible scenarios generated by InterDyn, allowing for rapid iteration and evaluation of control strategies.
5. **New Avenues for Content Creation**: Animators and content creators could leverage InterDyn to generate complex interactive scenes with reduced manual effort. Instead of laboriously keyframing every object's motion and interaction, they could define driving motions, and the AI would automatically generate the physically consistent consequences. This could democratize high-quality animation and visual effects.
6. **Probing AI's World Understanding**: The success of InterDyn in demonstrating force propagation and counterfactual reasoning highlights the depth of implicit knowledge encoded within large foundation models. This opens new research avenues to systematically "probe" and understand the extent of this latent physical and causal understanding, contributing to the development of more generally intelligent AI systems that possess a robust "world model."

While limitations exist, particularly with very complex non-translational forces and ambiguous depth, InterDyn lays a strong foundation. Its success points towards a future where AI systems can intuitively understand and generate the physics of our world directly from data, rather than through explicit programming, paving the way for more sophisticated and embodied artificial intelligence.
