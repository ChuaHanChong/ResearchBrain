# IRef-VLA: A Benchmark for Interactive Referential Grounding with Imperfect Language in 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> IRef-VLA introduces a large-scale benchmark and a novel task formulation for referential grounding in 3D scenes, explicitly addressing imperfect human language by enabling models to detect non-existent objects and suggest valid alternatives. The benchmark, derived from multiple real-world 3D scans, facilitates improved zero-shot generalization for existing models and demonstrates high accuracy for a graph-search baseline in identifying object existence and generating alternative suggestions.

## Problem

- Current 3D referential grounding benchmarks primarily assume perfect language and object existence, which is unrealistic for real-world human-robot interaction.
- Existing 3D vision-language datasets often lack the scale, diversity, naturalness of language, and detailed spatial reasoning (e.g., dense scene graphs) needed for robust embodied agents in complex 3D scenes.
- Models struggle with open-vocabulary input, language ambiguities, and generalizing across diverse 3D environments, often relying on limited 2D or human-annotated 3D data.

## Method

- Created **IRef-VLA**, a large-scale, synthetically generated benchmark dataset of 7,635 3D scenes with over 4.7 million referential statements, derived from multiple real-world 3D scan datasets.
- The dataset includes dense scene graphs with eight types of spatial relations and "imperfect" referential statements, which describe non-existent objects by altering attributes in existing true statements.
- Defined a novel task: "referential grounding with imperfect references," requiring models to detect non-existent objects and suggest valid alternatives, moving towards interactive clarification.

## Results

- 3D-VisTA trained on the full IRef-VLA dataset achieved **65.8%** accuracy on Sr3D and **44.9%** on Nr3D (ReferIt3D test sets), demonstrating improved zero-shot transfer compared to models trained on smaller datasets.
- For the imperfect references task, an augmented MVT model achieved an F1-Score of **78.3%** for object existence, while a graph-search baseline showed higher performance with an F1-Score of **94.4%**.
- The graph-search baseline generated alternative suggestions with an average similarity score of **61%** to the original imperfect statement, indicating relevant matches in class, attributes, and relations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating a large volume of heuristically-generated, view-independent referential statements and dense scene graphs significantly improves the zero-shot generalization capabilities of 3D vision-language models to external benchmarks.
> - Structured scene graph representations, when combined with LLM parsing, provide a robust mechanism for effectively determining object existence and generating contextually relevant alternative suggestions for imperfect language.
> - The IRef-VLA benchmark, with its diverse scene distributions and challenging imperfect language scenarios, drives the development of models with more robust 3D spatial reasoning and semantic understanding for interactive embodied AI.
