# IRef-VLA: A Benchmark for Interactive Referential Grounding with Imperfect Language in 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> IRef-VLA introduces a large-scale benchmark and a novel task formulation for referential grounding in 3D scenes, explicitly addressing imperfect human language by enabling models to detect non-existent objects and suggest valid alternatives. The benchmark, derived from multiple real-world 3D scans, facilitates improved zero-shot generalization for existing models and demonstrates high accuracy for a graph-search baseline in identifying object existence and generating alternative suggestions.

## Problem

- Current 3D referential grounding benchmarks primarily assume perfect language and object existence, which is unrealistic for real-world human-robot interaction.
- Existing 3D vision-language datasets often lack the scale, diversity, naturalness of language, and detailed spatial reasoning (e.g., dense scene graphs) needed for robust embodied agents in complex 3D scenes.
- Models struggle with open-vocabulary input, language ambiguities, and generalizing across diverse 3D environments, often relying on limited 2D or human-annotated 3D data.

## Method

- Created **IRef-VLA**, a large-scale, synthetically generated benchmark dataset of 7,635 3D scenes with over 4.7 million referential statements, derived from multiple real-world 3D scan datasets.
- The dataset includes dense scene graphs with eight types of spatial relations and "imperfect" referential statements, which describe non-existent objects by altering attributes in existing true statements.
- Defined a novel task: "referential grounding with imperfect references," requiring models to detect non-existent objects and suggest valid alternatives, moving towards interactive clarification.

## Results

- 3D-VisTA trained on the full IRef-VLA dataset achieved **65.8%** accuracy on Sr3D and **44.9%** on Nr3D (ReferIt3D test sets), demonstrating improved zero-shot transfer compared to models trained on smaller datasets.
- For the imperfect references task, an augmented MVT model achieved an F1-Score of **78.3%** for object existence, while a graph-search baseline showed higher performance with an F1-Score of **94.4%**.
- The graph-search baseline generated alternative suggestions with an average similarity score of **61%** to the original imperfect statement, indicating relevant matches in class, attributes, and relations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating a large volume of heuristically-generated, view-independent referential statements and dense scene graphs significantly improves the zero-shot generalization capabilities of 3D vision-language models to external benchmarks.
> - Structured scene graph representations, when combined with LLM parsing, provide a robust mechanism for effectively determining object existence and generating contextually relevant alternative suggestions for imperfect language.
> - The IRef-VLA benchmark, with its diverse scene distributions and challenging imperfect language scenarios, drives the development of models with more robust 3D spatial reasoning and semantic understanding for interactive embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the evolving field of multimodal artificial intelligence, particularly at the intersection of vision-language models (VLMs), large language models (LLMs), and embodied intelligence in 3D environments. Recent advancements in LLMs and VLMs, primarily trained on internet-scale 2D data, have shown capabilities in tasks such as Visual Question Answering, image retrieval, and image captioning. However, applying these models to 3D spaces for tasks like robotic navigation and human-robot interaction presents distinct challenges.

Current research in 3D vision-language grounding aims to enable agents to understand natural language instructions and ground them to the physical world, a crucial skill for interactive robotics. Existing 3D referential grounding datasets, such as ReferIt3D and ScanRefer, have established benchmarks for identifying objects based on natural language descriptions. While some datasets have attempted to scale up (e.g., SceneVerse, SpatialRGPT), they often face limitations related to the naturalness of synthetically generated language, the detail of object attributes, or the direct inclusion of 3D data. Challenges persist in dealing with scenes containing numerous similar objects, fine-grained object classes, and the complex spatial reasoning inherent in human referential language. Furthermore, human language can be imperfect, ambiguous, or refer to non-existent entities, a scenario largely unaddressed by prior benchmarks which typically assume a perfect match between reference and scene objects.

Semantic scene graph datasets (e.g., 3DSSG, Hydra, HOV-SG, ConceptGraphs) have explored representing 3D scenes with structured relationships, but their focus has often been on automatic graph generation or object localization via region references rather than fine-grained inter-object relations crucial for precise referential grounding. Instruction-following datasets (e.g., Room Across Room, Room-2-Room) address navigation but often simplify object disambiguation, making them less robust to scene changes or imperfect language.

Current referential object grounding models, despite progress and increased data scale, still exhibit limitations in accuracy, robustness, and generalization, particularly when facing open-vocabulary input or language ambiguities. While some work has explored interactive visual grounding and ambiguity resolution, these efforts are often confined to 2D images or limited human-annotated data, lacking the scale and 3D complexity required for robust embodied agents. This paper aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a large-scale 3D benchmark that explicitly incorporates scene graphs and imperfect language scenarios, moving towards more interactive and robust 3D navigation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of robust, interactive navigation systems by introducing a new benchmark dataset, IRef-VLA, for **Interactive Referential Vision and Language-guided Action in 3D Scenes with imperfect references**. The paper aims to address several specific challenges and limitations identified in the existing research landscape:

1. **Addressing Imperfect Language:** A central motivation is the recognition that human natural language instructions for robots are often imperfect, ambiguous, or may refer to objects that do not exist in the scene. Current referential grounding benchmarks predominantly assume perfect language and object existence, which is unrealistic for real-world deployment. The paper seeks to enable models to detect when a referenced object is not present and to suggest valid alternatives, thus defining a novel task formulation: "referential grounding with imperfect references."

2. **Enhancing 3D Spatial Reasoning and Semantic Understanding:** Real-world 3D scenes are complex, featuring numerous objects, fine-grained classes, and spatial relationships. The existing 3D datasets are often limited in scale or the naturalness and comprehensiveness of their language annotations, hindering the development of models capable of deep 3D spatial reasoning and semantic understanding. IRef-VLA aims to provide a large-scale, diverse dataset with rich annotations, including semantic object labels, attributes, and particularly, dense scene graphs with eight types of spatial relations, to facilitate this.

3. **Scaling 3D Vision-Language Data:** The success of 2D vision-language models has been significantly attributed to internet-scale data. However, the availability of large-scale, high-quality 3D vision-language data is comparatively limited. The motivation behind IRef-VLA is to curate the largest real-world dataset for referential grounding, combining scans from multiple existing datasets and synthetically generating referential statements and scene graphs at scale. This aims to provide the necessary data volume for training more generalizable and robust 3D vision-language models.

4. **Enabling Interactive Robotics:** The ultimate goal is to foster the development of embodied agents that can interact with humans to clarify uncertainties and achieve navigation goals. The proposed task of handling imperfect references and suggesting alternatives is formulated as an initial step towards multi-turn interactive grounding, moving beyond single-shot object retrieval. By providing traversable free space annotations, the dataset also supports references to areas, expanding navigation capabilities beyond just objects.

In summary, the key objective is to provide a comprehensive, large-scale benchmark that more closely reflects the complexities of human language and real-world 3D environments, specifically focusing on the challenges of imperfect referential language and the need for interactive clarification in embodied AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the creation of the IRef-VLA dataset and the establishment of baseline evaluations.

#### 3.1. Dataset Creation (IRef-VLA)

IRef-VLA is a synthetically-generated benchmark dataset derived from five real-world 3D scan datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D, Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D), 3RScan, ARKitScenes) and additional Unity-generated scenes. The dataset comprises 7,635 scenes, over 11.5K regions, 286K objects across 477 classes, 7.6 million inter-object spatial relations, and 4.7 million referential statements. Each scene is annotated with specific components:
- Scene point cloud
- Object list with semantic class labels, bounding boxes, and colors
- Traversable free spaces
- Region list with semantic labels and bounding boxes
- Scene graph of spatial relations (per room)
- Language statements with ground-truth annotations

#### 3.1.1. 3D Scan Processing

- **Point Cloud Generation:** Point clouds for ARKitScenes, Matterport3D, and ScanNet were sourced from PLY files. For HM3D, Unity, and 3RScan, point clouds were uniformly sampled from meshes, with colors derived from textures.
- **Object and Region Identification:** Semantic information from original meshes was used to identify regions and objects. ARKitScenes, 3RScan, and ScanNet typically feature single-room scenes, while Matterport3D and HM3D provide pre-segmented regions. Unity scenes were custom-segmented.
- **Semantic Labeling:** Objects received open-vocabulary class names, which were mapped to NYU40 and NYUv2 schemas. Dominant colors for each object were determined using a color clustering algorithm on its point cloud.
- **Traversable Free Space:** Each scan was processed to identify horizontally traversable free spaces. These separate regions were combined into sub-regions, and spatial relations were generated between these spaces and other objects to enable unambiguous references (e.g., "the space near the table").

#### 3.1.2. Scene Graph Generation

A scene graph of relations was heuristically generated for each scene based on yawed object bounding boxes. Eight types of semantic spatial relations were calculated exhaustively for every valid pair or triple of objects within a region. These relations were then filtered based on the semantic classes involved. The relation types include: Above, Below, Closest, Farthest, Between, Near, In, and On. Each relation type has specific definitions and associated properties (e.g., symmetric for "Near," ternary for "Between," contact-based for "On").

#### 3.1.3. Language Generation

Referential statements were synthetically generated using a template-based method based on the computed scene graphs.
- **Statement Variety:** Synonyms for each relation type were incorporated to enhance linguistic diversity.
- **Statement Properties:** Generated statements adhere to three principles:
 - **View-independent:** The target object's relation predicate does not depend on the observer's viewpoint.
 - **Unambiguous:** Only one possible target object exists for a given statement within a region.
 - **Minimal:** Statements follow Grice’s maxim of manner, using the fewest descriptors necessary to clearly disambiguate the target object.
- **Imperfect Statements:** To simulate real-world language imperfections, the dataset includes "imperfect" referential statements that describe non-existent objects. These were generated by altering one target or anchor object attribute in existing true statements to similar, but incorrect, values. This ensures contextual similarity to true statements while creating a false reference.

#### 3.2. Baseline Evaluation

The benchmark was evaluated on two distinct tasks: the original referential object grounding task and the extended referential grounding with imperfect references task.

#### 3.2.1. Referential Object Grounding

- **Models:** Two state-of-the-art supervised referential grounding models, MVT and 3D-VisTA, were used as baselines.
- **Training and Testing:** Models were trained on IRef-VLA data. Two training splits were used: one on the ScanNet scenes within IRef-VLA, and another on the full IRef-VLA dataset. The training involved official ScanNet/ReferIt3D splits for ScanNet data and an 80% train, 20% validation split for other scenes.
- **Generalizability Test:** Zero-shot transfer capabilities were evaluated by testing models trained on IRef-VLA directly on the Sr3D and Nr3D test sets from ReferIt3D, which consist of synthetically generated and human-uttered statements, respectively. Performance on IRef-VLA validation sets was also measured.

#### 3.2.2. Referential Grounding with Imperfect References

This task involves two subtasks: 1) identifying object existence and 2) suggesting alternatives when the object is not found. Evaluation was performed on a dedicated split of IRef-VLA corresponding to the ReferIt3D test split.
- **Metrics:** Binary classification metrics (True Positive, True Negative, False Positive, False Negative, F1-Score) were used for object existence. For alternative suggestions, a heuristic similarity score (`score_sim`) was calculated. This score measures the match between aspects (class, attributes, relations) of the suggested alternative and the original imperfect statement, with class and relation weighted higher.
- **Augmented SOTA Models:** Existing SOTA referential grounding models (specifically MVT) were augmented with a binary classification head. This head, consisting of a two-layer MLP, takes concatenated object features and predicts object existence. It was trained with a cross-entropy loss, with referential grounding losses applied only if the object truly exists.
- **Graph Search Baseline:** A graph-search method was implemented to demonstrate robustness for imperfect references and alternative generation.
 - **LLM Parsing:** GPT-4o-mini was used with few-shot prompting (five training samples) to parse input statements into a scene subgraph representation (target object, anchor objects, attributes, and relation in JSON format).
 - **Subgraph Search:** A breadth-first search was employed to find candidate target nodes, followed by depth-first searches to locate the complete referenced subgraph within the heuristically-generated scene graph.
 - **Alternative Generation:** If the exact subgraph was not found, the method extracted existing referential statements corresponding to partial subgraph matches. An LLM was then prompted in a multiple-choice question-answer (MCQA) style to select the statement closest to the input, offering alternative suggestions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IRef-VLA involved benchmarking state-of-the-art models on referential object grounding and introducing a new task for grounding with imperfect references.

#### 4.1. Referential Object Grounding Generalizability

The generalizability of models trained on IRef-VLA was assessed by evaluating their zero-shot transfer capabilities to the Sr3D and Nr3D test sets from the ReferIt3D benchmark, as well as on IRef-VLA's own validation splits.

- **Zero-shot Transfer Performance:**
 - **MVT:** When trained solely on the ScanNet portion of IRef-VLA, MVT achieved 50.0% accuracy on Sr3D and 29.7% on Nr3D. Training MVT on the full IRef-VLA dataset resulted in 41.0% on Sr3D and 25.9% on Nr3D. Compared to MVT trained on Sr3D (reproduced results: 59% on Sr3D, 31.8% on Nr3D), IRef-VLA (ScanNet) showed competitive performance on Nr3D despite not being trained on view-dependent statements.
 - **3D-VisTA:** When trained on the ScanNet portion of IRef-VLA, 3D-VisTA achieved 62.4% accuracy on Sr3D and 41.8% on Nr3D. Training on the full IRef-VLA dataset improved these to 65.8% on Sr3D and 44.9% on Nr3D. The baseline 3D-VisTA trained on Sr3D (reproduced results: 75.7% on Sr3D, 46% on Nr3D) generally performed better on Sr3D due to direct training on that data distribution.
- **Impact of Dataset Scale and Heuristics:**
 - The performance on Nr3D, particularly for 3D-VisTA, showed that training on IRef-VLA's view-independent statements still yielded strong zero-shot transfer, with a minimal gap (1.1% for 3D-VisTA trained on full IRef-VLA vs. Sr3D baseline) compared to models trained directly on Sr3D. This indicates that the pipeline for synthetically upscaling referential statements and introducing new relations in IRef-VLA enhances zero-shot capabilities.
 - Increasing the number of training scenes within IRef-VLA (from ScanNet-only to Full) generally improved 3D-VisTA's grounding performance across all test sets (e.g., IRef-VLA ScanNet split from 63.7% to 70.8%, Sr3D from 62.4% to 65.8%, Nr3D from 41.8% to 44.9%). MVT, being a smaller model, showed some underfitting.
 - 3D-VisTA trained on the full IRef-VLA data outperformed 3D-VisTA trained on SceneVerse zero-shot text (44.9% vs. 43.1% on Nr3D), suggesting that IRef-VLA's heuristic-driven statement generation produces more effective referential language for upscaling.
- **Benchmark Difficulty:** Both pre-trained baseline models (MVT and 3D-VisTA) exhibited significantly lower accuracy when generalizing to the IRef-VLA validation sets (MVT: 29%/17.2% on ScanNet/Full IRef-VLA; 3D-VisTA: 39.2%/24.8%). This highlights the challenge presented by the IRef-VLA benchmark, which incorporates diverse language and scene distributions, pushing models toward better generalization.

#### 4.2. Referential Grounding with Imperfect References

This section evaluated the ability to identify object existence and suggest alternatives.

- **LLM Parsing Accuracy:** The graph-search baseline's initial step, parsing input statements into scene subgraph representations using GPT-4o-mini, achieved a parsing accuracy of 94.0%. This metric directly influences the upper bound of downstream grounding and alternative scoring.
- **Object Existence Classification:**
 - **MVT + Binary Classifier:** This augmented SOTA model achieved a True Positive rate of 66.2% and a True Negative rate of 97.1%, with an F1-Score of 78.3%. This indicates a good ability to detect non-existent objects but a moderate success rate in confirming existing ones.
 - **Graph-Search Baseline:** This method demonstrated higher performance, with a True Positive rate of 90.4% and a True Negative rate of 98.9%, leading to an F1-Score of 94.4%. This suggests that when ground-truth calculated scene graphs are available, a structured search method can effectively determine object existence.
- **Alternative Generation Quality:** The graph-search baseline, when suggesting alternatives using its heuristic scoring system, achieved an average alternative similarity score of 61%. This score reflects that, on average, over half of the relevant aspects (class, attributes, relations) from the original statement are matched by the suggested alternatives, even without direct visual input. This provides a quantitative lower bound for alternative selection methods.

The results collectively indicate that the IRef-VLA dataset provides a robust platform for developing and evaluating 3D vision-language models. The benchmark challenges models not only in traditional referential grounding but also in explicitly handling imperfect language and providing interactive suggestions, which are critical for real-world robotic applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IRef-VLA benchmark dataset and its associated task formulation represent a significant contribution to the fields of 3D vision-language understanding, embodied AI, and interactive robotics.

#### 5.1. Advancing Robust 3D Scene Understanding

The dataset's scale, diversity of real-world 3D scenes, and rich annotations (point clouds, object attributes, traversable free spaces, and dense scene graphs) provide a comprehensive resource for training and evaluating models in complex 3D environments. By integrating structured scene graphs, IRef-VLA provides explicit spatial relational knowledge, which is crucial for deep semantic understanding and distinguishing between similar objects. This foundation can lead to more accurate and nuanced 3D scene comprehension in robotic agents.

#### 5.2. Enabling Interactive Robotic Navigation with Imperfect Language

The most notable impact of IRef-VLA lies in its novel formulation of "referential grounding with imperfect references." This task explicitly addresses the common real-world scenario where human instructions are ambiguous, contain errors, or refer to non-existent objects. The benchmark encourages the development of models that can not only detect such imperfections but also generate valid alternative suggestions. This capability is foundational for truly interactive robotic systems, allowing them to engage in multi-turn dialogues with human users to clarify intentions and robustly achieve navigation goals, moving beyond single-shot, perfect-match assumptions.

#### 5.3. Improving Model Generalizability and Real-world Deployment

The baseline evaluations demonstrate that models trained on IRef-VLA exhibit improved zero-shot generalization capabilities to external benchmarks like Nr3D, particularly with increased training data. This indicates that IRef-VLA's diverse language and scene distributions contribute to the development of more robust and transferable grounding models. The explicit challenge of the IRef-VLA validation sets highlights the benchmark's difficulty, pushing research towards agents that are less susceptible to domain shifts and more reliable in varied real-world settings. This directly supports the path towards deploying generalizable robotics agents that can operate reliably in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

#### 5.4. Providing a Comprehensive Research Resource

The public release of the dataset, source code for generation and baselines, and a visualization tool lowers the barrier for entry for researchers. This fosters collaborative development and benchmarking within the community, accelerating progress in 3D vision-language research. The heuristic scoring system for alternative suggestions provides an initial quantitative metric, while also acknowledging the need for future human-centric evaluations, guiding further research in this complex area.

In conclusion, IRef-VLA is poised to significantly impact the development of embodied intelligent agents by providing a realistic and challenging benchmark that addresses critical gaps in current 3D vision-language understanding, particularly regarding the robustness to imperfect human language and the necessity for interactive capabilities in real-world robotic applications.
