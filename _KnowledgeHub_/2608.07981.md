# Distilling Physical Priors into Streaming World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyS is a three-stage recipe for physically plausible streaming video world models: physics-aware supervised fine-tuning of a bidirectional 14B DiT on PhyS-120K, 120,804 annotated real-world physical-interaction clips, DMD distillation into a few-step 1.3B causal generator, then online RL with Temporal Credit Routing, which routes overlapping-window physical rewards to aligned denoising actions. PhysicsIQ gains reach 31.4% over causal baselines.

## Problem

- Generic bidirectional video teachers absorb only weak physical priors, since large-scale pretraining optimizes visual fidelity rather than real-world dynamics or causal state evolution.
- Bidirectional-to-causal distillation loses those already thin priors, so causal students drop long-range dependencies and accumulate physical violations across autoregressive rollouts.
- Prior physics video datasets rely on synthetic scenes, generated prompts, or narrow controlled motion, and reward signals stay video-level, hiding where a violation occurs.

## Method

- **PhyS**'s ==PhyS-120K==: process-centric pipeline collecting 120,804 real clips across fluid, soft-body, phase-transition, rigid-body categories, annotated by Qwen3-VL with object properties and causal event chains.
- Three-stage training: ==LoRA== flow-matching physics-aware SFT of a Wan2.1-14B bidirectional teacher, then ==DMD distillation== with self-rollout into a few-step 1.3B causal DiT student.
- ==Temporal Credit Routing==: window-level ==V-JEPA2== consistency plus ==RAFT== optical-flow reward, per-window group normalization, and overlap-weighted routing of advantages to each block's denoising transitions.

## Results

- Physics-aware SFT lifts the 14B teacher on PhysicsIQ from **26.9** to **31.8** (**+18.2%**), VideoPhy PC **+16.0%**, VideoPhy2 joint **+11.1%**, PhyGenBench average **0.399** to **0.473**.
- Full PhyS improves Self, Rolling, and Causal Forcing on PhysicsIQ by **23.7%**, **14.8%**, **31.4%** at unchanged **4-5** NFE, beating Flow-GRPO by **8.8-17.5%**.
- Ablations: full structured annotations beat description-only on the same **80K** clips (PhysicsIQ **31.4** vs **27.3**); V-JEPA2 reward tops human-agreement at **0.65**; overlap routing beats global and uniform.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured annotations of properties, ordered state changes, and causal links, not just more physics video, are what convert data into transferable physical priors.
> - Video-level RL rewards are too coarse for localized physical violations; routing overlapping-window advantages to temporally aligned denoising actions sharpens credit assignment.
> - Priors transfer unevenly through causal distillation: categories thin in the training data, like optics and materials, still regress despite overall gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of generative artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on video generation models and their application in constructing "streaming world models." Recent advancements in video generation have enabled the synthesis of videos with high visual fidelity and temporal coherence, largely due to large-scale video pre-training. These developments have laid a foundation for world models that can predict future visual states.

Current streaming world models often employ autoregressive (AR) approaches, which generate future frames block by block, conditioning each block on previously generated history. This causal formulation facilitates continuous streaming rollouts. A common training paradigm for these models involves distilling a pretrained bidirectional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) into a few-step causal generator, often referred to as "Forcing-based" methods.

However, a notable limitation in this landscape is the tendency of these models to produce predictions that violate fundamental physical constraints, causal relationships, and commonsense world knowledge, particularly during long-horizon rollouts. This issue stems from two primary factors:
1. **Limited Physical Priors in Teachers:** Generic bidirectional teachers, trained primarily for visual aesthetics and temporal coherence, acquire restricted physical priors during their visually-oriented pretraining.
2. **Loss During Distillation:** The limited physical priors that are acquired often suffer further degradation during the distillation process from a bidirectional teacher to a causal student model. This makes it challenging for the causal student to retain the long-range dependencies essential for physical causality.

The accumulation of such errors in autoregressive generation exacerbates physical and causal deviations over extended prediction horizons. This paper aims to address these identified limitations by enhancing the physical plausibility and causal consistency of streaming world models, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and physically-aware generative models for dynamic world understanding and simulation. It contrasts with approaches that rely on explicit physical solvers or language-guided global signals by focusing on internalizing physical knowledge through data and targeted optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, named PhyS, that enables streaming world models to predict future visual states with improved physical coherence and plausibility over long horizons. This overarching goal is motivated by the significant deficiencies observed in existing streaming world models, which frequently generate physically implausible or causally inconsistent video sequences.

The specific objectives that contribute to this main goal are:

1. **Acquire Comprehensive Physical Priors:** A key objective is to address the limitation of generic video generation models having insufficient physical understanding. The authors aim to collect and curate a large-scale dataset of real-world physical interactions to serve as a rich source of physically-grounded data. This dataset is intended to provide fine-grained supervisory signals for learning how physical dynamics evolve across various real-world scenarios, thereby enabling a teacher model to internalize robust physical priors.
2. **Efficient Transfer of Physical Priors:** Another objective is to effectively transfer the acquired physical priors from a high-capacity bidirectional teacher model to a lightweight, few-step causal generator. This transfer is critical for maintaining the efficiency required for streaming generation while ensuring that the physical understanding is preserved as much as possible, mitigating the loss observed in conventional bidirectional-to-causal distillation processes.
3. **Develop a Targeted Online Optimization Strategy:** The authors aim to refine the causal generator's behavior during online generation by providing physically-aware feedback. The motivation here is that global, video-level rewards are often too coarse to pinpoint specific physical violations, especially in long sequences. Therefore, an objective is to develop a mechanism that localizes feedback signals to specific temporal windows and aligns these signals with the corresponding denoising actions, thus enabling more precise optimization of physical consistency.

The motivation for pursuing these objectives is rooted in the current shortcomings of streaming world models for applications requiring an accurate representation of dynamic physical environments. Models that produce physically incoherent outputs are limited in their utility for tasks such as reinforcement learning, robotics, and physically-grounded simulations, where anticipating realistic future states is paramount. By improving physical plausibility, the research aims to make streaming world models more reliable and applicable for building interactive and intelligent agents that can operate within or reason about physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhyS framework is a three-stage approach designed to imbue streaming world models with physical priors and ensure physically coherent long-horizon rollouts.

#### 3.1. PhyS-120K Dataset Construction

The first step involves constructing a novel dataset, PhyS-120K, comprising 120,804 real-world physical-interaction videos.
- **Motivation:** Existing physics-related video datasets often rely on synthetic environments, generated prompts, or limited real-world diversity, which are insufficient for robustly training models on real-world physical dynamics.
- **Process-Centric Taxonomy:** The dataset organizes clips into four categories based on observable state evolution and interaction patterns: fluid dynamics (e.g., pouring, splashing), soft-body deformation (e.g., compression, stretching), phase transitions (e.g., melting, boiling), and rigid-body mechanics (e.g., collision, rolling).
- **Data Curation and Annotation Pipeline:**
 - **Collection:** Candidate videos are gathered from public platforms (YouTube, Pexels) and open-source datasets (WISA) using process-oriented queries.
 - **Segmentation:** Long videos are segmented into single-shot clips using TransNetV2.
 - **Filtering:** Multi-stage filtering is applied for motion quality (RAFT), camera artifacts, visible text, unsafe content (OpenNSFW2), clarity, and aesthetics (DOVER). Near-duplicate removal is also performed.
 - **Classification and Rebalancing:** Retained clips are classified into the four physical-process categories and rebalanced to prevent category dominance while preserving observable state evolution.
 - **Structured Annotation:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, is used to generate structured annotations. These annotations go beyond surface-level captions, describing object properties (e.g., stiffness, mass density), state changes (e.g., "flatten and deform"), and causal event sequences (e.g., "press head exerts a downward force on the soft ball, which yields..."). Human annotators randomly audit and refine these VLM-generated annotations to ensure quality and consistency with video content.
- **Training Use:** PhyS-120K serves as the source of real-world physical priors for supervised fine-tuning of the teacher model.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Physics-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

- **Objective:** To inject physical priors into a powerful bidirectional video generator.
- **Teacher Model:** A pretrained bidirectional Wan2.1-14B DiT model is used as the backbone.
- **Fine-tuning Process:** The teacher model is fine-tuned using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on the PhyS-120K dataset. The original flow-matching objective of the teacher is retained, and the model is trained on video-caption pairs from PhyS-120K, which include the structured physics-aware descriptions.
- **Outcome:** This stage yields `θ_tea`, a physics-aware bidirectional teacher model capable of generating videos with improved physical consistency.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Few-step Causal Distillation

- **Objective:** To transfer the physical priors from the physics-aware bidirectional teacher to a lightweight, few-step causal generator suitable for streaming inference.
- **Student Model:** A lightweight 1.3B causal DiT model (Wan2.1-1.3B) is used.
- **Distillation Method:** Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) (Yin et al. 2024) is employed. The student model performs an autoregressive self-rollout, where each generated block is conditioned on its own history, mirroring the inference-time causal generation.
- **Training Process:** The distillation optimizes a holistic distribution-matching objective `L_DMD(θ)`, which minimizes the KL divergence between the noised student-rollout distribution (`p_θ,τ`) and the noised teacher distribution (`p_tea,τ`). The teacher's score (`s_θtea`) is frozen, while the student's score (`s_gen,ξ`) is updated using the standard flow-matching objective.
- **Outcome:** A causal generator `G_θ` that can perform few-step autoregressive streaming generation while retaining physical priors.

#### 3.4. Stage 3: Temporal Credit Routing (TCR) for Online Policy Optimization

- **Objective:** To address the temporal coarseness of global rewards in policy optimization and further align the causal generator's behavior towards physically plausible rollouts.
- **Problem Addressed:** Physical violations are often localized events within a video, but global rewards provide a uniform signal across the entire sequence, making precise credit assignment difficult.
- **Key Operations:**
 - **Window-level Physical Feedback:**
 - For each condition (context frames `x_c` and text `c`), a group of `G` future rollouts (`{ˆx_i}`) is sampled using the current policy (`π_θold`).
 - The generated frames are covered by `J` overlapping temporal windows (`{W_j}`).
 - A frozen reward model `R_ϕ` evaluates the physical consistency of each generated window (`ˆx_i,W_j`) given its corresponding context window (`C_j`). `R_ϕ` combines a V-JEPA2 predictor (Assran et al. 2025) for future representation consistency and an optical-flow reward from RAFT (Teed and Deng 2020) to encourage motion.
 - Rewards `R_i,j` are then normalized across the `G` rollouts for each window `j` to calculate window-level advantages `A_i,j`, preventing easy or difficult temporal regions from dominating.
 - **Weighted Credit Routing:**
 - For each causal block (`B_m`), the advantages from all overlapping reward windows are aggregated.
 - Overlap weights (`O_m,j`), calculated based on the intersection ratio of `B_m` and `W_j`, are used to combine `A_i,j` into a block-level advantage (`b_A_m_i`). This `b_A_m_i` is shared by all denoising transitions generating block `B_m`.
 - **Policy Optimization:**
 - The causal generator's LoRA adapters are optimized using the stochastic transition policy of Flow-GRPO (Liu et al. 2025a).
 - The global rollout advantage is replaced with the overlap-routed advantage `b_A_m_i`.
 - A clipped surrogate objective `ℓ_TCR` (similar to PPO) and a KL penalty `K_i,m,s` (against a frozen reference policy `π_ref`) are used to update the model parameters `θ`, ensuring that policy updates are targeted and stable.

This three-stage methodology systematically addresses the challenge of physical plausibility in streaming world models, from data acquisition and teacher pre-training to distillation and fine-grained online reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings across various benchmarks and ablation studies, demonstrating the effectiveness of the PhyS framework.

#### 4.1. Injecting Physical Priors into the Bidirectional Teacher

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on PhyS-120K significantly improved the performance of the bidirectional Wan2.1-14B teacher model.
- **Quantitative Improvements (5-second generations, 40 NFE):**
 - PhysicsIQ: +18.2%
 - VideoPhy Physical Commonsense (PC): +16.0%
 - VideoPhy2 Joint Success Rate: +11.1%
 - PhyGenBench Average: +18.5%
- **Qualitative Observations:** The SFT teacher demonstrated better preservation of momentum transfer, deformation, phase separation, and melting compared to other models like WISA and VideoREPA, which often generated physically inconsistent content. These results indicate that the PhyS-120K dataset and structured annotations successfully infuse stronger real-world physical priors into the teacher model.

#### 4.2. Transferring Physical Priors to Causal Students

- Distillation from the SFT-enhanced teacher generally improved the performance of lightweight few-step causal DiTs (10-second streaming rollouts, 4-5 NFE).
- **PhysicsIQ Improvements:**
 - Self Forcing: from 16.9 to 17.4
 - Rolling Forcing: from 16.2 to 17.9
 - Causal Forcing: from 10.2 to 11.8
- **Other Metrics:** Most VideoPhy and VideoPhy2 PC and Semantic Adherence (SA) metrics also showed improvements.
- **Limitations:** The paper noted several regressions in certain metrics, suggesting that causal distillation does not uniformly preserve all physical priors, highlighting the challenge of this transfer process.

#### 4.3. Online Alignment with Physical Rewards (TCR Effectiveness)

- The full PhyS configuration, incorporating Temporal Credit Routing (TCR), consistently improved all three causal baselines without increasing their inference budget (4–5 NFE).
- **PhysicsIQ Improvements (over SFT Teacher baseline):**
 - Self Forcing: +23.7%
 - Rolling Forcing: +14.8%
 - Causal Forcing: +31.4%
- **Other Metrics:** Additional gains were observed on VideoPhy PC, VideoPhy2 Joint, and PhyGenBench Average.
- **Comparison to Flow-GRPO:** TCR variants outperformed corresponding Flow-GRPO baselines by 8.8%–17.5% on PhysicsIQ and 8.3%–39.4% on VideoPhy2 Joint. This outcome indicates that temporally localized credit assignment provides more precise optimization signals than video-level reward broadcasting.
- **Reward Model Efficacy:** The combined RAFT–V-JEPA2 reward function was found to be an effective feedback signal.
- **PhyGenBench Specifics:** Category-level gains on PhyGenBench were uneven, with some regressions for Optics and Material interactions, potentially reflecting limited coverage of these specific phenomena in PhyS-120K or their inherent difficulty in distillation.

#### 4.4. Long-Horizon Streaming Rollout Performance

- PhyS consistently improved long-horizon rollouts (15 and 20 seconds) for both Self and Rolling Forcing baselines.
- **Quantitative Improvements (VBench-Long and VideoPhy):** PhyS enhanced Temporal Quality, Dynamic Degree, Visual Stability, and VideoPhy PC and SA. For instance, at 15 seconds, Dynamic Degree increased by 6.88 and 8.14 points for Self and Rolling Forcing, respectively, and VideoPhy PC by 2.0 and 2.6 points. Similar improvements were observed at 20 seconds.
- **Qualitative Observations:** In long-horizon scenarios, baselines frequently violated physical constraints (e.g., failed momentum transfer, incorrect deformation, lack of diffusion). In contrast, PhyS maintained better physical plausibility and causal dependencies, preserving immediate physical responses and downstream state transitions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Dataset and Annotation Effectiveness:**
 - PhyS-80K with full structured annotations (object properties, state changes, causal relations) significantly outperformed generic data, WISA-80K, and PhyS-80K with only generic descriptions across VBench, PhysicsIQ, and VBench metrics. This emphasizes the value of real-world, process-centric data and rich, structured physical supervision.
- **Temporal Credit Routing and Reward Models:**
 - **Reward Model Agreement:** V-JEPA2 achieved the highest pairwise ranking agreement (0.65) with human preferences among tested reward models (VideoScore2, VideoPhy2, PhyDetEx), making it suitable for the semantic physical-consistency component.
 - **TCR Mechanism:** TCR attained higher training rewards by routing window-level feedback to corresponding denoising steps, indicating more targeted optimization.
 - **Routing Strategy:** Global routing of rewards negatively impacted performance. Uniform window-based rewards showed partial recovery, while the full TCR (Overlap-based routing) achieved the best performance across all five evaluation metrics, confirming the benefit of localized, weighted credit assignment.
 - **Reward Components:** The combination of RAFT (optical flow) and V-JEPA2 (semantic consistency) proved complementary, providing the strongest overall feedback signal.

Overall, the findings indicate that a multi-stage approach combining a novel physics-aware dataset, specialized supervised fine-tuning, and a targeted online reinforcement learning strategy with temporal credit routing can significantly enhance the physical plausibility and causal consistency of streaming world models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhyS framework and its associated findings present several areas of significance and potential impact within the domain of generative AI and beyond:

- **Improved Physical Plausibility in World Models:** The primary significance of this work lies in substantially improving the physical coherence and plausibility of long-horizon rollouts generated by streaming world models. This directly addresses a critical weakness of existing models, which often produce visually convincing but physically unrealistic videos. For applications where physical accuracy is paramount, such as embodied AI, robotics, and scientific simulations, this improvement is fundamental.
- **Novel Dataset for Physics-Aware AI:** The introduction of PhyS-120K is a notable contribution. As a large-scale, real-world physical-interaction video dataset with structured annotations of object properties and causal state transitions, it provides a unique and valuable resource. This dataset can facilitate future research and development of physics-aware AI models by offering observation-grounded dynamics supervision, bridging the gap between visually realistic generation and physically consistent prediction.
- **Advancement in Generative Model Training:** The three-stage training pipeline (Physics-Aware SFT, Few-step Causal Distillation, Temporal Credit Routing) represents a systematic approach to imbue complex physical understanding into generative models. This methodology could inspire similar multi-stage training strategies for incorporating other difficult-to-learn priors into various generative architectures.
- **Innovation in Reinforcement Learning for Generative Models:** Temporal Credit Routing (TCR) is a methodological innovation that tackles the challenging problem of temporal credit assignment in autoregressive generation. By localizing physical feedback through overlapping temporal windows and routing these rewards to specific denoising actions, TCR offers a more precise and effective optimization signal than traditional global rewards. This approach has the potential to be generalized to other domains requiring fine-grained temporal feedback in sequence generation tasks.
- **Enhanced Efficiency for Real-time Applications:** The ability of PhyS to achieve significant improvements without increasing the number of inference steps (NFE) for causal generators is crucial. This efficiency makes the physically plausible streaming rollouts suitable for real-time applications, interactive systems, and scenarios with strict latency requirements, expanding the practical utility of streaming world models.
- **Foundation for General AI and World Understanding:** By enabling models to better predict and understand physical interactions, this research contributes to building more robust and intelligent AI systems that can reason about and operate within the complex dynamics of the real world. This forms a stepping stone towards more general-purpose AI that exhibits human-like commonsense understanding of physical phenomena.

In summary, the PhyS framework, along with the PhyS-120K dataset and the TCR optimization strategy, represents a significant step forward in developing streaming world models that are not only visually compelling but also deeply grounded in the principles of physical reality, opening avenues for more reliable and intelligent AI applications.
