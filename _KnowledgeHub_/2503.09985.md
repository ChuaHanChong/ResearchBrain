# ES-Parkour: Advanced Robot Parkour with Bio-inspired Event Camera and Spiking Neural Network

> [!summary] Summary
> ES-Parkour integrates bio-inspired event cameras for robust perception and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) for energy-efficient control, enabling quadruped robots to perform challenging parkour tasks across diverse extreme lighting and high-speed environments. The system achieves an 88.3% reduction in computational energy consumption while maintaining comparable motor performance to ANN-based methods.

## Problem

- Traditional visual sensors in legged robots (e.g., depth cameras, LiDAR) suffer from low operating frequencies, sensitivity to extreme lighting conditions, and high computational demands.
- Current legged robot control systems based on deep neural networks consume significant energy, hindering operational endurance for battery-powered platforms.
- Achieving robust and agile robot locomotion in challenging, dynamic, and unstructured environments with conventional methods remains difficult.

## Method

- Integrate bio-inspired event cameras for high-frequency, robust perception across varied lighting, simulating event data generation within IsaacGym.
- Develop a ==Spiking Neural Network (SNN)== architecture, utilizing a spiking ResNet-18 vision encoder and an MLP actor, for energy-efficient control.
- Employ a distillation methodology to transfer complex reinforcement learning policies from a high-performing ANN teacher to an SNN student, minimizing output differences during training.

## Results

- The SNN-based system achieved robust parkour performance across various challenging terrains (hurdle, gap, parkour, step) and was the only tested method capable of operating in extreme lighting (overexposed, underexposed) and high-speed conditions.
- Computational operations for the SNN visual encoder were reduced to **0.46**:1 (ResNet) and **0.29**:1 (MLP) relative to their ANN counterparts.
- The approach demonstrated an **88.3%** overall reduction in theoretical computational energy consumption compared to ANN-based models, while maintaining comparable robot joint motor energy consumption for the given tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bio-inspired event cameras provide superior environmental adaptability for legged robot perception, enabling robust operation in extreme lighting and high-speed scenarios where traditional sensors typically fail.
> - Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer a substantial reduction in computational operations and energy consumption (up to 88.3%) for complex control policies compared to ANNs, without compromising motor performance.
> - ANN-to-SNN distillation is an effective strategy to leverage the training capabilities of ANNs for complex tasks and then translate them into energy-efficient SNNs for practical deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of quadruped robotics has witnessed rapid advancements in recent years, particularly in developing agile locomotion and complex motion capabilities through reinforcement learning (RL). However, these advancements often confront significant challenges related to environmental robustness, energy efficiency, and computational demands, which ES-Parkour directly addresses.

**Current Landscape and Limitations:**
- **Legged Robot Locomotion:** Research has largely focused on optimization-based methods (e.g., Trajectory Optimization, Model Predictive Control) and learning-based methods. Learning-based approaches, especially those integrating proprioception with vision, have shown promise in generalizing policies across diverse terrains using traditional sensors like depth cameras and LiDAR.
- **Robotic Parkour:** This is a particularly challenging benchmark for legged robots, demanding high levels of agility, balance, and precise perception to navigate complex, high-risk terrains. Existing solutions primarily rely on conventional sensors like depth cameras and LiDAR. While effective in controlled environments, these sensors suffer from crucial limitations:
 - **Low Operating Frequencies:** Their output rates (e.g., 30-60 Hz) are often too slow relative to the kilohertz frequencies required for precise joint control in agile movements.
 - **Sensitivity to Lighting:** They perform poorly in extreme lighting conditions (e.g., direct sunlight, low light, overexposure), hindering outdoor deployment.
 - **High Computational Demands:** Processing rich visual data from these sensors with deep neural networks (DNNs) requires significant computational power and energy, making them unsuitable for power-constrained mobile robots.
- **Event Cameras:** These bio-inspired sensors overcome many limitations of traditional cameras by asynchronously detecting pixel-level brightness changes at very high frequencies (kilohertz) and possessing a high dynamic range, making them robust to extreme lighting. Despite their potential, their application in *legged robots* has been limited due to system integration complexities.
- **Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs):** Inspired by biological brains, SNNs process information through sparse "spikes" rather than continuous values, offering inherent advantages in energy efficiency and robustness compared to conventional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). While SNNs have been explored in control tasks and multimodal integration, a unified perception and control framework using SNNs for *real-world legged robot applications* remains largely underexplored.

**ES-Parkour's Contribution and Position:**
This paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields, providing a novel solution that addresses the persistent limitations of existing methods.
- **Bridging the Gap:** ES-Parkour directly tackles the gap in legged robotics by integrating event cameras for robust, high-frequency perception with SNNs for energy-efficient control. This combination allows for a unified "brain-inspired" system pipeline, a significant departure from standard vision-based systems (Figure 2).
- **Novel System Design:** It pioneers a system-level design for quadruped robot parkour using this bio-inspired paradigm, offering new insights into perception and motion control. This is the first work to demonstrate complex quadruped control tasks across a variety of extreme environments using such a brain-inspired sensor (Table V).
- **Energy Efficiency:** By leveraging SNNs, the work significantly reduces computational burden and energy consumption, which is critical for extending the operational duration of battery-powered robots and reducing the need for bulky cooling systems.
- **Robustness:** The integration of event cameras ensures robust perception capabilities in diverse and challenging environments, including adverse lighting (overexposed, underexposed) and high-speed motion scenarios, where traditional sensors fail (Table V). This directly enhances the real-world deployability of legged robots.
- **ANN-to-SNN Distillation:** The successful transition of an end-to-end RL network from ANNs to SNNs through a distillation method is a key methodological advancement, simplifying the training of SNNs for complex tasks.

In essence, ES-Parkour represents a significant step forward in making agile legged robots more practical and versatile. It moves beyond incremental improvements in traditional paradigms by adopting a fundamentally different, biologically inspired approach to perception and control, thus opening new possibilities for robotic applications in demanding, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the ES-Parkour research are driven by the critical limitations of current quadruped robotic systems, particularly concerning perception, computational efficiency, and energy consumption in challenging environments.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limitations of Traditional Visual Sensors:** Current quadruped robots largely rely on traditional visual sensors such as depth cameras and LiDAR. While useful, these sensors suffer from several drawbacks:
 - **Low Frequency:** Their operational frequencies are often significantly lower than the joint control cycles of agile robots, leading to latency and potential instability during high-speed or dynamic maneuvers.
 - **Lighting Sensitivity:** They are highly susceptible to varying and extreme lighting conditions (e.g., direct sunlight, shadows, low light, overexposure), which severely limits their reliability and deployment in real-world, outdoor, and diverse environments.
 - **High Computational Demands:** Processing the dense data streams from these sensors with deep neural networks (DNNs) requires substantial computational resources and power, leading to increased energy consumption, larger battery requirements, and the need for bulky cooling systems, all of which constrain robot design and operational endurance.
2. **Need for Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** For quadruped robots to truly operate autonomously and effectively in unstructured, human-centric environments, they must possess perception and control systems that are robust to environmental variability, including rapid changes in lighting and complex terrains. The parkour task serves as an excellent benchmark for such challenging conditions.
3. **Drive Towards Energy Efficiency:** Mobile robots, especially battery-powered quadruped platforms, demand highly energy-efficient computational systems to extend their operational lifespan. Traditional ANNs often consume too much power for practical long-duration missions.
4. **Leveraging Bio-inspiration:** The biological world offers elegant solutions to perception and control challenges. Event cameras and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) are bio-inspired technologies that possess inherent advantages (e.g., high temporal resolution, high dynamic range, sparsity, energy efficiency) that align well with the demands of agile robotics.

**Key Objectives:**
Based on these motivations, the authors set out to achieve the following:
1. **Pioneer System-Level Design:** To develop and implement a novel, unified system-level design for quadruped robot parkour that integrates bio-inspired event cameras for perception and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) for control. This aims to provide fresh perspectives on how perception and motion control can be synergistically achieved.
2. **Enable Robust Perception Across Diverse Environments:** To demonstrate that this bio-inspired system can achieve robust perception and control capabilities for complex quadruped robot tasks across a wide variety of challenging environments, including those with extreme lighting conditions (e.g., overexposed, underexposed) and high-speed movements, where traditional systems typically fail.
3. **Achieve Significant Energy Reduction:** To substantially reduce the computational load and energy consumption of the robot's control system compared to traditional Artificial Neural Network (ANN)-based models, thereby enhancing the robot's operational efficiency and endurance.
4. **Validate ANN-to-SNN Distillation:** To successfully transition an existing end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) policy, initially trained as an ANN, to an SNN through a distillation method. This aims to leverage the performance learned by ANNs while gaining the energy efficiency of SNNs.
5. **Advance Bio-inspired Robot Learning:** To contribute to the broader field of bio-inspired robot learning by showcasing the practical feasibility and significant advantages of integrating event cameras and SNNs in complex robotic applications, thereby paving the way for further research and real-world deployment of neuromorphic systems in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ES-Parkour methodology integrates a bio-inspired perception system (event cameras) with a bio-inspired control network (SNNs) within a reinforcement learning framework. The approach involves a multi-stage process, starting with robust simulation, followed by ANN training and subsequent distillation to an SNN, and finally, rigorous evaluation of efficiency and performance.

**A. Simulation Environment and Event Camera Simulation:**
- **Simulation Platform:** The authors utilize **IsaacGym**, a high-performance robotic simulation platform known for its rich physical simulation capabilities, to efficiently train and test quadruped robots in complex scenarios.
- **Event Camera Simulation:** A key challenge was IsaacGym's lack of native event camera support. The researchers developed a custom algorithm to simulate event camera output:
 - It leverages the **brightness consistency assumption** and Taylor's expansion (Eq. 1-4) to approximate intensity changes.
 - It calculates pixel changes in real-time within the environment.
 - The simulation relies on a single depth image to derive optical flow (v(u)) and image gradients (∇L(u)), which are then used to generate event data based on intensity changes exceeding a threshold (Eq. 1, 4). This process ensures that the simulated event data closely mimics that of a real event camera.

**B. Spiking Neural Network (SNN) Architecture:**
- **Neuron Model:** The widely adopted **Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF)** model is employed to characterize the dynamic process of spike generation (Eq. 5-6). This model efficiently processes sparse spikes, reflecting biological neuron behavior and contributing to low energy consumption. During training, the simpler Integrate-and-Fire (IF) model is used, with a spiking timestep of 4.
- **Network Structure:**
 - **Vision Backbone (Encoder):** A spiking **ResNet-18** is chosen for its efficiency and effectiveness in processing visual information.
 - **Feature Fusion:** A **GRU module** is used to fuse latent features encoded from proprioceptive information (robot's internal state) and event features from the visual encoder.
 - **Actor Network:** A 3-layer spiking **MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron)** with sizes serves as the actor network, responsible for generating robot actions.

**C. Learning Process: Reinforcement Learning and Distillation:**
1. **Reinforcement Learning on ANN (Teacher Policy):**
 - **Framework:** The policy training is structured as a **Markov Decision Process (MDP)**, defined by (S, A, R, p, γ). The goal is to optimize policy parameters to maximize cumulative rewards (Eq. 7).
 - **Teacher Policy Training:** An Artificial Neural Network (ANN) serves as the "teacher." Its training follows the approach of (Extreme Parkour), where the policy learns to parkour by following instructions and optimizing rewards.
 - **Observations:** The ANN policy receives privileged information, such as terrain "scandots" (local terrain heightmap) and target yaw direction, which are acquirable in real-world scenarios. This avoids reliance on unobservable environmental factors like friction.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** The ANN is trained using a diverse set of obstacles, including gaps, steps, hurdles, and parkour terrain, to develop robust parkour capabilities.
2. **Distilling to SNN (Student Network):**
 - **Goal:** To transfer the complex decision-making behavior of the high-performing ANN teacher to an energy-efficient SNN student.
 - **Distillation Phase:** The SNN (student network) is trained to mimic the ANN (teacher network) by minimizing the **Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss** between their respective outputs for actions (L_action, Eq. 8) and yaw commands (L_yaw, Eq. 9).
 - **Interactive Fine-tuning:** After an initial "warm-up" phase where the SNN learns basic movements, the student network is further engaged in interactions with the simulation environment. This iterative process of fine-tuning and adjustment continues to minimize the loss under identical environmental conditions, ensuring the SNN closely matches the ANN's performance while gaining its inherent efficiencies.
 - **Training Setup:** The distillation process is conducted using 32 parallel robot simulation environments in IsaacGym. Event images are sampled at 10Hz, and training is performed on an NVIDIA 3090 GPU for 30 hours.
 - **Lighting Robustness:** A crucial aspect is that the SNN model, despite being distilled using ANN models trained under normal lighting (presumably with depth cameras), can maintain efficient and accurate inference under extreme lighting conditions due to the wide dynamic range of event cameras.

**D. Theoretical Energy Consumption Calculation:**
- **Metrics:** The authors calculate energy consumption based on **Synaptic Operations (SOPs)** for SNNs, derived from firing rates and time steps (SOPs(l) = fr × T × FLOPs(l)).
- **Hardware Model:** Energy costs are estimated assuming 32-bit floating-point Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) and Accumulate (AC) operations on 45nm hardware, with E_MAC = 4.6pJ and E_AC = 0.9pJ.
- **Calculation:** The total theoretical energy consumption for ES-Parkour (E_ES-Parkour) is calculated by summing the energy costs for all Convolutional (Conv) and Fully Connected (FC) layers, distinguishing between FLOPs for the first Conv layer and SOPs for subsequent layers (Eq. 10). This detailed calculation provides a quantitative measure of the energy savings achieved by SNNs.

This comprehensive methodology allows the researchers to develop, train, and rigorously evaluate a novel, bio-inspired robotic system capable of complex parkour tasks with enhanced robustness and significantly reduced energy consumption.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate the core hypotheses of ES-Parkour, demonstrating that the integration of event cameras and SNNs enables robust parkour performance with significant energy savings compared to traditional ANN-based models. The findings are primarily derived from extensive simulations.

**A. Parkour Performance and Environmental Robustness (Simulation):**
- **Diverse Scenarios:** The SNN strategy was evaluated across four challenging parkour scenarios: Hurdle, Gap, Parkour (general complex terrain), and Step (Figure 5). While specific success rates are presented (Hurdle: 45%, Gap: 60%, Parkour: 71%, Step: 29%), the paper emphasizes that the curriculum learning approach allows the robot to adapt to tasks of increasing difficulty and robustly handle changes in lighting conditions.
- **Superior Environmental Adaptability:** A key finding is the system's ability to operate effectively under *extreme lighting conditions* and *high-speed movements*. Table V explicitly compares ES-Parkour against other methods (Anymal Parkour, Extreme Parkour, Robot Parkour). ES-Parkour is the *only method* capable of robustly performing in all tested environmental conditions: normal-light, overexposed, underexposed, and high-speed scenarios. Other methods typically fail in overexposed, underexposed, or high-speed environments. This confirms the critical advantage of event cameras for real-world deployment.

**B. Computational Efficiency (Operations):**
- **Reduced Operations:** A direct comparison of the number of operations (FLOPs/SOPs) between ANN and SNN visual encoders (Table II) reveals significant computational savings with SNNs.
 - For the ResNet encoder, the SNN exhibited an operational efficiency ratio (OPs(SNN):OPs(ANN)) of **0.46:1**.
 - For the MLP encoder, this ratio was even lower, at **0.29:1**.
- **Mechanism of Efficiency:** This reduction is attributed to the sparse, event-driven nature of SNNs, where non-spiking (zero-value) portions of the feature maps do not consume computational resources during matrix operations, unlike ANNs where all activations are typically processed.

**C. Energy Consumption Analysis:**
- **Substantial Energy Savings:** The theoretical energy consumption analysis demonstrates that ES-Parkour achieves dramatic energy reductions across its modules compared to the ANN-based Parkour model (Table III).
 - **ResNet Encoder:** The SNN encoder consumed **0.11 mJ** compared to the ANN's **0.94 mJ**, representing an **88.29% energy saving**.
 - **MLP Encoder:** The SNN encoder showed a **73.33% energy saving**.
 - **Actor Module:** The SNN actor module achieved a **69.44% energy saving**.
- **Overall Reduction:** The abstract highlights an impressive **88.3% overall energy reduction** for the SNN-based model compared to the ANN-based model, showcasing the profound impact of using bio-inspired SNNs.
- **Preserved Motor Energy:** Despite significant computational energy savings, the average robot joint motor energy consumption remained comparable between the ANN and SNN models across different parkour tasks (Table IV). This indicates that the SNN-based control policy maintains similar physical exertion levels to the ANN teacher, implying that performance in terms of motor actions is preserved while computational overhead is drastically reduced.

**D. Distillation Process Success:**
- The successful transfer of complex parkour capabilities from an ANN teacher to an SNN student via the distillation method (Figure 3) is a key methodological achievement. This process effectively converts high-performance, but energy-intensive, ANN policies into efficient SNN counterparts.

In summary, the results unequivocally establish that ES-Parkour successfully integrates event cameras and SNNs to enable quadruped robots to perform challenging parkour tasks. The system achieves robust perception and control in diverse, extreme environmental conditions, a critical advantage over conventional systems. Most notably, it accomplishes this with an order of magnitude reduction in computational energy consumption, highlighting the transformative potential of bio-inspired approaches for mobile robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ES-Parkour research presents a significant advancement in quadruped robotics, with far-reaching implications for both fundamental research and practical applications. Its core significance lies in addressing the longstanding trade-offs between robot agility, environmental robustness, and energy efficiency through a novel bio-inspired paradigm.

**A. Addressing Core Limitations in Mobile Robotics:**
- **Overcoming Sensor Vulnerabilities:** The integration of event cameras directly tackles the Achilles' heel of traditional visual sensors (depth cameras, LiDAR): their sensitivity to lighting conditions and latency. By providing high-frequency, high dynamic range, and asynchronous event data, the system ensures robust perception in extreme lighting (overexposed, underexposed) and high-speed scenarios, which are common in real-world outdoor environments. This is a critical step towards truly autonomous and reliable robotic deployment beyond controlled laboratory settings.
- **Solving the Energy Bottleneck:** The adoption of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) drastically reduces the computational energy consumption of the control system (up to 88.3% reduction). For battery-powered mobile robots like quadruped platforms, this is transformative. It translates into significantly longer operational durations, reduced battery weight, and less reliance on heavy cooling systems, thereby enabling lighter, more agile, and more practical robot designs for sustained missions.
- **Reducing Computational Load:** Beyond energy, the lower operational count of SNNs (e.g., 0.29:1 for MLP encoder) means that complex control policies can be run on resource-constrained embedded hardware, paving the way for on-board, real-time intelligence without offloading computations to powerful external processors.

**B. Advancing Bio-inspired Robot Learning:**
- **Novel System-Level Integration:** ES-Parkour pioneers the first system-level design for quadruped robot parkour that successfully combines event cameras and SNNs. This demonstrates the practical feasibility and significant advantages of a fully bio-inspired perception-to-action pipeline, opening new avenues for research in neuromorphic robotics.
- **Effective ANN-to-SNN Distillation:** The successful distillation of a complex reinforcement learning policy from an ANN teacher to an SNN student provides a valuable methodology. This technique allows researchers to leverage the established training pipelines and high performance of ANNs, then efficiently convert them into energy-efficient SNNs, thus bridging the gap between current deep learning capabilities and future neuromorphic hardware.
- **Proof of Concept for SNNs in Real-World Tasks:** The demonstration of complex control tasks (parkour) across diverse environments using brain-inspired sensors and networks is a crucial proof of concept. It validates the potential of SNNs not just in theoretical studies but in demanding, dynamic robotic applications.

**C. Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
- **Enhanced Real-World Deployability:** The combined robustness to environmental conditions and high energy efficiency makes quadruped robots developed with this approach significantly more viable for real-world applications. This includes search and rescue missions in disaster zones (where lighting is unpredictable), inspection tasks in industrial settings (potentially with fast-moving machinery), environmental monitoring in natural terrains, and even military applications.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Robotics:** ES-Parkour lays a strong foundation for the development of next-generation intelligent robots that are inherently more resilient, autonomous, and sustainable. It highlights a paradigm shift from conventional "brute-force" computing to more elegant, biologically plausible, and energy-efficient computational models.
- **Inspiring Further Research:** While the current work is validated in simulation, its success provides strong motivation for continued research into integrating SNN chips with actual robots. This will involve addressing challenges in hardware implementation, real-time deployment, and further optimization of SNN architectures for even greater efficiency and performance. The "phased research" approach outlined by the authors, moving from simulation to hardware, is a sound strategy for sustainable scientific progress in this complex domain.

In conclusion, ES-Parkour marks a significant stride in robotics by demonstrating that bio-inspired sensing and processing can enable agile robots to operate robustly and efficiently in challenging, real-world conditions. This work not only pushes the boundaries of robot parkour but also offers a compelling vision for the future of intelligent, energy-efficient, and adaptable autonomous systems.
