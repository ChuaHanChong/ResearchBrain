# ES-Parkour: Advanced Robot Parkour with Bio-inspired Event Camera and Spiking Neural Network

> [!summary] Summary
> ES-Parkour integrates bio-inspired event cameras for robust perception and Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) for energy-efficient control, enabling quadruped robots to perform challenging parkour tasks across diverse extreme lighting and high-speed environments. The system achieves an 88.3% reduction in computational energy consumption while maintaining comparable motor performance to ANN-based methods.

## Problem

- Traditional visual sensors in legged robots (e.g., depth cameras, LiDAR) suffer from low operating frequencies, sensitivity to extreme lighting conditions, and high computational demands.
- Current legged robot control systems based on deep neural networks consume significant energy, hindering operational endurance for battery-powered platforms.
- Achieving robust and agile robot locomotion in challenging, dynamic, and unstructured environments with conventional methods remains difficult.

## Method

- Integrate bio-inspired event cameras for high-frequency, robust perception across varied lighting, simulating event data generation within IsaacGym.
- Develop a ==Spiking Neural Network (SNN)== architecture, utilizing a spiking ResNet-18 vision encoder and an MLP actor, for energy-efficient control.
- Employ a distillation methodology to transfer complex reinforcement learning policies from a high-performing ANN teacher to an SNN student, minimizing output differences during training.

## Results

- The SNN-based system achieved robust parkour performance across various challenging terrains (hurdle, gap, parkour, step) and was the only tested method capable of operating in extreme lighting (overexposed, underexposed) and high-speed conditions.
- Computational operations for the SNN visual encoder were reduced to **0.46**:1 (ResNet) and **0.29**:1 (MLP) relative to their ANN counterparts.
- The approach demonstrated an **88.3%** overall reduction in theoretical computational energy consumption compared to ANN-based models, while maintaining comparable robot joint motor energy consumption for the given tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bio-inspired event cameras provide superior environmental adaptability for legged robot perception, enabling robust operation in extreme lighting and high-speed scenarios where traditional sensors typically fail.
> - Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) offer a substantial reduction in computational operations and energy consumption (up to 88.3%) for complex control policies compared to ANNs, without compromising motor performance.
> - ANN-to-SNN distillation is an effective strategy to leverage the training capabilities of ANNs for complex tasks and then translate them into energy-efficient SNNs for practical deployment.
