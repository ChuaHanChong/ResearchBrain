# SLAT-Phys: Fast Material Property Field Prediction from Structured 3D Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> SLAT-Phys, from the University of Maryland, College Park, introduces an end-to-end network that predicts spatially varying material property fields for 3D objects directly from a single RGB image. The method leverages structured latent representations from a pretrained 3D generative model, achieving a ~120x speedup in inference time compared to prior reconstruction-based approaches while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Problem

- Accurate estimation of spatially varying mechanical properties (e.g., Young's modulus, density, Poisson's ratio) from visual data is a foundational challenge in computer vision and robotics.
- Existing vision-based methods rely on computationally expensive 3D reconstruction and feature extraction, requiring ~20 minutes per object and limiting real-time application.
- Manual annotation of physical properties is imprecise, time-consuming, and subjective, highlighting the need for automated solutions.

## Method

![[2603.23973-method.png]]
*The SLAT-Phys pipeline takes a single RGB image and extracts structured 3D latents. These latents are then processed by a physics decoder to predict material properties, which can be used for physics-based simulation.*

- An ==end-to-end feedforward network==, **SLAT-Phys**, is developed to predict ==per-voxel mechanical parameters== and discrete material labels directly from a ==single RGB image==.
- The approach utilizes a ==frozen, pretrained image-to-3D generative model encoder (TRELLIS)== to extract ==sparse structured latent (SLAT) features==, bypassing explicit 3D reconstruction.
- A ==lightweight sparse Swin-style transformer== acts as a ==physics decoder==, processing SLAT features to regress continuous properties (==Young's modulus, density, Poisson's ratio==) and classify discrete material types.

## Results

- **SLAT-Phys** achieved an approximate **121x** speedup over NeRF2Physics and a **128x** speedup over Pixie, reducing inference time to **~9.9 seconds** per object on an NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU.
- The method demonstrated competitive accuracy in material property estimation, with a Material Accuracy of **0.9453** and an Average Continuous MSE of **0.036**, comparable to or exceeding prior state-of-the-art methods.
- Physics-based simulations utilizing SLAT-Phys's predictions exhibited diverse and physically plausible deformation behaviors, confirming the model's ability to capture heterogeneous material properties for realistic object dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured latent representations learned by large-scale 3D generative models implicitly encode sufficient physically meaningful information for accurate material property prediction, beyond just geometry and appearance.
> - Directly regressing material properties from these latent features, without explicit multi-view 3D reconstruction, enables significant computational efficiency improvements.
> - A sparse transformer architecture can effectively operate on sparse 3D latent features to predict high-resolution, spatially varying material property fields.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding and estimating the physical properties of 3D objects from visual observations is a foundational challenge with applications in computer vision, physically-based simulation, and robotics. Accurate estimation of spatially varying mechanical properties, such as Young's modulus (E), density (ρ), and Poisson's ratio (ν), is critical for realistic deformation modeling, stable robotic manipulation, and the construction of high-fidelity digital twins.

Current 3D capture methods and datasets typically provide rich geometric information but lack annotations for physical properties. This necessitates manual annotation, which is often imprecise, expensive, and subjective. Research efforts to automate this process broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Differentiable Physics Solvers:** Methods like PhysDreamer and PhysTwin employ differentiable physics engines to iteratively optimize material parameters by matching simulated dynamics to observed signals. While capable of producing physically consistent parameters, these approaches are computationally intensive, often requiring hours of processing per scene, and the supervision signals can be sparse.
2. **Vision-based Pipelines:** A more recent trend involves using learned representations from visual or 3D features to predict spatially varying mechanical properties. Approaches such as NeRF2Physics and Pixie construct 3D semantic feature fields by projecting dense 2D image features onto explicit 3D reconstructions (e.g., via NeRF or Gaussian Splatting). These methods predict volumetric fields of material properties. However, these pipelines are computationally expensive, requiring significant time (approximately 20 minutes per object on high-end GPUs) due to the demanding 3D reconstruction and feature extraction stages. This computational burden restricts their scalability and applicability in real-time scenarios, such as interactive robotics tasks or large-scale dataset processing.

This work addresses the computational bottleneck of existing vision-based approaches by exploring the use of large-scale 3D asset generation models. These models, trained on extensive datasets, learn structured latent representations that implicitly encode rich geometric and semantic priors. The paper posits that these "generative priors" could be leveraged for physically grounded inference, thereby reducing computational costs and enabling faster material property estimation. The current research aims to bridge the gap between efficient 3D generative modeling and the need for fast, accurate physical property estimation from single images.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient, end-to-end method capable of predicting spatially varying material property fields of 3D assets directly from a single RGB image, without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction at inference time.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:
- **Accurate and Spatially Varying Material Property Estimation:** The goal is to estimate continuous mechanical parameters—Young’s modulus (E), density (ρ), and Poisson’s ratio (ν)—along with discrete material labels for individual voxels within a 3D object. Such detailed, spatially varying properties are crucial for high-fidelity physics-based simulations, where objects exhibit heterogeneous material compositions and behaviors (e.g., a flower vase with rigid parts and deformable leaves).
- **Elimination of Explicit 3D Reconstruction:** Prior vision-based methods typically rely on computationally expensive 3D reconstruction techniques (e.g., NeRF, F3RM) as a prerequisite for material property prediction. This work aims to bypass this costly stage by utilizing structured latent representations derived from a pretrained 3D generative model.
- **Significant Reduction in Computation Time:** The current state-of-the-art vision-based methods are slow, often requiring 20 minutes per object. A core motivation is to achieve a substantial speedup in inference time, making material property estimation feasible for real-time applications in robotics and for processing large datasets. This speed is particularly important for robotic tasks that require immediate understanding of physical properties to perform actions like stable grasping or force-controlled manipulation.
- **Leveraging Pretrained Generative Priors:** The authors hypothesize that structured latent features (SLAT) learned by large-scale 3D asset generation models (such as TRELLIS) already encode rich geometric and semantic information. These latents capture multi-view consistency and volumetric occupancy cues implicitly. The objective is to demonstrate that these latent representations also contain sufficient physically grounded information to support downstream physical reasoning tasks, thus serving as a unified foundation for both geometry and physics.
- **Enabling Simulation-Ready Digital Twin Generation:** By providing fast and accurate material property fields from a single image, the research aims to accelerate the creation of "simulation-ready" digital twins. This contributes to both Real2Sim and Sim2Real paradigms in robotics, where real-world objects need to be accurately represented in simulation environments.
- **Addressing Limitations of Manual Annotation:** Manual annotation of physical properties is a labor-intensive, often inaccurate, and subjective process. Automating this process reliably is a key motivation for this research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SLAT-Phys is an end-to-end feedforward network designed to predict spatially varying material property fields from a single RGB image. The methodology can be broken down into three main stages: SLAT Feature Extraction, Physics Decoder, and an optional Physics-Based Simulation step.

#### 3.1. A. Method Overview

The framework takes a single RGB image of a 3D asset as input. It then generates a sparse structured latent (SLAT) representation, which is subsequently processed by a neural decoder to predict per-voxel mechanical parameters (Young’s modulus E, density ρ, Poisson’s ratio ν) and discrete material labels. These predicted material fields, along with object geometry (also derived from SLAT), can then be used in a physics simulator.

#### 3.2. B. Problem Formulation

Given an input RGB image `I ∈ R^(H×W×3)`, the objective is to estimate a volumetric field of physical parameters `p_i = [E_i, ρ_i, ν_i, c_i]` for each voxel `v_i` in a discretized 64³ voxel grid `V`. This estimation is performed by mapping sparse latent features `{(x_i, z_i)}` from a 3D generative model to the physical parameters `f_θ: (x_i, z_i) → (E_i, ρ_i, ν_i, c_i)`.

#### 3.3. C. SLAT Feature Extraction

The first stage involves extracting structured latent features from the input image.
- **Pretrained Encoder:** A pretrained image-to-3D generation model, specifically the encoder from the TRELLIS framework, is utilized. This encoder is kept frozen during the entire training process of SLAT-Phys.
- **Sparse Latent Representation:** Given a single RGB image, the TRELLIS encoder outputs a sparse set of occupied voxel coordinates `x_i ∈ {0..., 63}³` and their corresponding 8-dimensional latent feature vectors `z_i ∈ R⁸`. These "Structured LATents" (SLAT) implicitly encode geometric and semantic information about the object's surface and form a compact 3D representation.

#### 3.4. D. Physics Decoder

The extracted SLAT features are then fed into a neural decoder responsible for predicting the physical properties.
- **Architecture:** The decoder is implemented as a lightweight sparse Swin-style transformer. It operates directly on the sparse set of occupied voxels and their associated latent features.
- **Components:** The network comprises an input projection layer, positional encoding, multiple sparse transformer blocks, and two output heads.
 - **Transformer Blocks:** Each block performs windowed self-attention over local voxel neighborhoods, enabling the capture of spatial dependencies within the latent features.
 - **Output Heads:**
 - **Regression Head:** Predicts the continuous physical parameters (Young’s modulus E, density ρ, and Poisson’s ratio ν). These parameters are normalized to a range of [-1, 1] using a `tanh` activation function.
 - **Classification Head:** Predicts a discrete material class label `c_i`.

#### 3.5. E. Training Data

- **PixieVerse Dataset:** The model is trained using the PixieVerse dataset, introduced by Pixie. This dataset consists of 1,624 high-quality single-object assets across 10 semantic categories.
- **Annotations:** Each asset in PixieVerse is annotated with spatially varying physical material properties at a 64³ voxel resolution. These annotations include normalized values for Young’s modulus (E), Poisson’s ratio (ν), and mass density (ρ), as well as an 8-class discrete material label. Young's modulus and density are provided in log-space and normalized to [-1, 1], while Poisson's ratio is linearly normalized. The annotations are generated through a semi-automatic pipeline combining vision-language models and CLIP feature fields with manual verification.

#### 3.6. F. Physics-to-SLAT Annotation Alignment

A crucial preprocessing step for training involves aligning the physics annotations from PixieVerse with the SLAT voxel grid generated by TRELLIS.
- **Discrepancy:** Since PixieVerse annotations and TRELLIS SLAT features originate from independent pipelines, they may have a rigid rotational offset despite sharing the same 64³ voxel resolution.
- **Alignment Process:** The Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm is used for alignment. The occupied SLAT voxels serve as the reference surface, and the boundary voxels of the Pixie physics occupancy mask are used as the source point cloud. To enhance robustness and avoid local minima, 64 candidate orientations (combinations of 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° rotations around each axis) are evaluated, and the best initialization for ICP is selected. A final ICP optimization estimates the rigid transformation `T ∈ SE(3)` to map the physics annotations into the SLAT coordinate frame. The aligned material properties are then queried at the SLAT voxel locations for supervision.

#### 3.7. G. Training Objective

The model is trained with a combined loss function that accounts for both continuous parameter regression and discrete material classification:
`L = λ_E L_E + λ_ρ L_ρ + λ_ν L_ν + λ_mat L_mat`
- `L_E, L_ρ, L_ν`: Mean Squared Error (MSE) losses for the normalized Young’s modulus, density, and Poisson’s ratio, respectively.
- `L_mat`: Cross-entropy (CE) loss for the discrete material class prediction.
- **Loss Weights:** The weights are set as `λ_E = λ_ρ = λ_ν = 1.0` and `λ_mat = 0.5`.
- **Optimization:** The model is trained using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 10⁻⁴ and a cosine annealing scheduling strategy. Mixed precision training and gradient accumulation are employed to optimize efficiency.

#### 3.8. H. Physics-Based Simulation (Downstream Application)

To demonstrate the utility of the predicted material fields, they can be integrated into a Material Point Method (MPM) solver.
- **Geometry from SLAT:** A Gaussian Splatting model is decoded from the same SLAT features to reconstruct the object's geometry. Each Gaussian corresponds to a simulation particle.
- **Parameter Transfer:** The predicted material properties (E, ρ, ν) are transferred from the SLAT voxel grid to the Gaussian particles via nearest-neighbor interpolation.
- **Simulation:** The resulting particles, with their associated spatially varying material parameters, are then used by the MPM solver to simulate object deformation under external forces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SLAT-Phys focused on its performance in predicting material properties and its computational efficiency compared to prior approaches.

#### 4.1. A. Ablation Study: Physics Decoder Architecture

An ablation study was conducted to assess the impact of the Physics Decoder's capacity on performance. Three models were evaluated: Small (64 channels, 4 blocks, 4 heads, 0.20M parameters), Medium (128 channels, 6 blocks, 8 heads, 1.19M parameters), and Large (256 channels, 8 blocks, 16 heads, 6.32M parameters). All models shared the same sparse voxel architecture and were trained under identical conditions.
- **Results:** As model capacity increased from Small to Large, consistent improvements were observed across all metrics:
 - **Log E error:** Decreased from 0.0285 (Small) to 0.0173 (Large).
 - **Log ρ error:** Decreased from 0.0630 (Small) to 0.0361 (Large).
 - **ν error:** Decreased from 0.0674 (Small) to 0.0540 (Large).
 - **Material Accuracy:** Increased from 0.9107 (Small) to 0.9453 (Large).
This indicates that higher representational capacity in the Physics Decoder is beneficial for accurately modeling and predicting physical properties. The Large model achieved the strongest overall performance.

#### 4.2. B. Quantitative Evaluation

The quantitative evaluation compared SLAT-Phys against NeRF2Physics and Pixie using mean squared error (MSE) for normalized continuous properties (log E, log ρ, ν) and classification accuracy for discrete material classes.
- **Material Property Estimation Accuracy (Table IIb):**
 - **Against NeRF2Physics:** SLAT-Phys achieved substantially better performance across all metrics. For instance, SLAT-Phys had a Material Accuracy of 0.9453 ± 0.112 compared to NeRF2Physics's 0.274 ± 0.01. Its Average Continuous MSE was 0.036 ± 0.049, significantly lower than NeRF2Physics's 0.858 ± 0.109.
 - **Against Pixie:** SLAT-Phys demonstrated competitive accuracy with Pixie. While Pixie reported slightly higher Material Accuracy (0.985 ± 0.011) and slightly lower Average Continuous MSE (0.056 ± 0.005), SLAT-Phys achieved comparable results (0.9453 ± 0.112 Material Accuracy, 0.036 ± 0.049 Average Continuous MSE), particularly in log E and log ρ errors. These results suggest that accurate material property estimation is achievable directly from structured latent representations without explicit multi-view reconstruction.
- **Run-Time Comparison (Table IIa):**
 - All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX A5000 GPU.
 - **Prior Methods:** NeRF2Physics and Pixie exhibited long runtimes due to their reliance on optimization-based 3D reconstruction pipelines (NeRF or F3RM training, rendering, voxelization). NeRF2Physics required approximately 1196 seconds (~20 minutes) per object, and Pixie required approximately 1261 seconds (~21 minutes) per object.
 - **SLAT-Phys:** In contrast, SLAT-Phys significantly reduced the total runtime to approximately 9.9 seconds per object. This efficiency is attributed to operating directly on the structured SLAT representation, eliminating the need for explicit 3D reconstruction, dense rendering, and voxelization.
 - **Speedup:** This represents an approximate 121x speedup over NeRF2Physics and a 128x speedup over Pixie, while maintaining competitive accuracy in material property prediction.

#### 4.3. C. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative evaluations involved physics-based simulations using material parameters predicted by SLAT-Phys (Figure 4).
- **Diverse Material Behaviors:** The simulations demonstrated the model's ability to capture varied and physically plausible dynamics for different objects:
 - A **snowman** fractured and collapsed upon impact, consistent with brittle and weak material properties. Notably, its attached stick arms remained rigid, illustrating the model's capacity for heterogeneous material field prediction.
 - A **flower** exhibited smooth bending and oscillatory motion in response to wind forces, reflecting flexible dynamics.
 - A **rubber duck** showed deformable motion during free fall, consistent with elastic, rubber-like materials.
- **Visual Fidelity and Unified Representation:** The rendered assets maintained high visual fidelity, as the Gaussian splats for geometry and appearance reconstruction were directly decoded from the same SLAT representation used for material prediction, without additional training. This suggests that the structured latent representation effectively captures both geometric and semantic information necessary for realistic rendering and physically consistent simulation from a single image.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SLAT-Phys represents a notable advancement in the field of physical property estimation from visual data, with several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Direct Material Field Regression:** SLAT-Phys is presented as the first method to directly regress continuous material property fields from a single RGB image without explicit 3D reconstruction or multi-view aggregation. This direct approach bypasses computationally intensive intermediate steps, paving the way for more efficient pipelines.
- **Validation of Structured Latent Representations:** The research demonstrates that the spatially organized SLAT features learned by large-scale 3D generation models (e.g., TRELLIS) encode physically meaningful information beyond just geometry and appearance. This finding suggests that these generative priors can serve as a potent and unified foundation for diverse 3D understanding tasks, including accurate estimation of Young’s modulus, density, and Poisson’s ratio.
- **Enabling Real-time Physical Reasoning:** By achieving competitive material prediction accuracy at an inference speed more than two orders of magnitude faster (120x speedup) than prior reconstruction-based methods, SLAT-Phys makes real-time material property estimation feasible. This has significant implications for applications demanding immediate physical understanding:
 - **Robotics:** Crucial for adapting grasp forces to prevent slippage, reasoning about interaction forces during manipulation, and estimating terrain traversability for autonomous navigation in complex environments.
 - **Interactive Virtual Environments:** Facilitates the creation of more realistic and interactive simulations where object behaviors are physically accurate.
- **Acceleration of Real2Sim and Sim2Real Paradigms:** The ability to rapidly generate simulation-ready digital twins from single images, when combined with fast single-view 3D asset generation, can significantly accelerate both Real2Sim and Sim2Real pipelines in robotics. This reduces the friction in transferring real-world observations into simulation and vice-versa.
- **New Research Direction:** The work establishes a new direction by bridging large-scale 3D generative modeling with material-aware physical inference. It suggests that rather than treating geometry reconstruction and physics estimation as separate stages, structured latent representations can offer a unified framework for both.

**Limitations:**
Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Annotation Quality Reliance:** The training relies on material annotations from the Pixie dataset, which are generated via a vision-language model (VLM)-based pipeline. This approach may necessitate extensive prompt engineering and specific object designs to achieve reliable annotations. Future work could investigate more physically grounded annotation strategies, such as those leveraging physically measured ranges from engineering databases.
- **Sole Reliance on Visual Input:** The current framework is limited to visual input. While effective, vision alone may not capture all relevant physical cues related to material properties. Incorporating multi-sensory learning, such as tactile or audio data, could enhance robustness and improve physical reasoning, as demonstrated by prior work like ObjectFolder. This multi-modal integration is identified as an important direction for future research.
