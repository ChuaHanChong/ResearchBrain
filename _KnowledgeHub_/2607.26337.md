# Reeling It In: Flexible Needle Pick Up via Thread Manipulation for Autonomous Suturing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of California San Diego, Johns Hopkins University, and Carnegie Mellon University developed an autonomous framework for surgical needle pickup that manipulates the suture thread instead of directly grasping the needle. The system achieved a 95% success rate for grasping and picking up needles in easy and medium scenarios on a da Vinci Research Kit, and successfully avoided tissue pinching in all evaluated trials.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous needle pickup methods in robotic surgery depend on direct visual access and reachability of the needle.
- Needles can be occluded, inaccessible, or positioned precariously on sensitive tissue in dynamic surgical environments.
- Direct grasping of needles carries a risk of pinching surrounding tissue or causing unpredictable needle movement.

## Method

- A framework was developed that uses ==suture thread manipulation== for ==indirect needle retrieval==, overcoming direct access limitations.
- The approach combines ==3D thread and tissue reconstruction== with a ==reliability-aware optimization== for selecting optimal thread grasping points.
- It employs a sequence of robot actions including ==context-aware circular thread pickup==, ==thread lengthening==, and a ==bi-manual 'fishing' motion== to bring the needle within reach.

## Results

- The framework achieved a **95%** success rate for both thread grasping and needle pickup in 'Easy' and 'Medium' experimental setups.
- It demonstrated **100%** success in avoiding tissue pinching or damage across all trials during ablation studies.
- Incorporating the thread reliability metric improved grasp success by **53.33%** compared to selection without it, and the circular pickup prevented needle dragging on tissue.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indirect manipulation of the suture thread provides a safer and more versatile method for needle pickup, particularly when direct needle access is compromised.
> - Integrating thread reconstruction reliability and tissue proximity into grasp-point selection significantly enhances grasping success and prevents tissue damage.
> - Specific circular lifting and thread lengthening motions are crucial for preventing the needle from dragging on tissue during retrieval.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robotic surgery is an evolving field that aims to automate repetitive and time-consuming tasks to reduce surgical burden and improve outcomes. A fundamental and frequently performed task within surgical operations is suturing, which necessitates constant manipulation of a suture needle and thread. Within this context, the specific subtask of suture-needle pickup is critical, as needles may be unexpectedly dropped or strategically released to reconfigure grasping.

Prior research on autonomous suture-needle pickup primarily relies on methods that guide a robot to directly approach and grasp the needle. These methods often involve visual servoing systems, haptic-guided shared control, or learning-based approaches such as imitation learning and reinforcement learning. While effective, these direct pickup strategies operate under the assumption that the needle is consistently observable and directly approachable by the robotic instruments.

However, in realistic surgical environments, these assumptions do not always hold. Needles can become occluded by tissue or other instruments, fall out of camera view, or be positioned in ways that make direct approach unsafe or impossible. Direct grasping of a needle lying on tissue also carries the risk of the gripper pinching surrounding tissue or the needle exhibiting unpredictable movement due to its slippery surface, potentially leading to tissue damage or manipulation failure.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing an alternative approach: leveraging the suture thread as an assistive tool for *indirect* needle pickup. This strategy acknowledges that the thread, being longer and more deformable than the rigid needle, offers more manipulation flexibility. This positions the research as an extension to previous work on suture thread manipulation, which has focused on localizing and grasping thread segments, often incorporating deep learning and reliability estimates for 3D thread reconstruction. The paper integrates and builds upon these thread manipulation techniques to develop a comprehensive framework specifically for reliable and safe needle retrieval, particularly in scenarios where direct needle access is compromised.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged limitations of existing autonomous needle pickup strategies in surgical robotics. Current methods, which largely depend on direct visual access and reachability of the needle, are insufficient for unstructured and dynamic surgical environments where the needle may be occluded, inaccessible, or positioned precariously on sensitive tissue.

The key objectives of this work are to develop and evaluate an autonomous framework that overcomes these limitations by:

1. **Enabling Indirect Needle Pickup:** The central objective is to propose and implement a method that utilizes the suture thread as an assistive tool for indirect needle retrieval. This approach aims to circumvent scenarios where the needle itself is visually occluded or physically inapproachable.
2. **Minimizing Tool-Tissue Contact:** A significant motivation is to enhance safety by reducing the risk of the robotic gripper pinching or damaging delicate tissue during needle pickup. The proposed indirect manipulation strategy aims to achieve this by operating primarily on the thread.
3. **Providing a Complete Workflow:** The research aims to deliver a comprehensive, end-to-end pipeline for thread-assisted needle pickup. This includes all necessary steps from environmental perception (thread and tissue reconstruction) to robot action (grasp-point selection, thread lifting, and bimanual needle fishing).
4. **Ensuring Robustness to Visual Uncertainty and Diverse Configurations:** The framework is designed to account for visual uncertainty inherent in real-world environments and to function effectively across various challenging thread configurations, including loops, overlaps, and partial occlusions.
5. **Optimizing Grasping for Safety and Reliability:** A specific objective is to develop a reliability-aware optimization algorithm for selecting the optimal thread-grasping point. This algorithm is designed to minimize the risk of tissue damage while maximizing the likelihood of a successful and stable grasp, considering both thread localization uncertainty and proximity to tissue.
6. **Developing Stable Thread Manipulation Strategies:** The work aims to devise specific robot maneuvers for lifting and manipulating the thread that prevent undesirable phenomena such as needle dragging on tissue or thread entanglement, thereby enhancing both safety and success rate during the reeling-in process.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to close the gap in applying autonomous robot policies to more complex and unstructured suturing environments, offering a safer and more versatile alternative to direct needle pickup.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed autonomous suture-needle pickup framework integrates several sequential steps, commencing from environmental perception and culminating in the stable retrieval of the needle. The overall process is outlined in Algorithm 1.

**A. Thread Reconstruction**
The framework initiates with 3D thread reconstruction, building upon previous work by Joglekar et al. Stereo images from the da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK) are processed to generate thread segmentation masks for both left and right views. These masks are then used for stereo matching to calculate disparities of thread pixels, which are subsequently re-projected into 3D space to form an unordered set of keypoints.
A manual keypoint ordering method is employed to arrange these keypoints sequentially from the needle to the thread’s tail. For each ordered keypoint, a local reliability region is defined in 3D space. This region is characterized by a depth bound, derived from the deviation from a least squares line fit, and an additional pixel bound in image space to account for segmentation uncertainty. These bounds define the lower and upper depth limits (k and k, respectively) for each keypoint. A 3D spline is then constructed using a constrained optimization problem, which includes a smoothing loss and constraints ensuring the spline passes through the defined reliability regions of the keypoints. The spline is uniformly sampled to generate a set of 3D thread points (P) and corresponding reliability values (R), which are inversely related to the size of their nearby reliability regions.

**B. Tissue Reconstruction**
Simultaneously with thread reconstruction, a 3D point cloud representation of the surrounding tissue (Q) is generated using stereo matching. The tissue surface normal (n) is assumed to be approximately parallel to gravity. To refine the tissue point cloud, points overlapping with the thread masks are initially removed to exclude inaccurate floating points. Further filtering is applied to remove tissue points within a specified radius (r) of the thread, which helps in isolating regions clearly belonging to the tissue surface. The final tissue normal vector (n) is obtained by averaging the normal vectors from the filtered tissue points. This reconstructed tissue data, Q_filtered and n, provides critical context for subsequent grasping and lifting operations.

**C. Thread Grasping-Point Selection**
The selection of the optimal grasping point (p_grasp) on the thread is formulated as a constrained optimization problem (Equation 11). The objective is to minimize a weighted sum of two terms: the thread length from the candidate point to the needle (l(p_i)) and the inverse of the distance from the candidate point to the nearest tissue point (d(p_i, Q_filtered)). Hyperparameters α and β control the relative importance of these terms.
Candidate grasping points are initially all reconstructed thread points. These candidates are then filtered based on three constraints:
1. **Above Tissue Constraint:** The upper depth bound of the candidate point, plus a safety margin (δ_s), must be above the nearest tissue point. This prevents selecting points too close to or embedded within the tissue.
2. **Reliability Constraint:** The reliability value (r_i) of the candidate point must exceed a predefined threshold (δ_r). This ensures that the selected point is within an acceptable error bound of the 3D reconstruction.
3. **Vertical Steepness Constraint:** The local segment of the thread around the candidate point must not exhibit significant depth change (defined by δ_v). This avoids selecting points on steep thread segments that are difficult to grasp accurately.
The point satisfying these constraints and minimizing the objective function is chosen as p_grasp.

**D. Thread Grasping**
Once p_grasp is identified, the gripper's pose for grasping is determined. The gripper's orientation (R_grasp) is configured such that its z-axis is tangent to the thread, pointing towards the needle, and its y-axis points towards the tissue. This orientation aims to maximize the free space for grasping and ensure the gripper remains within its joint limits during subsequent manipulations. The gripper approaches p_grasp with a distance offset (δ_offset) along its y-axis, aligning its orientation before translating to the target position.

**E. Circular Thread Pick Up**
Following initial grasping, the thread and needle are lifted away from the tissue. To minimize needle movement against the tissue and prevent snagging, a circular motion is applied. The robot, holding p_grasp, rotates around the approximate position of the needle's tail (p_1) until the vector from p_1 to p_grasp aligns with the tissue normal vector (n). This rotation (∆R_circular) is calculated using axis-angle representation. As ‘n’ is assumed parallel to gravity, this motion lifts the thread vertically, reducing the likelihood of the needle dragging on the tissue.

**F. Thread Lengthening**
After the circular pickup, the thread is straightened by lifting it further in the direction of the tissue normal (n). If the thread is longer than the maximum height of the surgical workspace (h_workspace), it is lifted to this maximum height. This motion is applied parallel to the gravity direction, stabilizing the thread dynamics and minimizing lateral forces, which further prevents the needle from contacting nearby tissues.

**G. Bi-manual Needle Fishing**
With the thread elevated and stabilized, a bi-manual "fishing" motion is initiated to reach and grasp the needle. Due to the needle's weight and the thread's flexural strength, its exact configuration after lifting can be uncertain. This uncertainty is modeled as a cone, parameterized by a potential deviation angle (θ) from the gravity direction. The step size (s) for the bi-manual motions is determined by this uncertainty and the gripper's open jaw width (w).
The process (Algorithm 2) involves two grippers taking alternating turns. If the remaining thread length is substantial, multiple fishing motions are performed. In each step, a grasping gripper moves toward a calculated goal position, closes its jaw, while the holding gripper releases the thread and retracts. The newly grasping gripper then lifts the thread to the original height of the holding gripper. The roles of the grippers are then switched, progressively "reeling in" the thread until the needle is within reach for a final grasp. The bi-manual grasping orientation (R_bi−grasp) is maintained throughout this process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed needle pickup framework was evaluated through real-world experiments conducted on a da Vinci Research Kit (dVRK), using a 36 mm 1/2-circle suture needle with a 45 cm thread. The hyperparameters for the grasping-point selection were set as α = 0.7, β = 1 (prioritizing tissue distance over needle proximity), safety margin δ_s = 1 mm, reliability threshold δ_r = 0.4, velocity threshold δ_v = 0.6, grasping-point offset δ_offset = 20 mm, and bi-manual fishing step size s = 15 mm.

**A. Experimental Setups and Overall Performance**
The framework was tested across four distinct setups:
- **Easy:** Minimal overlap/occlusions, thread elevated ≥ 10 mm, length ≤ 50 mm.
- **Medium:** Minimal overlap/occlusions, thread elevated < 10 mm, length 50-90 mm.
- **Hard:** One or more loops/overlaps, minimal occlusions, thread elevated < 10 mm, length 50-180 mm.
- **Occlusion:** Partial occlusion by gauze and silicon phantoms, random arrangements, length 50-180 mm.

The success rates were categorized into "Grasp" (successful thread grasp at optimal point) and "Pickup" (successful thread pickup and reaching within 10 mm of the needle).

- **Easy and Medium Setups:** The method achieved a 95% success rate for both Grasp and Pickup in both Easy and Medium setups (19 successful out of 20 trials for each category). The single failure in each setup was attributed to poor thread reconstruction, leading to a missed grasp.
- **Hard Setup:** For the Hard setup, the Grasp success rate remained high at 95% (19/20), indicating the robustness of the grasping-point selection even with complex overlapping thread arrangements. However, the Pickup success rate decreased to 70% (14/20). Failure cases during bi-manual fishing in this setup included:
 - Excessive thread slack causing the needle to anchor away from the gripper, pulling the thread outside the predicted uncertainty cone.
 - Uncertainty in thread and tissue reconstruction near self-intersections, limiting grasping-point generation.
 - Grasping multiple overlapping portions of the thread, which maintained its tangled shape and lay outside the uncertainty cone.
- **Occlusion Setup:** The Occlusion setup yielded a 90% Grasp success rate (18/20), comparable to the Hard setup. The Pickup success rate was 65% (13/20). Failure modes in this setup were similar to the Hard setup, with additional challenges:
 - Proximity of gauze to most thread portions, limiting available grasping points.
 - Thread catching on gauze during bi-manual fishing.
 - Multiple thread portions grasped, causing loops over the gripper.

The experiments indicated that while thread grasping (the initial phase) was generally robust, the subsequent bi-manual fishing phase was more susceptible to failure, particularly in complex or occluded configurations, primarily due to unpredictable thread dynamics and reconstruction uncertainty.

**B. Ablation Studies**

1. **Tissue Pinching:** Across all Easy, Medium, Hard, and Occlusion trials, no instances of tissue pinching or damage were observed during manipulation. This 100% success rate (80/80 trials without pinching) was attributed to the grasping-point selection strategy, which incorporates thread reconstruction reliability and thread-to-tissue proximity, as well as the context-aware circular pickup, lengthening, and bi-manual fishing motions that prioritize operating in the space above the tissue.

2. **Thread Reliability Effects:** A comparative study evaluated the impact of using the thread reliability metric in grasping-point selection. In 15 paired trials, using the reliability metric resulted in 8 successful grasps, whereas without the metric, only 7 successful grasps were achieved. This demonstrated a 53.33% improvement in grasp success by incorporating the reliability metric. Notably, in one trial, selecting a grasping point without the reliability metric led to tissue pinching, while in other failures, the gripper completely missed the thread due to reconstruction inaccuracies. Conversely, no grasp failures occurred when the reliability metric was utilized.

3. **Circular Pickup Effects:** The circular pickup and thread lengthening motions (Sections III-E and III-F) were compared against a direct lifting method. The proposed circular pickup method resulted in minimal needle-to-tissue contact. In contrast, the direct lifting method consistently showed the needle dragging across the tissue surface, indicating a higher risk of tissue damage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for autonomous suture-needle pickup that addresses critical limitations of prior methods, thereby advancing the capabilities of robotic surgery in less structured environments.

The primary significance lies in its departure from direct needle manipulation, which is often constrained by visibility and accessibility. By pioneering an indirect approach that leverages the suture thread, the framework offers a viable solution for scenarios where the needle is occluded, out of reach, or positioned precariously on sensitive tissue. This expands the operational envelope for autonomous suturing systems, making them potentially applicable in a wider range of surgical contexts, including those with unforeseen events like a dropped needle in a complex anatomical region.

The detailed methodology, encompassing 3D thread and tissue reconstruction, reliability-aware grasp-point selection, and context-aware manipulation strategies, contributes to enhancing both the safety and robustness of autonomous surgical tasks. The demonstrated ability to consistently avoid tissue pinching (100% success in ablation studies) and significantly reduce needle dragging against tissue via the circular pickup motion (as shown in comparative studies) directly translates to improved patient safety by mitigating the risk of inadvertent tissue damage. The incorporation of reliability metrics into grasp selection also shows a quantifiable improvement in grasping accuracy (53.33% improvement), leading to more consistent and successful operations.

The experimental validation on a dVRK, under increasingly challenging conditions (Easy, Medium, Hard, and Occlusion setups), demonstrates the practical applicability of the proposed pipeline. Achieving high success rates (95% in Easy and Medium setups) and maintaining robust grasping performance even in complex or partially occluded scenarios (95% grasp success in Hard, 90% in Occlusion) indicates the system's potential for real-world surgical integration. While needle fishing performance decreases under high complexity, identifying these specific failure modes provides clear directions for future improvements.

This work lays a foundation for subsequent autonomous suturing subtasks, such as needle regrasping and suture throwing, by reliably presenting the needle in a manipulable configuration. The framework's ability to handle deformable objects like suture threads with visual uncertainty is a contribution to robotic manipulation of soft tissues and flexible instruments. Future advancements, particularly in real-time feedback for unpredictable thread deformations and improved reconstruction under heavy occlusions, could further solidify the impact by enabling robust performance in even more dynamic and challenging surgical conditions, extending its applicability to related tasks like suture tightening and disentanglement.
