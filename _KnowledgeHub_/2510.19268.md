# Hierarchical DLO Routing with Reinforcement Learning and In-Context Vision-language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Hierarchical framework for long-horizon deformable linear object (DLO) routing through multiple clips: a VLM plans skill sequences (Insert, Pull, Flatten) via in-context reasoning and detects/recovers from failures, while an RL-trained (SAC) parameterized motion primitive executes precise insertion. Achieves 92% overall success across diverse routing scenarios, generalizing from 3-clip to 5-clip settings.

## Problem

- Prior imitation-learning cable-routing methods rely on limited demonstrations and generalize poorly out-of-distribution, degrading sharply when extended to more clips.
- Existing VLM-guided robot planners (e.g. legged locomotion) target rigid-object domains and lack safe low-level control suited to DLOs, where careless actions cause deformation or breakage.
- Prior failure-detection-and-recovery work either only detects failures without recovering, or targets rigid-object manipulation, leaving DLO-specific failure reasoning and recovery underexplored.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical framework== pairing a VLM high-level ==in-context planner== (GPT-5, ==chain-of-thought==) with low-level skills, deciding next skill, target clip, and task completion from scene and zoomed-in clip images.
- Three low-level skills: ==Insert== (==SAC==-trained ==parameterized motion primitive== with a reward combining rope_in/out, collision penalty, episode-length, distance, and flattening terms), plus predefined ==Pull== and ==Flatten== primitives.
- ==Failure recovery mechanism==: planner tracks consecutive insertion attempts via a step-limited counter in the CoT prompt and triggers Flatten to reorient the DLO into an insertion-feasible state without human intervention.

## Results

- Simulated insertion primitive: RL policy reaches **87%** success rate versus **45%** for a heuristic policy, with longer average signed endpoint distance (**2.59cm** vs **1.24cm**).
- Long-horizon routing: full method (VLM with failure reasoning + RL policy) hits **80-100%** success rate across implicit, spatial, attribute, and 4/5-clip settings, versus **47%** or lower for VLM without failure reasoning.
- Real-robot Franka Panda trials: method reaches **62.5%** success and **2.625** average clips inserted, versus **37.5%** and **1.75** clips for the Fixed-Order + RL baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling safe, reactive contact-rich control (learned via RL) from semantic long-horizon planning (handled by an in-context VLM) lets each component specialize without one system solving both.
> - Explicit failure-reasoning prompts (tracking attempt counts, invoking recovery skills) transfer performance gains disproportionately to harder, sharp-turn scenarios rather than easy ones.
> - State normalization into a target object's local frame (clip-relative coordinates) helps a single low-level policy generalize across widely varying object poses and configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable linear objects (DLOs), such as cables and ropes, presents significant challenges in robotics due to their inherent flexibility, under-actuated nature, and unpredictable deformation. These characteristics complicate both low-level action control and high-level task reasoning. Long-horizon DLO routing tasks, prevalent in industrial assembly and daily life, demand complex multi-step planning and reliable execution, requiring robots to adapt to non-linear dynamics, decompose abstract goals, and manage contact-sensitive interactions.

Previous research in deformable object manipulation has explored fundamental tasks like insertion and pulling but often exhibited limitations in generalization, typically focusing on single-skill or short-horizon scenarios. For instance, imitation learning approaches for cable routing have shown effectiveness within their training domains but struggled to generalize to out-of-distribution configurations, such as an increased number of clips.

The advent of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has enabled advances in robotic planning by leveraging commonsense reasoning and scene understanding. These models have been used for high-level task guidance and recovery strategies, particularly in unstructured environments for tasks like legged robot navigation. However, these VLM applications often target rigid-object manipulation and are not directly applicable to DLOs, where careless actions can lead to deformation or damage. The need for precise, safe, and robust low-level control, in addition to semantic high-level reasoning, remains a critical gap for long-horizon DLO manipulation.

This work addresses these challenges by proposing a hierarchical framework that integrates the high-level reasoning capabilities of VLMs with reinforcement learning (RL)-based low-level control for DLO routing. The research positions itself as a development towards more autonomous and robust robotic systems capable of handling complex, long-horizon deformable object manipulation tasks with improved generalization and integrated failure recovery, distinguishing it from prior work that either lacks generalizable high-level planning or robust low-level DLO control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a fully autonomous hierarchical framework capable of solving challenging long-horizon routing tasks for deformable linear objects (DLOs). This overarching goal is driven by the recognized difficulty of DLO manipulation and the limitations of existing robotic solutions.

The specific objectives of the proposed framework are:
- To enable robots to route DLOs through multiple clips, adapting to various configurations, based on implicit or explicit routing goals expressed in natural language. This involves generating feasible multi-step plans that respect the specified order or inferred intent.
- To integrate a high-level planner, utilizing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for in-context reasoning, with a set of low-level skills, specifically an insertion skill trained via reinforcement learning, for precise and robust execution.
- To enhance the framework's robustness and reliability over long horizons by introducing an autonomous failure recovery mechanism. This mechanism aims to detect execution failures and reorient the DLO into states conducive to successful insertion, thereby preventing repeated failures and task abandonment.
- To demonstrate generalization capabilities across diverse task scenarios. This includes adapting to varied object attributes (e.g., clip colors), spatial descriptions, implicit language commands, and extending the task complexity to configurations with an increased number of clips (e.g., from 3-clip to 4- and 5-clip settings).
- To ensure that the low-level manipulation skills are both safe and reliable, particularly for contact-sensitive operations like insertion, to prevent damage to the DLO or the environment.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several factors:
- **Ubiquity and Complexity of DLO Tasks:** DLO routing tasks are common in industrial settings, such as automobile assembly (routing wires), and in everyday life (e.g., cable management). Despite their prevalence, the inherent flexibility and under-actuated nature of DLOs make their robotic manipulation challenging, requiring advanced planning and control capabilities that are not yet fully realized.
- **Limitations of Current Robotic Approaches:** Existing methods for DLO manipulation often lack the ability to handle the non-linear dynamics of deformable objects over long horizons, decompose abstract routing goals into executable steps, or generate multi-step plans composed of diverse manipulation skills. Prior work often struggles with generalization to new configurations or increased task complexity.
- **Need for High-Level Reasoning:** Successful long-horizon DLO routing requires accurate high-level reasoning to synthesize feasible plans, sequence skills, and make adaptive decisions during execution. This includes interpreting abstract language commands and determining the correct order and orientation for routing.
- **Gap in VLM Application for DLOs:** While VLMs offer powerful commonsense reasoning and scene understanding, their application in DLO manipulation has been limited. Existing VLM-driven robotic systems typically focus on rigid objects, and their approaches are often not directly transferable to DLOs due to the specific challenges of contact-sensitive and deformable object interactions, where careless actions can be detrimental.
- **Importance of Robustness and Recovery:** In long-horizon tasks, failures are almost inevitable. The ability for a robotic system to autonomously detect, diagnose, and recover from execution failures is critical for achieving high success rates and enabling reliable deployment in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a fully autonomous hierarchical framework designed for long-horizon DLO routing. This framework integrates a high-level planner, powered by Vision-Language Models (VLMs), with a set of low-level manipulation skills, with a key skill trained via reinforcement learning.

### 4. Hierarchical Framework Structure

The framework operates on two distinct levels:
1. **High-level VLM Planner:** This component is responsible for abstract reasoning, task planning, and decision-making.
 - **Inputs:** The VLM receives a top-down scene image (I_scene), a user-specified text description of the routing goal (language prompt), and auxiliary information (e.g., DLO attributes, clip properties, environmental context). During execution, it also receives a zoomed-in view of the current target clip to infer task progress.
 - **Reasoning:** The VLM performs in-context learning to adapt to the task domain. It reasons about task progress by tracking the DLO head and identifying occlusions. It determines the sequence of clips for routing, selects the appropriate low-level skill (Insert, Pull, or Flatten), identifies the target clip for that skill, and decides when the task is complete.
 - **Planning Mechanism:** Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is employed to guide the VLM in analyzing the scene, including DLO-clip interactions, before selecting actions. The VLM is provided with structured definitions for each low-level skill, outlining their functional objectives, usage conditions, and scenarios when they should not be invoked. In-context examples (image-skill pairs) are also supplied to facilitate learning.
2. **Low-level Skills:** These skills perform the physical manipulation of the DLO. Three core skills are defined:
 - **Insert:** This is the most critical skill for DLO routing, designed to precisely pass the DLO through a clip. It is implemented as a parameterized motion primitive whose parameters are optimized by a reinforcement learning (RL) agent.
 - **Formulation:** The insertion skill is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with a state (S) comprising the clip's pose and the positions of DLO particles (p_1:n). The action space (A) consists of 3D Cartesian motion (p_g) and 1D rotation (q_g) of the robot gripper.
 - **Training:** A Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is used to train the RL policy in IsaacSim. The RL agent's observation includes DLO particle positions, clip pose and scale, and a binary indicator (`rope_in`) for partial insertion.
 - **Reward Function:** The reward function `r` is designed to encourage insertion progress (`rope_in`, `rope_out`), penalize collisions (`collide`), favor efficient insertions (`r_hor`), provide stage-specific guidance (`r_dist`), and encourage DLO flattening (`r_flat`) for easier subsequent insertions. Curriculum learning is applied, starting with larger clip openings and gradually narrowing them to accelerate convergence.
 - **Robustness:** To handle varying clip orientations, DLO state observations are transformed into the clip's local frame, and actions are executed within this local frame.
 - **Pull and Flatten:** These are auxiliary skills that adjust the DLO's configuration. They are implemented as predefined motion primitives, meaning their movements are pre-programmed rather than learned.
 - **Pull:** Adjusts the DLO by pulling it through a clip, setting up the next segment for insertion.
 - **Flatten:** A simple recovery skill that reinitializes the DLO to a straightened, insertion-possible state. This skill is invoked during failure recovery.

### 5. Hierarchical Planning & Failure Recovery

A key aspect of the framework is its failure recovery mechanism, which enhances robustness over long horizons.
- **Failure Detection:** During DLO routing, if insertion attempts repeatedly fail (e.g., DLO head twists or drifts from the clip opening), the VLM planner detects this condition. A counter tracks consecutive insertion failures, and a step limit prevents infinite loops.
- **Recovery Action:** Upon detecting a failure, the VLM planner invokes the `Flatten` skill. This low-level rescue skill reorients the DLO, making it amenable to subsequent insertion attempts.
- **Replanning:** After `Flatten` execution, the VLM replans and resumes the routing sequence, often by re-attempting insertion. This process is integrated into the CoT prompting.

### 6. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation:** The low-level RL policy for insertion is trained in IsaacSim and GarmentLab. Experiments are conducted using GPT-5 as the VLM planner.
- **Real-world:** A Franka Emika Panda robot with a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D415 camera is used. Segmentation of DLOs and clips is performed using SAM2. The MoveIt planner generates continuous trajectories. The system operates within the Robot Operating System (ROS) framework. The RL policy, trained in simulation with randomized rope configurations, is applied to the real robot without fine-tuning or domain adaptation.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed hierarchical framework through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios, addressing several key research questions.

### 8. A. Low-level Skill Performance (Simulation - Table I)

The RL-trained `Insert` policy demonstrates superior performance compared to a heuristic insertion policy.
- **Success Rate:** The RL policy achieves an 87% success rate for insertion, significantly higher than the heuristic policy's 45%.
- **Average Distance:** The RL policy achieves an average signed endpoint distance of 2.59 cm, indicating more consistent and complete DLO passage through the clip, compared to the heuristic policy's 1.24 cm.
- **Episode Length:** The RL policy averages 3.86 steps per episode, while the heuristic policy averages 2 steps. The longer episode length for the RL policy indicates it utilizes more steps for iterative refinement, leading to higher success.
These results establish the robustness and precision of the RL-trained low-level insertion skill, which is fundamental to the hierarchical framework.

### 9. B. Long-horizon DLO Routing (Simulation - Table II)

The hierarchical framework's performance is evaluated across various long-horizon routing strategies: Implicit Orders, Fixed Orders (Spatial), Fixed Orders (Attribute), and challenging 4/5-Clip settings.

- **Q1: Hierarchical Framework's Efficacy:** The proposed method, combining VLM with failure reasoning and RL policy, demonstrates superior performance across all task types. It achieves high success rates (80% to 100%) and consistently inserts a greater number of clips (2.7 to 4.3). While requiring slightly longer episode lengths compared to some baselines, this indicates a more thorough and robust task completion. In contrast, simpler baselines like "VLM w/o Failure Reasoning + RL Policy" and "Fixed Order + RL Policy" show significantly lower success rates, particularly in complex scenarios (e.g., 7% in Fixed Spatial/Attribute for VLM w/o failure reasoning). The "Symbolic Planner + RL Policy," which uses ground-truth information and a greedy strategy, performs well when its greedy order matches the correct one (e.g., 100% in 4/5-Clip) but still incurs longer episodes due to less efficient failure handling.

- **Q2: Impact of Failure Reasoning:** The integration of failure reasoning substantially improves performance. Comparing "VLM w/ Failure Reasoning + RL Policy" to "VLM w/o Failure Reasoning + RL Policy," the success rates are consistently higher with failure reasoning (e.g., 93% vs. 7% in Fixed Spatial/Attribute settings). Episode lengths are also generally shorter, indicating more efficient recovery from errors. In scenarios with complex turns (Fixed Spatial, Fixed Attribute), where DLO configurations are prone to making insertion difficult, failure reasoning proves critical, allowing the agent to recover and proceed.

- **Q3: Generalization to Multi-Clip Settings:** The framework demonstrates strong generalization to more challenging 4/5-clip settings, achieving a 100% success rate and fully inserting all clips. This indicates that the high-level VLM planner reliably predicts necessary skills, and the low-level RL policy performs consistently even with increased task complexity and extended horizons. Baselines without robust hierarchical planning and failure recovery show significant drops in performance in these extended settings.

- **Q4: Importance of RL Low-level Policy:** The performance of "VLM w/ Failure Reasoning + Heuristic Policy" (7-13% success rates) highlights the critical role of the learned RL low-level policy. The heuristic policy's lack of environmental awareness results in frequent failures, even when guided by a high-level VLM. This confirms that an adaptive, task-aware low-level skill is essential for precise and robust DLO manipulation, particularly in complex, contact-sensitive scenarios.

### 10. C. Real-world Robot Experiments (Table III, Fig. 5)

Real-world experiments using a Franka Emika Panda robot validate the framework's practical applicability.
- **Success Rate:** The proposed method achieves a 62.5% success rate and an average of 2.625 clips inserted. This outperforms the "Fixed Order + RL" baseline, which achieves 37.5% success and 1.75 clips inserted.
- **Challenges:** The real-world success rates are lower than in simulation, attributed to factors such as calibration errors, perception noise, and residual sim-to-real transfer gaps.
- **Validation:** Despite these challenges, the superior performance of the proposed method in the real world reinforces the necessity of explicit failure reasoning and closed-loop decision-making for handling real-world variations and disturbances.
- **Observed Failure Cases (Fig. 6):** Common failures include early termination before the last clip is fully inserted, unintended collisions due to incorrect `Flatten` commands after an insertion, and suboptimal plans where the VLM issues redundant `Insert` actions instead of `Pull`.

### 11. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a hierarchical framework for long-horizon DLO routing that integrates advanced high-level reasoning with robust low-level control. This work makes several contributions and has the potential for significant impact.

**Contribution to DLO Manipulation:** The framework offers a solution for long-horizon DLO routing tasks, which have historically been challenging due to the complex, non-linear dynamics of deformable objects. By addressing multi-step planning and reliable skill execution, it moves beyond the limitations of single-skill or short-horizon DLO manipulation approaches.

**Advancement in Robotic Planning and Control:** The integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for high-level, in-context reasoning with reinforcement learning (RL) for precise, task-aware low-level control represents an advancement in robotic autonomy. This approach effectively bridges the gap between abstract language commands and tangible robotic actions, particularly for complex deformable object manipulation, an area where VLM applications have been less developed compared to rigid object manipulation.

**Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The introduction of an autonomous failure detection and recovery mechanism is a key aspect of the work. By enabling the VLM to identify execution failures, reason about their causes, and trigger appropriate recovery actions (like the `Flatten` skill), the framework significantly improves the overall success rate and efficiency of long-horizon tasks. This capability is crucial for deploying robotic systems in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments where unforeseen challenges are common.

**Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability of the framework to generalize across diverse scenes, including varied object attributes, spatial descriptions, implicit language commands, and extended multi-clip configurations (up to 5 clips), indicates its scalability and adaptability. This generalization is essential for practical applications where task parameters can vary widely.

**Potential for Practical Applications:** The research has direct implications for industrial automation and daily life. Tasks such as routing wires in automobile assembly or managing cables in complex environments are often manual and labor-intensive. By automating these processes, the framework could lead to increased efficiency, reduced labor costs, and improved safety in manufacturing and service robotics.

**Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as faulty or suboptimal planning, suggest avenues for future research, including fine-tuning VLM planners for greater stability and exploring automated methods for integrating new skills into the VLM planner. These directions could lead to more capable and adaptable robotic systems for increasingly complex deformable manipulation tasks.
