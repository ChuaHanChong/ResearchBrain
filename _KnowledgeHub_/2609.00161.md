# IMPACT: Attention Is the Interaction Map for Scalable Interaction-Aware World Model Training

> [!summary] Summary
> IMPACT is a training framework for action-conditioned video world models that fixes a supervision-allocation mismatch in uniform MSE denoising. It treats cross-attention on manipulated-object text tokens as an interaction prior, samples candidate regions, calibrates them with detached local prediction errors into an interaction map, and reweights the denoising loss. It needs no external representations and no inference changes, raising WorldArena EWMScore from 58.65 to 62.46.

## Problem

- Prior interaction-aware world models constrain generation with external dense representations such as optical flow, depth, or reconstructed 3D structure, requiring auxiliary estimators or manual annotation.
- Those preprocessing costs grow with corpus size and duration, capping training scalability, and supervision quality is bounded by the accuracy of the external estimators.
- Globally averaged MSE denoising weights positions by spatial extent, so prevalent static content dominates gradients and sparse interaction regions stay under-supervised despite carrying the dynamics.

## Method

- ==Object-token grounding==: a frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B extracts the manipulated-object noun phrase from the instruction alone, and its subword token positions anchor ==cross-attention== aggregation across selected ==DiT== blocks and heads.
- ==Attention Distribution Sampling== samples K=8 coherent fixed-area candidate masks from the tempered attention logits with coarse-grid Gaussian perturbation, then weights each by its detached masked mean prediction error into an ==interaction map==.
- ==Interaction-Weighted Supervision== rescales per-position loss to [1, gamma=5] with weight normalization, and ==gradient routing== updates cross-attention parameters with the global loss and the rest with the weighted loss.

## Results

- On WorldArena, Wan 2.2-AC + IMPACT lifts EWMScore **58.65** to **62.46**, best Physics Adherence **55.87** and 3D Accuracy **92.56**; Cosmos-Predict 2.5 action rises **55.91** to **62.53** with Controllability **71.19**.
- On EgoDex human-hand manipulation, FVD drops from **366.12** to **110.94** and FID from **44.71** to **5.79**, with CLIP-Hand **0.921** to **0.952** and Hand IoU **0.693** to **0.772**.
- Ablation confirms complementarity: adding weighted supervision alone gives **+2.89** EWMScore over the MSE backbone, and calibrating the raw attention prior with sampled candidates adds a further **+0.92**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Loss allocation, not extra conditioning signals, can be the binding constraint: reweighting where supervision lands recovers gains previously bought with costly external dense representations.
> - A generative model's own cross-attention already encodes a usable spatial prior, but it is diffuse and needs calibration by prediction difficulty before it can target supervision.
> - When a training signal is derived from the model's own internals, route gradients so the objective cannot optimize its own localization signal into collapse.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models represent a significant area of advancement in artificial intelligence, particularly for embodied agents that require the ability to predict future observations and simulate environmental evolution under commanded actions. These models are crucial for tasks such as long-horizon planning, policy learning, and policy evaluation in interactive environments and robotic manipulation. Recent progress has leveraged large-scale pretrained video generation backbones to achieve high-fidelity predictions and coherent motion modeling.

Despite these advancements, existing world models often encounter difficulties in simulating physically plausible interactions, frequently exhibiting issues such as object deformation, discontinuous motion, weak action coupling, and inconsistent contact. Previous research has attempted to mitigate these limitations by incorporating external spatiotemporally dense representations into the generation process. These representations typically include motion-based signals like optical flow or point trajectories, or geometry-based cues such as depth maps, surface normals, reconstructed 3D scenes, or articulated hand meshes. While these external constraints can improve interaction dynamics, their acquisition often necessitates auxiliary estimators or manual annotations, which can be costly and limit the scalability of training. Furthermore, the quality of supervision derived from these external signals is inherently bounded by their accuracy.

This work diverges from these representation-guided approaches by focusing on the internal mechanics of world model training. It identifies a "supervision-allocation mismatch" within the standard globally averaged mean squared error (MSE) denoising objective, which uniformly weights all spatiotemporal positions. This uniform weighting disproportionately emphasizes prevalent static content, leaving the sparse, dynamic regions critical for action-conditioned interactions under-supervised. This can lead to models that produce visually coherent videos but fail to optimize interaction fidelity. The paper proposes that the internal cross-attention mechanisms of large pretrained video generation backbones already contain a spatiotemporal prior for interaction regions, which can be leveraged to address this mismatch without relying on external, costly representations. This approach seeks to enhance the physical plausibility and visual quality of generated interactions by strategically reallocating supervisory signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable training framework for world models that can generate physically plausible and visually coherent interactions without requiring external dense representations or inference-time modifications. This overarching goal is motivated by a specific identified limitation in current world model training paradigms.

The core motivation stems from the observation of a **supervision-allocation mismatch** in the standard training objective of video world models. This mismatch arises because:

1. **Globally Averaged MSE Denoising Objective:** World models, inheriting from general video generation, commonly employ a globally averaged Mean Squared Error (MSE) denoising objective. This objective uniformly weights the importance of all spatiotemporal positions within a video frame and across time.
2. **Dominance of Static Content:** In typical manipulation scenarios, the majority of the visual content in a video consists of static or slowly changing backgrounds and non-interacting elements. Under a uniform weighting scheme, these prevalent static regions contribute significantly to the overall loss, thereby dominating the optimization signal.
3. **Under-supervision of Dynamic Regions:** Conversely, the sparse dynamic-object regions that are critical for action-conditioned changes—where interactions (e.g., contact, object deformation, rapid motion) occur—receive a disproportionately low amount of supervision signal. These regions, despite their functional importance for defining interaction quality and physical plausibility, occupy a smaller spatial and temporal extent and are thus "outvoted" by the static content in the global average.
4. **Consequence of Mismatch:** As a result, world models trained with this standard objective may learn to produce visually coherent videos by primarily optimizing for the static background, while the intricate dynamics and physical consistency within the interaction regions remain under-optimized. This leads to common failure modes such as object deformation, discontinuous motion, weak action coupling, and inconsistent contact in generated interaction sequences.

Motivated by this identified mismatch, the authors propose leveraging an **internal spatiotemporal prior** that they hypothesize already exists within world models built on large-scale pretrained video generation backbones. Specifically, for manipulation instructions, the cross-attention mechanism, which aligns language tokens with spatiotemporal video representations, can provide attention maps associated with manipulated-object tokens. These maps are posited to serve as a natural prior for regions undergoing action-conditioned changes.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:

- **Develop a scalable framework (IMPACT):** Create a training method that can efficiently leverage this internal prior to reweight denoising supervision.
- **Mitigate supervision-allocation mismatch:** Redirect optimization signal towards sparse, dynamic interaction regions, ensuring they contribute to gradient updates based on their functional importance rather than their spatial extent.
- **Improve interaction generation:** Enhance the physical plausibility, interaction fidelity, and visual quality of generated videos, particularly within critical interaction zones.
- **Avoid external dependencies:** Achieve these improvements without relying on costly external dense representations (like optical flow or 3D geometry) or manual annotations, thereby ensuring training scalability and avoiding limitations imposed by the accuracy of auxiliary estimators.
- **Maintain inference efficiency:** Ensure that the proposed training modifications do not introduce any overhead or changes to the model's architecture or inference procedure.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **IMPACT (Interaction-aware Model training framework with Prior-guided Attention Calibration and Targeting)**, is designed to address the supervision-allocation mismatch in world model training by leveraging internal model signals. It operates within a standard latent video diffusion transformer architecture trained with flow matching and introduces two complementary components: Attention Distribution Sampling (ADS) in the forward pass and Interaction-Weighted Supervision (IWS) in the backward pass.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework is built upon a latent video diffusion transformer that uses flow matching. A variational autoencoder (VAE) converts input videos `x_0` into latent representations `z_0`. Conditioning information `c` includes language instructions `y`, a reference observation `x_ref`, and an action control signal `a`. During training, noise `ϵ` is added to `z_0` to create `z_t`, and the diffusion transformer `v_θ` predicts a velocity target. The standard training objective `L_global` uniformly averages the squared prediction error `ℓ_p` over all spatiotemporal latent positions `Ω`.

#### 3.2. Object-Token Grounding

IMPACT initiates by grounding the manipulated object specified in the language instruction `y`. A frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B model extracts the noun phrase referring to the manipulated object (e.g., "blue bowl" from "Right gripper stacks blue bowl on white tabletop"). This object phrase is then aligned with the sequence of text embeddings produced by the text encoder, identifying the set of corresponding text token positions `O`. This grounding step relies solely on the instruction, avoiding the need for visual annotations or external spatial estimators.

#### 3.3. Forward: Attention Distribution Sampling (ADS)

ADS is responsible for converting the semantic object grounding into an interaction map `G`. This involves three main steps:

1. **Object-conditioned attention distribution:** Within the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) cross-attention layers, visual queries attend to the text-token sequence. For specific blocks `B` and attention heads `H`, the cross-attention probabilities `A_p,j` between a visual position `p` and a text token `j` are computed. `A_p` is then derived by summing the attention probability mass assigned to the object-token set `O` and averaging it over selected blocks and heads. This `A_p` forms an object-conditioned proposal distribution over the video latent, indicating regions relevant to the manipulated object. This process leverages the model's internal attention mechanism without additional visual models or annotations.

2. **Candidate-region sampling:** Instead of using a single deterministic region, ADS samples `K` spatially coherent candidate regions. The attention map `A` is first detached from the computational graph (`sg[A]`) and tempered by an exponent `κ` to control concentration. It is then transformed into logits `Z`. For each candidate `k`, smoothed Gaussian noise `η_k` (sampled on a coarse grid and trilinearly interpolated) is added to `Z`, followed by a sigmoid activation, yielding a soft candidate `S_k`. To ensure fair comparison and fixed area, each `S_k` is then converted into a binary region `M_k` by retaining the largest `ρ|Ω|` values. This ensures all candidates cover the same number of latent positions.

3. **Prediction-error-based weighting:** Each binary candidate region `M_k` is evaluated based on its mean local prediction error `d_k`. This is calculated by averaging the *detached* per-position prediction errors `sg[ℓ_p]` within `M_k`. A higher `d_k` indicates that the content covered by `M_k` is currently more challenging for the model to predict. These candidate errors `d_k` are then converted into normalized weights `α_k` using an exponential function parameterized by `β`. Finally, the interaction map `G` is constructed as a weighted aggregation of all `K` candidate regions `M_k`, where each `M_k` is weighted by its `α_k`. The entire ADS pipeline (including `A_p` computation, sampling, and weighting) is performed without gradient tracking, which prevents the model from directly optimizing its attention prior or candidate selection to reduce the regional objective.

#### 3.4. Backward: Interaction-Weighted Supervision (IWS)

IWS utilizes the interaction map `G` to reallocate the denoising supervision signal.

1. **Weighted objective:** For each latent position `p`, a weight `w_p` is defined as `1 + (γ - 1) sg[G_p]`, where `γ ≥ 1` controls the maximum emphasis applied to interaction regions. Positions outside the estimated interaction region (where `G_p` is low) retain their original unit weight (`w_p=1`), while positions with high `G_p` receive stronger supervision (up to `γ`). The interaction-weighted denoising objective `L_IWS` is then computed as the weighted average of per-position errors `ℓ_p`, normalized by the total sum of `w_p`. This ensures that supervision remains global but is spatially reallocated to emphasize interaction-relevant areas without changing the overall loss scale.

2. **Gradient-decoupled optimization:** To prevent the interaction map (derived from cross-attention) from collapsing onto its own signal, a gradient routing strategy is employed. The DiT parameters are conceptually split into cross-attention parameters `θ_ca` and all other parameters `θ_rest`.
 - Gradients for `θ_ca` are computed using the *original global objective* (`L_global`).
 - Gradients for `θ_rest` are computed using the *interaction-weighted objective* (`L_IWS`).
 This decoupling ensures that the cross-attention mechanism, responsible for estimating interaction regions, is optimized by the standard global objective, while the rest of the model parameters learn from the interaction-focused, spatially reallocated supervision. This strategy aims to maintain the integrity of the attention prior while enhancing the model's ability to generate precise interactions.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference Cost

IMPACT reuses existing computations within the world model's forward pass (cross-attention probabilities, token-wise prediction errors). The additional computational overhead is primarily associated with sampling `K` candidate masks, calculating their mean errors, and constructing the interaction-weighted objective. Critically, no component of ADS or IWS is used during inference, meaning the trained world model retains its original architecture and inference speed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of IMPACT was evaluated through extensive experiments on two distinct manipulation settings: robotic-arm manipulation and human-hand manipulation. The evaluations encompassed both quantitative metrics and qualitative analysis, along with ablation studies to assess component contributions.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

**Robot-arm Manipulation (WorldArena benchmark):**
- **Overall Performance:** IMPACT consistently improved the performance of two different backbone families: Wan 2.2-AC and Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (action).
- **Cosmos-Predict 2.5 (action) + IMPACT:** Achieved the highest overall EWMScore of 62.53, representing a 6.62-point (11.8%) improvement over the MSE-trained baseline (55.91). This variant also demonstrated the best Controllability (71.19) and the second-best Motion Quality (68.22). Specific interaction metrics showed notable gains, with Interaction Quality increasing from 0.5500 to 0.6360 and Action Following from 0.0133 to 0.6260.
- **Wan 2.2-AC + IMPACT:** Raised the EWMScore from 58.65 to 62.46, a 3.81-point (6.5%) improvement. This variant achieved the best Physics Adherence (55.87) and 3D Accuracy (92.56), and the second-best Visual Quality (60.60).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** The best IMPACT result (62.53 EWMScore) surpassed several strong existing world models, including Wan 2.6 (61.86), CtrlWorld (59.70), and WoW (54.88).

**Human-hand Manipulation (EgoDex benchmark):**
- **Visual Metrics:** On the Wan 2.2-AC backbone, IMPACT significantly improved visual quality metrics. FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) was reduced from 366.12 to 110.94, and FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) decreased from 44.71 to 5.79 compared to the MSE-trained action-conditioned baseline.
- **Hand-Interaction Metrics:** IMPACT also led in specialized hand-interaction metrics, improving CLIP-Hand from 0.921 to 0.952 and Hand IoU from 0.693 to 0.772. These gains indicate enhanced local interaction fidelity and more accurate hand localization.
- **Consistency:** These results, combined with the robot-arm findings, indicate that IMPACT provides consistent performance improvements across diverse DiT backbones and control signals.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Generation Comparison (Figure 3):** Visual comparisons illustrated that baseline models often generated blurred or distorted contact regions and occasionally deviated from the specified target or instruction. In contrast, IMPACT produced interactions with sharper contact regions, more coherent object dynamics, and a stable surrounding scene, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating high-fidelity interaction regions.
- **ADS Calibration (Figure 4):** A visualization of the Attention Distribution Sampling process showed that the initial object-conditioned cross-attention map (`A`) was diffuse, spreading across the robot arm, workspace, and background. ADS refined this by sampling candidate regions and weighting them by their detached local prediction errors. Candidates covering the actual contact regions received higher weights, leading to an interaction map (`G`) that was more concentrated around the interacting arm, gripper, and manipulated object. This demonstrated how ADS refined a coarse attention prior into a more targeted supervision map.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Ablation (Table 3):** An ablation study on WorldArena using the Wan 2.2-AC backbone examined the contributions of IWS and ADS:
 - Starting from the baseline AC backbone (58.65 EWMScore), incorporating **IWS alone** provided a substantial gain, increasing the EWMScore to 61.54 (+2.89 points). This indicates the direct benefit of addressing the supervision-allocation mismatch.
 - Adding **ADS on top of IWS** (forming IMPACT) further improved the EWMScore to 62.46 (+0.92 points). This suggests that ADS effectively calibrates the cross-attention prior, leading to a more precise interaction map than directly weighting a raw, coarser prior.
 - The results indicate that both components are complementary and contribute to the overall improvement in interaction-region generation, leading to a total EWMScore improvement of 3.81 points.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for training interaction-aware world models that addresses a fundamental challenge in generating physically plausible and visually coherent interactions. The significance and potential impact of IMPACT can be outlined as follows:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation:** The work identifies and effectively mitigates the "supervision-allocation mismatch" inherent in standard world model training objectives. By reweighting denoising supervision towards sparse, critical interaction regions, IMPACT allows these areas to receive appropriate optimization signal, which was previously overshadowed by static scene content. This leads to more precise and robust modeling of physical interactions.

2. **Scalability and Efficiency:** A key contribution is achieving interaction-aware training *without* reliance on external, spatiotemporally dense representations (e.g., optical flow, depth, 3D structure) or manual annotations. Prior methods that depend on such data often face limitations in training scalability, data acquisition costs, and accuracy bounds imposed by auxiliary estimators. IMPACT's approach, which leverages internal model signals (cross-attention and local prediction errors), makes it inherently more scalable to large datasets and diverse tasks. Furthermore, the framework incurs no additional computational cost during inference, preserving the original model architecture and speed, which is crucial for real-world applications.

3. **Enhanced Interaction Fidelity and Physical Plausibility:** The quantitative and qualitative results consistently demonstrate that IMPACT improves interaction fidelity, physical plausibility, and visual quality in generated videos across both robotic-arm and human-hand manipulation tasks. This enhanced realism is vital for downstream applications.

4. **Generality Across Modalities and Backbones:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness across different DiT backbones (Wan 2.2 and Cosmos-Predict 2.5) and diverse control signals (robot-arm trajectories, human-hand poses) suggests its broad applicability. This generality indicates that IMPACT is a robust training methodology adaptable to various embodied AI scenarios.

5. **Impact on Embodied AI and Robot Learning:** Improved world models that can generate more realistic and physically consistent imagined futures have a direct and substantial impact on embodied artificial intelligence and robotics:
 - **Long-horizon Planning:** More accurate predictions of environmental responses enable more effective planning for complex, multi-step tasks.
 - **Policy Learning:** Realistic simulation provides scalable experience for training robust policies, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection.
 - **Policy Evaluation:** Better simulations allow for more reliable evaluation of learned policies before deployment, enhancing safety and performance.
 - **Foundation for Future Research:** By providing a scalable and effective method for training interaction-aware world models, IMPACT contributes a foundational advance that could accelerate research in areas such as physically grounded simulation, generative agents, and advanced robot control.
