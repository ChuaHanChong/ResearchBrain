# LikePhys: Evaluating Intuitive Physics Understanding in Video Diffusion Models via Likelihood Preference

> [!summary] Summary
> LikePhys introduces a training-free method for evaluating the intuitive physics understanding of video diffusion models by comparing their assigned likelihoods to physically valid versus invalid video pairs. This approach, leveraging denoising loss as a likelihood surrogate, achieved a Kendall
V τ correlation of 0.44 with human judgment and demonstrated effective disentanglement from visual quality metrics.

## Problem

- Video diffusion models (VDMs) frequently generate physically implausible outputs, limiting their utility as reliable world models for applications like robotics.
- Existing evaluation methods for intuitive physics often struggle to isolate physics correctness from visual appearance or introduce subjective biases from human or VLM judgments.
- Traditional "violation-of-expectation" paradigms are not directly applicable to text-conditioned generative models that lack image conditioning or explicit discriminative components.

## Method

- Proposed **LikePhys**, a training-free evaluation method that uses the denoising loss of VDMs as an ELBO-based likelihood surrogate to assess physics understanding.
- Constructed a controlled synthetic benchmark dataset in Blender, featuring 12 distinct physical scenarios with meticulously paired physically valid and invalid videos that are visually consistent.
- Introduced the Plausibility Preference Error (PPE) metric to quantify intuitive physics understanding by measuring the rate at which models assign higher or equal likelihood to invalid videos compared to valid ones.

## Results

- LikePhys's PPE-based model ranking showed a Kendall
V τ correlation of **0.44** with human judgments of physical plausibility, outperforming other state-of-the-art VLM-based evaluators.
- PPE demonstrated effective disentanglement from visual quality metrics, showing no significant correlation with aesthetic quality (r = -**0.05**) or subject consistency (r = -**0.01**).
- DiT-based architectures (e.g., Hunyuan T2V) exhibited lower PPE scores, indicating better physics understanding compared to older UNet-based models, with performance generally improving as model size and number of output frames increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LikePhys provides a robust, objective metric (PPE) that effectively disentangles a model's physics understanding from its capacity for high-fidelity visual generation.
> - Architectural advancements, particularly DiT-based models, and increased model scale (size, number of frames) correlate with improved intuitive physics understanding in VDMs.
> - Current video diffusion models demonstrate stronger understanding of structural and photometric regularities but struggle significantly with complex physics domains like fluid mechanics and fundamental laws requiring global coupling across time and space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video diffusion models (VDMs) have demonstrated an ability to generate visually compelling video sequences. However, a recurring challenge is the generation of physically implausible outputs, which limits their utility as reliable world models in applications such as robotics and autonomous driving. The broader research landscape is increasingly focused on endowing generative models with an understanding of the underlying physical laws that govern visual data.

Historically, evaluating intuitive physics understanding in general vision models has often relied on the "violation-of-expectation" paradigm. This approach assesses a model's ability to judge the plausibility of events, typically using synthetically generated videos that present physically valid versus invalid scenarios. Extending this paradigm to generative models, particularly text-conditioned video diffusion models, has proven challenging due as these models often lack direct image conditioning or are not inherently discriminative.

Recent attempts to evaluate physics in video generative models include methods leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for plausibility judgments or text-conditioned compliance measures. While these methods provide some insights, they often face limitations such as difficulties in disentangling physics correctness from visual appearance, susceptibility to subjective biases, and interpretive variance stemming from VLM judgments. Other approaches, like Physics-IQ and Morpheus, address disentanglement by comparing generated videos with real recordings through pixel/object-mask analysis or by fitting dynamical models to extract physical quantities. However, these often rely on image-conditioned generation, and their applicability to text-to-video generation remains less clear.

This work introduces LikePhys as an alternative evaluation method designed to address these limitations. By leveraging the density estimation capabilities inherent in VDMs, LikePhys proposes a training-free, likelihood-preference evaluation that aims to disentangle physics correctness from visual artifacts. It builds upon the violation-of-expectation paradigm but adapts it to the specific nature of diffusion models by evaluating their internal likelihood assignments, rather than relying solely on generated outputs or external discriminators. This positions LikePhys as a method for objectively assessing the implicit physics understanding learned by VDMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to accurately evaluate the intuitive physics understanding within video diffusion models. The authors identify this as a crucial step towards developing general-purpose, physically plausible world simulators.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and observations:
1. **Physical Plausibility Gap:** Despite advancements in visual quality, current video diffusion models frequently produce outputs that violate basic physical laws. This deficiency hinders their potential as reliable predictive models for real-world scenarios.
2. **Evaluation Difficulty:** Accurately assessing a model's "intuitive physics" capacity is challenging. Existing evaluation methods often struggle to isolate physics correctness from other visual attributes or introduce biases:
 - **Visual Appearance Confound:** Many methods are susceptible to visual appearance biases, where models might be penalized for generating visually unappealing but physically correct videos, or rewarded for aesthetically pleasing but physically incorrect ones.
 - **Subjectivity and Variance:** VLM-based evaluations can introduce subjective biases and interpretive variance due to the nature of natural language prompts and VLM judgments.
 - **Incompatibility with Generative Models:** Traditional violation-of-expectation paradigms, successful for discriminative vision models, are not straightforwardly adaptable to text-conditioned generative models that lack image conditioning or direct discriminative components.
3. **Need for Disentanglement:** There is a need for an evaluation method that can disentangle a model's understanding of physics from its capacity to generate high-fidelity, aesthetically pleasing visuals.
4. **Actionable Insights:** The authors aim to provide a quantitative metric that not only assesses physics understanding but also offers actionable insights into model design choices, training strategies, and domain-specific limitations.

To address these, the key objectives are:
- To propose LikePhys, a novel, training-free evaluation method that utilizes the denoising objective of VDMs as an ELBO-based likelihood surrogate to distinguish between physically valid and invalid video sequences.
- To construct a controlled synthetic benchmark dataset of valid-invalid video pairs, meticulously designed to isolate specific physics violations while maintaining consistent visual appearance.
- To establish the Plausibility Preference Error (PPE) as a metric that quantifies intuitive physics understanding, demonstrating its alignment with human judgment and its independence from visual quality metrics.
- To systematically benchmark the intuitive physics understanding of current state-of-the-art video diffusion models and analyze how factors like model architecture, size, training data, number of frames, and inference settings influence this capacity.
- To identify specific physics domains and laws where current models exhibit strengths or weaknesses, thereby highlighting areas for future research and improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LikePhys methodology is designed to evaluate intuitive physics understanding in video diffusion models (VDMs) by leveraging their implicit density estimation capabilities. The core approach is based on the assumption that a model with robust physics understanding will assign higher likelihoods to physically plausible video sequences compared to visually similar, but physically impossible, ones.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries on Video Diffusion Models and Likelihood Estimation

The method builds upon Diffusion Probabilistic Models, which learn the data distribution by inverting a forward noising process. The denoising network within a VDM predicts the noise added to a corrupted input. The noise prediction loss, typically a Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the ground truth noise and the predicted noise, serves as an ELBO-based surrogate for the negative log-likelihood of the data. Consequently, a lower denoising loss implies a higher likelihood assigned by the model to a given video.

#### 3.2. Physics Understanding as Likelihood Preference

The central tenet of LikePhys is to formalize physics understanding distributionally. A diffusion model is considered to have "perfect intuitive physics understanding" if, for any physically valid video (x$^+$) and a visually matched physically invalid video (x$^-$), it assigns a higher likelihood to x$^+$ than to x$^-$. Given the relationship between denoising loss and likelihood, this translates to the model exhibiting a lower denoising loss for x$^+$ than for x$^-$.

To quantify this, the **Plausibility Preference Error (PPE)** is introduced. For a given valid-invalid video pair, a "violation of expectation" occurs if the model assigns a denoising loss to the valid video that is greater than or equal to the denoising loss of the invalid video. The PPE is calculated by averaging these violations across all pairs within a scenario and then across multiple variations of that scenario. A lower PPE score indicates stronger intuitive physics understanding.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Benchmark Construction

A critical component of LikePhys is its synthetic benchmark dataset. Since real-world data cannot systematically isolate and control physics violations, the authors constructed a dataset using **Blender** (Community, 2018). The dataset comprises 12 distinct physical scenarios, categorized across four broad physics domains:

- **Rigid Body Mechanics (5 scenarios):** Ball Collision, Ball Drop, Block Slide, Pendulum Oscillation, Pyramid Impact. These cover dynamics like collision, periodic motion, gravity, and energy transfer.
- **Continuum Mechanics (2 scenarios):** Cloth Drape, Cloth Waving. These involve material deformation under gravity and aerodynamic forces.
- **Fluid Mechanics (3 scenarios):** Droplet Fall, Faucet Flow, River Flow. These address conservation of mass, viscosity, and surface tension.
- **Optical Effects (2 scenarios):** Moving Shadow, Orbit Shadow. These focus on light-object interactions.

For each scenario, the authors generated R=10 controlled variations. Within each variation, multiple physically valid videos (V$^+$) and physically invalid videos (V$^-$) were created. The invalid variants introduce a single, controlled violation of a specific physical law (e.g., superelastic bounces, ghosted shadows, temporal glitches). Crucially, the valid and invalid videos within a pair are rendered to be visually consistent in terms of camera angle, lighting, textures, and object geometry. This design ensures that any observed difference in the model's likelihood assignment can be primarily attributed to the physical plausibility rather than extraneous visual factors. All videos were rendered at 512 × 512 resolution with 60 frames.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Protocol Implementation

Pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models are evaluated in a zero-shot setting.
- **Inference Settings:** Each model's officially recommended spatial and temporal resolutions and frame rates are adopted.
- **Noise Perturbation:** Input videos are uniformly sampled to the required frame count and resized to the model's native resolution. They are then encoded into a latent representation. Gaussian noise is injected at 10 uniformly sampled diffusion timesteps.
- **Denoising Loss Calculation:** The noised latent is passed through the diffusion model's backbone (with classifier-free guidance at the specified scale), and the Mean Squared Error between the injected noise and the model's predicted noise is recorded. These per-step errors are averaged to yield a scalar denoising loss for each video.
- **Pairwise Comparison and PPE:** For each valid-invalid pair, their respective denoising losses are compared. If L$_{\text{denoise}}$(x$^+$) ≥ L$_{\text{denoise}}$(x$^-$), it counts as a preference error. The PPE is the aggregated rate of these errors across all pairs and variations.
- **Prompts:** Fixed prompt templates describing the physically valid event for each scenario are used, along with a shared negative prompt to discourage artifacts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the intuitive physics understanding of video diffusion models (VDMs):

#### 4.1. Model Ranking and Performance Gaps

- **Overall Performance:** The study benchmarked 12 state-of-the-art VDMs, revealing substantial performance differences in their Plausibility Preference Error (PPE). Lower PPE values indicate stronger physics understanding.
- **Architectural Trends:** Older UNet-based models (e.g., AnimateDiff, ZeroScope) generally exhibited higher error rates (often above 50%), suggesting limited ability to distinguish physically valid from invalid videos. In contrast, more recent DiT-based architectures (e.g., Hunyuan T2V, Wan2.1–T2V–14B, CogVideoX1.5–5B) demonstrated lower PPE scores, indicating a trend of improvement in physics understanding with architectural advancements.
- **Limitations:** Despite improvements, only a few models consistently achieved PPE below the 50% random-guess threshold across many scenarios. This suggests that current models still struggle with complex and chaotic dynamics, and their performance varies significantly across different physics domains.

#### 4.2. Alignment to Human Preference

- **Strong Correlation:** A human study was conducted where annotators rated generated videos for physical plausibility. The LikePhys PPE-based ranking of models showed a stronger overall Kendall’s τ correlation (τ = 0.44) with human annotations compared to other state-of-the-art VLM-based physics evaluators such as VideoPhy, VideoPhy2, and Qwen 2.5 VL. This indicates that models with lower PPE scores tend to be perceived by humans as more physically consistent.
- **Zero-Shot Advantage:** The PPE-based ranking was computed solely on the synthetic benchmark, without requiring model-generated videos for evaluation, unlike the VLM-based methods.

#### 4.3. Disentanglement of Visual Appearance

- **Orthogonal Evaluation:** The study investigated the correlation between PPE and established visual quality metrics from VBench.
 - PPE showed no significant correlation with aesthetic quality (r = -0.05), subject consistency (r = -0.01), or background consistency (r = -0.01). This suggests that LikePhys evaluates physical correctness as an independent dimension, largely orthogonal to general visual quality.
 - Moderate positive correlations were observed with motion smoothness (r = 0.15) and temporal flickering (r = 0.12). This is expected, as smooth, flicker-free motion can partially indicate physical plausibility, but these are not the primary focus of the metric.
- These results support the claim that LikePhys effectively disentangles physical reasoning from visual appearance.

#### 4.4. Analysis on Influencing Factors

- **Model Size and Training Data:** Larger models generally exhibited lower average PPE, indicating improved physics understanding. This trend was consistent across different architectures, with DiT-based models showing superior scalability. Similarly, larger training datasets generally correlated with lower average PPE, although this correlation was weaker than that observed for model size.
- **Number of Frames:** Most models showed a steady decrease in PPE as the number of output frames increased, implying that longer temporal contexts enable models to capture more complex interactions and improve implicit physical reasoning.
- **Classifier-free Guidance (CFG) Strength:** Varying CFG strength had only a marginal impact on physics understanding. This suggests that physics plausibility is primarily determined by the learned model distribution rather than inference-time noise prediction calibration, allowing CFG to be tuned for visual quality without compromising physics understanding.

#### 4.5. Analysis Across Physics Domains and Laws

- **Domain-Specific Performance:**
 - **Fluid Mechanics** consistently showed the highest average PPE and greatest variability across models, particularly for complex scenarios like river flows (often exceeding 70% PPE). This highlights the difficulty in modeling nonlinear, multiscale fluid dynamics.
 - **Rigid Body Mechanics** and **Continuum Mechanics** exhibited moderate errors with tighter agreement among models.
 - **Optical Effects** incurred the lowest errors, possibly due to photometric and geometric regularities being well-constrained by large-scale image and video corpora during training.
- **Law-Specific Performance:**
 - **Temporal Continuity** showed the largest variance across models, indicating challenges in maintaining long-range coherence in motion, especially with occlusions or many frames.
 - **Spatial Continuity** and **Conservation of Energy/Mass** also yielded high errors, consistent with the absence of explicit global constraints in standard training objectives.
 - **Geometric Invariance** and **Optical Consistency** were better satisfied, likely due to priors learned from abundant static imagery and short clips.
 - **Material Response** remained moderately challenging, reflecting difficulties with contact events, friction, and surface compliance.
- Overall, models demonstrate capacity for structural and photometric regularities but struggle with physical laws requiring global coupling across time and space.

#### 4.6. Ablation on Evaluation Protocol Design

- **Timestep Selection:** Investigating denoising loss differences across the entire noise schedule showed consistent preference trends, but the peak separation varied. Uniform sampling across 10 timesteps was adopted as a balance between computational efficiency and stable estimation.
- **Prompt Robustness:** The method demonstrated robustness to variations in text prompts for text-to-video models. No significant change in discriminative performance was observed with different prompt variants, confirming that the controlled data generation isolates physics-law compliance as the primary error source.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LikePhys represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of intuitive physics understanding in video diffusion models, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Objective and Robust Evaluation

LikePhys provides a training-free, objective, and quantitative metric (PPE) for evaluating implicit physics understanding in VDMs. By leveraging the model's internal likelihood estimates and meticulously controlled synthetic valid-invalid video pairs, it effectively disentangles physics correctness from visual appearance or aesthetic quality. This addresses a critical limitation of previous VLM-based or subjective evaluation methods, leading to more reliable and interpretable assessments. The demonstrated strong alignment with human judgment further validates its utility.

#### 5.2. Guiding Model Development

The systematic benchmarking and detailed analysis across various model architectures, scaling factors, and physics domains offer actionable insights for researchers and developers.
- **Architectural Design:** The finding that DiT-based models outperform UNet-based models in physics understanding highlights the benefits of Transformer-based architectures for spatio-temporal reasoning in this context.
- **Scaling Laws:** The clear trend of improved physics understanding with increased model size and number of frames provides guidance for future scaling efforts in VDM development.
- **Targeted Improvement:** Identifying specific physics domains (e.g., fluid mechanics) and laws (e.g., temporal continuity, conservation principles) where current models struggle points to critical areas requiring focused research. This could involve developing physics-aware training objectives, incorporating explicit physical constraints, or designing architectures with enhanced memory and multi-scale reasoning capabilities.

#### 5.3. Advancing World Models

By enabling a more rigorous assessment of physical plausibility, LikePhys contributes directly to the grand challenge of building general-purpose physically plausible world simulators. A VDM that can assign higher likelihoods to physically correct sequences and lower likelihoods to incorrect ones demonstrates a foundational step towards internalizing the laws of physics. Such models are crucial for applications requiring predictive capabilities, such as:
- **Robotics:** For robots to interact intelligently and safely with the physical world, they need to anticipate outcomes based on physical laws. VDMs with strong physics understanding could serve as predictive components in robotic control.
- **Autonomous Driving:** Simulating complex driving scenarios with realistic physics is vital for testing and training autonomous vehicles. Improved physics understanding in generative models could lead to more accurate and reliable driving simulators.

#### 5.4. Benchmark Portability

The demonstrated applicability of LikePhys to other intuitive physics benchmarks, like IntPhys Dev, indicates its portability and generalizability beyond the specific benchmark created for this study. This suggests it could become a standard tool for evaluating physics understanding across various generative vision models and datasets.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Directions

The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also open doors for future research:
- **Data Curation Cost:** The reliance on meticulously controlled synthetic data, while crucial for isolating physics violations, is resource-intensive. Future work could explore more automated or semi-automated methods for generating such matched pairs or adapting the methodology to leverage real-world data with less explicit control.
- **Access to Noise Prediction:** The requirement for direct access to the model's noise prediction error limits the evaluation of closed-source models. This highlights a need for standardized APIs or collaborative efforts to make such internal model states accessible for scientific evaluation.
- **Empirical Assessment:** LikePhys provides an empirical assessment of physics understanding without making prior assumptions about its underlying causes (e.g., training data distribution, optimization). Future work could delve deeper into diagnosing the root causes of observed strengths and weaknesses.

In conclusion, LikePhys offers a valuable framework for understanding and improving the physical reasoning capabilities of video diffusion models, moving the field closer to developing generative models that can reliably simulate and interact with the physical world.
