# LikePhys: Evaluating Intuitive Physics Understanding in Video Diffusion Models via Likelihood Preference

> [!summary] Summary
> LikePhys introduces a training-free method for evaluating the intuitive physics understanding of video diffusion models by comparing their assigned likelihoods to physically valid versus invalid video pairs. This approach, leveraging denoising loss as a likelihood surrogate, achieved a Kendall
V τ correlation of 0.44 with human judgment and demonstrated effective disentanglement from visual quality metrics.

## Problem

- Video diffusion models (VDMs) frequently generate physically implausible outputs, limiting their utility as reliable world models for applications like robotics.
- Existing evaluation methods for intuitive physics often struggle to isolate physics correctness from visual appearance or introduce subjective biases from human or VLM judgments.
- Traditional "violation-of-expectation" paradigms are not directly applicable to text-conditioned generative models that lack image conditioning or explicit discriminative components.

## Method

- Proposed **LikePhys**, a training-free evaluation method that uses the denoising loss of VDMs as an ELBO-based likelihood surrogate to assess physics understanding.
- Constructed a controlled synthetic benchmark dataset in Blender, featuring 12 distinct physical scenarios with meticulously paired physically valid and invalid videos that are visually consistent.
- Introduced the Plausibility Preference Error (PPE) metric to quantify intuitive physics understanding by measuring the rate at which models assign higher or equal likelihood to invalid videos compared to valid ones.

## Results

- LikePhys's PPE-based model ranking showed a Kendall
V τ correlation of **0.44** with human judgments of physical plausibility, outperforming other state-of-the-art VLM-based evaluators.
- PPE demonstrated effective disentanglement from visual quality metrics, showing no significant correlation with aesthetic quality (r = -**0.05**) or subject consistency (r = -**0.01**).
- DiT-based architectures (e.g., Hunyuan T2V) exhibited lower PPE scores, indicating better physics understanding compared to older UNet-based models, with performance generally improving as model size and number of output frames increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LikePhys provides a robust, objective metric (PPE) that effectively disentangles a model's physics understanding from its capacity for high-fidelity visual generation.
> - Architectural advancements, particularly DiT-based models, and increased model scale (size, number of frames) correlate with improved intuitive physics understanding in VDMs.
> - Current video diffusion models demonstrate stronger understanding of structural and photometric regularities but struggle significantly with complex physics domains like fluid mechanics and fundamental laws requiring global coupling across time and space.
