# Video-Skill-CoT: Skill-based Chain-of-Thoughts for Domain-Adaptive Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEO-SKILL-COT (VIDEO-SKOT) framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Models for video reasoning by automatically generating skill-aware Chain-of-Thought supervisions and employing skill-specific expert modules. This approach leads to average performance improvements of up to 5.70 accuracy points over baselines on benchmarks like VSI-Bench, alongside more accurate and coherent reasoning traces as rated by human evaluators.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning struggle to adapt to the diverse and domain-specific reasoning skills required for various video understanding tasks.
- Current CoT methods often rely on fixed, general-purpose reasoning traces that are insufficient for tasks demanding specialized skills like temporal, spatial, or narrative understanding.
- Manually annotating skill-specific reasoning traces for video QA datasets is labor-intensive and not scalable, limiting the development of adaptable video reasoning systems.

## Method

- An automated two-step process to generate ==skill-based CoT annotations==, involving MLLMs to extract, cluster, and produce skill-conditioned rationales from video QA pairs.
- ==Skill-specific expert learning== where N_experts LoRA modules are trained to specialize in distinct question groups, allowing the MLLM backbone (e.g., LLaVA-Video) to adapt efficiently to varied reasoning capabilities.
- A combined training objective that minimizes cross-entropy loss for both the final answer and the ==skill-grounded CoT tokens==, fostering explicit and verifiable multi-step reasoning.

## Results

- VIDEO-SKOT consistently improved accuracy across diverse benchmarks, including a **+4.10 point** gain on E.T.Bench, **+5.70** on VSI-Bench, and **+1.59** on CinePile compared to fine-tuned LLaVA-Video baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both skill-based CoT and skill-specific expert modules are essential and contribute synergistically to the framework's overall performance.
- Human evaluation showed skill-based CoT significantly outperformed regular CoT, scoring higher in correctness (**+2.09**), relevance (**+1.64**), and coherence (**+1.93**) on a 1-5 Likert scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective video reasoning in MLLMs requires domain-adaptive, skill-aware Chain-of-Thought processes, moving beyond generic reasoning traces.
> - High-quality, interpretable, and skill-conditioned CoT supervisions can be automatically constructed at scale using advanced MLLMs, eliminating the need for manual annotation.
> - Modularizing MLLM fine-tuning with lightweight LoRA experts for specific skill sets enables efficient specialization and enhances the model's adaptability across diverse video domains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on VIDEO-SKILL-COT is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **video understanding, multimodal large language models (MLLMs), and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.**

**Video Understanding with MLLMs:** Understanding complex video content is a grand challenge in AI, requiring models to integrate rich spatiotemporal cues and perform sophisticated reasoning. The advent of MLLMs, which combine powerful vision encoders with LLMs, has significantly advanced this field. Models like LLaVA-Video, Video-ChatGPT, and Video-LLaMA2 demonstrate impressive capabilities in answering questions about video content. However, these general-purpose MLLMs often struggle with tasks requiring highly specialized or domain-specific reasoning skills. For instance, a model proficient in identifying objects might fail to infer subtle emotional cues in a cinematic narrative or precisely ground temporal events.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** Originating in the NLP domain, CoT reasoning has proven effective in improving the logical reasoning capabilities of LLMs by breaking down complex problems into intermediate, explicit steps. This paradigm has been extended to MLLMs for video understanding, leading to "video-of-thought" approaches. While these methods generate multi-step rationales, they typically rely on "fixed, general-purpose reasoning traces" that are often insensitive to the specific skills required by different video domains (e.g., temporal understanding for event sequencing vs. spatial understanding for object relationships vs. narrative understanding for movie plots). The paper visually demonstrates this limitation by showing that questions from different video datasets cluster distinctly, implying unique underlying skill requirements.

**Addressing the Domain Adaptation Gap:** This paper directly addresses a critical limitation in current CoT-enabled MLLMs: their difficulty in adapting to **domain-specific reasoning skills**. Existing methods, even with CoT, tend to apply a monolithic reasoning process. VIDEO-SKOT proposes a solution that makes CoT reasoning *skill-aware* and *domain-adaptive*. By automatically identifying and leveraging relevant reasoning skills, the framework aims to enable MLLMs to perform more nuanced and accurate reasoning across diverse video content, from egocentric recordings to cinematic narratives. It shifts from generic, one-size-fits-all CoT to a modular, specialized approach that is more robust and interpretable. This positions the work as a significant step towards more adaptable and intelligent video reasoning systems, bridging the gap between general MLLM capabilities and the need for domain-specific expertise.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the VIDEO-SKILL-COT research are:

1. **To overcome the limitations of existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning methods in video understanding:** While CoT has improved complex video understanding, current approaches often employ fixed, general-purpose reasoning traces. The authors argue that these methods struggle to adapt to the diverse and domain-specific skills (e.g., event detection, spatial relation understanding, emotion understanding) required by various video contents.
2. **To enable domain-adaptive video reasoning for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** The goal is to develop a framework that allows MLLMs to effectively learn and apply reasoning strategies tailored to the unique demands of different video domains (e.g., cinematic narratives, egocentric videos, indoor scenes). This adaptability is crucial for robust performance across a wide range of real-world video tasks.
3. **To automatically construct and leverage skill-aware CoT supervisions:** A key motivation is to move beyond manual annotation of skill-specific reasoning traces, which is labor-intensive and not scalable. The paper aims to develop an automated method for generating high-quality, skill-conditioned CoT rationales, making the approach practical for various datasets.
4. **To enhance the interpretability and accuracy of video reasoning:** By explicitly breaking down reasoning into skill-based steps and utilizing specialized expert modules, the authors seek to make the reasoning process more transparent and verifiable, ultimately leading to more accurate answers. The qualitative analysis and human evaluation reinforce this objective.
5. **To develop a modular and parameter-efficient framework for skill specialization:** The authors are motivated to create an architecture that allows MLLMs to specialize in different skills without requiring extensive re-training or large computational resources, utilizing techniques like LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) for efficiency.

In essence, the research seeks to introduce a more intelligent, adaptive, and interpretable video reasoning paradigm by fundamentally re-thinking how Chain-of-Thought is applied in MLLMs, moving from generic reasoning to skill-aware, domain-specific expertise.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VIDEO-SKOT (VIDEO-SKILL-COT) is a novel video understanding framework that addresses domain-adaptive video reasoning by constructing and leveraging skill-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision. The methodology is divided into two main components:

#### 3.1. Skill-based CoT Annotation

This component automatically constructs high-quality, skill-conditioned CoT rationales for video QA tasks, eliminating the need for manual annotation. It involves two steps:

- **Step 1: Skill Description & Clustering (Fig. 1 Right-(a))**
 - **Skill Definition:** A skill is defined as a shared, high-level reasoning capability (e.g., temporal ordering, visual counting, spatial understanding) that recurs across multiple video QA examples within a specific domain.
 - **Skill Extraction:** For each training question `q` and its ground-truth answer `a`, an MLLM (specifically, GPT-4 is used for skill description, prompted with questions and answers) is prompted to describe the necessary skill to answer it (e.g., "Estimate distance between two objects using visual cues"). These descriptions are concise (6-12 words), focus on visual/temporal concepts, avoid audio cues, replace specific object names with generic terms, and eschew vague terms.
 - **Skill Taxonomy Formation:** All extracted skill descriptions are encoded into text embeddings using `all-mpnet-base-v2 SentenceTransformers`. These embeddings are then subjected to k-means clustering (with `k=N_skills=10`) to form a shared skill taxonomy. Each cluster centroid represents a prototypical skill.

- **Step 2: Skill-based CoT Collection (Fig. 1 Right-(b))**
 - **Skill Selection:** For each `(q, v)` pair, the top-K (specifically, top 3) most relevant skills are assigned by comparing the question's embedding to the clustered skill centroids.
 - **Skill-aware CoT Generation:** An MLLM (Gemini-2.0 Flash with video input is used) is prompted to produce intermediate sub-questions and corresponding answers, guided by the selected skills. This breaks down the complex question into manageable, skill-focused sub-tasks.
 - **CoT Cohesion & Filtering:** The generated sub-QA pairs are merged into a coherent CoT paragraph that explicitly reflects the assigned reasoning skills. To ensure quality, an LLM evaluator (GPT-4) is used to verify and filter out reasoning steps irrelevant to the correct answer, based on a specific prompt (Fig. 11 in the Appendix).
 - **Output:** Each training example is ultimately annotated with relevant expert labels `e` and a verified, skill-grounded CoT trace `r_s`.

#### 3.2. Skill-specific Expert Learning

With the skill-based CoT annotations prepared, this component focuses on training specialized MLLM experts.

- **Question Grouping:** All questions in the training set `D_train` are projected into the text embedding space (using `all-mpnet-base-v2 SentenceTransformers`) and undergo k-means clustering (with `k=N_experts=5`). Unlike the skill clusters in Sec. 3.2, these clusters represent groups of *questions*. Each training example is assigned to its closest question group.
- **Modularized Fine-tuning with LoRA:** Parameter-efficient training is conducted using `N_experts` expert LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) modules. Each LoRA expert specializes in a specific set of reasoning capabilities, corresponding to one of the `N_experts` question groups. This ensures task-specific adaptation while minimizing interference across skills. The backbone MLLM used is LLaVA-Video (7B), with additional experiments on Qwen2.5-VL (7B).
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained by minimizing a combined cross-entropy loss for predicting both the final answer (`L_answer`) and the CoT tokens (`L_CoT`), with a balancing weight `λ` (set to 0.5):
 `L = L_answer + λL_CoT = ℓ(f(q, v), a) + λℓ(f(q, v), r_s)`
 where `f_e` is the skill-specific expert MLLM chosen for the given `q`.
- **Inference:** During test time, an incoming test question is assigned to the closest question group by finding the nearest question embedding centroid. The corresponding LoRA expert for that group is then activated to generate the skill-guided reasoning trace `r_s` and the final answer `a`.

This methodology ensures that the model not only performs multi-step reasoning but does so by leveraging automatically discovered, domain-relevant skills through specialized modules, enhancing adaptability and interpretability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the consistent effectiveness of VIDEO-SKOT across various video understanding benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Outperformance on Diverse Benchmarks:** VIDEO-SKOT consistently surpasses strong baselines, including open-source MLLMs (mPLUG-Owl, Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaMA2, LLaVA-Video) and powerful closed-source models (GPT4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro/Flash).
 - On **E.T.Bench** (temporal understanding), VIDEO-SKOT achieved 22.21% accuracy, a +4.10 point improvement over fine-tuned LLaVA-Video.
 - On **VSI-Bench** (spatial understanding), it scored 53.15%, a +5.70 point gain.
 - On **CinePile** (movie narrative understanding), it reached 57.88%, a +1.59 point improvement.
 - These results highlight the framework's strong domain adaptation capabilities.
- **Ablation Studies:** The ablation studies confirm the complementary roles of the two main components:
 - Removing both skill-based CoT and skill-specific experts leads to significant performance degradation (41.04% average accuracy).
 - Removing only skill-specific expert modules (but keeping skill-CoT) results in lower performance (42.91% avg) than the full model (44.41% avg).
 - Removing only skill-based CoT (but keeping skill-specific experts) also degrades performance significantly (38.53% avg).
 - This demonstrates that both skill-based CoT annotations and the skill-specific expert learning framework are crucial and synergistically contribute to the model's performance.
- **Human Evaluation of CoT Quality:** A human evaluation involving five researchers assessed the quality of regular CoT versus skill-based CoT.
 - Skill-based CoT consistently outperformed regular CoT across all criteria: Correctness (+2.09 points), Relevance (+1.64 points), and Coherence (+1.93 points) on a 1-5 Likert scale.
 - This provides strong evidence that the proposed method produces more accurate, relevant, and human-readable explanations.
- **Robustness with Different Backbones:** VIDEO-SKOT maintained its superior performance when evaluated with the Qwen2.5-VL (7B) backbone on VSI-Bench, achieving the highest average performance (40.76%) compared to baselines.
- **Cross-dataset Generalization:** When trained on ET-Bench (source) and evaluated on CinePile (target), VIDEO-SKOT achieved an average performance of 56.21%, outperforming a fine-tuned LLaVA-Video (53.85%) and performing competitively with a LLaVA-Video model fine-tuned directly on CinePile (56.29%). This demonstrates its ability to transfer skill-guided reasoning across domains.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Improved CoT Annotations:** Comparison of regular CoT and skill-based CoT annotations (Fig. 2) shows that skill-based CoT is more structured and focused. Regular CoT often includes irrelevant details, while skill-based CoT identifies relevant skills and breaks down the task into focused sub-questions, leading to a clearer, more pertinent reasoning trace.
- **Enhanced Inference Rationale:** At inference time, models trained with regular CoT often produce incorrect reasoning and wrong answers. In contrast, VIDEO-SKOT generates temporally grounded and precise rationales that effectively support accurate answer generation (Fig. 3).
- **Visualization of Learned Skills:** Visualizations of clustered skill descriptions (Fig. 7) confirm that different video domains emphasize distinct skill sets, while some general skills are shared. For example, CinePile focuses on emotional/narrative skills, VSI-Bench on spatial understanding, and E.T.-Bench on temporal/action-related skills. The analysis of selected skills per task (Figs. 4, 5) further validates that VIDEO-SKOT effectively leverages these domain-specific skills.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that VIDEO-SKOT significantly enhances video understanding capabilities by introducing a novel, automated, and modular approach to skill-aware CoT reasoning, leading to improved accuracy, interpretability, and domain adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VIDEO-SKILL-COT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of video understanding and multimodal AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Domain Adaptability for MLLMs:** The most critical contribution is enabling MLLMs to adapt their reasoning capabilities to diverse and specialized video domains. By moving beyond fixed, general-purpose CoT, VIDEO-SKOT allows models to acquire and apply tailored skills for tasks ranging from temporal event understanding to nuanced narrative interpretation. This makes MLLMs more robust and applicable to a wider array of real-world video analysis scenarios, which often demand specialized expertise.

2. **Automated Skill-Aware CoT Generation:** The proposed automatic construction of skill-based CoT annotations is a major practical breakthrough. Manual annotation of reasoning traces, especially for domain-specific skills, is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. By leveraging powerful LLMs (like GPT-4 and Gemini-2.0) to extract skills, cluster them, and generate skill-conditioned rationales, the framework makes high-quality, interpretable supervision accessible at scale for any video QA dataset. This democratizes the development of more intelligent video reasoning systems.

3. **Improved Interpretability and Explainability:** The skill-based CoT directly breaks down complex video reasoning into explicit, understandable steps tied to specific skills. As validated by human evaluation, these rationales are more correct, relevant, and coherent than traditional CoT. This increased interpretability is crucial for building trust in AI systems, debugging model failures, and understanding *how* an MLLM arrives at its conclusions, which is particularly important in sensitive applications.

4. **Parameter-Efficient Specialization:** The use of lightweight LoRA adapters for skill-specific expert learning offers an efficient way to specialize MLLMs without catastrophic forgetting or the need to train entirely new, large models. This modular approach is computationally advantageous, making it feasible to deploy specialized reasoning modules for various domains without incurring massive computational overhead.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** VIDEO-SKOT lays a strong foundation for future work in several directions:
 - **Dynamic Skill Discovery:** Further research could explore more dynamic or hierarchical skill discovery methods, moving beyond fixed `k` clusters.
 - **Integration with Other Modalities:** While the paper focuses on visual/temporal skills, the framework could be extended to integrate audio or other multimodal cues into skill definitions.
 - **Active Learning for Skill Improvement:** The automated annotation pipeline could be integrated with active learning to iteratively improve skill descriptions and CoT quality.
 - **Robustness to Hallucinations:** While the paper notes occasional inaccuracies and hallucinations as a limitation, the skill-guided rationales might inherently offer better mechanisms for detecting and mitigating such issues by providing clearer intermediate checks.

In conclusion, VIDEO-SKOT pushes the state-of-the-art in video understanding by introducing an intelligent, automated, and modular framework for skill-based reasoning. Its impact will likely be felt in applications requiring deep, domain-specific understanding of video content, from content analysis and security to educational platforms and assistive technologies, paving the way for more human-like video intelligence.
