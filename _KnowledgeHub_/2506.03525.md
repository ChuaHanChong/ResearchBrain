# Video-Skill-CoT: Skill-based Chain-of-Thoughts for Domain-Adaptive Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The VIDEO-SKILL-COT (VIDEO-SKOT) framework enhances Multimodal Large Language Models for video reasoning by automatically generating skill-aware Chain-of-Thought supervisions and employing skill-specific expert modules. This approach leads to average performance improvements of up to 5.70 accuracy points over baselines on benchmarks like VSI-Bench, alongside more accurate and coherent reasoning traces as rated by human evaluators.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning struggle to adapt to the diverse and domain-specific reasoning skills required for various video understanding tasks.
- Current CoT methods often rely on fixed, general-purpose reasoning traces that are insufficient for tasks demanding specialized skills like temporal, spatial, or narrative understanding.
- Manually annotating skill-specific reasoning traces for video QA datasets is labor-intensive and not scalable, limiting the development of adaptable video reasoning systems.

## Method

- An automated two-step process to generate ==skill-based CoT annotations==, involving MLLMs to extract, cluster, and produce skill-conditioned rationales from video QA pairs.
- ==Skill-specific expert learning== where N_experts LoRA modules are trained to specialize in distinct question groups, allowing the MLLM backbone (e.g., LLaVA-Video) to adapt efficiently to varied reasoning capabilities.
- A combined training objective that minimizes cross-entropy loss for both the final answer and the ==skill-grounded CoT tokens==, fostering explicit and verifiable multi-step reasoning.

## Results

- VIDEO-SKOT consistently improved accuracy across diverse benchmarks, including a **+4.10 point** gain on E.T.Bench, **+5.70** on VSI-Bench, and **+1.59** on CinePile compared to fine-tuned LLaVA-Video baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both skill-based CoT and skill-specific expert modules are essential and contribute synergistically to the framework's overall performance.
- Human evaluation showed skill-based CoT significantly outperformed regular CoT, scoring higher in correctness (**+2.09**), relevance (**+1.64**), and coherence (**+1.93**) on a 1-5 Likert scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective video reasoning in MLLMs requires domain-adaptive, skill-aware Chain-of-Thought processes, moving beyond generic reasoning traces.
> - High-quality, interpretable, and skill-conditioned CoT supervisions can be automatically constructed at scale using advanced MLLMs, eliminating the need for manual annotation.
> - Modularizing MLLM fine-tuning with lightweight LoRA experts for specific skill sets enables efficient specialization and enhances the model's adaptability across diverse video domains.
