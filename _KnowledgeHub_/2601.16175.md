# Learning to Discover at Test Time

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce TTT-Discover, a test-time training framework that enables Large Language Models to learn and adapt during problem-solving, leading to new state-of-the-art solutions in diverse scientific and engineering challenges. The method achieved improved mathematical bounds, faster GPU kernels, and top scores in programming competitions using an open-source model at low computational cost.

## Problem

- Current AI systems, including powerful Large Language Models (LLMs), struggle with genuine scientific discovery, which requires generating ideas beyond their training data and existing human knowledge.
- Traditional test-time search methods with frozen LLMs do not allow the models to internalize new ideas or learn from failed attempts, limiting their adaptive capabilities.
- The goal of scientific discovery, which is to find a single, exceptional solution, mismatches the objective of standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) that typically maximizes expected reward for generalization.

## Method

- The approach formulates scientific discovery problems as ==Markov Decision Processes==, allowing an LLM policy to continuously learn and improve by interacting with the problem environment at test time.
- It implements an ==adaptive entropic objective function==, `J_β(θ) = E[log E[e^(βR)]]`, which dynamically adjusts `β` to prioritize finding single maximum reward solutions over average rewards.
- A ==PUCT-inspired reuse mechanism== (Polynomial Upper Confidence Trees) selects optimal initial states from a buffer of past solutions, fostering optimistic exploration and leveraging previous high-quality attempts.

## Results

- Achieved new upper bounds in mathematics, including improving Erdős' Minimum Overlap Problem (**0.380876**) and the First Autocorrelation Inequality (**1.50286**) over previous bests.
- Discovered state-of-the-art GPU kernels for the TriMul competition across various hardware, outperforming human experts with up to **2x speedup** on A100 and over **15%** on H100.
- Secured **1st place** in two AtCoder Heuristic programming competitions, 'Purse Seine Fishing' and 'Apple Incremental Game,' by outperforming all human submissions and existing AI benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Allowing LLMs to continually train and internalize feedback directly from the problem environment at test time enables them to truly adapt and generate novel solutions, moving beyond static search paradigms.
> - Customizing the RL objective and search strategy, specifically with an adaptive entropic objective and PUCT-inspired reuse, is critical for optimizing for a single, maximum-reward solution in discovery problems.
> - State-of-the-art scientific discovery can be achieved efficiently with open-source LLMs and modest computational resources, democratizing access to advanced AI-assisted research.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"Learning to Discover at Test Time" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Test-Time Training (TTT), and AI for scientific discovery.

**A. Test-Time Training (TTT) and Continual Learning:**
The paper positions itself as a significant advancement in the burgeoning field of Test-Time Training (TTT). Unlike conventional machine learning, where a model is frozen after training and deployed statically, TTT allows models to adapt and learn from individual test instances. This concept echoes older ideas like locally weighted regression and dynamic evaluation but is critically re-envisioned for modern LLMs and complex problems. The authors draw a parallel with human learning, where experience during "testing" (e.g., a challenging assignment or a daily commute) simultaneously serves as "training," improving future performance on specific tasks. This work extends the TTT paradigm from improving generalization under distribution shifts or few-shot reasoning (as seen in prior TTT work with self-supervision or nearest-neighbor fine-tuning) to actively *discovering* novel solutions.

**B. AI for Scientific Discovery:**
A central theme is using AI to push the boundaries of human knowledge by discovering new state-of-the-art solutions in scientific and engineering domains. This goes beyond merely automating existing tasks or solving well-defined problems where solutions are known. The paper targets problems where solutions often lie "out-of-distribution" from any pre-training data, demanding genuine innovation from the AI. This ambition places it alongside projects like AlphaFold (protein folding) and AlphaProof (mathematical reasoning), which have demonstrated AI's capacity for complex scientific problem-solving.

**C. Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Language Models (LLMs) as Agents:**
The core methodology involves applying RL directly to LLMs at test time. This is a powerful combination, building on the success of LLMs in code generation and reasoning, and leveraging RL's ability to optimize for specific objectives through iterative trial-and-error. Recent trends see LLMs increasingly employed as autonomous agents capable of interacting with environments, performing multi-step reasoning, and generating code. TTT-Discover specifically tailors RL to the unique demands of discovery problems, where the goal is a single, exceptional solution rather than optimizing average performance.

**D. Evolution Beyond Prior Search-Based Methods (AlphaEvolve, AlphaZero):**
The paper critically evaluates and extends existing test-time *search* methods, particularly evolutionary algorithms like AlphaEvolve. Prior work often relied on prompting a *frozen* LLM and using hand-crafted heuristics to guide the search process through a buffer of past attempts. TTT-Discover argues that while search scales with compute, *learning* has historically been more transformative in tackling hard problems (e.g., AlphaGo/AlphaZero mastering Go by learning policies, not just searching). By allowing the LLM to *continually train* on its own problem-specific experiences, TTT-Discover directly addresses the limitation of frozen models.

**E. Concurrent and Related Work:**
The authors transparently acknowledge concurrent efforts sharing similar high-level ideas, such as EvoTune, MiGrATe, and ThetaEvolve. They differentiate TTT-Discover by emphasizing its specialized learning objective and search subroutine, which are explicitly designed for the "discovery" goal (i.e., maximizing tail-end performance for a single best solution) rather than standard RL objectives. This highlights a subtle but crucial distinction in tailoring AI algorithms for truly novel problem-solving. The paper also discusses related concepts like "One Example RL" (training on a single *training* problem to generalize) and "RL on the Test Set" (improving average performance over a *set* of test problems), clarifying how TTT-Discover's focus on a *single test problem* for *peak performance* is unique.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Learning to Discover at Test Time" is to empower AI systems to achieve *new states of the art* in challenging scientific and engineering problems by enabling them to learn and adapt during the problem-solving process itself.

The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulty of **scientific discovery problems**. By definition, such problems require solutions that go beyond existing human knowledge and, consequently, beyond any data an AI model might have been pre-trained on. This implies a significant challenge in "out-of-distribution generalization," where standard, statically trained AI models often struggle.

The authors draw an intuitive analogy to human problem-solving: when faced with a genuinely hard task, humans don't just rely on pre-existing knowledge. Instead, they engage in a cyclical process of trial, failure, partial success, and most importantly, *learning from these experiences* to inform subsequent attempts. This iterative learning process leads to the internalization of new ideas, eventually enabling a breakthrough.

Current AI approaches for such problems, particularly those using LLMs, have largely focused on **test-time search**. Methods like AlphaEvolve generate many attempts by prompting a *frozen* LLM and use heuristic-driven evolutionary strategies to refine solutions. While effective, these methods treat the LLM as a static tool; the model itself does not fundamentally improve or "understand" the new ideas required to solve the specific, difficult problem at hand.

TTT-Discover's core motivation and objective are to address this limitation by integrating **reinforcement learning (RL) directly into the test-time problem-solving loop**. This means the LLM is not just searching the solution space but is actively *training* its own weights based on the experience gained from attempting to solve the specific test problem. The authors argue that this mirrors the historical success of learning over pure search in AI for complex domains like Go (AlphaGo/AlphaZero), and is crucial for problems requiring genuine discovery.

The paper highlights two critical distinctions of discovery problems from standard RL:
1. **Goal is a single best solution:** Unlike standard RL, which aims to find a policy that maximizes *average* reward across many attempts or generalizes to a class of problems, TTT-Discover's objective is to produce *one outstanding solution* for the specific test problem. The policy is merely a means to this end.
2. **Focus on the specific problem:** The training is tailored to solve *this very problem*, not to generalize to other problems. This allows for highly specialized and efficient learning, as the model's "training data" comes directly from its interactions with the problem environment.

By focusing on these unique aspects, TTT-Discover aims to move beyond simple search and enable LLMs to truly learn and "discover" novel, high-performing solutions for scientific challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TTT-Discover's methodology is built upon a novel integration of reinforcement learning principles with large language models, specifically tailored for discovery problems at test time.

**A. Environment Formulation:**
The first step is to frame each scientific problem as an RL environment, conceptually a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Problem Description (d):** The specific scientific problem is provided as a text description, serving as context for the LLM.
- **State (s):** A candidate solution to the problem (e.g., a mathematical construction, GPU kernel code, an algorithm).
- **Action (a):** Generated by the LLM policy, consisting of thinking tokens and/or code (e.g., Python code to modify a step function, Triton code for a kernel).
- **Transition Function (T):** Defines how an action `a` transforms the current state `s` into a new state `s'`. This often involves parsing and executing the generated code.
- **Reward Function (R(s)):** A continuous, verifiable metric that quantifies the quality of a candidate solution (e.g., inverse runtime for a kernel, a mathematical bound, an algorithm's score, or a denoising metric). Rewards are 0 for invalid solutions.
- **Discovery:** Defined as finding a state `s` where `R(s) > r_sota`, where `r_sota` is the best-known reward.

**B. Core Algorithm: Test-Time Training to Discover (Algorithm 1):**
TTT-Discover operates iteratively. In each step `i`:
1. **Reuse:** An initial state `s_i` and context `c_i` are sampled from a buffer `H_i` using a specialized `reuse` heuristic.
2. **Action Generation:** The LLM policy `π_θ_i` generates an action `a_i` conditioned on `d`, `s_i`, and `c_i`.
3. **Transition:** The environment applies `a_i` to `s_i` to produce a new state `s'_i`.
4. **Reward Evaluation:** The reward `r_i = R(s'_i)` is calculated.
5. **Buffer Update:** The current attempt `(s_i, a_i, s'_i, r_i)` is added to the buffer `H_i+1`.
6. **Policy Training:** The LLM's weights `θ_i` are updated to `θ_i+1` using a specialized `train` function based on the collected experience.
The process continues for `N` steps, returning the solution with the highest reward found.

**C. Innovations Over Naive RL and Search Methods:**
The paper highlights that standard RL algorithms are designed for policies that maximize *average* reward and generalize across problems. Discovery, however, demands maximizing *peak* performance on a *single* problem. To address this, TTT-Discover introduces two key components:

1. **Entropic Objective (for `train` subroutine):**
 - Instead of optimizing for the expected reward `E[R(s,a)]`, TTT-Discover uses an entropic objective `J_β(θ) = E[log E[e^(βR(s,a))]]`. This objective strongly favors actions with *maximum* reward (as `β -> ∞`), which aligns with the goal of finding a single, outstanding solution.
 - To maintain stability and adapt to the learning progress, an *adaptive β(s)* is used. `β(s)` is dynamically adjusted per initial state by constraining the KL divergence of the induced policy. This prevents too-large `β` early in training (instability) and too-small `β` later (vanishing advantages).
 - A KL penalty term is also added to the advantage function, similar to PPO/GRPO, to regularize policy updates.

2. **PUCT-inspired Reuse (for `reuse` subroutine):**
 - Instead of simple `Best-of-N` sampling or naive state reuse, TTT-Discover employs a Prioritized Upper Confidence Tree (PUCT)-inspired heuristic to select initial states from the buffer `H`. This balances exploration and exploitation.
 - **Max Reward Tracking:** Unlike standard PUCT in AlphaZero (which tracks mean value), `Q(s)` in TTT-Discover tracks the *maximum* reward achieved by any descendant solution starting from state `s`. This directly aligns with the "discovery" goal.
 - **Rank-based Prior (P(s)):** `P(s)` is proportional to the rank of `s` in the buffer, favoring high-reward states.
 - **Exploration Bonus:** The standard `sqrt(1+T)/(1+n(s))` term ensures that under-visited states (low `n(s)`) still receive an exploration bonus, preventing premature convergence to local optima.
 - **Lineage Blocking:** To promote diversity, the full lineage of an expanded state (ancestors and descendants in the current batch) is temporarily blocked from being selected again.

**D. Implementation Details:**
- **LLM:** An open-source model, `gpt-oss-120b`, is used. This is a crucial aspect, demonstrating that SOTA results can be achieved without proprietary frontier models.
- **Fine-tuning:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) with rank 32 is employed for efficient parameter updates at test time.
- **Compute:** Training runs are performed on Tinker, an API by Thinking Machines, at a cost of only a few hundred dollars per problem, making the approach practical and accessible.
- **Training Schedule:** 50 training steps, with 512 rollouts (solutions generated) per step.
- **Context Window:** The `gpt-oss-120b` context window of 32,768 tokens is managed by limiting the combined length of the prompt and thinking tokens.

This detailed methodology enables TTT-Discover to iteratively refine an LLM's ability to generate superior solutions, specifically targeting the discovery of new benchmarks rather than general competence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TTT-Discover demonstrates impressive performance across a diverse set of scientific and engineering challenges, consistently setting new state-of-the-art results. A key highlight is achieving these breakthroughs using an open-source LLM (gpt-oss-120b) and publicly available code, in contrast to previous SOTA often requiring closed, proprietary models.

**A. Mathematics Problems:**
TTT-Discover tackled open problems where even small numerical improvements are significant.
- **Erdős’ Minimum Overlap Problem:** Improved the upper bound to **0.380876**, surpassing AlphaEvolve's 0.380924 and the best human result of 0.380927. The discovered solution was an asymmetric 600-piece step function, different from prior symmetric constructions.
- **First Autocorrelation Inequality (AC1):** Achieved a new upper bound of **1.50286**, improving upon ThetaEvolve's 1.50314 and the human best of 1.50973. This involved a novel 30,000-piece step function. The underlying algorithms evolved from gradient-based optimization to leveraging linear programming with heuristics focusing on tight constraints.
- **Second Autocorrelation Inequality (AC2):** The method yielded a bound of 0.959, which did not surpass the AlphaEvolve V2 result of 0.9610.
- **Circle Packing:** Matched the best-known constructions for n=26 and n=32 using the Qwen3-8B model, though no new SOTA was set here. The algorithms found were simpler than prior work, relying on geometric initialization and SLSQP optimization.
- **Expert Review (Prof. Davide Torlo):** Confirmed the mathematical significance and verifiability of the improved bounds for Erdős' problem and AC1.

**B. GPU Kernel Engineering (GPUMode Competition):**
TTT-Discover applied to optimizing GPU kernels, a crucial component of modern AI.
- **TriMul (Triangular Matrix Multiplication):** Achieved new SOTA runtimes across all tested NVIDIA (A100, H100, B200) and AMD (MI300X) GPUs. For example, on H100, it reached **1161 µs**, significantly faster than the best human's 1371 µs. The discovered kernels focused on fusing memory-intensive operations (LayerNorm, gating) and delegating heavy matrix multiplications to highly optimized libraries (cuBLAS/rocBLAS). Notably, the kernel trained only on H100s generalized well to other architectures.
- **DeepSeek MLA (Multi-head Latent Attention):** While performing competitively (e.g., 1669.1 µs on MI300X Instance 1 compared to best human's 1653.8 µs), TTT-Discover did not statistically significantly outperform the top human submissions, often relying on `torch.compile` rather than fine-grained Triton optimization.
- **Expert Review (GPUMode Organizers):** Validated the fusion strategy and efficiency of the TriMul kernels, identifying it as similar to human expert approaches but executed "on better."

**C. Algorithm Engineering (AtCoder Heuristic Contest):**
The method demonstrated prowess in solving complex, real-world optimization problems.
- **ahc039 ("Purse Seine Fishing"):** Achieved a score of **567,062**, marginally outscoring the best human (566,997) and significantly outperforming previous AI baselines (e.g., ShinkaEvolve at 558,026, which used stronger frontier models). TTT-Discover started from an existing strong AI solution and improved it to 1st place.
- **ahc058 ("Apple Incremental Game"):** Starting from scratch, TTT-Discover achieved a score of **848,414,228**, surpassing the best human score (847,674,723) and other AI agents (e.g., ALE-Agent at 848,373,282).
The discovered algorithms for both problems involved sophisticated combinations of greedy heuristics, simulated annealing, and efficient data structures.

**D. Biology (Single-Cell Analysis Denoising):**
- **Denoising Task:** TTT-Discover improved the overall denoising score on the OpenProblems benchmark to **0.71 (PBMC) and 0.73 (Tabula)**, compared to 0.64 for the MAGIC baseline. The solution, initialized with MAGIC code, incorporated gene-adaptive transform ensembling, low-rank SVD refinement, and log-space polishing.
- **Expert Review (Prof. Eric Sun):** Acknowledged the empirical improvements on key metrics but emphasized the need for further validation regarding biological relevance for downstream tasks, highlighting a common challenge in AI for biology.

**E. Ablation Studies:**
Ablations on the TriMul kernel engineering task (H100) confirmed the efficacy of TTT-Discover's core components:
- The full TTT-Discover (adaptive entropic objective + PUCT reuse) achieved the best runtime (1203.10 µs).
- Ablating the entropic objective (constant β, expected reward) or the PUCT reuse (ϵ-greedy, no reuse) consistently led to slower runtimes and stagnation in reward distribution, demonstrating their synergistic importance. Naive RL and Best-of-N performed significantly worse.

In summary, TTT-Discover consistently demonstrated its ability to discover novel, state-of-the-art solutions across diverse, complex domains, validating its unique RL-at-test-time approach for specialized problem-solving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Learning to Discover at Test Time" represents a significant conceptual and empirical advance, with broad implications for both fundamental AI research and its application to scientific and engineering challenges.

**A. New Paradigm for Scientific Discovery with AI:**
The most profound impact of TTT-Discover is its demonstration of a viable paradigm for **AI-driven scientific discovery**. By enabling LLMs to *learn* at test time, rather than merely *search* with frozen knowledge, the work fundamentally shifts how AI can tackle problems requiring genuine innovation. This move from "solving known problems better" to "discovering new solutions" is crucial for accelerating scientific progress across disciplines. It validates the hypothesis that, for highly specific and challenging tasks, continuous, problem-centric learning can outperform static models, even those pre-trained on vast datasets.

**B. Enhanced Capability of LLMs for Specialized Tasks:**
The success of TTT-Discover showcases that even open-source LLMs can be dramatically enhanced for specialized, demanding tasks through targeted, test-time fine-tuning. This could influence future LLM development and deployment strategies, moving towards highly adaptive, self-improving agents tailored for specific applications. It suggests that the true potential of LLMs might lie not just in their pre-training scale but in their capacity for dynamic, context-specific adaptation.

**C. Accessibility and Reproducibility in AI Research:**
A major practical contribution is achieving state-of-the-art results with an **open model (gpt-oss-120b)** and **publicly available code**. This directly challenges the trend of proprietary frontier models dominating SOTA benchmarks. By lowering the barrier to entry, TTT-Discover promotes greater transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative development in AI research, allowing more researchers to build upon and integrate these powerful techniques without extensive resources or proprietary API access. The reported cost of "a few hundred dollars per problem" further underscores its accessibility.

**D. Advancing RL and Continual Learning:**
The paper refines Reinforcement Learning by introducing an **entropic objective** and a **modified PUCT reuse strategy** specifically tuned for discovery. This demonstrates how RL algorithms can be adapted to optimize for "peak performance" rather than average outcomes, a critical distinction for fields like scientific discovery. It also significantly advances the field of Test-Time Training by pushing beyond self-supervised generalization tasks to active, reward-driven problem-solving and model improvement.

**E. Tangible Impact Across Diverse Domains:**
The empirical results are striking. Setting new state-of-the-art in mathematics (Erdős' problem, autocorrelation inequalities), GPU kernel engineering (TriMul up to 2x faster), algorithm design (1st place in AtCoder competitions), and biology (improved single-cell denoising) illustrates the broad applicability and effectiveness of TTT-Discover. The corroborating expert reviews from leaders in these fields lend significant credibility to the reported breakthroughs.

**F. Future Research Directions:**
The authors explicitly outline crucial future work, particularly extending TTT-Discover to problems with sparse or binary rewards, and non-verifiable domains. This highlights that while the current method is powerful, it also opens new avenues for research into making AI-driven discovery even more general and robust.

In conclusion, "Learning to Discover at Test Time" is a landmark paper that introduces a powerful and versatile methodology for AI-driven scientific discovery. By uniquely integrating reinforcement learning with LLMs at test time, it not only pushes the boundaries of AI capabilities but also promotes a more open, accessible, and iterative approach to tackling humanity's most challenging problems.
