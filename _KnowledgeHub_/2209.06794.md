# PaLI: A Jointly-Scaled Multilingual Language-Image Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Google Research's PaLI, a jointly-scaled multilingual language-image model, achieves high performance across diverse vision-language tasks, including 149.1 CIDEr on COCO Captioning and 84.3% accuracy on VQAv2. The model leverages a new 4-billion parameter vision transformer (ViT-e) and a 100-language WebLI dataset, indicating that scaling vision components offers a favorable return on investment for multimodal capabilities.

## Problem

- Most existing large-scale vision-language models were English-centric, limiting global accessibility and applicability.
- Prior multimodal models often scaled language components disproportionately, under-exploring the benefits of larger vision backbones for multimodal tasks.
- A need existed for a flexible and unified interface to handle a wide array of diverse vision, language, and multimodal tasks within a single framework.

## Method

- **PaLI** employs a modular ==encoder-decoder Transformer== architecture, reusing pre-trained ==mT5== for language and large ==Vision Transformers== (**ViT-G**, **ViT-e**) for vision.
- The model introduces **WebLI**, a 10 billion image-text pair dataset covering over 100 languages, used in a diverse ==pre-training mixture== of eight objectives.
- All tasks are framed as a unified ==image+query to answer== text generation problem, simplifying the interface for various unimodal and multimodal tasks.

## Results

- PaLI-17B achieves **149.1 CIDEr** on COCO Captioning and **84.3%** accuracy on VQAv2, outperforming prior generative models.
- The model demonstrates robust multilingual understanding, surpassing previous models on Crossmodal-3600 (**53.6 average CIDEr**) and xGQA (**50.6% average accuracy**) across over **35 languages**.
- PaLI-17B retains the language understanding capabilities of its mT5 backbone, performing comparably on English-only (SuperGLUE) and multilingual (XTREME) language tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical validation demonstrates that jointly scaling both vision and language components leads to superior multimodal performance.
> - Scaling the visual component (ViT-G to ViT-e) provides a better return on investment in accuracy improvement per parameter compared to solely increasing language model capacity.
> - Training with massively multilingual data not only boosts multilingual performance but also unexpectedly improves performance on English-only tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of PaLI is situated firmly within the prevailing trend of scaling neural networks for both language and vision tasks, and more recently, for multimodal tasks. The authors explicitly acknowledge this context, citing milestones like T5, GPT-3, PaLM, and Chinchilla in language, and advancements in CNNs and Vision Transformers (ViTs) in vision. The core premise is that increased model capacity, coupled with vast amounts of data, leads to significant performance gains and emergent capabilities.

Prior to PaLI, numerous efforts had been made in vision-language (V&L) modeling, including SimVLM, Florence, CoCa, GIT, BEiT-3, and Flamingo. These models generally fall into two main categories: those relying on object detection systems for feature extraction, which limits scalability, and those, like PaLI, that leverage the Transformer architecture for end-to-end joint modeling. PaLI distinguishes itself from previous multimodal models in several key ways:

- **Joint and Balanced Scaling:** A critical contribution of PaLI is its emphasis on the *joint scaling* of both vision and language components, rather than disproportionately scaling only one modality (e.g., Flamingo with its 70B language model and 435M vision encoder). PaLI argues that scaling the vision backbone (introducing the 4-billion parameter ViT-e) yields a better return on investment in terms of accuracy improvement per parameter/FLOP, providing a crucial scaling roadmap for future V&L models. This challenges the prevailing assumption that language model capacity alone is the primary driver of multimodal performance.
- **Unified Task Interface:** PaLI adopts a flexible "image-and-text to text" generative interface, similar to other concurrent works like OFA and Unified-IO. This simplifies the diverse array of V&L tasks (captioning, VQA, scene-text understanding, object detection) into a single, consistent sequence-to-sequence problem, enabling transfer learning across tasks without task-specific heads.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained Unimodal Backbones:** To reduce training costs and capitalize on existing capabilities, PaLI reuses powerful pre-trained unimodal models: mT5 (a multilingual text-to-text Transformer) for language and large Vision Transformers (ViT-G and the new ViT-e) for vision. This approach allows PaLI to build upon the strong understanding and generation capabilities already present in these foundational models.
- **Massively Multilingual Training:** A significant innovation is PaLI's commitment to multilingualism. Unlike many large-scale image-text datasets (e.g., Conceptual Captions, ALIGN) which are English-only, PaLI introduces WebLI, a dataset containing 10 billion images and texts in over 100 languages. This enables PaLI to perform multimodal tasks across a wide linguistic spectrum, addressing a major gap in the global accessibility of advanced V&L models.

In essence, PaLI pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI by rigorously exploring the benefits of balanced cross-modal scaling, demonstrating the power of a unified generative interface, and unlocking large-scale multilingual capabilities through a novel dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of PaLI are multifaceted, reflecting a comprehensive effort to advance the state of multimodal AI:

- **Extend Large Language Model Success to Multimodality:** The core motivation is to generalize the remarkable success of large language models (LLMs) in various NLP tasks to the joint modeling of language and vision. This involves creating a model that can process both modalities seamlessly.
- **Develop a Flexible and Unified Task Interface:** To achieve broad applicability, a key objective was to design a single, consistent interface capable of handling a wide array of vision, language, and multimodal tasks. By casting all tasks as "image+query to answer" text generation, PaLI aims for simplicity, modularity, and scalability.
- **Quantify the Benefits of Joint Scaling:** A central hypothesis and motivation was to empirically investigate the impact of scaling both vision and language components concurrently. Specifically, the authors sought to understand if significantly larger vision models, commensurate with the scale of LLMs, could yield substantial performance gains in multimodal settings, thus providing a roadmap for future development. This led to the training of ViT-e, a 4-billion parameter Vision Transformer.
- **Efficiently Leverage Existing Capabilities:** The substantial computational cost of training very large models motivated the objective of reusing existing, pre-trained unimodal backbones (mT5 for language, ViT for vision). This strategy aimed to transfer their learned capabilities into the multimodal domain and reduce the overall training footprint.
- **Enable Massively Multilingual Multimodal Understanding:** Recognizing the English-centric nature of most existing V&L datasets and models, a critical objective was to develop a truly multilingual multimodal model. This necessitated the creation of WebLI, a massive image-text dataset spanning over 100 languages, to foster language-image understanding across diverse linguistic contexts.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Ultimately, the goal was to achieve state-of-the-art results across a variety of vision and language benchmarks (e.g., image captioning, visual question answering, scene-text understanding), demonstrating the efficacy of PaLI's design principles and scaling strategy.
- **Maintain Modular and Scalable Design:** The authors aimed for an architecture that remains simple and modular, facilitating further scaling and integration into different research and application contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PaLI's methodology is characterized by a modular architecture, strategic reuse of pre-trained unimodal models, a novel large-scale multilingual dataset, and a diverse mixture of pre-training objectives.

#### 3.1. Architecture

PaLI employs a simple yet powerful encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, fundamentally building upon the T5 framework.
- **Language Component:** For text understanding and generation, PaLI reuses pre-trained mT5 (massively multilingual Text-to-Text Transformer) models. The paper explores two sizes: mT5-Large (1B parameters) and mT5-XXL (13B parameters).
- **Vision Component:** For image processing, PaLI utilizes large Vision Transformers (ViT). It starts with the 1.8B parameter ViT-G and introduces a new, even larger 4B-parameter model called **ViT-e** ("enormous"). ViT-e maintains the same architectural principles and training recipe as ViT-G but scales up width, depth, and MLP dimensions, with its final weights "souped" from two cool-down phases.
- **Integration:** The Vision Transformer's output patch features (visual "tokens") are fed directly into the text encoder of the mT5 model via cross-attention, without any pooling. This design allows for a direct interaction between visual and textual information within the Transformer's attention mechanisms.
- **Unified Interface:** All tasks, whether unimodal or multimodal, are framed as a "image+query to answer" text generation problem. This means an input image is combined with a text prompt (e.g., "Generate the alt_text in EN:", "Answer in EN: what is this?") to produce a text output. This unified interface is used consistently across pre-training and fine-tuning.
- **Model Sizes:** Three main variants are presented:
 - **PaLI-3B:** mT5-Large (1B) + ViT-G (1.8B) ≈ 3B parameters.
 - **PaLI-15B:** mT5-XXL (13B) + ViT-G (1.8B) ≈ 15B parameters.
 - **PaLI-17B:** mT5-XXL (13B) + ViT-e (4B) ≈ 17B parameters.

#### 3.2. Data

To support its large-scale and multilingual objectives, PaLI introduces a new dataset:
- **WebLI Dataset:** This is a massively multilingual image-language dataset built from 10 billion images and 12 billion alt-texts from the public web, covering over 100 languages. It also includes 29 billion image-OCR pairs. To balance quality and scale, the authors filter WebLI to retain the top 10% (approximately 1 billion high-quality image-text pairs). The dataset undergoes near de-duplication against common V&L benchmarks to prevent data leakage.
- **Training Mixture:** PaLI is pre-trained on a diverse mixture of eight objectives (1.6 billion total examples) designed to span a wide range of general capabilities:
 1. **Span Corruption:** Text-only data for general language understanding (from mT5 pre-training).
 2. **Split-Captioning:** On WebLI alt-text, where a portion of the alt-text is used as input and the remainder as target.
 3. **Captioning:** On CC3M-35L (Conceptual Captions translated into 35 languages).
 4. **OCR:** On WebLI OCR-text data.
 5. **English and Cross-Lingual VQA:** Using native and translated VQ²A-CC3M-35L data, with English answers.
 6. **English and Cross-Lingual VQG (Question Generation):** Also based on VQ²A-CC3M-35L, generating questions for given answers.
 7. **English-only Object-Aware VQA:** Derived from Open Images, querying for object presence, lists, or attributes.
 8. **Object Detection:** A generative task from public datasets (Open Images, Visual Genome, Object365), predicting bounding box coordinates and labels as text.

#### 3.3. Model Training

- All PaLI variants are trained for one epoch over the entire 1.6B example pre-training dataset at 224x224 image resolution. Crucially, during this phase, **only the language component parameters are updated; the vision component (ViT) is frozen.**
- For the largest model, PaLI-17B, an additional high-resolution (588x588) pre-finetuning phase is performed for 10k steps (10M examples). In this phase, a simplified mixture of OCR, CC3M-35L, and VQ²A data is used, and **all PaLI parameters (including ViT) are updated.**
- Training utilizes JAX/Flax with the T5X framework, using Adafactor optimizer, linear warmup, and inverse square-root learning rate decay. The largest model (PaLI-17B) was trained on 1,024 Google Cloud TPU v4 chips for 7 days, with an additional 3 days for the high-resolution phase on 512 TPU v4 chips.

#### 3.4. Fine-tuning

- PaLI models are fine-tuned on various downstream tasks using standard cross-entropy loss. Specific resolutions (e.g., 490x490, 588x588 for PaLI-17B) and hyperparameters are set for each benchmark.
- For tasks involving scene-text (e.g., TextCaps, TextVQA), PaLI leverages OCR strings generated by a public automatic service as input.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PaLI achieves state-of-the-art results across a diverse range of vision-language tasks and provides critical insights into model scaling and multilingual capabilities.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Image Captioning (English):** PaLI-17B sets a new high on COCO Captioning (Karpathy split) with a CIDEr score of 149.1, surpassing strong generative models like BEiT-3 (147.6) and Flamingo (138.1) without CIDEr-optimization. It also performs strongly on out-of-domain captioning benchmarks like NoCaps (124.4 CIDEr), outperforming prior models on recognizing long-tail objects. For scene-text captioning, PaLI-17B achieves 160.4 on TextCaps and 124.7 on VizWiz-Cap (with OCR input).
- **Visual Question Answering (English):** In the challenging open-vocabulary generative setting, PaLI-17B achieves 84.3% accuracy on VQAv2, outperforming Flamingo (82.1%) and remarkably, even closed-vocabulary classification models like BEiT-3 (84.2%) and CoCa (82.3%). For questions requiring external knowledge, PaLI-17B achieves 64.5% accuracy on OKVQA, pushing the SOTA for the pretrain-finetune setup significantly. It also demonstrates strong performance on OCR-dependent VQA tasks like TextVQA (73.06%) and VizWiz-QA (74.4% test-dev / 73.3% test-std) when provided with OCR input.
- **Multilingual Captioning & VQA:** PaLI-17B significantly outperforms previous SOTA models on Crossmodal-3600 (53.6 average CIDEr across 35 languages) and cross-lingual/multilingual VQA benchmarks xGQA (50.6% average accuracy) and MaXM (67.3% on Hindi, 65.6% on Thai), demonstrating robust multilingual understanding.

#### 4.2. Model Scaling Insights

- **Joint Scaling is Key:** The most significant finding is the empirical validation that *jointly scaling both the vision and language components* leads to superior performance. Scaling from PaLI-3B to PaLI-15B (largely language scaling) shows gains, but the transition from PaLI-15B to PaLI-17B (which involves scaling the vision component from ViT-G to ViT-e, a +2B parameter increase) yields substantial performance improvements across multiple benchmarks.
- **Vision Scaling ROI:** Counter-intuitively, the paper demonstrates that scaling the visual component (ViT-G to ViT-e, an approximate 13% overall model size increase) provides a *better return on investment* (accuracy improvement per parameter) than much larger increases in the language model capacity. This suggests significant untapped potential in further scaling vision backbones for V&L tasks.
- **High-Resolution Pre-training Benefits:** The additional high-resolution pre-training phase for PaLI-17B consistently provides an extra performance boost (+2.0 points average), indicating the importance of higher visual fidelity.

#### 4.3. Language-Understanding Capabilities Preservation

- Despite being heavily pre-trained on V&L tasks, PaLI-17B successfully retains the language understanding capabilities of its unimodal mT5-XXL backbone. It performs comparably on English-only (SuperGLUE) and multilingual (XTREME benchmarks like XNLI, XQuAD, TyDiQA-GoldP) language tasks, preventing catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Image Classification

- PaLI-17B demonstrates strong zero-shot image classification capabilities by generating class names, achieving 72.11% on ImageNet and performing well on various out-of-distribution ImageNet variants. This outperforms 1-shot learning results from models like Flamingo.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Task Mixture Effectiveness:** Ablations confirm the positive impact of a diverse pre-training mixture. "Split-captioning" on WebLI is identified as the most critical objective. Object-related components also significantly boost performance, and OCR objectives specifically improve scene-text tasks.
- **Importance of Initializing from Unimodal Checkpoints:** Experiments show that initializing PaLI from pre-trained mT5 and ViT checkpoints is crucial; training from scratch leads to drastically inferior performance.
- **Freezing ViT during Pre-training:** Freezing the ViT backbone during the initial pre-training phase yields minor but consistent performance improvements on downstream tasks like COCO.
- **Benefits of Multilingual Data:** Training with the full multilingual WebLI data not only significantly boosts multilingual performance but also surprisingly leads to *better performance on English-only tasks* (e.g., COCO captions), suggesting that diverse multilingual data provides richer learning signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PaLI represents a significant leap forward in multimodal artificial intelligence, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Foundation Model for Multimodal AI:** PaLI emerges as a powerful foundation model capable of handling an unprecedented array of vision-language and even unimodal tasks within a single, coherent framework. Its strong performance across diverse benchmarks positions it as a versatile tool for general-purpose multimodal understanding and generation.
- **Rethinking Scaling Strategies:** The most impactful finding is the empirical evidence for the critical importance of *jointly and proportionally scaling* both vision and language components. This challenges prior trends that disproportionately focused on scaling language models for multimodal tasks. PaLI's demonstration that scaling the vision backbone yields a higher return on investment per parameter provides a crucial "scaling roadmap" for future research, guiding the design of more efficient and capable multimodal models. This insight could lead to a redistribution of research efforts and computational resources in the field.
- **Democratization of Multimodal AI through Multilingualism:** By building upon a massively multilingual dataset (WebLI) and demonstrating robust performance across over 100 languages, PaLI makes significant strides towards breaking the English-centric bias prevalent in AI research. This has immense potential for broadening the global accessibility and applicability of advanced AI systems, enabling users worldwide to interact with and benefit from multimodal models in their native languages.
- **Efficiency in Model Development:** The strategy of reusing powerful, pre-trained unimodal models (mT5 and ViT) for multimodal learning highlights an efficient paradigm for developing large-scale AI. This approach allows researchers to leverage extensive prior training efforts and readily available knowledge, significantly reducing the cost and complexity of developing new multimodal capabilities.
- **Unified Generative Interface:** The success of PaLI's simple "image+text to text" generative interface for a wide variety of tasks demonstrates the power of framing diverse problems uniformly. This elegant design simplifies model architecture, training, and deployment, offering a flexible paradigm for future multimodal systems.
- **Advancing Specific Applications:** Beyond foundational research, PaLI's state-of-the-art results directly impact practical applications. Its superior performance in image captioning, visual question answering (especially in open-vocabulary settings), and scene-text understanding (e.g., reading text in images) makes it highly valuable for assistive technologies, content creation, information retrieval, and human-computer interaction.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper not only provides solutions but also illuminates new avenues for research. The observed benefits of multilingual data for English-only tasks, and the unexplored potential of even larger vision backbones, suggest exciting directions for further investigation into cross-lingual transfer and multimodal scaling laws.

In summary, PaLI is a landmark contribution that pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI by showcasing the profound benefits of balanced component scaling and a truly multilingual approach, setting a new standard for performance and paving the way for more globally inclusive and capable AI systems.
