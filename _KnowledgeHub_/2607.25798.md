# Transformer Transformer: A Unified Model for Motion-Conditioned Robot Co-design

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Transformer Transformer, a unified Diffusion Transformer model for motion-conditioned robot co-design that generates robot embodiments, evaluates their performance, and controls them to execute target trajectories. The model demonstrates zero-shot optimization for unseen reward functions and multi-trajectory tasks, achieving faster optimization and superior performance compared to traditional methods, and was validated on a real-world ALOHA setup.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation capabilities are heavily dependent on physical embodiment, leading to unequal transfer of learned policies across different robot designs.
- Traditional robot co-design methods struggle with complex control dynamics, often relying on simplified representations or heuristic-based optimization, while data-driven approaches can overfit to specific reward functions.
- Existing cross-embodiment control and co-design pipelines are often fragmented, involving iterative processes with components trained on disjoint representations and objectives, which is computationally inefficient.

## Method

- A unified robot representation called ==RoboToken== is developed, which is a complete, flexible, and continuous-valued tokenization scheme for articulated robots' embodiment, states, and actions, suitable for neural network processing.
- A single ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== architecture, **Transformer Transformer**, is employed to simultaneously serve as a robot design generator, a performance critic, and an embodiment-aware controller.
- A unified ==dynamics training objective==, coupled with "==Dynamics Self-Guidance==," enables reward-agnostic learning, allowing for zero-shot optimization of user-specified reward functions at inference time without retraining.

## Results

- Transformer Transformer outperformed CMA-ES in both optimization speed and achieved performance across various design spaces, evaluating **128 candidates** much faster than CMA-ES could evaluate **5**.
- Dynamics Self-Guidance improved individual sample quality, yielding higher rewards (e.g., **242 to 341** on bimanual tasks) compared to the Zeroth-Order approach when sampling budgets were limited.
- An ALOHA robot optimized by the model for a cloth unfolding task reduced maximum joint speed by **30%** (from **2.57** to **1.82 rad/s**) and tracking position error by **73%** (from **13.0 cm** to **3.5 cm**) in real-world validation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A comprehensive, continuous tokenization (RoboToken) of robot embodiment and dynamics allows a single model to reason across diverse robot designs, states, and actions, compressing complex robot descriptions significantly.
> - Training a Diffusion Transformer on general robot dynamics enables it to act as a multi-functional co-design agent, unifying generation, evaluation, and control within one architecture.
> - Leveraging dynamics self-guidance during diffusion allows for efficient, first-order optimization of robot designs for novel reward functions at inference time, enhancing the quality of generated designs in challenging landscapes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is engaged in developing systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks. A prevalent approach involves training policies based on embodiment-agnostic end-effector trajectories, often derived from human demonstrations. However, the execution capabilities of these trajectories are critically dependent on the physical embodiment of the robot. This dependence frequently results in unequal transfer of learned policies across different robot platforms, highlighting that the robot's physical design can be a limiting factor in manipulation performance.

This research addresses the problem of robot co-design, where the robot's physical embodiment is optimized in conjunction with its operational requirements. Traditional methods for embodiment design and optimization often rely on gradient-based techniques, which typically require simplified embodiment representations (e.g., cage-based models) or heuristics. These methods generally struggle with domains involving complex control dynamics and contact interactions, such as those encountered by legged robots. Data-driven approaches offer flexibility but frequently exhibit overfitting to specific reward functions, limiting their generalization capabilities. Some dynamics-guided generation methods attempt to generalize across rewards by leveraging dynamics models; however, they commonly employ fixed open-loop or simplified kinematic actions, thereby sidestepping the challenge of full control problem integration.

In the realm of cross-embodiment control, GPU-accelerated simulators coupled with reinforcement learning (RL) have become standard for locomotion and whole-body control. While efforts exist to distill single-embodiment expert policies into shared policies that condition on embodiment, their robot representations are often incomplete, restricting their transferability and applicability to diverse designs.

Broader robot co-design strategies, including evolutionary algorithms and learned components (generators, critics, controllers), have demonstrated success in finding high-value designs given sufficient computational resources and a simulator. However, these pipelines typically involve iterative processes with components trained on disjoint representations and objectives, which can lead to inefficiencies and difficulties in acceleration.

This work positions itself within this landscape by proposing a unified framework that seeks to overcome these limitations. It aims to integrate robot embodiment generation, reward evaluation (critic), and cross-embodiment control into a single model. By developing a comprehensive robot representation and a unified architecture, the research endeavors to provide a more efficient and generalized solution for motion-conditioned robot co-design, moving beyond fragmented approaches and addressing the interdependencies between robot morphology and control performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that the physical embodiment of a robot is a critical yet often under-emphasized factor influencing its manipulation performance. The capabilities of a robot to effectively perform specific motions are inherently linked to its design. This presents a challenge where policies learned for manipulation tasks, often represented as embodiment-agnostic end-effector trajectories, exhibit varied performance across different robot bodies. This disparity in performance, while a limitation, also highlights an opportunity for optimizing robot embodiments to suit particular tasks.

The key objectives of this work are formalized as addressing the problem of motion-conditioned robot co-design. Given a set of target end-effector trajectories, typically derived from human demonstrations, and a set of user-defined reward functions, the objective is to automatically generate complete robot embodiments that maximize these rewards.

The concept of a "complete robot embodiment" in this context refers to a comprehensive consideration of a robot's design, encompassing large and heterogeneous design spaces. This includes reasoning over kinematic parameters (e.g., joint configurations, degrees of freedom), geometric attributes (e.g., link dimensions, primitive types), and dynamic properties (e.g., mass, inertia). Furthermore, the "user-defined reward functions" are designed to be flexible, capable of evaluating both intrinsic robot characteristics (such as size or mass penalties) and its dynamic behavior during task execution (such as tracking precision, joint velocity, or torque usage).

To achieve this comprehensive co-design goal, the paper proposes a unified model, Transformer Transformer, motivated by three specific contributions:

1. **Unified Robot Representation (RoboTokens):** The first objective is to develop a flexible and consistent tokenization scheme, named RoboTokens, capable of representing any articulated robot's embodiment, states, and actions. This representation aims to provide a foundational capability that allows a single neural network model to process and operate across diverse robot embodiment spaces. This addresses the problem of fragmented or incomplete representations in prior work.

2. **Unified Architecture:** The second objective is to create a single neural network architecture, a Diffusion Transformer (DiT), that can simultaneously function as a generator for robot designs, a critic for evaluating their performance, and a controller for task execution. By consolidating these co-design modules into one architecture, the aim is to simplify the overall optimization pipeline and enable efficient GPU-parallelized computation, addressing the computational overhead and iterative nature of multi-component systems.

3. **Unified Dynamics Training Objective:** The third objective is to train the network not on reward-specific signals, but rather on the general dynamics of diverse robot embodiments. This reward-agnostic approach is intended to allow for zero-shot optimization of user-specified reward functions at inference time. The model's dynamics predictions are converted into reward-specific score predictions, which are then used to guide the embodiment diffusion process towards high-value robot designs, a procedure termed "Dynamics Self-Guidance." This addresses the limitation of data-driven methods overfitting to single reward functions.

Collectively, these objectives aim to provide a consolidated, efficient, and generalized framework for robot co-design that can optimize robot embodiments for given manipulation tasks and user-defined performance criteria without requiring separate models or extensive retraining for new reward functions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around modeling the joint distribution of robot embodiments and their associated dynamics (states and actions) using a diffusion transformer architecture. This is facilitated by a novel, unified tokenization scheme and a training objective that enables multi-functional capabilities from a single model.

#### 3.1. RoboToken: A Unifying Robot Representation

RoboToken is introduced as a complete, learning-ready token representation for rigid articulated robots. Its design focuses on four key properties:

- **Complete:** It encompasses all essential attributes of a robot. This includes time-invariant embodiment tokens (representing links, fixed joints, dynamic joints like sliding/rotating, ball joints, and motors) and time-varying dynamics tokens (representing states and actions for each episode). For example, a link token contains physical parameters (primitive type, size, inertia), while a link state token includes the link's pose at a specific timestep. All data attributes within each token type are concatenated into a continuous-valued vector suitable for neural network processing. The schema specifies attribute encodings for various data types (binary for booleans/enums/integers, log/signed log for continuous values). To reduce context length, only free links and end-effector links are observed for pose information.
- **Flexible:** RoboToken supports diverse kinematics by allowing a variable number of embodiment tokens and arbitrary connectivity. Joints point to two connected links via IDs, and motors point to their controlled joints via IDs. This enables representation of passive joints. Heterogeneous state and action spaces are handled by varying the number of state and action tokens in the model's context. Positional embeddings are learned for these IDs.
- **Consistent:** It enforces standardized transform conventions for geometry and dynamics data, unlike some text-based robot description formats (e.g., MJCF) that prioritize human editing ease. This involves automatically splitting lumped inertias into individual geometry inertias (assuming uniform density and using the Parallel Axis Theorem) and collapsing redundant transforms in the preprocessing stage to avoid introducing unnecessary variance during learning.
- **Extensible:** The design facilitates the addition of new tokenization/detokenization logic for various tasks. For motion-conditioned trajectory tracking, target end-effector pose tokens are incorporated as conditioning tokens that are not noised during training.
- **Optimizability:** Unlike autoregressive, categorical text-based formats that are challenging to optimize over (lacking global controllability and differentiability), RoboTokens are continuous-valued and diffused, offering both properties.

#### 3.2. Transformer Transformer: A Unifying Architecture

The central architecture is a diffusion transformer (DiT) designed to handle variable sequence lengths of high-precision, continuous-valued RoboTokens. This single architecture performs multiple roles:

- **Motion-to-Robot Optimization:** Given a target motion trajectory, the model simultaneously diffuses embodiment and dynamics tokens. This non-autoregressive formulation predicts both robot design and its dynamic behavior, including actions, allowing for comprehensive reward assessment. This "Zeroth-Order Optimizer" runs multiple parallel diffusion processes, ranks the generated candidates using the specified reward function, and selects the best.
- **Cross-Embodiment Control:** The model acts as an embodiment-aware controller. Conditioned on the robot's embodiment, current state, and target motion, it predicts expert actions needed to track the motion. RoboToken's completeness provides necessary information (kinematics, dynamics, actuation), and its flexibility accommodates varying degrees of freedom.
- **Model & Training:** Each RoboToken type undergoes a token-type-specific linear projection into the DiT's latent space, followed by the addition of learned embeddings for token IDs. Planar transform augmentations are applied to account for SE(2) equivariance due to gravity. Sequences are padded and concatenated. The DiT is trained using a DDIM scheduler. For efficiency, episode timesteps are subsampled (8 timesteps). Random sampling of timesteps is used for motion-to-robot tasks, while consecutive sampling is used for control to capture temporal dependencies.

#### 3.3. Optimization with Dynamics Self-Guidance

To enhance the efficiency of the zeroth-order optimizer in challenging optimization landscapes, "Dynamics Self-Guidance" is introduced as a first-order diffusion guidance method.

- **Mechanism:** When a differentiable reward function is provided, its gradients can be backpropagated through the model's noisy reward predictions to guide the noisy embodiment predictions. Following classifier-guided DDIM, the DiT's prediction at each step (ϵk) is used to denoise tokens, allowing per-step reward gradients to steer samples towards higher-reward candidates. This process leverages the unified diffusion model, eliminating the need for separate dynamics models or differentiable simulators required by prior works. During inference, multiple parallel guided diffusion processes are executed, and the highest-reward design is returned.

#### 3.4. Data Collection & Generation

The training data comprises target motion trajectories and robot design spaces.

- **Target Motion Dataset:** 76 end-effector motion trajectories were collected using an extension of the UMI gripper, covering diverse skills. 56 trajectories were used for training, and 20 for validation, alongside bimanual dishwashing demonstrations.
- **Design Spaces:** Three design spaces were chosen to evaluate the framework across different co-design challenges:
 - **Kinematic Design (Trossen ViperX 300S):** Fixed-base arm with variations in mounting pose, DoF, joint orientations, and link lengths, emphasizing kinematic reachability.
 - **Dynamic Control (Quadruped Manipulator):** Variations in leg design (serial vs. spring-loaded), arm mounts (fixed vs. sliding), and length parameters for torso, legs, and arms, focusing on whole-body control, stability, and agility.
 - **Task Complexity (Mobile Bimanual Manipulator):** Different spine designs (fixed, sliding, bending) and length variations, designed for complex, coordinated, long-horizon bimanual tasks.
 The design spaces are combinatorial, with both discrete and continuous choices, generating diverse RoboTokens.
- **Control for Data Generation:**
 - **Non-legged Robots (ViperX, Bimanual):** Mink, a differential inverse kinematics library for MuJoCo, was used. It incorporates terms for tracking, posture regularization, and damping, accounting for joint position control lag.
 - **Legged Robots (Quadrupeds):** Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies were trained using PPO. One policy was trained for each discrete variation (e.g., leg design), with continuous variations included in the policy observation, resulting in 128 RL experts. These policies observe robot states, continuous variation parameters, and future target poses, and are robustified with regularization and in-training perturbations.
- **Data Quality and Size:** Episodes with poor tracking or early termination were filtered. Data generation for ViperX and ALOHA was biased towards higher-performance embodiments using a pre-sampling step with CMA-ES. The datasets included millions of episodes and billions of timesteps across the three design spaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several findings related to the capabilities of Transformer Transformer in unified robot co-design.

**RoboToken Unifies Diverse Robot Embodiment Spaces:**
The RoboToken representation demonstrated its ability to encode a variety of articulated robots from the MuJoCo Menagerie. This included dexterous hands, fixed-base arms, quadrupeds with manipulators, bipeds, and humanoids. These robots varied significantly in mass (from 0.65 kg to 67.5 kg) and degrees of freedom (6 to 35 active/passive joints). All these diverse robots were successfully tokenized into compact RoboToken sequences ranging from 28 to 101 tokens, a significant reduction (27-110 times more compact) compared to tokenizing their MJCF text descriptions with a general-purpose tokenizer like GPT-4o.

**Generated Designs Lie on the Training Manifold:**
The generative aspect of Transformer Transformer, leveraging a diffusion model, exhibited properties observed in diffusion models.
- **Manifold Adherence:** Generated designs were found to interpolate within the training data distribution, producing physically plausible outputs. The denoiser guided samples towards the data manifold, ensuring outputs respected physical constraints inherent in the training data. For example, the model did not generate hexapods from a quadruped-only dataset or link lengths exceeding the training range.
- **Mode Coverage:** An unconditional model was observed to generate each robot type with a probability consistent with its representation in the training distribution.
- **Cross-Attribute Coherence:** The generated outputs maintained coherent relationships between embodiment attributes, such as larger links having proportional masses and inertias, and motors being appropriately sized for their actuated joints, thus ensuring physical feasibility without explicit constraints.

**Transformer Transformer Optimizes Embodiments for Unseen Reward Functions Zero-Shot:**
The model demonstrated zero-shot optimization capabilities for generating robot hardware to maximize unseen reward functions, conditioned on unseen target tracking motions, at inference time. These rewards incorporated both embodiment-specific terms (e.g., size, weight) and dynamic behavior terms during tracking (e.g., tracking performance, torque/velocity usage).
- **Performance and Efficiency:** Across fixed-base and bimanual mobile design spaces, Transformer Transformer outperformed CMA-ES (a widely used evolutionary algorithm) in both optimization speed and achieved performance. The model’s efficiency was attributed to GPU parallelization across candidate designs (batch dimension) and its ability to reason about long-horizon dynamics from a small, parallelized set of episode timesteps (time dimension), as opposed to CMA-ES's sequential simulation for each candidate. This allowed Transformer Transformer to evaluate 128 candidates much faster than CMA-ES could evaluate 5.
- **Dynamics Self-Guidance:** This first-order guidance method improved the quality of individual samples, providing benefits where sampling budgets were limited or samples were computationally expensive. With a single sample, Dynamics Self-Guidance achieved higher rewards than the Zeroth Order approach (e.g., 242 to 341 reward on multi-trajectory bimanual tasks). However, with sufficiently large sampling budgets, the selection process of the Zeroth Order approach could close this gap.

**Diffusion Generates Diverse Designs:**
The model demonstrated the ability to produce diverse robot designs tailored to different target trajectories and reward functions. For instance, when optimizing for a "tossing" trajectory, the model tended to prefer larger, longer-armed robots for stability and velocity. Conversely, with a size regularization penalty, it converged on smaller robots. This adaptability to varying optimization landscapes was supported by RoboToken's comprehensive representation, which provided the reward functions with sufficient attributes to control the diffusion process.

**Diffusion Composition for Multi-Trajectory Optimization:**
The application of diffusion composition enabled zero-shot optimization for multiple unseen trajectories simultaneously. This approach generated designs that performed favorably across several trajectories, with efficient parallelization. In contrast, CMA-ES's optimization time scaled linearly with the number of trajectories, resulting in significantly longer runtimes for multi-trajectory tasks (e.g., 3 hours longer for CMA-ES).

**Better Designs with Test-Time Compute:**
Similar to observations in large language models, increasing test-time compute by searching over more parallel seeds led to the generation of improved robot designs. This behavior was consistent across all design spaces and reward functions, emerging from the learned model of robot embodiments and dynamics. While performance generally improved, it did not robustly increase indefinitely with more test-time compute.

**Transformer Transformer Controls Unseen Robots:**
The model demonstrated its capacity to self-validate generated designs by directly controlling them. A positive correlation (Pearson's r: 0.53) was observed between the tracking performance of the learned cross-embodiment controller and an oracle Mink controller. While some outlier embodiments existed, the majority clustered along the diagonal, indicating a promising step towards a single, unified cross-embodiment controller capable of replacing numerous specialized RL experts.

**Real-world Design Validation:**
An ALOHA setup was fabricated based on a design optimized by the model for a "Tracking Velocity" reward, specifically for cloth unfolding via flinging. The optimized design resulted in a 30% reduction in maximum joint speed (from 2.57 to 1.82 rad/s) and a 73% reduction in tracking position error (from 13.0 cm to 3.5 cm) compared to the original ALOHA design. The optimized robot featured longer, lighter links and an upside-down arm mounting, enabling a more efficient underarm swing. This real-world validation demonstrated the model's robustness against unmodeled dynamics and disturbances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Transformer Transformer as a notable development in robot co-design, offering a unified framework that integrates multiple aspects of robot design and control. The primary significance lies in its departure from fragmented, iterative pipelines towards a consolidated, single-model approach.

**Unified and Efficient Co-design:** The most immediate impact is the consolidation of embodiment generation, performance evaluation (critic), and cross-embodiment control into a single Diffusion Transformer. This unification, facilitated by the RoboToken representation, simplifies the co-design pipeline and enables GPU-accelerated processing, leading to significantly faster design optimization compared to traditional evolutionary algorithms. This efficiency has the potential to accelerate the iterative design-build-test cycle in robotics development.

**Zero-Shot Generalization:** The model's capacity to optimize for unseen reward functions and novel task trajectories at inference time, without requiring retraining, represents a substantial advancement. This reward-agnostic capability allows robot designers to define new objectives and immediately receive optimized designs, fostering exploration of diverse performance criteria. The demonstrated ability to optimize for multiple trajectories simultaneously further broadens its applicability, enabling the design of more versatile, general-purpose manipulation platforms rather than task-specific robots.

**Physically Plausible and Diverse Designs:** The inherent properties of the diffusion model ensure that generated robot designs adhere to the learned data manifold. This results in physically plausible and coherent robot attributes, reducing the need for extensive post-generation validation or manual refinement to ensure constructibility and functionality. The model's ability to produce diverse designs in response to varying task requirements and reward functions suggests a flexible tool for exploring a wide range of morphological solutions.

**Real-World Applicability:** The successful fabrication and real-world validation of an optimized ALOHA design underscore the practical utility and robustness of the proposed framework. The observed reductions in tracking error and joint velocity in a dynamic manipulation task (cloth unfolding) demonstrate that the model's predictions can translate into tangible performance improvements in physical robots, even in the presence of real-world complexities and unmodeled dynamics.

**Foundation for Future Robot Learning:** The development of RoboToken as a comprehensive and flexible robot representation could serve as a foundational element for future research. Its ability to represent diverse articulated robots in a consistent, learning-ready format lays groundwork for developing more generalized robot learning models, potentially leading to "foundation models" that reason about robot embodiment as a first-class citizen. Expanding RoboToken's scope to include complex geometries, environmental context, and tactile information could unlock further capabilities in sophisticated robot interaction and design.

In summary, Transformer Transformer contributes to making robot co-design more accessible, efficient, and adaptable, potentially enabling a new paradigm where robot hardware is no longer a static constraint but an integral, optimizable component of the task solution.
