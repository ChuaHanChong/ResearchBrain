# Compositional Zero-Shot Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper presents the first comprehensive taxonomy for Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL) methods, categorizing them based on their disentanglement strategies for attributes and objects. It consolidates existing knowledge, reviews benchmarks, and identifies key performance trends, open challenges, and future research directions in the field.

## Problem

- Machine learning models struggle to recognize novel combinations of known attributes and objects (e.g., 'striped elephant') without specific training data, due to the combinatorial explosion of possibilities.
- The rapidly growing and diverse field of Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL) lacked a systematic and comprehensive overview or unified analytical framework.
- Understanding how different CZSL methods balance learning distinct primitive representations versus modeling their contextual interactions was fragmented across various publications.

## Method

- Developed the first comprehensive ==taxonomy for CZSL methods==, classifying them into categories like no explicit disentanglement, textual, visual, and cross-modal disentanglement.
- Conducted a systematic review of state-of-the-art CZSL approaches, benchmark datasets (e.g., ==MIT-States==, UT-Zappos), and standard evaluation protocols.
- Performed an empirical comparative analysis of method performance across ==closed-world and open-world CZSL settings==, utilizing different architectural backbones like ResNet, ViT, and CLIP.

## Results

- Methods leveraging **CLIP**-based encoders consistently achieve superior performance across all CZSL categories and evaluation settings, highlighting the impact of large-scale vision-language pretraining.
- Visual disentanglement methods, especially those with augmentation or prototype-centric designs, generally demonstrate the strongest performance, particularly in the more challenging open-world setting.
- While cross-modal disentanglement approaches show strong potential in controlled closed-world scenarios, they often trail leading visual disentanglement methods in the open-world setting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentanglement, the explicit separation and representation of constituent primitives (attributes and objects), serves as a fundamental organizing principle for understanding CZSL methods.
> - The visual appearance and semantics of primitives are highly contextual, requiring robust models to effectively disentangle and combine them.
> - There is a clear need for methods that can handle unfeasible attribute-object combinations and generalize to truly unseen primitives in real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey positions itself within the evolving and crucial field of Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL), a specialized and increasingly vital sub-area of Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL).

**Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL)** is a machine learning paradigm that empowers models to classify objects from categories they have not encountered during training. This capability is achieved by leveraging auxiliary information, such as textual descriptions or semantic embeddings, to bridge the knowledge gap between "seen" classes (for which labeled data exists) and "unseen" classes. ZSL is fundamentally important for overcoming data scarcity, as it reduces the need for extensive data collection and retraining, thereby making AI models more flexible and adaptable to novel concepts.

**Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL)** takes this challenge a step further by focusing on recognizing novel *combinations* of previously seen concepts, rather than entirely new single classes. In CZSL, each class is typically defined as a composition of an object and an associated attribute (e e.g., "Green Parrot" vs. "Yellow Sparrow"). The core problem addressed by CZSL is the "combinatorial challenge": the number of possible attribute-object combinations grows exponentially with the number of attributes and objects. It becomes impractical, if not impossible, to gather training data for every conceivable composition (e.g., all combinations of colors and objects).

A critical and highly challenging aspect of CZSL is **contextuality**. The visual appearance and semantics of primitives (attributes and objects) are often highly dependent on their context. For instance, a "small" cat appears visually distinct from a "small" plane, and a "wet" car looks very different from a "wet" cat. Effectively modeling this inherent contextuality and disentangling the visual features of attributes and objects are crucial for robust compositional zero-shot recognition.

This paper fits into the broader research landscape by addressing a significant void: despite the rapid emergence and diversification of CZSL methods, there has been a notable *absence* of a comprehensive, foundational survey specifically dedicated to this area. The authors explicitly highlight this gap, citing the "burgeoning landscape of CZSL approaches" and providing compelling statistics (Fig. 2) that show a substantial increase in publications in top-tier venues, particularly from 2023 onwards. This surge in research underscores the urgent need for a systematic and fine-grained understanding of the methodological philosophies and their evolution. By providing the first such survey, this work aims to consolidate fragmented knowledge, offer a structured analytical framework, and guide future research efforts in this increasingly important and complex domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to present, to the authors' knowledge, the **first comprehensive survey specifically focused on Compositional Zero-Shot Learning (CZSL)**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations stemming from the rapid expansion and increasing complexity of the CZSL field:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap:** The most significant motivation is the observed absence of a foundational, comprehensive survey despite the rapid evolution and proliferation of diverse methods in CZSL. This gap makes it challenging for researchers to gain a systematic and fine-grained understanding of the field, compare different approaches effectively, and identify promising research directions.
- **Need for a Structured Categorization:** The burgeoning landscape of CZSL approaches lacks a coherent framework that articulates how various methods balance the fundamental requirements of learning distinct primitive representations versus modeling their contextual interactions. The survey aims to provide this structured categorization.
- **Guiding Research and Development:** By systematically reviewing the state-of-the-art, the authors intend for this survey to serve as a foundational resource. It seeks to guide and inspire further advancements by clarifying existing knowledge, highlighting successful paradigms, and pinpointing areas requiring more attention.

To achieve its primary objective and address these motivations, the survey outlines three key contributions:

1. **A Comprehensive Taxonomy:** The authors introduce the first comprehensive taxonomy of CZSL methods. This taxonomy is structured around the core principle of **disentanglement**, categorizing methods based on whether they explicitly disentangle primitives (attributes and objects) in the textual space, visual space, both modalities (cross-modal), or not at all. This hierarchical structure is designed to offer a precise, non-overlapping organization of the literature, providing new insights into methodological design choices and the evolution of progress.
2. **Trend Identification and Performance Analysis:** The survey aims to identify key performance trends and highlight the evolving dominance of different methodological paradigms. This includes a detailed comparative analysis of methods, illustrating their advantages and limitations in various problem settings (e.g., closed-world vs. open-world CZSL) and across different architectural backbones.
3. **Identification of Open Challenges and Future Directions:** A crucial objective is to consolidate and articulate the persistent challenges faced by current CZSL models. Building on these challenges, the survey proposes promising avenues for future research, intending to direct researchers toward impactful contributions and further innovation in the field.

Ultimately, the motivation for this survey is to bring clarity and structure to a complex and rapidly expanding domain, thereby accelerating progress towards more robust, scalable, and human-like compositional reasoning in artificial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic and analytical approach to construct their comprehensive survey of Compositional Zero-Shot Learning methods. Their methodology is characterized by a structured literature review, a novel taxonomy, and a thorough empirical analysis.

**Literature Review and Selection:**
The survey commenced with a systematic search on Google Scholar, employing keywords such as 'compositional zero-shot learning', 'state-object composition', and 'attribute-object recognition'. To ensure the quality and relevance of the included papers, the authors imposed strict selection criteria, focusing on:
- Leading computer vision and machine learning conferences (e.g., CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, NeurIPS, ICLR, AAAI, IJCAI, WACV).
- Journals with an impact factor greater than 7 (e.g., IEEE TPAMI, Pattern Recognition (PR), IEEE TMM).
This systematic selection process allowed the authors to capture the most influential and high-impact works in the CZSL field.

**Novel Taxonomy Based on Disentanglement:**
The cornerstone of their methodological approach is the introduction of a novel, comprehensive taxonomy for CZSL methods. This taxonomy is explicitly "grounded in disentanglement," which the authors define as the degree to which models explicitly separate and represent the constituent primitives (attributes and objects) of a composition. Disentanglement is chosen as the organizing principle because it directly addresses the two fundamental challenges of CZSL:
- **Discriminability:** The ability to learn distinct and robust representations for individual primitives.
- **Contextuality:** How the expression of a primitive depends on the object it modifies.

The taxonomy is structured hierarchically:
- **First Level Categories:** Methods are categorized based on *where and how* disentanglement is applied:
 - **No Explicit Disentanglement:** Approaches that model attribute-object compositions holistically without explicit separation.
 - **Textual Feature Disentanglement:** Methods that separate semantic embeddings of primitives within the language space.
 - **Visual Feature Disentanglement:** Approaches that isolate visual features of attributes and objects within image representations.
 - **Cross-Modal (Hybrid) Disentanglement:** Techniques that simultaneously disentangle primitives in both visual and textual spaces, often through cross-modal alignment.
- **Second Level Subcategories:** Within each first-level category, methods are further refined by their specific strategies for modeling attributes and addressing compositional challenges. Examples include prototype-based modeling, synthetic embeddings, augmentation, causal inference, and constraint-driven formulations. This provides a precise and non-overlapping organization of the literature.

**Problem Settings and Formulation:**
The survey clearly defines the different problem settings in CZSL:
- **Closed-world setting:** Where the set of possible test compositions is predefined and known, further divided into "Only-unseen Compositions" and "Generalized closed-world."
- **Open-world setting:** Introduced by Mancini et al., where the test set includes all possible attribute-object combinations, including unfeasible ones.
The authors also provide a formal mathematical formulation for CZSL, defining sets of attributes, objects, and compositions, and how training and test sets are constructed within these settings.

**Dataset and Evaluation Protocol Review:**
The paper includes a dedicated review of benchmark datasets commonly used in CZSL, such as MIT-States, UT-Zappos, and CGQA, detailing their statistics (number of attributes, objects, compositions, images) and characteristics. It also comprehensively summarizes the standard evaluation protocols and metrics, including:
- **Area Under the Curve (AUC):** Measures the model's ability to distinguish seen and unseen compositions across bias thresholds.
- **Seen Accuracy:** Performance on known compositions.
- **Unseen Accuracy:** Performance on novel compositions, directly measuring generalization.
- **Harmonic Mean:** A crucial metric that balances seen and unseen accuracy, penalizing models with significant performance disparity.

**Empirical Evaluation and Comparative Analysis:**
The methodological approach culminates in an empirical evaluation and comparative analysis. This involves a comprehensive table summarizing the performance of various state-of-the-art models on key datasets for both closed-world and open-world settings. Results are organized by the visual backbone (ResNet, ViT, CLIP) and the taxonomy categories, enabling the authors to identify key performance trends, advantages, and limitations of different approaches. This structured analysis links theoretical categorization with practical performance, reinforcing the insights derived from their taxonomy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation and comparative analysis conducted in the survey reveal several significant findings and trends across the different categories of Compositional Zero-Shot Learning methods and evaluation settings.

**1. Dominance of Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs):**
A consistent and overarching finding across all categories and evaluation settings is the superior performance of methods utilizing **CLIP-based encoders** (from 2023 onwards) compared to older ResNet or ViT backbones. This significant improvement in accuracy underscores the critical role of large-scale vision-language pretraining in providing stronger multimodal grounding, which benefits even before specific disentanglement strategies are applied.

**2. Performance Trends in Closed-World Setting:**
In the **closed-world setting**, where test compositions are fixed and known, the results generally align with the conceptual progression of the proposed taxonomy:
- **No Explicit Disentanglement:** These earlier approaches generally show the weakest performance, confirming that treating compositions holistically without explicit primitive separation limits generalization. While CLIP-based models like *Dynamic* show improved results due to richer contextual diversity, the category as a whole struggles with novel combinations.
- **Textual Disentanglement:** Methods in this category show noticeable improvements over no-disentanglement baselines. *Primitive-aware* methods, such as DFSP, consistently outperform *pairwise composition* methods (e.g., GIPCOL) by explicitly incorporating individual attribute and object embeddings into the composition score, demonstrating the value of primitive-level knowledge. However, textual disentanglement still lags behind visual disentanglement methods, suggesting the insufficiency of language alone to capture visual variability.
- **Visual Disentanglement:** This is the most active and effective category, yielding clear gains over textual approaches. *Augmentation-enhanced methods* (e.g., Retrieval), *prototype-centric methods* (e.g., CLUSPRO), and *conditional attribute modeling approaches* (e.g., CDS-CZSL) are among the top performers. Notably, **CLUSPRO achieves the highest accuracy** across all three benchmark datasets (MIT-States, UT-Zappos, CGQA) in this setting, highlighting the strength of grounding primitives in stable, diverse prototypes.
- **Cross-Modal Disentanglement:** These hybrid approaches, mostly emerging in 2024, demonstrate strong potential. *Encoder-augmented modeling methods* (e.g., CAILA and Troika) often match or even surpass the best visual models on diverse datasets like MIT-States and CGQA, indicating the advantage of deep cross-modal alignment. LLM-guided prompting (e.g., PLID) shows promise but currently performs less effectively than encoder-augmented counterparts.

**3. Performance Trends in Open-World Setting:**
The **open-world setting**, which includes all possible (and often unfeasible) attribute-object combinations, presents a significantly greater challenge, resulting in a sharp performance drop across all methods compared to the closed-world.
- **No Explicit Disentanglement:** Remains the weakest group. Interestingly, simple *primitive-level prediction* methods like KG-SP can sometimes rival more sophisticated *context-aware models* (Co-CGE), depending on the dataset, suggesting that the expanded label space complicates holistic composition.
- **Textual Disentanglement:** Continues to outperform no-disentanglement. The performance gap between *primitive-aware* (DFSP) and *pairwise composition* methods (GIPCOL) widens, reinforcing the importance of explicit primitive information for broader generalization in the open-world.
- **Visual Disentanglement:** This category consistently proves to be the most effective in the open-world. *Augmentation-based methods* (Retrieval) perform exceptionally well on large datasets like CGQA, while *prototype-centric methods* (CLUSPRO) maintain strong leads on MIT-States and UT-Zappos. *Auxiliary objective methods* (VisPrompt) also achieve competitive results. This highlights the robustness of visual disentanglement strategies in handling the expanded, noisy label space.
- **Cross-Modal Disentanglement:** In a notable divergence from the closed-world, cross-modal approaches generally **fall behind** the top visual disentanglement methods in the open-world setting. Even the strongest cross-modal model, CAILA, trails leading visual methods like CLUSPRO and Retrieval. This indicates that while cross-modal alignment holds promise, visual grounding alone currently provides more reliable performance when navigating the vast number of unfeasible compositions inherent in the open-world.

**Overall Summary of Findings:**
The survey establishes a clear progression in CZSL research from simple holistic modeling to unimodal disentanglement, and now towards cross-modal integration. While cross-modal approaches show strong potential in controlled settings, visual disentanglement currently offers more reliable performance, especially in the challenging open-world scenario. The widespread adoption of CLIP-based backbones signifies a crucial architectural shift that has profoundly impacted performance across the board.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey represents a significant contribution to the field of Compositional Zero-Shot Learning, offering a much-needed foundational resource that will profoundly impact future research directions and accelerate progress in the domain.

**1. Foundational Resource and Knowledge Consolidation:**
The most immediate impact of this survey is its role as the **first comprehensive taxonomy specifically focused on CZSL**. Prior to this work, the rapidly expanding literature was fragmented, making it difficult for researchers to understand the relationships between different methods, identify their underlying principles, and track the evolution of the field. This survey systematically consolidates this knowledge, providing a clear, structured analytical framework grounded in the concept of disentanglement. This systematic organization will serve as a definitive reference point, allowing both new and established researchers to quickly grasp the state-of-the-art and place their work within a coherent framework.

**2. Guiding Methodological Choices and Benchmarking:**
By identifying clear performance trends, particularly the superior performance of CLIP-based backbones, the survey offers practical guidance for future methodological development. Researchers can now confidently select more effective base models, saving considerable time and computational resources. Furthermore, the detailed comparative analysis across different problem settings (closed-world and open-world) and datasets provides a robust benchmark, enabling more rigorous evaluation and fostering fair comparisons between novel approaches. The insights into which categories perform best under specific conditions (e.g., visual disentanglement in open-world settings) can guide the design of more robust algorithms.

**3. Articulating Open Challenges and Inspiring Future Research:**
Perhaps the most impactful aspect of the survey is its comprehensive identification and articulation of pressing open challenges, which naturally delineate promising future research directions. These include:
- **Modeling Primitives and Contextuality:** The call for refining visual disentanglement and developing more robust cross-modal frameworks to capture the dynamic and context-dependent nature of attributes. This guides research towards more nuanced representations that go beyond simple separation.
- **Scaling to Open-World Evaluation:** The critical challenge of robustly handling unfeasible compositions without relying on external filters. This pushes the field towards models that intrinsically understand compositional feasibility, reflecting a deeper form of reasoning.
- **Generalization to Unseen Primitives:** The necessity of moving beyond fixed vocabularies to address real-world scenarios involving unseen attributes, objects, and incremental learning. This expands the scope of CZSL towards more adaptable and human-like intelligence.
- **Leveraging Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):** The discussion on exploiting the rich reasoning capabilities of LMMs (e.g., LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) while addressing critical concerns like data contamination and computational efficiency. This outlines a high-potential, yet challenging, frontier for integrating state-of-the-art AI models into CZSL.

**4. Fostering Innovation and Transparency:**
By providing a clear roadmap of unsolved problems, the survey is poised to stimulate innovative research. It encourages the development of solutions that combine scalability, robustness, and transparency, ultimately aiming to enable intelligent systems to reason about compositions with a flexibility and interpretability approaching human cognition. The open-science approach of sharing code links further promotes transparency and reproducibility, facilitating collaborative advancement in the field.

In conclusion, this survey acts as both a retrospective synthesis of past achievements and a forward-looking blueprint for future endeavors in Compositional Zero-Shot Learning. Its detailed analysis, novel taxonomy, and clear articulation of the research frontier are invaluable for consolidating understanding, guiding strategic research, and accelerating the development of more advanced, generalizable AI systems.
