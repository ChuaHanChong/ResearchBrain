# FieldGen: From Teleoperated Pre-Manipulation Trajectories to Field-Guided Data Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> FieldGen is a semi-automated framework for real-world robotic manipulation data generation, which decouples tasks into expert-supervised fine manipulation and field-guided automated pre-manipulation phases. It yields higher quality, more diverse datasets that improve policy success rates and generalization while significantly reducing human effort.

## Problem

- A critical scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world datasets for training robust robotic manipulation policies.
- Simulation-based data generation suffers from the "sim-to-real gap," limiting transferability of learned policies to physical robots.
- Traditional teleoperation is labor-intensive, expensive, and results in datasets with limited behavioral diversity due to stereotypical human motion patterns.

## Method

- FieldGen semi-automates real-world data collection by decoupling manipulation tasks into a precise, human-supervised fine manipulation phase and an automated, field-guided pre-manipulation phase.
- A Pre-Manipulation Field (PMF), composed of a cone field for position and a spherical field for orientation, is constructed from a small set of expert teleoperated demonstrations to guide automated trajectory generation.
- The FieldGen-Reward component augments generated data with explicit reward annotations, allowing policies to learn from a broader spectrum of successful and sub-optimal behaviors.

## Results

- Policies trained with FieldGen data achieved 35.5% to 45.8% higher average success rates across various tasks and policies compared to teleoperation-trained policies under equal collection time budgets.
- FieldGen-trained policies demonstrated superior generalization across varied initial end-effector poses, object placements, and unseen object instances, requiring fewer samples to reach high performance levels.
- The framework significantly reduced human effort, increasing the active data collection time ratio by 2.47x and the frame collection rate by 2.11x, while providing broader and more uniform spatial trajectory coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling manipulation into pre-manipulation and fine manipulation phases enables strategic application of human expertise and scalable automation, optimizing both quality and quantity of data.
> - Using cone and spherical fields with cycloid curves for pre-manipulation trajectory generation produces smooth, geometrically consistent, and diverse movements that are more robust for robot execution.
> - Providing explicit reward signals for varied quality trajectories allows downstream policies to develop more robust task representations and learn from a continuous distribution of behavioral success.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is at a critical juncture, with recent advancements in end-to-end embodied intelligence models demonstrating impressive generalization capabilities. However, a significant bottleneck remains: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world datasets needed to train truly robust and generalist policies. FieldGen directly addresses this fundamental challenge, positioning itself as a crucial step towards more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

Existing approaches to data collection for robotic manipulation generally fall into two categories, each with inherent limitations that FieldGen seeks to overcome:

- **Simulation-Based Data Generation:** Methods like RoboTwin, MimicGen, and DexMimicGen leverage simulation engines to generate vast amounts of data with procedural generation and domain randomization. These approaches offer unparalleled scalability, allowing for millions of diverse scenarios. However, their primary drawback is the persistent "sim-to-real gap." Policies trained purely in simulation often struggle to transfer robustly to physical robots due to discrepancies in physics, contact dynamics, material properties, and sensor characteristics. Furthermore, while diverse in terms of scene configurations, they often lack the rich "behavioral diversity" (variety in manipulation strategies, contact interactions) observed in human demonstrations.
- **Teleoperation and Manual Demonstration:** This traditional method involves human operators controlling robots to perform tasks, yielding high-quality, real-world demonstrations. However, teleoperation suffers from severe scalability constraints. It is labor-intensive, expensive, and subject to human limitations such as cognitive fatigue. Critically, human operators often converge to "stereotypical motion patterns," even when instructed to vary their movements, leading to datasets with limited behavioral diversity. Semi-automated approaches like PATO and GCENT attempt to alleviate operator load by automating repetitive subtasks, but they still rely on pre-trained policies and may inherit the diversity limitations inherent in human-demonstrated behaviors rather than generating new, diverse trajectories.

FieldGen carves out a unique and highly impactful niche by proposing a semi-automated, field-guided data generation framework that operates *directly in the real world*. Unlike simulation-only methods, FieldGen avoids the sim-to-real gap by generating real data. Unlike pure teleoperation, it drastically reduces human effort and significantly enhances behavioral diversity. The paper argues that manipulation naturally decomposes into two phases: a pre-manipulation phase (reaching and approaching) where variation is acceptable, and a fine manipulation phase (precise contact) requiring expert precision. By decoupling these, FieldGen strategically applies human expertise where it is most critical (fine manipulation) and automates the scalable generation of diverse trajectories for the less critical but voluminous pre-manipulation phase.

This work contributes to the broader research agenda of scaling up real-world robot learning, which is recognized as a key hurdle for generalist robots. By providing a practical and efficient method for creating large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world datasets, FieldGen helps pave the way for training more robust and generalizable robotic policies, ultimately accelerating progress in embodied AI and robotic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind FieldGen stems from the critical need for large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world datasets to train robust and generalizable robotic manipulation policies. The authors identify a fundamental trade-off in existing data collection methods:

- **Scalability vs. Quality in Simulation:** While simulation offers high scalability and diversity in scene generation, it inherently suffers from the "sim-to-real" gap, limiting the direct transferability of learned policies to physical robots. Moreover, behavioral diversity, particularly in contact-rich interactions, often remains limited.
- **Quality vs. Scalability/Diversity in Teleoperation:** Teleoperation provides high-fidelity, real-world demonstrations but is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and prone to human cognitive fatigue. Crucially, human operators tend to produce "stereotypical motion patterns," leading to datasets with insufficient behavioral diversity, which can hinder the generalization capabilities of learned policies.

To overcome this dilemma, the authors propose FieldGen with the following key objectives:

1. **Enable Scalable Real-World Data Collection:** To move beyond the limitations of teleoperation, FieldGen aims to semi-automate the data generation process on physical robots, significantly increasing the volume of real-world data that can be collected.
2. **Enhance Trajectory Diversity:** Address the issue of stereotypical motion patterns in human demonstrations by algorithmically generating a wide range of varied pre-manipulation trajectories, ensuring the resulting dataset covers a broader, more uniform state space.
3. **Maintain High Data Quality:** Ensure that despite automation, the collected data retains high fidelity, particularly for the critical fine manipulation phase, where human expertise is still leveraged.
4. **Minimize Human Supervision and Effort:** Drastically reduce the human involvement required for long-term data collection, freeing operators from repetitive tasks and cognitive fatigue, thereby making data acquisition more efficient and sustainable.
5. **Improve Downstream Policy Learning:** Ultimately, the goal is for policies trained on FieldGen-generated data to exhibit superior performance, including higher success rates, enhanced robustness, and better generalization across various tasks, initial conditions, and object instances, compared to policies trained on teleoperated data.
6. **Quantify Effort Reduction and Throughput:** Provide empirical evidence of the reduced operator workload and increased data generation throughput compared to traditional teleoperation.
7. **Explore Reward-Conditioned Learning:** Introduce a mechanism (FieldGen-Reward) to generate trajectories of varying quality with explicit reward annotations, allowing policies to learn from a broader spectrum of successful and sub-optimal behaviors, thus improving robustness and understanding of behavioral causality.

By achieving these objectives, FieldGen aims to provide a practical and effective framework for accelerating the development of capable and generalist robotic manipulation systems by bridging the gap between data quantity, quality, and diversity in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FieldGen introduces a novel, field-guided, semi-automated framework for real-world manipulation data generation, built upon the core principle of *phase decoupling*. The methodology is designed to leverage human expertise efficiently while automating the scalable generation of diverse trajectories.

**A. Problem Formulation: Decoupling Manipulation Phases**
The core insight is that manipulation tasks can be naturally divided into two phases with distinct requirements:
1. **Fine Manipulation Phase:** This phase involves precise, contact-rich interactions (e.g., grasping, placing objects precisely). It requires expert human precision, making teleoperated demonstrations most valuable here.
2. **Pre-manipulation Phase:** This involves reaching and approaching the object. During this phase, trajectory diversity is highly beneficial, as long as the robot converges to a valid manipulation configuration. This phase is amenable to automation.

The FieldGen pipeline capitalizes on this decomposition:
- **Stage 1: Fine Manipulation Trajectories (Human Supervision):** A small set of high-quality human teleoperated demonstrations are collected. These demonstrations focus on capturing critical manipulation poses and contact interactions, serving as expert supervision.
- **Stage 2: Pre-manipulation Field (F_Gen) Construction:** From these expert demonstrations, an abstract representation called the Pre-Manipulation Field (PMF), denoted as F_Gen, is constructed. This field models and extrapolates the demonstrated trajectories to guide automated trajectory generation.
- **Stage 3: FieldGen-based Reach Phase (Automated Generation):** Automated scripts leverage F_Gen to conduct large-scale, semi-automated data collection. Randomized initial observations are sampled from diverse end-effector configurations, and corresponding action sequences are generated by asymptotically rolling out towards the constructed F_Gen. This process synthesizes a large volume of observation-action pairs for the pre-manipulation phase.

**B. F_Gen Field Construction**
The F_Gen is decomposed into two components to control position and orientation independently:

1. **Cone Field for Position (F_pos):**
 - **Goal:** To constrain the end-effector to approach the manipulation position (`p_G`) along a specific axis (`u`) (opposite to the gripper closing axis).
 - **Mechanism:** The field defines a cone with a half-angle `θ`.
 - If the current end-effector position (`p_Q`) is *inside* the cone, the attraction vector guides it smoothly toward `p_G` using a *half-cycloid curve*. This curve ensures naturally smooth curvature variations.
 - If `p_Q` is *outside* the cone, it is first projected onto the cone surface, and then guided along the inner cycloid curve to `p_G`.
 - **Action:** The translational delta action (`∆p`) is derived from this cycloid curve.

2. **Spherical Field for Orientation (F_ori):**
 - **Goal:** To ensure the gripper aligns directly and smoothly with the target manipulation orientation (`R_G`).
 - **Mechanism:** It computes the relative rotation (`R_∆ = R_Gᵀ R_Q`) between the sampled orientation (`R_Q`) and the goal orientation. This relative rotation is then converted into an axis-angle representation (`ω`).
 - **Action:** A corrective angular velocity (`∆R = -K_R ω`) is applied, driving the end-effector towards `R_G`.

**C. Action Sequence Generation and Chunking:**
Once the full trajectory guided by F_Gen is computed, the length of the action sequence is determined by dividing the trajectory length by a parameter `β`, which controls the inter-frame distance. The system then extracts a chunk-sized segment of actions, padding with the last point if the sequence is shorter than the chunk size.

**D. FieldGen-Reward: Generating Diverse Reward-Annotated Trajectories**
To further enhance policy learning by providing explicit quality signals, FieldGen-Reward augments the generated data:
- **Mechanism:** For a given successful manipulation endpoint (`P_O`) from teleoperation, a sphere of radius `R` is defined around it.
- **Trajectory Generation:** New endpoints (`P_N`) are randomly sampled within this sphere. A new trajectory is then generated towards `P_N` using the FieldGen mechanism.
- **Reward Annotation:** The quality of this new trajectory is quantified by a continuous reward signal: `reward = 1 - d/R`, where `d` is the Euclidean distance between `P_O` and `P_N`.
- **Benefit:** This approach creates a continuous distribution of data quality (from perfect, reward=1, to less precise, reward approaching 0), allowing for the theoretical generation of infinite trajectories, each with an associated reward. This enables models to learn from a broader spectrum of behaviors, including sub-optimal but informative ones.

**E. Implementation Details**
Experiments were conducted on the Agibot G1 robot, using an NVIDIA Orin. Policies were trained from scratch using wrist-mounted RGB images as observations and end-effector delta poses as actions, with each action chunk comprising 30 steps. The policies evaluated included Diffusion Policy (DP), RDT-small, and ACT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments rigorously evaluated FieldGen across multiple dimensions, consistently demonstrating its superiority over traditional teleoperation for real-world robotic manipulation data collection.

**A. Equal-Time Data Effectiveness:**
- **FieldGen consistently outperforms teleoperation:** Under equal wall-clock collection budgets (e.g., 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 minutes), policies trained with FieldGen data achieved significantly higher success rates across all tested policies (DP, RDT-small) and manipulation tasks (Pick, Rotate Pick, Transparent Pick, Affordance Pick).
- **Substantial performance gains:** FieldGen showed an average improvement in success rates over teleoperation ranging from 35.5% to 45.8% at different time checkpoints.
- **Better scalability with time:** FieldGen's success rates grew more sharply and steadily with increased collection time compared to teleoperation. After 20 minutes, FieldGen-trained policies exceeded 80% success in all settings, with DP achieving 100% success on three of the four tasks, indicating the high quality and efficiency of the data.

**B. Data Quality and Generalization:**
- **Superior generalization:** For datasets of equal size (measured in data frames), FieldGen-trained policies consistently outperformed teleoperation-trained policies across three challenging generalization conditions: varying initial end-effector poses, varied object placements, and cross-instance generalization (unseen object instances).
- **Efficiency in learning:** Notably, for Diffusion Policy (DP), only 4,000 FieldGen samples were sufficient to achieve 100% success on both "Start EE Pose Generalization" and "Object Generalization" tasks. Teleoperation, even with 12,000 samples, struggled to achieve comparable performance, highlighting FieldGen's higher per-sample data quality.
- **Stable learning curve:** FieldGen exhibited a stable, upward-trending learning curve as dataset size increased, whereas teleoperation data led to more irregular and less effective performance scaling. This suggests FieldGen provides broader and more consistent coverage of the task-relevant state space.

**C. Trajectory Diversity and Spatial Coverage:**
- **Broader and more uniform coverage:** FieldGen-generated trajectories demonstrated significantly broader and more uniform spatial coverage compared to teleoperated data, even when teleoperation attempted to introduce diversity.
- **Quantified spatial coverage:** FieldGen achieved the highest spatial coverage (18.14%) compared to moderately diverse teleoperation (15.44%) and low-diversity teleoperation (9.04%).
- **Direct link to policy performance:** This enhanced spatial coverage directly translated into superior downstream policy performance. Policies trained on high-diversity FieldGen data achieved 83.3% success, significantly outperforming those trained on low-diversity teleoperation data (0% success) and moderately diverse teleoperation data (54.2% success).

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Curve Type (B'ezier vs. Cycloid):** The use of a cycloid curve for position field generation was found to be superior. Cycloid-based trajectories yielded higher success rates (e.g., 75% for DP vs. 58.3% for B'ezier), attributed to the cycloid's naturally smooth curvature and geometric consistency, which makes robot motion easier to execute compared to the potentially sharp curvature changes of B'ezier curves.
- **β Parameter:** The parameter `β`, controlling inter-frame distance, was found to be critical. An optimal value of `β = 0.0025` was chosen. Too small `β` led to oscillatory, slow movements; too large `β` resulted in premature gripper closure and task failure.

**E. Less Effort with Long-duration Data Collection:**
- **Significantly reduced human effort:** FieldGen drastically reduced operator cognitive and attentional load.
- **Increased Collection Time Ratio:** FieldGen achieved a mean Collection Time Ratio of 66.73%, meaning active data acquisition occurred for two-thirds of the session, a 2.47x increase over manual teleoperation's 27.07%.
- **Higher Frame Collection Rate:** FieldGen demonstrated a throughput of 1203.14 frames/min, which is 2.11x higher than manual teleoperation's 569.10 frames/min. These metrics confirm FieldGen's superior efficiency and scalability in real-world settings.

**F. Ablation on FieldGen-Reward:**
- **Enhanced performance with rewards:** The FieldGen-Reward component, which generates reward-annotated trajectories, significantly improved policy performance. The reward-conditioned Diffusion Policy (DP-R.) consistently and substantially outperformed the standard DP model across all tasks and time budgets.
- **Accelerated learning and robustness:** DP-R. achieved an average success rate of 79.2% after only 4 minutes of data collection (a 29.2% improvement over standard DP) and reached a near-perfect 95.9% at the 12-minute mark. This demonstrates that explicit quality signals enable the policy to learn more robust and effective task representations.

In summary, FieldGen effectively tackles the challenges of real-world robotic data collection, providing a method that is not only more efficient and scalable but also yields higher-quality, more diverse data that significantly enhances the performance and generalization capabilities of learned manipulation policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FieldGen represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI, offering a practical and effective solution to a long-standing challenge. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking the Data Bottleneck for Real-World Robotics:** The most profound impact of FieldGen lies in its ability to overcome the critical bottleneck of acquiring large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world robotic manipulation data. This has been a major impediment to the progress of generalist robots and embodied intelligence models. By enabling automated, scalable real-world data collection, FieldGen provides a crucial resource that has historically been lacking, much like large image datasets (e.g., ImageNet) catalyzed advancements in computer vision.

2. **Enhancing Robustness and Generalization of Robotic Policies:** The empirical results clearly demonstrate that policies trained on FieldGen-generated data exhibit superior robustness and generalization capabilities. Higher success rates across varied initial conditions, object placements, and even unseen object instances mean that robots can perform more reliably and adapt to a wider range of real-world scenarios. This translates directly into more capable and deployable robotic systems.

3. **Transforming Data Collection Efficiency:** FieldGen drastically reduces the human effort and time required for data collection. The 2.47x increase in "Collection Time Ratio" and 2.11x increase in "Frame Collection Rate" signify a paradigm shift from laborious, manual teleoperation to a semi-automated, highly efficient process. This reduction in operational cost and time makes large-scale real-robot dataset creation economically viable and practically feasible for more researchers and organizations.

4. **Novel Methodological Contribution: Phase Decoupling and Field-Guided Generation:** The core methodological innovation of decoupling manipulation into pre-manipulation and fine manipulation phases, combined with the use of attraction fields (cone and spherical fields) for automated trajectory generation, is a key conceptual contribution. This intelligent partitioning allows for targeted application of human expertise and scalable automation, a powerful strategy for complex task learning. The superiority of cycloid curves for smooth, geometrically consistent motion further refines this approach.

5. **Advancing Reward-Conditioned Learning:** The FieldGen-Reward component offers a valuable extension by providing explicit quality signals for generated trajectories. This enables policies to learn from a broader spectrum of behaviors, including sub-optimal ones, fostering a deeper understanding of task causality and enhancing policy robustness. This approach aligns with the growing trend of leveraging reward signals for more nuanced and efficient learning.

6. **Paving the Way for Generalist Robots:** By generating diverse, high-quality datasets that improve generalization, FieldGen directly contributes to the goal of developing generalist robots capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments. The ability to collect varied interaction data in the real world is fundamental for training models that can adapt and perform novel tasks.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper explicitly outlines future directions, such as extending the fields to multi-step tasks, incorporating uncertainty-aware control, and exploring scene synthesis for fully automated observation generation. This indicates that FieldGen is not just a standalone solution but a foundational framework that can be built upon to achieve even higher levels of automation and capability.

In conclusion, FieldGen offers a practical, scalable, and effective solution to a critical bottleneck in robotic manipulation. By marrying real-world fidelity with automated diversity and efficiency, it stands to accelerate the development of more intelligent, robust, and autonomous robotic systems, thereby having a profound impact on the future of embodied AI.
