# H-WM: Robotic Task and Motion Planning Guided by Hierarchical World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Noah’s Ark Lab and affiliated universities developed H-WM, a Hierarchical World Model that guides robotic task and motion planning by jointly predicting logical and visual state transitions. This model substantially improves performance on long-horizon tasks, achieving an average success rate of 64.8% compared to 6.4% for unguided methods on the LIBERO-LoHo benchmark.

## Problem

- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models show significant performance degradation on complex, long-horizon robotic tasks due to compounding errors and ambiguous goal specifications.
- Current intermediate guidance methods, such as LLM-based planners and visual world models, suffer from issues like semantic-execution misalignment or compounding prediction errors over extended horizons.
- Classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) offers long-horizon consistency but lacks direct visual grounding, leading to brittleness in unstructured environments.

## Method

- Developed a Hierarchical World Model (**H-WM**) that provides multi-level guidance by jointly predicting logical and visual state transitions.
- Incorporated a Logic World Model (M_L) that learns symbolic planning dynamics from data using a fine-tuned Large Language Model (LLM), performing long-horizon reasoning.
- Designed a Visual World Model to predict stable latent visual features for end-of-subtask states, conditioned on future logical transitions, instead of generating pixel-level images.

## Results

- H-WM-guided VLA policies achieved an average success rate of **64.8%** and a Q-score of **84.9%** on the challenging LIBERO-LoHo benchmark, significantly outperforming unguided methods (**6.4%** success, **55.3%** Q-score).
- Ablation studies showed that logical guidance alone improved success rate by over **40%**, with an additional >**17%** gain from visual guidance, demonstrating the synergistic effect of both.
- Real-world deployment on a UR5e robot for an 8-step table-cleaning task confirmed improved long-horizon success and more accurate pose generation when utilizing H-WM's guidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning symbolic planning dynamics directly from data using large language models can provide robust, long-horizon logical guidance even with incomplete state labels.
> - Predicting latent visual features for end-of-subtask states offers more effective and compact visual guidance compared to pixel-level image generation, reducing error accumulation.
> - The joint and synchronized prediction of logical and visual state transitions within a hierarchical framework is crucial for robust VLA execution in long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic systems capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse environments remains a significant challenge in robotics. Recent advancements in Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, often leveraging large pre-trained foundation models, have enabled a tighter coupling of multimodal perception and control, demonstrating improved generalization over traditional modular pipelines. However, many existing end-to-end VLA methods exhibit performance degradation on long-horizon tasks. This degradation is attributed to several factors, including compounding execution errors, ambiguous goal specifications, limited intermediate supervision, and potential overfitting to agent-centric representations.

To mitigate these issues, richer intermediate guidance has been explored through various paradigms, each with inherent limitations:
1. **LLM-based hierarchical planners:** These systems decompose tasks into subgoals or action sequences using large language models (LLMs). Their primary constraint lies in language as the sole intermediate interface, as LLMs may struggle with reasoning about physical constraints and their unstructured representations can lead to semantic–execution misalignment.
2. **World-model-based approaches:** These aim to provide predictive visual guidance. However, existing formulations often suffer from compounding prediction errors over extended horizons, which can degrade planning reliability, particularly in long-horizon settings. Many focus on pixel-level video generation, which can be difficult to ground in robot actions and prone to error accumulation.
3. **Classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP):** This approach achieves long-horizon consistency through explicit logical world models for symbolic reasoning. A limitation is its reliance on manually designed abstractions and engineered perception-to-symbol pipelines. This leads to weak alignment with raw visual observations, resulting in brittleness to perception noise and poor scalability in unstructured environments. TAMP typically operates independently of visual perception, preventing synchronized symbolic and visual state prediction.

Consequently, a comprehensive framework that delivers informative, grounded, and long-horizon–robust guidance for VLA execution, capable of combining the strengths of these disparate approaches while mitigating their individual shortcomings, has been an open research problem. This work positions itself by proposing a novel hierarchical world model designed to bridge these gaps, jointly predicting logical and visual state transitions to enable robust, long-horizon robotic planning and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a Hierarchical World Model (H-WM) that facilitates more effective intermediate guidance for Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models when executing complex, long-horizon tasks. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from the limitations of current robotic planning and control paradigms:

1. **Addressing Performance Degradation in Long-Horizon Tasks:** End-to-end VLA models often experience a decline in performance on multi-step, long-horizon tasks. This is primarily due to the accumulation of execution errors over time, ambiguous task specifications, insufficient intermediate supervision, and a tendency to overfit to agent-centric representations. The research aims to counteract these issues by providing a structured, stable form of guidance.
2. **Integrating Symbolic Reasoning with Visual Grounding:** Traditional TAMP methods offer robust long-horizon reasoning through symbolic logic but lack direct visual grounding, making them susceptible to perception errors and challenging to scale. Conversely, existing world models often focus on visual prediction (e.g., video generation) but struggle with the long-term consistency and action-grounding necessary for robot control due to compounding errors. The motivation is to create a unified framework that combines the long-horizon robustness of symbolic reasoning with the perceptual grounding of visual prediction.
3. **Providing Informative and Robust Intermediate Guidance:** The limitations of current intermediate guidance approaches, such as the semantic-execution misalignment of LLM-based planners and the error propagation in visual world models, highlight a need for more reliable and actionable guidance. The H-WM seeks to provide guidance that is both logically consistent over long horizons and perceptually relevant at the operational level.
4. **Enabling Synchronized Symbolic and Visual State Prediction:** Prior approaches often treat symbolic and visual predictions independently. A core motivation is to jointly predict logical and visual state transitions within a single framework. This integration aims to ensure that high-level symbolic plans are consistently grounded in low-level visual expectations, thereby mitigating error accumulation and enabling more robust execution across extended task sequences.
5. **Improving Generalization and Physical Feasibility:** By learning symbolic planning dynamics directly from data and aligning them with latent visual representations, the system aims to generalize better to various task instances and provide physically grounded guidance, even under incomplete logical state labels. This is intended to overcome the brittleness associated with manually designed planning domains and perception-to-symbol pipelines.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a robotic planning paradigm that can robustly manage long-horizon tasks by intelligently integrating symbolic logic and visual perception to provide clear, grounded, and consistent intermediate guidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Hierarchical World Model (H-WM) operates at two distinct temporal resolutions to provide multi-level guidance for robotic policies. The world models (logical and visual) are invoked once per subtask step (indexed by `m`), while the VLA (Vision-Language-Action) policy performs continuous low-level control at a higher frequency within each subtask (indexed by `t`).

**A. Symbolic Formalization (Preliminary)**
The paper leverages PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) for symbolic formalization, defining a planning domain `D = (P, A)` where `P` is a set of predicate symbols and `A` is a set of parameterized action schemas. Objects `O` are associated with predicates to form ground atoms. A symbolic logical state `X` is a set of true ground atoms. Each action schema `a` is defined by its `Pre(a)` (preconditions), `Add(a)` (add effects), and `Del(a)` (delete effects). A ground action is applicable if its preconditions are met in the current state `X_m`, and its execution deterministically transitions the state to `X_m+1`.

**B. Sub-goal Visual Representation (Preliminary)**
For each subtask `m`, the robot observes an RGB image `obs_m`, receives the next logical action `a_m+1`, the resulting logical state `X_m+1`, and its joint configuration `q_m`. A fixed vision encoder `ϕ(·)` maps images to a `d`-dimensional latent space `F`. A ground-truth subgoal image `g_m+1_goal` (corresponding to successful subtask completion) has a latent feature `f_m+1_goal = ϕ(g_m+1_goal)`. The visual world model is designed to predict a latent goal feature `f_m+1_pred ∈ F` conditioned on the subtask state.

**C. Logic World Model (M_L)**
The logical world model provides long-horizon reasoning within a symbolic space.
- **Learning:** Instead of relying on hand-crafted PDDL domains, `M_L` learns symbolic planning dynamics from data using a fine-tuned Large Language Model (LLM). Training data consists of curated episodes, where each subtask `m` is annotated with logical states `X_m`, actions `a_m`, observations `obs_m`, and robot configurations `q_m`. Symbolic action sequences and corresponding logical state transitions are converted into Chain-of-Thought (CoT) explanations to train the LLM, modeling explicit logical transitions and goal progression.
- **Inference:** During inference, `M_L` operates in two capacities:
 - `M_search_L`: Proposes candidate logical actions and predicts state transitions.
 - `M_eval_L`: Scores partial trajectories based on logical consistency and alignment with the overall task goal.
This dual role allows the learned model to function as both a predictive world model and a structured reward function, leveraging the base LLM's generalization for robustness to incomplete logical state labels.

**D. Visual World Model**
This component provides stable visual guidance by aligning symbolic state predictions with latent visual representations.
- **Mechanism:** It predicts latent visual features for the *end-of-subtask* state, constrained by future logical state transitions, rather than generating a sequence of pixel-level visual predictions. This aims to reduce error propagation and computational cost.
- **Architecture:** It comprises an "understanding expert" and a "prediction expert."
 - The understanding expert encodes `obs_m`, `a_m+1`, and `X_m+1` to form a joint representation that associates the logical state transition with its visual context.
 - Conditioned on this joint representation and `q_m`, the prediction expert outputs the latent visual subgoal feature `f_m+1_pred` through an iterative denoising process.
- **Training:** Supervision involves synchronizing logical states, logical actions, and corresponding terminal keyframes. `f_m+1_pred` is aligned with the ground-truth feature `f_m+1_goal` (obtained by encoding the goal image with a frozen vision encoder) using a sliced Wasserstein loss. During inference, this model is queried once per subtask, and the predicted feature remains fixed throughout its execution.

**E. Hierarchical World Model Guidance for VLA**
The VLA operates as the low-level robot motion policy, utilizing structured guidance from both the logical and visual world models.
- **Architecture:** The sub-goal VLA consists of three experts, implemented as decoder-only transformers initialized from PaliGemma:
 - **Understanding Expert:** Encodes the current observation `obs_t` and the logical action `a_m+1`, fusing visual and logical information.
 - **Goal Expert:** Processes the latent visual subgoal feature `f_m+1_pred`.
 - **Action Expert:** Conditions on `obs_t`, `a_m+1`, `f_m+1_pred`, and `q_t` to generate a sequence of low-level action chunks (k steps `α_t:t+k`).
- **Integration:** A cross-attention mechanism allows the action expert to attend to both the understanding and goal experts, integrating current observations with goal constraints for motion generation. Reverse information flow is restricted to maintain hierarchical structure and training stability.
- **Training:** The policy is trained end-to-end using a flow-matching objective.

**F. Subtask Completion and Transition Prediction**
To manage the sequential subtasks, a subtask completion predictor head is introduced.
- **Function:** Built upon the understanding expert, this head takes `obs_t` and `a_m+1` as input at the same frequency as the VLA. A dedicated [CLS] token is fed into a lightweight classification head to determine when the current subtask `a_m+1` is completed, enabling synchronized transitions within the hierarchical pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the effectiveness and generality of H-WM's guidance across multiple VLA control policies and benchmarks.

**A. Training Dataset:**
The models were trained on two primary datasets:
- A logically synchronized version of **LIBERO**: This variant provides frame-level alignment between robot states, visual observations, logical states, and logical actions. Annotations were generated through a two-stage process involving predicate classifiers and manual correction.
- The **RoboCerebra benchmark**: This dataset's task decompositions, logical actions, and predicates were adopted and unified for consistent representation.
To further challenge models on long-horizon tasks, a new benchmark, **LIBERO-LoHo**, was introduced. It comprises five tasks, each approximately doubling the task horizon of original LIBERO tasks (1–3 steps) by increasing object manipulation and dependencies, with tasks ranging from five to seven steps.

**B. Benchmark and Evaluation:**
Performance was primarily measured by **Success Rate** (percentage of successfully completed tasks). For extra-long horizon benchmarks like LIBERO-LoHo (up to 7 steps) and RoboCerebra (up to 20 steps), **Q-Score** (fraction of completed sub-goals over total sub-goals) was also reported to provide a more granular evaluation of task progress.

**C. Effectiveness of H-WM Guidance:**
- **LIBERO-LoHo (Table I):** H-WM-guided π_0.5 significantly outperformed its unguided counterpart, π_0.5, achieving an average success rate of 64.8% compared to 6.4%, and an average Q-score of 84.9% versus 55.3%. This represents an improvement of over 50% in success rate and nearly 30% in Q-score.
- **RoboCerebra (Fig. 3.b):** Similar gains were observed, with H-WM-guided π_0.5 showing improvements exceeding 10% in Q-score and nearly 10% in success rate compared to π_0.5. The base π_0.5 often achieved relatively high Q-Scores but low Success Rates, indicating partial completion but overall task failure due to missed intermediate steps or incorrect action ordering.
- **LIBERO-10 (Fig. 3.a):** While H-WM guidance still improved performance, the margin was smaller compared to the longer-horizon benchmarks. This is attributed to the shorter nature of LIBERO-10 tasks, where base VLAs can learn complete procedures without extensive guidance.

**D. Comparison to Baselines:**
H-WM-guided π_0.5 demonstrated consistent outperformance against a range of state-of-the-art VLA baselines, including π_0, π_0.5, OpenVLA, X-VLA, and GR00T, particularly on long-horizon tasks (Table I, Fig. 3.b). Raw VLAs were observed to struggle with long-range dependencies, often omitting or misordering actions. LLM-guided π_0.5, which uses natural-language-based task decomposition, also underperformed both H-WM-guided and Logic-guided variants. This suggests that the ambiguity and less structured nature of natural language, combined with its reduced grounding compared to H-WM's visual components, limits its effectiveness as guidance. The integrated logical reasoning and latent visual subgoals of H-WM provided more reliable and robust guidance. On LIBERO-10, H-WM ranked second due to the shorter task horizons reducing the need for hierarchical guidance.

**E. Ablation Study:**
- **Contribution of Logical Guidance:** The "Logic-guided π_0.5" variant, which excluded the visual world model, already surpassed the unguided π_0.5 by over 40% in success rate and 15% in Q-score (Table I). This indicates the substantial benefit of high-level symbolic reasoning in guiding long-horizon tasks.
- **Contribution of Visual Guidance:** Incorporating visual guidance from the H-WM in addition to logical guidance yielded further improvements, providing more than 10% additional gain in Q-score and 17% in success rate. This finding supports the hypothesis that visual world modeling provides actionable grounding for symbolic plans, enhancing the alignment between symbolic constraints and perceptual execution.
- **Latent Features vs. Pixel-level Generation:** A variant, "H-WM-Stable-Diffusion-π_0.5," replaced latent visual prediction with pixel-level image generation. This variant achieved smaller performance gains than the default H-WM, exhibiting over a 10% drop in overall success rate compared to H-WM-guided π_0.5. The analysis suggests that pixel-level generation might introduce unnecessary visual details and reconstruction noise, making latent features a more compact and effective form of guidance for VLAs.

**F. Real-World Experiment:**
An 8-step table-cleaning task was deployed on a UR5e robot in a real-world setting, requiring complex spatial reasoning and long-horizon planning. Comparison of step-wise success rates for π_0, π_0.5, and H-WM-π_0.5 (trained on 40 manually collected trajectories) demonstrated that logical guidance significantly improved long-horizon success, and the additional visual guidance further enhanced performance by enabling more accurate pose generation (Fig. 3.c).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Hierarchical World Model (H-WM) presented in this paper offers a significant contribution to the field of robotic planning and control, particularly for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks. Its primary significance lies in its ability to effectively bridge the gap between high-level symbolic reasoning and low-level perceptual grounding, a long-standing challenge in robotics.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability for Long-Horizon Tasks:** By jointly modeling logical and visual state transitions, H-WM provides stable, multi-level guidance that significantly mitigates the compounding errors and goal ambiguity often observed in end-to-end VLA models on extended tasks. The experimental results, particularly on the challenging LIBERO-LoHo and RoboCerebra benchmarks, demonstrate substantial improvements in task success rates and sub-goal completion scores. This suggests a pathway towards more reliable autonomous execution of intricate, multi-step robotic operations.
2. **Integration of Complementary Paradigms:** The framework successfully combines the strengths of classical Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) (long-horizon symbolic consistency) with modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (multimodal perception and control) and predictive world models (visual grounding). This integration addresses the limitations of individual approaches, offering a more comprehensive solution for robotic intelligence.
3. **Actionable Visual Grounding of Symbolic Plans:** The visual world model's prediction of latent visual subgoals, directly constrained by logical state transitions, provides a more effective and compact form of visual guidance compared to pixel-level image generation. This ensures that abstract symbolic plans are translated into perceptually meaningful intermediate states, enabling VLAs to generate more accurate and physically feasible motions.
4. **Scalability to Complex Environments:** By learning symbolic planning dynamics from data using fine-tuned LLMs, the logical world model gains robustness to incomplete logical state labels, reducing the brittleness associated with manually designed PDDL domains. This approach has the potential to scale to a wider range of unstructured environments where precise symbolic definitions are difficult to maintain.
5. **Foundation for Future Robot Learning:** The H-WM framework provides a structured approach for guiding robot learning, potentially reducing the data requirements for training VLA policies on complex tasks. The hierarchical nature of guidance can also serve as a blueprint for developing more interpretable and controllable robotic agents.

**Despite its demonstrated performance, the paper identifies several limitations:**

- **Increased System Complexity and Training Cost:** The introduction of additional model components (logical and visual world models) and distinct training stages leads to higher system complexity and increased computational overhead during training.
- **Reliance on Structured Logical State Representations:** The logical world model's effectiveness is predicated on the ability to formulate tasks meaningfully within a symbolic logical space. This might pose challenges for highly unstructured or ambiguous tasks that are difficult to represent with clear logical predicates.

**Future directions suggested by the authors to address these limitations and expand the framework's capabilities include:**

- Enhancing training efficiency to reduce the computational burden.
- Reducing the reliance on explicit logical supervision, potentially through self-supervised or weakly supervised learning of logical states.
- Extending the framework to incorporate additional sensory modalities, such as tactile or force feedback, for improved spatial reasoning and interaction capabilities.

Overall, H-WM represents a step toward building more intelligent and capable robotic systems that can tackle the complexities of real-world, long-horizon tasks by effectively integrating diverse forms of knowledge and guidance.
