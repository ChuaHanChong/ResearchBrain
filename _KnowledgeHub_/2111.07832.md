# iBOT: Image BERT Pre-Training with Online Tokenizer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ByteDance, Johns Hopkins University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and UC Santa Cruz developed iBOT, a self-supervised method for Vision Transformers that applies Masked Image Modeling (MIM) using an online tokenizer. This approach enables ViTs to learn rich visual representations by distilling knowledge from an evolving teacher network, achieving new state-of-the-art results on classification, detection, and segmentation benchmarks while demonstrating enhanced model robustness and a better understanding of part-level image semantics.

## Problem

- Successfully adapting BERT's Masked Language Modeling (MLM) to Vision Transformers (ViTs) proved challenging due to the lack of semantically meaningful visual tokens.
- Existing Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods either predicted raw pixel values (lacking high-level semantics) or relied on separately pre-trained, "offline" tokenizers that primarily captured low-level details and introduced multi-stage pipelines.
- There was a need for a self-supervised learning approach for ViTs that could efficiently learn rich representations encompassing both global and fine-grained local semantic information.

## Method

- **iBOT** introduces a ==teacher-student framework== for ==Masked Image Modeling==, where the teacher network acts as an "==online tokenizer==" by providing evolving soft targets for masked image patches.
- The student network is trained with a ==masked image modeling loss== that predicts the soft output distributions of masked patches from the teacher, eliminating the need for a separate tokenizer pre-training stage.
- A global ==[CLS] token self-distillation loss== is integrated to ensure the network learns robust global visual semantics, complementing the local patch-level prediction objective.

## Results

- iBOT achieved state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet-1K classification, reaching **84.0%** top-1 accuracy for ViT-B/16 and **87.8%** for ViT-L/16 (pre-trained on ImageNet-22K) via fine-tuning.
- It demonstrated superior performance on dense prediction tasks, achieving **51.2 AP_b** and **44.2 AP_m** for object detection and instance segmentation on COCO, and **50.0 mIoU** for semantic segmentation on ADE20K (ViT-B/16), outperforming prior self-supervised methods.
- iBOT models exhibited enhanced robustness to image corruptions, occlusions, and background changes, and learned emergent properties such as part-level semantics within image patches and highly discriminative self-attention maps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Masked Image Modeling (MIM) can be effectively implemented as a self-distillation task, where a student network predicts the output distributions of masked patches from a dynamically evolving teacher network, serving as an "online tokenizer."
> - Using soft probability distributions as targets for image patches is crucial for MIM, as individual patches often lack clear, discrete semantic meanings unlike language tokens.
> - Combining a global self-distillation loss (on the [CLS] token) with a local masked patch prediction loss is essential for the online tokenizer to acquire strong, multi-granularity visual semantics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in iBOT operates at the intersection of two rapidly evolving and impactful areas in deep learning: **Vision Transformers (ViTs)** and **self-supervised learning (SSL)**, specifically adapting the successful **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)** paradigm from Natural Language Processing (NLP) to computer vision.

**From NLP to Vision:** The success of Transformer architectures in NLP, largely driven by pre-training objectives like Masked Language Modeling (MLM) (e.g., BERT), revolutionized language understanding. MLM works by masking a portion of input tokens and training the model to predict the original masked tokens, thereby learning rich contextual representations. With the advent of Vision Transformers, which treat images as sequences of patches (tokens), researchers sought to port this highly effective pre-training strategy to the visual domain, leading to **Masked Image Modeling (MIM)**.

**Challenges in MIM:** However, directly applying MLM to images is not straightforward. In NLP, tokens (words, subwords) inherently carry semantic meaning, and robust tokenizers (e.g., WordPiece) are well-established. For images, defining "semantically meaningful visual tokens" is a fundamental challenge.
- **Early MIM attempts:** Some early approaches (e.g., Pathak et al., 2016; Atito et al., 2021) tried predicting raw pixel values, but this often leads models to focus on low-level details (like high-frequency noise) rather than high-level semantics, resulting in suboptimal performance for semantic understanding tasks.
- **BEiT's approach:** BEiT (Bao et al., 2021) was a significant step forward, using a pre-trained discrete Variational Autoencoder (d-VAE) (like DALL-E's encoder) as an "offline tokenizer" to generate visual tokens. While this provides some abstraction, the authors of iBOT point out that d-VAEs primarily capture low-level local details and, crucially, require a separate, "offline" pre-training stage on potentially extra datasets. This multi-stage pipeline is complex and limits adaptability.

**Self-Supervised Learning in Vision:** Concurrently, self-supervised learning has become dominant in computer vision, enabling models to learn powerful representations from unlabeled data. Most prominent SSL methods (e.g., MoCo, BYOL, DINO, SwAV) rely on **contrastive learning** or **knowledge distillation** by enforcing invariance between different augmented views of the same image. These methods primarily focus on learning **global visual representations** by matching or distilling features from whole images or their global crops, often neglecting the fine-grained internal structure of images. DINO, in particular, introduced the concept of "self-distillation," where a student network learns from an exponentially moving averaged teacher network, demonstrating impressive emergent properties in ViTs like attention maps that correspond to object parts.

**iBOT's Contribution to the Landscape:** iBOT positions itself as a significant advancement by directly addressing the limitations of existing MIM approaches while integrating the strengths of successful self-distillation methods.
- It proposes an "online tokenizer" that is jointly learned with the MIM objective, thus eliminating the need for a multi-stage pre-training pipeline.
- By formulating MIM as self-distillation (similar to DINO), it ensures that the "tokenizer" (the teacher network) learns semantically meaningful visual tokens, overcoming the low-level focus of prior tokenizers.
- It combines global semantic learning (via [CLS] token distillation, inspired by DINO) with local patch-level prediction (MIM), bridging the gap between global contrastive/distillation methods and local generative MIM approaches.
- The research aims to make MIM as effective and standard for Vision Transformers as MLM is for language Transformers, enabling ViTs to fully leverage their capacity for both global context and local detail understanding.

In summary, iBOT pushes the boundaries of self-supervised learning for Vision Transformers by introducing a novel, jointly learned online tokenizer for Masked Image Modeling. It effectively combines the strengths of various SSL paradigms to learn powerful, semantically rich, and robust visual representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives of the iBOT research are driven by the challenges and opportunities at the intersection of Masked Language Modeling (MLM) and Vision Transformers (ViTs):

1. **Replicate MLM Success in Vision:** The primary objective is to successfully transfer the highly effective BERT-like masked prediction paradigm, known as Masked Image Modeling (MIM), to Vision Transformers. The authors aim to establish MIM as a standard and powerful pre-training component for ViTs, mirroring its fundamental role in NLP.

2. **Develop a Semantically Meaningful Visual Tokenizer:** A key bottleneck for effective MIM is the lack of a suitable "visual tokenizer" that can segment images into semantically meaningful units, analogous to how WordPiece tokenizes text. Existing approaches either predict raw pixels (lacking semantics) or rely on pre-trained, offline tokenizers (like d-VAEs in BEiT) that tend to capture only low-level details and necessitate multi-stage training. The core motivation here is to design a tokenizer that understands high-level visual semantics.

3. **Achieve Online and Joint Tokenizer Learning:** To overcome the inefficiencies and inflexibility of multi-stage pre-training pipelines (where the tokenizer is trained separately and then fixed), iBOT aims to develop an "online tokenizer" that can be jointly optimized with the MIM objective. This would simplify the pre-training process and allow the tokenizer to adapt to the specific dataset and learning dynamics.

4. **Learn Rich and Robust Visual Representations:** The ultimate goal is to enable Vision Transformers to learn superior visual features that are effective across a diverse range of downstream tasks. This includes:
 - **Acquiring Strong Visual Semantics:** Ensuring the learned representations capture high-level semantic information from both global and local image perspectives.
 - **Improving Performance on Standard Benchmarks:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on image classification (ImageNet-1K), as well as dense prediction tasks like object detection, instance segmentation (COCO), and semantic segmentation (ADE20K).
 - **Enhancing Robustness:** Developing models that are more resilient to common image corruptions, background changes, and occlusions, thereby increasing their reliability in real-world scenarios.

5. **Uncover Emergent Properties of MIM-trained ViTs:** Beyond quantitative performance, the authors are motivated to investigate and understand the unique properties that emerge from training ViTs with the proposed MIM objective, particularly concerning how they interpret and represent local visual information (e.g., part-level semantics, attention patterns).

In essence, iBOT is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of BERT-like pre-training for Vision Transformers by intelligently designing an "online" and "semantically aware" visual tokenizer, leading to highly effective, robust, and versatile visual representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

iBOT ("image BERT pre-training with Online Tokenizer") introduces a novel self-supervised learning framework that integrates Masked Image Modeling (MIM) with self-distillation, featuring a unique "online tokenizer."

**Core Idea: MIM as Self-Distillation with an Online Tokenizer**
The central insight of iBOT is to conceptualize MIM as a knowledge distillation task. Instead of distilling knowledge from a pre-trained, fixed tokenizer (like BEiT's d-VAE), iBOT distills knowledge from a "teacher" network that itself is evolving during training. This teacher network effectively becomes the "online tokenizer."

**Framework Architecture (Inspired by DINO):**
iBOT employs a teacher-student network architecture, similar to DINO (Caron et al., 2021):
- **Twin Networks:** A student network ($f_s, h_s$) and a teacher network ($f_t, h_t$), both typically Vision Transformers (e.g., ViT, Swin Transformer) with a projection head (MLP).
- **Input Augmentation:** For an input image `x`, two augmented views `u` and `v` are generated (including multi-crop augmentation).
- **Masking for Student:** The student network receives *masked* versions (`u_hat`, `v_hat`) of these views. Specifically, block-wise masking is applied, where certain image patches are replaced with a `[MASK]` token (or a learned embedding `e_[MASK]`).
- **Unmasked Input for Teacher:** The teacher network receives the *original, unmasked* views (`u`, `v`).
- **Teacher Update via EMA:** The parameters of the teacher network ($\theta'$) are updated using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student network's parameters ($\theta$). This ensures the teacher provides a stable, progressively improving target for the student, acting as the online tokenizer. Crucially, gradients are stopped from propagating through the teacher network.

**Loss Functions – Twofold Self-Distillation:**
iBOT minimizes two types of self-distillation losses, combining global and local objectives:

1. **Masked Image Modeling Loss (`L_MIM`):**
 - **Objective:** For the masked patches in the student's input, the student network is trained to predict the *output distribution* of the corresponding *unmasked patches* from the teacher network.
 - **Mechanism:** `L_MIM` is a cross-entropy loss between the student's patch token predictions (`P_patch_θ(u_hat_i)`) for masked locations `i` and the teacher's patch token distributions (`P_patch_θ'(u_i)`) for the original unmasked patches.
 - **Crucial Design:** Instead of predicting a hard one-hot token ID (as in BEiT), iBOT predicts a *soft probability distribution* over patch tokens. This is vital because individual image patches often lack clear, discrete semantic meanings, making soft labels more appropriate for capturing their ambiguous nature. The teacher's output is also centered and sharpened (temperature scaling) before being used as a target.
 - **Online Tokenizer:** The teacher network's ability to produce these target distributions for patches, which are then used as supervision, is why it functions as the "online tokenizer."

2. **[CLS] Token Self-Distillation Loss (`L_CLS`):**
 - **Objective:** This loss (similar to DINO) ensures the overall network learns robust *global visual semantics*. It enforces consistency between the `[CLS]` token representations of different augmented views.
 - **Mechanism:** `L_CLS` is a cross-entropy loss between the student's `[CLS]` token prediction for a masked view (`P_[CLS]_θ(u_hat)`) and the teacher's `[CLS]` token distribution for a different (unmasked) view (`P_[CLS]_θ'(v)`). This loss is symmetrized by also computing `P_[CLS]_θ(v_hat)` vs. `P_[CLS]_θ'(u)`.
 - **Synergy:** This global objective is crucial for the online tokenizer (teacher) to acquire meaningful visual semantics, which then propagates to the patch-level predictions in `L_MIM`.

**Key Implementation Details:**
- **Shared Projection Heads:** The projection heads (`h`) for both the `[CLS]` token and the patch tokens are shared (i.e., `h_[CLS]_s = h_patch_s`, `h_[CLS]_t = h_patch_t`). This design choice empirically showed better performance, facilitating the transfer of high-level semantics learned by the `[CLS]` token distillation to the patch-level tokenization.
- **Masking Strategy:** Random block-wise masking is used. During multi-crop training, the masking ratio can randomly be set to 0 (effectively reducing to DINO) or a value between [0.1, 0.5], improving training stability.
- **Optimizer and Training:** AdamW optimizer, large batch sizes (e.g., 1024), and various ViT/Swin Transformer backbone sizes are used. Learning rate scheduling involves a linear ramp-up.

By combining `L_MIM` and `L_CLS`, iBOT leverages the strengths of both local masked prediction and global view-invariance. The "online tokenizer" concept, realized through EMA updates and soft target distributions, addresses the long-standing challenge of creating a semantically rich and efficiently trained visual tokenizer for MIM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

iBOT demonstrates significant advancements across a wide array of computer vision tasks, attributed to its novel online tokenizer and integrated self-supervised learning strategy.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **ImageNet-1K Classification:**
 - **Linear Probing & k-NN:** iBOT achieves state-of-the-art results for feature quality. For ViT-Base/16, it reaches 79.5% linear probing accuracy and 77.1% k-NN accuracy, outperforming prior methods like DINO. With ViT-L/16, it achieves 81.0% linear probing and 78.0% k-NN. The performance gain over DINO increases with larger models, indicating iBOT's scalability.
 - **Fine-tuning:** iBOT consistently outperforms other self-supervised methods and supervised baselines. For ViT-Base/16, it reaches 84.0% top-1 accuracy. With larger ViT-L/16 pre-trained on ImageNet-22K, it achieves an impressive 87.8% fine-tuning accuracy when fine-tuned at 512x512 resolution, surpassing BEiT by a notable margin.
- **Transfer Learning:** iBOT shows superior generalization ability when pre-trained on ImageNet-1K and fine-tuned on smaller downstream datasets (e.g., CIFAR10/100, iNaturalist, Flowers, Cars). It consistently achieves state-of-the-art transfer results, especially on larger datasets like iNaturalist, suggesting it learns more versatile features.
- **Semi-supervised and Unsupervised Learning:** iBOT exhibits higher label efficiency in semi-supervised settings (e.g., 1.6% better than DINO with 1% ImageNet labels) and learns stronger visual semantics for unsupervised clustering tasks (e.g., 1.8% higher NMI than prior SOTA).
- **Dense Downstream Tasks:**
 - **Object Detection & Instance Segmentation (COCO):** Using ViT-S/16 as a backbone, iBOT improves mean average precision (AP_b and AP_m) significantly over supervised Swin-T and other self-supervised methods (e.g., 49.4 AP_b for iBOT ViT-S/16 vs. 46.2 for supervised ViT-S/16). With ViT-B/16, it sets new state-of-the-art, achieving 51.2 AP_b and 44.2 AP_m.
 - **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K):** iBOT also demonstrates strong performance, with a 0.9 mIoU gain over supervised ViT-S/16. Notably, for ViT-B/16, it achieves 50.0 mIoU with UperNet, surpassing DINO by 3.2 mIoU and showing a strong advantage in capturing local semantics compared to BEiT (which performs poorly with a linear segmentation head).

#### 4.2. Emergent Properties of MIM-trained ViTs

- **Discovery of Pattern Layouts / Part-Level Semantics:**
 - iBOT learns meaningful patterns for image patches, encompassing both high-level semantics (e.g., headlight, dog's ear, human hair) and low-level details (textures, shapes, colors).
 - This is a crucial distinction from BEiT's offline tokenizer, which primarily captures low-level details, indicating that iBOT's online tokenizer truly learns richer semantic information.
- **Improved Image Recognition through Part Semantics:**
 - Quantitative analysis using part-wise linear classification (averaging top-k patch tokens based on attention scores) shows that iBOT's patch representations contain significant semantic information.
 - When using patch representations directly, iBOT substantially outperforms DINO (e.g., 5.9% higher accuracy with top-56 patches, and a remarkable 17.9% higher accuracy when using only the single top-attention patch). This suggests strong understanding of discriminative image parts.
- **Discriminative Parts in Self-Attention Maps:** Visualizations of self-attention maps reveal that iBOT-trained ViTs effectively segment different objects or parts within an image and focus on discriminative regions (e.g., wheel of a car, beak of a bird).
- **Enhanced Robustness:**
 - **Background Change:** iBOT is more robust to variations in background (tested on ImageNet-9), showing higher accuracy across most background-modified variants.
 - **Occlusion:** The model exhibits greater resilience to occlusion (random, salient, non-salient patch dropping), maintaining smaller performance drops, which is a direct benefit of the masked prediction pre-training objective.
 - **Out-of-Distribution Examples:** iBOT performs better on natural adversarial examples (ImageNet-A) and common corruptions (ImageNet-C), demonstrating improved generalization to unseen data perturbations.
 - **Shuffle Robustness:** iBOT is less sensitive to shuffled input patches compared to supervised baselines, implying less reliance on absolute positional embeddings for object recognition.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of `L_CLS`:** Experiments confirm that MIM alone (without `L_CLS`) leads to poor semantic learning (e.g., 9.5% k-NN accuracy), highlighting that `L_CLS` is essential for the online tokenizer to acquire strong visual semantics.
- **Online vs. Offline Tokenizers:** The online, self-distilling tokenizer of iBOT significantly outperforms fixed/pre-trained tokenizers (like DALL-E encoder or a standalone DINO used as a tokenizer), validating the benefits of joint optimization.
- **Soft vs. Hard Labels:** Using soft probability distributions as targets for patch tokens (softmax output) is found to be crucial for performance, outperforming hard one-hot token IDs, likely because image patches have ambiguous semantic meanings.
- **Shared Projection Head:** Sharing parameters between `[CLS]` token and patch token projection heads (`h_[CLS] = h_patch`) consistently yielded better results.

These findings collectively establish iBOT as a powerful and highly effective self-supervised pre-training method for Vision Transformers, not only achieving state-of-the-art results but also exhibiting desirable emergent properties that enhance understanding and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

iBOT represents a pivotal advancement in the field of self-supervised learning for computer vision, offering several layers of significance and potential impact:

1. **Bridging the NLP-Vision Gap for Masked Modeling:** iBOT successfully addresses the core challenge of adapting BERT's masked language modeling (MLM) to images. By introducing the concept of an "online tokenizer," it effectively creates a semantically meaningful visual counterpart to the linguistic tokenizer, making Masked Image Modeling (MIM) a highly viable and potent pre-training paradigm for Vision Transformers. This is a crucial step towards unifying powerful self-supervised learning principles across different modalities.

2. **Simplified and More Efficient Pre-training:** A major practical impact of iBOT is the elimination of the multi-stage training pipeline. Unlike methods requiring a separate, pre-trained tokenizer, iBOT's online tokenizer is jointly learned and updated via EMA. This streamlines the self-supervised learning process, making it more efficient, adaptable, and less resource-intensive in terms of separate model development and data requirements.

3. **Superior and Versatile Visual Representations:** The method yields state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive suite of vision tasks, including image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation. This widespread success indicates that iBOT learns exceptionally rich and versatile visual representations that are beneficial for both global image understanding and fine-grained, pixel-level tasks. This makes iBOT a strong foundation for a wide range of computer vision applications.

4. **Enhanced Model Robustness and Interpretability:**
 - **Part-Level Semantics:** The emergent property of learning "part-level semantics" within image patches is highly significant. It demonstrates that iBOT's internal representations are not just abstract but align with human-understandable visual components, leading to models that can better reason about local details.
 - **Robustness to Perturbations:** The improved robustness against common corruptions, occlusions, and background changes is a critical practical advantage. Models pre-trained with iBOT are more reliable in real-world, noisy, or varied environments, where clean, ideal inputs are rare. This is particularly valuable for safety-critical applications.
 - **Attention Mechanisms:** The clearer, more discriminative self-attention maps provide better insights into how ViTs process information, enhancing the interpretability of these complex models.

5. **Scalability and Future Research Directions:** iBOT's demonstrated scalability to larger models (e.g., ViT-L/16) and larger pre-training datasets (ImageNet-22K) suggests its potential for even more powerful models in the future. This work opens up new avenues for research into:
 - Exploring even larger-scale MIM pre-training on vast unlabelled datasets.
 - Further dissecting the learned part-level semantics and their applications.
 - Investigating how this self-supervised approach can contribute to multi-modal learning beyond vision (e.g., vision-language tasks).

6. **Accessibility and Community Impact:** By making the code and models publicly available, the authors significantly contribute to the broader research community. This allows other researchers and practitioners to build upon iBOT, reproduce results, and integrate its powerful pre-trained models into their own projects, accelerating advancements in the field.

In conclusion, iBOT represents a significant step forward in self-supervised learning for Vision Transformers. Its innovative "online tokenizer" approach for MIM not only achieves new benchmarks in performance and robustness but also offers a more elegant and efficient pre-training pipeline, solidifying the role of masked modeling as a cornerstone for future advancements in visual AI.
