# IGen: Scalable Data Generation for Robot Learning from Open-World Images

> [!summary] Summary
> IGen is a simulation-free pipeline that turns a single open-world image into paired robot observations and actions. Foundation vision models lift pixels into 3D point clouds and spatial keypoints, a VLM plans keypoint-conditioned SE(3) control functions, and rigid-motion point-cloud rendering synthesizes temporally coherent video. Policies fine-tuned solely on IGen data reach 66.7% real-world success, surpassing teleoperation under an equal collection-time budget.

## Problem

- Real-to-sim-to-real pipelines require explicit reconstruction of each physical workspace from multi-view capture, tying generation to scene-specific acquisition and blocking use of arbitrary in-the-wild images.
- Video generation models predict future frames but emit no explicit robot actions, and degrade on long-horizon or complex instruction-driven manipulation while consuming heavy compute.
- Teleoperated on-robot collection is labor-intensive and environment-specific, while human-video sources carry limited scene diversity and human embodiment bias, leaving internet images unexploited.

## Method

- **IGen**'s Scene Reconstruction: ==monocular metric depth==, segmentation masks, ==DINOv2== features with ==K-means== spatial keypoints, background inpainting, and ==image-to-3D object completion== re-posed via 6D estimation into an editable point cloud.
- Spatial Planning: a ==VLM== decomposes the instruction into sub-stages and emits Python ==keypoint-conditioned control functions== over end-effector ==SE(3)== poses, with grasp priors and a motion planner executing in simulation.
- Experience Synthesis: ==simulation-free rendering== composes static background, rigidly transformed grasped object, and rendered robot point clouds per timestep, plus ==spatial randomization== over sampled tabletop placement points for scale.

## Results

- Scene fidelity beats the Simpler real-to-sim baseline: average PSNR **27.00** versus **17.26**, SSIM **0.852** versus **0.683**, and up to **5.13x** lower perceptual LPIPS distance.
- On DreamGen Bench IGen nearly doubles baseline instruction-following successes under one VLM judge, needing only **8.3 GB** GPU and **18.6 s** per sample, roughly **30-200x** cheaper than video baselines.
- Fine-tuning a flow-based VLA on **1,000** IGen samples reaches **66.7%** real-world success versus **58.3%** for teleoperated demos at equal collection time; zero-shot was near **0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D structure plus VLM planning yields executable grounded actions where video generators cannot, favoring rendering pipelines over generative pixel prediction for action-labeled data.
> - Under an equal wall-clock budget, cheap synthetic scale can beat scarce human teleoperation, shifting the data question from per-demo quality to generation throughput.
> - The method inherits rigid-body assumptions: objects move rigidly with the gripper, so deformables, articulation, and contact-rich dynamics stay outside the generated distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning, particularly visuomotor policy learning, has demonstrated capabilities in enabling robots to perform manipulation tasks. However, the development of generalist robotic policies is significantly constrained by the demand for large-scale, high-quality, paired visual-action training data. Traditional methods for data collection on physical robots are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and often result in datasets that are specific to particular environments, thus limiting the generalization abilities of trained policies.

In contrast, open-world images, such as those abundantly available on the internet, offer a diverse and low-cost source of visual information. These images capture a wide array of real-world scenarios that are relevant to robotic manipulation. The advancement of large vision-language models (VLMs) has further highlighted the potential of open-world images for training robust perception systems. However, a significant challenge arises from the inherent nature of these images: while they provide semantic visual content, they lack the physically grounded, executable action sequences necessary for robot policy learning. This discrepancy prevents the direct and scalable utilization of open-world images for training embodied AI.

Existing approaches attempt to bridge this gap through various strategies:
- **Real-to-Sim-to-Real methods:** These methods aim to reconstruct physical workspaces into simulation environments for data generation. While capable of producing executable robot workspaces, they typically require extensive visual data collection (often multi-view observations) for scene-specific reconstruction, which limits their scalability and applicability to arbitrary open-world images. Such methods often involve creating "digital twins" or "digital cousins" of real scenes, but struggle to capture fine-grained details for realistic world modeling.
- **Experience synthesis from unstructured data:** This category includes approaches that leverage human demonstration videos or large-scale pre-trained video generation models. While offering some scalability, methods using human videos often suffer from limited scene diversity and human-induced biases. Video generation models, on the other hand, frequently face limitations in providing explicit robot actions, handling complex or long-horizon tasks, and can incur substantial computational costs. Furthermore, directly synthesizing robotic actions from videos can suffer from temporal discontinuities and geometric distortions.

IGen is positioned to address the limitations of these existing methods by offering a framework that directly transforms unstructured open-world images into grounded visuomotor data without requiring explicit physics-based simulation environments or extensive multi-view scene reconstruction. It aims to harness the vastness of internet images, which are the most abundant visual data source, to overcome the data bottleneck in robot learning and promote the development of scalable, general-purpose robotic policies. By integrating modern vision-language models and 3D reconstruction techniques, IGen seeks to automate the generation of robot behaviors and corresponding visual observations, thereby enabling robust policy training and real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind IGen stems from the increasing demand for large-scale, diverse, and high-quality training data for generalist robotic policies. Current methods of acquiring such data on physical robots are prohibitively expensive, labor-intensive, and inherently limited to specific operational environments. This data scarcity remains a significant impediment to the development of robotic policies that can generalize effectively across a wide range of real-world scenarios.

In parallel, open-world images, readily available from internet sources, represent a vast and diverse repository of visual information that often depicts scenes relevant to robotic manipulation tasks. The potential of these images to serve as a low-cost, scalable source for robot learning data is substantial. However, their primary limitation is the absence of associated robot actions or a structured understanding of 3D scene geometry, rendering them largely unusable for direct robot policy training.

The key objectives of this work are therefore to:

1. **Bridge the data gap:** Develop a framework that can effectively convert unstructured, open-world 2D images into physically grounded and executable visuomotor data suitable for training robotic policies.
2. **Achieve scalability and automation:** Enable the generation of large-scale robotic training data automatically, without human intervention for annotation or on-robot data collection. This includes supporting cross-scene generalization and instruction diversity.
3. **Transform 2D pixels into actionable 3D representations:** Convert raw 2D image data into structured 3D scene representations (such as point clouds and spatial keypoints) that robots can interpret for scene understanding, task reasoning, and motion planning.
4. **Facilitate task-consistent behavior generation:** Leverage the reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to translate high-level task instructions into detailed, low-level action plans and end-effector pose sequences (SE(3) trajectories) that are physically aligned and task-consistent.
5. **Synthesize realistic observations:** Generate dynamic scene evolutions and render temporally coherent visual observations that correspond to the executed actions, ensuring high visual fidelity and alignment with real-world perception.
6. **Enable direct policy transfer to the real world:** Produce visuomotor data of sufficient quality such that policies trained solely on this synthetic data can effectively generalize and successfully perform manipulation tasks in real-world environments, without requiring additional real-world demonstrations.

By achieving these objectives, IGen aims to transform the paradigm of robot data acquisition, providing a foundation for scalable robot learning that significantly reduces the reliance on traditional, costly, and limited data collection methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IGen is structured around a three-stage pipeline designed to generate task-specific robot actions and corresponding visual observations from open-world images.

#### 3.1. From Pixels to Structured 3D Representations

The initial stage focuses on converting unstructured 2D pixels from a single open-world image into a manipulable 3D workspace.
1. **Depth and Geometry Estimation:** A monocular geometric foundation model (specifically, Metric3Dv2) is employed to estimate the depth and geometric structure of the scene. For open-world images, a canonical camera model (fixed focal length) is used, while for known camera types, their intrinsic parameters are adopted. This process lifts image pixels into 3D space, generating a dense 3D point cloud.
2. **Object Identification and Segmentation:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) analyzes the task prompt to identify relevant objects. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) then generates masks (M) for these task-relevant objects.
3. **Spatial Keypoint Extraction:** Scene features are extracted using DINOv2. K-means clustering is applied to both the feature embeddings and the 3D coordinates, yielding a set of K spatial keypoints, K = {k_j ∈ R^3}, each with its 3D coordinates.
4. **Scene and Object Reconstruction:** An inpainting model (e.g., Inpaint Anything) is used to remove the target manipulable object from the original image and reconstruct the background (I_bg), which is then lifted to a dense 3D background point cloud (P_bg). For manipulated objects, a 3D generative reconstruction model (TRELLIS) is employed to infer their full 3D shape and appearance from the monocular view. These reconstructed objects are then repositioned to their original pose using 6D pose estimation.
5. **Spatial Domain Randomization:** To facilitate generalization, feasible placement points are extracted from supporting surfaces (e.g., tabletops) in the scene. These points serve as candidate spatial poses for object placement during data generation, enabling spatial randomization.

The output of this stage is a structured 3D scene representation, including dense point clouds for the background and reconstructed objects, and a set of spatial keypoints with their 3D coordinates.

#### 3.2. Spatial Planning for Behavior Generation

This stage leverages VLMs for high-level task planning and low-level action generation.
1. **VLM-Guided Task Planning:** The VLM receives the annotated image (with keypoints), their 3D coordinates, and the task instruction. It decomposes the overall task into a sequence of sub-stages (S = {S_i}), and for each sub-stage, it generates an action description linked to the identified keypoints.
2. **Low-Level Control Generation:** A programmable control language based on the end-effector's SE(3) pose is used. The VLM translates the high-level task stages (S) into executable low-level control functions (F = {f_i}). Each function f_i is a keypoint-conditioned solver that computes a reference end-effector pose from the spatial anchors. VLM infers parameters like translation distance and lift height from 3D keypoint coordinates.
3. **Grasping and Manipulation:** During the pre-manipulation phase, a grasping prior model (e.g., GraspGen) predicts the end-effector pose for precise object interaction, and the gripper state is determined by the point cloud width along the gripper’s principal axis. During manipulation, keypoints on the manipulated object are treated as rigidly attached to the end-effector, while others act as static scene anchors.
4. **Motion Planning and Simulation:** Starting from an initial robot state, a motion planner (Curobo) generates feasible trajectories to reach a set of sub-goals (X = {ˆx_i}) defined by the control functions. These trajectories are then executed in a simulation environment (Isaac Sim), producing an action sequence (A = {a_t}) over T time steps. Each action a_t includes the robot’s end-effector pose and joint positions.

This stage outputs a sequence of robot actions, detailing end-effector poses and joint positions over time.

#### 3.3. Experience Synthesis for Robot Learning

The final stage synthesizes visual observations synchronized with the generated robot actions.
1. **Robot Point Cloud Synthesis:** A simulated robot is placed at its planned pose (p_robot) in the simulation, and a virtual depth camera is positioned (p_cam) to match the viewpoint used for scene reconstruction. The action sequence (A) provides the end-effector pose trajectory (T = {T_t}). At each time step t, the simulation renders RGB and depth frames, which are then back-projected through the virtual camera (C) to construct the point cloud sequence of the robot’s motion (P_robot = {P_robot,t}).
2. **Dynamic Object Interaction:** The background (P_bg) is treated as a static point cloud. The manipulated object's point cloud (P_obj = {P_obj,t}) undergoes rigid-body transformations. If a grasp is established at time t_g (with object pose T_obj,t_g and end-effector pose T_t_g), for all subsequent time steps t during which the gripper holds the object, the object’s world pose evolves by rigidly following the end-effector. The transformation for P_obj,t is computed using the current end-effector pose T_t and the initial grasp transformation.
3. **Composite Point Cloud Sequence:** The complete task scene is represented as a composite point cloud sequence by combining the static background, the dynamic robot, and the transformed manipulated object: P_task = P_bg ∪ P_obj ∪ P_robot.
4. **Visual Observation Rendering:** The composite point cloud sequence (P_task) is rendered frame-by-frame through the virtual camera (C) to obtain the visual observations (O).

The output of IGen is a paired visual-action dataset (O, A), consisting of temporally synchronized visual observations and executable robot actions, ready for robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated IGen across three dimensions: visual fidelity of synthesized scenes, quality of generated robot behaviors, and transferability of policies trained on IGen data to real-world tasks.

#### 4.1. Scene Reconstruction Fidelity

IGen’s ability to generate visually realistic data from real-world images was assessed by comparing its reconstructed digital scenes against those generated by Real-to-Sim methods (specifically, Simpler) on the Simpler dataset.
- **Metrics:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS (computed using AlexNet, VGGNet, and SqueezeNet variants) were used to quantify the visual consistency between original real-world scenes and the reconstructed scenes.
- **Results:** IGen demonstrated superior performance across all evaluation metrics compared to the Real-to-Sim baseline. For instance, IGen achieved up to a 5.13× improvement in LPIPS scores, which measure perceptual discrepancy, indicating a significantly higher visual similarity between IGen-generated scenes and the corresponding real-world images. This suggests that IGen can produce visually faithful and realistic observations, which is crucial for mitigating the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Behavior Generation

The quality of IGen’s generated robotic behaviors, in terms of accuracy and instruction alignment, was evaluated qualitatively and quantitatively.

- **Qualitative Experiments:** Comparisons were made with Cosmos-Predict2 and TesserAct on unlabeled, open-world images with scene-specific manipulation instructions.
 - **Results:** IGen generated complete, coherent robotic behaviors that accurately followed task instructions and produced physically consistent motions and visual observations aligned with real-world physics. In contrast, baseline methods frequently exhibited temporal discontinuities, geometric distortions, and inaccurate motion execution, failing to produce instruction-aligned behaviors.

- **Quantitative Experiments:** Evaluation was conducted on the DreamGen Bench using two criteria: Instruction Following and Physics Alignment. Large Vision-Language Models (GPT-4o, Qwen-3-VL-Plus, GLM-4.5V) served as evaluation models.
 - **Results:** IGen outperformed all compared models in both Instruction Following and Physics Alignment. For example, IGen achieved nearly twice as many Instruction Following successes as the baseline when evaluated by Qwen-3-VL-Plus. These findings indicate that IGen produces high-quality visual observations and accurate action sequences, which are suitable for effective robot learning.

- **Computational Efficiency:** The efficiency of IGen was assessed by measuring average per-sample generation time and GPU memory utilization, comparing it with Cosmos-Predict2 and TesserAct.
 - **Results:** IGen demonstrated higher computational efficiency, requiring approximately 8.3 GB of GPU memory and 18.6 seconds per sample. This represents a substantial efficiency gain, being approximately 30× faster than TesserAct and 200× faster than Cosmos-Predict2 under similar GPU memory conditions. This efficiency supports large-scale robotic data synthesis.

#### 4.3. Robot Learning from Unstructured Images

The ultimate objective was to evaluate if policies trained solely on IGen-generated data could perform real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Hardware Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a Franka Research 3 robot arm, using a Microsoft Kinect RGB-D camera (RGB stream only) for perception.
- **Robot Policy:** The π0 vision-language-action model was fine-tuned using LoRA.
- **Tasks and Evaluation:** Diverse manipulation tasks were designed, including Placement (“Place the bottle”), Scene-Object Interaction (“Water the flowers”), and Object-Object Interaction (“Hit Box”). Policies were evaluated under five settings: zero-shot, 10 human-teleoperated samples, 100 human-teleoperated samples, 100 IGen-generated samples, and 1,000 IGen-generated samples.
- **Data Generation:** IGen generated 100 to 1,000 spatially randomized samples per task from a *single* camera-view image, without any on-robot data collection.
- **Performance Analysis:**
 - Zero-shot performance of π0 was near zero, indicating a need for fine-tuning.
 - Policies fine-tuned on IGen-generated data showed a significant increase in task success rates. For example, on the "Place Bottle" task, the success rate increased from 0.0% (zero-shot) to 75.0% with IGen data.
 - With 100 and 1,000 IGen-generated samples, policies achieved average success rates of 44.5% and 66.7%, respectively. These figures surpassed the success rates of 19.4% (100 samples) and 58.3% (1,000 samples) achieved by policies trained on human-teleoperated data within a comparable data collection time budget.
 - The policies trained on a larger scale of IGen-generated data demonstrated robust performance in executing task trajectories in the real world.

These findings suggest that IGen can generate effective robotic training data directly from a single image, enabling policies to learn and successfully perform real-world manipulation tasks without human-teleoperated demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IGen framework represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by addressing the critical challenge of data scarcity for training generalist robotic policies. Its ability to automatically transform unstructured open-world images into high-quality, physically grounded visuomotor data carries several implications:

1. **Scalable Data Acquisition:** IGen offers a scalable solution for generating vast quantities of diverse training data. By leveraging readily available internet images, it bypasses the logistical and financial bottlenecks associated with traditional, labor-intensive on-robot data collection, enabling an exponential increase in data volume.
2. **Enhanced Generalization:** The diversity of open-world images, coupled with IGen's spatial randomization capabilities, contributes to training robot policies that can generalize more effectively across varied real-world scenes and task settings. This is a crucial step toward developing truly generalist robots.
3. **Reduced Human Effort:** The framework eliminates the need for human annotation or teleoperation for data generation, significantly reducing the human effort and expertise required to build large robot datasets. This automation democratizes access to robot training data, allowing researchers and practitioners to focus on policy development rather than data acquisition.
4. **Improved Policy Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that policies trained solely on IGen-generated data can achieve or even surpass the performance of policies trained on human-teleoperated data, particularly at larger data scales. This validation highlights the quality and reliability of IGen's synthetic data for robust real-world deployment.
5. **Leveraging Open-World Visual Resources:** IGen provides a method to unlock the potential of the immense volume of unstructured visual data available online. It transforms this previously inaccessible resource into a powerful foundation for embodied AI learning, bridging the gap between perception (from images) and action (for robots).
6. **Lightweight Data Generation Pipeline:** By focusing on point cloud synthesis and leveraging pre-trained vision-language models, IGen offers an alternative to complex, physics-based simulation environments that often require detailed object modeling and environmental reconstruction. This makes the data generation process more efficient and adaptable to diverse scenes.

In conclusion, IGen has the potential to alleviate the fundamental bottleneck of limited real-world robot data, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and generalist robotic systems. Its contribution could accelerate research and development in robot learning by providing an efficient, scalable, and automated mechanism for generating essential training experiences.
