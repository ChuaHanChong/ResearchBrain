# Transformers in Vision: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive overview of Transformer models in computer vision, systematically organizing the rapidly expanding literature by architectural design and task applications. It synthesizes findings on how these models, originating from NLP, have been successfully adapted to achieve competitive performance across various vision tasks, including classification, detection, and segmentation.

## Problem

- Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), while dominant, often struggle with capturing long-range dependencies and global context in visual data.
- The rapidly growing body of research on applying Transformer models (successful in NLP) to computer vision lacked a dedicated, comprehensive, and structured review.
- A clear resource was needed to understand the motivations, architectural adaptations, and performance of Transformers across diverse vision tasks.

## Method

- Introduces foundational Transformer concepts, including ==self-attention== and supervised/self-supervised pre-training mechanisms.
- Develops a comprehensive ==taxonomy== to categorize vision Transformer models based on their architectural designs (e.g., uniform-scale, multi-scale, hybrid, self-supervised).
- Systematically reviews the application of these models across a wide array of computer vision tasks, highlighting task-specific adaptations and performance.

## Results

- Transformer models demonstrate strengths in vision including enhanced long-range dependency modeling, parallel processing capabilities, and scalability to large datasets.
- Many successful Vision Transformers integrate with CNNs, combining CNN's inductive biases for local features with Transformer's global context modeling.
- Transformer-based approaches have shown competitive performance in diverse tasks such as object detection (e.g., DETR), image generation (e.g., DALL·E), multi-modal tasks (e.g., CLIP), and 3D analysis.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers successfully transitioned from NLP to computer vision, achieving competitive performance across high-level and low-level tasks due to their ability to model long-range dependencies and inherent parallelism.
> - Architectural adaptations like patch-based inputs, hierarchical designs, and hybrid CNN-Transformer models have been crucial for integrating Transformers into vision and addressing their computational complexity.
> - Large-scale pre-training, especially self-supervised learning, is fundamental for Vision Transformers to learn robust and generalizable representations, often leading to emergent properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey is situated at the crucial intersection of natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision (CV), documenting a significant paradigm shift driven by the "Transformer" architecture. Originating from the "Attention Is All You Need" paper in 2017, Transformers revolutionized NLP, leading to models like BERT and GPT with unprecedented scalability and performance on various language tasks. Their ability to model long-range dependencies and inherent parallelism offered distinct advantages over traditional recurrent networks (e.g., LSTMs).

The success in NLP naturally "intrigued the vision community" to explore Transformers' applicability to computer vision problems. For a long time, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) were the undisputed backbone of virtually all successful computer vision systems, leveraging inductive biases like translational equivariance and local connectivity. However, CNNs inherently struggle with capturing global context due to their local receptive fields, requiring complex architectures (e.g., deeper layers, larger kernels, or specialized modules) to address this. Transformers, by contrast, are naturally suited as "set-functions," requiring minimal inductive biases and demonstrating excellent scalability, making them attractive for vision tasks that demand global understanding or exhibit diverse, irregular inputs.

This survey meticulously chronicles this cross-domain adaptation, which began with modest attempts to integrate self-attention into CNNs (e.g., non-local blocks) and rapidly evolved into "pure" Transformer-based vision models (Vision Transformers or ViTs). The paper highlights the significant challenge of adapting Transformers from sequential text data to structured visual data, often characterized by spatial and temporal coherence. While early ViTs required immense datasets (e.g., JFT-300M) to overcome their lack of inherent visual inductive biases, subsequent works (e.g., DeiT, Swin Transformer) have developed techniques to make them more data-efficient or hybridize them with CNNs, demonstrating competitive performance even on medium-sized datasets like ImageNet.

The survey uniquely contributes by providing a dedicated, comprehensive overview of Transformer models *specifically* in the computer vision domain. It distinguishes itself from earlier surveys that either focused on NLP Transformers or more general attention mechanisms. By systematically organizing the rapidly expanding literature on visual Transformers, the authors provide a vital resource for researchers to understand the architectural design space, application-specific adaptations, and the evolving relationship between Transformers and CNNs in vision. It essentially maps the landscape of an area that, as shown by the paper's Figure 1, experienced exponential growth in the years leading up to its publication.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of "Transformers in Vision: A Survey" is to provide a comprehensive and structured overview of the rapidly evolving landscape of Transformer models within the computer vision discipline.

The motivations driving this ambitious survey are multifaceted:
- **The Unprecedented Success of Transformers in NLP:** The "astounding results" achieved by Transformer models in natural language processing (e.g., BERT, GPT, T5) have undeniably sparked immense interest across other AI domains. Their ability to model "long dependencies between input sequence elements" and support "parallel processing" are compelling advantages. The dramatic scalability of these models, exemplified by GPT-3 and Switch Transformer with billions or even trillions of parameters, showcased their potential for handling vast amounts of data and learning highly complex representations.
- **Distinct Advantages for Vision Tasks:** Unlike traditional convolutional networks, Transformers "require minimal inductive biases for their design and are naturally suited as set-functions." This flexibility means they are less constrained by rigid assumptions about data structure, potentially allowing them to discover novel patterns. Furthermore, their "straightforward design" enables processing "multiple modalities (e.g., images, videos, text and speech) using similar processing blocks," paving the way for more unified multi-modal AI systems. These strengths have already led to "exciting progress on a number of vision tasks."
- **Need for a Dedicated and Comprehensive Resource:** Given the rapid proliferation of Transformer-based models in computer vision since their initial successful adaptations, a structured and up-to-date review was sorely needed. The authors explicitly state that "other literature reviews mainly focus on the NLP domain or cover generic attention-based approaches." This survey aims to fill that gap by "focusing on the newly emerging area of visual transformers," organizing approaches "according to the intrinsic features of self-attention and the investigated task."
- **Igniting Further Research:** By highlighting the "respective advantages and limitations of popular techniques" and providing an "analysis on open research directions and possible future works," the authors explicitly state their hope that "this effort will ignite further interest in the community to solve current challenges towards the application of transformer models in computer vision." This includes addressing issues like high computational cost, large data requirements, and the need for vision-tailored designs.

In essence, the survey aims to synthesize the burgeoning knowledge on Transformers in vision, providing foundational understanding, cataloging diverse applications, analyzing current state-of-the-art methods, and charting a course for future research to overcome existing limitations and fully harness the potential of this transformative architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a comprehensive literature review and survey, rather than presenting new empirical research. Its methodology is based on a structured analysis and synthesis of existing research papers on the application of Transformer models in computer vision.

The approach can be broken down into several key steps:

1. **Foundational Concepts Introduction:** The survey begins by establishing a strong conceptual base. It introduces the two key ideas underpinning conventional Transformer models:
 - **Self-Attention:** A detailed explanation of how self-attention mechanisms, including masked self-attention and multi-head attention, function to estimate the relevance between elements in a sequence. It also draws parallels and distinctions with convolutional operations, particularly regarding dynamic filter calculation and permutation invariance.
 - **(Self) Supervised Pre-training:** Discussion of the two-stage training mechanism (pre-training on large datasets, then fine-tuning on target tasks). Emphasis is placed on the crucial role of self-supervised learning (SSL) in enabling scalability and generalization for Transformers, categorizing SSL methods into generative, context-based, and cross-modal approaches.
 - **Seminal Transformer Models:** A brief tutorial on the original Transformer model (Vaswani et al., 2017) and Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT), highlighting their encoder-decoder structures, positional encodings, and pre-training objectives (e.g., Masked Language Model, Next Sentence Prediction).

2. **Taxonomy and Categorization of Vision Transformers:** The authors develop a clear taxonomy to organize the diverse landscape of vision models employing self-attention. This categorization is crucial for navigating the vast literature:
 - **Single-Head Self-Attention:** Models that integrate self-attention, often within CNN architectures (e.g., non-local blocks, criss-cross attention) or as stand-alone primitives (e.g., local self-attention, vector attention), aiming to enhance feature learning without fully replacing convolutions.
 - **Multi-Head Self-Attention (Transformers):** This is the core focus, categorized further into:
 - **Uniform-scale Vision Transformers (ViTs):** Direct adaptations of NLP Transformers maintaining consistent feature scales (e.g., original ViT, DeiT, T2T-ViT).
 - **Multi-scale Vision Transformers:** Hierarchical designs that progressively reduce token length and increase feature dimension, better suited for dense prediction tasks (e.g., Swin Transformer, PVT).
 - **Hybrid ViTs with Convolutions:** Models that combine the strengths of CNNs (for local feature extraction and inductive biases) and Transformers (for global context), often at the initial patch embedding or throughout the network (e.g., CvT, CoAtNets, NesT).
 - **Self-Supervised Vision Transformers:** Discussing how contrastive learning and other SSL techniques are applied to ViTs (e.g., DINO, EsViT) to improve data efficiency and transferability.

3. **Application-Specific Review:** The survey systematically reviews the application of Transformers across a wide array of computer vision tasks, detailing how models are adapted for each:
 - Object Detection (DETR, Deformable DETR, YOLOS)
 - Segmentation (Axial-Attention, SETR, SegFormer)
 - Image and Scene Generation (iGPT, DALL·E, TransGAN, SceneFormer)
 - Low-level Vision (IPT, TTSR, Colorization Transformer)
 - Multi-Modal Tasks (Visual Question Answering, Visual Reasoning, Visual Grounding, with single-stream like UNITER/Oscar and multi-stream like ViLBERT/LXMERT designs)
 - Video Understanding (VideoBERT, VTN, VisTR, ST-TR)
 - Low-Shot Learning (Cross-Transformer, FEAT)
 - Clustering (Set Transformers)
 - 3D Analysis (Point Transformer, PCT, METRO)

 For each application, the authors highlight specific architectural modifications, notable contributions, and performance benchmarks. Table 1 provides a concise summary of key design choices, input/label types, and loss functions across different tasks. Table 2 offers a comparative analysis of advantages and limitations.

4. **Identification of Open Challenges and Future Directions:** A critical part of the methodology is to critically assess the limitations of current Transformer models in vision and propose avenues for future research. These include:
 - High computational cost (quadratic complexity of self-attention).
 - Large data requirements (due to lack of inductive biases).
 - Need for vision-tailored Transformer designs.
 - Exploration of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) for ViTs.
 - Improving interpretability of Transformer decisions.
 - Developing hardware-efficient designs for deployment.
 - Moving towards integrating all modalities for general perception systems.

This structured, comprehensive, and forward-looking approach allows the survey to serve as a valuable reference, summarizing past achievements, clarifying current trends, and guiding future endeavors in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (from the Survey's Perspective)

The survey synthesizes a vast body of literature to present several key findings regarding the adoption and evolution of Transformers in computer vision:

1. **Successful Transition from NLP to CV:** The most overarching finding is that Transformer models, originally developed for sequence-to-sequence tasks in NLP, have successfully transitioned to and achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across a broad spectrum of computer vision tasks. This includes high-level tasks like image classification, object detection, and segmentation, as well as low-level tasks such as super-resolution and image generation, and complex multi-modal and 3D analysis.

2. **Transformer Strengths in Vision:**
 - **Long-Range Dependency Modeling:** Transformers excel at capturing global context and long-range relationships within images or video sequences, a limitation often faced by CNNs due to their local receptive fields (e.g., in panoptic segmentation, video action recognition).
 - **Parallel Processing:** The self-attention mechanism is inherently parallelizable, allowing for efficient processing of sequence elements, unlike recurrent networks.
 - **Minimal Inductive Biases:** While initially a challenge (requiring vast data), the minimal inductive biases of Transformers allow them to learn highly expressive and generalizable representations directly from data, potentially discovering novel patterns beyond human-engineered priors.
 - **Scalability:** Transformers demonstrate impressive scalability to very large capacity networks and huge datasets, leading to improved performance with more data and parameters (e.g., ViT, GPT-3).
 - **Multi-Modality:** Their design enables processing of multiple modalities (e.g., images, text, video, audio) using similar architectural blocks, fostering the development of unified multi-modal learning systems (e.g., CLIP, ViLBERT, VideoBERT).

3. **Architectural Adaptations for Vision:**
 - **Patch-based Input:** A common strategy involves dividing images into sequences of patches, which are then treated as tokens, akin to words in NLP (e.g., ViT). Positional encodings are crucial to maintain spatial information.
 - **Hierarchical Designs:** To address the quadratic complexity of global self-attention for high-resolution images and to better capture multi-scale features, hierarchical Transformers (e.g., Swin Transformer, PVT) have emerged, gradually reducing token count while increasing feature dimension.
 - **Local Attention Mechanisms:** Efficiency improvements often involve restricting self-attention to local windows or specific axes (e.g., Axial-Attention, Criss-Cross Attention) to reduce computational burden while still capturing relevant context.
 - **Hybrid CNN-Transformer Architectures:** Many successful Vision Transformers are hybrid models that combine the strengths of CNNs (for robust low-level feature extraction and inherent inductive biases) with Transformers (for global context modeling) (e.g., CvT, CoAtNets, LeViT). This often provides a balance between data efficiency and performance.

4. **Crucial Role of Pre-training and Self-Supervision:** For Transformers to achieve competitive performance, especially when trained from scratch, large-scale pre-training is almost universally required. Self-supervised learning (SSL) has been particularly effective, allowing models to learn meaningful representations from massive unlabelled datasets through pretext tasks (e.g., masked image modeling, contrastive learning). Models like DINO demonstrate surprising emergent properties (e.g., automatic segmentation) from self-supervised ViTs.

5. **Task-Specific Innovations:** The survey details how the core Transformer architecture is adapted for diverse tasks:
 - **Object Detection:** DETR reframed object detection as a set prediction problem, eliminating hand-crafted components like NMS and RPN, albeit with slow convergence. Deformable DETR addressed this by introducing sparse attention.
 - **Generative Models:** Transformers are capable of generating high-fidelity images (iGPT, DALL·E) and even 3D scenes (SceneFormer) or adversarial images (TransGAN).
 - **Low-level Vision:** Models like IPT demonstrate the ability of pre-trained Transformers to generalize across various image restoration tasks.
 - **Multi-modal Integration:** Various architectures (single-stream vs. multi-stream) and pre-training objectives (masked token prediction, cross-modality matching) have been explored to effectively fuse visual and linguistic information, leading to powerful models for VQA, visual reasoning, and retrieval (e.g., CLIP's zero-shot capabilities).
 - **3D Data:** The permutation invariance of Transformers makes them well-suited for processing unordered point clouds (Point Transformer, PCT) and even complex tasks like human mesh reconstruction (METRO).

In essence, the findings underscore that while Transformers offer profound advantages and have significantly advanced the state-of-the-art in computer vision, their optimal application often involves careful adaptation, strategic use of pre-training (especially self-supervised), and in many cases, symbiotic integration with convolutional neural networks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "Transformers in Vision: A Survey" carries significant importance, both in summarizing the current state of a rapidly evolving field and in delineating pathways for future research. Its potential impact spans several dimensions:

1. **Consolidating a Paradigm Shift:** The most profound significance of this work is its comprehensive consolidation of the paradigm shift from CNN-centric to Transformer-centric (or hybrid) approaches in computer vision. For decades, CNNs dominated vision. This survey meticulously documents how Transformers, despite their initial lack of visual inductive biases, have become a formidable alternative, demonstrating competitive or superior performance across a wide range of tasks. This provides a clear, authoritative reference for researchers navigating this transition.

2. **Guiding Research Directions:** By systematically outlining "Open Challenges & Future Directions," the survey acts as a roadmap for the research community. It highlights critical bottlenecks and encourages focused efforts to address them:
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The quadratic complexity of self-attention is a major barrier. Future research needs to focus on developing more efficient attention mechanisms (e.g., linear complexity, sparse attention, low-rank approximations) and hardware-aware designs to enable deployment on resource-constrained devices, thereby reducing their "carbon footprint."
 - **Data Efficiency:** The reliance on extremely large datasets (hundreds of millions or billions of images) for ViTs to achieve competitive performance is unsustainable for many applications. Future work must explore more data-efficient training strategies, including advanced self-supervised learning methods, effective distillation techniques, and the judicious incorporation of inductive biases through architectural design or training regimes.
 - **Vision-Tailored Designs:** The survey implicitly encourages moving beyond direct adaptations of NLP Transformers towards architectures that inherently understand the unique characteristics of visual data (e.g., 2D spatial structures, multi-scale features). This could lead to more efficient and specialized Vision Transformers.
 - **Interpretability:** Understanding *why* Transformers make certain decisions remains an open challenge. Improved interpretability tools and methods are crucial for building trust in AI systems, diagnosing biases, and informing the design of more robust models.

3. **Facilitating Cross-Modal Integration:** The inherent multi-modal capability of Transformers (their ability to process various data types using similar blocks) holds immense promise for developing more generalized AI systems. The survey's detailed section on multi-modal tasks underscores this potential, suggesting a future where a single model architecture could handle tasks spanning vision, language, audio, and more, moving towards "modality-agnosticism." This could lead to more holistic AI systems that mimic human perception.

4. **Informing Application and Development:** For practitioners and engineers, the survey offers a valuable resource for understanding the trade-offs and best practices when choosing between CNNs and Transformers, or designing hybrid solutions for specific applications. The detailed analysis of methods across tasks like object detection, segmentation, and low-level vision provides a foundation for developing robust real-world AI systems.

5. **Shaping the Future of AI Research:** The concluding remarks emphasize the potential for Transformers to "unleash the scalability and generalization" of neural networks. The survey encourages the community to "leverage the potential of Transformer models and improve on their current limitations." By fostering continued interest and directing efforts towards these challenges, this survey is poised to influence the trajectory of deep learning research in computer vision for years to come, potentially leading to breakthroughs in efficiency, generalizability, and the creation of more capable and accessible AI technologies.
