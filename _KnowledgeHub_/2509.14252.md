# LLM-JEPA: Large Language Models Meet Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> LLM-JEPA introduces a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)-based training objective for Large Language Models, enhancing fine-tuning and pretraining by improving accuracy, robustness against overfitting, and fostering more structured abstract representations. This method achieved superior performance across models like Llama, Gemma, and OpenELM on datasets including NL-RX-SYNTH, GSM8K, and Spider, while mitigating computational costs with a novel 'loss dropout' mechanism.

## Problem

- A methodological gap exists between vision and language model training, where vision tasks benefit from JEPAs for robust representation learning, but language tasks predominantly rely on reconstruction-based generative objectives.
- Current LLM training, focused on generative capabilities like next-token prediction, may not optimally foster the abstract and robust representations needed for complex reasoning and perception tasks.
- Generative models can be computationally intensive due to full input-space reconstruction and may inadvertently learn biases from surface-level features.

## Method

- Introduced ==LLM-JEPA==, a novel training objective that combines the standard autoregressive language modeling loss with a ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) loss==.
- Designed the JEPA component to leverage =='multi-view' data== (e.g., natural language descriptions and corresponding code or paraphrases) by predicting embeddings of one view from another.
- Implemented the JEPA objective with minimal architectural overhead by reusing the LLM's internal transformer layers as both encoder and predictor, facilitated by a special `[PRED]` token and custom attention mask, and introduced =='loss dropout'== to reduce computational cost.

## Results

- Achieved consistent and statistically significant accuracy improvements across various models (e.g., Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Gemma-2-2b-it) and datasets (e.g., NL-RX-SYNTH, GSM8K, Spider) in fine-tuning, with enhanced robustness against overfitting.
- Demonstrated encouraging results in pretraining, leading to improved representation quality and better downstream task performance (e.g., statistically significant improvements on Rotten Tomatoes and Yelp sentiment classification) while maintaining generative abilities.
- The 'loss dropout' technique effectively reduced computational overhead by up to **50%** for LD=0.5 and enabled faster convergence and higher accuracy under equivalent compute budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the standard autoregressive LLM loss does not implicitly minimize the JEPA objective, underscoring the explicit need for a JEPA term to learn robust and abstract representations.
> - LLM-JEPA induces a clearer, more structured organization in the latent space, making mappings between different views (e.g., Text and Code) approximately linear and confined to a narrow subspace.
> - The JEPA objective functions as a powerful regularization and representation-structuring mechanism, complementing LLMs' generative capabilities without diminishing them.
