# CoRDE: Concept-Prior Routed Diffusion Experts for Structural Generalization in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CoRDE unifies high-level semantic concept priors with diffusion Mixture-of-Experts for robot manipulation, addressing structural generalization and routing instability. The framework achieved improved task success rates on the LIBERO benchmark and matched teacher performance on D3IL while maintaining behavioral diversity and reducing inference latency by an order of magnitude, generating actions in 2.63 ms.

## Problem

- Monolithic diffusion policies for multi-task robot manipulation suffer from gradient conflicts, catastrophic interference, and limited structural generalization.
- Current Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) frameworks exhibit routing collapse and lack semantic interpretability due to data-likelihood-driven routing.
- Parameter-efficient adaptation methods like LoRA can introduce rank deficiency, potentially compromising behavioral diversity in generative models.

## Method

- Develops **CoRDE**, a structure-guided variational diffusion distillation framework that integrates ==concept-prior-driven responsibility== for stable expert allocation.
- Employs a parameter-efficient diffusion expert pool using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on a shared frozen backbone to manage computational overhead.
- Utilizes an Expectation-Maximization (EM) style self-organizing soft alignment algorithm to establish an interpretable mapping between task concepts and execution experts.

## Results

- Achieved the highest overall macro-average success rate across all four LIBERO suites, consistently outperforming monolithic Diffusion Policy and evidence-only Distill-MoE baselines.
- Matched or exceeded the task success rate of the monolithic Diffusion Policy teacher on D3IL tasks, while maintaining behavioral diversity with Task Entropy scores approximating the teacher.
- Reduced inference time to **2.63** ms for single-step action generation, an order-of-magnitude faster than the monolithic Diffusion Policy (**55.62** ms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level semantic concept priors with an adaptive annealing mechanism effectively guides expert routing, preventing routing collapse and fostering semantically aligned assignments.
> - A parameter-efficient LoRA expert pool can maintain high-fidelity action generation by accurately reconstructing the teacher's score function within assigned semantic regions.
> - The stochastic diffusion term of the underlying SDE is crucial for preserving multi-modal behavioral diversity, preventing mode collapse even with low-rank parameter adaptations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses limitations within the field of robot imitation learning, particularly concerning the application of diffusion models for multi-task and long-horizon robot manipulation. Diffusion models have become a prominent approach in this domain due to their capacity to model complex, multi-modal action distributions and generate smooth trajectories, leading to high task success rates.

However, existing monolithic diffusion policies face architectural constraints when deployed in complex scenarios. These limitations include gradient conflicts between distinct semantic sub-stages, resulting in catastrophic interference, and a lack of structural generalization capabilities. Additionally, their iterative denoising process contributes to substantial inference latency, and intractable likelihoods complicate policy optimization.

To mitigate these issues, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have been introduced, often through variational distillation methods like Variational Distillation of Diffusion (VDD), to decompose manipulation skills and accelerate inference. Despite these efforts, current MoE frameworks exhibit structural challenges. Their reliance on pure data-likelihood-driven routing frequently leads to routing collapse, where only a subset of experts is consistently utilized, and can distort the natural distribution of physical tasks. The resulting expert populations often lack human-interpretable semantic boundaries, and the instantiation of full-scale experts in MoE models can lead to parameter explosion and high expansion costs.

Furthermore, while techniques like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) offer parameter-efficient adaptation, their application in high-dimensional continuous action generation can introduce rank deficiency, potentially leading to a loss of behavioral diversity if the stochastic component of diffusion models is not adequately preserved. This research positions itself by proposing CoRDE to specifically address these unresolved issues by integrating high-level semantic concept priors with diffusion MoE models, aiming for stable, semantically aligned expert allocation, parameter efficiency, and preservation of behavioral diversity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the structural generalization challenge inherent in multi-modal robot manipulation within multi-task and long-horizon contexts. The authors identify several motivations for developing the CoRDE framework:

1. **Mitigate Gradient Conflicts and Catastrophic Interference:** Monolithic diffusion policies, by attempting to represent vastly different behavioral modes within a single network, suffer from gradient conflicts. This leads to interference between learning objectives for diverse sub-stages of a task, limiting their structural generalization. The motivation is to decompose policies into specialized experts to avoid these conflicts.
2. **Address Routing Collapse and Lack of Semantic Interpretability in MoE:** Existing unsupervised MoE frameworks rely on data-likelihood for routing, which can be unstable when processing noisy, multi-modal data. This often results in "winner-takes-all" scenarios, leading to routing collapse where experts are not effectively utilized or specialized. Additionally, the expert populations generated through such statistical means often lack clear, human-interpretable semantic boundaries, which can cause unpredictable routing decisions. The objective is to introduce a mechanism that guides expert routing with high-level semantic concepts to ensure stable, semantically aligned expert allocation.
3. **Overcome Parameter Explosion and High Expansion Costs:** Instantiating a full-scale neural network for each expert in an MoE architecture leads to a significant increase in model parameters. This poses challenges for deployment efficiency and scalability. The motivation is to develop a parameter-efficient expert pool to manage this computational overhead.
4. **Maintain Behavioral Diversity in Generative Models:** While parameter-efficient adaptation techniques like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) reduce computational costs, they can introduce rank deficiency, potentially impairing the behavioral diversity of generative models. Specifically, if the stochastic diffusion term—critical for maintaining the full-rank property of generative distributions in Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs)—is compromised, it can lead to mode collapse. A key objective is to ensure that parameter efficiency does not come at the expense of multi-modal action diversity.
5. **Accelerate Inference Latency:** Monolithic diffusion policies, with their iterative denoising process, incur substantial inference latency. Distilling these policies into MoE architectures can accelerate inference. The aim is to achieve this acceleration while maintaining or improving action quality and diversity.

To address these motivations, CoRDE proposes three specific contributions:
- A structure-guided variational diffusion distillation framework that integrates a concept-prior-driven responsibility mechanism, designed to mitigate routing collapse and foster semantically aligned expert allocation.
- A parameter-efficient diffusion expert pool constructed using LoRA, intended to reduce computational overhead while preserving action diversity via the stochastic diffusion term and supporting high-fidelity generation through bounded score approximation.
- An Expectation-Maximization (EM) style self-organizing soft alignment algorithm to establish an interpretable many-to-many mapping between macroscopic task concepts and microscopic execution experts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoRDE (Concept-prior Routed Diffusion Experts) is a structure-guided variational distillation framework designed to unify high-level semantic concept priors with the generative capabilities of diffusion Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models. The methodology involves distilling a pre-trained monolithic diffusion policy into a scalable, semantically aligned MoE architecture.

**A. Preliminaries: Distilling a Diffusion Policy into MoE**
The framework begins by acknowledging that diffusion policies model action generation as a conditional denoising process, learning a score function to predict noise in noisy action sequences. To address the inference latency and intractable likelihoods of these continuous teacher policies, CoRDE follows the Variational Distillation of Diffusion (VDD) approach, compressing the teacher into a single-step MoE student policy. This student policy generates actions by mixing outputs from K experts, each activated by a gating network $q_\psi(z|o)$. However, the paper notes that VDD's data-likelihood-driven routing often leads to routing collapse.

**B. Overall Framework of CoRDE**
CoRDE introduces a concept-prior to regularize the routing dynamics. As illustrated in Figure 1, the core components and their interactions are:
1. **Semantic Concept Extraction:** A frozen concept encoder processes multi-modal observations $o$ to extract a stable semantic distribution $p_\phi(c|o)$ over C semantic clusters. These encoders are designed to be label-free (e.g., HiMaCon, AutoCGP).
2. **Structural Prior Formulation:** A learnable soft mapping matrix, $P_{CE}(z|c)$, projects these semantic concepts into a structural expert prior $p_{struct}(z|o) = \sum_{c=1}^C p_\phi(c|o)P_{CE}(z|c)$. This matrix enables a many-to-many relationship between concepts and experts.
3. **Expectation (E) Step: Structure-Guided Responsibility Inference:**
 - **Behavioral Evidence:** This is quantified as $r(z|o, a_0) = q_\psi(z|o) \exp(-\|u_T - u_\theta^{(z)}\|^2_2)$, which measures the local score-fitting precision of expert $z$ against the teacher score $u_T$.
 - **Variational Objective:** The system minimizes a structure-regularized variational objective to infer the optimal variational responsibility posterior $q^*(z|o, a_0)$. This objective balances Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to the normalized behavioral evidence ($\bar{r}$) and to the structural prior ($p_{struct}$), weighted by an adaptive guidance strength $\lambda(o)$.
 - **Optimal Posterior:** The optimal $q^*(z|o, a_0)$ is derived as an exact geometric mean of the normalized behavioral evidence and the structural prior, with exponents controlled by $\lambda(o)$.
 - **Adaptive Annealing:** The guidance strength $\lambda(o)$ is adaptively annealed based on the normalized concept confidence $\gamma(o) = 1 - H(p_\phi(c|o)) / \log C$. This mechanism ensures that under ambiguous semantic states, the system gracefully falls back to evidence-driven allocation.
 - **Routing Entropy Bound (Theorem 1):** Theoretically, as $\lambda(o)$ approaches infinity, the entropy of the responsibility posterior $H(q^*)$ converges to the entropy of the structural prior $H(p_{struct})$, indicating stable expert assignment when the structural prior is confident.

**C. Maximization (M) Step: Parameter-Efficient LoRA Experts and EM Soft Alignment**
The M-step involves updating the parameters of the experts, the router, and the soft mapping matrix.
1. **Parameter-Efficient LoRA Experts:** To prevent parameter explosion, CoRDE employs a parameter-efficient expert pool. All experts share a frozen diffusion transformer trunk $h_\eta(o)$. Expert-specific differentiation is achieved solely through Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules injected into the linear projection layers. For each expert $z$, weights are parameterized as $W^{(z)} = W_0 + B^{(z)}A^{(z)}$, with a low-rank constraint.
 - **Expert Training:** Each expert $z$ minimizes a responsibility-weighted score-matching discrepancy against the teacher score $u_T$.
 - **Mixture L2 Score Approximation (Theorem 2):** Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the global score discrepancy of the combined student experts is upper-bounded by the responsibility-weighted sum of local expert errors. This supports high-fidelity generation under low-rank expert adaptation.
 - **Full-Rank Diversity Conservation (Remark):** Critically, while LoRA modules modify the deterministic drift term of the reverse Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) for action generation, the SDE's stochastic diffusion term (Wiener process) remains intact. This ensures a constant injection of full-rank isotropic noise, preserving multi-modal behavioral diversity and preventing mode collapse, regardless of the low-rank constraints on the network parameters.
2. **Router Decoupling:** The routing network $q_\psi(z|o)$ is explicitly decoupled from the downstream score-matching task. It is trained separately by minimizing the KL divergence between its output and the inferred responsibility posterior $q^*(z|o, a_0)$. This stabilization prevents gradient conflicts and encourages the router to align with the semantically-guided responsibilities.
3. **EM Soft Alignment for $P_{CE}$:** The soft mapping matrix $P_{CE}$ undergoes an Expectation-Maximization (EM) style self-organizing update. Mini-batch soft assignment counts are used to construct target distributions, which are smoothed with a Dirichlet prior. The matrix is then refined using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) update. This process establishes an interpretable many-to-many mapping between macroscopic concepts and microscopic experts without requiring unstable backpropagation through the mapping matrix itself.

**D. Algorithms: Training and Inference**
The CoRDE framework integrates these mathematical modules into two phases:
- **Algorithm 1 (Variational Distillation - Training):** This offline phase iteratively samples data, infers responsibility posteriors (E-step), and then updates the LoRA expert parameters, router network, and $P_{CE}$ mapping matrix (M-steps).
- **Algorithm 2 (State-Only Execution - Inference):** This online phase involves semantic extraction, state-only routing (where structural bias from $p_{struct}(z|o)$ is injected into router logits), and SDE denoising generation using the mixture score field from the dynamically activated LoRA experts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations of CoRDE were conducted across two robot imitation learning benchmarks: LIBERO for structural generalization and D3IL for multi-modal behavioral coverage and inference efficiency.

**A. LIBERO: Long-Horizon Multi-Task Performance**
CoRDE was compared against a monolithic Diffusion Policy (Teacher) and an Evidence-only Distill-MoE (VDD-based student without structural prior). Both CoRDE variants (using HiMaCon and AutoCGP concept encoders) consistently outperformed both baselines across all four LIBERO suites (L-Spatial, L-Object, L-Goal, L-Long), achieving the highest overall macro-average success rate.
- **Performance Enhancement:** The improvement was most significant in L-Long and L-Goal suites, which involve strong multi-stage semantic structures and temporal dependencies. This indicated that the structural prior introduced by CoRDE stabilized expert assignments, thereby mitigating compounding errors over extended task horizons.
- **Source of Gain:** The results demonstrated that the performance gains stemmed from the joint responsibility inference mechanism—fusing semantic structure with behavioral evidence—rather than merely from the deployment of an MoE architecture. The evidence-only Distill-MoE, while occasionally surpassing the monolithic teacher on specific subsets, generally performed worse than CoRDE.
- **Label-Free Concepts:** The consistent improvements observed with both HiMaCon and AutoCGP (which are label-free concept encoders) confirmed that effective structural expert division can be achieved through self-organization without manual semantic annotation.

**B. D3IL: Action Diversity and Inference Efficiency**
CoRDE was evaluated on seven D3IL tasks (Avoiding, Aligning, Pushing, Stacking-1, Stacking-2, Sorting (Image), Stacking (Image)) for task success rate, task entropy (behavioral diversity), and inference time.
- **High-Fidelity Action Generation (Score Inheritance):** CoRDE variants matched or exceeded the Task Success Rate of the monolithic Diffusion Policy teacher and the full-parameter VDD baseline across both state-based and image-based D3IL tasks. This finding empirically corroborated Theorem 2, indicating that the low-rank capacity of LoRA adapters was sufficient to accurately reconstruct the complex score function of the teacher policy within their assigned semantic regions.
- **Prevention of Mode Collapse (Diversity Conservation):** CoRDE achieved Task Entropy scores that closely approximated the original multi-modal Diffusion Policy teacher, and were significantly higher than standard deterministic baselines. This confirmed the theoretical assertion that preserving the SDE’s stochastic diffusion term guarantees the full-rank variance of the generative distribution, thereby maintaining action diversity despite the low-rank constraint on neural network parameters.
- **Inference Efficiency:** CoRDE achieved single-step action generation (Number of Function Evaluations, NFE=1) in 2.63 ms, which represents an order-of-magnitude reduction compared to the monolithic Diffusion Policy (55.62 ms with NFE=64). This efficiency was comparable to VDD, demonstrating that CoRDE offers accelerated inference without compromising action quality or incurring parameter explosion.

**C. Ablation Study: Routing Stability and Diversity Preservation**
An ablation study investigated the contribution of key CoRDE design choices, reporting LIBERO Mean Success, D3IL Success/Task Entropy, and routing diagnostics (H_route, H_post, PPL).
- **Without $P_{CE}$ (identity mapping):** Removing the soft concept-to-expert mapping reduced LIBERO Mean Success (0.76), D3IL Success (0.74), and Task Entropy (0.37), while increasing routing uncertainty (H_route 0.42) and decreasing expert usage (PPL 2.3). This confirmed the necessity of concept-expert decoupling for semantic consistency and robust routing.
- **Without Adaptive $\lambda(o)$ (fixed $\lambda$):** Using a fixed $\lambda$ decreased LIBERO Mean Success (0.82), D3IL Success (0.67), and Task Entropy (0.52), with lower PPL (1.4). This indicated that adaptive annealing of structural guidance is crucial for robust fusion and preventing over-reliance on ambiguous semantic priors.
- **Evidence-Only (Distill-MoE; $\lambda(o)=0$):** This variant, lacking structural guidance, achieved a LIBERO Mean Success of 0.79 and D3IL Success of 0.76. However, its Task Entropy was notably lower (0.32), and PPL was 2.9, suggesting it retained some task performance but suffered in terms of diversity and expert specialization compared to the full CoRDE.
- **Router Without Responsibility Supervision:** Removing the supervision of the router with the inferred posterior led to a significant degradation in performance (LIBERO Mean Success 0.62, D3IL Success 0.59, Task Entropy 0.37) and the lowest PPL (1.1), indicative of routing collapse. This result emphasized that supervising the router with inference-derived responsibilities is critical for stable and balanced expert utilization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoRDE framework contributes to robot imitation learning by addressing several challenges in the application of diffusion models for multi-task and long-horizon robot manipulation.

Firstly, CoRDE proposes a method to mitigate routing collapse and encourage semantically aligned expert allocations in Mixture-of-Experts architectures. By integrating a concept-prior-driven responsibility mechanism, it offers a more stable and interpretable way to assign tasks to specialized experts, moving beyond purely data-likelihood-driven routing which has shown limitations. This could lead to more reliable and predictable robot behaviors in complex, multi-stage tasks.

Secondly, the framework introduces a parameter-efficient expert pool using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) on a shared frozen backbone. This design significantly reduces the computational overhead associated with training and deploying MoE models, which traditionally suffer from parameter explosion. This efficiency makes it more feasible to apply such complex models in resource-constrained robotic systems.

Thirdly, CoRDE maintains high action fidelity and, critically, preserves multi-modal behavioral diversity. The theoretical analysis and empirical results confirm that the framework avoids the mode collapse often associated with low-rank approximations by ensuring the stochastic diffusion term in the underlying SDE remains intact. This capability is important for robots to reproduce the rich and varied solutions observed in human demonstrations, enhancing flexibility and adaptability in diverse environments.

The development of an Expectation-Maximization (EM) style self-organizing soft alignment algorithm for the concept-to-expert mapping further enhances the system's interpretability. This allows for a learned, many-to-many relationship between macroscopic task concepts and microscopic execution experts without requiring explicit human semantic annotation, thereby making the system more autonomous and generalizable.

The observed improvements in task success rates, maintenance of behavioral diversity, and significant acceleration of inference time, as demonstrated on the LIBERO and D3IL benchmarks, indicate that CoRDE offers a comprehensive approach to enhance the structural generalization, efficiency, and robustness of robot manipulation policies. Potential future work suggested by the authors, such as exploring richer open-vocabulary concepts and online adaptation, indicates that this framework could serve as a foundation for further advancements in intelligent robotic systems capable of more flexible and autonomous operation in real-world scenarios.
