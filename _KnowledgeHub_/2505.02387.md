# RM-R1: Reward Modeling as Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and collaborators present RM-R1, a new class of Reasoning Reward Models (REASRMS) that integrate deep, interpretable reasoning into the reward assignment process. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on three major reward model benchmarks by employing a two-stage training pipeline (reasoning distillation and reinforcement learning) and a structured "Chain-of-Rubrics" mechanism, providing transparent and coherent rationales for judgments.

## Problem

- Scalar-based Reward Models (ScalarRMs) are opaque, lacking interpretability or explicit reasoning steps, limiting their utility in complex, reasoning-intensive tasks.
- Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) offer free-form textual judgments but often provide superficial, unstructured, and less reliable reasoning, leading to suboptimal performance compared to ScalarRMs.
- Accurate human preference judgments in real-world scenarios require deep cognitive processes like criteria inference, trade-off analysis, and consequence simulation, which existing reward models do not fully embody.

## Method

- Introduces ==Reasoning Reward Models (REASRMS)==, a new class of generative reward models designed to integrate deep, interpretable reasoning into the reward assignment process.
- Proposes a ==two-stage training pipeline==: an initial Reasoning Distillation stage to bootstrap the model's ability to generate structured reasoning traces, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO to refine generalization and critical thinking.
- Develops a ==Chain-of-Rubrics (CoR)== rollout mechanism via a sophisticated system prompt that guides the model to adapt its reasoning strategy based on task type (e.g., self-solve for reasoning tasks, generate tailored criteria for chat tasks) prior to making a judgment.

## Results

- RM-R1 models achieve state-of-the-art performance across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB benchmarks, outperforming larger open-weight models and even proprietary models by up to **4.9%**.
- On the reasoning-intensive RM-Bench, RM-R1-DeepSeek-Distilled-Qwen-32B sets new state-of-the-art accuracy with **91.8%** in math and **74.1%** in code.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the two-stage training (distillation + RL), Chain-of-Rubrics prompting, and explicit query categorization, demonstrating that these components are crucial for superior performance and interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating robust, structured reasoning capabilities into reward modeling significantly enhances both RM's interpretability and its performance, particularly in complex scenarios.
> - A two-stage training pipeline combining reasoning distillation with subsequent reinforcement learning is highly effective for training high-performing Reasoning Reward Models (REASRMS), allowing for data efficiency and generalization.
> - Structured prompting mechanisms like Chain-of-Rubrics and explicit task categorization are crucial for enabling models to proactively generate effective reasoning traces and adapt evaluation strategies.
