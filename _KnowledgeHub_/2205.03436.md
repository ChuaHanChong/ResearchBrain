# EdgeViTs: Competing Light-weight CNNs on Mobile Devices with Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> EdgeViTs introduce a family of Vision Transformers that achieve competitive accuracy and superior on-device efficiency compared to leading lightweight CNNs and existing mobile-friendly ViTs on mobile hardware. These models demonstrate Pareto-optimal performance across accuracy-latency and accuracy-energy trade-offs on ImageNet-1K, and generalize effectively to dense prediction tasks.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) typically suffer from high computational complexity and large model sizes, which restrict their deployment on resource-constrained mobile and edge devices.
- Existing efficient ViTs, including specialized architectures like MobileViTs, have not effectively closed the performance gap with lightweight CNNs in terms of accuracy and practical on-device efficiency for mobile applications.
- Traditional efficiency metrics such as FLOPs and parameter counts are often indirect and inaccurate proxies for real-world inference latency and energy consumption on diverse mobile hardware platforms.

## Method

- **EdgeViTs** utilize a ==four-stage hierarchical pyramid network== architecture, which progressively reduces feature map resolution while expanding channel dimensions across stages to manage computational cost.
- A novel ==Local-Global-Local (LGL) bottleneck== re-engineers the self-attention mechanism, decomposing it into efficient ==local aggregation== (depth-wise convolutions), ==global sparse attention== (on uniformly sampled delegate tokens), and ==local propagation== (transposed convolutions).
- The architecture is designed for implementational friendliness, relying on standard computing operations optimized within mainstream deep learning frameworks (e.g., TorchScript) to ensure efficient on-device execution.

## Results

- EdgeViTs achieve up to **5.4%** higher ImageNet-1K Top-1 accuracy compared to MobileViTs, while demonstrating approximately **2-2.7 times** faster inference speeds on a Samsung Galaxy S21 CPU.
- EdgeViT-XXS surpasses MobileNet-v2 in accuracy (**74.4%** vs. **72.0%**) while running slightly faster (**32.8 ms** vs. **33.3 ms**) and exhibits superior energy-aware efficiency against most competing lightweight CNNs and all other ViTs.
- The models consistently achieve Pareto-optimal performance across accuracy-latency and accuracy-energy trade-offs, and generalize effectively by outperforming other visual backbones on COCO object detection/instance segmentation and ADE20K semantic segmentation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct on-device measurements of latency and energy consumption are crucial for a realistic assessment of mobile model efficiency, often revealing that theoretical FLOPs or parameter counts do not directly correlate with practical performance.
> - The proposed LGL bottleneck effectively balances local detail capture with global context modeling at a substantially reduced computational cost, making attention-based models viable for resource-constrained edge devices.
> - Integrating convolutional operations for local information aggregation within attention blocks proves more efficient and effective than purely self-attention-based local grouping, leading to improved accuracy and speed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has witnessed a significant architectural shift with the emergence of self-attention based models, particularly Vision Transformers (ViTs), as a competitive alternative to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). While ViTs have demonstrated strong recognition accuracies on various visual tasks, their inherent quadratic computational complexity with respect to input sequence length and their substantial model sizes often render them resource-demanding. This characteristic typically limits their deployment on platforms with constrained computational budgets, such as mobile devices, robotics, and embedded systems.

Recent research in ViTs has incorporated design elements from CNNs, such as hierarchical multi-stage structures and local convolution operations, to improve computational viability and leverage visual data's inductive biases. However, these adaptations have primarily focused on achieving higher accuracies in general computer vision tasks, rather than specifically addressing the strict efficiency requirements of mobile and edge platforms. Consequently, existing efficient CNN architectures, including MobileNets and EfficientNets, have largely maintained their status as the state-of-the-art for on-device applications, exhibiting a more favorable trade-off between inference latency and recognition accuracy.

A very recent attempt to bridge this gap introduced MobileViTs, designed for mobile devices by combining MobileNetV2 with ViT components. While a pioneering effort, MobileViTs still exhibit a performance deficit compared to their CNN counterparts. Additionally, their evaluation protocol, which emphasizes model size (parameter count) as a primary comparison metric, is increasingly considered less relevant given the expanding storage capabilities of modern mobile hardware.

This research contributes to the development of efficient ViTs by specifically targeting the challenges of mobile deployment. It aims to develop a family of light-weight ViTs that can not only achieve high accuracy but also demonstrate superior or comparable on-device efficiency, measured directly by latency and energy consumption, when compared to both established efficient CNNs and other ViT variants, particularly MobileViTs. The work addresses the under-studied direction of designing ViTs with practical on-device execution in mind, challenging the reliance on indirect metrics like FLOPs or parameter counts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce EdgeViTs, a new family of light-weight Vision Transformers, that for the first time enables attention-based vision models to effectively compete with the leading light-weight CNNs in terms of accuracy and on-device efficiency.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations related to the practical deployment of deep learning models on mobile and edge devices:

1. **Bridging the Performance Gap for ViTs on Mobile Devices:** Existing ViTs, despite their strong performance on high-resource platforms, have not effectively transitioned to mobile environments due to their computational and memory demands. Even specially designed "mobile-friendly" ViTs, such as MobileViTs, have lagged behind efficient CNNs. The motivation is to overcome this performance deficit and enable ViTs to be a viable, or even superior, option for resource-constrained applications.

2. **Focus on Practical On-Device Efficiency Metrics:** Traditional efficiency metrics like Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) and parameter counts are considered indirect and often inaccurate proxies for real-world on-device performance. The authors contend that FLOPs do not directly translate to actual latency or energy consumption due to various factors, including memory access costs, parallelism, and specific platform characteristics. The motivation is to shift the evaluation focus directly to the most critical practical metrics for mobile deployment:
 - **Inference Latency:** The actual time taken for a model to process an input on a target device. Low latency is essential for real-time applications.
 - **Energy Consumption:** The power consumed during inference. Minimizing energy usage is critical for battery-powered mobile devices to extend operational time.

3. **Re-evaluating Model Size Constraints:** While model size (parameter number) has historically been a significant constraint, the increasing storage capacity of modern mobile devices (e.g., 32GB or more) has relaxed this limitation. The authors argue that comparing models primarily based on parameter count, as done in some prior work, has become an outdated practice. Instead, a more balanced consideration of accuracy, latency, and energy is warranted.

4. **Ensuring Implementational Friendliness:** For widespread real-world deployment, models must be implementable efficiently using standard computing operations supported and optimized within generic deep learning frameworks (e.g., ONNX, TensorRT, TorchScript). Models that rely on specialized or non-standard operations (e.g., Swin Transformers' cyclic shift) can suffer from unsatisfactory on-device speeds, even if their theoretical FLOPs are low, due to a lack of framework-level optimization. The motivation is to design an architecture that is compatible with mainstream deployment toolchains.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to create a family of ViT models that are not only accurate but also genuinely efficient and deployable on a wide range of mobile and edge hardware, thereby expanding the applicability of attention-based models in resource-constrained settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for developing EdgeViTs is centered around a hierarchical pyramid network structure, combined with a novel, cost-effective Local-Global-Local (LGL) information exchange bottleneck designed to optimize the self-attention mechanism for mobile device efficiency.

**Overall Architecture:**
EdgeViTs adopt a four-stage hierarchical pyramid network architecture, a design pattern commonly found in recent efficient ViT variants. In this structure, the spatial resolution of features is progressively reduced across stages, while the channel dimension is expanded. Each stage comprises multiple transformer-based blocks, processing tensors of consistent shape within that stage, similar to ResNet-like networks. This multi-stage downsampling helps manage the computational cost of self-attention.
For stage-wise down-sampling, the architecture employs convolutional layers. Specifically, a 4x4 kernel with stride 4 is used for the first stage to down-sample the input feature by a factor of 4, while subsequent stages use a 2x2 kernel with stride 2. Conditional positional encoding, implemented using 2D depth-wise convolutions with a residual connection (3x3 kernels), is used to inject positional information. A two-layer perceptron with GeLU non-linearity serves as the Feed Forward Network (FFN).

**Core Innovation: Local-Global-Local (LGL) Bottleneck:**
The central contribution is the LGL bottleneck, which re-engineers the self-attention block to achieve efficient information exchange. The LGL bottleneck decomposes the standard self-attention into three sequential operations to balance local context aggregation with global long-range dependency modeling at a reduced computational cost:

1. **Local Aggregation (LocalAgg):** This module processes information from spatially proximate tokens. It uses efficient depth-wise and point-wise convolutions within a local window (k x k size) to aggregate local signals for each token. This step efficiently captures local features, leveraging the strengths of convolutions.

2. **Global Sparse Attention (GlobalSparseAttn):** To address the quadratic complexity of self-attention, this module operates on a sparse subset of "delegate tokens." These delegates are uniformly sampled across the feature map, with one token representing an r x r window. Self-attention is then computed only among these selected delegate tokens. This significantly reduces the computational burden from O(H²W²C) to O((HW/r²)²C), as the number of query, key, and value tokens are all reduced. The sampling rates (r) are configured differently for each stage: (4, 2, 2, 1) for stages 1 through 4, respectively.

3. **Local Propagation (LocalProp):** After the delegate tokens have exchanged global contextual information through sparse attention, this module diffuses the updated information back to the non-delegate tokens within their respective local r x r windows. This is achieved using depthwise separable transposed convolutions, with kernel size and stride equal to the sampling rate 'r' used in the global sparse attention. This step ensures that the global context learned by delegates benefits all tokens in the feature map.

The LGL bottleneck is formulated as a series of operations involving Layer Normalization (Norm), Local Aggregation, FFN, Global Sparse Attention, and Local Propagation, incorporating residual connections:
$X = \text{LocalAgg}(\text{Norm}(X_{in})) + X_{in}$
$Y = \text{FFN}(\text{Norm}(X)) + X$
$Z = \text{LocalProp}(\text{GlobalSparseAttn}(\text{Norm}(Y))) + Y$
$X_{out} = \text{FFN}(\text{Norm}(Z)) + Z$

**EdgeViT Variants:**
To cater to various computational budgets, the authors establish a family of EdgeViT variants:
- **EdgeViT-XXS:** Smallest variant (0.56 GFLOPs, 4.1M parameters).
- **EdgeViT-XS:** Medium variant (1.1 GFLOPs, 6.7M parameters).
- **EdgeViT-S:** Largest variant (1.9 GFLOPs, 11.1M parameters).
These variants are configured by adjusting the number of channels per stage and the number of LGL blocks per stage.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
- **ImageNet-1K Pre-training:** Models are trained from scratch on ImageNet-1K for 300 epochs following the DeiT recipe, using AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule, linear warm-up, and standard data augmentations.
- **On-Device Benchmarking:** Critical to the methodology, performance is measured directly on mobile hardware:
 - **Latency:** Measured on a Samsung Galaxy S21 (Snapdragon 888 chipset) using TorchScript lite interpreter via Android benchmarking app (CPU, full precision, batch 1).
 - **Energy Consumption:** Measured on a Snapdragon 888 Hardware Development Kit (HDK8350) using a Monsoon High Voltage Power Monitor, subtracting background power, and calculating average per-inference power and energy over 50 runs. An "energy-aware efficiency" metric (Top-1 accuracy gain per mJ of energy) is introduced for comparison.
- **Downstream Tasks:** Generalization capacity is evaluated on COCO object detection and instance segmentation (using RetinaNet and Mask R-CNN with FPN) and ADE20K semantic segmentation (using Semantic FPN), fine-tuning ImageNet-pretrained weights.
- **Ablation Studies:** Detailed experiments are conducted to validate the design choices of the LGL bottleneck, including comparisons of different local aggregation strategies (convolutional vs. self-attention based), delegate token sampling methods (max, avg, center), and local propagation mechanisms (presence/absence, bilinear interpolation vs. transposed convolution).

This methodology emphasizes practical, direct on-device performance measurements over theoretical proxies, aiming to provide a realistic assessment of light-weight ViT capabilities for mobile deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental validation of EdgeViTs across image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation tasks, focusing particularly on on-device efficiency.

#### 4.1. Image Classification on ImageNet-1K

- **Accuracy vs. FLOPs/Parameters:**
 - EdgeViTs consistently and significantly outperform other light-weight ViTs (e.g., PVT-v2, MobileViT, Uniformer, Twins-SVT, T2T-ViT, TNT, DeiT) at similar GFLOPs. For instance, EdgeViT-XXS and EdgeViT-S achieve 3.9% and 2.3% higher accuracy than PVT-v2-B0 and PVT-v2-B1, respectively. Compared to MobileViTs, EdgeViTs show accuracy gains of 5.4% (XXS), 2.8% (XS), and 2.7% (S) across complexity settings.
 - When compared to efficient CNNs, EdgeViT-XXS demonstrates superior accuracy (74.4%) to MobileNet-v2 (72.0%) and MobileNet-v3 0.75 (73.3%) at a similar model size, although with higher FLOPs. The study notes that efficient CNNs still retain a marginal advantage in the accuracy-FLOPs trade-off.

- **On-Device Latency (CPU ms):**
 - EdgeViTs exhibit strong on-device latency performance, comparable to leading efficient CNNs. EdgeViT-XXS runs slightly faster than MobileNet-v2 (32.8 ms vs. 33.3 ms) while achieving a 2.4% higher accuracy. EdgeViT-XXS is 9.8 ms slower than MobileNet-v3-0.75 but provides a 1.1% accuracy gain. EdgeViT-XS performs comparably to the auto-searched EfficientNet-B0 (54.1 ms vs. 52.1 ms).
 - EdgeViTs demonstrate clear advantages over other ViT models in latency. They are substantially faster than MobileViTs: EdgeViT-XXS is approximately 2 times faster, EdgeViT-XS is 2.7 times faster, and EdgeViT-S is 2.6 times faster, all while achieving higher accuracies.

- **On-Device Energy Consumption (mJ) and Energy-Aware Efficiency:**
 - The study introduces an energy-aware efficiency metric (%/msW, equivalent to accuracy gain per mJ of energy consumed) to facilitate comparison.
 - **Pareto-Optimality:** EdgeViTs are found to be consistently Pareto-optimal across both accuracy-latency and accuracy-energy trade-offs. This indicates that no other model can improve on either accuracy or efficiency without degrading the other metric.
 - EdgeViTs strictly dominate almost all other ViTs, with the retrained LeViT-384† being the only exception, positioned between EdgeViT-S and EdgeViT-XS.
 - When compared to CNNs, EdgeViTs compete with models like MobileNet-v3 and EfficientNet-B0, which are more efficient but less accurate. EdgeViT-XXS notably outperforms MobileNet-v2 by 2.2% in accuracy while maintaining a similar energy-aware efficiency.
 - The study identifies MobileViT models as performing poorly, being neither efficient nor accurate compared to other baselines.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on ImageNet-1K (EdgeViT-XXS base model)

- **Local Aggregation (LA):** The proposed convolutional LA operation improves performance. It achieves 74.4% Top-1 accuracy (32.8 ms) compared to 72.7% when LA is removed (Spatial-Reduced Self-Attention) and 74.0% (36.1 ms) when replaced with Locally-grouped Self-Attention (LSA). This supports the use of depth-wise convolutions for local context learning.
- **Global Sparse Attention Sampling:** Different delegate token sampling methods (max, avg, center) show similar accuracy performance. The default "center" sampling is marginally faster.
- **Local Propagation (LP):** Including the LP operator improves Top-1 accuracy by 0.5% (to 74.4%) with only 0.4 ms overhead compared to removing it (73.9% at 32.4 ms). Using bilinear interpolation for propagation (74.1% at 34.1 ms) is less effective than the proposed transposed convolutions.

#### 4.3. Dense Prediction (COCO Object Detection/Instance Segmentation & ADE20K Semantic Segmentation)

- **COCO Object Detection/Instance Segmentation:** EdgeViTs, when used as backbones, consistently outperform other visual backbones (e.g., PVTv2-B0/B1, ResNet18, PVTv1-Tiny) on RetinaNet and Mask R-CNN.
 - EdgeViT-XXS achieves a 1.5 AP gain over PVTv2-B0 on RetinaNet.
 - On Mask R-CNN, EdgeViT-XXS surpasses PVTv2-B0 by 1.7 AP (bounding box) and 0.7 AP (mask).
 - EdgeViT-S shows larger gains over PVTv2-B1: +2.2 AP on RetinaNet, and +3.0 APb / +1.2 APm on Mask R-CNN.
- **ADE20K Scene Parsing:** EdgeViTs also demonstrate superior performance in semantic segmentation using a Semantic FPN model.
 - EdgeViT-XXS outperforms PVTv2-B0 by 2.5% mIoU.
 - EdgeViT-S surpasses PVTv2-B1 by a margin of 3.4% mIoU.

These results indicate that EdgeViTs not only excel in classification but also generalize effectively as backbones for various dense prediction tasks, maintaining their efficiency advantages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces EdgeViTs, marking a development in the landscape of light-weight deep learning models for mobile and edge devices. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling ViT Competitiveness on Mobile Devices:** For the first time, this work presents a family of Vision Transformers that can compete with, and in several aspects, surpass, the best light-weight CNNs in the critical trade-off between accuracy and on-device efficiency. This establishes ViTs as a viable and effective architectural choice for resource-constrained platforms, challenging the long-standing dominance of CNNs in this domain.

2. **Shifting Evaluation Paradigms for Mobile AI:** The research rigorously advocates for and conducts evaluations based on direct on-device latency and energy consumption, rather than relying solely on indirect proxies like FLOPs or parameter counts. This methodological emphasis provides more realistic and practical insights into model performance for real-world deployment, potentially influencing future research and benchmarking practices in efficient AI. The findings, particularly the Pareto-optimality analysis, highlight that models with lower FLOPs might not necessarily be more efficient in practice.

3. **Advancing Efficient Architecture Design for Transformers:** The introduction of the Local-Global-Local (LGL) bottleneck represents a novel and cost-effective decomposition of the self-attention mechanism. By optimally integrating convolutions and sparse attention, LGL provides an efficient way to capture both local detail and global context without incurring the typical quadratic computational overhead of full self-attention. This architectural innovation offers a template for future designs of efficient attention-based models.

4. **Broadening Application Scope of Transformers:** By making ViTs efficient enough for mobile and edge devices, EdgeViTs unlock new possibilities for deploying attention-based models in real-time and low-power applications. This includes, but is not limited to, advanced mobile photography, augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) systems, on-device robotics, and autonomous driving, where timely and energy-efficient visual perception is critical.

5. **Refuting Prior Claims and Providing New Insights:** The comprehensive on-device evaluations challenge certain assumptions and claims in existing literature, particularly regarding the performance of previously introduced mobile-specific ViTs (e.g., MobileViTs). The study provides evidence that EdgeViTs achieve strict dominance over other ViTs and demonstrate favorable trade-offs when compared to the most efficient CNNs, even on largely unexplored metrics like energy-aware efficiency.

In conclusion, EdgeViTs represent a step towards integrating sophisticated attention mechanisms into practical, resource-limited applications. By offering a high-performance, energy-efficient, and deployable solution, this work contributes to the broader accessibility and utility of advanced deep learning models in everyday devices.
