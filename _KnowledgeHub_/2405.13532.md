# What Makes Good Few-shot Examples for Vision-Language Models?

> [!summary] Summary
> The research demonstrates that few-shot learning with Vision-Language models is highly sensitive to the chosen examples, often exhibiting significant performance variability with random sampling. Researchers from Fudan University, Bytedance, and Sensetime introduce two example selection methods, Montecarlo and Representativeness, which consistently outperform random sampling and active learning strategies across various datasets and few-shot frameworks.

## Problem

- Few-shot learning performance of pre-trained Vision-Language (VL) models shows high variability due to the specific choice of training examples.
- Standard practice of random sampling for few-shot examples leads to suboptimal and inconsistent results.
- Existing instance selection methods designed for single-modality tasks do not effectively address the unique challenges of multi-modal VL settings.

## Method

- Propose ==Gaussian Monte Carlo (Montecarlo)== to select examples that are "unfamiliar" to the pre-trained VL model, based on robustness to Gaussian noise perturbation in embedding space.
- Propose ==Representativeness (REPRE)== to select examples closest to their class centroid, aiming for highly characteristic data points.
- Evaluate both proposed and existing ==Active Learning== methods (Entropy, Margin) across 11 diverse image recognition datasets and three few-shot VL frameworks (Linear Probe CLIP, CoOp, MaPLe).

## Results

- The proposed Montecarlo and Representativeness methods consistently achieve better accuracy compared to random sampling and Active Learning strategies across all **11** tested datasets and **3** few-shot learners.
- Montecarlo notably outperformed other methods when integrated with CoOp, while Representativeness showed superior performance with MaPLe and Linear Probe.
- Active Learning methods (Entropy, Margin) frequently underperformed random selection, indicating their limitations in few-shot Vision-Language contexts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of few-shot examples significantly impacts Vision-Language model performance, challenging assumptions that random sampling is sufficient.
> - Traditional Active Learning methods based on uncertainty are generally ineffective in low-shot Vision-Language settings due to unreliable initial model predictions.
> - Effective few-shot examples for Vision-Language models are characterized by either their "unfamiliarity" to the pre-trained model or their "representativeness" of a class, rather than just model uncertainty.
