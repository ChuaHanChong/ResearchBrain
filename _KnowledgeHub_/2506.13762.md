# Touch begins where vision ends: Generalizable policies for contact-rich manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VITAL is a framework for precise, generalizable, contact-rich robotic manipulation, which learns from a minimal number of demonstrations and limited online interaction. It integrates visuotactile sensing, semantic data augmentation via vision foundation models, and residual reinforcement learning to achieve high success rates on tasks like USB insertion and card swiping while generalizing to unseen spatial configurations and cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Achieving precise, contact-rich robotic manipulation is challenging for existing data-driven methods (IL/RL) due to the need for millimeter-level accuracy.
- Data collection for fine-grained manipulation tasks is difficult and time-consuming, making it hard to acquire high-fidelity demonstrations, and learned policies often lack generalization to new environments.
- Traditional RL methods tend to overfit to training environments, making real-world deployment for precise tasks resource-intensive and prone to failure outside controlled settings.

## Method

- VITAL employs a multi-phase framework combining visuotactile behavior cloning from limited demonstrations (32 per task) with online residual reinforcement learning.
- A novel semantic augmentation pipeline utilizes vision foundation models (==Segment-Anything== 2) to dynamically generate diverse training backgrounds while preserving task-relevant visual features.
- A hierarchical inference strategy uses a Vision-Language Model (Molmo) for coarse spatial localization, followed by the end-effector-grounded visuotactile local policy for fine-grained execution.

## Results

- VITAL achieved superior in-domain performance, with 9/10 successes for 'Plug in Socket' and 'Insert USB,' and 10/10 for 'Card Swiping,' representing a **40%** average improvement over the strongest baseline.
- The framework demonstrated strong spatial and scene generalization, maintaining high success rates (e.g., 28/30 for 'Plug in Socket' in novel positions and 27/30 in novel scenes) even with combined perturbations.
- Ablation studies confirmed tactile sensing is essential, increasing success rates by an average of **40%** for contact-rich tasks, and semantic augmentations were critical for novel scene generalization (e.g., baselines failed without them).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While global scene context varies, local physical interactions for manipulation tasks often remain invariant, suggesting the possibility of learning a generalizable "local policy."
> - Foundation models (VLMs, segmentation models) can be leveraged to create robust visual encoders via semantic data augmentation, reducing the need for vast, diverse task-specific datasets.
> - Tactile sensing provides critical, direct contact feedback for millimeter-scale precision in contact-rich tasks, particularly when visual information is occluded or insufficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, driven by progress in deep learning and increasing data availability. However, precise, contact-rich manipulation remains a significant challenge. Existing approaches, broadly categorized into imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL), face distinct limitations that VITAL aims to address.

**Imitation Learning (IL):** While large-scale datasets have improved generalization for simpler pick-and-place tasks, IL struggles with fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation. Tasks like USB insertion or card swiping demand millimeter-to-sub-millimeter precision, making demonstration collection extremely difficult, time-consuming, and brittle. Policies learned via IL tend to overfit to the training data's specific environment configurations, leading to poor generalization to novel scenes or spatial variations. Even with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large pre-trained models, the challenge of acquiring high-fidelity data for precise contact tasks persists.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL offers an alternative by learning directly through online interaction, potentially refining policies beyond the limitations of demonstrations. However, traditional RL often yields brittle policies that overfit to the training environment's specific cues (e.g., scene layout, background distractors). This is compounded by data inefficiency and the sim-to-real gap, making real-world RL deployment for precise tasks difficult and resource-intensive.

**Addressing the Gaps:** VITAL positions itself at the intersection of these challenges, proposing a hybrid framework that synergizes the strengths of both IL and RL, enhanced by a thoughtful integration of sensing modalities and foundation models.
- **Visuotactile Sensing:** The paper emphasizes the critical role of tactile sensing, which provides direct contact feedback indispensable for precision, especially when visual cues are occluded or insufficient. This contrasts with many vision-only robot learning approaches and highlights a growing recognition in the field of the necessity of multi-modal sensing for complex manipulation.
- **Decomposition of Skills:** The idea of decomposing complex tasks into a "reaching" phase and a "local interaction" phase is a common strategy in robotics, but VITAL applies this with a novel combination of modern AI tools. This decomposition allows for specialized policies to handle different aspects of the task, where vision is leveraged for coarse spatial reasoning and tactile sensing for fine-grained contact.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models:** The use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Molmo for high-level scene reasoning and perception models like Segment Anything 2 for semantic segmentation represents a significant trend in robot learning. These models, trained on vast web-scale data, provide powerful priors that can enhance robot perception and generalization without requiring task-specific data. VITAL effectively integrates these models to reduce the burden of data collection and improve visual robustness.
- **Residual Reinforcement Learning:** The application of residual RL, which refines a behavior-cloned base policy, is a promising direction for achieving both generalization (from the base policy) and high performance (from online refinement). This approach is gaining traction as a way to combine the benefits of demonstrations (initial policy) with online interaction (fine-tuning) efficiently.
- **Generalization through Augmentation:** The innovative use of semantic augmentations during training, powered by vision foundation models, allows policies to learn generalizable visual encoders from limited demonstration data. This technique addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional data augmentation by selectively preserving task-relevant visual features while diversifying irrelevant background and lighting.

In essence, VITAL contributes to the broader landscape by demonstrating a principled and effective way to achieve robust, precise, and generalizable robot manipulation. It synthesizes state-of-the-art techniques—visuotactile sensing, foundation models, task decomposition, and residual reinforcement learning—to overcome the inherent limitations of data-driven approaches in contact-rich scenarios, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, precise, and generalizable policy learning framework for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. The authors identify a significant gap in current data-driven approaches, namely their struggle with tasks requiring millimeter-level precision and their lack of generalization to unseen environments.

The core motivations driving VITAL are:

1. **Bridging the Precision-Generalization Gap:** Existing imitation learning methods demand extensive, difficult-to-obtain demonstrations for precise tasks and often yield brittle, non-generalizable policies. Reinforcement learning, while capable of refinement, tends to overfit to training environments. VITAL aims to bridge this gap by enabling policies that achieve both high precision and strong generalization.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Precise Tasks:** Fine-grained manipulation tasks (e.g., USB insertion, card swiping) have extremely low error tolerance, making high-fidelity demonstration collection time-consuming, prone to errors, and challenging to scale. VITAL seeks to learn robust policies with remarkably few demonstrations (only 32 per task) and minimal online interaction (45 minutes of RL).
3. **Leveraging Complementary Sensing Modalities:** The authors are motivated by the observation that vision and tactile sensing have complementary strengths. Vision excels at scene-level reasoning and coarse alignment in pre-contact phases, providing spatial awareness. Tactile sensing is indispensable during contact-rich phases, offering direct feedback on forces and slip that vision cannot capture, and is inherently robust to lighting or occlusion issues. The objective is to effectively pair these modalities for optimal performance.
4. **Exploiting Invariant Local Interactions:** A key insight is that while the broader environmental context (e.g., background, object location) for a task may vary, the low-level physical interactions required for successful manipulation (e.g., the precise motion to insert a plug into a socket) remain largely consistent. The motivation is to capture this invariant "local policy" once in a canonical setting, allowing it to generalize by simply localizing the target in a new environment.
5. **Improving Visual Generalization from Limited Data:** Learning from raw RGB inputs in constrained settings often lacks the visual diversity needed for generalization. VITAL aims to overcome this by introducing semantic, task-aware data augmentation powered by vision foundation models. This is motivated by the idea that policies should focus on task-relevant visual features, which are stable, rather than irrelevant environmental noise.
6. **Refining Behavior-Cloned Policies:** Imitation learning provides a good starting point but can suffer from imperfect demonstrations. The motivation for incorporating residual reinforcement learning is to fine-tune these behavior-cloned policies, learning small corrective actions, thereby boosting precision and robustness without sacrificing the visual generalization already learned.

In summary, VITAL's objectives are to learn precise, generalizable, and data-efficient robotic manipulation policies for contact-rich tasks by intelligently combining visuotactile sensing, hierarchical task decomposition, semantic data augmentation using foundation models, and residual reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VITAL is a multi-phase framework designed to achieve robust, precise, and generalizable robotic manipulation through a synergistic combination of observation modalities, learning paradigms, and data augmentation techniques. The methodology is structured around three key phases: offline imitation learning, online reinforcement learning, and a hierarchical inference strategy.

#### 3.1. Generalizable Behavior Cloning through Semantic Augmentations (Phase 1: Offline Imitation)

The first phase focuses on learning a generalizable base visuotactile policy ($\pi_b$) from a limited number of expert demonstrations.

- **Data Collection:** Demonstrations are collected using a VR-based teleoperation system on a UFACTORY xArm 7 robot equipped with a two-fingered xArm Gripper and an AnySkin magnetic tactile sensor. For precise manipulation tasks, the target object's position is fixed during data collection, and the robot's initial position is randomly sampled in its vicinity. Critically, observations (wrist camera image, tactile readings) and actions are grounded in the robot's end-effector frame. This design choice aims to decouple the learned policy from absolute spatial coordinates, facilitating transfer to novel spatial configurations during inference. Only 32 demonstrations are collected per task.
- **Semantic Augmentation Pipeline:** To enhance visual generalization from limited data, VITAL employs a novel semantic augmentation pipeline:
 1. **Background Replacement:** Demonstrations are collected with a green screen background to enable procedural scene generation using tools like RoboEngine.
 2. **Task-aware Segmentation:** To prevent naive background filtering that might remove task-relevant visual features, a multi-stage segmentation pipeline is used:
 - Human annotators mark key points on task-relevant objects in a single reference frame.
 - DIFT-based correspondence matching propagates these annotations to the first frame of all demonstrations.
 - Segment-Anything 2 (a vision foundation model) performs instance segmentation based on these annotations.
 - XMem tracks the segmented masks temporally, separating task-relevant elements (robot, grasped object, target object) from augmentable background regions.
 3. This allows targeted background transformations (altering distractors, backgrounds, lighting) while preserving crucial contact-relevant visual features.
- **Base Policy Training:** The augmented visuotactile data is used to train the base policy ($\pi_b$) via behavior cloning.
 - **Visual Encoder:** A randomly-initialized ResNet-18 encodes the augmented wrist camera images.
 - **Tactile Encoder:** A multilayer perception (MLP) encodes the 15-dimensional tactile readings from the AnySkin sensor.
 - **Policy Network:** The encoded visual and tactile observations are fed into a transformer-based policy network (minGPT architecture).
 - **Action Prediction:** The policy predicts a chunk of 10 future actions using an MLP action head, trained with a mean squared error (MSE) loss. A temporal ensemble (weighted average of past chunked predictions) is applied during inference for smoother motion.

#### 3.2. Fine-tuning with Demonstration-guided Reinforcement Learning (Phase 2: Residual RL)

While the behavior-cloned base policy provides a generalizable starting point, it might only achieve modest success rates. To boost precision and robustness, VITAL employs online residual reinforcement learning (RL) to train a residual policy ($\pi_r$).

- **Residual RL Formulation:** Instead of learning a policy from scratch, a residual policy $\pi_r$ is learned that predicts small corrective actions (offsets, $a_r$) relative to the base action ($a_b$) predicted by the frozen base policy $\pi_b$. The final executed action $a = a_b + a_r$. This approach allows the RL process to refine the pre-learned behavior without discarding its generalization capabilities.
- **RL Algorithm:** VITAL uses n-step DDPG as the RL optimizer, a deterministic actor-critic based method.
- **Encoder Freezing:** Crucially, the visual and tactile encoders trained during behavior cloning are fixed during residual RL. This ensures that the visual generalization learned through semantic augmentations is preserved.
- **Inputs:** The residual actor network ($\pi_r$) takes the compressed visual and tactile representations ($z_i, z_t$) and the base action ($a_b$) as input to predict the residual offset ($a_r$). The residual critic ($Q_r$) evaluates state-action pairs $(z_i, z_t, a_b, a_r)$.
- **Reward Function:** The RL training uses a combination of a binary success reward (provided by a human at the end of the trajectory) and a dense L1 distance from the goal.
- **Augmentation during RL:** Semantic visual augmentations continue to be applied to rollouts during the RL training phase. This is a key insight, as it prevents the RL from overfitting to the specific training environment and helps maintain the policy's generalization.
- **Hyperparameters:** L2 weight regularization is applied to the actor network, and a linearly decaying standard-deviation schedule is used for exploration noise to ensure both flexibility and stable convergence.

#### 3.3. Inference (Phase 3: VLM-based Reaching & Local Execution)

VITAL's inference strategy employs a hierarchical approach for spatial and scene generalization.

- **Global Semantic Navigation:** For coarse localization and initial reaching, a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Molmo, is utilized. Given an external RGB-D observation and a natural language query ("Mark a point on the socket"), Molmo predicts a 2D coordinate for the target object. This 2D coordinate is then projected to 3D workspace coordinates using depth data and camera calibration.
- **Initial Robot Pose:** The robot samples an initial end-effector pose within a predefined region above the predicted target coordinate. This coarse initialization is designed to ensure the target object is within the wrist camera's field of view, aligning with the local policy's observation space.
- **Localized Visuotactile Control:** Once the robot is in the vicinity of the target object, the learned visuotactile local policy (the combination of $\pi_b$ and $\pi_r$) takes over to perform the fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation task. Because the local policy was trained with end-effector frame observations and actions, it inherently generalizes to different absolute spatial configurations.

**Key Design Insights as per the authors:**
1. **Foundation Models for Robust Visual Encoders:** Utilizing VLMs and segmentation models (Segment-Anything 2) enables training robust visual encoders via behavior cloning, reducing reliance on massive, diverse datasets.
2. **Improved Generalizability via Residual RL:** Residual RL refines the behavior-cloned policies by predicting corrective actions while preserving the visual generalization gained through augmentations.
3. **Tactile Sensing for Precision:** Tactile sensing significantly boosts performance in contact-rich tasks where vision is insufficient or occluded.

This comprehensive methodology allows VITAL to operate with high precision, generalize across diverse environments, and reduce the burden of data collection and online training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VITAL's experimental evaluation demonstrates its superior performance across a suite of precise, contact-rich manipulation tasks, especially concerning generalization capabilities. The key findings are summarized below:

- **Superior In-Domain Performance:**
 - VITAL significantly outperforms all baselines in controlled, in-domain settings (fixed object positions, green screen background). It achieves success rates of 9/10 (Plug in Socket), 9/10 (Insert USB), 10/10 (Card Swiping), 9/10 (Key in Lock), and 10/10 (Pick Bread) across 10 trials per task.
 - This represents an absolute improvement of 40% on average over the strongest baseline (ViSk).
 - The results highlight the efficacy of combining visuotactile behavior cloning with residual RL, showing that pretraining is crucial for sample-efficient online learning compared to RLPD (RL from scratch).

- **Strong Spatial Generalization:**
 - When tested on three novel target object positions outside the training distribution (while retaining the green screen background), VITAL maintains strong performance. For example, it achieves 28/30 for Plug in Socket and Card Swiping, 24/30 for Insert USB, and 22/30 for Key in Lock across 30 trials.
 - This confirms that grounding observations and actions in the robot's end-effector frame effectively enables spatial generalization, largely decoupling the policy from absolute object positions.
 - Baselines like BAKU and ViSk showed performance decline when objects approached the green screen edges, indicating their sensitivity to visual distribution shifts not encountered during training.

- **Robust Scene Generalization:**
 - VITAL demonstrates remarkable robustness to novel, cluttered scene configurations (varying backgrounds and distractors) while keeping the target object position fixed.
 - It achieves 27/30 (Plug in Socket), 23/30 (Insert USB), 25/30 (Card Swiping), and 24/30 (Key in Lock) in these unseen environments.
 - The strong performance of both VITAL and its behavior-cloned variant (VITAL-BC) underscores the critical role of **semantic augmentations** in enabling policies to disentangle task-relevant visual cues from environmental noise.

- **Generalization to Combined Perturbations (Spatial & Scene):**
 - When tested in scenarios with both novel target spatial positions and varied background appearances, VITAL consistently outperforms all baselines, achieving success rates like 29/30 (Plug in Socket) and 27/30 (Card Swiping, Key in Lock).
 - This validates VITAL's ability to handle complex real-world variability by synergizing localized observation spaces, semantic augmentations, and residual RL.

- **Critical Role of Tactile Sensing:**
 - Ablation studies confirm that tactile sensing is essential for millimeter-scale precision and reliability in contact-rich tasks.
 - Removing tactile input (comparing Visual BC vs. Visuo-Tactile BC, and their residual RL counterparts) reduced success rates by an average of 40% across tasks like Plug in Socket, USB Insertion, and Card Swiping.
 - Tactile sensing is particularly crucial when visual feedback becomes unreliable due to occlusion by the end-effector or the grasped object (e.g., Card Swiping where the card obscures the machine).

- **Importance of Semantic Augmentations:**
 - Further ablations show that semantic augmentations are vital for novel scene generalization. Without augmentations, both visual (BAKU) and visuotactile (ViSk) BC policies perform poorly in unseen scenes (e.g., 0/10 successes for BAKU and ViSk on scene generalization tasks).
 - Semantic augmentations enable both approaches to adapt to new visual conditions, with VITAL's full pipeline leveraging them during residual RL to further enhance performance without sacrificing generality.

- **Effectiveness of VLM Navigation:**
 - The integration of Molmo for VLM-based coarse navigation successfully allows VITAL to generalize to unseen object positions in cluttered scenes (e.g., 21/25 for Plug in Socket, 19/25 for Card Swiping across 25 trials).
 - This demonstrates the utility of combining high-level VLMs for spatial reasoning with localized visuotactile policies for precise execution, enabling zero-shot adaptation to novel spatial configurations.

In essence, the results validate VITAL's core hypotheses: that a combination of task decomposition, complementary sensing modalities, semantic visual augmentations, and residual reinforcement learning can achieve unprecedented precision and generalization for contact-rich robotic manipulation with minimal data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VITAL represents a significant step forward in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring high precision and robustness in unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from its effective solution to several long-standing challenges in robot learning:

- **Addressing the Precision-Generalization Dilemma:** The ability of VITAL to achieve approximately 90% success rates on complex, contact-rich tasks while generalizing to unseen spatial configurations and cluttered scenes is a major breakthrough. Traditional methods often had to sacrifice one for the other. VITAL demonstrates that it is possible to have both, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
- **Data Efficiency and Reduced Burden:** By learning generalizable policies from just 32 demonstrations and 45 minutes of online RL per task, VITAL drastically reduces the data collection and training burden. This is highly significant, as data acquisition is often the bottleneck in scaling robotic capabilities. This efficiency makes it more feasible for a wider range of users to train robots for custom, precise tasks.
- **Empowering Robots for Real-World Interaction:** Precise, contact-rich manipulation is ubiquitous in daily life, from plugging in devices to using tools. VITAL's capabilities open doors for robots to perform more dexterous and useful tasks in homes, offices, and industrial settings where precise physical interaction is required, moving beyond simple pick-and-place.
- **Synergistic Integration of AI Paradigms:** VITAL's novelty lies in its intelligent combination of diverse, cutting-edge AI techniques:
 - **Visuotactile Sensing:** The clear demonstration of tactile sensing's indispensable role, especially under visual occlusion, highlights the need for multimodal sensing in advanced robotics.
 - **Foundation Models:** The strategic use of VLMs for high-level localization and segmentation models for data augmentation showcases how large pre-trained models can be effectively integrated to provide powerful priors and improve visual generalization for robotic tasks.
 - **Residual RL:** The success of residual RL in refining behavior-cloned policies while preserving generalization offers a promising path for efficiently leveraging human demonstrations and online interaction.
- **Robustness to Environmental Variability:** The framework's demonstrated robustness to various environmental perturbations (novel object positions, cluttered backgrounds, distractors) is critical for transitioning robots from controlled lab settings to dynamic real-world scenarios.
- **Foundation for More Complex Skills:** VITAL's approach of learning "local policies" that are decoupled from global scene context could serve as a fundamental building block for constructing more complex, hierarchical robotic skills. A robot could potentially chain multiple such precise, generalizable local policies guided by a high-level planner or VLM.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by authors):**
While impactful, the authors acknowledge certain limitations:
1. **Limited Spatial Variations:** Current spatial generalization experiments focus on horizontal surfaces. Extending this to arbitrary 3D configurations (e.g., vertical or angled insertions) would further broaden applicability.
2. **Obstacle-Free VLM Navigation:** The VLM-guided reaching assumes obstacle-free paths. Enhancing VLMs for obstacle-aware path planning in cluttered environments would improve robustness in highly unstructured settings.
3. **Lab Conditions:** The evaluations are currently in controlled lab conditions. Testing in real-world home or industrial environments with unpredictable lighting, dynamic occlusions, and unstructured layouts would provide more comprehensive validation of VITAL's real-world robustness.

Despite these limitations, VITAL provides a robust and empirically validated framework that pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in precise, generalizable robotic manipulation. Its methodologies are likely to influence future research in data-efficient robot learning, multimodal sensing integration, and the application of foundation models in robotics, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems capable of intricate interactions with the physical world.
