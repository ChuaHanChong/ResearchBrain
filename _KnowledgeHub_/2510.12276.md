# Spatial Forcing: Implicit Spatial Representation Alignment for Vision-language-action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, Spatial Forcing (SF), implicitly develops 3D perception in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by aligning their internal visual representations with geometric knowledge from a pretrained 3D foundation model. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in robotic manipulation benchmarks, enhancing training efficiency by 3.8 times and data efficiency by 5.9 times compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily pretrained on 2D image data, lack the precise 3D spatial awareness essential for robust physical interaction in the real world.
- Existing methods for integrating 3D information (e.g., explicit 3D sensor inputs, depth map estimation) face practical issues such as sensor noise, hardware heterogeneity, data scarcity, and inherent performance limitations.
- Empirical analysis reveals that VLA visual embeddings, without specific guidance, do not encode meaningful spatial structures required for accurate action generation.

## Method

- ==Spatial Forcing (SF)== implicitly instills spatial comprehension in VLA models by supervising their intermediate visual embeddings with geometric representations.
- The method aligns the VLA's visual embeddings with features from a pretrained ==3D foundation model (VGGT)==, which provides rich, normalized spatial information, using a ==cosine similarity loss==.
- Alignment is applied to specific ==causal attention layers== of the VLA's backbone, augmented with positional embeddings, and introduces no additional computational overhead during inference.

## Results

- Spatial Forcing achieved an average success rate of **98.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming both 2D-based and explicit 3D-based VLA methods.
- SF demonstrated significant training efficiency, converging to the same success rates **3.8x** faster, and improved data efficiency by being **5.9x** more efficient than baselines.
- Real-world experiments across single-arm and dual-arm tasks showed consistently higher success rates, affirming SF's robustness and strong data utilization even with limited training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate visual tokens within VLA models can be effectively guided to encode richer spatial information, thereby enhancing action precision without relying on explicit 3D inputs.
> - Aligning VLA visual representations with a powerful 3D foundation model (VGGT) offers a robust and universal pathway to impart 3D spatial understanding.
> - The optimal integration point for spatial supervision is not the deepest or shallowest layer but rather an intermediate causal attention layer (e.g., 24th layer) to balance feature richness and spatial integration.
