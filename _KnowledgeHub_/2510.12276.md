# Spatial Forcing: Implicit Spatial Representation Alignment for Vision-language-action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, Spatial Forcing (SF), implicitly develops 3D perception in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by aligning their internal visual representations with geometric knowledge from a pretrained 3D foundation model. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in robotic manipulation benchmarks, enhancing training efficiency by 3.8 times and data efficiency by 5.9 times compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily pretrained on 2D image data, lack the precise 3D spatial awareness essential for robust physical interaction in the real world.
- Existing methods for integrating 3D information (e.g., explicit 3D sensor inputs, depth map estimation) face practical issues such as sensor noise, hardware heterogeneity, data scarcity, and inherent performance limitations.
- Empirical analysis reveals that VLA visual embeddings, without specific guidance, do not encode meaningful spatial structures required for accurate action generation.

## Method

- ==Spatial Forcing (SF)== implicitly instills spatial comprehension in VLA models by supervising their intermediate visual embeddings with geometric representations.
- The method aligns the VLA's visual embeddings with features from a pretrained ==3D foundation model (VGGT)==, which provides rich, normalized spatial information, using a ==cosine similarity loss==.
- Alignment is applied to specific ==causal attention layers== of the VLA's backbone, augmented with positional embeddings, and introduces no additional computational overhead during inference.

## Results

- Spatial Forcing achieved an average success rate of **98.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming both 2D-based and explicit 3D-based VLA methods.
- SF demonstrated significant training efficiency, converging to the same success rates **3.8x** faster, and improved data efficiency by being **5.9x** more efficient than baselines.
- Real-world experiments across single-arm and dual-arm tasks showed consistently higher success rates, affirming SF's robustness and strong data utilization even with limited training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate visual tokens within VLA models can be effectively guided to encode richer spatial information, thereby enhancing action precision without relying on explicit 3D inputs.
> - Aligning VLA visual representations with a powerful 3D foundation model (VGGT) offers a robust and universal pathway to impart 3D spatial understanding.
> - The optimal integration point for spatial supervision is not the deepest or shallowest layer but rather an intermediate causal attention layer (e.g., 24th layer) to balance feature richness and spatial integration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly evolving, driven by advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly in vision-language models (VLMs). Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a frontier in this domain, aiming to enable robots to understand human language instructions and execute precise actions in the physical world. However, a significant limitation of most existing VLA models is their foundation on VLMs predominantly pretrained on 2D image data. While these models excel at semantic understanding and instruction following, they often lack accurate spatial awareness crucial for operating in a 3D physical environment.

Existing approaches to address this 3D spatial awareness gap generally fall into two categories:
1. **Explicit 3D Sensor Inputs:** Methods like VidBot, PointVLA, GeoVLA, and 3D-CAVLA attempt to incorporate direct 3D sensor data, such as depth maps from depth cameras or point clouds from LiDAR. While these provide rich geometric priors, they face several challenges:
- **Data Quality:** 3D sensor data can be noisy and inaccurate.
- **Hardware Heterogeneity:** Robotic sensors vary widely in type, position, and calibration, leading to data inconsistencies.
- **Data Scarcity:** Many large-scale robot datasets lack corresponding depth information, limiting scalability.
2. **Estimated 3D Information:** Approaches like SpatialVLA and EVO-0 try to estimate 3D cues from 2D images using depth estimators. However, their performance is inherently capped by the accuracy and generalization capabilities of these estimators, often leading to sub-optimal robotic policies.

This paper positions "Spatial Forcing (SF)" as a paradigm shift, addressing the limitations of both explicit 3D inputs and estimated 3D cues. Instead of relying on direct or estimated 3D *inputs*, SF proposes an *implicit* method to instill spatial comprehension within VLA models. It operates by aligning the VLA's internal visual representations with geometric knowledge derived from a pretrained 3D foundation model (VGGT). This strategy aims to overcome the practical hurdles of 3D sensor integration and the performance ceiling of depth estimators, offering a more universal and scalable solution for grounding VLAs in the 3D physical world. By focusing on representation alignment, SF contributes to the growing body of work exploring how to imbue models with desired capabilities through auxiliary supervision signals in their latent space, rather than solely through input or output modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **implicitly develop 3D perception and comprehension capabilities in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby eliminating their dependence on explicit 3D sensor information or external depth estimators.**

The motivations for pursuing this objective are multi-faceted and rooted in the identified shortcomings of current VLA models:
1. **Lack of Spatial Awareness in 2D-Pretrained VLAs:** The core problem is that most VLA models, built upon vision-language models (VLMs) trained predominantly on 2D data, fundamentally lack the precise spatial awareness required for physical interaction in the 3D world. This deficiency hinders their ability to execute actions with the necessary precision and robustness.
2. **Limitations of Existing 3D Integration Strategies:** As discussed in the previous section, current methods to incorporate 3D information, either through direct 3D sensor inputs or estimated depth maps, are plagued by practical issues such as sensor noise, hardware heterogeneity, incomplete datasets, and the inherent performance limits of depth estimators. These challenges make it difficult to develop a universal, scalable, and reliable 3D strategy.
3. **Empirical Evidence from Depth Probing:** The authors provide a compelling empirical motivation through a "lightweight depth probing experiment." By training a simple depth estimation head (DPT) on the *frozen* visual embeddings of a mainstream VLA model, they observe that these embeddings, learned solely from 2D images, "fail to yield meaningful spatial structures." This observation directly reveals a significant "gap in the spatial reasoning capabilities of VLAs trained without external guidance." This quantitative analysis underscores the necessity for an intervention that can imbue VLA visual embeddings with richer spatial information.
4. **Leveraging Intermediate Representations:** The paper posits that "visual tokens as intermediate scene representations play a crucial role in generating action tokens and could be supervised properly." This insight motivates the idea of guiding these internal representations to encode richer spatial information, thereby naturally enhancing action precision.

In essence, the research aims to find a simple yet effective method to bridge the crucial gap between 2D semantic understanding and 3D spatial awareness in VLAs, bypassing the complexities and limitations of explicit 3D data inputs or estimations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Spatial Forcing (SF)**, is an implicit alignment strategy designed to instill spatial comprehension in VLA models without relying on explicit 3D sensor inputs or depth estimators during training or inference. The core idea is to supervise the intermediate visual embeddings of a VLA model by aligning them with geometric representations generated by a powerful pretrained 3D foundation model.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key components:

1. **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model Preliminaries:**
- SF is designed to work with auto-regressive VLA models, which are typically built on pretrained VLMs (e.g., Prismatic VLM, PaliGemma) and specialized action experts.
- The VLA model processes multi-view images (visual tokens), task instructions (linguistic tokens), and auto-regressively generates action tokens.
- The training objective for the VLA model primarily involves a standard action loss ($L_{action}$) that measures the discrepancy between predicted and ground-truth actions. The paper emphasizes that visual tokens, as intermediate scene representations, are crucial for precise action generation.

2. **3D Foundation Model: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT):**
- To provide the necessary spatial supervision signal, SF utilizes VGGT (Wang et al., 2025b). VGGT is a feed-forward model that takes a series of 2D images and outputs various 3D attributes of a scene, such as camera parameters, point maps, depth maps, and 3D point tracks.
- Crucially, the latent representations extracted from VGGT's transformer backbone are considered to inherently encode rich, normalized spatial information. These representations are chosen as the supervision signals due to their multi-view consistency and strong spatial perception abilities, derived from training on 2D-3D paired datasets.

3. **Spatial Forcing Alignment Strategy:**
- SF aligns the intermediate visual embeddings of the VLA model with the spatial representations produced by VGGT.
- For a set of multi-view input images `I`, VGGT generates pixel-level spatial representations `f_3D(I)`.
- To ensure that the supervised visual tokens (x_V) retain their critical positional order within the auto-regressive VLA process, these spatial representations are augmented with extra **positional embeddings (E)**.
- Before alignment, each VLA visual token `x_i` undergoes a transformation: batch normalization (`Γ`) followed by a two-layer MLP. This ensures compatibility in feature dimension with the target spatial representations.
- The alignment is enforced using a **cosine similarity score** to maximize the similarity between the processed VLA visual tokens and the augmented VGGT spatial representations:
 $L_{align} = - \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} S[\text{MLP} \cdot \Gamma(x^V_i), f_{3D_i}(I) + E]$
 where `S[·, ·]` denotes cosine similarity.

4. **Optimizing Alignment Layer:**
- The authors investigate aligning the VLA's visual embeddings at different causal attention layers.
- Through ablation studies, they find that supervising "relatively deep but not the deepest layers" is most effective. This is hypothesized to be because deeper layers might lose more vision-specific features, making them less amenable to spatial supervision, while shallow features might gradually lose spatial information in subsequent layers if constrained too early. The 24th layer (out of 32 in their VLM backbone) is identified as the optimal alignment point.

5. **Final Training Objective:**
- The overall training objective combines the standard VLA action loss ($L_{action}$) and the spatial alignment loss ($L_{align}$) with a weighting factor $\alpha$:
 $L_{SF} = L_{action} + \alpha L_{align}$
- An ablation study on $\alpha$ reveals that an intermediate value (0.5) provides the best balance, enhancing spatial comprehension without destabilizing the VLA's action prediction.

6. **Inference:**
- A significant advantage of SF is that **it introduces no additional structures or computational overhead during inference**. The VLA model, once trained with SF, operates identically to a standard VLA model, highlighting its high applicability and efficiency for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings from extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating the efficacy, efficiency, and robustness of Spatial Forcing (SF).

1. **Validation of Spatial Information Insufficiency (Depth Probing):**
- The initial depth probing experiment, where a DPT head was trained on frozen VLA visual embeddings, conclusively showed that these embeddings (learned solely from 2D images) lacked meaningful spatial structures. This validated the core hypothesis that current VLAs struggle with implicit 3D comprehension.
- Post-SF, the depth probing revealed that aligned embeddings inherently contain rich spatial information, leading to significantly better performance in depth prediction.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulation:**
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** SF achieved the best performance across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), with an average success rate (SR) of 98.5%. This surpassed both 2D-based VLAs (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%) and explicit 3D-based VLAs (e.g., GeoVLA at 97.7%, 3D-CAVLA at 98.1%) without requiring explicit 3D sensor inputs. This is a crucial finding, indicating SF's ability to imbue spatial awareness implicitly at a level comparable to or better than methods relying on direct 3D data.
- **RoboTwin Benchmark:** SF yielded substantial improvements over the base model ($\pi^0$) across all easy and hard tasks, achieving the highest average success rate. The significant improvement on hard tasks (involving domain randomization like scene clutter, lighting variation) highlights SF's ability to make the model focus on accurate object locations and relative spatial relationships, rather than spurious correlations.

3. **Enhanced Training Efficiency:**
- Experiments showed that SF significantly accelerates convergence. The model achieved the same success rates **3.8 times more quickly** than the base model (OpenVLA-OFT), demonstrating SF as an efficient learning pathway for rapid spatial understanding.

4. **Improved Data Efficiency:**
- SF dramatically improved data efficiency. With only 5% of the full dataset, SF reached 75.8% success rates, showing a **5.9 times more efficient** learning process for the same success rates compared to the baseline. This is highly valuable for real-world robotics where data collection is often costly and time-consuming.

5. **Component-wise Analysis (Ablation Studies):**
- **Target Representation:** VGGT, with its strong spatial perception abilities, provided the highest success rates when used as the target representation, confirming the importance of 3D-aware supervision. Using positional embedding (PE) with VGGT further enhanced performance, especially for long-horizon tasks.
- **Alignment Layer:** Aligning visual embeddings at the **24th layer** (out of 32) of the VLA's causal attention layers yielded the best results, suggesting an optimal trade-off between receiving vision-specific features and integrating them with spatial representations. Aligning at too shallow or too deep layers was less effective.
- **Weight Factor ($\alpha$):** An appropriate weight factor of 0.5 for the alignment loss was found to be optimal, balancing spatial comprehension with the VLA's primary action prediction task.

6. **t-SNE Visualization:**
- The t-SNE visualization showed that after SF alignment, the VLA features exhibited an almost identical distribution shape to the target spatial representations from VGGT, but with independent cluster centers. This indicates that SF successfully forces the VLA to learn the *relational structure* of spatial concepts while preserving its unique representational identity, avoiding representational collapse.

7. **Real-World Experiment Validation:**
- SF achieved consistently higher success rates across diverse real-world single-arm (stack glass cups, grasp right-side vegetable, place green block) and dual-arm (lift pot) tasks.
- Crucially, these real-world experiments were conducted with **limited training data** (40 demos for single-arm, 20 for bimanual), underscoring SF's strong data utilization capabilities and robustness in highly variable environments (e.g., light variation, target object variation, height variation, new embodiments).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Spatial Forcing" (SF) method presents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap in VLAs Robustly:** SF offers an elegant and practical solution to the long-standing challenge of grounding 2D-pretrained VLA models in the 3D physical world. By implicitly instilling spatial awareness through representation alignment, it bypasses the complexities and limitations associated with explicit 3D sensor data (noise, heterogeneity, scarcity) and the performance ceiling of depth estimators. This makes VLA models more robust and reliable for real-world deployment.

2. **Universal and Scalable Training Paradigm:** The method's independence from explicit 3D sensor inputs means it can be applied universally across various robotic platforms and datasets, including those lacking depth information. This greatly enhances the scalability of training generalist VLA policies, making it easier to leverage existing large-scale 2D vision-language datasets and diverse robotic data.

3. **Enhanced Efficiency in Learning:** The demonstrated improvements in training acceleration (3.8x faster) and data efficiency (5.9x more efficient) are critical for robotics. Real-world robot data is expensive and time-consuming to collect, and faster training reduces computational costs. SF's ability to achieve high performance with significantly less data lowers the barrier to entry for developing capable robotic systems and accelerates the research cycle.

4. **Improved Generalization and Precision:** By forcing VLAs to develop a richer understanding of spatial relationships, SF leads to more precise and spatially aware actions. This translates to better generalization capabilities in novel or varied environments, as evidenced by its strong performance on hard tasks in RoboTwin and real-world experiments with varying conditions (lighting, object types, heights). This is essential for robots to operate effectively in unstructured, dynamic environments.

5. **Advancing Latent Representation Learning:** The work highlights the power of representation supervision in guiding model capabilities. By showing that aligning intermediate visual embeddings can profoundly impact downstream task performance, SF contributes to the broader understanding of how to engineer more effective and interpretable latent spaces in large foundation models. The t-SNE visualization further provides insights into how this alignment occurs, influencing the *structure* of the feature space while preserving its distinct identity.

6. **Future Research Directions:** SF opens several avenues for future research. One could explore different 3D foundation models for supervision, investigate the optimal "depth" of alignment more systematically across diverse VLM architectures, or even apply similar implicit alignment strategies for other robotic capabilities (e.g., force sensing, material properties) by leveraging different types of foundation models. The concept of "forcing" specific types of knowledge into intermediate representations could be a powerful paradigm for developing more intelligent and adaptive embodied AI.

In summary, Spatial Forcing provides a simple, yet highly effective and impactful method that significantly enhances the spatial comprehension of VLA models. Its ability to overcome key limitations of previous approaches, coupled with substantial improvements in performance and efficiency, positions it as a crucial step towards more capable, generalizable, and deployable robotic systems.
