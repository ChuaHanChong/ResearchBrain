# Measure gradients, not activations! Enhancing neuronal activity in deep reinforcement learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST and Mila introduced GraMa, a gradient-based metric for quantifying neuronal activity, and ReGraMa, an intervention to reinitialize inactive neurons in deep reinforcement learning. This approach effectively identifies and reactivates neurons losing their learning capacity in complex architectures, leading to improved performance and stability across various Deep RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning agents often suffer from neuronal activity loss, where neurons become dormant, reducing learning capacity and impeding continual adaptation.
- Existing activation-based metrics, like the "τ-dormant neuron ratio," are ineffective in modern, complex Deep RL architectures due to multi-branch structures, non-ReLU activations, and normalization layers.
- The misalignment between a neuron's activation magnitude and its true learning potential in advanced models hinders effective diagnosis and mitigation of dormancy.

## Method

- The paper proposes **GraMa** (==Gradient Magnitude== Neural Activity Metric), a lightweight and architecture-agnostic metric that quantifies neuron-level learning potential based on gradient magnitudes.
- ReGraMa, an efficient neuron resetting mechanism guided by GraMa, selectively reinitializes inactive neurons by resetting their incoming and outgoing weights to restore learning capacity.
- The approach shifts focus from a neuron's "expressive ability" (activations) to its "learning capacity" (gradients) as a more robust indicator of health and potential for adaptation.

## Results

- GraMa demonstrates negligible computational overhead by leveraging existing gradient information in the backpropagation process.
- ReGraMa consistently improves learning performance and stability in DeepMind Control Suite tasks with residual net-based policies (BRO-net) and diffusion model-based policies (DACER) on MuJoCo-v4, significantly outperforming activation-based methods.
- The method is robust across diverse activation functions (ReLU, Tanh, Sigmoid) and exhibits greater robustness to threshold variations compared to activation-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Activation-based neuronal activity metrics lose statistical power in complex Deep RL architectures, often misclassifying truly inactive neurons and failing to restore learning capacity.
> - Gradient magnitudes serve as a more natural and direct proxy for a neuron's learning capacity and health, as they directly drive parameter updates.
> - Neurons identified by GraMa as having low learning capacity typically do not recover their activity independently, necessitating targeted intervention.
