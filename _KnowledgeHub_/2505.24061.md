# Measure gradients, not activations! Enhancing neuronal activity in deep reinforcement learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HKUST and Mila introduced GraMa, a gradient-based metric for quantifying neuronal activity, and ReGraMa, an intervention to reinitialize inactive neurons in deep reinforcement learning. This approach effectively identifies and reactivates neurons losing their learning capacity in complex architectures, leading to improved performance and stability across various Deep RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning agents often suffer from neuronal activity loss, where neurons become dormant, reducing learning capacity and impeding continual adaptation.
- Existing activation-based metrics, like the "τ-dormant neuron ratio," are ineffective in modern, complex Deep RL architectures due to multi-branch structures, non-ReLU activations, and normalization layers.
- The misalignment between a neuron's activation magnitude and its true learning potential in advanced models hinders effective diagnosis and mitigation of dormancy.

## Method

- The paper proposes **GraMa** (==Gradient Magnitude== Neural Activity Metric), a lightweight and architecture-agnostic metric that quantifies neuron-level learning potential based on gradient magnitudes.
- ReGraMa, an efficient neuron resetting mechanism guided by GraMa, selectively reinitializes inactive neurons by resetting their incoming and outgoing weights to restore learning capacity.
- The approach shifts focus from a neuron's "expressive ability" (activations) to its "learning capacity" (gradients) as a more robust indicator of health and potential for adaptation.

## Results

- GraMa demonstrates negligible computational overhead by leveraging existing gradient information in the backpropagation process.
- ReGraMa consistently improves learning performance and stability in DeepMind Control Suite tasks with residual net-based policies (BRO-net) and diffusion model-based policies (DACER) on MuJoCo-v4, significantly outperforming activation-based methods.
- The method is robust across diverse activation functions (ReLU, Tanh, Sigmoid) and exhibits greater robustness to threshold variations compared to activation-based baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Activation-based neuronal activity metrics lose statistical power in complex Deep RL architectures, often misclassifying truly inactive neurons and failing to restore learning capacity.
> - Gradient magnitudes serve as a more natural and direct proxy for a neuron's learning capacity and health, as they directly drive parameter updates.
> - Neurons identified by GraMa as having low learning capacity typically do not recover their activity independently, necessitating targeted intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (Deep RL) agents have demonstrated considerable success across various domains, including robotics, foundation model fine-tuning, and game playing, largely owing to the expressive power and adaptive capabilities of neural networks. However, a significant challenge in the field is the progressive loss of neuronal activity during training, leading to dormant neurons. This phenomenon reduces the network's learning capacity, impairs its ability to adapt to non-stationary data distributions, and hinders continual learning.

Existing research has identified the "dormant neuron phenomenon" and proposed methods to quantify and mitigate it. A prominent approach is the τ-dormant neuron ratio, an activation-based metric that measures neuronal inactivity using activation values. This metric has guided neuron-dependent reset mechanisms, such as ReDo, which selectively reinitialize identified dormant neurons to restore learning capabilities and prevent performance plateaus in simpler Deep RL settings, such as serial Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) with ReLU activations.

The current work identifies a critical gap: as Deep RL architectures evolve beyond simple MLPs to incorporate more complex components like residual connections, mixture of experts (MoEs), diffusion models, and diverse activation functions, activation-based metrics like the τ-dormant neuron ratio lose their statistical power. These advanced architectures imperceptibly alter the statistical properties of neuron activations, leading to a mismatch between a neuron's activation magnitude (expressive capacity) and its true learning potential (learning capacity). Consequently, existing reset mechanisms based on these metrics become less effective, failing to identify and reactivate genuinely inactive neurons in modern, high-capacity models.

This paper positions itself within the broader landscape by addressing this misalignment. It proposes a fundamental shift in how neuronal activity is quantified, moving from activation-based to gradient-based measurements. By introducing a new metric, GraMa, the authors aim to provide a more robust, architecture-agnostic approach to diagnose and mitigate neuronal activity loss in the context of modern Deep RL. This work contributes to the ongoing efforts to enhance the plasticity and continual learning capabilities of deep neural networks, particularly in dynamic and non-stationary learning environments characteristic of RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current methods for maintaining neuronal activity in Deep RL agents. While Deep RL has achieved success, a critical challenge is the "neuronal activity loss," where subsets of neurons become dormant as training progresses. This loss of plasticity impedes the network's ability to adapt to new data and learn continually, often leading to performance plateaus.

The existing paradigm for quantifying and addressing this issue primarily relies on activation-based metrics, such as the τ-dormant neuron ratio. This metric assesses a neuron's "expressive ability" by measuring its activation values. While effective for simple architectures like MLPs with ReLU activations, the authors identify a significant limitation: these activation-based approaches lose statistical power in more complex, modern Deep RL architectures.

The paper provides detailed motivation for why activation-based metrics fail in advanced settings:

1. **Multi-branch Network Structures:** In architectures like ResNets, information flows through multiple branches before fusion and activation. The final observed activation values after fusion no longer accurately reflect the individual contributions of neurons within each branch, making it difficult to pinpoint genuinely inactive neurons.
2. **Non-ReLU Activation Functions:** The clarity of "dead" (zero output) vs. "active" neurons provided by ReLU breaks down with functions like Leaky ReLU, Sigmoid, or Tanh. Neurons with near-zero or negative pre-activation values might still be expressive and contribute to learning, causing activation-based metrics to misclassify them as dormant.
3. **Normalization Layers:** Techniques like Layer Normalization modify the distribution of neuron outputs, making post-normalization activation values less representative of individual neuron functionality or learning capacity. This distortion can confuse activation-based dormancy detection.

Based on these observations, the core insight driving this work is that in advanced RL agents, maintaining a neuron's "learning capacity"—its ability to adapt via gradient updates—is more critical than preserving its "expressive ability" as measured by activations. Gradients directly drive parameter updates and thus serve as a more natural and direct proxy for a neuron's health and potential for learning.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Demonstrate Limitations:** To qualitatively and empirically show that widely adopted activation-based neuronal health measurements lose statistical power and lead to inefficient neuron identification in complex Deep RL architectures.
2. **Introduce GraMa:** To reframe neuronal health evaluation by developing GraMa (Gradient Magnitude Neural Activity Metric), a robust, lightweight, and architecture-agnostic metric that quantifies neuron-level learning potential based on gradient magnitudes. This metric is designed to overcome the limitations of activation-based methods.
3. **Develop ReGraMa:** To propose ReGraMa, an efficient neuron resetting mechanism guided by GraMa, capable of selectively reinitializing inactive neurons identified as having lost their learning capacity during training.
4. **Validate Effectiveness:** To conduct extensive experiments across diverse Deep RL algorithms and benchmarks (including MuJoCo and the DeepMind Control Suite) to empirically demonstrate that GraMa effectively reveals persistent neuron inactivity and that ReGraMa consistently improves learning performance and stability.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a more principled and effective approach to maintaining plasticity and mitigating neuronal activity loss in the context of rapidly evolving Deep RL architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a critical analysis of existing activation-based neuron activity metrics, the proposal of a novel gradient-based metric (GraMa), and the development of an intervention mechanism (ReGraMa) to leverage this new metric. The approach is validated through extensive empirical evaluations across various Deep RL settings.

#### 3.1. Critique of Activation-Based Neuron Activity Detection

The authors begin by systematically analyzing the shortcomings of activation-based metrics, specifically the τ-dormant neuron ratio, often implemented as ReDo. They hypothesize that these metrics misalign with actual learning potential in modern Deep RL architectures.

- **Motivating Example (BRO-net):** An initial evaluation using BRO-net (a residual network-based architecture) on the Dog Walk task demonstrates that ReDo fails to accurately identify neurons with low learning capacity and, conversely, incorrectly resets many neurons that possess high learning capacity but are currently less expressive (Fig. 1, Fig. 3).
- **Architectural Feature Analysis:** Three specific architectural features are identified as undermining the reliability of activation-based detection:
 1. **Multi-branch Network Structures:** In architectures like ResNets, information is fused from multiple branches. The final activation values do not accurately reflect individual neuron contributions within each branch, making activation-based assessment ineffective (Fig. 2a, Fig. 4a).
 2. **Non-ReLU Activation Functions:** Unlike ReLU, which creates a clear "dead" state for zero activation, functions like Leaky ReLU, Tanh, or Sigmoid allow neurons to be expressive even with negative or near-zero pre-activation values. Solely relying on activation magnitude for dormancy detection becomes unreliable (Fig. 2b, Fig. 4b).
 3. **Normalization Layers:** Techniques such as Layer Normalization modify the distribution of neuron outputs. Post-normalization activation values lose direct correspondence with individual neuron functionality, leading to misinterpretations by activation-based metrics (Fig. 2c, Fig. 4c).

#### 3.2. GraMa (Gradient Magnitude based Neuronal Activity Metric)

To address the identified misalignment, the paper proposes a shift in focus from activation values to gradient magnitudes for quantifying neuronal activity.

- **Core Principle:** The learning activity of a neuron is directly related to the magnitude of the gradient it receives during training. Gradients measure a neuron's capacity to respond to a given situation, directly driving parameter updates, making them a more natural proxy for learning capacity.
- **Definition:** GraMa quantifies the learning capacity score for neuron *i* in layer *ℓ* as:
 $G_ℓ^i = \frac{E_{x \sim D} |\nabla_{h_ℓ^i} L(x)|}{\frac{1}{H_ℓ} \sum_{k \in h} E_{x \sim D} |\nabla_{h_ℓ^i} L(x)|}$
 This formula normalizes the average gradient magnitude of neuron *i* with respect to the loss *L* over an input distribution *D*, by the average gradient magnitude across all neurons in layer *ℓ*.
- **Inactivity Criterion:** A neuron *i* in layer *ℓ* is considered inactive if its $G_ℓ^i$ score falls below a pre-set threshold *τ*.
- **Efficiency:** GraMa utilizes gradient information already computed during backpropagation, thus imposing negligible computational and memory overhead, unlike activation-based metrics that require storing intermediate forward pass outputs (Fig. 5, left).

#### 3.3. ReGraMa (Resetting neurons guided by GraMa)

Leveraging GraMa, the authors introduce ReGraMa, a targeted neuron resetting mechanism.

- **Procedure:** During training, at a fixed frequency (Δt steps), the activity of neurons across all layers is quantified using GraMa. Any neuron with $G_ℓ^i \le τ$ is identified as inactive.
- **Reinitialization:** Identified inactive neurons are reinitialized by resetting their incoming weights to the original weight distribution and setting their outgoing weights to zero. LayerNorm parameters, despite having few learnable parameters, are also found to be quite inactive and are reinitialized by ReGraMa.

#### 3.4. Analysis of Low Learning Capacity Neurons

- **Impact on Performance:** Experiments show a direct inverse relationship between the proportion of low learning capacity neurons (as identified by GraMa) and agent performance; increasing inactive neurons significantly degrades performance (Fig. 6a).
- **Irreversibility:** Tracking individual neurons deemed inactive by GraMa over training reveals that they typically do not recover their learning ability independently, underscoring the necessity of targeted resetting (Fig. 6b).

#### 3.5. Theoretical and Empirical Equivalence (Traditional Architectures)

- **Theorem 1:** For traditional MLPs with ReLU activation, the paper provides a proof that if a neuron is activation-dormant ($s_ℓ^i = 0$), then its gradient magnitude is also zero ($G_ℓ^i = 0$). This establishes a theoretical link between activation-based and gradient-based dormancy in simple cases.
- **Empirical Verification:** Experiments on CIFAR-100 with a traditional MLP and ReLU show that ReGraMa and ReDo yield similar performance improvements and identify comparable proportions of inactive neurons, confirming their equivalence in these simpler settings (Fig. 5, right).

#### 3.6. Superiority in Complex Architectures

- **Pruning Study:** To validate GraMa's effectiveness in complex architectures (e.g., BRO-net), neurons identified as inactive by either GraMa or activation-based methods are pruned. Pruning GraMa-identified neurons results in minimal performance loss, indicating that GraMa accurately detects truly uninformative neurons. In contrast, pruning based on activation values leads to greater performance degradation, suggesting misidentification (Fig. 7).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results demonstrating the limitations of activation-based metrics and the efficacy of the proposed GraMa metric and ReGraMa intervention.

#### 4.1. Limitations of Activation-Based Metrics (ReDo)

The paper systematically illustrates that traditional activation-based metrics, such as ReDo, struggle to accurately identify neuron inactivity in modern Deep RL architectures.
- **Misidentification in BRO-net:** As shown in Figure 1, ReDo fails to restore the learning capacity of agents using the BRO-net architecture (featuring residual paths and layer normalization) across tasks like Dog Stand, Dog Walk, and Dog Run. Further analysis in Figure 3 indicates that ReDo incorrectly resets a significant number of neurons that possess high learning capacity but might be less expressive at a given moment.
- **Vulnerability to Architectural Features:** Empirical validations confirm that activation-based methods are sensitive to:
 - **Multi-branch structures:** In Resnet-SAC, activation-based methods lose the ability to identify dormant neurons, limiting agent performance (Fig. 4a).
 - **Non-ReLU activation functions:** With Leaky ReLU in SAC, ReDo exhibits high reset activity early in training, leading to lower performance, suggesting it struggles to establish meaningful dormancy criteria (Fig. 4b).
 - **Normalization layers:** The inclusion of layer normalization in SAC causes activation-based methods to struggle with consistent neuron assessment (Fig. 4c).

#### 4.2. GraMa's Effectiveness as a Neuronal Activity Metric

GraMa is demonstrated to be a robust, lightweight, and architecture-agnostic metric for quantifying learning capacity.
- **Computational Efficiency:** GraMa imposes negligible computational and memory overhead because it utilizes gradient information already present in the optimization pipeline (Fig. 5, left).
- **Accuracy in Inactivity Detection:**
 - **Performance Correlation:** A direct negative correlation is shown between the proportion of low learning capacity neurons (as identified by GraMa) and the agent's performance (Fig. 6a).
 - **Irreversibility of Inactivity:** Neurons identified by GraMa as having low learning capacity do not spontaneously recover their activity over extended training periods (Fig. 6b), highlighting the need for intervention.
 - **Pruning Efficacy:** Pruning neurons identified as inactive by ReGraMa in a complex BRO-net agent results in minimal performance degradation (Fig. 7), indicating that GraMa accurately targets genuinely uninformative neurons.

#### 4.3. ReGraMa's Performance Enhancements Across Diverse Architectures

ReGraMa, guided by GraMa, consistently improves learning performance and stability in various Deep RL algorithms and benchmarks.

- **Residual Net-based Policy (BRO-net):** On four challenging DeepMind Control Suite tasks (Humanoid Stand, Dog Walk, Dog Stand, Humanoid Walk), ReGraMa consistently achieves higher returns and maintains a lower proportion of inactive neurons compared to ReDo and the vanilla baseline. This demonstrates its effectiveness in stabilizing learning dynamics in multi-branch architectures (Fig. 8, Tab. 8).
- **Network Scaling:** When BRO-net depth is increased, ReGraMa consistently preserves stable performance improvements, demonstrating its graceful scalability. In contrast, ReDo shows marginal or even degrading performance gains with deeper models (Fig. 9).
- **Diffusion Model-Based Policy (DACER):** For the DACER policy (based on a Unet backbone with Swish activations) on MuJoCo-v4 tasks (Ant, Walker2d), ReGraMa maintains robust and consistent performance, significantly outperforming ReDo, which provides only marginal improvements (Fig. 10, Fig. 11).
- **Activation Function Variants (SAC with Tanh/Sigmoid/ReLU):** ReGraMa effectively identifies low-quality neurons and mitigates inactivity across ReLU, Sigmoid, and Tanh activation functions, maintaining higher performance and lower inactivity levels. ReDo performs well with ReLU but degrades substantially with other activation functions (Fig. 12).
- **Robustness to Threshold (τ):** ReGraMa exhibits greater robustness to variations in the threshold τ compared to ReDo, consistently outperforming it across a range of τ values (Fig. 13).
- **Interaction with Regularization (Appendix C):** Combining ReGraMa with L2 initialization or weight decay individually further improves learning efficiency, with L2 initialization appearing particularly compatible. However, the simultaneous application of all three does not yield additional gains (Tab. 9).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Deep Reinforcement Learning by addressing a fundamental challenge: the loss of neuronal activity (plasticity) in advanced neural network architectures. The implications of this work are substantial, potentially influencing how deep learning models are designed, trained, and maintained, particularly in dynamic and complex environments.

**Enhanced Understanding and Diagnosis:** The introduction of GraMa represents a crucial advancement in the diagnostic capabilities for deep neural networks. By shifting the focus from activation magnitudes to gradient magnitudes, the paper offers a more accurate and robust way to quantify a neuron's "learning capacity" rather than just its "expressive ability." This refined metric is architecture-agnostic, meaning it is effective across a broader range of complex network designs (e.g., residual networks, diffusion models) and diverse activation functions, where traditional activation-based metrics fail. This provides researchers and practitioners with a more reliable tool to identify genuinely underperforming neurons.

**Improved Learning Stability and Performance:** The proposed intervention mechanism, ReGraMa, guided by the GraMa metric, consistently demonstrates improved learning performance and stability across multiple Deep RL algorithms and challenging benchmarks (MuJoCo, DeepMind Control Suite). This enhancement is critical for Deep RL agents, which often operate in non-stationary environments and require continuous adaptation. By effectively restoring neuronal activity, ReGraMa allows agents to maintain their ability to acquire new knowledge and adapt to evolving conditions, mitigating the common problem of performance plateaus during prolonged training.

**Broader Applicability beyond RL:** The core insight—that gradient magnitude is a superior indicator of learning potential compared to activation magnitude in complex architectures—is a fundamental contribution that extends beyond Deep RL. This principle could be applied to other domains of deep learning, such as continual learning in supervised settings, where maintaining plasticity and preventing catastrophic forgetting are key challenges. The architecture-agnostic nature of GraMa suggests its potential utility in diagnosing and addressing plasticity issues in large-scale transformer policies or other emerging complex models.

**Practical Tool and Future Research Directions:** GraMa's lightweight nature, leveraging information already available in the optimization pipeline, makes it a practical and easily implementable diagnostic and intervention tool. This paves the way for more principled approaches to maintaining the continuous learning ability of Deep RL agents. The findings also underscore that even high-capacity policies may suffer from neuron underutilization, opening new avenues for research into how this phenomenon impacts generalization, multi-task transfer, and long-term adaptation.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that the current evaluation is limited to three representative neural architectures due to computational constraints. Future work will extend GraMa's application to more complex settings, including large-scale transformer policies, multi-task, and continual Deep RL. Such extensions could further solidify GraMa's role as a foundational tool for understanding and preserving learning capacity in advanced deep learning systems.

In summary, this paper provides a robust solution to a pressing problem in Deep RL, offering both a superior diagnostic metric and an effective intervention strategy. By refocusing on gradient-based measures of neuronal health, the work significantly enhances the capabilities of modern Deep RL agents and establishes a valuable framework for future research into neural network plasticity.
