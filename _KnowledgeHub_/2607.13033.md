# DenseReward: Dense Reward Learning via Failure Synthesis for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A dense vision-language reward model, DenseReward, was developed to provide fine-grained, per-timestep feedback for robotic manipulation tasks by employing an automated pipeline for synthesizing diverse, physically realistic failure data. The model achieved a mean absolute error of 0.081 in dense reward prediction, surpassing existing models, and improved success rates in real-world robotic tasks by up to 40 percentage points.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning in robotics is hampered by sparse, binary reward signals, leading to credit assignment problems and sample inefficiency, especially in long-horizon tasks.
- Vision-language models (VLMs) used for reward prediction lack diverse and physically realistic failure trajectory data, as most training datasets are success-heavy and current 'pseudo-failure' generation methods are inadequate.
- Many existing reward models only provide sparse, trajectory-level feedback, offering insufficient guidance for intermediate actions and hindering efficient policy optimization.

## Method

- **DenseReward** utilizes an automated data generation pipeline that decomposes manipulation tasks into five ==canonical phases==, allowing for automated, dense, ==per-timestep reward labeling==.
- It employs a ==failure synthesis== mechanism that applies targeted perturbations to generate diverse, physically realistic failure trajectories (e.g., collisions, missed grasps, object falls) without manual annotation.
- A ==Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct==-based **DenseReward** model is trained on this generated dataset, finetuned to predict fine-grained scalar reward scores (0.000-1.000) for each timestep, representing task progress and failure modes.

## Results

- DenseReward achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of **0.081** in dense reward prediction, significantly outperforming general-purpose VLMs (MAE **0.289-0.293**) and existing robotic reward models (MAE **0.230-0.366**).
- In Model Predictive Control (MPC) tasks, DenseReward provided more effective guidance, resulting in the lowest average minimum distance (**0.229**) to the target compared to other reward models.
- Integrating DenseReward feedback into real-world online RL fine-tuning improved success rates by **30-40 percentage points** on challenging tasks (e.g., 'stack the cups' from **40%** to **80%**) with limited real-world rollouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automated synthesis of diverse, physically realistic failure trajectories is critical for training robust reward models that can accurately distinguish fine-grained task progress and various failure patterns.
> - Decomposing complex manipulation tasks into distinct canonical phases enables automated, dense reward assignment at each timestep, overcoming the challenges of manual annotation.
> - Dense, per-timestep reward signals provide superior guidance for robotic control and reinforcement learning compared to sparse, end-of-episode feedback, leading to more efficient learning and improved performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has achieved notable advancements through imitation learning (IL), leveraging large-scale demonstration datasets. However, reinforcement learning (RL) offers a complementary approach to surpass the performance ceilings imposed by demonstration data alone, allowing policies to improve through trial-and-error interactions. A significant bottleneck in the practical adoption of RL for robotics is the difficulty in obtaining reliable reward models that provide dense and informative feedback. Many existing RL approaches for robotics frequently rely on sparse binary rewards, typically provided only at the end of an episode, which leads to credit assignment problems and sample inefficiency, especially in long-horizon manipulation tasks.

Vision-language models (VLMs) have emerged as potential foundations for general-purpose robotic reward models, capitalizing on the broad semantic knowledge acquired during large-scale pretraining. Despite this potential, current VLM-based reward models exhibit two primary limitations. Firstly, they often require diverse failure trajectories to accurately learn failure modes and their underlying causes. However, most training datasets consist predominantly of successful demonstrations. Attempts to generate failure data, such as truncating successful episodes or constructing preference pairs from suboptimal rollouts, often produce "pseudo-failures" that do not fully capture the varied physical failure modes encountered during real robot execution, such as collisions, missed grasps, or object drops. Secondly, many existing reward models yield sparse, trajectory-level reward signals, providing a single label for an entire rollout. This sparsity limits the guidance available for policy optimization, as it offers insufficient feedback on the contributions of intermediate actions to overall success or failure.

DenseReward addresses these identified limitations by developing an automated pipeline for generating physically realistic failure data and by training a VLM to predict dense, per-timestep reward scores. This work positions itself as a development in creating more robust and informative reward signals, thereby enhancing the applicability and efficiency of reinforcement learning for complex robotic manipulation tasks, particularly in scenarios where diverse failure understanding and fine-grained progress feedback are crucial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop DenseReward, a dense vision-language reward model designed to provide fine-grained, per-timestep feedback for robotic manipulation tasks guided by vision and language instructions. This overall goal is motivated by two key challenges that impede the effective application of reinforcement learning in robotics:

1. **Acquiring Diverse Failure Data at Scale:**
 - **Challenge:** Reliable reward models require extensive and diverse data encompassing various failure modes to accurately distinguish between successful progress, partial completion, and different types of failures. Current methods for obtaining such data are largely inadequate. Human teleoperation for collecting failure trajectories is laborious and does not scale efficiently. Pseudo-failures, generated by modifying successful demonstrations (e.g., truncating successful episodes or relabeling suboptimal rollouts), fail to capture the physically realistic and diverse failure dynamics that occur during actual robot execution. These include scenarios like gripper collisions with the environment or objects, missed grasp attempts, or objects falling during transport.
 - **Motivation:** The inability of existing data generation methods to provide physically realistic and varied failure data prevents reward models from learning comprehensive representations of task success and failure. This limitation affects the robustness and accuracy of reward prediction, hindering effective policy optimization. Therefore, there is a need for an automated and scalable approach to synthesize diverse failure trajectories that reflect real-world physics.

2. **Obtaining Fine-Grained Reward Signals Beyond Sparse Trajectory-Level Success Labels:**
 - **Challenge:** Many existing reward models primarily provide sparse, binary (success/failure) or trajectory-level reward signals. Such sparse feedback only indicates the outcome at the end of an entire episode, offering limited guidance for intermediate actions. For long-horizon manipulation tasks, this results in severe credit assignment problems, where it is difficult for a reinforcement learning agent to discern which specific actions contributed positively or negatively to the final outcome. This makes policy learning sample-inefficient and challenging to scale.
 - **Motivation:** Efficient reinforcement learning requires dense, informative feedback that quantifies task progress at each timestep. Such fine-grained signals can guide the policy toward desired behaviors more effectively, enabling faster learning and better performance. The objective is to move beyond simple success/failure flags to a continuous reward signal that reflects incremental progress, degradation due to errors, and recovery from temporary failures.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to provide a practical path toward scalable reinforcement learning for robot manipulation. The development of DenseReward seeks to mitigate the dependency on laborious human annotation for failure data and to overcome the limitations of sparse reward signals, thereby facilitating more effective and efficient robotic policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DenseReward's methodology is structured around three core components: an automated data generation pipeline, a failure synthesis mechanism, and the training of the DenseReward model itself.

#### 3.1. Automated Data Generation

The first component focuses on creating a dataset with dense, per-timestep reward labels without manual annotation.

- **Dense Reward Formulation:** Unlike reward models that provide a single label per trajectory, DenseReward aims to learn a reward signal $r_t \in$ for every timestep $t$ of an episode.
- **Phase Decomposition:** To enable automated dense reward assignment, manipulation tasks are decomposed into five canonical phases:
 1. **Reach:** The robot moves its end-effector towards the target object.
 2. **Grasp:** The robot closes its gripper to secure the object.
 3. **Lift:** The robot raises the object off the surface.
 4. **Move:** The robot transports the object towards the target location.
 5. **Place:** The robot releases the object at the goal pose.
- **Automated Pipeline:** The generation process begins with randomly initializing a scene with an object and a container. GraspNet is used to predict multiple grasp pose candidates from multi-view RGB-D observations. CuRobo then performs collision-aware motion planning to select a feasible grasp. The robot executes a fixed sequence of six motion segments corresponding to the five manipulation phases. Phase boundaries are automatically detected using simulation state, for instance, Grasp starts on gripper-object contact, Lift begins when the object leaves the table, and Place starts when the end-effector enters a proximity radius of the target. This mechanism eliminates the need for human annotation for phase segmentation and initial reward labeling.

#### 3.2. Failure Synthesis

Building upon the automated generation, failure synthesis induces diverse, physically realistic failure trajectories.

- **Failure Modes:** Six distinct types of trajectories are defined and induced:
 1. **Success:** A complete and unperturbed execution where the object is successfully placed, with monotonically increasing reward.
 2. **Collision:** The robot collides with the environment or object. The reward increases until the collision, then decays.
 3. **Miss:** The gripper fails to grasp the object. Reward increases until the failed grasp, then decays.
 4. **Fall:** The object is successfully grasped and lifted but falls before reaching the target. Reward rises to a peak at the drop event, then decays.
 5. **Smooth:** Represents trajectories with suboptimal, inefficient motion, where the reward is scaled and penalized compared to precise execution.
 6. **Recover:** The robot encounters a collision but successfully recovers and completes the task. Reward drops during the collision, then resumes climbing after recovery.
- **Perturbations:** Specific perturbations are applied at different stages of the pipeline to induce these failure modes:
 - **Success:** No perturbations.
 - **Collision:** Motion planning is performed with collision avoidance disabled.
 - **Miss:** The grasp target pose is offset, causing the gripper to close without securing the object.
 - **Fall:** Random rotation perturbations are injected during the Move phase to destabilize and drop the object.
 - **Smooth:** Small Gaussian joint noise is added at each timestep to create a jittery, suboptimal trajectory.
 - **Recover:** An initial collision is induced, followed by replanning a clear path to task completion.
- **Validity Filtering:** Automated validity checks are applied at phase boundaries to filter out physically inconsistent or unintended failure outcomes (e.g., a "miss" perturbation accidentally resulting in a grasp), ensuring that the generated failure data accurately reflects the intended mode.

#### 3.3. DenseReward Training

The final component involves training the DenseReward model using the generated dataset.

- **Dataset:** A dataset of 27,000 episodes is constructed, comprising both successful and failure trajectories, with dense frame-level reward labels and failure mode annotations. The data integrates diverse simulated environments (RoboSuite, Isaac Sim) and real-world success/failure episodes (DROID), alongside trajectories from LIBERO, covering over 60 distinct manipulation objects and scenarios.
- **DenseReward Models:** The DenseReward model is built upon Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and finetuned. Given a language instruction, current visual observation, and historical frames, the model outputs a scalar reward score between 0.000 and 1.000. The training employs dense frame-level reward supervision, retaining three decimal places to enable the model to learn fine-grained distinctions in task progress. This design allows DenseReward to reason about the current execution state (e.g., correct progress, specific failure mode) and predict a precise estimate of task progress.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate DenseReward's capabilities in dense reward prediction and its utility in downstream robotic control and reinforcement learning tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluating Reward Prediction

- **Quantitative Results:** DenseReward achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.081 in dense reward prediction across all evaluated data sources (DROID, Isaac Sim, RoboSuite, LIBERO). This performance surpassed general-purpose vision-language models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, with MAEs of 0.289 and 0.293 respectively) and existing robotic reward models (e.g., RoboReward-4B/8B, with MAEs of 0.275 and 0.230 respectively, and Robometer with an MAE of 0.366).
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons indicated that DenseReward produced reward curves that more accurately aligned with task progress and exhibited less noise compared to baselines such as Molmo2-4B and RoboReward-8B. DenseReward demonstrated a greater capacity to capture fine-grained changes in progress throughout various success and failure trajectories.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in Model Predictive Control (MPC)

- **Setup:** DenseReward was evaluated for guiding actions within a sampling-based MPC framework on three object manipulation tasks (can, cup, lemon). At each step, 28 candidate actions were sampled and scored by the reward model, with the highest-scoring action being executed. The metric used was the minimum 3D distance between the robot end-effector and the target object, averaged over 10 episodes per task.
- **Results:** DenseReward achieved an average minimum distance of 0.229 across the three objects, which was the lowest among the tested models. It outperformed RoboReward-4B/8B (0.267 and 0.300 respectively) and VLAC-2B/8B (0.347 and 0.358 respectively), indicating that its predicted dense rewards provided more effective guidance for closed-loop action selection. Qualitative observations corroborated these findings, showing DenseReward leading to more consistent movement toward the target.

#### 4.3. Reinforcement Policy Learning with DenseReward (Simulated)

- **Setup:** DenseReward was integrated into online RL fine-tuning using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an initial π₀ policy on the LIBERO benchmark. DenseReward provided dense intermediate rewards, which were combined with the sparse simulator success signal.
- **Results:** The fine-tuning process with DenseReward led to improved success rates on the LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-10 suites compared to the sparse-reward PPO baseline. On LIBERO-Object, DenseReward achieved comparable final performance. These results suggest that the dense progress rewards provided by DenseReward can complement sparse task-completion signals, contributing to more competitive or improved learning curves in RL optimization.

#### 4.4. Policy Learning in the Real World

- **Setup:** Real-world online RL experiments were conducted using a DROID platform (Franka Research 3 arm) with DSRL to adapt a frozen π₀ policy. Two tasks were considered: "stack the cups" and "put ball in basket," both characterized by low initial success rates. Training was conducted with limited rollout budgets (approximately 10-20 real-world trajectories).
- **Results:** The integration of DenseReward feedback improved the success rate from 40% to 80% on "stack the cups" and from 30% to 70% on "put ball in basket." This indicates that DenseReward provides effective dense feedback for real-world policy learning, enabling substantial improvements to a base policy with a constrained number of real-world rollouts.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Generated Failure Data:** Training an ablated model without failure trajectories resulted in an increased MAE from 0.0809 to 0.1312. This suggests that failure trajectories are critical for learning fine-grained reward signals and accurately distinguishing incomplete or incorrect task progress from successful behavior.
- **Investigating Historical Frames:** The inclusion of temporal context via historical frames improved prediction accuracy. Using two historical frames yielded the lowest MAE (0.081), compared to one frame (0.088) or no frames (0.096). However, using three historical frames slightly increased the MAE to 0.086, suggesting a potential for diminishing returns or noise from excessive history.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DenseReward research presents several contributions with potential impact on robotic manipulation and reinforcement learning:

- **Addressing Data and Reward Sparsity Bottlenecks:** The work directly addresses two fundamental challenges in applying reinforcement learning to robotics: the scarcity of diverse failure data and the limitations of sparse reward signals. By providing an automated method for generating realistic failure trajectories and yielding dense, fine-grained reward signals, DenseReward mitigates these long-standing bottlenecks.
- **Automated and Scalable Failure Data Generation:** The proposed automated pipeline for synthesizing physically realistic failure trajectories, without requiring human labeling, offers a scalable approach to creating comprehensive datasets. This bypasses the laborious and time-consuming nature of manual data collection for failures, which often fails to capture the full spectrum of error modes. This mechanism could significantly accelerate research and development in robot learning by providing richer training data.
- **Improved Reward Modeling Performance:** DenseReward demonstrates superior performance in dense reward prediction compared to both general-purpose vision-language models and existing robotic reward models. This indicates a more accurate and nuanced understanding of task progress, partial completion, and various failure patterns, which is critical for effective learning.
- **Enhanced Downstream Robotic Applications:** The effectiveness of DenseReward in guiding Model Predictive Control (MPC) and improving Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies, in both simulated and real-world environments, suggests its practical utility. Its ability to enable policy improvement with limited real-world rollouts is particularly impactful for real-world robotics, where data collection is expensive and time-consuming.
- **Facilitating Scalable Reinforcement Learning:** By offering reliable, dense feedback, DenseReward contributes to making reinforcement learning more sample-efficient and robust for complex robot manipulation tasks. This work posits that failure-aware dense reward modeling is a viable path towards developing more capable and adaptable robot policies.
- **Resource Contribution to the Community:** The release of the dataset, trained reward models, and evaluation suite provides valuable resources for the research community, potentially fostering further innovation and benchmarking in failure-aware dense reward modeling for robot learning.

The findings suggest that DenseReward could significantly advance the capabilities of robots in understanding and executing manipulation tasks by providing a more informative and robust learning signal, thereby moving towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
