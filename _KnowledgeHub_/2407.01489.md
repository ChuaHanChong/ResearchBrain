# Agentless: Demystifying LLM-based Software Engineering Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentless resolves software issues with a fixed three-phase pipeline of hierarchical localization, multi-sample diff repair, and test-based patch selection. The LLM never chooses actions or calls tools. With GPT-4o it resolves 32.00% of SWE-bench Lite at $0.70 per issue, the best open-source result at the time, and the authors also release a cleaned subset, SWE-bench Lite-S.

## Problem

- Agent-based systems need hand-designed tool abstractions, and imprecise tool specifications or misused calls waste LLM queries and lower performance across the many iterative turns.
- Delegating action choice to the LLM gives little control. Agents often need 30 to 40 turns, wander into sub-optimal exploration, and are hard to interpret or debug.
- Current LLM agents reflect poorly on feedback. They cannot filter misleading information, so one wrong step compounds through later decisions, while SWE-bench Lite contains flawed problems that distort evaluation.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical localization== narrows from files to classes and functions to edit lines. It combines LLM prompting over a repository tree with ==embedding retrieval==, and uses compact ==skeleton views== of files.
- Repair samples 40 candidate patches, 10 for each of 4 sampled edit-location sets. Patches use a compact ==Search/Replace diff format== rather than regenerating whole code.
- ==Patch validation== generates LLM ==reproduction tests==, keeps those that reproduce the issue, and runs them with ==regression tests==. Survivors are ==normalized by AST== and ranked by ==majority vote==.

## Results

- On SWE-bench Lite with GPT-4o, Agentless fixes **96 of 300** issues (**32.00%**) at **$0.70** average cost. This is the highest among open-source tools, ahead of AutoCodeRover-v2 at **30.67%**.
- Hierarchical localization improves file-level recall from **78.67%** (prompting only) to **81.67%**. Skeleton format beats full files (**58.33%** vs **53.67%**) at **$0.02** vs **$0.15**. Adding reproduction tests lifts fixes from **81** to **96**.
- Manual analysis finds **4.3%** of issues contain the exact patch, **5.0%** are misleading and **10.0%** lack information. Removing them gives the **249**-problem Lite-S, where Agentless scores **33.73%** and ranks **9th**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simple, fixed, interpretable pipeline can match complex autonomous agents at lower cost. Add agent autonomy only where it clearly beats decomposing the task into staged steps.
> - Coarse-to-fine context reduction helps both accuracy and cost. Compact representations such as file skeletons and small diffs beat feeding long raw context to LLMs.
> - Sampling many candidates and filtering by executable checks helps, but generated tests are noisy (94 of 213 correct). Selection quality caps the gain, with an oracle upper bound of 42.0% vs 32.00%.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have profoundly influenced the field of software engineering, leading to unprecedented automation capabilities in tasks like code synthesis, program repair, and test generation. Initially, LLMs were primarily applied to more self-contained coding problems. However, real-world software development tasks, such as fixing bugs in large repositories or adding complex features, demand an understanding of vast codebases and inter-file dependencies, which is challenging for LLMs due to their limited context windows.

This challenge has spurred the development of **LLM-based software engineering agents**. Inspired by systems like Devin AI Software Engineer, these "autonomous LLM agents" represent a significant trend. They are designed to mimic human developers by iteratively performing actions, using tools (e.g., file editors, terminals, web search), observing environmental feedback, and planning future steps. Prominent examples include SWE-agent, Aider, Moatless, AutoCodeRover, and SpecRover, alongside numerous commercial counterparts. These agents aim for end-to-end problem-solving on complex, repository-level tasks, exemplified by benchmarks like SWE-bench.

However, the authors of AGENTLESS highlight several critical limitations of these agent-based approaches:
- **Complex Tool Usage/Design:** The abstraction layers for tool interaction require meticulous design and can lead to incorrect or imprecise tool usage, compounding errors due to their iterative nature.
- **Lack of Control in Decision Planning:** Delegating autonomous decision-making to LLMs, which might struggle with large action spaces and sub-optimal explorations, makes debugging difficult and prone to confusion.
- **Limited Ability to Self-Reflect:** Current LLMs struggle to filter out irrelevant or misleading information, leading to error amplification.

"AGENTLESS" directly challenges this prevailing trend of increasing complexity. It positions itself as a counter-narrative, proposing that a simpler, "agentless" approach can achieve competitive, or even superior, performance without the overhead and pitfalls of complex autonomous agents. By demystifying the necessity of complex agents, this work aims to "reset the baseline, starting point, and horizon for autonomous software agents," suggesting that the community might be over-engineering solutions when simpler, more structured approaches could be more effective and cost-efficient. The paper also contributes to the broader discussion on the quality and representativeness of software engineering benchmarks, specifically SWE-bench Lite, by proposing a refined version (SWE-bench Lite-S) and influencing the creation of SWE-bench Verified by OpenAI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind AGENTLESS stems from a critical, introspective question posed by the authors: **"Do we really have to employ complex autonomous software agents?"** This question arises from observing the inherent complexities and limitations of current LLM agent-based approaches in handling real-world software development tasks. Despite the perceived natural fit between human iterative problem-solving and LLM agents, the disparities in capabilities (e.g., precise tool usage, robust planning, self-reflection) lead to significant challenges.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the AGENTLESS research are:

1. **To develop and evaluate a simplistic "agentless" approach for automated software development:** The primary goal is to demonstrate that a straightforward, three-phase process—localization, repair, and patch validation—without autonomous LLM decision-making or complex tool interaction, can effectively resolve software development issues.
2. **To empirically challenge the necessity of complex LLM agents:** By building and rigorously evaluating AGENTLESS against state-of-the-art agent-based systems on a popular benchmark (SWE-bench Lite), the authors aim to provide compelling evidence that simpler designs can yield comparable or even superior results at a lower cost.
3. **To demystify the performance of LLM-based software engineering solutions:** The work seeks to provide clarity on what truly drives success in automated software development using LLMs, suggesting that well-structured, modular pipelines might be more effective than fully autonomous, complex agents.
4. **To analyze and improve the quality of existing benchmarks:** Recognizing potential issues and biases in SWE-bench Lite, a key objective is to manually classify problems and create a more rigorous subset (SWE-bench Lite-S) for more accurate evaluation of LLM capabilities. This aims to foster more meaningful comparisons in the community.
5. **To inspire future research in simpler, cost-effective autonomous software development:** The authors hope that AGENTLESS will serve as a new baseline, redirecting research efforts towards overlooked potential in less complex, more interpretable, and economically viable solutions.

In essence, the paper is motivated by a desire to bring simplicity, interpretability, and cost-effectiveness back into the forefront of autonomous software development research, advocating for a more pragmatic approach to integrating LLMs into the software engineering workflow.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AGENTLESS employs a deliberate "agentless" design, structured into three distinct and sequential phases: **Localization, Repair, and Patch Validation**. Unlike agent-based systems, the LLM in AGENTLESS does not autonomously decide future actions or operate with complex tools; instead, it is prompted to perform specific, well-defined tasks within each phase.

#### 3.1. Localization Phase

The objective of localization is to pinpoint the exact locations in the codebase that require modification. AGENTLESS uses a hierarchical, three-step process to efficiently narrow down potential bug sites:

1. **Localize to Suspicious Files:**
 - **Prompting-based:** AGENTLESS first constructs a concise "repository structure format" (similar to `ls -R` or `tree`) from the entire codebase, showing file and directory hierarchy. This structure, along with the issue description, is provided to the LLM, which is then prompted to identify a list of top-N suspicious files.
 - **Embedding-based Retrieval:** Complementing the prompting, an embedding-based retrieval method is used. First, the LLM identifies and filters "irrelevant folders" from the repository. Then, remaining files are chunked, and embeddings are computed for each chunk using `text-embedding-3-small`. The issue description is also embedded, and cosine similarity is used to retrieve relevant files.
 - **Combination:** The files identified by both methods are combined, selecting the top N most common files to form the final list of suspicious files.
2. **Localize to Related Elements:** For each suspicious file, AGENTLESS generates a "skeleton format," which includes only class, function, and variable declarations (signatures only, with class fields/methods and module-level comments). This condensed representation is provided to the LLM, prompting it to identify relevant classes and functions within these files that might need examination. This reduces context window size.
3. **Localize to Edit Locations:** Finally, the full code content of the identified relevant elements (from potentially different files) is provided to the LLM. The LLM then identifies the precise "edit locations," specified by line numbers, functions, or classes. AGENTLESS samples multiple sets of edit locations to maximize the chances of finding the correct ground truth.

#### 3.2. Repair Phase

Once the fine-grained edit locations are identified, the repair phase focuses on generating patches:

- **Context Window Construction:** For each identified edit location, a context window (e.g., ±10 lines around the location) is constructed. If multiple locations are identified, their context windows are concatenated, separated by "..." to indicate missing content.
- **Patch Generation:** The LLM (GPT-4o) is prompted to generate multiple candidate patches for each issue. Crucially, AGENTLESS asks the LLM to generate patches in a **Search/Replace diff format**. This format specifies the original code snippet to be replaced and the replacement snippet. This approach is more cost-efficient and reliable than generating full code, reducing hallucination. AGENTLESS generates up to 40 patches per bug (4 sampled edit location sets * 10 patches per set, including greedy and sampled).

#### 3.3. Patch Validation Phase

This phase is critical for selecting the best patch from the multiple candidates generated:

1. **Reproduction Test Generation:**
 - Since SWE-bench problems only provide regression tests (not bug-triggering tests), AGENTLESS leverages the LLM to synthesize **reproduction tests**. These tests are designed to first replicate the original issue and then verify if a fix has resolved it.
 - Multiple candidate reproduction tests are sampled. They are executed on the *original codebase* to filter for those that successfully reproduce the bug.
 - The most frequently occurring (normalized) successful reproduction test is selected as the final reproduction test for the issue.
2. **Patch Selection:**
 - **Regression Testing:** AGENTLESS first identifies a set of valid regression tests by running all existing tests on the original codebase and then prompting the LLM to identify any non-regression tests that should be excluded (as their expected behavior might change with the fix). Candidate patches are run against this regression test suite, keeping only those with the lowest number of regression failures.
 - **Reproduction Testing:** For the remaining patches, the selected reproduction test is run. Only patches that successfully pass the reproduction test (i.e., indicate the issue is resolved) are kept. If no patch passes the reproduction test, AGENTLESS falls back to only using regression test results.
 - **Majority Voting:** Patches are normalized (removing superficial differences like whitespace) by parsing them into Abstract Syntax Trees and unparsing them into a canonical format. The patch with the highest number of occurrences (after normalization and filtering) is selected as the final submission patch.

This structured, step-by-step approach, while leveraging LLMs for individual tasks, avoids the complexity of autonomous agents, making AGENTLESS interpretable, straightforward, and cost-effective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AGENTLESS primarily focuses on the SWE-bench Lite benchmark, with additional analysis on custom and OpenAI-verified benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on SWE-bench Lite

- **Highest Open-Source Performance:** AGENTLESS successfully resolves 96 out of 300 problems (32.00%) on SWE-bench Lite. This makes it the *highest-performing open-source approach* compared to all existing open-source agent-based tools evaluated.
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** AGENTLESS achieves this performance at an average cost of only $0.70 per problem, which is significantly lower than most prior agent-based approaches. While slightly more expensive than simple RAG baselines, AGENTLESS solves substantially more issues.
- **Competitiveness with Closed-Source:** Although some closed-source/commercial tools achieve higher solve rates (e.g., CodeStory Aide at 43.00%), AGENTLESS remains highly competitive, especially considering its much simpler and transparent design. Many top closed-source solutions do not release their methodology or trajectories, making direct comparison difficult.

#### 4.2. Unique Issues Fixed

- **Complementary Fixes:** AGENTLESS demonstrates the ability to fix unique issues not resolved by other top open-source tools (2 unique fixes) and even some high-performing commercial approaches (1 unique fix). This highlights its complementary nature and the value of its distinct, simplistic approach.
- **Influence on Other Tools:** The paper notes that some other successful tools (e.g., Isoform, RepoGraph, Bytedance MarsCode) are built upon or partly inspired by earlier versions of AGENTLESS, which might partially explain the lower number of unique fixes compared to some commercial solutions that have incorporated its ideas.

#### 4.3. Localization Performance

- **Competitive Accuracy:** AGENTLESS's hierarchical localization method proves competitive in identifying correct edit locations. The percentage of patches with correct locations (35.3% line, 52.0% function, 69.7% file) correlates well with its overall solve rate, demonstrating the effectiveness of its phased localization strategy.

#### 4.4. Reproduction Test Quality

- **Reproducibility vs. Verifiability:** AGENTLESS generates reproduction tests that successfully reproduce the original issue in 213 out of 300 problems. However, only 94 of these tests correctly verify the fix when applied to the ground truth patch. This indicates a challenge in generating comprehensive tests solely from issue descriptions, which may lack sufficient information.
- **Mitigation Strategy:** AGENTLESS's conservative patch selection (prioritizing regression tests first and only using reproduction tests if regression tests pass) helps mitigate the risk of incorrect reproduction tests selecting sub-optimal patches.

#### 4.5. Adoption and Influence

- **OpenAI Adoption:** A significant finding is the adoption of AGENTLESS by OpenAI as the "go-to approach" for showcasing the real-world coding performance of both GPT-4o and the new OpenAI o1 models on their new SWE-bench Verified benchmark. This demonstrates strong industry validation and confidence in the approach.
- **Inspiration for Others:** As mentioned, other open-source (RepoGraph) and closed-source (Isoform, Bytedance MarsCode) tools have been built upon or inspired by AGENTLESS.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study Insights

- **Localization:** Hierarchical localization significantly reduces context window size and improves performance compared to direct approaches or full file content. The combination of prompting and embedding-based retrieval for file localization is superior to either method alone. Irrelevant folder filtering for embedding-based retrieval is crucial for cost and performance.
- **Repair:** Sampling multiple edit locations (4 sets) and generating multiple patches (10 per set, total 40) significantly improves repair performance (from 80 fixes with greedy sampling to 96). The study reveals a high upper bound (126 fixes or 42.0%) if all generated patches were considered, highlighting the potential for better patch re-ranking.
- **Patch Validation:** Each step in patch validation incrementally improves performance. Majority voting alone achieves 77 fixes, adding regression tests improves to 81, and incorporating the LLM-generated reproduction tests boosts performance to 96 fixes, demonstrating the critical role of these validation steps despite their additional cost.

#### 4.7. Dataset Analysis and SWE-bench Lite-S / Verified

- **Problematic Issues in SWE-bench Lite:** Manual classification of SWE-bench Lite problems revealed significant biases: 4.3% contained exact ground truth patches, 9.7% contained complete natural language steps for the solution, 5.0% contained misleading solutions, and 10.0% lacked sufficient information to solve the issue.
- **SWE-bench Lite-S and SWE-bench Verified:** Based on this analysis, the authors constructed **SWE-bench Lite-S** (249 problems) by excluding these problematic issues, offering a more rigorous benchmark. OpenAI independently confirmed these issues and released **SWE-bench Verified**, which is a human-validated, filtered dataset. AGENTLESS maintains strong performance on both Lite-S (33.73%) and Verified (38.80%), performing best among GPT-4o-based solutions on Verified.
- **Performance by Problem Category:**
 - **Description Quality:** AGENTLESS performs well on problems with reproducible code examples, leveraging its reproduction test generation. Other agent-based approaches surprisingly show a drop.
 - **Solution in Description:** All tools perform better when solution steps are provided in natural language.
 - **Location Information:** Highest solve rates are for problems with location clues (natural language, stack trace). AGENTLESS is competitive when clues are present, but closed-source agent tools show an edge when no location clue is provided, indicating an area for future improvement for AGENTLESS in complex code search.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "AGENTLESS" carries substantial significance and holds the potential to significantly impact the trajectory of research and development in LLM-based software engineering automation.

1. **Challenges the Orthodoxy of Complexity:** The most profound impact of AGENTLESS is its direct and empirically supported challenge to the prevailing trend of developing increasingly complex, autonomous LLM agents for software development. By demonstrating that a simpler, cost-effective, and interpretable "agentless" approach can achieve state-of-the-art performance (especially among open-source solutions), the paper forces a re-evaluation of current research priorities. It suggests that the community might be over-engineering solutions and that carefully designed, modular pipelines can outperform complex, unconstrained autonomy given the current limitations of LLMs.

2. **Establishes a New Baseline:** AGENTLESS sets a strong, new baseline for automated software development. Its performance, coupled with its simplicity and low cost, makes it an attractive starting point for future research. Researchers can now compare new agent designs not just against other agents, but against a highly effective non-agentic system, potentially leading to more focused and efficient advancements.

3. **Enhances Interpretability and Debuggability:** The well-defined, three-phase structure of AGENTLESS (localization, repair, validation) inherently leads to greater interpretability compared to opaque, iterative agent trajectories. This modularity makes it easier to understand where the system succeeds or fails, facilitating debugging and targeted improvements.

4. **Cost-Effectiveness and Practicality:** By achieving high performance at a fraction of the cost of many agent-based systems, AGENTLESS highlights the practical viability of LLM-based automation for real-world scenarios. Its emphasis on concise diff formats for patches and hierarchical context reduction contributes to economic efficiency, making advanced automation more accessible.

5. **Catalyst for Benchmark Improvement:** The meticulous manual classification of SWE-bench Lite problems and the subsequent creation of SWE-bench Lite-S have already had a significant impact on the benchmarking landscape. This work exposed critical biases and inconsistencies in a widely used dataset, leading to the development and adoption of more rigorous evaluation standards, as exemplified by OpenAI's SWE-bench Verified. This initiative promotes more accurate and fair comparisons of LLM capabilities in software engineering.

6. **Industry Validation and Adoption:** The adoption of AGENTLESS by OpenAI as the default approach for showcasing the real-world coding performance of their cutting-edge models (GPT-4o and o1) is a powerful testament to its practical utility and robustness. This level of industry endorsement underscores the paper's immediate relevance and influence beyond academic circles.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies clear avenues for future work, such as improving code search for problems without explicit location clues (where current agents still hold an advantage) and developing more sophisticated patch re-ranking and selection techniques to leverage the high "upper bound" of correct patches generated by AGENTLESS.

In conclusion, "AGENTLESS" is a pivotal work that not only introduces a highly effective, simple, and cost-efficient approach to LLM-based software engineering automation but also critically re-evaluates the foundational assumptions driving much of the current research in this rapidly evolving field. It advocates for an approach that prioritizes structure and clarity, demonstrating that sometimes, less complexity yields more impact.
