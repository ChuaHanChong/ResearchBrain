# Just-In-Time Scene Graph Growth: Combating Perceptual Saturation in Long-Horizon Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> The JITOMA framework introduces a "just-in-time (JIT) scene graph growth" paradigm that addresses perceptual saturation in 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) for embodied agents. It dynamically activates detailed semantic processing only for task-relevant information, achieving improved task grounding accuracy and reducing active graph size compared to traditional methods, with up to 21 times faster processing than a caption-heavy baseline.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) construction methods operate "Ahead-of-Time" (AOT), exhaustively mapping and semantically expanding entire environments regardless of immediate task relevance.
- This AOT approach causes "perceptual saturation," leading to observation redundancy, increased semantic noise for reasoning components like LLMs, and high computational latency on resource-constrained edge platforms.
- Current "task-driven" frameworks primarily filter already over-semanticized graphs retrospectively, failing to proactively manage resource allocation during the initial graph construction process.

## Method

- The **JITOMA** (Just-In-Time On-demand Memory Activation) framework implements a ==just-in-time (JIT) scene graph growth== paradigm, integrating task reasoning, perception, and memory into a demand-driven process.
- It maintains a ==two-tier memory== system: ephemeral short-term hypotheses for transient observations and stable, under-specified ==dormant anchors== for the global environment.
- Task intent directly governs upstream perception and memory activation, triggering computationally expensive semantic operations (e.g., ==dense captioning==, ==functional inference==) only on a small, task-relevant subset of dormant anchors.

## Results

- JITOMA demonstrated robust grounding accuracy, achieving the best mR@1 and mR@3 scores across all evaluation tiers, including average gains of **5.0 IoU**, **9.4 mR@1**, and **7.3 mR@3** in long-horizon scenarios.
- The framework effectively bounded active graph dynamics, maintaining only **1-4** active objects for most tasks (up to **12** for complex tasks) compared to hundreds or thousands of active nodes in baseline methods.
- JITOMA maintained a stable Time Per Frame of **0.56-0.63 seconds** across various scenes and tiers, outperforming caption-heavy baselines by **9-21 times** in efficiency while providing substantially richer explicit semantics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maximizing global 3DSG accuracy does not necessarily translate to superior robotic execution; it can degrade success by causing "cognitive myopia" in LLM planners due to overwhelming irrelevant information.
> - A resource-conscious gating mechanism, inspired by human selective attention, can effectively prevent perceptual saturation by only activating fine-grained semantic processing for task-relevant regions.
> - Decoupling total accumulated environmental memory from active cognitive demand is crucial for scalable and efficient real-time operation in long-horizon tasks on resource-constrained platforms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence increasingly relies on 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) to provide structured representations of 3D environments, enabling agents to perceive, memorize, and reason effectively. Historically, 3DSG frameworks have progressed significantly, moving from early methods focused on extracting closed-set semantics to more recent advancements incorporating open-vocabulary concepts, driven by Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This evolution has further led to the development of task-driven and functional graphs, aiming to align graph construction and representation with downstream robotic tasks such as navigation and manipulation.

However, a fundamental limitation persists within most existing 3DSG construction pipelines: they operate under an "Ahead-of-Time (AOT)" or "build-everything-then-filter" paradigm. This approach entails exhaustively mapping and semantically expanding the entire environment regardless of immediate task relevance, under the implicit assumption of unbounded computational resources or post-exploration processing. This unconstrained accumulation of information leads to "perceptual saturation," characterized by severe observation redundancy, increased semantic noise in cognitive contexts for Large Language Models (LLMs), and unsustainable latencies, particularly for real-time applications on edge platforms. Even contemporary "task-driven" frameworks, while focusing on task-relevant information, often act as retrospective filters on an already over-semanticized graph rather than proactively managing resource allocation during the construction process.

This paper addresses this conceptual blind spot by proposing "just-in-time (JIT) scene graph growth" as an alternative paradigm. Inspired by human cognitive mechanisms, which selectively focus attention based on current intent, and by Just-In-Time compilation in software engineering, this work re-conceptualizes mapping as a process-oriented resource management problem along the temporal axis. It departs from the passive accumulation of massive global structures by ensuring that task-irrelevant environmental details remain dormant from the outset.

The proposed JITOMA framework is positioned as a closed-loop system that unifies task reasoning, perception, and memory into a single, demand-driven growth process. This differentiates it from prior attempts like FOUND-IT, which, while deferring explicit 3D instance extraction, still decouples the perception phase from active task reasoning. JITOMA aims to allow immediate task intent to proactively govern upstream perception, thereby eliminating observation redundancy at an architectural level and dynamically growing and distilling the subgraph strictly on-demand. This work contributes to the broader research landscape by offering a novel, resource-conscious approach to 3DSG construction that is designed for efficiency and real-time performance in long-horizon robotic tasks.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the issue of "perceptual saturation" in 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) construction for embodied agents operating in real-time, long-horizon scenarios on resource-constrained edge platforms. This saturation arises from conventional "Ahead-of-Time (AOT)" or "build-everything-then-filter" pipelines that exhaustively process and store semantic information about an entire environment, regardless of its immediate relevance to an ongoing task.

The specific motivations for developing the Just-In-Time On-demand Memory Activation (JITOMA) framework and its associated "just-in-time (JIT) scene graph growth" paradigm are multifaceted:

1. **Addressing Perceptual Saturation:** Existing AOT methods generate substantial observation redundancy and semantic noise. This noise overwhelms downstream reasoning components, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), leading to "cognitive myopia" where agents struggle to identify crucial information amidst irrelevant data. The paper empirically demonstrates this in an exploratory experiment (Section 3). When an LLM planner is given a command ("pack my eyeglasses for travel") with a full 3D scene graph of 18 objects (15 distractors), it fails to complete the full multi-step plan, omitting a critical final step. In contrast, providing only the 3 task-relevant objects via a JIT subgraph enables the same LLM to produce a complete and correct execution sequence. This finding confirms that maximizing global scene graph accuracy does not necessarily translate to superior downstream robotic execution; instead, it can degrade success.

2. **Mitigating Computational Latency and Memory Footprints:** The exhaustive nature of AOT pipelines incurs unsustainable latencies and large memory demands, making them impractical for real-time operations on computationally limited edge devices. The objective is to devise a method that significantly reduces active graph size and captioning latency while maintaining stable processing times, especially during dynamic task switching. This involves scaling computational expense with current cognitive demand rather than with the total accumulated environmental knowledge.

3. **Enhancing Task-Driven Efficiency:** While some existing methods are "task-driven," they primarily use tasks as retrospective filters or post-hoc aggregators. This means the underlying construction process remains blind to online resource allocation. A core objective of JITOMA is to establish a truly closed-loop system where task intent proactively governs upstream perception. This architectural shift aims to eliminate observation redundancy at its source, rather than attempting to filter it out later.

4. **Inspiration from Cognitive Principles:** The framework is explicitly inspired by human cognition. Humans do not exhaustively process and memorize every detail of an unfamiliar environment. Instead, they form lightweight impressions and only allocate resource-intensive operations (e.g., fine-grained detail resolution) to specific regions when a concrete intent emerges. JITOMA seeks to emulate this resource-conscious gating mechanism by maintaining a sparse, low-cost memory foundation and activating detailed processing only "just-in-time" upon a cognitive query.

By addressing these motivations, the paper aims to provide a foundational paradigm shift for 3DSG construction, moving from an unconstrained, build-everything approach to a highly selective, non-linear growth process where resource deployment is authorized exclusively on-demand. This objective is to create more efficient, robust, and scalable embodied agents capable of operating effectively in complex, dynamic, and long-horizon environments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The JITOMA (Just-In-Time On-demand Memory Activation) framework proposes a closed-loop approach that integrates task reasoning, perception, and memory into a just-in-time growth process for 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs). Its core principle is that a robot should only instantiate the detailed semantic information that a task immediately requires, rather than exhaustively mapping the entire environment.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Architecture Overview

JITOMA is designed for online 3DSG construction under long-horizon embodied execution. It manages a two-tier memory state at any given time `t`: ephemeral short-term hypotheses (`V_eph_t`) and long-lived dormant anchors (`V_anc_t`). Ephemeral hypotheses store transient observations, while dormant anchors are stable, under-specified visual impressions. Each dormant anchor `v_j` stores existence, coarse geometry, representative image crops, and a lightweight visual embedding (e.g., CLIP image embedding `z_j`), but crucially, it does not initially contain dense captions or functional subnodes.
When a natural-language command `q` is received, JITOMA retrieves and activates a subset of dormant anchors (`V_act(q)`). Only these activated anchors trigger computationally expensive semantic operations, such as dense node captioning and functional relational inference, within a dynamically grown, task-specific functional subgraph (`G_+(q)`). After task execution, reusable semantic products are distilled back into the corresponding dormant anchors, and temporary JIT products are discarded. This approach ensures that graph complexity scales with active cognitive demand rather than monotonically with exploration time.

#### 3.2. Intent Parsing and Frontend Just-In-Time Perception

Unlike traditional methods that filter pre-built graphs, JITOMA's task intent directly controls which observations are admitted into memory.
1. **Intent Parsing:** An LLM-based parser analyzes the command `q` to extract a structured concept set `C(q)`. This set comprises primary objects explicitly mentioned (`C_pri(q)`) and latent objects inferred as necessary for task completion (`C_lat(q)`), such as tools, containers, or destinations. For example, "clean the cup" yields {cup} as primary and {water faucet, sponge} as latent.
2. **Task-aware Observation Gating:** At each timestamp `t`, a class-agnostic segmentation tracker processes raw RGB-D input into object tracks `O_t`. The parsed concepts `C(q)` are then used by a vision-language alignment model to generate a top-down task heatmap `H_q_t` over the current image. This heatmap is applied to the object masks within `O_t` to calculate relevance scores (`s_obs_t,i(q)`). Only the top-K most relevant observations (`e_O_t(q)`) are admitted into the ephemeral memory (`V_eph_t`). This prevents redundant observations from being committed to memory.
3. **Task-Free Conservative Writing:** When no specific task is active, JITOMA operates in a conservative mode. It admits newly observed tracks but throttles repeated observations of already known regions. An observation is re-written only if it provides a sufficiently novel viewpoint or refines the geometric estimate of an existing anchor. This strategy maintains a global foundation for future queries while avoiding the over-accumulation seen in AOT pipelines.

#### 3.3. Dormant Memory Foundation

JITOMA's memory foundation is organized into two stages to separate long-term spatial awareness from active semantic reasoning:
1. **Ephemeral Hypotheses:** These are short-term buffers (`V_eph_t`) that accumulate lightweight geometric and visual evidence over a local temporal window. They remain semantically dormant. Hypotheses that do not meet stability checks are discarded.
2. **Dormant Anchors:** Stable ephemeral hypotheses are promoted to dormant anchors (`V_anc_t`). During promotion, a representative crop `¯x_j` is selected, and a lightweight visual key `z_j` (CLIP embedding) is computed. This `z_j` allows for efficient retrieval later. Anchors remain semantically dormant, storing only low-cost geometry, crops, and the CLIP embedding. Dense captions and functional subnodes are computed only upon task activation.

#### 3.4. On-Demand Memory Activation

Upon receiving a command `q`, JITOMA activates memory in a two-stage retrieval process:
1. **Lightweight Candidate Retrieval:** For each parsed concept `c` from `C(q)`, dormant anchors `v_j` are ranked based on the cosine similarity between the concept's CLIP text embedding (`CLIP_text(c)`) and the anchor's visual key (`z_j`), augmented with accumulated task-heatmap evidence (`h_c_j`). The top-3 candidates (`B(c)`) are retrieved.
2. **Dense Semantic Processing and Reranking:** Only the representative crops `¯x_j` of these few candidates in `B(c)` undergo expensive batched DAM captioning (`DAM_cap`) to generate detailed object descriptions `d_j`. An LLM reranker then evaluates the command `q`, concept `c`, and candidate-caption pairs (`(v_j, d_j)`) to select the single best anchor `v_*(c)` for each concept. These selected anchors form the activated set `V_act(q)`. Unselected candidates revert to their dormant state. This mechanism ensures expensive semantic processing is limited to a small, task-relevant subset of memory.

#### 3.5. Task-Conditioned Subgraph Growth

Once `V_act(q)` contains the necessary primary and latent objects, JITOMA grows them into functional graph nodes:
1. For each activated anchor `v_j`, its crop `¯x_j`, command `q`, and object caption `d_j` are input to a VLM.
2. The VLM predicts the task-relevant interactive part (`ℓ_q_j`), its 2D grounding mask (`m_q_j`), and its 3D bounding box (`b_q_j`).
3. Each localized part `p_q_j` becomes a functional subnode, which is then attached to its object anchor `v_j` with an edge `(v_j, p_q_j)`.
The resulting `G_+(q)` is the just-in-time grown subgraph, containing only the object and functional nodes essential for the current task's execution.

#### 3.6. Subgraph Distillation and Operating Modes

After `G_+(q)` is used for task execution, JITOMA collapses the active query overlay:
1. **Distillation:** Reusable semantic products (object captions `d_j` and functional subnodes `p_q_j`) are distilled and stored as dormant auxiliary attributes (`A_j`) within their respective anchors `v_j`. This information can be reused if the anchor is reactivated later.
2. **Discarding:** All other temporary JIT byproducts (heatmaps, masks, reranking candidates, temporary edges, active overlays) are discarded.
JITOMA supports three operating modes: task-free conservative writing, single-query just-in-time activation (with subgraph collapse post-execution), and concurrent query layers (where multiple active overlays share the same dormant anchor foundation).

#### 3.7. JITOMA-Bench Dataset and Benchmark

To evaluate these dynamic process-level mapping capabilities, the authors introduce JITOMA-Bench, which extends Clio-Bench. It addresses three gaps in existing benchmarks:
- **(G1) Peak Resource Dynamics:** Tracks maximum active graph size and computation over time, not just final states.
- **(G2) Long-Horizon Multi-Tasking:** Evaluates continuous adaptation under sequential task streams within the same scene.
- **(G3) Complex Intent Grounding:** Includes commands requiring multiple primary and latent objects.
JITOMA-Bench consists of three tiers:
- **Tier 1: Foundational Grounding:** Evaluates explicit single-ground truth (GT) tasks independently.
- **Tier 2: Long-Horizon Dynamics:** Evaluates continual adaptation to an evolving task stream.
- **Tier 3: Complex Cognitive Reasoning:** Evaluates complex instructions requiring multiple explicit and inferred latent objects.
Evaluation metrics include grounding accuracy (IoU, mR@K) and system efficiency (active object node count (Objs), peak active object-node count (Peak), and Time Per Frame (TPF)).

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted on JITOMA-Bench aimed to evaluate JITOMA's ability to mitigate perceptual saturation, bound active graph size, and maintain stable real-time performance. The results are summarized in Table 1 across three scenes (Apartment, Office, Cubicle) and three evaluation tiers, comparing JITOMA against ConceptGraphs, ReasoningGraph, and Clio.

#### 4.1. Grounding Accuracy and Cross-Tier Robustness (Q1)

JITOMA demonstrated strong and robust grounding accuracy across all three evaluation tiers:
- **Tier 1 (Foundational Grounding):** JITOMA achieved the best mR@1 and mR@3 scores in all three scenes and the best IoU in the Apartment and Office scenes.
- **Tier 2 (Long-Horizon Dynamics):** Crucially, JITOMA's performance did not degrade when transitioning from isolated tasks in Tier 1 to a continuous stream of tasks in Tier 2. Across all three scenes, JITOMA showed average gains of 5.0 IoU, 9.4 mR@1, and 7.3 mR@3. This indicates robust grounding under continuous task switching.
- **Tier 3 (Complex Cognitive Reasoning):** JITOMA consistently achieved the highest IoU, mR@1, and mR@3 in every scene for instructions involving multiple primary and latent objects. Even when baselines were augmented with JITOMA's LLM task parser (to separate parsing errors from graph retrieval), JITOMA maintained a substantial advantage. This suggests that JITOMA's gains stem not only from effective task parsing but also from its ability to activate and represent a compact, explicit task-relevant subgraph.

#### 4.2. Bounded Active Graph Dynamics (Q2)

JITOMA effectively decoupled the total accumulated memory size from the amount of graph structure actively processed by the current task:
- In Tier 1 and Tier 2, JITOMA typically maintained only one or two active objects at query time, with a peak of at most four simultaneously active nodes. This was achieved while retaining between 284 and 512 dormant anchors in its lightweight memory foundation.
- The active peak remained stable, at four or below, across all scenes from Tier 1 to Tier 2, demonstrating that continuous task switching did not lead to an unbounded increase in active graph complexity.
- In Tier 3, which involves more complex instructions and a greater number of primary and latent objects, the active graph size increased to six objects with a peak of twelve. This reflects the increased cognitive demand but remains significantly lower than the hundreds or thousands of active nodes maintained by baseline methods.
These results confirm that JITOMA scales expensive graph computation with current cognitive demand, rather than with the total explored scene content, addressing the perceptual saturation issue.

#### 4.3. Latency Versus Semantic Richness (Q3)

JITOMA's design emphasizes rich, actionable semantic information over minimal processing time, yet it maintains stable performance:
- JITOMA exhibited a stable Time Per Frame (TPF) of 0.56–0.63 seconds across all scenes and tiers.
- When moving from Tier 1 to the long-horizon Tier 2 setting, its TPF increased by only 0.01–0.02 seconds per frame, indicating that repeated task switching did not cause cumulative processing overhead.
- While some baselines like ReasoningGraph reported lower raw TPF, they retained hundreds to thousands of active nodes and showed substantially poorer performance on complex Tier 3 grounding. JITOMA's TPF, although slightly higher than Clio in some cases, provides substantially richer explicit semantics through batched DAM captioning and functional subnode generation, operations deliberately performed to enhance task actionability.
- JITOMA was approximately 9–21 times faster than the caption-heavy ConceptGraphs baseline on Tier 2.
Overall, JITOMA demonstrated a favorable trade-off by incurring a modest captioning overhead to produce explicit, actionable scene representations, while its just-in-time design effectively confined expensive semantic processing to task-relevant memory and maintained tightly bounded active graph size and cross-tier latency.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in this paper introduces the concept of just-in-time (JIT) scene graph growth, which represents a significant departure from conventional 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) construction paradigms. This work has several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Paradigm Shift in 3DSG Construction:** The core contribution is the proposal of JIT scene graph growth as a foundational paradigm, directly challenging the prevailing Ahead-of-Time (AOT) "build-everything-then-filter" approach. This shift is critical for resource-constrained, real-time embodied AI applications, addressing the inherent limitations of perceptual saturation, observation redundancy, and unsustainable latencies that plague AOT methods. By prioritizing "when and where" semantic structures are instantiated, JITOMA offers a more efficient and scalable alternative.

2. **Enhanced Resource Efficiency and Scalability:** JITOMA mitigates perceptual saturation by strictly limiting resource-intensive operations (like dense captioning and functional inference) to task-relevant subgraphs, while maintaining a lightweight foundation of dormant anchors for the entire environment. The experimental results demonstrate a substantial reduction in active graph size and memory footprint, and stable processing times even under long-horizon task switching. This efficiency is crucial for deploying embodied agents on edge platforms with limited computational resources, enabling them to operate effectively over extended periods in complex environments.

3. **Improved Cognitive Reasoning for Embodied Agents:** By providing downstream reasoning components, such as Large Language Models (LLMs), with a compact and task-relevant context rather than an overwhelming, noisy global graph, JITOMA enables more accurate and complete task planning. The motivation experiment illustrates how a sparse, just-in-time subgraph leads to successful multi-step task completion, whereas an unconstrained full graph causes "cognitive myopia" and planning failures. This has direct implications for the robustness and reliability of robot autonomy.

4. **Development of a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of JITOMA-Bench is a notable contribution to the field. This benchmark addresses critical gaps in existing evaluation protocols by incorporating metrics for peak resource dynamics, long-horizon multi-tasking, and complex cognitive reasoning involving both explicit and latent objects. JITOMA-Bench provides a more holistic framework for assessing the dynamic capabilities and real-time viability of 3DSG systems, offering a new standard that can guide future research and development in embodied AI.

5. **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** The framework's inspiration from human selective attention—processing fine-grained details only when a specific intent arises—suggests a robust and biologically plausible approach to AI system design. This alignment with cognitive principles could foster further interdisciplinary research and lead to more intuitive and effective AI architectures.

6. **Broader Impact on Robotics and AI:** The principles and methodology introduced by JITOMA could impact various subfields of robotics and AI, including real-time robot navigation, manipulation, human-robot interaction, and the development of foundation models for perception and action in dynamic environments. Its ability to create actionable, semantically rich representations on demand could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in practical, real-world scenarios.

In summary, this work proposes a fundamental shift in how 3DSGs are constructed and utilized, offering a more efficient, scalable, and cognitively aligned approach that is poised to enhance the capabilities of embodied agents in complex, real-time tasks.
