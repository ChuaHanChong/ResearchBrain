# EmbSpatial-Bench: Benchmarking Spatial Understanding for Embodied Tasks with Large Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University introduce EmbSpatial-Bench, the first benchmark for evaluating egocentric spatial understanding of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) in 3D embodied environments. They also develop EmbSpatial-SFT, an instruction-tuning dataset, demonstrating that targeted training significantly improves LVLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities for embodied tasks.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) lack robust and accurate spatial understanding from an egocentric perspective, which is crucial for embodied AI agents.
- Existing benchmarks for spatial understanding are inadequate, often using general 2D images and lacking the egocentric viewpoint and 3D scene context required by embodied tasks.
- The deficiency in embodied spatial understanding creates a bottleneck for deploying LVLM-driven embodied AI systems in real or simulated physical environments.

## Method

- A novel benchmark, **EmbSpatial-Bench**, was constructed using 3D embodied datasets (Matterport3D, AI2-THOR, ScanNet) to evaluate six egocentric spatial relationships (above, below, left, right, close, far) through multiple-choice questions.
- ==EmbSpatial-SFT==, an instruction-tuning dataset, was developed from Matterport3D to enhance LVLMs' spatial reasoning, including an auxiliary object localization task.
- State-of-the-art open-source and closed-source LVLMs were evaluated in a zero-shot setting, and a baseline LVLM (MiniGPT-v2) was fine-tuned on EmbSpatial-SFT with ablation studies.

## Results

- Zero-shot evaluation revealed low performance for current LVLMs on EmbSpatial-Bench, with Qwen-VL-Max achieving **49.11%** accuracy and MiniGPT-v2 **43.85%** (likelihood strategy), compared to **90.33%** human accuracy.
- Fine-tuning MiniGPT-v2 on EmbSpatial-SFT increased its likelihood accuracy by **34.25%**, reaching **78.10%**, demonstrating improved generalization to out-of-domain embodied environments.
- Ablation studies confirmed that tuning the LLM backbone (with LoRA) is crucial for performance gains, and including object localization data provided a further, albeit smaller, positive contribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art LVLMs, including GPT-4V and Qwen-VL-Max, exhibit insufficient egocentric spatial understanding for embodied tasks, performing significantly below human levels.
> - Instruction tuning with a specialized dataset (EmbSpatial-SFT) substantially improves LVLMs' embodied spatial perception, particularly when the LLM backbone is also tuned (via LoRA).
> - Models show a particular weakness in understanding depth-related spatial relations ('close', 'far') compared to horizontal and vertical relations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and rapidly evolving area at the intersection of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI). Embodied AI, which aims to create intelligent agents capable of perceiving, understanding, and interacting with the physical world, represents a significant frontier in general-purpose AI systems. Recent advancements in LVLMs have shown tremendous potential for these agents, particularly in understanding complex instructions and generating action plans based on visual inputs. Models like GPT-4V and Qwen-VL-Max exemplify this progress.

However, a key challenge remains: the accurate and robust spatial understanding of embodied environments from an agent's perspective. While LVLMs have demonstrated impressive general visual comprehension, several studies have highlighted their deficiencies in nuanced visual reasoning. For embodied agents, spatial relationships (e.g., "object A is to the left of object B" or "object C is closer than object D") are not merely descriptive but fundamental for effective interaction, navigation, and task execution. Without precise spatial understanding, an agent cannot reliably follow instructions like "pick up the cup on the table" or "navigate to the chair to your right."

The authors argue that existing benchmarks for spatial understanding are inadequate for evaluating LVLMs in embodied contexts for two main reasons:
1. **Perspective:** Most benchmarks depict spatial relationships from a general, often subjective, viewpoint within an image, rather than the crucial *egocentric perspective* required by an embodied agent. An agent needs to understand spatial relationships relative to its own position and orientation.
2. **Visual Scene Relevance:** Current benchmarks often rely on universal 2D image-text datasets (e.g., MSCOCO, Visual Genome), which lack the 3D environmental context and realism inherent in embodied tasks. These datasets may not adequately capture the complexities of real or simulated 3D spaces where agents operate.

This paper fills a significant void by introducing EmbSpatial-Bench, a benchmark specifically designed to address these limitations. By providing an egocentric, 3D-scene-based evaluation framework, the work establishes a more accurate measure of LVLMs' readiness for embodied tasks. Furthermore, by developing EmbSpatial-SFT, an instruction-tuning dataset, the authors offer a concrete path to improve these models' capabilities, thereby directly contributing to the advancement of more robust and reliable embodied AI systems. This research thus acts as a bridge, connecting the general power of LVLMs with the specific, intricate requirements of intelligent agents operating within dynamic physical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized gap between the burgeoning capabilities of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and the specific requirements for truly intelligent embodied agents. While LVLMs have shown promise in processing visual information and following instructions, their proficiency in understanding spatial relationships from an egocentric viewpoint within 3D embodied environments has not been thoroughly evaluated. This deficiency represents a critical bottleneck for the deployment of LVLM-driven embodied AI systems.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the paper are clearly defined:

1. **To Construct a Specialized Benchmark (EmbSpatial-Bench):** The foremost objective is to develop a novel benchmark specifically tailored for evaluating embodied spatial understanding in LVLMs. This benchmark must overcome the limitations of existing datasets by:
 - Focusing on an **egocentric perspective**, where spatial relationships are described relative to the agent's viewpoint.
 - Utilizing **visual scenes derived directly from diverse 3D embodied environments**, ensuring relevance to realistic embodied tasks.
 - Covering a comprehensive set of **six fundamental spatial relationships** (above, below, left, right, close, far) across all three dimensions.

2. **To Assess Current LVLM Capabilities:** A crucial objective is to empirically evaluate the performance of state-of-the-art LVLMs, including both prominent open-source and powerful closed-source models (such as GPT-4V and Qwen-VL-Max), on the newly constructed EmbSpatial-Bench. This evaluation aims to quantitatively expose the extent of their current limitations in embodied spatial understanding.

3. **To Develop an Enhancement Dataset (EmbSpatial-SFT):** Recognizing the need to bridge the identified performance gap, another key objective is to create an instruction-tuning dataset, EmbSpatial-SFT. This dataset is designed to provide LVLMs with targeted training data to improve their spatial reasoning abilities in embodied contexts.

4. **To Demonstrate Improvement through Instruction Tuning:** The final objective is to validate the effectiveness of EmbSpatial-SFT by fine-tuning an LVLM (MiniGPT-v2) on it and demonstrating significant improvements in spatial understanding across different embodied scenarios. This includes investigating the contributions of various components within the instruction-tuning data, such as object localization.

In essence, the paper aims to not only identify a critical weakness in current LVLMs for embodied AI but also to provide both a robust diagnostic tool (EmbSpatial-Bench) and a concrete solution (EmbSpatial-SFT) to advance the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic approach involving the construction of a novel benchmark, an instruction-tuning dataset, and a rigorous experimental evaluation.

#### 3.1. EmbSpatial-Bench Construction

The construction of EmbSpatial-Bench follows a meticulous pipeline designed to meet the requirements of embodied spatial understanding:

- **Spatial Image Sources:** The benchmark leverages three well-annotated 3D embodied datasets:
 - **Matterport3D (MP3D):** A large-scale dataset of indoor RGB-D scans.
 - **AI2-THOR:** A simulator for interactive 3D environments, often used for household tasks. Key images are selected based on PDDL annotations from ALFRED (Shridhar et al., 2020) to capture significant content changes after sub-goals.
 - **ScanNet:** A dataset of 3D reconstructions of indoor scenes.
 From these sources, viewpoints are randomly selected, and corresponding RGB-D images are captured.

- **Spatial Relation Extraction:** Instead of relying on potentially fallible object detectors, spatial relations are directly extracted from the 3D annotations. For each object, its 3D coordinates and camera parameters are used to derive 2D bounding boxes in the image. The benchmark focuses on six egocentric spatial relationships: `above`, `below`, `left`, `right`, `close`, and `far`.
 - For `above/below/left/right`, the relationships are determined by the relative positions of the *entire* bounding boxes.
 - For `close/far`, the average depth within the bounding box is used to ascertain distance relative to the viewer.

- **QA Generation:** The benchmark is formatted as multiple-choice questions.
 - For `above/below/left/right` relations, five templates are used to generate questions, with unrelated relations serving as false options.
 - For `far/close` relations, questions identify the farthest or closest object among a given set within the image.

- **Filtering and Human Verification:** To ensure high quality and reliability:
 - Initial filtering removes QA pairs with excessively large or small bounding boxes (e.g., bounding box length/width < 50 or > half of image dimension) to exclude improperly displayed objects.
 - Efforts are made to maintain a balanced distribution of spatial relations.
 - **Human verification** is conducted for each sample to confirm: (1) unique and clear identifiability of objects, (2) correctness of the described spatial relationship, and (3) validity of negative options.

#### 3.2. EmbSpatial-SFT Construction

To improve LVLMs' spatial understanding, the authors construct EmbSpatial-SFT, an instruction-tuning dataset:

- **Data Source:** This dataset is built exclusively from the training split of Matterport3D (MP3D) to enable zero-shot evaluation on the out-of-domain scenes from AI2-THOR and ScanNet in EmbSpatial-Bench.
- **Tasks Included:** It provides QA data for two tasks:
 - **Spatial Relationship Identification:** Consistent with EmbSpatial-Bench, approximately 25,000 training samples are generated using the automatic pipeline for identifying spatial relationships.
 - **Object Localization (Auxiliary Task):** This task aims to enhance the model's ability to ground target objects, a foundational skill for relationship identification. Models are asked to output the textual bounding box representation of inquired objects.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Models Evaluated:** A broad selection of 10 open-source LVLMs (e.g., BLIP2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-v2, LLaVA-1.6) and 2 powerful closed-source models (GPT-4V, Qwen-VL-Max) are assessed in a zero-shot setting.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Accuracy (Acc%) is used to quantify performance.
- **Evaluation Strategies:** Two strategies are employed to provide a robust assessment:
 - **Generation-based Strategy:** Directly extracts the predicted option from the model's free-form textual output.
 - **Likelihood-based Strategy:** Leverages the intrinsic generative nature of LVLMs by selecting the option with the highest probability, thereby separating instruction-following capacity from the core spatial understanding ability being evaluated.
- **Fine-tuning and Ablations:** MiniGPT-v2 is chosen as the baseline for fine-tuning on EmbSpatial-SFT. The trainable parameters include the visual connection module and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) modules within the LLM backbone. Ablation studies are conducted to understand the contribution of:
 - **LoRA tuning:** Comparing full LoRA tuning of the LLM backbone versus only fine-tuning the connection module (denoted as "w/o LoRA").
 - **Object Localization (OL) data:** Comparing performance with and without the auxiliary object localization task data (denoted as "w/o OL").

This comprehensive methodology ensures that the benchmark is robust, the evaluation is thorough, and the proposed enhancement strategy is rigorously tested.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on EmbSpatial-Bench yielded several critical findings regarding the capabilities of current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) in embodied spatial understanding and the effectiveness of the proposed instruction-tuning dataset.

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Performance of Current LVLMs

- **Insufficient Spatial Understanding:** The zero-shot evaluation of various LVLMs on EmbSpatial-Bench clearly exposed a significant deficiency in their embodied spatial understanding. Even leading closed-source models like GPT-4V and Qwen-VL-Max demonstrated unsatisfactory performance.
- **Performance Gap with Humans:** The best-performing LVLM, Qwen-VL-Max, achieved a generation accuracy of only 49.11%, while MiniGPT-v2 reached 43.85% under the likelihood strategy. This is dramatically lower than the human performance benchmark of 90.33%, indicating a substantial gap that current models need to bridge.
- **Specific Failure Modes:** A case study with GPT-4V (Appendix C) revealed that models struggle with two key aspects: (1) **Object Localization:** Misidentifying or misplacing objects within the visual scene, and (2) **Spatial Relationship Determination:** Even when objects are correctly localized, models can still incorrectly deduce their spatial relationship from an egocentric perspective. This suggests that the bottleneck is not solely in visual grounding but also in the reasoning over grounded spatial information.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Instruction Tuning with EmbSpatial-SFT

- **Significant Performance Improvement:** Fine-tuning MiniGPT-v2 on EmbSpatial-SFT consistently and substantially improved its spatial perception abilities.
 - Under the **likelihood evaluation strategy**, the overall accuracy increased by 34.25%, rising from 43.85% to 78.10%. This nearly doubles the model's original performance, bringing it significantly closer to human-level understanding in this specific task.
 - Under the **generation evaluation strategy**, the overall accuracy improved by 9.04%, from 23.93% to 32.97%. While not as dramatic as the likelihood improvement, this still represents a notable enhancement in instruction-following for spatial tasks.
- **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Scenarios:** The fine-tuned model demonstrated improved performance not only on the in-domain MP3D dataset but also generalized well to out-of-domain environments like AI2-THOR and ScanNet, although the improvement on AI2-THOR was slightly less pronounced. The authors attribute the comparatively smaller improvement in AI2-THOR to its predominantly simulated nature, which might differ more from the real-world scenes of MP3D and ScanNet used for training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study Insights

- **Importance of LLM Backbone Tuning (LoRA):** The ablation study confirmed that tuning the LLM backbone with LoRA is crucial for achieving high performance. The variant without LoRA (i.e., with a frozen LLM backbone, only fine-tuning the visual connection module) performed significantly worse. This finding suggests that the LLM backbone needs to learn explicit reasoning abilities for spatial understanding, rather than merely adjusting input visual representations, highlighting the necessity of integrated multimodal learning.
- **Contribution of Object Localization (OL) Auxiliary Task:** The inclusion of object localization data in EmbSpatial-SFT provided a noticeable, albeit smaller, positive contribution to the overall performance, leading to improvements of 0.47% (generation) and 0.76% (likelihood). This validates the authors' hypothesis that object grounding is a foundational skill that supports more complex spatial relationship identification.

#### 4.4. Performance Across Different Spatial Relations

- **Depth Perception Weakness:** The analysis of performance across individual spatial relations (Table 4) revealed a particular weakness in understanding depth-related relations (`close` and `far`) compared to horizontal (`left`, `right`) and vertical (`above`, `below`) relations, especially under the likelihood strategy. The authors suggest this might be due to the lack of explicit depth estimation training data in LVLMs and the increased complexity of identifying relative distances among four objects (for `close/far`) versus two (for other relations).

In summary, the findings robustly establish that while current LVLMs have potential for embodied tasks, their native spatial understanding from an egocentric perspective is severely limited. However, targeted instruction tuning using EmbSpatial-SFT, particularly with active learning of the LLM backbone, can significantly enhance these crucial capabilities, providing a clear path forward for developing more competent embodied AI agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant implications for the advancement of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) and the broader field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence. Its potential impact can be seen across several dimensions:

- **Standardized and Relevant Evaluation:** The introduction of **EmbSpatial-Bench** is perhaps the most immediate and impactful contribution. It fills a critical void by providing the first dedicated benchmark specifically designed for evaluating *egocentric spatial understanding in embodied environments*. Prior benchmarks were either too general (universal 2D images) or lacked the critical egocentric perspective, leading to a misrepresentation of LVLMs' true capabilities for embodied tasks. EmbSpatial-Bench offers a standardized, robust, and ecologically valid tool for researchers to assess and compare models, fostering more targeted development efforts.

- **Highlighting Critical Limitations of LVLMs:** The empirical findings, demonstrating the "insufficient capacity" of even advanced models like GPT-4V and Qwen-VL-Max in embodied spatial understanding, are crucial. This revelation precisely pinpoints a major bottleneck for the practical deployment of LVLM-driven embodied agents. By quantifying this gap against human performance, the work provides clear evidence of where current models fall short, guiding future research toward addressing these fundamental visual reasoning challenges.

- **Guidance for Model Development and Enhancement:** The proposed **EmbSpatial-SFT** instruction-tuning dataset offers a tangible solution to improve LVLMs' spatial reasoning. The significant performance gains observed after fine-tuning MiniGPT-v2 provide a clear recipe for enhancement. Furthermore, the ablation studies offer valuable insights:
 - The finding that tuning the LLM backbone (via LoRA) is critical underscores the need for deep integration of visual and linguistic reasoning, rather than merely superficial adjustments to visual features. This challenges the paradigm of simply concatenating pre-trained visual encoders and LLMs, suggesting that LLMs must actively learn to reason spatially.
 - The positive contribution of the object localization auxiliary task highlights the importance of foundational grounding skills for complex spatial understanding. This encourages the development of multi-task learning strategies for embodied AI training.

- **Advancing Embodied AI Capabilities:** By directly addressing spatial understanding – a cornerstone of perception and interaction for embodied agents – this research takes a vital step towards developing more capable and reliable embodied AI. Agents that can accurately perceive "where things are" relative to themselves are fundamental for tasks such as:
 - **Robotics:** Enabling robots to navigate, grasp objects, and execute commands in complex, unstructured environments.
 - **Virtual Assistants/AR/VR:** Creating more intuitive and effective interactions in virtual or augmented realities, where spatial cues are paramount.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improving the situational awareness and decision-making of autonomous vehicles or drones.

- **Stimulating Future Research:** The paper identifies several clear directions for future work:
 - The persistent weakness in depth understanding (e.g., `close/far` relations) calls for more research into how LVLMs can better incorporate 3D information and depth estimation into their visual processing.
 - The remaining gap between fine-tuned LVLMs and human performance suggests ongoing opportunities for developing more sophisticated spatial reasoning architectures and training methodologies.
 - The identified limitations regarding indoor environments and English-only evaluation prompt future exploration into outdoor scenarios and multilingual spatial understanding.

In conclusion, "EmbSpatial-Bench" is a highly impactful work that not only diagnoses a critical weakness in contemporary LVLMs for embodied tasks but also provides practical tools and clear guidance for addressing it. By fostering more accurate evaluation and targeted model improvements, this research significantly contributes to accelerating the development of truly intelligent, spatially aware embodied AI agents.
