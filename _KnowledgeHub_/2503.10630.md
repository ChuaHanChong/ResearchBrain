# UniGoal: Towards Universal Zero-shot Goal-oriented Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Tsinghua University researchers introduce UniGoal, a universal zero-shot framework designed for goal-oriented navigation that handles object, instance-image, and text-based goals within a single model. The approach unifies goal and scene representations using a consistent graph format, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across multiple benchmarks and outperforming several supervised universal navigation methods.

## Problem

- Current goal-oriented navigation systems are often task-specific (e.g., object-goal, image-goal, text-goal) and lack a universal framework.
- Supervised navigation methods require extensive, costly training data and suffer from poor generalization to new environments or real-world scenarios.
- Existing zero-shot navigation methods are typically specialized for individual sub-tasks, and some lose crucial structural and spatial information when converting visual input to text.

## Method

- **UniGoal** proposes a universal zero-shot framework that represents both the dynamic 3D scene and diverse goal types (object, instance-image, text) as uniform graphs.
- It employs a multi-stage scene exploration policy guided by a matching score derived from graph matching between the scene graph and the goal graph.
- A robust blacklist mechanism is implemented to prevent repetitive failed explorations by excluding previously matched elements that did not lead to success.

## Results

- UniGoal achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across Object-goal, Instance-image-goal, and Text-goal Navigation tasks on Matterport3D, Habitat-Matterport 3D, and RoboTHOR benchmarks.
- It notably outperformed task-specific zero-shot methods, such as Mod-IIN (**+4.1%** SR on HM3D for IIN), and surpassed supervised universal methods like PSL and GOAT (e.g., **+3.2%** SR over PSL for TN on HM3D).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the uniform graph representation, the multi-stage exploration policy, and the robust blacklist mechanism to UniGoal's overall performance and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying diverse goal types (object, image, text) and the dynamic scene into a consistent graph representation enables explicit, effective LLM-based reasoning for universal navigation.
> - A multi-stage, progressive exploration policy, guided by graph matching scores, significantly improves navigation efficiency and accuracy in unknown environments.
> - Zero-shot approaches leveraging powerful foundation models and structured graph reasoning can achieve competitive and even superior performance compared to training-based methods across various navigation tasks.
