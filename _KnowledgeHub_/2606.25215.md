# Reflective VLA: In-Context Action Consequences Make VLAs Generalize

> [!summary] Summary
> Reflective VLA conditions each action prediction on a rolling context of past observation-action-consequence triplets, not just the current frame, letting a dual-system VLA infer deployment-specific factors like camera geometry and calibration from interaction feedback. It improves success rate by 5.4 and 4.2 points over a matched reactive baseline under distribution shift, with no test-time fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Reactive VLAs predict actions from only the current observation, implicitly assuming it fully specifies action-relevant state, so embodiment-specific factors stay unresolved.
- Camera-to-robot geometry, robot calibration, and systematic actuation bias are hard to identify from a single frame, causing policies to overfit to training environments.
- Prior temporal-context and memory-based VLAs improve state tracking but lack explicit action-consequence binding needed to identify deployment-specific sensing and control factors.

## Method

- **Reflective VLA** packs each ==causal triplet==, consisting of a language-prefixed observation, the executed action chunk, and the action-aligned consequence observation, into a shared VLM token sequence.
- A ==shared-attention dual-system Mixture-of-Transformers== design routes all observation modalities, including third-person images, wrist images, and proprioception, through the VLM so the ==flow-matching== action expert attends densely to the full triplet prefix.
- **Block-causal training** supervises all K sampled frames jointly in a single forward pass using randomized inter-frame stride, and ==KV-cached inference== reuses past triplet keys and values for constant per-step cost.

## Results

- On LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-Plus-Hard, Reflective VLA improves average success rate by **5.4** and **4.2** points over the matched reactive baseline, reaching **87.7%** and **68.8%**.
- Ablations show context composition matters more than length: adding observation-action pairs without the consequence gains only **0.7** points (**73.1%** to **73.8%**), while the full triplet adds **4.7** points (**77.8%**).
- Real-world tests on unseen camera placements show the reactive baseline drops to **32%** on the box task and **16%** on the bowl task, while Reflective VLA reaches **76%** and **64%**, a **44-48** point gain.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-consequence binding, not context length alone, is the key ingredient: observation history or observation-action pairs without the aligned consequence barely help generalization.
> - Interaction feedback can substitute for explicit system identification: latent embodiment factors unidentifiable from a single frame become inferable once actions and their effects are observed jointly.
> - Sharing one token space and a block-causal mask lets in-context adaptation scale sub-linearly in latency, so richer deployment context need not sacrifice real-time control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language-action (VLA) models represents an advancement in robotic control, leveraging pretrained vision-language backbones and large-scale robot demonstration datasets to enable language-conditioned manipulation. These systems aim to integrate scene understanding, instruction following, and low-level control within a single model, thereby expanding the range of tasks generalist policies can perform.

A significant challenge in VLA research is the generalization of policies to deployment environments that were not encountered during training. Existing VLA models, often termed "reactive," typically predict the next action based solely on the current instruction and observation. This approach implicitly assumes that the current observation provides all necessary action-relevant state information. However, in embodied control, factors such as camera-to-robot geometry, robot calibration, or systematic actuation bias are specific to the embodiment and difficult to infer from a single static observation. Consequently, reactive policies may overfit to training environments and exhibit poor generalization to new deployment settings.

Prior work has explored methods to enhance VLA robustness and generalization. Some approaches focus on scaling training data or refining action representations. Other research lines have introduced temporal context and memory mechanisms to improve state tracking, task progress, and robustness to perceptual ambiguity. Examples include using short observation histories or spatiotemporal representations. However, these temporal context methods often lack explicit binding between an executed action and its subsequent observed consequence, a relationship identified as critical for disambiguating deployment-specific sensing and control factors.

Concurrently, the concept of in-context learning (ICL), originating from large language models, has been applied to sequential decision-making. ICL allows a model to infer task or environment structure from a few examples provided in its context, without explicit parameter updates. This paradigm has been explored in meta-reinforcement learning and transformer policies that adapt behavior from contextual experience.

Reflective VLA positions itself at the intersection of VLA development, generalization challenges, and in-context learning. It addresses the limitation of reactive and history-only VLA policies by explicitly incorporating observation–action–consequence triplets into the decision-making context. This formulation aims to enable online inference of embodiment-specific factors from interaction feedback, thereby improving cross-environment generalization without requiring test-time fine-tuning or a separate system-identification module. The work distinguishes itself from other temporal context methods by emphasizing the causal link between actions and their observed effects as the key signal for adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Reflective VLA research are as follows:

1. **Address Poor Cross-Environment Generalization in Reactive VLAs:** Current VLA models, despite training on diverse datasets, exhibit limited generalization to unseen deployment environments. This is a significant impediment to deploying robots in varied real-world scenarios. The core motivation is to enhance the adaptability and robustness of VLA policies when confronted with variations in sensing, embodiment, and environment configuration.

2. **Overcome the Ambiguity of Single Observations for Embodiment-Specific Factors:** Reactive VLA policies make decisions based on the current observation and instruction. However, crucial embodiment-specific factors—such as camera calibration, camera-to-robot geometry, or systematic actuation biases—are often challenging or impossible to infer from a single static observation. The mapping from these latent environmental factors (denoted as *z*) to a single observation (O_t) is many-to-one, rendering the inference of *z* ill-posed for reactive policies. This ambiguity causes policies to overfit to the specific *z* values encountered during training.

3. **Leverage Interaction Context as a Diagnostic Signal:** The research hypothesizes that the sequence of interactions—specifically, an observation, the executed action, and the resulting observation (an observation–action–consequence triplet)—can expose these otherwise hidden embodiment-specific factors. For instance, a commanded motion's pixel displacement in the subsequent observation can reveal camera extrinsics or calibration offsets. The motivation is to turn these hidden deployment factors into observable interaction evidence, allowing the policy to infer how the current environment responds to its actions.

4. **Formulate Cross-Environment Adaptation as an In-Context Learning (ICL) Problem:** Inspired by the success of large language models in inferring task structures from few-shot demonstrations, the authors propose casting cross-environment generalization in VLAs as an ICL problem. The objective is for the policy to infer the current deployment's characteristics online, from a small "prompt" of causal interaction triplets, thereby adapting its behavior without explicit parameter updates.

5. **Develop a Tractable and Efficient ICL Framework for Dual-System VLAs:** Implementing ICL with causal interaction triplets in dual-system VLAs presents architectural and computational challenges. The objectives include:
 - **Handling Heterogeneous Modalities:** To enable the action decoder to utilize interaction context effectively, all modalities (images, proprioception, continuous action chunks) must be processed within a shared VLM token space and attended to by the action expert.
 - **Enabling Parallel Training:** Naive ICL training, involving separate forward passes for each context frame, would be computationally expensive (scaling with *K* context frames). A key objective is to design a training mechanism, such as a block-causal mask, that allows all context frames to be supervised jointly in a single forward pass, making training tractable.
 - **Supporting Real-Time Inference:** Similarly, naive inference would require recomputing the entire historical prefix at every step, compromising real-time control. An objective is to implement a mechanism, like KV-caching, to reuse past computations and maintain a constant per-step inference cost.

In essence, the motivation is to equip VLA models with a "reflective" capacity, allowing them to learn online from their own interactions how their actions manifest in the observed environment, and thereby adapt their control policies to generalize more effectively across diverse real-world deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Reflective VLA extends a standard reactive dual-system VLA by integrating an in-context learning interface that utilizes observation–action–consequence triplets.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Standard Reactive VLA Formulation

A standard reactive VLA, denoted as π_θ, predicts an action chunk A_t = [a_t..., a_{t+C-1}] at control step *t* based solely on the language instruction (L) and the current multimodal observation (O_t): A_t ∼ π_θ(· | L, O_t). This formulation, common in dual-system VLAs, limits the action expert to the current observation, preventing it from utilizing past executed actions and their consequences.

#### 3.2. From Reactive to In-Context Generalization

The core idea is to move beyond the reactive paradigm by incorporating interaction history. Reactive VLAs struggle with latent embodiment-specific factors (z) because P(z | O_t) is ill-posed. Reflective VLA posits that a policy adapted to the current embodiment, π(A_t | L, O_t, z), can be approximated by marginalizing over a posterior P(z | H), where H is an accumulated interaction context. If H consists of observation–action–consequence triplets, the posterior P(z | H) ∝ P(z) ∏_i P_env(O'_i | O_i, A_i, z) explicitly depends on how the environment responds to actions, making *z* inferable. Thus, Reflective VLA conditions its decisions on H: A_t ∼ π_θ(A_t | L, H, O_t), where H = {(O_i, A_i, O'_i)}^(K-1)_{i=1} is the context of *K-1* causal triplets.

#### 3.3. Reflective VLA Implementation

**Observation–Action–Consequence Context:**
- **Triplet Structure:** Each historical triplet T_i records the observation before an action chunk (O_i), the executed action chunk (A_i), and the observation after the chunk completes (O'_i = O_{i+C}). This *action-aligned consequence* O'_i captures the visible effect of the action.
- **Action Embedding:** Each action chunk A_i is projected into eight tokens within the VLM token space via a learned projection g_A.
- **Self-Contained Units:** The language instruction L is re-inserted at the start of every triplet and the current query block, making each unit (L, τ_i, O_i, A_i, O'_i) self-contained.
- **Context Buffer (Inference):** During deployment, a rolling FIFO buffer stores the K-1 most recent triplets. A_i in these triplets is the policy's own predicted chunk, and O'_i is the observed subsequent state.

**Multimodal Prompt Construction:**
The model constructs a single multimodal sequence X_in = [T_{m1}..., T_{mK-1}, (L, τ_t, O_t)]. This sequence comprises the historical triplets (evidence about deployment response) and the current language-prefixed observation (the query for the next action chunk).

**Shared-Attention Dual-System Architecture:**
- **Unified Token Space:** All observation modalities (third-person images, wrist images, proprioceptive states) are routed through the VLM backbone into a shared token space. Proprioceptive states and action chunks are projected into this space.
- **Action Expert Integration:** A continuous flow-matching action expert is attached as a suffix. Crucially, it shares attention with the VLM prefix at every layer, allowing it to directly attend to all elements of the historical context and the current observation when generating A_t. This avoids information bottlenecks seen in some dual-system designs.

**Block-Causal Training:**
- **Efficiency:** To train with a context length of K without O(K) computational cost, Reflective VLA employs a block-causal attention mask. This allows supervision of all K sampled frames jointly in a single forward pass.
- **Sampling and Packing:** K ordered frames (t_1 < ... < t_K) are sampled from a trajectory. The first K-1 form historical triplets, and t_K is the current query. The block-causal mask ensures each frame acts as both context for later targets and a prediction target itself. Special tokens demarcate triplet boundaries.
- **Stride Randomization:** To prevent the policy from merely extrapolating actions from fixed temporal patterns, a randomized stride within a bounded range is used during training for inter-frame spacing.
- **Masking and Objective:** For each target frame t_k, its corresponding query slot ˆA_{t_k} attends to the instruction (L), all prior completed triplets (T_{t_j} for j < k), and its own current observation (O_{t_k}). It is masked from its own action A_{t_k} and consequence O'_{t_k}, and all subsequent triplets. The training objective sums the flow-matching action loss (L_act) over all valid targets: L_train = Σ^K_{k=1} L_act(A_{t_k}; V_{t_k}). This provides dense supervision while maintaining causality.

**KV-Cached Real-Time Inference:**
At deployment, VLM-side keys and values for past triplets are cached and reused. Only the newly observed frame or triplet needs to be encoded, keeping the per-step inference cost approximately constant despite the extended context, thus supporting real-time control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Reflective VLA was conducted across four simulation settings (LIBERO, SimplerEnv-Bridge, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Plus-Hard) and real-world robot experiments, focusing on in-distribution performance, robustness to distribution shifts, and the specific contribution of action consequences.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution Performance

- **LIBERO:** Reflective VLA achieved an average success rate of 97.6%, outperforming a matched reactive baseline (96.9%) by 0.7 percentage points and establishing a competitive performance against other state-of-the-art methods. Performance on the Long suite also showed a slight improvement (94.0% to 94.6%).
- **SimplerEnv-Bridge:** On this real-to-sim transfer benchmark, Reflective VLA reached 78.2% average success, improving over the reactive baseline (72.9%) by 5.3 percentage points.
- **Conclusion:** The integration of structured interaction context did not compromise the policy's nominal task-solving abilities on standard benchmarks; in some cases, it led to minor improvements.

#### 4.2. Robustness Under Perturbations (LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-Plus-Hard)

- **LIBERO-Plus:** Reflective VLA achieved 87.7% average success, surpassing the matched reactive baseline (82.3%), OpenVLA-OFT (80.7%), and MemoryVLA (81.5%). Significant gains were observed in categories directly affecting sensing or robot configuration, such as Robot (+22.9 pp), Background (+6.9 pp), and Noise (+5.2 pp). Language perturbations showed comparable performance, while Layout was the only category with a slight decrease.
- **LIBERO-Plus-Hard:** This custom benchmark, designed with shifts specifically affecting the action-to-observation mapping, further highlighted Reflective VLA's benefits. It achieved 68.8% average success, outperforming the reactive baseline (64.6%) and MemoryVLA (60.7%).
 - **Multi-camera shift (Camera†):** Reflective VLA improved from 74.0% (reactive) to 76.3%.
 - **Robot calibration shift (Rob. Calib†):** Reflective VLA improved from 55.2% (reactive) to 61.3%. The largest gain was in this category, supporting the hypothesis that the residual between commanded actions and their consequences is a key diagnostic signal.
- **Conclusion:** Reflective VLA demonstrated enhanced robustness and generalization capabilities under various distribution shifts, particularly those that alter the relationship between executed actions and observed environmental feedback.

#### 4.3. Ablations on Interaction Context

- **Context Composition (fixed K=8):**
 - Adding observation history alone ("O") showed no improvement over the reactive baseline (72.3% vs. 73.1% average success).
 - Adding observation–action pairs without consequences ("O, A") provided only a marginal gain (73.8%).
 - The full observation–action–consequence context ("O, A, O'") significantly improved performance to 77.8%, a 4.7 percentage point gain over the reactive baseline. This gain was most pronounced on the Robot Calibration shift (+6.1 pp).
 - **Conclusion:** The action-aligned consequence (O') within the triplet is identified as the critical adaptation signal, rather than just temporal context or executed actions alone.
- **Context Length (full O, A, O'):**
 - Increasing the context length (K) from 1 to 8 consistently improved robustness. Most of the gain was observed by K=4 (+3.6 pp over K=1), with diminishing returns thereafter.
 - **Conclusion:** A relatively small number of recent interaction triplets can capture most of the useful deployment-specific evidence.

#### 4.4. Latency-Accuracy Trade-off

- Reflective VLA with K=8 context frames incurred a 1.43x increase in inference latency compared to K=1 (178 ms vs. 124 ms). However, K=4, which captures most of the accuracy gains, resulted in only a 1.19x increase in latency, suggesting a practical operating point for real-time control. KV-caching facilitated this sub-linear latency growth.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments

- **Cross-Camera Generalization:** Reflective VLA was evaluated on a real robot under unseen camera placements.
 - **Seen Viewpoints:** Performance improved slightly over the reactive baseline (e.g., 88% to 92% on a box task, 68% to 72% on a bowl task), indicating no compromise in nominal performance.
 - **Unseen Viewpoints:** The reactive baseline experienced a sharp degradation (e.g., 32% box, 16% bowl). Reflective VLA achieved significantly higher success rates (76% box, 64% bowl), showing gains of +44 to +48 percentage points.
- **Conclusion:** Reflective VLA substantially improved generalization to novel camera viewpoints in the real world, benefiting both grasping robustness and camera-dependent spatial alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Reflective VLA: In-Context Action Consequences" presents a significant contribution to the field of vision-language-action models, primarily by addressing the persistent challenge of cross-environment generalization.

**Core Contributions:**
The paper's central contribution is the formalization of cross-environment generalization in VLAs as an in-context learning problem over *causal observation–action–consequence triplets*. This formulation allows policies to adapt their behavior based on how their actions manifest in the observed environment, without requiring explicit system identification modules or test-time fine-tuning. A key finding from ablations is that the *action-aligned consequence* (O') is the critical ingredient for this adaptation, demonstrating that merely providing longer observation histories or observation-action pairs is insufficient.

**Methodological Advancements:**
Reflective VLA introduces a dual-system VLA architecture that routes all multimodal observations and historical action consequences into a shared VLM token space, enabling direct attention by a continuous action expert. The development of a *block-causal mask* for training allows for efficient, parallel multi-frame supervision in a single forward pass, resolving a major computational challenge associated with longer contexts. Furthermore, the implementation of *KV-cached inference* ensures that the extended context does not compromise real-time control at deployment, maintaining near-constant per-step latency.

**Impact and Implications:**

1. **Enhanced Generalization:** The most direct impact is the improved generalization of VLA policies to environments with sensing, embodiment, and calibration shifts unseen during training. This is a critical step towards deploying generalist robots in diverse, unstructured real-world settings where precise pre-calibration is often impractical.
2. **Reduced Deployment Overhead:** By enabling online adaptation from interaction feedback, Reflective VLA can potentially reduce the need for extensive re-calibration or fine-tuning when a robot is moved to a new environment, streamlining the deployment process.
3. **More Robust Policies:** The ability to infer and adapt to embodiment-specific factors makes policies inherently more robust to variations such as perturbed camera extrinsics or systematic actuation biases, which are common in real-world robotics.
4. **Conceptual Shift in VLA Design:** The work advocates for a "reflective" paradigm in VLA, where policies actively learn from their interaction history, moving beyond purely reactive control. This conceptual shift could inspire new architectural designs and training methodologies for adaptive embodied agents.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework opens several avenues for future work, including scaling context length further, exploring reflection over longer temporal horizons to capture delayed effects or complex contact dynamics, and applying the framework to a wider variety of robot embodiments and tasks.

In summary, Reflective VLA offers a principled and effective method for improving the generalization capabilities of VLA models, representing a step towards more adaptive and robust robotic systems capable of operating reliably in unfamiliar environments.
