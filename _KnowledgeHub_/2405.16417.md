# CRoFT: Robust Fine-Tuning with Concurrent Optimization for OOD Generalization and Open-Set OOD Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced CRoFT, a fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language Pre-trained Models that concurrently optimizes for out-of-distribution generalization and open-set OOD detection. This approach, which theoretically links OOD detection to generalization via Hessian matrices, achieved improved performance in both areas, including a 25.3% reduction in FPR95 for OOD detection in one setting and a 4.3% boost in OOD generalization accuracy.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) for specific tasks often degrades their ability to generalize to out-of-distribution (OOD) data and detect novel, unseen classes.
- Real-world AI applications encounter both covariate shifts (requiring OOD generalization for known classes) and semantic shifts (requiring open-set OOD detection for unknown classes), challenges typically addressed independently.
- Current fine-tuning strategies for VL-PTMs lack a unified approach to simultaneously enhance robustness to distribution shifts and reliably identify novel categories.

## Method

- A lightweight ==adapter-tuning== strategy is applied to Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) to efficiently adapt them to downstream tasks.
- An ==Energy Distribution Reshaping (EDR) loss== is introduced to optimize open-set Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection by reshaping energy distributions, making unknown classes more distinguishable.
- ==Adversarial learning== is employed to generate worst-case covariate-shifted OOD features, which are then used to minimize a novel ==Hessian-based OOD generalization bound==.

## Results

- The framework achieved state-of-the-art performance in both Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization and open-set OOD detection across multiple benchmarks.
- CRoFT reduced the False Positive Rate at **95%** True Positive Rate (FPR95) by up to **25.3%** for OOD detection and improved OOD generalization accuracy by **4.3%** compared to baselines in various settings.
- Ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of the EDR loss and adversarial loss, and visualizations showed improved separation of feature and energy distributions for different data types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A theoretical connection exists between open-set Out-of-Distribution (OOD) detection and OOD generalization, demonstrating that optimizing for one can benefit the other through domain-consistent Hessians.
> - Minimizing the gradient magnitude of energy scores on in-distribution data effectively reshapes energy distributions, improving the discriminability of open-set OOD data.
> - Adversarial generation of worst-case covariate-shifted features during training helps the model learn more robust adapters for improved generalization to various data styles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed transformative advancements with the advent of large-scale Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) like CLIP, Grounding DINO, and MiniGPT-4. These models excel in open-vocabulary tasks by learning rich, transferable visual-semantic representations from vast datasets. However, their practical deployment in downstream applications often necessitates fine-tuning on specific tasks and datasets. This fine-tuning process, while improving performance on the in-distribution (ID) training data, paradoxically carries a significant risk: it can distort the model's general knowledge and impair its ability to generalize to data exhibiting distribution shifts.

Real-world machine learning systems operate in dynamic environments where distribution shifts are inevitable. These shifts manifest in two primary forms:
1. **Covariate Shifts:** These involve changes in the input distribution while the underlying class labels remain the same. Examples include variations in image styles, lighting conditions, or backgrounds for known classes (e.g., a "dog" image with a different artistic filter). Addressing this requires **OOD generalization**, where the model maintains performance on known classes across novel domains.
2. **Semantic Shifts (or Novelty Detection):** These involve encountering entirely new, unseen classes during testing that were not present in the training data. For instance, a model trained on common animals might encounter a "panda" at test time without having seen it during training. This necessitates **open-set OOD detection**, where the model can accurately distinguish these unknown classes from known ones, rather than misclassifying them.

Historically, research has largely treated OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection as separate problems, leading to specialized fine-tuning strategies or detection methods that excel in one area but often neglect or even degrade performance in the other. Fine-tuning techniques primarily focused on robustness to covariate shifts often assume a closed-set scenario, where all test classes are known. Conversely, methods for open-set detection might not explicitly optimize for generalization across varying styles of known classes.

This paper addresses a critical, yet underexplored, gap: the simultaneous improvement of OOD generalization on covariate shifts and effective detection of open-set unseen classes during the fine-tuning of VL-PTMs. The authors highlight that real-world applications, such as autonomous driving and medical imaging, inherently demand both capabilities for safety and reliability. By proposing a unified framework that concurrently optimizes for both tasks, CRoFT positions itself at the forefront of robust AI research, moving beyond piecemeal solutions to tackle a more holistic and realistic challenge in model deployment. Furthermore, the paper's theoretical contribution, which establishes a link between OOD detection objectives (via energy scores) and OOD generalization (via domain-consistent Hessians), offers a novel perspective that could inspire future integrated approaches in robust machine learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust fine-tuning framework for Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) that simultaneously achieves two critical goals:
1. **Enhance Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** Improve the model's ability to classify known classes accurately even when the input data exhibits covariate shifts (e.g., changes in style, environment) not seen during training. This refers to robust performance on closed-set OOD data.
2. **Facilitate Open-Set OOD Detection:** Enable the fine-tuned model to effectively detect and distinguish test-time unseen classes (semantic shifts) from the known, in-distribution classes.

The core motivation for these objectives stems from the inherent limitations of current VL-PTM fine-tuning paradigms and the demands of real-world applications. The authors identify several key motivational factors:

- **Real-World Data Complexity:** Machine learning systems in practical scenarios inevitably encounter diverse types of OOD data. This includes both closed-set OOD data (known classes with varied styles) and open-set OOD data (entirely new, unknown classes). Existing methods typically address these challenges independently, leaving a critical gap in holistic robustness.
- **Limitations of Current Fine-Tuning:** While VL-PTMs like CLIP show remarkable open-vocabulary capabilities, downstream fine-tuning often inadvertently distorts their general knowledge. This can lead to a trade-off where improved performance on specific ID tasks comes at the cost of reduced generalization to distribution shifts and impaired detection of novel classes.
- **CLIP's Initial Performance Gaps:** An initial assessment of CLIP reveals significant overlaps in energy distributions between closed-set ID and open-set OOD data, making it difficult to distinguish them. Furthermore, noticeable discrepancies between the feature distributions of closed-set ID and closed-set OOD data complicate achieving robust generalization. These empirical observations underscore the need for targeted fine-tuning strategies.
- **System Safety and Reliability:** In high-risk applications such as autonomous driving and medical imaging, misclassifying an unknown object or failing to detect a novel pathology can have severe consequences. It is paramount for models to not only perform well on known variations but also to reliably identify when they are encountering something truly new.
- **Underexplored Research Question:** The paper explicitly frames a critical, underexplored question: "When fine-tuning VL-PTMs to downstream tasks, how to improve models’ generalization ability to closed-set OOD data, while effectively detecting open-set unseen classes during fine-tuning?" This question highlights the specific research void CRoFT aims to fill.
- **Theoretical Linkage:** A key underlying motivation is the hypothesis that a connection exists between OOD detection and OOD generalization, which could enable concurrent optimization. The authors aim to theoretically establish this link (via Hessians) to build a more principled and unified framework.

By concurrently addressing both OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection, CRoFT aims to develop VL-PTMs that are not only accurate on their target tasks but also robust and reliable in the face of the complex, unpredictable data environments of the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CRoFT framework proposes a novel fine-tuning paradigm for Vision-Language Pre-trained Models (VL-PTMs) that concurrently optimizes for both OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection. The methodology is grounded in theoretical insights linking these two seemingly disparate tasks via Hessian matrices.

#### 3.1. Lightweight Fine-tuning Strategy

To ensure computational efficiency for large VL-PTMs like CLIP, CRoFT employs a lightweight fine-tuning approach. Instead of updating all parameters of the pre-trained encoders (denoted as $\theta_0$), it injects "adapters" in the form of one-layer linear projections into both the image and text encoders. Only the parameters of these linear projections (denoted as $\theta_l$) are optimized during fine-tuning, while $\theta_0$ remains frozen. This adapter-tuning strategy is a common and effective technique for efficiently adapting VL-PTMs to downstream tasks.

#### 3.2. Reshaping Energy Distribution for Open-Set OOD Detection (EDR Loss)

The first core component is the Energy Distribution Reshaping (EDR) loss ($L_e$), designed to improve open-set OOD detection.
- **Energy Score:** The framework leverages the widely used energy function (Liu et al., 2020) for OOD detection. The energy score $E_{\theta}(x)$ quantifies the plausibility of an input $x$ belonging to known classes, with lower energy scores indicating higher confidence for in-distribution data.
- **Optimization Objective:** While prior works often directly minimize energy scores on training data, CRoFT proposes a more nuanced objective: minimizing the squared magnitude of the gradient of the energy scores with respect to the fine-tuned parameters ($\theta_l$) on the training data (Proposition 3.1). Formally, $L_e = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left\| \nabla_{\theta_l} \log \sum_{j=1}^K \exp \langle z_I(x^{(i)}; \theta), z_T^{(j)}(\theta) \rangle \right\|^2$. This approach encourages the model to approach an optimal solution where the energy scores for ID data are minimized, thereby distinguishing their energy distribution from OOD data.

#### 3.3. Minimizing an EDR-Inspired OOD Generalization Bound with Adversarial Learning

The second core component focuses on improving OOD generalization by minimizing a novel Hessian-based OOD generalization bound.
- **Theoretical Connection (Lemma 3.5 & Theorem 3.6):** This is the paper's crucial theoretical contribution. Lemma 3.5 demonstrates that the local optimal solution of the proposed EDR loss (for OOD detection) implicitly leads to domain-consistent Hessians of the classification loss. Building on this, Theorem 3.6 shows that the EDR loss helps bound the generalization gap between source and target domains, implying that optimizing for open-set OOD detection *also* inherently aids OOD generalization.
- **Hessian-based OOD Generalization Bound (Theorem 3.3):** The authors derive a new generalization bound for the target domain's risk ($E_T(\theta)$). This bound highlights that OOD generalization performance is related to the performance gap between the minimum risk on the source domain ($\min_{\theta'} \hat{E}_S(\theta')$) and the empirical risk on worst-case covariate-shifted OOD data ($\hat{E}^c_S(\theta)$), along with a term involving the Hessian matrix.
- **Adversarial Generation of Worst-Case OOD Features ($L_c$):** Motivated by minimizing this bound, CRoFT introduces an adversarial learning procedure. It iteratively generates "worst-case covariate-shifted OOD image features" ($z^c_I$) from the ID image features ($z_I$). This generation process (Proposition 3.4) aims to create OOD samples that are maximally challenging for the model, preserving semantic information for correct classification but significantly differing from ID features. This is formulated as an optimization problem where a generator $g(\cdot)$ (a one-layer linear projection) creates $z^c_I = g(z_I)$ by minimizing a loss $L_c = -\frac{\lambda_1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \langle g(z_I^{(i)}), z_I^{(i)} \rangle + \hat{E}^c_S(\theta)$. The negative inner product term encourages the generated features to be distinct from the original, while $\hat{E}^c_S(\theta)$ ensures they remain difficult to classify.

#### 3.4. Overall Optimization Objective (CRoFT Loss)

The final CRoFT optimization objective combines the standard classification loss (cross-entropy) on ID data, the adversarial loss for generated OOD features, and the EDR loss applied to both ID and generated OOD features:

$L_{CRoFT} = \hat{E}_S(\theta) + \lambda_1 L_c + \lambda_2 (L_e(z_I) + L_e(z^c_I))$

Here, $\hat{E}_S(\theta)$ is the empirical classification loss on ID data, $L_c$ is the loss for optimizing on generated worst-case OOD features, and $L_e(z_I)$ and $L_e(z^c_I)$ are the EDR losses applied to ID and generated OOD features, respectively. $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ are hyperparameters balancing these terms. The framework iteratively updates the model parameters to minimize this combined loss, thereby concurrently improving OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted under two distinct data settings ("Setup-I" for large-scale ImageNet and "Setup-II" for cross-dataset images) consistently demonstrate the superiority of CRoFT in simultaneously addressing OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection compared to existing CLIP-based lightweight fine-tuning methods.

#### 4.1. Overall Superiority in Both Tasks

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** CRoFT establishes the overall best performance across both OOD generalization (measured by OOD ACC) and open-set OOD detection (measured by AUROC and FPR95).
- **Significant OOD Detection Improvement:** In Setup-I (ImageNet), CRoFT achieves an impressive reduction of up to **25.3% on FPR95** compared to the zero-shot CLIP model, and considerably lower FPR95 values than all competitive fine-tuning methods (e.g., CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter). Similarly, in Setup-II (cross-dataset), CRoFT outperforms the second-best FPR95 results by approximately **11%**, showcasing its effectiveness in detecting cross-dataset unseen classes. This highlights CRoFT's robust ability to distinguish known from unknown categories.
- **Enhanced OOD Generalization:** CRoFT demonstrates comparable or even superior generalization results on closed-set OOD test sets. For instance, in Setup-II, it boosts test accuracy on closed-test OOD data by an impressive **4.3%** compared to the vanilla CLIP-Adapter method, indicating better robustness to covariate shifts.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Component Effectiveness

- **Effect of EDR Loss ($L_e$):**
 - The ablation study shows that incorporating $L_e$ gradually decreases the FPR95 value over training epochs while maintaining ID test accuracy (Figure 3).
 - Compared to a vanilla energy reduction technique (EnergyMin), CRoFT (with $L_e$) significantly outperforms it, achieving about 15% lower FPR95 and better OOD generalization. This validates the theoretical finding (Theorem 3.6) that $L_e$ positively impacts OOD generalization.
- **Effect of Adversarial Loss ($L_c$):**
 - Removing $L_c$ leads to a decrease in OOD test accuracy, particularly in challenging scenarios like ImageNet-Sketch (Table 3) and specific domains in VLCS (Figure 4d). This confirms that $L_c$ successfully generates worst-case covariate-shifted features and, by optimizing on them, learns more robust adapters for OOD generalization.
 - The quality of generated OOD features is high, with their energy distributions closely resembling that of true closed-set OOD data, as shown by percentile analysis (Table 10).

#### 4.3. Feature Space and Energy Distribution Visualizations

- **Improved Discrimination:** t-SNE visualizations (Figure 4b, 4e) illustrate that CRoFT learns image features that are much better discriminated between closed-set and open-set data compared to zero-shot CLIP or CLIP-Adapter.
- **Reduced Overlap:** The energy distributions for ID, closed-set OOD, and open-set OOD data are significantly better separated by CRoFT compared to CLIP, with reduced overlap, thus facilitating OOD detection (Figure 5b, 5c).
- **Closer ID and Covariate-Shifted OOD:** CRoFT achieves much smaller distances between ID data and covariate-shifted OOD data (Figure 4c), indicating improved generalization to domain variations of known classes.

#### 4.4. Robust Few-Shot Learning Performance

- Additional experiments on standard few-shot learning benchmarks (Caltech101, Oxford Flowers, DTD, etc.) using CLIP RN50 show that CRoFT achieves higher test accuracy in most cases and secures the best average results across various training shots (Table 9). This includes stable improvements of about 1.5% on average under 1-shot and 2-shot settings, demonstrating its efficacy even with limited data.

In summary, CRoFT consistently outperforms strong baselines by effectively tackling the dual challenge of OOD generalization and open-set OOD detection, supported by both quantitative metrics and visual evidence of improved feature and energy distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRoFT framework represents a significant advancement in the robustness of Vision-Language Pre-trained Models, offering both theoretical insights and practical utility. Its impact can be multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Novel Connection:** The most profound theoretical contribution is establishing a principled connection between seemingly distinct tasks: open-set OOD detection and OOD generalization. By demonstrating that minimizing the gradient magnitude of energy scores (for detection) leads to domain-consistent Hessians of classification loss, which in turn bounds OOD generalization, the paper unveils a previously underexplored synergy. This theoretical bridge provides a deeper understanding of how robust properties of models are interconnected.
- **Hessian-based Bounds:** The introduction of a novel Hessian-based OOD generalization bound offers a new lens through which to analyze and optimize for model robustness under distribution shifts. This could inspire further theoretical work on relating local curvature properties of the loss landscape to global generalization performance in OOD settings.
- **Principled Unified Framework:** The theoretical underpinnings allow for a non-heuristic integration of objectives for OOD generalization and detection. This moves beyond empirical stitching of different techniques towards a more unified and principled approach, which is crucial for building reliable AI systems.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact

- **Enhanced Real-World Robustness:** CRoFT directly addresses a critical challenge in deploying AI models in unpredictable environments. By concurrently improving both OOD generalization (handling known classes with new styles) and open-set OOD detection (identifying truly novel classes), it significantly enhances the overall robustness and reliability of VL-PTMs.
- **Improved Safety-Critical Applications:** The ability to accurately classify known variations while simultaneously detecting unforeseen anomalies is paramount for safety-critical domains. In **autonomous driving**, CRoFT could help vehicles reliably recognize traffic signs under varying weather conditions *and* detect entirely new or unusual obstacles. In **medical imaging**, it could assist in diagnosing known conditions from diverse scan qualities *and* alert clinicians to novel pathologies not seen during training. This could prevent critical failures and improve human decision-making.
- **Efficient Fine-Tuning for Large Models:** By utilizing lightweight adapter-tuning, CRoFT offers an efficient solution for fine-tuning massive VL-PTMs. This makes the robustification process feasible and less computationally expensive, accelerating deployment and iterative improvement of models in real-world pipelines.
- **Broader Applicability:** While primarily demonstrated on CLIP, the principles of CRoFT, particularly the EDR loss and the adversarial generation of OOD features, could be extended to other VL-PTMs and even other deep learning architectures, fostering a wider adoption of robust fine-tuning practices.
- **Better Resource Utilization:** By finding a way to make OOD detection objectives also serve OOD generalization, CRoFT potentially allows for more efficient use of training data and computational resources, as a single optimization process benefits multiple robustness aspects.

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

- **Further Exploration of Hessian Properties:** The theoretical link between EDR loss and Hessians opens avenues for more explicit regularization of Hessian properties to enhance OOD generalization.
- **Adaptive Hyperparameter Tuning:** Investigating methods for adaptively setting $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ (the weights for $L_c$ and $L_e$) based on data characteristics or task requirements could further optimize performance.
- **Dynamic OOD Feature Generation:** Exploring more sophisticated or dynamic methods for generating worst-case OOD features could enhance the adversarial learning component.
- **Integration with Other Robustness Techniques:** Combining CRoFT with other domain generalization or OOD detection techniques could lead to even more comprehensive robustness solutions.

In conclusion, CRoFT provides a robust, theoretically grounded, and practically effective solution for a critical problem in modern AI. Its dual focus on generalization and detection, coupled with its efficiency, positions it as a valuable contribution with the potential to significantly impact the safety and reliability of AI systems in diverse real-world applications.
