# A Nonlinear MPC Framework for Loco-Manipulation of Quadrupedal Robots with Non-Negligible Manipulator Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Virginia Tech researchers developed a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework that enables quadrupedal robots to perform dynamic loco-manipulation tasks while accounting for the full-order dynamics of relatively heavy, torque-controlled manipulators. This approach achieves real-time control at 60 Hz and demonstrates robust performance on various terrains and with unknown payloads, showing an 58% higher success rate in challenging simulations compared to a kino-centroidal baseline.

## Problem

- Existing Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks for loco-manipulation often simplify or neglect manipulator dynamics, which can limit robustness for systems with high arm-to-base mass ratios.
- Integrating full-order manipulator dynamics with locomotion template models creates a computationally challenging optimization problem that hinders real-time control.
- Robustly controlling quadrupedal robots equipped with heavy, torque-controllable arms under dynamic conditions, external disturbances, and unmodeled environments is a significant challenge.

## Method

- A layered control architecture is proposed, featuring a high-level Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (N**MPC**) running at 60 Hz for trajectory optimization and a low-level nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC) at 500 Hz for torque computation.
- The high-level NMPC employs a decomposition strategy, coupling a Single Rigid Body (SRB) model for locomotion with a full-order floating-base dynamic model for the manipulator via holonomic constraints.
- The NMPC formulates a nonlinear optimization problem solved in real-time using the CasADi framework with IPOPT, employing warm-starting and selective simplification (e.g., constant manipulator inertia over the horizon) to manage computational complexity.

## Results

- The NMPC framework demonstrated real-time operation at 60 Hz, with **85.6%** to **87.7%** of solve times falling within 13-14 ms, without observing any infeasibilities.
- Hardware experiments on a Unitree Go2 with a Kinova arm showed robust loco-manipulation over rough terrains, carrying unknown 1 kg payloads, pushing a **24.4** kg cart, and performing various pick-and-place tasks.
- Simulations revealed that the proposed decomposition-based NMPC achieved an **86%** success rate in robust locomotion over challenging terrains, a **58%** improvement compared to a baseline kino-centroidal NMPC's **28%** success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the robot's dynamics into separate (locomotion and manipulation) models, coupled by rigid holonomic constraints, is crucial for achieving real-time NMPC when manipulator dynamics are non-negligible.
> - Balancing model fidelity by retaining SRB nonlinearities while selectively simplifying manipulator dynamics allows for computationally tractable and robust whole-body control.
> - Explicitly integrating full manipulator dynamics into the MPC framework significantly improves robustness and expands the operational envelope for small quadrupeds with heavy, torque-controlled arms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC), particularly when combined with reduced-order template models, has become a prominent method for trajectory optimization in dynamic legged locomotion. These template models, such as the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) and Single Rigid Body (SRB) models, offer simplified abstractions that enable computationally efficient control for complex, nonlinear locomotion systems. This approach has facilitated dynamic behaviors in legged robots.

However, the integration of robotic manipulators into legged platforms introduces additional complexity, transitioning from pure locomotion to loco-manipulation tasks. These tasks demand MPC algorithms capable of coordinating high-degree-of-freedom (DoF) systems under dynamic constraints, often involving multi-contact scenarios. Prior research in loco-manipulation planning has made progress, with approaches leveraging centroidal dynamics and full-body kinematics (kino-centroidal models) to achieve complex behaviors like throwing objects, opening doors, or pushing payloads. Robotic systems such as ALMA (ANYmal quadruped with DynaArm) have been used to demonstrate these capabilities.

A common simplification in many existing MPC-based loco-manipulation frameworks is the approximation or neglect of the full-order dynamics of the manipulator itself. While this simplification can maintain computational efficiency, it can limit the robustness and stability of the system, especially when the manipulator's dynamics are significant relative to the robot's base. This becomes particularly relevant for smaller quadrupedal robots equipped with relatively heavy, torque-controlled arms. For example, the integration of a 4.4 kg, 4-DoF Kinova arm with a 15 kg Unitree Go2 quadruped results in a substantial arm-to-base mass ratio (approximately 29%) and height ratio (216%). In such scenarios, neglecting arm dynamics can compromise performance.

Learning-based methods, including reinforcement learning (RL), have also been explored for loco-manipulation, often combining learned locomotion policies with model-based manipulation or using unified RL policies for whole-body control. These methods have shown promise for various tasks, including visuomotor control, pick-and-place, and fall damage mitigation. However, many RL-based approaches in small quadrupeds have focused on lightweight, position-controlled arms.

This research aims to address the identified gap by developing a model-based control framework that systematically integrates full-order manipulator dynamics with locomotion template models. It specifically targets situations where the manipulator dynamics are non-negligible, moving beyond the simplifications of kino-centroidal models and the limitations of approaches primarily suited for lightweight, position-controlled manipulators on smaller platforms. The work positions itself as the first model-based control method to tackle dynamic loco-manipulation with heavy, torque-controllable arms on small quadrupeds, pushing the operational limits of such platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to develop a unified and computationally efficient Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework for loco-manipulation tasks involving quadrupedal robots. A central challenge addressed by this work is the robust control of these robots when equipped with robotic manipulators whose dynamic properties (e.g., mass, inertia) are non-negligible in comparison to the base quadruped.

The specific objectives and motivations underpinning this work are:

1. **Systematic Integration of Full-Order Manipulator Dynamics:** Existing MPC frameworks for loco-manipulation often rely on reduced-order models or approximate the manipulator's dynamics (e.g., using kinematic models or simplified centroidal dynamics). This simplification can lead to inaccuracies and reduced robustness, especially for robots where the arm-to-base mass and inertia ratios are significant. The paper aims to explicitly integrate the full-order dynamic model of the manipulator into the MPC formulation.

2. **Coupling Locomotion Template Models with Full-Order Arm Dynamics:** The research seeks to combine the computational efficiency of reduced-order locomotion template models (such as the Single Rigid Body, SRB, model) with the detailed representation of full-order manipulator dynamics. The motivation is to retain the benefits of template models for efficient locomotion planning while accurately capturing the complex dynamic interactions introduced by the arm. This is intended to enable robust performance under conditions where the arm's motion significantly influences the robot's overall stability and dynamics.

3. **Achieving Real-Time, Computationally Efficient Control:** High-degree-of-freedom loco-manipulation tasks pose significant computational challenges for real-time control. A key objective is to develop an NMPC framework that can solve the complex optimization problem in real-time (e.g., at 60 Hz), making it practical for online deployment on hardware. This requires a control architecture that balances model fidelity with computational tractability.

4. **Enabling Robust Torque-Level Control for Loco-Manipulation:** Many existing approaches for smaller quadrupeds with arms utilize lightweight, position-controlled manipulators. This work aims to enable dynamic loco-manipulation using heavier, torque-controllable arms. The motivation is to extend the capabilities of mobile manipulators to tasks requiring significant interaction forces, precise force control, and robustness against external disturbances and uncertainties, which are better handled with torque-level control.

5. **Validation on Platforms with Non-Negligible Manipulator Dynamics:** The motivation for selecting the Unitree Go2 quadruped (15 kg) and the Kinova Gen2 4-DoF arm (4.4 kg) as the testbed stems from the fact that the arm's mass constitutes a substantial portion (29%) of the quadruped's mass, and its height ratio is also significant (216%). This setup provides a challenging scenario to validate the proposed framework's ability to handle non-negligible manipulator dynamics, external disturbances, payload variations, and uneven terrains, pushing the robot's physical limits.

In summary, the motivation is to bridge the gap between simplified MPC approaches for loco-manipulation and the demands of real-world tasks involving heavy, torque-controlled manipulators on compact legged platforms, thereby enhancing robustness and expanding the operational envelope of these robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a layered control architecture comprising a high-level Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework and a low-level nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC). This decomposition strategy is designed to manage the complexity of loco-manipulation while maintaining real-time computational efficiency.

#### 3.1. Layered Control Architecture

The control system operates with two main layers:
- **High-level NMPC:** Runs at 60 Hz, performing trajectory optimization for both locomotion and manipulation.
- **Low-level Nonlinear WBC:** Runs at 500 Hz, tracking the NMPC-generated locomotion trajectories with a full-order robot model and directly applying NMPC-generated torque commands for the manipulator.

#### 3.2. High-level NMPC Formulation (60 Hz)

The core of the high-level controller is a unified NMPC framework based on a decomposition strategy.

- **Model Decomposition:**
 - **Locomotion Template Model:** The Single Rigid Body (SRB) model is adopted for locomotion. Its state vector, $x_{SRB}$, includes the robot's center of mass (CoM) position and velocity ($p, \dot{p}$) and body orientation and angular velocity ($\theta, \omega$). The SRB dynamics are governed by Newton-Euler equations, considering ground reaction forces (GRFs) and an interaction wrench ($\lambda_{int}$) from the arm.
 - **Manipulation Model:** A floating-base dynamic model of the 4-DoF Kinova arm is incorporated. Its state vector, $x_{arm}$, includes the arm's base position and orientation ($q_b$) and shape variables ($q_s$) along with their derivatives. The arm's equations of motion are derived using Euler-Lagrange formalism, accounting for mass-inertia, Coriolis, centrifugal, and gravitational effects, input joint torques ($u_{arm}$), and the interaction wrench ($\lambda_{int}$).
 - **Holonomic Constraints:** Rigid contact conditions enforce that the base of the arm remains rigidly connected to the SRB. These constraints, relating the SRB's CoM position and orientation to the arm's base position and orientation, are expressed as $\phi(p, \theta, q_{arm}) = 0$. To enforce these during motion, they are differentiated twice with respect to time, resulting in a dynamic consistency condition $\ddot{\phi}(\cdot) = 0$. This constraint couples the SRB and arm dynamics and implicitly allows for the computation of the interaction wrench $\lambda_{int}$.

- **NMPC Optimization Problem:** The NMPC formulates a nonlinear optimization problem over a control horizon $N=8$ with a sampling time of $T_s = 16.67$ ms (60 Hz).
 - **Decision Variables:** The optimization variables include trajectories of SRB states and GRF inputs ($\xi_{SRB}$), arm states and joint torque inputs ($\xi_{arm}$), and the interaction wrench ($\lambda_{int}$) over the prediction horizon.
 - **Cost Function:** The objective function is a sum of subsystem-specific quadratic costs and a penalty on the interaction wrench: $J_{SRB}(\xi_{SRB}(\cdot)) + J_{arm}(\xi_{arm}(\cdot)) + J_{int}(\lambda_{int}(\cdot))$. Each subsystem cost includes terminal and stage costs, penalizing deviations from reference trajectories for states and the magnitude of control inputs. The interaction wrench cost penalizes its magnitude.
 - **Constraints:**
 - Discrete-time SRB and arm dynamics (Equations (3) and (5)).
 - Holonomic constraint (dynamic consistency condition $\ddot{\phi}(\cdot) = 0$).
 - GRF availability (e.g., for swing/stance phases) for locomotion.
 - Friction cone conditions for SRB GRFs.
 - Admissible torque limits for the arm.
 - **Real-time Implementation:** The nonlinear program (NLP) is solved using the CasADi framework with the IPOPT solver. A warm-start strategy (using the previous solution) and a limit of 10 solver iterations per NMPC cycle are employed to achieve real-time performance. Computational complexity is managed by neglecting the manipulator's Coriolis term and assuming a constant inertia matrix over the control horizon, which is updated at each NMPC cycle. Gravity and holonomic constraints remain nonlinear.

#### 3.3. Low-level Nonlinear WBC for Locomotion (500 Hz)

The optimal state and GRF trajectories generated by the high-level NMPC for the SRB subsystem are passed to a low-level nonlinear WBC, running at 500 Hz.

- **Purpose:** To track the NMPC-prescribed SRB trajectories using the full-order dynamics of the robot and compute the joint-level torques for locomotion.
- **Formulation:** The WBC is formulated as a real-time Quadratic Program (QP).
 - **Objective:** Minimizes a weighted sum of joint-level torques ($u_{full}$), the difference between full-order GRFs ($f_{full}$) and desired GRFs ($f_{des}$ from NMPC), and a defect variable ($\delta$) for virtual constraints.
 - **Constraints:**
 - Output dynamics: Virtual constraints, $y:= y_a - y_{des}$, ensuring position tracking for CoM, Euler angles, and swing foot Cartesian positions. Desired swing foot trajectories are generated using Bézier polynomials and Raibert's heuristics.
 - No slippage condition for stance feet.
 - Feasibility conditions for joint torques ($u_{full}$) and GRFs ($f_{full}$) within their admissible sets and the linearized friction cone.
- **Implementation:** The QP is solved using the qpSWIFT solver.

#### 3.4. Hardware and Simulation Setup

- **Hardware Platform:** Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot (15 kg, 12 actuated DoFs) augmented with a Kinova Gen2 4-DoF manipulator (4.4 kg, torque-controlled) and a 3-fingered gripper.
- **Control Interface:** A Sony DualShock 4 controller provides real-time reference commands for base velocity, orientation, and arm configuration.
- **Computational Infrastructure:** NMPC is executed offboard on a desktop PC, communicating with the robots via Ethernet.
- **Simulation Environment:** RaiSim physics engine is used for numerical validation.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific weighting matrices ($Q, P, R$) and gains ($K_P, K_D$) are tuned for both NMPC and WBC components to balance state tracking, control effort, and interaction force regulation. The friction coefficient ($\mu$) is adjusted based on terrain.

This layered approach, with its decomposition strategy and real-time solvers, aims to enable robust and dynamic loco-manipulation for systems with non-negligible manipulator dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed layered control framework was evaluated through extensive numerical simulations using the RaiSim physics engine and hardware experiments on a Unitree Go2 quadruped augmented with a Kinova Gen2 4-DoF arm.

#### 4.1. Real-Time Computation Performance

The NMPC framework, despite its inherent nonlinearity, was demonstrated to operate in real-time at a frequency of 60 Hz (16.67 ms sampling time). The distribution of NMPC solve times, as shown in Table I, indicates that for challenging scenarios like rough terrain and external disturbances, the majority of solve times (85.6% to 87.7%) fell within the 13-14 ms range, with only a small fraction (around 1%) exceeding the 16 ms budget. No infeasibility was observed during the experiments. The decomposition strategy was identified as critical; integrating full SRB and arm dynamics without it would render real-time NMPC computation impractical without substantial linearizations. The low-level WBC ran at 500 Hz using the qpSWIFT solver.

#### 4.2. Hardware Experiment Demonstrations (Qualitative Results)

The hardware experiments showcased the robustness and versatility of the loco-manipulation system across diverse tasks:

- **Robust Loco-Manipulation on Various Terrains:** The robot successfully walked over unmodeled terrains, including 5 cm-tall wooden blocks (17.9% of Go2’s standing height), grass, and gravel. The NMPC stabilized both the SRB and arm states despite these unstructured conditions, with a human operator providing time-varying reference velocities.
- **Robustness to Unknown Payloads and External Disturbances:**
 - The robot performed locomotion over rough terrain while carrying an unknown 1 kg payload.
 - While carrying the 1 kg payload, the robot withstood external push disturbances applied by a human. Figure 4(a) and (c) illustrate effective tracking of commanded base velocities despite these challenges.
- **Pushing and Pulling Objects:**
 - The robot successfully pushed a 13 kg wagon cart loaded with an additional 11.4 kg payload (125% of the Go2’s base mass). The payload was incrementally added, demonstrating robustness to significant and dynamic mass uncertainties. Figure 4(b) shows the NMPC's velocity tracking during this task.
 - The robot also demonstrated the ability to pull a 3.7 kg carry-on suitcase.
- **Pick-and-Place Tasks:** The system performed various pick-and-place operations, such as moving items between a table and the ground (e.g., puzzle cube, football, first-aid kit). This involved coordinating whole-body locomotion with precise arm movements based on joystick commands.
- **Opening Fridge, Door, and Drawer:**
 - **Fridge Task:** The robot opened a fridge door (requiring substantial force, using the fridge surface for frontal support), grasped a bottle, placed it on a countertop, and closed the door. Figure 5 demonstrates the arm's ability to track desired joint position trajectories during these complex sequences.
 - **Door Opening:** The robot rotated a door handle, pushed open a lab door (without a spring mechanism), and passed through.
 - **Drawer Task:** The robot opened a drawer, grasped a penholder cup, closed the drawer, and placed the cup on a tabletop.
These tasks highlighted the framework's ability to enable teleoperated manipulation, apply end-effector forces, and interact with unmodeled objects in representative home environments.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Comparison and Analysis (Simulations)

To quantitatively assess the proposed decomposition-based NMPC, simulations were conducted comparing its performance against a baseline kino-centroidal NMPC approach (similar to).

- **Scenario:** Robust locomotion of the Go2 with the Kinova arm at 0.5 m/s over 200 randomly generated terrains, each containing 20 blocks of varying heights (1-3 cm), widths (10-20 cm), and lengths (1-7 m).
- **Success Metric:** Defined as the robot successfully traveling 10 meters while maintaining stability.
- **Results:**
 - The proposed decomposition-based NMPC achieved an 86% success rate for robust locomotion.
 - The baseline kino-centroidal NMPC achieved a 28% success rate.
- **Conclusion:** The proposed method demonstrated a 58% higher success rate compared to the baseline. This result indicates that explicitly integrating full-order manipulator dynamics, as enabled by the decomposition strategy, significantly improves robustness for small quadrupedal robots equipped with relatively heavy manipulators (e.g., the 29% arm-to-base mass ratio of Go2 + Kinova arm), outperforming methods that simplify arm dynamics or are designed for heavier quadrupeds with lower arm-to-base mass ratios.

In summary, the main findings confirm that the proposed NMPC framework is computationally efficient, enabling real-time operation, and robustly handles dynamic loco-manipulation tasks under various uncertainties and disturbances on physical hardware. The quantitative comparison further substantiates its improved performance over traditional kino-centroidal approaches for systems with non-negligible manipulator dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of mobile manipulation, particularly for quadrupedal robots operating in dynamic and unstructured environments. The significance and potential impact of this work stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Dynamic Loco-Manipulation with Heavy, Torque-Controlled Arms on Small Quadrupeds:** A primary contribution is the development of a model-based control method that accounts for the full-order dynamics of relatively heavy, torque-controllable manipulators when mounted on compact quadrupedal platforms. Prior work often relied on lightweight, position-controlled arms or was validated on larger quadrupeds with lower arm-to-base mass ratios. This work addresses the challenging scenario of a 4.4 kg Kinova arm on a 15 kg Unitree Go2 robot (an arm-to-base mass ratio of 29%), pushing the physical limits of such integrated systems. This capability broadens the scope of tasks that small, agile quadrupedal robots can undertake.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Uncertainties and Disturbances:** The proposed framework demonstrated robust stability and performance in the presence of unmodeled terrain, unknown payload variations (up to 125% of the robot's base mass for pushing tasks), and external push disturbances. This robustness is critical for deploying robots in real-world, unpredictable environments where perfect models and stable conditions are rare. The quantitative comparison showed an 58% higher success rate in challenging terrains compared to baseline kino-centroidal NMPC, underscoring this enhanced robustness.

3. **Computational Efficiency for Complex Whole-Body Control:** The decomposition strategy, which couples a reduced-order locomotion template model (SRB) with a full-order manipulator dynamic model via holonomic constraints, enables the NMPC to solve complex, nonlinear optimization problems in real-time (60 Hz). This efficiency is crucial for reactive control in dynamic tasks and paves the way for integrating high-fidelity dynamic models into real-world robotic applications that demand rapid decision-making. The ability to handle SRB nonlinearities while selectively simplifying manipulator dynamics contributes to this tractability.

4. **Practical Application to Real-World Tasks:** The extensive hardware experiments showcased the robot's ability to perform diverse and practical loco-manipulation tasks, including walking over rough terrain, pushing/pulling heavy objects, pick-and-place operations, and interacting with common household objects like fridge doors, lab doors, and drawers. These demonstrations highlight the framework's potential for applications in logistics, assistance in home environments, inspection, and other service robotics domains where both mobility and manipulation are required.

5. **Foundation for Future Research in Hybrid Systems Control:** The systematic integration of distinct dynamic models (reduced-order for locomotion, full-order for manipulation) through rigid holonomic constraints provides a methodological blueprint for handling complex hybrid robotic systems. This approach could be extended to other multi-body robotic systems where different subsystems require varying levels of model fidelity for optimal control.

The impact of this work is significant for advancing the capabilities of mobile manipulators, particularly in pushing the boundaries of what small, dynamic legged robots with powerful arms can achieve in unstructured and uncertain environments. While the current validation uses a 4-DoF arm, which can increase operator effort in complex tasks, the framework lays a foundation for future exploration with higher-DoF arms, promising even greater manipulability and versatility.
