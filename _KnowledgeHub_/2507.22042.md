# A Nonlinear MPC Framework for Loco-Manipulation of Quadrupedal Robots with Non-Negligible Manipulator Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Virginia Tech researchers developed a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework that enables quadrupedal robots to perform dynamic loco-manipulation tasks while accounting for the full-order dynamics of relatively heavy, torque-controlled manipulators. This approach achieves real-time control at 60 Hz and demonstrates robust performance on various terrains and with unknown payloads, showing an 58% higher success rate in challenging simulations compared to a kino-centroidal baseline.

## Problem

- Existing Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks for loco-manipulation often simplify or neglect manipulator dynamics, which can limit robustness for systems with high arm-to-base mass ratios.
- Integrating full-order manipulator dynamics with locomotion template models creates a computationally challenging optimization problem that hinders real-time control.
- Robustly controlling quadrupedal robots equipped with heavy, torque-controllable arms under dynamic conditions, external disturbances, and unmodeled environments is a significant challenge.

## Method

- A layered control architecture is proposed, featuring a high-level Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (N**MPC**) running at 60 Hz for trajectory optimization and a low-level nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC) at 500 Hz for torque computation.
- The high-level NMPC employs a decomposition strategy, coupling a Single Rigid Body (SRB) model for locomotion with a full-order floating-base dynamic model for the manipulator via holonomic constraints.
- The NMPC formulates a nonlinear optimization problem solved in real-time using the CasADi framework with IPOPT, employing warm-starting and selective simplification (e.g., constant manipulator inertia over the horizon) to manage computational complexity.

## Results

- The NMPC framework demonstrated real-time operation at 60 Hz, with **85.6%** to **87.7%** of solve times falling within 13-14 ms, without observing any infeasibilities.
- Hardware experiments on a Unitree Go2 with a Kinova arm showed robust loco-manipulation over rough terrains, carrying unknown 1 kg payloads, pushing a **24.4** kg cart, and performing various pick-and-place tasks.
- Simulations revealed that the proposed decomposition-based NMPC achieved an **86%** success rate in robust locomotion over challenging terrains, a **58%** improvement compared to a baseline kino-centroidal NMPC's **28%** success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the robot's dynamics into separate (locomotion and manipulation) models, coupled by rigid holonomic constraints, is crucial for achieving real-time NMPC when manipulator dynamics are non-negligible.
> - Balancing model fidelity by retaining SRB nonlinearities while selectively simplifying manipulator dynamics allows for computationally tractable and robust whole-body control.
> - Explicitly integrating full manipulator dynamics into the MPC framework significantly improves robustness and expands the operational envelope for small quadrupeds with heavy, torque-controlled arms.
