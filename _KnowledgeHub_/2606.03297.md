# SplitAdapter: Load-Aware Humanoid Loco-Manipulation via Factorized Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> SplitAdapter introduces a factorized adaptation framework for humanoid loco-manipulation, enabling robust control of object payloads and diverse task conditions through disentangled contexts. The method demonstrated improved real-world performance on a Unitree G1 robot, achieving 26/27 full-task successes compared to 16/27 for the base policy, especially under heavy, out-of-distribution loads.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face significant sim-to-real transfer challenges in loco-manipulation, particularly with varying object payloads and dynamic mismatches.
- Existing adaptation methods using unified latent representations conflate load-related information with residual dynamics mismatch, leading to reduced robustness under heavy loads.
- Maintaining stable whole-body control for diverse object masses and pickup/placement heights is challenging for current humanoid controllers.

## Method

- Introduced **SplitAdapter**, a factorized adaptation framework augmenting a frozen pretrained humanoid policy with distinct object/load and dynamics-aware contexts.
- Utilizes dual-context history encoding, split world models, and GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization to enforce specialization of the factorized contexts.
- Leverages hierarchical Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) to inject contexts, with object/load contexts modulating earlier policy layers and dynamics-aware contexts refining later layers.

## Results

- Achieved an 86/90 Full-task success rate in simulation, showing improved robustness over baselines, especially for out-of-distribution 6 kg loads.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 robot, attaining 26/27 Full-task successes across varying masses and heights, compared to 16/27 for the base policy.
- The factorized latent space provided a clearer mass-dependent organization, with accurate online mass and loaded-state estimation improving stability under heavy loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing adaptation signals into object/load and dynamics-aware contexts enables more precise and robust policy adjustments than unified latent approaches.
> - Hierarchical FiLM allows for tailored policy modulation, where object/load context influences coarse behaviors and dynamics context refines later actions.
> - GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization helps enforce specialization between distinct latent representations, preventing information overlap.
