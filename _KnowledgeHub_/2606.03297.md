# SplitAdapter: Load-Aware Humanoid Loco-Manipulation via Factorized Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> SplitAdapter introduces a factorized adaptation framework for humanoid loco-manipulation, enabling robust control of object payloads and diverse task conditions through disentangled contexts. The method demonstrated improved real-world performance on a Unitree G1 robot, achieving 26/27 full-task successes compared to 16/27 for the base policy, especially under heavy, out-of-distribution loads.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face significant sim-to-real transfer challenges in loco-manipulation, particularly with varying object payloads and dynamic mismatches.
- Existing adaptation methods using unified latent representations conflate load-related information with residual dynamics mismatch, leading to reduced robustness under heavy loads.
- Maintaining stable whole-body control for diverse object masses and pickup/placement heights is challenging for current humanoid controllers.

## Method

![[2606.03297-method.png]]
*SplitAdapter enables a humanoid robot to handle varying payloads and pickup heights by factorizing adaptation signals into object-specific and dynamics-specific components.*

- Introduced **SplitAdapter**, a factorized adaptation framework augmenting a frozen pretrained humanoid policy with distinct object/load and dynamics-aware contexts.
- Utilizes dual-context history encoding, split world models, and GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization to enforce specialization of the factorized contexts.
- Leverages hierarchical Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) to inject contexts, with object/load contexts modulating earlier policy layers and dynamics-aware contexts refining later layers.

## Results

- Achieved an 86/90 Full-task success rate in simulation, showing improved robustness over baselines, especially for out-of-distribution 6 kg loads.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 robot, attaining 26/27 Full-task successes across varying masses and heights, compared to 16/27 for the base policy.
- The factorized latent space provided a clearer mass-dependent organization, with accurate online mass and loaded-state estimation improving stability under heavy loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing adaptation signals into object/load and dynamics-aware contexts enables more precise and robust policy adjustments than unified latent approaches.
> - Hierarchical FiLM allows for tailored policy modulation, where object/load context influences coarse behaviors and dynamics context refines later actions.
> - GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization helps enforce specialization between distinct latent representations, preventing information overlap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid loco-manipulation has seen advancements with learning-based systems demonstrating capabilities in controlling complex robot behaviors. However, a significant challenge remains in achieving robust sim-to-real transfer for these policies, particularly when dealing with variations in object payload and mismatches in robot and environment dynamics. These factors can alter the required whole-body coordination for tasks like lifting and transporting objects, and can cause policies to behave differently on physical hardware compared to simulation.

Previous adaptation methods frequently utilize recent observation history, encoding various factors into a single latent representation. These approaches often involve techniques such as supervised extrinsics estimation, privileged-state reconstruction, or world-model prediction. While effective for tasks like locomotion and disturbance adaptation, these unified latent approaches can conflate load-related information with residual dynamics mismatch. This can compromise the robustness of policies, especially under conditions involving heavy-load manipulation.

This research addresses these limitations by introducing a factorized adaptation framework. Instead of a single latent representation, it proposes separate context encoders for object/load characteristics and robot-side dynamics. This approach distinguishes itself from existing world-model-based adaptation methods that tend to compress all adaptation factors into a single latent. By explicitly decoupling these aspects, the work aims to enhance the robustness and reliability of humanoid loco-manipulation, particularly in scenarios involving significant payload variations and dynamic uncertainties. The paper builds upon established techniques like AMP-based policy learning (e.g., PhysHSI) and online adaptation through world models (e.g., AnyAdapter), but introduces a specialized architecture to handle the specific challenges of load-aware whole-body control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust control framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that can stably handle varying object masses and diverse pickup/placement heights. This becomes particularly critical in sim-to-real transfer scenarios, where the interplay between object-induced load variations and inherent robot dynamics mismatches presents considerable challenges during physical interaction.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in existing adaptation methodologies. Current history-based adapters often compress all relevant factors—such as object properties, payload, and robot dynamics discrepancies—into a singular latent representation. This compression can dilute the specificity of the adaptation signal, leading to reduced robustness, especially when the robot encounters heavy loads that significantly alter its whole-body dynamics and balance requirements. For instance, a heavy object or a low pickup height demands distinct whole-body coordination patterns, which a mixed latent representation might not adequately capture or differentiate from other dynamic perturbations.

Specifically, the paper seeks to achieve the following:
- **Improve Robustness under Load Variation:** Enhance the humanoid robot's ability to maintain stable whole-body control when manipulating objects of varying masses, including those outside the initial training distribution.
- **Improve Generalization to Diverse Task Conditions:** Enable the robot to perform loco-manipulation tasks effectively across a range of pickup and placement heights, which affect the required lifting postures and center-of-mass management.
- **Address Sim-to-Real Transfer Challenges:** Develop an adaptation mechanism that performs effectively in zero-shot real-world deployment, mitigating the impact of dynamics mismatches between simulation and reality.
- **Factorize Adaptation Signals:** Introduce a novel factorized adaptation structure that disentangles object/load-related information from robot-side dynamics mismatch. This separation is hypothesized to allow for more precise and robust policy adjustments.
- **Enhance Policy Modulation:** Utilize hierarchical Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) to inject the factorized adaptation contexts into a frozen pretrained policy, allowing different levels of the policy to be modulated based on the specific type of context (load vs. dynamics).
- **Validate the Factorization Mechanism:** Demonstrate that the proposed split world-model objectives and GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization effectively promote specialization between the object/load and dynamics-aware context encoders, reducing information leakage between them.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to overcome the limitations of monolithic adaptation strategies and provide a more sophisticated and effective solution for humanoid robots operating in dynamic, real-world manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SplitAdapter, is a load-aware factorized adaptation framework designed to augment a frozen pretrained humanoid box manipulation policy. It achieves this by estimating distinct object/load and dynamics-aware contexts from recent interaction history and injecting these contexts into the policy using hierarchical Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).

#### 3.1. Frozen Base Policy

The foundation of SplitAdapter is a pretrained AMP-based reinforcement learning policy for humanoid box manipulation, similar to PhysHSI. This policy controls the robot by outputting target joint positions, which are then executed by a low-level PD controller. Crucially, after its initial training, this base policy is frozen, meaning its parameters are not updated during the adaptation module optimization.

#### 3.2. Dual-Context History Encoding

To prevent the conflation of object/load effects with robot-side dynamics, SplitAdapter factorizes the adaptation process.
- **History Observation:** At each time step $t$, the adapter observes a recent history $H_t$ consisting of a sequence of policy observations and actions.
- **Shared History Encoder:** A neural network, $f_{hist}$, first processes this history $H_t$ to extract a compact shared representation $e_t$.
- **Factorized Context Branches:** The shared feature $e_t$ is then fed into two distinct context branches:
 - **Object/Load Branch ($f_{obj}$):** This branch outputs an object/load latent $z_{obj,t}$, an explicit estimate of the object's mass $\hat{m}_t$, and an estimate of the robot's loaded state $\hat{\ell}_t$. The effective payload estimate is derived as $m_{eff,t} = \hat{\ell}_t \hat{m}_t$. This branch is trained with supervised losses for mass estimation ($L_{mass}$) and loaded-state prediction ($L_{loaded}$), along with an optional $L_1$ sparsity regularizer ($L_{obj\_sparse}$).
 - **Dynamics-Aware Branch ($f_{dyn}$):** This branch produces a dynamics-aware latent $z_{dyn,t}$. It also includes an $L_1$ sparsity regularizer ($L_{dyn\_sparse}$).

#### 3.3. Split World Models

SplitAdapter employs world-model prediction as an auxiliary task to learn informative history embeddings, extending the concept of dynamics-aware adaptation. Instead of a single general predictor, the prediction target is divided into object transition and robot transition.
- **Object/Load World Model ($W_{obj}$):** This model predicts the object's delta transition ($\Delta\hat{o}_{obj,t+1}$), specifically changes in object position and orientation, based on the current robot state, action, estimated mass and loaded state, and the object/load latent $z_{obj,t}$. The loss $L_{objWM}$ quantifies the accuracy of these predictions.
- **Dynamics-Aware World Model ($W_{dyn}$):** This model predicts the next robot state ($\hat{s}_{t+1}$) conditioned on the current robot state, action, the dynamics-aware latent $z_{dyn,t}$, and the true object mass $m_t$. The loss $L_{dynWM}$ measures the accuracy of the robot state prediction.
Both world models are conditioned on the estimated payload ($m_{est,t}$), which encourages the latent variables to focus on other interaction-related context and residual dynamics mismatch, respectively.

#### 3.4. GRL-Based Cross-Adversarial Separation

To minimize information overlap between the object/load and dynamics-aware contexts, SplitAdapter introduces a Gradient Reversal Layer (GRL)-based cross-adversarial objective.
- **Adversary 1 ($A_{obj \leftarrow dyn}$):** An adversarial predictor attempts to predict the object transition from the dynamics-aware latent $z_{dyn,t}$. The GRL mechanism then discourages $z_{dyn,t}$ from encoding object/load transition information by inverting the gradient during backpropagation.
- **Adversary 2 ($A_{robot \leftarrow obj}$):** Conversely, another adversary attempts to predict the robot transition from the object/load latent $z_{obj,t}$. This discourages $z_{obj,t}$ from encoding robot dynamics and environment mismatch.
This adversarial training, combined with the split world models, aims to enforce specialization of the two branches.

#### 3.5. Hierarchical FiLM-Based Feature Modulation

The factorized contexts adapt the frozen base policy by modulating its intermediate feature representations.
- **FiLM Mechanism:** For a hidden activation $h$, FiLM applies $h' = \gamma(z) \odot h + \beta(z)$, where $\gamma(z)$ and $\beta(z)$ are context-dependent scale and bias terms generated from the latent context $z$.
- **Hierarchical Modulation:**
 - **Object/Load FiLM:** Generated from ($z_{obj,t}, m_{est,t}$), this modulates earlier layers of the frozen policy. This is intended to shape coarse, task-level behaviors like payload-conditioned lifting posture and whole-body coordination.
 - **Dynamics-Aware FiLM:** Generated from $z_{dyn,t}$, this modulates later layers, closer to the action head. This is designed to compensate for finer dynamics and environment mismatches.
The modulation layers are initialized to preserve the original frozen policy's behavior. The adaptation modules are trained using the same reinforcement learning objective and reward function as the base policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated SplitAdapter through sim-to-sim experiments in MuJoCo and zero-shot real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. The experiments aimed to assess performance under payload and pickup/placement-height variations, compare factorized vs. unified latent adaptation, and evaluate sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

The evaluation involved comparing SplitAdapter against a frozen base policy (PhysHSI), an AnyAdapter-style world-model FiLM baseline, and several ablated versions of SplitAdapter (w/o split latent, w/o hierarchical FiLM, w/o GRL). Experiments were conducted across object masses of 2 kg, 4 kg, and 6 kg (with 6 kg being out-of-distribution relative to training range of 0.5-5.0 kg) and pickup/placement heights of 0 cm, 30 cm, and 60 cm. Metrics included "Lift-up success" and "Full-task success" (approach, lift, transport, placement) over ten trials per condition.

- **Overall Performance Improvement:** SplitAdapter achieved higher overall Full-task success (86/90) and Lift-up success (90/90) compared to the base policy (71/90 Full-task, 86/90 Lift-up) and the AnyAdapter-style WM-FiLM baseline (75/90 Full-task, 86/90 Lift-up).
- **Heavy-Load Robustness:** The performance advantage of SplitAdapter was particularly evident in the heavy-load 6 kg conditions. While the base policy and WM-FiLM baseline often lost postural stability during transport and failed before placement, SplitAdapter maintained high success rates across all 6 kg pickup/placement-height variations, including the most challenging 6 kg, 0 cm condition. This suggests its adaptation is more effective under stronger load-induced dynamics changes.
- **Latent Space Organization:** t-SNE visualizations of adaptation representations showed that SplitAdapter's factorized latent space exhibited clearer mass-dependent organization compared to the unified latent baseline, indicating better preservation of load-related variation. Online estimation also demonstrated accurate mass and loaded-state prediction, which further aids policy response.
- **Generalization to Goal Directions:** SplitAdapter maintained stable transport trajectories across diverse goal directions for heavy loads (e.g., 6 kg, 0 cm), whereas the base policy exhibited deviations or failures due to balance disturbances.

- **Effect of Latent Splitting:** The "w/o split latent" variant yielded the lowest Full-task performance among SplitAdapter variants, particularly in the 6 kg regime. This suggests that a unified latent tends to mix load-related and dynamics-related signals, hindering effective adaptation. The factorized design also resulted in lower world-model transition losses for both object/load and robot-transition targets.
- **Effect of GRL:** Removing GRL resulted in a consistent, though smaller, performance drop. This indicates that cross-adversarial regularization contributes to the specialization of the two branches, as evidenced by a larger predictability gap between matched-target and cross-target predictions when GRL was used.
- **Effect of Hierarchical FiLM:** The "w/o hierarchical FiLM" variant showed reduced Full-task success but preserved high Lift-up success. This suggests that hierarchical FiLM is beneficial for the stability of subsequent transport and placement phases under changing load conditions, rather than just the initial lifting action.

#### 4.2. Real-World Evaluation

Zero-shot deployment on the Unitree G1 robot was conducted without fine-tuning. Nine conditions (masses: 2, 4, 6 kg; heights: 0, 30, 60 cm) were tested with three trials per condition.

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The performance improvements observed in simulation translated to real-world deployment.
- **Base Policy Performance:** The base policy performed reliably with 2 kg objects but deteriorated significantly with increasing mass, achieving 16/27 Full-task successes and 22/27 Lift-up successes overall. Failures were pronounced at 6 kg, often involving loss of grasp stability, torso alignment, or whole-body balance during transport and placement.
- **SplitAdapter Performance:** SplitAdapter achieved 26/27 Full-task successes and 27/27 Lift-up successes, demonstrating robustness across all 4 kg conditions and nearly all 6 kg conditions. This confirms that the factorized adaptation improves both initial lift success and, critically, the stability of subsequent transport and placement in real-world heavy-load scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SplitAdapter framework introduces a factorized adaptation approach that offers a notable advancement in the field of humanoid loco-manipulation, particularly for tasks involving varying payloads. By decomposing the adaptation process into object/load-aware and dynamics-aware contexts, the method addresses a core limitation of previous unified latent adaptation strategies, which often struggled with distinguishing and appropriately responding to different sources of environmental and task variation.

The key significance of this work lies in its demonstrated ability to enhance the robustness and reliability of humanoid robots under challenging conditions. The improvements in Full-task success, especially in heavy-load scenarios (e.g., 6 kg objects, which were out of the training distribution), highlight a practical step forward for deploying humanoids in real-world environments. The GRL-based cross-adversarial regularization and hierarchical FiLM modulation contribute to a more specialized and effective adaptation mechanism, allowing for fine-grained control adjustments based on the specific context.

The successful zero-shot transfer of SplitAdapter from simulation to a physical Unitree G1 robot, without additional real-world fine-tuning, indicates a reduction in the sim-to-real gap for complex loco-manipulation tasks. This capability is critical for accelerating the deployment of robotic systems, as it reduces the extensive and often costly real-world experimentation traditionally required.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Humanoid Versatility:** Humanoid robots equipped with SplitAdapter could more reliably perform a wider range of manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, handling objects of diverse masses and properties. This could include applications in logistics, manufacturing, domestic assistance, or disaster response.
- **Improved Safety and Efficiency:** The increased stability during heavy-load transport and placement could lead to safer operations, reducing the risk of dropping objects or robot instability. This also contributes to task efficiency by minimizing failures.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The factorized adaptation paradigm could serve as a basis for developing more sophisticated context-aware control systems for robots, encouraging further exploration into disentangled representations for other complex robotic behaviors.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** The robust sim-to-real transfer capability has the potential to shorten development cycles for robotic applications, as policies can be trained predominantly in simulation with greater confidence in their real-world performance.

While the current study focuses on rigid box manipulation and involves a limited scale of real-world evaluation due to hardware constraints, the principles demonstrated by SplitAdapter suggest a promising direction for creating more intelligent and adaptable humanoid robots capable of interacting dynamically with their physical surroundings. Future work could extend this approach to deformable objects, broader contact-rich manipulation scenarios, and a wider range of humanoid platforms and payloads.
