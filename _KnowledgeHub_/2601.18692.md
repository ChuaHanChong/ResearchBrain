# A Pragmatic VLA Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The LingBot-VLA foundation model empirically demonstrates that real-world VLA performance consistently scales with increasing pre-training data up to 20,000 hours without saturation. It achieves improved performance on 100 real-world manipulation tasks across multiple robotic platforms and exhibits enhanced training efficiency and data-efficient adaptation.

## Problem

- There was a lack of comprehensive empirical studies on how real-robot performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models scales with increasingly massive pre-training datasets.
- The VLA community lacked a highly optimized training codebase capable of efficiently conducting large-scale scaling evaluations on multi-node clusters due to I/O bottlenecks and communication overheads.
- Existing VLA evaluation methodologies often struggle to provide robust, large-scale real-world assessments across diverse robotic platforms and numerous tasks.

## Method

- Developed **LingBot-VLA**, a ==Mixture-of-Transformers== architecture integrating a pre-trained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as its semantic backbone and using ==Flow Matching== for continuous action modeling.
- Assembled a large-scale pre-training dataset of approximately **20,000 hours** of real-world teleoperated manipulation data from **9 distinct dual-arm robot embodiments**.
- Implemented systemic training optimizations including ==Hybrid Sharded Data Parallel==, ==FlexAttention==, and ==operator fusion via `torch.compile`== to enhance throughput and scalability.

## Results

- **LingBot-VLA** with depth achieved an average Success Rate (SR) improvement of **4.28%** and a Progress Score (PS) increase of **7.76%** over the π_0.5 baseline on the GM-100 real-world benchmark.
- The optimized codebase achieved a throughput of **261 samples per second** with an 8-GPU setup, representing a **1.**5x** to **2.8x** speedup** over existing VLA-oriented codebases and exhibiting near-linear scaling up to **256 GPUs**.
- Demonstrated superior data efficiency in post-training, outperforming π_0.5 even with **80 demonstrations** per task compared to π_0.5's full **130 demonstrations**, with the performance gap widening as data increased.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-world VLA performance consistently improves as pre-training data volume scales from 3,000 to 20,000 hours, showing no saturation and indicating continued benefits from larger datasets.
> - Integrating spatial awareness through a vision distillation approach with depth information enhances VLA models' success rates and progress scores in both real-world and simulated environments.
> - An optimized training codebase significantly improves efficiency, delivering substantial throughput speedups and near-linear scaling for large-scale VLA model development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models represents a significant area of research in robotics, aiming to enable robots to perform diverse manipulation tasks through natural language instructions. These models leverage large-scale pre-training to acquire generalizable skills that can be adapted to various tasks and robotic platforms.

Existing VLA foundation models have demonstrated advancements in multi-task execution and adaptability across multiple robot embodiments. However, the field has identified several gaps that limit the progression and practical deployment of these models:
- **Scaling Laws for Real-Robot Performance**: There is a recognized absence of comprehensive empirical studies detailing how real-robot performance scales with increasingly vast pre-training datasets. This lack of understanding impedes systematic development and data curation strategies for future VLA models.
- **Computational Efficiency in Training**: The community lacks highly optimized training codebases that can efficiently process massive volumes of data for scaling evaluations. Training large-scale VLA models on multi-node clusters frequently encounters challenges such as data I/O bottlenecks and communication overheads.
- **Spatial Reasoning and Depth Perception**: While traditional VLA models often excel in semantic understanding, they can exhibit limitations in precise geometric reasoning and depth perception, which are critical for complex spatial manipulation tasks. Efforts have been made to integrate spatial representations, but robust and efficient solutions are still under development.
- **Rigorous Real-world Evaluation**: Current evaluation methodologies for robot policies in real-world settings are often constrained by the need for extensive hardware parallelism. This typically restricts comparative studies to a limited number of methods across a few tasks, potentially leading to incomplete assessments of model generalizability and robustness.

This work, through the introduction of LingBot-VLA, aims to address these identified gaps. It focuses on providing empirical evidence for VLA scaling behavior on real-world data, developing an optimized training infrastructure, enhancing spatial awareness within the model, and establishing a comprehensive evaluation framework for multi-embodiment generalization and real-world robustness. By open-sourcing its components, LingBot-VLA seeks to contribute to the foundational understanding and accelerate the development of generalist robotic policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop LingBot-VLA, a pragmatic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model designed for effective generalization across diverse tasks and robotic platforms, while simultaneously ensuring cost efficiency in terms of data acquisition and computational resources for adaptation.

The specific objectives and underlying motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Systematic Investigation of VLA Scaling Laws**: A central motivation is to conduct a systematic empirical investigation into how real-world VLA model performance, measured by success rates, scales with respect to increasing data volume and diversity during pre-training. This addresses a critical gap in the existing literature regarding the understanding of scaling behaviors in real-world robot learning. The researchers aim to determine if performance improvements saturate at current data scales or continue to benefit from larger datasets, thereby providing empirical evidence crucial for guiding future VLA development and large-scale data curation efforts.
- **Development of a Computationally Efficient Training Codebase**: The efficient training of large-scale VLA models requires robust computational infrastructure. A key objective is to develop a highly optimized training codebase capable of efficiently handling massive volumes of data and supporting large-scale scaling evaluations. This is motivated by the recognized challenges of data I/O bottlenecks and communication overheads in multi-node cluster training environments, which can significantly increase training cycles and computational costs. By developing an efficient codebase, the project aims to lower the barrier to entry for large-scale robot learning research and make VLA development more economical.
- **Enhancing Spatial Awareness for Robust Manipulation**: While existing VLA models demonstrate semantic understanding, precise geometric reasoning and depth perception are often limitations, hindering performance in complex spatial manipulation tasks. The motivation here is to integrate explicit spatial awareness into the LingBot-VLA framework. This is achieved through a vision distillation approach that aligns vision-language model (VLM) embeddings with depth information, thereby enabling more precise perception and execution robustness in varied manipulation environments.
- **Establishing Robust Real-World Evaluation Standards**: To ensure that the developed model is reliable and deployable, a rigorous and comprehensive evaluation framework is necessary. The objective is to conduct a systematic assessment of LingBot-VLA across multiple real robotic platforms and a diverse set of tasks. This is motivated by the limitations of previous VLA evaluations, which were often confined to a few methods or tasks due to hardware constraints. By emphasizing task diversity and multi-platform consistency using a benchmark of 100 tasks, the researchers aim to establish new standards for sound VLA benchmarking in real-world scenarios.
- **Fostering Open Science and Community Advancement**: A final objective is to contribute to the broader scientific community by providing open access to the LingBot-VLA codebase, the trained base model, and the benchmark data. This is motivated by the desire to accelerate progress in robot learning, enable more challenging research tasks, and promote transparent and reproducible evaluation standards within the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing LingBot-VLA involved several stages: large-scale real-world data collection and labeling, a specific model architecture design, optimization for training efficiency, and a multi-faceted evaluation protocol on both real-world and simulation benchmarks.

#### 3.1. Pre-training Dataset

- **Data Collection**: The pre-training dataset was compiled from approximately 20,000 hours of real-world teleoperated manipulation data. This data was collected from nine distinct dual-arm robot embodiments: AgiBot G1, AgileX, Galaxea R1Lite, Galaxea R1Pro, Realman Rs-02, Leju KUAVO 4 Pro (bipedal), Qinglong (humanoid), ARX Lift2, and Bimanual Franka. These embodiments varied in degrees of freedom (6-DoF to 7-DoF arms), camera configurations (e.g., three RGB-D cameras, stereo camera, wrist cameras, head-mounted cameras), and teleoperation methods (VR-based or isomorphic arms).
- **Data Labeling**: A two-stage annotation process was used to generate precise language instructions:
 1. **Video Segment**: Human annotators decomposed multi-view robot videos into clips corresponding to predefined atomic actions. Static frames at the beginning and end of videos were removed to eliminate redundant information.
 2. **Instruction Annotation**: The Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B model was utilized to automatically annotate task and sub-task instructions for the obtained video clips. This automatic annotation was then subjected to human refinement.

#### 3.2. Model Training

- **Architecture**: LingBot-VLA employs a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture, analogous to BAGEL, which integrates a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM) (specifically, Qwen2.5-VL) with an initialized 'action expert' module.
 - **Components Interaction**: Vision-language and action modalities are processed through distinct transformer pathways, interconnected by a shared self-attention mechanism for layer-wise unified sequence modeling. This setup aims to leverage high-dimensional semantic priors from the VLM while minimizing cross-modal interference.
 - **Input Formulation**: The model receives multi-view operational images (three views for dual-arm robots), the task instruction, and robot proprioceptive sequences (initial states and action chunks). The observation context $O_t$ is defined as $[I_1^t, I_2^t, I_3^t, T_t, s_t]$, incorporating image tokens, task instruction, and robot state. The action sequence $A_t = [a_t, a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+T-1}]$ represents a temporal horizon of 50 predicted actions.
 - **Action Modeling**: Flow Matching is utilized for continuous action modeling, aiming to characterize the conditional distribution $p(A_t|O_t)$ by minimizing a Flow Matching objective, $L_{FM}$. This facilitates smooth robotic control.
 - **Causal Attention**: Blockwise causal attention is implemented across three functional blocks: vision-language tokens, robot state, and action tokens. This ensures that the action expert can access all observational knowledge while preventing information leakage from future actions.
 - **Spatial Awareness**: A vision distillation approach, inspired by existing works, is adopted to enhance spatial awareness. Learnable queries corresponding to the three-view images are processed by the VLM and then aligned with depth tokens from LingBot-Depth using a projection layer. A distillation loss $L_{distill}$ is minimized to integrate geometric information, enabling more precise perception.

#### 3.3. Training Efficiency Optimization

Given the high-frequency nature of action data, a focus was placed on developing an efficient training pipeline:
- **Distributed Strategy**: Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP), a PyTorch implementation of ZeRO, is used to shard optimizer states, model parameters, and gradients, thereby reducing GPU memory footprint. A Hybrid Sharded Data Parallel (HSDP)-inspired approach constructs specific "shard groups" for the action expert modules to mitigate communication overhead. A mixed-precision policy is applied, using torch.float32 for reductions and torch.bfloat16 for storage and communication.
- **Operator-Level Optimization**: FlexAttention is leveraged to optimize computations within the multimodal fusion process, which involves sparse attention. Operator fusion (via torch.compile) is also employed to decrease kernel launch overhead and maximize memory bandwidth utilization.

#### 3.4. Experiments and Evaluation Protocol

- **Large-scale Real-world Benchmark**: Evaluation was conducted on 25 physical robots spanning 4 distinct commercial platforms (AgileX, Agibot G1, Galaxea R1Pro, Leju KUAVO 4 Pro), all dual-arm with parallel-jaw grippers and multiple cameras. The GM-100 benchmark, comprising 100 diverse manipulation tasks, was used.
 - **Data for Benchmarking**: For each GM-100 task, 150 raw teleoperated trajectories were collected per task across three platforms. The top 130 trajectories, selected based on execution quality, were used for training. Task objects were standardized, and object poses were randomized to prevent overfitting.
 - **Comparison**: LingBot-VLA (with and without depth integration) was compared against three established VLA models: π
 0.5, GR00T N1.6, and WALL-OSS.
 - **Standardized Conditions**: All models were fine-tuned from publicly available checkpoints using identical post-training pipelines, datasets (130 filtered trajectories per task), and hyperparameters (batch sizes=256, epochs=20). Evaluation was conducted on the same robot units in randomized order, with controlled environmental setups. Each model underwent 15 trials per task-robot pair.
 - **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance was assessed using Success Rate (SR), defined as the proportion of trials completing all task steps within a 3-minute limit, and Progress Score (PS), which measures partial task completion through subtask checkpoints. Termination criteria included three consecutive subtask failures or safety-critical events.
- **Simulation Benchmark**: Simulation performance was evaluated on 50 representative manipulation tasks from the RoboTwin 2.0 suite. Models were fine-tuned on RoboTwin datasets for "clean" scenes (2,500 demonstrations) and "randomized" scenes (25,000 demonstrations), the latter including varied backgrounds, clutter, table perturbations, and lighting.
- **Training Throughput Analysis**: The efficiency of the LingBot-VLA codebase was compared against StarVLA, Dexbotic, and OpenPI using π-like model architectures (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-π and PaliGemma-3B-pt-224-π models) and the Libero dataset. Sample throughput (samples/s) was the primary metric, evaluated across 8 to 256 GPUs.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Scaling Experiments**: A subset of 25 GM-100 tasks was used to analyze the scaling laws of pre-training data, varying dataset duration from 3,000 to 20,000 hours.
 - **Data-efficient Analysis**: Data efficiency in post-training was evaluated on eight GM-100 tasks on the Agibot G1 platform, comparing LingBot-VLA's performance with limited demonstrations (80) against π
 0.5
 using a larger set (130).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provided several key findings regarding the scaling behavior, real-world and simulation performance, training efficiency, and data efficiency of LingBot-VLA.

#### 4.1. Scaling Experiments

- **Favorable Scaling Properties**: The study demonstrated that both the progress rate (PS) and success rate (SR) of LingBot-VLA consistently improved as the pre-training data duration increased from 3,000 hours to 20,000 hours. This upward trend showed no signs of saturation at the 20,000-hour mark. This suggests that larger volumes of real-world pre-training data lead to enhanced generalization and performance across diverse downstream tasks and embodiments. The trends observed across individual embodiments (Agibot G1, AgileX, and Galaxea R1Pro) largely aligned with the aggregated performance, indicating the robustness of this scaling law.

#### 4.2. Comparison on Real-world Benchmark (GM-100)

- **Superior Performance**: On the GM-100 benchmark across four robotic platforms, LingBot-VLA demonstrated performance advantages over compared baselines.
 - LingBot-VLA without depth integration consistently outperformed WALL-OSS and GR00T N1.6 in both Success Rate (SR) and Progress Score (PS).
 - The integration of depth-based spatial information in LingBot-VLA (with depth) further enhanced performance, achieving an average SR improvement of 4.28% and a PS increase of 7.76% over the π
 0.5
 baseline across the three wheeled embodiments.
- **Impact of Pre-training Data Similarity**: GR00T N1.6 exhibited average performance on Agibot G1, AgileX, and Leju KUAVO 4 Pro, but achieved SR and PS comparable to π
 0.5
 on the Galaxea R1Pro platform. This observation was attributed to the extensive inclusion of Galaxea R1Pro data during GR00T N1.6's pre-training, indicating that pre-training on data with high structural similarity to downstream tasks can significantly boost performance on those specific tasks.

#### 4.3. Comparison on Simulation Benchmark (RoboTwin 2.0)

- **Enhanced Multi-task Generalization**: In simulation on the RoboTwin 2.0 suite, LingBot-VLA showed advancements in multi-task settings compared to the π
 0.5
 baseline.
 - LingBot-VLA without depth achieved absolute success rate increases of over 3.76% in clean simulation environments and 8.58% in highly randomized scenarios.
 - With the integration of depth information via learnable query-based alignment, LingBot-VLA further improved, surpassing the baseline model by absolute margins of 5.82% in clean configurations and 9.92% in randomized configurations. This highlights the effectiveness of depth integration in extracting spatial priors for robust performance in varied simulation conditions.

#### 4.4. Training Throughput Analysis

- **High Training Efficiency**: The LingBot-VLA codebase exhibited the fastest training speeds compared to other open-source codebases (StarVLA, Dexbotic, OpenPI) for both Qwen2.5-VL-3B-π and PaliGemma-3B-pt-224-π models.
 - On an 8-GPU setup, it achieved a throughput of 261 samples per second for the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-π model, representing a 1.5x to 2.8x speedup over existing VLA-oriented codebases, depending on the VLM base model.
 - The codebase also demonstrated high scaling efficiency, with aggregate throughput closely tracking the theoretical linear scaling limit as the number of GPUs increased from 8 to 256.

#### 4.5. Data-efficient Analysis

- **Superior Data Efficiency**: In post-training experiments on eight representative GM-100 tasks, LingBot-VLA demonstrated superior data efficiency. With a limited budget of 80 demonstrations per task, LingBot-VLA outperformed π
 0.5
 which utilized a larger set of 130 demonstrations. The performance margin between LingBot-VLA and π
 0.5
 widened as the volume of post-training data increased, indicating LingBot-VLA's ability to achieve robust performance with fewer examples during adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and evaluation of LingBot-VLA present several points of significance for the field of robotic learning and VLA foundation models, with potential impacts across research, development, and real-world application.

- **Empirical Validation of Scaling Laws**: The research provides empirical evidence of favorable scaling properties in real-world robot learning, demonstrating that increasing the volume and diversity of pre-training data consistently improves downstream task performance without saturation up to 20,000 hours. This finding is significant as it provides concrete guidance for future VLA development strategies, particularly regarding investment in large-scale data collection and curation efforts. It suggests that continued data scaling remains a viable path to more capable generalist robot policies.

- **Advancement in VLA Model Capabilities**: LingBot-VLA demonstrates a measurable improvement in both generalizability and performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods in rigorous real-world and simulation benchmarks. The integration of depth information to enhance spatial awareness is a specific methodological contribution that leads to more precise perception and robust execution for complex manipulation tasks. This pushes the technical frontier for VLA models, enabling them to handle a wider range of scenarios and potentially more intricate tasks.

- **Improved Computational Efficiency for Research and Development**: The optimized codebase developed for LingBot-VLA significantly increases training throughput and scalability, achieving a substantial speedup over previous VLA-oriented frameworks. This reduction in training cycles and computational overhead lowers the financial and resource costs associated with large-scale VLA research and development. It democratizes access to advanced robot learning research by making it more feasible for institutions with moderate computing resources to conduct extensive experimentation and scale their models.

- **Establishment of Robust Evaluation Standards**: By conducting a systematic and large-scale assessment across 4 distinct robotic platforms and 100 diverse manipulation tasks using a controlled evaluation protocol, this work contributes to establishing more rigorous and transparent benchmarking standards for VLA models. The commitment to open-sourcing evaluation data and recordings further promotes verifiable benchmarks, which is crucial for fostering fair comparisons and robust scientific progress in the community.

- **Promotion of Open Science and Community Collaboration**: The decision to provide open access to the LingBot-VLA code, base model, and benchmark data is a substantial contribution to open science. This initiative accelerates research by allowing other researchers to build upon this work, replicate results, and explore new directions without proprietary barriers. It explicitly aims to enable the investigation of more challenging tasks and encourage the adoption of sound evaluation practices across the robot learning community.

- **Potential for Real-World Deployment**: The combination of superior performance, broad generalizability across different robot embodiments, and high computational efficiency positions LingBot-VLA as a candidate for practical real-world robotic applications. Its ability to learn from large-scale diverse data and adapt efficiently to new tasks makes it suitable for deployment in various manipulation scenarios where flexibility and robustness are required.

- **Future Research Trajectories**: The work also explicitly outlines future research directions, such as extending the model's versatility by integrating single-arm and mobile robotic data. This suggests a clear pathway for developing even more comprehensive generalist robot policies capable of operating in unconstrained and highly dynamic environments, further broadening the potential impact of VLA models.
