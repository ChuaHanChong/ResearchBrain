# Probabilistically-Safe Bipedal Navigation over Uncertain Terrain via Conformal Prediction and Contraction Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed a framework for probabilistically safe bipedal navigation over uncertain terrain by integrating Conformal Prediction for calibrated uncertainty quantification, an uncertainty-informed Model Predictive Control, and a contraction-based controller with flywheel torque. This approach enables bipedal robots to safely traverse challenging environments while maintaining high per-step invariance probabilities, such as 84.94% at an 85% confidence level on rough terrains.

## Problem

- Bipedal robots struggle with dynamic stability and falls when navigating unstructured terrains with significant elevation uncertainty.
- Existing terrain-adaptive controllers often rely on substantial prior terrain knowledge or fail to formally propagate uncertainty into low-level control, leading to overly conservative navigation.
- A persistent gap exists between simplified high-level planning models (e.g., LIPM) and the complex full-order dynamics of physical robots, causing tracking errors and instability.

## Method

- Quantifies terrain elevation uncertainty using Gaussian Processes with an Attentive Kernel, refined by Split ==Conformal Prediction== to yield tighter, data-calibrated confidence intervals.
- Employs an uncertainty-informed Model Predictive Control (MPC) that generates optimal CoM trajectories and footstep sequences with probabilistic safety constraints derived from Conformal Prediction.
- Designs a low-level robust feedback controller based on Control Contraction Metrics (CCM) and an Augmented LIPM, using flywheel torque to stabilize angular momentum and ensure robust tracking within probabilistically guaranteed Robust Control Invariant (RCI) tubes across hybrid state transitions.

## Results

- Conformal Prediction yielded significantly tighter average uncertainty bounds (e.g., **0.078** at **85%** confidence) compared to standard Gaussian Process bounds (**0.360**), allowing for more practical and less restrictive safety margins.
- The full proposed framework successfully navigated challenging bumpy/rough (T1) and wavy/coarse (T2) terrains, preventing falls and achieving goal reachability in all 15 trials, where baseline methods failed in over 5 trials.
- Achieved high per-step Robust Control Invariant (RCI) tube probabilities of **84.94%** for T1, **77.09%** for T2, and **77.53%** for T3 at an **85%** confidence level, validating the probabilistic safety guarantees for the robot's CoM trajectory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conformal Prediction effectively provides empirically calibrated and significantly tighter uncertainty bounds for terrain elevation, allowing for less conservative yet statistically reliable planning decisions.
> - Integrating probabilistic safety directly into the high-level MPC through CP-informed constraints allows the robot to make more flexible, 'risk-seeking' navigation choices over uncertain regions with quantifiable confidence.
> - Control Contraction Metrics combined with RCI tubes and saltation matrices provide a formal method for guaranteeing robust tracking and forward invariance of the robot's centroidal states, bridging the gap between simplified planning and full-order dynamics under uncertainty.
