# Probabilistically-Safe Bipedal Navigation over Uncertain Terrain via Conformal Prediction and Contraction Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed a framework for probabilistically safe bipedal navigation over uncertain terrain by integrating Conformal Prediction for calibrated uncertainty quantification, an uncertainty-informed Model Predictive Control, and a contraction-based controller with flywheel torque. This approach enables bipedal robots to safely traverse challenging environments while maintaining high per-step invariance probabilities, such as 84.94% at an 85% confidence level on rough terrains.

## Problem

- Bipedal robots struggle with dynamic stability and falls when navigating unstructured terrains with significant elevation uncertainty.
- Existing terrain-adaptive controllers often rely on substantial prior terrain knowledge or fail to formally propagate uncertainty into low-level control, leading to overly conservative navigation.
- A persistent gap exists between simplified high-level planning models (e.g., LIPM) and the complex full-order dynamics of physical robots, causing tracking errors and instability.

## Method

- Quantifies terrain elevation uncertainty using Gaussian Processes with an Attentive Kernel, refined by Split ==Conformal Prediction== to yield tighter, data-calibrated confidence intervals.
- Employs an uncertainty-informed Model Predictive Control (MPC) that generates optimal CoM trajectories and footstep sequences with probabilistic safety constraints derived from Conformal Prediction.
- Designs a low-level robust feedback controller based on Control Contraction Metrics (CCM) and an Augmented LIPM, using flywheel torque to stabilize angular momentum and ensure robust tracking within probabilistically guaranteed Robust Control Invariant (RCI) tubes across hybrid state transitions.

## Results

- Conformal Prediction yielded significantly tighter average uncertainty bounds (e.g., **0.078** at **85%** confidence) compared to standard Gaussian Process bounds (**0.360**), allowing for more practical and less restrictive safety margins.
- The full proposed framework successfully navigated challenging bumpy/rough (T1) and wavy/coarse (T2) terrains, preventing falls and achieving goal reachability in all 15 trials, where baseline methods failed in over 5 trials.
- Achieved high per-step Robust Control Invariant (RCI) tube probabilities of **84.94%** for T1, **77.09%** for T2, and **77.53%** for T3 at an **85%** confidence level, validating the probabilistic safety guarantees for the robot's CoM trajectory.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conformal Prediction effectively provides empirically calibrated and significantly tighter uncertainty bounds for terrain elevation, allowing for less conservative yet statistically reliable planning decisions.
> - Integrating probabilistic safety directly into the high-level MPC through CP-informed constraints allows the robot to make more flexible, 'risk-seeking' navigation choices over uncertain regions with quantifiable confidence.
> - Control Contraction Metrics combined with RCI tubes and saltation matrices provide a formal method for guaranteeing robust tracking and forward invariance of the robot's centroidal states, bridging the gap between simplified planning and full-order dynamics under uncertainty.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of bipedal robot locomotion has made significant strides, yet navigating unstructured, rough, and, crucially, *uncertain* environments remains a formidable challenge. This paper directly addresses this critical gap, positioning itself at the intersection of robust control, machine learning for uncertainty quantification, and hybrid systems theory in robotics.

**Current Limitations in Bipedal Locomotion:**
Existing research on bipedal navigation over rough terrain often falls short in several key areas:
- **Terrain Knowledge:** Many terrain-adaptive controllers (e.g.,) assume substantial prior knowledge of the terrain or make simplified piecewise linear approximations, which are impractical in truly unknown or dynamic environments.
- **Uncertainty Handling:** While some frameworks (e.g.,) incorporate terrain uncertainty into high-level traversability scores, they frequently neglect to propagate these uncertainties formally into footstep-level planning or low-level control, leaving the robot vulnerable to unexpected terrain variations. Other approaches, like and, conservatively bypass uncertain or high-elevation regions, which severely limits the robot's operational range and autonomy.
- **Model Mismatch:** High-level locomotion planning often relies on simplified reduced-order models (ROMs) like the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) for computational tractability (e.g.,). However, bridging the gap between these ROM-generated plans and the complex, full-order dynamics of a physical robot is a persistent problem, leading to tracking errors and instability.
- **Formal Safety Guarantees:** Providing rigorous, provable safety guarantees—especially probabilistic ones—for dynamic, hybrid bipedal systems operating in uncertain environments is an open challenge. Most safety approaches are often heuristic or limited to specific types of disturbances.

**Contributions to the Landscape:**
This paper makes significant advancements by integrating several state-of-the-art techniques to overcome these limitations:
- **Unifying Perception and Control with Probabilistic Safety:** It introduces a novel framework that formally quantifies terrain elevation uncertainty using Gaussian Processes (GP) combined with Conformal Prediction (CP). Unlike traditional GP confidence intervals, CP provides *calibrated*, data-driven uncertainty bounds, which are crucial for realistic probabilistic safety. This uncertainty quantification directly informs both high-level planning and low-level control.
- **Robustness via Contraction Theory:** The work leverages Control Contraction Metrics (CCMs) and contraction theory to design a robust feedback controller. This is a significant extension, as previous applications of contraction theory in robotics often focused on high-level motion planning for obstacle avoidance (e.g.,), not explicitly for the phase-portrait analysis of bipedal dynamics at the low-level to minimize ROM-full-order model discrepancies.
- **End-to-End Safety Guarantees:** By coupling CP-informed uncertainty bounds with contraction-based reachable tubes (RCI tubes), the framework offers probabilistic safety guarantees for footstep placement (high-level) and ensures the forward invariance of centroidal states (low-level), meaning the robot's actual trajectory is guaranteed to remain within a safe region around the desired plan with a specified confidence. This moves beyond simply avoiding obstacles to guaranteeing dynamic stability.
- **Addressing Model Mismatch:** The use of an Augmented LIPM with flywheel torque control and the formal analysis of disturbance propagation through saltation matrices for hybrid systems explicitly tackle the problem of model mismatch and discrete transitions inherent in bipedal locomotion, providing a more robust link between planning and execution.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries by allowing bipedal robots to traverse a *broader range* of uncertain terrains than previously possible, all while providing *formal, probabilistic guarantees* of dynamic stability and goal reachability. It represents a significant step towards truly autonomous and reliable bipedal navigation in complex, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **probabilistically safe planning and control strategies** that enable bipedal robots to traverse rough terrain with significant elevation uncertainty. The motivation stems from the inherent challenges and limitations of current bipedal locomotion systems in such environments.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Ensuring Dynamic Feasibility and Centroidal Robustness under Uncertainty:** Bipedal robots are inherently dynamic and unstable. Terrain uncertainty, where the actual ground elevation deviates from the robot's perception, can easily lead to loss of balance and falls. The core motivation is to ensure that even with uncertain terrain, the robot's locomotion remains dynamically feasible and its centroidal momentum is robustly regulated.
- **Providing Quantifiable Safety Guarantees:** Many existing approaches are heuristic or offer limited safety assurances. A central objective here is to introduce a formal way to quantify safety—specifically, a *probabilistic* measure of safety—such that the robot can operate with a user-specified confidence level. This is crucial for deployment in safety-critical applications.
- **Bridging High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control:** There's a common disconnect between simplified high-level planners (e.g., using LIPM) and complex full-body low-level controllers. The research aims to integrate these layers seamlessly, ensuring that the high-level plans are not only achievable but also robustly tracked by the full-order robot dynamics, even in the presence of terrain perturbations and model mismatches.
- **Enabling Traversal of Challenging and Uncertain Regions:** Current methods often lead to conservative navigation strategies, forcing robots to avoid highly uncertain or steep terrains. A key objective is to empower robots to confidently explore and traverse a wider range of such challenging environments, maximizing their utility in unstructured settings.
- **Leveraging Data for Better Uncertainty Quantification:** Standard model-based uncertainty quantification can be overly conservative. The motivation to use Conformal Prediction is to obtain tighter, more data-calibrated confidence intervals for terrain elevation, making the planning and control strategies more efficient and less restrictive.
- **Stabilizing Angular Momentum Induced by Terrain Variations:** Variations in terrain directly impact the robot's angular momentum, which, if not properly managed, can lead to instability. A specific goal is to design a control law that actively stabilizes the centroidal angular momentum, compensating for these terrain-induced perturbations.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for more intelligent, robust, and certifiably safe bipedal robots capable of operating autonomously in complex, real-world environments where perfect terrain knowledge is unavailable. It seeks to provide the theoretical and practical tools necessary to achieve this through a novel integration of probabilistic safety, robust control, and advanced terrain modeling.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates several advanced techniques across perception, planning, and control to achieve probabilistically safe bipedal navigation over uncertain terrain.

#### 3.1. Terrain Uncertainty Quantification (Section IV.B)

- **Gaussian Process (GP) Regression with Attentive Kernel (AK):** To model the terrain elevation map, the authors employ GP regression. They specifically use an Attentive Kernel (AK), which is designed to adapt to spatial variability in nonstationary terrain, providing more accurate predictions than traditional stationary kernels (e.g., RBF). The GP provides a mean elevation estimate and a variance measure at any given point.
- **Conformal Prediction (CP):** To address the limitations of GP confidence intervals (which can be overly conservative with sparse data or miscalibrated due to kernel assumptions), the authors couple the GP model with Split Conformal Prediction (CP). CP constructs data-calibrated confidence intervals (I_δ) that capture the true terrain elevation with a user-specified probability (1-δ). This is achieved by using a calibration dataset to empirically determine a nonconformity score threshold (C), resulting in tighter, yet statistically guaranteed, prediction intervals.
- **CP Safe Footstep Constraint (Lemma 1):** The calibrated CP intervals are then used to formulate a probabilistic safety constraint for footstep placement. For a given maximum feasible height change (Δh_max) between consecutive footsteps, Lemma 1 proves that by enforcing a modified constraint `c(z_q, z'_q+1) ≥ L*C` (where `z_q` is current height, `z'_q+1` is predicted next foot height, `L` is a Lipschitz constant, and `C` is the CP threshold), the true terrain height change will remain within the feasible limit with a probability of at least 1-δ. This is critical for high-level planning.

#### 3.2. High-Level Uncertainty-Informed Model Predictive Control (MPC) (Section IV.D)

- **Reduced-Order Robot Model:** The high-level planner utilizes a Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) to describe the Center-of-Mass (CoM) dynamics. Specifically, an Angular Momentum LIP (ALIP) model is used for sagittal dynamics, with lateral dynamics handled separately. This reduced-order model provides computational efficiency for long-horizon planning. The model is discretized to define states at foot-switching instants, incorporating estimated terrain slopes `∇_x,y μ(x_q, y_q)`.
- **MPC Formulation:** A finite-horizon MPC problem is formulated to generate optimal CoM trajectories and footstep sequences. The objective function penalizes the distance to the goal, heading error, and—crucially—the norm of the terrain traversal slope `∥∇_x,y μ(x_q, y_q)∥`, encouraging traversal over smoother regions.
- **Key Constraints:**
 - **CP-informed Safe Footstep Constraint (20d):** Directly integrates the probabilistic safety from CP, ensuring that planned footstep height changes are safe with confidence 1-δ.
 - **Orbital Energy Constraint (20f):** Enforces that the robot's orbital energy remains positive throughout each step. This ensures that the CoM has sufficient momentum to escape the gravitational pull of the current stance foot, preventing falls and guaranteeing stability in the phase-space. This constraint is extended to the RCI tube to ensure the *entire tube* remains in the stable region.

#### 3.3. Low-Level Robust Control via Contraction Analysis (Section IV.C)

- **Augmented LIP Model (Aug-LIPM):** For the low-level control and robust tracking, an Augmented LIP Model is used (Equation 5). This model extends the basic LIPM by incorporating a flywheel torque (τ_y) and explicit disturbance inputs (w), allowing for robust control design.
- **Disturbance Bounds:** The CP-derived terrain uncertainty is translated into a bounded disturbance `w_terrain` for the Aug-LIPM. This disturbance accounts for deviations between the estimated and true terrain height affecting the CoM dynamics. Additionally, a `w_model` disturbance term is introduced to represent discrepancies between the ROM and the full-order robot dynamics (Assumption 1).
- **Control Contraction Metrics (CCM):** CCMs are employed to design a feedback control law that ensures incremental stability. For the Aug-LIPM, a specific flywheel torque control law (`τ_CCM_y`, Equation 17) is derived. This controller exponentially stabilizes the actual robot CoM sagittal trajectory towards the desired trajectory prescribed by the high-level MPC planner.
- **Robust Control Invariant (RCI) Tubes:** Using the CCM and the disturbance bounds (`w_terrain`), time-varying ellipsoidal RCI tubes (`Ω(x_loc,⋆, t)`) are constructed around the desired MPC CoM trajectories (Definition 3). These tubes guarantee that if the robot's state starts within the tube, it will remain within the tube throughout a walking step with a probability of at least 1-δ (Lemma 2).
- **Saltation Matrix for Tube Propagation:** Bipedal locomotion is a hybrid system with discrete events (foot-switching). The saltation matrix is used to formally propagate the RCI tube bounds across these discrete transitions, ensuring the robust invariance property holds across multiple steps. Lemma 3 extends the probabilistic safety guarantee over multiple steps.

#### 3.4. Full-Order Dynamics and Integration with MuJoCo Simulation

- **Full-Body Robot Model:** The framework is validated on a full-order dynamics model of the Digit bipedal robot in MuJoCo.
- **Low-Level Actuation:** The ALIP planner generates desired foot placements. A passivity-based controller with ankle actuation is used for joint-level control. The `τ_CCM_y` torque is applied directly at the robot's CoM within the MuJoCo simulation environment to correct for off-nominal angular momentum.
- **Additive Disturbances:** The error dynamics between the full-order model and the ROM are analyzed, showing that the CCM controller can handle both terrain-induced disturbances (`w_terrain`) and model mismatch (`w_model`) by ensuring convergence to a disturbance-invariant set.

In essence, the methodology forms a tightly integrated, hierarchical control system. CP quantifies uncertainty from perception, which informs an MPC planner with probabilistic safety constraints. This planner generates nominal trajectories, which are then robustly tracked at the low level by a contraction-based controller that maintains the robot's state within probabilistically guaranteed invariant tubes, even under terrain and model uncertainties.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed framework is rigorously evaluated through physics-based simulations of the Digit bipedal robot in MuJoCo across various challenging terrains. The results consistently demonstrate the framework's superior performance in terms of stability, tracking accuracy, and safety guarantees.

#### 4.1. Conformal Prediction Effectiveness

- **Tighter Uncertainty Bounds:** A crucial finding is that Conformal Prediction (CP) successfully yields significantly tighter and less conservative confidence intervals for terrain elevation compared to standard Gaussian Process (GP) bounds. For example, at 85% confidence, the average CP threshold was C_AVG = 0.078, while the GP bound was 0.360. At 99.5% confidence, C_AVG = 0.060, while GP bound was 0.708. This indicates that CP provides more practical and less restrictive uncertainty estimates while maintaining the desired high confidence level. This is vital for enabling less conservative robot behavior.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Stability and Goal Reachability

- **Prevention of Falls on Challenging Terrains:** On terrains T1 (bumpy and rough) and T2 (wavy and coarse), the baseline MPC and the proposed framework *without* the `τ_CCM_y` (flywheel torque control) failed to reach the goal in more than 5 out of 15 trials. In contrast, the full proposed framework (with `τ_CCM_y`) successfully navigated these terrains, demonstrating its ability to prevent falls and achieve goal reachability in highly challenging environments. This highlights the critical role of the contraction-based torque control in maintaining stability against sudden terrain variations.
- **Improved Tracking and Efficiency on Moderate Terrains:** On terrain T3 (hilly with smoother surface), the full framework reduced the Average Norm Error (ANE) between the actual and desired CoM trajectories compared to the baseline and the variant without `τ_CCM_y` (0.039 vs. 0.051 and 0.052, respectively). This indicates more precise tracking of the MPC plan. Furthermore, for T3, both simulation time and the average number of steps were reduced compared to the baseline, suggesting more efficient traversal.

#### 4.3. Validation of Probabilistic Safety Guarantees (RCI Tube Invariance)

- **High Per-Step Invariance:** The proposed framework achieves high probabilities of the robot's CoM trajectory remaining within the Robust Control Invariant (RCI) tube, closely matching or exceeding the user-defined confidence level. For example, at an 85% confidence level, the probabilities were 84.94% for T1, 77.09% for T2, and 77.53% for T3. Even with model mismatch, the system largely preserves tube invariance. This significantly outperforms baseline methods (which failed or showed much lower invariance, e.g., 58.03% and 54.75% for T3), validating the probabilistic safety guarantees provided by the integrated CP and contraction analysis.
- **Compounded Safety for Shorter Horizons:** On smaller terrains (T4, T5) where a higher 99.5% confidence level was tested, the framework achieved over 92% per-step tube invariance. This translates to a compounded safety probability of approximately 90.5% for 20 consecutive steps ((0.995)^20 ≈ 0.905), strongly guaranteeing that the robot tracks the full, safe MPC plan over a practical short horizon.

#### 4.4. Impact of CP-informed MPC

- **Risk-Seeking Navigation:** The CP-informed footstep constraint (20d) in the MPC formulation enables the robot to adopt a "risk-seeking" strategy. Instead of conservatively avoiding uncertain regions, it can plan footsteps into areas with higher perceived uncertainty, provided that the true terrain variations are still probabilistically contained within the CP coverage intervals. This allows for more flexible and potentially faster traversal paths.

Overall, the results demonstrate that the combination of CP for data-calibrated uncertainty, an uncertainty-informed MPC, and a contraction-based flywheel torque controller is highly effective. It not only ensures probabilistic safety and dynamic stability but also improves tracking performance and enables more efficient navigation in complex and uncertain environments, showcasing a robust and verifiable approach to bipedal locomotion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of bipedal robotics, with profound implications for the capabilities and deployment of legged robots in real-world, unstructured environments.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Bipedal Autonomy in Challenging Environments

The most immediate impact is the ability for bipedal robots like Digit to reliably and safely navigate terrains with significant elevation uncertainty. Previously, such environments often necessitated conservative strategies or led to robot failure. This work empowers robots to traverse rough, bumpy, and sloped terrains with a quantifiable level of confidence, thereby expanding the operational envelope for inspection, search-and-rescue, logistics, and exploration tasks in real-world scenarios that are inherently uncertain.

#### 5.2. Groundbreaking for Formal Safety Guarantees in Robotics

This paper makes a pivotal contribution to the long-standing challenge of providing formal safety guarantees for dynamic, hybrid robotic systems. By integrating Conformal Prediction for robust uncertainty quantification with Control Contraction Metrics for robust tracking and invariant tube guarantees, the authors introduce a novel framework for *probabilistic safety certification*. This move from deterministic (often overly conservative) or heuristic safety measures to statistically rigorous, data-calibrated probabilistic guarantees is a critical step towards deploying truly trustable autonomous systems, particularly in human-robot interaction or safety-critical applications.

#### 5.3. Unified and Robust Planning and Control

The seamless integration of high-level probabilistic planning with low-level robust control is highly significant. The framework elegantly bridges the gap between abstract motion plans (from reduced-order models) and the complex realities of full-order robot dynamics. This holistic approach ensures that perceived uncertainties at the planning stage are actively managed and compensated for at the control execution level, leading to a far more resilient and reliable system. The use of a flywheel torque controller, specifically designed through contraction analysis, to mitigate terrain-induced angular momentum perturbations is a practical and effective solution for real-time dynamic stabilization.

#### 5.4. Practicality and Less Conservative Behavior

The adoption of Conformal Prediction is a key practical innovation. By producing tighter, data-calibrated uncertainty bounds, CP allows the robot to make less conservative decisions and potentially explore more aggressive or efficient paths, compared to methods relying solely on model-based (e.g., GP variance) or overly broad uncertainty estimates. This "risk-seeking" capability, backed by probabilistic guarantees, can significantly improve task completion time and energy efficiency.

#### 5.5. Advancing Contraction Theory for Hybrid Robotics

The application of contraction theory for phase-portrait analysis in bipedal locomotion, especially considering discrete transitions via saltation matrices, is a notable theoretical advancement. This work demonstrates a concrete and successful use case for extending contraction theory to analyze and guarantee robustness in complex, hybrid robotic systems, opening new avenues for research in formal methods for robot control.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The framework lays a strong foundation for future work, including:
- Validation on physical hardware to demonstrate real-world applicability.
- Incorporating other sources of uncertainty (e.g., actuation limits, friction coefficient variations, sensor noise) into the probabilistic safety framework.
- Exploring adaptive confidence levels based on environmental conditions or task criticality.
- Generalizing the approach to other legged robots or more complex manipulation tasks under uncertainty.

In conclusion, this paper provides a comprehensive and theoretically sound framework that significantly enhances the safety, robustness, and autonomy of bipedal robots navigating uncertain terrains. Its innovative blend of machine learning for uncertainty quantification, probabilistic safety guarantees, and robust control theory marks a substantial contribution to the field of robotics and sets a new benchmark for reliable robot deployment in challenging environments.
