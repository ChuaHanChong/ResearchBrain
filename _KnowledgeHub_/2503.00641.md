# How to Probe: Simple Yet Effective Techniques for Improving Post-hoc Explanations

> [!summary] Summary
> Post-hoc explanation quality for deep neural networks can be significantly improved by modifying how the classification layer (probe) is trained, with the study showing that Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss consistently enhances explanations over Cross-Entropy (CE) loss across various models and pre-training paradigms.

## Problem

- Deep Neural Networks often operate as 'black boxes', lacking transparency in their decision-making processes, which hinders their application in high-stakes domains.
- Existing post-hoc explanation methods are commonly assumed to be independent of model training details, an assumption that has been largely underexplored.
- The impact of diverse pre-training paradigms (e.g., self-supervised, vision-language) on the interpretability of models remains insufficiently understood.

## Method

- The authors employed ==linear probing==, freezing the model backbone and training various classification heads (probes) on the frozen features.
- They systematically compared standard ==Cross-Entropy (CE) loss== with ==Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss== for training the classification head.
- Explanation quality was evaluated using a diverse set of interpretability metrics, including object localization, pixel deletion, compactness, and complexity.

## Results

- Training the classification head with Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss **consistently and significantly** improved explanation quality across various interpretability metrics.
- The findings hold robustly across diverse pre-training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, vision-language), model architectures (CNNs, vision transformers), attribution methods, and datasets.
- Using interpretable B-cos MLPs as non-linear probes led to increased localization performance, demonstrating their compatibility with modern pre-training paradigms while preserving interpretability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of post-hoc attributions for pre-trained models heavily depends on how the classification head is trained, even when the model backbone remains frozen.
> - Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for the classification head consistently yields superior explanation quality compared to standard Cross-Entropy (CE) loss, which is hypothesized to be due to CE's shift-invariance.
> - More complex probes, such as interpretable B-cos MLPs, can effectively distill class-specific features from pre-trained representations, enhancing localization performance.
