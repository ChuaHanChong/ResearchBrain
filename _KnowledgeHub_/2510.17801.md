# Robobench: A Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models as Embodied Brain

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboBench introduces a comprehensive evaluation benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) acting as embodied brains, assessing five cognitive dimensions through real-world robotic data and a novel MLLM-as-world-simulator for planning. The benchmark reveals that current MLLMs still lag behind human performance across all evaluated cognitive abilities, particularly in complex planning and execution failure analysis.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for evaluating MLLMs as embodied "brains" offer fragmented coverage, assessing isolated cognitive abilities rather than integrated systems.
- Many current benchmarks lack realism and complexity, relying heavily on simulated environments and overlooking practical challenges like diverse robot embodiments and memory-driven navigation.
- Traditional planning evaluation methods are often superficial, using text similarity or multiple-choice formats, which fail to capture the physical feasibility and causal constraints of embodied actions.

## Method

- RoboBench establishes a comprehensive benchmark structured around five interdependent cognitive dimensions: instruction comprehension, perception reasoning, generalized planning, affordance prediction, and failure analysis.
- It curates datasets from large-scale real robotic data and in-house collections, encompassing diverse embodiments and complex real-world scenarios to enhance realism and reduce the sim-to-real gap.
- The benchmark innovates planning evaluation by proposing an "MLLM-as-world-simulator" framework to rigorously assess the embodied feasibility of plans by simulating object-state changes under physical and visual constraints.

## Results

- Gemini-2.5-Pro, while the top-performing MLLM, achieved **62.96%** in perception reasoning and **45.14%** in failure analysis, significantly behind human scores of **74.30%** and **63.99%** respectively.
- Closed-source MLLMs consistently outperformed open-source models by 10-**15%** across most evaluation dimensions, and performance generally improved with increased model size.
- The "MLLM-as-world-simulator" planning evaluation framework demonstrated strong alignment with human judgment, achieving a Pearson correlation coefficient of r=0.83, validating its effectiveness in assessing plan feasibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs possess only superficial embodied reasoning and world-modeling capabilities, consistently lagging behind human performance across all cognitive dimensions evaluated.
> - Specialized embodied training, as seen in RoboBrain-2.0-7B, effectively enhances multimodal reasoning and planning performance for robotics compared to general-purpose MLLMs of similar size.
> - Implicit instruction comprehension, fragile spatial-temporal perception, and complex long-horizon planning remain fundamental challenges for MLLMs, with execution failure diagnosis proving particularly difficult.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent robots capable of operating autonomously in complex, unstructured real-world environments is a grand challenge in artificial intelligence. This endeavor necessitates not only advanced motor control but also sophisticated cognitive abilities such as understanding instructions, perceiving dynamic surroundings, planning long-horizon actions, inferring object affordances, and diagnosing failures. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged as highly promising candidates for these high-level cognitive roles, often envisioned as the "System 2" in a dual-system robotic architecture, where System 1 handles low-level control.

However, the current research landscape for evaluating MLLMs as embodied "brains" suffers from significant limitations. Existing benchmarks predominantly focus on overall task success, offering limited insights into the underlying reasoning processes. Even those targeting embodied cognition typically exhibit three key shortcomings:
1. **Fragmented Coverage:** Most benchmarks assess isolated cognitive abilities like perception, planning, or error reflection in silos, failing to evaluate them as an integrated whole essential for seamless embodied interaction.
2. **Limited Task Realism and Complexity:** Many rely heavily on simulated environments, leading to a substantial "sim-to-real" gap. They often overlook practical challenges such as diverse robot embodiments, rich object properties, multi-view scene understanding, and memory-driven navigation.
3. **Simplistic Planning Evaluation:** Current planning evaluation methods often reduce complex reasoning to multiple-choice questions, text similarity metrics (e.g., BLEU), or subjective LLM-based scoring. These approaches fail to capture critical embodied priors, such as skill-object dependencies, execution-order flexibility, and most importantly, the physical feasibility of a predicted plan in a real-world context.

RoboBench directly addresses these critical gaps. By introducing a comprehensive benchmark that systematically evaluates MLLMs across five interdependent cognitive dimensions—instruction comprehension, perception reasoning, generalized planning, affordance prediction, and failure analysis—it moves beyond fragmented assessments. Furthermore, its emphasis on curating datasets from large-scale real robotic data and in-house collections, encompassing diverse embodiments and complex real-world scenarios, significantly enhances realism. Crucially, RoboBench innovates in planning evaluation by proposing an "MLLM-as-world-simulator" framework. This framework goes beyond symbolic matching to rigorously assess the embodied feasibility of plans by simulating object-state changes under physical and visual constraints, thus providing a much more faithful assessment of long-horizon reasoning. In essence, RoboBench seeks to define a new standard for evaluating the cognitive core of robotic systems, bridging the gap between advanced MLLM capabilities and the demands of robust, generalizable embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish RoboBench, a comprehensive and systematic evaluation benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) acting as the "embodied brain" in robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from several critical challenges and emerging opportunities in embodied AI:

1. **Addressing Core Robotic Challenges:** The overarching goal in robotics remains the creation of intelligent agents that can robustly perceive, reason, and act in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments. This requires high-level cognitive functions, which current MLLMs are beginning to exhibit.
2. **Leveraging MLLMs as the "Embodied Brain":** Recent advancements have positioned MLLMs as the "System 2" in a dual-system robotic architecture, handling high-level reasoning and decision-making (the "embodied brain"), while System 1 manages low-level control. This paradigm shift highlights the necessity for rigorous evaluation of MLLMs in this crucial cognitive role.
3. **Identifying Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** The authors identify significant shortcomings in existing embodied AI benchmarks:
 - **Incomplete Cognitive Coverage:** Current benchmarks often focus on isolated capabilities (e.g., only perception or only planning) rather than assessing the holistic cognitive abilities required for complex manipulation tasks.
 - **Lack of Realism and Complexity:** Many benchmarks rely on simplified simulations or datasets that do not fully capture the diversity of embodiments, attribute-rich objects, multi-view scenarios, and memory-driven navigation found in real-world robotic operations. This limits their ability to accurately reflect the performance of MLLMs in practical settings.
 - **Inadequate Planning Evaluation:** Existing methods for evaluating long-horizon planning are often superficial, relying on metrics like text similarity or multiple-choice formats, which fail to account for the physical and causal constraints inherent in embodied action. They do not effectively assess the *embodied feasibility* of a generated plan.
4. **Guiding Future MLLM Development:** By systematically uncovering the strengths and weaknesses of current state-of-the-art MLLMs as embodied brains, RoboBench aims to provide actionable insights. These insights are crucial for guiding researchers and developers in building next-generation MLLMs that are more robust, generalizable, and genuinely intelligent for robotic applications.
5. **Establishing a Unified Evaluation Scaffold:** Ultimately, RoboBench seeks to provide a standardized, comprehensive framework that allows for fair comparison and advancement across various MLLMs, fostering progress in the field of embodied AI by clearly defining and measuring high-level robotic cognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboBench adopts a multi-faceted methodology to comprehensively evaluate MLLMs as embodied brains, structured around five core cognitive dimensions, 14 capabilities, and 25 tasks, utilizing 6092 QA pairs. The approach emphasizes realism in data, breadth in cognitive coverage, and a novel, rigorous method for planning evaluation.

#### 3.1. Core Capabilities and Dimensions

The benchmark defines five interdependent cognitive dimensions, mirroring the full robotic manipulation pipeline (from intent interpretation to failure recovery):

1. **Embodied Instruction Comprehension:** Evaluates the MLLM's ability to understand human intentions, covering both explicit (clearly specified actions) and implicit (demand-based requests like "I'm thirsty") instructions. This assesses the grounding of indirect human goals.
2. **Embodied Perception Reasoning:** Focuses on the MLLM's capacity to extract task-relevant information from the environment. It spans four aspects: robotic-centric (embodiment type, viewpoint), object-centric (static/functional attributes), scene-centric (spatial relations, temporal grounding, causality), and task-centric (identifying instruction-relevant objects).
3. **Embodied Generalized Planning:** Assesses the MLLM's ability to decompose long-horizon goals into sequential steps and adapt plans across diverse scenarios. This includes cross-embodiment (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile manipulators, humanoid), cross-object (material, physical, world knowledge), cross-view (multi-view integration under occlusion), and cross-task (navigation planning from video) generalization.
4. **Embodied Affordance Prediction:** Evaluates the MLLM's capability to refine high-level subgoals into precise spatial cues for low-level execution. This covers static (contact points), dynamic (motion trajectories), and navigation (robot base positions) affordances.
5. **Embodied Failure Analysis:** Measures the MLLM's proficiency in detecting, diagnosing, and suggesting corrections for errors during execution. This dimension distinguishes between low-level execution errors (e.g., position misalignment, gripper failures) and high-level planning errors (e.g., wrong object, missing steps).

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction and Dataset

RoboBench's datasets are meticulously curated to ensure realism and diversity:

- **Data Sources:** Combines large-scale real-world robotic datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, RH20T) with in-house collected data, reducing the sim-to-real gap.
- **Annotation Pipeline (Figure 3):** A multi-stage process involving:
 - **Preprocessing:** Initial video and image processing.
 - **Annotation:** Enriched with annotations generated by MLLMs (e.g., Gemini for video segmentation, GPT for functional properties) and refined by human experts.
 - **Q&A Generation:** Standardized into diverse question formats (multiple-choice, planning, point prediction).
- **Task Specifics:**
 - **Instruction:** Paired explicit-implicit instruction design for planning tasks.
 - **Perception:** QA pairs generated from real robot data with metadata, curated object attributes, Gemini-segmented videos for temporal grounding, and human-labeled bounding boxes.
 - **Planning:** Uses high-quality robotic videos. Gemini generates structured annotations (task summaries, step-wise instructions, metadata), refined by humans. Steps are mapped to function templates (e.g., `pick_up(object)`). Includes Q1 (long-horizon), Q2 (next-step), and Q3 (task state estimation) tasks.
 - **Affordance:** Representative frames from the planning pool are sampled and annotated with static, dynamic, and mobile affordance types.
 - **Failure Analysis:** Execution errors from real-world data (RoboMIND) labeled by experts; planning errors synthesized by perturbing correct instructions.
- **Quality Control:** A two-stage process involving data filtering during construction (general and task-specific criteria) and post-construction validation by 20 professionally trained annotators. A majority-vote strategy is employed to refine and correct items.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

Metrics are tailored to each dimension:

- **Perception Reasoning & Failure Analysis:** Multiple-choice accuracy.
- **Affordance Prediction:** Euclidean distance for point prediction and RMSE for trajectory prediction, transformed into a normalized score.
- **Generalized Planning (Q1 - Long-horizon planning):** This is the most novel aspect, using an **"MLLM-as-world-simulator"** framework (Figure 5):
 - Tasks are modeled as a partially ordered set of atomic actions with inter-step constraints represented by a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG).
 - The MLLM acts as a world simulator to assess *embodied feasibility*.
 - **NodeCorrectness:** Measures the exact alignment of predicted skills, objects, and parameters with ground truth nodes.
 - **TaskCompletion:** An MLLM simulates the rollout of predicted plans, checking preconditions and updating a world state `W_t`. It detects critical object-level state changes (milestones, `S_star`) and verifies if the predicted plan achieves them under visual and physical constraints. This goes beyond symbolic matching to evaluate if a plan can *actually* cause the desired physical changes.
- **Generalized Planning (Q2 - Next-step planning):** MLLM judges the next action against ground truth, scoring skill, object, and parameters for reasonableness.
- **Generalized Planning (Q3 - Task state estimation):** Binary accuracy for predicting subtask completion.

#### 3.4. Experiments

The benchmark evaluates 14 state-of-the-art MLLMs (closed-source like GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude, Qwen-VL-Max; open-source like LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL; and embodied MLLMs like RoboBrain-2.0-7B), along with text-only LLMs and human performance as baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 14 state-of-the-art MLLMs on RoboBench revealed several fundamental limitations and key insights into their capabilities as embodied brains:

1. **Persistent Gap with Human Performance:** While Gemini-2.5-Pro consistently emerged as the top-performing MLLM across most dimensions, it still lags significantly behind human performance. For instance, in perception reasoning, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieved 62.96% compared to human's 74.30%. For affordance prediction and failure analysis, the gap was even wider (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 65.21% and 45.14% vs. Human: 82.63% and 63.99% respectively). This highlights that current MLLMs possess only superficial embodied reasoning and world-modeling capabilities compared to humans.

2. **Closed-Source Models Outperform Open-Source:** Closed-source MLLMs demonstrated a clear and consistent lead (often 10-15% margin) across most evaluation dimensions, with performance generally improving with model size within the same family (e.g., GPT-4o outperforms GPT-4o-Mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash). Open-source models only approached comparable results in perception reasoning, while significant gaps remained in instruction comprehension, planning, affordance, and failure analysis.

3. **Value of Embodied Training:** The specialized embodied MLLM, RoboBrain-2.0-7B, surpassed similarly sized general-purpose open-source models in perception reasoning, generalized planning, and affordance prediction. This finding underscores the effectiveness of domain-specific embodied training in enhancing multimodal reasoning and planning performance for robotics.

4. **Varying Difficulty Across Dimensions:** The results revealed substantial performance differences across task categories. Perception reasoning achieved relatively higher accuracy, suggesting a more mature capability, whereas generalized planning consistently proved to be the most challenging dimension for all models. This indicates where future research and development efforts are most critically needed.

5. **Fine-Grained Limitations in Cognitive Abilities:**
 - **Implicit Instruction Comprehension:** Models showed a severe degradation in performance (an average 30% drop) when tasked with implicit instructions compared to explicit ones. This points to a weak grounding of indirect human goals and a failure to fully integrate scene context for inferring task intent.
 - **Fragile Spatial-Temporal Perception:** MLLMs struggled with fundamental robotic perception and spatiotemporal reasoning. Specifically, they performed poorly on robot-view recognition and temporal grounding, achieving success rates as low as 43.48% and 49.68% respectively. Causal reasoning across time also remained weak for most models.
 - **Complex Planning as a Bottleneck:**
 - **Cross-Embodiment:** Models often failed to coordinate dual-arm operations or perform mobile manipulation effectively, indicating sub-optimal spatial search and movement decisions.
 - **Cross-Object:** Performance significantly dropped when tasks involved uncommon objects, symbolic reasoning, or required world knowledge, suggesting weak integration of heterogeneous information.
 - **Cross-View:** While multi-view inputs yielded modest improvements (e.g., GPT-4o from 33.66% to 38.51%), they did not close the gap to human performance, highlighting the benefit but also the remaining challenges in robust multi-view reasoning under occlusion.
 - **Shallow Affordance Understanding:** Even the best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieved 65.21% in affordance prediction, still 17 points behind human performance (82.63%), indicating a superficial understanding of how objects can be interacted with.
 - **Execution Failure Diagnosis is Extremely Hard:** Analyzing execution-level errors (e.g., position misalignment, trajectory deviations, gripper failures) proved to be the hardest dimension, with model scores ranging from 10-20%, significantly lower than planning error analysis (40-60%). Even human performance was relatively low (47.30%), underscoring the intrinsic difficulty of requiring fine-grained, expert-level embodied understanding for error diagnosis.

6. **Planning Error Analysis:** A detailed breakdown of planning failures showed that "Execution Errors" (missing/redundant actions, wrong functions) accounted for the majority of failures, followed by "Identification Errors" (aliasing, parameter mismatch) and "Common Sense Errors" (physics violations, spatial reasoning errors).

7. **Validation of MLLM-as-World-Simulator:** A human study validated the proposed planning evaluation metric. The "MLLM-as-world-simulator" framework achieved a higher Pearson correlation coefficient (r=0.83) with human judgments compared to a baseline LLM-based scoring method (r=0.73), confirming its effectiveness and alignment with human understanding of plan feasibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboBench represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, offering a robust and much-needed framework for evaluating the cognitive capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in robotics. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Establishes a Comprehensive Evaluation Standard:** By defining five core cognitive dimensions (Instruction Comprehension, Perception Reasoning, Generalized Planning, Affordance Prediction, and Failure Analysis) that cover the full spectrum of the manipulation pipeline, RoboBench provides the most holistic and systematic evaluation framework to date. This comprehensiveness moves beyond fragmented assessments, enabling a unified understanding of an MLLM's capabilities as an "embodied brain."
2. **Enhances Realism and Complexity in Benchmarking:** The benchmark's reliance on diverse real-world robotic data, covering various embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, humanoid), attribute-rich objects, multi-view scenes, and memory-driven navigation, significantly bridges the notorious "sim-to-real" gap. This ensures that evaluated models are tested against challenges closer to actual robotic deployment, leading to more practical and impactful research.
3. **Innovates Planning Evaluation with Embodied Feasibility:** The introduction of the "MLLM-as-world-simulator" framework is a pivotal innovation. By assessing not just symbolic plan fidelity but also the *embodied feasibility* of actions through simulated object-state changes under physical and visual constraints, RoboBench provides a far more accurate and rigorous measure of long-horizon planning ability. This is crucial for developing robots that can execute plans robustly in the physical world.
4. **Pinpoints Critical Limitations and Guides Future Research:** The detailed experimental results and fine-grained error analyses expose fundamental weaknesses in current MLLMs, such as difficulties with implicit instruction comprehension, fragile spatiotemporal perception, challenges in complex cross-scenario planning, shallow affordance understanding, and severe limitations in execution-level failure diagnosis. These findings serve as a clear roadmap, identifying the most critical bottlenecks and guiding future research efforts toward developing MLLMs that are truly robust, generalizable, and deeply grounded in embodied intelligence.
5. **Fosters Advancements in Embodied AI:** By providing a common scaffold and establishing a leaderboard, RoboBench facilitates fair comparisons between different MLLMs and encourages innovation. It will likely accelerate the development of next-generation MLLMs specifically tailored to overcome the identified cognitive challenges, thereby pushing the boundaries of what embodied intelligent agents can achieve in dynamic, unstructured environments.
6. **Clarifies the Role of the "Embodied Brain":** By explicitly framing MLLMs as the "embodied brain" (System 2) responsible for high-level cognition, the work clarifies the architectural role and necessary capabilities for MLLMs within robotic systems. This conceptual clarity can help standardize discussions and approaches in designing integrated robot intelligence.

In conclusion, RoboBench is more than just another dataset; it's a foundational tool that redefines how MLLMs are evaluated for embodied intelligence. Its comprehensive nature, commitment to realism, and innovative planning evaluation methodology will undoubtedly shape future research directions and accelerate the journey towards truly intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.
