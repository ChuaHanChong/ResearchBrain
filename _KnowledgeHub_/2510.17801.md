# Robobench: A Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models as Embodied Brain

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboBench introduces a comprehensive evaluation benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) acting as embodied brains, assessing five cognitive dimensions through real-world robotic data and a novel MLLM-as-world-simulator for planning. The benchmark reveals that current MLLMs still lag behind human performance across all evaluated cognitive abilities, particularly in complex planning and execution failure analysis.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for evaluating MLLMs as embodied "brains" offer fragmented coverage, assessing isolated cognitive abilities rather than integrated systems.
- Many current benchmarks lack realism and complexity, relying heavily on simulated environments and overlooking practical challenges like diverse robot embodiments and memory-driven navigation.
- Traditional planning evaluation methods are often superficial, using text similarity or multiple-choice formats, which fail to capture the physical feasibility and causal constraints of embodied actions.

## Method

- RoboBench establishes a comprehensive benchmark structured around five interdependent cognitive dimensions: instruction comprehension, perception reasoning, generalized planning, affordance prediction, and failure analysis.
- It curates datasets from large-scale real robotic data and in-house collections, encompassing diverse embodiments and complex real-world scenarios to enhance realism and reduce the sim-to-real gap.
- The benchmark innovates planning evaluation by proposing an "MLLM-as-world-simulator" framework to rigorously assess the embodied feasibility of plans by simulating object-state changes under physical and visual constraints.

## Results

- Gemini-2.5-Pro, while the top-performing MLLM, achieved **62.96%** in perception reasoning and **45.14%** in failure analysis, significantly behind human scores of **74.30%** and **63.99%** respectively.
- Closed-source MLLMs consistently outperformed open-source models by 10-**15%** across most evaluation dimensions, and performance generally improved with increased model size.
- The "MLLM-as-world-simulator" planning evaluation framework demonstrated strong alignment with human judgment, achieving a Pearson correlation coefficient of r=0.83, validating its effectiveness in assessing plan feasibility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs possess only superficial embodied reasoning and world-modeling capabilities, consistently lagging behind human performance across all cognitive dimensions evaluated.
> - Specialized embodied training, as seen in RoboBrain-2.0-7B, effectively enhances multimodal reasoning and planning performance for robotics compared to general-purpose MLLMs of similar size.
> - Implicit instruction comprehension, fragile spatial-temporal perception, and complex long-horizon planning remain fundamental challenges for MLLMs, with execution failure diagnosis proving particularly difficult.
