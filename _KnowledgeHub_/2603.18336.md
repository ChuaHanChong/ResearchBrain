# ManiDreams: An Open-Source Library for Robust Object Manipulation via Uncertainty-aware Task-specific Intuitive Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiDreams is an open-source library that implements a "sample-predict-constrain" paradigm for robust robotic object manipulation by explicitly representing and propagating uncertainty. It consistently outperforms baselines under varying noise and enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tasks like pushing and picking.

## Problem

- Robotic dynamics models primarily focus on minimizing prediction error, often neglecting to explicitly address and leverage inherent uncertainties in real-world manipulation.
- Uncertainties (perceptual, parametric, structural) are typically discarded after the training phase, leading to accumulated errors in planning and control loops.
- Existing planning systems lack modularity and reusability, often coupling dynamics models, cost functions, and action selection in a monolithic manner, limiting adaptation to diverse tasks or components.

## Method

- Introduces **ManiDreams**, a modular, open-source Python framework that implements a ==sample-predict-constrain== paradigm for ==uncertainty-aware manipulation planning==.
- Utilizes a ==Domain-Randomized Instance Set== (DRIS) as a universal ==distributional state representation== and a ==Task-Specific Intuitive Physics== (TSIP) interface for backend-agnostic ==forward dynamics prediction==.
- Integrates ==declarative caging constraints== to bound ==uncertainty propagation== and a flexible solver to evaluate candidate actions against distributional outcomes, enhancing robustness without policy retraining.

## Results

- ManiDreams consistently outperformed PPO baselines across various tasks (PushCube, PickCube, PushT) and escalating levels of perceptual noise, observation delay, and physics parameter randomization.
- Successfully deployed the framework for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a **Franka Panda** arm in cluttered environments for pushing and card picking tasks using a diffusion-based TSIP.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that increasing the number of DRIS instances (`m`) and solver samples (`N`) significantly improved success rates, with performance saturating beyond `m=16` and `N=16`.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly representing and propagating uncertainty throughout the planning loop, rather than suppressing it during training, is crucial for achieving robust robotic manipulation in real-world conditions.
> - A modular architecture with distinct abstractions for distributional state, dynamics prediction, constraints, and action selection enables composability, reusability, and independent development of components.
> - The 'sample-predict-constrain' paradigm effectively filters actions by evaluating their predicted distributional outcomes against safety constraints, providing a robust wrapper for existing policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dynamics models, including physics simulators and learned world models, are fundamental to robotic manipulation. However, the majority of these models primarily focus on minimizing prediction error, often neglecting the inherent uncertainties present in real-world manipulation tasks. These uncertainties stem from three primary sources: perceptual uncertainty (noisy and delayed observations), parametric uncertainty (inaccurate physical parameter estimation), and structural uncertainty (imperfections in the dynamics models themselves).

Existing methodologies largely address these uncertainties during the training phase. Approaches such as domain randomization introduce variability to bridge the sim-to-real gap, and learned world models attempt to absorb model mismatches into latent representations. However, once a policy is deployed, the explicit structure of these uncertainties is typically discarded. This oversight leads to an accumulation of errors when these uncertainty sources are handled independently, underscoring the necessity for a unified framework that can represent, propagate, and constrain them collectively within the planning loop.

Current simulation frameworks support domain randomization but generally propagate only a single object instance per environment, limiting their ability to model distributional outcomes. World models, whether physics engines or diffusion-based predictors, usually forecast single states without mechanisms for representing or propagating uncertainty across a spectrum of plausible outcomes. Although a "sample-predict-constrain" pattern—where candidate actions are sampled, their outcomes are predicted, and they are filtered by a cost or constraint criterion—is observed across various recent planning systems, these systems typically couple the dynamics model, cost function, and action selection monolithically. This monolithic design hinders the independent replacement or reuse of components. Furthermore, existing caging constraint implementations, while effective, are often tightly coupled to specific task geometries, lacking a reusable, declarative library.

ManiDreams addresses these limitations by providing a modular, open-source Python framework that unifies the representation, propagation, and constraint of uncertainties within a composable manipulation planning pipeline. It explicitly integrates perceptual, parametric, and structural uncertainties, offers a backend-agnostic interface for dynamics prediction, and provides a declarative constraint specification for action optimization. This positions ManiDreams as a general realization of the "sample-predict-constrain" paradigm, offering a more robust and flexible approach to robotic manipulation in uncertain environments compared to prior, more fragmented solutions.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The core motivation behind ManiDreams stems from the observation that real-world robotic manipulation is fundamentally characterized by uncertainty, yet most existing dynamics models prioritize minimizing prediction error over explicitly managing this uncertainty. The authors argue that robust manipulation under uncertainty is an *integration problem*: uncertainties must be explicitly represented, propagated, and constrained within the robot's planning loop, rather than being merely suppressed or absorbed during policy training. Current approaches often discard explicit uncertainty structures upon policy deployment, leading to vulnerabilities in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ManiDreams project are:

1. **To develop a composable, open-source framework:** The primary objective is to create a modular Python library that implements the "sample-predict-constrain" paradigm. This framework is designed with a plugin-based architecture, allowing for independent and interchangeable components for state representation, dynamics prediction, constraint specification, and action optimization.
2. **To establish a unified distributional dynamics model:** ManiDreams aims to represent, propagate, and constrain perceptual, parametric, and structural uncertainties jointly. This is achieved through a standardized interface that uses a distributional state representation (Domain-Randomized Instance Set, DRIS), a task-specific dynamics predictor (Task-Specific Intuitive Physics, TSIP), and caging constraints to manage the spread of uncertainty.
3. **To enhance robustness without retraining policies:** A central goal is to improve the robustness of existing base policies under various perturbations (perceptual noise, parametric variations, model inaccuracies) by wrapping them with the uncertainty-aware planning loop provided by ManiDreams, without requiring costly policy retraining.
4. **To demonstrate flexibility and practical applicability:** The framework aims to be flexible enough to integrate different dynamics backends (simulators or learned models), optimizers, and constraint types. This includes demonstrating its utility across diverse manipulation tasks (pushing, picking, catching) and proving its zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities on a physical robot.
5. **To provide a publicly available tool:** By open-sourcing ManiDreams, the authors intend to offer a practical and accessible platform to the robotics community, facilitating further research and application of uncertainty-aware manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

ManiDreams is structured as an open-source modular Python framework, designed around three core principles and implemented with a three-layer architecture.

**Design Principles:**
1. **Distributional-first:** Unlike conventional approaches that reason about single point estimates, ManiDreams treats a set of possible states as the fundamental unit of reasoning. This ensures that uncertainty is explicitly represented and propagated through all stages of the manipulation pipeline.
2. **Backend-agnostic:** All dynamics-dependent logic is abstracted behind a single forward-prediction interface. This allows for interchangeable use of various dynamics backends, whether they are GPU-accelerated simulators or learned world models, without requiring modifications to the constraint or solver modules.
3. **Minimal intrusion:** ManiDreams is designed to wrap any Gym-compatible environment with a thin adapter. This ensures that integrating the framework with existing policies or environments requires minimal code changes, making it easy to switch between direct policy execution and uncertainty-aware planning.

**System Architecture:**
The framework follows a three-layer organization, analogous to libraries like OMPL:
- **Layer 1 (Abstract Interfaces):** Defines the foundational abstract interfaces for the four core abstractions: Domain-Randomized Instance Set (DRIS), Task-Specific Intuitive Physics (TSIP), Caging Constraint (`CageDRIS`), and Solver (`SolverBase`), along with `ExecutorBase`.
- **Layer 2 (Concrete Implementations):** Provides concrete implementations for these abstract interfaces. This includes different physics backends (simulation-based and learning-based TSIPs), various caging constraints, multiple solvers (combining samplers and optimizers), and executors. These are unified within a single `ManiDreamsEnv` class.
- **Layer 3 (Task Integration):** This is the user-facing layer, where users configure specific manipulation tasks by supplying scene descriptions, pipeline configurations, and action samplers, without modifying the framework's core code.

**Core Abstractions:**
1. **State Representation (Domain-Randomized Instance Set - DRIS):**
 - The DRIS (`D`) is the universal state representation. It bundles observations with a distributional context, capturing perceptual and parametric uncertainty.
 - It consists of a discrete set of state-context pairs `D_t = {(s_t^(i), c^(i)) | c^(i) ∈ Ĉ}`, where `Ĉ = {c^(i)}` is a set of sampled contexts representing variations in physical parameters (e.g., mass, friction, geometry) for the target object.
 - The state projection `S_t = proj_S(D_t)` extracts the set of all instance states, enabling computation of distributional statistics. DRIS is modality-agnostic, supporting state vectors, images, or point clouds.
2. **Forward Dynamics (Task-Specific Intuitive Physics - TSIP):**
 - The TSIP (`F`) provides the forward-prediction interface, translating a DRIS and an action into a predicted next DRIS (`D_(t+1) = F(D_t, u_t)`).
 - This formulation converts stochastic dynamics over individual contexts into a deterministic mapping over distributions, with uncertainty explicitly contained in the representation.
 - ManiDreams includes a `SimulationBasedTSIP` (wrapping ManiSkill3 for parallel GPU evaluation of DRIS instances) and a `LearningBasedTSIP` (wrapping pre-trained diffusion models). Users can extend this by implementing the `next` interface.
3. **Caging Constraint:**
 - Caging constraints are designed to bound the spreading uncertainty of a DRIS, ensuring task-level safety (`S_(t+1) ⊆ S_(t+1)_cage`).
 - Each constraint exposes `evaluate` (returning a continuous cost) and `validate` (returning a binary containment flag).
 - Unlike classical state-validity checkers, caging constraints reason about the spread of a distribution rather than a single configuration. The framework provides built-in geometric, trajectory-based, plate-based, pixel-space, and composite constraints.
4. **Solver:**
 - The Solver closes the "sample-predict-constrain" loop. It combines a `sampler` (e.g., a policy's stochastic head, a Gaussian distribution, or a discrete set) that proposes N candidate actions, with an `optimizer` (e.g., N-best selection or MPPI) that scores these candidates based on the caging constraint and selects the best valid action.
 - This design enables a safety filter to be applied over existing policies without requiring their retraining.

**Framework Pipeline (Two-Level Execution Loop):**
ManiDreams operates with two nested loops:
- **Planning Loop (Inner Loop):** Given the current DRIS `D_t`, the solver generates N candidate actions. The TSIP then predicts the resulting DRIS for each candidate. The caging constraint evaluates and filters these predicted outcomes, and the optimizer selects the best valid action. This loop can iterate for refinement (e.g., in MPPI). If only one sample or no constraint is used, the pipeline reduces to direct policy execution.
- **Execution Loop (Outer Loop):** The action selected by the planning loop is appended to an action chunk. Once the chunk reaches a predefined length, it is dispatched to the real robot or simulator (executor). The executor then observes the resulting state, constructs an updated DRIS `D_(t+1)`, which feeds back into the next planning cycle. This outer loop runs at the physical control frequency, independent of the planning model, facilitating sim-to-real transfer by simply swapping the executor.

**DRIS Propagation:** At each `TSIP.next` call, the executor's current target pose is synchronized across all N evaluation environments. Each environment then resets its `m` DRIS copies with fresh perturbations. All `N × m` instances are stepped simultaneously under candidate actions. The backend aggregates the post-step positions, which naturally diverge due to initial perturbations and differing contact conditions, into per-candidate distributional statistics (mean and per-axis variance) stored in the returned DRIS.

**Operational Modes:** The framework supports an **online mode** for reactive tasks (inner loop runs once per control step) and a **plan-then-execute mode** where the inner loop plans a full action sequence over a horizon, which the executor then replays. This separation allows the planning model to use simplified dynamics while the executor runs high-fidelity simulation or real hardware.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experimental evaluation of ManiDreams encompassed five categories: runnable examples illustrating composability, real-world deployment, robustness benchmarks under uncertainty, ablation studies, and runtime overhead analysis.

**Runnable Examples (Composability and Flexibility):**
Three reference examples – random object pushing, card picking, and ball catching – demonstrated the framework's flexibility.
- **Pushing:** The task involved pushing a YCB object along a circular trajectory. It successfully utilized both a `SimulationBasedTSIP` and a `LearningBasedTSIP` (diffusion model) with identical cage, sampler, and solver configurations. This confirmed that TSIP backends can be swapped without changes to other modules.
- **Card Picking:** This 3D task involved pushing a card to a corner for picking. It employed a `Geometric3DCage`, a continuous random sampler, and an MPPI solver for iterative refinement, illustrating the independent replaceability of cage and solver components.
- **Ball Catching:** For dynamic ball catching, a pretrained PPO policy served as the sampler, generating candidate actions evaluated by a simulation-based TSIP. The solver selected the optimal action, confirming sampler flexibility.
- *Finding:* These examples collectively validated the modular design, demonstrating that ManiDreams allows for independent swapping of its core abstractions (TSIP backends, caging constraints, samplers, solvers) to suit different task requirements.

**Real Robot Deployment (Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer):**
The pushing and card picking examples were deployed on a Franka Panda arm by replacing the simulator executor with a `RealWorldExecutor`, without requiring fine-tuning.
- **Cluttered Push-Picking and Scooping:** Tasks involved pushing obstructing objects (corn, avocado) or scooping flat objects (steak, pizza) from a tabletop. A diffusion-based TSIP was used with pixel-based DRIS generated by SAM2 segmentation from an RGB camera. The DRIS was updated between action chunks.
- *Finding:* The real-world experiments confirmed the framework's executor independence and its capability for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, even in cluttered and high-uncertainty environments. The caging constraint played a role in guiding manipulations in complex scenarios.

**Robustness under Uncertainty (ManiSkill Benchmarks):**
ManiDreams was compared against a PPO baseline on three ManiSkill3 tasks (PushCube, PickCube, PushT) under increasing levels of perceptual noise, observation delay, and physics parameter randomization.
- *Finding:* ManiDreams consistently exhibited superior robustness compared to the PPO baseline across all nine task-perturbation combinations. The advantage was most pronounced at moderate perturbation levels, where the explicit evaluation of candidate actions against distributional predictions proved beneficial. At extreme perturbation magnitudes, the fixed DRIS configuration reached its limits, and both methods degraded. Task-specific degradation patterns were observed, indicating that tasks like PickCube are more sensitive to observation delay, while PushCube is more susceptible to physics variations.

**Ablation Study (PushCube):**
An ablation study on PushCube investigated the impact of three key parameters: number of DRIS instances (`m`), number of solver samples (`N`), and DRIS physics distribution width.
- **DRIS instances (`m`):** Success rate increased from 58% (m=1, point estimation) to 85% (m=16), with diminishing returns beyond 16 instances.
- **Solver samples (`N`):** Success rate increased from 52% (N=1, direct policy execution) to 87% (N=16), also showing diminishing returns.
- **Distribution width:** A medium distribution width ([0.5, 2]x default physics parameters) yielded the highest success rate (82%), outperforming narrow ([0.9, 1.2]x, 74%) and wide ([0.2, 3]x, 76%) settings.
- *Finding:* Both the number of DRIS instances and solver samples contribute positively to robustness, demonstrating the value of distributional representation and candidate evaluation. An appropriate physics distribution width is crucial, as too narrow underestimates uncertainty and too wide introduces excessive conservatism.

**Runtime Overhead (PushCube):**
The computational cost of the sample-predict-constrain loop was analyzed.
- **Baseline PPO:** 20.2 ms per step.
- **ManiDreams (simulation-based TSIP):** 4x2 configuration (N=4, m=2) incurred 49.6 ms, 8x4 incurred 56.2 ms, and 16x8 incurred 77.9 ms.
- **Diffusion-based TSIP:** 55.2 ms, comparable to the simulation-based 8x4 configuration.
- **Cost Breakdown:** Dominant costs were TSIP state synchronization and forward evaluation; policy sampling and cage evaluation were negligible.
- *Finding:* While some configurations exceed a 50 Hz control cycle, rendering them challenging for highly dynamic tasks, they remain practical for typical manipulation tasks operating at 10–20 Hz. Furthermore, in plan-then-execute mode, per-step latency does not constrain the physical control rate, making it suitable for longer-horizon planning.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

ManiDreams presents an open-source framework that effectively unifies uncertainty representation, propagation, and constraint within a composable pipeline for robotic manipulation. The primary significance of this work lies not in proposing a new state-of-the-art algorithm for a single task, but in creating a modular, accessible platform that renders uncertainty-aware manipulation practical through reusable building blocks.

**Significance:**
- **Unified Uncertainty Management:** The framework systematically addresses perceptual, parametric, and structural uncertainties by integrating them into a distributional state representation (DRIS), propagating them via Task-Specific Intuitive Physics (TSIP), and constraining them with caging mechanisms. This unified approach mitigates the accumulation of errors inherent in fragmented treatments of uncertainty.
- **Composability and Flexibility:** ManiDreams' plugin-based architecture for DRIS, TSIP, caging constraints, and solvers allows for independent swapping and reuse of components. This modularity facilitates the integration of diverse policies, optimizers, and dynamics backends (both simulation-based and learned models) into a single framework.
- **Enhanced Robustness without Retraining:** The framework improves the robustness of robotic manipulation under various perturbations by wrapping existing policies with its sample-predict-constrain loop, evaluating candidate actions against distributional outcomes. This provides a practical method to add safety and resilience without requiring expensive policy retraining.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The clear separation between the planning model and the physical executor enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, as demonstrated on real-world cluttered tasks, by merely exchanging the executor module.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** By providing a publicly available and well-documented library, ManiDreams contributes a foundational tool to the robotics research community, promoting reproducible research and accelerating development in robust robot learning and model-based control.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancing Robust Robotics:** ManiDreams has the potential to accelerate research in robust robot learning and model-predictive control by offering a standardized and flexible platform for developing and testing uncertainty-aware manipulation strategies.
- **Broader Real-World Deployment:** By explicitly addressing and managing uncertainties, the framework could lead to more reliable and trustworthy autonomous robotic systems, enabling their deployment in complex, unstructured, and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Facilitating Complex Task Design:** The declarative nature of its caging constraints and the ability to integrate vision-language models for task-level specification could simplify the design and implementation of safety-critical manipulation tasks.
- **Bridging Simulation and Reality:** The seamless sim-to-real transfer capability of ManiDreams can reduce the effort required to transfer policies trained in simulation to physical robots, making robot development cycles more efficient.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Current limitations include the manual specification of DRIS parameters and caging constraints, which often require task-specific tuning. The framework's performance is also dependent on the availability of suitable TSIP backends, and tasks with highly discontinuous dynamics may necessitate more expressive propagation models. Future work will explore automatic DRIS configuration from visual observations, integrate vision-language models for more intuitive task-level cage specification, and extend the framework to more generalized latent world models to broaden its applicability.
