# CDE: Curiosity-Driven Exploration for Efficient Reinforcement Learning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE) is an efficient framework designed to enhance Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models by integrating intrinsic curiosity signals derived from the actor's perplexity and the critic's uncertainty. This method achieved up to +10 Pass@16 accuracy on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and effectively mitigated issues like entropy and calibration collapse during LLM training.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from poor exploration, premature convergence, and entropy collapse, limiting their reasoning capabilities.
- Traditional exploration strategies, such as count-based methods, are computationally prohibitive for complex Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs due to high-dimensional embeddings and limited expressiveness.
- Naive RLVR training often leads to "calibration collapse," where the LLM's confidence progressively decouples from its correctness over time.

## Method

- **CDE** introduces ==perplexity-based actor curiosity==, where a bonus derived from the LLM's own perplexity (surprise) is added to the verifiable reward, using an adaptive clipping mechanism to guide exploration.
- CDE incorporates multi-head critic curiosity, extending the PPO framework with multiple critics whose value estimate variance serves as an exploration bonus, reflecting uncertainty and guiding policy toward underexplored regions.
- An adaptive clipping mechanism for curiosity bonuses and a staircase decay schedule for bonus weights are employed to ensure stable training and a controlled transition from exploration to exploitation.

## Results

- GRPO with the PPL bonus achieved an approximate +**2.4** point average improvement across mathematical reasoning datasets and a +8 point increase in Pass@**16** accuracy on the AIME24 dataset over standard GRPO.
- Multi-Head PPO consistently outperformed vanilla PPO with average gains of roughly +2 points and up to +**10** Pass@**16** accuracy increase on AIME datasets.
- CDE methods exhibited a slower but more stable increase in test accuracy early in training, eventually surpassing baselines, and effectively mitigated entropy collapse and "calibration collapse" observed in naive RLVR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intrinsic curiosity, formalised through actor perplexity and critic uncertainty, provides an efficient and scalable alternative to traditional count-based exploration methods for LLMs.
> - The perplexity bonus improves the model's calibration by intrinsically penalizing overconfident errors and encouraging diversity among correct responses.
> - "Calibration collapse," a phenomenon where LLM confidence decouples from correctness, is a critical issue in RLVR training, and the perplexity bonus effectively mitigates it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs through a paradigm known as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

**RLVR as a Breakthrough:** The paper acknowledges RLVR as a major breakthrough, enabling LLMs to be optimized directly using signals of final-answer correctness, thereby eliminating the need for complex and often fragile reward models. This has shown remarkable performance in domains like mathematics and coding, as evidenced by algorithms such as GRPO and DAPO.

**Addressing a Core Challenge in RLVR:** Despite its success, current RLVR methods face significant challenges, notably **poor exploration**, **premature convergence**, and **entropy collapse**. These issues are classic manifestations of the exploration-exploitation dilemma in RL, where models prematurely settle on suboptimal solutions rather than thoroughly exploring the solution space for better alternatives.

**Limitations of Existing Exploration Strategies for LLMs:**
- **Simple Heuristics (e.g., entropy bonuses, ε-greedy):** While widely used, these methods are often provably suboptimal or show debatable effectiveness in complex environments like Deep RL and especially LLM-based reasoning, as they inject undirected randomness rather than guided exploration.
- **Principled Count-Based Methods (e.g., UCB, LinUCB, LSVI-UCB):** These methods incentivize visiting rarely explored state-action pairs and offer strong theoretical guarantees in simpler settings (bandits, linear MDPs). However, applying them to LLMs for complex Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning poses severe practical limitations:
 - **Computational Intensity:** They often require computationally prohibitive operations like matrix inversion for high-dimensional embeddings of reasoning paths.
 - **Poor Expressiveness of Embeddings:** Even with approximations like SimHash for pseudo-counts, the paper empirically demonstrates that sentence embeddings struggle to meaningfully distinguish diverse CoT trajectories, leading to a collapse where most responses cluster into a few hash grids (Figure 1), rendering count-based exploration ineffective.

**CDE's Position in the Landscape:** The work directly addresses these limitations by introducing Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE), a framework that moves beyond explicit state-action counts. CDE aims to provide an **efficient, scalable, and intrinsically motivated** exploration mechanism specifically tailored for LLMs. It proposes leveraging the LLM's own internal sense of "novelty" or "surprise" to guide exploration, drawing inspiration from cognitive development. By formalizing curiosity using signals derived directly from the actor's confidence (perplexity) and the critic's uncertainty (variance of value estimates), CDE offers a principled approach that avoids the computational and representational bottlenecks of prior methods while grounding its approach in established RL theory. This positions CDE as a significant step forward in making RLVR more robust and effective for complex LLM reasoning tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for improving the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs)**, particularly in challenging domains like mathematics.

The motivations behind introducing Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE) are multifaceted:

1. **Overcome Poor Exploration in RLVR:** Existing RLVR methods, despite their power, are frequently plagued by issues such as premature convergence and entropy collapse. These phenomena indicate a training process that is overly biased towards exploitation, preventing the LLM from sufficiently exploring the vast space of possible reasoning paths to discover truly optimal or diverse solutions. The primary goal is to mitigate these problems and foster more thorough exploration.

2. **Address Practical Limitations of Traditional Exploration Strategies:** The authors recognize the inherent difficulties in directly applying classic RL exploration methods to the context of LLMs generating complex Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
 - **Computational Burden:** Methods relying on matrix inversions for state-action coverage become computationally prohibitive with high-dimensional LLM embeddings.
 - **Representational Challenges:** Simpler hash-based pseudo-counts, an attempt to bypass computational issues, still suffer from the "curse of dimensionality" and the limited expressiveness of fixed sentence embeddings to capture the diversity of complex CoT trajectories. These lead to ineffective exploration as distinct reasoning paths collapse into similar "counts."

3. **Leverage Intrinsic Curiosity as a Guiding Principle:** Inspired by cognitive development, where learning is often driven by an intrinsic curiosity to explore novel situations rather than explicit external counts of experiences, the authors propose an intuitive and biologically plausible approach. They hypothesize that an LLM, having been trained on extensive reasoning corpora, develops an internal model of what constitutes familiar versus novel reasoning patterns. The motivation is to formalize and harness this "intrinsic sense of curiosity" within the LLM itself as a more efficient and scalable guide for exploration.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a lightweight, efficient, and theoretically grounded exploration framework for RLVR-trained LLMs that addresses the current limitations, leading to more stable training, better reasoning performance, and a deeper understanding of LLM calibration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE) framework proposed by Dai et al. integrates intrinsic curiosity signals directly into the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm, specifically enhancing GRPO and PPO algorithms.

**Foundational RLVR Algorithms:**
The study builds upon two established RL algorithms:
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** A critic-free, REINFORCE-style algorithm where a group of candidate outputs is generated, and their advantages are normalized relative to the group's rewards. A clipped objective function prevents unstable updates, and a KL-penalty constrains deviation from the reference policy.
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** A canonical actor-critic algorithm that maintains both an actor (policy $\pi_\theta$) and a critic (value function $V_\phi$). It uses Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) for token-level advantages and a clipped objective, with a value loss for the critic.

**CDE Framework: Curiosity Signals from Actor and Critic:**
CDE formalizes curiosity using signals derived from two components of the LLM:

1. **Exploration Guided by Actor Curiosity (Perplexity-Based Bonus):**
 - **Curiosity Signal:** The actor's uncertainty about its own actions, measured by the **perplexity (PPL)** of its generated response. A higher PPL for a generated sentence indicates greater surprise or novelty, suggesting an underexplored region of the policy's distribution. The bonus $B_{actor}(q, o)$ is defined as the negative average log-probability of the generated sentence.
 - **Integration:** This perplexity-based bonus is added to the original verifiable reward $r(q, o)$ to form a total sentence-level reward $\tilde{r}(q, o)$. Crucially, an **adaptive clipping mechanism** is employed: $\tilde{r}(q, o) = r(q, o) + \omega_t \min(|r(q, o)|/\kappa, \alpha B_{actor}(q, o))$. This mechanism prevents "reward hacking" (generating high-perplexity but low-quality responses) and over-exploration by ensuring the bonus remains a controlled fraction of the original reward.
 - **Hyperparameters:** The bonus weight $\omega_t$ (with an annealing schedule, Staircase decay found most effective), clipping ratio $\kappa$, and scaling factor $\alpha$ control the exploration intensity.
 - **Theoretical Insight (Theorem 3.1):** The PPL bonus intrinsically penalizes overconfident errors (incorrect responses with low PPL) and encourages diversity among correct responses (correct responses with high PPL), thereby improving the model's calibration.

2. **Exploration Guided by Critic Curiosity (Multi-Head Variance Bonus):**
 - **Curiosity Signal:** The critic's uncertainty in its value estimates, reflecting the degree of data coverage for specific prompt-response pairs. Regions with sparse data lead to higher uncertainty (variance) in value estimates.
 - **Implementation:** A **multi-head critic architecture** is introduced. This involves extending the PPO framework with *K* critics, all sharing a common LLM backbone but each trained on a resampled subset of the collected trajectories (bootstrapping). The standard deviation across these *K* heads serves as the curiosity signal, $B_{critic}(q, o_{i, \le t+1})$, guiding the policy towards regions of high disagreement.
 - **Integration:** The $B_{critic}$ bonus is incorporated into the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) calculation for actor updates, using a similar adaptive clipping mechanism as the actor bonus. The advantage $\tilde{A}_{i,t}$ is combined with the bonus: $\tilde{A}_{i,t} = A_{i,t} + \omega_t \min(|\tilde{A}_{i,t}|/\kappa, \alpha B_{critic}(q, o_{i, \le t+1}))$.
 - **Critic Update:** Each critic head *j* is updated using a bootstrapped loss, where a subset $D_j$ of the collected trajectories (of size $\zeta|D|$) is sampled without replacement.
 - **Theoretical Insight (Theorem 3.2):** In the context of linear MDPs, the standard deviation across multi-head critics is shown to be a consistent estimator for classical count-based exploration bonuses, thereby grounding this approach in established RL principles.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Model:** Qwen3-4B-Base LLM.
- **Training Data:** DAPO-17K dataset.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Four challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks: MATH, AMC23, AIME24, and AIME25.
- **Framework:** All experiments are implemented within the Verl framework.

This methodology represents a comprehensive approach to integrate intrinsic motivation into LLM reinforcement learning, addressing both practical and theoretical aspects of efficient exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE) demonstrates consistent and significant performance improvements across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks, alongside revealing important insights into LLM training dynamics.

**Overall Performance Gains:**
- **GRPO with PPL Bonus:** Achieved an approximate **+2.4 point average improvement** across the evaluated datasets (MATH, AMC23, AIME24, AIME25) over the standard GRPO baseline. Notably, it showed a substantial **+8 point increase** in Pass@16 accuracy on the challenging AIME24 dataset.
- **Multi-Head PPO:** Consistently outperformed vanilla PPO, yielding average gains of roughly **+2 points** when using K=4 and K=16 heads. In many cases, it led to an approximate **+10 point increase** in Pass@16 accuracy on the AIME datasets.
- **Training Dynamics:** Unlike baseline methods that might show faster initial gains but plateau or decline, CDE methods (GRPO with PPL bonus and multi-head PPO) exhibited a **slower but more stable increase in test accuracy early in training**, eventually surpassing the baselines. This pattern is consistent with enhanced exploration, which prevents premature exploitation of suboptimal trajectories and allows for broader discovery of high-quality solutions.

**Insights into Actor Curiosity (PPL Bonus):**
- **Bonus Weight Decay is Crucial:** Experiments with different annealing schedules for the bonus weight ($\omega_t$) revealed that decaying the bonus weight is essential for stable convergence, enabling a gradual transition from exploration to exploitation. The **Staircase decay** schedule (strong initial exploration followed by abrupt removal of the bonus) proved most effective, outperforming no decay, linear, and cosine decay schedules. This highlights the importance of aggressive early exploration.
- **Alleviation of Entropy Collapse:** Analysis of policy entropy dynamics showed that the PPL bonus **mitigates entropy collapse** observed in baseline GRPO. This confirms its role in promoting broader exploration by maintaining a more stochastic policy, preventing it from collapsing into a narrow set of highly confident (but not necessarily optimal) actions. The Staircase decay also led to more stable entropy trajectories compared to persistent fluctuations with no decay.
- **Calibration Collapse Mitigation:** A novel phenomenon termed **"calibration collapse"** was identified in naive GRPO. Here, the model's confidence (inverse PPL) progressively decouples from its correctness over training – the gap between the PPL of correct and incorrect responses shrinks and vanishes. The PPL bonus, however, **sustained this separation throughout training**, indicating improved calibration. This means the model remained more confident when correct and less confident when incorrect, directly supporting Theorem 3.1.

**Insights into Critic Curiosity (Multi-Head Critic):**
- **Dynamics of Critic Bonus ($B_{critic}$):** The average value of $B_{critic}$ consistently **decreased over training steps**. This empirically supports its interpretation as a measure analogous to count-based exploration: as more trajectories are revisited, the disagreement among critic heads diminishes, reflecting increased data coverage and reduced uncertainty.
- **Cross-Dataset Uncertainty:** When evaluating $B_{critic}$ across different datasets, the training set (DAPO-17K) exhibited a smaller standard deviation compared to in-domain (AMC23) and out-of-domain (GPQA) validation sets. This further confirms that multi-head critics effectively capture and highlight **greater uncertainty on less frequently encountered or novel data**, aligning with the goal of guiding exploration.
- **Impact of Number of Heads (K):** Performance generally **improved with an increasing number of heads (K)**, with K=2 showing negligible gains, and performance gains beginning to plateau around K $\ge$ 4. This suggests that a modest number of heads is sufficient to capture most of the relevant epistemic uncertainty for effective exploration.
- **Robustness to Sub-sample Fraction ($\zeta$):** The model demonstrated **robustness to the sub-sample fraction $\zeta$** used for training individual critic heads, achieving stable performance for both $\zeta=0.5$ and $\zeta=1.0$ (no sub-sampling). While more heads might benefit from larger fractions, the overall results were consistent.

In summary, CDE consistently enhances LLM reasoning performance by fostering more efficient and principled exploration. The detailed analyses of actor and critic curiosity not only validate the theoretical underpinnings but also provide crucial insights into the training dynamics and calibration properties of RL-trained LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on Curiosity-Driven Exploration (CDE) presents several significant contributions and has the potential for a broad impact on the field of Large Language Models and Reinforcement Learning.

1. **Addressing a Critical LLM Training Challenge:** CDE directly tackles the fundamental problem of poor exploration, premature convergence, and entropy collapse in RLVR-based LLM training. These issues are major bottlenecks preventing LLMs from reaching their full reasoning potential and ensuring stable, robust learning. By providing an effective solution, CDE improves the reliability and performance ceiling of LLMs in complex tasks.

2. **Novel and Efficient Exploration Paradigm for LLMs:** The paper introduces a novel exploration paradigm that leverages intrinsic curiosity signals derived directly from the LLM's internal mechanisms (perplexity from the actor, value variance from the critic). This approach is highly significant because it bypasses the practical limitations of traditional count-based methods (computational intensity, poor expressiveness of embeddings for complex CoT trajectories). By using "intrinsic" signals, CDE offers a lightweight and scalable solution that seamlessly integrates into existing RLVR frameworks with minimal modifications.

3. **Strong Theoretical Foundations:** CDE is not merely a heuristic but is grounded in theoretical analysis. Theorem 3.1 provides an elegant interpretation of the perplexity bonus, linking it to confidence calibration by penalizing overconfident errors and promoting diverse correct responses. Theorem 3.2 connects the multi-head critic's variance to established count-based exploration bonuses in linear MDPs, providing a solid theoretical justification for its effectiveness. This combination of practical utility and theoretical backing strengthens the methodology's credibility.

4. **Empirical Validation and Performance Improvement:** The consistent and notable performance gains on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (up to +8 to +10 Pass@16 points on AIME datasets) demonstrate the practical efficacy of CDE. These empirical results corroborate the theoretical insights and showcase CDE's ability to unlock higher reasoning capabilities in LLMs.

5. **Discovery of "Calibration Collapse" and its Mitigation:** The identification and analysis of "calibration collapse" in naive RLVR training is a particularly significant finding. It exposes a critical failure mode where LLM confidence decouples from correctness, potentially leading to untrustworthy or "hallucinatory" outputs. CDE, specifically through the PPL bonus, effectively mitigates this collapse, leading to better-calibrated models that are more "faithful" – confident when correct and cautious when uncertain. This has profound implications for:
 - **Trustworthiness and Interpretability:** Better calibration means users can more reliably interpret an LLM's confidence scores, which is crucial for high-stakes applications.
 - **Advanced Inference Strategies:** Improved calibration supports the development of more effective inference-time techniques (e.g., self-certainty BoN, DeepConf) that rely on model confidence.
 - **Future Research on Hallucination:** The connection between calibration collapse and LLM hallucination (as hinted in the paper's conclusion) opens a promising new avenue for investigating and mitigating LLM unreliability.

6. **Guidance for Future RLVR Reward Design:** The finding that the PPL bonus (a multi-perspective reward) helps correct the issues of purely outcome-based RLVR reward design is impactful. It suggests that future research into RLVR should explore more sophisticated reward structures that consider aspects beyond just final answer correctness, potentially incorporating intermediate reasoning quality or confidence.

In conclusion, CDE offers a principled, efficient, and empirically validated approach to enhance exploration in RLVR for LLMs. Its theoretical insights into perplexity and critic uncertainty, coupled with the discovery and mitigation of calibration collapse, significantly advance our understanding of LLM training dynamics and lay the groundwork for developing more robust, reliable, and intelligent LLMs capable of sophisticated reasoning.
