# Humanoid-Gym: Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot with Zero-Shot Sim2Real Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> The Humanoid-Gym framework provides an open-source reinforcement learning environment for humanoid robots, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for locomotion. It achieves robust performance on RobotEra's XBot-S and XBot-L robots across varied terrains and introduces a novel Isaac Gym-to-MuJoCo sim-to-sim validation, with calibrated MuJoCo dynamics closely mirroring real-world robot behavior.

## Problem

- A notable absence of open-source resources tailored for training humanoid robots with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities.
- The significant and challenging sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid robots, hindering practical real-world deployment.
- The difficulty for researchers to rigorously validate learned policies without direct access to expensive physical humanoid robot hardware.

## Method

- Develops **Humanoid-Gym**, an open-source ==deep reinforcement learning== framework built on Nvidia Isaac Gym for massively parallel training of locomotion policies.
- Utilizes an ==Asymmetric Actor Critic== architecture with ==PPO==, incorporating detailed reward functions and comprehensive ==domain randomization== to enhance policy robustness.
- Integrates a novel ==sim-to-sim validation== pipeline, allowing policies trained in Isaac Gym to be rigorously tested in a meticulously calibrated MuJoCo environment before real-world deployment.

## Results

- Successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of locomotion policies to real RobotEra XBot-S and XBot-L humanoid robots, enabling movement on both flat and uneven terrains.
- Achieved high fidelity in MuJoCo's dynamics after calibration, with leg swing sine waves nearly identical to real-world trajectories and phase portraits showing significantly closer dynamics to the real robot than Isaac Gym.
- Released the Humanoid-Gym framework as an open-source tool, complete with system design and verification on multiple hardware platforms, making it accessible to the broader robotics research community.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extensive domain randomization combined with massively parallel DRL training in Isaac Gym can effectively bridge the sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid robots, enabling zero-shot transfer.
> - An intermediate sim-to-sim validation step, using a higher-fidelity simulator like MuJoCo calibrated to real-world dynamics, provides a crucial means to verify policy robustness and generalization.
> - Open-sourcing robust and well-designed RL frameworks for humanoids can significantly accelerate research and development in this challenging subfield of robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic locomotion has seen rapid advancements, particularly with the advent of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and high-fidelity, massively parallel simulators. This paper directly contributes to and pushes the boundaries of this landscape, specifically focusing on humanoid robots, which represent a pinnacle of complexity in legged locomotion.

Historically, DRL has achieved remarkable success in training agile locomotion for quadrupedal robots, demonstrating capabilities like traversing challenging terrains and performing dynamic maneuvers. Bipedal robots, such as Cassie, have also shown impressive progress, but humanoids present an even greater challenge due to their high degrees of freedom, inherently unstable bipedal nature, and the desire for human-like interaction and task execution in human-centric environments. The "sim-to-real gap" – the discrepancy between simulated and real-world physics – remains a significant hurdle, often being more pronounced for humanoids than for less complex robot morphologies.

Recent works have begun to address humanoid locomotion using advanced RL techniques, including transformer architectures and approaches that integrate upper-body control. However, a key gap identified by the authors is the notable *lack of open-source resources* specifically tailored for training humanoid robots with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer capabilities. This absence hinders the broader research community from quickly iterating and building upon prior work, unlike the more abundant resources available for quadrupedal robots.

Humanoid-Gym positions itself directly to fill this void. By providing an open-source framework built upon Nvidia Isaac Gym – a high-performance GPU-based physics simulator widely adopted for its parallel simulation capabilities – the authors are leveraging cutting-edge tools. Their work extends beyond typical sim-to-real efforts by introducing a novel "sim-to-sim" validation framework (from Isaac Gym to MuJoCo). This allows researchers to verify policy robustness and generalization in a more accurate, albeit slower, physics engine before attempting costly and time-consuming real-world deployments. This dual-simulation approach represents an important evolution in addressing the sim-to-real gap, offering a more robust pathway to real-world application.

In essence, Humanoid-Gym does not just present new results but also provides a crucial infrastructural contribution, democratizing access to powerful RL tools for humanoid robotics research and accelerating progress in this challenging domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Humanoid-Gym is driven by a clear set of objectives and motivations stemming from the unique challenges and opportunities presented by humanoid robots:

- **Primary Objective: Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer for Humanoid Locomotion.** The central aim is to develop a robust reinforcement learning framework that can train locomotion policies entirely within simulation and then deploy them directly onto physical humanoid robots without any further fine-tuning or adaptation in the real world. This "zero-shot" capability is crucial for reducing development time and cost, especially given the complexity and fragility of humanoid hardware.
- **Motivation:** Modern environments are predominantly designed for humans. Humanoid robots, with their anthropomorphic structure, are uniquely suited to navigate and interact within these environments. However, their complex kinematic and dynamic structures make robust control and real-world deployment exceptionally challenging, exacerbated by the substantial sim-to-real gap. Achieving zero-shot transfer is a significant step towards unlocking the full potential of humanoids in practical applications.

- **Secondary Objective: Provide an Easy-to-Use and Open-Source RL Framework.** The authors aim to create an accessible and well-documented codebase for the research community.
- **Motivation:** The current landscape lacks comprehensive, open-source tools specifically designed for humanoid RL with robust sim-to-real capabilities. This scarcity hinders widespread adoption and collaborative progress. By open-sourcing Humanoid-Gym, the authors seek to lower the barrier to entry for researchers, enabling them to focus on novel algorithms rather than re-implementing foundational components.

- **Third Objective: Integrate a Sim-to-Sim Validation Framework.** To enhance policy robustness and generalization, Humanoid-Gym includes a mechanism to test policies trained in Isaac Gym within MuJoCo.
- **Motivation:** While Isaac Gym offers unparalleled speed for DRL training, its physics engine, though good, might not always capture real-world dynamics with the same fidelity as more accurate, albeit slower, simulators like MuJoCo. Verifying policies in a different, highly accurate simulation environment like MuJoCo helps ensure that the learned behaviors are robust to variations in physics parameters and can generalize better, increasing confidence in their eventual real-world performance. This also provides a valuable tool for researchers without access to physical robots to rigorously test their policies.

- **Validation Objective: Demonstrate Efficacy on Multiple Humanoid Platforms.** The framework is designed to be validated on RobotEra’s XBot-S and XBot-L, two different sizes of humanoid robots.
- **Motivation:** Demonstrating successful zero-shot transfer across distinct robot hardware validates the framework's robustness, scalability, and practical applicability, moving beyond theoretical proof-of-concept to real-world utility.

In summary, the Humanoid-Gym project is motivated by the need to overcome the significant challenges of humanoid robot control in human-centric environments, specifically by providing an open-source, robust, and validated reinforcement learning framework capable of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, supported by a novel sim-to-sim validation step.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Humanoid-Gym framework employs a multi-faceted methodology that integrates massively parallel deep reinforcement learning (DRL), meticulous system design, a specialized reward structure, and a unique sim-to-sim validation process to achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The workflow begins with training policies using massively parallel DRL within Nvidia Isaac Gym. This training incorporates diverse terrains and extensive dynamics randomization to prepare the agent for varied real-world conditions. Subsequently, the trained policies undergo a sim-to-sim transfer validation in MuJoCo, a different physics simulator, to assess robustness. Finally, the policies are deployed on real humanoid robots in a zero-shot manner.

**Reinforcement Learning for Robot Control:**
- **Problem Formulation:** The control problem is framed as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), where the agent observes a partial state in the real world (o ∈ O) but has access to the full state in simulation (s ∈ S).
- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used, known for its stability and effectiveness in continuous control tasks.
- **Architecture:** An Asymmetric Actor Critic method is employed, where the critic has access to "privileged information" (full state) during training to learn a better value function, while the actor operates solely on partial observations for deployment.
- **Advantage Estimation:** Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is utilized to calculate advantage values, providing more stable and efficient learning.

**System Design:**
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint positions for a low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller. This allows the RL agent to command high-level movements while relying on the PD controller for precise joint control at a higher frequency.
- **Observation Space (for Actor):** The actor receives proprioceptive sensor data (joint positions and velocities, base angular velocity, Euler angles), a periodic clock signal [sin(2πt/C_T), cos(2πt/C_T)] indicating the gait phase, desired velocity commands (linear and angular), and the robot's last actions.
- **State Space (Privileged Information for Critic):** In addition to the observation space, the critic during training also accesses privileged information such as frictions, body mass, base linear velocity, external push forces/torques, tracking differences, and a periodic stance mask indicating planned foot contact patterns.
- **Control Frequencies:** The high-level policy operates at 100 Hz, while the low-level PD controller runs at a significantly higher 1000 Hz, ensuring fine-grained control.
- **Simulation Environments:** Nvidia Isaac Gym is chosen for its massively parallel GPU-based simulation, enabling rapid data collection for DRL. MuJoCo is used for sim-to-sim validation due to its reputation for accurate physical dynamics, which helps in robust policy verification.

**Reward Design:**
The reward function is meticulously crafted as a weighted sum of several components to guide the robot towards stable, commanded locomotion:
- **Velocity Tracking:** Rewards for matching desired linear (x, y) and angular (yaw) velocities.
- **Orientation Tracking:** Encourages maintaining a stable base orientation.
- **Base Height Tracking:** Rewards for keeping the robot's base at a target height (0.7m).
- **Velocity Mismatch:** Penalties for undesired linear (z) and angular (pitch, roll) velocities, promoting stability.
- **Contact Pattern:** Rewards for aligning actual foot contact detections with the periodic stance mask (swing vs. stance phases), crucial for stable gait.
- **Joint Position Tracking:** Rewards for keeping joints close to target positions defined by a sinusoidal reference motion.
- **Default Joint:** Rewards for staying close to a default joint configuration, promoting a natural posture.
- **Regularization Terms:** Penalties for:
 - **Energy Cost:** Minimizing motor torque and velocity product.
 - **Action Smoothness:** Reducing abrupt changes in actions over time.
 - **Large Contact:** Penalizing excessive contact forces, indicative of hard impacts.

**Domain Randomization:**
To mitigate the sim-to-real gap, comprehensive domain randomization is applied during training in Isaac Gym. This involves randomizing various physical parameters, including joint positions/velocities, angular velocities, Euler angles, system delays, friction coefficients, motor strength, and payload. This forces the policy to learn robust behaviors that generalize across a range of possible real-world parameters.

**Hardware Platform:**
The framework's efficacy is demonstrated on RobotEra’s XBot-S (1.2-meter tall) and XBot-L (1.65-meter tall) humanoid robots, showcasing its applicability to different robot scales and designs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that validate the effectiveness of Humanoid-Gym in achieving its objectives:

- **Successful Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The most significant finding is the successful deployment of policies trained exclusively in the Isaac Gym simulation directly onto real RobotEra XBot-S and XBot-L humanoid robots without any real-world fine-tuning. This zero-shot transfer was demonstrated for locomotion skills on both flat and uneven terrains. This is a crucial breakthrough, as the sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid robots is notoriously challenging. The ability of the robots to traverse significantly different terrains, including uneven landscapes that differed from the initial training scenarios, highlights the robustness and generalization capabilities of the learned policies and the efficacy of the chosen domain randomization techniques.

- **Effective Sim-to-Sim Validation:** The framework successfully integrates a sim-to-sim validation step, allowing policies trained in Isaac Gym to be rigorously tested in MuJoCo. This intermediate validation step is critical for ensuring policy robustness and generalization before real-world deployment. The authors emphasize that this capability allows researchers without physical robots to still validate their training policies effectively.

- **Meticulous MuJoCo Calibration:** A key enabling factor for the effective sim-to-sim validation was the meticulous calibration of the MuJoCo environment. The authors report that after calibration, MuJoCo's dynamics and performance closely mirrored those of the real-world environment. This finding is supported by quantitative comparisons:
 - **Leg Swing Sine Waves:** Trajectories of leg swing sine waves (representing joint positions over time) were nearly identical between the calibrated MuJoCo simulation and the real-world environment (Figure 3). This indicates a high fidelity in replicating joint movements.
 - **Phase Portraits:** A comparison of the phase portraits for the left knee joint and left ankle pitch joint revealed that the dynamics in calibrated MuJoCo were significantly closer to the real environment than those in Isaac Gym (Figure 4). This empirically validates MuJoCo's role as a more accurate physics proxy for the real world compared to the training simulator, thereby justifying its use for robust policy verification.

- **Open-Source Release:** The paper announces the release of the Humanoid-Gym framework as an open-source resource. While not a direct "result" of experiments, it is a key output and a finding in terms of community contribution. This open-source availability, coupled with meticulous system design and verification on multiple hardware platforms, signifies a robust and usable tool for the broader robotics research community.

In essence, the findings confirm that Humanoid-Gym offers a practical and effective solution for developing locomotion skills for humanoid robots. It demonstrates that with careful design, including specialized reward functions, extensive domain randomization, and a multi-fidelity simulation strategy, the significant challenge of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for highly complex robots can be successfully overcome.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Humanoid-Gym framework and its successful validation hold substantial significance and promise for a wide range of impacts across robotics research and application:

- **Accelerating Humanoid Robotics Research:** By providing an open-source, easy-to-use framework for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, Humanoid-Gym directly addresses a critical bottleneck in humanoid robotics. The lack of such resources has traditionally slowed progress. This framework will enable researchers globally to more easily develop, test, and deploy advanced locomotion skills for humanoids without the immense investment and complexity previously required. This democratization of access is likely to accelerate innovation in areas like agile movement, human-robot interaction, and complex task execution.

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Complex Systems:** The successful zero-shot transfer on humanoid robots of different sizes is a testament to the framework's robustness. Humanoids are arguably the most challenging robot platforms for sim-to-real transfer due to their high degrees of freedom, dynamic instability, and complex interactions with the environment. Demonstrating this capability sets a new benchmark and provides a blueprint for tackling sim-to-real challenges in other complex robotic systems.

- **Enhancing Policy Robustness and Generalization through Sim-to-Sim Validation:** The novel inclusion and empirical validation of the Isaac Gym-to-MuJoCo sim-to-sim transfer is a crucial methodological contribution. This approach offers an intermediate verification step that significantly boosts confidence in learned policies before real-world deployment. For researchers lacking access to expensive physical humanoid robots, this tool is invaluable, enabling them to rigorously test their policies against a highly accurate physics model, thereby reducing the risk of failure during eventual real-world experiments. This also promotes the development of more generalizable policies robust to minor discrepancies between simulators and the real world.

- **Practical Applications in Human-Centric Environments:** The use of RobotEra's XBot-S and XBot-L robots, which are industrial-grade humanoid platforms, indicates the immediate practical relevance of this work. Humanoid robots are envisioned for roles in logistics, elderly care, hazardous environment exploration, and human-robot collaboration. The ability to quickly and reliably transfer complex locomotion skills from simulation to these robots moves them closer to viable deployment in real-world, human-centric scenarios.

- **Foundation for Future Advanced Skills:** Robust locomotion is a prerequisite for more complex humanoid behaviors such as manipulation, navigation in cluttered environments, and intricate social interactions. By providing a solid foundation for dynamic and stable walking, Humanoid-Gym paves the way for integrating perception, planning, and more sophisticated control strategies to enable humanoids to perform a broader array of tasks.

- **Contribution to RL Best Practices:** The paper effectively showcases how a combination of massively parallel DRL, sophisticated reward engineering, extensive domain randomization, and multi-fidelity simulation environments can be synergistically employed to solve highly challenging real-world robotics problems. This serves as a valuable case study and a set of best practices for the broader reinforcement learning and robotics communities.

In conclusion, Humanoid-Gym is more than just a successful demonstration; it is a critical open-source infrastructure project that will significantly impact the trajectory of humanoid robotics research and its eventual real-world applications by making robust, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer more accessible and reliable.
