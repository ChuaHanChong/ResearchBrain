# Towards Open Vocabulary Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides the first exhaustive review of open vocabulary learning, a paradigm enabling AI systems to recognize categories beyond their training data, by consolidating methodologies across tasks like object detection and segmentation. The work categorizes over 50 models, highlighting the foundational role of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and identifying key trends, benchmarks, and future challenges in the field.

## Problem

- Conventional computer vision models operate under a 'close-set assumption,' limiting them to recognizing only categories seen during training, which is impractical for dynamic real-world applications.
- The inability to generalize to novel, unseen categories without costly re-annotation and retraining poses a significant bottleneck for deploying adaptable AI systems.
- A lack of a unified, comprehensive literature review exists specifically for open vocabulary learning, hindering systematic understanding and progression in this rapidly evolving field.

## Method

- The survey systematically defines ==open vocabulary learning==, distinguishing it from related concepts like zero-shot learning and open-set recognition.
- It provides a structured, task-wise categorization of methodologies across ==object detection, segmentation, video understanding, and 3D scene understanding==, analyzing how VLMs and language data are leveraged.
- The paper offers a comparative analysis of representative methods on standard benchmarks and datasets, accompanied by a discussion of challenges and future research directions.

## Results

- The survey found that **two-stage detectors** consistently outperform one-stage detectors in open vocabulary object detection, particularly with large backbones and sophisticated distillation/alignment techniques.
- For open vocabulary segmentation, models leveraging extensive VLM pre-training and large-scale caption data achieve the highest **mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)** scores.
- The work identifies base class overfitting, high training costs, and the absence of unified benchmarks as persistent challenges, suggesting future work in temporal information, advanced 3D understanding, and LLM integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP are foundational, enabling open vocabulary capabilities by transferring knowledge and aligning visual and linguistic features.
> - Auxiliary language data, such as image captions, serves as a cost-effective weak supervision signal, providing an expansive vocabulary for models to generalize to novel classes.
> - Generating pseudo-labels for novel categories and adapting existing close-set architectures with VLM-derived text embeddings are common and effective strategies across various tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual scene understanding has witnessed remarkable progress with deep neural networks across tasks like segmentation, tracking, and detection. However, most conventional approaches operate under a "close-set assumption," meaning they can only recognize categories explicitly defined and observed during training. This limitation poses significant challenges for real-world applications, where new or unfamiliar objects are constantly encountered, requiring costly and time-consuming re-annotation and retraining.

This survey addresses "open vocabulary learning" as a more general, practical, and effective paradigm to overcome the close-set limitation. It positions open vocabulary learning as a critical advancement beyond analogous concepts such as:
- **Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL):** While ZSL aims to generalize to unseen categories, it typically lacks examples of these novel objects during training and relies heavily on pre-defined word embeddings, leading to unsatisfying results in exploring visual information.
- **Open-Set Recognition (OSR):** OSR focuses on identifying known classes while rejecting unknown ones as "unknown," without attempting to classify the novel categories into specific types.
- **Open World Learning:** This paradigm involves identifying and incrementally learning new categories over time, often requiring human intervention for labeling, making it a dynamic, multi-step process.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection:** OOD detection focuses on identifying data that deviates from the training distribution without necessarily classifying the novel data.

Open vocabulary learning distinguishes itself by explicitly aiming to locate and recognize categories beyond the annotated label space, critically leveraging "visual-related language vocabulary data" (e.g., image captions or pre-trained Vision-Language Models like CLIP and ALIGN) as auxiliary supervision. This auxiliary data is more cost-friendly to acquire and provides an extensive vocabulary size, enabling models to generalize to novel classes, attributes, and motions.

The rapid progress of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which pre-train on massive image-text pairs and align visual and language features into a shared space, has been a significant catalyst for open vocabulary learning. VLMs enable current open vocabulary methods to bridge the gap between close-set and open-set scenarios, making models highly suitable for practical applications where extending object recognition to new domains is crucial without additional data collection or labeling.

This work fits into the broader research landscape by offering the **first exhaustive literature review on open vocabulary learning**. While existing surveys touch upon low-shot learning, multi-modal learning, or vision-language pre-training, they do not specifically consolidate the methodologies, settings, and benchmarks of the burgeoning field of open vocabulary learning. By providing a structured and comprehensive overview, this survey serves as a foundational reference for researchers, helping them navigate the advancements, identify key challenges, and explore future directions in this vital area of computer vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are clearly articulated by the authors:

1. **Consolidate Rapid Advancements:** The field of open vocabulary learning has been experiencing rapid progress, demonstrating impressive results across various visual scene understanding tasks. The authors identify a critical need to systematically track, summarize, and compare these recent developments.
2. **Fill a Gap in Existing Literature:** The authors explicitly state that, to their knowledge, no "exhaustive literature review" specifically on open vocabulary learning exists. Prior surveys have focused on related but distinct areas such as few-shot learning, zero-shot learning, multi-modal learning, or vision-language pre-training. This survey aims to fulfill this blank by focusing purely on the unique aspects of open vocabulary learning, including its specific methods, settings, benchmarks, and the integration of vision foundation models.
3. **Provide Comprehensive Background and Context:** A key objective is to provide a self-contained resource for researchers by elucidating the definition of open vocabulary learning, differentiating it from analogous concepts (zero-shot learning, open-set recognition, out-of-distribution detection), and tracing its historical development.
4. **Analyze Methodological Trends Across Tasks:** The survey aims to summarize and analyze common technical details and design elements across various pertinent tasks, including object detection, semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, video understanding, and 3D scene understanding. This involves dissecting how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and language data are leveraged in these diverse contexts.
5. **Benchmark and Comparative Analysis:** The authors intend to provide a comparative analysis of recent methodologies on commonly used datasets and benchmarks, offering a clear picture of current performance levels and the relative strengths of different approaches.
6. **Identify Challenges and Future Directions:** A crucial motivation is to synthesize current insights, pinpoint persistent challenges within the field, and discourse on prospective research trajectories, thereby guiding future research efforts and encouraging innovation.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to create an authoritative and up-to-date reference for the open vocabulary learning community, enabling a clearer understanding of its foundations, current state-of-the-art, and future potential.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This survey adopts a structured and systematic approach to analyze and synthesize the literature on open vocabulary learning. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Concept Definition and Delineation:** The survey begins by meticulously defining "open vocabulary learning" and distinguishing it from closely related concepts like open-set learning, open world learning, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, and zero-shot learning. This involves presenting formal mathematical definitions and a visual comparison to highlight subtle but important differences, providing a clear conceptual framework.
2. **Historical Review and Roadmap:** A historical overview charts the evolution of open vocabulary learning, identifying key milestones, influential works (e.g., OVR-CNN, CLIP, ViLD), and the rise of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This timeline helps contextualize current developments within the field's trajectory.
3. **Task-Specific Methodological Categorization:** The core of the survey's approach is a task-wise breakdown of methodologies. It covers:
 - **Preliminary Knowledge:** Before diving into open vocabulary methods, it first explains the fundamental principles of close-set object detection and segmentation (pixel-based and query-based), setting the stage for understanding how these are extended to open vocabulary settings.
 - **Vision Language Modeling:** This section elaborates on the foundational role of large-scale VLM pre-training (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN) and how visual grounding tasks relate to open vocabulary learning. A meta-architecture for transforming close-set detectors/segmenters into open vocabulary ones using VLM text embeddings is presented.
 - **Open Vocabulary Object Detection:** Methods are categorized into Knowledge Distillation, Region Text Pre-training, Training with More Balanced Data, Prompting Modeling, and Region Text Alignment.
 - **Open Vocabulary Segmentation:** Approaches are grouped by Utilizing VLMs for Recognition, Learning from Caption Data, Generating Pseudo Labels, Training without Pixel-Level Annotations, Jointly Learning Several Tasks, and Adopting Denoising Diffusion Models.
 - **Open Vocabulary Video Understanding:** This includes Video Classification and Object Tracking/Video Instance Segmentation, focusing on temporal fusion and association.
 - **Open Vocabulary 3D Understanding:** Methods for 3D Recognition, 3D Object Detection, and 3D Scene Understanding are reviewed, largely focusing on leveraging 2D VLM knowledge for 3D data.
 - **Closely Related Tasks:** Discussions on Class Agnostic Detection/Segmentation, Open World Object Detection, and Open-Set Panoptic Segmentation further contextualize the field.
4. **Identification of Common Methodological Themes:** Across these categories, the survey highlights recurring design elements and central themes that characterize open vocabulary approaches. For instance, the use of VLMs for transferring knowledge, auxiliary language data for weak supervision, pseudo-label generation, and sophisticated alignment techniques between visual and linguistic features are frequently discussed.
5. **Benchmarking and Performance Analysis:** For each task, the survey describes standard datasets (e.g., COCO, LVIS, ADE20K, UCF101, ModelNet40) and evaluation metrics (mAP, mIoU, PQ). It then presents detailed comparative tables summarizing the performance of representative works, highlighting state-of-the-art results and specific strengths of different methods and backbones.
6. **Challenges and Future Directions:** The survey culminates with a synthesis of insights, identifying current limitations (e.g., base class overfitting, high training costs, lack of unified benchmarks) and proposing future research directions (e.g., leveraging temporal information, advanced 3D understanding, custom task adapters, integration with incremental learning and Large Language Models).

The use of clear sections, subsections, figures (e.g., timeline, concept comparison, method taxonomy), and detailed tables (in the appendix) ensures a well-organized and easily digestible presentation of a complex and evolving research area. The paper also includes an active GitHub repository link to continuously trace related works, demonstrating a commitment to keeping the survey updated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey distills numerous findings and highlights key trends across the diverse landscape of open vocabulary learning:

**General Trends:**

- **Dominance of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** VLMs like CLIP and ALIGN are foundational. Most open vocabulary methods either directly distill knowledge from pre-trained VLMs, use their text encoders as zero-shot classifiers, or align visual features to their shared embedding spaces. This enables generalization to novel classes without explicit training data for those classes.
- **Leveraging Auxiliary Language Data:** Image captions and other forms of visual-related language data are extensively used as a weak supervision signal. This low-cost data provides a vast vocabulary, expanding models' generalizability beyond strictly annotated visual labels.
- **Pseudo-Labeling:** Generating pseudo bounding boxes or segmentation masks for novel categories using VLM knowledge or other unsupervised methods is a common strategy to augment training data and improve performance on unseen classes.
- **Architectural Adaptations:** Many methods build upon existing close-set detectors (Faster R-CNN, DETR) and segmenters (Mask2Former, FCN-based models) by replacing or modifying their classification heads with VLM-derived text embeddings. Query-based transformers are increasingly adopted for their flexibility.

**Specific Task-wise Findings:**

- **Open Vocabulary Object Detection (OVOD):**
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** Techniques like ViLD effectively transfer instance-level visual-to-visual knowledge from VLMs to detectors, often by aligning region embeddings with VLM image/text embeddings.
 - **Region Text Pre-training:** Approaches like GLIP and RegionCLIP leverage massive image-text pairs to pre-train detectors, learning robust region-text alignments. OWLv2 shows the power of self-training with pseudo-labels from web-scale datasets.
 - **Prompting Modeling:** Soft prompts or rich category descriptions are fed to VLM text encoders to generate better class embeddings, improving knowledge transfer.
 - **Performance:** Two-stage detectors (e.g., Faster R-CNN variants) generally outperform one-stage detectors, especially on COCO and LVIS datasets. Methods combining large backbones (e.g., RN50x64, Swin-B) with extensive pre-training and sophisticated distillation/alignment strategies achieve state-of-the-art. For instance, BARON and F-VLM demonstrate strong performance on COCO and LVIS.

- **Open Vocabulary Segmentation (OVSeg):**
 - **VLM Utilization:** LSeg pioneered aligning VLM text embeddings with dense pixel features. Methods like ZegFormer and MaskCLIP+ decouple segmentation from classification, using VLMs for mask classification.
 - **Caption-based Learning:** OpenSeg and CGG demonstrate the effectiveness of region-word grounding loss or combined grounding and generation losses from image captions.
 - **Training without Pixel-Level Annotations:** GroupViT and PACL show promising results by learning semantic segmentation from only image-caption pairs using contrastive learning, reducing annotation burden.
 - **Joint Task Learning:** X-Decoder and OpenSeeD explore unified frameworks that handle semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation, often by pre-training on mixed segmentation data and image-text pairs.
 - **Diffusion Models:** ODISE leverages the rich intermediate representations of text-to-image diffusion models for semantic information, while OVDiff uses them to generate prototypes for zero-shot segmentation.
 - **Performance:** Models leveraging large VLMs (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L/14, Stable Diffusion) and extensive training data (e.g., Conceptual Captions, Visual Genome) achieve the highest mIoU scores on datasets like ADE20K and PASCAL-VOC.

- **Open Vocabulary Video Understanding:**
 - **ActionCLIP and its variants:** Many approaches adapt image-based VLMs (like CLIP) for video by adding temporal fusion layers (e.g., ActionCLIP, I-VL, X-CLIP) or through efficient fine-tuning (ViFi-CLIP).
 - **Object Tracking and Video Instance Segmentation:** OVTrack extended VLM knowledge to multi-object tracking. MindVLT and OpenVIS address open vocabulary video instance segmentation by using frozen CLIP backbones and open vocabulary classifiers.
 - **Performance:** Open-VCLIP demonstrates state-of-the-art for video classification, highlighting the importance of continual learning and interpolated weight optimization for VLM transfer. MindVLT shows significant improvements in novel categories for video instance segmentation.

- **Open Vocabulary 3D Understanding:**
 - **2D-3D Bridging:** PointCLIP projects 3D point clouds to 2D depth maps for processing with 2D VLMs. ULIP and OpenShape aim to align 3D backbones directly with 2D image and language feature spaces, often with multi-modal (point cloud, image, text) triplets.
 - **3D Object Detection:** OV-3DETIC and OV-3DET decouple localization and recognition, adapting 2D detectors for 3D localization and aligning 3D regional features with VLM text features for classification.
 - **3D Scene Understanding:** PLA and OpenScene link 3D features with CLIP features by multi-view projections or dense captioning, enabling open vocabulary semantic segmentation in 3D.
 - **Performance:** Scaling up datasets (e.g., Objaverse-LVIS, OpenShape dataset with 876k shapes) and using larger 3D backbones significantly boost 3D zero-shot recognition. RegionPLC shows remarkable performance on novel categories for 3D semantic segmentation.

In summary, the survey indicates a strong reliance on pre-trained Vision-Language Models, diverse strategies for incorporating language knowledge, and a trend towards multi-task and multi-modal learning to tackle the complexity of real-world open vocabulary scenarios across different visual tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence, contributing to both academic research and practical applications.

**Significance:**

1. **Unified Framework and Conceptual Clarity:** By being the "inaugural exhaustive literature review" on open vocabulary learning, this work provides a much-needed unified framework and conceptual clarity. It systematically differentiates open vocabulary learning from related paradigms, which is crucial for fostering a shared understanding and directing future research precisely.
2. **Consolidation of a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The rapid pace of advancements in open vocabulary learning often leads to fragmented knowledge. This survey consolidates over 50 diverse models and approaches across various tasks, making it an invaluable resource for researchers to grasp the current state-of-the-art and identify major trends without sifting through countless individual papers.
3. **Benchmarking and Performance Insights:** The detailed comparative analysis of methods, datasets, and metrics offers critical insights into the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches. This assists researchers in selecting appropriate baselines, identifying areas for improvement, and developing more robust evaluation protocols.
4. **Highlighting the Role of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The survey emphasizes the transformative role of VLMs (like CLIP) in enabling open vocabulary capabilities. This underscores the importance of foundational models and encourages further research into leveraging and adapting these powerful pre-trained systems for downstream tasks.
5. **Identification of Gaps and Challenges:** By explicitly outlining current challenges such as base class overfitting, high training costs, and the need for better benchmarks, the survey guides researchers toward pressing problems, preventing redundant efforts and fostering targeted innovation.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Accelerated Research and Development:** The comprehensive nature of the survey can significantly accelerate research and development in open vocabulary learning. New researchers can quickly get up to speed, while experienced ones can identify novel research directions and collaborations.
2. **Deployment of More Robust AI Systems:** The core promise of open vocabulary learning is to enable AI systems to perceive and understand objects beyond their training data. This has direct implications for developing more robust, adaptable, and generalizable AI applications in real-world, dynamic environments.
3. **Real-world Applications:**
 - **Autonomous Driving and Robotics:** Autonomous vehicles need to recognize novel objects (e.g., new types of road signs, unexpected obstacles) safely and efficiently without explicit retraining. Robotics applications can benefit from recognizing new tools or objects in unstructured environments.
 - **Image and Video Content Analysis:** Open vocabulary models can categorize and search for arbitrary objects or concepts in vast image/video databases, revolutionizing content moderation, media analysis, and surveillance.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Identifying rare diseases or anatomical variations without extensive specialized datasets can be crucial for diagnosis and research.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** More natural and flexible interactions can be achieved if systems can understand a wider range of user-described objects.
4. **Informing Future Foundation Model Design:** The challenges and successes discussed in the survey can inform the design of next-generation vision and language foundation models, guiding their pre-training objectives and architectural choices to better support open vocabulary capabilities.
5. **Guidance for Future Directions:** The "Future Work" section serves as a roadmap for the community, pointing towards critical areas such as leveraging temporal information in videos, advancing 3D open vocabulary understanding, designing task-specific adapters for foundation models, integrating with incremental learning to handle dynamic data streams, and fully harnessing the knowledge within large language models (LLMs). This proactive guidance is essential for the continued evolution and practical relevance of the field.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work but a strategic contribution that structures knowledge, highlights opportunities, and paves the way for the next generation of generalizable and adaptable computer vision systems.
