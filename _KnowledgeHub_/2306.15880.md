# Towards Open Vocabulary Learning: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides the first exhaustive review of open vocabulary learning, a paradigm enabling AI systems to recognize categories beyond their training data, by consolidating methodologies across tasks like object detection and segmentation. The work categorizes over 50 models, highlighting the foundational role of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and identifying key trends, benchmarks, and future challenges in the field.

## Problem

- Conventional computer vision models operate under a 'close-set assumption,' limiting them to recognizing only categories seen during training, which is impractical for dynamic real-world applications.
- The inability to generalize to novel, unseen categories without costly re-annotation and retraining poses a significant bottleneck for deploying adaptable AI systems.
- A lack of a unified, comprehensive literature review exists specifically for open vocabulary learning, hindering systematic understanding and progression in this rapidly evolving field.

## Method

- The survey systematically defines ==open vocabulary learning==, distinguishing it from related concepts like zero-shot learning and open-set recognition.
- It provides a structured, task-wise categorization of methodologies across ==object detection, segmentation, video understanding, and 3D scene understanding==, analyzing how VLMs and language data are leveraged.
- The paper offers a comparative analysis of representative methods on standard benchmarks and datasets, accompanied by a discussion of challenges and future research directions.

## Results

- The survey found that **two-stage detectors** consistently outperform one-stage detectors in open vocabulary object detection, particularly with large backbones and sophisticated distillation/alignment techniques.
- For open vocabulary segmentation, models leveraging extensive VLM pre-training and large-scale caption data achieve the highest **mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)** scores.
- The work identifies base class overfitting, high training costs, and the absence of unified benchmarks as persistent challenges, suggesting future work in temporal information, advanced 3D understanding, and LLM integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP are foundational, enabling open vocabulary capabilities by transferring knowledge and aligning visual and linguistic features.
> - Auxiliary language data, such as image captions, serves as a cost-effective weak supervision signal, providing an expansive vocabulary for models to generalize to novel classes.
> - Generating pseudo-labels for novel categories and adapting existing close-set architectures with VLM-derived text embeddings are common and effective strategies across various tasks.
