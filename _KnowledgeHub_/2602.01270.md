# Mixture-of-World Models: Scaling Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning with Modular Latent Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Mixture-of-World Models (MoW), a modular architecture designed to scale multi-task reinforcement learning in visual environments by handling diverse observations and dynamics. The MoW agent achieved a 110.4% mean human-normalized score across 26 Atari games using 50% fewer parameters than an ensemble of single-task models, and set a new state-of-the-art for visual multi-task learning on Meta-World with a 74.5% average success rate.

## Problem

- Achieving sample efficiency in multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) within visual domains, where data collection is costly.
- The inability of monolithic world models to effectively capture heterogeneous visual observations and diverse underlying dynamics across multiple tasks.
- Lack of scalable world model architectures that maintain high-fidelity reconstruction and generalize well across diverse visual multi-task settings.

## Method

- A **Mixture-of-World Models** (MoW) architecture with a modular design, employing ==task-adaptive== Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for perception and a hybrid Transformer-based dynamics model with expert routing for temporal modeling.
- A gradient-based task clustering strategy during a warmup phase to efficiently group tasks and optimize parameter sharing for VAE, predictor, and critic network modules.
- Integration of specialized training losses, including a harmonious loss to mitigate gradient conflicts, a balance loss to ensure even utilization of expert modules, and an auxiliary task prediction head to foster task-discriminative latent representations.

## Results

- Achieved a **110.4%** mean human-normalized score on 26 Atari games, matching the performance of a state-of-the-art ensemble (**114.2%**) with a **50%** reduction in model parameters (972.5 MB vs 1977.5 MB).
- Established a new state-of-the-art for visual MTRL on Meta-World with a **74.5%** average success rate across 50 tasks in 300k environment steps per task, demonstrating superior sample efficiency over model-free baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of modular VAEs, expert Transformers, the task prediction head, harmonious loss, balance loss, and gradient-based task clustering to MoW's overall performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular architecture, comprising task-adaptive VAEs and expert Transformers, is effective for simultaneously capturing diverse visual inputs and dynamics across heterogeneous multi-task environments.
> - Gradient-based task clustering prior to training enables more meaningful parameter sharing and improved performance compared to random task partitioning for module assignment.
> - The combination of harmonious loss, expert balance loss, and an auxiliary task prediction head is critical for stabilizing training, preventing expert collapse, and promoting task-discriminative feature learning in complex modular MTRL systems.
