# 3D Whole-body Grasp Synthesis with Directional Controllability

> [!summary] Summary
> CWGrasp synthesizes 3D whole-body grasps for objects on receptacles by first sampling a plausible reaching direction via geometric ray-casting, then generating a compatible reaching body and grasping hand conditioned on that direction, then refining them together; it beats FLEX on realism (71% preference) using 500x fewer samples and 16x less compute.

## Problem

- FLEX generates a guiding hand grasp with GrabNet before any body/receptacle awareness, so the hand direction is random and often physically incompatible with how a body could actually approach it.
- Because the guiding hand lacks controllability, FLEX needs heavy post-hoc correction and exhaustive sampling of 500 candidate bodies to find one that matches without penetration.
- Existing body and hand synthesis models (GNet, GrabNet) condition only on object shape/location, offering no mechanism to control the direction of reach or grasp during generation.

## Method

- **ReachingField**: a probabilistic 3D vector field built by ==ray-casting== from the object, filtering rays that collide with the receptacle, ground, or arm volume, yielding a likelihood-weighted set of plausible reaching directions.
- **CReach**: a ==conditional VAE== extending GNet that generates a SMPL-X reaching body conditioned on a target wrist location plus a desired 3D arm direction, trained on CIRCLE and GRAB, supporting left/right arms.
- **CGrasp**: a ==conditional VAE== extending GrabNet that generates a MANO grasping hand conditioned on object shape plus a desired palm direction, using an ==InterField== hand-to-object distance representation and a ==direction loss==.

## Results

- Perceptual study across **35** participants per sample found CWGrasp preferred over FLEX **70.8%** of the time for full-body views and **71.6%** for zoomed hand views.
- CWGrasp matches FLEX's whole-body metrics on ReplicaGrasp while sampling only **1** body versus FLEX's **500**, cutting optimization runtime from **357s** to **23s**.
- CReach and CGrasp preserve conditioning accurately: mean angular error **7.67 degrees** (right arm) and **4.57 degrees** (palm), with CGrasp matching baselines like GrabNet and ContactGen on contact ratio and penetration metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Injecting geometry-based control signals early in a pipeline, rather than correcting incompatibilities late via optimization, can drastically cut sampling budget without sacrificing quality.
> - Decomposing a whole-body task into separately controllable, jointly-conditioned parts avoids the combinatorial search needed when parts are generated independently and reconciled after the fact.
> - Adding explicit directional controllability to a generative model need not trade off realism or diversity against an uncontrollable baseline, if the conditioning signal is grounded in real physical constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of synthesizing realistic 3D whole-body human grasps of objects, particularly when those objects are situated on a receptacle within a scene. This area is critical for various applications including animation, mixed reality, virtual assistants, robotics, games, and the generation of synthetic image datasets.

Existing research in this domain typically addresses either hand-only grasps or whole-body poses with limited hand dexterity. Many methods focus on isolated hands, utilizing statistical hand models like MANO or implicit shape representations, and are trained on datasets such as GRAB and DexYCB. Approaches for hand-only grasps involve learning contact maps, optimizing hand pose based on inferred contacts, or directly generating grasps through neural networks like GrabNet. However, these hand-only methods often lack the context of the full human body and the surrounding environment.

For whole-body grasps, the SMPL-X model is widely used for representing the human body. Prior work in whole-body interaction with scenes includes synthesizing human motion to reach target objects or generating static body poses that interact with scene elements like chairs. A notable method, FLEX, attempts to address the scarcity of whole-body grasp data by a two-stage approach: first generating a hand-only grasp (using GrabNet) and then optimizing a large number of sampled body poses to match this "guiding" hand.

The current work identifies two primary limitations within this landscape, particularly in methods like FLEX:
1. **Late Reasoning:** Body- and receptacle-aware reasoning is performed late in the synthesis process, after an initial hand grasp has been generated. This can lead to implausible initial hand directions that conflict with the body's ability to approach the object without scene penetration, necessitating extensive post-processing.
2. **Lack of Controllability:** Hand-only grasp generators (e.g., GrabNet) and some body generators (e.g., GNet) often lack direct control over the approach direction, resulting in grasps with random orientations that may not be physically viable or ergonomically natural in a scene context.

This paper positions itself by addressing these limitations through the introduction of directional controllability early in the synthesis pipeline and by integrating geometry-based reasoning from the outset. This approach aims to achieve more realistic and efficient whole-body grasp synthesis compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize realistic 3D whole-body human grasps for objects placed on receptacles within a scene. This task is motivated by several challenges inherent in existing methodologies, particularly the high computational cost and generation of physically implausible poses when global scene constraints and human body dynamics are not considered early in the synthesis process.

Specifically, the motivations for this work stem from observed limitations in prior methods:

1. **Addressing Implausible Grasp Directions:** Current hand-only grasp synthesizers, such as GrabNet, generate grasps with largely random palm directions. When these grasps are used to guide whole-body synthesis, as in FLEX, they often suggest an approach direction that is physically impossible for the human body to achieve without penetrating the surrounding receptacle or the ground. This necessitates significant and often complex post-processing corrections, which is inefficient. The objective is to introduce "receptacle awareness" early, enabling the synthesis of guiding hand grasps with plausible approach directions.

2. **Improving Computational Efficiency:** The existing approach of sampling a large number of diverse body poses (e.g., 500 in FLEX) and then individually refining each one to match a guiding hand is computationally expensive. This paper aims to significantly reduce this computational burden by generating "compatible" body and hand components from the start, requiring fewer samples and less extensive refinement.

3. **Introducing Directional Controllability:** A core motivation is to instill directional control into both the body's reaching motion and the hand's grasping pose. Existing body synthesis models (like GNet) and hand grasp models (like GrabNet) lack explicit input for desired arm or palm direction. The objective is to develop new models that can be conditioned on a specific 3D direction, ensuring that the generated body reaches and the hand grasps in a physically consistent and intended manner.

4. **Enhancing Realism and Plausibility:** Beyond just avoiding penetrations, the goal is to produce whole-body grasps that appear natural and coordinated. This involves ensuring that the body, arms, and hands are anatomically consistent, that the hand dexterously contacts the object, and that the entire body interacts realistically with the scene (e.g., standing on the ground, not penetrating the receptacle). The current work aims to achieve a higher degree of perceived realism compared to state-of-the-art methods.

5. **Supporting Left- and Right-Hand Grasps:** Many existing methods implicitly or explicitly focus on right-hand interactions. A specific objective is to develop a framework capable of generating both right-hand and left-hand whole-body grasps, increasing the versatility and applicability of the synthesis approach.

To achieve these objectives, the research defines four key contributions:
1. **ReachingField Model:** To generate plausible 3D reaching directions for an object, acting as a control signal.
2. **CReach Model:** To synthesize SMPL-X bodies that reach objects with a specified (right/left) arm direction.
3. **CGrasp Model:** To generate (right/left) MANO hands that grasp an object with a specified palm direction.
4. **CWGrasp Framework:** To integrate these components for efficient generation of dexterous, whole-body SMPL-X grasps for objects on receptacles, outperforming baselines in speed and realism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CWGrasp framework is designed to synthesize 3D whole-body grasps for an object situated on a receptacle by integrating geometry-based reasoning with controllable data-driven synthesis. The method consists of three novel modules: ReachingField, CReach, and CGrasp, which are combined and refined through an optimization stage.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The framework utilizes the **MANO** model for hand shape and pose representation and the **SMPL-X** model for whole-body representation. It extends components from **GrabNet** (specifically CoarseNet for initial grasp generation) and **GNet** (for body synthesis), both of which are VAE-based models.

#### 3.2. ReachingField – Reaching Direction

This module is designed to identify plausible 3D directions from which an object can be reached without body-receptacle collisions.
- **Ray Casting:** Rays are cast from the object's centroid to a uniformly sampled sphere in surrounding space.
- **Ray Filtering:** A multi-stage filtering process prunes implausible directions:
 - **Filter #1 (Arm/hand direction):** Rays intersecting the receptacle are removed, as they imply direct arm/hand penetration.
 - **Filter #2 (Body orientation):** Remaining rays are projected onto a horizontal plane, and further intersections with the receptacle are removed to prevent a body from easily approaching from certain directions. This step is conditional and can be disregarded if all rays intersect.
 - **Filter #3 (Standing places):** Vertical rays are cast from points along the curated rays to the ground. If these vertical rays collide with the receptacle or other occluders, the parent ray is pruned, ensuring a viable standing position for the body.
 - **Filter #4 (Wiggle room for arm volume):** To account for the body's volume, filtered rays are "swiped" within a small angular range around the vertical axis. Rays causing new intersections are discarded.
- **ReachingField Probabilistic Model:** The curated rays are assigned probabilities (`p_i`) based on object height and ray direction (e.g., objects near the ground are more likely to be grasped from above). This creates a probabilistic 3D vector field.
- **Inference:** Sampling this field yields a 3D reaching direction vector, which serves as a control signal for subsequent synthesis modules.

#### 3.3. CReach – Controllable Reaching Bodies

This module generates an SMPL-X body that reaches a target object with a desired arm direction. It is an extension of GNet.
- **Formulation:** It is conditioned on a normalized 3D arm direction vector (`d_arm`), which is the target direction for the SMPL-X elbow-to-wrist vector.
- **Training:** A conditional VAE is trained using a combination of GRAB and CIRCLE datasets. CIRCLE provides a richer range of reaching body poses. The CVAE's encoder and decoder are conditioned on body pose, translation, shape, target wrist location (as a proxy for object centroid), the desired `d_arm`, and a hand-interaction indicator (`h_int`) for right or left arm. An `L_d_arm` loss term is added to ensure alignment with the specified arm direction. Data is mirrored to balance right- and left-hand interactions.
- **Inference:** The decoder generates an SMPL-X body based on `d_arm` (from ReachingField), target object centroid, body shape, and `h_int`.

#### 3.4. CGrasp – Controllable Grasping Hands

This module generates a MANO hand grasp for an object with a desired palm direction. It extends GrabNet.
- **Formulation:** It is conditioned on a normalized 3D palm direction vector (`d_grasp`). The palm direction is defined by two pre-annotated vertices on the MANO outer palm. The module also incorporates 3D hand-to-object InterField vectors (`f_inter`), representing distances and directions from sampled hand vertices to the object.
- **Training:** A conditional VAE is trained on the GRAB dataset. The encoder takes object shape and `f_inter`. The decoder predicts MANO parameters and `f_inter`, conditioned on latent code, object shape, and `d_grasp`. `L_grasp` and `L_inter` loss terms are added to enforce direction and InterField alignment.
- **Inference:** The decoder generates a MANO grasp using `d_grasp` (from ReachingField) and object shape. A frozen, pre-trained RefineNet is appended for further refinement.

#### 3.5. CWGrasp – Whole-Body Synthesis

This framework integrates the above modules to produce a physically plausible SMPL-X whole-body grasp.
- **Combined Inference:** A single `d_arm` is sampled from ReachingField. This same direction conditions both CReach and CGrasp, generating an "already compatible" reaching body and a guiding grasping hand.
- **Optimization Objective:** An objective function `L_opt` is minimized, similar to FLEX but with modifications for ground and regularization:
 - `L_hm`: Hand matching term to align the SMPL-X hand with the guiding MANO hand.
 - `L_theta`: Body pose regularization to keep the pose on the CReach manifold.
 - `L_g`: Head-direction (gaze) term to ensure the body looks at the object.
 - `L_grd`: Ground-body penetration term to prevent body penetration and enforce contact with the ground.
 - `L_p`: Receptacle-body penetration term to prevent body penetration of the scene.
 - `L_reg`: Additional regularization (e.g., `cos-1` of foot-pelvis vector) to prevent unrealistic body tilts.
- **Search Space and Robustness:** Optimization operates in the original SMPL-X parameter space. To mitigate issues where CReach might produce a body already penetrating the receptacle, the body is initially translated by 1m along the floor-projected arm direction before optimization pulls it back, enhancing robustness.
- **Sample Efficiency:** CWGrasp samples only one body and one hand, in contrast to FLEX's 500 samples, significantly reducing runtime.
- **Left-Hand Interaction:** For left-hand grasps, CReach is conditioned for the left arm. The object and ReachingField's direction are mirrored, a right-hand grasp is generated, and then mirrored back. Optimization then proceeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CWGrasp involved various quantitative and qualitative metrics, demonstrating its performance compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Conditioning for CReach & CGrasp Accuracy

- **CGrasp:** Achieved a mean angular error of 4.57° for palm direction, indicating accurate preservation of the specified conditioning direction.
- **CReach:** Achieved a mean angular error of 7.67° for right-arm (RA) and 7.23° for left-arm (LA) direction. Wrist location Mean Squared Error (MSE) was 4 cm (RA) and 3.6 cm (LA). These values demonstrate accurate synthesis of both arm direction and wrist location based on the conditioning inputs (Table 1). Inference time for both models was approximately 0.46-0.47 seconds.

#### 4.2. Hand-Only Grasps (CGrasp)

CGrasp was evaluated on the GRAB dataset against DexGraspNet, ContactGen, and GrabNet.
- **Quantitative Metrics (Table 2):**
 - **Contact Ratio:** CGrasp achieved a contact ratio of 0.12, comparable to GrabNet (0.13) and slightly lower than DexGraspNet (0.11) and ContactGen (0.09).
 - **Penetration Percentage:** CGrasp showed a penetration percentage of 2.9%, which was higher than baselines (e.g., GrabNet 2.4%, DexGraspNet 0.13%).
 - **Penetration Volume:** CGrasp had a penetration volume of 1.16 mm³, comparable to other methods.
 - **Penetration Depth:** CGrasp had a penetration depth of 2.8 mm, similar to GrabNet (2.6 mm).
 - **Hand Pose Diversity:** CGrasp achieved a diversity of 6.72 cm, on par with GrabNet (6.72 cm) and ContactGen (6.75 cm), and slightly lower than DexGraspNet (7.08 cm).
- **Summary:** While CGrasp exhibited a slightly higher penetration percentage, it generally performed on par with the baselines across other metrics, notably while offering the additional benefit of directional controllability.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 10, S.4):** Contact heatmaps indicated that CGrasp grasps involved not only fingertips but also parts of the palm, suggesting richer contact patterns compared to baselines which primarily used fingertips.

#### 4.3. Whole-Body Grasps (CWGrasp)

CWGrasp was evaluated on the ReplicaGrasp dataset and compared against FLEX, the state-of-the-art.
- **Experimental Setup:** For 6 test objects and 6 training objects, 20 configurations per object were randomly sampled from ReplicaGrasp. FLEX generated 10 samples from 500 initial samples (best one used), while CWGrasp used a single sample.
- **Quantitative Metrics (Table 3):**
 - **Sample Efficiency and Runtime:** CWGrasp used 1 sample compared to FLEX's 500, resulting in a significantly faster average optimization time of 23 seconds, compared to FLEX's 357 seconds (approximately 16x faster).
 - **Body-Receptacle Penetration (%):** CWGrasp had 0.7%, higher than FLEX's 0.3%.
 - **Right Hand-Object Penetration (%):** CWGrasp achieved 0.7%, lower than FLEX's 1.16%.
 - **Right Hand-Object Contact Ratio:** CWGrasp achieved 0.3%, double that of FLEX (0.15%).
 - **Body Pose Diversity:** CWGrasp had 61.77 cm, comparable to FLEX's 63.86 cm.
- **Summary:** CWGrasp demonstrated competitive performance against FLEX, achieving significantly higher contact ratio and lower hand-object penetration, while being substantially more efficient in terms of samples and runtime.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 8, S.9-S.11):** CWGrasp produced more natural-looking body poses and richer contact patterns, with the palm actively involved in grasping, in contrast to FLEX, which often showed grasps primarily with fingertips and less natural overall body poses. CWGrasp also uniquely generated plausible left-hand whole-body interactions (Fig. S.12). The optimization stage significantly enhanced realism (Fig. S.13).
- **Perceptual Study:** A study with 35 participants comparing CWGrasp and FLEX outputs (full-body and zoomed-in views) indicated a clear preference for CWGrasp: 70.8% for full-body view, 71.6% for zoomed-in view, and 71.23% combined, indicating that CWGrasp produced grasps perceived as more realistic.
- **Failure Cases (Fig. S.6):** Identified failure cases included occasional arm penetration of the receptacle and instances where the body or legs became trapped in local minima during optimization, often in complex scene configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CWGrasp, a framework that advances the state of 3D whole-body grasp synthesis by addressing key limitations of prior approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Realism and Plausibility:** CWGrasp generates whole-body grasps that are perceived as more realistic compared to the state of the art, as supported by a perceptual study. This is attributed to its ability to produce natural-looking body poses, ensure richer hand-object contact (involving the palm, not just fingertips), and integrate physically plausible interactions with the scene (e.g., avoiding penetrations with receptacles). This advancement could lead to more immersive and believable virtual human characters in various applications.

2. **Increased Computational Efficiency:** A major impact of CWGrasp is its efficiency. By incorporating geometry-based reasoning and directional controllability early in the synthesis pipeline, the method reduces the need for extensive sampling and post-processing. It generates a single, compatible body and hand instead of optimizing hundreds of samples, resulting in a computational speed-up of approximately 16 times compared to methods like FLEX. This efficiency makes the generation of high-quality whole-body grasps more practical for applications requiring real-time or near real-time synthesis.

3. **Novel Directional Controllability:** The introduction of directional controllability for both the body's arm and the hand's palm is a novel contribution. The ReachingField model, CReach, and CGrasp provide explicit mechanisms to guide the synthesis process, ensuring that generated grasps align with a desired approach direction that is also physically feasible within the scene context. This allows for greater control and specificity in generating interactions, which is valuable for interactive applications and scene design.

4. **Broader Applicability:** CWGrasp's capability to uniquely generate both right- and left-hand whole-body grasps expands its utility across a wider range of scenarios and requirements. This feature makes the framework more versatile for animating diverse human-object interactions.

5. **Applications in Multiple Domains:** The advancements in realistic and efficient whole-body grasp synthesis have direct implications for:
 - **Animation and Games:** Creating more natural and engaging character interactions with objects.
 - **Mixed Reality/Virtual Reality:** Enhancing the realism of virtual assistants and avatars interacting with virtual environments.
 - **Robotics:** Providing a source of realistic human grasping data or models for learning and simulating complex robotic manipulation tasks.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Producing high-quality synthetic datasets for training machine learning models in areas like human pose estimation, object detection, and human-object interaction understanding, especially in complex scene contexts.

The identified future work, such as exploring bi-manual grasping, integrating intuitive physics reasoning for stability, and using generated grasps for motion synthesis, suggests continued research in expanding the framework's capabilities and addressing its current limitations, further enhancing its impact.
