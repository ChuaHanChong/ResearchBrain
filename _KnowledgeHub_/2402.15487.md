# RoboEXP: Action-Conditioned Scene Graph via Interactive Exploration for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboEXP enables robotic systems to autonomously build a dynamic, actionable understanding of complex environments by interactively exploring hidden objects and constructing an Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG). This approach achieved 70-90% success rates in challenging interactive tasks, outperforming prior LMM-based methods in object discovery and scene state recovery.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggled with perceiving and understanding dynamic environments where objects are hidden or require physical interaction to be revealed.
- Traditional scene representations were limited to static object relationships, lacking an explicit understanding of how actions change the environment.
- Current robotic exploration methods mostly involve viewpoint changes for navigation, rather than interactive manipulation to discover unobserved scene elements.

## Method

- Proposes the Action-Conditioned 3D ==Scene Graph== (ACSG) to explicitly represent both static object relations and dynamic action-object effects.
- Designs **RoboEXP**, an interactive exploration system that combines perception, memory, LMM-driven decision-making, and heuristic action primitives.
- Utilizes an LMM (GPT-4V) for common-sense reasoning to propose and verify exploration actions, guiding the robot's physical interactions.

## Results

- RoboEXP demonstrated 70-**90%** success rates across five complex interactive exploration tasks (Drawer-Only, Door-Only, Recursive, Occlusion), substantially outperforming LMM-only and heuristic baselines (0-**30%** success).
- The system effectively constructed accurate Action-Conditioned Scene Graphs, achieving significantly lower Graph Edit Distance and near-zero unexplored space compared to baselines.
- It showed high robustness to challenging illumination and varied backgrounds, adapted to human interventions, and extended to mobile robot platforms for household exploration tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An Action-Conditioned Scene Graph provides a powerful, actionable representation for robots to understand and interact with dynamic environments.
> - Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are effective for high-level action planning and verification when grounded by a structured scene representation and executed by reliable low-level primitives.
> - Interactive exploration, involving physical manipulation to reveal hidden objects, is fundamental for robots to achieve comprehensive scene understanding in real-world settings.
