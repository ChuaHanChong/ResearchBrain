# RoboEXP: Action-Conditioned Scene Graph via Interactive Exploration for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboEXP enables robotic systems to autonomously build a dynamic, actionable understanding of complex environments by interactively exploring hidden objects and constructing an Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG). This approach achieved 70-90% success rates in challenging interactive tasks, outperforming prior LMM-based methods in object discovery and scene state recovery.

## Problem

- Robotic systems struggled with perceiving and understanding dynamic environments where objects are hidden or require physical interaction to be revealed.
- Traditional scene representations were limited to static object relationships, lacking an explicit understanding of how actions change the environment.
- Current robotic exploration methods mostly involve viewpoint changes for navigation, rather than interactive manipulation to discover unobserved scene elements.

## Method

- Proposes the Action-Conditioned 3D ==Scene Graph== (ACSG) to explicitly represent both static object relations and dynamic action-object effects.
- Designs **RoboEXP**, an interactive exploration system that combines perception, memory, LMM-driven decision-making, and heuristic action primitives.
- Utilizes an LMM (GPT-4V) for common-sense reasoning to propose and verify exploration actions, guiding the robot's physical interactions.

## Results

- RoboEXP demonstrated 70-**90%** success rates across five complex interactive exploration tasks (Drawer-Only, Door-Only, Recursive, Occlusion), substantially outperforming LMM-only and heuristic baselines (0-**30%** success).
- The system effectively constructed accurate Action-Conditioned Scene Graphs, achieving significantly lower Graph Edit Distance and near-zero unexplored space compared to baselines.
- It showed high robustness to challenging illumination and varied backgrounds, adapted to human interventions, and extended to mobile robot platforms for household exploration tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An Action-Conditioned Scene Graph provides a powerful, actionable representation for robots to understand and interact with dynamic environments.
> - Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) are effective for high-level action planning and verification when grounded by a structured scene representation and executed by reliable low-level primitives.
> - Interactive exploration, involving physical manipulation to reveal hidden objects, is fundamental for robots to achieve comprehensive scene understanding in real-world settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses several critical gaps and challenges in the fields of robotics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence, particularly at the intersection of perception, reasoning, and manipulation.

**Bridging Static and Dynamic Scene Understanding:**
Traditional 3D scene graphs (e.g.,) have primarily focused on representing static relationships between objects (e.g., "cup on table," "monitor next to keyboard"). While valuable for descriptive scene understanding, they fall short in dynamic environments where objects can be hidden, moved, or interactively revealed. RoboEXP introduces the **Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG)**, a significant extension that explicitly models *interactive relationships* and *action effects* (e.g., "opening a fridge will reveal an apple inside"). This novel representation pushes the boundaries of scene understanding from static descriptions to actionable knowledge.

**Advancing Robotic Exploration from Navigation to Interactive Manipulation:**
Robotic exploration has historically been dominated by navigation tasks, where robots explore static environments by changing viewpoints to build maps or locate objects (e.g., information-theoretic or frontier-based methods). More recent work has begun to consider manipulation and active perception to understand scenes by changing environmental states. However, RoboEXP introduces the **novel task of interactive scene exploration**, where the robot *autonomously interacts* with the environment to discover objects that are not immediately visible. This shifts the paradigm from purely visual exploration to physical interaction as a primary means of information gathering, which is crucial for real-world scenarios like household tasks or industrial settings.

**Leveraging Foundation Models for Embodied AI:**
The paper positions itself firmly within the burgeoning trend of utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for robotics. Previous works have harnessed these models for common-sense reasoning, task planning, and code generation for control. RoboEXP takes this a step further by using an LMM (GPT-4V) not just for high-level planning but specifically for **guiding the exploration process** – proposing and verifying actions based on common-sense knowledge. This approach avoids the limitations of brute-force memorization by LMMs and instead grounds their knowledge into an explicit, structured representation (ACSG) that the robot can directly use for manipulation. This is a crucial step towards robust, general-purpose robotic systems that can operate in unknown and unobserved environments, extending beyond scenarios with known or observable objects.

In essence, RoboEXP is a foundational contribution that integrates state-of-the-art perception, LMM-driven decision-making, and robotic manipulation into a coherent system for building a dynamic, action-oriented understanding of complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to autonomously and efficiently explore complex environments, specifically to discover objects that are not immediately visible, and to build a rich, actionable understanding of these environments. This overarching goal can be broken down into several key objectives and their underlying motivations:

1. **Introduce and Address Interactive Scene Exploration:** The paper aims to define and tackle the novel task of "interactive scene exploration." The motivation stems from the limitations of prior robotic exploration research, which largely focused on static environments and viewpoint changes for navigation. For a robot to be truly useful in human environments (e.g., a household robot preparing breakfast), it must be able to interact with its surroundings – opening cabinets, lifting covers, manipulating objects – to find items that are hidden or require interaction to reveal. This capability is currently lacking in most robotic systems.

2. **Develop the Action-Conditioned Scene Graph (ACSG):** A core objective is to propose and implement the ACSG as a novel scene representation. This is motivated by the shortcomings of traditional 3D scene graphs, which primarily encode static spatial relationships. For interactive exploration and manipulation, a robot needs to understand not just *where* objects are, but *how* they relate through actions (e.g., "opening the fridge reveals the apple"). The ACSG explicitly models these action-conditioned relationships, providing both low-level geometric/semantic information and high-level action-effect associations.

3. **Construct the RoboEXP System for Real-World Application:** The research aims to build a practical, real-world robotic system, RoboEXP, that can effectively implement interactive scene exploration and construct the ACSG. The motivation here is to provide a concrete framework that leverages recent advances in large multimodal models (LMMs) and perception while enabling robust physical interaction. The system is designed to handle diverse scenarios, including rigid, articulated, nested, and deformable objects, and to operate in a zero-shot manner, meaning it doesn't require specific prior training for each new object or scene configuration.

4. **Demonstrate the Utility of ACSG for Downstream Manipulation Tasks:** Beyond exploration, a key objective is to show that the constructed ACSG is highly beneficial for a wide range of downstream manipulation tasks. The motivation is to prove that the effort of building such a rich scene graph translates into practical advantages, such as efficient object retrieval, task feasibility assessment (e.g., checking if a required object exists in the scene), and guiding complex multi-step manipulation plans.

In summary, the motivation is to move robots beyond merely seeing and navigating, towards truly understanding and interacting with dynamic, partially observable environments, a crucial step for deploying intelligent robots in complex, human-centric settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RoboEXP system tackles interactive scene exploration by formulating it as an active perception problem focused on incrementally constructing an Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG). The methodology integrates perception, memory, LMM-powered decision-making, and action modules in a closed-loop fashion.

#### 3.1. Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG)

At the heart of the system is the ACSG, a directed acyclic graph $G = (V, E)$.
- **Nodes (V):** Represent either objects (with semantics $s_i$ and geometry $p_i$) or actions (with high-level type $a_k$ and low-level primitives $T_k$).
- **Edges (E):** Encode four types of relations:
 - **Object-to-Object ($e_{o \rightarrow o}$):** Static spatial relationships (e.g., "door handle belongs to fridge," "plate on table").
 - **Object-to-Action ($e_{o \rightarrow a}$):** Indicates what actions can be performed on an object (e.g., "toy can be picked up").
 - **Action-to-Object ($e_{a \rightarrow o}$):** Shows objects revealed by an action (e.g., "banana can be reached if we open the cabinet").
 - **Action-to-Action ($e_{a \rightarrow a}$):** Preconditions between actions (e.g., "cabinet can be opened only if we move away the condiment").
The ACSG's key advantage is simplifying object retrieval: to find an object, the robot sequentially executes actions along the topological path from the root to the target object node.

#### 3.2. Interactive Exploration Formulation

The construction of a complete ACSG from partial observations is framed as an active perception and exploration problem. At each time step $t$, based on the current graph $G_{t-1}$ and observations $O_{t-1}$, the agent takes an action $A_t$, receives new observations $O_t$, and updates $G_t$. This involves adding new nodes (objects or actions) or updating existing ones. The goal is to discover and explore all nodes in the scene graph as quickly as possible, guided by a reward function:
$R_t = R_t^{graph} + R_t^{explore} + R_t^{time}$
- $R_t^{graph}$: Promotes discovering new nodes.
- $R_t^{explore}$: Rewards actions that reduce the set of unexplored nodes $U$.
- $R_t^{time}$: A small negative reward for each time step, encouraging efficiency.

#### 3.3. RoboEXP System Architecture

The system is comprised of four interconnected modules:

1. **Perception Module:**
 - Takes multi-view RGBD observations from a wrist-mounted camera (RealSense-D455).
 - Uses **GroundingDINO** for open-vocabulary object detection and **SAM-HQ** for high-quality segmentation.
 - Extracts per-instance **CLIP** semantic features, augmenting local-global image features with label text features for robust instance merging.

2. **Memory Module:**
 - **Low-level Memory:** Merges 2D-to-3D information from multiple viewpoints and across time steps. It uses an instance merging strategy similar to Lu et al., incorporating 3D IoU, semantic feature similarity, label similarity, and instance confidence. Crucially, it dynamically evaluates and updates outdated elements through depth tests, ensuring accurate reflection of environmental changes (e.g., an opened door). A voxel-based representation is used for efficiency and cleaner object representations.
 - **High-level Memory:** Analyzes relationships between objects and logical associations between actions and objects. Updates the ACSG by adding, deleting, or modifying nodes and edges based on changes in the low-level memory. This module also adds spatial relations (Algorithm 2) and action preconditions (Algorithm 3) to the graph.

3. **Decision-Making Module:**
 - Leverages a Large Multimodal Model (LMM), specifically **GPT-4V**, for common-sense reasoning, playing two pivotal roles:
 - **Action Proposer:** Given the current environment from the memory module, GPT-4V proposes the appropriate skill for unexplored objects (e.g., "Open the doors or drawers," "Pick up / Open the top object," "No action"). Prompts are designed to minimize ambiguity and align with the task.
 - **Action Verifier:** Checks the feasibility of a proposed action and identifies any obstructing objects in the scene based on ACSG information. This prevents the robot from attempting impossible actions.

4. **Action Module:**
 - Executes actions on the physical robot (UFACTORY xArm 7 with a soft gripper).
 - Consists of seven heuristic-based action primitives: "open the door," "open the drawer," "close the door," "close the drawer," "pick object to idle space," "pick back object," and "move wrist camera to position."
 - These primitives operate on low-level geometric information from the ACSG. For "open" actions, it uses handle position/geometry, PCA for gripper alignment, and normal vectors for opening direction (simulating motion for revolute joints). For "pick" actions, it uses object height and XY location (top-down grasping). "Move wrist camera" is parameterized by an expected pose for better views after interactions.

#### 3.4. Multi-step Reasoning and Scene Consistency

- **Action Stack:** A simple yet effective "planning" module that dynamically organizes the order of actions, facilitating multi-step and recursive reasoning (e.g., exploring nested Matryoshka dolls).
- **Greedy Strategy for Scene Recovery:** After exploration, the system attempts to return objects to their original states (e.g., closing open cabinets) to maintain scene consistency, making the system more practical for real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the significant efficacy and robustness of the RoboEXP system across a wide array of challenging interactive exploration scenarios in real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Interactive Exploration and ACSG Building

- **Quantitative Results (Table 1 & 2):** RoboEXP consistently outperforms a strong GPT-4V baseline, as well as heuristic and random baselines, across five diverse task types: Drawer-Only, Door-Only, Drawer-Door, Recursive (nested objects), and Occlusion (obstructing objects).
 - **Success %:** RoboEXP achieved 70-90% success rates, vastly superior to the GPT-4V baseline (0-30%) in complex scenarios.
 - **Object Recovery %:** While some baselines could randomly recover objects, RoboEXP consistently achieved higher, more reliable recovery rates (e.g., 97% for Drawer-Only, 100% for Door-Only) through systematic exploration.
 - **State Recovery %:** RoboEXP consistently recovered the scene to its initial state (100% for Drawer-Only, Door-Only, Drawer-Door, Recursive), a crucial aspect for practical deployment, which baselines struggled with or artificially inflated by taking no action.
 - **Unexplored Space %:** RoboEXP significantly reduced unexplored space (often 0% or low values), demonstrating its thoroughness compared to baselines (often 15-85%).
 - **Graph Edit Distance (GED):** RoboEXP produced ACSGs much closer to the ground truth (lower GED scores), indicating accurate scene understanding.
- **Efficiency (Table 3):** RoboEXP required fewer actions to construct a complete and accurate ACSG compared to baselines, demonstrating its efficiency and closer alignment with human action choices.
- **Qualitative Demonstrations (Fig. 2, 5):** Visualizations showcased RoboEXP's ability to:
 - Successfully explore articulated objects (cabinets, refrigerators) and retrieve hidden items.
 - Handle nested objects like Matryoshka dolls through recursive reasoning, building deep ACSGs.
 - Interact with deformable objects (fabric) to uncover items beneath.
 - Manage scenarios with obstructions (e.g., condiment blocking a door), highlighting the action verifier's role.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Generalization

- **Extreme Illumination and Random Backgrounds (Fig. 10):** RoboEXP maintained high success and object recovery rates (92-100%) even under challenging lighting conditions and varied backgrounds, indicating robust perception and decision-making.
- **Compatibility with Different LMMs (Table 4):** While GPT-4V showed superior performance, RoboEXP was also demonstrated to work with LLaVA-v1.6-34b, confirming its modular design allows for integration with various multimodal foundation models. This suggests adaptability to future LMM advancements.
- **Extension to Mobile Robots (Fig. 6):** The system was successfully adapted to a Hello Robot Stretch2, demonstrating its ability to perform interactive exploration in room-level household environments (e.g., finding an apple in a drawer, an e-reader under a cloth), requiring minimal modification to the action module.
- **Human Intervention (Fig. 11):** RoboEXP could autonomously adapt to dynamic changes:
 - **Adding New Objects/Structures:** Automatically detected and explored newly added cabinets.
 - **Object Removal/Addition:** Monitored human interactions and re-explored corresponding areas, updating the ACSG accordingly.

#### 4.3. Utility for Downstream Manipulation Tasks

- The constructed ACSG proved invaluable for tasks such as "table rearranging," enabling the robot to:
 - Swiftly locate and retrieve objects with precise prior knowledge of their location and required actions (e.g., a fork in a specific drawer).
 - Assess task feasibility by checking for the existence of required items (e.g., if a spoon is missing).
 - Guide complex, multi-step manipulation sequences.

Overall, the findings strongly validate RoboEXP's capacity to build comprehensive, actionable scene graphs through interactive exploration, significantly advancing robotic manipulation capabilities in complex, real-world settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboEXP system and the concept of the Action-Conditioned 3D Scene Graph (ACSG) represent a significant stride in robotics and embodied AI, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Enabling True Robotic Autonomy in Complex Environments:** The most immediate impact is on empowering robots to operate effectively in dynamic, partially observable, and unstructured environments like homes or offices. By allowing robots to actively explore and discover hidden objects through interaction, RoboEXP moves beyond reactive manipulation of visible items to proactive, intelligent engagement with the environment. This is critical for tasks requiring inventory checks, object retrieval, or general household assistance.

2. **A Powerful, Actionable Scene Representation:** The ACSG is a novel and highly impactful contribution. By explicitly embedding actions and their consequences into the scene graph, it provides a structured, high-level understanding that is directly actionable for robots. This goes beyond mere spatial or semantic descriptions, offering a blueprint for interaction and manipulation. The ability to topologically traverse the graph for object retrieval streamlines planning for complex tasks, making it a foundation for more intuitive robot programming and decision-making.

3. **Structured Integration of Foundation Models:** RoboEXP demonstrates a robust and principled way to integrate the vast common-sense knowledge of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) like GPT-4V into embodied robotic systems. Instead of relying on LMMs for brute-force memorization or generating entire action sequences that might fail in real-world environments, RoboEXP uses them for high-level decision-making (proposing and verifying actions) while grounding these decisions in an explicit, physical representation (ACSG) and executing them with reliable action primitives. This approach leverages the strengths of LMMs for reasoning while mitigating their potential weaknesses in direct, low-level control or real-world uncertainty.

4. **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** The system's demonstrated robustness to varied lighting, backgrounds, and its ability to handle different object types (rigid, articulated, nested, deformable) showcase its potential for broad applicability. Furthermore, its capacity to adapt to human interventions (e.g., adding/removing objects) indicates a path towards more flexible and cooperative human-robot interaction in dynamic settings.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Development:** The identified limitations (e.g., perception errors, need for more sophisticated skills) and proposed future directions provide a clear roadmap for further advancements. RoboEXP lays a strong groundwork for developing more generalizable skill modules, enhancing visual foundation models for open-world semantic understanding, and integrating temporal cues for more robust perception.

In conclusion, RoboEXP offers a compelling vision for future robots that can not only perceive their surroundings but also actively understand and shape them through interaction. This capability is essential for practical robotic deployment in complex, human-centric environments, ultimately facilitating their seamless integration into everyday human activities.
