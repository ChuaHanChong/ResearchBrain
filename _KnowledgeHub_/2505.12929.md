# Do Not Let Low-Probability Tokens Over-Dominate in RL for LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft Research Asia and CUHK researchers identify and address the issue of low-probability tokens dominating model updates during reinforcement learning training of large language models, introducing two methods - Advantage Reweighting and Lopti - that achieve up to 46.2% improvement on reasoning tasks while maintaining balanced parameter updates.

## Problem

- Low-probability tokens generate disproportionately large gradients during GRPO training of LLMs
- This gradient dominance impedes effective learning and precise adjustment of probability distribution across all tokens
- Prior work on GRPO update bias focused on sample quality, response length, or entropy collapse, leaving gradient-disproportionality of low-probability tokens unexplored

## Method

- **Advantage Reweighting**: Reduces weight of low-probability tokens by recalculating advantage based on token probability
- Low-Probability Token Isolation (Lopti): Updates low-probability tokens prior to high-probability tokens in a separated manner
- Derived a ==theoretical gradient-norm bound== proportional to (1-π) (π = token probability) that grounds and motivates both proposed reweighting methods

## Results

- **46.2%** improvement on K&K Logic Puzzle dataset for reasoning tasks
- Demonstrated effectiveness on math-related datasets (DSR and ORZ) and generalized beyond GRPO to other policy gradient-based RL algorithms like REINFORCE++
- Theoretical findings validated through empirical observations and ablation studies

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradient norm is theoretically bounded between values proportional to (1-π), where π is the token's probability
> - High-probability tokens are crucial for RL training despite generating smaller gradients
> - Update order matters significantly for effective training

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The paper is situated within the rapidly growing field of using reinforcement learning (RL) to improve large language models (LLMs). This area has gained prominence as researchers seek to enhance LLMs' reasoning capabilities, alignment with human preferences, and performance on complex tasks.
- **GRPO and its Challenges:** The paper specifically addresses challenges within Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm used to train LLMs. GRPO has shown promise in improving mathematical reasoning and code generation but also exhibits biases, such as favoring shorter responses. This paper builds upon existing work that aims to understand and improve GRPO.
- **Update Bias:** The paper identifies a previously underexplored source of update bias in RL training: the disproportionate influence of low-probability tokens due to their larger gradient magnitudes. This contributes a new perspective to the ongoing discussion of update biases in RL for LLMs.
- **Gradient Analysis and Token Probability:** The work delves into a gradient-level analysis, linking token probabilities to gradient magnitudes. This adds a theoretical dimension to the practical problem of update bias, grounding the proposed solutions in a more fundamental understanding of LLM training dynamics.
- **Novelty:** The paper claims to be the first to improve RL training from the "gradient-disproportionality" perspective, differentiating it from prior work that focused on sample quality, response length, or entropy collapse. This represents a novel contribution to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To identify and mitigate the issue of low-probability tokens dominating model updates during reinforcement learning training of large language models.
- **Motivation:**
 - The authors observed that low-probability tokens exert a disproportionate influence on model updates due to their large gradient magnitudes.
 - This dominance hinders the effective learning of high-probability tokens, whose gradients are essential for LLMs' performance.
 - The authors aim to improve the efficiency of RL training by promoting balanced updates across tokens with varying probabilities.
 - The paper seeks to enhance the reasoning capabilities of LLMs by focusing on more accurate and efficient parameter adjustments during RL fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Empirical Observation:** The authors begin with empirical observation of token probability distribution, advantages, gradient norms, and probability changes during GRPO training of LLMs. This reveals the tendency for low-probability tokens to have larger gradient magnitudes.
- **Theoretical Explanation:** A theoretical explanation is provided to ground the observation. The authors derive the gradients induced by individual tokens and demonstrate that the gradient norm of any intermediate activation is bounded between values proportional to (1-π), where π is the token's probability.
- **Proposed Methods:** Two novel methods are proposed to address the over-dominance of low-probability tokens:
 - **Advantage Reweighting:** Reduces the weight assigned to low-probability tokens by re-calculating the advantage based on token probability.
 - **Low-Probability Token Isolation (Lopti):** Separates low-probability tokens and updates them prior to high-probability tokens.
- **Experimental Validation:** The effectiveness of the proposed methods is evaluated through experiments on:
 - K&K Logic Puzzles dataset, a challenging reasoning benchmark.
 - Math-related datasets (DSR and ORZ) to assess generalization capabilities.
- **Baseline Comparison:** The proposed methods are compared to a naive GRPO baseline and existing SOTA language models on downstream tasks.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies are conducted to understand the importance of each component of the proposed methods and to analyze the impact of key hyperparameters.
- **Linguistic Analysis:** A linguistic analysis is performed to correlate the model's reasoning behavior and its final performance by analyzing the frequency of inference-related words.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Low-Probability Token Dominance:** The authors confirm that low-probability tokens generate disproportionately larger gradients compared to high-probability ones during GRPO training.
- **Impeded Learning:** This dominance impedes the precise adjustment of the probability distribution across all tokens.
- **Theoretical Justification:** They provide theoretical justification for the observed phenomenon, showing that the gradient norm is proportional to (1-π), where π is the token probability.
- **Improved Performance:** Both Advantage Reweighting and Lopti significantly improve the performance of GRPO-trained LLMs on the K&K Logic Puzzle dataset, achieving up to a 46.2% improvement in reasoning tasks.
- **Generalization to Math Datasets:** The methods show promise in improving performance on math-related datasets as well.
- **Importance of High-Probability Tokens:** Ablation studies demonstrate that high-probability tokens are crucial for RL training and cannot be disregarded.
- **Importance of Update Order (Lopti):** The ablation experiments confirm that updating low-probability tokens before high-probability tokens is key to the effectiveness of Lopti.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Addressing a Critical Issue:** The paper identifies and addresses a critical issue in RL training for LLMs that has received limited attention: the over-dominance of low-probability tokens.
- **Improving RL Efficiency:** The proposed methods have the potential to improve the efficiency of RL training by promoting balanced updates across tokens with varying probabilities.
- **Enhancing LLM Reasoning:** The research contributes to enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs, enabling them to perform better on complex tasks.
- **Practical Applicability:** The proposed methods are simple, effective, and can be easily integrated into existing RL training frameworks. The authors provide an algorithm to help with practical implementation.
- **Broad Applicability:** The findings and proposed methods are not limited to GRPO and can potentially be applied to other policy gradient-based RL algorithms. The authors validate this point by also demonstrating its effectiveness for REINFORCE++.
- **Future Directions:** This work opens up new avenues for research in RL for LLMs, particularly in areas related to gradient analysis, update bias, and token-level optimization.
- **Reproducibility:** The authors provide an implementation of the methods, contributing to the reproducibility of the research.
- **Societal Impact**: By contributing to the advancement of LLMs, this research has the potential to impact various applications, including education, healthcare, and customer service. However, it is crucial to address the ethical considerations associated with LLMs, such as bias and misuse.
