# All Robots in One: A New Standard and Unified Dataset for Versatile, General-Purpose Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> ARIO introduces a unified data standard and a large-scale, multi-modal dataset for embodied AI, integrating approximately 3 million episodes from real-world, simulated, and converted open-source data. This resource supports five sensory modalities and diverse robot types to facilitate the development of versatile, general-purpose embodied agents.

## Problem

- Existing large-scale robotic learning datasets often lack standardized formats, hindering efficient data processing and integration across different platforms and tasks.
- Current datasets suffer from insufficient data diversity, volume, and comprehensive sensory modality coverage, limiting the development of robust, general-purpose embodied agents.
- A critical gap exists in providing matched simulated and real-world data, which impedes research into effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap for practical robotics deployment.

## Method

- Developed ARIO, a new hierarchical data standard designed for unified collection, storage, and analysis of multi-modal, multi-embodiment data with timestamp-based synchronization.
- Constructed the ARIO dataset by combining new real-world teleoperated data, large-scale simulation generation, and the conversion of 62 existing open-source datasets into the ARIO standard.
- Integrated comprehensive sensory modalities (2D images, 3D vision, text, tactile, auditory) and diverse robot types (single-arm, bimanual, humanoid, mobile) to support versatile agent development.

## Results

- Successfully introduced the ARIO data standard, featuring a four-level hierarchical structure and a unified data format with timestamp alignment for comprehensive multi-modal, multi-robot data.
- Created the ARIO dataset, comprising approximately 3 million episodes across 258 series and 321,064 tasks, derived from converted open-source datasets (~2.3M episodes), new simulations (~703K episodes), and novel real-world collections (~3.6K episodes).
- The dataset supports five sensory modalities (image, 3D vision, audio, text, tactile) and includes a wide range of robot morphologies, control types, and manipulation/navigation skills, including complex bimanual and long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified data standard is crucial for overcoming data fragmentation, simplifying data sharing, and enabling the development of scalable, general-purpose embodied AI foundation models.
> - Integrating diverse sensory modalities and robot embodiments within a single, standardized dataset significantly enriches the learning potential for robust and nuanced agent behaviors.
> - A multi-pronged data acquisition strategy—combining high-quality real-world collection, scalable simulation generation, and efficient conversion of existing resources—is effective for building large-scale, diverse embodied AI datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of ARIO is situated at the forefront of **Embodied AI** research, a rapidly evolving field focused on enabling AI systems to interact intelligently with the physical world by integrating perception, cognition, and action. This paradigm shift requires increasingly sophisticated data resources that accurately reflect the complexities of real-world interactions.

The authors critically assess the current landscape of large-scale robotic learning datasets, such as RoboNet, RT-1, BC-Z, and notably, Open X-Embodiment. While these datasets have made significant contributions, the paper argues they suffer from several limitations that hinder the development of *versatile, general-purpose embodied agents*. These shortcomings include:
- **Lack of standardized formats:** Existing datasets often employ disparate structures, complicating data processing and integration across different platforms and tasks.
- **Insufficient data diversity:** Many datasets are task-specific or limited in the range of sensory modalities (often relying heavily on visual data), robotic platforms, and environmental variations they cover.
- **Inadequate data volume:** Despite some large collections, the sheer scale required for training robust, general-purpose agents, particularly with diverse modalities, remains a challenge.
- **Gaps in sensory modalities:** Few datasets simultaneously incorporate a comprehensive suite of inputs like 2D images, 3D vision, text, tactile, and auditory information.
- **Limited integration of simulation and real-world data:** This crucial aspect for studying the "sim-to-real" gap, essential for deploying agents trained in simulation to the real world, is often absent.

ARIO directly addresses these identified limitations. It aims to elevate the standard of embodied AI data by proposing a *unified data standard* and constructing a *large-scale, multi-modal, multi-embodiment dataset* upon it. This work fits into the broader trends of:
- **Foundation Models for Robotics:** Similar to the development of large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers (ViTs), there's a growing push to create general-purpose foundation models for robotics. Such models require vast, diverse, and standardized datasets like ARIO to learn broad capabilities that can be fine-tuned for specific tasks.
- **Multi-modal Learning and Sensory Integration:** The paper champions the necessity of integrating diverse sensory inputs, moving beyond purely visual perception to incorporate tactile, auditory, and textual cues, thereby enabling more nuanced and robust agent behaviors, akin to human perception.
- **Cross-Platform and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** By offering a standardized format for diverse robot types and integrating both simulated and real data, ARIO contributes significantly to research on transferring learned skills across different robotic platforms and bridging the simulation-to-reality gap, which are critical for practical robotics deployment.
- **Language-Guided Robot Learning:** With its emphasis on textual descriptions for tasks and episodes, ARIO provides rich contextual data that can be leveraged for training language-conditioned policies, a powerful approach for scaling robotic skill acquisition and human-robot interaction.

In essence, ARIO seeks to provide the foundational data infrastructure necessary for the next generation of highly capable, adaptable, and generalizable embodied AI agents, moving beyond task-specific solutions towards truly versatile robotic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ARIO stems from the recognition that the current landscape of embodied AI datasets, despite individual merits, collectively falls short in supporting the development of truly versatile and general-purpose agents. The authors highlight several critical deficiencies:

- **Lack of Standardization:** The absence of a unified data format across different robot types, tasks, and sensory inputs creates significant friction. Researchers spend considerable effort on data preprocessing and conversion, hindering efficient data utilization and collaborative development. Datasets like Open X-Embodiment, while aggregating data, still suffer from this structural inconsistency.
- **Insufficient Sensory Modalities:** Most datasets are predominantly visual. The physical world, however, is experienced through a rich tapestry of senses—touch, sound, and detailed 3D information. The lack of datasets comprehensively integrating 2D images, 3D vision, text, tactile feedback, and auditory inputs simultaneously limits the depth of perception and interaction abilities of trained agents.
- **Inadequate Data Volume and Diversity:** Training general-purpose models, especially in complex domains like embodied AI, demands massive amounts of diverse data covering a wide array of tasks, environments, and robot morphologies. Existing resources are often too small or too specialized to facilitate large-scale pre-training for broad generalization.
- **Challenge of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A critical hurdle in robotics is the gap between simulation-trained policies and their performance in the real world. A lack of datasets offering matched simulated and real-world data, or simulated data based on real-world scans, impedes effective research into bridging this gap.
- **Incompatibility Across Platforms:** Representing control actions and observational states for diverse robot forms (e.g., single-arm, dual-arm, humanoid, mobile) in a unified manner is a complex challenge not adequately addressed by current datasets.

To address these profound limitations, the **key objectives** of the ARIO project are clearly defined:

1. **Introduce ARIO (All Robots In One) as a New Data Standard:** This standard is designed to optimize existing datasets by providing a unified data format, ensuring comprehensive sensory modalities, and facilitating the combination of real-world and simulated data. The goal is to make data processing and utilization seamless across diverse embodied AI scenarios.
2. **Construct a Large-Scale Unified ARIO Dataset:** Building upon the proposed ARIO standard, the project aims to assemble an expansive dataset. This dataset is envisioned to contain approximately 3 million episodes, covering a vast number of series and tasks, sourced from both novel collections (real-world and simulation) and the conversion of existing open-source datasets.
3. **Enhance Training for Versatile, General-Purpose Embodied AI Agents:** By providing a superior data foundation, ARIO seeks to significantly improve the robustness, adaptability, and generalization capabilities of embodied AI models, enabling them to navigate and interact with the physical world in increasingly complex and diverse ways.
4. **Bridge the Gaps in Existing Data Resources:** Fundamentally, ARIO aims to rectify the deficiencies in current embodied AI data by providing a cohesive framework for data collection, representation, and accessibility, thereby accelerating research and development in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for building ARIO revolves around two core components: defining the **ARIO Standard** and constructing the **ARIO Dataset** through a multi-pronged data collection and transformation pipeline.

#### 3.1. The ARIO Standard

The ARIO standard is a meticulously designed framework for the standardized collection, storage, and analysis of embodied AI data. Its modular and hierarchical design aims to support scalable robot foundation model development and efficient algorithm testing.

- **Hierarchical Data Structure:** ARIO organizes data into four logical layers:
 - **Collection:** The highest level, grouping multiple `series`.
 - **Series:** Aligned with a specific scene and robot type (e.g., "Cobot Magic tabletop manipulation"). Each series contains an `information.yaml` file detailing the scene, robot, and sensors.
 - **Task:** Within each series, specific tasks are defined by natural language instructions (e.g., "picking an apple"). Each task includes a `description.yaml` outlining detailed instructions and required skills.
 - **Episode:** The lowest level, representing a single complete execution of a task, containing all synchronized observation and control data.
- **Unified Data Format with Timestamps:** A critical innovation is the standardized format for recording control and motion data from robots of varying morphologies. A timestamp mechanism is integrated to synchronize data from different sensor frame rates and robot action frequencies, ensuring accurate temporal alignment of multi-modal data. This allows for flexible representation of diverse control objects (e.g., joint angles for arms, absolute positions for mobile bases).
- **Comprehensive Sensory Modalities:** The standard mandates support for five key sensory modalities: 2D images, 3D vision (point clouds/depth maps), sound, text (instructions/descriptions), and tactile information. This breadth aims to create a richer perceptual dataset.
- **Metadata and Documentation:** Extensive metadata is embedded within `information.yaml` and `description.yaml` files at the series and task levels, respectively, ensuring clarity and traceability of the collected data.

#### 3.2. ARIO Dataset Construction

The ARIO dataset was compiled using a three-pronged strategy to achieve both scale and diversity, all converted to adhere to the ARIO standard:

1. **Collection from Real-World Scenarios (Ours):** This involved direct data acquisition using dedicated robotic platforms.
 - **Cobot Magic Platform (AgileX Robotics):** A bimanual mobile manipulation platform consisting of an AgileX Tracer AGV mobile base and four ARX ROBOTICS 6-DoF arms, equipped with Orbbec DaBai RGB-D cameras. Over 30 volunteers teleoperated this platform to collect 3,662 episodes across more than 60 diverse tasks. These tasks specifically focused on challenging categories like long-horizon, bimanual and fine manipulation, contact-rich interactions, human-robot collaboration, and deformable object manipulation, tasks where human teleoperation significantly enhances data quality compared to scripted policies. Data collected included joint positions, velocities, torques, end-effector poses, gripper states, and RGB-D observations, all recorded at high frequencies (e.g., 200Hz for proprioception, 30Hz for cameras) and subsequently post-processed into the ARIO format.
 - **Cloud Ginger XR-1 Platform (Dataa Robotics):** A 5G-enabled wheeled humanoid cloud robot with over 40 smart joints and dexterous hands. Human demonstrations were used to collect 800 episodes across three distinct tasks: "Pick up," "Put on," and "Push to." This provided data from a humanoid form factor, adding to the diversity of robot embodiments.

2. **Generation from Simulation Platforms (Ours):** Synthetic data was generated in various simulation environments to complement real-world data, ensuring scalability and control over environmental parameters.
 - **Habitat (HM3D):** Object navigation tasks (Habitat Challenge 2022 ObjectNav) were generated using a shortest path greedy follower agent in the Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D) photorealistic indoor scenes. This yielded 703,088 episodes from 1,198 tasks, providing extensive navigation data.
 - **MuJoCo (LLM-guided task generation):** Leveraging a framework for LLM-guided task generation ("Scaling Up and Distilling Down"), the UR5 robotic arm was simulated in MuJoCo for manipulation tasks like picking, placing, and opening drawers. This contributed 1,700 episodes, focusing on diverse object arrangements and scene complexities.
 - **SeaWave (UE5-based simulator):** Existing manipulation tasks from the SeaWave benchmark, designed to evaluate natural language instruction following, were converted into the ARIO format.

3. **Transformation from Open-Source Datasets:** This critical step involved converting existing large-scale datasets into the ARIO standard to immediately boost data volume and leverage prior community efforts.
 - **Open X-Embodiment:** A significant portion of the transformation involved converting 62 out of 72 datasets from this large-scale aggregation, yielding 2,326,438 episodes. This process required developing a dedicated conversion tool and carefully filtering out datasets with missing or unclear information to maintain data integrity.
 - **RH20T:** This dataset, featuring real-world teleoperated tasks with multiple cameras, kinematic data, and tactile sensors from seven different robotic arms, was converted, contributing 12,719 episodes. Challenges related to missing data and insufficient documentation were addressed during conversion.
 - **ManiWAV:** This unique dataset, notable for its inclusion of auditory data alongside visual and proprioceptive information, was converted. ManiWAV, comprising 1,297 episodes across four tasks, represents the *sole source of auditory data* within ARIO, highlighting its contribution to multimodal research.

By combining direct real-world collection, large-scale simulation, and the diligent transformation of existing open-source resources, the ARIO project meticulously constructed a vast and diverse dataset adhering to a newly proposed, unified standard.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The primary findings and results of this research are the successful introduction of the **ARIO standard** and the creation of the **ARIO dataset**, a significant resource for the embodied AI community.

#### 4.1. The ARIO Standard

The paper details the formalization of the ARIO standard, which serves as a cohesive framework for data collection, storage, and representation. Key features include:
- **Hierarchical Data Structure:** A clear four-level hierarchy (Collection, Series, Task, Episode) with accompanying metadata files (`information.yaml`, `description.yaml`) for comprehensive documentation and organization.
- **Unified Data Format:** A standardized approach to record control and motion data, adaptable to various robot morphologies and control objectives (e.g., joint angles, end-effector poses, navigation commands).
- **Timestamp-Based Data Alignment:** An embedded mechanism to synchronize diverse sensory inputs (e.g., camera, lidar, proprioception, tactile) and robot actions, crucial for multi-modal perception and control.
- **Comprehensive Sensory Modality Support:** The standard is designed to accommodate five distinct modalities: 2D images, 3D vision, sound, text, and tactile information.

#### 4.2. The ARIO Dataset

Building upon the ARIO standard, the researchers successfully assembled a large-scale, unified dataset with impressive statistics:
- **Overall Scale:** The ARIO dataset comprises approximately **3 million episodes** collected from **258 series** and **321,064 distinct tasks**.
- **Diverse Data Sources:**
 - **Transformation from Open-Source Datasets:** This constitutes the largest portion, with **2,326,438 episodes** (161 series, 319,761 tasks) converted from resources like Open X-Embodiment, RH20T, and ManiWAV. This highlights the successful integration and standardization of existing data.
 - **Generation from Simulation Platforms (ours):** Contributed **703,088 episodes** (95 series, 1,198 tasks) from platforms like Habitat, MuJoCo, and SeaWave.
 - **Collection from Real-World Scenarios (ours):** Provided **3,662 episodes** (2 series, 105 tasks) using the Cobot Magic and Cloud Ginger XR-1 platforms.
- **Comprehensive Sensory Modality Coverage:** ARIO successfully integrates data across five modalities: image, 3D vision, audio, text, and tactile information. Notably, ManiWAV's conversion introduced the critical audio modality, making ARIO one of the few datasets to support this.
- **Rich Scene and Skill Distribution:**
 - **Scenes:** The dataset predominantly features table and various indoor scenes (open domains, multi-room, living room, kitchen), with millions of episodes in table scenes alone, indicating a strong focus on manipulation in structured indoor environments.
 - **Skills:** ARIO encompasses a broad range of manipulation and navigation skills, including pick-and-place, twist, insert, press, cut, and more complex long-horizon, bimanual, contact-rich, human-robot collaboration, and deformable object manipulation tasks.
- **Diversity in Robot Parameters:** The dataset features a wide array of robot types, motion objects, physical variables, sensors, sensor positions, control modes, and operation modes:
 - **Robot Morphologies:** Includes single-arm, AGV (navigation), wheeled, bimanual, and humanoid robots.
 - **Motion Objects:** Covers endpoint, joint, and base control.
 - **Physical Variables:** Records rotation angles, positions, velocities, and forces/moments.
 - **Sensors:** Incorporates camera, RGB-D camera, audio sensor, and tactile sensor data.
 - **Sensor Positions:** Data from ego-centric (first-person), third-person, and gripper-mounted sensors.
 - **Operation Modes:** Includes kinesthetic, manipulation, teleoperation, imitation, and generation-based data.

The combined result is a data resource that is not only vast in scale but also exceptionally diverse and meticulously structured according to a new, unified standard. This directly addresses the critical shortcomings identified in existing datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ARIO, comprising both a novel data standard and a large-scale unified dataset, represents a highly significant contribution with the potential to profoundly impact the field of embodied AI in several ways:

1. **Enabling General-Purpose Embodied Agents:** The core significance of ARIO lies in its potential to accelerate the development of truly versatile and general-purpose embodied AI agents. By providing an unprecedented volume and diversity of standardized, multi-modal data across various robots and tasks, ARIO offers the foundational training material needed for models to learn broad capabilities that generalize across novel scenarios, hardware, and environments. This moves beyond task-specific solutions towards more adaptive and intelligent robotic systems.

2. **Standardization and Interoperability:** The ARIO standard itself is a monumental achievement. By offering a unified format for collecting, storing, and processing embodied AI data, it addresses a critical bottleneck in the field. This standardization will significantly simplify data sharing, integration, and analysis across different research groups and robotic platforms. It reduces the overhead associated with data wrangling, allowing researchers to focus more on model development and less on data preprocessing, thereby accelerating overall research progress.

3. **Advancing Multi-Modal Learning:** The comprehensive integration of five sensory modalities (2D image, 3D vision, sound, text, and tactile information) is crucial for developing robust and nuanced perception systems. ARIO's rich multi-modal data will enable the training of AI agents that can perceive and interact with the physical world in a manner more akin to humans, leveraging diverse sensory cues for better understanding and decision-making in complex, contact-rich tasks. The inclusion of auditory data, in particular, fills a significant gap.

4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The deliberate inclusion of both simulated and real-world data, with an emphasis on collecting data from the same robots in both settings, provides an invaluable resource for studying and mitigating the sim-to-real gap. This will facilitate the development of more reliable and transferable embodied AI agents, reducing the cost and time associated with real-world deployment.

5. **Foundation for Large-Scale Robotic Models:** ARIO's scale and diversity position it as a prime candidate for training "foundation models" specifically for robotics. Just as large datasets enabled breakthroughs in natural language processing and computer vision, ARIO can serve as the bedrock for training general-purpose robotic policies capable of understanding and executing a wide array of commands in diverse physical environments.

6. **Benchmarking and Reproducibility:** A standardized and open dataset facilitates more rigorous and reproducible benchmarking of new algorithms and models. Researchers can evaluate their methods against a common, high-quality data resource, fostering clearer comparisons and accelerating scientific progress.

7. **Fostering Collaboration and Open Science:** The project's availability, coupled with its acknowledgement of contributions from a diverse alliance of academic and industrial partners, underscores a commitment to open science. This collaborative spirit and open data access are vital for rapid advancements in a complex and resource-intensive field like embodied AI.

In summary, ARIO is not merely another dataset; it is a strategic effort to lay a standardized, comprehensive, and scalable data foundation for the next generation of embodied AI. Its potential impact spans from accelerating fundamental research to enabling the deployment of more capable and versatile robots in real-world applications.
