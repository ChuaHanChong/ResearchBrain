# SEBA: Sample-Efficient Black-Box Attacks on Visual Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SEBA (Sample-Efficient Black-Box Attacks) is a framework developed by researchers at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University that enables black-box adversarial attacks on visual reinforcement learning agents, particularly in continuous control tasks. The system significantly degrades victim agent performance while maintaining perturbation imperceptibility and achieving high sample efficiency by integrating a shadow Q model, a GAN for perturbation generation, and a world model.

## Problem

- Existing black-box adversarial attack methods for visual reinforcement learning (RL) are inefficient or ineffective, especially for continuous-action policies with high-dimensional image observations.
- Practical black-box attacks require minimal access to the victim agent's internals (no gradients/parameters) and low query costs to the environment, a challenge for high-dimensional continuous control.
- Adversarial perturbations need to be both highly effective in degrading performance and visually imperceptible to human observers.

## Method

- **SEBA** employs a two-stage alternating optimization process, leveraging a differentiable ==shadow Q-model== as a surrogate for the black-box victim agent's reward dynamics.
- A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) is used to create effective and visually imperceptible pixel-level perturbations, guided by the shadow Q-model's feedback.
- A learned world model generates synthetic transitions to drastically reduce the number of real environment queries needed for attack training, improving sample efficiency.

## Results

- SEBA achieved the lowest cumulative rewards across all MuJoCo continuous control tasks and maintained the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for imperceptibility (e.g., MuJoCo average FID **62.43** vs. **109.43** for PGD).
- The framework demonstrated superior query efficiency, requiring only **160K** environment and **800K** victim queries during training, a significant reduction compared to baselines like PA-AD and OPTIMAL (**4M** environment and victim queries each).
- SEBA successfully performed targeted attacks, achieving high success rates (e.g., **91.3%** on Walker Walk) in steering specific action dimensions, demonstrating fine-grained control over agent behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training the shadow critic on perturbed state distributions is crucial for accurately modeling agent performance under attack and providing effective guidance for perturbation generation.
> - Directly optimizing a generator with a stable shadow critic, rather than learning a policy in a high-dimensional perturbation space, is more effective for pixel-based continuous control attacks.
> - Integrating a world model allows for substantial reductions in real-environment query costs, making black-box attacks practical while maintaining attack strength and imperceptibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement learning (RL) from visual observations has become a foundational element in embodied artificial intelligence, facilitating applications such as robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, and visual control. These systems often learn control policies directly from raw pixel inputs, enabling operation in high-dimensional, dynamic environments. However, this reliance on visual perception renders them susceptible to adversarial perturbations, which are small, intentionally crafted modifications to inputs that can lead to significant performance degradation or system failures.

The field of adversarial robustness in RL has been investigated, but existing research has primarily focused on vector-based RL scenarios or discrete-action visual RL, such as environments in the Atari benchmark. In these settings, the state space is either low-dimensional or the policy network functions similarly to a classifier. Continuous-action visual RL, characterized by infinite action spaces and high-dimensional observation spaces, presents a more complex challenge for adversarial attacks. In this context, perturbations can induce intricate and long-horizon effects on system dynamics. Furthermore, attacking these systems under a black-box constraint, where an attacker has no access to the victim agent's internal model parameters or gradients, remains a significant, underexplored area. Prior black-box methods often incur high query costs to the environment, making them impractical for real-world applications where interactions are costly or time-consuming. This work addresses the identified gap by proposing a method specifically designed for sample-efficient black-box adversarial attacks on visual RL agents in continuous control tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient framework for performing black-box adversarial attacks on visual reinforcement learning agents, particularly those operating in continuous control environments. This objective is motivated by several challenges and considerations:

1. **Black-Box Constraint:** In practical scenarios, attackers typically lack access to the victim RL agent's internal architecture, parameters, or gradients. This constraint prevents the use of gradient-based attack methods, necessitating alternative strategies to infer vulnerabilities and craft adversarial perturbations solely from external observations of state, action, and reward.
2. **High-Dimensional Continuous Action and Observation Spaces:** Visual RL in continuous control involves processing high-dimensional image inputs and generating actions from an infinite space. This complexity makes it difficult for traditional black-box attack methods, often designed for lower-dimensional or discrete action spaces, to be effective and efficient. Adversarial perturbations in such settings must operate in a high-dimensional pixel space.
3. **Prohibitive Environment Query Cost:** Naive black-box attack strategies that rely on repeated queries to the real environment to estimate attack gradients or evaluate perturbation effectiveness are computationally expensive. Each interaction with the environment involves a sequential rollout, incurring significant time and resource costs. Reducing the number of required environment interactions is crucial for the practicality of such attacks.
4. **Balancing Competing Objectives:** An effective adversarial attack must balance three main objectives:
 - **Attack Strength:** Maximizing the degradation of the victim agent's performance (e.g., minimizing cumulative reward).
 - **Imperceptibility:** Ensuring that the adversarial perturbations are visually subtle and imperceptible to human observers, maintaining the visual fidelity of the input.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** Minimizing the number of interactions with the real environment and queries to the victim agent during the attack training process.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a principled framework that can model long-term reward effects, generate visually realistic perturbations, and significantly reduce dependence on real environment feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named SEBA (Sample-Efficient Black-Box Attacks), integrates several components within a two-stage iterative training procedure to achieve its objectives. The core components include a shadow Q model, a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) for perturbation generation, a world model for environment simulation, and an alternating optimization strategy.

**Problem Setup:**
The system operates within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) characterized by image observations ($s \in^{C \times H \times W}$) and continuous actions ($a \in \mathbb{R}^d$). The victim policy ($\pi(a|s)$) is treated as a black box, meaning only actions can be queried given an observation, without access to internal gradients or parameters. The attacker generates bounded perturbations $\delta = \text{clip}(G_\phi(s), -\epsilon, \epsilon)$ to form adversarial states $s' = \text{clip}(s + \delta, 0, 1)$. The attack objective is to minimize the victim's discounted return when acting on these perturbed states.

**Shadow Model Training (Shadow Critic $Q_{\text{shadow}}$):**
A shadow critic $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ is employed as a differentiable surrogate to estimate the victim policy's expected cumulative reward under adversarial perturbations. This model provides the necessary optimization signal for the attacker in a black-box setting. During training, the generator $G_\phi$ produces perturbations $\delta_t$ that are added to clean observations $s_t$ to create adversarial states $s'_t$. The victim policy then executes action $a_t$ based on $s'_t$, interacting with the environment to yield rewards $r_t$ and next states $s_{t+1}$. These transition tuples $(s'_t, a_t, r_t, s'_{t+1}, \text{done}_t)$ are stored in a replay buffer. The $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ model is updated using a temporal-difference (TD) objective, aiming to minimize the squared difference between the predicted Q-value and the TD target, which incorporates the immediate reward and the discounted expected Q-value of the next state. Critically, the victim policy $\pi$ is only used for action generation, not for gradient computation.

**GAN-based Perturbation Generation:**
To produce effective yet visually imperceptible perturbations, SEBA utilizes a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN). This GAN consists of a generator $G_\phi$ and a discriminator $D_\psi$. The generator creates bounded pixel perturbations, while the discriminator ensures perceptual realism by distinguishing between clean and adversarial states.
The generator and discriminator are optimized with opposing objectives. The discriminator's loss $L_D$ aims to correctly classify clean states as real and adversarial states as fake. The generator's loss $L_G$ has two components: a term that encourages the discriminator to misclassify adversarial states as real (promoting imperceptibility), and a second term that minimizes the predicted cumulative reward from the shadow critic $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ for the adversarial state-action pair (promoting attack effectiveness). This allows the generator to craft perturbations that are both visually subtle and effective in degrading the victim's performance.

**Two-Stage Alternating Optimization:**
To address the instability often encountered when simultaneously optimizing the generator $G_\phi$ and the shadow critic $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ due to their coupled objectives, SEBA employs a two-stage alternating training strategy:
- **Stage 1 (Shadow Model Update):** In this stage, the generator $G_\phi$ and discriminator $D_\psi$ are frozen. Adversarial interactions are rolled out to collect transition tuples, which are then used to update $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ via the TD loss. This ensures that the shadow critic accurately models the victim's reward dynamics under perturbations.
- **Stage 2 (GAN Update):** The shadow critic $Q_{\text{shadow}}$ is frozen. Adversarial states $s'$ are generated, and corresponding actions $a$ are obtained from the victim policy. The generator $G_\phi$ and discriminator $D_\psi$ are then updated based on their respective GAN objectives. This step optimizes the generator for attack effectiveness and imperceptibility, guided by the stable supervision from the fixed critic.

**World Model for Query Efficiency:**
To mitigate the high cost of real-environment queries, SEBA incorporates a learned world model ($W$). This world model is trained from a replay buffer containing real transitions. Following the IRIS framework, $W$ comprises a discrete image tokenizer (encoder $E$, decoder $D$) and an autoregressive Transformer ($G$) that predicts future latent tokens and rewards based on past state-action sequences. The world model is trained to minimize a joint prediction loss, which includes a negative log-likelihood for next latent token prediction and a mean squared error for reward prediction. Once trained, the world model can generate imagined transitions $(s'_t, a_t, \hat{r}_t, s'_{t+1})$ without interacting with the real environment. These synthetic transitions are used to update both the shadow critic and the generator. This approach allows for expansion of the replay buffer and significantly reduces real environment queries by a factor of $H$ (the model rollout horizon) for each real interaction.

**Overall Procedure:**
The SEBA framework initializes all models and a replay buffer. The world model is initially trained using a small set of real transitions. Then, the two-stage alternating optimization process iterates. During Stage 1, the shadow critic is updated using a mix of real and world-model-generated transitions. During Stage 2, the GAN components (generator and discriminator) are updated, also using a mix of real and synthetic data, with the shadow critic providing the attack signal. Periodic real-environment queries are interleaved to prevent model drift and maintain fidelity. Final evaluations of the attack are performed in the real environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments to evaluate SEBA across various visual RL tasks and against multiple baseline methods, assessing attack effectiveness, imperceptibility, and query efficiency.

#### 4.1. Pixel-Based MuJoCo Results (Continuous Control)

Experiments were conducted on five pixel-based continuous-control tasks from the MuJoCo simulator, using DrQ-SAC as the victim agent. Baselines included 2D image-space attacks (PGD, C&W, SimBA, Square) and vector-state RL attacker methods adapted to pixel control (Critic-Based, MAD, PA-AD, OPTIMAL).

- **Effectiveness (Reward Degradation):** SEBA achieved the lowest cumulative rewards across all MuJoCo tasks, indicating superior attack strength. For instance, on Cheetah Run, SEBA obtained a reward of $1.61 \pm 3.97$, significantly lower than PGD ($150.72 \pm 49.52$) and OPTIMAL ($271.73 \pm 34.72$). Similarly, on Reacher Hard, SEBA scored $0.30 \pm 0.90$ compared to OPTIMAL's $592.64 \pm 116.13$. This demonstrated SEBA's ability to induce long-horizon failures in image-based continuous control.
- **Imperceptibility (FID):** SEBA consistently achieved the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) across all tasks (e.g., $62.43$ for SEBA compared to $109.43$ for PGD and $93.04$ for OPTIMAL). A lower FID indicates that SEBA's perturbations maintain closer visual resemblance to clean observations, ensuring visual subtlety.
- **Query Efficiency:** SEBA exhibited high query efficiency. At execution time, SEBA required zero victim queries per step, unlike SimBA (400 queries/step) and Square (202 queries/step). During training, SEBA used $160$K environment queries and $800$K victim queries, which was substantially lower than PA-AD and OPTIMAL, both requiring $4$M environment and victim queries. This highlights SEBA's practicality under limited interaction budgets.
- **Comparison with PA-AD and OPTIMAL:** The paper highlights that while PA-AD (white-box) and OPTIMAL (black-box RL-based) are strong in vector-state settings, they degrade significantly in pixel-based continuous control. This is attributed to the extremely high-dimensional perturbation space ($3 \times 84 \times 84 \approx 2 \times 10^4$ dimensions), making RL-based exploration inefficient and gradient signals weak. SEBA's approach of directly training a generator guided by a shadow critic, rather than learning a policy in this high-dimensional space, proved more effective.

#### 4.2. Atari Experiments (Discrete Control)

SEBA was evaluated on three Atari games (Freeway, Pong, Alien) using Rainbow agents, which have pixel-based states but discrete action spaces.

- **Effectiveness (Reward Degradation):** SEBA consistently reduced rewards across all Atari games (e.g., Freeway: $34 \to 10$, Alien: $8858 \to 982$). It outperformed Critic-Based, MAD, and OPTIMAL baselines. While PA-AD achieved the strongest degradation, it is a white-box method and thus not comparable under black-box constraints.
- **Imperceptibility (FID):** SEBA achieved the lowest FID (81.7) among all methods, indicating visual imperceptibility of its perturbations in discrete-action settings.
- **Query Efficiency:** Similar to MuJoCo, SEBA maintained superior query efficiency, requiring $80$K environment queries and $400$K victim queries during training, and zero victim queries at attack time. This was significantly less than PA-AD and OPTIMAL, which required $2$M environment and victim queries.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study investigated the contribution of three key SEBA components: the discriminator (-D), perturbed states in Stage 1 (-Noise), and the world model (-WM).

- **Discriminator (-D):** Removing the discriminator led to a notable increase in FID (from $62.43$ to $97.18$), indicating reduced visual imperceptibility. The impact on reward degradation was smaller, suggesting its primary role is to ensure visually subtle perturbations rather than directly boosting attack strength.
- **Stage 1 Perturbed States (-Noise):** Replacing perturbed states with clean states in Stage 1 (i.e., training the shadow critic on clean inputs) resulted in the largest performance drop (e.g., Walker Walk reward increased from $35.74$ to $118.11$). This highlights the importance of training the shadow critic on the perturbed state distribution to ensure its value estimates accurately reflect the agent's performance under attack and provide meaningful guidance to the generator.
- **World Model (-WM):** Removing the world model resulted in comparable or slightly higher attack strength but significantly increased environment queries (from $160$K to $800$K). This confirms that the world model primarily contributes to sample efficiency by generating synthetic transitions, rather than directly enhancing attack performance.

#### 4.4. Targeted Attack Evaluation

SEBA was adapted to perform targeted attacks, aiming to steer specific action dimensions into a predefined target range.

- **Success Rate:** SEBA achieved substantially higher targeted-success rates compared to PGD and Critic-Based attackers across all evaluated tasks. For example, on Walker Walk, SEBA reached $91.3\%$ success, versus $37.2\%$ for PGD and $53.2\%$ for Critic-Based. On Reacher Hard, SEBA achieved $93.2\%$ success. This demonstrates SEBA's capability to not only degrade overall performance but also to exert fine-grained control over specific action components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a framework that advances the understanding and practical implementation of adversarial attacks on visual reinforcement learning systems. The significance and potential impact of SEBA can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Critical Gap:** SEBA directly tackles the challenge of black-box adversarial attacks on visual RL agents in continuous control tasks, a complex and underexplored area. This is particularly relevant as visual RL applications in robotics and autonomous systems continue to expand. By demonstrating the vulnerability of such systems under practical, limited-access conditions, the research highlights a critical area for future robustness improvements.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The integration of a learned world model significantly reduces the number of real-world environment interactions and victim policy queries required during attack training. This addresses a major bottleneck in black-box attacks, making the methodology more practical and scalable for real-world evaluation where interactions are often costly or time-consuming.
- **Superior Attack Effectiveness and Imperceptibility:** SEBA consistently achieved lower cumulative rewards (stronger attacks) and lower Fréchet Inception Distance (higher visual imperceptibility) compared to both white-box and black-box baselines across diverse benchmarks (MuJoCo and Atari). This indicates that SEBA can generate disruptive perturbations that are difficult for human observers to detect, posing a more insidious threat.
- **Generality Across RL Domains:** The experimental results demonstrate SEBA's effectiveness in both continuous-action MuJoCo environments and discrete-action Atari environments, showcasing its generalizability across different types of visual RL tasks.
- **Fine-Grained Control with Targeted Attacks:** The ability of SEBA to perform targeted attacks, steering specific action dimensions towards desired ranges, shows that the generated perturbations can be precisely controlled beyond mere performance degradation. This capability could be leveraged for more sophisticated adversarial manipulation or for diagnostic purposes in understanding policy vulnerabilities.
- **Implications for Robustness and Safety:** By providing an effective and efficient tool for evaluating the robustness of visual RL agents under realistic black-box threat models, SEBA contributes to the broader goal of developing more secure and reliable embodied AI systems. The findings underscore the necessity for robust defense mechanisms to ensure the safe deployment of visual RL in safety-critical applications. This work offers a principled framework for adversarial evaluation, guiding future research in building robust and trustworthy AI.
