# UniAct: Unified Motion Generation and Action Streaming for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and BIGAI developed UniAct, a unified framework that enables humanoid robots to interpret diverse multimodal instructions and translate them into stable, real-time physical actions. The system achieves sub-500 ms response latency and demonstrates superior performance, including an 83.1% success rate for text-to-motion tasks and 97.3% for trajectory-to-motion, while introducing the UA-Net dataset.

## Problem

- Bridging the "perception-control gap" in humanoid robots, allowing them to interpret complex, high-level multimodal instructions and translate them into low-level, real-time, stable physical actions.
- Existing end-to-end mapping methods struggle with long-term temporal dependencies and cross-modal reasoning, often leading to unstable motions, while hierarchical pipelines introduce unacceptable latency for real-time interaction.
- A scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and semantically rich multimodal datasets for humanoid motion generation hampers research progress.

## Method

- A two-stage framework integrating a fine-tuned ==Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)== with a causal streaming pipeline and a robust physics-based motion tracker.
- Employs Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) to unify heterogeneous inputs (text, music, trajectory, reference motion) into a shared, discrete token representation, which the MLLM then uses to autoregressively predict motion sequences.
- Implements a real-time causal motion decoding and streaming architecture via a server-client model, coupled with a client-side motion cache, to ensure sub-500 ms response latency for whole-body tracking on humanoid robots.

## Results

- Achieved superior performance across text-to-motion (**83.1%** success, **41.59%** R@1), trajectory-to-motion (**97.3%** success, 0.151m mean root error), and music-to-motion (**87.4%** success, **0.97** genre fidelity) tasks compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- Demonstrated real-time performance with a total response latency of 264 ms on an H100 GPU and below 500 ms on consumer GPUs, confirming responsive interaction capabilities.
- The FSQ discrete representation improved motion tracking robustness for low-quality (**95.4%** success vs. **76.2%** for raw) and unseen out-of-distribution motions (**95.2%** success) while maintaining high reconstruction quality, effectively refining imperfect inputs into stable robot actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified token representation created by FSQ allows a single MLLM to effectively reason across diverse input modalities and generate coherent motion sequences for humanoid robots.
> - Combining autoregressive MLLM generation with a causal streaming pipeline and robust motion tracking can overcome the latency issues of previous hierarchical systems, enabling real-time, responsive human-robot interaction.
> - Discretizing motion into a physically grounded codebook via FSQ significantly enhances tracking robustness by filtering noise and ensuring physically feasible movements, even from imperfect or out-of-distribution inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of interacting seamlessly and intelligently with the world is a grand challenge in AI and robotics. UniAct directly addresses a core bottleneck in this quest: **bridging the "perception-control gap"** – the chasm between high-level, multimodal human instructions and low-level, real-time, stable whole-body robot execution.

**Context of Humanoid Control:**
Recent advancements in humanoid control have led to remarkable progress in low-level tasks such such as agile locomotion, force control, and high-fidelity motion tracking. However, these successes often operate in isolation or rely on simplified input modalities. The challenge lies in enabling robots to interpret complex, heterogeneous instructions (e.g., "wave upon seeing a friend coming," "practice Chinese martial arts") and translate them into stable, real-time physical actions.

**Existing Paradigms and Their Limitations:**
The paper identifies two main paradigms attempting to bridge the perception-control gap:
1. **One-step end-to-end mapping:** Methods often using VAEs or diffusion models directly map inputs to actions. While promising low latency, they struggle with long-term temporal dependencies and the nuanced cross-modal reasoning required for complex instructions. They also tend to be brittle when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) observations or imperfect human demonstrations, leading to physically infeasible motions and robot instability.
2. **Two-step hierarchical pipelines:** These decouple motion generation from execution, improving instruction comprehension. However, they introduce significant computational overhead, planning challenges, and therefore, latency, making them unsuitable for real-time interaction.

**Leveraging Recent Breakthroughs:**
UniAct positions itself by capitalizing on two recent, powerful trends:
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** MLLMs excel at reasoning across diverse modalities (text, vision, audio) and understanding complex semantic relationships. By fine-tuning an MLLM, UniAct aims to tap into this high-level reasoning capability for interpreting instructions.
- **Robust Motion Trackers:** Modern physics-based motion trackers (like BeyondMimic, which UniAct adapts) are highly effective at executing reference motions with high fidelity while maintaining dynamic balance on physical hardware.

**Comparison with Multimodal Human Motion Generation:**
The field of human motion generation has seen an evolution from specialized text-driven systems to more versatile approaches incorporating spatial trajectories and musical signals. Recent unified frameworks (e.g., M3GPT, UDE, MotionCraft) aim to harmonize these modalities. However, a significant limitation of many of these methods, especially those relying on iterative diffusion processes, is their substantial computational latency. This makes them primarily suitable for offline generation rather than the instantaneous, real-time response demanded by humanoid control. UniAct specifically targets this real-time constraint.

**Addressing the Dataset Scarcity:**
A perennial problem in humanoid robotics is the scarcity of high-quality, diverse datasets. While some efforts have focused on motion retargeting from human MoCap data or creating specialized datasets for locomotion and manipulation (e.g., OmniH2O, TWIST), these often lack comprehensive semantic annotations and multimodal alignment. UniAct addresses this by introducing **UA-Net**, a large-scale, semantically rich multimodal benchmark designed to facilitate future research in this domain.

In essence, UniAct represents a critical step towards **general-purpose humanoid assistants** by integrating advanced perception and control into a unified, low-latency framework that is robust to real-world complexities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of UniAct is to enable humanoid robots to follow diverse multimodal instructions with human-level flexibility and responsiveness, thereby moving closer to the realization of versatile embodied agents. This broad goal is driven by several specific motivations and aimed at overcoming distinct challenges:

**Primary Motivation: Bridging the Perception-Control Gap in Real-Time**
The core motivation stems from the observed "significant bottleneck" between high-level multimodal perception and low-level whole-body execution. Current methods either lack sufficient semantic comprehension for complex instructions or introduce unacceptable latency, hindering real-time interaction. UniAct seeks to build a framework that is both semantically rich and computationally efficient.

**Specific Objectives:**

1. **Unified Interpretation of Diverse Multimodal Instructions:** To allow humanoid robots to interpret heterogeneous inputs such as natural language descriptions, musical rhythms, spatial trajectories, and even reference motions. This capability is essential for natural human-robot interaction in varied environments.
2. **High-Fidelity, Stable, and Real-Time Motion Execution:** To translate these complex instructions into physically stable, coherent, and natural robot actions without significant delay. The target is a sub-500 ms response latency, crucial for responsive interaction.
3. **Robustness to Imperfect and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Inputs:** To overcome the brittleness of existing methods when faced with noisy data, low-quality demonstrations, or novel scenarios. The system should generate physically feasible motions, preventing instability or falls.
4. **Decoupled and Scalable Architecture:** To design a system that effectively leverages the strengths of high-level models (like MLLMs for reasoning) and low-level controllers (for robust execution) without compromising real-time performance. This requires an efficient way to transfer information between these stages.
5. **Contribution of a Comprehensive Research Benchmark:** To address the scarcity of high-quality, multimodal datasets in humanoid robotics. UniAct aims to provide UA-Net, a large-scale benchmark with standardized evaluation protocols, to foster reproducible research and accelerate progress in the field.

**Key Innovations to Achieve These Objectives:**
UniAct proposes three core innovations to meet these objectives:
- **Unified Token Representation:** Mapping all heterogeneous inputs (language, music, trajectory, motion) into a shared, discrete token space via Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ). This enables seamless cross-modal translation and facilitates a common understanding for the MLLM.
- **Next-Token Prediction Paradigm:** Leveraging the autoregressive nature of LLMs for causal generation, which inherently minimizes response latency by predicting motion tokens sequentially.
- **Discrete Action Space for Physical Grounding:** By quantizing motions into a discrete codebook, UniAct constrains the generated movements to a physically grounded manifold. This design inherently improves tracking robustness by reducing the likelihood of generating infeasible or unstable robot actions, even from OOD inputs.

In essence, UniAct is motivated by the vision of intelligent, adaptable, and interactively responsive humanoid robots, and it proposes a novel architectural approach to overcome the critical technical hurdles in achieving this vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniAct introduces a sophisticated two-stage framework that integrates a fine-tuned Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with a causal streaming pipeline and a robust motion tracker. The methodology is meticulously designed to process diverse multimodal inputs, generate coherent motion sequences, and execute them in real-time with physical stability.

**Overall Architecture (Fig. 1 & 2):**
The framework comprises three core components:
1. **Multimodal Instruction Tokenization:** Unifying heterogeneous inputs (text, music, trajectory, reference motion) into a shared discrete token representation using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ).
2. **MLLM-driven Motion Generation:** A fine-tuned MLLM autoregressively predicts motion token sequences based on the unified input tokens.
3. **Real-time Whole-Body Tracking:** A causal decoder streams the generated motion tokens, which are then executed by a robust motion tracker on the physical humanoid robot.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Instruction Tokenization

The critical first step is to convert continuous and varied input signals into a discrete latent space compatible with transformer-based language models.
- **Text:** Tokenized directly using the native vocabulary of Qwen2.5, leveraging its pre-trained linguistic understanding.
- **Music:** Processed at 30 Hz to extract a 35-dimensional feature vector, including envelope, MFCC coefficients, chroma features, and beat indicators, capturing rhythmic and timbral qualities.
- **Trajectory:** Represented as angles of segmented line segments at 5 FPS. The angular difference in heading direction is discretized into 6-degree bins, resulting in a compact 60-code vocabulary.
- **Motion:** Robot Degree of Freedom (DoF) positions (29 DoFs for the Unitree-G1 robot) are used directly, avoiding the need for human-to-robot retargeting in this stage.

For music, trajectory, and motion, **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ)** is employed. Separate encoder-decoder pairs (1D convolutional networks with residual connections) compress continuous features into latent representations, which are then element-wise quantized. This process generates modality-specific vocabularies ($V_{music}, V_{traj}, V_{motion}$), ensuring temporal coherence and computational efficiency. The quantized latents are mapped to discrete token indices.

#### 3.2. Motion Generation

- **MLLM Fine-tuning:** A Qwen2.5-3B model is fine-tuned to autoregressively predict motion tokens.
- **Unified Vocabulary:** A shared vocabulary ($V_{unified}$) is created by concatenating $V_{text}, V_{music}, V_{traj}, V_{motion}$. Less frequent text tokens are substituted to manage vocabulary size.
- **Sequence Structure:** During training, each modality's token sequence is wrapped with special delimiter tokens. The complete input sequence concatenates all available modalities, followed by the target motion sequence. This explicit marking helps the model distinguish conditioning modalities from the motion tokens to be predicted.
- **Sliding Window:** For arbitrary-length conditions in trajectory and music, a sliding window approach samples fixed-length segments, allowing the model to learn from diverse temporal contexts.
- **Training Objective:** Standard autoregressive language modeling loss minimizes the negative log-likelihood of predicting the next motion token given previous tokens and the multimodal context.
- **Inference:** The MLLM generates discrete motion tokens autoregressively, which are then passed to the FSQ decoder to reconstruct continuous DoF positions.

#### 3.3. Causal Motion Decoding and Streaming

To achieve real-time execution, UniAct implements a two-part system:
- **Causal Decoding:** A convolutional network decodes generated motion tokens into humanoid DoF positions. The "causal" constraint ensures that motion at timestep *t* depends only on tokens up to *t*, enabling online inference without future information. Chunked decoding balances efficiency and latency by processing tokens in batches.
- **Real-time Streaming (Server-Client Architecture):**
 - **Server-side:** A GPU-equipped server processes multimodal instructions, tokenizes them, and feeds them to the MLLM for autoregressive motion generation.
 - **Client-side:** The generated discrete tokens are continuously decoded and streamed to the robot client via low-latency WebSocket communication.
 - **Motion Cache:** A client-side motion cache buffers incoming frames to synchronize the server's variable generation rate with the client's fixed 50 Hz control frequency, ensuring smooth motion playback.
 - **Contextual Smoothing:** 10 motion tokens are maintained as history context to facilitate smooth transitions between consecutive instructions.

#### 3.4. Humanoid Motion Tracking

- **Low-level Controller:** A modified BeyondMimic tracker serves as the low-level controller. The modification involves removing the global orientation component from the reference motion observation, allowing the policy to focus solely on relative joint configurations.
- **Observation Space:** Includes reference and current joint positions/velocities, gravity vector, angular velocity, and the previous action.
- **Execution:** The tracker outputs target joint positions that drive the robot via PD control, ensuring the robot follows the generated motion while maintaining dynamic balance and physical feasibility, even under external disturbances. This decoupling allows the tracker to reactively adjust joint commands.
- **Training:** The tracker is trained using PPO with a comprehensive reward function that includes tracking rewards (root orientation, body link positions/orientations, velocities) and penalty terms (action rate, joint limits, undesired contacts). Domain randomization is applied to enhance robustness.

#### 3.5. The UA-Net Dataset

To support and evaluate the framework, UniAct introduces UA-Net, a comprehensive multimodal humanoid motion dataset.
- **Modalities:** Text-to-motion (20 hours), trajectory-to-motion (20 minutes, augmented to 10+ hours using motion matching), and music-to-motion (376 retargeted dance sequences from FineDance).
- **Motion Capture:** Utilizes an OptiTrack system (48 high-speed cameras, 120 Hz, 43-marker configuration) with 10 professional performers (dancers, athletes, motion specialists).
- **Retargeting:** Human motions are retargeted to a 29-DoF Unitree-G1 compatible model using GMR, with manual refinement to preserve fidelity and respect humanoid constraints.
- **Text Annotation:** Over 10,000 expressive motion sequences with detailed descriptions, covering 1,400 common action verbs (1.8x HumanML3D coverage), organized lexicographically.

This robust methodology, combining cutting-edge MLLMs with tailored quantization and real-time control, offers a comprehensive solution to the complex problem of multimodal humanoid control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniAct demonstrates significant advancements across multiple dimensions of multimodal humanoid control, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines and exhibiting robust real-time performance.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Humanoid Control Performance (Table 1)

UniAct was rigorously evaluated across text-to-motion, trajectory-to-motion, and music-to-motion tasks against strong baselines (LangWBC, UH-1, OmniH2O+MDM, BeyondMimic+MDM).
- **Superior Performance:** UniAct achieved the highest performance across most metrics in all three modalities.
 - **Text-to-motion:** Significantly improved text-motion alignment (lower MM-dist, higher R-precision, e.g., 41.59% R@1 vs. 24.53% for BeyondMimic+MDM) and higher success rates (83.1% vs. 65.3%).
 - **Trajectory-to-motion:** Demonstrated superior locomotion capabilities with the lowest mean root error (0.151m vs. 1.284m for BeyondMimic+MDM) and the highest success rate (97.3%).
 - **Music-to-motion:** Produced strong genre correspondence (0.97 genre fidelity vs. 0.77 for BeyondMimic+MDM) and high success rates (87.4%).
- **Motion Quality (FID):** UniAct consistently achieved lower FID scores, indicating that its generated motions are closer in distribution to the high-quality training data, signifying more natural and accurate movements.
- **Diversity:** While UniAct's diversity scores were generally lower than diffusion-based two-step methods (e.g., MDM), the authors attribute this to the inherent characteristic of diffusion models generating more varied but potentially less controlled outputs. UniAct prioritizes controlled, faithful execution.
- **Ablation Studies:** Reducing the FSQ codebook size (0.25x codes) or increasing downsampling rate (2x downsampled) led to performance degradation, validating the chosen quantization settings as optimal for balancing fidelity and efficiency.

#### 4.2. Computational Time Analysis (Table 3)

Real-time performance is a cornerstone of UniAct's design. The system achieves impressive low-latency metrics:
- **Sub-500 ms Response Latency:** Total delay (including data transmission for new commands) was measured at 264 ms on an H100 GPU, 340 ms on an RTX 5090, and 461 ms on an RTX 4090. All these fall within the target sub-500 ms range, enabling responsive interaction.
- **Low Model Latency:** The core model latency (motion generation, token decode, motion track) is very low (e.g., 52.1 ms on H100), demonstrating the efficiency of the MLLM and causal pipeline. Data transmission constitutes the largest portion of the *total* delay, primarily when transitioning to new commands.
- **Real-time Control:** The system operates effectively at the client's fixed 50 Hz control frequency (20 ms per frame).

#### 4.3. Motion Tracking Evaluation with Discrete Representation (Table 2, Fig. A1)

UniAct's discrete motion representation via FSQ was shown to enhance tracking robustness.
- **Robustness to Low-Quality Inputs:** When tracking motions with simulated Gaussian noise and temporal jitter (low-quality references), BeyondMimic + FSQ (Ours) achieved a significantly higher success rate (95.4% vs. 76.2% for raw BeyondMimic) and lower MPJPE (36.10 cm vs. 68.34 cm). This demonstrates that the FSQ tokenization effectively filters out noise by constraining motions to a physically feasible manifold, refining imperfect inputs.
- **Generalization to Unseen Motions:** For unseen, out-of-distribution (OOD) motions extracted from online videos, UniAct's discrete representation maintained a high success rate (95.2%) and acceptable MPJPE (45.61 cm), indicating that tokenization helps guide the tracker towards physically plausible movements even for novel actions.
- **Reconstruction Quality:** The FSQ approach maintained high reconstruction quality for training set motions (100% success rate), confirming that the quantization does not unduly sacrifice fidelity for robustness.

#### 4.4. Compositional Cross-Modal Control (Fig. 4 & A7)

UniAct successfully demonstrated the ability to combine instructions from different modalities.
- **Upper-body/Lower-body Fusion:** The framework can independently generate upper-body actions from language (e.g., waving) and lower-body trajectories (e.g., walking along a curved path), then fuse them into coherent whole-body movements.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Through fine-tuning the tracking policy with composed pairs, the system achieves zero-shot generalization to unseen action-trajectory combinations, enabling the robot to perform actions like "making a phone call" while simultaneously following arbitrary paths, even if only stationary versions of these actions were in the training data.

In summary, UniAct delivers on its promise of real-time, robust, and versatile multimodal humanoid control, backed by strong quantitative and qualitative evidence, including comprehensive ablation studies and a novel dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniAct represents a substantial step forward in the field of humanoid robotics and embodied intelligence, offering several significant implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Enabling General-Purpose Humanoid Assistants

The most profound impact of UniAct lies in its ability to bring us closer to truly general-purpose humanoid robots. By effectively bridging the perception-control gap with low latency and high robustness, UniAct allows robots to interpret and execute a wide array of human instructions—from abstract language commands to precise trajectories and expressive musical cues. This versatility is fundamental for developing robots that can seamlessly integrate into human environments, assisting in diverse tasks in homes, workplaces, and public spaces, moving beyond narrow, specialized applications.

#### 5.2. Real-time, Responsive Human-Robot Interaction

The achievement of sub-500 ms response latency is a critical breakthrough. Prior methods often sacrificed real-time performance for semantic understanding or vice-versa. UniAct's causal decoding and streaming architecture allows for instantaneous reactions, making interactions with humanoids feel more natural and intuitive. This responsiveness is vital for safety, efficiency, and user acceptance in collaborative or assistive robotics scenarios.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness through Physically Grounded Motion Generation

The use of Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) to constrain generated motions to a physically grounded, discrete manifold is a key innovation. This approach inherently improves the robustness of the system against noisy inputs, imperfect demonstrations, or out-of-distribution observations. In real-world deployments, robots inevitably encounter unpredictable data; UniAct's ability to "refine" such inputs into stable, feasible movements significantly reduces the risk of instability, falls, or hardware damage, making robot operation safer and more reliable.

#### 5.4. Advancing Cross-Modal and Compositional Control

UniAct's demonstrated capability for compositional cross-modal control, where different body parts can be independently controlled by different modalities (e.g., upper body by language, lower body by trajectory), opens new avenues for sophisticated robot behaviors. This zero-shot generalization to novel action-trajectory combinations drastically expands the robot's behavioral repertoire without requiring exhaustive training data for every possible permutation, thus improving scalability and adaptability.

#### 5.5. Community Contribution: The UA-Net Dataset

The introduction of UA-Net, a large-scale, comprehensive multimodal humanoid motion dataset, is a significant contribution to the research community. The scarcity of such high-quality, semantically rich benchmarks has long hampered progress in embodied intelligence. UA-Net provides a standardized platform for evaluating multimodal instruction following, fostering reproducible research and accelerating the development of new algorithms and models in humanoid control. Its meticulous organization, diverse content (text, trajectory, music), and extensive coverage of action verbs set a new standard for dataset quality in this domain.

#### 5.6. Interdisciplinary Impact

The diverse authorship from fields like AI, computer science, and psychological and cognitive sciences at Peking University, coupled with the involvement of BIGAI and state key labs, highlights the interdisciplinary nature of this problem and the power of collaborative research. UniAct demonstrates how insights from different disciplines can converge to address complex challenges in embodied AI, potentially inspiring similar cross-disciplinary efforts.

#### 5.7. Future Trajectory

While acknowledging limitations such as challenges with highly dynamic movements (e.g., rapid jumping) and a current lack of object manipulation capabilities, UniAct clearly lays out a path for future research. Addressing these limitations will further enhance the versatility and utility of humanoid robots. The current work provides a strong foundation upon which more sophisticated controllers and object-centric representations can be built.

In conclusion, UniAct is not merely an incremental improvement but a critical architectural advancement that pushes the boundaries of real-time, robust, and versatile humanoid control. Its comprehensive approach, strong experimental validation, and significant data contribution promise to accelerate the development of intelligent, interactive, and indispensable humanoid robots for the future.
