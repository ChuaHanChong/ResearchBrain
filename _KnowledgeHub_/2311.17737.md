# GenZI: Zero-Shot 3D Human-Scene Interaction Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> GenZI generates 3D humans interacting with arbitrary scenes without any captured 3D interaction data. Given a scene, a text prompt, and a coarse 3D point, it uses latent-diffusion inpainting with cross-attention dynamic masking to imagine 2D humans in multiple rendered views, then robustly lifts the detected 2D poses into a SMPL-X body via view-consistency-weighted optimization with penetration and prior terms, winning over 87% of user preferences.

## Problem

- Existing human-scene interaction synthesis requires supervised training on captured 3D interaction data, which is expensive to record, track, and annotate at scale.
- Available interaction datasets are tiny, covering only about 12 indoor scenes and 11 object categories, so learned models fail on outdoor or unseen object arrangements.
- Prior methods take fixed action-object label pairs plus 3D instance segmentations as control, preventing free-form language control over arbitrary scene geometry.

## Method

- ==Dynamically-masked latent diffusion inpainting== derives the human inpainting mask each denoising step from binarized cross-attention heat maps of human tokens, removing manual mask specification. This drives **GenZI**.
- Robust 3D lifting fits ==SMPL-X== pose and shape to multi-view 2D joints using a Geman-McClure kernel plus optimizable per-view consistency weights that downweight conflicting inpainted hypotheses.
- Total energy adds ==VPoser== body prior, joint angle prior, shape regularization, scene ==SDF== penetration and ==BVH== self-penetration terms; iterative refinement re-inpaints using the rendered body silhouette as mask.

## Results

- On the 8-scene, 38-action Sketchfab set, **30** participants preferred GenZI over every baseline more than **87%** of the time; unary realism rating **3.6** versus below **2.0**.
- GenZI attains the best CLIP semantic score **0.2710**, diversity entropy **2.7304** and non-collision **0.9767** against single-view baselines, with contact score **0.9868** on par.
- Ablations drop CLIP to **0.2639** without dynamic masking, **0.2694** without view consistency, **0.2664** without iterative refinement; on PROX-S it matches trained baselines with best non-collision **0.992**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 2D vision-language generative priors carry enough interaction semantics that 3D tasks can bypass scarce 3D capture entirely, using multi-view imagination plus optimization.
> - Diffusion cross-attention maps are usable as free segmentation signal, letting inpainting self-localize its edit region instead of relying on hand-drawn masks.
> - Stochastic multi-view hypotheses conflict, so treat view selection as an optimizable variable; diversity and contact metrics can reward physically implausible results and mislead evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic 3D human-scene interactions (HSI) is a long-standing and challenging problem in computer vision and graphics. Understanding how humans interact with their environment, often termed "affordances," is crucial for applications ranging from robotics and architectural design to video games and virtual reality.

Historically, approaches to 3D HSI synthesis have predominantly relied on **supervised learning** from meticulously captured 3D interaction data. Prominent methods like PiGraphs, POSA, COINS, and those by Zhang et al. and Hassan et al. have made significant strides in modeling human poses and contacts within 3D scenes. However, these methods face a fundamental limitation: the **scarcity and high cost of collecting large-scale, diverse 3D HSI datasets**. Capturing precise 3D human poses interacting with varied 3D environments requires specialized equipment, controlled conditions, and extensive annotation efforts. Existing datasets, such as PROX, are often limited in the number of scenes (e.g., PROX has only 12 indoor scenes) and types of interactions they cover, severely restricting the generalizability of learning-based models to novel scenes or arbitrary actions. These models often struggle when presented with out-of-distribution scenes or interaction types not seen during training, making them less applicable for flexible content generation.

GenZI enters this landscape by proposing a radical shift: **zero-shot 3D HSI generation**. This means synthesizing 3D human interactions without requiring any explicit training on 3D human-scene interaction data. This approach is made possible by leveraging the recent advancements in **large Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as Stable Diffusion and CLIP. These VLMs have demonstrated an astonishing ability to understand and generate 2D images based on natural language descriptions, having implicitly learned a vast semantic space of objects, actions, and compositions from internet-scale data. GenZI's innovation lies in distilling these rich 2D interaction priors into 3D space, effectively circumventing the bottleneck of 3D data acquisition. By tapping into the immense knowledge embedded in 2D VLMs, GenZI aims to provide a highly flexible and generalizable solution for HSI synthesis, applicable to diverse environments and arbitrary text-based controls, a capability largely absent in previous supervised methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of GenZI is to **synthesize plausible 3D humans interacting with arbitrary 3D scenes without requiring any 3D human-scene interaction training data**. This "zero-shot" capability is the core innovation.

The motivation behind this objective is multifaceted:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The prohibitive cost and difficulty of collecting large, diverse 3D HSI datasets have severely limited the scope and generalization of existing supervised learning approaches. GenZI seeks to bypass this bottleneck entirely by leveraging readily available 2D data and the knowledge implicitly learned by large VLMs.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Flexibility:** By moving beyond fixed, data-driven vocabularies of actions and scene types, GenZI aims to enable the synthesis of diverse interactions in a wide variety of 3D environments, including both indoor and outdoor scenes, and with novel objects or arrangements.
- **Enabling Intuitive Control:** The approach is designed to be controlled easily and flexibly through natural language descriptions, allowing users to specify desired interactions with simple text prompts (e.g., "riding a motorcycle," "sitting on a bench"). This user-friendly interface lowers the barrier for content creation.
- **Broadening Application Potential:** Achieving controllable and generalizable HSI synthesis holds immense potential for numerous applications, including:
 - **Robotics:** For simulating human-robot interaction and training robots to understand human intentions and affordances.
 - **Architectural Design:** To visualize how people might inhabit and interact with designed spaces.
 - **Video Games and Virtual Reality (VR):** For populating virtual worlds with dynamic, realistic characters and enhancing immersion.
 - **Digital Content Creation:** To generate complex scenes for films, animations, and advertisements without manual posing.

In essence, GenZI aims to democratize 3D HSI synthesis by removing the dependency on costly 3D datasets and instead leveraging the rapidly advancing capabilities of 2D vision-language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (GenZI)

GenZI proposes a novel optimization-based, multi-view approach that distills interaction priors from large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to synthesize 3D human-scene interactions in a zero-shot manner.

#### 3.1. Overview

Given a 3D scene (S), a natural language text prompt (Γ) describing the desired interaction, and a coarse 3D point location (p) for the interaction, GenZI outputs a posed 3D human (B). The 3D human is parameterized using the SMPL-X model, allowing for differentiable optimization of its pose (Θ), shape (Φ), global orientation (R), and translation (t). The overall process involves two main stages, followed by iterative refinement:
1. **Inpainting Multi-view Interaction Hypotheses:** Leveraging a VLM to generate plausible 2D human-scene compositions from multiple rendered views of the 3D scene.
2. **Robust Lifting to a 3D Interaction:** Optimizing the 3D human's pose and shape for consistency with the generated 2D hypotheses.

#### 3.2. Inpainting Multi-view Interaction Hypotheses (2D Stage)

- **Multi-view Rendering:** The 3D scene S is first rendered from *k* virtual camera views looking at the specified point *p*. These rendered scene images ({I_i}) capture the scene context from various perspectives, crucial for the subsequent 2D VLM-based inpainting. Cameras are randomly sampled on a hemisphere around *p* and filtered for visibility.
- **Inpainting with Dynamic Masking:** A state-of-the-art latent 2D diffusion model (specifically, Stable Diffusion Inpainting) is used to generate new images (I-bar_i) where a human is inpainted into the rendered scene image I_i, adhering to the text prompt Γ and 2D scene context.
 - A key innovation here is **Dynamic Masking**. Traditional inpainting requires a manually specified binary mask for the region to be inpainted. GenZI automates this by dynamically adapting the mask throughout the denoising process of the diffusion model. This is achieved by leveraging the internal **cross-attention maps** of the VLM. These maps indicate semantic correlations between image pixels and text tokens. By extracting heatmaps corresponding to "human" tokens (e.g., "woman," "man") from the cross-attention map at each diffusion time step, summing them, and binarizing, a context-aware mask is automatically generated and updated. This avoids masking out crucial scene elements that the human should interact with (e.g., a chair for sitting), leading to more coherent 2D human poses.

#### 3.3. Robust Lifting to a 3D Interaction (3D Stage)

Given the multi-view 2D images with inpainted humans ({I-bar_i}), the goal is to optimize the 3D human model B to be consistent with these 2D observations.
- **2D Pose Estimation:** For each inpainted image I-bar_i, an off-the-shelf 2D pose estimator (AlphaPose) infers a set of 2D joint positions (J_i) and their confidence scores (c_i) for the inpainted human.
- **Objective Function (E_total):** The optimization for B's parameters (R, t, Θ, Φ) minimizes a comprehensive objective function:
 `E_total = λ_PF * E_PF + λ_VS * E_VS + λ_BP * E_BP + λ_BS * E_BS + λ_SC * E_SC + λ_SP * E_SP`
 where λ are scalar weights for each term.
 - **Robust View-consistent 3D Pose Fitting (E_PF, E_VS):** This is the primary term, minimizing the discrepancy between the projections of the 3D SMPL-X joints (J-hat) and the detected 2D joints (J_i) from multiple views. Crucially, to handle inconsistencies and noise in the 2D VLM hypotheses across views, GenZI introduces optimizable **view consistency scores (w_i)** for each view. A robust Geman-McClure error function (ρ) is applied to per-view joint fitting constraints. The weights w_i allow the optimization to adaptively focus on views that provide more consistent and reliable 2D pose information, downweighting inconsistent ones. An additional term (E_VS) encourages at least a minimum number of views (τ) to contribute significantly.
 - **Regularizations:**
 - **Body Pose (E_BP):** Includes a VPoser body prior on the latent pose (Θ) and a simple angle prior (E_JA) on decoded joint rotations (Theta-hat) to prevent extreme or unnatural bending.
 - **Body Shape (E_BS):** Regularizes the shape parameters (Φ) to ensure a plausible body shape, using a Mahalanobis distance to the SMPL-X shape distribution.
 - **Scene Penetration (E_SC):** Utilizes a pre-computed signed distance field (SDF) of the scene (S) to penalize interpenetration between the 3D human mesh and the scene geometry. It encourages physical contact while avoiding deep penetration.
 - **Self-Penetration (E_SP):** Prevents the human body mesh from interpenetrating itself, based on bounding volume hierarchies.

#### 3.4. Iterative Refinement

After the initial 2D inpainting and 3D lifting, an iterative refinement scheme is applied. In this stage, instead of dynamic masking, the silhouette of the currently optimized 3D human (B) is rendered for each camera view and used as a more precise and consistent mask (M) for the latent diffusion inpainting. This process iteratively improves the consistency of the 2D pose hypotheses across views, leading to a more accurate and realistic 3D HSI synthesis.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

GenZI is implemented in PyTorch. It uses 16 cameras for multi-view rendering and 50 denoising steps for Stable Diffusion Inpainting. Dynamic masking aggregates 16x16 cross-attention maps. Text prompts are augmented with "a woman" and "wearing a white shirt and blue pants, full body" to guide VLM generation. The robust 3D lifting optimizes E_total for 1.6K steps (approx. 3 minutes on an NVIDIA A100 GPU) using gradient descent, with τ=3 for view consistency. Iterative refinement is performed once.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GenZI was evaluated extensively through quantitative metrics and qualitative comparisons, including user studies, on diverse 3D scenes.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **Sketchfab Dataset:** A custom dataset comprising 8 large-scale 3D scenes (indoor and outdoor, e.g., Venice city, gym, food truck) with varying geometric complexities. 38 actions with approximate point locations were defined across these scenes.
 - **PROX-S Dataset (Supplementary):** Used for further comparison with existing indoor scene interaction synthesis methods. This dataset has 3D instance segmentations and interaction annotations (action-object pairs). GenZI adapted by mapping labels to natural language.
- **Baselines:** Since no direct zero-shot, text-guided 3D HSI baseline exists, GenZI compared against adapted related approaches:
 - **COINS:** A state-of-the-art supervised method for compositional human-scene interaction, trained on a closed vocabulary of indoor actions/objects. Adapted by manually segmenting objects and mapping text prompts.
 - **Hassan et al.:** 3D human estimation from a single RGB image. Adapted by taking the single best-view inpainted image (based on CLIP score) as input.
 - **Ours-Single View (Ours-SV):** An ablation of GenZI's 3D lifting stage, constrained to use only a single best-view inpainted image as input, similar to Hassan et al.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Metrics

- **Perceptual Studies:** Two user studies involving 30 participants assessed:
 - **Binary-Choice Study:** Participants chose which of two generated samples (from different methods) was more realistic and semantically matched the text.
 - **Unary Test:** Participants rated realism and text consistency on a 1-5 scale for individual samples.
- **Semantic Consistency:** Measured by the **CLIP score**, which quantifies the image-text cosine similarity. The 3D interaction is re-rendered into *k* views, and average CLIP scores are computed.
- **Diversity:** Measured by clustering SMPL-X parameters (K-means, 20 clusters) and computing the entropy of cluster ID histograms and mean distance to cluster centers.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Assessed by **non-collision score** (ratio of body vertices with positive SDF values, indicating no penetration) and **contact score** (ratio of body meshes with scene contact, indicating interaction).

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results

- **Perceptual Studies (Fig. 4):** GenZI was overwhelmingly preferred (over 87% of the time in binary choice) and received the highest average realism rating (3.6 vs. <2.0 for baselines) in the unary test. This strongly indicates GenZI's ability to synthesize realistic and semantically accurate interactions.
- **Semantic Consistency (Table 1):** GenZI achieved the best average CLIP score (0.2710), confirming its superior semantic alignment with text prompts compared to baselines (Hassan et al. 0.2598, Ours-SV 0.2613).
- **Diversity & Physical Plausibility (Table 1):** GenZI showed the best diversity entropy and non-collision scores, with competitive contact scores. The paper notes that diversity/collision metrics might not fully reflect perceptual quality, as diverse but implausible results (common in single-view baselines) can inflate these numbers.
- **PROX-S Evaluation (Table 3, Supplementary):** GenZI achieved competitive performance on PROX-S despite being zero-shot, outperforming PiGraph-X and POSA-I, and performing comparably to COINS, which was specifically trained on PROX-S with explicit 3D supervision.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Fig. 5, Fig. 9)

Visual examples clearly demonstrate GenZI's superiority:
- **Generalization:** GenZI successfully synthesizes interactions in highly diverse outdoor and novel indoor scenes (e.g., "climbing a ladder," "riding a recumbent bike") where COINS, trained on a closed set, fails to generalize, producing implausible poses or ignoring scene context.
- **Realism:** GenZI's generations are visually more realistic and contextually coherent. Baselines often result in floating humans, incorrect contact, or poses that are semantically inconsistent with the scene.
- **Multi-View Advantage:** Comparisons with single-view baselines (Hassan et al., Ours-SV) highlight the crucial role of multi-view integration in achieving geometrically plausible 3D poses that respect scene constraints.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Fig. 6, Table 2)

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of GenZI's key components:
- **Dynamic Masking (w/o DM):** Without dynamic masking (using random masks instead), the interaction quality degrades, leading to incoherence (e.g., person floating above a stool), validating its role in providing scene-aware masks.
- **View Consistency (w/o VC):** Fixing view weights (w=1) without robust view selection leads to averaged, less expressive, or inconsistent 3D poses due to conflicting 2D hypotheses. This confirms the necessity of adaptive view weighting.
- **Iterative Refinement (w/o IR):** Performing only one iteration results in lower quality synthesis, often with human-scene penetration. Iterative refinement significantly improves consistency and plausibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GenZI represents a significant leap forward in 3D human-scene interaction generation due to its pioneering zero-shot capability. Its core significance lies in:

- **Circumventing the 3D Data Bottleneck:** By demonstrating that plausible 3D HSI can be synthesized without requiring any explicitly captured or learned 3D interaction data, GenZI fundamentally addresses the most significant barrier in this research area. This shift enables applications that were previously impractical due to data acquisition costs.
- **Effective Knowledge Distillation from 2D VLMs:** The work showcases a highly effective methodology for leveraging the vast, implicitly learned semantic knowledge within large 2D vision-language models for complex 3D synthesis tasks. This paradigm of "2D-to-3D knowledge transfer" is a powerful direction for future research in 3D content generation, especially as VLMs continue to grow in capability.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Flexibility:** Unlike previous methods limited by their training datasets, GenZI's reliance on VLMs allows it to generalize to diverse 3D scenes (both indoor and outdoor) and a wide array of human actions described by natural language, providing unprecedented flexibility in content creation.
- **Intuitive Language-based Control:** The ability to generate complex 3D interactions from simple text prompts makes GenZI highly accessible and intuitive for users, opening up new possibilities for designers, artists, and creators who may not have specialized 3D modeling skills.

**Potential Impact:**
GenZI has profound implications for several fields:
- **Robotics:** For simulating realistic human environments and training robots to understand and anticipate human actions and affordances. This could lead to more robust and human-centric robot designs.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Gaming:** It can enable the rapid population of virtual worlds with dynamic, interactive characters, reducing development time and increasing immersion.
- **Architectural and Product Design:** Designers can easily visualize and test how people will interact with proposed spaces or products, leading to more human-centered designs.
- **Digital Content Creation:** For animation, film, and advertising, GenZI offers a powerful tool to generate complex human interactions within 3D scenes, streamlining production workflows.
- **Fundamental AI Research:** It contributes to the broader goal of grounding AI models in 3D physical reality and understanding the intricate relationships between humans and their environments, pushing the boundaries of generative AI beyond 2D outputs.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations, primarily that its performance is inherently tied to the capabilities of the underlying latent diffusion models used for 2D inpainting. As these VLMs improve rapidly, GenZI is poised to directly benefit. Another practical limitation is the inference time of diffusion models, which can be slow due to their iterative nature. However, ongoing research in faster diffusion sampling techniques could mitigate this.

In conclusion, GenZI represents a significant step towards general, controllable 3D human-scene interaction synthesis, paving the way for more intuitive and data-efficient 3D content creation pipelines by ingeniously bridging the gap between vast 2D knowledge and complex 3D tasks.
