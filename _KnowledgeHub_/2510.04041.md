# SITCOM: Scaling Inference-Time COMpute for VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> The SITCOM framework from Carnegie Mellon University enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating an inference-time model-based planning mechanism. This system leverages a learned transformer-based dynamics model for multi-step action rollouts, achieving an average success rate of 72% on various simulated robotic manipulation tasks and substantially outperforming reactive VLA baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily operate in a "one-shot" manner, predicting only the immediate next action without explicit lookahead or planning capabilities.
- This lack of foresight leads to compounding errors in dynamic environments and limits the ability of VLAs to execute complex, multi-step tasks requiring long-horizon planning.
- Traditional robot control policies struggle with high data collection costs and poor generalization to novel environments.

## Method

- **SITCOM** introduces an inference-time planning framework that augments pre-trained VLAs with model-based rollouts and reward-ranking capabilities, inspired by ==Model Predictive Control==.
- An efficient transformer-based dynamics model is developed to simulate multi-step action sequences, pre-trained on BridgeV2 and fine-tuned on target environments (SIMPLER) with a DAgger-inspired adaptation strategy to mitigate error accumulation.
- The framework samples candidate actions, simulates their future trajectories using the dynamics model, evaluates trajectories with a reward model, and executes only the first action of the highest-scoring plan in a receding horizon loop.

## Results

- SITCOM (World Model) achieved an average success rate of **72%** in SIMPLER manipulation tasks, a significant improvement over the fine-tuned OpenVLA-SFT (**48%**) and the baseline OpenVLA (**1%**).
- Fine-tuning the dynamics model on in-domain SIMPLER trajectories reduced its Frechet Inception Distance (FID) from **17.0** to **11.2** and Optical Flow Loss (OFL) from **1.665** to **0.992**, indicating improved state prediction.
- The DAgger-inspired training strategy for the dynamics model effectively improved reconstruction quality and reduced prediction drift over longer horizons compared to single-timestep prediction training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating model-based planning significantly transforms reactive, single-step VLAs into proactive, long-horizon planners, crucial for complex robotic tasks.
> - Domain adaptation through fine-tuning is essential for both the core VLA policy and the learned dynamics model to effectively bridge the "Real2Sim" gap and ensure practical performance.
> - Scaling inference-time compute by increasing the breadth (number of candidate rollouts) and depth (length of rollouts) can yield continuous performance gains for challenging tasks, despite potential computational increases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is undergoing a significant transformation, driven by advances in deep learning and large-scale datasets. Traditionally, robot control policies have been constrained by the high cost of collecting labeled data, limited generalization to novel environments, and difficulties in planning over long horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a solution but suffers from poor sample efficiency and potential safety concerns in real-world interactions. World models, which learn predictive environment representations, have emerged to address these issues but often struggle with the diversity and complexity of real-world scenarios.

More recently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have gained prominence by enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and translate them into single-step control commands. Pioneering works like OpenVLA, RT-1-X, and Octo have demonstrated remarkable success in grounding language and achieving generalization across diverse tasks and objects. However, these VLA models primarily operate in a "one-shot" manner, predicting the immediate next action without explicit lookahead or planning capabilities. This limitation makes them vulnerable to compounding errors in dynamic environments and inhibits their ability to execute complex, multi-step tasks requiring long-horizon planning, such as sequential object manipulation or navigation in cluttered spaces.

SITCOM directly addresses this critical gap in the VLA landscape by integrating model-based planning—inspired by Model Predictive Control (MPC)—into existing VLA frameworks. MPC is a well-established control strategy that involves iteratively predicting future system behavior using a dynamics model, optimizing control inputs over a receding horizon, and executing only the first action of the optimal plan. SITCOM adapts this principle to VLAs, transforming them from reactive, single-step controllers into proactive, long-horizon planners.

The work builds upon the foundation of large-scale datasets like BridgeV2, which provide diverse manipulation data for pretraining. It positions itself as complementary to other VLM/VLA advancements, such as those leveraging Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision for explicit task decomposition. Instead of explicit reasoning, SITCOM uses implicit future evaluation through simulated rollouts and reward-driven selection. This approach contrasts with computationally intensive generative video models that rely on diffusion backbones, opting instead for an efficient transformer-based dynamics model. Furthermore, while reward modeling using large pre-trained models (e.g., "LLM-as-a-Judge") is an active area, SITCOM primarily uses simulator-based reward signals for interpretable evaluation during planning. By enhancing VLAs with an efficient, scalable inference-time planning mechanism, SITCOM contributes to the broader goal of developing more robust, generalist, and autonomous robotic systems capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SITCOM stems from the inherent limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in complex robotic tasks. While VLAs excel at interpreting natural language instructions and translating them into single-step control commands, they typically operate without a sense of future consequences. This "one-shot" decision-making capability leads to several critical challenges:
1. **Lack of Lookahead:** VLAs cannot anticipate the effects of their actions, making it difficult to plan for multi-step goals or navigate dynamic environments where sequential decisions are crucial.
2. **Compounding Errors:** Small inaccuracies in single-step predictions can accumulate over time, leading to significant deviations from the desired trajectory and eventual task failure. The models struggle to recover from such errors.
3. **Limited Long-Horizon Planning:** Complex robotic tasks, such as assembly or precise manipulation, often require a sequence of coordinated actions over an extended period. Current VLAs are ill-equipped to handle such long-horizon planning effectively.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the SITCOM project are:

- **To propose the SITCOM framework:** A general-purpose, inference-time planning framework designed to augment any pre-trained VLA with model-based rollout and reward-ranking capabilities. This framework aims to transform reactive VLAs into robust, long-horizon planners.
- **To develop an efficient transformer-based dynamics model:** This model is crucial for simulating multi-step action rollouts. It is pre-trained on large-scale datasets (BridgeV2) and then fine-tuned on target environments (SIMPLER) to bridge the "Real2Sim" gap. A specific objective is to incorporate a DAgger-inspired adaptation strategy to mitigate error accumulation and distributional shift during long-horizon predictions.
- **To provide insights into performance gains:** The authors aim to analyze how varying the number of candidate action sequences (breadth) and the length of future predictions (depth) impacts the overall performance and robustness of the VLA.
- **To validate the approach comprehensively:** Through extensive evaluation across multiple tasks and settings within the SIMPLER environment, demonstrating that SITCOM consistently outperforms strong baselines and enables more reliable long-horizon planning for robotic manipulation.

In essence, SITCOM seeks to endow VLAs with a proactive, predictive capacity, enabling them to move beyond instantaneous command execution towards intelligent, foresight-driven control for real-world robotic challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SITCOM's methodology is centered on an inference-time planning algorithm that integrates a pre-trained VLA with a learned dynamics model and a reward function, inspired by Model Predictive Control (MPC).

**Core SITCOM Framework (Algorithm 1):**
At each decision step, the robot receives an initial observation (image `I_0`) and a task instruction `T`.
1. **Action Sampling:** The VLA model, `π_VLA(I_0, T)`, proposes `n` candidate actions. To encourage exploration and diverse plans, these actions are sampled with a high temperature.
2. **Trajectory Rollouts:** For each of the `n` candidate actions, a multi-step trajectory rollout of length `l` is simulated. Two distinct methods are explored for these rollouts:
 - **SITCOM (EnvSim):** This variant uses an oracle simulator (another instance of the real environment) to execute the candidate actions and predict subsequent states. This serves as an upper bound on performance, assuming perfect dynamics.
 - **SITCOM (Dynamics):** This variant uses a *learned dynamics model*, `f_dyn(I, a)`, to predict the next image `I'` given the current image `I` and action `a`. Within each rollout, subsequent actions are greedily selected by the VLA (`arg max_a π_VLA(I_i_t, T)`), based on the predicted states from the dynamics model.
3. **Reward-Based Trajectory Selection:** After `l` steps, a reward model `r(I_0, I_l, T)` evaluates the final state `I_l` of each of the `n` simulated trajectories. The reward function is designed to be task-specific but generalizable, incorporating metrics like gripper-object gap, object-destination distance, and grasp success indicators.
4. **Real-World Execution:** The trajectory with the highest reward score is selected, and only its first action is executed in the real environment.
5. **Replanning:** This entire process is repeated at the environment’s replanning frequency, effectively performing a receding horizon control loop until the task is successfully completed or terminated.

**Dynamics Model Training:**
The learned dynamics model `f_dyn(.)` is an encoder-decoder transformer architecture. The encoder processes image patches and concatenates them with action information, while the decoder predicts patches for the next state.
- **Loss Function:** Training uses a combination of L1 pixel-wise loss (for low-level accuracy) and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) loss (for perceptual realism).
- **Training Data & Strategy:** The model is pre-trained on approximately 25,000 trajectories from the large-scale BridgeV2 dataset to enable generalizable dynamics learning. To mitigate the "Real2Sim" gap and adapt to the specific visual and physical characteristics of the evaluation environment, the model is then fine-tuned on in-domain SIMPLER trajectories. Crucially, the model is trained to predict future states from its *own predictions* for `l_train` steps, a DAgger-inspired adaptation strategy that helps reduce distributional shift and error accumulation during long-horizon autoregressive rollouts at inference time.

**VLA Finetuning:**
The chosen baseline VLA, OpenVLA, is fine-tuned to adapt it to the SIMPLER environment. Since expert trajectory data for SIMPLER was unavailable, the authors curated a dataset of 100 expert trajectories. This curation involved:
1. Running a pre-trained model to generate initial trajectories.
2. Applying heuristic rules to identify successful executions.
3. Human filtering to ensure high-quality expert demonstrations.
The VLA is then trained using a standard cross-entropy loss over discretized action tokens on this curated dataset.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Environment:** SIMPLER, a suite of open-source simulated environments for generalist robot manipulation, featuring a 7-DOF WidowX robotic arm.
- **Tasks:** Four distinct manipulation tasks (e.g., "Put Carrot on Plate," "Stack Green Block on Yellow Block").
- **Metrics:** Average Success Rate (primary), Partial Success Rate, and Time (as a proxy for computational cost).
- **Baselines:** OpenVLA, RT-1-X, and Octo (leading pre-trained VLA architectures), alongside OpenVLA-SFT (OpenVLA fine-tuned on SIMPLER data).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation of SITCOM across multiple tasks and settings in the SIMPLER environment yielded several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Comparison (Table 2)

- SITCOM substantially improved task completion rates compared to baseline VLAs. The baseline OpenVLA achieved a mere 1% average success rate, highlighting a significant "Real2Sim" gap when directly transferring real-world pre-trained models to simulation.
- Fine-tuning OpenVLA on SIMPLER trajectories (OpenVLA-SFT) significantly boosted performance to an average success rate of 48%. This demonstrates the critical importance of domain adaptation for both VLAs and dynamics models.
- SITCOM (EnvSim), which uses an oracle simulator for rollouts, achieved the highest average success rate of 76%. This variant showcases the upper bound of SITCOM's potential, demonstrating the benefit of planning when perfect dynamics are available.
- SITCOM (World Model), employing the learned dynamics model for rollouts, achieved a comparable average success rate of 72%. This result is particularly crucial as it indicates that a learned model can effectively drive the planning process, bridging the gap between an idealized oracle and practical deployment. Both SITCOM variants dramatically outperformed the baselines, validating the effectiveness of the proposed simulation-guided planning approach.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Rollout Parameters

- **Breadth (Number of Rollout Candidates):** Scaling the number of candidate rollouts was found to be beneficial (Figure 4). Performance gains were continuous for some tasks, with saturation observed around 25 candidates for others. While increasing candidates linearly increases planning time (Table 4), the pipeline is inherently parallelizable, suggesting that computational overhead can be managed with sufficient hardware resources.
- **Depth (Length of Rollouts):** Varying rollout lengths demonstrated task-dependent benefits (Figure 5). For challenging tasks like "Put Eggplant in Basket," longer rollouts led to improved performance, indicating that more in-depth planning is advantageous for complex sequential actions. However, the performance of the *dynamics model itself* was observed to decrease with increased rollout length, which can sometimes lead to a drop in overall SITCOM performance for certain tasks due to error accumulation.

#### 4.3. Dynamics Model Evaluation (Table 3)

- The transformer-based dynamics model's effectiveness was quantified using Frechet Inception Distance (FID) and Optical Flow Loss (OFL) metrics.
- The base model, pre-trained solely on BridgeV2, showed a respectable FID of 17.0 and OFL of 1.665, demonstrating its generalizability to unseen environments.
- Fine-tuning the dynamics model on in-domain SIMPLER trajectories significantly improved its performance, reducing FID to 11.2 and OFL to 0.992. These improvements indicate that fine-tuning enhances the model's ability to generate realistic, temporally coherent future states and better capture task-relevant dynamics, effectively mitigating the Real2Sim gap for the world model.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Discussion and Failure Modes

- **VLA Policy Improvement:** Fine-tuning OpenVLA on SIMPLER trajectories reduced the Real2Sim gap, boosting success rates significantly (e.g., from 0% to 50% for "Put Carrot on Plate"). However, even the fine-tuned VLA still struggled with fine-grained control and generalization to out-of-distribution scenes.
- **Dynamics Model Limitations:** Initial training of the world model (predicting single timesteps) led to compounding object reconstruction errors during extended rollouts (Figure 7, middle row). A DAgger-style adaptation strategy, where the model used its own predictions during training, largely improved reconstruction quality over longer horizons (Figure 7, bottom row). Nevertheless, prediction drift and occasional object reconstruction failures persisted at test time (Figure 8), particularly when policy-generated trajectories diverged from the training distribution. This was attributed to training the dynamics model predominantly on successful trajectories, suggesting a need to incorporate failure states for greater robustness.

In summary, SITCOM successfully enhances VLAs by introducing model-based planning, demonstrating significant performance gains. The research highlights the critical role of both VLA and dynamics model fine-tuning to address the Real2Sim gap and underscores the benefits of scaling inference-time compute. It also pinpoints areas for future improvement in dynamics model robustness for long-horizon, out-of-distribution predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SITCOM framework represents a significant step forward in making Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models more practical and robust for real-world robotic applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhancing VLA Capabilities:** SITCOM directly addresses the fundamental limitations of existing VLAs, which are primarily reactive and lack lookahead. By integrating Model Predictive Control (MPC)-inspired planning, SITCOM transforms one-shot VLAs into proactive, long-horizon planners. This is a critical advancement for enabling robots to execute complex, multi-step tasks that require sequential reasoning and recovery from errors.
- **Generalizable Framework:** The framework is designed to augment *any* pre-trained VLA, offering a versatile solution that can be applied to diverse robotic platforms and tasks without requiring extensive re-training of the core VLA policy itself. This modularity reduces development effort and promotes wider adoption.
- **Bridging the Real2Sim Gap:** The paper underscores the importance of domain adaptation for both VLAs and learned dynamics models. The fine-tuning of the base VLA on in-domain trajectories and the dynamics model on SIMPLER data significantly improved performance, demonstrating a practical strategy to bridge the discrepancies between real-world pre-training data and simulated deployment environments.
- **Efficient Dynamics Modeling:** SITCOM contributes an efficient, transformer-based dynamics model that predicts future frames in pixel space. Unlike computationally expensive generative video models, this model is lightweight enough for multi-step rollouts, making real-time inference-time planning feasible. The DAgger-inspired adaptation strategy for the dynamics model further enhances its ability to perform robust long-horizon predictions.
- **Systematic Evaluation of Planning Parameters:** The research provides valuable insights into how planning parameters (number of rollout candidates and rollout length) affect performance, guiding future research in optimizing inference-time computation for different task complexities.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **More Autonomous and Capable Robots:** By enabling long-horizon planning, SITCOM paves the way for robots to tackle more sophisticated and autonomous tasks in unstructured environments. This includes complex manipulation, assembly, navigation in dynamic settings, and even domestic assistance, where robots need to understand instructions and plan multiple steps ahead.
- **Reduced Data Dependency and Improved Generalization:** While SITCOM still requires some in-domain data for fine-tuning, its reliance on learned dynamics and simulated rollouts can potentially reduce the need for extensive, task-specific real-world data collection for every new complex task. The ability to plan makes policies more robust to variations and capable of adapting to unseen situations within the simulated horizon.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Error Recovery:** The planning mechanism allows the robot to "think ahead," evaluate multiple possible action sequences, and select the one leading to the most favorable outcome. This inherent lookahead capability significantly improves the robot's ability to recover from unexpected events or minor execution errors, leading to more reliable task completion.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as the dynamics model's bias towards successful trajectories and challenges in real-world deployment, provide clear directions for future research. This includes exploring probabilistic dynamics models (e.g., action-conditioned video diffusion models), incorporating failure states into training, and developing robust sim2real transfer techniques.
- **Scalable Inference-Time Computing:** The emphasis on parallelizable rollouts highlights a path towards scaling up the computational aspect of planning, making it practical for real-time control frequencies in diverse robotic applications.

In conclusion, SITCOM offers a compelling solution to augment the decision-making capabilities of VLAs, moving them closer to truly intelligent and autonomous agents. Its impact will likely be felt across robotic manipulation, enhancing both the research landscape and the practical utility of robots in real-world scenarios.
