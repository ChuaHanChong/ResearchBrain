# LIBERO-MAX: Do Robot Policies Adapt When the World Changes?

> [!summary] Summary
> LIBERO-MAX is a manipulation benchmark of 8,000 paired rollouts testing whether robot policies cope with one persistent mid-task change: target or receptacle relocation, camera shift, sensor noise, lighting, visual theme, distractors, or obstacles. Each Dynamic rollout replays the matching Base rollout's exact action prefix before the event, so the event's effect is isolated. All fourteen VLA, hybrid, and world-action policies lose 11.0-25.7 success points.

## Problem

- Simulation robustness benchmarks such as LIBERO-Plus and LIBERO-PRO fix perturbations at reset, so they never test whether policies cope when the world changes after acting begins.
- Comparing independently sampled rollouts with and without a change confounds the event's effect with reset difficulty, policy randomness, and earlier actions that diverge before the change.
- Dynamic-manipulation work such as DynamicVLA and DOMINO studies moving objects and reactive control, but lacks a controlled, matched measure across diverse event types and policy families.

## Method

- ==Paired Base/Dynamic protocol==: Dynamic restores the initial state, replays Base's exact ==executed action prefix==, then applies one ==persistent event==, scoring four ==paired success/failure outcome transitions==.
- Eight event types across geometry, observation, appearance-and-clutter, and path families, each fired by a deterministic ==pre_grasp_proximity trigger== when the end effector first nears the designated entity.
- ==Temporal diagnostics==: trigger and ==post-event query coverage==, ==stale-action exposure== counting queued chunk actions, reset-versus-mid-task camera controls, ==query-cadence sweeps==, plus an 800-pair **LIBERO-MAX** Lite subset.

## Results

- Every policy drops **11.0-25.7** points: MolmoAct2 falls from **80.3%** to **66.9%** and π0.5 from **79.7%** to **65.7%**, while events break **20.8%-56.1%** of Base successes.
- Target relocation is the most damaging event for **11 of 14** policies; geometry losses span **21.2-52.4** points, while illumination and visual-theme changes cost little.
- Under camera shift X-VLA scores **1.3%** from reset but **12.4%** mid-task; across **18** query-cadence settings Dynamic success stays **11.1-23.0** points below Base.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robustness to conditions fixed at reset does not imply robustness to changes during execution; inherited state and committed actions shape whether a policy recovers.
> - Querying more often does not fix mid-task failures: policies receive post-event observations in most cases yet still fail, which points to missing re-planning competence.
> - World-action models show no consistent robustness advantage over VLAs, and aggregate gaps can hide regressions, so paired per-case outcomes should accompany success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Picture a robot arm halfway through a reach when the object it is reaching for moves. The actions it has already taken were chosen for the old scene, so its next actions may no longer fit. Researchers tested this by replaying the same approach twice, once without the change and once with it. Across fourteen tested policies, success is lower with the mid-task event.

Target relocation: the manipulated object moves after approach.

### 2. Same past, different continuation

Benchmarks such as LIBERO-Plus test robustness to changed conditions. They typically set those conditions before the first action and keep them fixed. LIBERO-MAX introduces one change after execution begins. It then compares the result with a no-change rollout that has followed the exact same actions up to that point.

Each comparison is a pair of rollouts:

- **Base** continues without an added event.
- **Dynamic** replays Base's exact actions up to the event, then continues with the change.

The shared action sequence is the **executed prefix**. Each pair also holds the task, initial state, instruction, and policy seed fixed. Every case counts in the success-rate denominator. That includes cases that never reach the trigger or never receive a later policy query.

The main track uses 8,000 pairs. One event is introduced at a deterministic pre-grasp proximity trigger and persists through the rollout.

### 3. Every policy loses ground

Task success rate is the fraction of assigned episodes completed. On LIBERO-MAX, MolmoAct2's success rate was **80.3% in Base and 66.9% in Dynamic**, a paired Dynamic-minus-Base change of **−13.4 percentage points**. MolmoAct2 had the highest Dynamic success, but it still lost **20.8% of its Base successes**. That conditional regression is a different measure from the overall gap.

The study evaluates fourteen pretrained policies on the same 8,000 pairs. Every policy's overall success rate falls, by **11.0–25.7 percentage points**. These policies also differ in training, scale, and serving protocols, so the comparison does not isolate an architecture effect.

The matched 8,000-pair evaluation, using each policy's released serving settings. The paired comparison holds the task, initial state, seed, and pre-event actions fixed. Open circles are Base and filled squares are Dynamic, joined by arrows. Every arrow points to lower Dynamic success. For MolmoAct2: 80.3% Base, 66.9% Dynamic, −13.4 points.

### 4. Viewpoint is not the whole story

A skeptic might ask whether camera-shift failures simply reflect a hard changed view. The alternative is that they also depend on the history from which the robot encounters that view. A control on the same 1,000 camera cases compared three conditions.

For X-VLA, success was **68.2% in Base**. It was **1.3% when the changed view was present from reset** and **12.4% when that view was introduced mid-task**. Mid-task exceeded Reset by **11.1 points** (95% paired interval **[9.2, 13.1]**). The other two tested policies also had higher Mid-task than Reset success.

This is evidence that outcomes depend on both changed-view competence and execution history. It does not identify history as the only cause. Reset and Mid-task differ in visited states, exposure time to the changed view, and remaining task budget. The control covers only three policies.

### 5. A test for change

LIBERO-MAX's contribution is a paired test. It compares what happens when the same executed past leads into two different continuations. This helps locate failures that arise after action begins.

Its boundary is clear. It is a simulated benchmark of manipulation using LIBERO's abstractions. Its main track tests one persistent event. It does not establish transfer to physical robots, repeated or reversible changes, or safe deployment. Endpoint success and post-event queries also do not establish internal event detection or deliberate replanning.
