# Instant-Fold: In-Context Imitation Learning for Deformable Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Instant-Fold introduces an in-context imitation learning framework for complex deformable object manipulation, enabling robots to learn and execute diverse garment folding tasks from a single human demonstration. The system achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with 60.9% real-world success on unseen garments by employing deformation-aware temporal contrastive pretraining and a flow-matching transformer architecture.

## Problem

- Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM) is challenging due to high-dimensional, partially observable states and long-horizon, topology-changing interactions.
- Existing robotic methods struggle to infer and execute multiple valid manipulation modes (e.g., spatial execution, temporal ordering) from compact instructions or limited demonstrations.
- Achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for DOM is difficult because of representation challenges for non-rigid objects and the scarcity of diverse real-world data for in-context learning.

## Method

- The **Instant-Fold** framework formulates DOM as an in-context ==imitation learning== problem, utilizing a flow-matching ==transformer== architecture to generate long-horizon dual-arm action trajectories.
- A self-supervised temporal contrastive pretraining method is introduced to learn robust, deformation-aware visual representations from 3D point clouds, leveraging dense simulator particle geometry and sparse semantic keypoints.
- The policy conditions on automatically extracted keyframes from a single human demonstration, using a context encoder with 3D relative attention, learned summary tokens, and state-event tokens to distill demonstration intent.

## Results

- Instant-Fold achieved superior performance in simulation, outperforming primitive-based baselines with **95.8%** context accuracy and **58.3%** conditional success rate on held-out folding modes.
- The framework successfully demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real transfer to a real dual-arm robot, achieving an average success rate of **60.9%** on eight unseen real garments, surpassing baselines that required real-world data or fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the temporal contrastive pretraining, context encoder, 3D ALiBI, and keypose auxiliary loss each contributed significantly to the policy's performance and generalization capabilities, particularly on held-out tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Single human demonstrations provide a richer and more precise interface for specifying complex deformable manipulation tasks, enabling better context-following and final geometry quality compared to language conditioning.
> - A dedicated temporal contrastive pretraining approach for deformable objects is crucial for learning topology-consistent visual representations that generalize effectively across diverse deformations and objects.
> - Structured context encoding within the policy, including 3D relative attention and an auxiliary keypose prediction task, is vital for effectively aggregating demonstration information and guiding long-horizon action generation in deformable manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM) represents a significant challenge in robotics due to the complex nature of deformable objects. These objects possess high-dimensional, partially observable states that undergo long-horizon, topology-changing interactions. Furthermore, their manipulation often allows for multiple valid modes depending on user preference, which can vary in spatial execution and temporal ordering.

Previous research in DOM has explored various approaches to representation, sim-to-real transfer, and imitation learning. Representations for deformable objects typically fall into categories such as particle-based, mesh-based, keypoint-based, or learned latent states. While keypoint-based methods are compact and computationally efficient for planning and control, they can struggle with simultaneously preserving spatial fidelity and task-relevant structure, and may lose fine-grained details or be sensitive to occlusions. The presented work addresses this by proposing a representation of deformable objects as geo-semantic tokens that combine 3D position with deformation-aware semantic features.

Sim-to-real clothes folding often relies on parameterized action primitives, where policies predict grasp points or affordances from visual observations. These methods offer stability and data efficiency but often involve sequential, open-loop executions with limited adaptability. Other approaches employ flow-based or model-predictive control, providing flexibility and closed-loop operation but often requiring high-dimensional observations or task-specific geometric representations. The current work aims to achieve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with a closed-loop, continuous action policy.

In-Context Imitation Learning (ICIL) has emerged as a paradigm for task adaptation using a few demonstrations without gradient-based fine-tuning. Existing ICIL methods often involve explicit alignment, meta-learning, or foundation-model-based approaches. However, these methods primarily focus on rigid-body settings, where object states can be approximated by low-dimensional pose representations. Deformable manipulation, conversely, necessitates reasoning over continuously evolving, high-dimensional observations and manipulation procedures. This paper extends ICIL to the domain of deformable object manipulation by introducing a novel architecture designed for long-horizon dual-arm tasks. It addresses the representation challenge by learning deformation-aware features and the data challenge by training entirely in simulation with a diverse dataset.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform complex deformable object manipulation (DOM) tasks by learning from a single human demonstration in context. The authors introduce "Instant-Fold," an in-context imitation learning (ICIL) framework designed to infer and execute diverse manipulation modes, including variations in spatial execution and ordering, directly from a single demonstration without requiring gradient updates.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in DOM:
- **High-Dimensional and Partially Observable States:** Deformable objects like cloth have a vast number of degrees of freedom, making their state estimation and modeling difficult.
- **Long-Horizon and Topology-Changing Interactions:** Manipulation sequences for deformable objects are often extensive and involve significant changes in the object's topology (e.g., folding, crumpling), which complicates task planning and execution.
- **Multiple Valid Manipulation Modes:** A single goal state (e.g., a folded shirt) can often be achieved through various intermediate manipulation sequences, reflecting different preferences or styles. Specifying these precisely and compactly through language is difficult.
- **Limitations of Language-based Instruction:** Humans often use demonstrations rather than purely verbal instructions for complex deformable skills, as demonstrations effectively convey temporal structure, intermediate goals, and subtle spatial conventions. This observation underpins the central premise that a single demonstration can provide a richer and more precise task interface for specifying *how* an object should be manipulated.
- **Representation Challenges for Generalization:** Existing vision models, typically trained on static internet images, struggle with the dramatic changes in visual appearance and feature representation that occur as a deformable object undergoes non-rigid transformations. This variability can lead a model to misinterpret different manipulation trajectories as entirely distinct tasks, hindering generalization.
- **Data Scarcity for In-Context Learning:** The success of ICIL in other domains relies on massive, diverse datasets, which are not readily available for DOM. Generalizing to unseen deformation trajectories requires exposure to broad variations in object geometry, intermediate states, and manipulation modes, an endeavor that is prohibitively expensive and difficult to scale in the real world.

To address these motivations and challenges, the paper presents three specific contributions:
1. Formulating DOM as an ICIL problem and introducing a flow-matching transformer architecture for long-horizon deformable in-context manipulation.
2. Proposing a temporal contrastive pretraining method specifically tailored for ICIL to learn topology-consistent representations for deformable objects.
3. Demonstrating successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without requiring any real-world data collection or fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Instant-Fold framework comprises two primary stages: deformation-aware temporal contrastive pretraining and in-context policy learning. The system is designed to learn robust visual representations and then condition action generation on a single demonstration.

#### 3.1. Deformable Representation

The method adopts a geometry-first approach for deformable observations. From a masked RGB-D observation, valid depth pixels are back-projected into a 3D point cloud. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is then applied to select a fixed number (N=64) of points across the garment surface. Visual features are extracted from these sampled 3D points by projecting them back to the image plane and interpolating features from a pretrained image encoder. Each "cloth token" thus comprises a 3D position and its associated semantic feature.

#### 3.2. Temporal Contrastive Pretraining

This stage aims to learn a visual encoder that is sensitive to topological changes while robust to nuisance variations (texture, lighting). It uses a self-supervised objective leveraging dense particle-based simulator geometry.
- **Correspondence Definition:** Simulator particle geometry defines correspondence targets. The goal is to ensure that tokens corresponding to the same physical cloth location remain close in feature space despite non-rigid deformation over time.
- **Loss Function (`L_same`):** A soft-weighted supervised contrastive loss is used. For an anchor token, positives are other tokens from the same cloth identity pool assigned to the same particle. Weights for positives are derived from mutual visibility and Gaussian token-to-particle assignment confidence. The comparison set includes all valid non-self tokens in the denominator.
- **Cross-Cloth Contrastive Term (`L_cross`):** To promote generalization across different garments, this term uses sparse semantic keypoints. Matching keypoints across different cloth instances are treated as positives, with dense in-batch tokens from other garments serving as negatives. This term includes a curriculum-style weighting (0.3/0.5/1.0) for same-trajectory, same-cloth cross-trajectory, and cross-cloth matching.
- **Encoder Training:** A frozen DINOv3 backbone (ViT-S/16+) is used, with LoRA adapters applied to the final blocks and a lightweight projection/contrastive head. Feature aggregation occurs from multiple transformer depths. Training utilizes strict PK sampling to ensure same-cloth positives within each batch. After pretraining, the contrastive head is discarded, and the LoRA adapters and projection module are transferred to policy learning.
- **Preprocessing and Trajectory Filtering:** Static frames are filtered out based on particle movement. Overly long sequences are subsampled to a fixed frame budget.

#### 3.3. In-Context Policy Learning

This stage formulates DOM as an in-context trajectory generation problem. Given a current observation (visual state and robot proprioception) and a keyframe sequence from a single demonstration, the policy predicts a future action trajectory.
- **Context Keyframe Extraction:** Keyframes are automatically extracted from demonstrations based on end-effector state events (e.g., gripper open/close transitions), summarizing distinct deformation phases and key robot interactions.
- **Action Representation:** Each robot state is represented by a 3D Cartesian position (x, y, z) and binary gripper openness. Per-gripper actions are deltas in this space, forming a bimanual action. States and actions are expressed in the camera frame.
- **3D Relative Attention:** Robot state and noisy action tokens are embedded with their 3D coordinates. RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) is applied in geometry-bearing attention blocks to encode relative positional information.
- **Context Encoder:** This component maps context keyframes into a structured representation. Keyframes are tokenized into cloth and robot-state tokens.
 - **Per-frame Spatial Attention:** Models local cloth-robot interactions within each keyframe using RoPE and 3D ALiBI.
 - **Joint Spatio-Temporal Self-Attention:** Mixes tokens across all keyframes after flattening and adding temporal sinusoidal embeddings. This accounts for token ordering not being temporally consistent across frames.
 - **Context Encoding Mechanisms:**
 - **3D ALiBI:** Biases spatial attention towards nearby cloth-robot pairs.
 - **Learned Summary Tokens:** Distill global demonstration intent into compact tokens.
 - **State-Event Tokens:** Preserve sparse gripper events (grasp/release cues).
- **Flow Matching Action Decoder:** This decoder fuses the encoded demonstration context and current-scene tokens via cross-attention.
 - Noisy trajectory tokens then cross-attend to this "scene context" (which includes state-event tokens).
 - Refinement occurs through joint self-attention blocks.
 - Denoising timestep and current proprioceptive state are injected via AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization).
 - An auxiliary keypose prediction task, prepending noisy next-keypose tokens, provides short-horizon subgoal supervision.
 - **Training Objective:** Rectified flow matching for Cartesian positions and binary cross-entropy for gripper openness, with an additional loss for the auxiliary keypose prediction.
- **Data Generation:** Demonstrations are generated in a FleX-based simulator using 360 Cloth3D meshes. 19 folding modes with execution variants are defined, generating 80K total trajectories. Domain randomization covers camera, appearance, initial gripper poses, cloth drop, sleeve disturbances, and wrinkle noise. An additional 40K pretraining-only trajectories with random deformations are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation and real-world settings evaluate the effectiveness of Instant-Fold, its components, and its generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Representation Pretraining

- The dense-negative temporal formulation combined with the soft contrastive objective demonstrated superior performance among pretraining variants in tracking correspondence, as detailed in Appendix B.
- Qualitative evaluations (Figure 4) indicate that the learned encoder effectively transfers from simulation to real-world scenarios. The generated representations exhibit strong temporal consistency and spatial coherence, outperforming raw DINOv3 features (which showed less temporal consistency under large deformations) and UniGarmentManip's PointNet features (which appeared more mottled).
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of multi-layer DINOv3 feature aggregation and curriculum weighting for cross-cloth correspondence and overall stability.

#### 4.2. Policy Learning

- **Demonstration vs. Language Conditioning:** Demonstration conditioning substantially improved fold execution quality compared to language conditioning. For held-out tasks, while context-following accuracy was similar (75.0% for language, 73.5% for demo without pretraining), demonstrations nearly doubled conditional success rate (C-SR@95: 42.3% vs. 20.2%) and yielded better final geometry (Geom. 2.24 vs. 3.36; W1 0.140 vs. 0.280). This suggests demonstrations provide richer geometric structure beyond mere task intent.
- **Impact of Pretraining:** Pretraining consistently improved both language- and demo-conditioned policies, with the most significant gains observed on held-out folds. Full pretraining on a broader set of 16 folding modes and random deformations achieved the strongest overall performance, leading to 95.8% Ctx. Acc., 58.3% C-SR@95, 1.89 Geom., and 0.099 W1 on held-out folds. This indicates that comprehensive pretraining yields more useful and generalizable representations.
- **Ablation Study of Policy Components:** All proposed design choices contributed to policy performance. Removing the context encoder, keypose auxiliary loss, or summary tokens led to the largest degradations in held-out performance, emphasizing the importance of structured context aggregation and explicit action-phase supervision. The 3D ALiBI component was crucial for generalization, preventing a significant drop in held-out performance when removed.
- **Scaling with Context Diversity:** Increasing the diversity of training contexts primarily enhanced held-out generalization. Held-out C-SR@95 improved from 38.7% (4 contexts) to 63.7% (16 contexts), while seen-fold performance rose more gradually. This suggests that broader context coverage encourages learning more transferable folding behaviors.
- **Comparison with Prior Work (Simulation):** Instant-Fold outperformed established primitive-based methods (ClothFunnels, UniFolding, UniGarmentManip) in simulation on both in-distribution FleX and for sim-to-sim transfer to Isaac Lab. It achieved higher accuracy and lower variance on a shared folding mode (mode 1), despite operating in a continuous end-effector space.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

- The policy successfully transferred zero-shot to a real dual-arm robot. Given a single human demonstration, it executed closed-loop folding on eight unseen real garments (Figure 11).
- Instant-Fold achieved an average success rate of 60.9% on real garments, outperforming baselines that required real-world data collection or fine-tuning (e.g., UniFolding 29.7%, UniGarmentManip 26.6%).
- Identified failure modes included robot kinematic/controller errors, severe overhead-camera occlusions (especially during second-stage folds), sim-to-real physics discrepancies (e.g., fabric draping, slippery surfaces), camera calibration drift, gripper slippage, and segmentation failures. Gripper occlusion modeling during training helped mitigate some sim-to-real mismatch.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Instant-Fold framework represents an advancement in deformable object manipulation by introducing an in-context imitation learning (ICIL) paradigm. Its significance lies in demonstrating that a single human demonstration can be a rich and effective interface for specifying complex deformable manipulation tasks, including variations in execution style.

The primary impacts include:
- **Advancement in Deformable Object Manipulation (DOM):** The work addresses key challenges in DOM, particularly the high-dimensionality and multiple valid modes of manipulation, by providing a method to infer and execute diverse manipulation strategies directly from a single example.
- **Novel Representation Learning:** The proposed temporal contrastive pretraining method specifically designed for deformable objects enables the learning of visual representations that are consistent under non-rigid transformations. This is critical for robust generalization in DOM tasks.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is a notable achievement, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and fine-tuning. This enhances the practicality and scalability of robotic cloth manipulation solutions.
- **Generalization to Unseen Modes and Objects:** The framework exhibits robust generalization capabilities, performing well on unseen garments and held-out folding modes, which is essential for deploying such systems in varied environments.
- **Scalability of ICIL:** The results suggest that ICIL is a promising direction for capturing the inherent diversity of valid manipulation modes in DOM, potentially forming a foundation for more generalized in-context reasoning in this domain.

While promising, the paper acknowledges several limitations, including the reliance on reliable object segmentation, starting from foldable (non-crumpled) configurations, focus on a single object category (garment tops), and a manually designed, limited context library. Future work on automatic context exploration could further unleash the potential of ICIL for broader generalization to entirely unseen deformable object manipulation tasks. The Instant-Fold framework offers a foundation for developing more adaptable and general-purpose robotic systems for handling deformable materials.
