# Reasoning with Sampling: Your Base Model is Smarter Than You Think

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Harvard University developed power sampling, a training-free method leveraging the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm to sample from a sharpened distribution of a base large language model. This technique unlocks latent reasoning capabilities, achieving single-shot performance comparable to or exceeding reinforcement learning post-training methods across various tasks, while also preserving generation diversity.

## Problem

- A lack of clarity on whether reinforcement learning (RL) fundamentally teaches new reasoning skills to LLMs or merely sharpens existing, latent capabilities.
- High computational costs, reliance on curated datasets, and the need for external verifiers inherent in current RL-based LLM post-training methods.
- RL-posttrained models often suffer from a 'collapse in diversity,' sacrificing multi-shot reasoning performance (pass@k) for single-shot gains.

## Method

- Samples from a =='power distribution'== p_α (where α≥1) of the base LLM, which mathematically reweights the original distribution to favor higher-likelihood sequences.
- Employs the ==Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithm==, a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique, to enable approximate sampling from the unnormalized power distribution.
- Introduces an ==iterative, block-wise autoregressive MCMC approach== to progressively refine sequence segments, managing complexity and improving mixing times for long-sequence generation.

## Results

- Achieved single-shot reasoning performance comparable to or surpassing state-of-the-art RL-posttraining methods (like GRPO) on in-domain (e.g., MATH500) and out-of-domain (e.g., HumanEval, GPQA) tasks, with Phi-3.5-mini-instruct showing a **+51.9%** accuracy gain over its base model on HumanEval.
- Demonstrated performance gains are training-free, dataset-free, and verifier-free, offering a significantly more efficient and broadly applicable approach than RL-based methods.
- Maintained and improved multi-shot generation diversity (pass@k accuracy) compared to both base models and GRPO, effectively avoiding the 'collapse in diversity' common in RL-posttraining approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sampling from the power distribution p_α is fundamentally distinct from traditional low-temperature sampling, as it focuses on upweighting tokens with 'few but high likelihood future paths' essential for reasoning.
> - Base large language models possess greater latent reasoning capabilities than conventionally assumed, which can be effectively elicited purely through sophisticated inference-time sampling without additional training.
> - The success of power sampling reinforces the 'distribution sharpening' hypothesis, suggesting that much of the improvement from RL-posttraining comes from refining the model's likelihood of selecting existing high-quality solutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental question at the forefront of large language model research: how to best enhance and elicit reasoning capabilities from LLMs. The dominant paradigm for achieving this has been **reinforcement learning (RL) post-training**, particularly RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR), epitomized by methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) (Shao et al., 2024). These RL techniques have shown significant empirical success in improving LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks across domains like mathematics, coding, and science.

However, a burgeoning body of literature (e.g., He et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025) has raised critical questions about the nature of the capabilities gained through RL post-training. Specifically, researchers debate whether RL truly instills *novel reasoning behaviors* in LLMs or merely *sharpened* versions of capabilities already latent within the base models. Evidence supporting the "sharpening" hypothesis includes observations that RL-posttrained models often suffer from a "collapse in diversity" for multi-shot reasoning (pass@k performance) compared to base models, and that their reasoning traces tend to be highly concentrated at high likelihoods under the base model. This implies that RL might be refining the model to consistently select the "best" path from its existing repertoire, rather than discovering entirely new solution pathways.

"Reasoning with Sampling" directly intervenes in this debate by exploring an alternative hypothesis: that comparable, or even superior, reasoning capabilities can be *elicited from base models at inference time solely through sophisticated sampling techniques*, without any additional training. By proposing a training-free, dataset-free, and verifier-free method, the authors challenge the perceived necessity of the costly and complex RL post-training pipeline for achieving high single-shot reasoning performance.

Furthermore, the work seamlessly integrates concepts from classical Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods and ideas from the diffusion model literature (such as annealed or tempered distributions) into the context of autoregressive LLM sampling. This cross-pollination of techniques positions the paper at the intersection of fundamental generative modeling theory and practical LLM enhancement, bridging distinct but related areas of AI research. It seeks to demonstrate that advancements in sampling, traditionally applied to fields like statistical physics and diffusion models, hold immense untapped potential for unlocking the latent reasoning power of LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether sophisticated inference-time sampling from base LLMs can achieve single-shot reasoning capabilities comparable to, or even exceeding, those attained through reinforcement learning post-training. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

- **Challenging the Dominant Paradigm:** The authors aim to directly address the ongoing debate regarding whether RL-posttraining fundamentally creates new reasoning abilities or merely sharpens existing ones. By demonstrating competitive performance without training, they lean heavily into the latter hypothesis, suggesting that base models are "smarter than you think" and their full potential is underutilized by conventional sampling.
- **Overcoming RL Drawbacks:** Reinforcement learning for LLMs, while effective, comes with inherent challenges. These include:
 - **Computational Cost of Training:** RL requires extensive training compute and often complex hyperparameter tuning, which can be unstable.
 - **Data Scarcity and Curation:** It necessitates curated and diverse post-training datasets, which can be labor-intensive and expensive to acquire.
 - **Verifier Dependence:** RLVR methods rely on an automated verifier or reward signal, limiting their applicability to domains where correctness can be easily and objectively verified. This paper explicitly seeks to develop a verifier-free approach.
 - **Diversity Collapse:** A known issue with RL-posttraining is the degradation of generation diversity (evidenced by pass@k performance), where models become too focused on high-reward paths, potentially sacrificing exploration and robustness. The authors are motivated to find a method that retains diversity.
- **Exploring Inference-Time Scaling:** The paper introduces the concept of expending "additional compute at inference time" as a novel axis for enhancing reasoning. Rather than training a larger or more specialized model, the motivation is to squeeze more performance out of existing base models through smarter sampling strategies during deployment.
- **Broadening Applicability:** By being training-free, dataset-free, and verifier-free, the proposed method is motivated by the desire to extend high-performance reasoning capabilities to domains where constructing reward functions or collecting extensive feedback is difficult or impossible, moving beyond easily verifiable tasks.

In essence, the authors are motivated to unlock latent reasoning capabilities in LLMs more efficiently and broadly, by re-evaluating the role of inference-time processing and drawing inspiration from advanced statistical sampling theory.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around **sampling from a "power distribution" $p^\alpha$** of the base LLM, rather than directly from the base model's original distribution $p$.

- **Motivation for Power Distribution ($p^\alpha$):** The authors hypothesize that if RL-posttraining primarily "sharpens" the base model's distribution, then explicitly sampling from a sharpened version of $p$ should yield similar benefits. The power distribution $p^\alpha$ (where $\alpha \ge 1$) achieves this by mathematically reweighting the original distribution: sequences with higher likelihoods under $p$ are disproportionately upweighted, while lower-likelihood sequences are downweighted. This biases sampling towards regions of higher base model likelihood.

- **Distinction from Low-Temperature Sampling:** A crucial conceptual contribution is the formal distinction between sampling from $p^\alpha$ and traditional low-temperature sampling. While low-temperature sampling applies the exponentiation to *conditional* next-token distributions, sampling from $p^\alpha$ applies it to the *joint* distribution over entire sequences. The authors mathematically prove (Proposition 1) and illustrate with an example (Example 1) that these are not equivalent. Low-temperature sampling "greedily" averages future likelihoods, potentially favoring tokens with many low-likelihood completions. In contrast, sampling from $p^\alpha$ inherently accounts for "future paths" by exponentiating all possible future completions before computing next-token probabilities. This leads to the key observation (Observation 1) that $p^\alpha$ upweights tokens that have "few but high likelihood future paths" – a characteristic highly valuable for reasoning tasks, where "critical windows" or pivotal tokens can make or break a solution.

- **Metropolis-Hastings (MH) Algorithm:** Direct sampling from $p^\alpha$ is computationally intractable because the distribution is unnormalized over the vast space of possible token sequences. To circumvent this, the authors employ the **Metropolis-Hastings (MH) algorithm**, a classic Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) technique. MH allows for approximate sampling from an unnormalized probability distribution by constructing a Markov chain that converges to the target distribution. It only requires knowing the *relative* weights of the target distribution and a tractable proposal distribution. The authors propose using "random resampling" as the proposal distribution, where a random token index `t` in a sequence is selected, and the sequence from `t` onwards is re-sampled using an arbitrary "proposal LLM." This ensures the necessary irreducibility and aperiodicity conditions for MH convergence.

- **Power Sampling with Autoregressive MCMC (Algorithm 1):** To address the practical challenges of MCMC, particularly the slow mixing times in high-dimensional sequence spaces, the authors introduce an iterative, block-wise approach. Instead of sampling an entire long sequence at once, the algorithm progressively samples from a series of intermediate power distributions over increasing sequence prefixes (e.g., $p(x_{0:B})^\alpha$, then $p(x_{0:2B})^\alpha$, and so on).
 1. For each block `k`, given a fixed prefix `x_0:kB`, the algorithm initializes the next `B` tokens by autoregressive sampling using a "proposal LLM" (chosen as the base model, often with low-temperature sampling).
 2. It then runs `N_MCMC` Metropolis-Hastings steps to refine this `B`-token segment. In each step, a random index within the current (k+1)B-length sequence is chosen, and the subsequent tokens are resampled from the proposal LLM. The proposed new sequence is then accepted or rejected based on the MH acceptance ratio, which compares the relative $p^\alpha$ likelihoods of the current and proposed sequences.
 3. Once `N_MCMC` steps are completed, the (k+1)B-length prefix is fixed, and the process moves to the next block.

This sequential MCMC approach leverages the autoregressive nature of LLMs, avoids pathological initializations, and helps manage the complexity of MCMC in high-dimensional spaces. The hyperparameters are $\alpha$ (power exponent), `B` (block size), and `N_MCMC` (number of MCMC steps). The computational cost is quantified, showing it scales with these parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed power sampling algorithm:

- **Comparable to and Outperforming RL-Posttraining:** The most significant finding is that power sampling achieves substantial boosts in single-shot reasoning performance, often matching or even surpassing the accuracies and scores of state-of-the-art RL-posttraining methods (specifically GRPO) across a variety of tasks and base models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-3.5-mini-instruct).
 - On **in-domain tasks** like MATH500 (where GRPO is typically trained), power sampling achieved accuracies nearly on par with GRPO. For instance, Qwen2.5-Math-7B improved from 49.6% (Base) to 74.8% (Ours), close to GRPO's 78.5%.
 - On **out-of-domain reasoning tasks** such as HumanEval and GPQA, power sampling frequently matched or *outperformed* GRPO. A striking example is Phi-3.5-mini-instruct on HumanEval, where the base model scored 21.3%, GRPO 13.4%, and power sampling achieved 73.2% (+51.9% over base, and significantly outperforming GRPO).
 - For **non-verifiable general tasks** like AlpacaEval 2.0, power sampling consistently outperformed GRPO, suggesting its broader applicability.

- **Training-Free, Dataset-Free, and Verifier-Free:** The performance gains are achieved without any additional training, curated datasets, or access to an external reward signal/verifier. This fundamentally distinguishes it from RL-based approaches and highlights the latent capabilities within base models.

- **Reasoning Trace Characteristics:**
 - **Likelihoods and Confidences:** Power sampling successfully targets and generates sequences from higher likelihood and higher confidence regions of the base model's distribution. While GRPO samples tend to be extremely concentrated at the highest likelihood peaks, power sampling maintains a noticeable spread, correlating with strong empirical accuracy.
 - **Reasoning Trace Lengths:** Surprisingly, power sampling naturally encourages the generation of longer reasoning traces (e.g., average 679 tokens for MATH500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B) similar to RL-posttrained models, without any explicit objective to do so. This emergent behavior suggests that longer, more detailed reasoning steps often correspond to higher likelihood paths under the power distribution.

- **Sustained Diversity (Pass@k Performance):** A critical finding is that power sampling avoids the "collapse in diversity" that afflicts RL-posttraining. Its pass@k accuracy curve is consistently better than both the base model and GRPO for k > 1. This means it achieves high single-shot performance *without compromising multi-shot capabilities or generation diversity*, offering the "best of both worlds" in contrast to RL methods which often trade multi-shot diversity for single-shot gains.

- **Hyperparameter Robustness and Computational Cost:**
 - The choice of $\alpha=4.0$ was found to be optimal, but performance remained relatively stable for $\alpha \ge 2.0$, indicating robustness.
 - Accuracy steadily increased with the number of MCMC steps (`N_MCMC`), with significant gains observed even at small `N_MCMC` (e.g., from 0 to 2 steps).
 - The additional inference-time compute incurred by power sampling (e.g., ~8.84x standard inference for MATH500) is comparable to or less than the cost of one epoch of GRPO training, suggesting a reasonable trade-off between compute and performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of "Reasoning with Sampling" hold significant implications for the field of large language models:

- **Re-evaluation of Base Model Capabilities:** The paper makes a compelling case that existing, pre-trained base LLMs are far more capable at single-shot reasoning than generally assumed. Their latent intelligence is often obscured by suboptimal inference-time sampling strategies. This challenges the notion that complex, costly post-training paradigms are the *only* way to unlock advanced reasoning.
- **A Paradigm Shift in LLM Enhancement:** By demonstrating that substantial reasoning gains can be achieved purely through inference-time sampling, the paper presents a viable alternative or complement to resource-intensive training-based methods like RLHF/RLVR. This opens up a new avenue for "inference-time scaling," where additional compute at deployment can be dynamically traded for higher output quality, offering greater flexibility and efficiency.
- **Broader Applicability for Reasoning:** The training-free, dataset-free, and verifier-free nature of power sampling is a critical advantage. It expands the scope of tasks where high-quality reasoning can be elicited, particularly in domains where explicit reward signals are unavailable, difficult to define, or costly to obtain (e.g., highly subjective tasks, creative writing, complex scientific hypothesis generation without immediate verification). This could democratize access to advanced LLM reasoning for a wider array of applications.
- **Reaffirmation of Distribution Sharpening Hypothesis:** The success of sampling from a power distribution reinforces the hypothesis that much of the perceived improvement from RL-posttraining comes from "distribution sharpening" – making the model more likely to pick existing high-quality solutions – rather than teaching fundamentally new reasoning skills. This deepens our theoretical understanding of LLM capabilities and how they are modulated.
- **Stimulus for Future Research in Sampling:** The work highlights the untapped potential of classical MCMC and advanced sampling techniques for LLMs. It is likely to spur further research into developing more efficient, robust, and theoretically grounded sampling algorithms tailored for the autoregressive nature of language models. This could lead to a renaissance of research at the intersection of statistical inference, generative modeling, and natural language processing.
- **Addressing Diversity Concerns:** The ability to achieve high single-shot performance without sacrificing multi-shot diversity is a significant advantage over current RL methods. This could lead to more robust and versatile LLM applications, especially those requiring multiple candidate solutions or creative exploration.

In conclusion, this paper provides a powerful demonstration that our base LLMs are inherently more intelligent than current sampling methods reveal. By re-imagining how we sample, the authors present a pragmatic, effective, and theoretically informed pathway to better reasoning, with broad implications for both research and practical applications of LLMs.
