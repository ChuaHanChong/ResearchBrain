# Cross-Hand Latent Representation for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego and UC Berkeley introduced XL-VLA, a framework utilizing a unified, embodiment-invariant latent action space to enable scalable vision-language-action models for dexterous manipulation. This approach yielded improved cross-hand training and robust zero-shot generalization to new robot hands and tasks.

## Problem

- The high cost of data collection required to train vision-language-action (VLA) models for each new and diverse dexterous robotic hand.
- The absence of a universal action representation, leading to embodiment-specific VLA policies that struggle with data reuse and generalization across different robot morphologies.
- Inefficient reuse of collected demonstration data and limited zero-shot generalization capabilities to novel robotic hands or unseen task configurations.

## Method

- Developed **XL-VLA**, a framework integrating a standard VLA backbone with pre-trained, ==hand-specific latent== encoders and decoders.
- Created a shared, compact latent action space using a ==multi-headed VAE==-style autoencoder, independently trained with reconstruction, retargeting, and KL regularization losses.
- The VLA policy operates entirely within the frozen, embodiment-invariant latent space, where hand identity only dictates the specific encoder/decoder used to map to/from raw joint spaces.

## Results

- XL-VLA achieved a mean success rate of **0.72** across four dexterous hands and ten tasks, a **40%** relative improvement over a π0 baseline (**0.32**).
- Demonstrated a **57%** relative improvement (**0.825** vs **0.525**) for a Unitree G1 humanoid when co-training with data from other robot systems, despite variations in state/action lengths.
- Consistently outperformed kinematic retargeting (RT) baselines in zero-shot task generalization to unseen hand-task combinations, never underperforming RT and often by substantial margins, particularly for fine-grained dexterous tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified latent action space effectively abstracts away embodiment-specific details of diverse dexterous hands, enabling shared policy learning and reducing data dependency.
> - Aligning fingertip geometry across different hands through a differentiable forward kinematics-based retargeting loss is crucial for shaping a meaningful cross-embodiment latent space.
> - Operating VLA models within a carefully constructed embodiment-invariant latent space consistently outperforms policies trained directly on raw, diverse joint spaces for complex dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the evolving field of robot learning, particularly at the intersection of vision-language-action (VLA) models, dexterous manipulation, and cross-embodiment learning. Recent advancements in VLA modeling have enabled robots to interpret visual scenes and natural language instructions for executing complex behaviors. These systems often treat actions as an additional output modality, drawing inspiration from the success of large-scale vision and language models.

A significant challenge in extending VLA to robotics, especially for dexterous manipulation, is the embodiment-specific nature of robot action spaces. Unlike language, which has a relatively stable vocabulary, robotic action parameterizations (e.g., joint positions) vary considerably across different robotic hands. This presents obstacles for scalable robot learning, as training VLA models for dexterous manipulation typically requires extensive demonstrations for each specific robotic hand. As new dexterous hand embodiments are continuously developed, collecting new datasets for each becomes costly and impractical, hindering the scalability and generalization of robot learning systems.

Prior VLA and cross-embodiment research has primarily focused on robotic arms equipped with parallel grippers. This work distinguishes itself by focusing on dexterous manipulation, which involves substantially more complex and capable multi-fingered hands. It also emphasizes real-world datasets and physical robot evaluations, addressing the robustness of methods under significant cross-embodiment variation. The paper aims to define a unified action representation that can be shared across a family of diverse dexterous robots and to seamlessly integrate new robots with different action spaces.

The research aligns with approaches that construct unified latent action spaces to factor out embodiment-specific details, thereby enabling transfer and zero-shot reuse across different robotic platforms. This approach contrasts with methods that rely on human video supervision, imitation learning with motion retargeting to bridge morphology gaps, or generative models to synthesize action-consistent trajectories across bodies. By creating an embodiment-invariant latent space, this work contributes to the development of more generalizable and data-efficient dexterous manipulation systems capable of adapting to rapid hardware innovation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a vision-language-action (VLA) framework that facilitates scalable cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation by introducing a unified, embodiment-invariant latent action space. This approach is motivated by several challenges inherent in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **High Cost of Data Collection for Diverse Dexterous Hands:** Training reliable VLA models for dexterous manipulation necessitates large-scale datasets. However, the rapid proliferation of new dexterous robotic hands means that collecting sufficient demonstration data for each unique embodiment is often prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This creates a bottleneck for expanding the capabilities of VLA systems to a wider array of robotic hardware.
2. **Lack of a Universal Action Representation:** Unlike vision and language, where foundational models benefit from relatively stable and universal input/output representations, robotic action spaces are inherently tied to the specific morphology and actuation of a robot. For dexterous hands, joint position parameters vary significantly across different designs, making it difficult to develop a single VLA policy that can control multiple hands without extensive re-training or adaptation.
3. **Inefficient Data Reuse:** Existing VLA models typically operate on raw joint spaces, meaning data collected for one hand is not readily transferable or reusable for another hand with different kinematics. This leads to inefficient use of accumulated demonstration data and slows down the development of generalist robot policies.
4. **Challenges in Zero-Shot Generalization:** The embodiment-specific nature of action spaces hinders the ability of VLA models to generalize to novel robotic hands or unseen task configurations in a zero-shot manner. A method is required that allows a policy trained on a subset of hands or tasks to be directly applied to new hand-task combinations.

To address these motivations, the authors propose XL-VLA, a framework designed to:
- Learn a unified latent action space that is shared across diverse dexterous hands, making it embodiment-invariant.
- Integrate this latent space into standard VLA architectures, allowing for seamless cross-embodiment training.
- Enable efficient reuse of both existing and newly collected data, as the action representations are normalized to a common latent space.
- Facilitate zero-shot generalization to new hand–task combinations, reducing the need for extensive data collection and re-training for every new hand or task.
- Outperform baseline VLA models operating directly in raw joint spaces, demonstrating the practical effectiveness of the latent representation for scalable dexterous manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The XL-VLA framework is designed to enable language-guided, vision-based cross-embodiment dexterous manipulation through a unified latent action space.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The system controls absolute joint rotations $q^{(h)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_h}$ for a dexterous hand $h$ with $d_h$ actuated joints. The policy operates on "action chunks," where each action $q^{(h)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times d_h}$ represents a sequence of 64 joint-position commands sampled at 20 Hz (3.2 seconds of motion). At each control step $t$, the policy receives a history of joint states, the previously executed action chunk $q^{(h)}_t$, current image observations $V$, and a language instruction $T$. It then predicts the next action chunk $q^{(h)}_{t+1} = F(q^{(h)}_t, V, T)$. The objective is to predict embodiment-consistent joint-rotation trajectories conditioned on multimodal inputs using a unified, multi-task VLA model. The model is trained to be hand-agnostic, with hand identity $h$ only used to select the appropriate encoder/decoder mapping between joint space and the shared latent action space.

#### 3.2. Pipeline Overview

The XL-VLA framework consists of two primary components:
1. **VLA Backbone:** This component encodes multimodal inputs (vision $V$ and language $T$). The design follows $\pi_0$, which combines vision and language encoders with an action expert.
2. **Pretrained Latent Encoders and Decoders:** A set of hand-specific encoders $E_h$ and decoders $D_h$ map between the raw joint space of each hand and a shared, compact latent vector space.

During VLA training, the VLA backbone receives a short history of latent action tokens, together with vision and language tokens. For each hand $h$, a hand-specific encoder $E_h$ maps the previous absolute joint-position action chunk $q^{(h)}_t$ into a latent vector $z_t = E_h(q^{(h)}_t)$. The VLA model predicts the next latent chunk $\hat{z}_{t+1}$, which is then decoded by the embodiment-specific decoder $D_h$ to obtain the next joint command chunk $\hat{q}^{(h)}_{t+1} = D_h(\hat{z}_{t+1})$. A crucial aspect is that all latent encoders and decoders are frozen during VLA finetuning, meaning the VLA policy operates entirely within the embodiment-invariant latent space. The hand identity $h$ is never an explicit input token to the VLA backbone; it merely dictates which specific encoder/decoder pair is used.

#### 3.3. Latent Space Definition and Modeling

A shared latent action space is introduced to provide a unified representation for all dexterous hand embodiments. This latent space is pretrained independently of the VLA model.
- **Modeling:** A multi-headed VAE-style autoencoder is employed. For each hand $h$, a hand-specific encoder $E_h$ and decoder $D_h$ are defined. The encoder maps a joint configuration $q^{(h)}$ to the parameters of a Gaussian posterior $(\mu^{(h)}, \sigma^{(h)})$, from which a latent code $z$ is sampled. The decoder reconstructs the joint configuration $\hat{q}^{(h)} = D_h(z)$. Each encoder and decoder is implemented as a lightweight MLP.
- **Training Constraints:** To shape a meaningful cross-embodiment latent space, three constraints are applied:
 1. **Reconstruction Loss ($L_1$):** Ensures that each hand-specific encoder-decoder pair functions as an autoencoder, preserving hand-specific kinematics. $L_1 = \frac{1}{|H|} \sum_{h \in H} \text{MSE}(\hat{q}^{(h)}, q^{(h)})$.
 2. **Retargeting Loss ($L_2$):** Aligns fingertip geometry across different hands using differentiable forward kinematics (FK). For each hand, FK maps joint positions to fingertip positions, and fingertip displacements (pinch distances and directions) are defined for thumb-finger pairs. $L_2$ penalizes discrepancies in these displacements between a source hand $s$ and target hands $t$. The loss includes components for both distance and direction, weighted to emphasize tighter pinches. This loss encourages consistent pinch behaviors from the same latent code across different hands.
 3. **Latent Loss ($L_3$, KL Regularization):** Regularizes the latent embedding to follow a smooth prior distribution, specifically a standard Gaussian $N(0, I)$. $L_3 = E_q[\text{KL}(q(z|q) || N(0, I))]$. This promotes a well-behaved latent space for sampling and interpolation.

#### 3.4. Training Data and Protocol for Latent Space

The latent autoencoder is trained in a self-supervised manner without demonstration or inverse kinematics (IK)-generated trajectories. For each hand, random joint configurations within hardware limits are sampled. Each $q^{(s)}$ is encoded to a latent $z$, then decoded by all decoders $\{D_t\}_{t \in H}$. The self-decoding $D_s(z)$ contributes to $L_1$, while cross-hand decodings $D_t(z)$ for $t \ne s$ contribute to $L_2$. Losses are aggregated, and all encoders and decoders are jointly optimized.

#### 3.5. Total Latent Objective

The full latent training loss combines these components: $L_{\text{latent}} = L_1 + L_2 + \beta L_3$, with fixed weights $\beta=10^{-5}$, $\lambda_{\text{dis}}=2000.0$, $\lambda_{\text{dir}}=5.0$, and $\lambda_{\text{exp}_{\text{dis}}}=12.0$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates XL-VLA through comprehensive experiments, focusing on its effectiveness in cross-embodiment VLA training, zero-shot skill transfer, and the intrinsic properties of the latent action space.

#### 4.1. Tasks and Dataset

The study utilized a dataset of 2M state-action pairs collected via teleoperation across four dexterous hands (Ability Hand, Paxini DexH13, X-Hand1, and Inspire Hand) performing 10 manipulation tasks: Prepare Fruits (PF), Stack Cans (SC), Sort Cans (SoC), Hand over Bottle (HB), Re-organize Lemons (RL), Pour Sauce (PS), Re-arrange Boxes (RB), Push Sugar (PuS), Pour Sugar (PoS), and Push Cans (PC). Each task involved 50 demonstrations per hand.

#### 4.2. Hardware and Experiment Settings

Experiments were conducted on a bimanual 7-DoF xArm platform and a Unitree G1 humanoid, both equipped with various dexterous hands. XL-VLA was initialized with weights from $\pi_0$ and trained for 60K steps with a batch size of 128 on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs.

#### 4.3. Main Results: Effectiveness of VLA + Latent Integration

- **Cross-Hand Data Scaling:**
 - XL-VLA was compared against a $\pi_0$ baseline trained as a single shared policy across the four hands and ten tasks (Table 2).
 - The $\pi_0$ baseline exhibited inconsistent and generally low performance, with a mean success rate of 0.32, attributed to significant kinematic and actuation differences across hands.
 - XL-VLA achieved consistently higher success rates across all hands and tasks, with a mean success rate of 0.72 (+40% relative improvement).
 - Per-embodiment gains were notable: Ability Hand improved from 0.37 to 0.73, Paxini Hand achieved the highest performance at 0.78, and XHand, despite being mechanically distinct, improved from 0.29 to 0.70.
 - Particularly large improvements were observed in dexterity-heavy tasks such as Sort Cans, Hand over Bottle, and Re-arrange Boxes, suggesting the effectiveness of the embodiment-invariant latent space in capturing fine-grained manipulation behaviors.

- **Cross-Robot Data Scaling:**
 - The benefit of the unified latent space was also demonstrated across different robot systems (tabletop xArm and humanoid G1).
 - Co-training data from all embodiments on four tasks (PF, HB, PS, PoS) showed that XL-VLA significantly boosted performance for the G1 humanoid (Figure 5, Table 6).
 - XL-VLA achieved a mean success rate of 0.825 on G1, representing a 57% relative improvement over the $\pi_0$ baseline (0.525), despite variations in state/action lengths across robot systems.

- **Zero-Shot Task Generalization:**
 - XL-VLA's ability to generalize to unseen tasks and hand combinations in a zero-shot manner was evaluated by holding out tasks for each hand during training (Figure 4).
 - A $\pi_0$+RT baseline, which used kinematic retargeting to transfer policies trained on XHand to other embodiments, was used for comparison.
 - XL-VLA consistently outperformed the retargeting VLA baseline across all embodiments and tasks, often by substantial margins, and never underperformed it.
 - Gains were particularly pronounced for fine-grained dexterous tasks (e.g., HB, RB), where geometric retargeting typically struggled.
 - This indicates that the latent action representation provides genuine zero-shot benefits over retargeting-based transfer.

#### 4.4. Ablation Results: Effectiveness of the Latent Action Space

- **Latent Replay Comparison:**
 - The latent action space was compared against LAD, a supervised latent-space retargeting method (Table 4).
 - Latent replay experiments, where demonstrations from two source hands were replayed on two target hands, showed XL-VLA achieved mean success rates of 0.82 (Ability+Inspire) and 0.81 (Paxini+XHand).
 - LAD achieved 0.60 and 0.61 on the same pairs. This suggests that XL-VLA's unsupervised latent alignment captures embodiment-invariant structure more effectively, leading to more reliable cross-hand trajectory replay.

- **Design Choice Comparison:**
 - An ablation study investigated the impact of architectural and loss-design choices for the latent action space (Table 5).
 - The final configuration (H$_{128 \to 64}$ with 32 latent dimensions) showed strong performance across reconstruction accuracy, cross-embodiment retargeting, latent-space continuity, and interpolation smoothness metrics.
 - Removing the reconstruction loss ($L_1$) or either component of the retargeting loss ($L_2$) significantly degraded performance. For instance, removing $L_1$ increased Joint RMSE from 5.476 to 61.672. Removing $L_2$ (both components) increased RT_dir from 10.492 to 71.765 and RT_dist from 6.295 to 62.809.
 - Performance remained stable across a range of hidden sizes and latent dimensions, with degradation primarily occurring when the latent size was excessively large (e.g., L$_{128}$), which hindered embodiment-invariant structure.
 - The chosen configuration offered an effective balance between model capacity and latent compactness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces XL-VLA, a framework that addresses critical challenges in scalable robot learning for dexterous manipulation. By developing and integrating a unified, embodiment-invariant latent action space into a vision-language-action model, the work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Scalability for Dexterous Manipulation:** XL-VLA provides a solution to the problem of requiring extensive, embodiment-specific data for each new dexterous robotic hand. By learning a shared latent representation, it enables efficient training across diverse hardware, which is crucial as new hand designs rapidly emerge. This significantly reduces the data collection burden and computational costs associated with deploying VLA models on different robotic platforms.
2. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The embodiment-invariant latent space facilitates strong zero-shot generalization to novel hand-task combinations. This means a policy trained with XL-VLA can control an untrained hand on an unseen task without requiring additional data or retraining, a capability that standard VLA models and kinematic retargeting methods struggle to achieve. This is a step towards more generalist robot policies that are not confined to a single hardware setup.
3. **Data Efficiency and Reuse:** The unified latent representation allows for the efficient reuse of existing and newly collected demonstration data across heterogeneous robotic hands. Instead of disparate datasets, data contributes to a common action representation, thereby maximizing the value of collected robotic experiences.
4. **Flexible and Modular System Design:** The plug-and-play interface for newly introduced hands is a key design feature. New hand embodiments can be integrated by simply developing their specific encoder and decoder for the shared latent space, without necessitating a complete overhaul or re-training of the entire VLA policy. This modularity streamlines the development and deployment of advanced robotic systems.
5. **Advancement in VLA Modeling:** The work demonstrates that operating in a carefully constructed latent action space consistently outperforms VLA models operating directly in raw, diverse joint spaces. This highlights latent action spaces as a powerful foundation for building more robust and adaptable robot control systems, particularly for complex tasks involving dexterous manipulation.

Overall, this research contributes to the development of more unified, adaptable, and data-efficient robotic manipulation frameworks. The ability to abstract away embodiment-specific details through a latent representation moves the field closer to generalist robots that can readily adapt to new hardware and perform a wider range of tasks with minimal effort.
