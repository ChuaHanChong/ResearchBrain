# Control-A-Video: Controllable Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with Motion Prior and Reward Feedback Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Control-A-Video introduces a controllable text-to-video diffusion model that leverages content and motion priors alongside a Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning algorithm to generate high-quality, temporally consistent videos. It achieves superior text and control map alignment, improving objective technical quality by 2.3 MUSIQ points and reaching a higher ImageReward score.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models often produce videos with low visual quality, artifacts, and blur.
- Maintaining temporal consistency, including object identity and smooth motion across frames, is a significant challenge for T2V models.
- Current T2V methods lack fine-grained control over generated video content and motion, unlike advancements seen in text-to-image models with control maps.

## Method

- **Control-A-Video** integrates a ==first-frame condition== scheme (content prior) to leverage Text-to-Image capabilities and simplify temporal modeling.
- Introduces ==motion-adaptive noise priors== (Residual-based and Flow-based) to infuse motion information directly into the latent space for improved consistency.
- Develops a ==Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning== (ST-ReFL) algorithm that uses multiple ==reward models== to optimize for motion consistency and subjective/objective video quality.

## Results

- Achieved the highest CLIP prompt score (**0.290**) for text alignment and the lowest depth map errors (**0.089**) for control signal adherence compared to state-of-the-art models.
- Demonstrated improved objective technical quality (**73.3** MUSIQ score, a **2.3**-point increase) and the highest aesthetic quality (**1.03** ImageReward score).
- User studies confirmed superior performance in textual alignment (**4.4**), consistency (**4.5**), and visual quality (**4.2**) ratings against baselines on a 1-5 scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling content from temporal modeling through a first-frame condition allows the model to focus more effectively on motion generation.
> - Initializing latent noise with motion-adaptive priors significantly improves frame consistency and reduces flickering by preserving inter-frame correlation.
> - Optimizing T2V models with human-aligned reward signals (ST-ReFL) for both technical quality and motion consistency leads to systematically superior generated videos.
