# Control-A-Video: Controllable Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with Motion Prior and Reward Feedback Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Control-A-Video introduces a controllable text-to-video diffusion model that leverages content and motion priors alongside a Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning algorithm to generate high-quality, temporally consistent videos. It achieves superior text and control map alignment, improving objective technical quality by 2.3 MUSIQ points and reaching a higher ImageReward score.

## Problem

- Existing text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models often produce videos with low visual quality, artifacts, and blur.
- Maintaining temporal consistency, including object identity and smooth motion across frames, is a significant challenge for T2V models.
- Current T2V methods lack fine-grained control over generated video content and motion, unlike advancements seen in text-to-image models with control maps.

## Method

- **Control-A-Video** integrates a ==first-frame condition== scheme (content prior) to leverage Text-to-Image capabilities and simplify temporal modeling.
- Introduces ==motion-adaptive noise priors== (Residual-based and Flow-based) to infuse motion information directly into the latent space for improved consistency.
- Develops a ==Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning== (ST-ReFL) algorithm that uses multiple ==reward models== to optimize for motion consistency and subjective/objective video quality.

## Results

- Achieved the highest CLIP prompt score (**0.290**) for text alignment and the lowest depth map errors (**0.089**) for control signal adherence compared to state-of-the-art models.
- Demonstrated improved objective technical quality (**73.3** MUSIQ score, a **2.3**-point increase) and the highest aesthetic quality (**1.03** ImageReward score).
- User studies confirmed superior performance in textual alignment (**4.4**), consistency (**4.5**), and visual quality (**4.2**) ratings against baselines on a 1-5 scale.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling content from temporal modeling through a first-frame condition allows the model to focus more effectively on motion generation.
> - Initializing latent noise with motion-adaptive priors significantly improves frame consistency and reduces flickering by preserving inter-frame correlation.
> - Optimizing T2V models with human-aligned reward signals (ST-ReFL) for both technical quality and motion consistency leads to systematically superior generated videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual content generation, particularly text-based methods, has advanced significantly in recent years. Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models, such as Stable Diffusion, DALL-E 2, and Imagen, have demonstrated the capability to generate high-quality images from text prompts. These models have also been extended to provide personalized and conditional generation, allowing for finer control over the output images through techniques like Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, ControlNet, and T2I-Adapter.

Building on the success of T2I models, research has expanded into Text-to-Video (T2V) generation. Initial T2V diffusion models, like Imagen Video and Make-A-Video, adapted T2I architectures to generate coherent videos from text. However, extending T2I techniques to video presents distinct challenges. Existing T2V methods often struggle with producing videos that simultaneously exhibit high visual quality, temporal consistency (maintaining object identity and smooth motion across frames), and adherence to specified control signals. Some approaches leverage T2I models for one-shot or zero-shot video generation but may compromise consistency or diversity. Other works introduce conditional video generation with control maps, similar to T2I conditional generation, but still face difficulties in achieving robust quality and motion consistency.

This work positions itself within the controllable T2V generation landscape. It aims to address the limitations of current methods by integrating content and motion priors, along with a novel reward feedback learning mechanism, into a diffusion-based framework. By building upon pre-trained controllable T2I models (specifically ControlNet) and adapting them for video, Control-A-Video seeks to enhance output quality, ensure motion consistency, and maintain fine-grained control via reference control maps (e.g., edge and depth maps). This research contributes to the ongoing effort to develop T2V models that can generate visually appealing and temporally coherent videos with user-specified conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a controllable Text-to-Video (T2V) diffusion model, named Control-A-Video, that can generate high-quality and motion-consistent videos conditioned on both text prompts and reference control maps.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges observed in existing T2V methods:
- **Video Quality Deficiencies:** Current T2V models frequently produce videos with artifacts, blur, and lack overall aesthetic appeal. The paper distinguishes between "technical quality" (absence of artifacts, blur) and "aesthetic quality" (subjective visual appeal), indicating a need to improve both aspects.
- **Motion Consistency Issues:** Maintaining consistency in generated videos is a significant challenge. This includes "object consistency" (ensuring subjects and backgrounds remain stable and identifiable across frames) and "motion smoothness" (ensuring realistic, flicker-free movement that adheres to physical principles). Existing models often struggle with these aspects, leading to flickering, object distortion, or illogical motion.
- **Limited Controllability:** While some T2V models exist, finer-grained control over the generated video content and motion, similar to the advancements seen in T2I models with control maps, is still an area requiring development. The integration of conditions like edge or depth maps for video generation is not consistently achieved with high quality and consistency.
- **Inefficiency in Denoising Training:** The standard denoising training process for diffusion models may not inherently optimize for subjective aspects like aesthetic quality or complex motion consistency across frames, leading to suboptimal generation. Relying solely on large-scale datasets for improvement can be resource-intensive.

To address these motivations, the authors propose specific strategies:
- **Content Prior:** To improve content consistency and leverage knowledge from the image domain, a first-frame condition scheme is introduced. This aims to simplify the learning task by disentangling content and temporal modeling, allowing the model to focus on generating subsequent frames based on a stable initial frame.
- **Motion Prior:** To enhance motion consistency and reduce flickering, novel noise initialization strategies are proposed. These aim to infuse motion information from reference videos directly into the latent space, making the initial noise distribution more relevant to the desired video motion.
- **Reward Feedback Learning:** To systematically improve both video quality and motion consistency beyond standard denoising objectives, a Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning (ST-ReFL) algorithm is introduced. This algorithm uses multiple reward models to evaluate and optimize the generated videos based on defined quality and consistency metrics, thereby guiding the diffusion model towards superior outputs.

By tackling these challenges, Control-A-Video aims to advance the state-of-the-art in controllable T2V generation, enabling the creation of videos that are not only visually appealing and consistent but also precisely guided by user inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Control-A-Video framework integrates several novel components and training strategies into a diffusion-based model to achieve controllable, high-quality, and motion-consistent text-to-video generation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The model builds upon existing Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models, specifically Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) and ControlNet.
- **Temporal Adaptation:** To enable video generation, additional trainable temporal layers are incorporated into both the UNet (denoising backbone) and ControlNet (conditional control module). This includes adding a 1D temporal layer to each 2D layer (convolution and attention layers).
- **Spatial-Temporal Self-Attention:** A key architectural enhancement is the implementation of a spatial-temporal self-attention mechanism. This mechanism allows for joint modeling of spatial and temporal relationships, capturing dependencies across frames. Features from individual frames are processed through 2D convolution or spatial attention, then aggregated and passed to a 1D convolutional layer or temporal attention for frame modeling. By concatenating the K (key) and V (value) features of all `N` frames, each position in the output query `Q` gains a global perception of the entire video sequence, promoting inter-frame consistency.

#### 3.2. Training with Content Prior (First-Frame Condition)

To leverage the generative capabilities from the image domain and simplify temporal modeling, the model employs a first-frame condition scheme during training.
- **Content Disentanglement:** During training, noise is added to all video frames except the first one. The model is then trained to denoise these noisy frames, conditioned on the original (un-noised) first frame, the text prompt, and control maps. This approach aims to disentangle content (derived from the first frame) from temporal motion modeling, allowing the model to focus more specifically on motion reconstruction.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective minimizes the squared difference between the ground truth noise ($\epsilon$) and the noise predicted by the model ($\epsilon_\theta$), conditioned on the noisy latent state ($x_t$), timestep ($t$), text prompt ($c_p$), control maps ($c_f$), and the encoded first frame ($E(v_1)$).
- **T2I-I2V Inference Pipeline:** For inference, a two-stage "Text-to-Image-to-Image-to-Video" (T2I-I2V) pipeline is used. First, the initial frame ($v_1$) is generated using a T2I model conditioned on a text prompt and a first-frame control map. This generated image then serves as the content prior for generating the subsequent video frames.
- **Auto-Regressive Generation:** A benefit of this first-frame conditioning is the ability to auto-regressively generate longer videos by treating the last generated frame of an iteration as the initial frame for the next segment.
- **Classifier-Free Guidance:** The model incorporates a sampling strategy that extends Classifier-Free Guidance to video generation, using both text guidance ($\omega_t$) and video guidance ($\omega_v$) scales to control adherence to the text prompt and overall motion smoothness, respectively.

#### 3.3. Motion-adaptive Noise Prior

To improve frame consistency and ensure that the initial noise distribution aligns with the desired motion, two novel noise initialization strategies are proposed:
- **Motivation:** Analysis of video latents shows high correlation between consecutive frames. Standard Gaussian noise disrupts this correlation. Motion-adaptive noise aims to preserve this similarity.
- **Residual-based Noise Prior (RNI):** This method initializes noise based on the pixel-level residual between consecutive frames of a source video. By downsampling the residual, noise patterns are made consistent in static regions (where residual is below a threshold) and distinct in moving regions, controlling motion smoothness. The algorithm updates $x_i$ by blending $x_{i-1}$ with $x_i$ based on a residual mask.
- **Flow-based Noise Prior (FNI):** This approach computes optical flow between consecutive frames in pixel space, then downsamples it to the latent space. The flow information is propagated to align subsequent noise latents with the expected motion, resulting in coherent video motion. The algorithm updates $x_i$ by warping $x_{i-1}$ using the latent flow.
These priors are applied to the noisy latents (except the first frame) during the first stage of training, ensuring that the initial noise distribution better reflects the video's underlying motion structure.

#### 3.4. Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning (ST-ReFL)

To further enhance video quality and motion consistency, a second training stage introduces ST-ReFL, an algorithm leveraging multiple reward models.
- **Two-Stage Training:** After initial noise prediction training, the model undergoes ST-ReFL.
- **Reward Models:**
 - **Motion Consistency Rewards:**
 - *Residual-based Motion Reward ($R_{mr}$):* Quantifies motion consistency based on pixel residuals between generated frames and the input video.
 - *Flow-based Motion Reward ($R_{mf}$):* Measures consistency using optical flow differences between generated and input video motion fields.
 The motion loss ($L_{motion}$) is a weighted sum of the negative values of these rewards.
 - **Video Quality Rewards:**
 - *Technical Quality Reward ($R_{qt}$):* Assessed using the MUSIQ metric, focusing on distortions like over-exposure, noise, and blur.
 - *Aesthetic Quality Reward ($R_{qa}$):* Employing a pre-trained reward model based on human ratings (e.g., ImageReward) to evaluate subjective visual appeal.
 The quality loss ($L_{quality}$) is a weighted sum of rectified linear unit (ReLU) functions applied to the difference between upper boundaries ($b_{qt}$, $b_{qa}$) and the respective rewards, aiming to push rewards above these thresholds.
- **Optimization Process (Algorithm 2):** During ST-ReFL, a noisy latent ($x_T$) is sampled, then partially denoised without gradients to an intermediate timestep ($t+1$). This $x_{t+1}$ is then denoised with gradients to $x_t$, from which a prediction of the clean video ($x'_0$) is derived. This predicted video is decoded and scored by the quality and motion reward models. A combined spatio-temporal loss ($L_{ST} = L_{motion} + L_{quality}$) is calculated, and the diffusion model's parameters are updated via gradient descent to maximize these rewards. The process involves optimizing the model by iteratively predicting and evaluating video segments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents comprehensive experiments to evaluate Control-A-Video qualitatively and quantitatively, demonstrating its performance in controllable text-to-video generation.

#### 4.1. Controllable Video Generation

- **Control Map Versatility:** The model successfully generates videos conditioned on various control maps, including depth maps, Canny edge maps, and HED edge maps. Depth maps, providing less structural information, resulted in more diverse video outputs, while edge maps led to videos with enhanced details and structural adherence. For instance, transforming a human into a cartoon robot showed more intricate clothing details when using edge maps compared to depth maps.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art:** Control-A-Video was qualitatively compared against Animatediff, Text2Video-Zero, and VideoComposer, particularly for video editing and style transfer tasks (e.g., transforming a dog running or a camel walking).
 - Animatediff produced consistent subjects but sometimes introduced background noise affecting visual quality.
 - Text2Video-Zero often failed to achieve the correct style and produced blurry subjects.
 - VideoComposer struggled with maintaining object consistency and generating style-aligned content.
 - Control-A-Video consistently produced videos that were described as clearer, more consistent, and accurately aligned with the given text prompt and control maps.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Comparison

The model was evaluated against other end-to-end models using 30 video clips from the Davis dataset, measuring text alignment, semantic consistency, control signal consistency, frame quality, and aesthetic metrics (Table 1).
- **Text Alignment (CLIP prompt score):** Control-A-Video achieved the highest CLIP prompt score (0.290), indicating superior alignment between generated videos and text prompts.
- **Control Signal Consistency (Depth Map Errors):** The model exhibited the lowest depth map errors (0.089), demonstrating better adherence to the input control maps.
- **Frame Quality (MUSIQ, ImageReward, Aesthetic-predictor):**
 - Control-A-Video scored 73.3 on MUSIQ, an improvement of 2.3 points over the previous best, indicating higher objective technical quality.
 - It achieved the highest ImageReward score (1.03), showing enhanced aesthetic quality.
 - Its aesthetic-predictor score (5.73) was comparable to Animatediff (5.79).
- **Semantic Consistency (CLIP frames score):** Control-A-Video achieved a high CLIP frames score (0.959), comparable to the best (Text2video-Zero at 0.964). The paper suggests Text2video-Zero's higher score might be due to its videos having smaller ranges of movement.
- **User Study:** In a user study involving 20 participants, Control-A-Video received the highest average ratings across textual alignment (4.4), consistency (4.5), and visual quality (4.2) on a 1-5 scale.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Motion-adaptive Noise Prior (RNI and FNI):**
 - *Qualitative:* Visualizations (Figure 5) showed that without motion prior, generated videos exhibited flickering and artifacts, particularly in backgrounds. With both optical flow-based (FNI) and residual-based (RNI) priors, videos were more consistent, with stable backgrounds and coherent subject motion.
 - *Quantitative (Table 2):* Both RNI and FNI improved CLIP prompt scores, CLIP frames scores, depth map errors, and ImageReward scores compared to a baseline without motion priors. FNI generally showed slightly better performance than RNI across these metrics, notably increasing ImageReward by 0.194 points over baseline. MUSIQ scores, however, slightly decreased with motion priors. This suggests motion priors prioritize consistency and aesthetic appeal over pure technical quality as measured by MUSIQ in the first stage.
- **Spatial-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning (ST-ReFL):**
 - *Qualitative (Figure 6):* ST-ReFL optimization led to noticeable improvements. For instance, a dog running through poles gained clearer textures and lifelike facial features, and issues like color changes on floors or artifacts on car wheels were reduced, demonstrating improved frame quality and consistency.
 - *Quantitative (Table 3):* Ablating individual reward signals highlighted their specific contributions:
 - Removing the aesthetic reward compromised overall video clip metrics and frame-level aesthetic evaluation (lower ImageReward).
 - Removing the technical reward led to the lowest MUSIQ score, indicating its importance for objective frame quality.
 - Removing the residual reward resulted in worse depth errors and frame quality.
 - Removing the optical flow reward led to a decrease in motion consistency and ImageReward score.
 The full Control-A-Video model, incorporating all reward signals, achieved a balanced performance across all metrics, culminating in the best overall results, with the highest ImageReward score (1.03) and strong performance on other metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, Control-A-Video, that advances the capabilities of controllable Text-to-Video (T2V) diffusion models. The work introduces several strategies that collectively enhance video generation quality, temporal consistency, and adherence to control signals.

The significance of this work lies in:
- **Improved Controllability:** By effectively integrating reference control maps (edge, depth) with text prompts, Control-A-Video offers a finer degree of control over generated video content and motion, which has been a challenge in T2V synthesis. This expands the practical utility of T2V models for specific creative or functional requirements.
- **Enhanced Temporal Consistency:** The proposed motion-adaptive noise priors (residual-based and optical flow-based) represent an innovative approach to infuse motion information directly into the latent space. This mechanism helps preserve the inherent correlation between video frames, leading to a reduction in flickering and improved object consistency across the generated video sequence.
- **Systematic Quality Optimization:** The introduction of Spatio-Temporal Reward Feedback Learning (ST-ReFL) is a notable contribution. By leveraging diverse reward models for both motion consistency and subjective/objective video quality, the framework moves beyond traditional denoising objectives to systematically optimize for attributes highly valued by human perception. This methodology could be transferable to other generative tasks requiring nuanced quality improvements.
- **Efficient Content and Temporal Modeling:** The first-frame condition scheme efficiently disentangles content from temporal dynamics, allowing the model to leverage existing text-to-image knowledge and focus on motion generation. This approach also enables auto-regressive generation, facilitating the creation of longer video sequences.

The potential impact of Control-A-Video is multifaceted:
- **Advancement in Creative Tools:** The enhanced controllability and quality can significantly benefit creative industries, enabling artists, animators, and content creators to generate complex, custom video content more efficiently and with greater precision. This includes applications in video editing, style transfer, and rapid prototyping of visual narratives.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts of motion-adaptive noise priors and reward feedback learning for video diffusion models provide new avenues for research in generative AI. Future work could explore more sophisticated prior formulations, integrate additional reward signals (e.g., related to physics or narrative coherence), or apply similar feedback mechanisms to other complex generative tasks.
- **Accessibility of Video Generation:** By making high-quality, controllable video generation more robust and accessible, Control-A-Video contributes to democratizing advanced video synthesis capabilities.

In summary, Control-A-Video pushes the boundaries of diffusion-based video synthesis by addressing key challenges in quality, consistency, and control through a well-integrated system of content priors, motion priors, and reward-based optimization.
