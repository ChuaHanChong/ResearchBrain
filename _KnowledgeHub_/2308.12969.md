# ROAM: Robust and Object-Aware Motion Generation Using Neural Pose Descriptors

> [!summary] Summary
> ROAM proposes a framework for synthesizing realistic 3D character interactions with novel objects by decomposing the problem into object-aware goal pose generation and goal-driven motion synthesis. The system generalizes to unseen object geometries, reduces training data requirements significantly, and produces motions that users prefer for naturalness and semantic coherence.

## Problem

- Existing scene-aware character animation methods exhibit limited generalization to novel objects that differ from their training data.
- Current automatic approaches for diverse human-object interactions require prohibitively large and costly datasets for training.
- Achieving elaborate and natural-looking character interactions with environmental assets often demands extensive manual effort from professional animators.

## Method

- A **Goal Pose Synthesis (GPS)** module leverages ==SE(3)-equivariant== ==Neural Descriptor Fields (NDF)== and ==Skeletal Pose Descriptors (SPD)== to automatically adapt a character's reference pose to the geometry of unseen target objects.
- A lightweight **Neural State Machine (l-NSM)**, trained on minimal exemplar object interaction data, synthesizes character motion from initial locomotion to the automatically generated object-aware goal pose.
- A ==bidirectional pose blending== mechanism is integrated into l-NSM to enable seamless transitions between unconditional locomotion and goal-conditioned interaction, supporting real-time interactive control.

## Results

- A perceptual user study found ROAM's generated motions to be significantly preferred over state-of-the-art methods (NSM, SAMP, COUCH) for both naturalness and semantic coherence (**p<0.001**).
- ROAM achieved significantly lower Hip Position Error (**1.33**) and Hand Position Error (**4.12**) compared to baseline methods, demonstrating superior accuracy in reaching automatically computed object-aware goal poses.
- The Goal Pose Synthesis module successfully adapted reference poses to unseen chairs and sofas of varying shapes, scales, and orientations, qualitatively showing robustness to diverse object geometries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the problem into object-aware goal pose generation and goal-driven motion synthesis allows for enhanced generalization and significantly reduced data requirements for each sub-task.
> - SE(3)-equivariant neural pose descriptors enable the automatic adaptation of human poses to diverse unseen object geometries, eliminating the need for manual contact point labeling.
> - A combination of a lightweight motion generation network and a bidirectional blending scheme facilitates natural and smooth transitions between general locomotion and specific object interactions, even with minimal training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Data-driven 3D virtual character animation has seen advancements in modeling human motion. Realistic virtual characters are integral to computer animations and immersive applications such as games, movies, and virtual/augmented reality. A common scenario involves virtual characters interacting with various environmental assets, like furniture. Historically, achieving elaborate and natural-looking interactions has often required significant manual effort from professional animators.

The broader research landscape in human motion and scene interaction can be categorized into several areas: human motion and performance capture, scene-agnostic animation, fine-grained grasping and manipulation synthesis, interaction with dynamic objects, and inverse problems related to inferring object states from human interactions. Within scene-aware character animation, a subset of existing works has focused on specific interaction types, such as sitting on a chair.

A persistent challenge in these areas, particularly for methods generating scene-aware character animation, is the lack of generalization to novel objects that differ from those present in the training datasets. Even when trained on extensive motion capture (mocap) datasets with diverse objects and annotated interactions, current automatic approaches often do not perform robustly on objects outside the training distribution. This limitation becomes significant due to the vast number of potential object-motion combinations, making it impractical to collect and annotate datasets covering every possible interaction with every object variant. Previous methods either tend to be constrained by object instances closely resembling those in training data or necessitate large and costly data collections, often involving manual labeling of interaction points.

ROAM addresses this challenge by proposing an alternative strategy designed to reduce the reliance on extensive and diverse human-object interaction datasets while enhancing generalization to unseen objects of the same category. It aims to provide a complete framework for virtual character animation with object interaction capabilities, particularly focusing on robustness to variations in unseen object styles and scales. This work positions itself as a response to the need for a fundamentally new approach to character-environment interaction modeling that circumvents the limitations of prior data-intensive and generalization-restricted methods.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for synthesizing natural 3D character animations that involve scene interactions, with a focus on achieving robustness and generalization to novel scene objects that were not part of the training distribution.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in existing automatic approaches for 3D virtual character motion synthesis:

- **Lack of Generalization:** Current methods, even those trained on large and diverse motion capture datasets with annotated interactions, exhibit limited generalization capabilities when faced with new objects that differ from their training data. For example, a model trained on a specific set of chairs might struggle to generate plausible sitting animations on a chair with a different height, armrest design, or back support.
- **High Data Requirements:** Naïvely training a generalizable neural network to synthesize complex interactions (like sitting or lying) across a wide variety of objects would necessitate collecting and annotating an exponentially large dataset of object-interaction motions. This process is both expensive and laborious, representing a significant barrier to scalability and broad applicability.
- **Manual Intervention:** Elaborate character-object interactions frequently require manual work from professional animators. The goal is to automate this process to reduce production costs and time.
- **Rigidity to Scene Transformations:** Many prior methods are not SE(3)-equivariant, meaning they are sensitive to rigid transformations (translation and rotation) of the object, often requiring manual labeling of goal positions and orientations at inference time. This adds to the manual burden and reduces practical usability.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose ROAM with the following specific objectives:

- **Robustness to Unseen Objects:** Develop a motion model capable of generating plausible object-aware poses and motions for previously unseen objects within the same category (e.g., various chairs and couches) without requiring specific training on those instances.
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** Achieve generalization with minimal training data for paired human-object interaction sequences. Specifically, the method aims to function effectively when trained with motion capture data involving as few as one reference object per category.
- **Automated Goal Pose Generation:** Introduce a mechanism for automatically adapting a reference pose to a novel object's geometry, eliminating the need for manual labeling of contact points or target locations during inference.
- **Seamless Motion Transition:** Synthesize natural character motion that smoothly transitions from locomotion (e.g., walking) to interaction (e.g., sitting or lying down) with the target object.
- **Real-time Control:** Enable interactive character control and high-level goal-conditioned motion synthesis in real-time.

By addressing these points, the research aims to provide a more practical and scalable solution for generating realistic virtual character interactions in diverse digital environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROAM employs a divide-and-conquer strategy, segmenting the problem of virtual character animation with object interaction into two primary stages: Goal Pose Synthesis (GPS) and a lightweight Neural State Machine (l-NSM) for goal-driven motion synthesis. This modular design is intended to reduce the data requirements for each individual task.

#### 3.1. Goal Pose Synthesis (GPS) Module

The GPS module is responsible for adapting a character's reference pose, P, from a known reference object, O, to a target pose, P', on a previously unseen target object, O'. This adaptation accounts for the target object's geometry, which may vary in aspects such as height, armrest type, or back support.

- **Neural Descriptor Fields (NDF):** The core of GPS involves leveraging an implicit feature representation. A neural network, Ψ, is trained to estimate an SE(3)-equivariant descriptor field around a given 3D object. This Ψ is modeled as a continuous coordinate-based MLP that maps a 3D point `x` to a descriptor `z_x`, conditioned on the input object `O`. The architecture of Ψ comprises a PointNet encoder (Φ) to condense the object `O` into a latent code, and an occupancy network (Ω) that uses this latent code and the query point `x` to predict occupancy values. The SE(3)-equivariance of the descriptor is inherent due to the use of VectorNeurons. The descriptor `z_x` is formed by concatenating activations from multiple layers of Ω, capturing hierarchical object-centric boundary information and spatial relationships. The NDF is trained on object-only datasets (e.g., ShapeNet categories of chairs and sofas), removing the need for human-object interaction data at this stage.

- **Skeletal Pose Descriptor (SPD):** To transfer a skeletal pose, the authors introduce a Skeletal Pose Descriptor, Z(O, P). This is constructed by aggregating the neural descriptors Ψ by querying at the 3D kinematic joint positions of the pose F(P) and their sampled neighborhoods (from a Gaussian distribution) to enhance robustness against descriptor quality variations at specific points. Because each joint descriptor `z_x` is SE(3)-equivariant, the aggregated pose descriptor Z also inherits this property, making the method robust to rigid transformations of the object without requiring prior knowledge of its orientation or translation.

- **Goal Pose Optimization:** Given the SPD, the optimal target pose P' for the unseen target object O' is found by formulating an energy minimization problem. The primary data term, E_d, minimizes the discrepancy between Z(O, P) and Z(O', P'). Since the forward kinematics function F(P) is differentiable, gradients can back-propagate into P'. To ensure plausible human poses and prevent unnatural configurations, several regularization terms are added to the objective function:
 - **Angle Constraints (E_a):** Penalizes joint angles that exceed fixed physiological bounds, mitigating unnatural twists.
 - **Floor Penetration (E_f):** Penalizes any joint positions that fall below the ground plane.
 - **Pose Regularization (E_p):** (referred to as E_r in the supplementary material) Ensures the target pose does not drastically deviate from the canonicalized reference pose, helping to avoid local minima and maintain naturalness, especially for symmetric objects where descriptor features might be ambiguous.
 The optimization process is conducted in three sequential stages to address the high-dimensional, non-linear nature of the energy function and avoid sub-optimal results: initially optimizing only root translation and rotation, then jointly optimizing all Degrees of Freedom (DoF) without regularization, and finally activating regularization terms with specific weights.

#### 3.2. Interactive Motion Generation (l-NSM)

Once the GPS module provides the object-aware goal pose P', the l-NSM component synthesizes the character's motion from a starting (e.g., standing) pose to this goal pose.

- **Lightweight Neural State Machine (l-NSM):** This autoregressive network generates idling, walking, sitting, and transitioning motions. Unlike the original Neural State Machine (NSM), l-NSM is "lighter" as it does not rely on environment or interaction sensors, given that the goal pose is already object-aware. It comprises two main encoders:
 - **Frame Encoder (Φ_f):** Encodes the character's current state, including joint positions, rotations, linear joint velocities, root trajectory information (past and future ground-plane projections and 2D orientation vectors), and soft action labels (idle, walking, sitting, standing).
 - **Goal Encoder (Φ_g):** Encodes the target goal pose, including its joint positions, ground-truth goal trajectory and orientations, and one-hot encoded action labels.
 A decoder (Ψ) then takes the concatenated latent codes from both encoders to predict the motion for the next frame, including inverse poses and trajectories relative to the goal coordinate system, floor contact predictions, and gait phase. l-NSM is trained using motion capture data that involves only a single exemplar object per category (e.g., one chair, one sofa).

- **Bidirectional Pose Blending:** To integrate unconditional locomotion and goal-conditioned transitions seamlessly and address instabilities during mode switching, an adapted bidirectional control routine is employed. This mechanism blends ego-centric forward motion and trajectory predictions with estimated goal-centric inverse motion and trajectory predictions. A blending weight, `λ_b`, which is a product of the current interaction action value and a distance-based weight, regulates this blend. This allows l-NSM to generate smooth object interaction and locomotion, supporting both low-level interactive control and high-level goal-conditioned motion synthesis in real-time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ROAM involved numerical comparisons with state-of-the-art methods and a user study, demonstrating its effectiveness in motion plausibility and robustness to unseen object types.

#### 4.1. Perceptual User Study

A perceptual user study was conducted with 50 participants comparing ROAM against NSM, SAMP, and COUCH. Participants were asked to choose their preferred motion sequences based on two criteria: "naturalness of the motion" (Realism) and "agreement of the sitting motion with the chair geometry" (Semantics).

- **Realism and Semantics:** ROAM was preferred over NSM, COUCH, and SAMP by a statistically significant margin (p<0.001 for all three comparisons) in terms of both naturalness and semantic coherence. This indicates that users perceived ROAM's generated motions as more realistic and better adapted to the object's geometry.
- **Baseline Performance:** The study noted that NSM, despite requiring manually labeled goal positions, performed better in user preference for motion generation compared to SAMP and COUCH, which are more recent.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

Quantitative analysis focused on the accuracy with which the motion synthesis module reached the intended target, particularly for unseen objects.

- **Goal Distance Error:** Position Error (PE) metrics were used, specifically Hip PE (distance of the hip joint to the goal position) and Hand PE (distance of hand positions to generated hand contact labels). For ROAM, the hip and hand joints of the automatically computed goal pose P' served as targets.
 - ROAM (with bidirectional blending) achieved significantly lower Hip PE (1.33) compared to NSM (2.01), SAMP (1.86), and COUCH (10.12).
 - ROAM also demonstrated lower Hand PE (4.12) compared to COUCH (10.47).
 - An ablation without bidirectional blending for ROAM showed substantially higher errors (Hip PE 7.30, Hand PE 21.37, Avg PE 14.31), indicating the critical role of this blending scheme.
 - The average per-joint position error (Avg PE) between the goal pose P' and the l-NSM output for ROAM was 2.73, further supporting its accuracy in reaching the full-body goal pose.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples illustrated the ability of the GPS module to adapt reference poses to various target geometries.

- **Pose Adaptation:** The GPS module successfully adapted reference sitting and lying poses to unseen chairs and sofas of different shapes, scales, and orientations. This included chairs with varying back supports, armrests, and overall dimensions, as well as different sofa types for sitting and lying poses.
- **Robustness to Geometry Variation:** An analysis of the difference between the optimized novel pose and the reference pose as the target chair's Chamfer distance from the reference chair increased showed an expected trend. The goal pose's root translation, root orientation, and root-aligned 3D joint positions adjusted significantly when the target geometry deviated considerably from the reference, demonstrating the method's ability to adapt to large variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments were performed to assess the impact of different design choices within the goal pose optimization.

- **Sequential Optimization:** The proposed three-stage sequential optimization routine for the goal pose (rigid transform first, then all DoF, then all DoF with regularization) yielded superior results compared to jointly optimizing all DoF from the start. Joint optimization often led to sub-optimal results and unnatural twists in body joints, particularly when the initial pose orientation was significantly different.
- **Angle Constraints (E_a):** Including angle constraints in the loss function was shown to be critical for generating more natural joint configurations, especially for challenging poses and out-of-distribution chairs, by penalizing physiologically implausible joint angles.
- **Pose Regularizer (E_p):** The pose regularizer, which limits deviation from the canonical reference pose, helped prevent the optimization from converging to implausible poses or getting stuck in local minima, particularly for symmetric objects where descriptor features could lead to non-unique solutions.
- **Point Query Strategy:** Quantitative ablations on point querying strategies for the Skeletal Pose Descriptor indicated that "Bone Sampling" (adding query points around the midpoints of limbs in addition to joint positions) resulted in less penetration during PyBullet simulations. However, the overall success rate of non-penetrating poses was not substantially affected by the specific sampling strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ROAM framework presents several areas of significance and potential impact within the field of 3D virtual character animation and human-computer interaction:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** A core contribution is the demonstrated ability to synthesize robust and plausible human-object interactions that generalize to previously unseen objects of the same category. This addresses a significant limitation of prior methods, which often struggle with out-of-distribution objects or require extensive, specific training data for each object instance.
- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The "divide-and-conquer" strategy, particularly the object-aware goal pose synthesis module, substantially reduces the need for large, diverse, and labor-intensively annotated motion capture datasets. By requiring motion capture data with only a single exemplar object per category, ROAM significantly lowers the cost and effort associated with data acquisition and annotation. This makes high-quality interaction synthesis more accessible.
- **Automated Goal Pose Generation:** The introduction of SE(3)-equivariant neural pose descriptors for automated goal pose generation eliminates the reliance on manual labeling of contact points, goal positions, or orientations at inference time. This automation streamlines the animation pipeline and enhances usability for animators and content creators.
- **Seamless and Natural Motion Synthesis:** The lightweight Neural State Machine (l-NSM) combined with a novel bidirectional pose blending scheme facilitates seamless transitions from locomotion to object interaction. This results in natural and user-preferred motions, as validated by the perceptual study, improving the realism of virtual characters.
- **Real-time Interactive Control:** The l-NSM component supports both low-level interactive control and high-level goal-conditioned motion synthesis in real-time. This capability is valuable for interactive applications such as games, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR), where dynamic and responsive character behavior is essential.
- **Foundation for Scalable Immersive Experiences:** By providing a principled and practical approach to robustly generating human-object interactions with minimal data, ROAM contributes to the creation of more scalable and immersive visual experiences in virtual worlds. It lays groundwork for more sophisticated character behaviors in diverse digital environments without requiring bespoke solutions for every new object.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights avenues for future research, such as integrating dense human body geometry and physics-based constraints to further enhance realism and prevent minor penetrations, as well as expanding the framework to a broader range of interaction types beyond sitting and lying. This potential for extension signifies its foundational impact on future advancements in physics-aware and geometrically precise human-object interaction.
