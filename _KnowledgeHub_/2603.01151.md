# D-REX: Differentiable Real-to-Sim-to-Real Engine for Learning Dexterous Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> D-REX, an engine developed by USC and UC Berkeley researchers, identifies object mass from real-world robot interactions using differentiable physics and Gaussian Splats. This real-to-sim-to-real framework enables training force-aware dexterous grasping policies from human demonstrations, achieving an average 86% success rate on tabletop objects.

## Problem

- Bridging the sim-to-real gap, specifically in accurately modeling physical dynamics (e.g., object mass) within simulations from real-world visual observations and robot interactions.
- Developing robust dexterous manipulation policies from human demonstrations, challenged by the embodiment gap between human and robot hands and the need to account for unobserved forces.
- Lack of scalable methods for creating high-fidelity, physically plausible digital twins of real-world objects for robot learning tasks.

## Method

- A differentiable real-to-sim-to-real engine that reconstructs high-fidelity visual and geometric models using Gaussian Splats and identifies object mass through differentiable physics by minimizing trajectory discrepancies between real and simulated planar pushing tasks.
- A pipeline to transfer human demonstration videos into simulation-executable robot demonstrations by reconstructing human hand-object poses and retargeting them to a robotic hand.
- Training force-aware dexterous grasping policies conditioned on the identified object mass, employing a hybrid position and force control framework to ensure stable grasps under diverse object dynamics.

## Results

- D-REX achieved accurate object mass identification with percentile errors ranging from 4.8% to 12.0% across diverse objects and under 13 grams deviation for objects with varying internal densities.
- Force-aware grasping policies trained with identified mass achieved an average 86% success rate in tabletop grasping across eight diverse objects, outperforming DexGraspNet 2.0 (76%) and Human2Sim2Robot (55%) baselines.
- Policies conditioned on automatically identified mass performed comparably to, or better than, policies using ground-truth mass values, and generalized more robustly to out-of-distribution mass values than domain randomization baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate physical parameter identification, particularly object mass via differentiable physics, is crucial for closing the sim-to-real gap and enabling robust manipulation transfer.
> - Force-aware grasping policies, explicitly conditioned on object mass, exhibit greater stability and higher success rates compared to policies lacking dynamic awareness, especially for objects with varying weights.
> - The integration of photorealistic 3D reconstruction (Gaussian Splats) with differentiable physics engines provides an effective method for automatically creating physically plausible digital twins directly from real-world interaction data.
