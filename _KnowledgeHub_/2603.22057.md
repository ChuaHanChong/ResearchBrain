# SpatialBoost: Enhancing Visual Representation through Language-Guided Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework developed at KAIST, RLWRLD, and NAVER Cloud enhances existing vision encoders with 3D spatial understanding by leveraging linguistic descriptions generated from visual data through a Large Language Model and a dual-channel attention mechanism. The approach improved DINOv3's semantic segmentation mIoU on ADE20K by 3.8% and its average robot task score on CortexBench by 8.0 points.

## Problem

- Vision encoders, primarily trained on 2D data, often lack comprehensive understanding of 3D spatial relationships between objects and environments.
- This deficiency limits their effectiveness in applications requiring 3D spatial awareness, such as 3D scene understanding, monocular depth prediction, and robotic control.
- Existing methods for 3D spatial learning, like multi-view data collection or full multimodal model re-training, are often resource-intensive and lack scalability for generalizable pre-training.

## Method

- A three-stage training framework (==Feature Alignment==, ==Visual Instruction Tuning==, ==Vision Encoder Fine-tuning==) adapts existing pre-trained vision encoders.
- Dense 3D spatial information from 2D images is converted into structured ==linguistic expressions== using specialized vision models and a ==Large Language Model== (LLM).
- A ==dual-channel attention mechanism== is integrated during vision encoder fine-tuning to acquire new spatial knowledge while preserving pre-trained general visual capabilities.

## Results

- Improved DINOv3's semantic segmentation mIoU on ADE20K by **3.8%** (from **55.9%** to **59.7%** with linear probing) and its monocular depth estimation RMSE on NYUd from **0.31** to **0.25**.
- Enhanced vision-based robot learning, with DINOv3's average score on CortexBench increasing by **8.0** points (from **72.8** to **80.8**).
- Achieved significant gains in complex 3D-centric tasks, including a **3.5%**p improvement in DINOv3's SQA3D BLEU-1 score and an increase in Registration Recall at **0.05**m from **86.9%** to **97.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language can effectively convey dense and hierarchical 3D spatial knowledge, serving as a scalable supervision signal for enhancing vision encoders.
> - A multi-turn Chain-of-Thought reasoning process, ranging from pixel-level to object-level and scene-level spatial queries, facilitates comprehensive 3D understanding.
> - The dual-channel attention mechanism provides an efficient method to inject new knowledge into pre-trained models without catastrophic forgetting of existing representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has experienced significant advancements through large-scale pre-trained image representation models, often referred to as vision encoders. These models have demonstrated considerable success across a spectrum of 2D vision tasks, including image classification, semantic segmentation, and vision-language understanding, primarily by extracting transferrable semantic information from extensive 2D image datasets like ImageNet.

However, a prevailing limitation of these models is their training regimen, which predominantly relies on 2D image data. Consequently, they frequently exhibit deficiencies in capturing and reasoning about the inherent 3D spatial relationships between objects and backgrounds present in real-world scenes. This limitation curtails their efficacy in downstream applications that inherently demand 3D spatial awareness, such as nuanced 3D scene understanding, monocular depth prediction, and vision-based robotic control. For instance, vision-language models have been observed to struggle with discerning relative 3D spatial arrangements of objects.

Existing approaches to address this 3D spatial awareness gap include training vision models on multi-view images, which intrinsically encode spatial information. While methods utilizing multi-view data have shown promise in specific areas like robot control, their scalability and broad applicability are often constrained by the necessity for meticulously curated multi-view datasets or data generated from simulation environments. The acquisition of such data for large-scale generalizable pre-training presents substantial practical challenges. Another line of research leverages language as a supervisory signal for learning visual representations. These methods, exemplified by models like CLIP, have primarily focused on improving semantic understanding or object localization within 2D contexts, without explicitly targeting the dense and hierarchical understanding of 3D spatial relationships. Furthermore, many multimodal learning approaches require joint pre-training of vision and language modalities from scratch, incurring considerable computational costs and limiting the efficient adaptation of existing powerful pre-trained vision encoders.

SpatialBoost positions itself within this landscape by proposing a scalable framework that enhances the spatial awareness of *existing* pre-trained vision encoders. It aims to inject 3D spatial knowledge, conveyed through linguistic descriptions, without necessitating extensive multi-view data collection or full re-training of multimodal models. By converting dense 3D spatial information from 2D images into structured linguistic expressions and leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for knowledge injection, SpatialBoost seeks to overcome the aforementioned limitations, offering a more efficient and adaptable pathway to endow vision encoders with robust 3D spatial reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for SpatialBoost stems from the observed limitations of current state-of-the-art pre-trained vision encoders. Despite their high performance on various 2D vision tasks, these models frequently fail to capture the complex 3D spatial relationships that exist between objects and their environments in real-world scenarios. This deficiency is attributed to their predominant training on large-scale 2D image datasets, which inherently lack explicit 3D spatial information.

The lack of 3D spatial awareness in vision encoders manifests as sub-optimal performance in several critical downstream applications:
1. **3D Scene Understanding**: Models struggle to accurately discern the relative positions, orientations, and depths of objects within a 3D scene.
2. **Monocular Depth Prediction**: While some models perform depth estimation, their representations may not fully encode comprehensive geometric understanding.
3. **Vision-based Robotic Control**: Robots relying on these vision encoders may perform poorly in tasks requiring precise manipulation or navigation that depend on a robust understanding of the 3D environment.

To address these limitations, the authors hypothesize that while general 2D vision models may lack explicit 3D understanding, specialized vision models are capable of inferring cues such as object positions and point depths from standard 2D images. This extracted spatial information can then be systematically converted into an explicit, structured representation by leveraging natural language. Language is recognized for its ability to compose information sequentially and hierarchically, a property that can be exploited to construct labels that capture dense spatial relationships within a scene, from fine-grained pixel-level details to broader scene-level arrangements. Furthermore, recent research has indicated the potential of linguistic descriptions as scalable supervision signals for visual representation learning.

Building on this hypothesis, the key objectives of the SpatialBoost framework are:
1. **Enhance Spatial Awareness**: To systematically improve the 3D spatial understanding capabilities of existing pre-trained vision encoders.
2. **Language-Guided Knowledge Injection**: To inject 3D spatial knowledge, expressed in linguistic descriptions, into vision encoders using a Large Language Model (LLM). This involves converting visual 3D information into a textual format that LLMs can process and relate to visual features.
3. **Scalable and Efficient Adaptation**: To provide a scalable training framework that can adapt powerful pre-trained vision encoders without requiring their complete re-training from scratch or the collection of extensive, difficult-to-acquire multi-view datasets.
4. **Preservation of Existing Knowledge**: To introduce a mechanism that allows the vision encoder to acquire new spatial understanding without catastrophically forgetting its pre-trained general visual knowledge. This is crucial for maintaining performance on non-spatial tasks.
5. **Hierarchical Spatial Understanding**: To develop a multi-turn Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process that progressively incorporates dense spatial knowledge, building a hierarchical understanding from pixel-level to object-level and then to scene-level relationships.
6. **Empirical Validation**: To validate the effectiveness of SpatialBoost by applying it to state-of-the-art vision encoders and evaluating performance gains across a diverse range of benchmarks requiring both 3D perception and general vision abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpatialBoost is a three-stage training framework designed to enhance the spatial understanding of pre-trained vision encoders by leveraging language-guided reasoning. The core idea involves converting dense 3D spatial information from 2D images into linguistic expressions, which are then used to inject this spatial knowledge into vision encoders via a Large Language Model (LLM).

#### 3.1. Training Pipeline

The framework integrates a vision encoder (f\_V), a trainable projection module (g\_P), and an LLM (f\_L) into a multi-modal architecture. The training process unfolds in three stages, all utilizing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) loss:

- **Stage 1: Feature Alignment**: The objective is to align the visual feature space of the vision encoder with the textual embedding space of the LLM. The projector `g_P` (implemented as a 2-layer MLP) is trained to map image features `f_V(x)` into the LLM's token embedding space. During this stage, both `f_V` and `f_L` parameters are frozen, with only `g_P` being optimized. This stage contributes to stable vision-language alignment.

- **Stage 2: Visual Instruction Tuning**: This stage extends the alignment to the LLM itself. The vision encoder `f_V` remains frozen, while `g_P` and `f_L` are fine-tuned. This tuning is performed using a combination of existing single-view visual instruction data (e.g., LLaVA-Instruct-158K) and a newly constructed *multi-view VQA dataset*. This enables the LLM and projector to effectively handle questions involving multi-view visual inputs.

- **Stage 3: Vision Encoder Fine-tuning with Dual-Channel Attention**: This is the final stage where the vision encoder `f_V` is fine-tuned to acquire enhanced spatial understanding. The LLM `f_L` parameters are frozen, and `f_V` along with `g_P` are optimized. To prevent catastrophic forgetting of the vision encoder's existing pre-trained knowledge, a **dual-channel attention mechanism** is introduced. For each attention layer `Attn(·)` in `f_V`, an additional attention layer `Attn+(·)` is added. `Attn+` is initialized with the same weights as `Attn`. The outputs of `Attn` and `Attn+` are merged via a learnable mixture factor `α = sigmoid(a)`. During training, only the parameters of `Attn+` and `α` are updated, while the original `Attn` parameters remain frozen. This allows the model to gradually incorporate new spatial knowledge without discarding pre-trained capabilities. The fine-tuning in this stage utilizes a *multi-turn visual spatial reasoning dataset*.

#### 3.2. Dataset Generation for Spatial Knowledge

A crucial aspect of SpatialBoost is the creation of specialized datasets for language-guided spatial reasoning:

- **Multi-view VQA Dataset (for Stage 2)**:
 - **Data Sources**: 3D datasets (ScanNet, Mip-NeRF360, MVImgNet) and egocentric video data (Ego4D).
 - **Image Pair Selection**: Image pairs (x\_i, x\_j) are filtered based on their LPIPS similarity (0.35 ≤ LPIPS(x\_i, x\_j) ≤ 0.65) to ensure a meaningful visual difference without being entirely disparate.
 - **Question Generation**: GPT-4o is employed to generate three types of general multi-view visual questions: common VQA, adversarial VQA, and multi-choice VQA, aimed at enhancing the model's ability to process and answer queries from multiple visual perspectives.

- **Multi-turn Visual Spatial Reasoning Dataset (for Stage 3)**: This dataset is designed to inject dense and hierarchical spatial information using a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning approach across 12 sequential QA turns.
 - **Single-view Data Generation**: 100K images from the SA1B dataset are sampled and filtered using a CLIP-based open-vocabulary classification model to ensure the presence of multiple objects. Specialized vision models like Depth-pro (for metric depth estimation) and image segmentation models are used to extract 3D point clouds from these 2D images.
 - **Multi-view Data Generation**: 200K samples from egocentric video datasets and 3D datasets are filtered using the LPIPS criterion. For these, a 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) is used to extract 3D point clouds. Interpolated views are also added and validated by GPT-4o to expand multi-view configurations.
 - **Hierarchical QA Design**:
 - **Pixel-level (5 turns)**: Focuses on granular geometric information, querying absolute or relative 3D positions of points (e.g., depth values at specific coordinates, comparing depths between two points).
 - **Object-level (4 turns)**: Addresses semantic spatial information of objects, using 3D bounding cubes. Questions include predicting bounding cube coordinates or relative positional relationships between objects (e.g., "Is A to the left of B?"). This level leverages pixel-level information as rationale.
 - **Scene-level (1 turn)**: Requires coherent 3D spatial understanding to predict exact distances between multiple objects (e.g., "How far is A from B?").
 - **General Scene Caption (2 turns)**: Appended after spatial reasoning turns, generated by GPT, to enhance the vision encoder's general knowledge without overfitting to purely spatial features.
 - **LLM for Generation**: GPT-4o is used to synthesize the QA pairs, building sequential answers that facilitate CoT reasoning.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **LLM Backbone**: Qwen-2.0-7B.
- **Projector**: 2-layer MLP, following LLaVA-1.5 architecture.
- **Training Resources**: 4x NVIDIA Tesla A100 GPUs.
- **Hyperparameters**: Learning rates and batch sizes are specified for each stage, with hyperparameter searches conducted for optimal performance.
- **Encoder Backbones**: State-of-the-art vision encoders such as OpenCLIP ViT-G/14, SigLIPv2 ViT-g/16, DINOv2 ViT-g/14, and DINOv3 ViT-7B/16 are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpatialBoost was evaluated extensively across various vision tasks, demonstrating performance enhancements for several state-of-the-art vision encoders.

#### 4.1. Dense Prediction Tasks (Monocular Depth Estimation and Semantic Segmentation)

- **Depth Estimation (NYUd, KITTI)**: SpatialBoost consistently improved geometric understanding. For instance, OpenCLIP's RMSE on NYUd decreased from 0.53 to 0.40 with a linear head, and DINOv3's RMSE decreased from 0.31 to 0.25 (linear head) and 0.25 to 0.21 (DPT head), indicating better depth accuracy (Table 1).
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K, Pascal VOC)**: Semantic spatial understanding also improved. DINOv3's mIoU on ADE20K increased from 55.9% to 59.7% (linear probing) and from 60.3% to 63.1% (multi-scale evaluation), achieving state-of-the-art performance with a 3.8% gain over pre-trained DINOv3 (Table 2).

#### 4.2. Complex 3D-centric Tasks (Lexicon3D Benchmark)

- **Vision-Language Reasoning (ScanQA, SQA3D)**: SpatialBoost led to comprehensive improvements. DINOv3's BLEU-1 score on SQA3D improved from 51.4% to 54.9% (3.5%p gain), and on ScanQA from 40.6% to 43.3% (Table 3).
- **Visual Grounding (ScanRefer)**: Accuracy on ScanRefer increased across all tested encoders. DINOv3 improved from 56.2% to 61.1% (Table 3).
- **Geometric Understanding (Registration Recall, Relative Translation Error)**: Significant gains were observed. DINOv3's Registration Recall at 0.05m improved from 86.9% to 97.5%, and Relative Translation Error decreased from 0.10m to 0.06m (Table 3).
- **3D Semantic Understanding (ScanNet)**: MLLM's ability to interpret 3D semantics improved. OpenCLIP's mIoU dramatically increased from 6.9% to 54.9%, and DINOv3 improved from 69.1% to 70.6% (Table 3). These results indicate effective injection of robust spatial knowledge.

#### 4.3. Vision-based Robot Learning (CortexBench)

- SpatialBoost significantly enhanced robot task performance across diverse locomotion and manipulation tasks. DINOv3's average score on CortexBench increased from 72.8 to 80.8, representing an 8.0 point improvement (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Image Classification and Retrieval Tasks (General Vision Abilities)

- Despite not being explicitly spatial tasks, SpatialBoost improved performance in general vision benchmarks. DINOv3's ImageNet linear probing accuracy increased from 88.4% to 90.2%, and Oxford-Hard image retrieval mAP improved from 60.7 to 64.1 (Table 5). This suggests that spatial enhancement generalizes to improve overall visual representations without overfitting.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Effect of LLM-based Fine-tuning**: LLM-based supervision consistently outperformed pixel-level supervision methods (e.g., linear layers, SAM, VGGT decoders) in improving classification, segmentation, depth estimation, and vision-language reasoning, validating language's effectiveness for dense information transfer (Table 6).
- **Effect of Multi-turn Visual Reasoning**: The hierarchical ordering of reasoning (pixel → object → scene levels) was found to be optimal. Forward ordering yielded better performance compared to shuffled or reverse orderings, indicating the importance of structured reasoning (Table 7).
- **Effect of Single-view and Multi-view Data**: While both single-view and multi-view data independently contributed to performance gains, their combination achieved the highest results across tasks, confirming their complementary nature (Table 7, Table 16 in Appendix).
- **Comparison with Naive Post-training**: Simple fine-tuning with original pre-training objectives or other naive post-training schemes did not yield effective representations, often causing performance degradation, unlike SpatialBoost (Table 8).
- **Effect of Dual-channel Attention Layer**: This mechanism was crucial for preserving pre-trained knowledge while acquiring new spatial understanding. Unlike full fine-tuning or LoRA, dual-channel attention prevented catastrophic forgetting and even enhanced performance on general tasks (Figure 6, Table 17 in Appendix).
- **Dataset Scalability**: Performance improvements consistently scaled with larger dataset sizes, indicating the framework's robust scalability potential (Figure 5, Table 18 in Appendix).
- **Application to Spatial-aware Vision Encoders**: SpatialBoost successfully improved vision encoders (e.g., TIPS, PE-Core) that already possessed strong spatial awareness, demonstrating its complementary nature to existing methods (Table 9).
- **Bias Propagation**: Analysis indicated negligible performance differences between reasoning data generated from vision foundation models (VFM-based) and ground-truth annotations (GT-based), suggesting marginal bias propagation (Table 19 in Appendix).
- **Application to Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLM)**: SpatialBoost also enhanced the visual encoders of MLLMs (e.g., InternVL3, Qwen3-VL), leading to improved classification, segmentation, depth estimation, and VQA performance across various benchmarks (Tables 20, 21 in Appendix).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SpatialBoost framework represents a significant advancement in enhancing visual representation models, particularly by addressing a fundamental limitation of predominantly 2D-trained vision encoders: their lack of comprehensive 3D spatial awareness.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Work

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap**: The core contribution is the development of a scalable and efficient method to inject sophisticated 3D spatial knowledge into existing pre-trained vision encoders. This directly tackles the challenge of enabling models trained on 2D data to understand and reason about 3D space.
2. **Leveraging Language for Dense Spatial Information**: The research effectively demonstrates that natural language, when structured appropriately through a multi-turn Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning process, can serve as a potent and scalable supervision signal for conveying dense and hierarchical 3D spatial information. This approach is more flexible and potentially less resource-intensive than collecting vast quantities of explicitly labeled 3D or multi-view data.
3. **Efficient Knowledge Transfer**: SpatialBoost's three-stage training pipeline, particularly the use of a projection module and selective fine-tuning, offers an efficient method to adapt powerful pre-trained vision encoders without requiring costly re-training from scratch.
4. **Preservation of Generalization**: The innovative dual-channel attention mechanism is a key architectural contribution. It allows the model to learn new spatial capabilities without catastrophically forgetting its extensive pre-trained general visual knowledge. This is evidenced by performance improvements not only on 3D-centric tasks but also on general image classification and retrieval.
5. **Hierarchical Reasoning**: The multi-turn pixel-level, object-level, and scene-level reasoning dataset construction is a novel approach to guide models in building a robust, layered understanding of spatial relationships.
6. **Broad Applicability and Robustness**: The consistent performance gains observed across a diverse array of tasks—including dense prediction, complex 3D scene understanding, vision-based robot learning, and even general image classification and retrieval—underscore the broad applicability and robustness of the enhanced representations. The framework is shown to be complementary to existing spatial-aware vision encoders and effective for improving MLLMs.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

1. **Advancements in Embodied AI**: The significant improvements in vision-based robot learning tasks indicate a direct impact on embodied AI research. Robots equipped with SpatialBoost-enhanced vision encoders could exhibit more precise navigation, manipulation, and interaction capabilities in complex 3D environments.
2. **Enhanced 3D Scene Understanding**: Applications requiring detailed comprehension of 3D environments, such as autonomous driving, augmented reality, virtual reality, and architectural visualization, could benefit from vision encoders capable of robust 3D spatial reasoning.
3. **Improved Multimodal Systems**: The successful application to MLLMs suggests that SpatialBoost can contribute to creating more capable and spatially aware multimodal models, leading to more accurate visual question answering and richer human-AI interaction.
4. **Foundation Model Development**: The work provides a strong foundation for future research into designing and enhancing vision encoders, guiding the development of models that are intrinsically aware of the 3D world.
5. **Reduced Data Annotation Burden**: By leveraging linguistic descriptions generated by LLMs from inferred 3D cues, the framework offers a pathway to reduce the reliance on prohibitively expensive direct 3D ground-truth annotations for scaling spatial learning.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

While the current reliance on existing vision models for generating spatial reasoning data presents a limitation, the authors acknowledge this and encourage future efforts in large-scale ground-truth spatial annotation. Overall, SpatialBoost represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and spatially aware visual AI systems.
