# GS-Agent: Creating 4D Physical Worlds With Generative Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A multi-agent framework, GS-Agent, generates dynamic, physically realistic 4D virtual worlds from natural language descriptions by integrating a physics engine into an iterative code generation and refinement loop. The system demonstrably produces worlds with superior physical plausibility and instruction alignment compared to purely generative video models and other agentic systems, confirmed by quantitative metrics and user studies.

## Problem

- Traditional 4D world creation relies on extensive manual effort and specialized expertise, making it time-consuming and resource-intensive for achieving physical plausibility and dynamic interactions.
- Existing generative foundation models (e.g., text-to-video diffusion) often struggle to maintain physical plausibility, temporal consistency, and fine-grained controllability in dynamic scenes, frequently producing outputs that violate real-world physics.
- Prior agentic systems were primarily focused on static 3D scenes or lacked a deep, integrated mechanism for physically grounded world creation, limiting their application to dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed **GS-Agent**, an end-to-end ==multi-agent framework== comprising a ==Manager Agent==, ==Entity Agent==, and ==Render Agent==, designed to generate realistic, dynamic, and controllable 4D physical worlds from natural language.
- Integrated a physics engine (==Genesis==) directly into an ==iterative feedback loop== where agents generate executable code, execute it, and refine their actions based on multimodal feedback including physics boundary checks, runtime information, and visual output.
- Leveraged specialized agents for task decomposition, 3D asset retrieval/generation, physically plausible material tuning, entity placement and motion control, and comprehensive scene visualization including camera positioning, motion, and lighting.

## Results

- GS-Agent achieved superior physical plausibility, with a State-PIS of **0.83**, and instruction alignment, with an Alignment Score of **32.2**, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art text-to-video models (e.g., Sora-2, Wan2.2) and other agentic systems.
- User studies demonstrated human preference for GS-Agent, with highest mean scores for Physical Plausibility (**4.33**), Camera Controllability (**4.32**), and Content Alignment (**4.70**) on a 5-point Likert scale.
- The system exhibited emergent capabilities such as autonomous error detection and recovery (e.g., programmatically fixing a leaking bathtub asset) and allowed fine-grained manipulation of generated 4D worlds through natural language commands.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding a deterministic physics engine within an agentic generation framework mathematically guarantees physical plausibility and spatiotemporal consistency, overcoming common limitations of purely data-driven generative models.
> - A multi-agent architecture with specialized roles (Manager, Entity, Render) enhances the system's reasoning and execution reliability by effectively decomposing complex world-building tasks.
> - An iterative code-generation and multimodal feedback loop, powered by a large language model backbone, enables autonomous error detection and recovery, along with fine-grained control over dynamic 4D worlds via natural language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of dynamic, physically realistic virtual worlds from natural language descriptions represents a significant area of research with applications spanning embodied AI, autonomous driving, gaming, and film production. Traditionally, the creation of such 4D worlds (3D scenes evolving over time) has relied on extensive manual effort from computer graphics professionals. This process involves meticulous fine-tuning of materials, motions, scene construction, lighting, and camera trajectories, demanding specialized expertise and considerable time.

Recent advancements in generative foundation models, particularly in language understanding, image synthesis, video generation, and 3D/4D scene creation, have offered new avenues for automating content generation. Models like text-to-image and text-to-video diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing visual content from textual prompts. However, these data-driven approaches often encounter limitations when tasked with generating dynamic scenes that maintain physical plausibility, offer precise controllability, or ensure temporal and spatial consistency. They tend to produce visually appealing outputs but may violate fundamental physical laws or struggle with coherent object interactions over time.

A complementary direction, inspired by human creative workflows, involves leveraging the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) to construct agentic systems. These systems can plan, critique, and iteratively refine generation processes. Previous work has explored agentic systems for tasks such as autonomous software engineering (e.g., SWE-Agent) and the crafting of static 3D scenes using tools like Blender.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these fields. GS-Agent differentiates itself by explicitly addressing the challenge of generating *dynamic and physically plausible 4D worlds* by integrating powerful physics engines directly into an agentic, foundation model-based framework. Unlike purely generative models that learn from pixel distributions and may hallucinate physics, or prior agentic systems focused on static 3D scenes, GS-Agent aims to automate the complex process of physically grounded world creation, thereby offering a solution that combines the creative potential of natural language interfaces with the consistency and realism provided by physics simulations. It seeks to bridge the gap between abstract textual commands and concretely simulated, physically consistent virtual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the development of a system capable of creating dynamic and physically realistic 4D worlds directly from natural language descriptions. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations within existing methods:

1. **Ensuring Physical Plausibility and Controllability**: A central motivation stems from the observed shortcomings of current generative foundation models. While these models can synthesize impressive visual content, they frequently struggle with maintaining physical plausibility and offering fine-grained control over dynamic interactions. Videos generated by these models often exhibit phenomena that defy real-world physics, such as objects moving unrealistically, materials behaving inconsistently, or interactions lacking proper energy transfer. GS-Agent seeks to overcome these issues by embedding a physics engine into the generation loop, thus mathematically guaranteeing that simulated interactions adhere to physical laws. This also aims to provide users with precise control over physical parameters and dynamic events, which is typically difficult with purely data-driven models.

2. **Automating Complex World Creation Workflows**: Traditional computer graphics pipelines for creating dynamic 4D worlds are resource-intensive, requiring specialized human experts to perform tasks like 3D asset curation, material parameter tuning, object placement, motion choreography, lighting setup, and camera path design. This manual effort represents a significant barrier to entry for many potential users and slows down content production. GS-Agent is motivated by the desire to automate this entire process by emulating human workflows, where high-level directives are broken down into manageable tasks, delegated to specialized components, and iteratively refined. This automation aims to significantly reduce human effort and expertise requirements.

3. **Broadening Applications in AI and Content Creation**: The ability to rapidly generate diverse and physically realistic 4D worlds has extensive implications across multiple domains:
 - **Embodied AI and Robotics**: Realistic simulation environments are crucial for training and evaluating embodied agents and robots. By providing physically grounded synthetic data, GS-Agent could help narrow the "sim-to-real" gap, enabling agents to learn behaviors in safer and more varied virtual settings before deployment in the physical world.
 - **Autonomous Driving**: Generating complex and realistic driving scenarios, including various weather conditions, traffic dynamics, and unexpected events, can enhance the development and testing of autonomous vehicles.
 - **Gaming and Film Production**: Lowering the technical barrier to creating complex animated scenes and virtual environments could empower content creators, designers, and storytellers. It offers a more accessible way to generate expressive narratives and rich interactive experiences, potentially leading to new creative formats and accelerated production cycles.

By addressing the twin challenges of physical plausibility and controllability through an automated, agentic, and physics-integrated framework, GS-Agent aims to establish a new paradigm for 4D world generation that is both scalable and reliable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GS-Agent operates as an end-to-end multi-agent framework designed to generate realistic, dynamic, and controllable 4D physical worlds from natural language descriptions. Its methodology is inspired by human workflows in creating virtual environments, decomposing the complex task into specialized components and integrating a physics engine into an iterative feedback loop.

**Core Architecture: Multi-Agent Framework with Generative Simulation in the Loop**
The system comprises three specialized agents—the Manager Agent, Entity Agent, and Render Agent—which collaboratively interact with a physics engine (specifically, Genesis) via code. These agents generate and refine executable code, request multimodal feedback (including physics boundary checks, runtime information, images, and videos), and communicate with each other to iteratively construct the 4D world until the natural language goals are satisfied.

**1. Manager Agent**
The Manager Agent serves as the orchestrator of the world-building process:
- **Task Decomposition and Delegation**: It interprets the user's natural language description, reasons about the necessary scene components, and formulates a multi-step plan. It then assigns sub-tasks, one at a time, to either the Entity Agent or the Render Agent based on their expertise.
- **Scene Configuration**: It configures global simulation and visualization options, such as time step, substeps, gravity, solver-specific settings (e.g., Material Point Method (MPM) grid resolution, Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) particle size), and visualization parameters (shadows, background color).
- **Timeline Control**: It regulates the temporal progression of the simulation by advancing or resetting the scene. This allows for probing intermediate states, evaluating modifications, and performing multi-stage rollouts. Upon verification, it initiates the final execution and video capture.

**2. Entity Agent**
The Entity Agent specializes in managing the physical objects within the scene:
- **3D Model Asset Retrieval**: It queries a curated library of 3D assets (e.g., BlenderKit, PolyHaven) using a semantic index built from text and image embeddings. It retrieves top candidates along with metadata like file paths, descriptions, preview images, and bounding box information.
- **3D Model Asset Generation**: If suitable assets cannot be retrieved, the agent attempts to generate them using text-to-3D models (e.g., Meshy). If this fails, it falls back to constructing morphologies using primitive shapes.
- **Entity Placement**: Based on asset metadata and scene constraints, it computes plausible scale, orientation, and position for objects. It ensures objects are placed on surfaces, avoid interpenetration, and maintain adequate spacing. It queries the scene state and requests visual feedback from the Render Agent to correct misalignments or collisions.
- **Physically Plausible Material Tuning**: It initializes material parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio for deformable objects) using common-sense priors and iteratively refines them based on real-time physics feedback from the engine to ensure stable and plausible behavior. Material parameters are also aligned with solver constraints for stability.
- **Entity Motion**: It controls object motions by editing per-step control functions during simulation. This includes PD controllers, direct position/velocity/force commands for rigid bodies, and programmable emitters for particle-based materials, enabling scripting of kinematic trajectories and transient events.

**3. Render Agent**
The Render Agent handles all aspects of scene visualization:
- **Camera Positioning**: It determines camera placement and intrinsic parameters (e.g., field of view) by interpreting natural language directives (e.g., specific viewpoints or framings). It queries the spatial state of entities to position the camera consistently with the requested perspective.
- **Camera Motion and Video Capturing**: It represents camera motion as explicit control code, executed over the simulation, to specify trajectories (e.g., orbits, dolly movements, entity-tracking). Camera updates are synchronized with simulation time steps to ensure temporal coherence. It also configures video recording.
- **Lighting**: It configures illumination by adopting directional lights, environment maps, or local light sources. It adjusts lighting placement and intensity to achieve desired mood or emphasis while maintaining visual and physical coherence and avoiding artifacts.

**Interaction and Feedback Loop**
Agents communicate using a `SendMessage` tool and interact with the physics engine through structured tool interfaces. The entire process is iterative: agents generate code, execute it, receive multimodal feedback (including visual output from the Render Agent), evaluate the results against the natural language description, and refine their actions until the desired 4D world is achieved. The underlying foundation model backbone for the agents is primarily GPT-5, providing the reasoning and code generation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GS-Agent focused on its ability to construct 4D physical worlds that are both physically plausible and align with natural language descriptions, alongside offering fine-grained control. Experiments were conducted using two distinct evaluation suites and compared against various baselines.

**Evaluation Setup:**
- **Evaluation Suites**:
 - **NewtonGen benchmark**: 24 scenes designed to test 12 distinct physical laws (e.g., parabolic motion, deformation).
 - **Complex scenes suite**: 30 novel scenes featuring non-continuous multi-object interactions (e.g., collisions) and dynamic camera controls, designed to assess new capabilities.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Physical Invariance Score (PIS)**: Measures the relative standard deviation of theoretically invariant physical quantities.
 - *Video-PIS*: Computed from video pixels using SAM2 segmentation.
 - *State-PIS*: Directly extracted from the physics engine's ground-truth 3D center-of-mass kinematics, providing a more rigorous measure for agent-simulation frameworks.
 - **Alignment Score**: Assesses fidelity between generated video and text instructions using Perception Encoder feature similarity.
 - **Aesthetic**: Utilizes the VBench aesthetic metric.
 - **User Study**: 15 participants rated videos on a 5-point Likert scale across Physical Plausibility, Camera Controllability, Content Alignment, and Aesthetics.
- **Baselines**:
 - **Text-to-Video Generative Models**: Sora-2 and Wan2.2 (state-of-the-art diffusion models).
 - **Foundation Agents**: SWE-Agent (a software engineering agent) and a variant, SWE-Agent w/ Visual (enhanced with a Vision-Language Model for visual feedback).
- **Implementation**: Genesis served as the physics engine, and GPT-5 was the default foundation model backbone. Videos were rendered at 720p.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **Physical Plausibility**: GS-Agent achieved a Video-PIS of 0.71 and a State-PIS of 0.83, significantly outperforming all baselines. Text-to-video models showed Video-PIS scores between 0.46 and 0.62, while SWE-Agent variants ranged from 0.41 to 0.49 for Video-PIS and 0.44 to 0.57 for State-PIS.
- **Instruction Alignment**: GS-Agent demonstrated the highest Alignment Score of 32.2, compared to baselines ranging from 25.8 to 30.6.
- **Aesthetics**: GS-Agent achieved an Aesthetic score of 47.6. This was competitive with Sora2 (48.5) but lower than Wan2.2 (58.1).
- **User Study**: Human evaluators strongly preferred GS-Agent, awarding it the highest mean scores for Physical Plausibility (4.33), Camera Controllability (4.32), and Content Alignment (4.70). For Aesthetics, GS-Agent (3.86) was rated higher than SWE-Agent variants but slightly lower than Sora2 (4.18).

**Qualitative Results:**
- Text-to-video diffusion models frequently generated visually appealing but physically incorrect scenes (e.g., unrealistic sponge fractures, inaccurate fluid dynamics for a bullet piercing water, or temporal inconsistencies like a static background despite camera rotation).
- SWE-Agent and its visual variant produced physically plausible simulations but often struggled with fully satisfying textual instructions and achieving high-quality rendering.
- GS-Agent consistently balanced physical realism, instruction fidelity, and visual coherence in complex scenarios involving liquids, deformable objects, and rigid bodies with dynamic camera and lighting controls.

**Emergent Capabilities:**
- **Autonomous Error Detection and Recovery**: GS-Agent exhibited the ability to detect and rectify unexpected simulation failures (e.g., a leaking 3D asset bathtub) by programmatically applying fixes (e.g., rigid material patches) and verifying the corrected output without human intervention.
- **Fine-grained Controllability**: The system allowed users to intuitively manipulate generated 4D worlds through natural language commands, such as adjusting camera trajectories, spawning new entities, or precisely tuning physical parameters like object velocity.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Multi-Agent Design**: Consolidating all tool access into a single agent significantly degraded performance (State-PIS dropped from 0.83 to 0.42). This suggests the multi-agent design enhances reasoning and execution reliability by decomposing complex tasks and reducing context distraction.
- **Specialized Agent-Simulator Interface**: Removing the specialized interface (comparing GS-Agent to SWE-Agent w/ Visual) resulted in lower performance (State-PIS decreased from 0.83 to 0.57), indicating the interface's crucial role in abstracting raw simulator operations into semantically meaningful tools for stable and accurate world generation.
- **LLM Backbone Robustness**: GS-Agent demonstrated successful operation across different LLM backbones (gpt-5, gemini-3-pro, Qwen3.5-27B), with performance scaling with the backbone's capabilities, although smaller models incurred an expected penalty.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GS-Agent presents several implications for the fields of generative AI, robotics, and content creation:

**Generation of Multimodal 4D Environments**: A primary significance of GS-Agent is its capability to generate more than just video pixels. The system inherently constructs a rich, multimodal 4D environment that includes metric depth maps, precise segmentation masks, surface normals, and particle-level dynamics. This comprehensive output distinguishes it from purely video-generating models and positions it as a foundation for constructing sophisticated virtual worlds.

**Foundation for Embodied AI and Robotics**: The executable simulation script produced by GS-Agent can be seamlessly integrated with robotic embodiments. This provides a scalable pipeline for generating diverse, physically accurate synthetic data crucial for reinforcement learning and evaluation in robotics and embodied AI. By enabling the creation of controlled and varied training environments, GS-Agent has the potential to accelerate the development of intelligent agents and improve the sim-to-real transferability of learned behaviors.

**Guaranteed Physical and Temporal Consistency**: A critical bottleneck in developing interactive world models is maintaining spatiotemporal consistency over long horizons. GS-Agent addresses this by grounding world construction in procedural code executed by a deterministic physics engine. This approach offers a mathematical guarantee of internal consistency across geometry, motion, and material interactions, providing a robust framework for future controllable world models that can overcome the consistency challenges faced by diffusion-based generative models.

**Democratization of Content Creation**: The framework significantly lowers the technical barrier to creating complex, physically grounded 4D environments. This accessibility can empower independent developers, educators, and creators in industries like gaming and film, reducing the reliance on extensive manual animation and specialized engineering expertise. This could lead to a broader range of creative content and new forms of interactive experiences.

**Advancing World Simulation Paradigms**: GS-Agent is envisioned as a foundational step toward a new paradigm for 4D world generation, bridging generative models, embodied intelligence, and physics-based simulation. This integration suggests a future where interactive, consistent, and large-scale world simulators can be constructed with unprecedented ease and fidelity.

**Limitations and Considerations**:
The paper acknowledges several limitations, including the inherent constraints of current simulation technology regarding fidelity and scalability, and the imperfect cinematic understanding and self-critique capabilities of current foundation models. Issues such as "context contamination" leading to confirmation bias and misattribution of failures in the agent's self-correction process are noted. The potential for the agent to hallucinate non-existent physics engine APIs also represents a challenge. Furthermore, the broader impact section discusses dual-use risks, such as the potential for generating highly credible misinformation or aiding malicious planning, and economic/environmental considerations related to job displacement and computational resource consumption. The authors suggest mitigation strategies like digital provenance (watermarks) and safety guardrails within LLM backbones.
