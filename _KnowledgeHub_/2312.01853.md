# Robot Synesthesia: In-Hand Manipulation with Visuotactile Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Robot Synesthesia performs in-hand object rotation with a dexterous Allegro hand by projecting binary force-sensing-resistor readings into 3D as a tactile point cloud, fused at the input level with camera and proprioception-augmented point clouds and encoded by PointNet. A PPO teacher with oracle state is distilled via behavior cloning plus DAgger into this visuotactile student, which transfers to hardware zero-shot and solves double-ball and three-axis rotation.

## Problem

- Tactile signals are sparse and low-dimensional while vision is dense and high-dimensional, so networks struggle to fuse them into coherent decisions.
- Proprioception-only or binary-touch-only rotation policies lack explicit object information, limiting them to z-axis rotation and convex shapes.
- Prior visuotactile rotation work uses 2D optical-tactile images and needs continuous fingertip contact, restricting it to small objects and weak 3D reasoning.
- Vision and touch each carry their own simulation-to-reality domain gap, and bridging both at once compounds transfer error.

## Method

- **Robot Synesthesia** representation: points sampled from meshes of triggered ==FSR== sensors form a ==tactile point cloud==, merged at ==input level== with camera and kinematics-augmented clouds, one-hot tagged, in palm frame.
- Teacher policy trained by ==PPO== on ==oracle state== (object pose, velocity, ==PointNet== shape embedding, binary contact, joint targets) with a six-term rotation reward penalizing velocity, torque, work, and control error.
- ==Two-stage distillation== into a ==PointNet== student: ==behavior cloning== on 5120k teacher transitions, then ==DAgger== fine-tuning; student runs at 10 Hz and deploys with no real-world fine-tuning.

## Results

- Teacher with oracle state beat proprioception-plus-touch and end-to-end visual RL on all five tasks, e.g. **1045.3** versus **620.9** and **127.8** cumulative reward on double-ball rotation.
- In simulation the synesthesia student led on the hardest tasks: **504.0** versus **344.1** reward on four-way wrench and **407.7** versus **148.6** on double balls.
- Real-world zero-shot transfer gave the largest gains: **22.9** rotations on double balls versus **18.8** for feature-level fusion, and **1.5** versus **0.25** on wrench.
- PointNet critical-point analysis showed **42.7%** of selected points came from tactile points, the rest from fingertips, edges, and palm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing modalities in a shared geometric space at the input beats late feature concatenation, since the encoder can reason about spatial relations directly.
> - Choosing a representation with an intrinsically small domain gap, point clouds and binarized contact, can matter more for transfer than simulation randomization tricks.
> - Learning vision-based control end-to-end by reinforcement learning is data-inefficient; a privileged-state teacher distilled into a perception student is far cheaper.
> - Simulation advantages can understate real gains: fusion methods looked similar in simulation but separated clearly on hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is positioned within the field of dexterous manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in robotic in-hand manipulation that necessitate the integration of visual and tactile feedback. Human interaction with the environment routinely leverages a synergistic combination of vision and touch for complex tasks, a capability robots have historically struggled to replicate.

Previous approaches to dexterous manipulation have included classical control methods, which are limited by their reliance on expert-engineered dynamics models. More recent advancements have utilized deep model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) and imitation learning to improve sample efficiency and naturalness of manipulation behaviors.

A key area of focus in recent research is enabling robots to generalize in-hand manipulation to novel objects. Efforts have explored various sensing modalities:
- Proprioceptive history to infer object position and physical properties, primarily effective for simpler tasks like z-axis rotation.
- Binary tactile signals combined with proprioception, which proved too sparse for detailed geometric attributes or non-convex shapes.
- Depth information and non-parametric policies on combined vision and touch signals to aid object rotation.
- RGB images from optical tactile sensors and depth images, though this often required continuous contact and was limited to smaller objects rotated on fingertips.

The integration of visual and tactile modalities (visuotactile manipulation) is recognized as fundamental for enhancing robot manipulation. Existing methods typically handle this integration in two ways:
- **Representation:** Using RGB images or depth for vision, and raw sensor readings, binary contact vectors, or vision-based tactile images for touch.
- **Fusion Strategy:** Combining modalities at the feature level (separate feature extractors, then concatenation) or at the input level (e.g., when both modalities are represented as images).

This paper identifies two primary hurdles in current visuotactile robot learning:
1. **Distinct Nature of Modalities:** Tactile data is generally sparse and low-dimensional, offering limited contextual information, while visual data is dense, high-dimensional, and rich in environmental detail. Effectively synergizing these distinct data types within a neural network remains a challenge.
2. **Sim-to-Real Transfer Gap:** Transferring visuotactile skills learned in simulation to the real world is complicated by individual domain gaps for each modality, which become compounded when attempting to bridge them concurrently for a combined model.

The presented work directly addresses these challenges by proposing a novel point cloud-based tactile representation, inspired by human tactile-visual synesthesia. This approach aims to unify distinct sensory inputs from the outset into a single 3D space, which contrasts with existing methods that often process modalities separately before fusion or rely on 2D image-based representations. By providing explicit 3D information about object and sensor locations, the authors aim to facilitate more complex 3D spatial reasoning and reduce the Sim-to-Real gap by treating the modalities as an integrated entity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a robot policy that can effectively leverage multi-modal feedback from vision and touch to perform dexterous in-hand manipulation.

The motivation for this work is rooted in the observation that humans effortlessly combine vision and touch for complex manipulation tasks, demonstrating remarkable flexibility and robustness, even in scenarios with partial occlusion. For example, threading a needle involves visual guidance for alignment and tactile feedback for precise thread positioning.

The authors aim to overcome two specific technical challenges that prevent robots from achieving similar levels of visuotactile synergy:

1. **Integrating Distinct Sensory Modalities:** Traditional approaches struggle to combine the inherently different natures of sparse, low-dimensional tactile information (e.g., from Force-Sensing Resistors) and dense, high-dimensional visual data (e.g., from cameras). To address this, the paper proposes "Robot Synesthesia," a novel point cloud-based tactile representation. This method involves "painting" tactile data from FSRs onto a unified 3D space alongside visual point clouds, inspired by human tactile-visual synesthesia where individuals perceive color from touch. This approach is intended to preserve the spatial relationship among robot links, tactile sensors, and the object, thereby offering richer spatial information and facilitating more effective reasoning about robot actions by allowing the robot to "see" its tactile interactions.

2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Transferring skills learned in simulation to the real world is complicated by domain discrepancies for both visual and tactile modalities. The proposed point cloud representation for tactile data, which can be easily generated in both simulated and real environments using robot kinematics, aims to mitigate this. By treating vision and touch as an integrated 3D entity from the beginning, the method seeks to reduce compounding errors during Sim-to-Real transfer.

The research focuses on the challenging task of in-hand object rotation, involving both single and multiple objects, around the x, y, and z axes. The robot is required to interact with a diverse set of objects using visuotactile sensing while simultaneously preventing them from slipping. A particularly emphasized benchmark is the simultaneous rotation of two balls, a task described as highly challenging due to its high degrees of freedom, complex interaction patterns, and the need for accurate visual localization when tactile feedback alone cannot differentiate between the balls. To efficiently train policies for these tasks, the authors employ a teacher-student learning pipeline, where an oracle-based teacher policy trained in simulation guides a visuotactile student policy for real-world deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a comprehensive system design for visuotactile dexterous manipulation, utilizing a specific hardware setup, a novel sensor data representation, and a teacher-student reinforcement learning pipeline.

### 4. System Setup

- **Hardware:** An XArm6 robot arm equipped with a 16-degree-of-freedom Allegro Hand.
- **Tactile Sensing:** 16 Force-Sensing Resistors (FSRs) are attached to the palm and finger links. These sensors provide raw contact signals that are subsequently binarized (0 or 1) based on a predetermined threshold to reduce the Sim-to-Real gap.
- **Visual Sensing:** A Microsoft Azure Kinect camera is positioned to observe the robot hand in both simulated and real environments. The camera provides depth images, which are then converted into point clouds.
- **Proprioception:** The system also utilizes the robot's joint positions (q_t).
- **Simulation Environment:** Isaac Gym serves as the rigid body physics simulator.
- **Control Frequency:** Consistent at 10Hz in both simulation and real-world.

### 5. Tactile-Visual Synesthesia (Visuotactile Representation)

This is the core innovation, aiming to unify distinct tactile and visual modalities into a single 3D spatial representation.
- **Tactile Point Cloud (P_touch_t):** For each FSR that detects contact (binary signal o_t,i = 1), points are sampled from the sensor's mesh to form a tactile-based point cloud.
- **Camera Point Cloud (P_c_t):** Generated from the depth camera.
- **Augmented Point Cloud (P_a_t):** Created by sampling points on the robot's mesh at its current pose, modeling the spatial relationship between the hand and the object.
- **Unified Input:** P_c_t, P_a_t, and P_touch_t are concatenated into a single point cloud (P_t). To distinguish between the sources of these points, a one-hot vector is appended to each point. All point clouds are transformed to the hand's palm frame before being fed into the neural network. The number of sampled points is N_c = 512 for camera, N_a = 8 * n_link (where n_link=21), and N_t = 8 * n_touch (where n_touch is the number of active sensors).

### 6. Problem Formulation (Markov Decision Process)

- **State Space (S):** Comprises the Allegro hand's joint position (q_t), binarized tactile signal (o_t), the target rotation axis (k), the previous joint position target (q_hat_t), and the unified point cloud (P_t = [P_c_t, P_a_t, P_touch_t]). Three historical states are stacked with the current state.
- **Action Space (A):** A 16-dimensional relative control command (a_t) for the robot's joints. A PD controller then drives the robot hand to a new target position (q_hat_t+1 = q_hat_t + a_hat_t), where a_hat_t is an exponentially weighted average of the current and previous actions.
- **Reward Function (R):** A weighted sum of six components designed to encourage robust, stable rotation:
 - `r_rot`: Rewards the object's rotation angle.
 - `r_vel`: Penalizes the object's linear velocity to prevent undesired translation.
 - `r_dist`: Decreases as the distance between the object and fingertips increases, promoting continuous interaction.
 - `r_torq`: Penalizes high joint torques.
 - `r_work`: Penalizes the work performed by the controller.
 - `r_ctrl`: Penalizes the control error between command targets and actual robot motion.
 - An additional, significant penalty is applied if the object falls from the hand.

### 7. Teacher-Student Training Pipeline

This two-stage approach addresses the data inefficiency of training policies with high-dimensional observations directly via RL.
- **Stage 1: Teacher Policy Training:**
 - **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used in simulation.
 - **Input:** Low-dimensional, oracle state information including joint position (q_t), binary tactile signal (o_t), rotation axis (k), previous position target (q_hat_t), object's ground-truth position (x_t), velocity (v_t), angular velocity (w_t), and an object's shape feature embedding (f). For tasks requiring generalization across objects, the shape feature is obtained from a pre-trained PointNet encoder.
 - **Network Architecture:** Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) are used for both policy and value networks, incorporating 3 historical states.
- **Stage 2: Student Policy Training (Distillation):**
 - The teacher policy is distilled into a student policy that uses visuotactile input.
 - **Input:** Joint position (q_t), binary tactile signal (o_t), rotation axis (k), previous position target (q_hat_t) (with 3 historical states), and the unified point cloud (P_t).
 - **Network Architecture:** A PointNet encoder processes the point cloud input, and its latent vector is concatenated with the other inputs before being fed into an MLP.
 - **Distillation Method:** A two-stage process:
 1. **Behavior Cloning (BC):** Initial pre-training using a large dataset (5120k transitions) collected from the teacher policy.
 2. **Dataset Aggregation (DAgger):** Fine-tuning to improve robustness.

### 8. Benchmark Problems and Object Dataset

The policies are evaluated on three distinct in-hand object rotation tasks:
- **Wheel-Wrench Rotation:** Rotating an artificial multi-way wheel wrench along the z-axis, requiring both visual identification of interaction points and tactile feedback.
- **Double-Ball Rotation:** Simultaneously rotating two identical balls around each other along the z-axis, a task emphasizing visual localization due to similar tactile feedback from the balls.
- **Three-Axis Rotation:** Generalization to rotate various artificial objects (cuboids, cylinders, polygons) around x, y, or z axes in simulation, and daily objects of distinct shapes and sizes in the real world.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation was conducted in two stages: simulation-based training and real-world deployment, with a focus on understanding the benefits of visuotactile modalities and the proposed synesthesia approach.

### 10. Simulated RL Training with Different Sensing Capabilities (Teacher Policy)

- **Comparison:** The teacher policy, with access to ground-truth object pose, was compared against a Partially-observable-State (PS/Non-visual RL) policy (using proprioception and contact signals) and a Visual RL policy (trained with visual input from scratch).
- **Results (Table I, Figure 6):** The teacher policy achieved significantly higher Cumulative Rotation Reward (CRR) and Time-to-Fall (TTF) across all benchmark tasks (4-way wrench, double-ball, and multi-object x/y/z rotation). For instance, in 4-way Wrench rotation, the teacher policy had a CRR of 1011.1 and TTF of 47.5 seconds, compared to 440.7 CRR and 22.6 TTF for PS, and 10.9 CRR and 8.1 TTF for Visual RL.
- **Finding:** These results indicate that ground-truth object pose information is crucial for robust and precise manipulation, especially for complex tasks. Furthermore, directly training vision-based policies using RL is data-inefficient and struggles to achieve high performance within comparable training epochs, validating the necessity of the teacher-student pipeline for high-dimensional visuotactile inputs.

### 11. Simulated Imitation Learning with Different Sensing Capabilities (Student Policy)

- **Comparison:** Student policies with various combinations of sensing modalities were evaluated: "Touch" (binary contact only), "Cam+Aug" (camera and augmented point clouds), "Touch+Cam+Aug" (basic fusion), and "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" (the proposed method with tactile point clouds).
- **Results (Table II):**
 - For simpler objects and tasks, visual policies (Cam+Aug, Touch+Cam+Aug) could achieve comparable dexterity.
 - However, for more challenging tasks like 4-way Wrench and Double-Ball Rotation, the "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" policy consistently outperformed all baselines. For example, in Double-Ball Rotation, "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" achieved a CRR of 407.7 and TTF of 17.1 seconds, while "Touch+Cam+Aug" had a CRR of 148.6 and TTF of 9.6 seconds.
- **Finding:** The "Robot Synesthesia" approach, which integrates tactile data into a unified point cloud with visual information, significantly enhances dexterity, particularly for tasks requiring complex 3D spatial reasoning and object interaction. This suggests that explicit spatial representation of tactile interactions improves the policy's ability to reason about actions.

### 12. Real-World Deployment

- **Transferability:** Policies were transferred to a real robot without any fine-tuning.
- **Results (Table III):** The "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" policy demonstrated superior performance in the real world, achieving the highest Cumulative Rotation Angle (CRA) and Time-to-Fall (TTF) across most tasks compared to other visuotactile and non-visual baselines.
 - For Double-Ball Rotation, "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" achieved 22.9 CRA and 36.6 TTF, whereas "Touch+Cam+Aug" resulted in 18.8 CRA and 32.7 TTF, and "Non-visual RL" only managed 0.2 CRA and 28.6 TTF.
 - In the 4-way Wrench task, "Touch+Cam+Aug+Syn" achieved 1.5 CRA and 43.0 TTF, while other visual policies were limited to 0.25 CRA and 60.0 TTF.
- **Generalization:** The policy successfully generalized to novel objects of distinct shapes and sizes in the three-axis rotation task in real-world scenarios.
- **Behavioral Observations:** Policies incorporating vision, particularly with the synesthesia approach, exhibited more cautious and adaptive behaviors, making adjustments to recenter objects. In contrast, policies lacking visual perception tended to execute rigid motion sequences, leading to objects becoming stuck or falling.
- **Finding:** The tactile point cloud representation contributes to a low Sim-to-Real gap, facilitating effective transfer. The integrated visuotactile synesthesia approach provides enhanced robustness and adaptability in real-world complex manipulation tasks, highlighting the benefits of fusing these modalities in a cohesive 3D spatial representation.

### 13. Qualitative Analysis: Visualization of PointNet Intermediates

- **Analysis:** By visualizing the points implicitly selected by the PointNet encoder during evaluation (via its max pooling layer), the authors found that approximately 42.7% of these critical points originated from the tactile-based point cloud. The remaining selected points were predominantly from the fingertips, edges of fingers, and the palm.
- **Finding:** This indicates that the PointNet encoder effectively leverages the "Robot Synesthesia" design, extracting meaningful features not only from visual cues but also from the spatially embedded tactile information crucial for action prediction.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method for integrating visual and tactile sensing, addressing recognized challenges in dexterity and Sim-to-Real transfer.

The primary significance lies in the proposed **"Robot Synesthesia" approach**, which unifies disparate visual and tactile sensory data into a cohesive 3D point cloud representation. This represents a methodological advancement in multi-modal sensor fusion for robotics, moving beyond feature-level concatenation or input-level fusion of similar data types. By explicitly encoding tactile information within the 3D spatial context of the visual scene, the system may derive a richer understanding of contact interactions and object states.

The demonstrated ability to perform **complex in-hand manipulation tasks**, such as the simultaneous rotation of two objects and multi-axis rotation of various novel objects, indicates an enhancement in robotic dexterity. These tasks are typically challenging due to their high degrees of freedom, intricate contact dynamics, and the need for adaptive control. The observed improvements over baselines, particularly in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning and robust contact handling, suggest that the integrated visuotactile sensing system allows for more sophisticated and adaptable manipulation.

A notable impact is the successful **Sim-to-Real transfer** of policies without requiring any real-world fine-tuning. This attribute, attributed to the low domain gap of the proposed tactile point cloud representation, is a crucial step towards deploying learning-based robotic systems in practical applications. It reduces the substantial effort and resources typically associated with real-world data collection and model adaptation, accelerating the development cycle for dexterous manipulation capabilities.

The adoption of a **teacher-student learning pipeline** provides an efficient paradigm for training policies with high-dimensional visuotactile inputs. By leveraging a teacher policy trained with oracle state information in simulation, the data inefficiency often associated with direct reinforcement learning for complex sensor inputs is mitigated. This approach facilitates the acquisition of robust behaviors that can be distilled into a student policy capable of operating with real-world sensor data.

The qualitative analysis, which shows that the policy's point cloud encoder actively selects tactile-based points alongside critical visual points, provides insight into the **interpretability of the learning process**. This suggests that the network is indeed utilizing the integrated tactile information as intended, contributing to its decision-making.

In conclusion, this work offers a novel approach to visuotactile integration that enhances robotic dexterous manipulation capabilities, improves Sim-to-Real transferability, and provides an efficient learning framework. These advancements could contribute to the development of more capable and adaptable robots for a range of applications requiring intricate object interaction, such as manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics. The authors' commitment to releasing their code may also facilitate further research and development in this area.
