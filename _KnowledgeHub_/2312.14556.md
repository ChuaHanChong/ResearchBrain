# CaptainCook4D: A Dataset for Understanding Errors in Procedural Activities

> [!summary] Summary
> CaptainCook4D is an egocentric 4D dataset of 384 recordings (94.5 hours) capturing eight people cooking 24 recipes across 10 real kitchens with HoloLens2 and GoPro RGB, depth, audio and hand/head pose. Task graphs define valid step orders; participants either follow them or induce errors, yielding 5.3K step and 10K fine-grained action annotations over seven error categories. Baselines for error recognition, multi-step localization and procedure learning all score poorly.

## Problem

- Existing procedural video datasets record only correct executions, so systems cannot learn to detect the deviations that actually occur during long step-by-step tasks.
- Error-containing datasets target assembly or chemistry in lab environments, missing real-kitchen cooking where ingredient shape and color change continuously and often irreversibly.
- No prior dataset categorizes error types with descriptions, and few pair synchronized depth, audio and hand-pose with egocentric video for procedural reasoning.

## Method

- Per-recipe ==task graphs== are directed acyclic graphs of step dependencies; sampled topological orders give participants valid execution sequences and define ordering-error ground truth.
- Three error-induction protocols, ==Impromptu==, ==Disordered Steps== and ==Induct Error==, plus dual-layer annotation review, yield seven labelled error categories with per-step descriptions.
- Baselines: **SupervisedER** variants V1 sub-segment MLP, V2 step-level transformer, V3 multimodal transformer; **ZeroShotER** prompt-and-predict VLMs with Llama3-generated category-specific prompt ensembles.

## Results

- Best supervised error recognition reaches **55.39** F1 (Omnivore V2, step split); ImageBind V3 fusing video, audio, depth and text gives **56.02** F1 on the recording split.
- Zero-shot VLMs stay weak: Video-LLaVa **41.8** F1 and TimeChat **46.1** F1 with category prompt ensembles, versus **6.7** and **2.26** F1 for single-prompt variants.
- Localization collapses under errors: Omnivore mAP at **0.1** IoU falls **34.65** to **12.51** on error test sets; procedure learning averages **15.78** precision versus **7.61** random.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal aggregation matters: step-level transformers over sub-segment features beat per-second classification, and adding audio, depth and text lifts error recognition further.
> - Models trained only on correct executions degrade sharply on erroneous ones, so robustness benchmarks need deviation test splits, not just held-out normal data.
> - Current VLMs answer local visual questions but fail when a judgement requires integrating evidence across distant intervals of a long video.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "CaptainCook4D" addresses a crucial gap in the broader research landscape of procedural activity understanding. Existing datasets, while instrumental in advancing the field, largely focus on individuals correctly performing step-by-step tasks. These include widely recognized datasets like Epic-Kitchens, Ego4D, 50 Salads, Breakfast, and Assembly101. While some recent datasets (e.g., Assembly101, HoloAssist, IndustReal) have started to incorporate errors, they typically concentrate on assembly/disassembly tasks involving objects with static shapes and colors.

CaptainCook4D distinguishes itself by focusing on **cooking activities**, a domain characterized by continuous changes in object shape, color, and state (e.g., ingredients being chopped, mixed, cooked, changing form and appearance). This inherent complexity makes error recognition significantly more challenging and offers a rich testbed for AI systems. Furthermore, the dataset features **egocentric 4D data** (synchronized RGB, depth, audio, and spatial information like hand/head poses) collected in **real kitchen environments**, as opposed to simulated or laboratory settings. This multimodal, real-world egocentric perspective is vital for developing robust AI systems that can operate effectively in naturalistic human environments, minimizing occlusions and capturing the user's direct point of view.

The paper positions itself against established tasks like Temporal Action Localization (TAL), Error Recognition, and Procedure Learning. It argues that existing TAL methods need to be robust to deviations. For error recognition, it highlights the unique challenges of cooking, making it valuable for transferability to sensitive domains like medicine or chemistry. For procedure learning, the dataset's longer average step length presents a new challenge for existing algorithms. By offering a diverse range of intentional and unintentional errors with detailed annotations, CaptainCook4D provides an unprecedented resource for developing AI systems capable of not just understanding procedural steps, but critically, identifying and reasoning about deviations and mistakes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "CaptainCook4D" project is to **enhance AI systems' understanding of procedural activities and improve their ability to recognize and anticipate errors**. The authors articulate a pressing need for AI systems that can guide users effectively, especially in tasks where errors can have significant, sometimes detrimental, consequences.

The core motivations behind creating the CaptainCook4D dataset are:

- **Addressing the "error gap" in existing datasets:** Most current datasets for procedural activity understanding showcase correct executions, lacking examples of mistakes. To build AI systems that can identify errors, training data must include both normal and erroneous procedural performances.
- **Capturing realistic procedural complexities:** The chosen domain of cooking activities is inherently complex. It involves diverse ingredients, tools, and actions, often leading to cascading or non-cascading errors that can alter the environment significantly. This complexity provides a challenging yet realistic testbed for AI models.
- **Providing a multimodal and egocentric perspective:** The dataset aims to provide richer information by capturing data from the participant's first-person (egocentric) view using multiple synchronized sensors (RGB, depth, audio, spatial data). This multimodal egocentric perspective is crucial for robust activity understanding, minimizing occlusions, and better inferring user intent and environmental state.
- **Enabling development of transferable AI algorithms:** By focusing on cooking activities with dynamic object properties, the dataset seeks to foster the development of error recognition methods that are easily transferable to other sensitive domains like medical procedures or chemical experiments, where visual cues of state changes are vital.
- **Benchmarking existing methods and spurring new research:** The dataset is specifically designed to benchmark capabilities for error recognition (supervised and zero-shot), multi-step localization, and procedure learning, thereby highlighting limitations of current state-of-the-art approaches and stimulating further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the CaptainCook4D dataset involved a rigorous and well-structured methodology encompassing data collection planning, execution, and extensive annotation.

**Data Collection Planning:**
- **Domain and Task Selection:** Cooking was chosen for its procedural complexity and diverse, predominantly benign, error types. 24 recipes were curated from WikiHow, covering various cuisines and requiring different culinary tools.
- **Task Graphs:** For each recipe, action-centric task graphs (directed acyclic graphs) were constructed. These graphs represent the partial order of steps, where nodes are steps and edges denote dependencies. This structure ensures valid recipe execution orders and aids in error verification.
- **Sensor Selection:** A dual-device strategy was employed:
 - **GoPro Hero 11:** Mounted on the head, it captured high-quality 4K RGB video at 30 fps, providing a wide field of view.
 - **Hololens2:** Used in research mode, it captured RGB frames (360p, 30 fps), depth streams (depth_ahat mode), microphone audio, and spatial data (head and hand tracking, IMU sensor data – accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer). This compensated for the Hololens2's limited RGB field of view and frame rate limitations in research mode.
- **Data Capturing System:** A custom, versatile system was developed with a React JS frontend, Python backend, Firebase database, and Redis message queue. It served as a tablet-based user interface for displaying instructions and recording errors, and as a data-capturing application integrating Hololens2 (via a custom Unity streamer) and GoPro.
- **Participants:** Eight voluntary participants contributed data across 10 different kitchens in the US. They received training on basic culinary skills and recipe knowledge.

**Data Collection Protocol:**
- **Normal Recordings:** Participants followed a pre-constructed topological order of steps from the task graph, aiming for accurate execution. Unintentional errors were later annotated.
- **Error Recordings:** Three strategies were used to induce errors:
 - **Impromptu:** Participants induced errors spontaneously and retrospectively annotated them via a web interface. This captured many unintentional errors.
 - **Disordered Steps:** Participants were given pre-prepared scripts with intentional missing steps or ordering errors.
 - **Induct Error:** Participants used the web interface to script their own errors for a recording, which were then displayed on the tablet for them to follow.
- In total, 384 recordings (94.5 hours) were collected: 173 normal (50.05 hours) and 211 error (44.41 hours).

**Data Annotation:**
- **Dual-Layer Review:** Each video was annotated by the recorder and then reviewed by a second person for accuracy.
- **Coarse-Grained Actions/Steps:** Start and end times for each recipe step were marked using a Label Studio interface. Steps were defined to include pre- and post-conditional actions, making them significantly longer than fine-grained actions.
- **Fine-Grained Actions:** For 20% of the data, a web-based tool leveraging OpenAI's Whisper API (for speech-to-text) was used to provide detailed action annotations.
- **Error Descriptions and Categorization:** Participants categorized induced errors for each step based on guidelines into seven types: Preparation, Measurement, Technique, Timing, Temperature, Missing Steps, and Ordering Errors. These were linked to step annotations.

**Benchmarking:**
The dataset was benchmarked for three main tasks using features from pre-trained video recognition models (3D-ResNet, SlowFast, X3D, VideoMAE, ImageBind) and state-of-the-art VLMs (Video-LLaVa, TimeChat), with various data splits (Environment, Person, Recipes, Recordings, Steps, Recording Type).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The benchmarking experiments on CaptainCook4D revealed significant challenges and insights across procedural understanding tasks:

#### 4.1. Error Recognition

- **Supervised Error Recognition (SupervisedER):**
 - **Architectural Variants:** Three variants (V1: MLP on 1-sec sub-segments; V2: Transformer on all sub-segments per step; V3: Multimodal Transformer) were tested. V2 models consistently outperformed V1, indicating the importance of long-term temporal cues within a step.
 - **Multimodal Advantage:** Incorporating additional modalities (audio, text, depth) into V3 models significantly improved performance, with the full V, A, D, T (Video, Audio, Depth, Text) model achieving up to 70.41% accuracy and 70.41 AUC on the 'Step' split. This highlights the richness of the 4D data.
 - **Backbone Performance:** Omnivore-based models generally performed best, demonstrating the utility of strong pre-trained video features. Omnivore-based V2 models showed similar performance to ImageBind-based V3 models, suggesting robust visual understanding can sometimes compensate for missing modalities.

- **Zero-Shot Error Recognition (ZeroShotER):**
 - **VLM Performance:** State-of-the-art VLMs (Video-LLaVa, TimeChat) struggled significantly. V1 (single general prompt per step) yielded very low F1 scores (e.g., 6.7% for Video-LLaVa). V2 (prompt-ensembling with error category-specific queries) improved F1 scores (e.g., 41.8% for Video-LLaVa, 46.1% for TimeChat) but remained relatively low.
 - **Complexity of Task:** The low scores indicate that VLMs, while adept at short segment interpretation, struggle with long videos, ego-centric views, and complex questions requiring semantic understanding of context, meaning, and error causes (e.g., "Did the person chop a *completely peeled* cucumber?"). This suggests a need for more sophisticated VLM architectures for procedural activity understanding.

- **Error Category Recognition (Zero-Shot):**
 - Performance varied across error categories, with F1 scores remaining low (e.g., Order Error: 22.3% for Video-LLaVa, 9.2% for TimeChat; Measurement Error: 0% for Video-LLaVa, 10.7% for TimeChat). This points to significant class imbalance and difficulty in fine-grained error detection for VLMs.

- **Early Error Recognition (SupervisedEER):**
 - Models trained to recognize errors from only the first half of a step showed a significant drop in performance compared to full-step error recognition. V2 models surprisingly performed worse than V1, suggesting that transformer's context-awareness did not help noisy early signals, reinforcing the task's extreme difficulty.

- **Anomaly Detection:**
 - Self-supervised anomaly detection methods (SSMCTB, SSPCAB) achieved only marginal improvement over random chance (around 50% AUC), underscoring the challenge of identifying errors as anomalies without explicit supervision in this complex domain.

#### 4.2. Multi-Step Localization (MSL)

- **Overall Performance:** Omnivore-based models consistently outperformed other backbones across all data splits (Environment, Person, Recipes), achieving mAP scores up to 48.16% (I_t=0.1) on the 'Person' split.
- **Impact of Feature Segment Length:** Ablation studies showed that using features extracted from longer video sub-segments (3-sec, 4-sec) enhanced performance compared to 1-sec segments, suggesting that more temporal context benefits localization.
- **Generalization Challenges:** Models struggled to generalize when tested on new environments (Environment split), highlighting the impact of environmental variations.
- **Robust Multi-Step Localization (RobustMSL):**
 - When models were trained *only* on normal recordings and evaluated on test sets of normal (T_n) vs. error (T_e) recordings, there was a substantial drop in performance on T_e. For Omnivore, mAP dropped from 34.65% (T_n) to 12.51% (T_e) at I_t=0.1 on the 'Environment' split. This indicates that current MSL methods are not robust to deviations and training on correct procedures alone does not sufficiently prepare them for erroneous activities.

#### 4.3. Procedure Learning

- **Performance vs. Random:** Models trained with self-supervised procedure learning methods (M1: Cycleback Regression loss; M2: Cycleback + Contrastive IDM loss) significantly outperformed the random baseline, demonstrating the feasibility of inferring procedural steps from the dataset (e.g., Average IOU of 5.82% for M2 vs. 2.22% for Random).
- **Comparative Performance:** However, these models scored lower on CaptainCook4D compared to other existing procedure learning datasets. The authors attribute this to the dataset's unique challenge of longer key step durations, requiring algorithms to process and learn from more extended, granular sequences.

In summary, CaptainCook4D successfully benchmarks various procedural understanding tasks, revealing that while multimodal features offer advantages, current AI methods still face significant hurdles in semantically understanding and recognizing errors, especially in complex, real-world egocentric video settings with dynamic object states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CaptainCook4D dataset represents a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision, machine learning, and human-computer interaction, with substantial potential impact in several areas:

- **Advancing Error Understanding in AI Systems:** This dataset is a pioneering effort in providing a comprehensive, multimodal, egocentric dataset specifically designed for understanding errors in real-world procedural activities, particularly in the dynamic domain of cooking. This directly addresses a critical limitation in existing datasets, which largely focus on ideal, error-free performances.
- **Enabling Robust AI for Real-World Guidance:** By offering synchronized 4D data (RGB, depth, audio, spatial information) of both correct and erroneous human actions in natural environments, CaptainCook4D is instrumental in developing robust AI systems capable of:
 - **Proactive Assistance:** Moving beyond simple task recognition to actively monitor user performance, identify deviations, and potentially offer real-time guidance or corrections.
 - **Transferability to High-Stakes Domains:** The lessons learned from handling complex cooking errors, where objects and states change continuously, can be directly applied to more critical applications such as surgical assistance, chemical experiment monitoring, or complex assembly processes in industry, where mistakes can have severe consequences.
 - **Improving Human-Robot Collaboration:** Enabling robots or AI agents to better understand human intent and identify when a human operator is making a mistake, thereby facilitating more effective and safer collaborative task execution.
- **Driving Innovation in Multimodal and Egocentric Vision:** The multimodal nature of the data (synchronized visual, audio, and depth) encourages the development of novel fusion techniques and learning architectures that can leverage diverse sensory inputs for a more holistic understanding of human actions and their context. The egocentric perspective provides unique challenges and opportunities for analyzing first-person interactions, hand-object manipulation, and user focus.
- **Benchmarking and Future Research Catalyst:** The paper thoroughly benchmarks several core tasks (error recognition, multi-step localization, procedure learning), revealing the limitations of current state-of-the-art methods in this challenging setting. This clear identification of gaps is crucial for inspiring new research directions and the development of more sophisticated algorithms. Future work can extend the dataset to other domains, tackle advanced tasks like object state change detection, scene graph generation and anticipation, temporal adaptation, robust learning, and improved action anticipation and planning in procedural videos.
- **Development of Explainable AI (XAI):** The detailed error annotations and categorization can also contribute to the development of XAI systems that can not only detect an error but also explain *why* it occurred and *how* it deviates from the correct procedure, enhancing trust and utility for human users.

In conclusion, CaptainCook4D fills a critical void by providing a realistic, comprehensive, and challenging dataset for error understanding in procedural activities. Its immediate and long-term impact will be in advancing the capabilities of AI systems to offer intelligent, context-aware assistance and guidance in a wide array of real-world human tasks.
