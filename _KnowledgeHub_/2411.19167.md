# HOT3D: Hand and Object Tracking in 3D from Egocentric Multi-View Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> A new dataset, HOT3D, provides over 833 minutes of egocentric, multi-view, hardware-synchronized videos of hands and objects with high-fidelity 3D ground truth. Experiments show multi-view approaches significantly improve accuracy for 3D hand pose tracking and 6DoF object pose estimation compared to single-view methods.

## Problem

- A lack of large-scale, high-fidelity datasets for egocentric, multi-view 3D hand and object tracking.
- Existing datasets often provide exocentric views, lack hardware synchronization for multi-view data, or have less precise 3D ground truth.
- Challenges in robustly tracking both hands and objects in 3D from a first-person perspective, hindering advancements in AR/VR applications.

## Method

- Developed HOT3D, a dataset featuring over 833 minutes of egocentric, multi-view videos captured from Meta's Project Aria and Quest 3 headsets.
- Annotated high-quality 3D ground truth for hand and object poses using a precise passive marker-based motion-capture system.
- Conducted experiments demonstrating the utility of the multi-view data for 3D hand pose tracking, 6DoF object pose estimation, and 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects.

## Results

- 3D hand pose tracking showed a 41% relative improvement in Mean Keypoint Position Error when using two-view input compared to single-view.
- 6DoF object pose estimation saw recall rates improve by 8-12 percentage points (13-34% relative gain) with multi-view input compared to single-view.
- For 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects, the stereo matching method achieved significantly higher recall (e.g., 64.4% at 5cm) than monocular depth estimation (e.g., 14.3% at 5cm).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view egocentric data consistently provides superior accuracy and robustness compared to single-view data for 3D hand and object tracking tasks.
> - Fusing information from synchronized multiple cameras aids 3D reasoning, helps overcome domain gaps in training data, and handles occlusions more effectively.
> - Precise 3D localization of unknown in-hand objects is achievable through stereo matching of visual features, outperforming monocular depth-based approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has long pursued the understanding of human-object interaction, driven by applications ranging from robotics to skill transfer and AR/VR. This paper specifically addresses a critical gap in existing datasets for **egocentric, multi-view 3D hand and object tracking**.

Previous research on 3D hand pose estimation has evolved from RGB-D to RGB/monochrome images, with datasets like NYU, MSRA, and more recently, Stereo and UmeTrack. Similarly, 6DoF object pose estimation has progressed from RGB-D (LM, YCB-V, T-LESS) to RGB-only images (OnePose). While many datasets exist for either hands or objects, or even generic hand-object interactions with 2D annotations, robust 3D tracking of *both* hands and objects, especially from the first-person perspective, remains challenging.

The paper extensively reviews "Datasets with hands and objects" and meticulously positions HOT3D among them, highlighting its unique contributions (Table 1):
- **Egocentric Perspective:** Many existing datasets (e.g., DexYCB, HO-3D, H2O-3D) are exocentric (third-person view), while egocentric data is crucial for AR/VR. Datasets like H2O and HOI4D provide egocentric RGB-D but often lack multi-view synchronization or precise ground truth.
- **Multi-View and Hardware-Synchronized:** HOT3D is the *first* large-scale dataset offering multi-view, hardware time-synchronized egocentric videos captured with real headsets. This is a crucial distinction, as current AR/VR devices commonly feature multiple cameras, and leveraging multi-view data is essential for robust 3D perception without power-intensive active depth sensors. Other multi-view datasets like H2O use RGB-D or are not hardware-synchronized.
- **Real Headsets:** Unlike datasets that use helmet-mounted cameras or mock-ups (e.g., ARCTIC, H2O), HOT3D uses real, commercially available (Quest 3) and research prototype (Project Aria) headsets from Meta, making the data highly representative of future AR/VR deployments. HO-Cap is another dataset recorded with a real headset (HoloLens), but its annotations are obtained from RGB-D, potentially lower accuracy.
- **High-Quality Ground Truth:** HOT3D leverages a precise motion-capture system (OptiTrack) for ground-truth 3D poses of hands and objects, offering a level of accuracy comparable to ARTIC. Many other datasets rely on optimization procedures from RGB-D (e.g., HO-3D, DexYCB), which can be less accurate.
- **Diverse Interactions and Objects:** The dataset includes non-trivial hand-object interactions beyond simple pick-up/put-down, simulating kitchen, office, and living room scenarios with 33 diverse rigid objects and 19 subjects. This expands beyond datasets focusing on specific object sets (e.g., YCB objects) or limited interaction types (e.g., static grasps in ContactPose, DexYCB).

In essence, HOT3D addresses a significant void by providing a large-scale, high-fidelity, egocentric, multi-view dataset that directly supports the development of perception systems for next-generation AR/VR devices and contextual AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is the **introduction and public release of HOT3D**, a novel dataset for egocentric hand and object tracking in 3D. The authors outline several specific motivations and goals for creating this resource:

- **Accelerate Computer Vision Research:** The fundamental motivation is to provide a robust benchmark and training resource to advance the state-of-the-art in understanding hand-object interaction. Existing datasets have limitations in terms of egocentric perspective, multi-view synchronization, ground-truth quality, or scenario diversity, hindering progress in real-world AR/VR applications.
- **Enable Multi-View Egocentric Vision:** A key aim is to specifically enable and benchmark methods that leverage multi-view and temporal information from egocentric cameras. The authors highlight that while multi-view data is common on AR/VR devices, its effectiveness for hand and object tracking has been largely unexplored.
- **Support AR/VR and Contextual AI Applications:** The broader goal is to facilitate the development of vision-based systems that can accurately capture 3D motion, shape, and contact of hands and objects. Such systems are crucial for applications like:
 - Transferring manual skills (human-to-human via AR glasses, human-to-robot).
 - Enabling AI assistants to better understand user context and actions.
 - Developing new input capabilities for AR/VR (e.g., turning physical surfaces into virtual keyboards, physical objects into multi-functional tools).
- **Provide High-Quality Ground Truth:** To ensure reliable benchmarking, the dataset prioritizes accurate ground-truth 3D poses of hands and objects, obtained via a motion-capture system, along with high-resolution 3D object models with PBR materials.
- **Facilitate Diverse Vision Tasks:** While primarily for 3D tracking, HOT3D is designed to support a range of tasks, including 3D hand tracking, model-based 6DoF object pose estimation, 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects, CAD-free tracking via object onboarding sequences, 3D object reconstruction, and 2D detection/segmentation of hand-object interactions. It also encourages research leveraging eye gaze information for intent prediction or foveated sensing.

In summary, the paper aims to provide the foundational data necessary to bridge the gap between academic research and practical, robust egocentric hand-object interaction understanding for future AR/VR and AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around the meticulous **collection and annotation of the HOT3D dataset**, followed by **demonstrative experiments** to showcase its utility.

#### 3.1. HOT3D Dataset Collection

- **Hardware:** Recordings were made using two Meta headsets:
 - **Project Aria:** A research prototype of AI glasses, providing one RGB (1408x1408) and two monochrome (640x480) image streams, 3D scene point clouds (from SLAM), and eye gaze signal. All cameras have fisheye lenses.
 - **Quest 3:** A commercial VR headset, providing two monochrome (1280x1024) image streams from its front cameras.
 - All image streams are hardware-synchronized and recorded at 30 fps.
- **Subjects and Objects:** 19 diverse subjects participated, interacting with 33 diverse rigid objects (household, office items). Hand shapes for each subject were scanned and provided in UmeTrack and MANO formats. Objects were scanned to create high-resolution 3D mesh models with PBR materials.
- **Scenarios:** Beyond simple pick-up/observe/put-down actions, subjects performed actions typical for kitchen, office, and living room environments. These scenarios were captured in a lab setting where lighting, furniture, and decorations were regularly randomized to increase diversity.
- **Scale:** The dataset comprises over 833 minutes of recordings (3.7M+ images from 1.5M+ multi-view frames).
- **Ground-Truth Annotation:** This is a crucial aspect. High-quality 3D poses of hands and objects were obtained using a **passive marker-based motion-capture (Mocap) system** (OptiTrack cameras) in a dedicated lab. Small (3mm) optical markers were attached to hands (19 per hand) and objects (around 10 per object). Hand poses were fitted to UmeTrack/MANO models, and object poses were derived by aligning tracked markers to their registered 3D model locations. Mocap data was 7DoF-aligned with headset SLAM output for Aria.
- **Data Splits:** The dataset is split into training (13 subjects, 1M frames) and test (6 subjects, 0.5M frames) sets. Ground-truth annotations for the test set are held back for dedicated evaluation servers.
- **Curated Clips:** 3832 5-second (150-frame) clips are provided for focused benchmarking, ensuring quality annotations and visibility of hands/objects.
- **Object Onboarding Sequences:** Specific sequences are included for each object to enable model-free object tracking or 3D reconstruction, showing static objects or objects manipulated by hands.

#### 3.2. Experimental Approach (Baselines and Evaluation)

The authors demonstrate the dataset's utility by evaluating multi-view methods against single-view counterparts for three representative tasks:

1. **3D Hand Pose Tracking:**
 - **Method:** UmeTrack, a state-of-the-art hand tracker.
 - **Setup:** Trained on UmeTrack dataset, HOT3D-Quest3 training sequences, or a combination. Evaluated in both single-view and two-view modes. Input includes ground-truth 2D hand bounding boxes.
 - **Metric:** Mean Keypoint Position Error (MKPE).
2. **6DoF Object Pose Estimation:**
 - **Method:** FoundPose, a training-free method using DINOv2 features, extended by the authors for multi-view input.
 - **Setup:** Objects "onboarded" with CAD models. Evaluated on every 30th frame of test HOT3D clips (Aria and Quest 3). Input includes ground-truth 2D object segmentation masks.
 - **Metric:** Recall rate (fraction of correct poses where translational and rotational errors are below thresholds like 5cm/5°, 10cm/10°, 20cm/20°). The multi-view extension uses generalized PnP.
3. **3D Lifting of Unknown In-Hand Objects:**
 - **Methods:** Three baselines developed by the authors:
 - **HandProxy:** Uses ground-truth 3D palm center as object location.
 - **MonoDepth:** Based on monocular depth prediction (Depth Anything V2) and SLAM point clouds (Aria only).
 - **StereoMatch:** Leverages DINOv2 feature matching between stereo images for 3D triangulation.
 - **Setup:** Evaluated with both ground-truth and predicted 2D segmentation masks (from Mask R-CNN trained on an in-house dataset).
 - **Metric:** Recall rate (fraction of correct locations where offset from ground-truth 3D object bounding box center is below thresholds like 5cm, 10cm, etc.).

The experiments primarily focus on showing the advantage of multi-view data, which is uniquely enabled by HOT3D's synchronized camera streams.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments consistently demonstrate the superior performance of multi-view methods over their single-view counterparts across all evaluated tasks, validating the core premise of the HOT3D dataset.

1. **3D Hand Pose Tracking (UmeTrack):**
 - Training on a combination of UmeTrack (Quest 2 data) and HOT3D-Quest3 data significantly improves generalization and accuracy.
 - The most striking result is the **41% relative improvement** in MKPE when using the two-view mode compared to single-view, after training on both datasets (e.g., MKPE on HOT3D-Quest3 dropped from 15.4mm (single-view) to 10.9mm (two-view)).
 - This highlights that multi-view input not only offers robustness but also helps overcome domain gaps between datasets recorded with different camera configurations.

2. **6DoF Object Pose Estimation (FoundPose):**
 - The proposed multi-view extension of FoundPose (using 2-3 views depending on the headset) **significantly outperforms** the original single-view version.
 - Recall rates improved by **8-12 percentage points** (absolute improvement), translating to a **13-34% relative improvement**. For example, on HOT3D-Aria, recall at 5cm, 5° increased from 25.2% (single-view) to 33.8% (three-view). On HOT3D-Quest3, it increased from 28.9% (single-view) to 36.9% (two-view).
 - The authors attribute this to additional constraints for 3D reasoning and better handling of object occlusions from multiple viewpoints. The generalization ability of DINOv2 across different camera types (RGB vs. monochrome, different resolutions) was also noted as a key factor.

3. **2D Segmentation of In-Hand Objects:**
 - Off-the-shelf EgoHOS showed a noticeable accuracy decline on HOT3D due to domain gap.
 - Their Mask R-CNN baseline trained on an in-house dataset performed better. Crucially, the **MRCNN-DA (using predicted depth from Depth Anything V2)** was the top performer, achieving significantly higher mIoU scores (e.g., 55.2% on Aria vs. 42.6% for EgoHOS, and 54.7% on Quest3 vs. 33.1% for EgoHOS).
 - This suggests that incorporating depth information, even predicted, greatly aids in disambiguating in-hand objects from the background, which is a prerequisite for 3D lifting.

4. **3D Lifting of In-Hand Objects:**
 - The **StereoMatch method consistently outperformed MonoDepth** and the HandProxy baseline, especially at stricter accuracy thresholds.
 - With ground-truth 2D masks, StereoMatch achieved recall rates like 64.4% (Aria, 5cm threshold) and 76.4% (Quest 3, 5cm threshold), significantly higher than MonoDepth's 14.3% (Aria, 5cm threshold).
 - Even when using predicted 2D masks (from MRCNN-DA), StereoMatch maintained its lead (e.g., 42.6% recall on Aria vs. 11.1% for MonoDepth at 5cm).
 - The HandProxy baseline, while reaching high recall at very lenient thresholds (e.g., 98.4% at 30cm for Aria), performed poorly at tighter thresholds (0.5% at 5cm), indicating that a dedicated 3D lifting method is necessary for fine-grained localization.
 - The 3D localization error of MonoDepth was primarily along the optical axis, highlighting the challenges of monocular depth estimation for precise 3D object localization.

These findings strongly support the value of multi-view egocentric data for robust 3D perception tasks critical for AR/VR and contextual AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOT3D dataset and the accompanying analysis hold significant implications for the future of computer vision, human-computer interaction, and the development of immersive technologies.

- **Enabling Future AR/VR Experiences:** The most immediate impact is on AR/VR. Accurate, real-time 3D understanding of hands and objects from an egocentric perspective is foundational for natural user interfaces, intuitive object manipulation in mixed reality, and context-aware AI. HOT3D provides the essential, high-quality data to train and evaluate the perception models needed for these advanced capabilities on devices like Meta Quest and Project Aria.
- **Driving Multi-View Egocentric Vision Research:** By explicitly demonstrating the superior performance of multi-view methods, HOT3D validates a crucial research direction. This encourages researchers to develop more sophisticated algorithms that effectively fuse information from multiple synchronized camera streams, moving beyond the limitations of monocular vision or sparse sensor setups. This is particularly relevant for power-constrained wearable devices where active depth sensors are often impractical.
- **Standardized Benchmark:** The public release of a large-scale, meticulously annotated dataset, coupled with established evaluation metrics and strong baselines, creates a new standard benchmark for various tasks related to hand-object interaction. This will foster reproducible research, enable fair comparisons between different methods, and accelerate innovation in the field. The co-organization of public challenges (e.g., BOP Challenge 2024, Hand Tracking Challenge at ECCV 2024) further amplifies its role as a benchmark.
- **Advancing Contextual AI:** The dataset's inclusion of diverse everyday scenarios (kitchen, office, living room) and multi-modal data (eye gaze, SLAM point clouds from Aria) opens avenues for research into "contextual AI." Understanding user intent, predicting actions, and providing intelligent assistance requires not just recognizing objects and hands, but understanding their interaction within a meaningful environment.
- **Facilitating Skill Transfer and Robotics:** For robotics, HOT3D can aid in teaching robots dexterous manipulation skills by observing human demonstrations. For human skill transfer, it can enable AR guidance systems for complex tasks (e.g., assembly, sports techniques) by capturing and replaying expert movements.
- **Broader Impact on 3D Perception:** Beyond hands and objects, the principles and data from HOT3D could inspire similar multi-view egocentric datasets for other types of scene understanding, 3D reconstruction, and interaction analysis. The inclusion of PBR materials for object models also aids in generating more photorealistic synthetic training data, further closing the reality gap.

In conclusion, HOT3D is more than just a dataset; it is a strategic research artifact that addresses a critical need in egocentric 3D perception. Its quality, scale, and multi-view nature position it as a foundational resource that will significantly influence the development of next-generation AR/VR experiences and intelligent systems capable of understanding and interacting with the physical world from a human perspective.
