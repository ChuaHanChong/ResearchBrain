# HOT3D: Hand and Object Tracking in 3D from Egocentric Multi-View Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> A new dataset, HOT3D, provides over 833 minutes of egocentric, multi-view, hardware-synchronized videos of hands and objects with high-fidelity 3D ground truth. Experiments show multi-view approaches significantly improve accuracy for 3D hand pose tracking and 6DoF object pose estimation compared to single-view methods.

## Problem

- A lack of large-scale, high-fidelity datasets for egocentric, multi-view 3D hand and object tracking.
- Existing datasets often provide exocentric views, lack hardware synchronization for multi-view data, or have less precise 3D ground truth.
- Challenges in robustly tracking both hands and objects in 3D from a first-person perspective, hindering advancements in AR/VR applications.

## Method

- Developed HOT3D, a dataset featuring over 833 minutes of egocentric, multi-view videos captured from Meta's Project Aria and Quest 3 headsets.
- Annotated high-quality 3D ground truth for hand and object poses using a precise passive marker-based motion-capture system.
- Conducted experiments demonstrating the utility of the multi-view data for 3D hand pose tracking, 6DoF object pose estimation, and 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects.

## Results

- 3D hand pose tracking showed a 41% relative improvement in Mean Keypoint Position Error when using two-view input compared to single-view.
- 6DoF object pose estimation saw recall rates improve by 8-12 percentage points (13-34% relative gain) with multi-view input compared to single-view.
- For 3D lifting of unknown in-hand objects, the stereo matching method achieved significantly higher recall (e.g., 64.4% at 5cm) than monocular depth estimation (e.g., 14.3% at 5cm).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view egocentric data consistently provides superior accuracy and robustness compared to single-view data for 3D hand and object tracking tasks.
> - Fusing information from synchronized multiple cameras aids 3D reasoning, helps overcome domain gaps in training data, and handles occlusions more effectively.
> - Precise 3D localization of unknown in-hand objects is achievable through stereo matching of visual features, outperforming monocular depth-based approaches.
