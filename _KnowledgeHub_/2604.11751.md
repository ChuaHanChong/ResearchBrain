# Grounded World Model for Semantically Generalizable Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> A planning system called Grounded World Model-Model Predictive Control (GWM-MPC) enables robots to execute tasks specified by natural language instructions with enhanced semantic generalizability by learning a world model within a frozen, vision-language-aligned latent space. It achieves an 87% success rate on a new semantic generalization benchmark, WISER, significantly outperforming prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor Model Predictive Control (MPC) systems rely on specific goal images, which are difficult to obtain for novel tasks and lack flexible user interaction.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models fine-tuning large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from 'knowledge forgetting,' leading to poor semantic generalization to unseen instructions or visual inputs.
- Current benchmarks for VLAs lack comprehensive evaluation of true semantic generalizability, often focusing on visual interference rather than genuinely novel semantic challenges.

## Method

- A ==Grounded World Model (GWM)== is learned to predict future outcomes within a ==frozen latent space== of a pretrained multimodal retrieval model (==Qwen3-VL-Embedding==), enabling language-based goal specification for ==MPC==.
- A novel ==Rendering-based Action Tokenization (RAT)== method represents robot joint actions as images, allowing a single vision encoder to process both observations and actions in an ==embodiment-agnostic== manner.
- The **GWM-MPC** framework employs ==KNN== for efficient trajectory proposal and selects optimal actions by maximizing ==cosine similarity== between predicted future outcome embeddings and language-encoded goal embeddings.

## Results

- GWM-MPC achieved an **87%** success rate on the WISER benchmark's test set (tasks with unseen semantics), substantially outperforming the average VLA success rate of **22%** and the best VLA's **47%**.
- The GWM, trained solely on Panda robot data, demonstrated zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization by achieving an **83%** success rate on test tasks when controlling an xArm6 robot.
- Ablation studies showed that the Rendering-based Action Tokenization (RAT) was crucial for generalizability, and that a learned action encoder (GWM-MPC-AC) resulted in poor semantic generalization similar to other VLAs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling action generation from semantic understanding by operating in a frozen, VLM-aligned latent space prevents 'knowledge forgetting' and allows for robust semantic generalization.
> - Rendering robot actions into visual tokens (RAT) enables zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization and aligns action representations with visual observations for effective world model learning.
> - Decomposing natural language task instructions into sub-prompts for scoring significantly boosts performance and leverages the compositional language understanding of the foundation model, unlike some end-to-end VLAs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within visuomotor Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly concerning semantic generalization and goal specification.

**World Models and Model Predictive Control (MPC):** World models are functions that predict future outcomes given a current state and a sequence of actions. They enable agents to understand, predict, and plan within environments. Planning with world models is typically achieved through MPC, where candidate trajectories are proposed, their future outcomes predicted, and the optimal trajectory selected based on a cost function. Modern world models are often trained with videos to capture realistic physics and dynamics. While some operate in pixel space, latent world models (e.g., DINO-WM, JEPA-WM) are gaining prominence for visuomotor planning as they bypass the computational expense of pixel reconstruction. In these latent models, the cost function for MPC is commonly a Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the embedding of a predicted future state and a goal image.

**Challenges with Existing Approaches:**
1. **Goal Image Dependency:** A primary limitation of current latent world models in visuomotor MPC is the reliance on a goal image. Obtaining a specific goal image can be challenging, particularly for novel tasks or environments where no prior demonstrations are available.
2. **Limited Interactivity:** Specifying goals via images offers limited flexibility and interactivity compared to natural language instructions, which are more intuitive for human operators.
3. **Semantic Generalization in VLAs:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models aim to leverage pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to achieve semantic generalization, enabling policies to perform tasks even with unseen instructions or visual inputs. However, fine-tuning VLMs for robotic control often leads to "knowledge forgetting," where the model loses its broad multimodal understanding, resulting in poor generalization to novel, yet solvable, tasks. Existing VLAs frequently exhibit high performance on training data but a significant drop in success rate on out-of-distribution test sets. Furthermore, current benchmarks often lack comprehensive evaluation for semantic generalizability, focusing more on visual interference or trivial object pose changes rather than genuinely novel semantic challenges.

This paper positions itself by proposing a method that addresses these challenges: enabling language-based goal specification in MPC through a world model trained in a vision-language-aligned latent space, thereby enhancing semantic generalizability beyond current VLA capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a planning system that achieves robust semantic generalization and allows for task specification through natural language, overcoming limitations observed in existing visuomotor MPC and VLA paradigms.

The detailed motivations are:
1. **Enabling Language-Based Goal Specification for MPC:** Current latent world models in MPC primarily use goal images to define tasks. This approach is impractical for many real-world scenarios due to the difficulty of obtaining precise goal images for novel tasks and its lack of user-friendliness. The authors aim to replace this image-based goal definition with natural language instructions, providing a more intuitive and flexible interface for human-robot interaction.
2. **Improving Semantic Generalizability:** Traditional VLA models, which fine-tune large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), frequently suffer from "knowledge forgetting." This phenomenon causes models to perform well on training data but fail to generalize to tasks involving novel visual signals or referring expressions, even if the underlying physical motions are within the model's learned repertoire. The research is motivated by the need to build VLAs that genuinely inherit and leverage the open-world knowledge and aligned vision-language feature spaces of pretrained foundation models, thereby achieving true semantic generalization. This implies that the policy should succeed on tasks with unseen textual instructions and visual cues, provided the required motions were part of the training data.
3. **Disentangling Action Generation and Semantic Understanding:** The authors propose to integrate a world model with MPC in a way that separates the mechanism of generating actions from the process of semantic understanding. By learning a transition function in a frozen, vision-language-aligned latent space, the system can leverage the robust semantic knowledge of a large pretrained multimodal retrieval model without altering its weights. This design aims to prevent knowledge forgetting and directly translate the foundation model's video understanding capabilities into generalizable planning.
4. **Developing a Benchmark for Semantic Generalizability:** Recognizing the limitations of existing benchmarks in evaluating semantic generalization, a key motivation is to introduce a standardized, comprehensive, and scalable benchmark (WISER) specifically designed to assess whether VLAs can perform tasks with novel visual signals and referring expressions. This benchmark aims to clearly expose the gap between training and test performance, highlighting models' true generalization capabilities.
5. **Achieving Cross-Embodiment Generalization:** The research also aims to explore methods that allow for generalization across different robot embodiments without requiring additional training. This is motivated by the desire for more flexible and adaptable robotic systems.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between high-level language instructions and low-level robot control by creating a world model that understands the semantic implications of robot actions in a zero-shot manner, thereby paving the way for more general-purpose robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach involves learning a **Grounded World Model (GWM)** that operates within a frozen, vision-language-aligned latent space of a pretrained multimodal retrieval model, integrated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework. This system, referred to as GWM-MPC, aims to enable semantic generalizable planning with natural language goals.

#### 3.1. Semantic Generalization in Planning Formulation

The problem is framed around learning a policy from a dataset `D` of `I` trajectories, each containing sequences of observations, joint positions, actions, and a natural language task instruction `ℓ`. The goal is for a policy `f(o_t, j_t, ℓ)` to generate an action chunk `a_t:t+c`. The core challenge is achieving *semantic generalization*, meaning the policy should succeed on tasks with novel `ℓ` and `o_t` that differ from `D`, as long as the necessary motions are present in `D`. The authors argue that parametric end-to-end policies often fail to generalize beyond the demonstrated motions or instructions, leading to "visual shortcuts" and poor language understanding.

#### 3.2. Model Predictive Control (MPC) Framework

GWM-MPC employs a three-step MPC framework:
1. **Trajectory Proposal:** Instead of sampling-based or gradient-based methods, KNN (K-Nearest Neighbors) is used to retrieve candidate trajectories from the training dataset `D`. Given the current joint position `j_t`, `N=12` best-matching demonstrated action sequences `A_t:t+c` are retrieved based on MSE between `j_t` and `j_i_k`. This method avoids learning additional action generation parameters and is efficient for sparse, small sets of valid trajectories.
2. **Future Outcome Prediction (GWM):** For each proposed action sequence `a_n_t:t+c` from `A_t:t+c`, the GWM predicts its future outcome embedding `p_t`. The GWM is trained to predict these latent embeddings directly, bypassing computationally intensive pixel-space reconstruction.
3. **Optimal Trajectory Selection:** A score function, based on cosine similarity, is used to select the best action sequence. The pretrained multimodal retrieval model, Qwen3-VL-Embedding, encodes the natural language task instruction `ℓ` into a goal embedding `z_g` and the predicted future outcome embeddings `p_t` into `z_n_t`. The action sequence `a_n*_t:t+c` whose predicted future outcome `z_n_t` has the highest cosine similarity with `z_g` is selected for execution. The foundation model's weights remain frozen during this process.

#### 3.3. Grounded World Model (GWM) Details

- **Vision-Language-Aligned Latent Space:** The GWM learns its transition function within the latent space of Qwen3-VL-Embedding. This model is chosen for its ability to encode images, text, and videos into a shared embedding space, making it suitable for computing cosine similarity between predicted outcomes and language instructions. Its capacity to understand temporal action sequences is also considered beneficial.
- **Rendering-based Action Tokenization (RAT):** To feed action sequences into the vision encoder of Qwen3-VL-Embedding, a novel "Rendering-based Action Tokenization" (RAT) is introduced. Since actions are target joint positions, these actions are sequentially rendered into images using the robot's URDF and the main camera's extrinsics and intrinsics. This converts action sequences into visual representations that can be uniformly encoded by the Qwen vision encoder alongside observations `o_t`. RAT is claimed to be training-free, embodiment-agnostic, and enables zero-shot generalization to different robots.
- **GWM Training:** The GWM `P_θ` is parameterized as a standard transformer model. It takes an encoded feature vector `e_t = E(o_t, a_t:t+c)` (derived from the current observation and rendered action sequence using Qwen's vision encoder) as input and predicts the future outcome embedding `p_t = P_θ(e_t)`. The supervision signal `¯e_t = E(o_t:t+c)` is derived from ground truth future image sequences, and training uses an MSE loss in the vision encoder's latent space. Crucially, no language supervision is required during GWM training, preserving the foundation model's original language understanding capabilities.

#### 3.4. WISER Benchmark

To evaluate semantic generalizability, the **World-knowledge Integrated Semantic Embodied Reasoning (WISER) Benchmark** was developed.
- **Task Structure:** Tasks involve picking a cube and placing it onto a specific mark or image. The robot motions required are simple and rigid, ensuring that failures are due to semantic misunderstanding rather than motion learning difficulties.
- **Categories and Splits:** The benchmark includes 24 categories of world knowledge (e.g., numbers, food, animals, landmarks). Each category has a training scene and a test scene, both with identical layouts (four cubes, three images). This results in 12 pick-and-place tasks per scene, totaling 288 training tasks and 288 test tasks.
- **Generalization Challenge:** Test tasks are constructed with world knowledge, visual signals (e.g., cube colors), and referring expressions that are entirely *unseen* during training. However, the *motions* required to complete these tasks are identical to those demonstrated in the training set, making the test tasks inherently solvable. This setup necessitates zero-shot generalization of semantic understanding.
- **Simulation and Data Collection:** The benchmark is developed using ManiSkill for GPU-parallelized simulation. Demonstrations (6 trajectories per task, 1728 total) were collected using a Franka Panda robot with MPlib, recording camera streams, joint positions, gripper states, and instructions.
- **Metrics:** Evaluation uses binary metrics for Grasp (correct cube picked), Reach (gripper TCP reaches goal), and Success (cube correctly placed, defined as Grasp × Reach).

#### 3.5. Score Function Design (Appendix Detail)

The score function leverages Qwen3-VL-Embedding with a specific prompting strategy. A retrieval-oriented system prompt guides the model. Task instructions `ℓ` are decomposed into sub-task prompts (e.g., `ℓ_pick`, `ℓ_place`). These sub-task prompts, along with initial `o_0` and current `o_t` observations, are encoded by Qwen3-VL-Embedding to produce `z_pick_g` and `z_place_g`. Cosine similarities between predicted future embeddings (`z_n_t`) and these goal embeddings are computed and normalized via softmax. The final selection score `S_n` dynamically switches between `σ_n_pick` (if object not grasped) and `σ_n_place` (if object grasped). This decomposition is shown to significantly boost GWM-MPC performance and mitigate overfitting to sentence structures, unlike in some VLAs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative results demonstrating the semantic generalizability of GWM-MPC compared to various state-of-the-art VLA baselines on the WISER benchmark, supported by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on WISER Benchmark (Table 1)

- **VLA Baselines:** A diverse set of VLA models (InstructVLA, SmolVLA, WALL-OSS, GR00T-N1.6, InternVLA-A1, π0.5, π0, XVLA, UniVLA, Motus) were evaluated.
 - **Training Set Performance:** On the training set (288 tasks), VLAs achieved an average success rate of 90%, with many models reaching near 100% success (e.g., SmolVLA, WALL-OSS, GR00T-N1.6, π0). This indicates their ability to overfit or learn the demonstrated tasks.
 - **Test Set Performance (Semantic Generalization):** On the test set (288 tasks with unseen visual signals and referring expressions), traditional VLAs exhibited a significant drop in performance, achieving an average success rate of only 22%. This low score demonstrates their poor semantic generalizability. Some VLAs, like SmolVLA and π0, performed as low as 8%, comparable to random trajectory retrieval (8.3%). The primary failure mode was incorrect cube grasping or misplacement onto a random image. While some models showed better grasping rates (e.g., InstructVLA at 79%, GR00T-N1.6 at 72%), their overall success rates remained low, indicating a failure in correctly interpreting the placement instruction.
- **GWM-MPC Performance:**
 - **Training Set Performance:** GWM-MPC achieved a 92% success rate on the training tasks, comparable to the average VLA performance.
 - **Test Set Performance (Semantic Generalization):** GWM-MPC demonstrated strong semantic generalization, achieving an 87% success rate on the test set. This is a substantial improvement over the VLA average of 22% and the best VLA (InstructVLA) at 47%. This result indicates that GWM can effectively capture and utilize scene semantics by recognizing predicted future robot behaviors in relation to language instructions, even with novel visual and linguistic cues. The failures were primarily attributed to incorrect scoring and action selection rather than an inability to perform the required motions.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Further Analysis

- **DreamDojo-MPC:** An explicit pixel-space world model, DreamDojo, integrated into MPC, showed low success rates (15% train, 17% test). This suggests that while it reconstructs pixels, it struggles to accurately follow actions or translate them into semantically meaningful outcomes for scoring.
- **Rendering-based Action Tokenization (RAT) vs. Learnable Action Encoder (GWM-MPC-AC):**
 - **GWM-MPC-AC** (using a learnable module for raw numerical action encoding) achieved 74% success on training but only 24% on the test set. This performance gap mirrors that of other VLAs, indicating that learning action embeddings from scratch can lead to poor semantic generalizability.
 - **RAT** (GWM-MPC) significantly outperformed GWM-MPC-AC, suggesting that representing actions as images and encoding them with the same vision encoder as observations facilitates alignment and feature extraction, which is crucial for semantic generalization.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization (GWM-MPC-xArm6):** By using RAT, the GWM trained on Panda robot data could be directly applied to control an xArm6 robot without retraining. GWM-MPC-xArm6 achieved 87% success on training and 83% on test tasks with the xArm6. This demonstrates RAT's embodiment-agnostic nature and the zero-shot cross-embodiment generalization capability of GWM.
- **Data Efficiency (GWM-MPC w/ ½ D):** Training GWM on half the dataset (144 tasks with single demonstrations) resulted in a competitive 72% test success rate. This indicates GWM's relative data efficiency, partly attributed to its reliance on KNN for action proposals rather than learning a complex action generation model.
- **Performance Upper Bound (GT-MPC):** The GT-MPC experiment, which used ground-truth future representations (effectively assuming a perfect GWM), achieved 90% success on training and 93% on test tasks. This suggests that the Qwen3-VL-Embedding is a bottleneck, as even with perfect future predictions, the system's performance is capped by the foundation model's scoring accuracy. Interestingly, GWM-MPC achieved slightly higher training success (92%) than GT-MPC (90%), implying that GWM's predictions might introduce a regularizing effect.
- **Necessity of World Model (MPC w/o GWM):** The "MPC w/o GWM" experiment, where future outcomes were not predicted (only current observation encoded), yielded very low success rates (8% train, 9% test). This confirms that a world model is essential for predictive planning and that simply encoding the current state is insufficient for task completion.
- **Score Function Design (Prompt Decomposition - Table 3):** Decomposing the task instruction into `ℓ_pick` and `ℓ_place` sub-prompts for GWM-MPC led to improved test success (87% vs. 73% with a single `ℓ` prompt). This indicates that GWM-MPC leverages the foundation model's compositional language understanding. In contrast, applying the same decomposition to InstructVLA caused a significant performance drop, suggesting that VLM-based VLAs can overfit to specific sentence structures and lose generalizability when instructions are rephrased or decomposed.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness (Figure 5):** The Qwen3-VL-Embedding was found to be robust to varying replanning intervals. However, both prediction horizon and future subsampling rate required specific thresholds to achieve satisfactory performance. Too short a horizon or excessive subsampling of future frames could hinder the foundation model's ability to infer policy intention and accurately score robot behaviors. Larger models were observed to perform better.
- **Visual Grounding for InstructVLA (Appendix):** An evaluation of the base Eagle-2B model (used by InstructVLA) showed 81% accuracy in spatially localizing destination images in test scenarios. This suggests that the VLM itself possesses strong visual understanding, but fine-tuning for robotic control (InstructVLA's 51% TCP reaching success) compromises this capability, reinforcing the knowledge forgetting hypothesis.
- **GT-MPC for LIBERO-goal (Appendix):** On the LIBERO-goal benchmark (tasks requiring semantic understanding), GT-MPC achieved an average 71.4% success rate in a zero-shot setting. This further validates Qwen3-VL-Embedding as an effective video classifier for action selection, and indicates that its main limitations arise when scenarios are outside its pre-trained knowledge or when the action generator is not robust.

#### 4.3. Training and Inference Efficiency

- GWM training is computationally efficient, requiring 20 GPU hours on the WISER benchmark.
- The reliance on KNN for action proposals reduces data reliance compared to learning-based action generation.
- Inference efficiency is lower than VLAs because GWM-MPC requires `N=12` GWM inferences for each MPC step, and the Qwen encoder processes inputs sequentially rather than in parallel due to reported batching bugs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the fields of robot learning, planning, and vision-language understanding, addressing critical limitations of current approaches.

1. **Enhanced Semantic Generalizability:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating a method (GWM-MPC) that achieves substantially higher semantic generalizability compared to existing VLA models. By learning a world model in a *frozen, vision-language-aligned latent space*, GWM-MPC effectively leverages the open-world knowledge of large multimodal retrieval models without experiencing "knowledge forgetting." This allows the system to generalize to tasks with novel visual signals and referring expressions, a crucial step towards more adaptable and intelligent robotic systems capable of operating in diverse, unstructured environments.

2. **Language-based Goal Specification for MPC:** The work successfully transitions visuomotor MPC from relying on static goal images to dynamic natural language instructions. This improves the interactivity and user-friendliness of robot systems, making it easier for humans to convey complex or novel tasks to robots without needing to provide specific visual examples.

3. **Disentanglement of Action and Semantic Understanding:** The architecture effectively disentangles action generation (via KNN retrieval or other action proposal mechanisms) from semantic understanding (via the frozen foundation model). This modularity prevents the semantic knowledge from being diluted or overwritten during policy learning, maintaining the strong language and visual comprehension capabilities of the pretrained model. This approach offers an alternative to end-to-end VLA fine-tuning, which often struggles with retaining foundational knowledge.

4. **Novel Rendering-based Action Tokenization (RAT):** The introduction of RAT is a notable methodological contribution. By rendering joint positions into images, RAT provides a training-free and embodiment-agnostic way to encode actions. This enables zero-shot generalization to different robot embodiments (demonstrated with the xArm6), which is a key step towards developing generalist robot policies that are not tied to a specific hardware platform.

5. **Introduction of WISER Benchmark:** The creation of the WISER benchmark addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of semantic generalizability for VLAs. Its design, featuring unseen visual signals and referring expressions with conserved underlying motions, provides a rigorous and scalable tool for researchers to assess true generalization capabilities beyond mere visual interference or object pose perturbations. This will likely foster further research into robust and generalizable robot learning.

6. **Identification of Foundation Model Bottleneck and Future Directions:** The finding that the performance of GWM-MPC is ultimately bottlenecked by the Qwen3-VL-Embedding model itself provides a clear direction for future research. This suggests that improvements in the underlying multimodal retrieval model, potentially through fine-tuning with robot-specific data, could further enhance the planning system's capabilities.

In summary, this research offers a robust and generalizable framework for language-conditioned robotic planning by effectively harnessing the semantic understanding of large foundation models. Its contributions pave the way for more intuitive human-robot interfaces, robots capable of performing novel tasks in new environments, and a more principled approach to evaluating semantic generalization in embodied AI.
