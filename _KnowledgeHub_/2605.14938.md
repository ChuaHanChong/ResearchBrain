# Octopus: History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization for Continual Learning in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Octopus introduces a two-stage continual learning framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), employing History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization (HiFGO) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on the UCIT benchmark, improving average accuracy by 2.14% over prior methods, while operating entirely without historical data.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when continually learning new tasks.
- Existing regularization-based methods, particularly parameter orthogonality, are often insufficient to fully prevent inter-task interference.
- Prior gradient-based orthogonalization methods for continual learning typically require access to historical data, posing privacy and storage challenges.

## Method

- **History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization (HiFGO)** identifies inter-task interference by computing ==gradients of previous task parameters on the *current* task's data distribution== (GPWC).
- A ==two-stage finetuning strategy== first allows unconstrained adaptation to new tasks for plasticity, then applies constrained refinement with HiFGO to maintain stability.
- ==GPWC== is approximated using only the most recent task's parameters to maintain a constant computational cost, avoiding linear growth with the number of tasks.

## Results

- Octopus achieves state-of-the-art results on the UCIT benchmark, improving Average accuracy by **+2.14%** and Last accuracy by **+6.82%** over previous best methods.
- The framework also outperforms previous state-of-the-art on the CoIN benchmark by **+1.99%** in Average accuracy and **+2.29%** in Last accuracy.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that HiFGO provides superior resistance to forgetting, yielding a Backward Transfer (BWT) of **+0.41**, and the two-stage strategy boosts Immediate and Last metrics by over **8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gradient orthogonality between new task updates and previous task loss gradients is theoretically more critical for preventing catastrophic forgetting than parameter orthogonality.
> - Gradients of Previous parameters Within Current data distribution (GPWC) can effectively serve as a history-free proxy for past task gradients, capturing inter-task interference and high-curvature Hessian directions.
> - A two-stage fine-tuning process, separating unconstrained adaptation from constrained refinement, effectively balances model plasticity for new tasks and stability for retaining prior knowledge.
