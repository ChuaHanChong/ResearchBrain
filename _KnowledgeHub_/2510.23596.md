# Think Twice: Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model (BR-RM) introduces a two-turn framework to improve Large Language Model evaluation by mitigating "judgment diffusion." This approach adaptively focuses on critical evaluation criteria and performs a conditioned re-evaluation, achieving a new state-of-the-art average score of 84.2% across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reward models (RMs) for LLMs suffer from "judgment diffusion," where attention is spread thinly across evaluation criteria, leading to shallow analysis and missed subtle errors.
- While LLM solvers benefit from multi-step "think-twice" reasoning strategies, most reward models operate in a "one-shot" manner, lacking instance-adaptive focus or a conditioned second pass for deep scrutiny.
- Traditional scalar RMs and current generative/reasoning RMs often fail to detect nuanced factual errors, local contradictions, or logical inconsistencies in LLM outputs.

## Method

- A ==two-turn Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model (BR-RM)== is proposed, reframing reward modeling as a structured reasoning and generation task.
- The first turn involves =="Adaptive Branching"== where the model selects a small subset of instance-critical evaluation criteria and generates an "issue sketch" for each response, focusing its initial analysis.
- The second turn, =="Branch-Conditioned Rethinking"==, re-evaluates responses through the lens of the issues and criteria identified in the first turn, guided by task-specific evaluation hierarchies, to produce a detailed comparative judgment and final decision.

## Results

- The BR-RM-Qwen-14B model achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an average score of **84.2%** across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB benchmarks, and the highest accuracy on RM-Bench (**85.9%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the "rethinking" turn and its "conditioned" nature are critical, with their removal causing significant performance drops (up to **3.7** and **2.8 points** respectively).
- The smaller BR-RM-Qwen-8B model outperformed much larger single-turn baselines (e.g., GPT-4o, 70B scalar models) in average score, demonstrating the efficiency benefits of targeted two-turn reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying a structured, adaptive "think-twice" reasoning process from LLM solvers to reward models (judges) effectively mitigates "judgment diffusion" and enhances evaluation sensitivity.
> - A two-turn reasoning structure provides the optimal balance between reasoning depth and stability, as increasing the number of turns beyond two leads to performance degradation.
> - A straightforward reward function combining a binary outcome reward with strict format checks is most effective for training the structured generative reward model.
