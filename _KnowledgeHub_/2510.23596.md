# Think Twice: Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model (BR-RM) introduces a two-turn framework to improve Large Language Model evaluation by mitigating "judgment diffusion." This approach adaptively focuses on critical evaluation criteria and performs a conditioned re-evaluation, achieving a new state-of-the-art average score of 84.2% across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing reward models (RMs) for LLMs suffer from "judgment diffusion," where attention is spread thinly across evaluation criteria, leading to shallow analysis and missed subtle errors.
- While LLM solvers benefit from multi-step "think-twice" reasoning strategies, most reward models operate in a "one-shot" manner, lacking instance-adaptive focus or a conditioned second pass for deep scrutiny.
- Traditional scalar RMs and current generative/reasoning RMs often fail to detect nuanced factual errors, local contradictions, or logical inconsistencies in LLM outputs.

## Method

- A ==two-turn Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model (BR-RM)== is proposed, reframing reward modeling as a structured reasoning and generation task.
- The first turn involves =="Adaptive Branching"== where the model selects a small subset of instance-critical evaluation criteria and generates an "issue sketch" for each response, focusing its initial analysis.
- The second turn, =="Branch-Conditioned Rethinking"==, re-evaluates responses through the lens of the issues and criteria identified in the first turn, guided by task-specific evaluation hierarchies, to produce a detailed comparative judgment and final decision.

## Results

- The BR-RM-Qwen-14B model achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an average score of **84.2%** across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB benchmarks, and the highest accuracy on RM-Bench (**85.9%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the "rethinking" turn and its "conditioned" nature are critical, with their removal causing significant performance drops (up to **3.7** and **2.8 points** respectively).
- The smaller BR-RM-Qwen-8B model outperformed much larger single-turn baselines (e.g., GPT-4o, 70B scalar models) in average score, demonstrating the efficiency benefits of targeted two-turn reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying a structured, adaptive "think-twice" reasoning process from LLM solvers to reward models (judges) effectively mitigates "judgment diffusion" and enhances evaluation sensitivity.
> - A two-turn reasoning structure provides the optimal balance between reasoning depth and stability, as increasing the number of turns beyond two leads to performance degradation.
> - A straightforward reward function combining a binary outcome reward with strict format checks is most effective for training the structured generative reward model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly employing multi-step "thinking models" such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT), scratchpads, and reflect-revise strategies to externalize intermediate reasoning steps and allocate additional computational resources for complex problem-solving. These approaches enhance the reliability and accuracy of LLMs by allowing them to surface hypotheses, check local inferences, and identify errors. The concept of a "deliberate second pass" or "thinking twice" has demonstrated empirical benefits in improving the reasoning capabilities of LLMs when acting as solvers.

However, the methods used for evaluating and providing feedback to these LLMs, specifically Reward Models (RMs) in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) pipelines, have largely retained a "one-shot" design. Traditional scalar RMs compress various quality dimensions into a single scalar score in a single forward pass. More recent Generative Reward Models (GenRMs) generate critiques or rationales before scoring, which improves transparency, but often still collapse their analysis into a single global decision under a fixed rubric. Reasoning Reward Models (ReasonRMs) allocate test-time compute for judgment, but frequently reason broadly across a rubric without enforcing instance-adaptive focus or a targeted second pass conditioned on identified issues.

This prevailing "one-shot" evaluation paradigm in RMs leads to what the authors term "judgment diffusion." This phenomenon describes a situation where a model's attention is spread thinly across numerous evaluation criteria, resulting in diluted focus and shallow analysis. Consequently, RMs may miss subtle factual inaccuracies, local contradictions, or minor logic bugs, and their decisions can be disproportionately influenced by superficial factors such as stylistic elements rather than substantive quality.

This paper addresses this limitation by introducing a "think-twice" principle to reward modeling, drawing inspiration from its success in LLM solvers. It proposes a structured, two-turn reasoning process for RMs designed to counteract judgment diffusion and enhance the detection of nuanced errors. By explicitly structuring the evaluation into adaptive branching and conditioned rethinking, this work aims to provide more robust, sensitive, and reliable reward signals for LLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified weakness in existing reward models (RMs): "judgment diffusion." The authors observed that when RMs are required to assess responses holistically in a single pass, their evaluative attention tends to spread thinly across numerous potential criteria. This leads to two main issues:

1. **Focus Dilution**: The attention of the reward model is not sufficiently concentrated on any single dimension, preventing in-depth scrutiny. This can cause the model to overlook subtle yet critical errors or to be swayed by superficial aspects of a response. A preliminary study conducted by the authors, analyzing token allocation of a contemporary ReasonRM (Guo et al., 2025) on the RM-Bench benchmark, empirically supported this, showing that the baseline model distributed its generative tokens broadly across many dimensions, including non-critical ones, rather than focusing on the most relevant aspects for a given task (Figure 1, Appendix A).

2. **Shallow Analysis**: Instead of deeply probing concrete failure modes or verifying specific claims, judgments remain at a surface level. Empirical studies have indicated that RMs often miss subtle factual slips or localized reasoning errors, and their evaluations can be susceptible to stylistic factors rather than substantive quality.

To address these limitations, the key objective of this work is to introduce a two-turn reward modeling framework, named Branch-and-Rethink Reasoning Reward Model (BR-RM), that integrates focus and targeted rethinking into the evaluation process. The framework aims to:

- **Mitigate Judgment Diffusion**: By transforming the all-at-once scoring into a focused, second-look reasoning process, the model is compelled to allocate its evaluative compute more strategically.
- **Improve Sensitivity to Subtle Errors**: Through a structured, issue-driven analysis, the model is expected to become more adept at detecting nuanced yet consequential errors that often elude single-pass evaluators.
- **Enhance Practicality and Scalability**: The proposed method seeks to achieve these improvements while remaining compatible with standard RLHF pipelines and maintaining operational feasibility.

Essentially, the motivation is rooted in the idea that if LLMs benefit from multi-step, deliberate reasoning as "solvers," then their "judges" (RMs) should similarly employ structured thinking to provide more accurate and reliable feedback, thereby improving the overall alignment and performance of LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Branch-and-Rethink (BR-RM), reframes the reward modeling task from a direct regression on a scalar value to a structured reasoning and generation process. It utilizes a two-stage generative workflow to produce an explicit deliberation trace, aimed at mitigating judgment diffusion and enhancing analytical depth.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

Instead of learning a scalar function $R_\phi(x, y)$ that assigns a score to a response $y$ for a prompt $x$, BR-RM trains a policy $\pi_\theta$ to generate a two-turn deliberation trace, $\tau$, for each comparison triplet $(x, y_1, y_2)$. The trace culminates in a final preference $\hat{z} \in \{1, 2\}$, which is extracted from the generated text. The objective is to train $\pi_\theta$ to produce traces where $\hat{z}$ aligns with the ground-truth label $z$. The complete trace is a concatenation of outputs from each stage: $\tau = \tau_1 \circ \tau_2$.

#### 3.2. The Branch-and-Rethink Framework: Stage 1: Adaptive Branching for Focused Evaluation

This first stage directly targets focus dilution. The reward model is constrained to perform the following:
- **Criteria Selection**: It selects a small subset of instance-critical evaluation criteria, $\mathcal{C}_{sel}$, from a predefined universal set of candidates, $\mathcal{C}$ (e.g., Factual Accuracy, Logical Coherence, Safety Awareness, Implementation Capability, Computational Precision, Instruction Adherence, Creativity). This forces the model to hypothesize the most salient dimensions for the given comparison.
- **Preliminary Analysis**: Conditioned on the selected criteria, the model generates a concise preliminary analysis, $\alpha_j$, for each response $y_j$. This analysis sketches potential weaknesses or strengths relevant to the chosen dimensions.
The output of this stage, $\tau_1 = (\mathcal{C}_{sel}, \alpha_1, \alpha_2)$, serves as a focused analytical scope for the subsequent stage. (Prompts detailing this stage are provided in Appendix C, Figure 8).

#### 3.2.2. Stage 2: Branch-Conditioned Rethinking for Deep Analysis

This second stage aims to prevent shallow analysis. With the analytical scope established by $\tau_1$, the model performs a targeted re-evaluation.
- **Targeted Re-evaluation**: The model re-reads the responses through the specific lens of the dimensions flagged in Stage 1. This means verifying facts, checking logical consistency, or examining localized bugs, as guided by the preliminary analysis.
- **Hierarchical Guidance**: To ensure principled deliberation, task-specific evaluation hierarchies, $\mathcal{H}_{task}$, are incorporated. For example, accuracy-driven tasks might prioritize Correctness and Methodology over Clarity.
The model, conditioned on $x, y_1, y_2,$ and $\tau_1$, generates the second part of the trace, $\tau_2$, which includes a detailed comparative judgment and the final decision, $\hat{z}$. (Prompts detailing this stage are provided in Appendix C, Figure 9).

#### 3.3. Learning via Reinforcement Learning: The GRPO Training Algorithm

The authors employ Generalized Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO, Shao et al. 2024), a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), selected for its training stability and efficiency in supporting two-turn traces. The training loop optimizes the policy $\pi_\theta$ to maximize a composite reward function over the distribution of generated traces. The GRPO objective balances reward maximization with training stability using a clipped surrogate objective and a KL-divergence penalty against a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$. An advantage $A(\tau)$ is calculated as a group-relative, critic-free value: for each prompt, $K$ traces are sampled, a terminal reward $r(\tau)$ is computed per trace, then the per-prompt mean is subtracted, and the result is divided by the per-prompt standard deviation to whiten the value. This scalar advantage is uniformly assigned to all tokens in the trace.

#### 3.3.2. Reward Function Design

The composite reward function $R(\tau)$ guides the learning process through two terms:
$R(\tau) = R_{format}(\tau) + w R_{outcome}(\tau)$, where $w$ balances the terms.
- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$)**: A strict binary penalty for any deviation from the prescribed two-stage format. $R_{format}(\tau) = 0$ if $\tau$ is well-formed, and $\lambda_{format}$ (a negative constant, e.g., -100) if malformed. This term ensures the model adheres to the structured generative format.
- **Binary Outcome Reward ($R_{outcome}$)**: This rewards the substantive quality of the trace based on the correctness of its final decision. It is applied only if the trace is structurally valid ($R_{format}(\tau) = 0$). $R_{outcome}(\tau) = 0$ if $\hat{z} = z$ (correct), and $-1$ if $\hat{z} \neq z$ (incorrect).
The same composite reward $R(\tau)$ is assigned separately to the generation of the first turn ($\tau_1$) and the second turn ($\tau_2$ conditioned on $\tau_1$). This allows for integration into standard GRPO implementations without requiring explicit multi-turn RL support.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data**: A diverse mix of preference datasets, including HelpSteer3, Skywork Reward Preference-80K, Code-Preference-Pairs, and Math-Step-DPO-10K.
- **Evaluation Data**: Model performance is measured by preference accuracy on RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB, covering reasoning, general knowledge, safety, and human preference alignment.
- **Baselines**: Compared against three categories: Scalar RMs, Generative RMs, and advanced Reasoning RMs.
- **Implementation Details**: Utilizes NeMo-RL for a custom two-turn environment. Trained on Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-14B models for 400 optimization steps. Inference uses vLLM for high-throughput evaluation. Further details on hyperparameters are provided in Appendix D, Table 3.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of the Branch-and-Rethink (BR-RM) approach across multiple reward modeling benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

BR-RM-Qwen-14B achieved the highest average score of 84.2% across RewardBench, RM-Bench, and RMB, establishing new state-of-the-art performance. Specifically, it recorded the highest score on RM-Bench (85.9%) and ranked top-2 on RMB (74.7%). The smaller BR-RM-Qwen-8B model also demonstrated strong performance, surpassing larger baselines, including GPT-4o and 70B scalar models, in average accuracy (82.6%). This indicates that adaptive two-turn reasoning can enhance both efficiency and robustness.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Baselines

- **Scalar RMs**: Models such as INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B exhibited high performance on RewardBench (95.1%), but showed a significant drop on reasoning-intensive benchmarks like RM-Bench (70.9%) and RMB (70.5%). This suggests that merely scaling parameters in scalar RMs yields diminishing returns for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Generative RMs (GenRMs)**: These models, which produce critiques before scoring, showed improved robustness but not consistent superiority over scalar models. For example, Skywork-Critic-Llama-3.1-70B performed well on RewardBench (93.3%) but struggled on RMB (65.6%). GPT-4o achieved a balanced profile but still lagged behind leading scalar and reasoning RMs.
- **Reasoning RMs (ReasonRMs)**: Reasoning mechanisms demonstrated clear performance gains primarily in models larger than 14B parameters. The 32B RM-R1 models achieved strong results (e.g., up to 83.9% on RM-Bench), whereas smaller reasoning models (e.g., 7B) underperformed, indicating that sufficient model capacity is a prerequisite for effective reasoning. BR-RM models achieved a superior balance across benchmarks compared to these.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was performed to quantify the contribution of each architectural component (Figure 3, Table 7):
- **"Branching Only"**: Removing the second "rethinking" turn resulted in the most significant performance degradation (e.g., a 3.7-point drop on RMB for the 14B model). This suggests that without the holistic re-evaluation, the model over-emphasizes initial granular flaws.
- **"Unconditioned Rethinking"**: A version with two turns but without adaptive branching (using a generic re-evaluation prompt) showed performance drops of up to 2.8 points. This indicates that instance-adaptive focus from the first turn is critical for localized error correction and efficiency in the second pass.
- **"Single-Turn Model"**: Merging both turns into a single instruction led to shallower analysis and a decline in performance. This highlights the necessity of a deliberate, multi-step pipeline to avoid cognitive overload.

#### 4.4. Reward Design Analysis

The study explored various reward function designs (Figure 4, Table 8):
- **Removing Format Checks**: This allowed the model to bypass reasoning steps, leading to ambiguous feedback and slower learning. Strict format checks were found to be essential for structural integrity and clear feedback.
- **Scoring on a Scale**: Using a numerical scale (e.g., -3 to 3) for preference strength caused the model to focus on predicting exact values rather than relative quality, hindering performance because the ultimate objective was a binary win/loss outcome.
- **Additional Reward for Branching**: Attempting to reward the first reasoning step with indirect, calculated values led to computational expense and infrequent, erratic reward signals, destabilizing training.
- **Conclusion**: The most effective reward design proved to be a simple binary outcome reward combined with strict format checking, ensuring direct alignment between the training task and the evaluation objective.

#### 4.5. Reasoning Turn Scaling

Investigating the optimal number of reasoning turns revealed that performance peaked at 2 turns (Figure 5). Additional turns (3 or 4) resulted in performance degradation, hypothesized to be due to "over-thinking," hallucinating errors, or drifting from the original context. This suggests that a single hypothesis generation step followed by a verification step achieves an optimal balance between reasoning depth and stability.

#### 4.6. Distribution of Selected Branches

Analysis of branch selection on RM-Bench (Figure 6) indicated sparse and adaptive activation, with an average of 2.5 dimensions selected per instance. Specific dimensions, such as "Safety Awareness" or "Implementation Capability," predominantly activated within their respective domains (91% and 94% activation). This demonstrates that the universal set of criteria functions as a flexible semantic basis, allowing the model to dynamically map novel failure modes to high-level cognitive categories and effectively prune the search space, thus preventing focus dilution.

#### 4.7. Inference Efficiency

While scalar RMs are faster (e.g., InternLM2-7B-Reward at 0.015s), they reach a performance ceiling. BR-RM-Qwen-8B, despite being a two-turn model, was faster (9.5s per instance) than the single-turn RM-R1-Qwen-Instruct-32B (10.3s) while achieving higher average accuracy (82.6% vs. 81.2%) (Table 2). This indicates that targeted two-turn reasoning can be more computationally efficient than simply increasing the parameter count of a single-turn model. The authors further contend that in high-stakes alignment pipelines, the quality of the reward signal is often more critical than generation speed, and BR-RM's latency can be amortized in asynchronous RL setups.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of reward modeling by introducing the "branch-and-rethink" framework, which addresses the issue of "judgment diffusion" in current reward models. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Reward Signal Quality**: By enforcing a structured, two-stage judgment process—adaptive branching followed by branch-conditioned rethinking—the BR-RM framework yields more focused and in-depth evaluations. This approach mitigates the problem of attention dilution and shallow analysis observed in single-pass reward models, leading to more stable and nuanced preference signals. These higher-quality reward signals are crucial for effectively training Large Language Models (LLMs) through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or AI Feedback (RLAIF), ultimately contributing to the development of more reliably aligned and performant LLMs.

- **Enhanced Error Detection**: The framework's ability to first identify instance-critical dimensions and then perform a targeted re-read allows it to detect subtle factual inaccuracies, logical inconsistencies, or localized bugs that often go unnoticed by conventional reward models. This increased sensitivity to fine-grained errors can lead to more robust model training and better quality control for generated LLM outputs.

- **Conceptual Paradigm Shift**: The work transfers the "think-twice" principle, which has proven effective in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs as "solvers," to their role as "judges." This suggests a broader applicability of multi-step, deliberate reasoning architectures across different functions of LLMs, potentially inspiring similar structured approaches in other evaluation or meta-learning tasks.

- **Efficiency and Scalability**: The empirical results indicate that the BR-RM framework can achieve state-of-the-art performance with models of relatively smaller sizes (e.g., an 8B model outperforming much larger baselines). This is achieved through intelligent allocation of computational resources during inference, focusing on relevant dimensions rather than simply scaling model parameters. This demonstrates a path towards more efficient and practical reward modeling, especially relevant in scenarios where computational budgets are constrained or when the quality of the reward signal outweighs raw speed.

- **Reduced Bias**: By scrutinizing specific, identified issues rather than making broad, holistic judgments, the framework may inherently reduce susceptibility to superficial heuristics or stylistic biases (e.g., verbosity bias) that can influence traditional reward models.

- **Future Research Directions**: The paper outlines several potential extensions that highlight the framework's versatility:
 - **Integration with External Tools**: Incorporating verification tools like retrieval systems or code runners to objectively check claims and enhance factual accuracy.
 - **Dynamic Evaluation Rules**: Developing methods for automatically generating dynamic, instance-specific evaluation rules to replace static rubrics, further increasing adaptability.
 - **Uncertainty-Guided Re-evaluation**: Utilizing model uncertainty to adaptively decide when and how deeply to re-evaluate an output, optimizing the trade-off between computational cost and evaluation depth.

In summary, the "branch-and-rethink" framework offers a structured and effective approach to addressing fundamental limitations in reward modeling, paving the way for more reliable LLM alignment and opening new avenues for research into structured reasoning in AI evaluation systems.
