# NVILA: Efficient Frontier Visual Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NVILA, developed by a collaboration including NVIDIA and MIT, introduces a family of open Visual Language Models that achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on diverse image and video benchmarks while drastically improving efficiency across training, fine-tuning, and deployment. The models demonstrate training cost reductions of 1.9-5.1x and inference speedups of up to 2.8x, enabling practical deployment on resource-constrained devices and democratizing VLM development.

## Problem

- Current state-of-the-art Visual Language Models (VLMs) incur prohibitively high training costs, requiring hundreds of GPU days.
- Fine-tuning VLMs for domain-specific applications is memory-intensive, often exceeding 64GB, which limits accessibility on standard hardware.
- Deploying VLMs on resource-constrained edge devices or in real-time applications faces significant computational and memory challenges due to their large size.

## Method

- Introduces a =="Scale-Then-Compress" architectural paradigm== with ==Dynamic-S2== for images and temporal averaging for videos to efficiently handle high-resolution and long-context visual inputs.
- Employs efficient training techniques including unsupervised dataset pruning (==DeltaLoss==) to reduce data redundancy and FP8 mixed precision training for higher throughput.
- Proposes optimized fine-tuning strategies with differentiated learning rates and efficient Layer Normalization (LN) tuning for the vision encoder, along with specialized inference engine optimizations including ==W8A8 and W4A16 quantization==.

## Results

- NVILA matches or surpasses the accuracy of many leading open-source models and performs competitively with powerful proprietary models across diverse image and video benchmarks.
- The models reduce VLM training costs by **1.9x** to **5.1x** and accelerate inference by **1.6x** to **2.8x** compared to baselines through quantization and optimized kernels.
- NVILA enables VLM fine-tuning under **24 GB** of GPU memory, making adaptation accessible on consumer-grade GPUs, and demonstrates real-time robotic navigation on a single laptop GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "scale-then-compress" paradigm, combining adaptive scaling with aggressive token reduction, effectively balances visual information capture and computational efficiency for high-resolution images and long videos.
> - Holistic optimization across the entire VLM lifecycle, from data selection and training to fine-tuning and deployment, is crucial for achieving substantial efficiency gains.
> - Fine-tuning specific sub-components (e.g., Layer Normalization layers of the vision encoder) can make VLM adaptation memory-efficient and accessible without sacrificing accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual language models (VLMs) have demonstrated substantial progress in integrating visual and textual information, leading to advancements in various applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, and medical imaging. Recent research has primarily concentrated on enhancing VLM accuracy and expanding their application domains, with the performance of both proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) and open-source alternatives (e.g., InternVL2, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-OneVision) showing continuous improvement. These advancements often involve larger model sizes and more extensive training datasets.

However, the efficiency of VLMs, particularly concerning their computational and memory demands across their lifecycle—training, fine-tuning, and deployment—has received comparatively less attention. Training state-of-the-art VLMs is a resource-intensive process, requiring hundreds of GPU days and significant computational costs, which can constitute a barrier to entry for many researchers. Fine-tuning these models for specialized domains is also memory-intensive, often exceeding the capabilities of consumer-grade GPUs. Furthermore, deploying VLMs in edge applications (e.g., robots, mobile devices) necessitates models that operate within strict computational budgets.

This work addresses this recognized gap by focusing on optimizing both the efficiency and accuracy of VLMs. It aims to develop a family of open-source VLMs that can perform competitively with leading models while significantly reducing resource requirements across their entire operational lifecycle. The paper positions itself as exploring systematic solutions for end-to-end VLM efficiency, an area identified as less explored compared to performance-centric advancements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop NVILA, a family of open visual language models that achieves an optimized balance between efficiency and accuracy. This overarching goal is motivated by several challenges inherent in the current VLM landscape:

- **High Training Costs:** Training state-of-the-art VLMs is computationally expensive and time-consuming. The paper notes that a 7B VLM can require up to 400 GPU days, which creates a substantial barrier for researchers and limits the accessibility of advanced VLM development. The objective is to reduce these training costs.
- **Memory-Intensive Fine-Tuning:** Adapting VLMs to specialized domains through fine-tuning often demands considerable GPU memory. For instance, fully fine-tuning a 7B VLM can exceed 64GB of GPU memory, a capacity often unavailable on consumer-level hardware. The motivation is to enable more memory-efficient fine-tuning processes.
- **Resource-Constrained Deployment:** VLMs are increasingly targeted for deployment in edge applications, such as robotics or embedded systems, where computational resources and energy budgets are limited. The objective is to improve the efficiency of VLM deployment to facilitate their use in such environments.
- **Information Loss in Visual Processing:** Traditional VLM architectures, like the original VILA, often employ fixed and relatively low spatial and temporal resolutions for visual inputs (e.g., 448x448 images, 8-14 video frames). This can lead to a significant loss of fine-grained spatial and temporal details, thereby limiting the model's accuracy on tasks requiring high-resolution understanding or long-video comprehension, particularly for text-heavy images or long-duration videos. The motivation is to develop an architecture that can process higher-resolution visual inputs without incurring prohibitive computational costs.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives for NVILA include:
- Developing a novel model architecture that can efficiently process high-resolution images and long videos by employing a "scale-then-compress" strategy for visual tokens.
- Systematically enhancing VLM efficiency throughout the entire lifecycle, encompassing training, fine-tuning, and deployment stages.
- Achieving competitive or superior accuracy compared to existing leading open and proprietary VLMs across a diverse set of image and video benchmarks.
- Reducing training costs, prefilling latency, and decoding latency to make VLMs more accessible and practical for various applications.
- Enabling new capabilities such as temporal localization, robotic navigation, and medical imaging applications through the efficient and accurate VLM framework.
- Releasing code and models to promote reproducibility and further research in efficient VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NVILA framework systematically addresses VLM efficiency and accuracy across model architecture, training, fine-tuning, and deployment. The approach is primarily built upon the VILA model, incorporating several modifications and optimizations.

#### 3.1. Efficient Model Architecture

NVILA's architecture comprises a visual encoder, a projector, and a token processor (LLM). It utilizes SigLIP as the vision encoder, a two-layer MLP as the projector, and Qwen2 as the LLM. The core architectural improvement is the "scale-then-compress" paradigm, designed to first capture detailed visual information and then efficiently process it by reducing token count.

- **Spatial "Scale-Then-Compress":**
 - **Scaling:** Instead of fixed low-resolution inputs, NVILA employs Dynamic-S² (Dynamic Spatial Super-resolution) to process images. Dynamic-S² is an extension of S², which resizes images into multiple scales (e.g., 448², 896², 1344²) and processes them by splitting into 448² tiles. Each tile is processed independently by the vision encoder, and features are stitched back. Dynamic-S² further refines this by adaptively maintaining the original aspect ratio at the largest scale, rather than forcing a square aspect ratio, thereby reducing distortion for images with extreme aspect ratios. This multi-scale processing aims to preserve high-resolution details.
 - **Compression:** After scaling, visual tokens are compressed. While VILA uses a 2x2 spatial-to-channel (STC) reshape for a 4x token reduction, pushing this further in VILA led to significant accuracy drops. NVILA introduces an additional **Visual Encoder Pre-training (VEP)** stage to jointly tune the vision encoder and projectors. This VEP stage is applied after initial projector alignment, specifically to mitigate accuracy loss when using more aggressive spatial token reductions (e.g., 3x3 STC reshape, reducing tokens by a factor of 9), enabling a 2.4x speedup in both training and inference compared to a non-compressed baseline.

- **Temporal "Scale-Then-Compress":**
 - **Scaling:** The approach increases the number of uniformly sampled frames from videos (e.g., from 8 to 32 frames) to capture more temporal information. This is supported by additional video-supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
 - **Compression:** To manage the increased temporal tokens, temporal averaging is applied. Frames are partitioned into groups, and visual tokens within each group are temporally pooled. This reduces temporal redundancy, as consecutive frames often contain similar information, while retaining spatiotemporal details. This allows for a 4x reduction in temporal tokens while maintaining accuracy. Further scaling to 256 frames with 8x compression is also explored.

#### 3.2. Efficient Training

To reduce the cost and time associated with training large VLMs, NVILA incorporates algorithmic and system-level optimizations.

- **Dataset Pruning:**
 - **Motivation:** Large SFT datasets often contain redundancy or examples that are either too easy or too hard, which may not contribute effectively to training.
 - **Method:** NVILA leverages **DeltaLoss** to score training examples. DeltaLoss compares the output probabilities of a "large" model and a "small" model on answer tokens. Examples are filtered out if both models answer correctly or incorrectly (DeltaLoss near 0, suggesting easy/distracting), or if the small model is correct but the large model fails (negative DeltaLoss, suggesting distraction). Examples where the small model is incorrect but the large model is correct (positive DeltaLoss) are considered "helpful." This method is used to prune the training set, for example, by 50%, to maintain accuracy while significantly reducing data size.
 - **Evaluation:** DeltaLoss-based pruning is compared against random and cluster pruning, demonstrating competitive or superior performance, particularly on tasks like DocVQA.

- **FP8 Training:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard FP16/BF16 training is computationally intensive. FP8 offers potential for greater computational and memory efficiency on supported hardware (NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell architectures).
 - **Method:** NVILA integrates FP8 mixed precision training, borrowing implementation from COAT. This involves performing matrix multiplications in FP8 and, in some configurations, quantizing gradients, weight master copies, and first-order momentum to FP8.
 - **Impact:** FP8 training allowed for larger batch sizes (e.g., from 4 to 16 without gradient checkpointing), resulting in up to a 2x speedup in throughput. When gradient checkpointing is enabled, FP8 still provided a 1.2x speedup compared to BF16, further enhanced by using a cross-entropy kernel from Liger to reduce memory usage.

#### 3.3. Efficient Fine-Tuning

For domain-specific adaptation, NVILA focuses on optimizing Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods.

- **Methodology:** The research identifies two key observations for efficient fine-tuning of VLMs (ViT + LLM):
 1. **Differential Learning Rates:** The learning rate for the Vision Transformer (ViT) part should be set significantly lower (5-50x smaller) than that for the Language Model (LLM) part.
 2. **LayerNorm Tuning for ViT:** Tuning only the Layer Normalization (LN) layers of the ViT can achieve performance comparable to applying LoRA to the entire vision encoder, but with improved computational efficiency, reducing training time by 25%.
- **Result:** This configured setup (LLM with LoRA or QLoRA, ViT LayerNorm tuning with differential learning rates) allows fine-tuning to various downstream tasks with competitive accuracy under 24 GB of memory.

#### 3.4. Efficient Deployment

NVILA includes a specialized inference engine with quantization techniques for efficient deployment, particularly for edge applications.

- **Phased Optimization:** The inference process is divided into pre-filling and decoding stages, with different optimization strategies.
 - **Pre-filling (Compute-Bounded):** After token compression reduces the LLM's workload, the vision tower becomes the bottleneck. W8A8 quantization is applied to the vision tower to reduce Time-To-First-Token (TTFT).
 - **Decoding (Memory-Bounded):** For the LLM backbone, W4A16 quantization (following AWQ) is employed. The implementation is further optimized by introducing FP16 accumulation to W4A16 GEMM kernels, resulting in a 1.7x kernel speedup without accuracy compromise.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative results demonstrating NVILA's performance in terms of both accuracy and efficiency across various benchmarks and tasks.

#### 4.1. Accuracy Results

- **Image Benchmarks (Table 8):** NVILA was evaluated on 10 diverse image benchmarks (e.g., AI2D, ChartQA, DocVQA, MMMU, TextVQA, VQAv2).
 - NVILA-8B and NVILA-15B models demonstrated competitive or superior accuracy compared to leading open-source models (e.g., Qwen2-VL, InternVL2, Pixtral) in their respective size categories.
 - On general visual question answering and text-heavy tasks, NVILA-8B and NVILA-15B achieved results comparable to or better than some proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro).
 - Specifically, NVILA-8B achieved a high score on AI2D among open-source models. Performance on reasoning and knowledge benchmarks (MMMU, RealworldQA, MathVista) generally improved with increasing model size.

- **Video Benchmarks (Table 9):** Evaluated on several video understanding benchmarks (e.g., ActivityNet-QA, LongVideoBench, MLVU, MVBench, Video-MME), including long-duration videos.
 - With its long-context capability (processing up to 256 frames via scale-then-compress), NVILA-8B achieved high performance across all evaluated video benchmarks.
 - NVILA-8B demonstrated performance levels comparable to GPT-4o mini and surpassed many larger models.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Results

- **Overall Efficiency (Figure 1):**
 - **Training Cost:** NVILA reduced training costs by 1.9-5.1x compared to LLaVA-OneVision (the only baseline with publicly disclosed training costs). For image models, it was 5.1x faster, and for video models, 1.9x faster.
 - **Inference Latency:** Compared to Qwen2-VL (8B parameters), NVILA achieved a 1.6-2.2x speedup in the pre-filling stage and a 1.2-2.8x speedup during the decoding stage.
 - **Accuracy during efficiency gains:** These efficiency improvements were achieved without compromising accuracy, with NVILA maintaining on-par or superior accuracy across benchmarks.

- **Spatial "Scale-Then-Compress" (Table 1):**
 - Using Dynamic-S² for spatial scaling improved accuracy by up to 30% on text-heavy benchmarks (e.g., DocVQA accuracy increased from 61.3% to 91.1%).
 - Compressing visual tokens via spatial pooling (3x3) introduced a moderate accuracy drop, which was largely recovered by the Visual Encoder Pre-training (VEP) stage (e.g., DocVQA accuracy recovered from 82.3% to 88.8%). This allowed for a 2.4x speedup in training and inference compared to a baseline without such compression.

- **Temporal "Scale-Then-Compress" (Table 2):**
 - Scaling from 8 to 32 frames improved Video-MME accuracy by over 5%.
 - Temporal averaging for 4x compression maintained high accuracy while reducing tokens. Further scaling to 256 frames with 8x compression resulted in a high-performing 7B model on Video-MME.

- **Dataset Pruning (Table 3):**
 - Using DeltaLoss to prune 50% of the training data resulted in a negligible performance drop on average (e.g., IM-10 score reduced from 75.6 to 75.5), while significantly outperforming random and cluster pruning. This implied a 2x speedup in training data processing without substantial accuracy loss.

- **FP8 Training (Table 4):**
 - FP8 training increased throughput by 2.0x (without gradient checkpointing) and 1.2x (with gradient checkpointing) compared to BF16, while maintaining competitive accuracy on MMMU and Video-MME.

- **Efficient Fine-Tuning (Table 5):**
 - Tuning ViT LayerNorm layers with a suitable learning rate ratio (ViT LR 5-50x smaller than LLM LR) achieved comparable accuracy to LoRA for ViT, but with better memory efficiency (19.2GB vs. 20.1GB) and higher throughput (4.5 iter/s vs. 3.4 iter/s). Using QLoRA for LLM further reduced memory to 10.2GB.

- **Efficient Deployment (Table 6, Figure 5):**
 - W8A8 quantization for the vision tower combined with W4A16 for the LLM backbone (with FP16 accumulation) reduced Time-To-First-Token (TTFT) by 2.2x for video and 1.6x for image inputs compared to a W4A16 Qwen2-VL.
 - This setup also achieved a 2.8x higher decoding throughput for image inputs and 1.2x for video inputs compared to Qwen2-VL served by vLLM.

#### 4.3. More Capabilities

- **Temporal Localization (Table 10):** NVILA-8B achieved superior results (e.g., Mean IoU of 34.8, Precision@0.5 of 32.1) on ActivityNet-RTL for temporal localization, outperforming LITA (7B/13B) and VILA-1.5-8B.
- **Robotic Navigation (Table 11):** Integrated into the NaVILA framework, NVILA-8B demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on the R2R Val-Unseen benchmark, with improved metrics such as Success Rate (SR) of 53.3% and SPL of 48.8% using only RGB inputs. The system was demonstrated to operate in real-time at 1Hz on a single laptop GPU.
- **Medical Application (Table 12):** Within the NVILA-M3 framework, NVILA-8B showed enhanced performance in various medical tasks (VQA, Report Generation, Classification) by integrating domain-expert models, achieving, for example, 85.5% accuracy on medical VQA and 92.9% BLEU-4 on medical report generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of NVILA marks a significant contribution to the field of visual language models by addressing the critical challenge of efficiency without compromising accuracy. This work's primary significance lies in demonstrating that high-performing VLMs can be developed and deployed with substantially fewer resources across their entire lifecycle.

**Key aspects of its significance include:**

- **Bridging the Efficiency-Accuracy Gap:** NVILA provides a systematic solution to the long-standing trade-off between VLM performance and resource consumption. By achieving comparable or superior accuracy to leading open and proprietary models while significantly reducing training costs (1.9-5.1x faster), prefilling latency (1.6-2.2x faster), and decoding latency (1.2-2.8x faster), NVILA establishes a new frontier for efficient VLMs.
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Research Acceleration:** The considerable reduction in training and fine-tuning costs lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers. This democratizes access to advanced VLM capabilities, enabling more institutions and individuals with limited computational resources to train, adapt, and experiment with these models. This could accelerate innovation and research in multimodal AI.
- **Broader Deployment Opportunities:** The substantial improvements in inference efficiency and memory footprint make NVILA particularly well-suited for resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices, robotics, and mobile applications. This expands the practical applicability of VLMs beyond large-scale data centers, facilitating their integration into real-world, real-time systems for tasks like autonomous navigation and interactive assistants.
- **Robust Architectural Innovations:** The "scale-then-compress" paradigm, featuring Dynamic-S² for spatial processing and temporal averaging for videos, demonstrates an effective strategy for handling high-resolution and long-context visual inputs. This approach ensures that fine-grained visual details are preserved while managing computational complexity, offering a valuable design principle for future VLM development.
- **Comprehensive Optimization Strategy:** Beyond architecture, the paper’s systematic investigation into efficiency throughout the VLM lifecycle—including dataset pruning via DeltaLoss, FP8 training, and tailored fine-tuning and deployment quantization strategies—provides a holistic blueprint for optimizing complex deep learning models. These techniques can be adapted and extended to other large-scale AI models.
- **Enabling New Capabilities:** The demonstrated capabilities in temporal localization, robotic navigation, and medical applications highlight NVILA's versatility and robustness. Its efficiency allows for real-time operation in scenarios such as robotic control, while its accuracy in specialized domains like medicine suggests potential for significant impact in critical applications.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to releasing code and models fosters reproducibility and encourages the broader AI community to build upon this work. This open-source approach can catalyze further advancements in efficient VLM research and development.

In summary, NVILA's significance lies in its comprehensive approach to VLM efficiency, providing a high-performance, resource-optimized, and versatile framework that has the potential to expand the reach and practical impact of visual language models across numerous scientific and industrial domains.
