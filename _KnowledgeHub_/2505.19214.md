# Omni-Perception: Omnidirectional Collision Avoidance for Legged Locomotion in Dynamic Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou) developed Omni-Perception, an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework enabling legged robots to perform omnidirectional collision avoidance by directly processing raw, spatio-temporal LiDAR data. The system achieved 70% success avoiding aerial obstacles and 90% for moving humans in real-world tests, significantly outperforming a native robot system, and incorporates a custom high-fidelity LiDAR simulator and a novel hierarchical perception network.

## Problem

- Legged robots often lack robust spatial awareness and rapid reactive collision avoidance in complex, dynamic 3D environments due to reliance on proprioceptive feedback or limited depth-camera-based perception.
- Existing exteroceptive methods (e.g., depth cameras) are prone to lighting sensitivity, narrow fields of view, noise, and struggle with complex 3D obstacles like overhangs or aerial clutter.
- Direct integration of raw LiDAR point clouds into end-to-end learning policies for legged robots has been largely unexplored due to data dimensionality, computational demands, and lack of high-fidelity, scalable LiDAR simulations.

## Method

- An end-to-end reinforcement learning policy leverages raw, spatio-temporal LiDAR point clouds for robust omnidirectional collision avoidance and velocity tracking in dynamic 3D environments.
- A custom, high-fidelity, GPU-accelerated LiDAR simulation toolkit, based on ==NVIDIA Warp== and Taichi, enables scalable and realistic training with support for diverse LiDAR models and efficient dynamic object rendering.
- The Proximal-Distal Risk-Aware Hierarchical Network (PD-RiskNet) processes point clouds by partitioning them into proximal (near-field) and distal (far-field) subsets, each processed by a dedicated GRU, to extract risk-aware spatial embeddings.

## Results

- The custom LiDAR simulator accurately reproduced real-world Livox Mid-360 scan patterns and outperformed Isaac Sim in efficiency, supporting 4096 environments with 16k rays at 220 ms rendering time where Isaac Sim ran out of memory.
- An ablation study showed PD-RiskNet achieved a **76.7%** success rate in dynamic obstacle avoidance simulation, significantly outperforming simpler point cloud processing methods (e.g., FPS + GRU at **30%** success).
- Real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot demonstrated **70%** success in avoiding aerial obstacles and **90%** for moving humans, compared to **0%** success for the native Unitree system in both categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly processing raw, spatio-temporal LiDAR data within an end-to-end RL framework significantly enhances a legged robot's spatial awareness and reactive agility for omnidirectional collision avoidance.
> - A hierarchical perception architecture, such as PD-RiskNet, is effective for efficiently interpreting high-dimensional LiDAR data by separately analyzing near-field and far-field information for risk assessment.
> - Developing a custom, highly optimized LiDAR simulation environment with realistic noise modeling and efficient parallel raycasting is critical for scalable training and successful sim-to-real transfer for complex LiDAR-based robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robot locomotion has witnessed significant advancements, particularly with the advent of reinforcement learning (RL), enabling robots to exhibit remarkable agility and robustness on various terrains. However, a persistent challenge remains in endowing these robots with robust spatial awareness and rapid reactive collision avoidance in complex, dynamic 3D environments.

Historically, legged robot controllers often operate predominantly on proprioceptive feedback, which, while effective for basic locomotion, renders the robots "blind" to external obstacles or unexpected changes in the environment. To overcome this limitation, exteroceptive sensing has been integrated. Depth cameras, for instance, have been widely adopted, often generating intermediate representations like 2.5D elevation maps for terrain analysis and foothold planning. While these methods have improved navigation over uneven ground, they face inherent limitations: depth cameras are sensitive to lighting conditions, possess limited fields of view, are susceptible to noise, and the intermediate map representations struggle to capture complex 3D obstacles such as overhangs or aerial clutter. Furthermore, the computational overhead of maintaining and processing these representations can be substantial within fast control loops. Approaches that decouple perception, planning, and control often result in conservative behaviors, preventing the robot from fully exploiting its inherent agility.

LiDAR sensors, in contrast, have become a cornerstone in fields like autonomous driving and mobile robot mapping due to their ability to provide dense, lighting-invariant 3D measurements. They offer rich geometric information crucial for understanding complex spatial environments. Despite this immense potential, the direct integration of raw LiDAR point clouds into end-to-end learning-based locomotion policies for legged robots has remained largely underexplored. This gap is primarily due to the high dimensionality and unstructured nature of point cloud data, the computational demands of real-time processing within high-frequency control loops, and the formidable challenge of creating high-fidelity, efficient LiDAR simulations necessary for scalable training and effective sim-to-real transfer.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap by proposing an end-to-end RL framework that leverages raw, spatio-temporal LiDAR data for omnidirectional collision avoidance in legged robots. It aims to surmount the limitations of existing depth-based or decoupled approaches, thereby pushing the capabilities of legged robots in unstructured and dynamic 3D settings beyond the current state-of-the-art.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable legged robots to achieve agile, safe, and high-speed locomotion in complex 3D environments, characterized by diverse static and dynamic obstacles, by developing a policy that performs robust omnidirectional collision avoidance.

The specific key objectives and motivations driving this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Current Perception Methods:** The authors are motivated by the shortcomings of existing perception approaches for legged robots. Proprioception-only controllers lack environmental awareness, while depth-camera-based methods suffer from lighting sensitivity, narrow fields of view, noise, and computational overhead associated with intermediate representations (e.g., elevation maps), which themselves fail to adequately represent complex 3D structures.
2. **Unlock LiDAR's Potential for End-to-End Legged Locomotion:** Despite LiDAR's proven capabilities in providing rich, lighting-invariant 3D data, its direct integration into end-to-end learning policies for legged robots has been limited. The authors aim to bridge this gap, positing that direct processing of raw LiDAR point clouds can provide the holistic spatial awareness needed for superior navigation.
3. **Achieve Omnidirectional Collision Avoidance:** A primary goal is to equip legged robots with the ability to detect and react to obstacles from any direction, including aerial clutter, ground hazards, and moving agents. This "omnidirectional" capability is crucial for safe operation in truly unstructured and dynamic environments.
4. **Develop an Efficient and Robust Perception Module (PD-RiskNet):** To facilitate direct LiDAR processing, a novel perception network is required that can efficiently interpret high-dimensional spatio-temporal point cloud data to assess environmental risks. The development of PD-RiskNet is a core objective to enable this.
5. **Enable Scalable and Realistic Training through High-Fidelity Simulation:** A significant barrier to LiDAR-based RL is the difficulty in accurately simulating LiDAR physics and parallelizing training environments. A key objective is to develop a custom, high-fidelity, cross-platform LiDAR simulation toolkit with realistic noise modeling and efficient raycasting to enable scalable training and effective sim-to-real transfer.
6. **Integrate Perception and Control for Reactive Agility:** The authors advocate for an end-to-end framework where perception is tightly coupled with control, avoiding conservative behaviors that arise from decoupled planning stages. This allows the robot to fully exploit its agility while maintaining safety.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to fundamentally advance the autonomous capabilities of legged robots, allowing them to navigate safely and effectively in human-centric and complex natural environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Omni-Perception" framework proposed in this paper is an end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) policy designed for legged robots to achieve omnidirectional collision avoidance and velocity tracking by directly processing raw LiDAR point clouds.

The methodology comprises three main components: a custom high-fidelity LiDAR simulation toolkit, the Proximal-Distal Risk-Aware Hierarchical Network (PD-RiskNet) for perception, and a risk-aware locomotion policy trained with RL.

1. **Custom LiDAR Rendering Framework:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing simulators (e.g., Isaac Gym, Gazebo) often lack comprehensive support for diverse LiDAR models (like non-repetitive scanning LiDARs) or struggle with parallel execution and efficiency, especially with dynamic objects.
 - **Backend:** The framework leverages NVIDIA Warp for GPU acceleration and Taichi for cross-platform compatibility (even CPU-only environments).
 - **Optimized Mesh Management:** To handle massively parallel environments efficiently, static geometry (terrain, fixed obstacles) in each environment has its Boundary Volume Hierarchy (BVH) built only once. For dynamic entities (robot, moving obstacles), their meshes are aggregated into a *single, global dynamic mesh* across all environments. Only the vertex positions of this global dynamic mesh are updated at each timestep, avoiding the computationally expensive process of rebuilding BVHs for each dynamic object in every environment.
 - **Synchronous Update and Raycasting:** This centralized update mechanism for dynamic meshes, synchronized with the sensor update frequency, allows all parallel environments to query up-to-date dynamic geometry efficiently.
 - **LiDAR Model Support:** The framework supports a wide range of commercial LiDARs, including non-repetitive scanning Livox series (simulating their characteristic scan patterns using real device data) and conventional rotating LiDARs from Velodyne and Ouster.

2. **PD-RiskNet: Processing Spatio-Temporal Point Clouds:**
 - This novel perception module is at the core of the framework, interpreting raw LiDAR point clouds to assess environmental risk.
 - **Point Cloud Partitioning:** Incoming raw point clouds (P_raw) are divided into two subsets based on a vertical angle threshold:
 - **Proximal Point Cloud (P_proximal):** Represents the near-field environment, characterized by higher density and local geometric details.
 - **Distal Point Cloud (P_distal):** Covers the far-field environment, typically sparser with larger range variations.
 - **Proximal Pathway:** To process P_proximal efficiently, Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is used to obtain a representative sparse subset. These points are then sorted by spherical coordinates and fed into a dedicated Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). During training, this GRU's feature extraction is supervised using a "Privileged Height" signal, guiding it to learn representations relevant to local terrain and risks. A history of N_hist frames (10 frames as per Appendix) is processed.
 - **Distal Pathway:** For P_distal, Average Downsampling is applied to manage sparsity and reduce outlier influence, creating a more uniform representation. This downsampled data, combined from current and preceding N_hist-1 frames, is also sorted by spherical coordinates and processed by a separate GRU to extract features capturing dynamic environmental context at range.
 - **Output:** The embeddings from both proximal (187 features) and distal (64 features) GRUs are concatenated to form the LiDAR-derived spatial embeddings (f_PD).

3. **Risk-Aware Locomotion Policy:**
 - **Policy Architecture:** The locomotion policy is an MLP (Actor network) with hidden layers of and ELU activation functions.
 - **Observation Space:** The policy receives concatenated inputs:
 - Processed LiDAR features (f_PD) from PD-RiskNet.
 - Proprioceptive state history (joint positions/velocities, base linear/angular velocities, projected gravity).
 - Task command (desired linear and angular velocities).
 - **Action Space:** The policy outputs target joint positions, which are then fed to a low-level PD controller.
 - **Reward Function:** Designed to encourage velocity tracking and active collision avoidance:
 - **Linear Velocity Tracking with Avoidance (r_vel_avoid):** Encourages tracking a modified target linear velocity. This target combines the external command (v_cmd) with a dynamically computed *avoidance velocity* (V_avoid). V_avoid is calculated by dividing the robot's 360° horizontal surroundings into N_sec (36) angular sectors. If the minimum obstacle distance (d_j) in a sector falls below a threshold, an avoidance velocity component (V_avoid,j) is generated, pointing away from that sector, with magnitude increasing exponentially as proximity increases. The total V_avoid is the vector sum, and the reward penalizes deviation from (v_cmd + V_avoid).
 - **Distance Maximization (r_rays):** Rewards maintaining safety by maximizing the capped distances of n representative distal LiDAR rays, pushing the robot towards open areas.
 - **Auxiliary Rewards:** Standard rewards for stable locomotion, including penalties for z-velocity, foot stumble, link collision, joint limit violation, high joint torques/velocities/accelerations, and action smoothing.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Applied during PPO training to enhance sim-to-real transfer. This includes randomizing robot physical parameters (mass, CoM, friction, motor strength, joint offsets, gravity offset, proprioception latency) and LiDAR perception characteristics (random masking of 10% of points to small distances, and 10% noise added to measured distances).

This comprehensive methodology, integrating a custom high-fidelity simulation environment, a novel perception network, and a carefully designed risk-aware reward function within an end-to-end RL framework, allows for robust and agile legged locomotion with omnidirectional collision avoidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the efficacy of the Omni-Perception framework across multiple dimensions, from simulator fidelity to real-world deployment.

1. **LiDAR Fidelity Evaluation:**
 - The custom LiDAR simulator was validated by comparing its output (Livox Mid-360 model) to real-world data from a Unitree G1 robot and to Isaac Sim LiDAR.
 - **Finding:** The simulated point clouds closely matched the real-world data, accurately capturing the non-repetitive scan pattern of the Livox sensor and critical effects like robot self-occlusion. This high fidelity is crucial for minimizing the sim-to-real perception gap.

2. **Comparison with Existing Simulators and Efficiency Analysis:**
 - The custom LiDAR rendering framework was compared against established platforms like NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Gazebo in terms of features and rendering speed.
 - **Finding:** The custom framework offers superior feature support, including hybrid solid-state LiDARs (non-repetitive scan), dynamic irregular objects, and self-occlusion, which are often lacking in other platforms (Table 2). In terms of efficiency, Omni-Perception's simulator significantly outperforms Isaac Sim, particularly with increasing numbers of rays and parallel environments (Table 1). For example, with 16k rays and 4096 environments, Isaac Sim runs out of memory (OOM), whereas Omni-Perception maintains a rendering time of 220.0 ± 1.1 ms. This demonstrates the framework's scalability for large-scale RL training.

3. **PD-RiskNet Ablation Study:**
 - To validate the design of PD-RiskNet, an ablation study was performed in simulation with dynamic obstacles over 30 trials. Four approaches for processing point cloud features were compared.
 - **Finding:**
 - Direct MLP processing of raw point clouds failed due to Out of Memory (OOM), highlighting the computational challenge of unprocessed point clouds.
 - Simpler downsampling approaches like FPS + MLP (33.3% success rate, 93.3% collision rate) and FPS + GRU (30.0% success rate, 70.0% collision rate) performed very poorly, with robots frequently timing out or getting stuck rather than successfully navigating.
 - **Our PD-RiskNet achieved a significantly higher success rate of 76.7%**, albeit with a collision rate of 56.7%. This demonstrates that the hierarchical, risk-aware processing with separate proximal and distal pathways is crucial for effective spatial awareness and collision avoidance, far surpassing simpler methods.

4. **Real-World Deployment:**
 - The Omni-Perception policy was deployed on a physical legged robot (Unitree Go2) and tested in various challenging real-world scenarios, including complex terrain (rocky terrain, dense grass) and dynamic human interference.
 - **Finding:**
 - The robot successfully demonstrated robust omnidirectional obstacle avoidance and reactive behaviors (Figure 1, Figure 5).
 - A quantitative comparison against the native Unitree system across 30 trials for different obstacle types revealed significant improvements by Omni-Perception (Table 4):
 - **Aerial obstacles:** Omni-Perception achieved 70% success, while the native Unitree system failed completely (0%). This highlights Omni-Perception's superior 3D spatial awareness.
 - **Moving humans:** Omni-Perception achieved 90% success, whereas the native system again failed (0%), showcasing its robustness in dynamic environments.
 - **Static obstacles:** Both systems achieved 100% success.
 - **Small obstacles:** Omni-Perception had 83% success, slightly lower than the native system's 100%, indicating a potential area for improvement, possibly related to the stated limitations of small object perception.
 - Overall, the real-world experiments validated the framework's ability to enable agile and safe locomotion in challenging, dynamic 3D settings, demonstrating strong velocity tracking and robust omnidirectional avoidance capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Omni-Perception framework represents a significant step forward in the field of legged robot locomotion and autonomous navigation. By directly integrating raw LiDAR point clouds into an end-to-end reinforcement learning policy, this work addresses a critical gap in the existing research landscape and offers several profound implications:

1. **Breaking New Ground in LiDAR-Driven Legged Locomotion:** This is presented as the first framework to achieve 3D spatial awareness and robust omnidirectional collision avoidance for legged robots by directly processing raw, spatio-temporal LiDAR point clouds within an end-to-end RL architecture. This paradigm shift moves beyond the limitations of depth-camera-based systems and traditional decoupled planning, paving the way for more responsive and agile behaviors.
2. **Enhanced Spatial Awareness and Robustness:** The ability to leverage the dense and lighting-invariant 3D information from LiDAR enables legged robots to perceive and react to a much wider range of obstacles, including aerial clutter, complex non-planar structures, and dynamic agents, with greater reliability than previous methods. The real-world results, particularly in avoiding aerial obstacles and moving humans, underscore this enhanced robustness.
3. **Novel Perception Architecture (PD-RiskNet):** The introduction of PD-RiskNet, with its hierarchical processing of proximal and distal point clouds and risk-aware feature extraction, is a key technical contribution. This architecture efficiently handles the high dimensionality of LiDAR data, extracting relevant features for collision assessment while being amenable to end-to-end learning. The ablation study clearly demonstrates its necessity and effectiveness.
4. **High-Fidelity, Scalable Simulation for Robotics Research:** The custom LiDAR simulation toolkit developed as part of this work is a valuable contribution in itself. Its high fidelity, efficiency (outperforming Isaac Sim), and cross-platform compatibility address a crucial bottleneck in training complex perception-driven policies. This toolkit can accelerate future research in LiDAR-based robot learning by providing a robust and scalable training environment.
5. **Expanding Legged Robot Capabilities and Applications:** By enabling truly agile and safe navigation in unstructured, dynamic 3D environments, Omni-Perception broadens the potential applications of legged robots. This could include deployment in challenging domains such as search and rescue, industrial inspection, logistics in cluttered warehouses, or even personal assistance in human-centric spaces, where high mobility and reliable collision avoidance are paramount.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper openly acknowledges certain limitations, which also hint at future research avenues:
- **Environmental Geometry Challenges:** Performance can degrade in highly unstructured vegetation like dense grass, where LiDAR struggles to extract reliable geometric features. A potential solution proposed is introducing semantic segmentation into the framework to better categorize environmental elements.
- **Sim2Real Fidelity Trade-off:** The use of sampling strategies in simulation, while effective for transfer, inherently simplifies fine-grained geometric details. This could be a limiting factor for extremely precise navigation around very small or sparse objects (as seen in some failure cases, e.g., thin rods). Future work could focus on improving simulation fidelity, developing more advanced domain randomization/adaptation techniques, or incorporating online learning for on-the-fly policy refinement.

In summary, Omni-Perception represents a significant advance by demonstrating how direct, end-to-end learning from raw LiDAR data can unlock unprecedented levels of spatial awareness and reactive agility in legged robots. The technical innovations in both perception and simulation offer a robust foundation for future developments in autonomous legged locomotion.
