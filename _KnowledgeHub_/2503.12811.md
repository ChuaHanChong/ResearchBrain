# A Multi-Power Law for Loss Curve Prediction Across Learning Rate Schedules

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and partners derive a Multi-Power Law (MPL) that accurately predicts the training loss curves of large language models under different learning rate schedules, enabling optimization of training efficiency while reducing the computational cost of hyperparameter tuning through theoretical analysis and empirical validation across multiple model architectures.

## Problem

- Existing scaling laws don't account for learning rate schedules, limiting their practical utility
- Hyperparameter tuning for large language models is extremely resource-intensive
- Understanding the relationship between learning rate schedules and training loss evolution

## Method

- Developed ==Multi-Power Law (MPL)== combining ==learning rate sum matching== with ==decay-induced loss reduction==
- Created a theoretical framework explaining power-law behavior in optimization landscapes
- Implemented schedule optimization based on loss predictions

## Results

- MPL accurately predicts loss curves for unseen schedules and longer training horizons
- Discovered learning rate schedules that outperform standard cosine decay
- Provided theoretical justification through analysis of quadratic loss functions
- Demonstrated applicability across different model sizes and architectures

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training runs with same cumulative learning rate tend to have similar losses
> - Loss reduction exhibits power-law relationship with learning rate decay
> - Optimized schedules naturally resemble Warmup-Stable-Decay pattern

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Hyperparameter Optimization and Scaling Laws:** This work directly addresses a significant challenge in training large language models (LLMs): the resource-intensive process of hyperparameter tuning. It builds upon the existing body of research on scaling laws, such as the Chinchilla scaling law, which aims to predict model performance based on model size and training data.
- **Learning Rate Scheduling:** The paper specifically focuses on the impact of learning rate (LR) schedules, a critical hyperparameter often overlooked by existing scaling laws. It recognizes the limitations of previous approaches that either ignored LR schedules or fixed them to a specific family, such as cosine decay.
- **Empirical Analysis of LLM Training Dynamics:** The research aligns with the trend of empirically analyzing the training dynamics of LLMs to gain insights into their behavior and improve training efficiency. This approach is particularly relevant given the computational cost of training LLMs, which makes exhaustive experimentation impractical.
- **Connection to Recent LR Schedule Research:** The paper acknowledges and builds upon recent work on LR schedules, particularly the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedule. The fact that the Multi-Power Law (MPL) can be used to discover a schedule resembling WSD provides further validation of its importance and potential.
- **Theoretical Foundations of Scaling Laws:** There has been work trying to explain the scaling laws in terms of the data manifold dimension or information theory. Furthermore, several works analyzed the scaling laws of linear models, assuming certain power-law properties in the input data or ground truth functions. The theoretical component of the analyzed research connects to this broader effort by providing a theoretical explanation for their scaling law assuming power-law properties in the optimization landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop an empirical law that accurately predicts the training loss curve of LLMs under different LR schedules. This is referred to as the "schedule-aware loss curve prediction" problem.
- **Motivation:**
 - Reduce the cost of hyperparameter tuning for LLMs by providing a formula that can predict the impact of different LR schedules on model performance.
 - Address the limitations of existing scaling laws that do not account for LR schedules, limiting their ability to provide practical guidance.
 - Enable the discovery of better LR schedules that can improve training efficiency and potentially outperform commonly used schedules like cosine decay.
 - Gain a deeper understanding of the relationship between LR schedules and the evolution of training loss in LLM pretraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Empirical Derivation:** The authors use a "bottom-up" approach to derive the Multi-Power Law.
 - They start by analyzing simple two-stage LR schedules and observing the relationship between LR changes and loss reduction.
 - They then extend their analysis to multi-stage schedules and finally generalize to arbitrary schedules.
 - Throughout the derivation, they emphasize the importance of "learning rate sum matching," which suggests that training runs with the same cumulative LR tend to have similar losses.
- **Multi-Power Law Formulation:** The proposed Multi-Power Law (MPL) combines a power law based on the sum of learning rates with additional power laws to account for the loss reduction effect induced by LR decay. The correction term captures the effect of LR decay in further reducing the loss.
- **Extensive Validation:** The authors validate the MPL on various model sizes, architectures, and training horizons. They compare its performance to existing scaling laws and baselines.
- **LR Schedule Optimization:** They use the MPL to predict the final loss of different LR schedules and optimize the schedule to minimize the predicted loss.
- **Theoretical Analysis:** In order to justify their claims, they present a theoretical analysis for quadratic loss functions, demonstrating that the Multi-Power Law emerges when the Hessian and noise covariance matrices exhibit power-law structures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Accuracy of MPL:** The Multi-Power Law accurately predicts the loss curves of LLMs under different LR schedules, even for unseen schedules and longer training horizons.
- **Superiority over Existing Scaling Laws:** The MPL outperforms existing scaling laws, such as the Chinchilla scaling law, by incorporating LR dependency and predicting the entire loss curve.
- **Discovery of Better LR Schedules:** By minimizing the predicted final loss using the MPL, the authors are able to find an optimized LR schedule that outperforms the commonly used cosine schedule.
- **Resemblance to WSD:** The optimized schedule bears some resemblance to the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedule, but achieves a slightly lower final loss.
- **Power Law in Loss Reduction:** The loss reduction term in the MPL exhibits a power-law relationship with the LR decay, which is different from the exponential decay assumed by some other models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Improved Hyperparameter Tuning Efficiency:** The MPL can significantly reduce the cost of hyperparameter tuning for LLMs by providing a formula that can predict the impact of different LR schedules on model performance.
- **Enhanced Training Efficiency:** The discovery of better LR schedules using the MPL can improve training efficiency and potentially lead to faster convergence and better model performance.
- **Deeper Understanding of LLM Training Dynamics:** The MPL provides insights into the relationship between LR schedules and the evolution of training loss in LLM pretraining.
- **Potential for Automation:** The MPL could be used to automate the process of LR schedule design, reducing the need for manual tuning and experimentation.
- **Scalability:** The research is relevant to the training of even larger models and datasets, where the cost of experimentation is extremely high.
- **Practical Applications:** The proposed multi-power scaling law is arguably more general, as it can predict the entire loss curves for various LR schedules, even including discontinuous and non-monotonic ones. This allows us to optimize the LR schedule by minimizing the predicted loss over all possible LR decay schedules, which may not be easily achievable using other methods.

**Overall Assessment:**

The research paper presents a significant contribution to the field of large language model training. The Multi-Power Law is a novel and effective approach for predicting loss curves and optimizing LR schedules. The empirical validation is extensive and the results are promising. This work has the potential to have a significant impact on the efficiency and effectiveness of training LLMs. There is also theoretical evidence that further justifies the claims in the paper.
