# Steerable Visual Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> SteerViT introduces a method to equip any pretrained Vision Transformer with language-steerable visual representations by integrating lightweight gated cross-attention layers for early text-visual fusion. This approach achieves a Pareto improvement by enabling fine-grained control over visual features with natural language, demonstrated by a 96.0% top-1 retrieval accuracy on the CORE benchmark, while preserving the original encoder's representation quality with only 21 million trainable parameters.

## Problem

- Pretrained Vision Transformers (ViTs) often exhibit a saliency bias, focusing on prominent objects and limiting utility for less salient or specific visual concepts.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer prompt guidance but produce language-centric representations with high computational costs, making them less effective for general visual tasks requiring high fidelity.
- Existing vision-language models like CLIP use late fusion, where text does not influence the foundational visual feature extraction process, limiting true steerability of visual features.

## Method

- **SteerViT** integrates a frozen pretrained ViT backbone with a frozen text encoder, using a ==multimodal adapter== to project text embeddings into a visual-aligned space.
- ==Gated cross-attention layers== are inserted into every other Transformer block of the frozen ViT, allowing visual patch tokens to query adapted text tokens for ==early fusion==.
- The model is trained using a ==referential segmentation pretext task== on diverse datasets, optimizing ==soft cross-entropy loss== to route textual information to corresponding visual patches.

## Results

- **SteerViT** achieved **96.0%** top-1 retrieval accuracy on the new CORE benchmark for text-conditioned retrieval of non-salient objects, significantly outperforming DINOv2 (**43.7%**).
- On the MOSAIC Localization benchmark, **SteerViT** attained a PR-AUC of **50.2%** for redirecting attention to specific objects within composite images, compared to DINOv2's **14.3%**.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot domain transfer, achieving **82.1** PRO on MVTec AD for anomaly segmentation and outperforming dedicated methods on the VisA dataset, by using anomaly-focused text prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Early vision-language fusion via gated cross-attention is crucial for enabling fine-grained, text-driven steerability of visual features while preserving the base encoder's quality.
> - The level of detail in natural language prompts directly controls the granularity of the resulting steerable visual representations, allowing for instance-level discrimination.
> - A lightweight adaptation of existing ViTs can achieve a Pareto improvement in steerability and representation quality, offering an efficient alternative to large, resource-intensive MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Pretrained Vision Transformers (ViTs), such as DINOv2 and MAE, have established themselves as foundational models for generating generic visual features applicable across various downstream tasks, including image retrieval, classification, and segmentation. However, these representations are typically "query-agnostic," meaning they process images without external guidance, leading them to predominantly focus on the most salient visual elements within an image. This focus often neglects less prominent or contextually important objects, a phenomenon potentially influenced by common dataset biases toward central, dominant objects.

In contrast, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) integrate textual prompts to guide their processing. While MLLMs offer a degree of steerability, their representations tend to be "language-centric," potentially diminishing visual fidelity for tasks requiring precise visual understanding. Other vision-language models, such as CLIP, typically employ "late fusion," where visual and textual features are encoded independently before being combined. This architecture limits the influence of text on the initial visual encoding process.

The paper identifies a gap: a lack of visual representations that simultaneously offer "steerability"—the capacity to adapt to textual prompts to focus on specific, potentially non-salient concepts—and maintain high "representation quality" suitable for diverse generic visual tasks. Existing approaches generally exhibit a trade-off between these two desiderata. Steerable Visual Representations (SteerViT) positions itself as a new class of models designed to bridge this gap by enabling fine-grained, language-guided control over visual features while preserving the robust quality of underlying ViT representations. This work proposes an alternative paradigm to MLLMs by conditioning the visual encoder on language, rather than conditioning the language model on vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop visual representations whose global and local features can be precisely steered using natural language prompts. This objective is driven by several motivations and aims to address specific limitations of existing visual representation models:

- **Addressing Saliency Bias:** Current generic visual representations, particularly from pretrained ViTs like DINOv2, tend to concentrate on the most prominent or salient objects in an image. This behavior is often attributed to dataset biases, where dominant objects are more frequently annotated or appear more centrally. However, many practical applications, such as fine-grained localization or specialized retrieval, require models to focus on less prominent or specific concepts within an image (e.g., a "remote control" on a "bookshelf" rather than a "cat" occupying the foreground). The motivation is to overcome this inherent saliency bias, allowing representations to adapt to specific user-defined interests.
- **Emulating Human Perception:** The authors draw inspiration from human visual processing, noting that human gaze patterns and interpretation of images can be significantly influenced by prior textual priming or task-specific instructions. This "top-down, task-guided attention" in human vision suggests a potential benefit for AI systems that can similarly modulate their visual encoding based on linguistic cues.
- **Combining Steerability with Representation Quality:** The research posits two core desiderata for ideal visual representations that are not simultaneously met by existing models:
 - **Steerability:** The representation should be adaptable to input text, enabling it to encode any desired object or concept within an image, irrespective of its saliency. Furthermore, it should be able to organize the embedding space based on specific textual attributes or categories.
 - **Representation Quality:** The steered representation must retain its effectiveness for a broad range of general visual tasks, including retrieval, classification, and segmentation, without degradation in performance. This implies that the textual conditioning should enhance, rather than compromise, the underlying visual feature richness.
- **Limitations of Current Approaches:**
 - **Unimodal ViTs (e.g., DINOv2):** Provide high-quality generic visual features but lack any mechanism for external textual guidance, making them unsteerable.
 - **Cross-modal Encoders (e.g., CLIP):** While they operate in a shared vision-language space, their visual features are extracted independently, and textual input cannot influence the visual encoding process at inference time (late fusion).
 - **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Offer some degree of steerability through prompts. However, the fusion mechanism, often occurring early in the language model, can lead to language-dominant representations that may have diminished visual fidelity and limited precise controllability for visual tasks. These models also typically involve billions of parameters, incurring substantial computational costs.
 - **Open-Vocabulary (OV) Localization Models (e.g., SAM3):** Are highly steerable for localization tasks, but their intermediate representations are specialized and may not generalize well to other diverse visual tasks.

The paper's motivation is to develop an efficient and general framework that overcomes these limitations, offering a method to "steer" visual representations with language effectively and efficiently, while preserving the integrity and versatility of the visual features.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Steerable Visual Representations (SteerViT) framework is designed to integrate textual conditioning directly into the visual encoding process of a pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT). The methodology centers on an "early fusion" approach, where language influences the visual feature extraction at intermediate layers.

#### 3.1. Architecture

SteerViT comprises four main components:

- **A. Visual Encoder:** A frozen, pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT), such as DINOv2 ViT-B/14, forms the backbone. This ViT processes an input image \(X_v \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}\) into a sequence of \(N\) patch tokens \(Z_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_v}\) and potentially a `[CLS]` token. Crucially, all original ViT parameters are kept frozen throughout the training process, meaning any new capacity or steerability arises exclusively from the newly introduced multimodal adapter components. The approach is demonstrated to work with other ViT backbones like SigLIP and MAE.
- **B. Text Encoder:** A frozen, pretrained text encoder, specifically RoBERTa-Large, is used to encode input textual prompts \(X_t\). This encoder produces token-level embeddings \(Z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_t}\), where \(L\) is the number of text tokens and \(d_t\) is the text embedding dimension. Freezing the text encoder maintains its linguistic knowledge.
- **C. Multimodal Adapter:** Each text embedding \(Z_t\) is \(\ell_2\)-normalized and then projected into a visual-aligned embedding space \(H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_v}\) using a trainable two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). This MLP facilitates the transformation of textual features into a format compatible with the visual encoder's internal dimensions.
- **D. Gated Cross-Attention Layers:** To enable the fusion of textual conditioning, lightweight trainable cross-attention (CA) layers are interleaved within the frozen ViT blocks. These CA layers are inserted into every other Transformer encoder block (e.g., 6 CA layers for a 12-block ViT-B). In these CA layers, the visual patch tokens \(Z_v^{(\ell)}\) from layer \(\ell\) serve as queries, while the adapted text tokens \(H_t\) serve as keys and values. The output of the cross-attention, denoted \(\hat{Z}_v^{(\ell)}\), is then integrated into the ViT's residual stream using a `tanh` gate with a layer-specific learnable scalar \(\alpha_\ell\). The gate is initialized such that \(\alpha_\ell = 0\), rendering the model identical to the frozen ViT at initialization (since \(\tanh(0) = 0\)). During optimization, \(\alpha_\ell\) can move away from zero, gradually activating the conditioning pathway and allowing the visual encoding process to incorporate language-based clues.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

To compel the visual encoder to leverage textual clues, SteerViT employs a referential segmentation pretext task. Given an image \(X_v\) and a prompt \(X_t\) referring to a target object, the model is trained to predict which visual patches correspond to the described region.

- **Ground Truth:** The ground-truth \(y_i\) for each patch is defined as the fraction of foreground pixels from a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask, which is then patchified to align with the ViT’s \(n \times n\) grid.
- **Loss Function:** A linear classifier head maps each patch representation \(Z_v^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^d\) to a mask probability \(p_i\) via a softmax function. The model is trained using a soft cross-entropy loss \(L = - \sum_{i=1}^{n \times n} y_i \log p_i\). This loss encourages the cross-attention layers to effectively route textual information into the corresponding visual patch tokens, thereby producing steerable representations. The patch-level segmentation approach simplifies training and avoids the need for a separate pixel-level decoder.

#### 3.3. Training Data

SteerViT is trained on a diverse mixture of referential segmentation and grounding datasets to expose it to varied visual domains and textual expression styles. The datasets include:
- RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg
- Visual Genome (preprocessed as in MDETR)
- LVIS
- Mapillary Vistas (with synthetic referential expressions from Describe Anything)

This combined dataset totals 162,000 unique images and 2.28 million image-text pairs, providing a wide range of scene complexities, expression lengths, and visual domains.

The overall approach introduces only approximately 21 million trainable parameters through the cross-attention layers and multimodal adapter, while keeping both the visual and text encoders frozen. This design aims for computational efficiency while enabling robust multimodal interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical validation of SteerViT's properties across several benchmarks and tasks.

#### 4.1. COnditional REtrieval (CORE): Steering Global Semantics with Text

The CORE benchmark measures how effectively a model's global features can be steered by text to retrieve images containing a specified, potentially non-salient, object within a scene.
- **Observation:** Query-agnostic encoders (MAE, DINOv2) exhibit low accuracy (20% and 44% respectively), failing to retrieve images based on the specified object due to their focus on dominant scene concepts. Cross-modal models (CLIP, SigLIP) with late fusion show negligible improvements.
- **SteerViT Performance:** SteerViT achieves 96% retrieval accuracy, demonstrating that textual prompts effectively shift its global representation from scene-level similarities to the queried object. UMAP visualizations confirm that SteerViT forms object-specific clusters when prompted, unlike DINOv2 which clusters by scene.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** MLLMs and OV localization models are steerable but less efficient (MLLMs with billions of parameters) or specialized (OV localization). SteerViT substantially outperforms InternVL3-1B and InternVL3-2B by 49 and 20 percentage points, respectively, with only 21M trainable parameters. SAM3 approaches SteerViT's retrieval accuracy but its features lack generality for other tasks.
- **Prompt Dependence:** Conditioning SteerViT on a random, incorrect object class drastically reduces its performance, confirming that its steerability is genuinely driven by the prompt.
- **Finding 1:** SteerViT successfully steers its visual features with text towards queried concepts, whereas standard encoders predominantly focus on prominent visual cues. This capability also translates to real-world conditional retrieval on the GeneCIS benchmark, where SteerViT achieves 25.4% R@1, outperforming DINOv2 (9.6%) and a task-specialized baseline (18.7%).

#### 4.2. MOSAIC Localization: Text Enables Targeted Attention

This benchmark assesses SteerViT's ability to route and aggregate global representations via attention to query-relevant tokens within a composite image mosaic.
- **Observation:** DINOv2's attention maps consistently focus on the most prominent objects in the mosaic. In contrast, SteerViT, when prompted, redirects its attention to the specified object, even if it is small or less salient, and can attend to multiple instances of the same object class.
- **Quantitative Results:** SteerViT achieves a PR-AUC of 50.2%, substantially higher than DINOv2's 14.3%, indicating effective text-guided attention redirection.

#### 4.3. Preserving Visual Representation Quality while Steering

This section evaluates whether steerability comes at the cost of overall representation quality, measured by performance on generic vision tasks.
- **Trade-off Analysis:** A Pareto frontier analysis reveals that OV localization models, while steerable, produce specialized features with poor performance on generic tasks. MLLMs offer moderate performance but are computationally intensive. Query-agnostic encoders provide rich features but no steerability.
- **SteerViT's Position:** SteerViT occupies a distinct position on this frontier, achieving high steerability while preserving the representation quality of the underlying ViT for tasks like fine-grained classification and dense segmentation.
- **Control Knob:** The `tanh`-gated cross-attention mechanism acts as a continuous control knob. Scaling the learned gating parameters `αℓ` allows interpolation between the original ViT and a fully text-conditioned state. An optimal scaling factor of ω=0.6 for DINOv2 and SigLIP even slightly improves their original representation quality while unlocking high steerability. For MAE, representation quality monotonically improves with ω.
- **Finding 2:** SteerViT establishes a class of visual representations that can be steered with text without compromising the inherent representation quality of the foundational vision encoder.

#### 4.4. Text Specificity Guides Semantic Granularity

Using the Personal Object Discrimination Suite (PODS), the research investigates how the level of detail in textual prompts influences the granularity of steerable representations.
- **Observation:** Coarse prompts (e.g., "mug") yield slightly worse performance than vanilla DINOv2 for discriminating specific instances. However, enriching prompts with instance-level descriptions (e.g., MLLM-generated detailed descriptions) significantly boosts performance (58.1% PR-AUC), surpassing DINOv2 fine-tuned on synthetic data (48% PR-AUC) and approaching fine-tuned DINOv2 performance on retrieval tasks (77.3% vs 79.6% NDCG). This highlights SteerViT's ability to leverage detailed prompts for fine-grained distinction.
- **Finding 3:** The granularity of steerable visual representations is directly controlled by the level of detail provided in the text conditioning.

#### 4.5. Visualizing and Analyzing the Steered Embedding Space

UMAP projections of PASCAL-VOC images demonstrate how text conditioning reshapes the embedding space topology.
- **Semantic Hierarchy:** DINOv2 embeddings rigidly cluster by object class. When conditioned on "animal," SteerViT forms macro-clusters for animal and non-animal classes, while preserving fine-grained structures within these groups. Further conditioning on "bird" increases bird/non-bird separability without destroying higher-level semantic macro-separation.
- **Compositional Attributes:** Conditioning on a compositional attribute like "eye" causes animate classes (including "person") to cluster together, overriding purely semantic category boundaries.
- **Finding 4:** SteerViT can dynamically reorganize its embedding space using text, enabling control over both the level of semantic abstraction and the criteria for clustering.

#### 4.6. Text Facilitates Domain Transfer

The adaptability of SteerViT to novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks is demonstrated through zero-shot anomaly segmentation (AS) on the MVTec AD and VisA industrial datasets.
- **Performance:** SteerViT achieves 82.1 PRO on MVTec AD, matching dedicated AS methods (e.g., FADE at 84.5) and significantly outperforming off-the-shelf segmentation models (SAM3 at 54.5). On VisA, it surpasses FADE in ROC_P (92.1 vs. 91.5) and PRO (82.0 vs. 79.3).
- **Finding 5:** SteerViT can utilize natural language to transfer the capabilities of rich unimodal vision encoders to OOD domains without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning.

#### 4.7. Analysis of SteerViT (Ablations)

Ablation studies investigate key architectural and training design choices:
- **Early vs. Late Fusion:** Early fusion is critical for fine-grained tasks (e.g., PODS performance significantly reduced with late fusion), as it allows text to influence intermediate visual encoding.
- **Role of Gating:** The zero-initialized `tanh` gate is crucial for preserving frozen features, with ungated cross-attention leading to performance drops across multiple metrics.
- **Language Projector:** A two-layer MLP for text projection is more effective than a single linear layer for modality alignment.
- **Generalization Across Backbones:** SteerViT consistently improves steerability across different ViT backbones (DINOv2, SigLIP, MAE), with larger gains observed for less semantically rich base features (e.g., MAE).
- **Training Objective:** Referential segmentation with patch-level masks consistently outperforms a pointing objective (Gaussian kernel at bounding box center) by teaching "what" an object looks like, not just "where."
- **FFN:** Omitting the gated feed-forward network (FFN) from the cross-attention blocks provides comparable or better performance while reducing parameter count, particularly for MAE.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Steerable Visual Representations (SteerViT) presents a significant development in multimodal AI, addressing a recognized limitation in existing visual representation models. The work's primary significance lies in its ability to simultaneously achieve strong representational steerability with natural language and high visual representation quality, a combination previously unattained by existing approaches.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**
- **Bridging a Performance Gap:** SteerViT effectively bridges the gap between generic, high-quality, but unsteerable Vision Transformers and steerable but often specialized or visually compromised multimodal models. This is demonstrated by its performance on the proposed CORE benchmark and its ability to maintain or improve performance on standard vision tasks.
- **Efficiency in Multimodal Understanding:** By employing an "early fusion" mechanism with lightweight cross-attention layers inserted into frozen ViT blocks, SteerViT achieves high steerability with a comparatively small number of trainable parameters (approximately 21 million). This contrasts with large MLLMs that require billions of parameters for moderate steerability and often incur higher computational costs. This efficiency suggests a more scalable path for integrating language guidance into visual processing.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Zero-Shot Transfer:** The research demonstrates that text prompts enable SteerViT to generalize zero-shot to diverse, out-of-distribution tasks, such as personalized object discrimination and industrial anomaly detection. In these tasks, SteerViT matches or outperforms dedicated, specialized methods without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. This capability suggests a paradigm shift where rich unimodal vision encoders can be flexibly adapted to new domains and tasks through linguistic conditioning alone.
- **Flexible Feature Control:** SteerViT exhibits precise control over the granularity and organization of its embedding space. Text specificity can dictate semantic abstraction, ranging from coarse categories to fine-grained instance distinctions. Furthermore, the model can reorganize its feature space based on compositional attributes (e.g., shared object parts), demonstrating a deeper understanding of visual properties beyond hierarchical categories.
- **New Paradigm for Vision-Language Integration:** The work proposes conditioning the visual encoder on language as an alternative to conditioning the language model on vision, which is common in MLLMs. This approach is shown to yield vision-centric multimodal representations with robust visual fidelity.

The potential impact of SteerViT is broad. It could lead to more adaptable and user-friendly vision systems where natural language serves as an intuitive and efficient mechanism for directing visual attention and interpretation. This could benefit applications requiring fine-grained object search, context-aware analysis, and adaptable anomaly detection in various industries, ultimately making powerful visual AI models more controllable and accessible for diverse, real-world scenarios.
