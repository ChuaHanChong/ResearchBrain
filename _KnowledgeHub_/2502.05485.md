# HAMSTER: Hierarchical Action Models For Open-World Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HAMSTER, a hierarchical robot manipulation system developed by researchers from NVIDIA, University of Washington, and University of Southern California, combines large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level 3D-aware policies. This approach enables robust open-world generalization by fine-tuning the VLM on diverse, inexpensive off-domain data, leading to a 20% absolute improvement in success rate over monolithic VLA models across various generalization axes.

## Problem

- Monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with data scarcity, requiring vast amounts of expensive on-robot observation-action data.
- Monolithic VLAs often operate at low inference frequencies, hindering dexterous and dynamic manipulation tasks.
- Smaller, specialized robot policy models are brittle to environmental changes and struggle with sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- A hierarchical architecture where a high-level VLM predicts coarse 2D paths and a low-level 3D-aware policy executes precise actions.
- The high-level VLM is fine-tuned on diverse, inexpensive off-domain data (e.g., action-free videos, simulation data) to enable cross-domain transfer.
- The low-level policy is conditioned on the VLM's 2D path along with 3D and proprioceptive inputs and trained on a small, task-specific real-robot dataset.

## Results

- HAMSTER achieves an average of **20%** absolute improvement in success rate over state-of-the-art monolithic VLAs (OpenVLA) across seven axes of real-world generalization.
- The system demonstrates strong generalization across unseen object-goal combinations, visual changes, novel objects, and complex long-horizon tasks like unfolding towels.
- HAMSTER shows high data efficiency for the low-level policy, achieving twice the success rate of standard 3D-DA models with **50%** less training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical decomposition, using a 2D path as an intermediate representation, allows effective utilization of abundant 'off-domain' data to fine-tune high-level VLMs for real-world robotic generalization.
> - Decoupling VLM inference from high-frequency action prediction enables the use of powerful VLMs for high-level reasoning without compromising the speed and dexterity of low-level control.
> - Fine-tuning a VLM on diverse cross-domain data significantly improves its ability to generate meaningful paths for novel real-world robotic tasks, outperforming zero-shot VLM prompting.
