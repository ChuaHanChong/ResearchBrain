# HAMSTER: Hierarchical Action Models For Open-World Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HAMSTER, a hierarchical robot manipulation system developed by researchers from NVIDIA, University of Washington, and University of Southern California, combines large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level 3D-aware policies. This approach enables robust open-world generalization by fine-tuning the VLM on diverse, inexpensive off-domain data, leading to a 20% absolute improvement in success rate over monolithic VLA models across various generalization axes.

## Problem

- Monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with data scarcity, requiring vast amounts of expensive on-robot observation-action data.
- Monolithic VLAs often operate at low inference frequencies, hindering dexterous and dynamic manipulation tasks.
- Smaller, specialized robot policy models are brittle to environmental changes and struggle with sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- A hierarchical architecture where a high-level VLM predicts coarse 2D paths and a low-level 3D-aware policy executes precise actions.
- The high-level VLM is fine-tuned on diverse, inexpensive off-domain data (e.g., action-free videos, simulation data) to enable cross-domain transfer.
- The low-level policy is conditioned on the VLM's 2D path along with 3D and proprioceptive inputs and trained on a small, task-specific real-robot dataset.

## Results

- HAMSTER achieves an average of **20%** absolute improvement in success rate over state-of-the-art monolithic VLAs (OpenVLA) across seven axes of real-world generalization.
- The system demonstrates strong generalization across unseen object-goal combinations, visual changes, novel objects, and complex long-horizon tasks like unfolding towels.
- HAMSTER shows high data efficiency for the low-level policy, achieving twice the success rate of standard 3D-DA models with **50%** less training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical decomposition, using a 2D path as an intermediate representation, allows effective utilization of abundant 'off-domain' data to fine-tune high-level VLMs for real-world robotic generalization.
> - Decoupling VLM inference from high-frequency action prediction enables the use of powerful VLMs for high-level reasoning without compromising the speed and dexterity of low-level control.
> - Fine-tuning a VLM on diverse cross-domain data significantly improves its ability to generate meaningful paths for novel real-world robotic tasks, outperforming zero-shot VLM prompting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general robot manipulation policies that can operate effectively in open-world environments has been a notoriously difficult challenge in robotics. Historically, robot policies have struggled with generalization beyond their training environments, especially when faced with novel objects, environments, or task variations.

The advent of large foundation models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has fueled optimism that similar generalization capabilities could be achieved in robotics. This has led to a recent surge in **monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, LLARVA) that directly finetune pre-trained VLMs to output robot actions. While these models leverage the powerful semantic reasoning of VLMs, they face significant limitations:
1. **Data Scarcity:** They rely heavily on vast amounts of expensive "on-robot" observation-action data, which is laborious and costly to collect. Existing robotics datasets, despite community efforts, are still limited in size, quality, and diversity compared to the data used for training general VLMs or Large Language Models (LLMs).
2. **Inference Frequency:** Monolithic VLAs often operate at low inference frequencies, hindering their ability to perform dexterous and dynamic manipulation tasks that require high-rate control.

In parallel, **smaller, specialized robot policy models** (e.g., RVT-2, 3D-DA, Diffusion Policy) have demonstrated impressive dexterity and robustness for specific tasks, often involving contact-rich manipulation and 3D reasoning. These models can achieve high-precision control with relatively smaller datasets. However, they are typically brittle to drastic changes in the environment or semantic task descriptions and struggle with sim-to-real transfer due to visual and dynamic gaps.

HAMSTER strategically positions itself to **marry the strengths of these two paradigms**: the broad generalization of large VLMs and the local robustness and efficiency of smaller policy models. The core innovation lies in its **hierarchical design**, where the VLM does not directly predict actions but instead generates an *intermediate representation*—a coarse 2D path—that guides a low-level, 3D-aware control policy.

This work builds upon existing ideas of hierarchical control and intermediate representations (e.g., point-based affordances, trajectory-based task specifications like RT-Trajectory). However, HAMSTER's key differentiating insight is that this specific hierarchical decomposition allows for the effective utilization of **abundant and cheap "off-domain" data** (e.g., action-free videos, simulation data, human videos) to fine-tune the high-level VLM. This directly addresses the data scarcity challenge facing monolithic VLA models. Unlike previous work (e.g., RT-Trajectory) that might use pre-trained VLMs off-the-shelf for guidance, HAMSTER demonstrates the critical importance of *fine-tuning* the VLM on diverse, off-domain data to significantly improve real-world generalization. Furthermore, by passing a 2D path, it offers a more expressive interface than single-point predictions, and by incorporating 3D and proprioceptive inputs into the low-level policy, it provides richer sensory context than purely 2D policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the state-of-the-art in general robot manipulation by developing policies that exhibit robust open-world generalization. This overarching goal is motivated by several critical limitations in current robotic learning paradigms:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The prohibitive cost and effort involved in collecting large-scale, on-robot interaction data is a major bottleneck. The authors aim to find a promising remedy by leveraging readily available, cheaper "off-domain" data sources that lack precise action labels, such as action-free videos, human-drawn sketches, or simulation data.
- **Bridging Generalization Gaps:** While large foundation models excel in vision and language, achieving comparable levels of generalization in robotics remains elusive. The objective is to demonstrate that a hierarchical architecture can enable superior cross-domain transfer, allowing the high-level VLM to bridge significant domain gaps (differences in embodiments, dynamics, visual appearances, and task semantics) between its finetuning data and real-robot testing scenarios.
- **Marrying Strengths of Different Models:** The authors seek to combine the open-world semantic reasoning and generalization capabilities of large VLMs with the efficiency, local robustness, and dexterity of smaller, specialized policy models. This aims to create a system that is both broadly capable and precisely controllable.
- **Enabling Dexterous and Long-Horizon Tasks:** Monolithic VLAs are constrained by their inference frequency, limiting their application to tasks requiring rapid, fine-grained control. A key objective is to design a system where the VLM's contribution is decoupled from high-frequency action prediction, thus enabling the low-level policy to handle dexterous and long-horizon tasks efficiently.
- **Promoting Open-Source Development:** By building upon open-source VLMs and low-level policies, the project aims to serve as a fully open-sourced enabler for the community, accelerating further research and development in vision-language-action models.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the twin challenges of data-hungry monolithic models and brittle specialized policies by proposing a hierarchical VLA framework that can effectively learn from diverse, inexpensive data sources and generalize robustly to novel, real-world manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HAMSTER (Hierarchical Action Models with SeparaTEd Path Representations) employs a two-tier architecture to achieve its objectives: a high-level VLM for coarse path prediction and a low-level policy for precise action execution.

#### 3.1. Overview of the HAMSTER Architecture

The system operates by first querying a high-level VLM (once or a few times per episode) to generate a coarse 2D path. This 2D path represents the desired end-effector trajectory and gripper state changes (open/close) on the input camera image. This intermediate representation then serves as guidance for a low-level policy, which operates on rich 3D and proprioceptive inputs to produce precise, spatially-aware robot actions at a high frequency.

#### 3.2. High-Level VLM for 2D Path Prediction

The high-level VLM's role is to predict a 2D path, denoted as $p = [(x_t, y_t, \text{gripper_open}_t)]_t$, given a monocular RGB image ($img$) and a language instruction ($z$).

- **VLM Backbone and Finetuning:** The base VLM used is **VILA-1.5-13b (Lin et al., 2024)**, a 13-billion-parameter vision-language model pre-trained on interleaved image-text datasets and video captioning data. While pre-trained VLMs can be prompted, they struggle with zero-shot path prediction. Therefore, HAMSTER finetunes this VLM using a standard supervised prediction loss to maximize the log-likelihood of target tokens. The crucial aspect here is the composition of the finetuning data.
- **Off-Domain Data Sources ($D_{off}$):** A key hypothesis of HAMSTER is that finetuning the VLM on diverse, inexpensive, "off-domain" data enables cross-domain transfer to real-world robotic control. None of this data comes from the specific real-robot deployment environment.
 - **Pixel Point Prediction:** This dataset, derived from the RoboPoint dataset (Yuan et al., 2024b), consists of 770k tasks. It teaches the VLM to reason about pixel-object relationships by predicting lists of 2D points or bounding boxes corresponding to objects of interest based on language prompts. This helps the VLM ground its understanding of "what" to manipulate.
 - **Simulated Robot Data:** 320k 2D end-effector paths were automatically generated from the RLBench simulator (James et al., 2020), which simulates a Franka robot performing various tabletop manipulation tasks. These paths are extracted via proprioceptive projection and are then *simplified* using the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm to create coarse trajectories (typically 2-5 points for short-horizon tasks). This data grounds the VLM's understanding of "how" a robot might perform a task.
 - **Real Robot Data:** Approximately 110k 2D end-effector paths were extracted from existing, online robot datasets like Bridge (Walke et al., 2023) and DROID (Khazatsky et al., 2024). These datasets feature different robot embodiments (e.g., WidowX arm) and setups from the test robot, effectively serving as diverse video data where the end-effector is tracked.
 - **Co-training for World Knowledge:** A 660k-sample Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset (Liu et al., 2024c) is also included during finetuning to help the VLM retain broad world knowledge and general semantic reasoning capabilities.
- **Prompting Strategy:** A specific text prompt (detailed in Appendix B.1) is used during finetuning and inference to guide the VLM to output paths and gripper states in a structured token format (e.g., `[(x, y), (x, y), <action>Open Gripper</action>...]`).

#### 3.3. Low-Level Policy Learning

The low-level policy, $\pi_\theta(a | s, o, z, p)$, generates robot actions conditioned on proprioceptive inputs ($s$), perceptual observations ($o$, including RGB image and pointcloud), optional language instruction ($z$), and the crucial 2D path ($p$) from the VLM.

- **Policy Architectures and Inputs:** The authors investigate two state-of-the-art 3D-aware policy architectures: RVT-2 (Goyal et al., 2024) and 3D Diffuser Actor (3D-DA) (Ke et al., 2024). These policies utilize rich 3D information from standard depth cameras.
- **Path Conditioning:** To incorporate the variable-length 2D path, the primary method involves *overlaying the path onto the RGB image observation*. Colored trajectories (blue-to-red gradient for temporal progression) and circles (green for closing, blue for opening) are drawn on the image. An alternative, explored in experiments, is to concatenate the original RGB image with a separate RGB image containing only the drawn path, creating a 6-channel input.
- **Policy Training:** The low-level policies are trained on a relatively small-scale (320 episodes) *task-specific dataset* collected via human teleoperation on the actual robot hardware. During training, *oracle 2D paths* (derived from proprioception projection) are used as ground truth, with a small amount of random noise added to simulate potential inaccuracies from the VLM's predictions during inference. Standard supervised imitation learning objectives are used.

#### 3.4. Inference Speed and Decoupling

A significant advantage of HAMSTER's hierarchical design is the decoupling of VLM inference from high-frequency robot control. Unlike monolithic VLAs that query the large VLM at every action step (which is computationally expensive and slow), HAMSTER queries the high-level VLM only once or a few times at the beginning of an episode to generate the 2D path. The low-level policy then executes actions based on this path, allowing for high-frequency, dexterous control without being constrained by the VLM's inference speed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation, conducted across both simulated and real-world scenarios, demonstrates HAMSTER's superior generalization, data efficiency, and robustness compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Real-World Generalization Performance

- **Overall Superiority:** HAMSTER significantly outperforms both state-of-the-art monolithic VLAs (OpenVLA) and non-VLM 3D policies (RVT-2, 3D-DA). It achieves an average of 20% absolute improvement in success rate (a 50% relative gain) over OpenVLA across seven different axes of generalization. Compared to non-VLM 3D policies, HAMSTER shows an even greater lead, improving performance by over 3x on average.
- **Broad Generalization:** HAMSTER demonstrates strong generalization across various challenging axes: unseen object-goal combinations, visual changes (table texture, lighting, distractor objects), unseen language instructions (semantic variations), novel spatial object relationships, novel objects, and combinations of multiple variations. This indicates its ability to effectively transfer knowledge from diverse off-domain data to real-world complexities.
- **Diverse Task Capabilities:** The system successfully handles a range of tasks, including both 'pick and place' and non-prehensile tasks such as 'press buttons' and 'knock down objects,' and even complex long-horizon tasks like unfolding towels and opening drawers, despite not being explicitly trained on these specific complex tasks in the in-domain dataset.
- **Monolithic VLA Limitations:** An ablation study showed that finetuning OpenVLA on the same RLBench simulation data used for HAMSTER's VLM provided minimal benefit, reinforcing the claim that monolithic architectures struggle to effectively leverage cross-domain data due to inherent mismatches in action and observation spaces.

#### 4.2. Simulation-Based Validation

- **Colosseum Benchmark:** On the Colosseum benchmark, designed to test generalization under significant visual and semantic variations, HAMSTER+3D-DA outperforms a vanilla 3D-DA implementation by an average of 31%. This confirms that the path guidance improves robustness to visual variations, as the policy can focus on the path rather than extraneous visual features.

#### 4.3. VLM Generalization Capabilities and Design Impact

- **Camera View Invariance:** HAMSTER demonstrates robustness to novel camera angles, significantly outperforming OpenVLA, which benefits from the VLM's training on diverse off-domain tasks that implicitly expose it to various viewpoints.
- **Path Representation (Appendix G):** Experiments exploring different 2D path representations (Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP) simplified paths vs. fixed-point paths) indicate that RDP-simplified paths generally lead to better performance, especially for smaller VLM backbones. This suggests that fewer, more meaningful points (especially gripper-action points) are more effective for VLM learning.
- **Impact of VLM Finetuning Data:** A human evaluation study comparing different VLM variants for path generation revealed two key insights:
 - HAMSTER's VLM, explicitly finetuned on cross-domain data, significantly outperforms zero-shot path generation from powerful closed-source VLMs like GPT-4o (even with Code-as-Policies assistance).
 - The inclusion of low-fidelity simulation data (RLBench) during VLM finetuning demonstrably improves real-world path prediction performance, highlighting the powerful cross-domain transfer capability enabled by HAMSTER's design.
- **Retention of Commonsense Knowledge:** Quantitative evaluation on 15 core VQA and multimodal reasoning benchmarks showed that HAMSTER's VLM maintains performance comparable to its base model (VILA1.5-13B), indicating that the specialized finetuning for robotics does not compromise its broad commonsense knowledge, validating its design as a general-purpose VLM.

#### 4.4. Data Efficiency

- HAMSTER shows strong demonstration efficiency for the low-level policy. With only 50% of the training data, HAMSTER+3D-DA still achieved a success rate twice that of the standard 3D-DA model, indicating that the high-level VLM's guidance significantly reduces the data requirements for downstream imitation learning.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Failure Modes (Appendix E)

The paper provides a detailed analysis of observed failure modes, categorizing them into:
1. **Trajectory Prediction Failures:** The VLM might misunderstand the language goal, predict incorrect trajectories (wrong object or direction), or fail to adapt to dynamic environmental changes, as trajectories are generated upfront.
2. **Trajectory Adherence Failures:** 2D trajectory predictions can lead to 3D ambiguities (e.g., above vs. behind an object). Also, the low-level policy may sometimes deviate from the predicted path.
3. **Action Execution Failures:** Despite correct trajectory following, issues like incorrect grasp angles can lead to task failure.

Interestingly, RVT-2 with path guidance showed more failures related to trajectory adherence (72%) compared to 3D-DA (10%), hypothesized to be due to RVT's re-projection step complicating 2D path adherence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HAMSTER represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of general robot manipulation for open-world scenarios, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Revolutionizing Data Collection for Robotics:** The most profound impact of HAMSTER is its demonstration of how to effectively leverage vast amounts of cheap, "off-domain" data for robotic learning. By proving that fine-tuning a VLM on readily available sources like simulation data, human videos, or existing action-free robot videos can significantly improve real-world control, HAMSTER provides a powerful pathway to bypass the prohibitive cost and limitations of traditional on-robot data collection. This could dramatically accelerate the development and deployment of capable robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** HAMSTER's superior performance in generalizing across diverse visual, semantic, and task variations highlights a critical step towards truly adaptable robots. By combining the semantic reasoning of VLMs with precise low-level control, the framework enables robots to handle novel scenarios that would typically cause monolithic VLA models or specialized policies to fail. This pushes the boundaries of what is possible with robot learning.
- **Practicality and Efficiency:** The hierarchical architecture addresses the critical issue of inference speed that plagues monolithic VLA models. By decoupling the high-level VLM's infrequent predictions from the low-level policy's high-frequency control, HAMSTER offers a practical solution for deploying large, powerful VLMs in real-time robotic applications, leading to more dexterous and dynamic manipulation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** As an "open-sourced enabler," HAMSTER provides a strong baseline and a conceptual framework for future research. It encourages further exploration into hierarchical control, intermediate representations, and novel ways to integrate multimodal foundation models into embodied AI.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its significant contributions, the authors acknowledge several limitations that also point towards promising avenues for future research:

- **2D vs. 3D Understanding:** The current intermediate representation is limited to 2D paths, preventing the VLM from having a native 3D spatial understanding. Future work could investigate methods for the VLM to produce 3D predictions or richer 3D representations.
- **Bandwidth of Interface:** The 2D path interface is "bandwidth limited," meaning it cannot convey nuanced information like force, torque, or rotational intentions. Exploring "learnable intermediate interfaces" that are more expressive and dynamically adaptive is a promising direction.
- **Broader Off-Domain Data Utilization:** While HAMSTER successfully leverages various off-domain sources, directly training VLMs from even larger-scale, uncurated human video datasets could further enhance generalization and common-sense knowledge for robotics.

In conclusion, HAMSTER offers a compelling solution to the long-standing challenge of open-world robot manipulation. Its hierarchical design, coupled with an innovative approach to data utilization, sets a new benchmark for generalization in robotics and paves the way for more capable and widely applicable robotic systems.
