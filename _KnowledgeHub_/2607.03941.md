# WSA$_1$: a 3D-Centric World-Spatial-Action Model for Generalizable Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The WSA1 model introduces a 3D-centric World-Spatial-Action (WSA) paradigm for generalizable robot control, integrating action-caused 3D world modeling, 2D visual thinking, and 3D inverse dynamics. The larger WSA1-L version achieved an 80.3% average success rate on real-world manipulation tasks, boosting performance by over 20% compared to previous open-source models, while utilizing only 1,000 hours of real-robot data.

## Problem

- Existing Robot Foundation Models (RFMs) like Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAM) lack explicit 3D dynamics and bidirectional causal modeling between robot actions and world changes, limiting their reliability for complex 3D spatial tasks.
- The acquisition of large-scale real-world robot demonstration data for imitation learning is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive, hindering RFMs from learning generalizable physical commonsense and adapting to unseen scenarios.
- Existing world-action models treat 2D visual planning and 3D geometric structure separately, so visual plans are not dynamically grounded in consistent 3D scene representations as the environment evolves.

## Method

- Developed **WSA1**, a 3D-centric ==World-Spatial-Action (WSA)== joint modeling paradigm, which unifies action-caused 3D world prediction, 3D-consistent 2D visual thinking, and ==3D inverse dynamics== into a single model.
- Designed a multi-modal transformer with a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture, featuring dedicated 2D and 3D Spatial Experts and a 3D Action Expert interconnected by a ==bidirectional 3D world-action causal attention== mechanism.
- Implemented a two-stage training strategy, including pre-training on 6,000 hours of diverse heterogeneous data (5,000h sim, 900h human, 1,000h real robot) followed by post-training, to learn data-efficient, transferable world-action priors.

## Results

- **WSA1-L** (6B parameters) achieved an **80.3%** average success rate and **86.5%** completeness on seven real-world manipulation tasks, representing over a **+20%** performance improvement compared to the second-best open-source RFM (π0.5 at **54.9%** SR).
- Demonstrated leading performance on simulation benchmarks, with **WSA1-L** achieving **93.1%** SR on the challenging RoboTwin2.0 'hard' setting and **98.2%** average SR on LIBERO, surpassing existing open-source baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full WSA framework, integrating 2D visual thinking and 3D world prediction with bidirectional causal attention, yields the best performance, and pre-training contributes a substantial **+9%** SR gain on its own.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly co-modeling world dynamics, spatial cognition, and action generation in a unified 3D-centric representation, inspired by human physical intelligence, is crucial for improving robot generalization and robustness.
> - Bidirectional causal attention between 3D world states and robot actions within a multi-modal transformer enables joint learning of how actions affect the 3D world and how 3D changes dictate actions.
> - Integrating 2D visual thinking with 3D world modeling within the WSA framework provides complementary benefits, with 2D handling semantic/temporal changes and 3D addressing spatial geometry and contact-rich interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses advancements and limitations within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically concerning Robot Foundation Models (RFMs) for generalist robotic systems.

**Current Landscape:**
The prevailing RFM paradigms are identified as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Action Models (WAM).
- **VLA Models:** These models typically build upon pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), augmenting them with an action expert. They operate on an "understand-then-execute" principle, leveraging the VLM's capabilities for open-world visual understanding to generate robot actions conditioned on semantic-level comprehension. Examples include RT-2, OpenVLA, π0, and π0.5. While successful in transferring semantic knowledge, VLAs are characterized as reactive frameworks that base decisions primarily on current observations, often lacking predictive planning and explicit 3D causal reasoning.
- **WAM Models:** In contrast, WAMs adopt an "imagine-then-execute" approach, constructing imagination-centric robot policies based on predictive video generation models. These video models act as visual planners, with an action expert functioning as an inverse dynamics model to generate control signals corresponding to planned visual frames. Examples include Motus, Fast-WAM, GeoPredict, and PointWorld. While introducing an element of planning, existing 3D WAMs are often described as following a unidirectional "predict-then-execute" pipeline.

**Limitations of Existing Paradigms:**
The paper identifies two fundamental challenges that impede the generalization of current VLAs and WAMs in real-world scenarios:
1. **Lack of Explicit 3D Dynamics and Causal Modeling:** Both paradigms are noted for lacking an explicit mechanism for jointly modeling 3D world dynamics and the mutual constraints between robot actions and world changes. This absence of explicit 3D world-action mutual constraints is stated to limit their reliability in real-world tasks that require 3D spatial cognition and robust adaptation to environmental changes.
2. **Data Scarcity for Generalization:** A primary training strategy for RFMs is imitation learning, which necessitates extensive real-world expert demonstration data. The acquisition of such data at scale is described as prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive, thereby hindering models from learning generalizable physical commonsense required for adaptation to unseen scenarios.

**WSA1's Contribution to the Landscape:**
WSA1 aims to address these identified gaps by proposing a novel 3D-centric World-Spatial-Action (WSA) modeling paradigm. This paradigm is motivated by cognitive science and neuroscience research suggesting that human physical intelligence relies on constructing and maintaining internal 3D spatial models, predicting action consequences, and planning interactions through co-modeling world, space, and action in a unified 3D representation. WSA1 seeks to instantiate these principles in a robot foundation model by unifying action-caused 3D world modeling and 3D inverse dynamics into a single model, enabling bidirectional causal learning and fostering data-efficient generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the field of embodied AI by creating versatile robots capable of exhibiting human-level physical intelligence. The motivation stems from observed limitations in current robot foundation models and insights from human cognition.

**Primary Motivation:**
Human physical intelligence is described as critically dependent on an innate ability to construct and maintain an internal 3D spatial model of the world. This mental model facilitates parsing 3D structure, predicting how actions will causally alter world states, and planning/executing dexterous motor interactions. This "co-modeling of world, space, and action in a unified 3D representation" is considered essential for generalist robots, given that physical interactions inherently occur in 3D space and adhere to physical laws. The development of an RFM capable of capturing these relationships is therefore considered a critical breakthrough.

**Specific Objectives to Address Current RFM Gaps:**
The research explicitly aims to tackle the aforementioned challenges by constructing a data-efficient modeling paradigm. This paradigm is designed to enable robots to jointly model the interdependencies between 3D world evolution and physical behaviors from demonstrations, thereby overcoming generalization bottlenecks and learning transferable world-action priors. This involves a unification of three learning tasks within the WSA framework:

1. **World Causality Level (Action-Caused 3D World Prediction):** To endow the robot with the ability to understand how its behaviors causally affect the 3D world. This objective focuses on explicitly modeling the consequences of actions on the 3D environment.
2. **Spatial Cognition Level (3D-Consistent 2D Visual Thinking):** To empower the robot to dynamically adjust its visual plan in response to changes in the 3D world. This involves ensuring that the robot's visual understanding and planning are grounded in and consistent with the 3D geometric reality.
3. **Action Execution Level (3D Inverse Dynamics Model):** To fully close the loop between 3D spatial world modeling and real-world interaction. This objective involves learning a model that can map planned 3D world state transitions back to executable robot actions, enabling goal-directed behavior that accounts for 3D spatial changes.

By integrating these three levels, the research aims to enable a robot to not only generate goal-directed actions but also predict their causal consequences on the 3D world and dynamically adjust its behavior accordingly, with a focus on achieving this with data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WSA1 is presented as a novel robot foundation model built on a 3D-Centric World-Spatial-Action (WSA) joint modeling paradigm.

**Problem Definition and Notation:**
The robot control problem is framed as learning a policy to predict future robot actions `A_t = (a_t+1, ..., a_t+H)` given current observations `O_t = {v_t, s_t, l}` (visual observation, robot proprioception, and language instruction). Unlike prior VLA and WAM approaches that rely on unidirectional mappings (2D semantic-action or 2D/3D world-action), WSA models robot control as a joint learning problem that captures interdependencies. This involves jointly learning three complementary conditional distributions:
- `p(g_t+1:t+K | a_t+1:t+H, O_t)`: Action-conditioned 3D World Model.
- `p(v_t+1:t+N | g_t+1:t+K, O_t)`: 2D visual thinking.
- `p(a_t+1:t+H | g_t+1:t+K, O_t)`: 3D Inverse Dynamics.

**Model Architecture:**
WSA1 is built upon a unified multi-modal transformer utilizing a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture. It integrates 2D visual generation, future 3D prediction, and robot action generation through three main components:

1. **2D Spatial Expert (2D-SE):** This component `f_2D(O_t, G_t)` serves as the world-aware visual thinking branch. It is initialized from pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., QWen3-VL or Wan2.2) to predict abstracted tokens `h_v` for subgoal images, focusing on essential visual features rather than raw pixels.
2. **3D Spatial Expert (3D-SE):** This expert `f_3D(O_t, A_t)` is responsible for action-caused 3D world modeling. It predicts latent representations `h_g` for future 3D scenes given current observations and robot actions, capturing the causal effect of robot behavior on the 3D world. Its architecture is derived from the 2D-SE.
3. **3D Action Expert (3D-AE):** Implemented as a denoising diffusion transformer `f_act(O_t, G_t, A_hat_t)`, this component predicts a full action chunk. It maps predicted 3D latent evolution back to executable actions, representing the 3D inverse dynamics.

**3D-Centric World-Action Causal Attention:**
The three experts are interconnected within the MoT architecture via a shared attention mechanism that enforces specific dependency rules:
- All predicted tokens (for visual subgoals, 3D scenes, and actions) can attend to current observation tokens.
- Visual tokens of future subgoal images can attend to 3D scene tokens, promoting 2D-3D visual consistency.
- Crucially, 3D scene tokens and action tokens have full bidirectional attention access, enabling joint learning of action-conditioned 3D dynamics prediction and 3D scene-aware action generation. This bidirectional attention is central to modeling world-action interdependence, reflecting a causal inductive bias.

**Model Instantiation:**
Two model scales are implemented:
- **WSA1-B:** A base model with 3 billion parameters, initialized from Qwen3-VL-2B.
- **WSA1-L:** A larger variant with 6 billion parameters, built on Wan2.2-5B.

**Learning Objectives:**
The model is optimized using a total loss function `L_total = L_2D + L_3D + L_ACT`, combining three distinct losses:
1. **3D World-Aware Visual Thinking (L_2D):** An MSE loss minimizing the discrepancy between predicted subgoal image tokens (`f_2D(O_t, G_t)`) and ground-truth tokens (`f_enc(V_t)`) extracted by a VAE-based tokenizer (e.g., COSMOS).
2. **Action-Conditioned 3D World Modeling (L_3D):** An MSE loss minimizing the error between predicted future 3D states (`f_3D(O_t, A_t)`) and ground-truth 3D geometric states (`f_g(G_t)`) from a pre-trained 3D foundation model (e.g., Depth-Anything).
3. **3D Inverse Dynamics Modeling (L_ACT):** A flow matching loss that minimizes the error between the predicted flow field and the ground-truth flow field for continuous action generation, conditioned on current observations and 3D scene state evolution.

**Training Strategy:**
A two-stage strategy is employed:
1. **Pre-training:** On diverse data sources to acquire general behavior priors.
2. **Post-training:** On specific manipulation tasks to adapt learned priors to target embodiments.
Critically, WSA models co-learn all three capabilities during both pre-training and post-training stages.

**Pre-training Data Recipe:**
The model is pre-trained on 6,000 hours of heterogeneous demonstration data, emphasizing data diversity to achieve data-efficient learning. This includes:
- **Simulation Data (~5,000 hours):** From InternData-A1 and RoboTwin2.0, providing diverse manipulation trajectories for learning scene dynamics, object interaction, and coordinated control.
- **Human Data (~900 hours):** Egocentric human manipulation videos from EgoDex, enhancing understanding of hand-object interaction and scene evolution.
- **Real-world Robot Data (~1,000 hours):** From RoboChallenge and AgiBot-World, aiding in reducing the sim-to-real gap and improving robustness in physical environments.
This mixture covers 8 different robot embodiments and over 300 robot control tasks, including more than 50 atomic manipulation skills. Specific losses (L_2D, L_3D, L_ACT) are applied depending on the data source, with `L_ACT` typically masked for human data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments focused on evaluating WSA1's performance on real-world robot control tasks and simulation benchmarks, alongside ablation studies to understand the contribution of its core components and training strategy.

**Data Efficiency:**
WSA1 models were pre-trained on a total of 6,000 hours of demonstration data, with only 1,000 hours derived from real robot interactions. This is highlighted as a relatively low data requirement for achieving strong generalization, especially concerning real-robot data, compared to other approaches.

**Real-World Task Performance:**
Evaluation was conducted on seven tabletop manipulation tasks using two robot platforms: unimanual AgileX PiPER and bimanual ARX Lift2. Tasks included RGB Block Sorting, Object Organization, Trash Cleaning, Pen Holder Placement, Toy Box Organization, Sweep Trash, and Unzip Pencil Bag. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR) and Completeness (C).
- **WSA1-B (3B parameters):** Achieved an average SR of 77.5% and an average C of 82.7%.
- **WSA1-L (6B parameters):** Achieved an average SR of 80.3% and an average C of 86.5%.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** WSA1 models substantially outperformed other open-source RFMs. For instance, π0 achieved 33.8% SR, π0.5 achieved 54.9% SR, and InternVLA-A1 achieved 39.2% SR. This represents an average boosted performance of over +20% compared to the second-best model (π0.5). The performance improvements were consistent across both single-arm and bimanual platforms, with WSA1 achieving the highest success rates on individual tasks.

**Simulation Benchmark Performance:**
- **RoboTwin2.0:** This dual-arm manipulation benchmark involves 50 tasks in both clean and randomized environments.
 - WSA1-B achieved a 92.7% SR in the challenging 'hard' setting.
 - WSA1-L achieved a 93.1% SR.
 - **Comparison:** WSA1 models exhibited leading performance among open-source models (e.g., surpassing LingBot-VA at 91.5% and Fast-WAM at 91.8%). It also demonstrated competitive performance relative to closed-source models such as MotuBrain (96.1%). WSA1 showed generalizable capabilities on long-horizon and spatially demanding tasks like "Move Pillbottle Pad," "Stack Blocks Three," and "Open Microwave."
- **LIBERO:** This benchmark assesses four robot capabilities: spatial understanding, object-level manipulation, goal-conditioned execution, and long-horizon reasoning.
 - WSA1-B achieved a 97.4% average SR.
 - WSA1-L achieved a 98.2% average SR.
 - **Comparison:** WSA1 models achieved competitive results against all compared methods, outperforming representative VLA baselines.

**Ablation Studies:**
1. **Investigation of WSA Modeling:** Conducted on RoboTwin2.0, this study assessed the contribution of the three learning tasks (visual thinking, 3D world prediction, and 3D action generation).
 - Baseline (action-only π-style VLA): 60% SR.
 - Action + Visual Thinking: 76% SR.
 - Action + 3D World Prediction: 75% SR.
 - Full WSA (Action + Visual Thinking + 3D World Prediction): 80% SR.
 - **Finding:** Both 2D visual thinking and 3D world modeling improve action generation, with distinct benefits (visual thinking for semantic/temporal changes, 3D modeling for spatial geometry/contact-rich tasks). The full WSA framework, integrating both, yielded the best performance, indicating that learning world dynamics in both 2D and 3D latent spaces is essential and that the bidirectional 3D world-action causal mechanism enhances robustness.
2. **Effect of Pre-training:** Also on RoboTwin2.0, this study compared different pre-training strategies.
 - WSA modeling only in post-training (without pre-training): ~80% SR.
 - WSA modeling in pre-training, but only action generation optimized in post-training: 89% SR.
 - WSA modeling in both pre-training and post-training: 93% SR.
 - **Finding:** Pre-training contributed a substantial performance gain (from 80% to 89% SR). Applying WSA modeling in both stages further boosted performance to 93%. This suggests that pre-training provides general-purpose representations and manipulation priors, while continued co-learning of 2D visual, 3D world, and action generation capabilities refines task-specific dynamics, temporal prediction, and 3D spatial reasoning.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
Visualizations demonstrated that WSA1's predicted visual plans captured task objectives and aligned with generated 3D world states. Executed action trajectories were observed to align with the predicted 3D goal states. This supported the model's ability to unify visual thinking, 3D world dynamics, and physical action generation within a shared latent space, leading to robust and generalizable manipulation behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel approach to robot foundation models with several implications for the field of embodied AI.

**Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** WSA1 directly addresses recognized shortcomings in existing VLA and WAM paradigms, specifically their perceived lack of explicit understanding of 3D physical dynamics and bidirectional causality between robot actions and changes in the 3D world. By integrating these aspects, the model aims for more reliable and adaptable robot control.

**A New Modeling Paradigm:** The introduction of the 3D-centric World-Spatial-Action (WSA) joint modeling paradigm offers an alternative to purely reactive or unidirectionally predictive models. This paradigm, inspired by human cognitive processes of spatial grounding, forward predictive modeling, and inverse motor control, suggests a path toward more sophisticated robot intelligence.

**Data-Efficient Generalization:** A significant aspect of this research is the demonstration that generalizable robot foundation models can be achieved with a relatively modest amount of real-robot data (1,000 hours within a total of 6,000 heterogeneous demonstration hours). This finding challenges the prevailing notion that tens of thousands of real-robot hours are strictly necessary, potentially making advanced RFM development more accessible and affordable.

**Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** The observed competitive performance across diverse simulated and real-world benchmarks, including a reported average of +20% improvement over state-of-the-art RFMs on real-world tasks, suggests that the WSA paradigm contributes to more robust and generalized manipulation capabilities. This is particularly relevant for tasks requiring complex spatial reasoning, object interaction, and long-horizon planning.

**Transferable Physical Interaction Priors:** The work indicates that 3D-centric world-spatial-action joint modeling enables robots to learn transferable physical interaction priors. This implies that the model's learned knowledge is not merely task-specific but can be applied across different tasks, platforms, and environmental conditions, fostering the development of generalist robotic systems.

**Implications for Future Research:** By demonstrating the utility of explicit 3D world dynamics and bidirectional causality, WSA1 provides a foundation for future research in embodied AI, particularly in areas aiming for improved physical intelligence, adaptation to unseen scenarios, and the integration of diverse data sources for robust robot learning.
