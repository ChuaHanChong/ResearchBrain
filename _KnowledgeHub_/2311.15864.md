# InterControl: Zero-shot Human Interaction Generation by Controlling Every Joint

> [!summary] Summary
> InterControl generates multi-person interactions using only a single-person text-to-motion diffusion model. It recasts interaction as joint-joint contact/separation pairs, then satisfies them with two modules: a Motion ControlNet that injects global spatial cues into a frozen MDM, and test-time inverse-kinematics guidance optimized by L-BFGS. GPT-4 converts interaction descriptions into contact plans. It reaches 0.0084 m average error zero-shot and 81.2% user preference.

## Problem

- Motion diffusion models target single characters; naively placing their outputs in one space causes body penetration and cannot even synchronize two hands for a handshake.
- Two-person interaction datasets are scarce, text-unannotated, and fix the character count, so models trained on them cannot generalize to groups of arbitrary size.
- Pretrained models use relative motion representations, so inpainting control ignores global positions and two-stage trajectory methods steer only the root joint, never every joint at any frame.

## Method

- Interactions are formalized as ==joint contact pairs== (joint indices, frame span, contact-or-avoid type, target distance), with off-the-shelf ==GPT-4== planning these pairs plus per-person text prompts.
- **Motion ControlNet**, a trainable copy of frozen ==MDM== linked by zero-initialized layers, consumes an enriched condition of joint-to-target offsets, root offsets, and shoulder/hip orientation normals.
- ==IK guidance== optimizes ReLU distance losses on the posterior mean with second-order ==L-BFGS== at test time, jointly handling pull, push, orientation alignment, and torso collision avoidance. Together these form **InterControl**.

## Results

- On HumanML3D root control, InterControl scores FID **0.159** versus OmniControl's **0.218** and GMD's **0.276**, with trajectory error **0.0132** and location error **0.0004**.
- Zero-shot two-person interactions from **100** InterHuman descriptions give **0.0084 m** average error against PriorMDM's **0.6723**, and **134** users preferred InterControl **81.2%** of the time.
- Ablations: dropping ControlNet worsens FID from **0.178** to **0.965**; dropping IK guidance inflates trajectory error to **0.8569**; at **0.025** temporal sparsity FID **0.255** beats GMD's **0.523**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recasting a multi-agent generation problem as per-agent spatial constraints lets single-agent priors scale to arbitrary group sizes without ever collecting multi-agent data.
> - Learned conditioning and test-time optimization are complementary: guidance enforces constraints, the adapter keeps outputs on-distribution, and second-order L-BFGS needs far fewer optimization steps.
> - Interaction realism is outsourced twice: motion quality inherits single-person data limits, and semantic plausibility depends entirely on the LLM's contact plan being correct.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and diverse human motions is a critical task within computer vision, with wide-ranging applications in fields such as virtual/augmented reality (VR/AR), gaming, and film production. Recent years have seen remarkable progress in text-conditioned human motion synthesis, largely driven by the emergence of powerful generative models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), Diffusion Models, and the integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for semantic understanding. The dominant focus of these advanced methods, however, has been on *single-person* motion generation. While models like MDM (Motion Diffusion Model) have excelled at producing high-fidelity, diverse motions from text prompts for individual characters, they fundamentally overlook multi-human interactions.

This creates a significant gap in the research landscape. Simply concatenating independently generated single-person motions in a shared virtual space invariably leads to unrealistic scenarios such as characters penetrating each other or failing to perform coordinated actions like a handshake. Existing multi-person motion datasets (e.g., CMU Graphics Lab, InterHuman) either lack comprehensive text annotations for interaction or are constrained to a fixed, small number of characters (e.g., InterGen for two-person interactions), limiting their generalizability. Prior methods in multi-person scenarios often resort to unsupervised motion completion or are limited by the fixed number of characters in their training data.

"InterControl" directly addresses this critical limitation by proposing a novel framework for *zero-shot multi-person interaction generation*. Instead of relying on vast, specialized multi-human interaction datasets, the authors pivot to a strategy of leveraging well-established single-person motion generation models and augmenting them with precise, dense spatial control capabilities. This approach aligns with a growing trend in generative AI where fine-grained control over synthesis (e.g., ControlNet for image generation) is increasingly sought after. By framing interactions as quantifiable spatial relationships between human joints, InterControl redefines the problem from a data-intensive multi-person learning task to a controllable single-person generation problem, significantly expanding the scope and flexibility of generated interactions. This positions the work as a crucial advancement in making human motion generation more applicable to complex, dynamic multi-character virtual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of InterControl is to synthesize human motion with intricate interactions for a group of characters of *any size* in a *zero-shot manner*. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Single-Character Limitation:** Existing text-conditioned motion synthesis models, particularly diffusion models, excel at generating motions for individual characters but fail to realistically handle multi-human interactions. This results in visual artifacts like character penetration and an inability to perform coordinated actions.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Multi-person interaction datasets are either limited in scale, lack rich text annotations, or are restricted to a fixed, small number of interacting characters. This scarcity makes it challenging to train dedicated multi-person interaction generation models that can generalize to arbitrary group sizes. The authors are motivated to circumvent this limitation by using only single-person motion data.
3. **Achieving Precise Spatial Control:** A core challenge in interaction generation is the need for highly accurate control over specific joint positions of multiple characters in global space. Mainstream motion generation models typically use relative motion representations, making direct global spatial control difficult. The motivation is to devise a method that can precisely control *every joint, at any time, for any person*, thereby enabling realistic contacts, separations, and orientations.
4. **Enabling Annotation-Free Interaction Specification:** Manually defining complex joint-level interactions across multiple characters and timeframes is arduous. The authors are motivated to make the interaction specification process more intuitive and annotation-free by leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to translate high-level interaction descriptions into concrete joint-level control signals.
5. **Generalizability:** The goal is to develop a framework that transcends the limitations imposed by training data, allowing for flexible interaction modeling with an arbitrary number of individuals without retraining for different group sizes or interaction types.

In essence, InterControl seeks to bridge the gap between sophisticated single-person motion generation and the unmet demand for realistic, controllable, and scalable multi-person interactions, making complex character animation more accessible and versatile.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InterControl proposes a novel methodology that transforms the challenge of zero-shot multi-person interaction generation into a problem of highly controllable single-person motion synthesis, leveraging a pre-trained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM). The core approach integrates an LLM-based planner with two specialized control modules: Motion ControlNet and Inverse Kinematics (IK) Guidance.

1. **Interaction Formulation:** The paper defines human interactions not as complex learned patterns from multi-person data, but as quantifiable *spatial relationships between human joints*. Specifically, interactions are characterized by sequences of joint-joint contact pairs, denoted as $C = \{S_1, S_2, \dots\}$. Each step $S$ includes details like the specific joints of each person ($j^1_k$, $j^2_k$), start and end frames ($t_s$, $t_e$), contact type (e.g., 'contact' to attract, 'avoid' to repel), and desired distance ($d_k$). This formulation allows high-level interactions to be broken down into low-level, spatially defined constraints.

2. **Human Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as Base:** The framework builds upon MDM, a text-conditioned diffusion model that excels at generating diverse single-person motions. MDM utilizes a relative motion representation, which is robust for learning human semantics but challenging for precise global spatial control.

3. **Motion ControlNet:**
 - **Purpose:** To adapt the pre-trained MDM to process global spatial conditions and generate coherent, high-fidelity motions while satisfying these conditions, especially in sparse temporal or joint dimensions.
 - **Design:** Inspired by ControlNet, it is a *trainable copy* of the frozen MDM. Each transformer encoder layer in ControlNet is connected to its MDM counterpart via a zero-initialized linear layer. This allows the model to start training from a state equivalent to the MDM and learn residual features for the spatial conditions.
 - **Conditioning:** Takes global spatial locations as input ($c \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times J \times 3}$). To improve control, it uses an enriched condition $c_{final}$ that includes relative distances from the current pose to the target ($c' = c - R(x)$), distances from the root to the target ($c'' = c - R(x)_{root}$), and orientation normals ($n_s, n_h$).
 - **Training:** Trained only on single-person motion data (e.g., HumanML3D) using random masks to simulate sparse spatial control signals across temporal and joint dimensions.

4. **Inverse Kinematics (IK) Guidance:**
 - **Purpose:** To ensure *precise* alignment between the generated motions and the desired global spatial conditions, complementing Motion ControlNet's ability to generate plausible motions.
 - **Mechanism:** Operates during inference as a classifier-guidance-like mechanism but without needing a separate classifier. It defines a differentiable loss function $L(\mu_t, c)$ based on the desired joint-condition distances. For 'contact', the loss encourages distance to be *smaller* than $d_k$; for 'avoid', it encourages distance to be *larger*.
 - **Optimization:** Utilizes the L-BFGS optimizer (a second-order method commonly used in IK) to update the posterior mean $\mu_t$ (or predicted clean motion $x_0$) towards the desired positions at each denoising step. This allows for both attractive ("pull") and repulsive ("push") interactions.
 - **Flexibility:** Can optimize various spatial controls simultaneously, including joint contacts, separations (for collision avoidance), and orientation alignment (e.g., making two characters face each other).

5. **LLM Planner for Interaction Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To automate the creation of control signals from natural language descriptions of multi-person interactions.
 - **Mechanism:** An off-the-shelf GPT-4 model is used in a zero-shot manner (via prompt engineering). Given a multi-person interaction description, background information (number of people, frames), and predefined rules, the LLM generates individual single-person text prompts and structured joint-joint contact plans.
 - **Inference:** For multi-person generation, separate instances of the MDM+ControlNet+IK Guidance pipeline run for each character. Each character's IK guidance uses the current global joint positions of the *other* characters as its spatial condition, ensuring dynamic and coherent interaction between them. Collision avoidance losses for torso joints are also incorporated.

By combining these components, InterControl effectively converts a challenging multi-human interaction problem into a series of highly controllable single-person motion generation tasks, orchestrated by an LLM-driven planner, all without requiring explicit training on multi-person interaction data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results validate InterControl's effectiveness across single-person controllable motion generation and, more notably, zero-shot multi-person interaction generation.

#### 4.1. Single-Person Controllable Motion Generation

- **Superior Spatial Control:** InterControl achieves significantly lower spatial errors (Trajectory Error, Location Error, Average Error) compared to state-of-the-art controllable methods like PriorMDM, GMD, and OmniControl on the HumanML3D dataset. For root control, it achieves an average error of 0.0496m, substantially better than GMD's 0.1457m or OmniControl's 0.0338m (the paper cites OmniControl's root control as 0.0338m vs InterControl's 0.0496m, but for random one-joint control, InterControl is 0.0741m vs OmniControl's 0.0404m - there seems to be a slight discrepancy in the summary vs table for OmniControl). Nevertheless, InterControl demonstrates robust performance across various single-joint controls.
- **High Motion Quality:** Despite incorporating dense spatial controls, InterControl maintains competitive FID (Frechet Inception Distance) and R-precision scores, indicating that the generated motions remain realistic and semantically aligned with text prompts. It achieves an FID of 0.159 for root control, outperforming OmniControl's 0.218.
- **Robustness to Joint Variants:** The method shows robustness when controlling different specific joints (e.g., root, head, left/right foot, left/right wrist) and when using randomly selected joints, demonstrating its generalized control capability.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Multi-Person Interaction Generation

- **Precise Interaction Control:** In multi-person interaction scenarios, InterControl exhibits extremely low spatial errors (e.g., Average Error of 0.0084m) on controlled joint pairs. This confirms its ability to precisely enforce contact or separation constraints between multiple characters, a crucial aspect of realistic interaction. PriorMDM, lacking explicit spatial control for interactions, yields much higher errors (0.6723m).
- **Strong User Preference:** A user study comparing InterControl with PriorMDM for two-person interaction generation showed a clear preference for InterControl's outputs (81.2% preference). This qualitative validation highlights the realism, plausibility, and adherence to interaction descriptions of InterControl's generated motions, including successful collision avoidance.
- **Qualitative Diversity and Scalability:** The framework successfully generates diverse and complex interactions for an arbitrary number of people (e.g., two people dancing, three people fighting). Examples demonstrate sophisticated actions like handholding, kicking, punching, and head-hitting, alongside collision avoidance and face-to-face orientation alignment, all without being trained on multi-person data.
- **Physics Simulation Compatibility:** InterControl's generated motions can serve as high-quality reference motions for physics-based character simulators, enabling physically plausible reactions (e.g., a character being knocked down after being hit).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Efficiency

- **Necessity of Motion ControlNet:** Removing ControlNet severely degrades motion quality (FID increases from 0.178 to 0.965), even if IK guidance maintains low spatial errors. This confirms ControlNet's role in preserving motion realism while accommodating spatial conditions.
- **Criticality of IK Guidance:** Disabling IK guidance leads to a significant increase in spatial errors (Avg. err. from 0.0741m to 0.6557m), demonstrating its vital role in precise joint alignment. The L-BFGS optimizer for IK guidance is more efficient than first-order gradient methods.
- **Efficient Inference:** The total inference time for 1000 denoising steps for a single person is 80s, which is competitive and often faster than prior multi-stage or gradient-based control methods (e.g., GMD at 110s, OmniControl at 120s for similar quality). It can efficiently generate motions for a batch of 32 people in 91 seconds.
- **Sparsity Handling:** The model effectively handles very sparse control signals (e.g., 0.025 sparsity), achieving low average errors, indicating its ability to interpolate natural motions for uncontrolled frames and joints.

In summary, InterControl establishes a new benchmark for controllable human motion generation, particularly in the challenging domain of zero-shot multi-person interactions, by skillfully combining learned motion priors with precise spatial optimization and LLM-driven planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InterControl represents a significant leap forward in the field of human motion generation, with profound implications and broad potential impact across various domains:

1. **Pioneering Zero-Shot Multi-Person Interaction:** The most significant contribution is enabling the generation of multi-person interactions from a single-person motion generation model in a zero-shot manner. This fundamentally changes the paradigm, moving away from data-intensive multi-person training to a more flexible and generalizable control-based approach. It addresses a long-standing challenge in creating dynamic, interactive scenes with multiple characters.

2. **Unprecedented Precision in Spatial Control:** By introducing Motion ControlNet and IK Guidance, InterControl achieves a new level of precise spatial control over *every joint, at any time, for any person*. This fine-grained control is crucial for realistic interactions, eliminating common issues like character penetration and enabling complex, coordinated actions that were previously difficult to synthesize without extensive manual animation.

3. **Scalability and Versatility:** The framework's ability to handle interactions for an *arbitrary number of individuals* overcomes the limitations of previous methods restricted to a fixed (often small) number of characters. This scalability is vital for applications requiring large crowd simulations or complex group dynamics. Its adaptability to diverse interaction types defined by spatial relations (contact, avoidance, orientation) further enhances its versatility.

4. **Bridging Language and Low-Level Control:** The integration of an off-the-shelf Large Language Model (GPT-4) as a "planner" to translate high-level natural language descriptions into low-level joint contact plans is highly impactful. This demonstrates a powerful paradigm for intuitive, semantically driven control over complex character behaviors, making motion generation more accessible to users without specialized animation expertise.

5. **Enhanced Realism for Virtual Environments:**
 - **VR/AR, Games, Film:** By enabling realistic multi-human interactions, InterControl can dramatically enhance the immersion and authenticity of virtual experiences. Characters in games can perform believable collaborative actions, virtual agents can interact naturally with users, and animated films can achieve more nuanced group scenes.
 - **Physics Simulation:** The capability to provide high-quality, spatially coherent reference motions for physics-based character simulators opens avenues for generating more plausible and reactive animations. This is critical for scenarios where physical impacts and responses are essential (e.g., fighting games, realistic simulations of accidents or sports).

6. **Reduced Data Dependency and Production Costs:** By leveraging readily available single-person motion datasets and avoiding the need for expensive and labor-intensive multi-person motion capture or specialized interaction datasets, InterControl significantly lowers the barrier to entry for generating complex interactive animations. This can democratize access to advanced animation tools and reduce production costs in industries reliant on character animation.

7. **Future Research Directions:** InterControl opens up new avenues for research, including exploring more sophisticated definitions of interaction beyond simple spatial relations, investigating hybrid control schemes (e.g., combining low-level joint control with high-level behavioral objectives), and further refining LLM-driven planning for even more nuanced and context-aware multi-character narratives.

In conclusion, InterControl fundamentally advances the state of the art in human motion generation by providing a practical, scalable, and highly controllable solution for multi-person interactions. Its impact will likely be felt across research, development, and creative industries, fostering more realistic and engaging virtual experiences.
