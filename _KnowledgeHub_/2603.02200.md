# Adaptive Confidence Regularization for Multimodal Failure Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Adaptive Confidence Regularization (ACR) is the first framework built specifically for multimodal failure detection, i.e. flagging misclassified in-distribution predictions. It combines an Adaptive Confidence Loss that penalizes confidence degradation, where fused confidence drops below a unimodal branch's, with Multimodal Feature Swapping, which synthesizes failure-aware outliers by exchanging contiguous cross-modal feature blocks. ACR beats prior best methods by up to 9.58% AURC, 1.63% AUROC and 15.45% FPR95.

## Problem

- Failure detection is well studied in unimodal settings but its extension to multimodal systems remains largely unexplored, ignoring complementary cross-modal cues entirely.
- Out-of-distribution scores such as Energy, Entropy and MaxLogit target semantic shift and underperform even a plain maximum-softmax-probability baseline when repurposed for misclassification detection.
- Outlier exposure methods like OpenMix need large auxiliary outlier datasets and, being unimodal, cannot synthesize failures arising from cross-modal conflict or sensor misalignment.

## Method

- **Adaptive Confidence Loss**: a hinge penalty averaging max(0, unimodal confidence minus fused confidence) over modalities, forcing fusion synergy and suppressing overconfident unimodal branches during joint training.
- **Multimodal Feature Swapping**: exchanges a contiguous block of n_swap feature dimensions between modality embeddings, producing ==failure-aware outliers== labeled with a soft mix of true class and an extra outlier class.
- Joint objective: classification cross-entropy plus outlier cross-entropy on swapped features plus the weighted confidence loss; inference keeps plain ==maximum softmax probability== over the original C classes.

## Results

- On HMDB51 ACR cuts FPR95 from **52.07%** to **41.96%** and AURC from **29.56** to **19.97** versus MSP; HAC AURC drops **42.90** to **27.41**.
- Ablation: Adaptive Confidence Loss alone reaches **90.32** AUROC, Feature Swapping alone **90.55**, combined **92.02**, and swapping beats noise, drop and feature-mixing augmentations.
- Gains transfer to SemanticKITTI image-plus-LiDAR segmentation (AURC **33.90** to **21.90**, mIoU **59.25** to **63.56**) and to multimodal OOD detection, average FPR95 **35.75** to **24.15**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Confidence degradation, fused prediction less confident than a unimodal branch, is a cheap failure signal: misclassified samples show it 22-52 points more often.
> - Out-of-distribution machinery does not transfer to misclassification detection; failures live inside the in-distribution manifold, so hard near-manifold negatives beat external outlier exposure.
> - Synthesize failures where they come from: breaking cross-modal consistency while preserving intra-modal semantics matters more than generic noise, and contiguous corruption beats scattered.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Multimodal failure detection asks a model to flag its own incorrect predictions so they can be rejected or checked by a person, rather than only identifying unfamiliar, out-of-distribution inputs. Picture an action recognizer that combines video and optical flow: users need to know when not to trust its answer. The paper compares the confidence of the fused answer with the confidence of each input stream. On four action-recognition datasets, incorrect predictions were more likely than correct ones to have lower confidence after fusion than in at least one individual modality.

Confidence degradation ratio (%) for correct (orange) and incorrect (blue) predictions: HMDB51 27.0 vs 59.4, EPIC-Kitchens 25.9 vs 49.0, HAC 28.7 vs 81.1, Kinetics-600 30.1 vs 52.1. This is an association in these evaluations, not evidence that degradation causes an error.

### 2. A signal from disagreeing streams

The authors call this pattern **confidence degradation**. The fused prediction's maximum class probability is lower than the maximum class probability from at least one unimodal prediction. The analysis uses the standard Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP) score. The gap between incorrect and correct predictions is 32.4, 23.1, 52.4, and 22.0 percentage points across HMDB51, EPIC-Kitchens, HAC, and Kinetics-600. The pattern is an association, not proof that degradation causes errors. The idea that failures stem from conflicting signals is the authors' interpretation.

### 3. How ACR trains for disagreement

ACR makes two training changes and requires no new inference-time detector or external outlier dataset. Modality-specific encoders produce embeddings and unimodal predictions, and a fusion classifier produces the combined prediction.

The first change is **Adaptive Confidence Loss (ACL)**. It penalizes a fused confidence that falls below either unimodal confidence. It does not penalize a fused confidence that is already above both. In the authors' proposed account, this loss works alongside ordinary classification loss and can discourage an overconfident unimodal branch when that branch conflicts with the fused prediction.

The second change is **Multimodal Feature Swapping (MFS)**. It works in four steps:

1. Select a contiguous block of dimensions from each modality embedding.
1. Exchange the blocks.
1. Concatenate the altered embeddings.
1. Train on the resulting synthetic example.

The soft target for each synthetic example mixes the original class label with a new outlier class. The outlier weight increases with the amount swapped. The authors describe MFS as a targeted regularizer for cross-modal inconsistency, not a simulation of all possible failures.

The proposed ACR training procedure. The confidence-loss path compares unimodal and fused confidence. The feature-swapping path exchanges feature segments, synthesizes an outlier, and assigns an additional class label.

### 4. What the tests establish

The evaluation uses four metrics:

- **AURC** integrates error risk over the fraction of predictions retained. It is reported multiplied by 1,000, and lower is better.
- **AUROC** summarizes the trade-off between detecting correct and incorrect predictions. Higher is better.
- **FPR95** is the fraction of errors missed as "correct" at a 95% true-positive rate. Lower is better.
- **ACC** is ordinary in-distribution accuracy.

The main test is HMDB51 action recognition with video and optical flow, with failure detection evaluated on known classes. Compared with MSP, ACR improves AUROC from **88.28% to 92.02%**, lowers FPR95 from **52.07% to 41.96%**, and reduces AURC from **29.56 to 19.97**. These are failure-detection metrics, not measures of time or computation savings.

The component ablation checks whether both changes matter. In this test, ACL and MFS each improve on the MSP reference, and the combination performs best across the reported metrics. This does not prove that the authors' proposed explanations are the only reasons for the gains.

HMDB51 ablation (AURC×1,000; other metrics in %). MSP: 29.56, 88.28, 52.07, 86.20. ACL: 24.48, 90.32, 43.97, 86.77. MFS: 25.11, 90.55, 46.22, 86.43. ACL + MFS: 19.97, 92.02, 41.96, 87.23. This is an ablation comparison, not a direct measurement of the internal causal mechanism.

### 5. Scope

ACR uses the mismatch between fused and unimodal predictions as a training signal and practices on synthetic cross-modal inconsistencies. It is a promising training recipe for improving confidence-based error detection in the evaluated settings. It is not proof of safety in deployment or a universal failure detector. The study tests specific datasets and modality combinations. The authors identify adversarial attacks on the detector and generalization to more disparate modalities as open questions.
