# Learning Humanoid Standing-up Control across Diverse Postures

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and partner institutions developed HOST, a reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to learn robust, posture-adaptive standing-up motions from diverse initial postures. The policies, trained entirely in simulation, directly transferred to the Unitree G1 robot, achieving a 100% success rate across tested real-world terrains and demonstrating significant resilience to external disturbances and varied environments.

## Problem

- Classical model-based standing-up methods are computationally intensive, sensitive to disturbances, and lack robustness to real-world uncertainties.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for standing up often rely on predefined motion trajectories or human demonstrations, limiting their adaptability to diverse, un-demonstrated initial postures.
- Training RL agents from scratch for complex whole-body tasks frequently leads to "violent" or jerky motions in reality due to the sim-to-real gap and unconstrained exploration, posing safety concerns.

## Method

- The HOST (Humanoid Standing-up Control) framework utilizes a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) based reinforcement learning approach with a comprehensive, multi-stage reward design.
- A Multi-Critic (MuC) architecture is introduced, assigning separate critics to different reward groups to better manage and balance gradients from a large set of objectives.
- To facilitate exploration and ensure smooth motions, a curriculum-based vertical pulling force guides initial learning, and an action rescaler progressively tightens action bounds; domain randomization is extensively applied to improve sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The HOST framework achieved a 100% standing-up success rate on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot across various real-world terrains, including flat ground, platforms, walls, and slopes, directly from simulation.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the multi-critic architecture was essential for any success (0% without it), while the force curriculum and action bound significantly improved exploration and motion smoothness respectively.
- The policies exhibited high robustness to external disturbances, such as impacts and heavy payloads (up to 12kg), and showed emergent capabilities like fall recovery and dynamic balance on a 15° slippery slope.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-critic architecture is crucial for effectively balancing numerous and potentially conflicting reward functions inherent in complex, multi-stage tasks like humanoid standing-up.
> - Curriculum learning, specifically a vertical pulling force curriculum, is highly effective for accelerating exploration from difficult, fully fallen states, while an action rescaler curriculum ensures smooth, non-violent motions for real-world deployment.
> - Extensive domain randomization and training across diverse simulated terrains are vital for bridging the sim-to-real gap, enabling direct transfer of complex whole-body humanoid policies to hardware without real-world adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Standing-up control is a fundamental and critical capability for humanoid robots, forming a crucial bridge between passive states (e.g., after a fall, from a seated position) and active locomotion or manipulation. Traditionally, this task has been addressed through two main avenues:

- **Classical Model-Based Approaches:** These methods typically involve handcrafted motion trajectories, model-based motion planning, or trajectory optimization. While effective in generating precise motions in controlled environments, they suffer from several limitations. They are computationally intensive, requiring significant real-time computation or offloading to external machines. More critically, they are highly sensitive to external disturbances (e.g., pushes, uneven terrain) and rely on precise analytical models of the robot and its environment, which are often imperfect in the real world due to factors like inaccurate actuator modeling and unexpected contact dynamics. This limits their robustness and real-world applicability. Examples include early works focusing on specific lie-down-to-stand-up motions.

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Approaches:** RL offers a promising alternative by learning control policies with minimal modeling assumptions, allowing robots to adapt to complex dynamics and unforeseen situations. Recent advancements have enabled impressive bipedal locomotion, whole-body control, and even complex loco-manipulation tasks. However, RL for standing-up control presents unique challenges:
 - **High Dimensionality and Dynamics:** Standing up involves complex, time-varying contact points, multi-stage motor skills (righting, kneeling, rising), and precise angular momentum control, making RL exploration difficult across the robot's many degrees of freedom (DoFs).
 - **Prior Data Dependency:** Many existing RL approaches leverage predefined motion trajectories or human demonstrations to guide exploration, which limits their scalability to diverse, un-demonstrated postures (e.g., standing up from a sofa, or after a fall on an uneven surface).
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap and Violent Motions:** Training RL agents from scratch with broad exploration strategies in simulation often leads to "violent" or "jerky" motions when transferred to physical hardware due to high torque limits and a lack of real-world motion constraints in the reward function. This poses significant safety and durability concerns for physical robots.

This paper, "Learning Humanoid Standing-up Control across Diverse Postures," directly addresses these gaps. It aims to develop an RL-based framework that enables humanoid robots to learn robust, posture-adaptive standing-up control *from scratch* (i.e., without prior motion trajectories) and transfer these policies effectively to the *real world*. By doing so, it bridges the divide between purely simulated solutions and real-world deployment for a critical, underexplored capability, moving towards truly autonomous and versatile humanoid robots capable of recovering from arbitrary fall postures and adapting to varied environments. The work draws inspiration from quadrupedal fall recovery but extends it to the inherently more unstable bipedal system, pushing the state-of-the-art in humanoid autonomy and robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and versatile control framework that enables humanoid robots to learn to stand up from diverse initial postures in real-world environments using reinforcement learning.

The key motivations behind this objective are:

- **Enhancing Humanoid Autonomy and Applicability:** Current humanoid robot systems often assume a pre-standing posture, limiting their utility in real-world scenarios such as transitioning from a seated position, recovering from unexpected falls, or starting from arbitrary orientations after system reboots. Unlocking autonomous standing-up capability is fundamental for broadening the real-world applications of humanoid robots, making them more resilient and truly autonomous.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Methods:**
 - **Model-based limitations:** Classical model-based methods, while precise, are computationally expensive, sensitive to real-world disturbances, and require meticulous tuning and accurate models, which are difficult to obtain for complex, high-DoF robots.
 - **RL limitations (prior data dependence):** Many RL approaches for standing up rely on predefined motion trajectories or demonstrations, restricting their ability to adapt to novel or diverse starting postures not covered in the training data. The goal is to learn from scratch to achieve true posture adaptivity.
 - **Sim-to-real gap and violent motions:** Training directly in simulation often results in policies that produce violent, jerky, or unstable motions on real hardware due to high torque limits and unconstrained exploration. Ensuring smooth, stable, and safe real-world deployment is a critical, often overlooked, aspect.
- **Tackling the Complexity of Standing-Up:** The standing-up task for a humanoid is inherently complex, involving highly dynamic whole-body coordination, time-varying contacts, multi-stage motor skills, and precise angular momentum control. This complexity makes pure RL exploration challenging, necessitating novel architectural and training strategies.

In summary, the authors aim to develop an RL framework, **HOST (Humanoid Standing-up Control)**, that can learn robust, posture-adaptive, and real-world deployable standing-up motions for humanoid robots without relying on prior motion data. This capability is envisioned as a crucial step towards seamless integration with locomotion and manipulation systems, significantly expanding the practical utility of humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes **HOST (Humanoid Standing-up Control)**, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to teach humanoid robots to stand up from diverse postures. The methodology addresses several key challenges inherent in this complex, multi-stage, and contact-rich task.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Observation Space ($s_t$):** The robot perceives its proprioceptive state, including angular velocity of the base ($\omega_t$), roll and pitch of the base ($r_t, q_t$), joint positions ($p_t$) and velocities ($\dot{p}_t$), the last action ($a_{t-1}$), and an action rescaler ($\beta$). To implicitly enhance contact detection, the policy also receives the previous five states.
- **Action Space ($a_t$):** Actions represent the difference between current and next-step joint positions. A PD controller translates these into joint torques ($\tau_t = K_p \cdot (p_d_t - p_t) - K_d \cdot \dot{p}_t$). The action rescaler $\beta$ implicitly controls the maximal torque and motion speed.
- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used for its stability and efficiency in large-scale parallel training.

#### 3.2. Key Challenges & Solutions

The HOST framework introduces several novel components to overcome the identified challenges:

**a) Reward Design & Optimization (Multi-Critic RL):**
- **Multi-Stage Task:** Standing up involves distinct motor skills: righting the body, kneeling, and rising. The task is divided into three stages based on the robot's base height ($h_{base}$), and specific reward functions are activated in each stage.
- **Complex Reward Functions:** A comprehensive set of reward functions (detailed in Appendix A, Table VI) is designed, categorized into four groups:
 - **Task Reward ($r_{task}$):** High-level objectives (e.g., head height, base orientation).
 - **Style Reward ($r_{style}$):** Shapes the motion (e.g., penalizing large joint deviations, encouraging ankle parallelism, penalizing horizontal foot contact forces).
 - **Regularization Reward ($r_{regu}$):** Ensures smooth and efficient motion (e.g., penalizing joint acceleration, action rate, torques, power, and velocity, enforcing joint limits).
 - **Post-Task Reward ($r_{post}$):** Specifies desired behaviors after standing (e.g., low angular/linear velocity, upright base orientation, target height, stable upper-body posture).
- **Multi-Critic (MuC) Architecture:** To handle the large number of reward functions and their potential for conflicting gradients, the framework employs multiple critics. Each reward group is treated as a separate "task" with its own dedicated critic ($V_{\phi_i}$), which estimates returns independently. These individual advantages are then aggregated into an overall weighted advantage for policy optimization, balancing objectives more effectively and simplifying hyperparameter tuning.

**b) Exploration Challenges (Force Curriculum):**
- **Difficulty in Exploration:** The transition from a fallen state to stable kneeling is particularly difficult for RL agents to explore.
- **Solution: Curriculum-Based Vertical Pulling Force:** Inspired by human infant development, an upward pulling force ($F$) is applied to the robot's base. This force is initially strong at the beginning of training and only takes effect when the robot achieves a near-vertical (ground-sitting) orientation. The magnitude of this force progressively decreases as the robot demonstrates the ability to maintain a target height, gradually weaning the robot off assistance.

**c) Motion Constraints (Action Bound and Smoothness Regularization):**
- **Problem:** High DoFs and powerful actuators can lead to violent, jerky motions undesirable for real-world deployment.
- **Solution: Action Rescaler ($\beta$):** An action rescaler $\beta$ is introduced to scale the action output, implicitly limiting the maximal joint torques and motion speed. Similar to the force curriculum, $\beta$ gradually decreases from 1 to 0.25 during training, tightening the action bounds as the policy matures.
- **Solution: Smoothness Regularization (L2C2):** The L2C2 method is integrated to prevent motion oscillation. This applies regularization to both the actor and critic networks by penalizing large changes between consecutive states, promoting Lipschitz continuous policies and value functions.

**d) Sim-to-Real Gap (Terrain Design and Domain Randomization):**
- **Problem:** Discrepancies between simulation and the real world (e.g., physics parameters, sensor noise, actuator models).
- **Solution: Diverse Terrain Design:** Four distinct terrains are designed in simulation to create a wide variety of initial postures representative of real-world scenarios: flat ground, a platform (e.g., a sofa), a wall (e.g., leaning against a wall), and a slope. This trains the robot to be posture-adaptive rather than ground-specific.
- **Solution: Domain Randomization:** Various physical parameters are randomized during training, including body mass, center of mass (CoM) offset, friction, restitution, PD gains, torque offsets, motor strength, control delay, and initial joint angles/scales. This forces the policy to be robust to variations and uncertainties, facilitating direct transfer to the real robot.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Simulator:** Isaac Gym is used with 4096 parallel environments for efficient training.
- **Robot Model:** Unitree G1 humanoid robot (23 DoFs).
- **Network Architecture:** Actor network is a 3-layer MLP; critic networks are 2-layer MLPs.
- **Control Frequencies:** PD controller at 200 Hz (sim) / 500 Hz (real), control policies at 50 Hz.
- **PPO Parameters:** Standard parameters (discount factor 0.99, clip ratio 0.2, entropy coefficient 0.01).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive simulation and real-world experimental results to validate the effectiveness of the HOST framework.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The experiments evaluate performance across four terrains (Ground, Platform, Wall, Slope) using four key metrics: Success Rate (E_succ), Feet Movement (E_feet), Motion Smoothness (E_smth), and Energy Consumption (E_engy).

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Multiple Critics (MuC) are Crucial:** HOST without MuC (HOST-w/o-MuC) achieved a 0% success rate across all terrains, highlighting the absolute necessity of the multi-critic architecture for learning and balancing the complex, multi-stage reward functions.
 - **Force Curriculum Enhances Exploration:** Without the force curriculum (HOST-w/o-Force), the robot failed to stand up on ground, wall, and slope terrains, only achieving a low success rate on the platform. Even with curiosity-based exploration (HOST-w/o-Force-RND), performance remained poor. This confirms the force curriculum's effectiveness in accelerating exploration, especially from fully fallen states.
 - **Action Bound Prevents Abrupt Motions:** Policies trained without the action bound (HOST-w/o-Bound) achieved high success rates but exhibited excessively violent motions, as indicated by significantly worse E_feet, E_smth, and E_engy metrics. The curriculum-based action bound in HOST effectively constrained motion speed, leading to smoother, safer motions.
 - **Smoothness Regularization Prevents Oscillation:** Removing L2C2 (HOST-w/o-L2C2) resulted in higher E_smth and E_engy, indicating more oscillatory and less energy-efficient motions, validating the regularization's role.
 - **Medium History Length Yields Best Performance:** Short history lengths (HOST-History0) underperformed, especially in contact-rich scenarios like the Wall terrain. Longer histories generally improved performance but could slightly reduce smoothness and increase energy, with HOST-History5 (the default) striking a good balance.

- **Trajectory Analysis (UMAP & 3D plots):** UMAP visualizations of joint-space trajectories showed distinct but consistent motion patterns for each terrain, adapting to different starting postures. 3D trajectory plots revealed coordinated hand and foot movements, demonstrating effective whole-body coordination and posture adaptability.

- **Robustness Analysis:** The learned policies exhibited remarkable robustness to various simulated disturbances: CoM position offset, consistent sagittal force, initial joint angle offset, and random torque dropout. This robustness is attributed to domain randomization and the learned adaptive control. The importance of historical information for maintaining robustness was underscored by the poor performance of HOST-History1 under disturbances.

- **Trade-off Analysis:** There's an inverse relationship between motion speed (fall-to-standing time) and smoothness: longer times generally yield smoother motions. Energy consumption also increased with fall-to-standing time, with terrain-specific variations (e.g., Wall terrain requiring higher energy despite slower motion due to pushing against the surface). This analysis reinforces the necessity of constrained motion speed for practical deployment.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Experiments

The policies trained in simulation were directly deployed on the Unitree G1 robot without any real-world adaptation techniques.

- **Smooth Regularization Improves Real Motions:** Comparisons between HOST and HOST-w/o-L2C2 on real hardware confirmed that smoothness regularization significantly reduced motion oscillations and improved stability, particularly on challenging terrains like the 10.5° slope. HOST achieved a 100% success rate across all tested laboratory scenes (Ground, Platform, Wall, Slope) with superior smoothness.
- **Generalization to Outdoor Environments:** HOST successfully generalized to unseen outdoor terrains, including grassland, wooden platforms, and stone roads, demonstrating stable and smooth standing-up motions. It also handled complex outdoor scenarios like stone platforms and tree-leaning postures, highlighting its real-world adaptability.
- **Sim-to-Real Analysis:** Domain randomization terms significantly reduced the sim-to-real gap, with CoM position randomization being particularly influential. Phase plots of knee and hip joints revealed discrepancies in joint velocities between simulation and reality, suggesting a remaining gap in joint torque models, despite which the controllers remained effective.
- **Emergent Properties:**
 - **Robustness to External Disturbance:** The robot demonstrated impressive resilience to external impacts (e.g., a 3kg ball), obstructive objects on the ground, and heavy payloads up to 12kg (twice the trunk mass).
 - **Fall Recovery:** The policies exhibited a surprising ability to recover from very large external forces without fully falling down, quickly regaining an upright posture.
 - **Dynamic Balance:** HOST enabled the robot to maintain dynamic balance on a 15° slippery slope, adapting posture and CoM in real-time.
 - **Standing Stability:** After standing, the robot maintained strong stability against further external disturbances.
- **Prone and Side-lying Postures:** HOST was shown to learn standing up from prone and side-lying postures on the ground, extending its versatility. However, significant differences in motion patterns between prone and supine postures currently limit joint training, requiring stricter constraints for hip joints in prone postures.
- **Extension to Large-size Robots:** HOST was successfully extended to Unitree H1 and H1-2 humanoid robots with minor tuning. Observations included greater reliance on upper-body contact and high hip actuation. Sim-to-real gaps were noted, particularly the need for higher joint stiffness to compensate for insufficient torques (consistent with G1 observations) and deviations in upper-body posture, warranting future investigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOST framework represents a significant advancement in humanoid robot control, particularly for the fundamental yet challenging task of standing up. Its core contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enabling Robust, Posture-Adaptive Standing-Up from Scratch:** The most notable achievement is demonstrating that humanoid robots can learn to stand up from diverse, arbitrary initial postures in the real world *without relying on predefined motion trajectories or human demonstrations*. This liberates robots from restrictive starting conditions, making them far more versatile and autonomous in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap Effectively:** The combination of carefully designed multi-stage rewards, a multi-critic architecture, curriculum-based exploration, explicit motion constraints (action rescaler and smoothness regularization), and extensive domain randomization addresses the sim-to-real gap more effectively than previous methods. This allows for direct transfer of policies from simulation to real hardware with high success rates, smoothness, and robustness, a critical step for practical robot deployment.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Resilience:** The emergent properties observed in real-world experiments—such as high tolerance to external forces, ability to navigate obstacles, handling significant payloads, fall recovery, and dynamic balance on slippery slopes—demonstrate an unprecedented level of robustness for humanoid standing-up control. This resilience is vital for robots operating in unpredictable human environments.
- **Foundational for Future Humanoid Capabilities:** Standing-up control is a prerequisite for seamless operation in many real-world scenarios. By solving this problem robustly, HOST paves the way for:
 - **Advanced Fall Recovery:** Robots can autonomously recover from various fall configurations, greatly improving their reliability and safety.
 - **Continuous Operation:** Enables robots to transition between different states (e.g., sitting to walking, lying to standing) without human intervention.
 - **Integration with Locomotion and Manipulation:** A robust standing-up capability allows for smoother integration with existing bipedal locomotion and loco-manipulation systems, creating more holistic and capable humanoid robots.
 - **Broader Real-World Applications:** From assisting in domestic environments to performing tasks in industrial settings or disaster recovery, robots capable of reliable self-righting will have expanded utility.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Perception Integration:** Relying solely on proprioception can lead to failures when robots collide with surroundings or need to infer complex environmental geometry (e.g., standing from a sofa that's too soft or in a cluttered space). Integrating perceptual capabilities (e.g., vision, lidar) would enhance environmental awareness and collision avoidance.
- **More Diverse Postures & Joint Training:** While capable of prone and side-lying, joint training across all possible supine and prone postures is currently challenging due to interference. Developing methods to seamlessly learn from a wider range of initial body orientations would improve overall generalization.
- **Deeper Integration with Existing Systems:** Although the work focuses on standing up, explicit demonstrations of its integration into broader humanoid control architectures (e.g., transitioning from standing to walking, or standing up to perform manipulation) would showcase its full potential.
- **Addressing Torque Discrepancies for Larger Robots:** The observations regarding sim-to-real gaps in joint torques for larger robots (H1, H1-2) highlight the need for more accurate actuator modeling or adaptive control strategies that can compensate for these physical discrepancies.

Overall, HOST represents a significant step towards developing truly autonomous, robust, and versatile humanoid robots, laying crucial groundwork for their expanded role in diverse real-world applications.
