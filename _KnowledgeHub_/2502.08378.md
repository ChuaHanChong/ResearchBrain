# Learning Humanoid Standing-up Control across Diverse Postures

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and partner institutions developed HOST, a reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to learn robust, posture-adaptive standing-up motions from diverse initial postures. The policies, trained entirely in simulation, directly transferred to the Unitree G1 robot, achieving a 100% success rate across tested real-world terrains and demonstrating significant resilience to external disturbances and varied environments.

## Problem

- Classical model-based standing-up methods are computationally intensive, sensitive to disturbances, and lack robustness to real-world uncertainties.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for standing up often rely on predefined motion trajectories or human demonstrations, limiting their adaptability to diverse, un-demonstrated initial postures.
- Training RL agents from scratch for complex whole-body tasks frequently leads to "violent" or jerky motions in reality due to the sim-to-real gap and unconstrained exploration, posing safety concerns.

## Method

- The HOST (Humanoid Standing-up Control) framework utilizes a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) based reinforcement learning approach with a comprehensive, multi-stage reward design.
- A Multi-Critic (MuC) architecture is introduced, assigning separate critics to different reward groups to better manage and balance gradients from a large set of objectives.
- To facilitate exploration and ensure smooth motions, a curriculum-based vertical pulling force guides initial learning, and an action rescaler progressively tightens action bounds; domain randomization is extensively applied to improve sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The HOST framework achieved a 100% standing-up success rate on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot across various real-world terrains, including flat ground, platforms, walls, and slopes, directly from simulation.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the multi-critic architecture was essential for any success (0% without it), while the force curriculum and action bound significantly improved exploration and motion smoothness respectively.
- The policies exhibited high robustness to external disturbances, such as impacts and heavy payloads (up to 12kg), and showed emergent capabilities like fall recovery and dynamic balance on a 15° slippery slope.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-critic architecture is crucial for effectively balancing numerous and potentially conflicting reward functions inherent in complex, multi-stage tasks like humanoid standing-up.
> - Curriculum learning, specifically a vertical pulling force curriculum, is highly effective for accelerating exploration from difficult, fully fallen states, while an action rescaler curriculum ensures smooth, non-violent motions for real-world deployment.
> - Extensive domain randomization and training across diverse simulated terrains are vital for bridging the sim-to-real gap, enabling direct transfer of complex whole-body humanoid policies to hardware without real-world adaptation.
