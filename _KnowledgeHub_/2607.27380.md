# VideoCoCo: Code-as-CoT for Physically-Consistent Video Generation via an Agentic Dual-Engine System

> [!summary] Summary
> VideoCoCo improves physical consistency in text-to-video generation by using executable Blender code as a process-level chain of thought. A coding agent writes a Blender program that renders a deterministic low-fidelity draft. A draft-conditioned video editor then turns the draft into a photorealistic video. It lifts OmniWeaving from 0.475 to 0.558 on PhyGenBench and from 52.18% to 77.88% on VBench-2.0.

## Problem

- Text-to-video models must infer a scene's full temporal evolution from a compressed prompt while also synthesizing appearance, which the paper calls Causal Opacity, so physical dynamics often come out wrong.
- Existing video chain-of-thought methods use text plans, layouts, or sparse keyframes as intermediates, which are non-executable or temporally sparse and cannot instantiate the whole spatiotemporal process.
- Explicit physics methods rely on fixed rules or templates that generalize poorly to open-world prompts, while implicit methods (rewards, preferences) give little control over the process of a specific prompt.

## Method

- **Executable Simulation Engine**: a coding agent writes a self-contained ==Blender Python program== specifying scene, objects, and temporal evolution, then a sandbox executes it into a deterministic, temporally dense ==white-clay draft video==.
- **Generative Video Engine**: an instruction agent composes an ==appearance-only editing instruction== from the prompt and draft, and a ==draft-conditioned video editor== restyles the draft into photorealistic video while preserving its motion.
- **VideoCoCo-3K**: 3,000 ==draft-instruction-target triplets== built with a Seedance 2.0 teacher editor, used to adapt an OmniWeaving-initialized editor via a ==conditional denoising loss==, with ==LoRA== or full tuning.

## Results

- PhyGenBench: average rises from **0.475** to **0.558**, best overall and ahead of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B at **0.544**, with the largest gains on material (**+0.133**) and thermal (**+0.078**) dynamics.
- VBench-2.0: average rises from **52.18%** to **77.88%** (**+25.70 points**), with best mechanics (**92.31%**) and thermotics (**72.92%**); material is second at **68.42%**, behind CogVideoX-1.5 at **83.19%**.
- Ablation: the tuning-free variant already lifts PhyGenBench from **0.475** to **0.506**, so the draft alone supplies the physics; LoRA (**0.558**) beats full fine-tuning (**0.535**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating process reasoning from appearance realization lets each model specialize: code fixes what happens and when, and the editor only decides how it looks.
> - Executable intermediates help most where learned visual priors are weakest, such as thermal and material dynamics, because code contributes dynamics rather than surface realism.
> - Narrow adaptation tasks like restyling simulated drafts favor lightweight LoRA over full fine-tuning, which risks overfitting small data and drifting from pretrained priors; limits are added latency and Blender expressiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen substantial advancements in generative models, particularly in the domain of text-to-video (T2V) synthesis. Modern T2V diffusion models, such as Open-Sora, HunyuanVideo, Wan, CogVideoX, and Step-Video-T2V, have demonstrated a capacity for producing visually realistic and temporally coherent videos. These developments represent a step toward generative world modeling, where machines aim to understand and simulate temporal dynamics.

Despite progress in visual fidelity, a significant challenge remains: generating videos that exhibit physically consistent dynamics. Current T2V models often infer the temporal evolution of a scene implicitly from highly compressed text prompts. This leads to a "Causal Opacity," where the underlying physical principles governing an event's evolution are not explicitly stated in the prompt, forcing the model to reconstruct complex spatiotemporal processes while simultaneously synthesizing visual appearance. Consequently, generated videos, while visually plausible, may violate physical laws (e.g., incorrect object interactions, unrealistic material behaviors, or inconsistent environmental effects).

To address this, researchers have explored intermediate representations to guide video generation, drawing inspiration from chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning in large language models. Existing CoT approaches for video generation typically fall into three categories:
1. **External Planning CoT:** Utilizes textual plans, layouts, or keyframes (e.g., VChain, VideoDirectorGPT) to condition a video generator.
2. **Visual-State CoT:** Reasons through a sequence of intermediate visual states within the generated video (e.g., VideoRLVR, ChEaP).
3. **Test-Time Search CoT:** Involves sampling multiple candidate videos and revising or selecting among them during inference (e.g., Video-T1, temporal backtracking).

While these methods offer improvements in planning and structural consistency, their intermediate representations often remain descriptive or selective. They can be non-executable, temporally sparse, or lack the capacity to instantiate a complete, inspectable, and revisable process. For instance, keyframes are sparse, and textual plans may still suffer from underspecification.

This work builds upon the emerging concept of "Code-as-CoT," previously applied to text-to-image generation (e.g., DraCo, CoCo), where executable code serves as an intermediate representation. VideoCoCo extends this paradigm to video generation, aiming to provide a dense, executable, and transparent process-level intermediate for synthesizing physically consistent temporal dynamics, thereby differentiating itself from prior CoT methods that offer less direct control over the underlying physical process. It positions itself as a method that explicitly grounds video generation in a simulated spatiotemporal realization, addressing the limitations of both explicit (rule-based, poor generalization) and implicit (data-learned priors, limited control) physics-aware generation paradigms.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the physical consistency of generated videos by explicitly externalizing the spatiotemporal process implicit in a text prompt. The authors identify a core limitation in current text-to-video models: the "Causal Opacity" arising from the need to implicitly infer complex physical dynamics from compressed natural language descriptions. This implicit inference often leads to videos that are visually appealing but physically inaccurate.

The motivation for VideoCoCo is rooted in addressing this challenge by:

1. **Overcoming limitations of existing T2V models:** Current models struggle to recover complete spatiotemporal processes that adhere to physical principles while simultaneously synthesizing photorealistic appearances. The simultaneous demand on the model to perform both complex reasoning and high-fidelity rendering from a sparse prompt is identified as a bottleneck for physical consistency.
2. **Addressing shortcomings of prior Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches for video:** While CoT has been explored for visual generation, existing methods typically rely on intermediate representations (e.g., text plans, layouts, keyframes, visual states) that are descriptive, temporally sparse, or non-executable. These representations limit the ability to instantiate, control, and inspect the complete, continuous spatiotemporal process. The authors argue for a more robust intermediate that can explicitly define "what happens and when."
3. **Introducing an executable and inspectable process-level intermediate:** The central motivation is to establish executable code as a "process-level chain of thought." By generating code that specifies scene evolution, the system can commit to a concrete, deterministic process before any visual rendering. This allows for transparency and direct control over the physical dynamics.
4. **Decoupling process-level reasoning from visual realization:** The research aims to separate the complex task of determining physically accurate dynamics from the task of generating photorealistic visuals. This decomposition allows each component of the system to specialize, improving both consistency and fidelity. The simulation engine handles "how the scene evolves," while the generative video engine handles "how it looks."
5. **Developing a framework for agentic code synthesis and simulation:** The goal is to build a system where an AI agent can synthesize a program (specifically, Blender Python code) that explicitly models the scene and its temporal evolution. This program is then executed in a sandboxed environment to produce a low-fidelity, but physically accurate, spatiotemporal draft.
6. **Enabling draft-conditioned photorealistic video generation:** A subsequent objective is to adapt a generative video model to transform these simulated drafts into high-fidelity, photorealistic videos, ensuring that the visual realization respects the underlying physical dynamics encoded in the draft. This requires novel training data.
7. **Creating a dedicated dataset:** A specific motivation is to construct VideoCoCo-3K, a dataset of draft–instruction–target triplets, to provide the necessary supervision for training the generative video engine to accurately translate simulated drafts into photorealistic outputs while preserving spatiotemporal coherence.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for T2V systems to move beyond mere visual plausibility to physically grounded world modeling, achieved through an explicit, controllable, and executable intermediate representation of physical processes.

### 3. Methodology and approach

VideoCoCo employs an agentic dual-engine framework designed to separate process-level reasoning from photorealistic visual realization. This system translates a text prompt into a physically consistent video by first simulating the dynamics and then rendering the visuals.

#### 3.1. Executable Simulation Engine

This initial engine is responsible for externalizing the implicit physical process described in a text prompt into an explicit, executable form.

- **Code-as-CoT Program Synthesis:** Given a user text prompt (p), a coding agent (A_code) is employed to synthesize a self-contained Blender Python program (c). This program explicitly defines the scene's objects, their physical properties, and their temporal evolution. The choice of Blender Python is deliberate due to three key properties:
 - **Explicit:** All objects, motions, and interactions must be declared, which prevents the underspecification common in natural language or sparse visual plans.
 - **Executable:** The program can be run to commit the system to a concrete, verifiable process.
 - **Inspectable:** The code is readable, editable, and re-executable, making the intermediate reasoning transparent. This contrasts with non-executable or sparse intermediate representations in prior CoT methods for video.
- **Sandboxed Execution and Draft Rendering:** The synthesized Blender program (c) is executed within an isolated Blender environment (B). This sandboxed execution ensures deterministic behavior and provides diagnostic signals for any coding or rendering errors. The output is a low-fidelity, white-clay spatiotemporal draft (d). This draft is intentionally stylized (without photorealistic materials or complex lighting) but is temporally dense, with each frame accurately representing a physically instantiated state of the programmed process, serving as a structural scaffold for the subsequent visual generation phase.

#### 3.2. Generative Video Engine

Once the physical process is instantiated as a draft, the second engine focuses on transforming this draft into a high-fidelity, photorealistic video. This engine's scope is deliberately constrained to appearance, leveraging the draft for spatiotemporal structure.

- **Editing Instruction Construction:** To bridge the semantic gap between the abstract prompt (p) and the stylized draft (d), an instruction agent (A_edit) processes both to compose an appearance-focused editing instruction (e). This instruction describes the desired subjects, materials, lighting, and cinematic style for the final video. Crucially, it is formulated to avoid redefining the motion already present in the draft, ensuring that the visual style complements rather than contradicts the simulated dynamics.
- **Draft-Conditioned Video Editing:** The generated draft (d) and the editing instruction (e) are then jointly fed into a draft-conditioned video editor (G_theta). This model produces the final photorealistic video (v_hat). The decomposition of responsibilities ensures that (d) provides the spatiotemporal structure and physical evolution, while (e) dictates the photorealistic appearance.
 - A challenge is that the white-clay simulation style of the drafts falls outside the typical training distribution of off-the-shelf video editors. This necessitates specific adaptation for G_theta to faithfully interpret the motion from the draft while generating realistic visuals.

#### 3.3. VideoCoCo-3K: Data for Draft-Conditioned Editing

To enable the generative video engine (G_theta) to handle simulated drafts effectively, a specialized dataset, VideoCoCo-3K, was constructed.

- **Teacher-Based Triplet Construction:** The dataset is built at scale using an agentic data construction pipeline. For each collected prompt (p_i), the executable simulation engine generates a draft (d_i), and the instruction agent composes an editing instruction (e_i). To obtain a corresponding photorealistic target video (y_i), a high-fidelity teacher editor (G_T), specifically Seedance 2.0, is invoked. Seedance 2.0 was selected for its ability to produce photorealistic frames while faithfully preserving the motion dictated by (d_i).
- **Dataset Composition:** VideoCoCo-3K comprises 3000 triplets of (d_i, e_i, y_i), ensuring alignment between physically grounded processes and their photorealistic realizations. Prompts used in evaluation benchmarks and their near-duplicates are excluded. Each triplet also retains the original prompt and Blender program as metadata for inspectability and future extension.

#### 3.4. Editor Adaptation and Inference

- **Training Objective:** The draft-conditioned editor (G_theta) is adapted using the VideoCoCo-3K triplets. A standard conditional denoising objective is minimized, where (d_i) and (e_i) act as conditions and (y_i) serves as the photorealistic target. This objective teaches the editor to synthesize videos consistent with the target's noise structure, conditioned on both the simulated draft and editing instruction. G_theta is initialized from the OmniWeaving base generator.
- **Adaptation Strategies:** Two strategies are explored: full fine-tuning of the editor parameters and parameter-efficient LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation).
- **End-to-End Inference:** During inference, VideoCoCo operates automatically from a text prompt. The coding agent synthesizes the Blender program, the sandbox renders the draft, the instruction agent composes the editing instruction, and the adapted editor generates the final video. The entire pipeline is designed to be fully automatic, inspectable, and reproducible, with no requirement for human intervention or auxiliary inputs beyond the initial prompt.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of VideoCoCo was evaluated through quantitative comparisons on two physics-focused video benchmarks, PhyGenBench and VBench-2.0, and through an ablation study on editor adaptation strategies.

#### 4.1. Code-as-CoT Improves Physical Consistency

- **PhyGenBench Evaluation:**
 - VideoCoCo significantly improved the average physical consistency score for the OmniWeaving baseline from 0.475 to 0.558. This represents the best overall average score among all tested models, surpassing strong open-source baselines like Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.544).
 - VideoCoCo achieved the highest per-category consistency scores in mechanics (0.558), optics (0.613), and material dynamics (0.525). It ranked second in thermal dynamics (0.511), closely behind Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (0.533).
 - The most substantial gains over the OmniWeaving baseline were observed in material dynamics (+0.133) and thermal dynamics (+0.078). These are categories where appearance-driven generators typically exhibit weaker priors due to the complexity of implicit physical inference. This finding supports the premise that explicit code-based dynamics contribute most where visual priors are weakest.
- **VBench-2.0 Evaluation:**
 - On VBench-2.0, which assesses intrinsic faithfulness and physical plausibility, VideoCoCo raised the OmniWeaving baseline's average plausibility from 52.18% to 77.88%, a gain of 25.70 percentage points. This also marked the best average score among all evaluated systems.
 - VideoCoCo achieved the highest scores in mechanics (92.31%) and thermotics (72.92%), showing improvements of 29.52 and 20.84 points, respectively, over the base generator.
 - It ranked second in material dynamics (68.42%), trailing CogVideoX-1.5 (83.19%).
 - The consistent pattern across both benchmarks suggests that simulation-grounded drafts effectively enhance the dimensions of physical plausibility that are challenging for purely data-driven generative models.

#### 4.2. Both Drafting and Tuning Contribute

An ablation study on PhyGenBench explored the contribution of the executable draft and different editor adaptation strategies:

- **Tuning-Free Variant:** Conditioning the unmodified OmniWeaving editor directly on the simulation draft (without any fine-tuning) already improved the average consistency score from 0.475 to 0.506. This variant showed the most significant gains in the thermal (0.433 to 0.478) and material (0.392 to 0.508) categories. This finding isolates the contribution of the executable draft itself, demonstrating that the draft, rather than additional training, is primarily responsible for supplying physical dynamics.
- **Full Fine-tuning Variant:** Fully fine-tuning the editor parameters on VideoCoCo-3K further increased the average score to 0.535.
- **LoRA Tuning Variant:** Using parameter-efficient LoRA tuning achieved the highest average score of 0.558, surpassing full fine-tuning. LoRA tuning improved every category over the base generator. The superior performance of LoRA over full fine-tuning, despite updating fewer parameters, is attributed to its ability to acquire the specific skill of restyling white-clay drafts into photorealistic videos while retaining the strong visual priors of the base generator. Full fine-tuning, conversely, was more prone to overfitting the limited triplet data and drifting from these priors.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (Figure 3 in the original paper) illustrated that for prompts involving specific physical phenomena, such as dry-ice sublimation or vacuum-induced bottle collapse, the OmniWeaving baseline often produced visually plausible videos that nevertheless violated the intended physical process. In contrast, VideoCoCo generated videos that accurately followed the executable draft, realizing the correct dynamics with photorealistic appearance.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The VideoCoCo framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of generative AI and world modeling:

1. **Validation of Executable Code as Chain-of-Thought:** The research empirically validates that executable Blender code can serve as an effective, controllable, and inspectable process-level intermediate representation for generating physically consistent video. This provides a concrete example of how AI systems can reason through explicit, verifiable steps for complex generative tasks.
2. **Advancement in Physical Consistency:** By achieving state-of-the-art average performance on established benchmarks like PhyGenBench and VBench-2.0, VideoCoCo demonstrates a substantial improvement in the physical plausibility of generated videos. The specific gains in thermal and material dynamics highlight its ability to address complex physical phenomena where traditional, appearance-driven models typically struggle.
3. **Decoupling of Reasoning and Realization:** The dual-engine design effectively separates the challenging tasks of spatiotemporal simulation (process-level reasoning) and photorealistic rendering (visual realization). This modularity simplifies each sub-problem, allowing specialized components to excel at their respective functions, which contributes to both the physical accuracy and visual fidelity of the generated videos.
4. **Enhanced Controllability and Inspectability:** The use of explicit, executable code for defining scene dynamics offers a higher degree of control and transparency compared to implicit learning approaches. Researchers and users can inspect the generated code, understand the system's reasoning, and potentially edit the code to fine-tune specific physical behaviors, thereby increasing the system's interpretability and debuggability.
5. **New Data Resource for Draft-Conditioned Editing:** The creation of VideoCoCo-3K, a dataset of draft–instruction–target triplets, addresses a critical data gap in training generative video models for draft-conditioned editing. This dataset provides aligned supervision for adapting models to translate simulated processes into photorealistic videos, and its public availability (implied by the project page and arXiv listing) could stimulate further research in this area.
6. **Foundation for Generative World Modeling:** By enabling machines to generate videos with a deeper understanding of underlying physical laws, VideoCoCo contributes to the broader goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and generative world modeling. It moves beyond merely producing visually plausible outputs towards modeling how the world realistically evolves over time.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including the integration of more specialized physical simulation engines (e.g., Taichi) to handle highly complex phenomena (like turbulent fluids) that might be challenging for Blender alone. It also suggests exploring knowledge distillation techniques to internalize these explicit executable priors directly into end-to-end video models, potentially reducing inference-time latency associated with external simulation.

Despite introducing additional inference latency and being bounded by the expressiveness of the underlying Blender simulator, VideoCoCo represents a methodological shift towards more explicit, physics-grounded approaches in video generation.
