# AffordSim: A Scalable Data Generator and Benchmark for Affordance-Aware Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AffordSim is a simulation framework for robotic manipulation that integrates open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction to guide grasp generation, leading to task-relevant data. It introduces a 50-task benchmark to evaluate affordance reasoning and employs comprehensive domain randomization, including 3D Gaussian Splatting for backgrounds, enabling policies trained on its data to achieve up to 25% average zero-shot success on a real robot.

## Problem

- Generating scalable training data for robotic manipulation tasks is challenging, as current methods rely on labor-intensive manual contact annotations or generic grasp estimators lacking task semantics.
- Generic grasp estimators often select stable but functionally irrelevant grasps on objects, leading to task failures, and do not inherently provide information for complex object-to-object interactions.
- Existing domain randomization techniques often use synthetic textures and simplified backgrounds, creating a noticeable appearance gap that hinders zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- **AffordSim** integrates a ==VLM-powered== module for automated scene generation and affordance query extraction from natural language descriptions.
- It employs an ==affordance-aware trajectory collection== pipeline that uses an ==open-vocabulary 3D affordance model== (**VoxAfford**) to predict functional regions, followed by a ==two-stage grasp selection== process to ensure task-relevant grasp planning.
- A comprehensive ==domain randomization== module, featuring ==cross-viewpoint background replacement== using ==3D Gaussian Splatting== from real photographs, is included to enhance sim-to-real transfer capabilities.

## Results

- On the 50-task benchmark, AffordSim's affordance-guided trajectory collection reaches per-tier success of **79%** (medium) and **64%** (hard) — versus generic AnyGrasp's **15%** and **3%** — recovering **93%** of the manual-annotation success rate without any per-object annotation.
- On the 20-task simulation benchmark, the strongest policies reach **56%** (Pi 0.5) and **54%** (VLA-adapter) overall; the Pi 0.5–BC gap widens to nearly **30** points on hard tasks, with failures concentrated in precise post-grasp alignment and long-horizon action chains.
- Zero-shot sim-to-real on a real Franka FR3 (10 trials/task, no fine-tuning) averages **24%** — Pi 0.5 **25%**, VLA-adapter **23%**, DP **8%**, ACT **3%**, BC **0%** — with a per-task gradient of placing up to **40%**, pouring **30%**, and hanging only **10%**, confirming the bottleneck is affordance difficulty, not a simulation artifact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating 3D affordance prediction directly into the data generation process is crucial for producing task-relevant manipulation trajectories, significantly outperforming generic grasp estimators on affordance-critical tasks.
> - The accuracy and generalization capabilities of the underlying 3D affordance prediction model are primary factors influencing overall task success, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained affordance understanding.
> - Advanced domain randomization, particularly the use of 3D Gaussian Splatting for realistic background replacement, effectively bridges the sim-to-real appearance gap, enabling successful zero-shot transfer for trained policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation heavily relies on simulation for generating training data and benchmarking policy performance. Existing simulation platforms, such as Isaac Sim, RoboTwin 2.0, ManiSkill3, RoboCasa, GenManip, RLBench, and others, have advanced in areas including scene diversity, GPU-parallelized rendering, and unified task suites. However, a common limitation across many of these platforms is the implicit or absent treatment of object affordances during trajectory collection.

Current approaches to generating manipulation trajectories in simulation typically fall into two categories:
1. **Manual Contact Annotations**: This method involves human experts authoring task-specific grasp poses for each object, as seen in systems like RoboTwin 2.0. While effective for specific, annotated tasks, this approach does not scale efficiently to new objects or tasks, as each addition requires a fresh round of human intervention.
2. **Generic Grasp Estimators**: These estimators score grasp candidates based on stability without incorporating task semantics. While scalable, they frequently select stable grasps on object regions that are functionally irrelevant to the task, leading to downstream action failure (e.g., grasping the handle of a mug when the task requires hanging it by the handle). Attempts to mitigate this by post-hoc filtering with semantic masks (e.g., in GenManip) are often insufficient because the generic estimator may not have originally proposed stable candidates within the desired functional region. Furthermore, generic grasp estimators typically model only the grasping action, providing limited or no information for object-to-object interactions required by complex tasks.

A complementary area of research involves using Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to automate task and scene authoring or to scale demonstrations from small seed sets (e.g., GenSim2, RoboGen). However, these systems often inherit the same affordance gap by relying on underlying generic grasp pipelines.

In parallel, 3D affordance prediction has developed as a standalone perception problem. Early work focused on predicting actionable regions on point clouds with fixed label sets, primarily evaluated on detection metrics rather than executable manipulation trajectories. More recent efforts have explored open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction using free-form language queries (e.g., VoxAfford, OpenAD, 3D-AffordanceLLM). While these models can identify affordance regions, their output of per-point affordance scores does not directly translate into kinematically feasible grasp poses without a subsequent planning stage, which prior work in this area typically does not provide.

Domain randomization is a established technique for facilitating sim-to-real transfer by diversifying visual and physical parameters during training. However, the visual randomization in many prior methods often relies on synthetic textures and simplified backgrounds, which can leave a noticeable appearance gap when transferring to real-world scenes.

AffordSim aims to bridge these gaps by explicitly integrating open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction directly into the data generation pipeline. This positions affordance reasoning as an upstream constraint for grasp generation, addressing the limitations of both manual annotations and generic grasp estimators on affordance-dependent tasks. It also enhances domain randomization by incorporating advanced techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting for realistic background replacement, thereby improving the visual fidelity for sim-to-real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for AffordSim stems from the challenges associated with generating data for robot manipulation tasks that are dependent on object affordances. Many everyday manipulation skills require a robot to interact with a specific, functional region of an object for the task to succeed. Current simulation data generation methods exhibit limitations in handling such tasks:

1. **Inscalability of Manual Annotations**: Manually annotating grasp poses for each object and task, while precise, is labor-intensive and does not scale to the vast number of objects and tasks encountered in real-world scenarios. Each new asset or task requires significant human effort.
2. **Lack of Semantic Awareness in Generic Grasp Estimators**: Generic grasp estimators prioritize grasp stability over task semantics. This often leads to the selection of stable grasps on object regions that are not functionally relevant to the task (e.g., grasping the body of a mug when the handle is needed for hanging). Post-hoc filtering with semantic masks is inadequate because the initial grasp proposals may not include candidates within the desired functional region. Furthermore, these estimators provide no inherent mechanism for reasoning about object-to-object interactions, which are crucial for many manipulation tasks (e.g., pouring, hanging).

The paper identifies that the root cause of these challenges is the implicit or absent integration of affordance reasoning during trajectory collection. AffordSim proposes to address this by making affordance explicit—computing it as a distinct, initial step that constrains the grasp generation process. This approach aims to shape the candidate grasp space from the outset, ensuring that only grasps within predicted functional regions are considered, thereby pre-emptively eliminating stable but functionally incorrect grasps.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Scalable Affordance-Aware Data Generator**: To create a simulation framework that automatically synthesizes task-relevant scenes and generates manipulation trajectories by integrating open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction. This aims to eliminate the need for per-object manual contact annotations.
2. **Establish an Affordance-Aware Benchmark**: To curate a benchmark comprising 50 diverse manipulation tasks that explicitly demand affordance reasoning. This benchmark is designed to evaluate how effectively a policy understands and utilizes object affordances, rather than merely measuring success on grasp-stable scenarios.
3. **Enhance Sim-to-Real Transfer Capabilities**: To improve the visual realism of simulated data for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer by implementing a comprehensive domain randomization module. This includes novel cross-viewpoint background replacement using 3D Gaussian Splatting from real photographs.
4. **Validate Policy Training and Performance**: To demonstrate the utility of the generated data by training vision-language-action policies and evaluating their performance, including zero-shot transfer to a real robotic platform, thereby confirming the practical value of affordance-guided data generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AffordSim is a simulation framework implemented within NVIDIA Isaac Sim, designed to translate natural-language task descriptions into diverse manipulation demonstrations for imitation learning. The framework operates through a pipeline of three interconnected modules: VLM-based scene generation, affordance-aware trajectory collection, and domain randomization.

#### 3.1. VLM-Powered Task and Scene Generation

This module automates the creation of simulation scenes and the extraction of affordance queries from natural-language task descriptions.
- **Semantic Parsing**: A Vision-Language Model (VLM), instantiated with Qwen-VL-Plus, processes the natural-language task description. It generates a structured scene graph, which includes identified objects, their attributes, and spatial relationships. For example, a description like "Give me a desktop scene, including a bowl, a microwave, a kettle. Pick up the Kettle by its handle and pour into the bowl" is parsed into semantic components.
- **Asset Retrieval**: Based on the VLM's output, appropriate 3D USD assets are retrieved from an indexed library. The VLM specifies target categories and semantic attributes (e.g., color, articulation type), and the framework binds corresponding assets.
- **Scene Graph Assembly**: The VLM's scene graph dictates the assembly of the simulation scene. Each node in the graph specifies an object's category, USD asset path, initial 6-DoF pose, color, and whether it is manipulated or static. Spatial relationships parsed from the input are used to enforce collision-free object placement through rejection sampling.
- **Affordance Query Extraction**: Concurrently, the VLM extracts free-text affordance queries. One query is generated for each functional contact region of every manipulated object, ensuring that the policy is directed to the task-relevant parts. For instance, in the kettle example, queries might include "Graspable Handle" for the kettle, "Pour Out" for its spout, and "Pour In" for the bowl's rim.

#### 3.2. Affordance-Aware Trajectory Collection

This module translates the affordance queries into executable manipulation trajectories.
- **3D Affordance Prediction**: The canonical point cloud of the manipulated object (shipped with its USD asset) is combined with the corresponding affordance query. This input is fed into an open-vocabulary 3D affordance model (specifically, VoxAfford). The model outputs a per-point binary mask, M_a, which highlights the queried affordance region on the object's surface.
- **Two-Stage Grasp Selection**:
 - **Stage 1: Sampling Grasps on the Affordance Peak**: Mean-shift clustering is applied to the masked points in M_a to identify distinct affordance regions. The centroid of each cluster (p) is taken as a representative contact point. At each point p, the local surface normal (n) is estimated using PCA on its nearest neighbors. K approach directions (d_k) are sampled on the hemisphere opposite to n, with a fixed polar tilt and varying azimuth. The wrist yaw is determined to ensure the gripper's closing axis is perpendicular to d_k and aligns with the local affordance geometry. This process generates K candidate grasps, C = {g_k}.
 - **Stage 2: Motion-Planner Scoring**: For each candidate grasp g_k, cuRobo is employed to plan a collision-free pre-grasp trajectory (τ_k) from the robot's current joint configuration to g_k. The quality of each trajectory is scored inversely proportional to its joint-space arc length, with a score of 0 assigned to colliding trajectories. The grasp with the highest score (g*) and its associated trajectory (τ_k*) are selected.
- **Motion Planning and Execution**: The selected pre-grasp trajectory is augmented with subsequent segments for gripper closure, lifting, and task completion, all planned by cuRobo while respecting scene collision meshes. The complete task trajectory is then executed in the simulator.

#### 3.3. Domain Randomization for Sim-to-Real Transfer

To minimize the reality gap and facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, AffordSim incorporates domain randomization along five axes:
- **Background Substitution**: The workspace background is replaced with either random textures from a diverse library or, more notably, cross-viewpoint renders of real-world scenes. The latter involves reconstructing real deployment scenes using 3D Gaussian Splatting from 10–20 photographs, with Depth Anything 3 for view synthesis. This enables rendering realistic backgrounds from arbitrary camera viewpoints within the simulator.
- **Lighting**: Randomization includes variations in the type, position, intensity, and color of light sources.
- **Object Texture**: Object surfaces are randomized using PBR (Physically Based Rendering) albedo properties and textures from a photo asset library.
- **Object Pose**: Initial object positions (xyz) and orientations (quaternion) are perturbed within specified ranges.
- **Image Noise**: Gaussian pixel noise is added to the rendered RGB observations.

#### 3.4. Benchmark Design

AffordSim introduces a benchmark of 50 affordance-aware tasks, categorized into seven manipulation skills: pick & place (20 tasks), open/close (2), pull/push (5), hang (2), pour (7), stack (4), and long-horizon composite (10). These tasks are specifically chosen to require interaction with functional object regions. The benchmark supports five robot embodiments (Franka, Kinova, Sawyer, UR5e, xArm7) and uses an asset library of 500+ rigid and articulated USD objects, including 50 objects with human-annotated affordance ground truth for oracle comparisons.
- **Difficulty Tiers**: Tasks are divided into three tiers:
 - **Easy, Generic-Grasp Tasks (10)**: Success can be achieved with any stable grasp.
 - **Medium, Affordance-Critical Tasks (30)**: Require contact at specific functional regions, posing challenges for generic grasp estimators.
 - **Hard, Composite Affordance Tasks (10)**: Involve complex sequential dependencies (long-horizon) or exceptionally tight precision/stability margins within short-horizon settings.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Task success rate (SR) is measured over 100 simulation rollouts, with automatic checks based on rigid-body poses and articulation states. Multi-step tasks also report per-stage SRs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AffordSim encompassed four key experimental axes: affordance integration for trajectory collection, imitation learning performance, affordance model ablation, and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Affordance Integration on the 50-task Benchmark

This evaluation compared five grasp methods across all 50 benchmark tasks, under conditions of aggressive object pose randomization.
- **Method Comparison**: The methods were: Manual (a manually tuned heuristic), AnyGrasp (generic stability-based), AnyGrasp+Filter (AnyGrasp with a 2D semantic mask filter), AffordSim (our affordance-guided method), and Human Aff. (oracle with human-annotated ground truth affordances).
- **Trajectory Collection Success Rates (SR%)**:
 - **Easy Tasks (Average of 10)**: Human Aff. (100%), Manual (100%), AffordSim (98%), AnyGrasp+Filter (87%), AnyGrasp (67%).
 - **Medium Tasks (Average of 30)**: Human Aff. (93%), Manual (85%), AffordSim (79%), AnyGrasp+Filter (45%), AnyGrasp (15%).
 - **Hard Tasks (Average of 10)**: Human Aff. (86%), Manual (72%), AffordSim (64%), AnyGrasp+Filter (16%), AnyGrasp (3%).
- **Observations**: AffordSim demonstrated a significant advantage over generic grasp estimators, particularly on Medium and Hard tasks, approaching the performance of manually tuned methods without per-object annotation. AnyGrasp and AnyGrasp+Filter showed severe limitations beyond Easy tasks and completely failed (0% SR) on "hang" tasks due to their inability to reason about post-grasp object-to-object interactions. AffordSim also exhibited a specific out-of-distribution failure mode on "hang" tasks (e.g., 25% on "hang cup"), where the required grasp (cup body to free the handle) was under-represented in the affordance backbone's training data. This suggests that while AffordSim’s approach is effective, the performance is reliant on the affordance model's generalization capabilities.

#### 4.2. Imitation Learning on AffordSim Data

Five imitation learning baselines (BC, Diffusion Policy (DP), ACT, VLA-adapter, Pi 0.5) were trained exclusively on AffordSim data for 20 representative tasks, with randomization applied only to object pose.
- **Overall Performance**: Pi 0.5 (56% average SR) and VLA-adapter (54% average SR) outperformed DP (37%), ACT (31%), and BC (13%).
- **Difficulty Scaling**: Success rates consistently decreased with increasing task difficulty across all baselines. Easy tasks yielded high average SRs (Pi 0.5: 89%), while Medium tasks saw a drop (Pi 0.5: 60%), and Hard tasks were notably challenging (Pi 0.5: 24%).
- **Sub-Task Analysis**: The "pick" stages of multi-stage tasks generally achieved high success rates (93–100% for VLA-adapter and Pi 0.5), indicating that AffordSim's affordance-guided grasps are executable. However, failures predominantly occurred in downstream, post-grasp functional stages, such as precise alignment in pouring or hanging, or lengthy action chains in stacking tasks. This suggests that the bottleneck lies in precise post-grasp alignment and robust long-horizon action sequencing rather than the initial affordance-grounded grasp.

#### 4.3. Affordance Model Ablation

To isolate the impact of the affordance prediction component, AffordSim was evaluated by swapping its VoxAfford backbone with four other open-vocabulary 3D affordance models (OpenAD, IAGNet, LASO, 3D-AffordanceLLM) on a subset of 10 tasks.
- **Results**: VoxAfford consistently yielded the highest trajectory collection success rates across all difficulty tiers (average 76%), with the performance gap between VoxAfford and other models widening as task difficulty increased.
- **Observations**: The trajectory SR closely correlated with the published mIoU rankings of the affordance models on detection benchmarks. This result indicates that the accuracy and robustness of the underlying 3D affordance prediction model is a dominant factor influencing overall task success, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained affordance understanding.

#### 4.4. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

The five imitation learning baselines, trained solely on AffordSim data, were deployed zero-shot on a real Franka FR3 robot for four representative tasks (placing, pouring, hanging). The real workspace background was reconstructed using 3D Gaussian Splatting with Depth Anything 3.
- **Success Rates**: Pi 0.5 achieved the highest average success rate at 25%, followed by VLA-adapter at 23%. DP, ACT, and BC lagged significantly, with 8%, 3%, and 0% average success, respectively.
- **Task-Specific Performance**: Placing tasks showed success rates up to 40%, pouring tasks up to 30%, and hanging tasks up to 10%.
- **Observations**: The relative performance ordering of the policies and the difficulty gradient across different task types mirrored the simulation results. This demonstrates that AffordSim data, combined with advanced domain randomization, enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for capable policies, and that the identified challenges (e.g., precise post-grasp alignment) are not merely simulation artifacts but reflect real-world robotic manipulation difficulties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AffordSim represents a notable advancement in the generation of simulation data for robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks that are affordance-dependent. Its primary significance lies in explicitly integrating open-vocabulary 3D affordance prediction into the data generation pipeline, which differentiates it from prior approaches that rely on non-scalable manual annotations or semantically-unaware generic grasp estimators.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Scalability of Data Generation**: By automating scene synthesis, affordance query extraction, and grasp selection based on natural language and 3D affordance models, AffordSim offers a scalable alternative to labor-intensive manual annotation. This can significantly reduce the cost and time associated with creating large-scale, diverse datasets for robot learning.
2. **Development of Affordance-Aware Policies**: The introduction of a 50-task benchmark specifically designed to evaluate affordance reasoning encourages the development of robotic policies that genuinely understand object functionality, rather than simply optimizing for stable grasps. This benchmark can serve as a standardized tool for assessing progress in this critical area of robot intelligence.
3. **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The comprehensive domain randomization strategy, particularly the novel use of 3D Gaussian Splatting for realistic, cross-viewpoint background replacement, directly addresses the appearance gap between simulation and the real world. This capability is crucial for enabling zero-shot transfer of policies trained in simulation to physical robots, reducing the need for costly real-world data collection and fine-tuning.
4. **Advancement in Vision-Language-Action Models**: The experimental results demonstrate that advanced VLA policies (e.g., Pi 0.5, VLA-adapter) can effectively leverage AffordSim data for improved performance on complex affordance-aware tasks, including successful zero-shot transfer to real robots. This highlights the potential of such integrated approaches for generalist robot learning.
5. **Foundation for Future Research**: AffordSim provides a framework and benchmark that can support future research directions, such as extending affordance prediction to dynamic and contact-force affordances, handling deformable and bimanual manipulation, and achieving broader real-world deployment. The identified bottlenecks, particularly in precise post-grasp alignment and long-horizon action chaining, offer clear avenues for further policy development.

In summary, AffordSim contributes to the field by providing a principled, scalable, and robust method for generating affordance-aware manipulation data, thereby facilitating the training and evaluation of more capable and generalizable robotic systems.
