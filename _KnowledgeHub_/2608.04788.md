# Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Observation-Calibrated Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Observation-Calibrated Self-Distillation (OCSD) resolves a confounding issue in agentic reinforcement learning by precisely disentangling the influence of future observations from replay context in self-distillation signals. This method consistently improves performance and generalization across various LLM agent benchmarks while providing more accurate token-level supervision.

## Problem

- LLM agents for complex tasks face sparse, trajectory-level rewards in reinforcement learning, requiring dense, token-level supervision.
- On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) aims to provide this supervision, but signals from privileged future observations are conflated with score shifts from the replay scaffold itself.
- This conflation leads to imprecise attribution of token-level support and inconsistent performance gains in agent training.

## Method

- **OCSD** constructs structurally matched "Full Evidence" (with actual future observations) and "Observation-Ablated Evidence" (with placeholders) replay views.
- An ==observation residual== is computed by comparing log-probabilities from these two views, isolating the specific impact of future observations.
- This calibrated signal modulates the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== advantage at high-uncertainty interaction steps, identified via ==Negative Log-Likelihood==.

## Results

- OCSD consistently achieved stronger overall success rates across ALFWorld, WebShop, and Search-QA benchmarks, improving over GRPO by up to **14.0 points** on ALFWorld with Qwen3-8B.
- The method demonstrated superior out-of-domain generalization on Search-QA across all model scales, suggesting more robust and transferable policies.
- The calibrated observation residual showed better alignment with local environment feedback, achieving an AUROC of **0.707** in distinguishing valid from invalid actions, compared to **0.654** for uncalibrated Full support.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Uncalibrated privileged replay support can be inconsistent and even detrimental, highlighting the need to disentangle the true signal from contextual confounds.
> - Structural calibration through differential scoring against an ablated view provides a more precise and reliable token-level supervision signal.
> - Prioritizing high-uncertainty steps for calibration effectively focuses the supervision, improving learning efficiency and avoiding noise from indiscriminate application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Model (LLM) agents for complex, long-horizon interactive tasks, such as web navigation, embodied control, and information seeking, is a prominent area of artificial intelligence research. These tasks typically involve multiple steps of observation, reasoning, and action, making reinforcement learning (RL) a common paradigm for training such agents. Methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are often employed.

A challenge in training these agents with RL is the sparsity of rewards. Traditional GRPO variants often apply a single trajectory-level reward to all tokens within a multi-step trajectory, which provides limited, coarse guidance for updating individual tokens. This limitation motivates the use of dense, token-level supervision.

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) has emerged as a technique to generate such fine-grained supervision. OPSD re-evaluates generated tokens under a "privileged replay view" after a rollout. This privileged information can be derived from subsequent environment observations, completed interaction trajectories, or distilled experience summaries. Existing agentic self-distillation methods generally interpret the probability change induced by this replay view as the direct "support" contributed by the hindsight information, assuming other contextual elements of the replay do not significantly affect token scores.

This paper identifies a specific confounding issue within this OPSD framework, particularly when future environment observations serve as privileged information. The core problem is that the "support" signal derived from a full replay view may conflate the actual privileged information (future observation) with score shifts induced by the additional context and structure of the replay scaffold itself (e.g., replay format, naturalized future actions). This makes it difficult to attribute the observed changes in token scores solely to the crucial future observation.

Prior work on calibration and reliability of privileged signals has addressed related issues, such as reducing hint-induced style shifts, controlling for reference-specific shortcuts, or capturing prediction changes associated with reasoning specialization. This research distinguishes itself by focusing specifically on disentangling the effects of future environment observations from the influence of the shared replay scaffold in agentic replay signals. By addressing this attribution confound, the work aims to enhance the precision and reliability of token-level supervision in agentic RL, leading to more effective and stable training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve a specific confounding issue observed in agentic reinforcement learning when On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) is used with privileged replay views derived from future environment observations. The authors identify that the "support" signal obtained from a Full replay view, which incorporates future observations, can reflect not only the information contained in these observations but also score shifts introduced by the replay scaffold itself, such as the replay format and naturalized future action schemas. This conflation makes it challenging to accurately attribute changes in token probabilities directly to the invaluable future environment feedback.

The motivation for addressing this confounding issue is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhancing Attribution Accuracy:** Without proper calibration, the signal derived from privileged replay may misrepresent the true impact of future observations on token choices. This can lead to imprecise or misleading gradients during the policy update, potentially hindering effective learning. For instance, some tokens might receive strong "support" due to the structural cues of the replay format rather than their relevance to the actual future environment state.

2. **Improving Supervision Quality:** Agentic reinforcement learning often suffers from sparse trajectory-level rewards. OPSD aims to provide dense, token-level supervision, but if this supervision is confounded, its quality diminishes. The goal is to purify this signal, ensuring that the token-level guidance specifically reflects the predictive power or alignment with future observations.

3. **Achieving Consistent Performance Gains:** Previous direct applications of privileged replay support have yielded inconsistent gains, sometimes even performing worse than baseline GRPO. The authors hypothesize that this inconsistency is partly due to the uncalibrated nature of the privileged signal. By calibrating this signal, the research aims to unlock more stable and robust performance improvements across diverse tasks and model scales.

4. **Promoting Local Environment Feedback Alignment:** The paper explicitly seeks to ensure that the derived token-level supervision aligns more closely with local environment feedback. The confounding effect can mask this alignment. By isolating the contribution of actual observations, the resulting signal should more accurately indicate whether a generated token or action is consistent with subsequent environment states.

In summary, the core objective is to move beyond a simple "privileged view" to a *calibrated* privileged view, where the influence of actual future observations on token scores is precisely isolated from the extraneous influences of the replay context. This is expected to provide a more targeted and effective form of token-level supervision for training LLM agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Observation-Calibrated Self-Distillation (OCSD), is designed to address the confounding issue in agentic privileged replay scoring by isolating the impact of future observations. OCSD integrates with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to provide token-level supervision.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The work builds on the standard LLM agent framework where an agent interacts with an environment, generating token sequences ($y_k$) that form actions based on an interaction context ($h_k$). Rewards ($R(\tau)$) are typically terminal and trajectory-level. GRPO assigns a group-relative advantage ($A_i$) to all tokens in a trajectory, but this advantage is uniform and does not distinguish individual token contributions. On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) computes token-level support ($\delta_{i,k,t}$) by re-scoring generated tokens under a privileged replay view ($E_{i,k}$) compared to the original context, indicating increased or reduced support.

#### 3.2. Construction of Structurally Matched Replay Views

OCSD constructs two distinct but structurally matched replay views to enable calibration:
- **Full Evidence ($E^F_{i,k}$):** This view incorporates actual future observations (e.g., $o_{k+1}, o_{k+2}$) and "naturalized schemas" of future actions (e.g., $g(a_{k+1})$). Naturalized schemas preserve the coarse intent and syntactic structure of future actions but replace specific, high-information arguments (like concrete entities or queries) with generic semantic roles (e.g., "take an item from a receptacle"). This view represents the complete hindsight information typically used in OPSD.
- **Observation-Ablated Evidence ($E^A_{i,k}$):** This view is designed to be structurally identical to the Full evidence in terms of field order, replay format, and future-action scaffold. However, it explicitly replaces the actual future observation content with a fixed placeholder phrase, "Observation: not provided."

By contrasting these two views, OCSD aims to isolate the effect of the actual future observation, as the extraneous elements of the replay scaffold are common to both.

#### 3.3. Observation-Calibrated Token Signal

From the scores derived using these two views, OCSD defines the **observation residual** ($e_{i,k,t}$):
$e_{i,k,t} = \log \pi_F(y_{i,k,t} | h_{i,k,t}, E^F_{i,k}) - \log \pi_A(y_{i,k,t} | h_{i,k,t}, E^A_{i,k})$
This residual directly compares the log-probabilities assigned by the Full Teacher and the Observation-Ablated Teacher to the same token. The student log-probability terms (which would appear in the full $\delta_F$ and $\delta_A$ definitions) cancel out. This residual captures the incremental difference between the two views, specifically discounting score changes attributed to the shared replay scaffold.
The unbounded log-probability difference ($e_{i,k,t}$) is then mapped to a bounded observation-calibrated token signal ($q_{i,k,t}$):
$q_{i,k,t} = \tanh(e_{i,k,t}/2)$, where $q_{i,k,t} \in [-1, 1]$.
The sign of $q_{i,k,t}$ indicates whether the Full Teacher assigns higher or lower support, while its magnitude reflects the degree of disagreement between the views.

#### 3.4. NLL-Guided Step Selection

To efficiently apply the calibrated signal and avoid noisy updates, OCSD prioritizes "high-uncertainty" interaction steps. For each step $k$ in a trajectory $\tau_i$, a step uncertainty score ($u_{i,k}$) is calculated as the negative average log-likelihood of all tokens generated at that step under the old policy. A higher $u_{i,k}$ indicates greater uncertainty. From each trajectory, the top $\rho$ fraction of steps with the highest uncertainty scores are selected for calibration. This ensures that calibration is focused on steps where the model is less confident and potentially most in need of fine-grained guidance.

#### 3.5. Sign-Preserving Integration with GRPO

OCSD integrates this calibrated token signal into the GRPO objective. It preserves the update direction determined by the trajectory-level GRPO advantage ($A_i$) but uses $q_{i,k,t}$ to modulate the *strength* of individual token updates within the selected steps. The calibrated advantage for a token ($A^{OCSD}_{i,k,t}$) is defined as:
$A^{OCSD}_{i,k,t} = A_i (1 + \beta \text{sgn}(A_i) q_{i,k,t})$ if $k$ is a selected step, and $A_i$ otherwise.
Here, $\beta \in$ controls the modulation strength. The term $\text{sgn}(A_i)$ ensures that the calibration signal $q_{i,k,t}$ (which can be positive or negative) adjusts the magnitude in a direction consistent with the overall trajectory advantage. For example, if $A_i$ is positive (good trajectory), a positive $q_{i,k,t}$ amplifies the positive advantage, while a negative $q_{i,k,t}$ reduces it. This guarantees that the calibrated advantage preserves the sign of the original trajectory advantage.
The standard GRPO surrogate objective is then modified by replacing the trajectory-level advantage with this calibrated token-level advantage, while still incorporating importance sampling and KL regularization.

#### 3.6. End-to-End Training Procedure

The training procedure involves:
1. **Rollout:** The current policy samples groups of trajectories.
2. **GRPO Advantage Calculation:** Terminal rewards are used to compute group-relative trajectory advantages.
3. **NLL-Guided Step Selection:** High-NLL steps are identified within each trajectory.
4. **Dual-View Replay Scoring:** For selected steps, the Full and Observation-Ablated views re-score generated tokens to compute the observation residual and calibrated signal.
5. **Advantage Modulation:** The calibrated signal modulates the GRPO advantage at the token level for selected steps.
6. **Policy Update:** The model parameters are updated by maximizing the modified GRPO objective.
OCSD adds minimal computational overhead, as replay scoring and modulation are efficient, introducing only a small percentage overhead relative to the shared GRPO pipeline.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated OCSD across three agentic benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop, Search-QA) using three scales of Qwen3 models (1.7B, 4B, 8B).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

OCSD consistently achieved the strongest overall results across all benchmarks and model scales compared to various baselines.
- **ALFWorld:** OCSD attained overall success rates of 55.5% (Qwen3-1.7B), 82.8% (Qwen3-4B), and 87.2% (Qwen3-8B). This represented improvements over GRPO by 8.9, 12.2, and 14.0 points, respectively.
- **WebShop:** OCSD reached success rates of 54.4%, 73.7%, and 78.1% for the respective Qwen3 scales.
- **Search-QA:** OCSD demonstrated average Exact Match (EM) scores of 43.1%, 47.5%, and 49.1%.
OCSD also showed strong performance across ALFWorld task categories, including basic and multi-step object manipulation tasks (e.g., Pick and Pick2).

#### 4.2. Inconsistent Gains of Direct Privileged Replay Support

The research highlighted that direct use of privileged replay support often yields inconsistent improvements. Standalone OPSD performed substantially worse than GRPO across models and environments, particularly showing poor and unstable performance on Search-QA. Even directly combining OPSD with GRPO (GRPO+OPSD) did not guarantee stable gains, sometimes underperforming the original GRPO, such as on ALFWorld with Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B. This finding underscores the importance of the calibration mechanism introduced by OCSD.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Distribution Shift

OCSD achieved the best out-of-domain (OOD) results among compared methods on Search-QA across all model scales. This suggests that the benefits of OCSD are not limited to the training distributions and extend to unseen retrieval and question-answering datasets, indicating improved generalization.

#### 4.4. Diagnostic Analyses

- **Word-Level Preference:** An analysis of high-NLL interaction steps on ALFWorld revealed that the upper tails of Full and Observation-Ablated support signals contained substantially overlapping words. In contrast, the observation residual (difference between Full and Ablated) highlighted a distinct set of words. This indicated that Full-view support conflated information from future observations with changes from the replay scaffold, whereas the residual was more sensitive to the actual future observation.
- **Functional Role Variation:** Words related to reasoning and environment entities/states showed a higher relative contribution from the observation residual compared to action and format words. This indicated that the difference between the two replay views (Full vs. Ablated) varied meaningfully across functional roles in the generated output.
- **Consistency with Local Environment Feedback:** The step-averaged observation residual achieved a higher AUROC (0.707) in distinguishing valid from invalid actions compared to Full support (0.654) and Observation-Ablated support (0.464). This demonstrated that the calibrated residual aligned more closely with local environment feedback. This improvement was stable under standardization and stratified matching.

#### 4.5. Ablation Analysis (using Qwen3-4B on ALFWorld and WebShop)

- **"w/o Ablated Teacher":** This variant, directly using the Full Teacher's signal without calibration, outperformed GRPO but was inferior to the complete OCSD. This confirmed that future observations provide useful supervision but also validated the presence of the confounding issue that OCSD addresses.
- **"w/ Random Selection":** Replacing NLL-guided step selection with random selection still yielded better performance than GRPO, but less than full OCSD. This indicated that residual calibration is beneficial, but targeting high-uncertainty steps improves efficiency.
- **"w/o Step Selection":** Applying residual calibration to all interaction steps (i.e., without step selection) resulted in performance similar to or below GRPO. This suggested that indiscriminate calibration can introduce low-value or noisy signals, emphasizing the importance of focused calibration.
- **"w/o Sign Alignment":** Removing the advantage-dependent sign term in the modulation (Eq. 11) reduced performance to around the GRPO level. This confirmed that the residual's interpretation and application should be consistent with the direction of the trajectory-level advantage.

#### 4.6. Observation Swapping Analysis

When the realized future observation in the Full view was replaced with a randomly sampled "swapped" observation from another trajectory, the AUROC for distinguishing valid from invalid steps significantly decreased (e.g., from 0.803 to 0.542 for Qwen3-1.7B). This demonstrated that the discriminative ability of the residual is primarily dependent on the correct correspondence between the future environment feedback and the current action, not merely the presence of arbitrary additional observation text.

#### 4.7. Computational Budget

OCSD introduced minimal overhead to the training process. Dual-view replay scoring and residual modulation required only 7.52 and 0.12 seconds per iteration, respectively, amounting to a total overhead of 1.40% relative to the shared GRPO pipeline. This indicates that OCSD maintains training efficiency comparable to standard GRPO.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of agentic reinforcement learning by addressing a fundamental limitation in the application of on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) for Large Language Model (LLM) agents. The identified "attribution confound" in privileged replay scoring, where the influence of future observations is mixed with that of the replay scaffold, represents a significant hurdle to obtaining precise token-level supervision.

The proposed **Observation-Calibrated Self-Distillation (OCSD)** method offers a robust solution to this problem. By systematically contrasting structurally matched Full and Observation-Ablated replay views, OCSD successfully disentangles the specific contribution of future observations, leading to a more accurate and reliable token-level signal.

The significance and potential impact of this work are several-fold:

1. **Improved Supervision Quality for LLM Agents:** OCSD provides a cleaner, more attributable token-level supervision signal. This can lead to more effective learning for LLM agents, especially in long-horizon tasks where sparse, trajectory-level rewards are insufficient. The diagnostic analyses confirm that the calibrated residual aligns better with local environment feedback, suggesting that agents learn more relevant correlations between their actions and subsequent observations.

2. **Enhanced Training Stability and Performance:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop, Search-QA) and various model scales (Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, 8B) indicate that OCSD contributes to more stable and higher-performing agent training. The observed instability and sometimes detrimental effects of direct OPSD baselines highlight the practical importance of OCSD's calibration mechanism.

3. **Better Generalization Capabilities:** OCSD's superior performance in out-of-domain evaluation on Search-QA suggests that the more precise supervision fosters better generalization. Agents trained with OCSD appear to learn more robust and transferable policies, which is crucial for real-world applications where agents encounter varied or unseen scenarios.

4. **Foundation for Future Research in Privileged Information:** By highlighting and resolving a specific confounding issue, OCSD sets a precedent for more rigorous design and application of privileged information in self-distillation. It opens avenues for investigating other potential confounds in various forms of privileged guidance (e.g., reasoning traces, expert demonstrations) and developing similar calibration techniques.

5. **Computational Efficiency:** The minimal computational overhead introduced by OCSD ensures that these performance and reliability gains do not come at a significant cost, making it a practical solution for large-scale LLM agent training.

In conclusion, OCSD advances the state-of-the-art in training LLM agents by providing a method to derive more precise and effective token-level supervision from privileged future observations. This enhances agent learning, stability, and generalization, paving the way for more capable and robust LLM agents in complex interactive environments.
