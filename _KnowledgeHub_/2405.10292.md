# Fine-Tuning Large Vision-Language Models as Decision-Making Agents via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework is introduced for directly fine-tuning Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL) to enhance their interactive decision-making capabilities. The method, integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning, enables a 7B VLM to outperform larger commercial models like GPT-4V and Gemini on multi-step visual-language tasks.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) trained via supervised instruction-tuning struggle with dynamic, multi-step, goal-directed tasks requiring continuous environmental interaction.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) end-to-end to fine-tune VLMs for complex multimodal decision-making tasks has been largely unexplored.
- Understanding how Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning can be leveraged and integrated into the active learning process of VLMs via RL.

## Method

- The framework directly fine-tunes the entire VLM (vision encoder, LLM backbone) using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)== for goal-directed tasks.
- VLMs are prompted to generate structured outputs including ==Chain-of-Thought reasoning== and a text-based action, which is then parsed for environmental execution.
- A novel ==scaling factor "λ"== is introduced to correctly estimate action probabilities by balancing the influence of CoT and action log-likelihoods during ==policy gradient updates==.

## Results

- The RL fine-tuned 7B LLaVA model achieved **45.5%** average success on gym_cards and **21.7%** on ALFWorld, surpassing GPT-4V (**25.4%**, **19.4%**) and Gemini (**28.6%**, **13.5%**) respectively.
- Ablation studies showed that removing Chain-of-Thought reasoning significantly reduced performance, for instance, a **21.2%** drop on gym_cards tasks.
- Empirical tuning of the "λ" scaling factor revealed that moderate values (**0.3**–**0.5**) were crucial for optimal performance during RL fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct reinforcement learning fine-tuning is an effective paradigm for enhancing the decision-making abilities of VLMs beyond static instruction following.
> - Chain-of-Thought reasoning is a crucial component for efficient exploration and improved performance when VLMs are actively learning through RL, rather than just a prompting technique.
> - Careful weighting of Chain-of-Thought tokens versus action tokens (via the "λ" factor) is essential for stable and effective RL training of VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at a critical intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within artificial intelligence: Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Embodied AI.

**Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Recent years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of large language models (LLMs), which have then been extended to multimodal inputs, giving rise to VLMs like GPT-4V, Gemini, and LLaVA. These models excel at understanding and generating human-like responses based on both visual and textual information, often through "visual instruction tuning." However, the prevailing paradigm for training these VLMs largely relies on supervised learning from vast, pre-collected datasets. While effective for static instruction following (e.g., visual question answering, image captioning), this approach falls short when it comes to dynamic, multi-step, goal-directed tasks that require continuous interaction with an environment. The paper identifies this limitation, stating that instruction tuning "may not be able to efficiently learn optimal decision-making agents in multi-step goal-directed tasks from interactive environments."

**Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL is the established paradigm for training agents to make sequential decisions in interactive environments to maximize a reward signal. While RL has achieved superhuman performance in domains like games (Go, Dota 2, Atari), its application to large foundation models, particularly VLMs, is a more recent frontier. Previous work has largely focused on applying RL to LLMs for text-based tasks (e.g., dialogue generation, summarization, or text-based games). Some RL from Human Feedback (RLHF) techniques have been used to align LLMs and VLMs with human preferences, but this is distinct from learning optimal policies for complex multi-step tasks from direct environmental interaction. This paper breaks new ground by performing *end-to-end VLM fine-tuning with RL* for goal-directed, multi-step tasks that inherently require both vision and language understanding. It explicitly differentiates itself from prior works that either use RL for text-only LLMs or integrate frozen VLMs/LLMs into a training pipeline without directly fine-tuning the entire VLM.

**Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** CoT prompting has emerged as a powerful technique to elicit complex reasoning abilities in LLMs and VLMs by encouraging them to generate intermediate reasoning steps before arriving at a final answer. This work integrates CoT not merely as a prompting technique for frozen models, but as an *integral part of the RL training process*. The paper investigates how CoT reasoning affects the learning dynamics and overall performance when the VLM is actively fine-tuned with RL, identifying it as a crucial component for efficient exploration and performance improvement. This extends the understanding of CoT's role from static reasoning to dynamic, interactive learning.

**Embodied AI:** The paper evaluates its framework in embodied AI environments (ALFWorld), which bridge the gap between abstract AI reasoning and physical interaction. This is a crucial step towards developing truly intelligent agents that can operate in the real world. By demonstrating improved performance in such environments, the work contributes to the grand challenge of building general-purpose embodied agents.

In summary, this research addresses a critical gap in the field by proposing a principled way to train VLMs for interactive decision-making, moving beyond static instruction following. It synergizes the representational power of VLMs with the learning capabilities of RL, while uncovering the vital role of Chain-of-Thought reasoning in this integrated paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop an algorithmic framework for directly fine-tuning Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) using reinforcement learning (RL) to enable them to act as effective decision-making agents in multi-step, goal-directed tasks that require both visual recognition and language understanding.**

The core motivations behind this objective stem from the limitations of existing VLM training paradigms:

- **Bridging the Gap between Instruction Following and Decision-Making:** While large VLMs fine-tuned on instruction-following datasets have shown remarkable capabilities in language reasoning across various scenarios, this supervised learning paradigm is inherently passive. It trains models on pre-collected data without direct interaction with an environment. This makes it suboptimal for learning *optimal decision-making policies* in multi-step, interactive environments where agents need to adapt, explore, and learn from trial and error. The paper highlights that if pre-collected datasets lack sufficient diversity for decision-making scenarios, instruction tuning alone will fail to impart robust decision-making skills.
- **Leveraging RL for Interactive Learning:** Reinforcement learning, by its nature, is designed for training agents in multi-step interactive environments. It allows agents to learn optimal behaviors by directly interacting with the environment and receiving feedback (rewards). The authors recognize RL as the natural paradigm to "unleash the learning capabilities of VLM agents" in such settings.
- **Extending RL to Multimodal Models:** While RL has been widely applied to purely text-based LLMs, its end-to-end application to VLMs – models that process both visual and textual inputs and produce textual actions – for goal-directed multi-step tasks has been largely unexplored. This paper aims to fill this gap.
- **Exploiting Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning in Interactive Settings:** CoT prompting has proven effective for improving reasoning in static settings. The authors hypothesize that CoT, by encouraging intermediate reasoning steps, could also facilitate more efficient exploration and better decision-making in dynamic RL environments. A key motivation is to understand and quantify the impact of CoT reasoning when VLMs are actively learning through RL.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance:** A practical motivation is to develop VLM agents that can surpass the performance of powerful commercial models like GPT-4V or Gemini in interactive decision-making tasks, even with smaller model sizes (e.g., a 7B model).

In essence, the research seeks to move VLMs beyond merely "understanding" and "generating" to "acting intelligently" in complex, interactive, and visually-rich environments by integrating the power of RL and carefully designed prompts that harness the models' intrinsic reasoning abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithmic framework directly fine-tunes Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Reinforcement Learning (RL) for multi-step, goal-directed decision-making tasks. The core idea is to treat the VLM as a policy network that takes visual and language inputs and outputs text-based actions, while integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. The methodology addresses key challenges of applying RL to open-ended text generation models.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview (Figure 1 & 2)

The framework operates in a loop:
1. **State Observation:** At each time step, the VLM receives the current visual observation (RGB image) and a pre-designed prompt as input.
2. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Generation:** The VLM is prompted to generate an utterance containing a chain-of-thought reasoning (e.g., "thoughts" on the state and decision process) followed by a text-based action.
3. **Action Parsing:** The open-ended text output is parsed into an executable action for the environment.
4. **Environment Interaction:** The parsed action is sent to the environment, which updates the state and provides a task reward.
5. **RL Fine-tuning:** The task reward is then used to fine-tune the *entire* VLM (vision encoder, LLM backbone, MLP projector) using an RL algorithm, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

#### 3.2. Prompt Design for Domain-Specific Outputs (Section 4.1)

- A crucial element is the *task-specific prompt* (`v_in_t`). This prompt is dynamically constructed at each time step and includes:
 - A description of the task.
 - The legal action space for the current observation.
 - The *desired output format*, explicitly requiring a JSON structure with a "thoughts" field (for CoT) and an "action" field (for the executable command).
- This structured prompting guides the VLM's output, making it amenable to parsing and leveraging its generative capabilities for reasoning.

#### 3.3. Post-Processing Open-Ended Text for Legal Actions (Section 4.2)

- The VLM's output (`v_out_t`) is open-ended text. A post-processing function `f` is designed to extract the executable action.
- This function specifically searches for the ` "action": "a_t" ` keyword within the VLM's output.
- If a valid action is found, it's mapped to a symbolic or text-based action required by the environment.
- **Handling Invalid Outputs:** If the VLM fails to generate a legal action in the specified format, the framework defaults to a random exploration strategy by selecting a legal action uniformly at random from the environment's action space. This ensures continuous interaction and prevents training halts due to malformed outputs.

#### 3.4. Estimating Action Probabilities of VLM Policies (Section 4.3)

- A key challenge in applying policy gradient RL to text generation is correctly estimating the probability of a *parsed action* (`a_t`) from the sequence of generated tokens (`v_out_t`).
- A naive approach would sum the log-likelihoods of *all* tokens in `v_out_t`. However, since the CoT reasoning part (`v_tht_t`) is typically much longer than the action part (`v_act_t`), the naive log-likelihood would be dominated by the CoT, potentially diluting the signal from the actual action.
- To address this, the authors introduce a **scaling factor `λ ∈`**:
 `log π_θ(a_t | o_t, v_in_t) ← λ log π_θ(v_tht_t | o_t, v_in_t) + log π_θ(v_act_t | o_t, v_in_t, v_tht_t)`
- This factor scales down the influence of the CoT tokens on the action probability estimate, allowing the learning signal to be more focused on the relevant action token sequence. Empirical tuning of `λ` is found to be crucial, with moderate values (0.2-0.5) yielding the best performance.

#### 3.5. RL Algorithm and Training (Section 4.4, Algorithm 1, Appendix C.1)

- **Algorithm:** The framework uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) as the RL algorithm, a widely adopted and robust policy gradient method.
- **VLM Fine-tuning:** The *entire* VLM (CLIP vision encoder, Mistral LLM backbone, and MLP projector) is fine-tuned. Due to computational constraints, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used to efficiently fine-tune the model parameters.
- **Pre-training / Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Before RL training, the LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7b model is first supervised fine-tuned on instruction-following data (manually constructed for `gym_cards`, GPT-4V collected for `ALFWorld`). This provides a reasonable initial instruction-following capability, similar to the initial SFT stage in RLHF.
- **Value Head:** A 3-layer MLP is added as a value head on top of the VLM's output hidden states to estimate the value function, enabling the use of Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
- **Computational Setup:** Experiments are conducted on multi-GPU setups (8 A100s) using DeepSpeed for efficiency.

#### 3.6. Evaluation Tasks (Section 5)

To thoroughly evaluate the framework, two distinct domains are used:
- **`gym_cards` (Original Domain):** Four tasks designed to test fine-grained visual recognition and arithmetic/language reasoning:
 - **NumberLine:** Deterministic, move a number to a target.
 - **EZPoints:** Deterministic, form an equation to reach 12 from two cards.
 - **Points24:** Deterministic, form an equation to reach 24 from four cards (harder).
 - **Blackjack:** Stochastic, classic card game requiring strategic decision-making.
- **`ALFWorld` (Embodied AI Domain):** Combines a text-based interactive environment with a visual instruction-following dataset, requiring visual semantic understanding and navigation (e.g., "Pick & Place," "Heat & Place"). This environment has a state-dependent admissible action space, adding complexity.

This comprehensive methodology allows for end-to-end training of VLMs in interactive settings, systematically addressing the challenges of open-ended text outputs and the integration of reasoning processes like CoT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of their RL fine-tuning framework for VLMs, especially highlighting the critical role of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

#### 4.1. Enhancing VLM Decision-Making Capabilities (Section 6.1)

- **Consistent Improvement:** The proposed method consistently improves the decision-making capabilities of the base LLaVA-v1.6-Mistral-7b model (referred to as "LLaVA-sft") across all tested tasks.
 - On `gym_cards` arithmetic tasks, performance improved significantly from 18.4% (LLaVA-sft) to 45.5% (Ours), a 27.1% increase.
 - On `ALFWorld` visual semantic reasoning, performance improved from 17.7% (LLaVA-sft) to 21.7% (Ours), a 4.0% increase.
- **Outperforming Commercial Models:** A key finding is that the RL fine-tuned 7B LLaVA model ("Ours") manages to surpass the performance of larger, commercial models like GPT-4V and Gemini on most tasks.
 - On `gym_cards`, "Ours" achieved an average success rate of 45.5%, significantly outperforming GPT-4V (25.4%) and Gemini (28.6%).
 - On `ALFWorld`, "Ours" achieved 21.7%, marginally outperforming GPT-4V (19.4%) and Gemini (13.5%). This is particularly impressive given the scale difference.
- **Superiority over CNN-based RL:** The VLM-based RL approach ("Ours") also outperformed a CNN-based RL baseline ("CNN+RL"), which uses the same CLIP vision encoder but lacks the sophisticated language understanding and generation capabilities of the full VLM. This indicates that the VLM's inherent multimodal understanding is crucial, beyond just visual feature extraction.

#### 4.2. Understanding the Role of CoT Reasoning (Section 6.2)

- **Crucial for Performance:** Ablation studies reveal that removing the CoT reasoning (`w/o CoT`) from the training process leads to a significant decrease in performance across almost all tasks.
 - For `gym_cards`, removing CoT resulted in a 21.2% drop in average success rate (from 45.5% to 24.3%).
 - For `ALFWorld`, the drop was 5.4% (from 21.7% to 16.3%).
- **Enabling Learning for Deterministic Tasks:** CoT reasoning was particularly crucial for deterministic arithmetic tasks like `NumberLine` and `EZPoints`, where the method "fails to improve these two tasks without the CoT reasoning." This suggests CoT assists in guiding exploration and learning more effectively in tasks requiring precise logical steps.
- **Impact of Scaling Factor (λ):** The scaling factor `λ` (which balances the influence of CoT log-likelihood vs. action log-likelihood) is important.
 - Experiments show that a **moderate `λ` (between 0.3 and 0.5)** enables effective training.
 - Setting `λ` too large (≥ 0.7) or too small (≤ 0.1) degrades overall performance. A large `λ` over-weights the CoT, potentially making the action signal less salient, while a small `λ` reduces the beneficial effect of CoT reasoning on RL training.

#### 4.3. Limitations and Challenges

- **Points24 Difficulty:** Even with RL fine-tuning, the `Points24` task remains very challenging, with all methods achieving very low success rates. This is attributed to the task requiring highly accurate visual perception of four cards and generating much longer, complex equations, often leading to failures due to inaccurate visual perception or flawed language reasoning (as shown in Appendix C.5 failure examples).
- **Computational Cost:** Training VLMs with RL is computationally expensive, limiting the number of environment steps (15k for `gym_cards`, 5k for `ALFWorld`). This is a general challenge for VLM/LLM research.
- **Action Space Size:** The authors note that the performance gain is somewhat limited by the size of the action space. `ALFWorld` has a much larger and more complex action space than `gym_cards`, which might explain the smaller relative performance gains observed.

Overall, the findings strongly support the hypothesis that direct RL fine-tuning significantly enhances the decision-making capabilities of VLMs, and critically, that integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning is not just a prompting trick but a fundamental component for effective learning in these interactive settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Fine-Tuning Large Vision-Language Models as Decision-Making Agents via Reinforcement Learning" holds significant implications for the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the development of more capable and general-purpose intelligent agents.

#### 5.1. Advancing General-Purpose AI Agents

- **Beyond Instruction Following:** The work marks a crucial step in moving VLMs beyond static instruction-following and classification tasks towards dynamic, interactive decision-making. By showing that VLMs can be directly fine-tuned with RL, it opens a pathway for these powerful multimodal models to learn complex, sequential behaviors in diverse environments. This is a fundamental capability for truly general-purpose AI.
- **Competitive Performance with Smaller Models:** The demonstration that a 7B LLaVA model, after RL fine-tuning, can outperform commercial black-box models like GPT-4V and Gemini on interactive tasks is highly significant. This suggests that the proposed method offers a more efficient path to developing high-performing decision-making agents, potentially reducing the reliance on massive, proprietary models and fostering more accessible research.

#### 5.2. Deepening the Understanding of Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

- **CoT as a Learning Mechanism:** The paper provides strong empirical evidence that CoT reasoning is not just a pre-computation or a way to elicit better answers from frozen models, but a *crucial component for enabling and enhancing RL training*. By showing that removing CoT significantly degrades performance, especially in deterministic tasks, the research suggests that the explicit intermediate reasoning steps generated by CoT can serve as a scaffold or a more informed exploration strategy for the RL agent. This shifts CoT's perceived role from merely an output format to an integral part of the learning process itself.
- **Guidance for Future Prompt Engineering:** The discovery of the optimal scaling factor `λ` for CoT log-likelihood provides practical guidance for future research in training large generative models with RL. It highlights the delicate balance required when integrating structured outputs like CoT into gradient-based learning, ensuring that the learning signal remains relevant to the desired actions.

#### 5.3. Implications for Embodied AI and Robotics

- **More Capable Embodied Agents:** The success in `ALFWorld` demonstrates the framework's potential for embodied AI. VLMs fine-tuned with RL could enable agents to perceive complex visual scenes, understand natural language instructions, plan multi-step actions, and execute them in simulated (and eventually real-world) environments more effectively. This could accelerate progress in areas like robotic manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Learning from Interaction:** The ability to learn directly from environmental rewards, rather than relying solely on pre-annotated datasets, is vital for agents operating in open-ended or novel environments where comprehensive datasets are impractical to collect.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The paper itself points to several exciting future directions:
- **Exploring Different Prompting Techniques:** While highlighting CoT's importance, the authors acknowledge that more sophisticated prompting strategies could be explored within the RL framework.
- **Scaling to Larger Action Spaces and More Complex Tasks:** The observed limitations on `Points24` and the modest gains in `ALFWorld` (compared to `gym_cards`) suggest a need for further research into scaling the method to even larger and more difficult tasks, possibly involving more diverse action spaces or longer planning horizons.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Continued work on making RL fine-tuning of VLMs more computationally efficient will be critical for broader adoption and larger-scale experimentation.

In conclusion, this paper presents a foundational contribution to the field by demonstrating a robust and effective method for training VLMs as interactive decision-making agents using RL. Its findings on the indispensable role of Chain-of-Thought reasoning are particularly insightful, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptive, and general-purpose AI systems.
