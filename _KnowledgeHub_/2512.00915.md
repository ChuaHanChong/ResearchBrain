# Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning in Symmetry-Breaking Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yonsei University and UC Berkeley developed Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning (PE-RL) to enable robust and sample-efficient learning in environments where symmetries are only partially observed. Their methods, PE-DQN and PE-SAC, achieved improved sample efficiency and maintained stability, outperforming strictly equivariant and approximate equivariant baselines across diverse discrete and continuous control tasks, especially in settings with significant symmetry-breaking.

## Problem

- Existing equivariant reinforcement learning (RL) methods assume perfectly group-invariant Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which is often violated in real-world environments.
- Local symmetry-breaking, caused by factors like complex dynamics or actuation limits, can lead to errors propagating globally through Bellman backups, resulting in suboptimal policies or training instability.
- Prior approximate equivariance techniques relax symmetry constraints globally, potentially sacrificing sample efficiency benefits or becoming unstable with extensive symmetry violations.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Partially Group-Invariant== MDP (**PI-MDP**) theoretical framework, which uses a measurable gating function to selectively apply group-invariant or standard Bellman backups.
- Developed practical PE-RL algorithms (PE-DQN for discrete, PE-SAC for continuous control) that learn a binary gating function to detect symmetry-breaking regions by monitoring disagreement between an equivariant and an unconstrained one-step predictor.
- Utilized gated value mixtures for critics and gated policies for actors, allowing the system to adaptively switch between equivariant and non-equivariant learning components based on the detected symmetry status.

## Results

- PE-DQN consistently outperformed all baselines in Grid-World tasks with increasing symmetry-breaking (obstacles, reward-level violations, and complex dynamics), demonstrating robustness and higher returns.
- PE-SAC showed faster learning and superior asymptotic performance in MuJoCo locomotion tasks (e.g., Ant) and maintained stability in challenging robotic manipulation tasks (UR5e reach) where other equivariant methods failed.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using hard (binary) gating for the critic and separate network trunks for critics, policies, and predictors provided the most stable training and best performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local violations of group symmetry in MDPs induce global value errors that propagate through repeated Bellman backups.
> - The PI-MDP framework provides a theoretical mechanism to mitigate error propagation by interpolating between the true MDP and its group-invariant approximation.
> - Learning to dynamically gate between equivariant and non-equivariant representations, based on local predictions of symmetry-breaking, is crucial for robust and efficient learning in partially symmetric environments.
