# Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning in Symmetry-Breaking Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yonsei University and UC Berkeley developed Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning (PE-RL) to enable robust and sample-efficient learning in environments where symmetries are only partially observed. Their methods, PE-DQN and PE-SAC, achieved improved sample efficiency and maintained stability, outperforming strictly equivariant and approximate equivariant baselines across diverse discrete and continuous control tasks, especially in settings with significant symmetry-breaking.

## Problem

- Existing equivariant reinforcement learning (RL) methods assume perfectly group-invariant Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which is often violated in real-world environments.
- Local symmetry-breaking, caused by factors like complex dynamics or actuation limits, can lead to errors propagating globally through Bellman backups, resulting in suboptimal policies or training instability.
- Prior approximate equivariance techniques relax symmetry constraints globally, potentially sacrificing sample efficiency benefits or becoming unstable with extensive symmetry violations.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Partially Group-Invariant== MDP (**PI-MDP**) theoretical framework, which uses a measurable gating function to selectively apply group-invariant or standard Bellman backups.
- Developed practical PE-RL algorithms (PE-DQN for discrete, PE-SAC for continuous control) that learn a binary gating function to detect symmetry-breaking regions by monitoring disagreement between an equivariant and an unconstrained one-step predictor.
- Utilized gated value mixtures for critics and gated policies for actors, allowing the system to adaptively switch between equivariant and non-equivariant learning components based on the detected symmetry status.

## Results

- PE-DQN consistently outperformed all baselines in Grid-World tasks with increasing symmetry-breaking (obstacles, reward-level violations, and complex dynamics), demonstrating robustness and higher returns.
- PE-SAC showed faster learning and superior asymptotic performance in MuJoCo locomotion tasks (e.g., Ant) and maintained stability in challenging robotic manipulation tasks (UR5e reach) where other equivariant methods failed.
- Ablation studies confirmed that using hard (binary) gating for the critic and separate network trunks for critics, policies, and predictors provided the most stable training and best performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Local violations of group symmetry in MDPs induce global value errors that propagate through repeated Bellman backups.
> - The PI-MDP framework provides a theoretical mechanism to mitigate error propagation by interpolating between the true MDP and its group-invariant approximation.
> - Learning to dynamically gate between equivariant and non-equivariant representations, based on local predictions of symmetry-breaking, is crucial for robust and efficient learning in partially symmetric environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The application of group symmetries as an inductive bias has emerged as a strategy in machine learning to enhance generalization and data efficiency. In reinforcement learning (RL) and continuous control, leveraging equivariance has shown promise in improving sample efficiency, particularly in scenarios where data collection or exploration can be costly. The majority of existing equivariant RL methods operate under the assumption of a group-invariant Markov Decision Process (MDP), which implies that the reward and transition functions remain unchanged under specific group transformations. This invariance is then exploited to induce symmetry in optimal policies or value functions.

However, real-world environments frequently exhibit deviations from these ideal symmetry assumptions. Factors such as complex dynamics, actuation limits, or specific reward designs can locally break symmetries, leading to a "partially symmetric" environment. When conventional equivariant RL methods, grounded in strict group-invariant MDPs, are applied to such environments, even localized symmetry violations can introduce errors into Bellman backups. These errors are then prone to propagating across the entire state–action space, resulting in global inaccuracies in value estimation, suboptimal policies, or potential training instability.

Prior research on handling approximate equivariance has typically involved relaxing equivariance globally, often by modifying network architectures to tolerate violations. While these approaches can offer some robustness, they may compromise the sample efficiency benefits associated with strict equivariance and can become unstable when symmetry-breaking is extensive, as the relaxation is applied indiscriminately.

This work addresses the limitations of existing methods by proposing a framework that specifically manages local symmetry-breaking without globally relaxing equivariance. It aims to selectively apply symmetry-based inductive biases only where they are valid, thereby retaining the benefits of equivariance in symmetric regions while robustly adapting to areas where symmetry is violated. This approach distinguishes itself by focusing on the local mitigation of equivariance errors during the Bellman backup process, rather than global architectural modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while group symmetries offer a powerful inductive bias for improving generalization and sample efficiency in RL, real-world environments rarely conform to perfectly group-invariant MDPs. Instead, various practical constraints—such as complex dynamics, physical actuation limits, or specific reward shaping—often introduce local violations of these symmetries.

The central problem identified is that when existing equivariant RL methods, which rely on the assumption of group-invariant MDPs, encounter such local symmetry-breaking, the errors introduced during one-step Bellman backups do not remain localized. Instead, these errors propagate across the entire state–action space, leading to global value estimation inaccuracies. This propagation can result in the learning of suboptimal policies or even training failures, undermining the advantages that equivariance is intended to provide. Prior attempts to address this, primarily through approximate equivariance techniques, tend to relax symmetry constraints globally. While offering some degree of robustness, these methods can lose the sample efficiency benefits of strict equivariance and may struggle with extensive symmetry violations because they do not selectively apply the inductive bias.

To overcome these challenges, the research sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Analyze Error Propagation:** To formally analyze and clarify how local violations of group symmetry induce global value errors within the context of Bellman backups. This involves quantifying the pointwise discrepancies between true and group-invariant MDPs and demonstrating how these local errors are amplified through repeated backups.
2. **Introduce Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP):** To propose a novel theoretical framework, the Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP), which provides a mechanism to selectively apply group-invariant or standard Bellman backups. This framework is designed to function based on where symmetry properties hold or are broken, thereby mitigating error propagation.
3. **Develop Practical RL Algorithms:** To build upon the PI-MDP framework and develop practical reinforcement learning algorithms suitable for both discrete and continuous control tasks. These algorithms, named Partially Equivariant (PE)-DQN for discrete control and PE-SAC for continuous control, are designed to leverage the benefits of equivariance in symmetric regions while maintaining robustness in symmetry-breaking regions.
4. **Demonstrate Empirical Efficacy:** To empirically evaluate the proposed PE-RL algorithms across a range of benchmarks, including Grid-World, locomotion, and manipulation tasks. The goal is to demonstrate that these methods significantly outperform conventional RL, strictly equivariant methods, and existing approximate-equivariant baselines in terms of sample efficiency and robustness, particularly as the degree of symmetry-breaking increases.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical elegance of equivariant RL and the practical realities of environments with imperfect symmetries, offering a more robust and efficient approach to learning in complex, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured in two main parts: a theoretical framework establishing the Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP) and the development of practical Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning (PE-RL) algorithms.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework: Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP)

The framework begins by defining two MDPs: the true environment $M_N = (S, A, R_N, P_N, \gamma)$ and a canonical group-invariant approximation $M_E = (S, A, R_E, P_E, \gamma)$. $M_E$ is constructed by averaging the true rewards ($R_N$) and transition kernels ($P_N$) over the symmetry group $G$ using the Haar measure. This averaging process ensures that $R_E$ and $P_E$ inherently satisfy the group-invariance condition.

Pointwise discrepancies between $M_N$ and $M_E$ are then quantified:
- $\epsilon_R(s, a):= |R_N(s, a) - R_E(s, a)|$ for reward differences.
- $\epsilon_P(s, a):= \frac{1}{2} \int_S |P_N(s'|s, a) - P_E(s'|s, a)| ds'$ (total-variation distance) for transition kernel differences.
Symmetry is defined as "preserved" at $(s, a)$ if both $\epsilon_R(s, a) = 0$ and $\epsilon_P(s, a) = 0$; otherwise, it is "broken".

**Error Propagation Analysis:**
- **Lemma 1 (One-step Bellman error):** This lemma quantifies how local mismatches perturb a single Bellman backup. It states that for any bounded Q-function, the difference between Bellman backups under $M_N$ and $M_E$ is bounded by $\epsilon_R(s, a) + 2\gamma \|V_Q\|_\infty \epsilon_P(s, a)$. This bound, denoted $\delta(s, a)$, represents the pointwise one-step approximation error.
- **Proposition 1 (Value-function gap):** This proposition shows that this local error propagates globally. The $\infty$-norm difference between the optimal value functions of $M_N$ and $M_E$ is bounded by $\frac{1}{1-\gamma} \|\delta\|_\infty$. This indicates that even local symmetry-breaking, if not addressed, can lead to significant global deviations in value functions.

**Definition of PI-MDP:**
The core of the framework is the Partially Group-Invariant MDP ($M_H$). It introduces a measurable gating function $\lambda: S \times A \to$ that interpolates between $M_E$ and $M_N$:
- $R_H(s, a):= (1 - \lambda(s, a)) R_E(s, a) + \lambda(s, a) R_N(s, a)$
- $P_H(\cdot | s, a):= (1 - \lambda(s, a)) P_E(\cdot | s, a) + \lambda(s, a) P_N(\cdot | s, a)$
This construction ensures $M_H$ is a valid MDP.

**Properties of PI-MDP:**
- **Theorem 1 (Partially group-invariant optimality operator):** The Bellman optimality operator for $M_H$, denoted $T_H$, is a convex combination of $T_E$ and $T_N$: $(T_H Q)(s, a) = (1 - \lambda(s, a)) (T_E Q)(s, a) + \lambda(s, a) (T_N Q)(s, a)$. It is also a $\gamma$-contraction, guaranteeing a unique fixed point $Q_H^*$.
- **Corollary 1 (Proximity bound):** This corollary bounds the deviation of $Q_H^*$ from the true optimal value $Q_N^*$: $\|Q_H^* - Q_N^*\|_\infty \le \frac{1}{1-\gamma} \|(1 - \lambda) \delta\|_\infty$. This bound highlights that if $\lambda(s, a) = 1$ when symmetry is broken (i.e., $\delta(s, a) > 0$), or if symmetry is preserved ($\delta(s, a) = 0$) and $\lambda(s, a)$ is ideally $0$, then $Q_H^*$ closely approximates $Q_N^*$. The theoretical framework also extends to entropy-regularized (soft) MDPs.

#### 3.2. Practical Algorithms: Partially Equivariant Reinforcement Learning (PE-RL)

Building on the PI-MDP, PE-RL algorithms (PE-DQN for discrete, PE-SAC for continuous control) are designed to function within existing deep RL frameworks.

### 4. Learning the Gating Function $\lambda(s, a)$

- A binary gating function $\lambda_\omega(s, a) \in \{0, 1\}$ is learned to detect symmetry-breaking regions.
- This is achieved by monitoring the *disagreement* between two one-step predictors: an equivariant predictor $\hat{P}_E$ (constrained by group symmetries) and an unconstrained predictor $\hat{P}_N$.
- Both predictors are trained on observed transitions $(s, a, r, s')$ from the replay buffer to approximate the environment dynamics (and reward, if applicable).
- **Disagreement Metric $d(s, a)$:** For discrete state spaces, $d(s, a)$ is the total-variation distance between $\hat{P}_N(\cdot|s, a)$ and $\hat{P}_E(\cdot|s, a)$. For continuous spaces, it's the squared difference between their predicted state increments. If reward-level symmetry-breaking is considered, reward prediction differences are also included.
- **Pseudo-label Generation:** Running statistics (mean and standard deviation) of $d(s, a)$ are maintained. A threshold $\tau_t = \mu_t + \kappa \sigma_t$ is used (or a batchwise quantile for challenging dynamics) to classify samples. Samples with $d(s, a) > \tau_t$ are labeled as symmetry-breaking ($y(s, a) = 1$), otherwise $y(s, a) = 0$.
- **Training $\lambda_\omega$:** A binary cross-entropy loss is used to train $\lambda_\omega(s, a)$ based on these pseudo-labels. During RL updates, $\lambda_\omega$ is treated as fixed, and its gradients are stopped. Stochastic hard gating (Bernoulli sampling from $\lambda_\omega(s,a)$) is applied for stability, with an exponential moving average of gating probabilities used for target Q-updates. A warm-start period is used to stabilize predictors.

### 5. Gated Value Mixtures for Critics

- The critic $Q_\theta(s, a)$ is parameterized as a gated mixture: $Q_\theta(s, a) = (1 - \tilde{\lambda}(s, a)) Q_{E,\theta}(s, a) + \tilde{\lambda}(s, a) Q_{N,\theta}(s, a)$, where $\tilde{\lambda}(s, a)$ is the sampled binary gate.
- $Q_E$ is an equivariant critic, and $Q_N$ is an unconstrained critic. This allows the system to switch between the equivariant and non-equivariant value estimations based on the detected symmetry status.
- Standard DQN or SAC objectives are used for training $Q_\theta$, with the gated parametrization.

### 6. Gated Policy for Actors (PE-SAC)

- For continuous control (SAC), a state-only gating function $\lambda_\zeta(s)$ is used for the policy.
- The policy $\pi_\phi(\cdot | s)$ is defined as a product-of-experts (PoE) blend: $\pi_\phi(\cdot | s) \propto \pi_{E,\phi}(\cdot | s)^{1-\tilde{\lambda}_\zeta(s)} \pi_{N,\phi}(\cdot | s)^{\tilde{\lambda}_\zeta(s)}$. This form naturally arises from SAC's policy improvement steps when using a state-only gate.
- $\lambda_\zeta(s)$ is trained via expectile regression (with a high expectile level $\tau \to 1$) to approximate $\max_a \lambda_\omega(s, a)$. This ensures $\lambda_\zeta(s)$ conservatively activates if *any* action at state $s$ is flagged as symmetry-breaking by $\lambda_\omega$. A binary gate $\tilde{\lambda}_\zeta(s)$ is sampled for policy updates.
- The use of a state-only gate for the actor is motivated by the intractability of reparameterization gradients for action-dependent gating functions in continuous action spaces.

**Network Architecture:**
Separate network trunks are used for critics, policies, and one-step predictors for stability and modularity. Equivariant networks leverage architectures like MDP-Homomorphic Networks (for Grid-World) or EMLP layers (for continuous control). Non-equivariant components are standard MLPs.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated PE-DQN and PE-SAC across discrete and continuous control tasks against vanilla RL, strictly equivariant methods, and approximately equivariant baselines.

#### 7.1. Discrete Control (Grid-World)

- **Increasing Obstacles (Symmetry-Breaking):** In Grid-World environments with an increasing number of fixed obstacles (which break rotational symmetry), PE-DQN consistently outperformed all baselines.
 - Strictly equivariant DQN showed significant performance degradation as symmetry-breaking increased.
 - Approximate equivariant methods offered only minor improvements over vanilla DQN in both sample efficiency and final return.
 - PE-DQN demonstrated robustness by maintaining strong performance, suggesting its ability to adapt and route updates to the non-equivariant head in symmetry-broken regions, consistent with the theoretical prediction that value errors remain controlled when the gate correctly routes away from equivariance.
- **Reward-Level Symmetry-Breaking:** In variants where obstacles became passable but incurred negative rewards (breaking symmetry via reward function), PE-DQN achieved the highest returns. RPP-DQN (an approximate equivariant baseline) was the strongest competitor but still lagged behind PE-DQN in both sample efficiency and final performance.
- **Complex Dynamics (Stochastic Transitions):** With stochastic transitions and numerous obstacles, PE-DQN continued to exhibit the best returns and learning speed, indicating that its disagreement-based gating mechanism remained effective even under noisy and complex dynamics.

#### 7.2. Continuous Control (Locomotion & Manipulation)

- **Locomotion (MuJoCo):**
 - **Hopper:** PE-SAC learned faster than all baselines and achieved a competitive asymptotic return, though vanilla SAC sometimes reached a slightly higher final return.
 - **Ant:** PE-SAC clearly dominated in both sample efficiency and final performance, demonstrating robust benefits in this environment.
 - **Swimmer:** In this environment, where symmetry is nearly exact, strictly equivariant and approximate equivariant SAC achieved the highest final returns. PE-SAC converged quickly to a slightly lower but still competitive performance level, which is expected when exact symmetry can be fully exploited.
- **Manipulation:**
 - **Fetch Reach:** PE-SAC, exact equivariant SAC, and approximately equivariant SAC performed similarly in this relatively simpler task.
 - **UR5e Reach:** This task involved substantial symmetry-breaking due to realistic dynamics (e.g., free end-effector orientation, collisions, kinematic singularities). In this challenging setting, strictly equivariant and approximately equivariant SAC variants became unstable or collapsed, whereas PE-SAC remained stable and achieved the best overall returns by adaptively shifting towards the non-equivariant policy and value functions.

#### 7.3. Ablation Studies

- **Actor-Gating Schemes:** The main method's trainable state gate ($\lambda_\zeta(s)$) performed similarly to simpler sampled-max variants (MAX K4, MAX K8), suggesting that the latter can be reasonable alternatives when architectural simplicity is preferred.
- **Hard vs. Soft Gating:** Empirically, using a hard (binary) gate for the critic was found to be more stable and perform better than a soft mixture (where $\lambda_\omega(s,a) \in$ acts directly as a mixing weight) or a fixed gate. Soft gating sometimes failed to learn, supporting the choice of hard gating in the practical algorithms.
- **Shared vs. Separate Trunks:** Using entirely separate network trunks for the critics, policy, and one-step predictors (the default approach) provided the most stable training and best performance. Sharing trunks between the Q-network/policy or combining all sharing schemes generally harmed performance or led to training failures.
- **$\kappa$ Sensitivity:** The method demonstrated robustness to the exact value of the disagreement coefficient $\kappa$, which controls the threshold for labeling symmetry-breaking samples. A coarse estimate of whether symmetry violations are sparse or common was sufficient for robust performance.

In summary, the results consistently support the efficacy of the proposed partially equivariant framework. PE-DQN and PE-SAC demonstrate improved sample efficiency in symmetric regimes and enhanced robustness in symmetry-broken regimes across diverse discrete, continuous, and realistic robotic control tasks, by selectively mitigating local equivariance errors.

### 8. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for addressing a critical challenge in equivariant reinforcement learning: maintaining the benefits of symmetry exploitation in real-world environments where symmetries are often imperfectly realized. By introducing the Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP), the work provides a theoretical foundation that quantifies the impact of local symmetry-breaking on global value function errors and offers a principled way to mitigate this.

The development of practical algorithms, Partially Equivariant DQN (PE-DQN) and Partially Equivariant SAC (PE-SAC), represents a significant step towards enabling robust and sample-efficient RL in complex, partially symmetric environments. The experimental results consistently demonstrate that these algorithms outperform existing baselines—including vanilla RL, strictly equivariant methods, and other approximate equivariant approaches—across a variety of discrete and continuous control tasks, including locomotion and robotic manipulation. This superior performance is particularly evident in scenarios with substantial symmetry-breaking, where strictly equivariant methods tend to fail or become unstable.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The ability to selectively apply equivariance, rather than relaxing it globally, makes RL agents more robust to real-world complexities like physical constraints, unexpected obstacles, or reward perturbations that break ideal symmetries. This is crucial for deploying RL in practical applications.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency and Generalization:** By leveraging symmetry where it holds, the algorithms retain the benefits of improved sample efficiency and generalization that equivariance offers, which is particularly valuable in data-scarce domains or those requiring extensive exploration.
- **Theoretical Advancement:** The PI-MDP framework provides new theoretical insights into error propagation in equivariant RL and offers a blueprint for designing adaptive symmetry-aware learning systems. The formal bounds on value function gaps contribute to a deeper understanding of approximate symmetries.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated success across diverse domains (Grid-World, MuJoCo locomotion, Fetch and UR5e manipulation) suggests the framework's broad applicability to various control problems where symmetries might be present but imperfect.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The approach lays groundwork for further research in adaptive symmetry-aware learning. Future extensions, such as applying PE-RL to vision-based control, could significantly advance the practicality of reinforcement learning for real-world robotic systems, where visual observations often possess complex symmetries that are also prone to occlusions or sensor noise.

While the approach introduces increased computational overhead due to auxiliary predictors and gating networks, and may offer marginal benefits in environments with pervasive symmetry-breaking (where it effectively defaults to a non-equivariant network), these limitations are balanced by its effectiveness in scenarios where symmetry-breaking is localized. This positions PE-RL as a valuable contribution to developing intelligent agents that can effectively learn and operate in environments with a realistic blend of symmetric and asymmetric properties.
