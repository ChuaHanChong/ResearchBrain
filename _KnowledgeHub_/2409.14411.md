# Scaling Diffusion Policy in Transformer to 1 Billion Parameters for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ScaleDP introduces a novel Transformer architecture that enables Diffusion Policy models for visuomotor robot control to scale effectively up to 1 billion parameters. This approach overcomes previous architectural limitations, leading to superior performance and enhanced generalization across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Diffusion Policy models built with Transformer architectures (DP-T) failed to scale, with performance often degrading as model size increased.
- Unstable training dynamics, specifically large gradient magnitudes in the observation fusion module, prevented DP-T from leveraging increased parameters effectively.
- The robotics field lacked a scalable diffusion-based imitation learning framework that could benefit from scaling laws observed in other large AI models.

## Method

- ScaleDP introduces an ==Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN)== block to replace cross-attention-based observation fusion, stabilizing training dynamics by mitigating large gradients.
- The architecture employs non-causal attention within its Transformer blocks, allowing each action token to attend to the entire predicted action sequence for improved robustness and consistency.
- The overall ScaleDP architecture is systematically scaled from 10 million to 1 billion parameters by jointly increasing layer count, hidden dimensions, and attention heads.

## Results

- ScaleDP-Huge (1 billion parameters) achieved an average **21.6%** success rate improvement over DP-T on MetaWorld simulation tasks and demonstrated stronger data scaling capabilities.
- On real robot tasks, ScaleDP-H yielded substantial average improvements over DP-T: **36.25%** on single-arm and a notable **75%** on complex bimanual manipulation tasks.
- The model exhibited robust visual generalization to unseen conditions, including changes in object appearance, entirely different object types, varying lighting, and the presence of distractors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unstable training dynamics, particularly due to large gradients in the observation fusion module, were the root cause preventing Diffusion Policy Transformers from scaling effectively.
> - Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) is an effective mechanism for conditioning in diffusion models, promoting stable training and enabling successful architectural scaling.
> - Removing causal masking in the self-attention of action sequence prediction improves policy robustness by allowing the model a complete view of the predicted action chunk.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: diffusion models, transformer architectures, and robot imitation learning. It critically addresses a known limitation in the scalability of existing methods, aiming to unlock new capabilities in visuomotor robot control.

**Diffusion Models in Generative AI and Robotics:** Diffusion models have emerged as a leading paradigm in generative artificial intelligence, achieving state-of-the-art results across diverse data modalities such as images, audio, video, and 3D content. Their ability to generate high-fidelity and diverse samples by progressively reversing a noise process has made them incredibly powerful. Recently, the application of diffusion models has expanded into robotics, particularly for imitation learning, reinforcement learning, grasping, and motion planning. Diffusion Policy (DP), introduced by Chi et al. (2023), has become a prominent technique for learning end-to-end visuomotor robot control, capable of representing complex and multimodal action distributions inherent in human demonstrations.

**The Era of Scaling Laws and Large Models:** A defining trend across machine learning domains, exemplified by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and large vision models, is the "scaling law." This principle posits that increasing model size and training data typically leads to predictable improvements in performance, generalization, and even the emergence of novel capabilities. The field has observed models scaling from millions to billions and even trillions of parameters, driving significant breakthroughs. Robotics research has a strong desire to achieve similar scaling benefits, envisioning foundation models for robots that can tackle a wide array of tasks with minimal task-specific training.

**Transformer Architectures as the Backbone:** The Transformer architecture, introduced by Vaswani et al. (2017), is the backbone of almost all modern large models due to its efficiency in processing sequential data and its scalability. Diffusion Policy models often leverage Transformers to process observation and action sequences.

**Addressing the Scalability Gap in Diffusion Policy:** Despite the individual successes of diffusion models, Transformers, and scaling laws, their combined application in Diffusion Policy for robotics has faced a critical bottleneck: scalability. Previous studies and the authors' own preliminary investigations showed that Diffusion Policy in a Transformer architecture (DP-T) did not exhibit the expected scaling behavior. Instead of improving, performance often deteriorated with increasing model size (e.g., adding more layers or heads). This fundamental limitation prevented DP-T from fully leveraging larger datasets and computational resources, hindering its potential for generalization and sophisticated task learning.

This paper directly tackles this gap. By identifying and resolving the architectural issues that impede scaling, it seeks to bring the benefits of large models – such as enhanced generalization, improved data efficiency, and potentially emergent robotic behaviors – to the domain of diffusion-based visuomotor control. The work positions itself as a pioneering effort to extend the success of scaling laws to diffusion-based imitation learning, setting a new benchmark for model size in this specific subfield. By comparing against leading imitation learning methods like Octo, Beso, MDT, DP-Unet, and ACT, the authors firmly establish their work within the competitive landscape of state-of-the-art robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to **develop a Diffusion Policy model based on the Transformer architecture that can effectively scale up to a significantly larger number of parameters, thereby achieving superior performance, improved generalization capabilities, and better data efficiency for visuomotor robotic manipulation.**

This primary objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Scalability Problem in Diffusion Policy Transformers (DP-T):** The most immediate motivation stems from the observed failure of existing DP-T models to scale. The authors confirm previous findings and present their own empirical evidence showing that simply increasing the depth or number of attention heads in DP-T leads to a *decrease* in performance rather than an improvement. This counter-intuitive behavior is a major roadblock to developing more capable robot policies.

2. **Unlocking the Benefits of Scaling Laws in Robotics:** The success of scaling laws in other AI domains (e.g., natural language processing with LLMs, computer vision with large vision transformers) has demonstrated that larger models, when properly designed and trained, can exhibit emergent properties, enhanced generalization, and significantly higher performance. The authors are motivated to bring these transformative benefits to robotic control, specifically to diffusion-based policies which have shown great promise in handling the inherent multi-modality of robot actions.

3. **Identifying and Resolving the Root Cause of Scaling Issues:** Through detailed analysis, the authors pinpoint the fundamental challenge: **unstable training dynamics caused by large gradients in the observation fusion module** within the conventional DP-T architecture. The cross-attention mechanism used for conditioning was found to exacerbate this instability in deeper networks. Motivated by this diagnosis, the paper aims to propose specific architectural modifications to mitigate these gradient issues.

4. **Improving the Handling of Multimodal Action Distributions:** Diffusion Policy is inherently designed to model multimodal action distributions. The authors are motivated to enhance the network's ability to represent and leverage this multimodality more effectively, especially as the model scales. This is implicitly addressed by designing architectural components that facilitate stable learning of complex distributions.

5. **Enhancing Generalization to Unseen Visual Conditions:** A major goal in robotics is to develop policies that can generalize robustly to variations in object appearance, shape, lighting, and the presence of distractors. The authors are motivated to design a scalable architecture that inherently improves visual generalization, reducing the need for extensive data augmentation or task-specific fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to push Diffusion Policy models beyond their current capacity limitations, enabling them to tackle more complex, diverse, and realistic robotic manipulation tasks by effectively harnessing the power of scale.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **Scalable Diffusion Transformer Policy (ScaleDP)**, an architecture specifically designed to overcome the scaling limitations of Diffusion Policy in Transformer (DP-T) for visuomotor learning. The methodology revolves around identifying the architectural bottlenecks in DP-T and proposing targeted modifications.

**Problem Setup:**
The task is visuomotor imitation learning. The goal is to learn a conditional action distribution $p(A_t | o_t, x_t)$ from an expert dataset $D = \{\tau_0, \tau_1, \dots, \tau_n\}$. Each trajectory $\tau_i$ consists of paired raw visual observations ($o$) and proprioceptive information ($x$). Proprioceptive information includes the robot's 6D end-effector pose (position and rotation) and gripper width. The policy predicts a chunk of future actions $A_t = \{a_t, \dots, a_{t+C}\}$ for the next $C$ timesteps.

**Diffusion Policy (DP) Foundation:**
ScaleDP builds upon the Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) framework. DP models the conditional action distribution by learning a score function $\epsilon_\theta$ that predicts the noise added to an action sequence $A_t^k$ at diffusion step $k$. The training objective is an MSE loss: $\ell(\theta) = (\epsilon_k - \epsilon_\theta(o_t, x_t, A_t^k + \epsilon_k, k))^2$. During inference, actions are generated by iteratively denoising from Gaussian noise.

**Identification of Scaling Limitations in DP-T:**
The authors performed preliminary experiments on MetaWorld using a vanilla DP-T architecture. They observed that increasing the number of Transformer heads or layers did not lead to performance improvements; in fact, it often caused performance degradation (e.g., success rate dropping from 80.1% with 8 layers to 74.6% with 14 layers). Further analysis revealed that deeper DP-T models suffered from unstable training due to **large gradient magnitudes**, particularly in the observation fusion module, leading to unbalanced optimization paces across layers. This was a critical insight guiding their architectural modifications.

**Key Architectural Modifications in ScaleDP:**

1. **Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) Block for Observation Fusion:**
 - **Problem:** The conventional DP-T architecture uses a cross-attention mechanism to fuse conditional information (timestep $k$ and observation $o$) with action embeddings. The authors found this approach to generate large gradients, leading to unstable training in deeper networks.
 - **Solution:** ScaleDP replaces this cross-attention-based fusion with Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). Inspired by its success in image generation models, AdaLN directly regresses the dimension-wise scale ($\gamma$) and shift ($\beta$) parameters for layer normalization from the sum of the embedding vectors of timestep $k$ and observation $o$. The AdaLN operation is defined as: $AdaLN(x) = (\gamma(k, o) + 1) \cdot x + \beta(k, o)$.
 - **Benefit:** This method allows the model to adaptively change the distribution of noise action embeddings based on the conditions, promoting more stable training dynamics by normalizing parameter distribution and mitigating the large gradient issue. Figure 1 (right) empirically confirms that ScaleDP maintains lower gradient magnitudes even with increased layers.

2. **Non-causal Attention:**
 - **Problem:** Traditional Transformer decoders (like those used in original DP-T, following) often employ masked self-attention to ensure that each token only attends to preceding tokens (causal masking). For policy prediction, this means an action can only "see" past actions.
 - **Solution:** ScaleDP removes this causal mask in the self-attention layers within its Transformer blocks, allowing each action embedding to attend to *all* other action tokens in the sequence (both past and future).
 - **Benefit:** The authors argue that for visuomotor policies, where a chunk of future actions is predicted but often only the first action is executed, seeing the entire predicted trajectory (both past and "future" within the chunk) makes the model more robust to compounding errors during prediction. It allows for better consistency and a holistic understanding of the action sequence.

**Overall ScaleDP Architecture:**
- **Input Processing:** Multi-view images are processed by ResNet50 encoders (global, left wrist, right wrist) and concatenated. Proprioceptive information is also embedded.
- **ScaleDP Blocks:** The processed observation and timestep embeddings are fed into a sequence of $N$ ScaleDP blocks. Each block integrates the AdaLN mechanism for conditional fusion.
- **Scaling Configurations:** The authors define five distinct model sizes, ranging from 10 million (Tiny) to 1 billion (Huge) parameters, by jointly scaling the number of layers ($N$), hidden dimension size ($d$), and number of attention heads. This systematic scaling allows for a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed architecture's scalability.
- **Output:** After the final ScaleDP block, another adaptive layer norm and a linear layer decode the sequence of noise action tokens into the predicted noise, which is then used in the DDPM denoising process.

This carefully designed methodology aims to create a robust and scalable architecture for diffusion-based robot policies, directly addressing the limitations of previous approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted both in simulation and on real robots demonstrate that ScaleDP successfully scales Diffusion Policy models, leading to significant performance gains and improved generalization.

**A. Simulation Experiments (MetaWorld)**

- **Comparison with DP-T:** ScaleDP-Tiny (Ti), despite having a similar parameter count to the vanilla DP-T, consistently outperforms DP-T across all four difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard, very hard) on 50 MetaWorld tasks. This indicates that the architectural improvements themselves, even without increased scale, lead to more efficient model utilization.
- **Model Scaling:** As the ScaleDP model size increases from Tiny (10M) to Huge (1B) parameters, the success rate on MetaWorld tasks consistently improves. This is a crucial validation of ScaleDP's ability to scale effectively, demonstrating that performance enhances with increased computational capacity—a behavior absent in vanilla DP-T. The largest ScaleDP model (ScaleDP-H) achieves an average improvement of 21.6% over DP-T.
- **Data Scaling:** Larger ScaleDP models show a greater capacity to leverage additional training data. While smaller models plateau in performance after a certain number of demonstrations, larger models continue to improve significantly with more data. This highlights that ScaleDP's architecture enables models to extract more meaningful patterns and insights from increased data.
- **Learning Efficacy (Convergence Rate):** Larger ScaleDP models exhibit more effective convergence, achieving higher success rates and lower training losses during the training process, both in MetaWorld (Assembly task) and on real-world tasks. This suggests improved training dynamics and stability with increasing scale.

**B. Real Robot Experiments (Franka and UR5 Arms)**

- **Overall Performance:** ScaleDP consistently outperforms DP-T across all model sizes on 7 diverse real-world tasks (4 single-arm Franka tasks and 3 bimanual UR5 tasks). The largest ScaleDP-H model achieves substantial average improvements over DP-T: 36.25% on single-arm tasks and a remarkable 75% on bimanual tasks.
- **Single-Arm Tasks:** On Franka tasks like "Close Laptop," "Flip Mug," "Stack Cube," and "Place Tennis," ScaleDP-H achieves very high success rates (95%, 95%, 90%, 70% respectively), dramatically surpassing DP-T (80%, 70%, 50%, 5%). Notably, ScaleDP-L and -H outperform state-of-the-art imitation learning methods like ACT.
- **Bimanual Tasks:** For more complex bimanual tasks like "Put tennis ball into bag," "Sweep trash," and "Bimanual Stack Cube," ScaleDP-H achieves near-perfect performance (100%, 95%, 100%). This is a stark contrast to DP-T, which struggled significantly (e.g., 0% success on "Bimanual Stack Cube"). The results clearly validate the scalability and robustness of ScaleDP for complex multi-robot coordination.
- **Ablation Study on Non-causal Attention:** Removing causal masking in the self-attention layers significantly boosts performance across all ScaleDP model sizes, particularly for larger models. For instance, on the "Bimanual Stack Cube" task, ScaleDP-L's success rate jumped from 20% to 90% (a 70% improvement) with non-causal attention. This confirms the benefit of allowing the policy to "see" the entire predicted action chunk for trajectory consistency and robustness.

**C. Visual Generalization**

- **Appearance Generalization:** ScaleDP-L successfully generalizes to target objects with altered colors (e.g., different colored cube/mug) without requiring data augmentation during training. In contrast, DP-T fails to recognize these objects. This indicates ScaleDP's ability to learn robust shape-based object representations.
- **Object Generalization:** ScaleDP-L demonstrates robust generalization to objects of entirely different sizes and shapes (e.g., replacing a block with a cup or a mug with tape). This is a more challenging generalization and underscores the model's flexibility.
- **Light Generalization:** ScaleDP-L adapts effectively to minor variations in ambient lighting conditions.
- **Distractor Generalization:** ScaleDP-L shows greater robustness to the introduction of distractors in the scene compared to DP-T. While significant distractors can still pose challenges, ScaleDP-L exhibits superior adaptability, suggesting it's less prone to being misled by irrelevant scene elements.

In summary, the main findings conclusively establish that ScaleDP effectively addresses the scalability challenge of Diffusion Policy in Transformer architectures. By enabling models to scale from 10 million to 1 billion parameters, it achieves substantial improvements in performance and generalization across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The architectural innovations, particularly AdaLN and non-causal attention, are shown to be critical for this success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "Scaling Diffusion Policy in Transformer to 1 Billion Parameters for Robotic Manipulation" holds significant implications for the field of robotics and artificial intelligence, potentially catalyzing future advancements in visuomotor control.

1. **Breaking the Scalability Barrier for Diffusion Policy:** The most profound significance lies in successfully demonstrating the scalability of Diffusion Policy within a Transformer architecture to 1 billion parameters. Prior to this work, Diffusion Policy in Transformer (DP-T) notoriously struggled to scale, often exhibiting performance degradation with increased model size. ScaleDP overcomes this critical limitation, providing the first clear evidence that diffusion-based imitation learning can benefit from the scaling laws that have revolutionized other areas of AI. This opens up entirely new possibilities for developing more powerful and general-purpose robot policies.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization in Robotic Manipulation:** By enabling effective scaling, ScaleDP achieves state-of-the-art or superior performance across a diverse set of simulated MetaWorld tasks and challenging real-world single-arm and bimanual manipulation tasks. The dramatic improvements, especially on complex bimanual tasks where vanilla DP-T entirely failed, demonstrate the practical utility of larger models. Furthermore, the robust visual generalization capabilities across appearance, object variations, lighting, and distractors mean robots can operate more reliably in varied and unstructured environments without extensive re-training or data augmentation. This is crucial for real-world deployment.

3. **Architectural Insights for Scalable Generative Models:** The paper provides valuable architectural insights by identifying the large gradient issue in the observation fusion module as a key bottleneck for DP-T scalability. The proposed solutions, particularly the Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) block and non-causal attention, are not only effective but also offer a deeper understanding of designing stable and scalable generative models, especially for complex conditional generation tasks like policy learning. These insights could be transferable to other domains where conditional diffusion models are applied.

4. **Paving the Way for Foundation Models in Robotics:** The success of scaling Diffusion Policy models to 1 billion parameters directly aligns with the broader ambition of creating "foundation models" for robotics. Just as large language models have transformed NLP, large, generalist robot policies capable of learning from vast datasets and exhibiting broad generalization are a holy grail for robotics. ScaleDP provides a crucial architectural template and empirical validation for this direction within the imitation learning paradigm. This work suggests that future robot models could be trained on massive, diverse datasets, potentially leading to emergent manipulation skills and a reduced need for task-specific engineering.

5. **Industrial Impact and Practical Applications:** The involvement of Midea Group, a major industrial player, highlights the practical relevance of this research. More robust, generalizable, and performant robot policies have direct implications for industrial automation, manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics. Robots capable of adapting to varying object types, colors, and environmental conditions with fewer demonstrations could significantly lower deployment costs and increase automation flexibility in factories, warehouses, and even homes.

6. **Future Research Directions:** This work explicitly "paves the way for scaling up models for visuomotor learning," opening numerous avenues for future research. This includes exploring even larger models, investigating the emergent properties of such scaled policies, integrating multi-modal inputs beyond vision and proprioception (e.g., language instructions), and applying these scalable architectures to more complex, long-horizon, or few-shot learning scenarios in robotics.

In conclusion, ScaleDP represents a significant leap forward in diffusion-based robot policy learning by demonstrating that these models can indeed scale effectively. By addressing fundamental architectural challenges, the authors have unlocked a new level of performance, generalization, and potential for future foundation models in robotics, with profound implications for both academic research and industrial applications.
