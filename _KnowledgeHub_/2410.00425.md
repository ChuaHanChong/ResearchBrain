# ManiSkill3: GPU Parallelized Robotics Simulation and Rendering for Generalizable Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiSkill3 introduces a GPU-parallelized robot simulation and rendering framework designed for generalizable embodied AI, achieving simulation and rendering speeds of over 30,000 frames per second and utilizing 2-3 times less GPU memory compared to competitors. The platform enables efficient training for visually-driven robot learning and demonstrates successful zero-shot sim2real transfer to real hardware.

## Problem

- High cost and logistical challenges of collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world robotic datasets for training complex AI policies.
- CPU-based simulation frameworks are too slow for data-intensive methods like reinforcement learning, especially with visual inputs.
- Existing GPU-parallelized simulators often support a narrow range of tasks, lack heterogeneous simulation capabilities, and have limited or memory-intensive parallel rendering for visual inputs.

## Method

- Introduces ManiSkill3, an open-source, GPU-parallelized robotics simulator and benchmark built on the SAPIEN engine and NVIDIA PhysX.
- Implements holistic GPU parallelization for physics, visual rendering (RGB, depth, point clouds), and internal computations, minimizing Python/PyTorch overhead.
- Provides complete support for heterogeneous GPU simulation, allowing diverse objects, articulations, and scenes to be simulated simultaneously in parallel environments.

## Results

- Achieves simulation and rendering speeds of up to 30,000+ FPS, reducing visual reinforcement learning training times from hours to minutes.
- Uses 2-3x less GPU memory than competing platforms for visual rendering, enabling larger experiments and on-device visual reinforcement learning.
- Demonstrates successful zero-shot sim2real transfer to a real robot with a 91.6% success rate on a cube picking task and validates real2sim digital twins with a 0.9284 correlation to real-world performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - GPU parallelization must extend beyond physics to include visual rendering and other computations for practical visual robot learning.
> - Enabling heterogeneous simulation across parallel environments is crucial for training generalizable policies that adapt to diverse scenes and objects.
> - Optimizing GPU memory usage is vital for scaling visual reinforcement learning experiments, allowing for larger models and replay buffers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is grappling with the grand challenge of achieving robust and generalized manipulation capabilities in real-world settings. A primary bottleneck has been the prohibitive cost and logistical challenges of collecting large-scale, high-quality real-world robotic datasets for training complex AI policies. While approaches like real-world teleoperation and reinforcement learning (RL) exist, they often suffer from low data efficiency, high setup costs, and practical limitations for widespread deployment.

This paper positions ManiSkill3 as a critical advancement in addressing these limitations, specifically by leveraging and significantly improving **GPU-parallelized simulation**. The broader research landscape for robot learning can be broadly categorized into:

- **Real-World Data Collection:** Efforts like Open-X and DROID focus on collecting vast amounts of real-world robot data. However, they face issues with data quality, consistency, and the sheer human labor required, making scaling difficult.
- **CPU-Based Simulation Frameworks:** Platforms such as Robocasa, Habitat, AI2-THOR, OmniGibson, and RLBench offer diverse environments and tasks, including complex room-scale scenes. However, their reliance on CPU-based simulation backends severely limits their speed (often running magnitudes slower than real-time) and their utility for data-intensive methods like reinforcement learning, often relegating them to imitation learning or motion planning.
- **GPU-Parallelized Simulators:** More recently, simulators like NVIDIA's Isaac Gym/Lab (which uses Isaac Sim's backend) and Mujoco's MJX (accessible via Brax) have emerged as powerful tools. These have revolutionized areas like robot locomotion by enabling massive parallel data generation for RL. However, for manipulation tasks, they still present significant limitations:
 - **Narrow Task Range:** Often support a limited set of manipulation scenarios.
 - **Lack of Heterogeneous Simulation:** Difficulty in simulating entirely different scenes (e.g., varying objects, articulations, or numbers of objects) in parallel environments. This is crucial for generalizable learning.
 - **Limited Visual Rendering Capabilities:** While fast for state-based RL, their parallel rendering for visual inputs (RGB, depth, point clouds) can be slow or memory-intensive, making visual RL impractical for complex manipulation.
 - **High GPU Memory Usage:** Can quickly hit memory limits, restricting the scale of parallel environments or the complexity of models.

ManiSkill3 directly addresses these gaps. Building upon the legacy of ManiSkill1 and ManiSkill2, it aims to unify and enhance the capabilities of GPU-parallelized simulation for *generalizable manipulation with visual inputs*. It proposes a comprehensive solution that not only matches or exceeds the performance of existing GPU simulators but also introduces critical features like heterogeneous simulation and significantly improved GPU memory efficiency for visual rendering. By open-sourcing the framework, ManiSkill3 seeks to democratize access to high-performance robot simulation, fostering accelerated research in visuomotor policies and sim2real transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of ManiSkill3 is to introduce and open-source a **state-visual GPU parallelized robotics simulator and benchmark specifically designed for generalizable manipulation**, addressing the current limitations that hinder large-scale embodied AI research and sim2real transfer.

The motivation behind ManiSkill3 stems from several critical challenges in robot learning:

- **Accelerating Visual Robot Learning:** Existing GPU simulators, while fast for state-based control, are often too slow or memory-intensive when operating on visual inputs (RGB, depth, point clouds). This makes visual reinforcement learning, which is essential for developing robust real-world policies, impractical due to long training times. ManiSkill3 aims to drastically reduce these training times from hours to minutes, enabling faster iteration and more complex experimentation.
- **Enabling Generalizable Manipulation:** Current simulators often support only a narrow range of scenes and tasks, leading to policies that lack generalization capabilities. The motivation is to provide a framework that supports training on a diverse set of tasks and allows for **heterogeneous simulation**—where each parallel environment can feature different objects, articulations, or even entire room-scale scenes. This variability is crucial for learning policies that can generalize to unseen real-world scenarios.
- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap:** A significant barrier to real-world robot deployment is the sim2real gap. ManiSkill3 is motivated to provide features and setups that explicitly facilitate robust sim2real and real2sim transfer. This includes capabilities for realistic rendering, extensive domain randomization, and tools for aligning simulated and real-world environments.
- **Democratizing Access to Scalable Robot Learning:** High-performance simulation tools can be complex and inaccessible. ManiSkill3 aims to provide an **easy-to-use, unified, object-oriented API** that simplifies task creation and management, lowering the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners. Its open-source nature (Apache-2.0 license) further promotes community contributions and broader adoption.
- **Providing Comprehensive Resources:** Beyond the simulator itself, the motivation is to offer a complete ecosystem, including a wide range of GPU-parallelized environments, over 20 different robot embodiments, millions of demonstration frames, and a comprehensive set of baselines for both reinforcement learning and learning-from-demonstrations algorithms. This holistic approach aims to serve as a robust benchmark and development platform for the community.

In essence, ManiSkill3 is driven by the need for a more performant, versatile, and accessible simulation platform that can unlock new frontiers in generalizable and visually-driven robotic manipulation research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ManiSkill3’s methodology is centered on building a highly efficient, GPU-parallelized simulation and rendering framework tailored for generalizable manipulation tasks. The approach leverages and extends existing robust technologies while introducing novel design principles.

**Core Simulation and Rendering Engine:**
- ManiSkill3 builds upon the **SAPIEN** simulation engine, which is open-source and provides the foundation for GPU-parallelized simulation and rendering. This distinguishes it from Isaac Lab, which relies on the closed-source Isaac Sim.
- For physics simulation, ManiSkill3, similar to Isaac Lab, utilizes **NVIDIA PhysX**, a widely adopted and robust physics engine capable of high-performance simulations.

**GPU Parallelization and Optimization:**
- **Holistic Parallelization:** ManiSkill3 extends GPU parallelization to multiple aspects beyond just physics, including simulation steps, rendering of visual inputs (RGB, depth, segmentation, point clouds, voxels), and various internal computations.
- **Minimal Overhead:** A key design principle is minimizing Python/PyTorch overhead in the system. This is crucial for achieving high simulation and rendering frame rates (up to 30,000+ FPS) by keeping operations on the GPU as much as possible.
- **GPU Memory Efficiency:** The framework is specifically designed to use significantly less GPU memory (2-3x lower than competitors like Isaac Lab). This enables on-device visual RL, larger neural networks, and replay buffers without encountering out-of-memory errors, which is vital for off-policy RL algorithms.
- **Heterogeneous Simulation:** A standout feature is its complete support for heterogeneous GPU simulation. This is achieved through a data-oriented system design and an easy-to-use API that efficiently manages GPU memory for objects and articulations with varying degrees of freedom across parallel environments. This allows for diverse scenes, objects, and robot configurations to be simulated simultaneously, fostering generalizable learning.

**Task and Environment Design:**
- **Diverse Categories:** ManiSkill3 provides an extensive range of 12 distinct task categories (e.g., table-top, mobile manipulation, room-scale, locomotion, dextrous, drawing/cleaning, vision-tactile, digital twins) and over 20 different robot embodiments. This diversity is achieved through a flexible task-building API and serves as a comprehensive benchmark.
- **Templates for Extensibility:** The focus is not just on quantity but on providing well-documented templates and examples that users can easily build upon to create their own custom environments and robots.
- **Integration of External Assets:** It incorporates environments from ReplicaCAD, AI2-THOR, and RoboCasa, and integrates tools like CoACD for generating simulatable collision meshes from complex 3D assets.

**User-Friendly API and Tooling:**
- **Object-Oriented API:** ManiSkill3 offers a clear, object-oriented API for managing articulations, links, joints, and actors, eliminating complex tensor indexing often found in other GPU simulators. This simplifies environment creation and interaction.
- **Unified Control:** Provides pre-built and configurable controller options (joint position, inverse kinematics) parallelized on the GPU, building on PyTorch Kinematics.
- **Feature-Rich Tooling:** Includes utilities for domain randomization (camera poses, object materials), trajectory replay, and controller action conversion, streamlining various research workflows.

**Dataset Generation and Baselines:**
- **Scalable Demonstration Generation:** Employs a multi-pronged approach: motion planning for simple tasks, RL-generated data for tasks with defined rewards, and an online imitation learning pipeline (using RFCL or RLPD) that can scale up from a few teleoperated or hardcoded demonstrations.
- **Trajectory Replay:** A flexible tool allows replaying trajectories with different observation types (state, RGBD) and reward configurations, facilitating data reuse and analysis.
- **Comprehensive Baselines:** Offers implementations of popular RL algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD-MPC2) and learning-from-demonstrations methods (Behavior Cloning, Diffusion Policy, ACT, PerACT), with configurations tuned for both wall-time and sample efficiency. It also supports evaluation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like Octo and RT-X.

**Sim2Real and Real2Sim Approaches:**
- **Vision-Based Sim2Real:** Demonstrated using a low-cost Koch robot arm. The approach involves training PPO on simulation images, employing domain randomization (camera pose, lighting, object properties), and utilizing green screening for background alignment. A key aspect is using a consistent delta joint position controller across sim and real.
- **Real2Sim Validation:** Ports SIMPLER environments to ManiSkill3, showing strong correlation between real-world and simulated success rates for generalist policies, validating its utility as an evaluation digital twin.
- **VR Teleoperation:** Provides an immersive VR teleoperation system compatible with mainstream devices, allowing for real-time human control over robots in simulation and real-world (via depth camera point clouds), crucial for generating human demonstrations.

This multifaceted methodology, from low-level GPU optimization to high-level API design and comprehensive benchmarking, positions ManiSkill3 as a robust and versatile platform for advancing embodied AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ManiSkill3 demonstrates significant advancements across performance, diversity, and practical applicability in robot learning:

**Performance and Efficiency:**
- **High FPS:** Achieves impressive simulation + rendering speeds of up to **30,000+ FPS** in benchmarked environments. This is a substantial acceleration, enabling tasks that previously took hours to train to be completed in minutes.
- **Superior GPU Memory Usage:** A critical finding is ManiSkill3's efficiency in GPU memory. It uses **2-3x less GPU memory** than other platforms like Isaac Lab, especially for common real-world camera resolutions (e.g., 3.5GB vs. 14.1GB for 128 parallel environments with a 640x480 camera). This efficiency is crucial for on-device visual RL, allowing for larger replay buffers and neural networks.
- **Training Time Reduction:** For visual tasks like PickCube with RGB observations, training time with PPO was reduced from approximately 80 minutes (ManiSkill2 CPU simulation) to around **10 minutes** on ManiSkill3. For state-based tasks, the speedup was even more dramatic, with training time reduced by **15x** (from minutes to about 1 minute).
- **Simulation vs. Simulation+Rendering:** While simulation-only speeds are comparable to Isaac Lab (with ManiSkill3 often slightly faster and more memory-efficient), ManiSkill3 significantly outperforms Isaac Lab in **simulation+rendering benchmarks**, particularly at higher camera resolutions (up to 2x faster and 4x less memory usage). This confirms its strength for visual-input-based learning.

**Diversity and Generalizability:**
- **Comprehensive Environment Range:** ManiSkill3 successfully supports and provides **12 distinct categories of environments** and over **20 different robot embodiments** out-of-the-box. This includes novel task types like drawing/cleaning and integrated room-scale scenes, demonstrating its versatility.
- **Heterogeneous GPU Simulation Validation:** The framework effectively allows for the simulation and rendering of **completely different objects, articulations, and even entire room-scale scenes in each parallel environment**. This is a unique capability that directly enables more generalizable learning approaches by training on diverse data simultaneously.

**Sim2Real and Real2Sim Effectiveness:**
- **Zero-Shot Sim2Real Success:** A key finding is the successful **zero-shot deployment of a vision-based policy trained entirely in simulation to a real Koch robot arm**, achieving a **91.6% success rate** on a cube picking task. This was achieved using only RGB inputs and robot joint positions, without demonstrations or privileged state information, and leveraged techniques like green-screening and domain randomization.
- **Strong Sim-Real Correlation:** For real2sim digital twins (porting SIMPLER environments), ManiSkill3 achieved a correlation between real-world and simulation success rates of **0.9284** and a Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV) of 0.0147. These metrics are comparable to the original SIMPLER findings, indicating ManiSkill3's accuracy as an evaluation tool for policies trained on real-world data.
- **Accelerated Digital Twin Evaluation:** ManiSkill3's digital twins can evaluate generalist robot policies like Octo at **60x to 100x the speed of the real world**, approximately 10x faster than original SIMPLER implementations, due to fast parallel rendering and flexible GPU controllers.

**Baseline Performance and Utilities:**
- **Robust Baselines:** The provided RL (PPO, TD-MPC2) and LfD (Diffusion Policy, ACT, PerACT) baselines demonstrate competitive performance on various tasks, validating the platform's utility for diverse learning paradigms.
- **Impact of Domain Randomization:** The sim2real experiment underscores the importance and effectiveness of GPU-parallelized domain randomization (camera poses, lighting, textures) for training robust policies.
- **VR Teleoperation Utility:** The integrated VR teleoperation system is shown to be effective for immersive data collection, providing accurate human-to-robot pose translation and 4K stereo video feedback.

In summary, ManiSkill3 significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot simulation by combining high performance, exceptional GPU memory efficiency, unparalleled task diversity, and robust sim2real capabilities, all within an accessible open-source framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiSkill3 represents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robotics, with the potential to profoundly impact several areas of research and development:

- **Accelerating Robot Learning Research:** The most immediate impact is the dramatic reduction in training time for visually-driven robot learning algorithms. By enabling high-speed, GPU-parallelized simulation and rendering, ManiSkill3 makes it feasible to run millions of environment steps in minutes, accelerating the development and iteration cycle for complex visuomotor policies. This shifts the bottleneck from data generation to algorithmic innovation, opening doors for exploring more sophisticated RL and learning-from-demonstrations (LfD) approaches.

- **Enabling Generalizable Robotics:** The unique support for **heterogeneous GPU simulation** is a game-changer for generalizable robot learning. Researchers can now train policies on vastly diverse scenes and object configurations simultaneously, leading to agents that are inherently more robust and capable of generalizing to unseen environments and tasks in the real world. This is critical for moving beyond narrow, task-specific robot skills towards more versatile, general-purpose robots.

- **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap More Effectively:** ManiSkill3 provides a robust, reproducible framework for both **sim2real and real2sim transfer**. The demonstrated zero-shot sim2real success with a low-cost robot highlights a pathway to more accessible and practical real-world robot deployment. The accurate real2sim digital twins further provide a reliable benchmark for evaluating generalist policies, fostering the development of models that truly perform well in real environments. This capability is vital for the eventual widespread adoption of learned robotic skills.

- **Democratizing Access to Advanced Simulation:** By being open-source and providing a user-friendly, object-oriented API, ManiSkill3 lowers the barrier to entry for researchers, particularly those without extensive experience in complex simulation environments. Its focus on GPU memory efficiency also means that powerful experiments can be conducted on more modest hardware setups, making cutting-edge robot learning research accessible to a broader community. This democratization fosters increased participation and innovation.

- **Catalyst for New Research Directions:** The comprehensive suite of environments, diverse robot models, scalable data generation pipelines, and readily available baselines make ManiSkill3 an invaluable benchmark. It encourages researchers to tackle more complex manipulation challenges, explore novel visual representations (e.g., point clouds, voxels at scale), and develop new algorithms for both RL and LfD. The inclusion of VR teleoperation also provides a powerful tool for human-in-the-loop data collection and intervention.

- **Foundational Platform for Embodied AI:** ManiSkill3's design, building upon the established SAPIEN and PhysX, positions it as a foundational platform for future embodied AI research. Its commitment to addressing core limitations in scalability, generalization, and sim2real transfer means it can serve as a robust testing ground for the next generation of intelligent agents that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured environments.

In conclusion, ManiSkill3 is not merely an incremental improvement; it is a comprehensive, open-source ecosystem that addresses critical bottlenecks in robot learning. Its innovations in GPU parallelization, heterogeneous simulation, and practical sim2real capabilities are poised to significantly accelerate the pace of research in generalizable embodied AI, bringing us closer to robots that can robustly and intelligently interact with the real world.
