# Qwen-RobotManip Technical Report: Alignment Unlocks Scale for Robotic Manipulation Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Qwen-RobotManip, a Vision-Language-Action foundation model, demonstrates that aligning heterogeneous robotic data enables effective data scaling and emergent generalization capabilities for manipulation tasks. The model achieves robust performance across various out-of-distribution scenarios and real-world deployments by integrating a unified cross-embodiment formulation and a large-scale open-source pretraining corpus.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation foundation models often exhibit limited generalization due to data scarcity, lack of diversity, and the heterogeneity of robotic data across different embodiments.
- Incompatibility in observation and action representations across diverse robot platforms leads to interference rather than synergy during multi-source data training.
- Standard in-domain benchmarks for robotic tasks frequently fail to distinguish genuine generalization from pattern memorization, limiting true assessment of model capabilities.

## Method

- Developed **Qwen-RobotManip**, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model built upon Qwen-VL, featuring a decoupled VLM backbone and a ==Diffusion Transformer action expert==.
- Introduced a unified alignment framework with a canonical 80-dimensional state-action vector and camera-frame delta pose representation for end-effector actions, ensuring numerical consistency across embodiments.
- Constructed a large-scale pretraining corpus of approximately 38,100 hours by integrating over 11,000 hours of open-source robot data and ~24,808 hours of human-to-robot synthesized demonstrations.

## Results

- Substantially outperformed prior state-of-the-art models on OOD benchmarks, showing a **7.0**-point gain on LIBERO-Plus, a **21.5**-point gain on RoboTwin-Clean2Rand Hard, and a **22.6**-point gain on RoboTwin-IF.
- Demonstrated emergent generalization capabilities, including zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer with a **3.2x** improvement, robust error recovery, and strong bimanual coordination.
- Achieved a 1st place ranking in the RoboChallenge Table30-v1 Generalist Track with a **45%** success rate and validated robust performance on diverse real-world tasks across multiple physical robot platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified alignment of heterogeneous robotic data is a prerequisite for effective data scaling, transforming diverse data into synergistic training signals for enhanced generalization.
> - Out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation metrics are crucial for accurately distinguishing genuine generalization from pattern memorization in robotic manipulation foundation models.
> - Dual-stream co-training with large-scale VLM data is essential for preserving the broad perceptual, linguistic, and spatial reasoning capabilities of the VLM backbone, which are critical for robust language-conditioned control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of foundation models for robotic manipulation is an active area of research, building upon the success of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal models (VLMs) in achieving strong generalization through the alignment of heterogeneous data sources and training at scale. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics have demonstrated capabilities on standard benchmarks, but often exhibit limited generalization when evaluated on out-of-distribution (OOD) settings that involve variations in embodiment, task structure, or workspace layout.

Current limitations in robotic manipulation foundation models are twofold:
1. **Data Scarcity and Diversity:** Robotic demonstration corpora are often concentrated in narrow teleoperation setups, lacking sufficient embodiment and task diversity to facilitate large-scale generalization. Unlike text data, manipulation data is heterogeneous by nature, expensive to collect, and narrow in scope.
2. **Lack of Alignment:** Even with diverse data, incompatibility in observation and action representations across different robot embodiments can lead to interference rather than synergy during training. Prior efforts towards cross-embodiment generalization, such as shared architectures or unified action tokenization, have not consistently ensured numerical consistency for the same physical motion across different embodiments, limiting the transferability of learned capabilities.

This work addresses these challenges by proposing that alignment is a prerequisite for effective data scaling in robotic manipulation. It positions itself as an investigation into whether the scaling recipe of LLMs can be successfully applied to robotics by first establishing a robust alignment framework, then leveraging it to absorb manipulation data at a scale previously unsustainable. The paper contrasts its approach with existing VLA models that may achieve competitive in-domain performance but lack genuine OOD generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the scaling methodology successfully applied to language and multimodal foundation models—namely, aligning heterogeneous data under a unified formulation and training at abundant scale—can be adapted to robotic manipulation to achieve genuine generalization.

The motivation for this investigation stems from several challenges inherent to robotic manipulation data:
- **Heterogeneity:** Robotic manipulation data is inherently diverse, originating from various robot platforms, sensing modalities, and control paradigms. This heterogeneity makes it difficult to combine data sources effectively without a unified framework.
- **Cost of Collection:** Unlike readily available internet-scale text data, high-quality robotic manipulation data is expensive and time-consuming to collect, limiting the scale and diversity of existing datasets.
- **Limited Diversity:** Existing datasets often capture a narrow range of embodiments and tasks, hindering the development of truly generalizable policies.

To overcome these challenges, the research sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Develop a Unified Alignment Framework:** The core motivation is to create a framework that harmonizes heterogeneous manipulation data across critical dimensions:
 - **Representation Alignment:** Establish a canonical state-action representation that can accommodate diverse robot morphologies within a single template, allowing different robots to share a common understanding of states and actions.
 - **Motion Alignment:** Ensure that physically similar motions are numerically consistent across different coordinate frames, particularly by grounding end-effector actions in the visual domain to align with visual observations.
 - **Behavioral Alignment:** Enable policies to adapt to embodiment-specific kinematic or dynamic characteristics without explicit programming, leveraging contextual information.
2. **Enable Large-Scale Multi-Source Training:** Build a data infrastructure capable of absorbing and harmonizing diverse data sources at scale, including:
 - **Human-to-Robot Synthesis:** Create a scalable pipeline to convert readily available egocentric human demonstrations into robot-compatible trajectories across multiple robot platforms, augmenting the scarcity of real robot data.
 - **Rigorous Data Curation:** Implement a multi-stage pipeline to clean, harmonize, and validate heterogeneous real-robot and synthetic datasets, ensuring signal quality and consistency.
3. **Preserve Pre-trained VLM Capabilities:** Integrate the manipulation training with existing vision-language model (VLM) pre-training to prevent degradation of the VLM backbone's perceptual and reasoning capabilities, which are crucial for interpreting novel instructions and generalizing to unseen visual contexts.
4. **Establish New Evaluation Standards:** Motivated by the observation that standard in-domain benchmarks often fail to distinguish genuine generalization from pattern memorization, the research aims to introduce and utilize out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation settings that more faithfully measure true generalization, including instruction following and cross-embodiment transfer.
5. **Validate Real-World Generalization:** Demonstrate the emergent generalization capabilities of the developed model on physical robot platforms across various real-world deployment scenarios, moving beyond simulated environments.

By addressing these objectives, the researchers aim to demonstrate that with proper alignment, it is possible to build generalizable robotic manipulation foundation models at scale using existing open-source data, thereby lowering the data barrier for the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QWEN-ROBOTMANIP is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model built upon Qwen-VL. Its methodology is structured around enabling large-scale, multi-source training through a unified alignment framework and robust data pipelines.

#### 3.1. Main Architecture

The model employs a decoupled architecture comprising:
- **Vision-Language Backbone:** Qwen3.5-4B is used as the backbone, a natively multimodal model with early vision-language fusion. It processes multi-view camera inputs and natural language instructions, generating contextual representations (e.g., last-layer hidden states) for the action expert.
- **Action Expert:** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) (N=10 blocks, D_act=768) acts as a flow-matching expert for continuous action generation. It processes proprioceptive state and noisy action tokens, performing self-attention and alternating cross-attention to visual (even blocks) and language (odd blocks) hidden states from the VLM. It is conditioned on denoising timestep embeddings and learned camera embeddings. The expert is trained with a flow-matching objective, minimizing mean squared error on the predicted velocity field.

#### 3.2. Cross-Embodiment State and Action Representation

A key innovation is an 80-dimensional canonical vector representation for states and actions, structured with two 29-dimensional per-arm blocks (joint positions, end-effector pose, gripper state, dexterous hand joints) and 22 reserved dimensions.
- **State Vector:** Values are expressed in absolute coordinates.
- **Action Vector:** Joint actions are absolute, while end-effector actions are relative deltas from the current state. End-effector orientation deltas are 3D rotation vectors.
- **Masking:** Per-dimension binary masks are applied during training to exclude zero-padded dimensions, ensuring gradients only flow through semantically populated entries.

#### 3.3. Unified End-Effector Motion Prediction

To address inconsistencies in end-effector pose recording across datasets, all end-effector actions are grounded in a camera-frame delta pose representation.
- **Camera-frame Delta Pose:** Actions are expressed as relative pose changes in the reference camera's coordinate frame, aligning the action space with visual observations. This requires calibrated camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
- **Camera-aware Positional Encoding (CaPE):** Camera parameters are injected into the DiT’s cross-attention layers via CaPE. This encoding, applied to keys, queries, values, and attention outputs, implicitly encodes inter-view relative poses, enhancing geometric consistency.
- **End-effector-aware Conditioning:** The DiT is further conditioned via additive embeddings for:
 - End-effector type (single-arm, dual-arm left/right, egocentric head, mobile base).
 - An auxiliary flag indicating the availability of calibrated camera parameters, switching between camera-frame delta and robot-base relative action modes.
- **Multi-view Reference Camera Selection:** During training, a reference camera is selected for multi-view settings (random external/wrist view for single-arm; common head-mounted/third-person or individual wrist cameras for dual-arm).

#### 3.4. Embodiment Prompt

A structured prompt is used to condition the policy, containing fields for:
- `Embodiment`: e.g., `robot_aloha`
- `Instruction`: high-level task description
- `Speed`: episode length in binned timesteps
- `FPS`: temporal sampling rate
- `Camera View Direction`: camera position relative to the arm
These fields help the model account for morphological, temporal, and control differences. Random dropout of `embodiment`, `speed`, and `fps` fields (15% probability) is used during training to improve robustness to incomplete inputs.

#### 3.5. In-Context Policy Adaptation

Inspired by LLMs, QWEN-ROBOTMANIP incorporates an in-context policy adaptation mechanism.
- **Execution Context Representation:** Recent execution history (observation-action triplets) is encoded. Historical frames are processed by the VLM visual encoder, while proprioceptive states and action chunks are projected into the VLM hidden space by MLPs with learned temporal and slot position embeddings.
- **History Integration:** Context tokens are appended to the VLM input sequence for joint processing with visual and language tokens under causal self-attention (unified mode).
- **Stochastic Context Sampling:** During training, context windows are sampled from random positions within an episode, preventing the model from relying on immediate temporal proximity and encouraging learning of broader behavioral profiles.

#### 3.6. Training

- **Dual-Stream Co-Training:** The model is trained simultaneously on a VLA stream (robot data, egocentric human videos, human-to-robot synthetic data) and a VLM stream (large-scale vision-language data, Section 2.5). A 9:1 ratio of robot to VL data is adopted.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **Flow Matching Loss (L_FM):** For VLA samples, a flow-matching objective is minimized on predicted velocity fields, with gradients applied to both the VLM backbone and action expert. A composed binary mask (per-dimension slot, step validity, per-hand validity) restricts the loss to valid entries.
 - **VLM Next-Token Prediction Loss (L_VLM):** For VLM samples, a standard autoregressive next-token prediction objective is used.
 - **Overall Objective:** L = L_FM + λ L_VLM (λ=0.1). Separate learning rates are used. The action expert performs K_repeat=8 diffusion steps per training sample to amortize VLM forward pass cost.
- **Post-Training Recipe:**
 - **Domain-specific Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned on task-specific demonstration data (e.g., for benchmarks) without the L_VLM. Color jitter augmentation is applied.
 - **Co-Training in Post-Training (Optional):** A mixed post-training strategy co-trains the model with a subset of pre-training data filtered by distributional proximity to the target domain, aiming to mitigate VLA-to-VA degradation (where language conditioning erodes, becoming a vision-action pattern matcher).

#### 3.7. Data Sources

The training corpus includes:
- **Robotic Datasets:** Over 11,000 hours from nine open-source datasets (e.g., OXE, AgiBotWorld-Beta, RoboMIND, DROID, RH20T), covering single-arm, dual-arm, mobile, and humanoid manipulation in simulation and real world.
- **Egocentric Human Datasets:** Approximately 1,933 hours from EgoDex, VITRA, and EgoVerse, with hand pose annotations converted to a unified MANO representation.
- **Human-to-Robot Data Synthesis:** An end-to-end pipeline converts egocentric human demonstrations into robot trajectories across 15 bimanual robot platforms, yielding ~24,808 hours of synthesized data. This involves:
 - **Action Alignment:** Retargeting human hand keypoints to robot end-effector positions and gripper widths, and constructing gripper orientations. Savitzky–Golay filtering and Gaussian-weighted SLERP smooth trajectories.
 - **Visual Alignment:** Replacing human hands with robot models. SAM3 segments human arms, ProPainter inpaints them, and robot models are rendered into the cleaned background using optimized base poses (via MuJoCo IK) and depth-guided compositing.
 - **Action Speed Alignment:** Frame subsampling for egocentric data to match robot data speed distributions.
- **Data Curation and Pre-Processing:** A five-stage signal filtering pipeline (sudden change detection, state-action trend alignment, extreme value filtering, FK consistency, base frame/orientation alignment) is applied to all datasets. Three additional cross-modal checks (instruction consistency, video-state consistency, video quality filtering) ensure data quality.
- **Vision-Language Co-training Datasets:** Approximately 28M data points, including general visual understanding, spatial perception, OCR, multimodal specialized knowledge, instruction following, and embodied-centric VL data (Embodied Chain-of-Thought, Egocentric Video Understanding, 2D Trajectory Prediction). This is crucial for preserving VLM backbone capabilities and bridging VLM knowledge to action generation.

#### 3.8. Deployment

Real-Time Chunking (RTC) is used for inference, asynchronously generating the next action chunk while the robot executes the current one to mitigate latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents QWEN-ROBOTMANIP, demonstrating emergent generalization capabilities by leveraging a large-scale pretraining corpus (~38,100 hours) constructed solely from open-source robotic manipulation datasets and human demonstration videos.

#### 4.1. Standard vs. OOD Benchmarks

- **Standard Benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin):** QWEN-ROBOTMANIP achieves competitive or state-of-the-art performance (e.g., 99.1% on LIBERO, 93.4%/92.5% on RoboTwin Easy/Hard). However, the paper argues that these in-distribution benchmarks often fail to distinguish genuine generalization from pattern memorization, as models trained from scratch can achieve comparable results.
- **OOD Benchmarks:** A clear performance separation emerges on OOD benchmarks (LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin-Clean2Rand), where QWEN-ROBOTMANIP substantially outperforms models without large-scale robot pretraining. This supports the assertion that OOD evaluation is a more reliable measure of genuine generalization.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities

QWEN-ROBOTMANIP consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art models across various OOD evaluation axes:
- **Task and Scene Generalization:**
 - LIBERO-Plus: 91.4% (vs. 84.4% for π^0.5), a 7.0-point improvement. Ablations show significant gains in Camera viewpoint and Robot initial state perturbations, suggesting VLA pretraining provides robust spatial reasoning and state generalization.
 - RoboTwin-Clean2Rand (Hard): 69.4% (vs. 47.9% for π^0.5), a 21.5-point improvement. Minimal performance degradation from Easy to Hard settings, retaining ~86% of Easy performance (compared to 66% for π^0.5). Performance under Background randomization can exceed Easy settings, indicating diverse real-world scenes in pretraining.
 - RoboCasa365: 35.9% (vs. 16.9% for π^0.5), a 19.0-point improvement, with strong performance on Composite-Unseen tasks (14.9%, nearly tripling the next-best).
 - EBench: 45.6% (vs. 27.1% for π^0.5), an 18.5-point improvement. Striking gains on dexterous tabletop tasks (50.0% SR, 70 score vs. 12.9% SR, 32 score for π^0.5). Performance remains stable under compounded perturbations (Mix condition SR 46.8%), indicating strong generalization robustness.
- **Instruction Following (RoboTwin-IF):** QWEN-ROBOTMANIP scores 72.2% (vs. 49.6% for π^0.5), a 22.6-point improvement. This indicates the dual-stream co-training strategy and pretraining corpus preserve genuine language-conditioned control, rather than reliance on visual shortcuts.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer (RoboTwin-XE):** Achieves 23.9% average success using camera-frame EEF actions (vs. 7.5% for π^0.5), a 3.2x improvement. Joint-space control performs poorly (<5%), while camera-frame EEF actions enable successful transfer by abstracting morphological differences.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- **CobotMagic ALOHA (In-Domain):** Achieves an 88.6% average success rate (vs. 42.9% for π^0.5), demonstrating reliable basic manipulation and better scaling to challenging tasks (e.g., `block-in-drawer-compartment`, `three-block-stacking`).
- **CobotMagic ALOHA (Out-of-Domain):** Achieves an 87.5% average success rate (vs. 37.5% for π^0.5), showing strong robustness to cluttered scenes, novel objects, spatial references, and lighting changes (`banana-on-towel` under disco light).
- **ARX ALOHA (Few-shot Adaptation):** Outperforms baselines on four of five tasks with minimal demonstrations, showing more robust long-horizon execution (`Put Blocks`) and fine-grained rotational control (`Unscrew Cap`).
- **ARX ALOHA (Cross-embodiment Skill Transfer):** Achieves 55.0% on four novel tasks (vs. 12.5% for ablated variants), demonstrating that the unified action space and EEF representation enable skill-level transfer across kinematically different embodiments with no task-specific data.
- **RoboChallenge Table30-v1 Generalist Track:** Ranks 1st with 45% success rate and 59.83 process score, surpassing DM0_generalist (37% success).
 - **Strong Bimanual Coordination:** Achieves 40% on bimanual tasks (vs. 21.2% for π^0.5), attributed to bimanual robot data and human-to-robot synthesis.
 - **Robust Pick-and-Place:** Achieves 63.3% on pick-and-place tasks (vs. 48.3% for DM0), attributed to large-scale cross-embodiment pretraining and unified action space.
 - **Emergent Retry Behavior:** QWEN-ROBOTMANIP exhibits spontaneous retry behavior upon failed manipulation attempts, significantly improving fault tolerance and task success.
 - **Breakthrough on Challenging Tasks:** Achieves leading performance on several high-difficulty, long-horizon tasks (e.g., `plug in network cable`, `pour fries into plate`, `arrange paper cups`) where most baselines fail.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Action Space Alignment for Data Scaling:** Models with unified representations (Ours w/o UnifiedEEF, Ours) exhibit clear log-linear data scaling (decreasing MSE with increasing data). Ours (camera-frame delta pose) achieves the lowest end-effector MSE. This translates to higher downstream task success on OOD settings, confirming that alignment is crucial for converting data volume into improved capabilities.
- **Embodiment Prompt and In-Context Policy Adaptation:** Structured embodiment prompts provide modest gains. In-context policy adaptation, using historical observation-action chunks, provides substantial improvements (+5.0 points) once sufficient denoising steps (e.g., 10) are provided to resolve initial instability, suggesting it acts as an implicit kinematic prior for dynamic adaptation.
- **Human-to-Robot Synthetic Data:** Adding human-to-robot (H2R) synthesized data improves performance on OOD benchmarks (e.g., +4.0 gain on RoboTwin-Clean2Rand Hard, +1.9 on LIBERO-Plus Total), particularly for camera viewpoint and robot initial pose variations. This confirms that raw ego data contributes through visual diversity, and the H2R pipeline unlocks further gains via action and visual alignment.
- **VL Data Co-training:** Removing VL data from pre-training results in performance degradation, especially on challenging RoboTwin benchmarks (e.g., 8.2 points on RoboTwin-Clean2Rand Hard, 7.0 points on RoboTwin-IF). This indicates VL co-training is essential for maintaining broad perceptual, linguistic, and spatial reasoning capabilities. Incorporating VL data in post-training further improves OOD generalization, particularly for language-related tasks. Mixed post-training with VL and auxiliary VLA data effectively mitigates overfitting and leads to continuous performance improvement on OOD tasks, with the UnifiedEEF module identified as a critical prerequisite.
- **Architecture Design:** The "last-layer cross-attention" variant, where learnable query tokens cross-attend to the VLM's last-layer hidden states, achieves the highest average success rate on LIBERO-Plus with lower computational cost, demonstrating efficiency and effectiveness.

#### 4.5. New Features After Alignment

The UnifiedEEF module is identified as a critical prerequisite for mixed post-training to prevent representation collapse, enabling the model to effectively assimilate mixed VLM/VLA data and push performance to 75.8% on instruction following. Camera-frame delta EEF actions consistently improve in-distribution control, enable compositional skill transfer across embodiments (4.4x improvement over baselines), and support zero-shot deployment on unseen robots (1.65x improvement over joint control), confirming its role as an embodiment-agnostic action interface.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents QWEN-ROBOTMANIP as a VLA foundation model, providing an affirmative answer to the applicability of the alignment-then-scale paradigm, previously successful in language and multimodal models, to robotic manipulation. The work emphasizes that unified cross-embodiment formulation is a prerequisite for effective data scaling, transforming heterogeneous data into synergistic training signals.

The findings carry several implications:
- **Data Barrier Reduction:** The construction of a large-scale (~38,100-hour) pretraining corpus using only open-source robot data and egocentric human videos, without proprietary collection, suggests that the data barrier for manipulation foundation models may be lower than previously assumed, given effective synthesis and curation infrastructure.
- **Unified Alignment Framework:** The proposed framework, encompassing canonical state-action representation, camera-frame delta pose parameterization, and in-context policy adaptation, is demonstrated to be critical not just for accommodating diverse embodiments but for enabling data scaling itself. Ablation studies show that effective alignment leads to predictable performance improvements with increased data volume.
- **Evolution of Evaluation Metrics:** The systematic comparison highlighting the inadequacy of standard in-domain benchmarks for measuring genuine generalization is significant. The introduction of OOD evaluation settings (e.g., LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin-IF, RoboTwin-XE, RoboCasa365, EBench) offers a more faithful measure of transferable capabilities for robotic foundation models.
- **Emergent Generalization:** The model exhibits emergent generalization capabilities, including zero-shot instruction following, robustness to perturbations, reactive error recovery, and cross-embodiment knowledge transfer, validated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments. These capabilities are crucial for deploying robots in novel and unstructured environments.
- **Real-World Applicability:** Validation on diverse physical platforms (AgileX ALOHA, Franka, UR, ARX) across various scenarios, including few-shot adaptation and zero-shot transfer, demonstrates the practical utility and robustness of the model under real-world deployment conditions. The leading performance in the RoboChallenge Table30-v1 generalist track further substantiates its multi-task generalization ability.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
- **Synthesis Quality:** The human-to-robot synthesis pipeline, while scalable, introduces distributional gaps due to retargeting approximations and inpainting artifacts, which can limit the effective quality of synthesized data.
- **Simulation Dominance:** The OOD evaluations, while robust, are still predominantly simulation-based, necessitating broader real-world evaluation.
- **Control Latency:** The fixed action chunk length and inference latency of the current system may constrain applicability to tasks requiring highly reactive, sub-second control.

**Future directions suggested by the authors include:**
- Incorporating more robot morphologies and task domains into the pretraining corpus.
- Improving synthesis fidelity through more accurate hand-robot retargeting and physically grounded rendering techniques.
- Integrating agentic systems for longer-horizon reasoning and manipulation.
- Advocating for a shift in community evaluation standards from in-domain benchmark performance to OOD generalization, which is crucial for real-world deployment.
