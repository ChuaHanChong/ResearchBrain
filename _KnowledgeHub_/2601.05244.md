# GREx: Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation, Comprehension, and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University researchers created new generalized referring expression tasks (GREx) for segmentation, comprehension, and generation, accompanied by the gRefCOCO dataset to support multi-target and no-target expressions. Their ReLA baseline model, featuring dynamic region-language attention, showed improved performance on these complex scenarios and established new baselines for classic referring expression tasks.

## Problem

- Classic referring expression (REx) tasks and datasets were limited to a 'one expression, one object' paradigm, making them impractical for real-world scenarios.
- Existing REx methods struggled with expressions referring to multiple objects (multi-target) or no objects at all (no-target), leading to inefficient interactions or undefined behaviors.
- The absence of large-scale, high-quality datasets for multi-target and no-target expressions hindered systematic research into these generalized REx challenges.

## Method

- Introduced ==Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation (GRES)==, Comprehension (GREC), and Generation (GREG) benchmarks to accommodate expressions referring to arbitrary numbers of objects.
- Constructed ==gRefCOCO==, a large-scale, high-quality dataset extending RefCOCO with **90,064** multi-target and **34,537** no-target expressions, ensuring diverse and realistic annotations.
- Proposed ==ReLA (ReLAtionship modeling block)==, a transformer-based baseline method with novel Region-Image Cross Attention (RIA) and Region-Language Cross Attention (RLA) to model complex relationships and fine-grained attributes.

## Results

- ReLA significantly outperformed re-implemented state-of-the-art classic RES methods on gRefCOCO, demonstrating over **4%** cIoU improvement for GRES.
- The method achieved **61.90%** Pr@F1 for GREC on the gRefCOCO validation set, showing strong performance in multi-instance bounding box prediction.
- ReLA also established new state-of-the-art results on classic RES benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, G-Ref) when trained on single-target data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling complex relationships through dynamic region feature aggregation (RIA) and combined region-word/region-region interactions (RLA) is crucial for generalized referring expression tasks.
> - The explicit prediction of the number of target objects (N_O) is vital for accurate instance-level comprehension, especially for distinguishing multi-target from no-target scenarios.
> - Classic REx models fail to generalize to multi-target and no-target expressions, highlighting the necessity of dedicated datasets and methodologies for these generalized tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multi-modal information processing, particularly at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, has seen significant advancements in tasks like Referring Expression Segmentation (RES), Comprehension (REC), and Generation (REG), collectively termed REx. These tasks aim to ground natural language expressions to visual objects or generate expressions for selected objects. Historically, these REx tasks have been largely constrained by a "one expression, one object" paradigm, heavily influenced by foundational datasets like ReferIt and RefCOCO. This means an expression is expected to refer to a single, unambiguous object in an image.

This paper, "GREx," addresses a critical limitation within this established landscape: the inability of classic REx methods and datasets to handle real-world complexities such as multi-target expressions (one expression referring to multiple objects) and no-target expressions (an expression referring to no object in the image). While some recent complementary datasets like Ref-ZOM and GRD have begun to explore multi- and no-target scenarios, they often suffer from limitations such as synthetic data generation or limited diversity, as highlighted by the authors. This work positions itself as a significant generalization, aiming to make REx tasks more robust, flexible, and applicable to natural human-machine interactions.

The broader research landscape is also characterized by the rapid evolution of transformer-based architectures, vision-language models (VLMs), and large language models (LLMs). The paper's proposed baseline method, ReLA, leverages these modern architectures (Swin Transformer, BERT) and introduces novel attention mechanisms to explicitly model complex relationships. By introducing the gRefCOCO dataset, the authors provide a new benchmark that challenges current state-of-the-art methods, including advanced MLLMs, to move beyond single-target constraints and effectively reason about groups of objects, attributes, and non-existent targets. This work, therefore, pushes the boundaries of multi-modal AI towards more nuanced contextual understanding and generation, laying groundwork for future advancements in more generalized visual perception and language interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the GREx paper are threefold:

1. **To introduce new generalized benchmarks for referring expression tasks:** The authors propose Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation (GRES), Generalized Referring Expression Comprehension (GREC), and Generalized Referring Expression Generation (GREG), collectively termed GREx. These benchmarks extend the classic REx tasks by allowing natural language expressions to refer to an arbitrary number of target objects, including multi-target (two or more objects) and no-target (zero objects) scenarios, which were previously undefined or poorly handled.
2. **To construct a large-scale, high-quality dataset (gRefCOCO) to support the GREx benchmarks:** Recognizing the lack of suitable datasets that capture multi-target and no-target expressions in a realistic manner, the authors created gRefCOCO. This dataset systematically defines GREx tasks with rich, realistic expressions, segmentation masks, and bounding box annotations, and is designed to be backward-compatible with RefCOCO.
3. **To propose a robust baseline method (ReLA) for GRES and GREC:** The paper introduces ReLA (ReLAtionship modeling block) as a new baseline approach specifically designed to address the challenges of complex relationship modeling and fine-grained attribute capture inherent in generalized referring expression tasks.

The motivations behind these objectives stem directly from the significant limitations of classic REx methods in real-world applications:

- **Limited Applicability:** Existing methods and datasets typically assume a one-to-one mapping between an expression and an object. This constraint makes them impractical for scenarios where users naturally refer to multiple objects (e.g., "all people," "the two red cars") or when an object is not present (e.g., "the blue elephant").
- **Inefficiency and User Experience:** For multi-target scenarios, classic methods would require multiple sequential inputs and model calls, leading to inefficient and unnatural interactions. For instance, segmenting "All people" would necessitate separate expressions for each individual.
- **Undefined Behavior for No-Target Expressions:** When an expression refers to a non-existent object, classic RES/REC models often produce erroneous or arbitrary outputs, leading to unreliability in practical systems. GREx aims to provide a defined response for such cases (e.g., an empty mask/box).
- **Lack of Holistic Reasoning for Generation:** Classic REG generates expressions for single objects. For multiple selected objects, it would generate separate, potentially redundant or awkward descriptions. GREG aims to generate concise and natural expressions that capture shared semantics and relationships among a set of objects.
- **Need for Comprehensive Benchmarking:** The absence of a large-scale dataset with diverse multi-target and no-target expressions hinders systematic research into these crucial aspects of generalized referring expressions.

By addressing these motivations, GREx aims to significantly enhance the flexibility, robustness, and practicality of referring expression tasks, paving the way for more intuitive and powerful human-machine interaction, image editing, and content generation applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of GREx encompasses both the development of a novel dataset and the proposal of a new deep learning architecture.

#### 3.1. gRefCOCO Dataset Construction

The authors constructed **gRefCOCO**, a large-scale dataset extending the widely-used RefCOCO dataset, to support the GREx tasks. Key aspects of its construction include:
- **Data Scale:** It comprises 259,859 expressions (including 90,064 multi-target and 34,537 no-target) referring to 61,316 objects across 19,994 images.
- **Annotation Process:** An interactive, game-like web-based annotation system was developed, involving both annotators and validators. Annotators select target instances (or indicate no-target) and write corresponding referring expressions. Validators then independently attempt to identify the targets based solely on the image and expression. This ensures high-quality and unambiguous annotations.
- **Multi-target Features:** Annotators were given freedom to select logically related or similar objects. This leads to expressions involving:
 - **Counting:** E.g., "The two people on the far left."
 - **Compound Sentences:** E.g., "A and B," "A except B," requiring understanding of complex long-range dependencies.
 - **Domain of Attributes:** Different objects sharing some attributes while having unique ones.
 - **Complex Relationships:** Amplified frequency and intricacy of relational descriptions.
- **No-target Features:** Rules were introduced to ensure diversity and difficulty:
 - Expressions cannot be totally irrelevant to the image, requiring semantic connection (e.g., "The kid in blue" when kids are present but none in blue).
 - Annotators could select misleading expressions from other RefCOCO images.
- **Backward Compatibility:** A subset of single-target expressions is inherited from RefCOCO, enabling comparison with classic REx methods.
- **Partitioning:** The dataset follows UNC splitting of RefCOCO (train, val, testA, testB), ensuring strict non-overlap for evaluation.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Metrics

To accurately assess performance on the generalized tasks, new metrics were introduced or adapted:
- **GRES Metrics:**
 - **cIoU and Pr@X:** Standard cumulative Intersection over Union and Precision@X, adapted to consider no-target samples as true positives if no foreground pixels are predicted.
 - **gIoU (Generalized IoU):** A new metric that calculates the mean per-image IoU, addressing the bias of cIoU towards larger objects. For true positive no-target samples, IoU is 1; for false negatives, it's 0.
 - **N-acc. (No-target accuracy) & T-acc. (Target accuracy):** Measure the model's ability to correctly identify no-target samples and the impact on target-containing samples, respectively.
- **GREC Metrics:**
 - **Precision@(F1=1, IoU≥0.5) (Pr@F1):** A new metric for instance-level bounding box prediction. A prediction is successful if it has no false positives or false negatives (F1 score of 1) at an IoU threshold of 0.5. For no-target samples, F1=1 if no boxes are predicted.
 - **N-acc. & T-acc.:** Similar to GRES, for bounding box predictions.
- **GREG Metrics:**
 - **METEOR and CIDEr:** Standard natural language generation metrics used for evaluating grammatical fluency, semantic completeness, and consensus with human references.

#### 3.3. Baseline Method: ReLA (ReLAtionship modeling block)

The proposed ReLA method, tailored for GRES and GREC, is built upon a transformer-based architecture:
- **Feature Extraction:** A Swin Transformer acts as the visual encoder (F_i), and BERT serves as the language encoder (F_t).
- **Pixel Decoder:** F_i is fed into a pixel decoder to produce mask features (F_m).
- **ReLAtionship Modeling Block:** This is the core innovation, dynamically assembling semantic-related image features for PxP regions. Unlike fixed patch-based methods, ReLA dynamically determines region shapes and sizes, and strongly links each query to a specific image region.
 - **Region-Image Cross Attention (RIA):** Takes F_i and PxP learnable Region-based Queries (Q_r) as input. It uses cross-attention to dynamically gather features for each region, creating attention maps (A_ri) that associate queries with relevant spatial areas. This allows flexible, soft aggregation of region features (F'_r), capturing sub-instance level details.
 - **Region-Language Cross Attention (RLA):** This module further refines F'_r by modeling interactions:
 - **Self-attention:** Captures dependencies between different image regions.
 - **Multi-modal Cross-attention:** Establishes relationships between regions (F'_r as Query) and linguistic content (F_t as Key/Value), generating language-aware region features (F_r2).
 - **Fused Region Feature (F_r):** F'_r, region self-attention output, and F_r2 are summed and processed by an MLP to form comprehensive interaction-aware region features.
- **Output Heads:**
 - **GRES:** Region filters (F_f) derived from F_r are multiplied with F_m to generate regional segmentation masks (M_r). The final mask (M) is a weighted aggregation of M_r using probabilities (x_r) derived from F_r.
 - **GREC:** An MLP appended to F_r predicts bounding box coordinates (b_n).
 - **Target Number Prediction ($N_O$):** A global average pooling on F_r followed by an MLP predicts the number of target objects. This is crucial for GREC to determine the number of output boxes and for no-target detection (predicting 0).
- **Multi-task Joint Training:** GRES and GREC are trained simultaneously using a combined loss function (Eq. 2), including cross-entropy for masks, minimaps, and $N_O$, and L1/GIoU loss for bounding boxes. This design allows tasks to mutually benefit and enhances efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation provides strong evidence for the necessity of GREx benchmarks and the effectiveness of the proposed ReLA method.

#### 4.1. Necessity of the gRefCOCO Dataset

- Ablation studies clearly demonstrate that classic REx models, when trained solely on single-target datasets like RefCOCO, fail to generalize to multi-target and no-target scenarios on gRefCOCO (Fig. 9). They either predict only one target for multi-target expressions or produce irrelevant masks for no-target expressions, underscoring the critical need for a dataset like gRefCOCO.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of the ReLA Method (Ablation Studies)

- **RIA Design:** Dynamic region feature aggregation through the RIA module significantly outperforms rigid image splitting or average pooling, showing gains in gIoU and Pr@F1 (Table 2). Explicit correspondence between queries and spatial image regions (via minimap supervision) is crucial, preventing queries from becoming generic learnable tokens.
- **RLA Design:** Modeling both region-word and region-region interactions within the RLA module leads to substantial performance improvements (Table 3). Compared to a basic feature fusion baseline, combining language cross-attention and region self-attention provides gains of 5.39% gIoU and 5.87% Pr@F1, highlighting the importance of explicit dependency modeling.
- **Number of Regions:** An optimal partitioning into 10x10 regions (P=10) was found to balance fine-grained attribute capture and computational resources (Table 4).
- **Importance of $N_O$ (Target Number Prediction):** The explicit prediction of the number of target objects ($N_O$) is vital, especially for GREC. Replacing $N_O$ with a binary classifier or simple thresholding leads to a significant drop in Pr@F1 for GREC (e.g., from 61.90% to 37.58%) (Table 5). For GRES, where a single mask is predicted, $N_O$'s impact is minimal, confirming its role in instance-level tasks.
- **Joint Training:** Multi-task joint training of GRES and GREC shows comparable or slightly boosted performance compared to single-task variants (Table 6), demonstrating that the tasks can mutually benefit within the unified ReLA framework.
- **Computational Efficiency:** ReLA introduces only a modest increase in parameters and inference time while achieving significant performance gains, confirming its practical applicability (Table 7).

#### 4.3. GRES Results

- ReLA significantly outperforms re-implemented state-of-the-art classic RES methods (MattNet, VLT, LAVT) on the gRefCOCO dataset, particularly benefiting from its explicit relationship modeling (Table 9). For example, adding ReLA to LAVT improves cIoU by over 4% on the val set.
- In no-target identification, ReLA achieves better N-acc. (56.29% on val set) compared to classic RES methods (Table 10). However, the remaining ~40% missed no-target samples highlight an ongoing challenge, possibly due to deceptive expressions very similar to real instances.
- ReLA also sets new state-of-the-art results on classic RES benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, G-Ref) when trained solely on those datasets, demonstrating its general effectiveness beyond GREx (Tables 11 and 12).

#### 4.4. GREC Results

- Classic REC methods, designed for single-target scenarios, struggle significantly on GREC, exhibiting poor Pr@F1 scores even after adaptation (Table 14).
- The proposed $N_O$-based output strategy for GREC (predicting the number of boxes) is shown to be superior to adaptive thresholding or Top-k strategies, which suffer from performance sensitivity or inherent limitations in multi-target/no-target scenarios (Table 13, Fig. 13).
- ReLA achieves strong performance on GREC on gRefCOCO (Table 14), with the Swin-B variant achieving 61.90% Pr@F1 on the val set.
- Qualitative analysis of failure cases (Fig. 14) reveals remaining challenges related to highly misleading expressions, distinguishing subtle attributes, and precisely locating multiple instances to satisfy IoU thresholds.

#### 4.5. GREG Results

- Evaluation on GREG reveals that both classic REG methods and advanced Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) experience a marked performance drop when transitioning from single-target to multi-target expressions (Table 15).
- While zero-shot MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-7B show better METEOR scores (indicating fluency), their CIDEr scores (indicating alignment with ground truth) are only slightly better than classic REG methods, suggesting they struggle with grounding complex multi-object visual semantics.
- Failure cases (Fig. 15) illustrate that models often fail to capture the shared semantics of selected object sets, generate generic descriptions, or mistakenly describe distractors, highlighting the need for dedicated modeling of compositionality and group-level reasoning.

#### 4.6. Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS)

- With minor adaptations, ReLA demonstrates its versatility by achieving new state-of-the-art results on RVOS benchmarks (MeViS and Ref-YouTube-VOS), showing its ability to capture spatiotemporal object movements effectively (Table 16).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GREx paper introduces a fundamental shift in how referring expression tasks are conceived and evaluated, moving beyond the restrictive "one expression, one object" paradigm to embrace the complexities of real-world language and vision interactions. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enabling Real-World Applications:** By supporting multi-target and no-target expressions, GREx makes RES, REC, and REG tasks far more practical and robust for applications such as advanced human-machine interfaces, intelligent image/video editing, comprehensive visual content generation, and sophisticated visual search where users naturally refer to groups of objects or inquire about the absence of an item.
2. **New Research Directions and Benchmarks:** The introduction of the GREx benchmarks (GRES, GREC, GREG) and the accompanying large-scale gRefCOCO dataset provides a critical foundation for future research. It systematically exposes the limitations of existing methods and poses new, challenging problems for the community, stimulating innovation in vision-language modeling. The gRefCOCO dataset, in particular, is noted for pioneering support for expressions referring to an arbitrary number of objects with rich, realistic annotations, serving as a crucial resource for MLLMs, diffusion models, and zero-shot learning as already evidenced by subsequent works citing it.
3. **Advancing Vision-Language Understanding:** The proposed ReLA baseline method, with its novel Region-Image Cross Attention (RIA) and Region-Language Cross Attention (RLA) mechanisms, demonstrates the importance of explicitly modeling fine-grained sub-instance attributes and complex region-region and region-language relationships. This methodological contribution provides valuable insights into how models can better integrate visual and linguistic cues for nuanced comprehension.
4. **Unified and Efficient Frameworks:** The multi-task joint training strategy for GRES and GREC within ReLA highlights the potential for developing unified, efficient models that can handle diverse referring expression tasks without requiring separate architectures or extensive additional data. This promotes resource efficiency and simplifies model deployment.
5. **Identifying Key Challenges:** The paper meticulously analyzes the challenges inherent in GREx, particularly the significant performance drops observed for multi-target GREG and the ongoing difficulties in robustly handling deceptive no-target expressions. These identified challenges serve as clear signposts for future research, encouraging focused efforts on set-aware representation learning, contrastive learning, multi-instance-aware decoding, and better integration of commonsense knowledge from LLMs.
6. **Broader Impact on Multimodal AI:** The work contributes to the broader goal of generalized AI perception by pushing models to interpret more flexible and natural language inputs. Its extension to Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS) further demonstrates the versatility of the proposed approach, hinting at its applicability across various multimodal tasks.

In summary, GREx represents a significant leap forward in the field of referring expressions, transforming tasks from constrained academic exercises into powerful tools capable of tackling the inherent complexities and natural variations of human language in real-world visual contexts.
