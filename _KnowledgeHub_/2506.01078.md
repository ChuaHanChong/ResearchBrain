# GThinker: Towards General Multimodal Reasoning via Cue-Guided Rethinking

> [!summary] Summary
> GThinker proposes a "Cue-Rethinking" pattern that allows Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to actively re-interpret visual cues and revise their reasoning paths for general vision-centric tasks. The 7B parameter model achieves 81.5% average accuracy on the M3CoT benchmark, outperforming larger closed-source models and showing improved performance on MMStar (+2.5%) and MathVista (+4.5 points).

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) over-rely on rigid Chain-of-Thought (CoT) structures, which are effective for structured academic problems but fail to adapt to diverse general reasoning scenarios.
- MLLMs often make inferences without sufficient visual grounding, leading to errors when visual cues are ambiguous, incomplete, or contradictory.
- Existing models lack self-correction mechanisms, proceeding with flawed reasoning without revising their interpretation of visual information or their reasoning paths.

## Method

- **GThinker** introduces a novel "==Cue-Rethinking== Pattern" that enables models to actively re-interpret visual cues and revise their reasoning, involving initial free-form reasoning, a rethinking trigger, and systematic cue-based revision.
- The model employs a two-stage training pipeline: a "Pattern-Guided Cold Start" stage uses supervised fine-tuning on annotated data to teach the Cue-Rethinking pattern, followed by an "Incentive Reinforcement Learning" stage using the DAPO algorithm for generalization.
- A new high-quality dataset, GThinker-11K, was constructed with 7,000 samples for cold start (including 'negative reasoning' annotations for flawed responses) and 4,000 samples for reinforcement learning, generated through multimodal iterative annotation and automatic verification.

## Results

- GThinker-**7B** achieved an **81.5%** average accuracy on the M3CoT benchmark, outperforming the closed-source O4-mini model and demonstrating superior performance on 8 out of 9 subsets.
- The model showed an average improvement of **2.1%** on general scenarios, specifically reaching **66.4%** on MMStar (+**2.5%**) and **70.1%** on RealWorld QA (+**1.6%**).
- Performance on structured domains also improved, with **72.7%** on MathVista (+4.5 points), **26.6%** on MathVision (+1.5 points), and **40.7%** on MMMU-Pro (approximately +4 points improvement).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active re-interpretation and revision of visual cues are crucial for enabling flexible and general vision-centric reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models.
> - A two-stage training approach combining supervised pattern learning with outcome-reward reinforcement learning effectively teaches and generalizes complex multimodal reasoning capabilities.
> - High-quality, meticulously annotated datasets, especially those incorporating 'negative reasoning' (flawed cue identification), are essential for developing robust self-correction mechanisms in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) excel at structured reasoning tasks like mathematics and science problems, where Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches can systematically break down problems into logical steps. However, these models struggle significantly with general vision-centric reasoning tasks that require flexible interpretation of visual cues and the ability to revise their understanding when visual information is ambiguous or contradictory.

GThinker addresses this limitation by introducing a novel "Cue-Rethinking" pattern that enables models to actively re-interpret visual cues and revise their reasoning paths. This approach bridges the gap between the structured thinking that works well for academic problems and the flexible, visually-grounded reasoning required for real-world scenarios.

### 2. The Problem with Current Multimodal Reasoning

Current MLLMs face several key challenges in general multimodal reasoning:

1. **Over-reliance on structured patterns**: Existing models apply rigid CoT structures that work well for math and science but fail to adapt to the diverse patterns required for general scenarios.

2. **Insufficient visual grounding**: Models often make inferences without properly grounding them in visual information, leading to errors when visual cues are misleading or incomplete.

3. **Lack of self-correction mechanisms**: Unlike humans who naturally re-examine visual information when encountering inconsistencies, current models proceed with flawed reasoning without revision.

4. **Limited applicability of reinforcement learning**: While RL has been successful in enhancing reasoning for math/science problems with clear correct answers, it struggles with general scenarios where rewards are harder to define and process reward models are difficult to construct.

### 3. The Cue-Rethinking Approach

The core innovation of GThinker is the **Cue-Rethinking Pattern**, which consists of three key stages:

#### 3.1. Initial Reasoning

The model begins with free-form reasoning about the question and image, explicitly tagging any referenced visual cues using `<vcues_*>` tags. This stage allows for flexible application of various reasoning strategies without rigid structural constraints.

#### 3.2. Cue-Rethinking Trigger

After initial reasoning, a prompt triggers the rethinking phase: "Let's check each visual cue and corresponding reasoning before reaching the final answer." Importantly, this trigger occurs after the initial reasoning is complete, preserving natural thought flow.

#### 3.3. Cue-Based Rethinking

The model systematically revisits all previously marked visual cues. If inconsistencies or errors are identified in the visual interpretation or subsequent inferences, the model revises the problematic cues and re-engages in reasoning based on the corrected visual information.

### 4. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

GThinker employs a sophisticated two-stage training approach that combines supervised learning with reinforcement learning:

#### 4.1. Stage 1: Pattern-Guided Cold Start

This stage uses supervised fine-tuning to explicitly teach the model the Cue-Rethinking pattern:

- **Pattern-Guided Selective Formatting**: Samples with flawed visual cues are formatted for full Cue-Rethinking, while others use free-form reasoning. This teaches the model to adaptively perform reasoning or rethinking as needed.

- **Supervised Fine-tuning**: The model learns from 7,000 high-quality annotated samples that demonstrate the Cue-Rethinking pattern across diverse domains.

#### 4.2. Stage 2: Incentive Reinforcement Learning

Following the cold start, this stage uses outcome-reward RL to generalize capabilities:

- **Algorithm**: Uses Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO), which is particularly effective for long-chain reasoning tasks.

- **Hybrid Reward Design**: Accommodates different task types:
 - Multiple-choice: Exact answer matching
 - Math problems: Math-Verify for numeric/symbolic answers
 - Open-ended tasks: Guided summarization for matching
 - Format reward: Ensures adherence to think-answer structure

### 5. Dataset Construction

A critical contribution is the creation of **GThinker-11K**, a high-quality dataset comprising:

#### 5.1. Cold Start Data (7K samples)

- **Multimodal Iterative Annotation**: Uses multiple advanced MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GPT-4o, O1, O3) in an iterative process to generate coherent, long-chain reasoning paths with rich visual cues.

- **Negative Reasoning Annotation**: Collects naturally flawed responses from smaller models, then uses O3 to identify and correct missing, uncertain, or misleading visual cues.

- **Automatic Verification**: Uses Gemini 2.5 Pro to verify format compliance, answer consistency, and reasoning coherence.

#### 5.2. Reinforcement Learning Data (4K samples)

Additional diverse samples curated from public datasets to enhance generalization during the RL stage.

### 6. Experimental Results

GThinker-7B achieves impressive performance across multiple benchmarks:

#### 6.1. Overall Performance

- **M3CoT**: 81.5% average accuracy, outperforming the closed-source O4-mini model and excelling on 8 out of 9 subsets
- **General scenarios**: 2.1% average improvement, with 66.4% on MMStar (+2.5%) and 70.1% on RealWorld QA (+1.6%)
- **Mathematics**: 72.7% on MathVista (+4.5 points) and 26.6% on MathVision (+1.5 points)
- **Science**: 40.7% on MMMU-Pro (approximately +4 points improvement)

#### 6.2. Key Findings

1. **Balanced Performance**: GThinker maintains strong performance across structured domains while significantly improving on general scenarios
2. **Efficiency**: Achieves superior results with a 7B parameter model compared to much larger closed-source alternatives
3. **Component Validation**: Ablation studies confirm the critical importance of both high-quality data annotation and the two-stage training approach

### 7. Impact and Significance

GThinker represents a significant advancement in multimodal reasoning with several key contributions:

#### 7.1. Technical Contributions

- **Novel Reasoning Pattern**: The Cue-Rethinking pattern provides a flexible framework that combines structured thinking with adaptive visual re-interpretation
- **Effective Training Pipeline**: The two-stage approach successfully bridges supervised learning and reinforcement learning for complex reasoning tasks
- **High-Quality Dataset**: GThinker-11K addresses a critical data gap for general multimodal reasoning research

#### 7.2. Broader Impact

- **Towards General AI**: Enables more adaptable AI systems that can handle diverse, real-world visual reasoning scenarios
- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction**: Models with better visual understanding and self-correction capabilities can provide more reliable assistance
- **Cross-Domain Applications**: Potential applications in robotics, medical imaging, education, and content analysis

#### 7.3. Research Directions

GThinker opens several avenues for future work:
- Development of more sophisticated visual cue identification mechanisms
- Extension to other modalities beyond vision and text
- Investigation of other forms of reflective reasoning patterns
- Creation of more comprehensive multimodal reasoning benchmarks

The work demonstrates that with appropriate training methodologies and high-quality data, relatively compact models can achieve remarkable performance in complex multimodal reasoning tasks, making advanced AI capabilities more accessible and practical for real-world deployment.
