# MARCUS: An agentic, multimodal vision-language model for cardiac diagnosis and management

> [!summary] Summary
> MARCUS, an agentic multimodal vision-language model developed by researchers at Stanford and UCSF, interprets raw ECGs, echocardiograms, and cardiac MRI data for cardiac diagnosis and management. It demonstrated diagnostic accuracies up to 91% for single-modality tasks and 70% for multimodal tasks, outperforming frontier models, while also effectively eliminating mirage reasoning through an inference-time confidence-assessment protocol.

## Problem

- Cardiovascular disease diagnosis relies on time-intensive interpretation of complex, multimodal tests (ECG, echocardiogram, CMR), leading to clinical bottlenecks and requiring specialized expertise amidst workforce shortages.
- Existing AI models for cardiology are often limited to single-modality inputs, are primarily text-based, or susceptible to 'mirage reasoning', which restricts their clinical utility and reliability in complex diagnostic scenarios.
- There is a need for scalable, timely, and accurate AI tools that can synthesize information from multiple diagnostic modalities and engage in interactive clinical reasoning, ensuring outputs are genuinely grounded in visual evidence.

## Method

- Developed **MARCUS**, a generative ==agentic vision-language model== with a ==hierarchical architecture== that includes modality-specific expert models (for ECG, echocardiography, CMR) coordinated by a ==multimodal orchestrating agent==.
- Trained the system on a large corpus of 13.5 million multimodal cardiac images from Stanford University, paired with physician text reports, utilizing a multi-stage pipeline involving ==pre-training==, ==supervised fine-tuning==, and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- Implemented a ==confidence-assessment protocol== within the orchestrator that uses ==counterfactual probing== (rephrased and image-absent queries) to detect and mitigate 'mirage reasoning' during inference.

## Results

- MARCUS achieved diagnostic accuracies up to **91%** for single-modality tasks (ECG) and **70%** for complex multimodal tasks, consistently outperforming frontier general-purpose VLMs by **34-45** percentage points on single-modality tasks and almost threefold on multimodal assessments (P < 0.001).
- The agentic orchestrator successfully identified all instances of mirage reasoning, resulting in a **0%** mirage rate for MARCUS across all modalities and test cohorts, without suppressing correctly grounded responses.
- The system consistently generated responses of higher clinical value in open-ended clinical reasoning tasks (mean Likert scores up to **3.65** vs. 1.47-2.60 for frontier models) and demonstrated robust generalizability across internal and external validation cohorts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular, agentic architecture coordinating modality-specific expert models through natural language enables robust multimodal synthesis and interactive clinical reasoning, closely mimicking human diagnostic workflows.
> - Training on extensive, high-fidelity, domain-specific clinical data is crucial for achieving superior performance in medical AI, significantly outperforming general-purpose frontier vision-language models.
> - Inference-time verification through counterfactual probing and confidence assessment can effectively mitigate 'mirage reasoning' in vision-language models, a critical safety feature for clinical applications.
