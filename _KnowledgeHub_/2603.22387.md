# Efficient Universal Perception Encoder

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta Reality Labs and FAIR introduce the Efficient Universal Perception Encoder (EUPE), a family of compact models capable of universally strong performance across image understanding, vision-language modeling, and dense prediction tasks. It employs a "scaling up, then scaling down" distillation strategy, where a large proxy model first unifies knowledge from multiple domain experts, then distills it to efficient student encoders, outperforming prior agglomerative methods and matching or exceeding domain-specific experts.

## Problem

- Individual foundation vision encoders typically specialize in limited task domains, leading to suboptimal performance or the necessity of deploying multiple models.
- Deploying multiple specialized encoders or large foundation models on resource-constrained edge devices is computationally impractical due to limited compute, memory, and energy.
- Existing multi-teacher knowledge distillation methods struggle to effectively transfer diverse knowledge to efficient backbone architectures, resulting in performance gaps for compact models.

## Method

- A novel three-stage =="scaling up, then scaling down"== ==distillation pipeline== is proposed to effectively transfer universal knowledge to efficient student encoders.
- First, a large ==proxy teacher model== (e.g., **ViT-G**) aggregates and unifies diverse knowledge from multiple domain-expert foundation models (e.g., **PEcore-G**, **DINOv3-H+**, **PElang-G**).
- Subsequently, this single, unified proxy teacher distills its comprehensive knowledge into smaller, efficient student encoders (e.g., **ViT-B/S/T**, **ConvNeXt** family), followed by a ==multi-resolution finetuning== stage to enhance resolution robustness.

## Results

- **EUPE-ViT-B** achieves performance on-par with or superior to individual domain-expert encoders of the same size across image understanding, vision-language modeling, and dense prediction benchmarks.
- EUPE models consistently outperform previous agglomerative encoders (e.g., **RADIOv2.5-B**, **DUNE-B**) across most VLM and dense prediction benchmarks, often by substantial margins.
- The methodology successfully creates a family of efficient models (**ViT** and **ConvNeXt** from Tiny to Base) that demonstrate balanced universal performance, with notable improvements in dense prediction for tiny models and enhanced VLM capabilities for ConvNeXt variants.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient student models, due to their limited capacity, more effectively absorb and unify diverse feature representations when learning from a single, already unified large proxy teacher rather than directly from multiple disparate foundation teachers.
> - A multi-stage distillation approach that includes both fixed-resolution training for general knowledge transfer and multi-resolution finetuning for spatial robustness is crucial for balanced performance across diverse vision tasks.
> - Careful selection of the teacher foundation models for the proxy's distillation stage is vital for maximizing multi-task robustness, particularly for vision-language modeling capabilities like OCR.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has witnessed significant advancements driven by foundation vision encoders, which leverage sophisticated architectures like convolutional neural networks (e.g., ResNet, ConvNeXt) and vision transformers (e.g., ViT, Swin Transformer). These models are trained using diverse paradigms, including full supervision, weak supervision on text-image pairs (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP), and self-supervision (e.g., DINOv2, MAE). The representations learned by these models are transferable to a wide array of downstream vision tasks, spanning classical image understanding (classification, retrieval), dense prediction (segmentation, depth estimation, keypoint correspondence), and more recently, vision-language modeling (VLM) tasks.

Despite their individual strengths, a notable characteristic of most single foundation vision encoders is their specialization. Models like CLIP or SigLIP, trained on text-image pairs, typically excel in image understanding and VLM but often exhibit suboptimal performance on dense prediction tasks. Conversely, self-supervised models such as DINO and the Segment Anything Model (SAM) demonstrate proficiency in dense prediction but may lack satisfactory vision-language capabilities. This domain specificity necessitates careful selection of an encoder for a given application or the deployment of multiple encoders for multi-domain use cases, leading to increased computational overhead or performance compromises.

Addressing this limitation, prior research has explored methods to create more versatile or "universal" encoders. The Perception Encoder (PE) by Bolya et al. (2025) introduced alignment tuning for intermediate layers, resulting in variations specialized for image understanding, dense prediction, and VLM. However, these still represent distinct task-specific models. Agglomerative methods, such as RADIO (Ranzinger et al., 2024; Heinrich et al., 2025) and DUNE (Sarıyıldız et al., 2025), have attempted to synthesize knowledge from multiple expert teacher models into a single student encoder through label-free knowledge distillation. While these methods have shown promise for larger student models, the current work identifies a performance limitation when applied to efficient backbones, where they often fail to match the performance of individual domain experts.

This research positions itself within this landscape by focusing on the development of an *efficient* universal perception encoder. The motivation stems from the increasing demand for deploying AI models on smart edge devices, which are characterized by limited computational resources and often require simultaneous handling of multiple tasks. The paper argues that existing agglomerative techniques are insufficient for this context, necessitating a novel approach to knowledge distillation that effectively imbues small, efficient models with universal capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **Efficient Universal Perception Encoder (EUPE)** that simultaneously achieves high inference efficiency and provides universally robust feature representations for a diverse range of downstream computer vision tasks. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Domain Specificity of Foundation Models:** Current foundation vision encoders typically specialize in one or two task domains, such as image understanding, dense prediction, or vision-language modeling. This specialization forces downstream applications to either select a suboptimal encoder for tasks outside its primary domain or deploy multiple encoders, which is computationally expensive. The goal is to overcome this limitation by creating a single encoder that performs well across domains.

2. **Enabling Multi-Tasking on Edge Devices:** Smart edge devices are integral to many modern user experiences, but they operate under stringent computational constraints. Running multiple specialized AI models for different tasks (e.g., simultaneously performing object detection, depth estimation, and visual question answering) on such devices is often infeasible due to limited memory and processing power. A universal encoder is motivated by the need to enable versatile multi-task AI capabilities within these constrained environments.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Agglomerative Methods for Efficient Models:** Previous approaches to create universal encoders, particularly agglomerative methods like RADIO, involve distilling knowledge from multiple powerful teacher models into a single student. However, the authors observe that these methods exhibit "clear limitations when applying it to efficient backbones." Specifically, efficient student encoders trained with these methods show significant performance gaps compared to domain-expert models of similar size, especially on dense prediction and VLM tasks. This suggests that smaller models lack the inherent capacity to directly absorb and unify diverse feature representations from multiple large, domain-specific teachers.

4. **Proposing a Novel Distillation Principle:** The core motivation behind the EUPE methodology is the hypothesis that for efficient encoders to achieve universal capability, a direct "scaling down" from multiple diverse teachers is insufficient. Instead, the authors propose an indirect approach: "first scaling up and then scaling down." This principle suggests that a heavy, high-capacity "proxy teacher" is necessary to first unify the knowledge from various foundation teachers, and only then should this consolidated knowledge be distilled into the efficient student encoder. This multi-stage approach is intended to facilitate the effective transfer of complex, multi-domain representations to smaller models.

In summary, the research aims to provide a practical solution for deploying advanced, versatile AI on ubiquitous edge devices by developing an efficient, universally capable vision encoder through a carefully designed knowledge distillation pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Efficient Universal Perception Encoder (EUPE) is developed through a multi-stage knowledge distillation pipeline, founded on the principle of "scaling up, then scaling down." This pipeline simplifies the distillation process to focus on the efficacy of this principle.

**Pipeline Overview (Three Stages):**

1. **Stage 1: Multi-Teacher Distillation into a Large Proxy Model.**
 - **Objective:** To aggregate diverse knowledge from multiple domain-expert foundation models into a single, high-capacity "proxy model." This proxy model, typically a heavy model (e.g., 1.9B parameters), is selected for its capacity to learn universally good representations.
 - **Teachers:** Representative foundation encoders are chosen from different task domains:
 - **PEcore-G (1.9B):** Expert in zero-shot image classification and retrieval.
 - **DINOv3-H+ (840M):** Expert in dense prediction.
 - **PElang-G (1.7B):** Critical for vision-language modeling.
 - **Process:** Label-free images are fed in parallel to all teachers at their native resolutions and to the proxy model. The proxy model's class and patch tokens are compared with those from each teacher to compute distillation losses.
 - **Output:** A large proxy model capable of unifying representations from various domains.

2. **Stage 2: Fixed-Resolution Distillation from the Proxy Model to an Efficient Student Encoder.**
 - **Objective:** To transfer the universal knowledge consolidated by the proxy model into the target efficient encoder. The hypothesis is that learning from a single, unified proxy teacher is more effective for capacity-limited efficient encoders than directly learning from multiple disparate domain experts.
 - **Initialization:** The efficient student encoder is initialized from scratch.
 - **Resolution:** Training is conducted at a fixed input resolution (256x256). This choice aims for computational efficiency, enabling a longer learning schedule.
 - **Efficient Student Architectures:** The target efficient encoders include Vision Transformers (ViT-B, ViT-S, ViT-T) and ConvNeXt models (ConvNeXt-Base, ConvNeXt-Small, ConvNeXt-Tiny), typically with fewer than 100M parameters.

3. **Stage 3: Multi-Resolution Finetuning from the Proxy Model to the Efficient Student Encoder.**
 - **Objective:** To enhance the student encoder's adaptability to downstream tasks requiring various input resolutions and to learn representations at different granularities.
 - **Initialization:** The student encoder is initialized using the checkpoint obtained from Stage 2.
 - **Resolution:** An image pyramid (e.g., 256, 384, 512) is used. Both the student and the proxy teacher randomly select a scale from this pyramid independently during each iteration.
 - **Schedule:** This stage employs a shorter learning schedule compared to Stage 2 due to the higher computational cost associated with multi-resolution inputs.

**Distillation Flow (Applicable across all stages):**

The core distillation mechanism involves comparing the outputs of the student encoder with those of the teacher (or proxy model).
- **Outputs:** Both student `S(x_S; θ)` and teacher `T_i(x_T_i; ϕ_i)` produce a class token (y_c) and patch tokens (y_p).
- **Adapter Heads:** To bridge potential differences in feature dimensions, 2-layer MLP adapter heads (`H_c`, `H_p`) are appended to the student's outputs, projecting them into the teacher's feature space.
- **Spatial Alignment:** For patch tokens, 2D interpolation is used to match spatial resolutions between student and teacher outputs.
- **Distillation Loss:** The loss `L_i` for each teacher `i` is a sum of class token loss `L_c_i` and patch token loss `L_p_i`.
- **Parameter Updates:** Student parameters (`θ`) and adapter head parameters (`ψ`) are learnable, while teacher/proxy parameters (`ϕ`) are frozen.

**Loss Function:**
- **Class Token Loss:** Defined as the cosine similarity loss between the adapted student class token and the normalized teacher/proxy class token.
- **Patch Token Loss:** A weighted combination of cosine similarity loss (weight α=0.9) and smooth L1 loss (weight β=0.1) for the adapted student patch tokens and normalized teacher/proxy patch tokens.
- **Total Loss:** The overall distillation loss `L` is the sum of `L_i` for all teachers (Stage 1) or the single proxy (Stages 2 & 3).

**Feature Normalization:**
Teacher/proxy outputs are normalized (mean subtraction and standard deviation division) before loss computation. This technique, found effective for stabilizing feature distillation, is performed by computing statistics on a small batch of training data and then fixing them for the remainder of training, which is different from on-the-fly normalization for scalability.

**Training Data:**
All stages utilize the DINOv3 dataset (Siméoni et al., 2025), which comprises LVD-1689M and ImageNet1k. A data sampling strategy with a 10% probability of drawing from ImageNet1k (for homogeneous batches) is employed. Data augmentation techniques (random resized cropping, horizontal flipping, color jittering, Gaussian blur, random solarization) are applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EUPE-ViT-B and other EUPE family models demonstrates their performance across three primary task domains: image understanding, vision-language modeling (VLM), and dense prediction.

**Overall Performance of EUPE-ViT-B:**
EUPE-ViT-B, with its ViT-B backbone, demonstrates a balanced performance profile, achieving results that are on-par with or superior to individual domain-expert models of comparable size. It also consistently outperforms existing agglomerative methods designed for efficient backbones.

**Comparison with Agglomerative Methods:**
- EUPE-ViT-B exhibits a performance advantage over RADIOv2.5-B and DUNE-B across all VLM benchmarks (TextVQA, SQA, Realworld, POPE, GQA, MMEp) and most dense prediction tasks (SPair, ADE20k). A marginal gap exists with DUNE-B on NYUv2 depth estimation.

**Comparison with Domain Experts:**
- **Image Understanding (IN1k-ZS, IN1k-KNN):** EUPE-ViT-B achieves performance comparable to leading image understanding experts such as PEcore-B, SigLIP2-B, and DINOv3-ViT-B.
- **Dense Prediction (SPair, NYUv2, ADE20k):** EUPE-ViT-B matches or exceeds the performance of the dense prediction expert DINOv3-ViT-B, notably outperforming it on ADE20k segmentation and achieving comparable results on SPair. Its performance on NYUv2 is marginally below the best domain expert.
- **Vision-Language Modeling (TextVQA, SQA, Realworld, POPE, GQA, MMEp):** EUPE-ViT-B surpasses VLM experts like PEcore-B and SigLIP2-B on Realworld, GQA, and POPE, while maintaining comparable performance on TextVQA, SQA, and MMEp.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Necessity of Stages (Table 2):**
 - **Stage 2 Only:** Direct distillation from multiple teachers to the efficient student results in weaker VLM performance and poor dense prediction.
 - **Stage 1 & 2:** Incorporating the proxy model (Stage 1) significantly improves VLM tasks (e.g., TextVQA, Realworld), but dense prediction still lags behind the full pipeline.
 - **Stage 1 & 3:** Performing multi-resolution distillation after Stage 1 yields strong dense prediction performance (e.g., SPair, NYUv2) but results in significant gaps in VLM metrics. This setup is also computationally intensive for extended training.
 - **Full Pipeline (Stage 1 & 2 & 3):** This configuration achieves the best overall balance, enhancing VLM metrics without substantially compromising image understanding or dense prediction performance.

- **Choice of Teacher Foundation Models (Table 3):**
 - Combining PEcore-G and DINOv3-H+ provides good image understanding and dense prediction but poor VLM OCR performance.
 - Adding SigLIP2-G, an expert in VLM, unexpectedly degrades OCR metrics when combined with PEcore-G and DINOv3-H+, suggesting potential feature incompatibility between two CLIP-style models (PEcore-G and SigLIP2-G).
 - Adding PElang-G, a language-focused model derived from PEcore-G, proves to be a complementary choice. It significantly improves OCR and general VLM performance without detrimental effects on image understanding or dense prediction, leading to its selection as part of the default teacher set.

- **Performance of Proxy Models (Table 4):**
 - The performance of various proxy models confirms that PEcore-G excels in VLM and DINOv3-H+ in dense prediction.
 - The combination of PEcore-G, PElang-G, and DINOv3-H+ creates a proxy model with a robust foundation across all three task domains, particularly benefiting VLM tasks with PElang-G. These proxy performance trends largely align with the final student encoder results, indicating effective knowledge transfer.

- **Impact of Scaling Up Teachers and Proxy (Appendix A, Tables 7 & 8):**
 - Scaling both the DINOv3 teacher and the proxy model to 7B parameters improves proxy performance on most benchmarks.
 - However, distilling this 7B proxy into a ViT-B student shows mixed results: slight improvements in image understanding and dense prediction but a general degradation in VLM quality. This suggests the substantial size difference (7B proxy to 86M student) might impede full knowledge distillation, prompting consideration of progressive distillation in future work.

- **Impact of Datamix (Appendix A, Table 9):** Training on LVD-1689M yields better performance across almost all benchmarks compared to MetaCLIP, despite MetaCLIP having more images, suggesting LVD's higher data quality.

- **Impact of Patch Loss Weight for DINOv3 (Appendix A, Table 10):**
 - Higher patch loss weight for DINOv3 improves image understanding and dense prediction but negatively impacts VLM.
 - Ignoring DINOv3's patch tokens (weight = 0.0) significantly degrades dense prediction.
 - A balanced weight (1.0, same as other teachers) is chosen for overall robust performance.

**Feature Visualization (Figures 1, 4, 5):**
Qualitative visualization of PCA-projected dense patch tokens demonstrates that EUPE models generate features that simultaneously exhibit semantic coherence, fine-grained detail, complex spatial awareness, and text-awareness. In contrast, image-text trained models (PEcore, SigLIP2) show noisy features; DINOv3 has sharp but less fine-grained features; and other agglomerative models (RADIO, DUNE) either lack semantic coherence or fine-grained discrimination. Ablation visualizations confirm that the full three-stage pipeline produces sharper and more semantically coherent features than partial pipelines.

**Full Family of EUPE Models (Tables 5 & 6):**
The EUPE family includes ViT (Tiny, Small, Base) and ConvNeXt (Tiny, Small, Base) variants. These models offer a range of sizes for different computational budgets, consistently providing balanced performance. Notably, smaller EUPE ViT and ConvNeXt models show significant gains in dense prediction and improved VLM capabilities compared to existing domain-expert counterparts of similar size.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on the Efficient Universal Perception Encoder (EUPE) presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of computer vision and its applications:

1. **Novel Distillation Paradigm for Efficient Models:** The core methodological contribution is the introduction of a "scaling up, then scaling down" distillation pipeline. This approach addresses a critical challenge in knowledge distillation for efficient models, specifically their limited capacity to directly assimilate knowledge from multiple, diverse expert teachers. By leveraging a large proxy model to first aggregate and unify this knowledge, the EUPE method enables effective knowledge transfer to compact student models, a capability that prior agglomerative methods struggled to achieve at small scales. This paradigm shift could influence future research in efficient multi-teacher distillation.

2. **Creation of Versatile and Efficient Vision Encoders:** EUPE provides a family of models (ViT and ConvNeXt variants) that offer universally strong and balanced performance across disparate computer vision tasks, including image understanding, dense prediction, and vision-language modeling. This is achieved without task-specific finetuning, demonstrating a high degree of zero-shot transferability. The availability of models spanning various parameter counts and inference costs allows for practical deployment across a spectrum of computational budgets.

3. **Enabling Advanced AI on Edge Devices:** The primary application impact of EUPE lies in empowering sophisticated, multi-task AI experiences on smart edge devices. These devices typically have severe constraints on compute, memory, and power. EUPE's efficiency combined with its universal perception capabilities makes it suitable for scenarios where a single model needs to handle multiple perceptual tasks simultaneously, thereby expanding the potential for on-device AI in areas like augmented reality, robotics, and smart home systems. It mitigates the need for either sacrificing performance by relying on a single, suboptimal specialized model or incurring prohibitive costs by deploying multiple task-specific models.

4. **Practical Baseline and Resource for Future Research:** By releasing the full family of EUPE models and the associated code, the authors establish a practical and robust baseline for future research. This provides a readily available resource for researchers to explore advancements in proxy teacher scaling, investigate more sophisticated distillation techniques (e.g., progressive distillation for larger teacher-student capacity gaps), and further improve universal representations for efficient and multi-task settings. The detailed ablation studies also offer insights into the crucial design choices, such as teacher selection, training stages, and hyperparameter tuning, which can guide subsequent work.

5. **Qualitative Improvement in Feature Representations:** The feature visualizations indicate that EUPE produces qualitatively superior and more balanced dense feature representations compared to existing models. These features simultaneously capture semantic coherence, fine-grained details, complex spatial structures, and text-awareness, which is essential for robust performance across diverse tasks. This qualitative improvement underscores the effectiveness of the proposed distillation strategy in learning a rich and versatile visual understanding.
