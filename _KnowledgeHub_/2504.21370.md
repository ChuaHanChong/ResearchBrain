# ShorterBetter: Guiding Reasoning Models to Find Optimal Inference Length for Efficient Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The "ShorterBetter" method introduces a reinforcement learning framework that guides large language models to identify an optimal inference length for efficient reasoning. This approach effectively reduces output token length by 50-80% across diverse reasoning and coding tasks while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate excessively long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces for complex reasoning, leading to a phenomenon called "overthinking."
- This verbosity results in substantial computational inefficiencies, including higher inference costs, increased latency, and greater energy consumption.
- Beyond inefficiency, excessive reasoning can degrade model performance by introducing redundancy, irrelevant information, or compounding errors, often observed as a U-shaped performance curve.

## Method

- Propose "**ShorterBetter**," a novel reinforcement learning (RL) method that leverages a ==dynamic reward signal== to guide reasoning models toward efficient inference lengths.
- Define an Optimal Reasoning Length (OL) and approximate it with Sample Optimal Length (SOL), which identifies the shortest correct response length among a group of rollouts or averages lengths if no correct response is found.
- Optimize the model's policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a reward function that balances correctness and deviation from the calculated SOL.

## Results

- Achieved significant output token length reductions of 50-**80%** across various in-domain math, general reasoning, and coding benchmarks for both **1.5B** and **7B** models.
- Maintained or improved accuracy, with average accuracy gains of +**2.5%** to +**7.1%** on in-domain tasks, while minimizing accuracy drops on out-of-domain tasks.
- Consistently attained the highest Accuracy-Efficiency (AE) scores compared to other length-reduction baselines like Training Efficient and O1-Pruner, demonstrating superior balance between conciseness and correctness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An intrinsic optimal reasoning length exists for each problem, dependent on model capacity and difficulty, which balances the need for sufficient analysis with conciseness.
> - The Sample Optimal Length (SOL) provides an effective, data-driven approximation of this unobservable optimal length, enabling self-supervised learning for efficient reasoning without external length constraints.
> - Reasoning trace analysis reveals that ShorterBetter refines CoT outputs by increasing the proportion of pivotal reasoning steps and reducing non-substantive statements, unnecessary verification, and over-exploration.
