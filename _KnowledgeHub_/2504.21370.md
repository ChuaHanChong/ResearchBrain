# ShorterBetter: Guiding Reasoning Models to Find Optimal Inference Length for Efficient Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The "ShorterBetter" method introduces a reinforcement learning framework that guides large language models to identify an optimal inference length for efficient reasoning. This approach effectively reduces output token length by 50-80% across diverse reasoning and coding tasks while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate excessively long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces for complex reasoning, leading to a phenomenon called "overthinking."
- This verbosity results in substantial computational inefficiencies, including higher inference costs, increased latency, and greater energy consumption.
- Beyond inefficiency, excessive reasoning can degrade model performance by introducing redundancy, irrelevant information, or compounding errors, often observed as a U-shaped performance curve.

## Method

- Propose "**ShorterBetter**," a novel reinforcement learning (RL) method that leverages a ==dynamic reward signal== to guide reasoning models toward efficient inference lengths.
- Define an Optimal Reasoning Length (OL) and approximate it with Sample Optimal Length (SOL), which identifies the shortest correct response length among a group of rollouts or averages lengths if no correct response is found.
- Optimize the model's policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a reward function that balances correctness and deviation from the calculated SOL.

## Results

- Achieved significant output token length reductions of 50-**80%** across various in-domain math, general reasoning, and coding benchmarks for both **1.5B** and **7B** models.
- Maintained or improved accuracy, with average accuracy gains of +**2.5%** to +**7.1%** on in-domain tasks, while minimizing accuracy drops on out-of-domain tasks.
- Consistently attained the highest Accuracy-Efficiency (AE) scores compared to other length-reduction baselines like Training Efficient and O1-Pruner, demonstrating superior balance between conciseness and correctness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An intrinsic optimal reasoning length exists for each problem, dependent on model capacity and difficulty, which balances the need for sufficient analysis with conciseness.
> - The Sample Optimal Length (SOL) provides an effective, data-driven approximation of this unobservable optimal length, enabling self-supervised learning for efficient reasoning without external length constraints.
> - Reasoning trace analysis reveals that ShorterBetter refines CoT outputs by increasing the proportion of pivotal reasoning steps and reducing non-substantive statements, unnecessary verification, and over-exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and emerging challenge within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs): the trade-off between reasoning capability and computational efficiency. The advent of powerful reasoning models like OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 has showcased significant performance gains on complex tasks by generating extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces, often likened to human-like System-2 "slow thinking." This approach involves detailed intermediate steps, self-verifications, and exploration of alternative solution paths, contributing to enhanced accuracy and robustness on challenging benchmarks.

However, the broader research community has increasingly identified a phenomenon dubbed "overthinking," where excessively long reasoning processes become counterproductive. This overthinking leads not only to substantial computational inefficiencies—manifesting as increased token usage, higher latency, and greater energy consumption—but can also degrade performance by introducing redundancy, irrelevant information, and even compounding errors. Studies have empirically and theoretically demonstrated a U-shaped performance curve with CoT length, where beyond a certain point, more reasoning does not necessarily improve, and can even harm, accuracy. This work by Yi et al. is positioned to tackle this very problem.

Existing approaches to efficient LLM reasoning primarily fall into categories such as:
- **Explicit Length Control:** Methods like L1 (Aggarwal and Welleck, 2025) or DAST (Shen et al., 2025) which impose a user-specified or adaptively adjusted length budget.
- **Reference-Based Comparison:** O1-Pruner (Luo et al., 2025a) incentivizes shorter reasoning paths by comparing generated lengths against those from a reference model.
- **Sampling-Based Baselines:** Kimi 1.5 (Team, 2025) and Training Efficient (Arora and Zanette, 2025) utilize the shortest or average lengths from multiple sampled responses as reward baselines.

"ShorterBetter" distinguishes itself from these methods by proposing an **autonomous, data-driven approach** that aims to discover an *intrinsic optimal reasoning length (OL)* for each problem, rather than imposing external length constraints or relying on predefined reference models. The paper hypothesizes that an OL exists, dependent on model capacity and problem difficulty, representing a "sweet spot" between insufficient analysis and counterproductive redundancy. By approximating this unobservable OL with a novel "Sample Optimal Length (SOL)," ShorterBetter offers a more dynamic and adaptive solution to the overthinking problem, aiming to guide models towards efficient reasoning without manual supervision. This approach provides a complementary yet distinct contribution to the ongoing efforts to balance reasoning power with operational efficiency in LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "ShorterBetter" research is to **enhance the efficiency of Large Language Models (LLMs) in reasoning-intensive tasks without compromising, and ideally improving, their accuracy.** This overarching goal is driven by a critical observation in the current landscape of advanced LLMs: the phenomenon of "overthinking."

The key motivations behind this work are:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency:** While extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces improve reasoning capabilities, they lead to significantly longer outputs. This verbosity translates directly into higher computational costs, increased latency, and greater energy consumption, making the deployment of these powerful models less practical, especially in resource-constrained environments.
2. **Mitigating Performance Degradation from Overthinking:** Beyond just inefficiency, the paper highlights that excessive reasoning can be counterproductive, leading to diminishing returns or even negative impacts on model performance. The authors identify specific problematic patterns: verbose repetitions, premature abandonment of promising paths, continued exploration after finding a correct answer, and a collapse of reasoning coherence in very long outputs. This suggests that "more thinking" is not always "better thinking."
3. **Exploiting Implicit Optimal Reasoning Lengths:** The authors are motivated by the hypothesis that an inherent, problem- and model-dependent "optimal reasoning length" (OL) exists. This OL represents the ideal amount of reasoning—neither too short (insufficient analysis) nor too long (redundancy/overthinking)—for a model to arrive at a correct answer. The challenge lies in the unobservability of this intrinsic OL, thus motivating the development of a practical approximation.
4. **Developing an Autonomous and Adaptive Solution:** Current methods often rely on explicit length budgets or comparisons with reference models, requiring manual intervention or predefined targets. The motivation for ShorterBetter is to create a self-supervised reinforcement learning (RL) method that allows reasoning models to autonomously learn and adapt to their optimal reasoning lengths, thereby removing the need for external, often arbitrary, control.

In summary, the key objectives articulated in the paper are:
- To propose **ShorterBetter**, a novel RL method that uses Sample Optimal Length (SOL) as a dynamic reward signal to guide reasoning models towards efficient inference lengths.
- To empirically demonstrate that this method significantly **reduces output token length (50-80%)** on both in-domain and out-of-domain reasoning tasks, while **maintaining or improving accuracy**.
- To introduce and utilize novel reasoning trace analysis frameworks to provide **mechanistic insights** into how ShorterBetter enhances reasoning efficiency by refining the structure of CoT traces (e.g., reducing unnecessary repetition, self-verification, and over-exploration).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ShorterBetter proposes a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to teach LLMs to dynamically identify and generate optimally concise Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces. The methodology is built upon three core components: formalizing Optimal Reasoning Length (OL), approximating it with Sample Optimal Length (SOL), and optimizing the model policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 3.1. Optimal Reasoning Length (OL): Formal Definition

The paper first formally defines the Optimal Reasoning Length (OL), denoted *ℓ*^*c,ε*(*x*^*i*; *θ*). For a given prompt *x*^*i* and a policy model *M*^*θ* with parameters *θ*, OL is the minimum length *ℓ(y)* of a response *y* such that two conditions are met:
1. **Correctness:** The response *y* achieves a normalized correctness score *s_I(y, y*_i)* above a threshold *c* (i.e., *s_I(y, y*_i) ≥ c*), where *y*_i* is the reference response.
2. **Reliability:** The probability of generating *y* from the model *p_θ(y|x_i)* is above a threshold *ε* (i.e., *p_θ(y|x_i) ≥ ε*).

This definition captures the intuition that OL is problem-dependent and model-dependent, varying with problem difficulty and the model's capability to generate concise, correct responses. For tasks with binary verifiable answers (like math or coding problems), the correctness score simplifies to *I(y = y*_i)* ∈ {0, 1}, and *c* is set to 1.

#### 3.2. Sample Optimal Length (SOL): A Practical Approximation

Since directly computing OL is infeasible due to unknown model output distributions, ShorterBetter introduces the Sample Optimal Length (SOL) as a practical, sample-based approximation. For a given prompt *x*^*i*, the method generates *n* candidate responses (rollouts) *G(x_i) = {y_1..., y_n}* from the current policy *p_θ(·|x_i)*. The SOL for this group is then defined as:
- If at least one response *y_j* in *G(x_i)* is correct (*I(y_j = y*_i) = 1*), SOL is the length of the **shortest correct response** among them: *min {ℓ(y_j) : y_j ∈ G(x_i), I(y_j = y*_i) = 1}*.
- If no response in *G(x_i)* is correct, SOL is the **average length** of all responses in the group: * (1/n) Σ_j=1^n ℓ(y_j)*.

This design ensures SOL is entirely data-driven, adapts to difficulty and model improvements, and handles failure cases neutrally by not biasing towards extreme lengths when no correct signal is present.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

A specific reward function *r(y_j)* is defined for each generated response *y_j* based on SOL:
*r(y_j) = α · I(y_j = y*_i) − β · |ℓ(y_j) − ℓ_SOL(G(x_i))|*
Here, *α* and *β* are positive hyperparameters that balance the correctness reward against the penalty for deviating from the SOL. A higher *α* emphasizes accuracy, while a higher *β* emphasizes length reduction.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization via GRPO

To optimize the model using this SOL-based reward, ShorterBetter adopts the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm (Shao et al., 2024). GRPO is well-suited for group-based reward designs. It calculates within-group advantage estimates *A*^*j,k* for each token *k* of the *j*-th sample as *(r_j - mean(r)) / std(r)*, where *mean(r)* and *std(r)* are computed across the rewards of the *n* samples in the group.
The GRPO loss function encourages the model to increase the likelihood of high-reward samples while using a clipped policy improvement objective and a KL divergence penalty to constrain deviation from a frozen reference policy *M*^*θ_ref* (a copy of the model before a few gradient updates). This ensures stable training.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B.
- **Training Data:** DeepScaleR-preview (40K math problems from AIME, AMC, Omni-MATH, Still dataset).
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - **In-domain:** Held-out subsets of the training math datasets (AIME, Olympiad-Bench, AMC, Minerva).
 - **Out-of-domain:** MathQA, MMLU, Big-Bench Hard (BBH), LiveCodeBench, MBPP, HumanEval (covering general reasoning and coding).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy, Output Length (average tokens), and Accuracy-Efficiency (AE) Score (a composite metric balancing length reduction against accuracy, with asymmetric penalties for accuracy drops).
- **Hyperparameters:** Batch size *N*=64, rollout group size *n*=8, learning rate 1 × 10^-6. Tested *α*=1 and *α*=2 (with *β*=0.001 fixed). GRPO specific: *γ*=0.001, *ε*=0.2.
- **Baselines:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (unmodified base model), Qwen2.5-Instruct (non-reasoning baseline), Training Efficient (Arora and Zanette, 2025), and O1-Pruner (Luo et al., 2025a).

#### 3.6. Reasoning Structure Analysis

To understand *how* ShorterBetter refines reasoning, the authors developed two novel LLM-as-a-judge (using Gemini 2.5 Flash Preview) frameworks:
1. **Output Length after First Appearance of Correct Answer:** Measures the percentage of output tokens generated *after* the model first states the correct answer, indicating post-solution verbosity.
2. **Breakdown of Reasoning Traces:** Categorizes each sentence in a CoT trace into five mutually exclusive functional categories: "Pivotal Reasoning," "Productive Elaboration & Calculation," "Exploring Alternatives," "Verification & Self-Correction," and "Non-Substantive Statement." This provides a fine-grained token-level distribution of reasoning behaviors. The reliability of this LLM-as-a-judge framework was validated through human verification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that ShorterBetter effectively reduces the inference length of reasoning models while maintaining or improving accuracy across a diverse set of tasks.

#### 4.1. Significant Length Reduction with Accuracy Preservation

- **1.5B Models (Figure 1):** ShorterBetter-1.5B (with α=1 and α=2) achieved substantial length reductions compared to the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B baseline.
 - **In-domain math tasks:** Average length reductions of 77.6% (α=1) and 79.2% (α=2), accompanied by average accuracy *improvements* of +2.5% and +3.2%, respectively.
 - **Out-of-domain tasks:** Average length reductions of 77.0% (α=1) and 75.5% (α=2), with minimal average accuracy changes of –0.8% and –1.6%, respectively.
- **7B Models (Table 1):** ShorterBetter-7B (α=2) showed similar strong performance.
 - **In-domain math tasks:** Average length reduction of 62.1% with an average accuracy improvement of +7.1%.
 - **Out-of-domain tasks:** Average length reduction of 62.3% with a minimal average accuracy change of -0.48%.
- **AE Scores:** ShorterBetter-7B consistently attained the highest Accuracy-Efficiency (AE) Scores across all evaluated tasks, outperforming other length-reduction baselines like Training Efficient and O1-Pruner. This metric highlights ShorterBetter's superior ability to reduce length without sacrificing correctness.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** ShorterBetter achieved stronger length-reduction capabilities compared to prior methods, which the authors attribute to the clear and robust signals provided by the SOL design during RL.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Structure Analysis

The novel reasoning trace analysis frameworks provided mechanistic insights into how ShorterBetter achieves its efficiency gains.
- **Output Length after First Appearance of Correct Answer (Figure 3, Left):** For problems where both ShorterBetter-7B and Distill-7B provided correct answers, ShorterBetter-7B significantly reduced the percentage of tokens generated *after* the first correct answer was stated. This reduction ranged from 15% to 20% across various math benchmarks (AMC, MATH, Olympiad, Minerva), indicating that the model reaches conclusions more efficiently and avoids unnecessary post-solution reasoning.
- **Breakdown of Reasoning Traces (Figure 3, Right):** A fine-grained, token-level analysis on the MATH benchmark revealed structural shifts in ShorterBetter-7B's reasoning compared to Distill-7B:
 - **Increased Meaningful Reasoning:** Proportions of "Pivotal Reasoning" (+15.6%) and "Productive Elaboration & Calculation" (+6.8%) increased, indicating a denser concentration of solution-oriented thought.
 - **Reduced Redundancy and Over-exploration:** Proportions of "Non-Substantive Statements" decreased by 4.8%. More significantly, "Verification & Self-Correction" decreased by 14.1%, and "Exploring Alternatives" decreased by 3.9%. This suggests ShorterBetter promotes more confident, decisive, and streamlined reasoning trajectories, with less time spent on unnecessary detours, repetitions, or excessive self-checks once a correct path is identified.
- **Human Verification of LLM-as-a-judge:** A human verification study on the AMC dataset confirmed the reliability of the LLM-as-a-judge framework, with high agreement (94.23% sentence-level, 93.01% token-level) with human annotations across reasoning categories.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of SOL Design (Figure 5):** Ablation experiments showed that naively setting the target length to the shortest response (either with or without correctness reward) or partially replacing SOL with the shortest length when no correct responses exist, led to training collapse within 100 steps. This highlights the critical role of SOL's balanced design (shortest *correct* or average length) for stable and effective training.
- **SOL's Inherent Properties (Figure 4):** Discarding the explicit correctness reward (setting α=0) while keeping the SOL design resulted in drastic accuracy fluctuations but maintained effective training. This suggests SOL inherently combines signals for both correctness and conciseness, though an explicit correctness term is crucial to prevent accuracy degradation.

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics and Generalization

- **Training Convergence (Figure 8):** Both SOL values and the mean deviation of generated outputs from their respective SOLs steadily decreased during training, indicating that ShorterBetter successfully guides the model towards optimal and more consistent reasoning lengths.
- **Generalization to Different Architectures (Table 6):** ShorterBetter demonstrated successful generalization by applying effectively to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, a model with a different architectural backbone, further confirming the robustness of the method.
- **Correlation between Errors and Length (Figure 9):** An analysis showed that incorrect reasoning traces were significantly longer than correct ones (by ~5.5K tokens for 1.5B and ~5.9K for 7B models), providing empirical motivation for targeting length reduction in models prone to "overthinking."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ShorterBetter" research by Yi, Wang, and Li presents a significant advancement in addressing the critical balance between reasoning capabilities and computational efficiency in Large Language Models (LLMs). Its implications span both the theoretical understanding of LLM reasoning and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Formalization of Optimal Reasoning Length (OL):** The paper provides a formal definition of OL, acknowledging its dependence on problem difficulty and model capacity. This contributes to a more rigorous theoretical framework for understanding LLM reasoning processes.
- **Novel Self-Supervised Reward Mechanism:** The introduction of Sample Optimal Length (SOL) as a dynamic, self-supervised reward signal is a novel conceptual contribution. It allows models to implicitly discover their ideal reasoning paths without requiring explicit, often arbitrary, human-defined length budgets or reference model outputs. This shifts the paradigm from externally controlled efficiency to autonomously learned optimization.
- **Deeper Insight into Reasoning Structure:** The innovative LLM-as-a-judge frameworks for analyzing reasoning traces offer unprecedented fine-grained insights into how LLMs structure their thoughts. By categorizing reasoning steps (e.g., pivotal, exploratory, verification), the work moves beyond mere input-output analysis to deconstruct the internal "thought" process, revealing *what kind* of reasoning is compressed or amplified. This can inform future research into more interpretable and controllable LLM reasoning.

#### 5.2. Practical Impact and Implications

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** By achieving 50-80% reductions in output token length, ShorterBetter directly translates into substantial cost savings. Reduced token usage means lower API costs for commercial LLM providers and users, faster inference times, and significantly less energy consumption. This makes high-performance reasoning models more economically viable and environmentally friendly.
- **Improved User Experience:** Faster response generation leads to a smoother and more responsive user experience in applications requiring complex reasoning, such as automated problem-solving, code generation, or sophisticated query answering.
- **Broader Deployment of Reasoning LLMs:** The improved efficiency allows for the deployment of powerful reasoning LLMs in resource-constrained environments or for real-time applications where latency is critical. This could expand the accessibility and utility of advanced AI in various sectors, from education and scientific research to industrial automation.
- **Mitigation of "Overthinking" Issues:** The method effectively tackles the "overthinking" phenomenon, where excessively long reasoning paths become detrimental. By guiding models to be more concise and focused, ShorterBetter prevents the introduction of irrelevant information, reduces redundant self-verification, and promotes more coherent reasoning trajectories, potentially improving overall reliability.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** Demonstrated effectiveness across different model sizes (1.5B, 7B, 8B), architectural backbones (Qwen, Llama), and diverse in-domain and out-of-domain tasks (mathematics, general reasoning, coding) highlights the method's robustness and broad applicability.

#### 5.3. Future Directions

The paper acknowledges several promising future directions. Extending ShorterBetter to **open-ended reasoning tasks** (where correctness scores are non-binary) and evaluating it on **even larger-scale reasoning models** will be crucial next steps. Furthermore, the detailed reasoning trace analysis opens avenues for developing more fine-grained, **behavior-aware optimization strategies**. Understanding which specific types of reasoning (e.g., exploration vs. verification) are most amenable to compression could lead to even more nuanced and effective techniques for refining LLM reasoning.

In conclusion, ShorterBetter represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and resource-efficient LLMs. By enabling models to autonomously learn optimal reasoning lengths, it promises to make advanced AI reasoning more practical, scalable, and ultimately, more useful across a wide array of applications.
