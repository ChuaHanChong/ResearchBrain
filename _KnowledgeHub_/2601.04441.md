# Improving and Accelerating Offline RL in Large Discrete Action Spaces with Structured Policy Initialization

> [!summary] Summary
> Structured Policy Initialization (SPIN), a two-stage framework for offline reinforcement learning in large discrete combinatorial action spaces, achieves higher asymptotic performance and significantly faster training times. It pre-trains an Action Structure Model to capture action dependencies before optimizing the control policy, leading to more robust and efficient learning.

## Problem

- Offline reinforcement learning in large discrete combinatorial action spaces faces intractable computational complexity due to the exponential growth in joint actions.
- Existing methods often struggle to capture coherent and valid inter-sub-action dependencies, leading to suboptimal or nonsensical actions.
- The simultaneous learning of action representation and control policy in complex spaces often results in slow convergence and unstable learning.

## Method

- **SPIN** decouples ==action structure== learning from policy optimization by introducing a two-stage framework.
- An Action Structure Model (ASM) is pre-trained in a self-supervised manner using a masked conditional modeling objective to capture the manifold of plausible actions from static datasets.
- A policy network is then trained with the ASM's learned representation frozen, leveraging lightweight components for efficient policy optimization, compatible with actor-critic offline RL algorithms.

## Results

- SPIN achieved the highest overall average return of 594.1 across benchmarks, surpassing the next-best baseline (SAINT at 572.1) and showing over **13%** improvement on challenging `random-medium-expert` datasets.
- The framework demonstrated superior training efficiency, reaching **95%** of asymptotic performance up to 3.8 times faster than baselines, with the ASM pre-training contributing a minor portion of total wall-clock time.
- A linear probe showed the pre-trained ASM improved exact-match accuracy for full action tuples by **45x** (to **4.52%**) compared to a random initialization, quantifying its ability to capture cross-dimensional dependencies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the learning of action structure from control significantly improves both the asymptotic performance and training efficiency of offline RL agents in large combinatorial action spaces.
> - The pre-trained Action Structure Model effectively captures cross-joint action coordination, providing a strong inductive bias that simplifies the subsequent control policy learning task.
> - The quality of the pre-trained action representation is critical for downstream policy performance, enabling rapid adaptation and high returns even with minimal policy training steps.
