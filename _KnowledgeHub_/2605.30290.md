# Self-Trained Verification for Training- and Test-Time Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Trained Verification (STV) proposes a method to train verifiers using self-supervision, leveraging reference solutions to provide detailed diagnostic feedback. This approach significantly enhances test-time iterative refinement, for example, doubling pass@1 accuracy on hard math problems and enabling smaller models to outperform much larger, unverified counterparts. It also fosters training-time self-improvement for generators, leading to a 30% relative increase in standalone performance.

## Problem

- Current reasoning model verifiers offer shallow or generic critiques and miscalibrated scores, impeding effective test-time refinement and training-time self-improvement.
- A supervision gap exists for training verifiers to provide detailed diagnostic feedback (e.g., identifying specific logical flaws), as final correctness labels do not offer this granularity.
- Existing strategies to improve verifier quality often rely on costly human annotations or stronger, external models, limiting scalability.

## Method

- Introduces Self-Trained Verification (**STV**), which trains a ==student verifier== to imitate a teacher verifier. The teacher is privileged with a reference solution to reliably diagnose flaws, generating a self-supervision signal.
- STV combines On-Policy Distillation (OPD) to align the student's verdict and feedback distribution with the teacher's, and Verdict-Reinforcement Learning (Verdict-RL) to improve the verifier's accept/reject accuracy.
- Proposes Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) training, where a generator is trained via standard reinforcement learning to explicitly incorporate diagnostic feedback from a frozen, STV-trained verifier during its multi-turn training rollouts.

## Results

- STV approximately doubled final-round pass@1 accuracy on hard math problems (e.g., from **17.8%** to **27.4%** on 'Hard' DAPO) and achieved a **14x** relative increase on 'Hardest' scientific reasoning problems.
- An STV-guided Qwen3-**8B** generator outperformed significantly larger models (e.g., Qwen3-**32B**, Qwen3-**235B**-A22B) operating without verification on challenging reasoning tasks.
- ViL training improved the generator's standalone (round-0) pass@1 accuracy by roughly **30%** relative, demonstrating gains beyond the plateau observed with continued standard RLVR, and STV verifiers showed 3-**5x** higher precision at the same coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An asymmetry exists where LLMs can more reliably diagnose errors in a candidate solution when presented with a ground-truth reference, allowing for the generation of high-quality diagnostic self-supervision.
> - Providing specific, diagnostic feedback is more effective for iterative refinement and generator training than merely providing a binary correctness signal or generic critiques.
> - Training a generator to actively learn from diagnostic feedback instills more generalized reasoning capabilities, leading to improvements in its standalone performance beyond what traditional RL methods achieve.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of reasoning models capable of self-improvement at scale has been a persistent objective in artificial intelligence. Current approaches to self-improvement typically manifest in two primary forms: test-time verification-refinement (V-R) loops and training-time self-training methods.

Test-time V-R loops involve a generator proposing a solution, a verifier assessing it and providing feedback, and the generator subsequently refining its output. This iterative process has been instrumental in recent advancements on complex reasoning tasks, such as those encountered in international mathematical olympiads (IMO) and frontier mathematics. Simultaneously, training-time self-training techniques leverage the model's own generated and verified attempts to augment its training data, fostering continuous learning.

However, a critical bottleneck common to both paradigms is the quality of the verifier. Existing verifiers frequently provide shallow or generic critiques and suffer from miscalibrated scores. This limitation can lead to V-R loops amplifying plausible but incorrect reasoning rather than rectifying it, and self-training methods incorporating flawed self-generated data, thereby hindering actual improvement. A fundamental challenge in improving verifier quality lies in a supervision gap: while a final-answer label can indicate whether a solution is wrong, it does not inherently provide the detailed diagnostic feedback needed to identify *why* a plausible but incorrect solution is flawed (e.g., a missed boundary case, an invalid reduction, or an misused lemma). Current strategies to circumvent this gap often rely on meta-verifiers trained on human feedback or leverage stronger, external models to provide the necessary diagnostic signal.

This research addresses this verifier quality bottleneck by proposing a scalable method for training verifiers that can provide specific, actionable feedback without requiring human annotation or external stronger models. By improving the verifier, this work aims to unlock the full potential of both test-time iterative refinement and training-time self-improvement, thereby advancing the field's capacity for autonomous reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable large language models (LLMs) to achieve self-improvement at scale for complex reasoning problems by overcoming limitations in current verification mechanisms. The authors identify the verifier as the central bottleneck preventing effective self-improvement in both test-time iterative refinement and training-time self-training processes.

The specific motivations and objectives for this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Address Verifier Quality Limitations:** Existing verifiers struggle to provide high-quality, diagnostic feedback. They often produce shallow critiques or miscalibrated scores, leading to scenarios where V-R loops fail to improve accuracy despite additional computational effort, and self-training inadvertently incorporates flawed data. The primary motivation is to develop a method that can train verifiers to offer more precise and actionable feedback.

2. **Overcome the Supervision Gap for Diagnostic Feedback:** Training a verifier to identify specific logical flaws in a solution (e.g., missed edge cases, incorrect assumptions) is difficult because direct training signals for such nuanced error diagnosis are scarce. Final outcome labels only indicate correctness, not the underlying reasons for error. The authors are motivated by the observation that while a model might struggle to autonomously identify a flaw, it can often pinpoint errors when presented with a reference solution alongside a candidate solution. This changes the task from generative problem-solving to comparative analysis, which is an easier capability to train.

3. **Develop Self-Trained Verification (STV):** Based on this key asymmetry (easier diagnosis with a reference solution), a core objective is to propose a novel pipeline, Self-Trained Verification (STV), that leverages this "privileged information" (the reference solution) to create a self-supervision signal for training verifiers. The goal is for this trained verifier to then function effectively without access to the reference at test time.

4. **Enhance Test-Time Iterative Refinement:** A key objective is to demonstrate that STV-trained verifiers significantly improve the effectiveness and scalability of test-time V-R loops on challenging problems, such as hard mathematics and scientific reasoning. This includes making refinement more robust against verifier score inflation and enabling greater accuracy gains over multiple refinement rounds compared to existing baselines.

5. **Introduce Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) Training for Generators:** Beyond test-time improvements, the research aims to explore how a high-quality STV verifier can facilitate training-time self-improvement for the generator itself. The objective is to develop a procedure, ViL, where the generator is explicitly trained to better incorporate feedback from the frozen, STV-trained verifier within V-R loops during its training phase.

6. **Break RLVR Performance Plateaus:** A specific motivation for ViL is to achieve generator performance gains beyond the typical convergence plateaus observed with standard reinforcement learning from verifiable reward (RLVR). The authors investigate whether learning from diagnostic feedback during training can instill more general reasoning capabilities in the generator, potentially improving its standalone performance even without a verifier present at inference time.

7. **Analyze Mechanisms of Improvement:** The research also aims to systematically analyze *why* STV works, specifically by examining its impact on verifier calibration (mitigating reward hacking) and the quality of generated feedback. Furthermore, an objective is to understand how STV-guided V-R reshapes the generator's output distribution, comparing it against simpler methods like Best-of-N to ascertain if it enables structured exploration and genuinely improved reasoning rather than mere sharpening of existing solution modes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Self-Trained Verification (STV) and Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) training, designed to improve both test-time and training-time self-improvement for reasoning models.

**Self-Trained Verification (STV)**
STV addresses the challenge of training verifiers to provide detailed diagnostic feedback without human annotations. The core idea is to leverage a "privileged" setting where a model, when provided with a reference solution, can more reliably identify flaws in an attempted solution. This asymmetry is converted into a self-supervision signal.

1. **Teacher-Student Setup:** The approach defines two versions of a verifier, both using the same underlying large language model (LLM) but with different prompts:
 - **Student Verifier (Vθ):** This is the unconditioned verifier, Vθ(· | x, y_r-1), which takes a problem `x` and a generated solution `y_r-1` and produces a verdict (`accept` or `reject`) and natural-language feedback. This is the verifier used at test time.
 - **Teacher Verifier (V*):** This is the reference-conditioned verifier, V*(· | x, y_r-1, y*(x)), which is additionally shown the ground-truth reference solution `y*(x)`. This teacher is assumed to be more capable at diagnosing errors due to this privileged information.

2. **Training Objective for Vθ:** The student verifier Vθ is trained to imitate the output distribution of the teacher verifier V*. The training involves two main components:
 - **On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** This is the primary method for aligning the student's output distribution (z = (verdict, feedback)) with the teacher's. The loss function, L_OPD(θ), minimizes the α-divergence (specifically, Jensen-Shannon divergence with α=0.5) between Vθ(· | x, y_r-1) and V*(· | x, y_r-1, y*(x)). The pairs (x, y_r-1) are drawn from the generator's rollouts on training prompts, ensuring the student is trained on the types of solutions it will encounter at test time. The authors specifically choose OPD over Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) based on the hypothesis that OPD, being on-policy, handles potential distribution shifts better than SFT, which can struggle when the student drifts from the teacher's generated sequences.
 - **Verdict-Reinforcement Learning (Verdict-RL):** An additional RL component, L_RL(θ), is used to improve the accuracy of the verifier's `accept`/`reject` verdict. The reward signal is binary: `I[v_r = I[y_r-1 = y*(x)]]`, meaning a reward is given if the verifier's verdict matches the ground-truth correctness of the solution.
 - **Full STV Objective:** The final STV objective is a weighted sum of these two components: L_STV(θ) = L_OPD(θ) + λ ⋅ L_RL(θ).

**Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) Training for Generator**
Once an STV verifier Vθ is trained and frozen, it can be used to further train the generator G.

1. **Standard RL on Generator:** ViL instantiates standard reinforcement learning for the generator. The training process involves multi-turn V-R rollouts for each problem `x`.
2. **V-R Rollout:**
 - The generator G produces an initial solution `y_0`.
 - The frozen STV verifier Vθ evaluates `y_0` and provides a verdict `v_1` and feedback `f_1`.
 - If `v_1` is `reject`, the generator refines `y_1` based on `x`, `y_0`, and `f_1`. This process continues for `R` rounds or until an `accept` verdict.
3. **Reward for Generator:** The generator's reward is based on the verifiable correctness of the final solution `y_r` against the ground-truth `y*(x)`. Only the generator's parameters are updated during this phase; the STV verifier remains frozen.

**Experimental Setup**
- **Base Model:** Qwen3-8B is used as the foundational LLM for both generator and verifier in main experiments.
- **Generators:** Experiments involve a base Qwen3-8B generator and a "continual-trained" generator (already converged under standard RLVR).
- **Datasets:** Hard mathematical problems from DAPO and scientific reasoning problems from SciKnowEval are used. Problems are filtered and split into "Hard" and "Hardest" bins based on the base Qwen3-8B's pass@1 performance.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** 32 independent V-R loops are run for up to 20 rounds per test problem.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against pipelines with untrained verifiers, verifiers trained solely on verdict accuracy (Verdict-RL), meta-verifiers (proxied by GPT-5.2), SFT-trained verifiers, and larger generator models (Qwen3-32B, Qwen3-235B-A22B) without verification.
- **Ablation Studies:** These investigate the impact of oracle use (e.g., prefix-conditioning) and the contribution of trained feedback versus verdict accuracy.
- **Analysis:** The authors analyze verifier calibration (precision-coverage curves, reward hacking diagnostics) and how V-R reshapes output distributions (pass@k, comparison with Best-of-N).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across both test-time and training-time self-improvement:

**Test-Time Self-Improvement (Verifier-Guided Refinement)**

1. **Substantial Performance Gains with Base Generator:** On hard DAPO math problems, the STV verifier roughly doubled the final-round pass@1 accuracy over an untrained verifier, with gains accumulating across 20+ refinement rounds. For instance, on the "Hardest" split, STV increased pass@1 from near zero to 5.5%, and on "Hard" problems, from 17.8% to 27.4%. In contrast, alternative methods like Verdict-RL, Meta-verifier RL (using GPT-5.2 as a proxy), and SFT on teacher traces showed only marginal or no improvement, often saturating quickly.
2. **Outperformance of Larger Models:** An STV-guided Qwen3-8B generator achieved higher final-round pass@1 than a much larger Qwen3-32B generator operating without verification (5.5% vs 2.7% on Hardest, 27.4% vs 17.8% on Hard). This suggests that trained verification can effectively substitute for increased generator scale on challenging reasoning tasks.
3. **Domain-General Capability (Scientific Reasoning):** On SciKnowEval, STV demonstrated significant improvements, lifting pass@1 from 1.5% to 21.0% on "Hardest" problems (a 14x relative increase) and substantially on "Hard" problems. The STV-guided Qwen3-8B also outperformed even a 30x larger Qwen3-235B-A22B without verification, indicating the bottleneck addressed by STV is general across scientific domains.
4. **Gains Compound on Stronger Generators:** Even when starting with a continual-trained generator already converged under RLVR (which showed marginal self-verification improvement), applying STV training yielded further substantial gains in V-R performance, demonstrating that STV's benefits are additive and not absorbed by an already strong generator.
5. **Weak-to-Strong Verification:** Smaller STV-trained verifiers (e.g., 4B and 1.7B models) achieved performance competitive with or matching a larger, untrained 8B verifier. The 4B STV verifier was competitive with the 8B STV verifier, and the 1.7B STV verifier matched the untrained 8B verifier, indicating potential for significant computational savings.

**Training-Time Self-Improvement (Verifier-in-the-Loop Training, ViL)**

1. **Beyond RLVR Plateau:** ViL training, starting from an already RL-converged generator, resulted in a 33% relative gain in final-round pass@1 when the STV verifier was present at test time. Crucially, the same amount of additional compute spent on continuing standard RLVR yielded no further improvement.
2. **Standalone Generator Improvement:** More notably, ViL training improved the generator's standalone performance (round-0 pass@1, without any verifier at test time) by roughly 30% relative (e.g., from 10.7% to 14.7% on Hardest problems). This suggests that training a generator to incorporate diagnostic feedback instills more general reasoning capabilities that manifest even in its initial attempts.
3. **Ablation Studies on Oracle Use:**
 - Prefix-conditioning (training the generator to continue from partial reference solutions) yielded lower pass@1 scores than ViL with STV verifier, indicating that diagnostic feedback is more valuable than direct exposure to gold prefixes.
 - ViL with *self-verification* (where the generator provides its own feedback without any oracle) still showed round-0 improvement, underscoring that the V-R loop itself drives some training-time self-improvement. However, the STV verifier provided superior performance in later rounds, confirming the value of the trained verifier's higher-quality feedback.

**Analysis of STV's Effectiveness**

1. **Calibrated Verifier Scores:** STV significantly mitigated the issue of miscalibrated verifier scores. Unlike untrained verifiers where scores inflated without corresponding accuracy gains, STV verifiers demonstrated better calibration. They achieved a 3-5x higher precision at the same level of coverage, with precision actively increasing with coverage, ensuring that accepted solutions were more reliably correct with more verification rounds.
2. **Improved Feedback Quality:** By isolating the contribution of feedback (using ground-truth verdicts), STV feedback provided an additional 3.2% pass@1 gain on Hard problems compared to uninformative feedback, confirming that STV training enhances the diagnostic quality of the natural language feedback independent of verdict accuracy.

**How STV Shapes the Output Distribution**

1. **Diversity Preservation:** V-R with STV did not suppress diversity. Pass@k generally improved across the first 10 refinement rounds, indicating structured exploration rather than just sharpening existing modes. A sweet spot for verification rounds was observed, with harder problems often requiring more rounds to reach peak pass@k.
2. **Refinement vs. Resampling:** For the base and STV-trained generators, V-R with STV significantly outperformed Best-of-N (BoN) at matched computational cost. This provides evidence that the iterative refinement process, guided by STV feedback, actively reshapes the solution distribution rather than merely sharpening it. The STV verifier also improved BoN performance in all cases, indicating its general utility. For the continual-trained generator (not explicitly trained to use feedback), refinement did not notably outperform BoN, suggesting that the ability to use feedback for reshaping requires specific training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Self-Trained Verification (STV) as a scalable method for enhancing the quality of verifiers, addressing a fundamental bottleneck in the self-improvement capabilities of reasoning models. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Unlocking Scalable Self-Improvement:** STV provides a practical and scalable recipe for training verifiers without reliance on expensive human annotations or stronger external models. By leveraging the asymmetry of diagnosis (easier with a reference solution), it offers a novel approach to generate high-quality supervision signals for diagnostic feedback. This directly enables more effective self-improvement at scale for LLMs.

2. **Transformative Test-Time Performance:** The demonstrated improvements in test-time iterative refinement are substantial, enabling base models to achieve accuracy levels that surpass much larger models operating without verification. This finding suggests a paradigm shift where investing in trained verification can be a more computationally efficient strategy for solving hard reasoning problems than simply scaling up generator parameters. This could democratize access to high-performance reasoning for resource-constrained settings or make current frontier models even more capable.

3. **Novel Path to Training-Time Self-Improvement:** The introduction of Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) training provides a new avenue for training-time generator improvement. The most impactful finding here is the observed increase in the generator's *standalone* performance, even at round 0 without a verifier. This suggests that explicit training with diagnostic feedback teaches the generator more generalized reasoning skills, breaking through previously observed plateaus in standard reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). This indicates a deeper learning process where models learn *how* to reason by internalizing feedback mechanisms.

4. **Mitigation of Reward Hacking:** The analysis demonstrating STV's ability to produce better-calibrated verifier scores and improved precision-coverage characteristics is crucial. This directly addresses the problem of "reward hacking" in feedback loops, where models might learn to satisfy a flawed verifier rather than truly improving their reasoning. STV's robust verification system promotes genuine solution correctness.

5. **Foundation for Iterative Improvement Cycles:** The work suggests an exciting iterative route to self-improvement. As better verifiers enable more reliable test-time refinement and richer generator training, these stronger generators can, in turn, produce more sophisticated attempts and errors. These harder attempts could then serve as valuable data for future verifier training, creating a virtuous cycle of continuous enhancement for both components of a reasoning system.

6. **Broader Applicability and Future Research:** The domain-general nature of STV, shown on both math and scientific reasoning tasks, implies its potential applicability to other complex domains like code generation, planning, and open-ended reasoning. The research also opens up several promising future directions, including exploring other self-supervision signals, optimizing compute allocation between generator and verifier training, and scaling these methods to even larger models. Learning to verify without human-graded feedback stands out as a key frontier for autonomous reasoning systems.
