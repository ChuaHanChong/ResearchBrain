# Self-Trained Verification for Training- and Test-Time Self-Improvement

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Trained Verification (STV) proposes a method to train verifiers using self-supervision, leveraging reference solutions to provide detailed diagnostic feedback. This approach significantly enhances test-time iterative refinement, for example, doubling pass@1 accuracy on hard math problems and enabling smaller models to outperform much larger, unverified counterparts. It also fosters training-time self-improvement for generators, leading to a 30% relative increase in standalone performance.

## Problem

- Current reasoning model verifiers offer shallow or generic critiques and miscalibrated scores, impeding effective test-time refinement and training-time self-improvement.
- A supervision gap exists for training verifiers to provide detailed diagnostic feedback (e.g., identifying specific logical flaws), as final correctness labels do not offer this granularity.
- Existing strategies to improve verifier quality often rely on costly human annotations or stronger, external models, limiting scalability.

## Method

- Introduces Self-Trained Verification (**STV**), which trains a ==student verifier== to imitate a teacher verifier. The teacher is privileged with a reference solution to reliably diagnose flaws, generating a self-supervision signal.
- STV combines On-Policy Distillation (OPD) to align the student's verdict and feedback distribution with the teacher's, and Verdict-Reinforcement Learning (Verdict-RL) to improve the verifier's accept/reject accuracy.
- Proposes Verifier-in-the-Loop (ViL) training, where a generator is trained via standard reinforcement learning to explicitly incorporate diagnostic feedback from a frozen, STV-trained verifier during its multi-turn training rollouts.

## Results

- STV approximately doubled final-round pass@1 accuracy on hard math problems (e.g., from **17.8%** to **27.4%** on 'Hard' DAPO) and achieved a **14x** relative increase on 'Hardest' scientific reasoning problems.
- An STV-guided Qwen3-**8B** generator outperformed significantly larger models (e.g., Qwen3-**32B**, Qwen3-**235B**-A22B) operating without verification on challenging reasoning tasks.
- ViL training improved the generator's standalone (round-0) pass@1 accuracy by roughly **30%** relative, demonstrating gains beyond the plateau observed with continued standard RLVR, and STV verifiers showed 3-**5x** higher precision at the same coverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An asymmetry exists where LLMs can more reliably diagnose errors in a candidate solution when presented with a ground-truth reference, allowing for the generation of high-quality diagnostic self-supervision.
> - Providing specific, diagnostic feedback is more effective for iterative refinement and generator training than merely providing a binary correctness signal or generic critiques.
> - Training a generator to actively learn from diagnostic feedback instills more generalized reasoning capabilities, leading to improvements in its standalone performance beyond what traditional RL methods achieve.
