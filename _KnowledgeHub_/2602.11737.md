# Mask What Matters: Mitigating Object Hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models with Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich, Amazon, and MBZUAI developed Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD), an inference-time method that uses DINO Vision Transformers to create object-aligned auxiliary views for Visual Contrastive Decoding. This approach reduced object hallucination in MLLMs, achieving up to a 1.5% accuracy gain on the POPE benchmark and improving average scores on the MME hallucination subset for models like LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate responses referring to objects not present or incorrectly grounded in images, a phenomenon known as object hallucination.
- Existing Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) methods often rely on generic, heuristic image-level perturbations that do not precisely target object extents.
- Some VCD approaches depend on prompt- and model-specific cross-modal signals, potentially introducing computational overhead, complexity, or circularity.

## Method

- The work introduces ==Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD)==, enhancing standard VCD with a novel approach for generating auxiliary views.
- Pre-trained ==DINO Vision Transformers== are leveraged to extract object-centric saliency maps from input images, exploiting their inherent ability to identify semantic regions.
- Auxiliary views are created by ==masking out the most salient object regions== based on a quantile threshold of these saliency maps, with the masked areas filled by a neutral background.

## Results

- OA-VCD consistently outperformed baseline methods on the POPE benchmark, achieving up to **1.5%** higher Accuracy and **1.6%** higher F1 score compared to AGLA on LLaVA-v1.5 (7B).
- The method led to improved average scores on the MME hallucination subset, showing notable gains in "Existence" and "Color" categories for both LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the method's robustness to different choices of neutral background (e.g., blur, black, mean color) and masking area ratio (γ).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-aligned auxiliary views, derived from self-supervised ViT attention, provide a more semantically targeted and stronger contrast signal for VCD compared to generic perturbations.
> - The method is prompt-agnostic and model-agnostic, enabling broad applicability across various MLLMs without requiring model-specific fine-tuning or internal states.
> - Generating these targeted auxiliary views requires only a single cacheable forward pass through a DINO ViT, ensuring computational efficiency during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities across various tasks, including image captioning and visual question answering, by integrating visual and textual information. Despite their performance, MLLMs frequently exhibit a phenomenon known as "object hallucination," where the model generates descriptions or answers that refer to objects not present or not accurately grounded in the provided image. This issue compromises the reliability and trustworthiness of MLLM outputs, particularly in applications requiring high factual accuracy.

A prominent line of research addresses object hallucination during the inference stage through techniques like visual contrastive decoding (VCD). VCD operates by contrasting the MLLM's next-token distributions under the original visual input with a perturbed "auxiliary view" of the same input. The objective is to suppress tokens that remain likely even when visual support for them is diminished in the auxiliary view, thus preventing the model from relying solely on language priors or internal biases.

Previous advancements in VCD have focused on generating more effective auxiliary views. For instance, some methods explore selecting optimal augmentations from a predefined pool (e.g., VSCoDe), while others attempt to align perturbations with prompt-relevant regions using image-text matching models (e.g., AGLA). However, these approaches often face limitations such as relying on heuristic, image-level perturbations that may not precisely target object extents, or depending on prompt- and model-specific cross-modal signals, which can introduce circularity or increase computational overhead.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing an improvement to VCD that addresses these limitations. It seeks to generate semantically meaningful, object-aligned auxiliary views in a prompt-agnostic and model-agnostic manner. By leveraging the object-centric attention mechanisms of self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs), this work aims to create counterfactual auxiliary views that specifically remove salient visual evidence, thereby producing a more targeted and stronger contrast signal for mitigating object hallucinations. This approach contributes to the ongoing effort to enhance the factual grounding and robustness of MLLMs without requiring additional training or significant computational resources.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to mitigate object hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enhancing the effectiveness of Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD). Specifically, the authors aim to improve VCD by developing a novel method for constructing object-aligned auxiliary views.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and existing challenges:

1. **Prevalence of Object Hallucination**: Despite their advanced capabilities, MLLMs frequently generate responses that include objects or details not present in the input image. This "object hallucination" is a significant limitation, eroding user trust and hindering the deployment of MLLMs in sensitive applications where factual accuracy is paramount. This phenomenon often arises from models relying on strong language priors learned during pre-training rather than robust visual grounding.
2. **Limitations of Existing VCD Auxiliary View Generation**: Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) has emerged as a promising inference-time technique to combat hallucination by contrasting model outputs from an original image and a perturbed auxiliary image. However, the efficacy of VCD heavily depends on the quality and specificity of the auxiliary view.
 - **Heuristic Perturbations**: Many existing VCD methods employ heuristic image-level perturbations (e.g., blurring, cropping, color changes) to create auxiliary views. These perturbations are often generic and may not precisely remove the visual evidence for specific objects, leading to suboptimal contrast signals.
 - **Lack of Object Alignment**: Such image-level perturbations do not necessarily align with the actual object extents within an image. This means they might either insufficiently disrupt the visual evidence for hallucinated objects or inadvertently obscure evidence for valid objects, diminishing the effectiveness of the contrastive signal.
 - **Prompt and Model Dependence**: Some advanced VCD approaches attempt to create more targeted auxiliary views by using cross-modal signals (e.g., image-text matching models) to identify prompt-relevant regions for masking (e.g., AGLA). While effective in some scenarios, these methods introduce prompt- and model-dependent processes, potentially leading to increased computational overhead, complexity, and a risk of circularity where biased attention guides the masking intended to correct that bias.
3. **Desire for an Efficient and Generalizable Solution**: The authors seek a method for auxiliary view generation that is:
 - **Prompt-agnostic**: It should not require specific textual prompts or queries to guide the masking process.
 - **Model-agnostic**: It should be applicable across different MLLMs without requiring model-specific fine-tuning or internal model states beyond standard forward passes.
 - **Computationally Efficient**: It should integrate seamlessly into the existing VCD pipeline with minimal additional computational cost, ideally requiring only a single cacheable forward pass for the auxiliary view.
 - **Semantically Meaningful**: The generated auxiliary views should provide targeted, object-level perturbations that effectively disrupt visual evidence for salient objects without relying on external supervision or complex cross-modal interactions.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to develop a more robust, efficient, and broadly applicable VCD framework capable of significantly reducing object hallucinations in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, termed Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD), improves upon the existing VCD framework by introducing a novel approach to generate object-aligned auxiliary views. The overall methodology is divided into two main components: a review of Visual Contrastive Decoding and the detailed process for generating auxiliary views by removing salient visual evidence.

#### 3.1. Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) Foundation

The work builds upon the standard VCD framework. Given an MLLM with parameters $\theta$, a textual query $x$, and an original visual input $v$, the model generates a response autoregressively by sampling tokens $y_t$ from the probability distribution $p_\theta(y_t \mid v, x, y_{<t})$.

VCD introduces an auxiliary view $v'$ of the original image $v$. The core idea is to form a contrastive distribution that amplifies differences between the probabilities derived from the original view and the auxiliary view. This is achieved by combining the logits from both views:
$p_{\text{vcd}}(y \mid v, v', x) = \text{softmax}((1 + \alpha) \text{logit}_\theta(y \mid v, x) - \alpha \text{logit}_\theta(y \mid v', x))$
where $\alpha \ge 0$ controls the strength of the contrast.

To prevent penalizing valid outputs or promoting implausible ones, the method incorporates Adaptive Plausibility Constraints (APC). APC restricts the set of candidate tokens $y_t$ to a subset $\mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}(y_{<t})$, which includes only tokens whose original probabilities $p_\theta(y_t \mid v, x, y_{<t})$ are above a certain threshold relative to the maximum probability. Specifically, $y_t \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}(y_{<t})$ if its probability is at least $\beta$ times the maximum probability for any token, where $\beta \in (0, 1]$. Tokens outside this set are assigned a zero probability in the auxiliary distribution. The final decoding rule combines VCD with APC to sample tokens from this adjusted distribution.

#### 3.2. Generation of Object-Aligned Auxiliary Views

The core innovation of this work lies in how the auxiliary view $v'$ is constructed. Instead of generic perturbations, the method generates $v'$ by specifically masking out the most salient object regions in the original image. This process leverages the inherent object-centric attention capabilities of self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs).

1. **Saliency Map Extraction**:
 - An input image $v \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ is fed into a pre-trained self-supervised ViT, specifically DINO (Caron et al., 2021). DINO is known for producing attention maps that are highly correlated with semantic segmentation masks without explicit supervision.
 - The attention of the `[CLS]` token on the heads of the *last layer* of the DINO ViT is extracted.
 - These attention maps from multiple heads are averaged.
 - The averaged map is reshaped to the patch grid size of the ViT and then upsampled to the original image dimensions $(H, W)$ to create a high-resolution saliency map, denoted as $\tilde{S}$. Higher values in $\tilde{S}$ indicate regions of greater visual prominence or evidence.

2. **Mask Generation**:
 - To create an auxiliary view that *removes* salient visual evidence, a binary mask is generated based on a quantile threshold of the saliency map $\tilde{S}$.
 - A parameter $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ specifies the desired area ratio of the region to be removed.
 - The quantile threshold $\lambda_\delta$ is calculated such that it corresponds to the $(1-\gamma)$-th quantile of the saliency values in $\tilde{S}$.
 - A binary mask $M_\delta$ is then constructed where pixels with saliency values above $\lambda_\delta$ (indicating the most salient regions) are marked for removal. Formally, $M_\delta = 1\{ \delta (\tilde{S} - \lambda_\delta) > 0 \}$, with $\delta = -1$ for removing the most salient region. (Note: $\delta=1$ would remove the least salient region, which would highlight evidence, as in some prior work).

3. **Auxiliary View Composition**:
 - Once the binary mask $M_\delta$ is obtained, the auxiliary view $v'$ is generated by replacing the masked-out salient regions with a neutral background.
 - Let $B$ represent the neutral background. The auxiliary view is computed as: $v' = M_\delta \odot B + (1 - M_\delta) \odot v$, where $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication.
 - The paper explores several choices for the neutral background $B$:
 - **Mean color**: The global mean color of the original image, per channel.
 - **Blur**: A spatially Gaussian blurred version of the original image, preserving low-frequency color and illumination.
 - **Black**: A constant field corresponding to pure black in the model's normalized RGB space.

This methodology aims to generate prompt-agnostic, semantically meaningful, and comprehensive object-level perturbations with a single cacheable forward pass, making it efficient and broadly applicable for VCD.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed Object-Aligned Visual Contrastive Decoding (OA-VCD) method on two popular object hallucination benchmarks and across two different Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs): LLaVA-v1.5 (7B) and Qwen-VL (7B). The method is compared against regular decoding, VCD with noise-based image perturbation (Leng et al., 2024), and AGLA (An et al., 2024).

#### 4.1. Performance on POPE Benchmark

The Polling-based Object Probing Evaluation (POPE) benchmark measures object existence hallucination using binary (Yes/No) queries across three negative-sampling regimes: Random, Popular, and Adversarial. The metrics reported are Accuracy and F1 score.

- **LLaVA-v1.5 (7B)**:
 - As shown in Table 1, OA-VCD consistently outperforms all baselines across all three POPE subsets.
 - On the Random subset, OA-VCD achieves an Accuracy of 89.5% and an F1 score of 88.5%, surpassing the strongest baseline, AGLA, which recorded 88.0% Accuracy and 86.9% F1. This represents a gain of 1.5% in Accuracy and 1.6% in F1 over AGLA.
 - Similar trends are observed for the Popular and Adversarial subsets, where OA-VCD registers 85.7% Accuracy (vs. AGLA's 85.1%) and 81.9% Accuracy (vs. AGLA's 81.2%), respectively. The F1 scores also show consistent improvements.
- **Qwen-VL (7B)**:
 - Table 2 indicates that OA-VCD also demonstrates consistent gains on Qwen-VL (7B).
 - For the Random subset, OA-VCD achieves 88.0% Accuracy and 86.5% F1, compared to AGLA's 87.4% Accuracy and 85.7% F1.
 - Improvements are maintained across Popular and Adversarial subsets, with OA-VCD showing slightly higher Accuracy and F1 scores than AGLA.

These results suggest that masking salient regions based on DINO attention provides a more effective contrast signal for VCD, leading to reduced object hallucination across different MLLMs and POPE settings.

#### 4.2. Performance on MME Hallucination Subset

The MME benchmark's hallucination subset evaluates object- and attribute-level hallucinations across categories such as Existence, Count, Position, and Color. The primary metric is Average Score (Accuracy + Accuracy+). Experiments were run with three random seeds, and average performance is reported.

- **LLaVA-v1.5 (7B)**:
 - Figure 3 illustrates that OA-VCD achieves the best average score across all categories for LLaVA-v1.5.
 - Specific categories show notable improvements: OA-VCD significantly outperforms baselines in "Existence" and "Color," which are often susceptible to spurious object correlations (Table 6 in Appendix). For instance, OA-VCD scores 195.00 in Existence (vs. AGLA's 181.67) and 165.00 in Color (vs. AGLA's 156.66).
- **Qwen-VL (7B)**:
 - Figure 4 and Table 7 show a similar pattern for Qwen-VL, with OA-VCD achieving the highest average score.
 - Improvements are observed across all categories, with "Existence" (173.33 vs. AGLA's 170.00) and "Color" (183.33 vs. AGLA's 178.33) again showing notable gains.

These findings reinforce the efficacy of the proposed method in mitigating diverse forms of object and attribute-level hallucinations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on the POPE MSCOCO subset with LLaVA-v1.5 (7B) to evaluate the impact of different parameters:

- **Neutral Backgrounds**: Table 3 investigates different choices for the neutral background (Blur, Black, Mean color) used to fill masked regions, with a fixed threshold $\gamma=0.8$.
 - All three background methods yielded comparable performance, with "Black" and "Mean color" slightly outperforming "Blur" in most settings (e.g., Black F1 of 90.7% vs. Mean F1 of 90.5% vs. Blur F1 of 89.4% for Random subset).
 - The results indicate robustness of the method to the specific choice of neutral background.
- **Thresholds ($\gamma$)**: Table 4 examines the impact of varying the area ratio $\gamma$ (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8) for removing salient regions, using the mean color background.
 - Performance remained largely consistent across different $\gamma$ values, with only modest changes. For the Random subset, F1 scores ranged from 90.0% to 90.5%.
 - This suggests that the method is not overly sensitive to the precise threshold parameter, indicating robustness.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Case Study

A qualitative case study (Figure 2) on the LLaVA-Bench demonstrates the method's effectiveness in generating more factually grounded captions. Regular decoding produced hallucinations such as "additional dogs" and "two people in the background" for an image containing only one dog in a painting. In contrast, OA-VCD successfully mitigated these hallucinations, producing a coherent and informative caption that accurately describes the image's content. This illustrates the method's ability to steer MLLM outputs away from spurious correlations and toward visually grounded descriptions.

In summary, the empirical evidence demonstrates that object-aligned auxiliary views, constructed by leveraging DINO attention, consistently yield a stronger and more semantically targeted contrast signal for VCD. This leads to reduced object hallucination across various evaluation metrics, MLLMs, and benchmark types, outperforming both generic VCD and more complex cross-modal masking approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by offering an effective and efficient method to mitigate object hallucination, a critical challenge in their deployment. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Factual Grounding and Reliability**: The primary impact is the improvement in the factual accuracy of MLLM outputs. By reducing object hallucinations, the proposed method increases the reliability and trustworthiness of MLLMs, making them more suitable for applications where factual consistency between generated text and visual input is paramount. This includes areas like automated image captioning, visual question answering, and content generation.
2. **Novel Auxiliary View Generation for VCD**: The work introduces a novel paradigm for constructing auxiliary views in Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD). Unlike previous methods that relied on heuristic image-level augmentations or prompt-dependent cross-modal signals, this approach leverages the object-centric attention properties of self-supervised Vision Transformers (specifically DINO) to create semantically meaningful, object-aligned perturbations. This represents a more targeted and intelligent way to generate counterfactual visual inputs.
3. **Prompt-Agnostic and Model-Agnostic Solution**: A key strength of the method is its independence from specific prompts or MLLM architectures. The DINO ViT used for saliency map generation is a general-purpose, self-supervised model. This "plug-and-play" capability means the method can be seamlessly integrated with various existing MLLMs and used across a wide range of tasks without requiring model-specific fine-tuning or modifications. This broad applicability increases its utility and potential for widespread adoption.
4. **Computational Efficiency**: The method boasts low computational overhead, requiring only a single cacheable forward pass through the DINO ViT to generate the auxiliary view. This efficiency makes it practical for real-world deployment, as it does not significantly increase inference time, a common concern with complex decoding strategies.
5. **Robustness to Parameters**: The ablation studies demonstrate that the method is relatively robust to variations in the masking threshold and the choice of neutral background filler. This indicates a stable performance profile, reducing the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning across different scenarios.
6. **Addressing Limitations of Prior Work**: By avoiding reliance on prompt-dependent cross-modal signals, the method circumvents potential issues of circularity where biased attention might guide masking. It also moves beyond generic, image-level perturbations to provide object-level insights into visual saliency, leading to a stronger and more relevant contrast signal.
7. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research into more sophisticated auxiliary view generation. The identified limitations, such as potential under/over-masking in cluttered scenes or the simplicity of neutral background filling, point towards exploring advanced techniques like diffusion-based image inpainting. This could further refine the quality of auxiliary views and potentially lead to even greater gains in hallucination mitigation.

In essence, this research significantly advances the field by providing a robust, efficient, and generalizable inference-time solution for a critical problem in MLLMs, thereby contributing to the development of more trustworthy and reliable multimodal AI systems.
