# Proprioceptive-only State Estimation for Legged Robots with Set-Coverage Measurements of Learned Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for proprioceptive-only state estimation in legged robots integrates learned dynamics-based pseudo-measurements into an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) using calibrated set-coverage statements, rather than a Gaussian noise assumption. This method maintains estimator consistency under non-Gaussian errors and achieves competitive accuracy with perception-based systems in challenging real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Exteroceptive sensors, crucial for robot state estimation, are vulnerable to degradation in challenging environments, necessitating robust proprioceptive-only alternatives.
- Traditional learning-based proprioceptive state estimators often assume Gaussian noise for learned pseudo-measurements, which frequently fails under realistic or out-of-distribution conditions, leading to filter inconsistency and divergence.
- Existing robust filtering methods that relax the Gaussian assumption can be computationally prohibitive for real-time, onboard deployment on resource-constrained legged robots.

## Method

- A proprioceptive-only state estimation framework, the **Set-Coverage IEKF**, is developed, built upon an ==Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF)==, to estimate robot base frame state.
- A learned dynamical observer (==GRU network==) generates instantaneous body-frame velocity pseudo-measurements from histories of joint angles, velocities, and torques.
- Instead of assuming Gaussian noise, a novel fusion method characterizes the uncertainty of these learned pseudo-measurements using distribution-free, calibrated ==set-coverage statements== and integrates them into the IEKF through a ==KL-minimal posterior update== with efficient ==Gaussian moment matching==.

## Results

- Monte-Carlo simulations showed that the set-coverage update maintained near-nominal consistency (**NEES ≈ 3**) under misspecified non-Gaussian errors, while the standard IEKF became severely inconsistent (**NEES ≈ 99**).
- Real-world experiments on Vision60 and Spot quadruped robots demonstrated significantly reduced absolute position error compared to other proprioceptive baselines, particularly in challenging terrains and during sharp maneuvers (e.g., APE_trans of **6.832 m** vs. **45.689 m** for Vel. IEKF on Spot's DOWNSTAIR sequence).
- The proposed method achieved accuracy competitive with, and occasionally surpassed, a perception-based odometry baseline (GaRLILEO), while maintaining computational efficiency with sub-millisecond update rates (approx. **0.07 ms** per update).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prediction errors from learned proprioceptive models often exhibit non-Gaussian, biased, or multimodal characteristics in real-world deployments, invalidating the standard Gaussian noise assumption and causing filter overconfidence.
> - Characterizing measurement uncertainty through set-coverage statements (e.g., error lies within specified bounds with a certain probability) provides a robust, distribution-free approach to uncertainty quantification for non-Gaussian errors.
> - A KL-minimal update strategy, implemented with efficient reduced-dimensional truncated moment matching, enables real-time incorporation of these set-coverage constraints into a Gaussian filter, effectively re-injecting uncertainty and maintaining consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate state estimation is a prerequisite for autonomous operation of legged robots, particularly in challenging, unstructured environments such as subterranean exploration or industrial inspection. Historically, state estimation for these robots has relied on a combination of exteroceptive sensors (e.g., LiDAR, cameras, radar) and proprioceptive sensors (e.g., Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) and joint-level measurements).

Exteroceptive sensors offer highly informative data but are susceptible to environmental degradation (e.g., dust, smoke, darkness, vegetation), leading to intermittent or biased perception and potential system failure. In contrast, proprioceptive sensing remains robust under these conditions and is routinely utilized for high-rate control. This distinction motivates the development of proprioceptive-only state estimation pipelines, which can maintain reliable tracking when exteroceptive data is unreliable or unavailable.

Traditional proprioceptive state estimation methods often fuse IMU data with leg kinematics using filtering (e.g., Kalman filters) or factor-graph approaches. These methods are effective when foot contacts are accurately detected and modeled, but their performance can degrade under imperfect contact conditions.

More recent approaches leverage the rich information embedded in the time histories of joint angles, velocities, and torques during locomotion. These learning-based methods train neural networks to extract motion-relevant patterns from proprioceptive histories, generating "pseudo-measurements" such as body-frame velocity or relative displacement. These pseudo-measurements are then fused with IMU data within a filter, treating the neural network as a "virtual sensor."

A common practice in these learning-based pipelines is to model the virtual sensor's prediction error as zero-mean Gaussian noise. This assumption facilitates integration with efficient Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) frameworks. However, this Gaussian assumption often breaks down in realistic deployments, especially when training data is limited or when the robot encounters conditions outside the training distribution. In such "limited-data regimes," epistemic uncertainty can dominate, leading to prediction errors that are biased, non-Gaussian, or multimodal. When these miscalibrated pseudo-measurements are treated as Gaussian, the filter can become overconfident, resulting in inconsistent estimates and potential divergence.

Existing methods that relax the Gaussian assumption, such as robust Kalman filtering, set-membership methods, or distributionally robust formulations, often involve trade-offs. Robust Kalman filters may handle outliers but are less effective against persistent biases. Set-membership methods provide state sets rather than point estimates, and their representation can become complex. Distributionally robust formulations can be computationally intensive, often requiring particle filtering.

This research aims to address the limitations of Gaussian noise assumptions in learning-based proprioceptive state estimation. It introduces a novel framework that avoids explicit Gaussian modeling for pseudo-measurement noise, opting instead for a distribution-free uncertainty representation. This positions the work as an advancement in developing robust, real-time, and computationally lightweight state estimators for legged robots operating in challenging and perceptually degraded environments, without sacrificing the point-estimate output typically preferred for control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research arises from the limitations of current state estimation techniques for legged robots, particularly in dynamic and perceptually challenging environments.

**Motivation:**

1. **Vulnerability of Exteroceptive Sensors:** While exteroceptive sensors (e.g., LiDAR, cameras) can provide highly informative measurements, they are prone to failure or degradation in adverse environmental conditions such as dust, smoke, darkness, or poor texture. This vulnerability necessitates robust fallback mechanisms for continuous autonomous operation.
2. **Robustness of Proprioceptive Sensing:** Proprioceptive sensors (IMU, joint encoders) are unaffected by external visual or range degradations and are crucial for stable robot control. Developing state estimation solely based on these sensors offers an inherently more robust solution for navigation in difficult conditions.
3. **Limitations of Traditional Proprioceptive Methods:** Classical proprioceptive state estimators often rely on accurate foot contact detection and modeling. Their performance can deteriorate under imperfect or ambiguous contact conditions, limiting their robustness in complex terrains.
4. **Fragility of Gaussian Noise Assumption in Learned Models:** Recent learning-based approaches utilize neural networks to infer navigational pseudo-measurements (e.g., body velocity) from proprioceptive histories. These methods commonly assume a Gaussian noise model for the network's prediction error to integrate with efficient Kalman filter frameworks. However, this assumption frequently breaks down under realistic conditions, especially with limited or unrepresentative training data. Such "misspecified" noise can be non-Gaussian, biased, or multimodal, leading to overconfident filter estimates, inconsistency, and potentially divergence.
5. **Need for Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification:** There is a need for methods that can robustly quantify and incorporate the uncertainty of learned pseudo-measurements without imposing restrictive parametric assumptions on their error distribution. These methods should ideally capture both aleatoric (inherent) and epistemic (model uncertainty due to limited data) uncertainties.
6. **Computational Efficiency for Onboard Deployment:** Any proposed state estimation solution for legged robots must be computationally inexpensive and capable of real-time execution to support high-rate control and continuous onboard operation. Existing robust filtering methods can be computationally prohibitive.

**Key Objectives:**

Based on these motivations, the authors established the following specific objectives:

1. **Develop a Novel Proprioceptive-Only State Estimation Framework:** To propose a state estimation framework for legged robots that models the uncertainty of learned dynamics-based pseudo-measurements using calibrated set-coverage statements, thereby explicitly avoiding the restrictive and often inaccurate Gaussian noise assumption.
2. **Formulate an Efficient Fusion Method:** To design a practical and computationally inexpensive method for systematically incorporating these distribution-free set-coverage measurements into a recursive Gaussian filtering pipeline, ensuring real-time applicability for onboard estimation.
3. **Validate Robustness and Consistency:** To extensively validate the proposed approach through both Monte-Carlo simulations and real-world quadruped experiments. This validation aims to demonstrate improved robustness under non-Gaussian error behaviors, prevent divergence observed with Gaussian baselines, and maintain consistency.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enhance the reliability and autonomy of legged robots in challenging environments where exteroceptive sensors fail and where traditional proprioceptive or Gaussian-assumption-based learning methods fall short.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed state estimation framework integrates learned proprioceptive dynamics into an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) structure, addressing the challenge of non-Gaussian measurement noise via set-coverage statements.

**A. Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) Foundation**

The work builds upon the IEKF framework for legged robots, which estimates the robot's base frame state relative to a global frame.

- **State Representation:** The robot's navigation state (orientation `GR`, velocity `GvI`, position `GpI`) is modeled as an element of the matrix Lie group SE2(3). This state `χ` is augmented with IMU biases `b` (accelerometer `ba` and gyroscope `bg`). The augmented error state `δX` is parameterized in the Lie algebra.
- **IMU Propagation:** The standard IEKF prediction step propagates the nominal state dynamics using IMU measurements `I˜a`, `I˜ω`, accounting for biases and Gaussian noise processes for acceleration, angular rate, and bias drift. The error-state covariance is propagated via linearization.
- **Invariant Measurement Output:** A right-invariant output `y_t = χ⁻¹_t d` is defined, where `d` is a known constant vector. For legged robots, this output directly relates to the body-frame velocity `IvI`. The measurement model is `y_t = χ⁻¹_t d + ν_t`, where `ν_t` represents measurement noise.

**B. Learned Proprioceptive Dynamics Model**

The core idea is to infer body-frame velocity (a navigational quantity) from joint-level proprioceptive signals, which do not directly measure it.

- **Proprioceptive Observation Model:** A history of joint angles (`θ`), velocities (`θ̇`), and torques (`τ`) is fed into a learned dynamical observer, `ϕψ`. This observer, implemented as a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network (chosen for its performance on limited data), updates an internal latent state `h` and outputs an instantaneous pseudo-measurement of the body-frame velocity, `I˜vI_t`.
- **Supervised Training:** The GRU network `ϕψ` is trained using supervised trajectories with ground-truth body-frame velocities obtained from motion capture or high-accuracy reference estimators. A single GRU layer with 64 hidden units is trained on sequences of 1000 steps (10ms time interval).
 - **Datasets:** Vision60 (internal dataset with motion-capture ground truth) and Spot (public dataset with high-precision perception-based ground truth) are used. For controlled training, a single representative trajectory is used from each dataset.
- **Gaussianity Failure:** The paper highlights that while prediction errors `e_t = I˜vI_t - IvI_t` might approximate a Gaussian distribution on training data, they often deviate significantly (e.g., multimodal, skewed, heavy-tailed) on unseen test data due to epistemic uncertainty and domain shift. This renders the standard Gaussian noise assumption for `νt` invalid.

**C. Error-State Coverage Statements (Novel Contribution)**

To address the limitations of Gaussian noise models, the authors introduce a distribution-free uncertainty characterization.

- **Set-Coverage Statements:** Instead of assuming a specific distribution, the prediction error `e_t` is characterized by a set-coverage statement: `P(|e_t| ≤ ϵ) ≥ γ`. Here, `|·|` denotes element-wise absolute value, `ϵ ∈ R³` are element-wise absolute bounds, and `γ ∈ (0,1)` is the desired confidence level. This statement guarantees that, with probability `γ`, the true error lies within the defined hyperrectangle `[-ϵ, ϵ]`.
- **Calibration:** These coverage bounds `ϵ` are obtained using distribution-free Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) procedures, such as conformal prediction or scenario optimization, applied post-hoc to a separate calibration dataset. This approach accommodates non-Gaussian, multimodal, and heavy-tailed error distributions without requiring costly retraining.
- **Error-State Feasible Set:** The velocity-error coverage statement is translated into an equivalent constraint on the augmented error state `δX_t`. Through first-order approximation of the right-invariant error, the coverage constraint `|e_t| ≤ ϵ` defines a feasible set `C_t` for `δX_t` as a box constraint (`l_t ≤ HδX_t ≤ u_t`), where `H` is a linear map. This ensures `P(δX_t ∈ C_t) ≥ γ`.

**D. Coverage-Constrained Update (Novel Contribution)**

The core mechanism for fusing the set-coverage measurement with the IEKF.

- **KL-Minimal Posterior Update:** Given a prior Gaussian estimate `N(sμ_t, sΣ_t)` for `δX_t`, the goal is to find a posterior distribution `p*(δX)` that: (i) assigns at least `γ` probability mass to the feasible set `C_t`, and (ii) minimally deviates from the prior in terms of Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence.
- **Theorem 1:** The paper presents a theorem showing that this KL-minimal posterior has a closed-form solution. If the prior probability mass within `C_t` (denoted `π_t`) is already greater than or equal to `γ`, the prior itself is the optimal posterior. If `π_t < γ`, the optimal posterior is a piecewise rescaling of the prior distribution, scaled up inside `C_t` and scaled down outside `C_t` to satisfy the `γ` coverage.
- **Gaussian Moment Matching:** Since the optimal posterior from Theorem 1 is generally non-Gaussian, a Gaussian approximation is restored by matching its first two moments (mean `μ+t` and covariance `Σ+t`). This involves computing truncated moments (mean and second moment) of the prior within `C_t` and its complement, and then combining them weighted by `γ` and `(1-γ)`.
- **Reduced-Dimensional Implementation:** To make the computations efficient, the calculations for `π_t` and truncated moments are performed in a reduced 3D space (`z_t = HδX_t`) rather than the full 15D state space. The prior in this `z`-space is also Gaussian. After obtaining the updated moments in `z`-space, they are lifted back to the full state space using standard Kalman gain-like formulas (`μ+t = sμ_t + K_t(μ+z,t - sμz,t)` and `Σ+t = sΣ_t + K_t(Σ+z,t - S_t)Kᵀ_t`).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The probability `π_t` and truncated moments are approximated using a randomized (quasi-)Monte Carlo estimator. Using 1000 samples, the method achieves sub-millisecond update rates (approximately 0.07 ms per update on a laptop CPU).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed proprioceptive-only state estimator included Monte-Carlo simulations and real-world experiments on quadruped robots.

**A. Monte-Carlo Simulations**

- **Computational Performance of Truncated Moment Computations:**
 - The randomized (quasi-)Monte Carlo estimator for Gaussian box probabilities and moments demonstrated a practical runtime-accuracy trade-off.
 - With N=1000 samples, the computations required approximately 0.0664 ms per update on an i9-13950HX CPU, with relative errors for probability, mean, and covariance moments being sufficiently small (e.g., 2.00e-5 for probability error, 1.48e-2 for mean error, 3.14e-2 for covariance error relative to a 10^7 sample reference). This confirms real-time applicability.
- **Filter Behavior Comparison (Consistency and Accuracy):**
 - **Unbiased Gaussian Regime:** When the measurement error was truly zero-mean Gaussian and correctly specified in the filter, the standard IEKF achieved the best accuracy (lowest RMSE) and near-nominal consistency (NEES ≈ 3). The set-coverage update showed higher RMSE in this ideal scenario, which is attributed to its weaker information utilization (only mass in the set, not the full likelihood shape).
 - **Misspecified Non-Gaussian (Biased) Regime:** When measurement errors were generated from a Gaussian mixture with non-zero component means (simulating persistent, trial-dependent bias and non-Gaussianity), the standard IEKF became severely inconsistent, exhibiting a very high NEES (e.g., 99.8873) due to overconfident corrections under an incorrect likelihood model. In contrast, the set-coverage update maintained near-nominal consistency across various confidence levels (γ) and remained competitive in terms of RMSE, preventing divergence. This robustness is attributed to its ability to re-inject uncertainty along constrained directions.

**B. Real-World Experiments**

The proposed method was evaluated on two real-world datasets, Vision60 and Spot, and compared against: (i) a contact-aided leg kinematics-based IEKF (Leg IEKF), (ii) a learned pseudo-measurement model with a Gaussian IEKF update (Vel. IEKF), and (iii) for the Spot dataset, a perception-based radar-leg-inertial odometry baseline (GaRLILEO).

- **Calibration and Coverage Statements:**
 - Error processes were assumed to be β-mixing, allowing for approximate independent calibration samples through subsampling.
 - Split-conformal prediction was used to obtain per-axis absolute error bounds (`ϵ`) for a joint confidence level (`γ=0.8`). Calibrated bounds were obtained as `ϵ = [0.05, 0.2, 0.05]ᵀ` for Vision60 and `ϵ = [0.22, 0.05, 0.1]ᵀ` for Spot.
- **Vision60 Dataset Results:**
 - The standard Leg IEKF exhibited significant vertical drift and high absolute position error (APE_trans).
 - The Vel. IEKF (Gaussian learned model) generally improved performance on flat ground.
 - The proposed set-coverage method yielded the lowest absolute position errors among the proprioceptive baselines (average APE_trans of 0.402 m compared to 1.130 m for Leg IEKF and 0.424 m for Vel. IEKF).
- **Spot Dataset Results:**
 - This dataset features diverse terrain, elevation changes, and long trajectories, posing significant challenges for proprioceptive-only odometry.
 - Proprioception-only baselines (Leg IEKF and Vel. IEKF) accumulated substantial drift during sharp turns and abrupt elevation changes (e.g., in DOWNSTAIR and OVERPASS sequences), with Vel. IEKF sometimes showing very high APE_trans (e.g., 45.689 m for DOWNSTAIR).
 - The proposed estimator remained accurate and stable in these challenging conditions, showing significantly reduced drift compared to other proprioceptive baselines. For instance, in DOWNSTAIR, its APE_trans was 6.832 m compared to 45.689 m for Vel. IEKF.
 - The proposed method achieved similar accuracy to the perception-based GaRLILEO baseline (average APE_trans of 2.736 m for ours vs. 2.523 m for GaRLILEO) and even surpassed it on the QUAD sequence (3.180 m vs. 7.347 m).
- **Overall Performance:** The results indicate that the proposed approach effectively handles non-Gaussian noise in learned pseudo-measurements, leading to superior robustness and consistency compared to existing proprioceptive methods, particularly in scenarios where sensor noise is misspecified. It also demonstrated competitive performance against perception-driven baselines. Approximately 70-95% of the updates actively used the coverage constraint (π < γ), confirming its frequent necessity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for proprioceptive-only state estimation that addresses a critical limitation in current learning-based approaches for legged robots: the assumption of Gaussian noise in learned measurement models. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Degraded Environments:** By avoiding the restrictive Gaussian noise assumption and utilizing distribution-free set-coverage statements, the proposed framework offers a more robust state estimation solution. This directly benefits legged robots operating in perceptually degraded conditions (e.g., darkness, dust, smoke, uniform textures) where exteroceptive sensors fail, ensuring continuous and reliable navigation.
2. **Improved Consistency and Prevention of Divergence:** The work demonstrates that under misspecified non-Gaussian noise conditions, the proposed method maintains estimator consistency, whereas traditional Gaussian-assuming filters (e.g., IEKF with learned pseudo-measurements) become overconfident and prone to divergence. This consistency is crucial for long-term autonomous operations and safe robot deployment.
3. **Principled Uncertainty Quantification for Learned Models:** The method provides a principled way to quantify and integrate the uncertainty from learned dynamics models, especially when training data is limited or when the robot encounters out-of-distribution scenarios. This is a significant advancement for incorporating machine learning components into safety-critical robotic systems.
4. **Computational Efficiency for Onboard Deployment:** The developed fusion technique, leveraging reduced-dimensional computations and quasi-Monte Carlo integration, is computationally inexpensive (sub-millisecond update rates). This makes it suitable for real-time, onboard state estimation, a practical requirement for autonomous mobile robots.
5. **Broader Applicability to Robotic State Estimation:** While demonstrated on legged robots, the concept of using set-coverage statements for measurement uncertainty and integrating them into a Gaussian filter via KL-minimal projection and moment matching has potential applicability to other robotic platforms or estimation problems where learned measurement models exhibit non-Gaussian errors.
6. **Competitive Performance with Perception-Based Systems:** The real-world experiments show that this proprioceptive-only approach can achieve accuracy competitive with, and in some cases superior to, perception-based odometry baselines. This suggests that robust proprioceptive estimation can reduce reliance on complex and vulnerable exteroceptive sensor suites, or serve as a highly reliable fallback.
7. **Foundation for Future Multi-Sensor Fusion:** The authors indicate future work on extending this framework to include exteroceptive measurements. This suggests that the robust handling of proprioceptive uncertainty could form a strong foundation for more resilient multi-sensor fusion systems, where individual sensor modalities might have complex, non-Gaussian error characteristics.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to the field of robust state estimation by providing a practical and principled method to integrate learned, non-Gaussian measurement models into recursive filters, thereby enhancing the reliability and autonomy of legged robots in challenging and unpredictable environments.
