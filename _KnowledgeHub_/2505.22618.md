# Fast-dLLM: Training-free Acceleration of Diffusion LLM by Enabling KV Cache and Parallel Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Fast-dLLM introduces training-free methods to accelerate Diffusion-based Large Language Models by enabling KV cache and confidence-aware parallel decoding. The approach achieves up to 27.6x throughput improvement on benchmarks like GSM8K and MBPP, while maintaining generation quality, making Diffusion LLMs more competitive for practical deployment.

## Problem

- Diffusion-based Large Language Models (LLMs) lack a standard Key-Value (KV) cache, leading to redundant computations due to their bidirectional attention mechanism.
- Direct parallel decoding in Diffusion LLMs often causes a drop in generation quality due to the "conditional independence assumption" in sampling procedures.
- Open-sourced Diffusion LLMs struggle to achieve the high inference speeds implied by their theoretical non-autoregressive nature, unlike highly optimized autoregressive models.

## Method

- Introduces a ==block-wise approximate Key-Value (KV) Cache== adapted for the bidirectional attention of Diffusion LLMs, reusing activations to reduce redundant computations.
- Proposes a ==confidence-aware parallel decoding mechanism== that dynamically selects tokens for unmasking based on their predicted confidence scores, improving generation quality during parallel inference.
- The entire framework is designed to be ==training-free==, allowing direct application to existing pre-trained Diffusion LLMs for immediate acceleration.

## Results

- Achieves up to **27.6x** throughput improvement on Diffusion LLMs (e.g., LLaDA on GSM8K) compared to vanilla baselines.
- Maintains generation quality with negligible accuracy loss (within **1-2** percentage points) across all evaluated text-only and multimodal benchmarks.
- The combination of KV Cache and parallel decoding provides the highest speedups, with KV Cache alone contributing **2x-3.6x** and parallel decoding adding further acceleration, extending benefits to multimodal tasks with up to **9.9x** speedup for LLaDA-V.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - KV activations exhibit high similarity across adjacent inference steps within a block, enabling effective approximate caching for bidirectional attention models.
> - Greedy parallel decoding can yield the same optimal sequence as greedy sequential decoding when token confidences are sufficiently high, providing a theoretical basis for confidence-aware parallel generation.
> - Dynamically adjusting the degree of parallelism based on token confidence mitigates the "curse of parallel decoding" without requiring complex auxiliary models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been predominantly shaped by autoregressive (AR) models, which generate text token by token in a sequential manner. While incredibly powerful, AR models face inherent limitations in inference speed due to their sequential nature, making parallel generation challenging. To mitigate this, AR models heavily rely on Key-Value (KV) caching to reuse attention computations from previously generated tokens, significantly accelerating subsequent steps.

Recently, Diffusion-based Large Language Models (Diffusion LLMs) have emerged as an exciting alternative. Drawing inspiration from successes in image and audio generation, these models leverage a denoising process to iteratively refine noisy (masked) text into coherent sequences. A key theoretical advantage of Diffusion LLMs is their potential for non-autoregressive, parallel token generation. Early demonstrations, such as Mercury (>1,000 tokens/second) and Google DeepMind's Gemini Diffusion (>1,400 tokens/second), have showcased impressive potential throughput, far exceeding typical AR models.

However, despite this theoretical promise, practical implementations of open-sourced Diffusion LLMs have struggled to achieve comparable inference speeds. This gap arises from two primary challenges:
1. **Lack of KV Cache:** Unlike AR models, the bidirectional attention mechanism inherent in diffusion models (where all tokens attend to each other, including future masked tokens) fundamentally complicates the direct application of a standard KV cache. This leads to redundant computations during iterative denoising steps.
2. **Quality Degradation in Parallel Decoding:** While Diffusion LLMs are designed for parallel generation, directly sampling multiple tokens simultaneously often leads to a significant drop in generation quality. This is due to the "conditional independence assumption" often made in their sampling procedures, which overlooks the subtle yet crucial interdependencies between tokens. Attempting to generate tokens like "high house" instead of "high card" due to independent sampling exemplifies this "curse of parallel decoding."

Fast-dLLM directly addresses these two critical bottlenecks. It aims to bridge the performance gap between the theoretical potential of Diffusion LLMs and their practical inference speed, making them viable and efficient alternatives to AR models. By tackling the KV cache limitation and the quality degradation in parallel decoding, Fast-dLLM seeks to unlock the full inference acceleration potential of Diffusion LLMs, enabling their practical deployment in real-world applications where speed and quality are paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Fast-dLLM is to achieve significant, training-free acceleration of Diffusion-based Large Language Models (Diffusion LLMs) while preserving their generation quality. The motivation stems from the observed discrepancy between the theoretical potential for high-speed, non-autoregressive generation in Diffusion LLMs and their actual performance in open-source implementations.

Specifically, the authors are motivated by two key practical issues:
1. **Addressing the Absence of Key-Value (KV) Cache:** Autoregressive (AR) LLMs heavily rely on KV cache to store and reuse attention computations, dramatically speeding up inference by avoiding redundant calculations for previously processed tokens. Diffusion LLMs, due to their unique bidirectional attention mechanisms, have historically lacked such a mechanism. This forces the model to recompute attention over the entire sequence at each denoising step, leading to substantial computational overhead and hindering practical deployment. The objective here is to introduce a KV cache mechanism specifically adapted for the bidirectional nature of diffusion models.
2. **Mitigating Quality Degradation in Parallel Decoding:** The inherent parallelism of Diffusion LLMs, allowing multiple tokens to be generated simultaneously, is their most promising feature for speed. However, prior work and empirical observations (e.g., LLaDA) have shown that aggressively decoding multiple tokens in parallel often leads to a noticeable drop in generation quality. The authors identify the root cause as the violation of critical token dependencies under the conditional independence assumption used in many multi-token prediction procedures. Their objective is to devise a parallel decoding strategy that intelligently leverages parallelism without compromising the coherence and quality of the generated text.

By tackling these two intertwined challenges, Fast-dLLM aims to enable open-sourced Diffusion LLMs to close the performance gap with highly optimized AR models, thereby making Diffusion LLMs a more competitive and practical choice for high-throughput text generation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Fast-dLLM's methodology integrates two novel, complementary strategies to accelerate Diffusion LLM inference: a block-wise approximate Key-Value (KV) Cache and a confidence-aware parallel decoding mechanism. The entire pipeline is designed to be training-free, meaning it can be applied directly to existing pre-trained Diffusion LLMs.

#### 3.1. Key-Value Cache for Block-Wise Decoding

The primary challenge in applying KV Cache to Diffusion LLMs lies in their full, bidirectional attention, where each token attends to all others. This differs from autoregressive models where attention is typically causal (tokens only attend to preceding ones). Fast-dLLM addresses this by adopting a **block-wise generation manner** and introducing an *approximate* KV Cache.

The mechanism works as follows:
- **Block-wise Generation:** The output sequence is divided into blocks. The model generates tokens for one block at a time.
- **Initial Cache Computation:** For the initial prompt and the first block, the KV cache is computed and stored.
- **Cache Reuse within Block:** During the multiple denoising steps required to generate tokens *within* a block, the KV activations for the parts of the sequence *outside* the current block (i.e., the prompt and previously generated blocks, or other masked blocks in a bidirectional context) are reused from the stored cache. This significantly reduces redundant computations.
- **Cache Update after Block Completion:** Once a block is fully decoded (or sufficiently refined), the KV cache for *all* tokens (including the newly generated ones in the completed block) is updated. This update can be efficiently fused with a subsequent decoding step, incurring negligible overhead compared to recomputing everything.
- **Justification for Approximation:** The effectiveness of this approximate cache stems from empirical observations (visualized in Figure 3) showing that KV activations exhibit "high similarity" across adjacent inference steps, especially within a block. This implies that reusing the cache introduces minimal approximation error, thereby preserving model performance.
- **DualCache Extension:** Fast-dLLM further proposes a "DualCache" variant. While the basic approach primarily focuses on caching "prefix" tokens (prompt + previously generated blocks), DualCache extends this to also cache "suffix" tokens, which are initially all masked tokens under the block-wise scheme. This bidirectional caching further maximizes computational reuse and acceleration.

#### 3.2. Confidence-Aware Parallel Decoding

The "curse of parallel decoding" in Diffusion LLMs arises because the current multi-token prediction procedures often sample tokens independently based on their marginal probabilities, ignoring potential interdependencies in the true joint distribution (e.g., "high card" where "high" and "card" are correlated). To address this without introducing complex auxiliary models, Fast-dLLM proposes a "confidence-aware" strategy.

The core idea is to dynamically select which tokens can be "safely" decoded in parallel based on their predicted confidence:
- **Confidence Scoring:** At each iterative denoising step, for all currently masked tokens, the model computes a confidence score (e.g., the maximum softmax probability for a token).
- **Threshold-based Selection:** Only those masked tokens whose confidence score exceeds a predefined *global threshold* are selected to be unmasked and sampled in the current step. Tokens with lower confidence remain masked and are reconsidered in subsequent steps.
- **Progress Guarantee:** To prevent infinite loops if no tokens meet the threshold, a fallback mechanism ensures that at least the token with the highest confidence is always unmasked.
- **Theoretical Justification (Theorem 1):** The paper provides a formal theorem that characterizes the conditions under which greedy parallel decoding (sampling from the product of marginals) is equivalent to greedy sequential decoding (sampling from the true joint distribution) in a "high-confidence regime."
 - **Equivalence Condition:** The theorem states that if for each token, the model's confidence in the chosen token `x*` is sufficiently high (specifically, `p_j(X_i_j = x_i_j | E) > 1 - ε` and `(n+1)ε <= 1`, where `n` is the number of tokens and `ε` is a small error term), then greedy parallel decoding yields the same optimal sequence as greedy sequential decoding. This provides a mathematical basis for the thresholding strategy.
 - **Distance and Divergence Bounds:** The theorem also quantifies the L_p distance and KL divergence between the true joint distribution and the product of marginals, showing that these divergences are bounded and small when the confidence is high.
- **Factor-based Parallel Decoding:** As an extension, Fast-dLLM introduces a "factor-based" adaptive strategy. Instead of a fixed threshold, this method dynamically determines the number of tokens to decode (`n`) by sorting token confidences and selecting the largest `n` such that `(n+1)(1 - c^(n)) < f`, where `f` is a fixed hyperparameter and `c^(n)` is the `n`-th highest confidence. This formulation directly leverages the theoretical bound to adaptively control parallelism.

Algorithm 1 provides a comprehensive overview of how these two strategies (KV Cache and Confidence-Aware Parallel Decoding) are integrated into a block-wise pipeline. By combining these methods, Fast-dLLM effectively balances speed and quality, addressing the main practical impediments to Diffusion LLM deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Fast-dLLM's experimental evaluation demonstrates significant performance improvements across multiple Diffusion LLM architectures and benchmarks, achieving substantial speedups with minimal impact on generation quality.

**Experimental Setup:** All experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU. Hyperparameters for KV Cache (block size 4-32) and parallel decoding (confidence threshold 0.5-1.0) were tuned. The default configuration used PrefixCache with a block size of 32 and a threshold of 0.9.

**Models and Benchmarks:**
- **Text-only Models:** LLaDA, LLaDA-1.5, and Dream-Base.
- **Text-only Benchmarks:** GSM8K (arithmetic reasoning), MATH (advanced math problems), HumanEval (code generation), and MBPP (code completion).
- **Multimodal Model:** LLaDA-V (vision-language variant of LLaDA).
- **Multimodal Benchmarks:** MathVista and MathVerse (math problems grounded in visual scenes).

**Key Performance and Speed Results:**
- **Overall Throughput Improvements:** Fast-dLLM achieves remarkable speedups, peaking at **27.6x throughput improvement** on LLaDA (8-shot, generation length 1024 on GSM8K) compared to the vanilla baseline, with only a negligible accuracy loss.
- **Individual Component Contributions:**
 - **KV Cache Alone:** Introduces substantial speed improvements, typically yielding a **2x to 3.6x speedup** across tasks and sequence lengths.
 - **Parallel Decoding Alone:** Provides additional acceleration, often pushing speedups to **4x–6x**, particularly for longer generation lengths.
- **Combined Effect (Fast-dLLM):** The combination of KV Cache and parallel decoding leads to highly pronounced improvements. For instance:
 - On LLaDA, throughput increased by up to **11x** (GSM8K, length 512) and **9.2x** (MBPP, length 512).
 - On Dream-Base, peak gains were **7.8x** (MBPP, length 512) and **5.6x** (GSM8K, length 512).
- **Accuracy Preservation:** Crucially, these efficiency gains are achieved with **negligible impact on accuracy**. Across all benchmarks and settings, the accuracy of Fast-dLLM remains within 1-2 percentage points of the original backbone, and in some cases, a slight accuracy improvement is even observed. This validates the effectiveness of the approximate caching and confidence-aware decoding.
- **Scalability with Length and Prefill:** Longer sequences and higher prefill lengths (e.g., 8-shot vs. 5-shot) proportionally benefit more from Fast-dLLM's techniques, indicating that the methods effectively amortize computation over larger contexts. DualCache, which caches both prefix and suffix tokens, consistently outperforms PrefixCache for longer generation lengths.
- **Multimodal Performance (LLaDA-V):** Fast-dLLM extends its benefits to multimodal tasks. On MathVista and MathVerse, it achieves up to **9.9x speedup** with minimal accuracy degradation. The paper notes LLaDA-V's sensitivity to small block sizes, leading to a "refresh-based" caching strategy for this model, updating the cache every few steps with a full block length (96 tokens) rather than smaller blocks.

**Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
- **Cache Block Size:** Analysis shows a trade-off: smaller blocks maximize accuracy but incur overhead, while larger blocks reduce accuracy but improve throughput. A block size of 32 was identified as the optimal trade-off point.
- **Dynamic Threshold vs. Fixed Tokens-per-Step:** The confidence-aware, dynamic thresholding strategy consistently outperforms fixed tokens-per-step baselines. It achieves higher accuracy with comparable or fewer inference steps, demonstrating adaptive parallelism.
- **Factor-based Decoding:** This adaptive strategy proves to be even more effective than the fixed threshold, yielding **1.4-1.5x higher throughput** while maintaining competitive accuracy, showcasing its robustness and efficiency.
- **Parallel Token Counts:** An analysis of dynamic parallelism reveals that the average number of tokens generated in parallel peaks during the middle stages of decoding (steps 30-60) when the model is most confident, and then slightly declines towards the end.
- **Throughput vs. Batch Size:** While PrefixCache significantly accelerates LLaDA (up to 5x), especially at smaller batch sizes, Diffusion LLMs still struggle to match the high absolute throughput of autoregressive models like LLaMA at very large batch sizes. This is attributed to the inherent compute-bound nature of diffusion models compared to LLaMA's transition from memory-bound to compute-bound performance at scale. However, Fast-dLLM still substantially closes the gap for practical deployment scenarios.

In summary, Fast-dLLM provides robust, quantifiable evidence that its proposed techniques can effectively address the core performance limitations of Diffusion LLMs, making them significantly faster and more viable for real-world applications without compromising their generation quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Fast-dLLM represents a significant advancement in the practical deployment and usability of Diffusion-based Large Language Models (Diffusion LLMs). Its contributions have profound implications across several dimensions:

**1. Enabling Practical Deployment of Diffusion LLMs:** Historically, the substantial gap between the theoretical parallelism of Diffusion LLMs and their actual, sub-optimal inference speeds in open-source implementations has been a major barrier to widespread adoption. Fast-dLLM directly addresses this by introducing critical optimizations that deliver **up to 27.6x speedup** with negligible accuracy loss. This level of acceleration makes Diffusion LLMs genuinely competitive with, or even superior to, highly optimized autoregressive (AR) models in terms of inference throughput, especially for longer generation sequences and multi-turn interactions. This achievement moves Diffusion LLMs from a promising research concept to a practical, deployable technology.

**2. Bridging the Performance Gap with Autoregressive Models:** For a long time, the efficiency and quality of AR models, particularly with the benefit of KV Cache, set the standard for LLM deployment. Fast-dLLM effectively "closes this performance gap" by ingeniously adapting KV caching mechanisms to the bidirectional nature of diffusion models and by proposing a theoretically grounded, confidence-aware parallel decoding strategy. This means that developers and researchers now have a viable, highly efficient non-autoregressive alternative that can handle complex language generation tasks.

**3. Generalizability Across Models and Modalities:** The demonstrated effectiveness of Fast-dLLM across different Diffusion LLM architectures (LLaDA, Dream), diverse task types (math reasoning, code generation, general language understanding), and even multimodal scenarios (LLaDA-V on vision-language tasks like MathVista and MathVerse) highlights the broad applicability and robustness of the proposed framework. This generalizability is crucial for a new optimization technique, suggesting it can benefit a wide range of current and future diffusion-based generative models.

**4. Advancing Non-Autoregressive Generation:** Fast-dLLM significantly contributes to the ongoing research in non-autoregressive (NAR) generation. Prior NAR methods often faced a trade-off between speed and generation quality. By identifying and mitigating the "curse of parallel decoding" through confidence-aware selection and providing a theoretical basis (Theorem 1) for when parallel decoding is safe, the paper pushes the boundaries of what is possible with NAR models. It shows that high-quality parallel generation is achievable without resorting to complex, computationally expensive auxiliary models.

### 6. Impact on Research and Development

 - **New Research Avenues:** The success of Fast-dLLM will likely spur further research into optimizing diffusion-based models, exploring more advanced caching strategies, and refining adaptive parallel decoding algorithms.
 - **Resource Efficiency:** Achieving high throughput without requiring additional training (training-free) means that existing large Diffusion LLMs can immediately benefit from these optimizations, reducing the computational resources and time required for deployment. This is particularly valuable for organizations without vast training compute resources.
 - **Industry Adoption:** NVIDIA's direct involvement strongly suggests that these techniques are engineered with real-world industry applications in mind. Faster and more efficient LLMs are critical for scaling AI services, reducing operational costs, and enabling new real-time applications in areas like interactive AI, conversational agents, and creative content generation.

In conclusion, Fast-dLLM is a pivotal work that not only solves critical practical challenges in Diffusion LLM inference but also fundamentally shifts the perception of these models from theoretical curiosities to highly efficient, deployable alternatives to autoregressive LLMs. Its innovations pave the way for broader adoption and continued advancements in the field of generative AI.
