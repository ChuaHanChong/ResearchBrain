# CLiViS: Unleashing Cognitive Map through Linguistic-Visual Synergy for Embodied Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> CLiViS introduces a training-free framework for embodied visual reasoning, synergistically combining Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) via a dynamic Cognitive Map. The method achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across benchmarks, including 55.4% on OpenEQA, 23.0% on EgoTempo, and 69.4% on EgoSchema, demonstrating enhanced performance with longer video durations and robustness across various VLM backbones.

## Problem

- Embodied Visual Reasoning (EVR) tasks struggle with accurate spatiotemporal perception from long, egocentric videos due to limited fields of view and complex dynamics.
- Complex, free-form user instructions in EVR demand sophisticated compositional reasoning that existing methods often lack.
- Prior Socratic-based approaches lose crucial fine-grained visual details through fixed captions, while end-to-end VLMs generally lack robust high-level logical planning capabilities.

## Method

- ==CLiViS== is a ==training-free framework== orchestrating an iterative linguistic-visual synergy between LLMs (for reasoning) and VLMs (for perception).
- It dynamically constructs and updates a =='Cognitive Map'== (composed of a ==Scene Navigation Graph== and an ==Object Relation Graph==) and an 'Evidence Memory' module to represent the evolving embodied scene.
- The LLM generates precise sub-instructions for the VLM to perform focused perception on specific video segments, then parses VLM responses to update the cognitive map and evidence memory.

## Results

- CLiViS achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy across all evaluated benchmarks: **55.4%** on OpenEQA, **23.0%** on EgoTempo, and **69.4%** on EgoSchema, with an overall average of **48.4%**.
- The framework outperformed Socratic-based models by **20.2%** and end-to-end VLMs by **2.9%**, showcasing superior logical planning and retention of visual detail.
- It demonstrated more pronounced improvements with increasing video duration (e.g., a **6.5%** accuracy gain for videos ">= 30s" on OpenEQA) and exhibited model-agnostic effectiveness across various VLM backbones.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synergistically combining LLMs for high-level reasoning and VLMs for fine-grained perception effectively bridges the perception-reasoning gap in embodied visual reasoning.
> - A dynamic and structured 'Cognitive Map' provides an evolving semantic representation of the scene, crucial for integrating long-term spatiotemporal cues and supporting compositional reasoning.
> - Iterative LLM-VLM dialogue, guided by prompt engineering, enables robust multi-step inference, enhanced interpretability, and a self-correcting mechanism for VLM-generated descriptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Embodied Visual Reasoning (EVR), also known as Embodied Question Answering (EQA). EVR focuses on enabling intelligent agents to interpret and act upon complex, free-form instructions by processing egocentric video input. This capability is relevant for applications in robotics and autonomous driving, requiring semantic understanding and spatiotemporal reasoning in dynamic environments from a first-person perspective.

The current landscape of EVR research faces two primary challenges:
1. **Spatiotemporal Perception:** Egocentric videos are typically long, feature dynamic scenes, and have a limited field of view, making it difficult to extract relevant spatiotemporal information.
2. **Compositional Reasoning:** User instructions are often complex and diverse, demanding models that can break down intricate queries into a series of logical steps and integrate visual and linguistic cues coherently.

Existing methodological approaches in EVR generally fall into two categories, each with its own limitations:
- **Socratic-based strategies:** These methods employ a two-stage process. A video captioning model first translates visual content into textual descriptions, which are then fed, along with the instruction, to Large Language Models (LLMs) for reasoning. While these approaches leverage the semantic reasoning capabilities of LLMs, they often suffer from information loss due to fixed, instruction-agnostic captions that may omit fine-grained, context-sensitive visual details crucial for complex tasks.
- **End-to-end Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** These models aim to integrate visual perception and reasoning within a single framework by fusing vision and language modalities at the feature level. This design mitigates information loss inherent in cross-modal conversion and offers open-vocabulary perception capabilities. However, current VLMs typically exhibit deficiencies in high-level logical planning and structured semantic reasoning, hindering their ability to perform multi-step compositional reasoning (e.g., event localization, object recognition, relation extraction) required for complex instructions.
- **Recent Video Reasoning Models:** Some recent efforts (e.g., Video-R1, VideoAgent, VideoTree) have attempted to enhance video understanding and reasoning by dynamically sampling frames or employing rule-based learning. However, these often involve substantial training costs or reduce the LLM to a passive selector, thereby not fully exploiting its potential for sophisticated planning and structured reasoning.

CLiViS is introduced to address these limitations by leveraging the complementary strengths of LLMs for high-level reasoning and VLMs for detailed visual perception. It proposes a training-free framework that aims to bridge the gap between low-level visual input and high-level semantic reasoning through an iteratively evolving structured representation of the scene, referred to as a Cognitive Map. This approach seeks to provide a more balanced and effective solution for Embodied Visual Reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop a robust and interpretable framework for Embodied Visual Reasoning (EVR) that effectively addresses the dual challenges of precise visual perception and complex compositional reasoning in dynamic egocentric video environments. The motivation stems from the observed limitations of prior EVR methodologies:

1. **To overcome the information bottleneck of static video captions:** Existing Socratic-based approaches, which convert video into static text descriptions, frequently discard fine-grained visual details essential for accurate reasoning. A core motivation is to enable task-oriented, flexible visual perception that can adapt to specific instructional queries, thereby ensuring that relevant visual information is consistently leveraged.

2. **To enhance the high-level reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** While end-to-end VLMs excel at open-vocabulary perception, they often struggle with complex logical planning, multi-step inference, and structured reasoning required to fully understand and execute elaborate instructions. The research aims to integrate the robust reasoning and planning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to guide VLM-based perception, thereby overcoming this limitation without extensive training.

3. **To establish a dynamic and structured scene representation:** Traditional methods often lack an organized, evolving understanding of the embodied scene. A key objective is to design a "Cognitive Map" that serves as a dynamic, graph-based representation of regions, entities, actions, and their relationships. This map is intended to continuously update throughout the reasoning process, providing a rich, structured context that bridges low-level visual input and high-level linguistic reasoning. This dynamic nature is crucial for handling the temporal complexities and evolving states within egocentric videos.

4. **To improve the interpretability of the reasoning process:** Complex AI systems often operate as "black boxes," making their decision-making opaque. The researchers aim to introduce an "Evidence Memory" module that explicitly accumulates instruction-relevant rationales extracted at each reasoning step. This module is designed to provide transparent decision paths, enhancing the interpretability and auditability of the model's conclusions.

5. **To develop a training-free and generalizable framework:** The goal is to create a solution that can leverage pre-trained LLMs and VLMs without requiring additional task-specific training. This emphasis on a training-free design aims to enhance the framework's generalizability across various benchmarks and reduce computational overhead, making it adaptable to diverse embodied visual reasoning tasks.

In summary, the motivation behind CLiViS is to synergize the strengths of LLMs and VLMs to create an iterative, interpretable, and comprehensive EVR framework capable of accurate perception and complex, multi-step reasoning over long-term egocentric videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLiViS (Cognitive map through Linguistic-Visual Synergy) is a novel training-free framework designed for Embodied Visual Reasoning. It operates by orchestrating a synergistic collaboration between Large Language Models (LLMs) for high-level planning and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for open-world visual perception. The core of its approach is a dynamic Cognitive Map that evolves throughout the reasoning process, providing a structured representation of the embodied scene.

The inference process of CLiViS unfolds through three main stages:

#### 3.1. Cognitive and Memory Initialization

1. **Video Segmentation:** The input egocentric video (V) is first segmented into fixed-length clips (e.g., 30 seconds).
2. **Coarse-grained Description:** For each video segment, the VLM is invoked to generate coarse-grained visual descriptions.
3. **Initial Information Extraction:** The LLM analyzes these descriptions to extract salient entities (objects, regions, persons), actions, and their inter-entity relationships.
4. **Key Entity Identification:** Conditioned on the language instruction (I), the LLM further identifies and marks the entities most relevant to the question.
5. **Map and Memory Construction:** An initial Cognitive Map (M) is constructed, encompassing entities with their attributes, actions, and relations. Simultaneously, an Evidence Memory (E) module is initialized to store the original question, interaction history, and any task-relevant rationales extracted from this initial phase.

#### 3.2. Linguistic-Visual Synergy and Cognitive Update (Iterative Reasoning Loop)

This stage involves an iterative interaction between the LLM and VLM, dynamically refining the scene understanding:

1. **Information Sufficiency Assessment:** The LLM integrates the current state of the Cognitive Map (M) and Evidence Memory (E) to assess whether sufficient information has been gathered to answer the instruction (I).
2. **Sub-Instruction Generation:** If the information is deemed insufficient, the LLM generates a focused sub-instruction (T_i) for a specific temporal video segment (V_T_i). This sub-instruction guides the VLM to perform targeted perception, such as localizing an object, recognizing an action, or describing a relation. The LLM acts as a planner, decomposing the main task into a sequence of subtasks.
3. **Task-Oriented Visual Perception:** The VLM executes the sub-instruction on the specified video segment, extracting task-relevant visual cues.
4. **Parsing and Update:** The responses from the VLM are then parsed by the LLM to extract new key entities, relations, and supporting rationales. This new information is used to update both the Cognitive Map (M) and the Evidence Memory (E).

 - **Cognitive Map Update:** The `Update(M, VLM_output)` function integrates new information into the graph structure. It identifies new entities, relations, or actions absent in the existing map. Conflict resolution is handled by a temporal precedence principle, where newer VLM observations override older, contradictory information. Key entities relevant to the question are continuously managed to maintain focus.
 - **Evidence Memory Update:** The `Update(E, VLM_output)` function distills the VLM's response into an "evidence atom"—a language rationale about the query, its associated time span, and the involved objects, regions, or actions. This accumulates high-level semantic cues relevant to the instruction.

#### 3.3. Integrated Reasoning and Answer Generation

1. **Final Assessment:** The iterative loop continues until the LLM determines that enough information has been collected to answer the question, or a predefined maximum number of iterations is reached.
2. **Response Generation:** The LLM then integrates the complete reasoning context derived from the updated Cognitive Map (M) and Evidence Memory (E) to generate the final response (R) to the original instruction. If the LLM output is not the final answer, it is interpreted as the next subtask.

#### 3.4. Cognitive Map and Evidence Memory Structure

- **Cognitive Map (M):** Defined as `M = {G_nav, G_rel}`.
 - **Navigation Graph (G_nav):** A graph capturing the temporal structure of the video. Nodes represent distinct time segments, and edges indicate temporal adjacency. Each node records areas, entities, actions, and captions within its segment.
 - **Relation Graph (G_rel):** A graph modeling fine-grained entity-level relationships. Nodes represent visual entities or actions, and edges denote semantic relations (e.g., spatial, agent-object interaction, functional dependency). It records dense object attributes and semantic interactions.
- **Evidence Memory (E):** A lightweight buffer storing high-level semantic cues in the form of evidence atoms `(r, τ, O)`, where `r` is a language rationale, `τ` is the associated time span, and `O` is the set of involved objects, regions, or actions.

#### 3.5. Prompt Design for Synergy

Meticulously designed prompts orchestrate the LLM-VLM collaboration at each critical stage. These prompts guide the VLM in generating descriptions, the LLM in initializing and updating the cognitive map and evidence memory, and in generating targeted sub-instructions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness and generality of CLiViS were evaluated through extensive experiments across multiple egocentric video question answering benchmarks and foundation models.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Benchmarks:**
 - **OpenEQA:** Over 1,600 QA pairs from real-world environments, evaluated on a subset of 1,079 QA pairs with ScanNet videos.
 - **EgoSchema:** Built on Ego4D, consisting of 3-minute egocentric clips. Evaluated on the official validation set of 500 questions.
 - **EgoTempo:** A benchmark for temporal reasoning with 500 QA cases across 10 categories, designed for fine-grained assessment of dynamic visual reasoning in long-term egocentric videos.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard accuracy for all methods. For multiple-choice EgoSchema, overall accuracy. For open-ended OpenEQA and EgoTempo, responses are scored against reference answers using Qwen2.5-Max on a 5-point Likert scale, with scores ≥ 4 considered correct. Videos were further split into short (< 30 sec) and long (≥ 30 sec) groups to assess the impact of video length.
- **Compared Methods:**
 - **Socratic-based Models:** Two-stage pipelines combining VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, VideoLLaMA3) with LLMs (Qwen2.5-Max, DeepSeek-V3).
 - **End-to-End VLM Models:** Open-source models including Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, InternVL3, and VideoLLaMA3.
 - **Video Reasoning Models:** VideoAgent, VideoTree, LVNet, and Video-R1. VideoAgent, VideoTree, and LVNet are training-free.
- **Implementation Details:** Qwen2.5-Max served as the default LLM. VLMs used were 7B–8B variants. Videos were segmented into 30-second intervals for long-term reasoning. The maximum number of dialogue rounds was limited to 10.

#### 4.2. Key Results and Findings

1. **Consistent State-of-the-Art Performance:** CLiViS achieved the highest performance across all three benchmarks. With InternVL3 as the VLM backbone, it recorded 55.4% on OpenEQA, 23.0% on EgoTempo, and 69.4% on EgoSchema, yielding an overall average accuracy of 49.4%. This indicates its ability to integrate perception and reasoning in a unified, training-free framework.

2. **Performance Advantage Across Paradigms:** CLiViS demonstrated an average gain of 20.2% over Socratic-based models, 2.9% over end-to-end VLMs, and 14.3% over video reasoning methods. This is attributed to CLiViS's integration of high-level language reasoning from LLMs, open-vocabulary perception from VLMs, and multi-step inference, which enables a dynamic hypothesis-verification loop that overcomes the limitations of single-step reasoning in other training-free methods like VideoTree/LVNet.

3. **Impact of Video Length:** CLiViS showed a greater margin of improvement over baselines on longer videos (≥ 30 seconds) compared to shorter ones (< 30 seconds). For example, using Qwen2.5-VL on OpenEQA, CLiViS improved performance by 3.5% on short videos and 6.5% on long videos. This result suggests the framework's effectiveness in aggregating long-range visual and semantic cues through iterative LLM-VLM collaboration and the dynamic cognitive map.

4. **Model-agnostic Effectiveness:** Experiments confirmed that CLiViS consistently improved performance across different VLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, VideoLLaMA3) when paired with the same LLM (Qwen2.5-Max). For instance, on EgoTempo, CLiViS increased InternVL3's accuracy from 17.0% to 23.0% (+6.0%). This validates the framework's generalizability and ability to enhance reasoning regardless of the specific VLM backbone.

5. **Ablation Studies:** Studies on EgoTempo with InternVL3 and Qwen2.5-Max provided insights into the contribution of each component:
 - **Navigation Graph:** Removal led to a 2.4% accuracy drop, highlighting its role in temporal localization.
 - **Relation Graph:** Removal resulted in a 1.6% drop, confirming its importance for fine-grained spatial context.
 - **Evidence Memory:** Disabling it caused a 0.6% reduction, indicating its utility in tracking rationales.
 - **Multi-round Interaction:** Collapsing the iterative LLM-VLM interaction into a single round resulted in a substantial 10.5% drop, emphasizing the criticality of iterative linguistic-visual synergy.
 - **LLM for Reasoning:** Replacing the LLM (Qwen2.5-Max) with a VLM (InternVL3) for high-level reasoning led to a significant 12.4% performance drop, confirming the fundamental necessity of a powerful, dedicated LLM for complex embodied reasoning.

6. **Inference Latency Analysis:** On the EgoSchema benchmark, CLiViS (195s) demonstrated a superior balance between accuracy and runtime compared to VideoTree (71s, lower accuracy) and VideoAgent (644s, lower accuracy), suggesting a favorable accuracy-computation trade-off.

7. **Reasoning Dynamics:** A multivariate bubble chart illustrated that while most videos were resolved within 2-3 reasoning rounds, longer and more complex videos required 3-5 rounds. A positive correlation between reasoning rounds and accuracy was observed within each duration range, indicating that deeper reasoning contributes to higher-quality responses. This highlights CLiViS's ability to dynamically modulate reasoning rounds based on temporal complexity.

8. **Qualitative Results:** Detailed case studies showed that CLiViS performed comprehensive scene parsing, clear task decomposition, demonstrated self-correcting abilities by resolving inconsistencies in VLM descriptions, and provided strong interpretability through traceable decision paths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLiViS framework introduces a novel paradigm for Embodied Visual Reasoning (EVR), representing a notable advancement in the field by addressing critical limitations of previous approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be delineated as follows:

1. **Bridging Perception and Reasoning:** CLiViS effectively integrates the complementary strengths of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). By positioning the LLM as a high-level planner that orchestrates VLM-driven perceptual subtasks, the framework moves beyond static video captioning or end-to-end models with limited reasoning capabilities. This synergistic approach enables more precise visual grounding and sophisticated multi-step inference for complex instructions.

2. **Dynamic and Structured Scene Understanding:** The introduction of a dynamic Cognitive Map, composed of a Navigation Graph and a Relation Graph, provides a structured and evolving representation of the embodied scene. This map continuously refines its understanding of regions, entities, actions, and their relationships through iterative LLM-VLM interactions. This dynamic representation is crucial for handling the spatiotemporal complexities and long-term dependencies inherent in egocentric videos, offering a more coherent context for reasoning than static or unstructured memory.

3. **Enhanced Interpretability:** The incorporation of an Evidence Memory module, which accumulates instruction-relevant rationales at each reasoning step, contributes to greater transparency. This explicit record of reasoning steps makes the decision-making process of CLiViS more interpretable and auditable, a desirable quality for complex AI systems, especially in sensitive applications.

4. **Generality and Robustness:** The training-free nature of CLiViS, combined with its consistent performance improvements across diverse benchmarks (OpenEQA, EgoSchema, EgoTempo) and various VLM backbones, demonstrates its generality and robustness. The framework's ability to adapt to different foundational models suggests its potential applicability in various embodied AI scenarios without requiring extensive re-training. Its particular strength in handling long-term visual dependencies is a key advantage for real-world tasks.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research in Embodied AI:** CLiViS provides a blueprint for leveraging the capabilities of powerful pre-trained LLMs and VLMs in an interactive and structured manner. This could inspire further research into more sophisticated agent architectures that integrate planning, perception, and memory management for embodied agents. The findings, especially from the ablation studies, underscore the distinct roles of LLMs for high-level reasoning and VLMs for perception, providing valuable insights for the design of future multimodal AI systems.

6. **Potential Applications:** The advancements demonstrated by CLiViS have direct implications for applications requiring intelligent agents to understand and act within dynamic environments based on natural language commands. These include:
 - **Robotics:** For tasks like object manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction where robots need to interpret complex instructions and perceive dynamic surroundings.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** For scene understanding, anomaly detection, and decision-making based on visual input and high-level directives.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** To help individuals with visual impairments or in environments requiring context-aware assistance.

While the paper acknowledges that real-world latency remains a challenge for current zero-shot methods, CLiViS presents a step towards more intelligent, generalizable, and interpretable embodied visual reasoning systems, paving the way for more sophisticated and versatile AI agents.
