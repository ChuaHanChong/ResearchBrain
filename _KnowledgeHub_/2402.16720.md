# Think2Drive: Efficient Reinforcement Learning by Thinking in Latent World Model for Quasi-Realistic Autonomous Driving (in CARLA-v2)

> [!summary] Summary
> Think2Drive is the first model-based RL planner for CARLA Leaderboard v2. A DreamerV3-style latent world model learns BEV-mask driving transitions and serves as a neural simulator, so the actor-critic planner trains by imagined rollouts, plus seven autonomous-driving-specific fixes (policy reset, warm-up curriculum, termination-priority replay, steering cost). It reaches expert-level driving in 3 days on one A6000, with 98.6% route completion.

## Problem

- Rule-based planners need hand-tuned rules per scenario and scale poorly, since corner cases combinatorially explode and new rules conflict with existing ones.
- Imitation learning starves on long-tail corner cases with sparse demonstrations, and model-free RL is too data-hungry for the slow, cumbersome CARLA simulator.
- CARLA v2 added 39 quasi-realistic events with long routes and dense scenarios, and no prior learning-based method reported success on them.

## Method

- A ==DreamerV3==-based latent world model (==RSSM== encoder, sequence, reward, termination, decoder heads) learns transitions of 128x128x34 BEV masks plus ego speed and prior action, over 4 history frames.
- An ==actor-critic== planner trains entirely on imagined latent rollouts with a 15-step clipped horizon, outputting 30 discretized throttle/steer/brake actions under speed, travel, deviation and steering rewards.
- Seven driving-specific bricks: planner weight reset, automated dense-scenario route generator, termination-priority replay, steering cost, warm-up curriculum, incremental planner training ratio, and an asynchronous wrapped CARLA environment.

## Results

- On CornerCaseRepo (**1,600** train, **390** eval routes), Think2Drive scores **89.0** weighted driving score and **99.6%** route completion versus model-free Roach at **54.8** and **96.4%**.
- On official CARLA v2 routes it reaches **91.7** weighted driving score, **56.8** driving score, and **98.6%** route completion, while a PPO expert collapses to **0.6** and **1.0%**.
- Ablations show every brick matters, with policy reset and warm-up curriculum most critical; per-scenario success ranges from **0.61** (AccidentTwoWays) to **1.0** (HardBrake, HighwayCutIn).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rasterized BEV dynamics are predictable enough that a learned latent simulator can replace expensive physics rollouts, making model-based RL far more sample-efficient than model-free.
> - Generic world-model RL does not transfer unchanged to driving; targeted fixes for local optima, sparse long-tail events and control smoothness carry most of the gain.
> - Privileged-information experts leave a large perception gap: distilling into a raw-sensor student dropped the weighted driving score from 89.0 to 29.6.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving (AD) research has witnessed substantial advancements, yet a significant challenge remains: reliably navigating complex urban environments, especially those involving "corner cases" – rare, intricate, or ambiguous scenarios. Traditionally, AD systems relied on manually-crafted rule-based planning methods, which struggle to scale due to the combinatorial explosion of potential scenarios and the difficulty of ensuring compatibility among an ever-growing set of rules. This limitation has spurred a strong trend toward learning-based "neural planners" that can adapt flexibly to diverse driving conditions by leveraging large datasets and computational power.

Existing benchmarks like HighwayEnv and CARLA Leaderboard v1 (CARLA v1) have facilitated initial progress, but their scenarios often simplify real-world driving. For instance, CARLA v1 could be effectively solved by rule-based approaches with minimal code. This gap between simulated environments and real-world complexity motivated the release of **CARLA Leaderboard v2 (CARLA v2)** in November 2022. CARLA v2 significantly ups the ante by introducing 39 quasi-realistic, long-tail corner cases – such as navigating construction zones into opposite lanes, yielding to emergency vehicles, or handling aggressive traffic flows at junctions. These scenarios pose formidable challenges that prior rule-based or even model-free reinforcement learning (RL) agents (like Roach) have failed to address, with no reported successful (100% route completion) solutions on CARLA v2 prior to this work. PDM-lite, a rule-based planner, was released after the initial arXiv version of Think2Drive and can solve CARLA v2, but it requires different hyperparameters for different scenarios, implying significant manual tuning. This paper explicitly aims to provide a *learning-based* solution that handles these corner cases flexibly and effectively without such manual intervention.

Within the realm of learning-based AD, two prominent paradigms are imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL). IL struggles with the long-tail nature of corner cases due to sparse demonstration data. Model-free RL, while successful in some domains, suffers from data inefficiency, making it impractical for the "cumbersome" CARLA simulator and the vast state space of AD. This paper critically highlights the under-exploration of **model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL)** in complex AD environments. While a few MBRL methods have been applied to simpler AD benchmarks, they have not yet tackled the complexity of CARLA v2.

Think2Drive distinguishes itself by proposing the **first model-based RL method** designed to tackle the CARLA v2 benchmark. It leverages the "world model" concept, popular in general RL (e.g., DreamerV3), to learn environment dynamics and efficiently train a neural planner in a compact latent space. This approach aligns with industry trends, as epitomized by Elon Musk's statements on Tesla's shift towards neural planners for control functions. By demonstrating the efficacy of MBRL for quasi-realistic AD, Think2Drive pioneers a new direction, bridging advanced RL techniques with the pressing need for robust and scalable autonomous driving solutions for challenging, real-world-like scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a highly efficient and effective learning-based planner capable of mastering the complex, quasi-realistic urban driving scenarios presented in the CARLA Leaderboard v2 benchmark. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Address Limitations of Traditional and Existing Learning-Based AD:**
 - **Rule-based planning:** These methods are inherently limited by their inability to exhaustively cover the infinite spectrum of real-world driving scenarios, especially the "long-tail" or corner cases. Their complexity grows exponentially with more rules, making maintenance and compatibility difficult.
 - **Imitation Learning (IL):** While effective in some AD contexts, IL struggles when expert demonstrations for rare, complex corner cases are sparse, leading to insufficient learning signals.
 - **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (MFRL):** Although applied in AD (e.g., Roach), MFRL methods are notoriously data-inefficient. This becomes a significant bottleneck in complex, high-dimensional environments like CARLA v2, where interacting with the physical simulator for massive data collection is "cumbersome" and time-consuming.

2. **Overcome the Challenges of CARLA Leaderboard v2:**
 - CARLA v2, with its 39 novel common events, represents a substantial leap in complexity from v1. These scenarios require sophisticated, nuanced control and decision-making that conventional methods have not mastered.
 - Prior to Think2Drive, no reported success (100% route completion) existed for CARLA v2, highlighting its difficulty and the urgent need for new solutions.
 - The benchmark's extended route lengths, high scenario density, and exponential decay scoring mechanism further amplify its difficulty, making robust and consistently high performance elusive.
 - The sparse nature of corner cases in official routes presents a "long-tail problem" for learning algorithms.

3. **Harness the Advantages of Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL):**
 - The authors are motivated to explore MBRL for AD due to its inherent strengths: **data efficiency** (by allowing the agent to "imagine" experiences within a learned world model) and **flexibility** in handling complex scenarios.
 - They posit that the transition dynamics of AD scenes, especially under rasterized Bird's-Eye View (BEV) representations, are relatively amenable to being learned by a world model, unlike highly stochastic environments.
 - MBRL offers the potential for much faster iteration speeds for training the planner, as rollouts can be conducted in parallel within the neural world model, circumventing the slow physical simulator.

4. **Develop a Unified and Robust Neural Planner:** The goal is to develop a single neural planner that can adapt to the varied demands of all 39 CARLA v2 scenarios, including those requiring seemingly contradictory policies (e.g., maintaining distance vs. aggressive merging), without scenario-specific manual tuning.

In essence, the paper aims to break new ground by applying and adapting model-based RL to solve the formidable and previously unsolved CARLA v2 benchmark, thereby demonstrating a highly efficient, scalable, and robust learning paradigm for quasi-realistic autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Think2Drive revolves around a **model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)** paradigm, where a compact latent world model learns the environment's transitions, acting as a neural simulator to efficiently train an agent (planner). The approach is named "Think2Drive" because the planner "thinks" by performing rollouts in the learned latent space, rather than directly interacting with the computationally expensive CARLA simulator.

**Problem Formulation:**
The planner's task is to output control signals (throttle, steer, brake) given privileged information (bounding boxes, HD-Map, traffic lights) represented as BEV semantic segmentation masks, along with ego-vehicle speed, previous action, and relative height. The environment's transition function is learned by the world model.

**World Model Learning (Leveraging DreamerV3):**
The world model is trained using the structure and objectives of **DreamerV3**, a state-of-the-art MBRL algorithm. It comprises several components:
- **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM):** Decomposes the state into a stochastic representation ($z_t$) and a deterministic hidden state ($h_t$) to model both aspects of the environment.
- **Encoder:** Maps raw input observations ($x_t$) to the latent representation ($z_t$).
- **Sequence Model:** Predicts future hidden states ($h_{t+1}$) based on current state, action, and history.
- **Reward Predictor:** Forecasts rewards ($r_t$) associated with a given model state.
- **Termination Predictor:** Predicts episode termination flags ($c_t$).
- **Decoder:** Reconstructs the raw input ($x_t$) from the latent state, ensuring informative representation.
The world model's training loss combines a prediction loss (for input reconstruction, reward, and termination), dynamics loss, and representation loss, using techniques like symlog loss and free bits from DreamerV3 for stable training.

**Planner Learning (Actor-Critic in Latent Space):**
The planner is trained using an **actor-critic architecture**, where the planner model is the actor, and a separate critic model evaluates states.
- **Efficient Rollouts:** The key advantage is that the planner learns by performing rollouts within the learned world model's latent space, significantly boosting data efficiency compared to physical simulator interactions.
- **Objective:** The planner ($\pi_\eta$) aims to maximize the expected discounted return predicted by the world model's reward predictor.
- **Critic:** Learns to evaluate the value of states under the planner's policy, aiding the actor's learning.
- **Return Estimation:** Expected return is estimated over a clipped horizon ($T=15$) and uses the critic for the remaining return, with DreamerV3's bucket-sorting rewards and two-hot encoding for stability.

**Input and Output Representation:**
- **Input:** BEV semantic segmentation masks ($I_{RL} \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W \times C}$) with $H=128, W=128, C=34$. Channels represent various static (road, lanes, traffic lights) and dynamic (vehicles, pedestrians) objects. Historical masks are used ($T=4$). Additional ego-vehicle states ($v_{RL} \in \mathbb{R}^K$) like speed and previous control action are also fed as input.
- **Output:** The continuous action space (throttle, steer, brake) is discretized into **30 distinct actions** to reduce complexity.

**Reward Shaping:**
The total reward ($r$) is a composite of four parts:
1. **Speed reward ($r_{speed}$):** Encourages safe speed relative to other objects.
2. **Travel reward ($r_{travel}$):** Promotes progress along the target route.
3. **Deviation penalty ($p_{deviation}$):** Penalizes deviation from the lane center.
4. **Steering cost ($c_{steer}$):** Penalizes large changes in steering, promoting smooth control (Brick 4).

**Devised "Bricks" for AD-Specific Challenges:**
The authors identify seven major obstacles in applying MBRL to quasi-realistic AD and propose specific techniques ("bricks") to address them:
1. **Policy Degradation (Brick 1: Reset Technique):** To prevent the agent from getting trapped in local optima for easy scenarios, the planner's weights are randomly re-initialized mid-training, leveraging the dense rewards from the stable world model for a robust cold-start.
2. **Long-Tail Nature (Brick 2: Automated Scenario Generator):** To address sparse scenarios in official routes and limited diversity, an automated generator creates numerous short routes with dense, varied scenarios. The `CornerCaseRepo` benchmark is also built for scenario-specific evaluation.
3. **Long-Tail Nature & Inefficient Exploration (Brick 3: Termination-Priority Replay):** To prioritize valuable learning signals, the world model samples either randomly or with equal probability from frames preceding episode terminations, focusing on critical states.
4. **Vehicle Heading Stabilization (Brick 4: Steering Cost Function):** A steering cost, defined as the difference between current and last steer values, is incorporated into the reward function to encourage stable and smooth control.
5. **Steep Learning Curve (Brick 5: Curriculum Learning / Warm-up Training):** The RL model first undergoes warm-up training on simple lane-following and turning scenarios to acquire basic driving skills before tackling complex corner cases.
6. **Delayed Learning Signals (Brick 6: Incremental Planner Training Ratio):** The training ratio for the planner model is incrementally increased to four times that of the world model by the end of training, expediting planner convergence.
7. **Simulator Efficiency (Brick 7: Wrapped RL Environment):** CARLA is wrapped as an RL environment with standardized APIs, and its running efficiency is boosted via asynchronous reloading and parallel execution, reducing significant waiting times during resets (e.g., >40s per reset).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Think2Drive research presents several significant findings and compelling results, demonstrating its pioneering success in addressing the CARLA Leaderboard v2 benchmark.

**Training Efficiency:**
- Think2Drive achieved **expert-level proficiency in CARLA v2 within 3 days of training on a single A6000 GPU**. This highlights the exceptional data efficiency of their model-based RL approach, which is a significant improvement over data-intensive model-free methods.

**Benchmarking and Evaluation:**
- **CornerCaseRepo Benchmark:** The authors introduced a novel benchmark, `CornerCaseRepo`, comprising 1,600 training routes and 390 evaluation routes. Each route is short (<300m) and contains only one type of scenario, enabling decoupled training, evaluation, and fine-grained debugging of performance across specific corner cases.
- **Weighted Driving Score (WDS):** To address the limitations of CARLA's official exponential decay scoring (which unfairly penalizes models for longer routes with fixed infraction rates), they proposed a new metric: `WDS = RC * Π(penalty_i ^ n_i)`, balancing route completion (RC), infraction number ($n_i$), and scenario density.

**Performance on CornerCaseRepo (Table 1 & Figure 1):**
- **Think2Drive (privileged information):** Achieved a **Weighted Driving Score (WDS) of 89.0±0.2** and an impressive **Route Completion of 99.6±0.1%**. Infraction penalties were significantly lower across all categories (e.g., 0.16 collision pedestrians, 1.2 collision vehicles, 0.14 red light, 0.03 stop sign).
- **Roach (Model-Free RL baseline):** Performed substantially worse, with a WDS of 54.8±0.5 and a Route Completion of 96.4±1.1%. Roach suffered from much higher infraction numbers (e.g., 0.85 collision pedestrians, 8.42 collision vehicles, 0.56 red light, 0.49 stop sign). This clearly demonstrated the superiority of Think2Drive's model-based approach over state-of-the-art model-free RL in this challenging environment.
- **Think2Drive + TCP (End-to-End, Raw Sensors):** This student model, trained via imitation learning on data from Think2Drive, showed a large performance gap (WDS 29.6±0.2, RC 85.88±8.26%). This highlights the inherent difficulty of perception tasks and the gap between expert-level privileged information and raw sensor inputs.

**Performance on Official CARLA Leaderboard v2 Routes (Table 3):**
- **Think2Drive:** Achieved a Driving Score of 56.8, a **Weighted Driving Score of 91.7**, and a Route Completion of **98.6%**. Critically, the paper states, "To our best knowledge, so far there is no reported success (100% route completion) on CARLA v2," implying Think2Drive is the first to achieve this level of performance, especially for a learning-based neural planner.
- **PPO (Model-Free Expert on CARLA v2):** Performed abysmally, with a Driving Score of 0.7, WDS of 0.6, and RC of 1.0%. This significant failure further underscores the limitations of model-free RL and the unique challenges of CARLA v2 for such methods, which rapidly converge to suboptimal policies.

**Infraction Analysis on Hard Scenarios (Table 2):**
- Think2Drive demonstrated varied success rates across the 39 scenarios, ranging from 0.61 (AccidentTwoWays) to 1.0 (HardBrake, HighwayCutIn).
- Scenarios like `AccidentTwoWays`, `ConstructionTwoWays`, `HazardAtSidelaneTwoWays`, and `MergerIntoSlowTraffic` were particularly challenging. These "TwoWays" scenarios require sophisticated lane negotiation into opposite traffic flows with tight time windows, often leading to collisions. "JunctionNegotiate" types (e.g., SignalizedRightTurn) also proved difficult due to aggressive traffic flows requiring rapid acceleration and safe merging.

**World Model Prediction Visualization (Figure 2):**
- Visualizations of the world model's open-loop predictions of BEV masks demonstrated its capability to generate authentic future states based on agent actions, confirming its effectiveness in learning the environment's transition function. This provides qualitative evidence for the core MBRL component.

**Ablation Study (Figure 3):**
- An ablation study on the six "bricks" (excluding the foundational automated scenario generator and wrapped RL environment) confirmed the critical contribution of each:
 - **Brick 1 (Reset Technique) and Brick 5 (Warm-up Training)** had the most substantial impact, suggesting they are crucial for preventing local optima and handling the steep learning curve.
 - **Brick 3 (Priority Sampling)** improved exploration efficiency.
 - **Brick 4 (Steering Cost)** enhanced vehicle stability.
 - **Brick 6 (Incremental Planner Train Ratio)** expedited convergence speed.
The absence of any single brick significantly diminished Think2Drive's performance, validating the necessity of these tailored adaptations for AD.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Think2Drive represents a significant stride in autonomous driving research, with profound implications for the development of robust and efficient AD systems, particularly for challenging urban environments. Its primary significance lies in being the **first model-based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) approach to successfully achieve expert-level proficiency in CARLA Leaderboard v2**. This achievement breaks a crucial barrier, as CARLA v2's 39 quasi-realistic corner cases had remained unsolved by prior learning-based methods, including state-of-the-art model-free RL agents.

The potential impact of Think2Drive is multi-faceted:

1. **Validation of Model-Based RL for AD:** This work definitively validates MBRL as a highly promising paradigm for complex autonomous driving tasks. Its demonstrated data efficiency, completing training on a single GPU in just three days, addresses a critical bottleneck faced by data-hungry model-free RL and imitation learning methods in high-fidelity simulators like CARLA. This efficiency is paramount for tackling the "long-tail problem" of rare corner cases, where gathering sufficient real-world or simulated data is impractical.

2. **Robustness and Generalization through "Bricks":** The systematic identification and addressing of AD-specific challenges through the devised "bricks" (e.g., policy reset, scenario generation, termination-priority replay, steering cost) showcase how generic MBRL frameworks can be tailored to the unique demands of autonomous driving. These techniques enhance the robustness, stability, and learning efficacy of the planner, contributing to its ability to handle a diverse range of complex scenarios without manual tuning for each case.

3. **New Tools for AD Research and Development:**
 - The introduction of the **CornerCaseRepo benchmark** provides a valuable tool for the AD research community. By decoupling scenarios into short, single-event routes, it facilitates more granular training, evaluation, and debugging, enabling researchers to pinpoint specific weaknesses and strengths of AD models more effectively.
 - The proposed **Weighted Driving Score (WDS)** offers a more balanced and intuitive metric for evaluating AD performance in environments like CARLA v2, moving beyond the potentially misleading official scoring system that penalizes models disproportionately for longer routes.

4. **Foundation for Future End-to-End AD Systems:** Think2Drive's proficient planner, developed using privileged information, can serve as an invaluable **expert or teacher model** for collecting high-quality driving data. This expert data can then be used to train perception-based, end-to-end autonomous driving models (e.g., those taking raw sensor inputs), helping to bridge the gap between perception and planning and enabling the development of more robust student models that can generalize better to real-world complexities. This aligns with the group's prior work on end-to-end AD and offers a clear path for future research.

5. **Alignment with Industry Trends:** The paper's success in developing a purely learning-based neural planner for planning and control directly resonates with the direction articulated by industry leaders like Elon Musk, who foresee neural networks replacing traditional rule-based control code in future AD systems. Think2Drive provides strong academic evidence that this vision is increasingly attainable, particularly with the strategic application of advanced RL paradigms.

In conclusion, Think2Drive not only provides a breakthrough solution for a previously unsolved, highly challenging autonomous driving benchmark but also establishes a robust and efficient MBRL framework that is directly applicable and scalable for future research and development in quasi-realistic and potentially real-world autonomous driving applications.
