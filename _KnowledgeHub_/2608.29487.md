# Blind Dexterity: Whole-Body Humanoid Manipulation via Pure Proprioception

> [!summary] Summary
> Blind Dexterity trains a Unitree G1 humanoid to perform whole-body loco-manipulation using only joint encoders and compliant PD control, no cameras, tactile skins, or force sensors. Because commanded-minus-measured joint deflection is proportional to torque, policies read contact through proprioception and actively probe objects. Blind RL policies reach 92.9% football trapping, 89.7% skateboard mounting, and 88.3% suitcase lifting, far above distilled students.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion runs blind on proprioception, yet manipulation on the same platforms still assumes cameras, markers, or object trackers for object localization.
- Tactile skins and force/torque sensors treat touch as extra hardware to bolt on, ignoring contact information already latent in existing joint encoder readouts.
- Prior proprioception-only manipulation studies stayed in simulation or with simplified tactile models, never transferring blind contact-driven skills to a real humanoid.

## Method

- ==PPO== policies over a short proprioceptive history (K=5, 0.1 s) with previously commanded joint targets included, letting the actor recover the ==PD tracking residual== as an implicit contact signal.
- ==Asymmetric actor-critic== in Isaac Sim with ==domain randomization==; the critic sees privileged object pose while the deployed ==MLP== actor sees only encoders, IMU, and past actions.
- A separately optimized ==auxiliary state estimator== regresses object pose, orientation, and size from the same history; a ==variable-stiffness== head outputs per-joint gain multipliers for gentle contact search.

## Results

- Blind football trapping reached **92.9%** success with localization error dropping to about **2 cm** after first contact, while the DAgger-distilled student collapsed to **31.2%**.
- On skateboard mounting, blind policies hit **88.2-89.7%** versus **8.2%** distilled; suitcase lifting reached **88.3%** with variable stiffness cutting tipping by **4-5 points**.
- IMU-free walking survived pushes at **90.4%** with previous actions observed, falling to **89.1%** survival and much worse velocity tracking and inertial estimation when actions were removed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compliant PD control turns joint encoders into a coarse whole-body tactile channel; observing previous commanded actions is the cheap ingredient that unlocks it.
> - Teacher-student distillation fails under partial observability because students copy the object-position prior instead of learning active search behaviors.
> - Feeding an explicit state estimate back into the policy gave no consistent gain, suggesting raw short histories already encode what control needs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen progress in legged humanoid locomotion, where robots demonstrate robust navigation across challenging terrains, including stairs, primarily relying on proprioceptive feedback and learned control policies. This suggests that internal proprioceptive signals contain information about ground contact and the robot's state. In contrast, humanoid manipulation research has often emphasized exteroceptive sensing modalities, such as body-mounted cameras, or the integration of specialized tactile skins and force/torque sensors to capture touch as a distinct sensing channel. This divergence in sensing strategies between locomotion and manipulation raises questions regarding the full potential of proprioception in complex manipulation tasks.

Previous work in proprioceptive sensing has explored its sufficiency for robust legged locomotion and manipulator arms, with some research estimating whole-body contact locations and external forces from existing joint sensors. This paper extends this inquiry by investigating closed-loop whole-body humanoid behaviors and employing separate estimators to analyze task-relevant object state derived from proprioceptive histories generated through interaction.

Recent learning-based control approaches, such as SoftMimic and GentleHumanoid, have integrated impedance control to enhance stability and safety in whole-body reinforcement learning, enabling skills like hugging. This paper diverges by treating compliant joint-space dynamics and the resultant joint deflections as an informative sensing channel for interactive perception and manipulation, rather than solely for stability. Related work by Miller et al. explores simulated vision-free manipulation using proprioception and simplified tactile sensors, but without real-world transfer.

The paper also relates to the field of tactile sensing and interactive perception, where purposeful, temporally extended motion and contact strategies are known to generate informative tactile signals. Research has demonstrated the use of contact for object recognition and inferring properties through exploratory actions. This study builds upon these principles by reinterpreting compliant PD control and existing proprioception as a low-resolution, whole-body, active tactile sensor, thereby training policies that develop their own exploratory strategies without additional specialized hardware.

In systems that fuse proprioception with vision, the individual contribution of each modality is often not isolated. This research aims to address this by explicitly isolating the role of proprioception, positing it as a complementary modality to vision, particularly in scenarios involving occlusion or suboptimal lighting conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the extent to which a humanoid robot can manipulate and perceive its environment using only its internal proprioception. This exploration is pursued by deliberately avoiding exteroceptive sensors typically used in manipulation, such as cameras, optical tracking markers, dedicated tactile skins, or force/torque sensors.

The motivation for this minimalist sensing approach stems from several observations:
1. **Exploration of Proprioceptive Potential:** While proprioception is established for robust locomotion, its full capacity for dexterous manipulation and environmental perception remains largely unexplored, especially when decoupled from exteroceptive inputs. The authors propose that joint encoder readouts, particularly their evolution under purposeful compliant contact, can function as an implicit "whole-body tactile channel."
2. **Challenging the Status Quo:** Current humanoid manipulation research heavily relies on high-fidelity exteroceptive sensing. By demonstrating capabilities with minimal sensing, the research aims to establish a robust baseline and highlight an underappreciated sensing modality inherent in compliant actuation systems.
3. **Active Perception through Contact:** The research is motivated by the hypothesis that policies can actively probe the environment through contact-rich motions. This interaction causes task-relevant object state, such as pose, to become increasingly decodable from short histories of proprioceptive data.
4. **Complement to Vision:** The study is motivated by the potential for proprioception-only policies to serve as a reliable complement or alternative to vision-based systems, particularly in conditions where vision might be compromised (e.g., occlusion, poor lighting, or sensor failure).
5. **Practicality and Accessibility:** By relying solely on joint encoders and PD-controlled actuation—components widely available on commercial robots and low-cost motors—the research seeks to demonstrate a practical and accessible foundation for dexterous manipulation, potentially reducing system complexity and cost.

To address these motivations, the study articulates two specific objectives:
1. **Empirical Demonstration of Blind Dexterity:** To demonstrate that policies restricted to onboard proprioception can solve a suite of qualitatively different blind dexterity tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. These tasks include push-resilient bipedal walking without IMU input, active soccer ball trapping with a foot, dynamic localization and mounting of a randomly positioned skateboard, and finding and lifting a suitcase by its handle.
2. **Quantification of Proprioceptive Information:** To train auxiliary estimators that map short proprioceptive histories to task-relevant features (e.g., object pose). This aims to quantify the amount of object-centric information that becomes decodable after contact and to assess whether explicit estimator feedback improves control performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation and qualitative hardware validation on a physical robot. The core approach centers on leveraging existing proprioceptive sensors—specifically joint encoders and an Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)—without the aid of external cameras, depth sensors, force/torque sensors, or tactile arrays.

**A. System and Sensing:**
The Unitree G1 humanoid robot is used for experiments. This robot provides built-in proprioceptive feedback: IMU readings (projected gravity vector, angular velocity) and encoder-reported joint positions and velocities for each actuator. The robot's low-level controllers expose a PD interface, allowing the policy to output desired joint positions which the servos track. In specific experiments, IMU data is intentionally withheld to assess purely encoder-based sensing capabilities. "Pure proprioception" refers to policies using all onboard internal sensing, while "encoder-only" specifically denotes policies without IMU data.

**B. Problem Formulation:**
Each task is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP). The actor policy receives onboard proprioceptive observations and previous actions, while object pose and physical parameters remain latent. The policy, denoted πθ(at | o≤t), is trained to maximize expected return for contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks.

**C. Reinforcement Learning Formulation:**
Policies are trained in simulation using deep reinforcement learning, specifically the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm implemented in RSL-RL.
- **Policy Architecture:** A feedforward Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) maps a short history of recent proprioceptive observations and actions to an action. The observation history ht is defined as {ot−K+1, at−K+1. . . , ot−1, at−1, ot}, where K is the history length (K=5 for manipulation tasks, K=1 for locomotion).
- **Implicit Contact Signal:** The policy observes current joint positions (qt), velocities (q̇t), and the previously commanded joint position action (a_q_t-1). From these, it can implicitly derive the joint-tracking residual (e_t = q_des_t-1 - q_t), which under PD control, is proportional to torque (τPD ≈ Kp,t et - Kd q̇t). This residual changes due to external contact, providing a noisy, spatially coarse proxy for contact-induced joint loading. The short observation history allows the policy to perceive the evolution of this signal.
- **Reward Function:** Reward terms incentivize task completion, maintain balance, encourage smooth motion, and include shaping terms for safety and to penalize excessive joint velocities or joint limit violations.
- **Simulation and Transfer:** Isaac Sim is used for training. Domain randomization is applied to physical parameters (link masses, friction coefficients, motor strengths) to mitigate the simulation-reality gap and promote robustness for hardware transfer.
- **Training Parameters:** Policies operate at 50 Hz. Simulation uses a 0.005 s physics time step with an action decimation of 4. Actor and critic are MLPs with hidden layers and ELU activations. Asymmetric actor-critic training is used, where the critic receives privileged simulator state (e.g., ground-truth object pose) unavailable to the actor during deployment. PPO specific parameters are detailed in the paper.

**D. Variable Stiffness Control (for Suitcase task):**
For tasks requiring compliant interaction, a variant (denoted +VS) allows the policy to additionally output per-joint gain multipliers (αt = σ(aαt) ∈ (0, 1)). This modifies the torque calculation (τt = αt [Kp(q_des_t - q_t) - Kd q̇t]), enabling the robot to adapt its stiffness for tasks like softer contact search or stiffer insertion and lifting.

**E. Proprioceptive State Estimation (Auxiliary Tool):**
To quantitatively analyze the information content within proprioception, an auxiliary state estimator (ϕ) is trained.
- **Architecture:** An MLP with hidden layers and ELU activations.
- **Input and Output:** It receives the same history (ht) as the policy and outputs an estimate (ẑt) of task-specific state features (zt), such as an object's pose or size, which are available as ground truth during simulation training.
- **Training:** Trained concurrently with the policy using supervised learning with an L2 regression loss on empirically standardized targets.
- **Role:** The estimator is a separate network; its loss does not influence the PPO objective or policy gradients. It serves purely as a diagnostic tool to assess how much object state can be decoded from proprioceptive histories. In ablation studies (+SE variants), its previous prediction (ẑt−1) is appended to the policy's observations to evaluate its potential impact on control.

**F. Suite of Tasks:**
The empirical study involves four tasks, each designed to progressively increase the demands on blind proprioceptive control:
1. **IMU-Free Walking and Push Recovery:** Assesses locomotion robustness when IMU feedback is removed. Ablates IMU and previous action inputs.
2. **Active Football Localization and Trapping:** Requires the robot to localize and trap a randomly placed football using only foot contact. Compares a privileged policy, a distilled policy, and blind policies with/without state estimator feedback.
3. **Blind Skateboard Mounting:** Involves dynamic interaction with an underactuated, articulated object to mount it. The estimator predicts 3D position and 6D orientation.
4. **Gentle Blind Manipulation (Suitcase):** Challenges the robot to locate a recessed handle on a slender suitcase and lift it gently without tipping. Ablates state estimator feedback and variable stiffness control.

For each task, policies are quantitatively evaluated in simulation and qualitatively validated on the real Unitree G1 robot to confirm sim-to-real transfer feasibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical findings across four progressively challenging tasks, supported by quantitative data from simulation and qualitative observations from hardware deployment.

**A. IMU-Free Walking and Push Recovery:**
- **Locomotion with Minimal Sensing:** Policies trained without IMU readings (-IMU+PE) were capable of locomotion in simulation and transferred to the real robot, demonstrating push recovery capabilities. This suggests that joint encoders alone can encode sufficient information about ground contact and body forces for inferring orientation and inertial state.
- **Role of Previous Actions:** The inclusion of previous commanded joint targets (a_t-1) in the observation space was observed to be critical. Policies trained without both IMU and previous actions (-IMU-PE) showed significantly reduced ability to recover from pushes and to estimate inertial measurements accurately (Table I, Fig. 2). The -IMU+PE policy, while less robust than the IMU-equipped (+IMU+PE) variant, still recovered from perturbations, and its estimator could reconstruct inertial measurements with reasonable accuracy.

**B. Active Football Localization and Trapping:**
- **Active Seeking Behavior:** Blind policies trained from scratch (-SE and +SE) developed active seeking behaviors. This involved systematically sweeping the area with one foot, often with ankle wiggling, and then turning towards and trapping the ball upon contact (Fig. 4). This behavior illustrates interactive perception, where the robot generates informative contacts.
- **Performance Comparison:** The "Privileged" policy, with ground-truth ball state, achieved near-perfect success. In contrast, the "Distilled" policy, trained to imitate the privileged teacher, performed poorly, primarily relying on planting its foot near the average spawn location rather than actively seeking. The blind-from-scratch policies (-SE and +SE) achieved success rates of 92.9% and 86.5% respectively (Table II).
- **Localization Accuracy:** The auxiliary state estimator showed that upon first contact, the ball's localization error dropped sharply, often to approximately 2 cm, indicating that a brief proprioceptive history (0.1s) after contact contains sufficient information for accurate ball localization.
- **State Estimator Feedback:** Explicit state estimator feedback (+SE) did not consistently improve mean success rate compared to -SE, though the best-performing seed for +SE achieved a high success rate (99.3%). Variability in early training for +SE was noted, potentially due to unreliable estimates.

**C. Blind Skateboard Mounting:**
- **Interactive Perception Strategies:** Blind policies learned sophisticated active contact strategies for locating and mounting the skateboard. These included initial sweeping, making contact with the nose or tail of the board, and dragging the board. These behaviors systematically reduced the estimator's board-pose error (Fig. 5).
- **Performance:** The "Distilled" policy again performed poorly, succeeding only when the skateboard was reset centrally. Both blind-from-scratch variants (-SE and +SE) demonstrated high success rates (88.2% and 89.7% respectively), significantly outperforming the Distilled policy and showing more consistent performance than the Privileged baseline (Table III).
- **Pose Estimation Improvement:** Contact with the inclined nose or tail produced a sharp drop in position error along the board’s major axis. Dragging the board reliably induced motion along its major axis, substantially improving the yaw estimate.

**D. Gentle Blind Manipulation (Suitcase):**
- **Handle Localization and Lifting:** Blind policies were able to locate the recessed handle of a suitcase and lift it, even with randomized positions, heights, and masses. The process involved initial table contact to localize the suitcase vertically, followed by side contacts to improve the overall pose estimate, and then detailed hand movements near the top edge to search for the handle opening (Fig. 6).
- **Effect of Variable Stiffness:** Variable stiffness (+VS) consistently improved success rates and reduced tipping by approximately 4-5 percentage points compared to fixed-stiffness (-VS) policies (Table IV). Softer +VS policies often employed sliding motions to find the handle, while fixed-stiffness policies tended to use repeated "tapping."
- **Performance:** The Privileged policy achieved the highest success rate (95.5%). The Distilled policy performed worst, frequently tipping the suitcase. Blind policies, especially with variable stiffness (Blind+VS-SE: 88.3%, Blind+VS+SE: 85.1%), showed robust performance in finding and lifting the suitcase.
- **State Estimator Feedback:** Similar to the football task, explicit state estimator feedback (+SE) did not provide a consistent mean benefit. However, the best single-seed success and lowest estimation error were achieved by the +VS+SE variant.

**E. Cross-Task Findings:**
- **Superiority of Blind-from-Scratch:** Policies trained from scratch consistently outperformed distilled student policies across all object-interaction tasks. Distillation struggled in pre-contact phases due to a lack of object-influenced proprioceptive signals, indicating a limitation of imitation learning in partially observable, exploration-demanding settings.
- **Sufficiency of Raw Proprioceptive Histories:** Explicit state-estimator feedback (+SE) did not consistently yield significant mean improvements. This suggests that short raw proprioceptive histories often provide sufficient information for control, and inaccurate estimates during early training can sometimes hinder optimization.
- **Decodability of Object State:** Across all tasks, estimation errors systematically decreased after informative contacts, demonstrating that task-relevant object state can be reliably decoded from encoder-based proprioceptive histories, even without explicit reward for estimator accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates that joint encoder-based proprioception, when combined with compliant actuation, constitutes a practical and capable substrate for blind whole-body loco-manipulation on a commercial humanoid robot. This finding has several implications and potential impacts:

1. **Reinterpretation of Sensing:** The work redefines the role of compliant PD-controller tracking deviations, treating them not just as control residuals but as an implicit "whole-body tactile channel." This perspective suggests that many existing commercial robots, equipped with compliant actuators and joint encoders, already possess an underutilized sensing modality for interaction.
2. **Interactive Perception through Proprioception:** The policies developed in this study exhibit interactive perception, actively probing the environment through movements to generate informative contacts. This active exploration allows task-relevant object state to become progressively decodable from short proprioceptive histories, highlighting a fundamental aspect of intelligent robotic interaction.
3. **Robust Baseline for Dexterous Manipulation:** The demonstrated capabilities of blind policies, particularly in tasks involving spatial uncertainty and dynamic interaction, establish encoder feedback as a robust baseline. This foundation can be valuable in scenarios where traditional exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras) are challenged by occlusion, poor lighting, or environmental variability. It offers an alternative or complementary approach to vision-centric manipulation.
4. **Implications for Robot Design and Cost:** By showing that complex manipulation can be achieved with minimal external sensing, the research suggests avenues for developing more cost-effective and simpler robot systems. Leveraging existing internal sensors reduces the need for expensive, specialized tactile hardware or high-resolution vision systems in certain applications.
5. **Limitations of Imitation Learning in POMDPs:** The consistent superior performance of blind-from-scratch policies over distilled students underscores a critical limitation of imitation-based approaches in partially observable environments that require active exploration. This highlights the importance of learning exploration strategies rather than solely mimicking privileged behavior when sensory information is incomplete.
6. **Foundation for Layered Sensing:** The findings position proprioception as a natural and strong foundation upon which richer sensing modalities, such as vision and dedicated tactile hardware, can be layered. This could lead to more resilient and versatile robotic systems that combine the strengths of different sensor types.
7. **Advancement in Humanoid Capabilities:** The demonstration of tasks like IMU-free push-resilient walking, active football trapping, and blind skateboard mounting on a real humanoid robot pushes the boundaries of what is considered achievable with proprioception alone, contributing to the broader development of autonomous humanoid capabilities.
