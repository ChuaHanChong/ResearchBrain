# Physics-informed Value Learner for Offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed Physics-informed Eikonal-regularized Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning (Eik-HIQL), which integrates a novel Eikonal PDE-based regularizer into Goal-Conditioned Value Function (GCVF) estimation for offline reinforcement learning. This framework significantly improves GCVF accuracy and generalization by encouraging a distance-like cost-to-go structure, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines on navigation and locomotion tasks within the challenging OGbench.

## Problem

- Existing Offline GCRL algorithms struggle to accurately estimate Goal-Conditioned Value Functions (GCVFs) from limited and potentially biased offline datasets, leading to suboptimal policies.
- Learned GCVFs often exhibit poor generalization to unseen state-goal pairs, particularly in long-horizon tasks, which limits real-world applicability.
- Many current methods lack structural priors, making them susceptible to data limitations and yielding less robust or interpretable value functions.

## Method

- Introduced a novel ==Eikonal PDE==-based regularizer, inspired by the Eikonal equation, into the GCVF estimation loss of the Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning (HIQL) framework.
- The regularizer encourages the GCVF to approximate a distance field by penalizing deviations from a unit gradient norm with respect to the state, effectively promoting a 1-Lipschitz continuous value function.
- Designed as a model-free approach, the Eikonal regularizer avoids the need for explicit system dynamics and potential numerical instabilities associated with model-based methods.

## Results

- Eik-HIQL consistently outperformed its non-regularized counterpart (HIQL) and other state-of-the-art baselines, showing performance improvements exceeding **100%** in 7 out of 31 tasks on the OGbench, particularly in large navigation and locomotion scenarios.
- The method generated GCVFs that more accurately reflected the underlying maze geometry, leading to improved goal-directed policy performance compared to baselines.
- Eik-HIQL showed limited advantages in interactive, contact-rich domains such as antsoccer and manipulation, suggesting the Eikonal regularizer's smoothness assumptions are less applicable where dynamics are discontinuous.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-informed regularization, specifically derived from the Eikonal PDE, can effectively inject a geometric inductive bias into GCVF estimation, significantly enhancing its accuracy and generalization.
> - A simple constant speed profile (S(s)=1) within the Eikonal regularizer is highly effective, outperforming more complex, obstacle-aware profiles by promoting a consistent 1-Lipschitz GCVF without introducing unnecessary complexity.
> - The Eikonal regularizer demonstrates broad compatibility with various temporal-difference-based GCRL algorithms, indicating its potential as a general plug-in enhancement for offline value learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has seen significant advancements, particularly with methods leveraging large datasets. Offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) is an area that seeks to apply RL to dynamic systems using passively collected, often large-scale and multitask datasets, without requiring explicit reward annotations or continuous interaction with the environment. This approach is particularly relevant for domains where online data collection is costly, unsafe, or impractical, such as autonomous navigation, locomotion, and manipulation.

Despite its promise, Offline GCRL faces several challenges. These include accurately estimating Goal-Conditioned Value Functions (GCVFs) from datasets with limited coverage of the state-action space, effectively extracting policies from potentially imperfect value functions, and generalizing to novel or unseen state-goal pairs, especially in long-horizon tasks. GCVF estimation is considered a fundamental challenge, as improvements in this area can propagate to policy learning and generalization capabilities.

Physics-informed (Pi) inductive biases represent a research direction aimed at enhancing learning processes by embedding structural constraints derived from physical laws, such as symmetries, conservation principles, or consistency with Partial Differential Equations (PDEs). Prior work has demonstrated that Pi methods can introduce physically or geometrically meaningful structures into learned functions, which can improve sample efficiency and generalization. For instance, some approaches have explored objectives derived from the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation in continuous-time optimal control to enforce first-order derivative consistency in critic networks within Offline RL. Gradient norm penalties, related to the Eikonal PDE, have also been used in generative modeling and model-based RL to regularize Q-functions and mitigate overfitting.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by introducing a novel, model-free Pi regularizer specifically for GCVF estimation in Offline GCRL. Unlike prior HJB-based methods that often require explicit system dynamics and can suffer from numerical instability, or generic gradient penalties primarily used for training stabilization, the proposed Eikonal regularizer is grounded in continuous-time optimal control theory and aims to induce a specific geometric inductive bias (a distance-like cost-to-go structure) into the GCVF. This work represents a new application of the Eikonal PDE as a regularization objective in value-based RL, particularly in the Offline GCRL setting, aiming to improve generalization and performance in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and generalization capabilities of Goal-Conditioned Value Function (GCVF) estimation within the Offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) framework. This improvement is sought particularly for applications involving long-horizon tasks and environments where available offline datasets provide limited coverage of the state-action space.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current Offline GCRL approaches:
- **Inaccurate GCVF Estimation:** Existing algorithms often struggle to accurately estimate GCVFs from sparse and potentially biased offline data. This can lead to suboptimal policies that fail to effectively reach goals, especially in complex environments with obstacles, as illustrated by the example of a non-Pi algorithm failing to account for maze structures (Fig. 1b).
- **Limited Generalization:** Learned GCVFs frequently exhibit poor generalization to unseen state-goal pairs, which is critical for real-world applications requiring adaptability.
- **Handling Long-Horizon Tasks:** Tasks requiring extended sequences of actions to reach a goal can be particularly difficult for Offline GCRL due to the accumulation of errors in value estimation over long trajectories.
- **Lack of Structural Priors:** Many existing methods rely heavily on data without incorporating domain-specific structural knowledge. This can make them susceptible to the limitations of the offline dataset, leading to less robust and less interpretable value functions.

To address these issues, the authors propose leveraging Physics-informed (Pi) inductive biases. Specifically, they are motivated by the Eikonal Partial Differential Equation (PDE), which describes wave propagation and intrinsically encodes distance-like or cost-to-go structures. By integrating a regularizer derived from the Eikonal PDE, the authors aim to:
- **Impose Geometric Inductive Bias:** Encourage the learned GCVF to approximate a distance field, where the value function's gradient magnitude reflects the local cost of movement towards a goal. This helps the value function to align with the underlying cost-to-go structures inherent in optimal control problems.
- **Improve Value Estimation Accuracy:** By enforcing this distance-like structure, the regularizer is expected to produce smoother, more consistent, and globally coherent value functions, even when trained on limited or irregular offline data. This helps mitigate extrapolation errors common in offline settings.
- **Enhance Generalization:** A more structured and geometrically consistent GCVF is posited to generalize better to unseen states and goals, as it better captures the intrinsic navigational properties of the environment (e.g., maze layouts, obstacle avoidance).
- **Increase Sample Efficiency:** By providing a strong inductive bias, the method might reduce the number of training steps required to learn effective GCVFs compared to unregularized or generic approaches.

The work specifically highlights that, unlike HJB-based methods, the proposed Eikonal regularizer is model-free, simplifying implementation and avoiding the numerical instabilities associated with approximating system dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Physics-informed Eikonal-regularized Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning (Eik-HIQL), extends the Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning (HIQL) framework by integrating a novel Eikonal PDE-based regularizer into the GCVF estimation process.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is framed as an Offline GCRL task within a finite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP), characterized by (S, G, A, T, R, P_g, ρ_0, γ). Here, S is the state space, G is the goal space (assumed to be equivalent to S), A is the action space, T is the transition probability function, R(s, g) is a goal-conditioned reward function (R(s, g) = -1 if s ≠ g, and 0 otherwise), P_g is the goal distribution, ρ_0 is the initial state distribution, and γ is the discount factor. The agent's objective is to maximize the expected sum of discounted rewards by learning a goal-conditioned policy π: S × G → P(A). Learning occurs solely from a static offline dataset D of trajectories.

**Hierarchical Implicit Q-Learning (HIQL) Foundation:**
Eik-HIQL builds upon HIQL, an existing state-of-the-art Offline GCRL algorithm. HIQL incorporates two main components:
1. **Robust GCVF Estimation:** This uses an action-free variant of implicit Q-learning, inspired by expectile regression. The value loss is defined as:
 $L_V(\theta_V) = E_{(s,s') \sim D, g \sim P_g} [L_ι^2(R(s, g) + \gamma V_{\bar{\theta}_V}(s', g) - V_{\theta_V}(s, g))]$
 where $V_{\theta_V}$ is the GCVF network, $\bar{\theta}_V$ are target network parameters, and $L_ι^2(x) = |\iota - 1(x < 0)|x^2$ is the expectile loss with $\iota \in [0.5, 1]$. Expectile regression replaces the max operator in the Bellman equation, aiming to mitigate issues with overestimated values for out-of-distribution actions in offline settings.
2. **Hierarchical Actor:** This consists of a high-level policy $\pi_{hi}$ (predicts subgoals $s_{t+k}$ from current state $s_t$ and goal $g$) and a low-level policy $\pi_{lo}$ (generates actions $a_t$ to reach subgoals $s_{t+k}$ from current state $s_t$). This hierarchy addresses low signal-to-noise ratios in GCVF estimates. Both policies are trained using an advantage-weighted regression-style objective based on the learned GCVF.

**Physics-informed Eikonal Regularizer:**
The core contribution is the introduction of an Eikonal regularizer into the GCVF estimation loss. This regularizer is inspired by the Eikonal equation, a first-order non-linear PDE: $||∇_s T(s, g)||^2 = 1/S(s)^2$, where T is travel-time (a cost-to-go function) and S(s) is a speed profile.

**Theoretical Connection to Optimal Control:**
The authors establish a connection between the Eikonal PDE and the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation, which is fundamental to continuous-time optimal control. They demonstrate that the Hamiltonian, $H(s, g, ∇_s V(s, g)) \equiv \inf_{a \in A}[c(s, a) + ∇_s V(s, g)^T f(s, a)]$, where $c(s,a)$ is cost and $f(s,a)$ is dynamics, is upper-bounded by an Eikonal-like residual: $c^*(s) + ||∇_s V(s, g)||F^*(s)$, where $c^*(s) = \inf_a c(s, a)$ and $F^*(s) = \sup_a ||f(s, a)||$. In the specific case of isotropic dynamics ($f(s,a)=a$, $||a||=1$) and constant cost $c(s,a)$, the Hamiltonian simplifies to $c^*(s) - ||∇_s V(s, g)||$. This connection highlights that the Eikonal PDE captures a related spatial structure to the HJB equation, making it a natural approximation when dynamics are unknown.

**Eikonal-regularized Implicit V-learning Loss:**
Based on this, the authors propose the following combined loss for GCVF estimation:
$L_V(\theta_V) = E_{(s,s') \sim D, g \sim P_g} [L_ι^2(R(s, g) + \gamma V_{\bar{\theta}_V}(s', g) - V_{\theta_V}(s, g)) + (||∇_s V_{\theta_V}(s, g)|| \cdot S(s) - 1)^2]$
The second term, $(||∇_s V_{\theta_V}(s, g)|| \cdot S(s) - 1)^2$, is the Eikonal regularizer.
- $||∇_s V_{\theta_V}(s, g)||$ is the Euclidean norm of the GCVF's gradient with respect to the state input $s$, computed via automatic differentiation.
- $S(s)$ is a pre-specified speed profile. Initial experiments explored complex profiles like exponential and linear functions of distance to obstacles, but a simple constant $S(s)=1$ was found to be most effective. This choice effectively encourages the GCVF to be 1-Lipschitz continuous ($||∇_s V_{\theta_V}(s, g)|| \approx 1$), promoting smoother and more generalizable value estimates. The authors state that constant $S(s) > 0$ does not affect policy gradients due to uniform rescaling.
- The regularization is applied as a squared difference from 1, designed for numerical stability.

**Overall Eik-HIQL Algorithm:**
1. **Value Estimation:** Minimize the Eikonal-regularized loss $L_V(\theta_V)$ to learn the GCVF $V_{\theta_V}$.
2. **Policy Extraction:** Train the hierarchical actor (high-level $\pi_{hi}$ and low-level $\pi_{lo}$) using advantage-weighted regression, guided by the estimated GCVF.

This model-free approach aims to leverage the geometric properties of the Eikonal PDE to inject a structural inductive bias into the GCVF, thereby improving sample efficiency and generalization, particularly in environments with smooth dynamics where the optimal value function is Lipschitz continuous.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Eik-HIQL was conducted on the challenging OGbench benchmark, which includes a variety of goal-conditioned tasks spanning navigation, locomotion, and manipulation, differing in dynamics complexity, dimensionality, and task structure.

#### 4.1. Speed Profiles and HJB Regularizer Comparison

An ablation study was performed to assess different choices for the speed profile $S(s)$ in the Eikonal regularizer, including constant $S(s)=1$, an exponential profile ($S_{exp}(s)$), and a linear profile ($S_{lin}(s)$). These profiles assign higher speeds to states far from obstacles and lower speeds to states near obstacles, intending to encourage obstacle avoidance. Additionally, the Eikonal regularizer was compared against an HJB regularizer ($L_{HJB_V}(\theta_V)$), which approximates system dynamics using finite differences ($s' - s$).

- **Key Observation:** The simple constant setting $S(s)=1$ consistently outperformed both the more complex exponential and linear speed profiles, and the HJB regularizer, especially in large and stitching (composite dataset) navigation tasks (Table 1).
- **Reasoning:** The authors attribute this to two factors: (1) Offline GCRL datasets typically contain trajectories already within the feasible space, meaning complex obstacle avoidance priors offer limited additional benefit. (2) The simplicity of $S(s)=1$ leads to more efficient and stable GCVF learning, while complex profiles requiring privileged information (like obstacle coordinates) can introduce unnecessary complexity and artifacts (Fig. 5).

Based on these findings, $S(s)=1$ was adopted for all subsequent experiments to ensure a fair comparison with baselines and because it demonstrably yields superior results in the evaluated settings.

#### 4.2. Eik-HIQL vs. HIQL

A direct comparison between Eik-HIQL and its non-regularized counterpart, HIQL, was performed to isolate the impact of the Eikonal regularizer.

- **Performance Gains:** Eik-HIQL consistently outperformed HIQL across various navigation tasks, with the benefits being most pronounced in larger mazes and stitching regimes (Table 2). Specifically, improvements exceeding 100% were observed in 7 out of 31 evaluated tasks.
- **GCVF Structure:** Eik-HIQL produced GCVFs that better reflected the underlying maze geometry (Fig. 1a vs. 1b), leading to improved goal-directed performance. HIQL often failed to capture this structural information.
- **Limitations:** In interactive, contact-rich domains such as antsoccer and manipulation, where dynamics may exhibit discontinuities, Eik-HIQL offered limited advantages over HIQL.

#### 4.3. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Offline GCRL Baselines

Eik-HIQL was compared against other state-of-the-art Offline GCRL algorithms, including Quasimetric RL (QRL) and Contrastive RL (CRL).

- **Overall Superiority:** Eik-HIQL consistently outperformed or matched all baselines, particularly excelling in the most challenging settings (Table 2). This includes antmaze-stitch tasks (complex locomotion + composite datasets) and humanoidmaze tasks (high-dimensional states, unstable control).
- **Baseline Performance:** QRL performed well in simpler domains like pointmaze due to its quasimetric structure. CRL was competitive in some navigate variants but struggled in high-dimensional and stitched tasks.
- **Contact-Rich Domains:** Consistent with the HIQL comparison, Eik-HIQL performed comparably to baselines in antsoccer and manipulation. The authors attribute this to the discontinuous dynamics in these domains, which violate the smoothness assumptions underlying the Eikonal regularizer (Fig. 3).

#### 4.4. Additional Experiments (General Compatibility)

The authors demonstrated that the Eikonal regularizer is broadly compatible with other temporal-difference-based GCRL algorithms. It was successfully integrated into Goal-Conditioned IQL (GCIQL) and Goal-Conditioned Implicit V-Learning (GCIVL), resulting in Eik-GCIQL and Eik-GCIVL, respectively. These variants also showed performance improvements over their non-regularized counterparts (Table 4), confirming the general applicability of the Eikonal regularizer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and effective method for improving GCVF estimation in Offline GCRL, with several implications for the field:

- **Enhanced Value Function Estimation:** The Eikonal regularizer effectively induces a distance-like inductive bias, promoting consistent gradient magnitudes and yielding robust, globally consistent GCVFs that accurately capture environmental structure. This property is crucial for overcoming the challenges posed by limited and potentially biased offline datasets.
- **Improved Generalization and Scalability:** Eik-HIQL demonstrated superior performance and generalization capabilities, particularly in complex, long-horizon tasks such as large-scale mazes and trajectory stitching scenarios. These are areas where traditional methods often struggle, highlighting the scalability benefits of the physics-informed approach.
- **Broad Compatibility:** The Eikonal regularizer's compatibility with various temporal-difference-based GCRL algorithms (HIQL, GCIQL, GCIVL) suggests that it can be a widely applicable tool for enhancing existing and future offline RL methods.
- **Leveraging Domain Knowledge:** This work underscores the potential of Physics-informed methods to inject meaningful structural priors into learning algorithms. By deriving a regularizer from the Eikonal PDE, the authors provide a principled way to incorporate geometric domain knowledge without requiring explicit system dynamics models, which is a significant advantage in model-free settings.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Practical Applications:** The improved generalization and robustness of Eik-HIQL could lead to more reliable autonomous systems in domains like navigation and locomotion where offline data is common and safety is paramount. The ability to learn from passive data without explicit rewards makes it suitable for complex real-world deployments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for further exploration of physics-informed inductive biases in RL.
 - **Task-Adaptive Regularization:** Future research could focus on developing more sophisticated, task-adaptive speed profiles S(s) that can better handle environments with varied dynamics, including contact-rich interactions. This could extend the benefits to manipulation tasks where the current approach shows limitations.
 - **Safe GCRL:** Investigating how S(s) can be designed to explicitly encode safety constraints or collision avoidance, particularly in cluttered environments, represents a promising direction for Safe GCRL.
 - **Hybrid Dynamics:** Addressing the limitations in tasks with discontinuous or non-Lipschitz dynamics, perhaps by combining smoothness priors with mechanisms tailored for discrete contact states or representation learning, could broaden the applicability of Eikonal regularization.
 - **Multi-Agent Systems:** Extending physics-informed value learning to multi-agent settings could be explored, potentially improving coordination and planning in collaborative or competitive environments.

Overall, the paper demonstrates a method that addresses fundamental challenges in Offline GCRL, enhancing the scalability and generalization of value estimation through a principled physics-informed approach.
