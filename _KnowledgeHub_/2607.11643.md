# Xiaomi-Robotics-U0: Unified Embodied Synthesis with World Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 introduces a 38-billion-parameter multimodal autoregressive world model that unifies general image/video generation with embodied synthesis, preserving foundation model generalization while adapting to robot-centric multi-view reasoning and interaction. The system achieved superior multi-view consistency and instruction following compared to existing models, and augmented data generated by it improved real-world robot policy robustness by enhancing task completion progress in interference conditions from 36.9% to 63.2%.

## Problem

- General image/video foundation models struggle with strict multi-view consistency, accurate geometric/physical coherence, and adherence to robot embodiment constraints in embodied AI applications.
- Existing embodied world models adapt pre-trained foundation models by specializing on smaller, less diverse robot-specific datasets - scarce and costly to collect at scale - which often weakens the inherited generalization capabilities.
- The lack of structured controllability in existing models makes it difficult to precisely manipulate specific aspects of generated embodied scenes and interactions.

## Method

- A 38-billion-parameter ==multimodal autoregressive== model, **Xiaomi-Robotics-U0**, built on ==EMU3.5== and ==Qwen-3-32B==, unifies all generation tasks as ==next-token prediction== within a single discrete vocabulary.
- A diverse training corpus curated from six domains (general image-text, embodied manipulation, autonomous driving, egocentric, 3D reconstruction, game data), VLM-annotated for ==structured control==, trains the model in two continued setups: single-step training for visual generation/embodied transfer, and ==sequential training== with interleaved subtask sequences and multi-frame-rate videos for long-horizon reasoning.
- An inference-accelerating adaptation (**FlashAR+**) is implemented, introducing a ==vertical prediction head== for parallel ==anti-diagonal decoding== of image tokens, enabling faster high-resolution image generation.

## Results

- Outperformed GPT-Image-2.0 in embodied transfer (depth consistency SI-RMSE **0.1407** vs **0.4007**, segmentation mIoU **0.7621** vs **0.4105** on the Easy split) and achieved a higher win rate in human pairwise evaluations for embodied scene generation.
- Data augmentation with Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-generated data significantly improved the robustness of real-world robot policies, increasing average task completion progress in interference conditions from **36.9%** to **63.2%**.
- Achieved the highest overall EWMScore (**73.64**) on the WorldArena benchmark for video generation, leading in Instruction Following (**93.86**) and Interaction Quality (**87.30**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified autoregressive framework integrates broad visual knowledge from foundation models with embodied intelligence requirements; post-training on diverse embodied datasets with a unified objective preserves generalization (preventing catastrophic forgetting) while enhancing physical and geometric reasoning.
> - Structured multi-modal conditioning (workspace, objects, lighting, background) enables fine-grained, controllable embodied scene and video generation with strong multi-view and temporal consistency.
> - Foundation world models can function as scalable data engines for embodied intelligence, autonomously generating diverse and realistic robot experiences to address data scarcity and improve policy robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in foundation image and video generation models, such as Stable Diffusion, GPT-Image-2.0, and Sora, have demonstrated capabilities in semantic understanding, controllable generation, and visual reasoning through training on large-scale internet data. These models are proficient in synthesizing realistic images and videos from various multimodal inputs, exhibiting generalization beyond their training data distribution. These capabilities present a potential starting point for embodied intelligence, where robots are required to reason about complex environments and predict future interactions.

However, direct application of these general-purpose foundation models to embodied scenarios introduces specific challenges. Embodied generation requires strict multi-view consistency, accurate geometric and physical coherence across multiple camera perspectives, adherence to explicit robot embodiment constraints, and consistent interaction dynamics over time. Generated observations must be compatible with robot kinematics, camera calibration, and downstream manipulation policies, rather than merely appearing visually realistic. Consequently, applying existing foundation models often results in inconsistent geometry, implausible robot states, and incompatibility with robot control.

Existing embodied world models, such as Dreamer Series, World Action Models (WAMs), and Qwen-RobotWorld, attempt to bridge this gap by adapting pre-trained foundation models using robot trajectories or egocentric manipulation videos. A limitation of these approaches is that their adaptation paradigms often diverge fundamentally from foundation model pre-training. They typically specialize models solely on robot-specific datasets, which are considerably smaller, less diverse, and more repetitive compared to internet-scale visual corpora. This specialization can lead to a weakening of the generalization capability inherited from the initial foundation models, thereby limiting the diversity, controllability, and scalability of embodied generation.

This work, Xiaomi-Robotics-U0, addresses these limitations by proposing a unified framework that integrates foundation image and video generation with embodied generation. It reformulates embodied synthesis as an extension of general generation tasks, rather than a specialization, aiming to preserve the broad visual knowledge of foundation models while effectively adapting them to robot-centric multi-view reasoning and interaction modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified embodied world model that supports a diverse range of generation and prediction tasks within a single autoregressive framework. This model aims to overcome the inherent challenges in applying general foundation models to embodied intelligence, specifically the need for multi-view consistency, geometric coherence, and adherence to robot embodiment constraints.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

- **Preserving Generalization**: Current methods that adapt pre-trained foundation models to embodied settings often do so by fine-tuning exclusively on robot-specific datasets. This process can lead to catastrophic forgetting, where the model loses the rich semantic understanding and diverse generation capabilities acquired during large-scale pre-training. Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 seeks to prevent this by continuously training on both general-domain and embodied datasets, thereby maintaining the generalization of the pre-trained world foundation model.
- **Addressing Embodied Constraints**: Embodied scenarios demand strict adherence to physical and geometric realities, which are not intrinsically modeled by general image generation models. This includes consistent object positions, scales, and occlusions across multiple camera views, as well as plausible robot states and interaction dynamics. A key objective is to imbue the model with this understanding.
- **Enabling Structured Controllability**: The ability to precisely control aspects of generated scenes, such as workspace, background, foreground objects, target objects, and lighting, is crucial for both scene synthesis and data augmentation. The project aims to introduce a structured control formulation that allows for fine-grained editing while preserving multi-view consistency and interaction dynamics.
- **Extending to Sequential World Modeling**: Moving beyond static scene generation, the research aims to enable the model to generate temporally coherent manipulation videos from initial scenes. This capability is motivated by the potential to transform the model into a scalable trajectory generation engine, providing abundant synthetic data for downstream robot policy learning.
- **Scalable Data Generation**: A significant motivation is to establish foundation world models as scalable data engines for embodied intelligence. By autonomously generating diverse and realistic robot experiences, the model could address the data scarcity problem in robot learning and facilitate the development of more robust and generalized policies.
- **Unified Framework**: The motivation for a unified framework stems from the desire to consolidate various generation tasks—including text-to-image, image editing, embodied scene generation, embodied transfer, and embodied video generation—under a single autoregressive objective. This approach promotes knowledge sharing across tasks and enhances overall model efficiency and capability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 is formulated as a 38-billion-parameter multimodal autoregressive model designed for unified embodied synthesis. The methodology integrates a foundation model's generalization with embodied intelligence requirements through a specific architecture, data curation, labeling, and training strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The model learns the joint distribution over subsequent multi-modal sequences using standard next-token prediction (NTP), conditioned on a multi-modal context (text, image, robot control tokens). This unified approach allows knowledge sharing across various tasks.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The architecture is built upon the open-sourced EMU3.5, initialized from the Qwen-3-32B decoder-only Transformer. Images are tokenized using the IBQ Tokenizer, achieving a 16x16 spatial compression ratio. By extending the Qwen vocabulary with the IBQ codebook, all modalities (text, image, robot control) are represented within a unified discrete vocabulary. All generation tasks are framed as a single autoregressive sequence modeling problem, avoiding task-specific prediction heads. An inference-accelerating adaptation inspired by FlashAR is implemented, introducing a vertical prediction head for anti-diagonal decoding of image tokens, enabling parallel generation and up to 82.9x faster image generation at 1024x1024 resolution compared to the original NTP. This is further enhanced by vLLM integration for optimized KV-cache management and continuous batching.

#### 3.3. Dataset Curation and Labeling

A diverse training corpus was assembled from six domains:
- **General image-text data**: ShareGPT4V and similar benchmarks for visual-semantic alignment.
- **Embodied manipulation data**: Combines real-world (AgiBotWorld-Beta, Open X-Embodiment, proprietary MiBot) and simulated sources (RoboTwin2.0, GenieSim, InternData-A1) for physical interaction understanding.
- **Autonomous driving data**: Cosmos-Drive-Dreams for structured outdoor scenes.
- **Egocentric data**: EgoWalk for first-person perspective understanding.
- **3D reconstruction data**: ScanNet++ v2, DL3DV-10K for dense multi-view correspondences and geometry.
- **Game data**: Open-P2P for diverse visual distributions beyond real-world captures.

Data filtering includes motion blur detection, image quality detection, and gripper-state alignment. The dataset comprises 9.5 million single-step samples (56.4 billion tokens) and 2.6 million video clips (49.6 billion tokens).

A unified annotation pipeline, leveraging Qwen3-VL-235B (a vision-language model), generates dense, structured annotations:
- **General Scene Annotation**: Holistic captions for keyframes from all video data.
- **Structured Embodied Scene Annotation**: Decomposes embodied manipulation scenes into five semantic dimensions (workspace, task objects, irrelevant objects, lighting, background) for fine-grained control.
- **Geometric Modality Extraction**: Temporally consistent inverse depth maps are extracted using Video Depth Anything.
- **Trajectory-level Subtask Decomposition**: HDBSCAN clustering segments robot trajectories for keyframe sampling and VLM-generated subtask plans.

Annotated data is reorganized into task-specific formats:
- **Embodied Transfer**: Inputs include system instruction, scene description, multi-view depth maps; output is multi-view RGB images.
- **Scene Generation**: Inputs include system instruction, scene description; output is multi-view RGB images.
- **Video Generation**: Inputs include system instruction, task instruction, initial observation frame, optionally robot mask images as action guidance; output is a sequence of 15–25 RGB frames.

Data balancing employs sample-level reweighting based on data domains and task types, with a focus on maintaining coverage of robotic manipulation scenarios, eliminating redundancy, and prioritizing long-tail categories.

#### 3.4. Training

Two continued training setups are employed:
- **Single-step training**: Jointly trains Text-to-Image (T2I), Any-to-Image (X2I), Embodied Scene Generation, and Embodied Transfer datasets. This reinforces general visual generation, prevents catastrophic forgetting, and enables synthesis of physically plausible robot observations and cross-scene multi-view generation.
- **Sequential training**: Extends the model to long-horizon embodied reasoning. This involves:
 - Image-text interleaved subtask-subgoal sequences to learn task decomposition and future observation prediction.
 - Multi-frame-rate video sequences (1, 3, and 5 FPS) from various large-scale manipulation video datasets (e.g., InternData-A1, GenieSim) to capture both long-horizon planning and fine-grained dynamics.

#### 3.5. Inference

The FlashAR+ extension is used for inference acceleration, applying parallel autoregressive generation only to the final target image region while keeping multi-source conditioning unchanged. A prefix-conditioned step-causal mask allows target image tokens to attend to the conditional prefix but only to earlier anti-diagonal steps within the target image. The loss function includes horizontal and vertical auxiliary losses to stabilize the H/V gated fusion. vLLM integration further optimizes decoding, achieving significant speedups while maintaining generation quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 demonstrates capabilities across both single-step and sequential embodied generation tasks, with reported improvements over comparison models.

#### 4.1. Embodied Transfer

On a benchmark of 300 samples (150 Easy, 150 Hard), Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 reportedly outperformed GPT-Image-2.0 across all objective metrics related to depth consistency (SI-RMSE, δ1, AbsRel), structural fidelity (Canny F1, Precision, Recall), and semantic grounding (Segmentation mIoU, Recall). For instance, on the Easy split, its Depth SI-RMSE was 0.1407 compared to GPT-Image-2.0's 0.4007, and Segmentation mIoU was 0.7621 compared to 0.4105. Qualitatively, Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 generated multi-view-consistent RGB images that faithfully adhered to both edited textual descriptions and multi-view depth conditions, preserving object identity, appearance, and arrangement across views. In contrast, GPT-Image-2.0 produced visually plausible single views but frequently violated input depth and lacked cross-view consistency.
The model also demonstrated zero-shot data augmentation by generating diverse visual alternatives (background, workspace, lighting) for real-world manipulation trajectories, extending visual coverage beyond the training set.

#### 4.2. Embodied Scene Generation

In human-based pairwise evaluations on a benchmark of 400 LLM-generated scene prompts, Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 reportedly achieved a higher win rate against GPT-Image-2.0. It showed advantages in multi-view geometric consistency while maintaining comparable instruction-following performance. Qualitative examples illustrate the model's ability to generate geometrically coherent multi-view observations with consistent spatial layouts for diverse robot arm types and scene descriptions, including those trained on simulated data, suggesting sim-to-real generalization.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

To assess the impact on downstream policy learning, Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 was used to augment expert demonstrations for three real-world tabletop manipulation tasks (Store Earphones, Fold Towel, Pack Box). A π₀.₅ policy was post-trained on either original clean data or a mixture of clean and Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-augmented data (style-transferred episodes).
In evaluations across a "base group" (in-distribution conditions) and an "interference group" (held-out backgrounds and lighting), the Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-Aug policy performed comparably in the base group. However, it reportedly exhibited substantially higher task completion progress in the interference group across all tasks, indicating improved robustness to visual distribution shifts. For example, the average task completion progress in the interference group was 63.2% for Xiaomi-Robotics-U0-Aug compared to 36.9% for the Original policy. This suggests that the augmentation process enhances visual invariance for downstream robot policies.

#### 4.4. Video Generation

On the WorldArena benchmark, Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 (referred to as UNIS) achieved the highest overall EWMScore (73.64) among over 100 submitted models. It reportedly led in Instruction Following (93.86) and Interaction Quality (87.30), indicating strong semantic alignment and physically plausible object interactions. The model also scored highly in Perspectivity (98.84) and Motion Smoothness (95.51), reflecting accurate 3D viewpoint geometry and stable motion.
Furthermore, the model demonstrated the ability to generate novel videos conditioned on language instructions from *self-synthesized* initial scenes. Examples included accurate contact modeling (grasping/placing a cup), dynamic background changes (person walking), and realistic physics for deformable objects (bending paper).

#### 4.5. Text-to-Image & Image Edit Benchmarks

Evaluations on GenEval (T2I) and ImgEdit Basic (image editing) indicated that Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 retained strong general image generation and editing capabilities after embodied post-training. While it performed competitively on most appearance-oriented generation and local editing tasks, some performance drops were noted in highly complex scenarios requiring multi-constraint composition, precise spatial grounding, or symbolic binding, relative to dedicated open-domain image generators like Qwen-Image.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 represents an advancement in the integration of general-purpose foundation models with embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

- **Unified Framework for Embodied Synthesis**: By unifying text-to-image generation, image editing, multi-view embodied scene generation, embodied transfer, and embodied video generation within a single autoregressive framework, the model demonstrates a novel approach to transferring broad visual knowledge from foundation models to embodied intelligence. This integration is reported to preserve the generalization capabilities of the underlying foundation model while acquiring robot-specific reasoning.

- **Enhanced Data Generation for Robot Learning**: The capability to generate high-quality, diverse, and geometrically consistent synthetic manipulation trajectories and videos positions Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 as a scalable data engine. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning: the scarcity and cost of real-world robot interaction data. The model can provide a continuous stream of varied robot experiences, which can be leveraged for policy learning and improving agent robustness.

- **Improved Robustness of Downstream Robot Policies**: The real-world experiments demonstrate that data augmentation using Xiaomi-Robotics-U0 can significantly improve the out-of-distribution robustness of downstream robot policies. This impact is crucial for the deployment of robots in uncontrolled, real-world environments where visual conditions can vary substantially from training data. By generating diverse visual appearances (backgrounds, lighting, textures) while preserving robot states and actions, the model contributes to policies that generalize better to novel scenes.

- **Advanced Controllability and Multi-view Consistency**: The introduction of a structured control formulation that disentangles semantic dimensions (workspace, background, objects, lighting) enables fine-grained, controllable scene manipulation. Coupled with robust multi-view consistency, this allows for the generation of coherent 3D scenes from diverse prompts, a capability that is particularly important for robots operating with multiple cameras.

- **Advancement in World Model Capabilities**: The results on benchmarks like WorldArena suggest that foundation world models can effectively function not only as predictive embodied world models but also as powerful data generators. This expands the perceived utility of large generative models, indicating their potential to simulate complex physical interactions and dynamic environments.

Despite its capabilities, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on intermediate depth estimation for embodied transfer, the separate generation of scenes and videos (which can lead to accumulated errors in long rollouts), and the context window size limiting long-duration video modeling. Addressing these aspects in future work could further enhance the model's utility and lead to more advanced agentic embodied systems.
