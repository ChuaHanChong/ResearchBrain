# ApexNav: An Adaptive Exploration Strategy for Zero-Shot Object Navigation with Target-centric Semantic Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> ApexNav is a framework for zero-shot object navigation that dynamically switches between semantic-guided and geometry-based exploration, while employing a target-centric semantic fusion method to robustly identify objects. The approach establishes state-of-the-art performance in simulated environments and demonstrates successful real-world deployment on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Existing Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON) methods often exhibit inefficient exploration strategies due to an over-reliance on semantic reasoning, particularly in environments with weak or ambiguous cues.
- Current ZSON approaches suffer from unreliable target identification, stemming from noisy single-frame object detections and simple fusion methods that can permanently embed high-confidence misdetections.
- Many exploration strategies lack adaptability, using fixed two-stage processes or greedy frontier selection, which can lead to oscillatory behaviors and suboptimal paths in complex environments.

## Method

- An adaptive exploration strategy dynamically switches between semantic-based planning (using an ==Asymmetric Traveling Salesperson Problem==) and geometry-based expansion (nearest-frontier), based on the strength and distribution of semantic scores.
- A target-centric semantic fusion mechanism accumulates multi-frame object detection information, incorporates LLM-derived insights on similar objects and adaptive confidence thresholds, and penalizes undetected objects to filter out false positives.
- A safe waypoint navigation module guides the agent's movement by evaluating discrete actions based on both efficiency (towards local targets) and safety (avoiding obstacles via ESDF maps) to prevent collisions.

## Results

- ApexNav achieved state-of-the-art performance in simulation, with relative improvements of **19.8%** in Success Rate (SR) and **16.9%** in Success weighted by inverse Path Length (SPL) on the HM3Dv2 dataset, and an **11.3%** higher SPL on MP3D compared to previous best methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the adaptive exploration strategy and target-centric semantic fusion, demonstrating that the proposed fusion method outperforms max-confidence fusion in robustness against false positives.
- The framework was successfully deployed on an AgileX LIMO robot in real-world environments, achieving a **100%** success rate over 6 trials with an SPL of **0.723**, and exhibiting practical real-time performance on consumer-grade hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adapting the exploration strategy by switching between semantic-guided and geometry-based modes, inspired by human search behavior, improves efficiency in diverse environments by balancing targeted search with broad coverage.
> - Robust target identification requires more than simple semantic fusion; maintaining long-term object memory, applying absence penalties for transient detections, and leveraging LLM reasoning for context-aware confidence updates are crucial to mitigate false positives.
> - Combining global path optimization for semantically rich regions (Asymmetric TSP) with local greedy expansion for exploring unknown areas proves to be more effective than uniform or utility-based exploration strategies for ZSON.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav)**, a fundamental challenge in robotics and artificial intelligence where an autonomous agent must navigate an unknown environment to find a specified target object. Historically, ObjectNav methods have progressed from reinforcement learning-based end-to-end approaches requiring extensive training and high computational costs, to modular learning-based methods that reduced training effort but still suffered from generalization issues.

The recent paradigm shift, driven by advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has led to the emergence of **Zero-Shot Object Navigation (ZSON)**. ZSON methods, unlike their data-driven predecessors, can generalize to novel environments and unseen objects without task-specific training, offering superior sim-to-real transfer capabilities. However, existing ZSON approaches face two critical limitations that ApexNav seeks to address:

1. **Inefficient Exploration due to Over-reliance on Semantics:** Many current ZSON methods (e.g., L3MVN, VLFM, SG-Nav) heavily depend on semantic reasoning for exploration. While powerful in semantically rich areas, this approach becomes inefficient or even misleading in environments with weak semantic cues, occlusions, or visually ambiguous objects (e.g., a blank wall, a plant that could be anywhere). Some methods use fixed two-stage strategies (e.g., SemUtil), which lack adaptability. Others utilize greedy frontier selection based on semantic scores, leading to oscillatory behaviors or suboptimal paths.
2. **Unreliable Target Identification due to Misdetections:** The success rate of ZSON is highly dependent on accurate object detection. Existing methods often rely on single-frame detections, making them vulnerable to transient false positives, or use max-confidence fusion for semantic maps, which can permanently embed high-confidence misdetections. While some methods introduce validation modules (e.g., TriHelper, SG-Nav), they often lack a robust, multi-frame fusion mechanism that can effectively filter out detector errors in complex, cluttered scenes.

ApexNav distinguishes itself by tackling these specific challenges. It proposes an adaptive exploration strategy that dynamically switches between semantic and geometry-based exploration based on the environment's semantic richness, and introduces a target-centric semantic fusion mechanism that robustly identifies targets by maintaining long-term memory and context-aware confidence updates. This work advances the state-of-the-art by providing a more efficient and reliable framework for ZSON, bridging a critical gap in current methodologies and pushing towards more robust embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the ApexNav research are to develop a zero-shot object navigation (ZSON) framework that is both **more efficient and more reliable** than existing state-of-the-art methods. This overarching goal is motivated by two critical limitations observed in current ZSON approaches:

1. **Addressing Inefficient Exploration:** The paper highlights that existing ZSON methods often exhibit inefficient exploration strategies. Many approaches overly rely on semantic reasoning to guide the agent, which is problematic when semantic cues are weak, ambiguous, or non-existent (e.g., facing a blank wall, encountering occlusions, or searching for objects that appear in various contexts). This over-reliance can lead to prolonged exploration, unnecessary movements, and a reduction in overall navigation efficiency. The motivation here is to enable the agent to intelligently adapt its exploration strategy based on the availability and strength of semantic information in the environment.
2. **Enhancing Robustness Against Misdetections:** Another significant challenge is the susceptibility of current methods to misdetections. Single-frame object detections are inherently noisy, and even fusion strategies like max-confidence fusion can be compromised by sporadic, high-confidence false positives that are difficult to correct later. This unreliability leads to "false positive" failures where the agent incorrectly identifies a non-target object and terminates the task. The motivation is to develop a more robust target identification mechanism that can effectively integrate multi-frame observations and distinguish the true target from visually similar objects or detector errors, thereby improving the overall success rate.

Inspired by human search behavior—where individuals quickly explore unknown areas when cues are weak, focus their search when strong cues emerge, and gather additional evidence before confirming a target—ApexNav aims to imbue autonomous agents with similar adaptive and evidence-gathering capabilities. The key motivations converge on creating a ZSON system that is both intelligent in its exploration and resilient in its perception, leading to superior performance in complex, unknown real-world and simulated environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ApexNav is a zero-shot object navigation framework designed for enhanced efficiency and reliability. Its methodology comprises several interconnected modules: Environmental Mapping, an Adaptive Exploration Strategy, Target-centric Semantic Fusion, and Safe Waypoint Navigation.

**A. Environmental Mapping:**
The agent builds two types of 2D maps:
1. **Frontier Map:** This probabilistic grid map classifies cells as free, occupied, or unknown using raycasting from depth images. Frontiers, defined as free cells adjacent to unknown ones, are incrementally updated and clustered using Principal Component Analysis for computational efficiency.
2. **Semantic Score Map:** A 2D grid storing relevance scores between environmental regions and the target object. It uses BLIP-2 (a Vision-Language Model) to compute cosine similarity scores between RGB images and a text prompt (e.g., "Seems there is a <target object> or a <target room> ahead"). LLMs are employed offline to infer potential room names and augment prompts. Scores are projected onto free grids and weighted by confidence based on viewing angle. Multi-frame semantic information is integrated using a confidence-weighted average, with a squared weighting term for confidence to emphasize more reliable predictions.

**B. Adaptive Exploration Strategy:**
This module dynamically switches between semantic-based and geometry-based exploration.
1. **Mode-switching Criteria:** The strategy analyzes the distribution of semantic scores across frontier clusters. It calculates the max-to-mean ratio (`r`) and standard deviation (`σ`) of these scores. If both `r` and `σ` exceed predefined thresholds (`r_t`, `σ_t`), indicating strong and uneven semantic cues, the agent switches to semantic-based exploration. Otherwise, it defaults to geometry-based exploration to prioritize coverage.
2. **Semantic-based Exploration:** When strong semantic cues are present, the agent selects a subset of high-scoring frontiers and solves an **Asymmetric Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP)** using the Lin-Kernighan-Helsgaun (LKH-Solver). The cost matrix for the TSP is defined by A*-computed navigation distances between the agent's position and frontier centers, ensuring an optimal visiting sequence that balances semantic relevance with global path efficiency, thus avoiding oscillations common in greedy approaches.
3. **Geometry-based Exploration:** When semantic scores are uniform or weak, the agent employs a **nearest-frontier strategy**. This greedy approach prioritizes rapid expansion of coverage to acquire new information, which ablation studies showed to be more effective than TSP-based planning for ObjectNav's goal-directed nature when semantic guidance is limited.

**C. Target-centric Semantic Fusion:**
This method enhances the reliability of target identification by maintaining long-term memory of the target and visually similar objects.
1. **Object Reasoning with LLMs:** Offline, an LLM (DeepSeek-V3) is prompted using Chain-of-Thought to generate a list of objects commonly misclassified as the target (`L_obj`), potential target rooms, and an adaptive confidence threshold (`c_th`) for the target based on its characteristics (e.g., size).
2. **Object Detection and Segmentation:** For each RGB image, YOLOv7 (for COCO objects) or Grounding-DINO (for others) detects objects from `L_obj`. Mobile-SAM segments detected instances, and their 3D point clouds are extracted and denoised.
3. **Semantic Label Confidence Fusion:** This core component incrementally updates object clusters. Each cluster stores multi-label information: a 3D point cloud (`pt_l_o`), confidence (`c_l_o`), and accumulated detection volume (`n_l_o`, approximated by down-sampled point cloud points).
 - **Fusion Logic:** A new detection is merged into intersecting clusters. The point cloud is merged, and confidence (`c_l_o`) and detection volume (`n_l_o`) are updated as a weighted average, where the weights are proportional to the detection volumes. This emphasizes larger, more reliable observations.
 - **Absence Penalty:** If a previously detected object in a cluster is *not* present in the current frame's field of view, its confidence is penalized by integrating a "zero confidence" detection with a volume determined by its current visible points. This effectively lowers confidence for transient or incorrect detections, mitigating persistent false positives (e.g., Fig. 6b).
 - **Best-matching Label:** For each cluster, the label with the highest `c_o * n_o` (confidence times detection volume) is selected as the most probable identification.
4. **Selection of Reliable Targets:** An object cluster is deemed a "reliable target" if its best-matching label is the target object and its fused confidence (`c_o`) exceeds the LLM-derived adaptive threshold (`c_th`). The agent navigates to reliable targets. If none are found after exploration, "suspected targets" (below `c_th`) are considered.

**D. Safe Waypoint Navigation:**
Once a frontier or object waypoint is selected, this module guides the agent's movement. Instead of directly following shortest paths (which can lead to collisions), it evaluates discrete actions based on both efficiency and safety:
1. **Efficiency Cost:** Encourages movement towards a local target (`p_local`) along the A*-computed shortest path, penalizing actions that increase distance.
2. **Safety Cost:** Uses a local ESDF map to compute distances to obstacles (`d_obs`). Actions whose paths pass too close to obstacles (within `d_th`) are heavily penalized, promoting collision-free navigation.
The action with the lowest total cost is chosen. For real-world deployment, MINCO is used to generate continuous, collision-free spatiotemporal trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ApexNav demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in both simulated and real-world environments, addressing critical limitations of prior ZSON methods.

**A. Simulation Experimental Results:**
Evaluations were conducted on HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2, and MP3D datasets, using Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by inverse Path Length (SPL) as key metrics.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** ApexNav consistently outperforms existing SOTA methods:
 - On HM3Dv1, it achieves relative improvements of 5.5% in SR (over TriHelper) and 8.6% in SPL (over VLFM).
 - On HM3Dv2, it establishes a new baseline with significant improvements: 19.8% increase in SR and 16.9% improvement in SPL compared to the previously best-performing VLFM.
 - On MP3D, while SR is marginally lower than SG-Nav, ApexNav achieves an 11.3% higher SPL, indicating superior efficiency.
These results validate ApexNav's enhanced efficiency, largely attributed to the adaptive exploration strategy, and its increased reliability, stemming from the target-centric semantic fusion.

**B. Failure Cause Analysis:**
Analysis across datasets categorized failures into: Different Floor, False Positive, No Frontier, Stepout, and Missing Target.
- **False Positives (over 11%):** Primarily caused by persistent detector errors and incorrect fusion, despite the improvements from ApexNav's fusion.
- **Different Floor (HM3Dv1 & MP3D > 13%):** A limitation of ApexNav's 2D mapping approach, which struggles with multi-floor tasks.
- **No Frontier:** Often linked to 2D mapping limitations, especially when starting near a staircase where vertical distances are ambiguous.
- **Stepout:** More prevalent in larger scenes (HM3Dv2, MP3D), indicating that the 500-step limit can be a constraint.
- **Missing Target:** Rare, mostly for small or ambiguous objects, pointing to current detector limitations.

**C. Ablation Study on Adaptive Exploration Strategy:**
- **Strategy Comparison:** Two-stage (adaptive) strategies consistently outperform single-stage (pure semantic or pure geometric) strategies. The combination of TSP for semantic-based exploration and greedy for geometry-based exploration yields the highest overall performance.
- **Outperformance vs. Utility-based:** ApexNav's adaptive strategy outperforms utility-based methods (which divide semantic score by path length) by effectively prioritizing semantically promising regions even if distant, avoiding undervaluation due to path cost.
- **Exploration Metrics (`σ_t`, `r_t`):** Varying the thresholds for mode-switching revealed a "hump-shaped" performance curve, indicating an optimal balance between semantic and geometric information. Over-reliance on either reduces efficiency, confirming the importance of adaptive switching.

**D. Ablation Study on Target-centric Semantic Fusion:**
- **Importance of Fusion:** Removing the semantic fusion module (either with low or high detector confidence thresholds) drastically reduces SR and SPL across all datasets, underscoring the critical role of fusion. ApexNav's approach, using a lower DCT and refining candidates through fusion, shows greater robustness than relying on a high DCT alone.
- **Superiority over Max-fusion:** ApexNav's fusion method outperforms max-confidence fusion, preventing task failures caused by high-confidence mislabeling by effectively penalizing absence and integrating detection volume.
- **Contribution of Components:** Ablating "similar objects" (LLM reasoning) or "observation penalty" (confidence reduction for undetected objects) also reduced performance, confirming the effectiveness of each sub-module in mitigating false positives and improving robustness.

**E. Ablation Study on Safe Waypoint Navigation:**
- Removing the safe waypoint navigation (i.e., relying solely on shortest path) leads to a significant decline in SR and SPL, and an increase in Distance-to-Goal (DTG). This highlights that the safe navigation component is crucial for preventing collisions and ensuring stable, efficient progress.

**F. Real-world Deployment:**
- ApexNav was successfully deployed on an AgileX LIMO robot in dormitory and laboratory environments. It achieved a 100% success rate over 6 trials, with an SPL of 0.723, average traveled distance of 15.85m, and average traveled time of 56s.
- **Runtime Analysis:** Module runtimes on consumer-grade hardware (RTX 4060 Laptop GPU, AMD R9 7945HX CPU) generally fall within desired real-time intervals, affirming ApexNav's practical deployability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ApexNav framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Zero-Shot Object Navigation, offering both theoretical and practical implications:

**A. Enhanced Efficiency and Reliability:**
The core significance lies in ApexNav's ability to address the two major bottlenecks in ZSON: exploration efficiency and target identification reliability.
- **Adaptive Exploration:** By dynamically switching between semantic-guided and geometry-based exploration, ApexNav achieves more efficient navigation in diverse environments, adapting to the varying availability of semantic cues. This adaptive nature, inspired by human behavior, reduces redundant movements and accelerates target localization, especially in scenarios where semantic information is sparse or misleading.
- **Robust Target Identification:** The target-centric semantic fusion method significantly improves the robustness of object recognition against noisy detectors and visually similar objects. By maintaining long-term memory, leveraging LLM reasoning for similar objects and confidence thresholds, and applying context-aware confidence updates (including an absence penalty), ApexNav mitigates false positives that often plague other methods, leading to higher success rates.

**B. State-of-the-Art Performance:**
ApexNav establishes new benchmarks on widely used datasets (HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2, MP3D), demonstrating superior performance in both success rate (SR) and path efficiency (SPL). This validates its architectural design and the effectiveness of its novel modules, pushing the capabilities of embodied AI agents in complex, unknown indoor environments.

**C. Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer and Practical Deployability:**
The successful real-world experiments on a physical robot (AgileX LIMO) confirm the practicality and robust generalization capabilities of ApexNav. The demonstrated real-time performance of its modules on consumer-grade hardware further highlights its potential for real-world applications beyond research simulators, making it a promising candidate for deployment in various robotic tasks like search and rescue, household assistance, or industrial inspection.

**D. Advancing Embodied AI and Human-Robot Interaction:**
By enabling robots to navigate and find objects more intelligently and reliably in unfamiliar settings, ApexNav contributes to the broader goal of creating more autonomous and capable embodied AI agents. Such advancements lay the groundwork for more sophisticated human-robot interaction scenarios where robots can understand and fulfill high-level commands, even for objects they haven't been explicitly trained on.

**E. Insights for Future Research:**
The paper also openly discusses current limitations, which are valuable for guiding future research:
- **3D Mapping and Multi-Floor Navigation:** The current 2D mapping struggles with multi-floor environments, suggesting a need for integrated 3D semantic mapping.
- **Handling Hidden Objects:** ApexNav assumes visible targets, but real-world objects are often hidden, necessitating object interaction and manipulation capabilities.
- **Finer-grained Exploration and Ambiguous Objects:** Challenges with small or ambiguous objects call for more robust detection models and finer-grained exploration strategies, potentially leveraging richer language priors from Vision-Language Navigation.

In conclusion, ApexNav represents a significant step forward in zero-shot object navigation, offering a robust and efficient framework that addresses key challenges faced by autonomous agents operating in dynamic and unknown environments. Its contributions pave the way for more intelligent and practical robotic systems capable of performing complex object-seeking tasks.
