# SpaceDG: Benchmarking Spatial Intelligence under Visual Degradation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SpaceDG dataset and benchmark assess the spatial intelligence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) under various visual degradations, finding that existing models perform worse on degraded inputs but that targeted fine-tuning significantly improves their robustness and can exceed human performance.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for MLLM spatial intelligence assume 'perfect observation' with pristine visual inputs, neglecting real-world imperfections.
- Embodied and autonomous systems frequently encounter visual degradations (e.g., blur, low light, weather) that are not accounted for in current evaluations.
- The robustness of MLLMs for fine-grained spatial intelligence specifically under visual degradation has been largely unaddressed.

## Method

- A physically grounded degradation synthesis engine was developed to create geometry-consistent degraded images from 3D scenes while preserving spatial ground truth.
- A large-scale dataset, **SpaceDG**, and a human-verified benchmark, **SpaceDG**-Bench, were constructed with 9 physically realistic visual degradation types.
- Twenty-five MLLMs were evaluated in zero-shot settings, and models were fine-tuned on **SpaceDG** to investigate degradation-aware training.

## Results

- All 25 evaluated MLLMs demonstrated reduced accuracy on degraded inputs; for example, Gemini-3.1-Pro's accuracy dropped from **63.1%** (clean) to **56.7%** (degraded).
- A SpaceDG-SFT-Qwen3 model achieved **66.1%** accuracy on degraded inputs, outperforming human performance (**59.5%**) under similar conditions by **6.6** percentage points.
- Low-light and haze degradations caused the most significant performance drops, while object-centric and fine-grained semantic perception tasks were most vulnerable.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual degradations consistently impair spatial reasoning across all MLLMs, leading to universal performance drops.
> - Degradation-aware supervised fine-tuning on the SpaceDG dataset substantially improves MLLM performance and robustness, even allowing models to surpass human performance under degraded conditions.
> - The impact of degradations varies by type and task, with low-light/haze and fine-grained object-centric tasks being most sensitive.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated advancements in spatial intelligence, bridging 2D visual recognition with 3D physical reasoning. This capability is fundamental for visual cognition and is crucial for applications such as embodied agents and autonomous systems. Existing benchmarks, including Cambrian-S, VST, and SenseNova-SI, have been developed to evaluate these models, often reporting high performance on spatial tasks. However, a common characteristic of these benchmarks is the assumption of "perfect observation," meaning they predominantly utilize clean, high-resolution, and well-illuminated images.

In real-world environments, visual observations are frequently affected by various degradations stemming from imperfect sensing pipelines, dynamic motion, adverse weather conditions, lens distortions, and data compression. These degradations are not simulated artifacts but common occurrences that significantly impact the quality of visual inputs. While low-level vision and computational photography have extensively studied these degradations (e.g., motion blur, low-resolution imaging, low-light, adverse weather), and recent work has begun to assess general MLLM robustness to common image corruptions for semantic understanding and object recognition, the robustness of MLLMs specifically for fine-grained spatial intelligence under such imperfect visual conditions has remained largely unexplored.

This work addresses this gap by introducing a dataset and benchmark that incorporate realistic visual degradations while preserving underlying 3D spatial structures. It leverages advances in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as an efficient and expressive 3D representation for novel view synthesis and scene reconstruction. By integrating physically grounded degradation models with 3DGS, the research aims to generate degraded observations that are geometrically consistent and realistic, thereby providing a more representative evaluation of MLLM spatial intelligence in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental question: "How robust is the spatial intelligence of current MLLMs when visual observations are imperfect?" Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks predominantly evaluate MLLMs under idealized conditions with pristine visual inputs, overlooking the pervasive visual degradations encountered in real-world deployments. Such degradations include motion blur, low light, adverse weather, lens distortion, and compression artifacts, which are inherent to physical and resource-constrained environments. The current lack of evaluation under these realistic conditions creates a critical gap between reported model performance and actual utility in applications like embodied agents and autonomous systems.

To address this deficiency, the research established three core requirements for a suitable benchmark:
1. **Realistic Visual Degradations:** The benchmark must introduce degradations that accurately reflect real-world phenomena rather than artificial corruptions.
2. **Preservation of Underlying 3D Spatial Structure:** The degradations must be applied in a manner that ensures the ground truth 3D spatial information remains invariant and accurate.
3. **Support for Diverse Spatial Reasoning Tasks with Reliable Ground Truth:** The benchmark should encompass a wide range of spatial reasoning tasks and provide unambiguous, physically grounded answers.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work were:
- **To introduce SpaceDG:** The first large-scale dataset specifically designed for degradation-aware spatial understanding, constructed using a physically grounded degradation synthesis engine that integrates with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering.
- **To establish SpaceDG-Bench:** A human-verified benchmark derived from SpaceDG, comprising high-quality questions that span diverse spatial reasoning categories and incorporate various visual degradation types.
- **To conduct a comprehensive evaluation:** To systematically assess the performance of current MLLMs under imperfect visual observations using SpaceDG-Bench, thereby identifying the robustness gap in their spatial intelligence.
- **To demonstrate the efficacy of degradation-aware training:** To investigate whether fine-tuning MLLMs on the SpaceDG dataset can improve their robustness to visual degradations without compromising performance on clean images.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of SpaceDG, a dataset and benchmark for degradation-aware spatial understanding, built upon an automated data engine.

**Data Engine Overview:**
The SpaceDG data engine integrates 3DGS-based scene representation, QA pair initialization, and a physically realistic degradation synthesis pipeline.

**3D Data Collection:**
The process begins by leveraging the Holi-Spatial pipeline, which converts raw video streams into geometry-consistent 3D semantic scenes. For each video, depth and camera-pose priors are estimated using DepthAnything-v3 and COLMAP, respectively. These priors are used to optimize a geometrically constrained 3DGS representation, yielding a renderable scene with calibrated camera poses and dense depth maps. Semantic masks are obtained from key frames using SAM3, then lifted and associated across views using reconstructed depth, camera poses, and bounding-box Intersection over Union (IoU) to create object-level 3D instances with 3D bounding boxes.

**QA Pairs Generation:**
Spatial question-answer (QA) pairs are generated directly from the reconstructed 3D scene information.
1. **Instance Descriptions:** For each 3D instance, a view-invariant language description is generated by prompting a VLM based on its appearance in its highest-confidence SAM3 mask image. These descriptions enable natural object referencing in questions.
2. **View Sampling:** For multi-view questions, image pairs are sampled from the scene's covisibility matrix, ensuring non-trivial camera motion. Covisibility is determined by reprojecting depth-supported 3D points from one view to another and checking depth-based reprojection consistency.
3. **Structured QA Templates:** Structured QA templates are instantiated for various tasks, with answers computed from camera extrinsics, 3D box centers, object extents, and relative directions. Answers are formatted as multiple-choice options, binary decisions, or metric values.
4. **Ambiguity Filters:** Geometric ambiguity filters discard ill-defined spatial configurations, such as triplets with intersecting bounding boxes or rotations too close to boundaries. Language ambiguity filters use a VLM-based judge to ensure unique identification of objects referenced in descriptions, followed by manual verification.

**Degradation Synthesis:**
A physically grounded degradation pipeline is developed to ensure physical realism and multi-view consistency. Degradations are applied directly within the 3DGS rendering process or in the linear light domain, ensuring the underlying 3D spatial ground truth remains invariant. Nine representative degradation types are synthesized across four categories:
1. **Optical & Dynamic Degradations:**
 - **Defocus:** Modeled using the thin-lens approximation, where the Circle of Confusion (CoC) radius is added to the 2D projected covariance matrix of each Gaussian during rasterization, with opacity compensation for energy conservation.
 - **Distortion:** The standard pinhole projection model in the CUDA rasterizer is modified using an equidistant polynomial model to simulate non-linear geometric warping from wide-angle or fisheye lenses.
 - **Motion Blur:** Simulated by continuous time integration of linear light, interpolating camera poses via Spherical Linear Interpolation (Slerp) and accumulating frames over multiple sub-steps to produce realistic directional and rotational blur.
2. **Meteorological Degradations:**
 - **Haze:** Simulated using Koschmieder’s law, leveraging accurate depth maps rendered from 3DGS to enforce depth-dependent visibility decay.
 - **Water Droplets:** A procedural multi-layer height map generates pixel-wise surface normals, which are used to compute localized refraction offsets based on Snell’s law, combined with local optical blurring and Phong specular highlights.
3. **Photometric Degradations:** (Performed in the linear light domain before final image encoding)
 - **Low-light:** Scene illumination is scaled by a small exposure coefficient. A physics-based sensor noise model is injected, incorporating photon shot noise (Poisson distribution) and read noise (Gaussian/Tukey-Lambda distribution).
 - **Over-exposure:** A large exposure gain is applied, and standard sensor noise is injected. Values are clipped to the sensor’s maximum capacity prior to Gamma encoding, simulating saturation and irreversible detail loss.
4. **Digital Degradations:** (Performed directly on the encoded 2D image matrix)
 - **JPEG Compression:** Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) block quantization is explicitly applied, controlled by a quality factor, to introduce ringing and blocking artifacts.
 - **Low-resolution:** Images are downsampled to a lower resolution by a scale factor and then upsampled to the original dimensions, truncating high-frequency spatial details.

**SpaceDG Dataset and SpaceDG-Bench:**
- **SpaceDG Dataset:** Comprises approximately 971,090 QA instances across 162,071 images from 584 real indoor scenes, covering all nine physically synthesized degradation types.
- **SpaceDG-Bench:** A manually curated and verified benchmark established from 320 scenes disjoint from the SpaceDG training set. It contains 1,102 unique high-quality QA pairs (379 single-view, 723 multi-view). Each benchmark item includes one clean (original) condition and nine degraded conditions, resulting in 9,918 VQA pairs. The benchmark is balanced at the image level, with 1,725 images per degradation condition.

**Spatial Questions Design:**
The benchmark systematically designs 11 distinct question categories to comprehensively assess spatial intelligence in single-view and multi-view settings:
- **Camera-centric:** Tasks requiring estimation of camera translation distance and relative rotations (yaw, pitch, roll) between viewpoints.
- **Object-centric:** Tasks involving object counting, object direction, distance estimation, and fine-grained 3D spatial extents ([w, l, h]).
- **Camera-object Relational:** Tasks evaluating inter-object and camera-object spatial relations, including cross-view direction and relative positioning.

**Quality Verification:**
A two-stage filtering pipeline is employed:
1. **MLLM Filter:** Qwen3-VL-32B acts as an automated judge to identify and discard ambiguous questions.
2. **Human Review:** A human expert manually screens the remaining QA pairs via a dedicated interface, followed by independent review by two experts to remove any remaining ambiguities, incorrect answers, or ill-formed options, resulting in the final verified benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted a comprehensive evaluation of 25 MLLMs on SpaceDG-Bench, including proprietary (e.g., GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.1-Pro), open-source general (e.g., Qwen3.5, InternVL3.5), spatial-intelligence (e.g., Cambrian-S, SenseNova-SI), and robotic-brain models (e.g., ACE-Brain-0). All evaluations were performed in zero-shot settings, using Accuracy for multiple-choice and binary questions, and Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) for numerical answers.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Visual Degradations Consistently Impair Spatial Reasoning:** All evaluated MLLMs exhibited reduced performance under degraded visual inputs compared to clean images. For instance, Gemini-3.1-Pro, a high-performing proprietary model, dropped from 63.1% accuracy on clean images to 56.7% on degraded images. Open-source models like InternVL3.5-38B showed a similar trend, decreasing from 52.9% to 47.7%. This indicates a substantial robustness gap in current MLLMs' spatial intelligence under realistic conditions. Non-image baselines confirmed that degraded images still retain essential visual information, suggesting that the benchmark questions are not solvable through language shortcuts alone.

2. **Humans Struggle with Extreme Visual Degradation:** A human-level assessment on a 900-question subset of SpaceDG-Bench revealed that human performance dropped from 80.4% accuracy on clean images to 59.5% under degraded conditions, representing a 20.9% decrease. This finding suggests that severe visual corruptions challenge not only MLLMs but also human observers in fine-grained spatial judgment, highlighting the inherent difficulty of the task.

3. **Degradation-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Improves Performance:** Fine-tuning MLLMs on the SpaceDG dataset significantly improved their spatial reasoning capabilities and robustness. For example, SpaceDG-SFT-Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct improved its accuracy from 49.1% to 73.2% on clean images and from 42.1% to 66.1% on degraded inputs. Notably, under degraded conditions, the fine-tuned model (66.1%) surpassed the human reference performance (59.5%) by 6.6 percentage points. Ablation studies indicated that degradation-augmented SFT specifically enhances robustness without performance degradation on clean inputs and generalizes to unseen corruptions. Evaluations on mixed-degradation scenarios further confirmed these robustness gains. Additionally, fine-tuning on SpaceDG did not adversely affect performance on other general benchmarks (MMSI-Bench, MindCube).

4. **Spatial Fine-tuning Enhances Visual Robustness but Reduces Degradation Comprehension:** Spatially fine-tuned and robotic brain models demonstrated a smaller performance drop when transitioning from clean to degraded inputs (average 5.5% decline) compared to general-purpose models (average 7.6% decline), indicating a higher inherent robustness. However, when explicitly provided with degradation type and severity in prompts, general-purpose models showed consistent performance gains, whereas spatially fine-tuned models exhibited minimal or even negative improvement. This suggests that spatial fine-tuning may encourage the development of degradation-agnostic visual representations, prioritizing task-level robustness over explicit sensitivity to image quality cues.

**Degradation-wise Correlation Analysis:**
Using the absolute point-biserial Pearson correlation coefficient (|r|) to quantify sensitivity, the analysis revealed:
- **Overall:** Low-light and haze consistently caused the most significant performance drops, while over-exposure and distortion had comparatively weaker effects.
- **Answer Format:** Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) questions exhibited higher degradation correlation than Numerical Answer (NA) questions.
- **Task Group:** Object-centric tasks were the most sensitive to visual degradations, whereas camera-centric tasks remained relatively robust.
- **Atomic Question Type:** Fine-grained semantic perception tasks (e.g., object existence estimation, object counting) showed the highest correlation, while global understanding tasks (e.g., camera translation) showed the lowest. This suggests that visual degradations primarily impair fine-grained semantic perception, disproportionately affecting tasks requiring detailed visual grounding.

**Degradation-guided Spatial Reasoning and Error Analysis:**
A two-stage Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning approach, where the MLLM first identified the degradation type before spatial reasoning, resulted in a 1.8% performance decrease for Gemini-3.1-Flash-Lite, suggesting that this CoT strategy was not beneficial in this context. Error analysis categorized degradation-induced errors into four types:
1. **Degradation attribution errors:** Misidentification of the degradation type, leading to an incorrect reasoning premise.
2. **Spatial relation distortion:** Degraded visual cues biasing orientation and relative-position judgments.
3. **Artifact-induced errors:** Spurious patterns from compression artifacts, blur, or low-resolution textures misleading object counting or metric estimation.
4. **Low-visibility guessing:** Models producing overconfident answers despite acknowledging poor observability, rather than performing calibration or providing conservative responses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SpaceDG and SpaceDG-Bench addresses a critical void in the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), shifting the focus from idealized visual inputs to realistic degraded observations. This work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Reality-Performance Gap:** By providing the first large-scale dataset and human-verified benchmark for degradation-aware spatial understanding, SpaceDG enables a more realistic assessment of MLLMs' capabilities. This is particularly crucial for real-world applications of embodied agents and autonomous systems, where visual inputs are inherently imperfect due to environmental factors, sensor limitations, and data processing.

2. **Quantifying Robustness Gaps:** The comprehensive evaluation across 25 diverse MLLMs consistently reveals that visual degradations substantially impair spatial reasoning. This quantitatively exposes a significant robustness gap that existing benchmarks, reliant on pristine images, previously overlooked. Such quantification is essential for understanding current model limitations and guiding future research.

3. **Guiding Future MLLM Development:** The findings provide actionable insights for developing more robust MLLMs. The demonstration that supervised fine-tuning on degradation-augmented data (SpaceDG) markedly improves performance on both clean and degraded images, and can even surpass human performance under degraded conditions, highlights a promising direction: degradation-aware training is a practical path toward building resilient spatial intelligence. This suggests that MLLMs can learn to better exploit visual cues in challenging observations, rather than merely imitating human perception.

4. **Identifying Vulnerable Spatial Reasoning Aspects:** The detailed degradation-wise correlation analysis reveals that fine-grained object-level perception (e.g., object counting, existence estimation) is more vulnerable to degradations compared to global geometric reasoning tasks (e.g., camera translation). This granular understanding can direct research efforts to fortify specific aspects of spatial intelligence that are most susceptible to visual corruptions.

5. **Facilitating Research on Error Mechanisms:** The categorization of degradation-induced errors (e.g., attribution errors, spatial relation distortion, artifact-induced errors, low-visibility guessing) offers a framework for deeper diagnostic analysis. This can help researchers develop targeted mitigation strategies and improve model transparency in reasoning under uncertainty.

In summary, SpaceDG and SpaceDG-Bench are foundational resources that will likely stimulate research into MLLM robustness, encouraging the development of models that can reliably perform spatial reasoning not just in controlled laboratory settings, but also in the complex, imperfect, and unpredictable visual environments of the real world.
