# Omni-R1: Towards the Unified Generative Paradigm for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University and collaborators introduced Omni-R1, a framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models to perform unified generative multimodal reasoning by generating diverse intermediate images during problem-solving. Its zero-shot variant, Omni-R1-Zero, achieves comparable or superior performance to direct supervision by bootstrapping step-wise visualizations, thereby reducing annotation costs, enhancing general multimodal understanding, and reducing hallucination.

## Problem

- Existing interleaved-modal reasoning methods employ single task-specific patterns, limiting generalizability across diverse multimodal reasoning skills (e.g., zooming, marking, predicting).
- MLLMs face challenges in generating "functional images" (e.g., images with marked numbers or highlighted regions) as intermediate reasoning steps, which are often unnatural and task-specific.
- Developing generative multimodal reasoning models is hindered by the high cost and scarcity of human-annotated, step-by-step interleaved reasoning traces.

## Method

- A ==unified generative multimodal reasoning paradigm== is proposed where a single MLLM generates diverse intermediate images (e.g., zoomed views, bounding boxes) as part of its internal reasoning process.
- The Omni-R1 framework employs a two-stage optimization: ==Perception-Aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (PeSFT)== with cross-entropy and perception loss, followed by ==Perception-Calibrated Relative Policy Optimization (PeRPO)== using a composite reward.
- ==Omni-R1-Zero== addresses data scarcity by bootstrapping step-wise visualizations from text-only Chain-of-Thought prompts, synthesizing interleaved trajectories for PeSFT without human annotations.

## Results

- Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero significantly surpassed baseline models (Anole, Zebra-CoT) across all **four** "Uni-Task" slices of the `Omni-Bench` benchmark, with the most pronounced gains in Vision-Operational Scenes.
- Omni-R1-Zero, trained with bootstrapped supervision, yielded stronger overall gains than Omni-R1 (which used limited human annotations), demonstrating effective generative multimodal reasoning without costly human-annotated interleaved rationales.
- Both Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero achieved superior performance on general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MME-P, MM-Vet, POPE), indicating enhanced generalizability, improved perception-oriented capabilities, and reduced object hallucination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Large Language Models can perform unified reasoning by generating diverse intermediate visual evidence (e.g., zoom-ins, markings) within their problem-solving steps, moving beyond mere interpretation.
> - Perception-aligned supervised fine-tuning and perception-calibrated reinforcement learning are crucial for training MLLMs to generate functional and visually coherent intermediate images.
> - Bootstrapping synthetic interleaved reasoning traces from text-only Chain-of-Thought data effectively enables advanced generative multimodal reasoning without extensive human annotation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is progressing from basic perception to more advanced reasoning capabilities. Initially, multimodal reasoning methods primarily focused on text-based reasoning, where images served as context for generating textual rationales and final answers. This paradigm involved MLLMs processing visual information to inform subsequent textual reasoning steps.

More recent research has emphasized the importance of incorporating visual information directly into intermediate reasoning steps, moving beyond purely textual thought processes. This approach is termed "interleaved-modal reasoning." Examples include methods that utilize external tools to zoom in on specific image regions for fine-grained analysis (e.g., DeepEyes) or those that generate intermediate visual states to solve spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., MVoT). These advancements demonstrated the value of visual interaction during reasoning for tasks such as Visual Question Answering (VQA) or understanding spatial relationships.

However, a noted limitation of existing interleaved-modal reasoning methods is their tendency to rely on a single, task-specific reasoning pattern. This specialization often restricts their generalizability across the diverse array of multimodal tasks that exist. For instance, a method optimized for zooming might not inherently support object marking or visual prediction. Many real-world multimodal problems require a combination of distinct visual reasoning skills, such as localizing evidence, drawing auxiliary lines, highlighting instances, or predicting visual state transitions. Current MLLMs typically do not integrate these diverse skills within a single, unified framework.

This research positions Omni-R1 as an effort to address these limitations by proposing a "unified generative multimodal reasoning" paradigm. This paradigm aims to internalize and unify diverse multimodal reasoning skills within a single MLLM by generating intermediate images as part of the reasoning process. This approach seeks to enable a single model to perform a broad spectrum of visual operations on demand, thereby enhancing its generalizability and applicability to a wider range of complex multimodal tasks, similar to the concept of "Omni models" that exhibit multimodal generation capabilities. By doing so, Omni-R1 attempts to move beyond specialized reasoning patterns towards a more integrated and flexible multimodal intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a general and unified multimodal reasoning paradigm. This paradigm aims to enable a single MLLM to perform a diverse set of multimodal reasoning skills by generating intermediate visual information during the reasoning process.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified challenges and opportunities:

1. **Addressing the Diversity of Multimodal Tasks and Skills:** The authors observe that practical multimodal tasks necessitate a broad spectrum of reasoning skills. For instance, general VQA might require "zoom-in" or "grounding" to pinpoint relevant evidence, while tasks in visual-operational scenes, such as robotic manipulation, could benefit from "visual prediction" to anticipate future states. Other tasks might demand "auxiliary line drawing" for geometric problems or "marking" for enumerating objects. Current MLLMs often specialize in one or a few of these skills, limiting their applicability to the full range of multimodal challenges. The motivation is to create an MLLM that can flexibly invoke and execute these diverse skills as needed within a unified framework.

2. **Unifying Reasoning Skills through Generative Processes:** Inspired by the advancements in generative models, the authors propose that these diverse multimodal reasoning skills can be unified and internalized within a *generative paradigm*. This means that instead of relying on external tools or rigid, pre-defined pipelines, the MLLM would *generate* intermediate images that embody these reasoning steps. For example, "zoom-in" could be realized by generating a magnified view of a specific region, and "grounding" by generating an image with annotated bounding boxes. This generative approach is intended to provide a more integrated and adaptable reasoning process, allowing the model to "think visually" by producing its own visual aids.

3. **Overcoming Challenges in Implementing Generative Multimodal Reasoning:** The authors identify two significant practical challenges in realizing this unified generative vision:
 - **Functional Image Generation:** The intermediate images required for reasoning are often not natural images but "functional" ones. These can include images with highlighted regions, drawn lines, bounding boxes, or numbered objects. Such "unnatural" yet highly specific and meaningful visual outputs pose a unique challenge for MLLMs, which are typically trained on natural image distributions. Ensuring that generated images are not only visually coherent but also functionally accurate and relevant to the reasoning task is critical.
 - **Costly Interleaved-Modal Reasoning Annotations:** Training models to perform step-by-step generative multimodal reasoning typically requires large datasets of interleaved reasoning trajectories, where each step includes both textual rationale and corresponding visual evidence (e.g., an intermediate image). Such datasets are expensive and time-consuming to create, leading to a scarcity of high-quality supervised data for this complex task. The motivation is to develop methods that can learn generative multimodal reasoning effectively, even with limited or no human-annotated interleaved data.

By addressing these motivations and challenges, this work aims to advance the capabilities of MLLMs towards more general, adaptable, and autonomous multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Omni-R1, a framework designed to achieve unified generative multimodal reasoning, and Omni-R1-Zero, an extension that tackles the challenge of limited interleaved reasoning annotations.

#### 3.1. Omni-R1: Two-Stage SFT+RL Framework

Omni-R1 employs a two-stage optimization procedure:

**Stage 1: Perception-Aligned Supervised Fine-Tuning (PeSFT)**
This initial stage focuses on teaching the MLLM the interleaved reasoning format and how to predict diverse Uni-Skills by generating functional images. It combines two loss components:

- **Cross-Entropy (CE) Loss (L_CE):** This standard loss is applied to the entire multimodal reasoning token trajectory. It trains the model to imitate the desired interleaved generative reasoning format, encouraging it to reproduce the sequence of textual rationales and visual tokens. For image-token segments, it provides local supervision for Uni-Skills, guiding the model to generate appropriate functional images within the context.
- **Perception Loss (L_Pe):** To address the challenge of generating "functional" and potentially "unnatural" images, this loss is introduced to stabilize image token generation. It aligns the final-layer hidden states (h_t) corresponding to image tokens with the embeddings (E[c_t]) from a frozen visual codebook (e.g., from a VQVAE). By enforcing this alignment, the perception loss acts as a perceptual prior, ensuring that the autoregressively generated image tokens maintain visual coherence and conform to the codebook's latent space geometry, thereby making the functional image generation more stable.
- **Overall Objective:** The PeSFT stage optimizes a combined objective: L_PeSFT = L_CE + λ L_Pe, where λ is a weighting coefficient set to 1.

**Stage 2: Perception-Calibrated Relative Policy Optimization (PeRPO)**
Following SFT, this stage refines the policy using reinforcement learning (RL) on Uni-Tasks, including those without explicit multimodal annotations. PeRPO is built upon a group-relative RL objective and aims to optimize long, interleaved multimodal sequences with a specific reward mechanism:

- **Perception-Calibrated Reward (R):** This composite reward guides the RL process and is composed of three weighted components:
 - **Accuracy (R_Acc):** A rule-based verifier computes a reward in by parsing the final textual answer and comparing it to the ground truth. It checks for numeric correctness, symbolic/textual equivalence, and domain-specific patterns, providing feedback on the overall task success.
 - **Format (R_Fmt):** A binary reward (0 or 1) assigned based on whether the generated trajectory adheres to a well-formed reasoning and answer format that can be reliably parsed. This ensures structural compliance.
 - **Perception (R_Pe):** This component directly assesses the quality and coherence of intermediate functional image generations. It measures the 2D Total Variation (TV) on the codebook embeddings (Z) of the generated image-token segments. Low TV energy indicates visual smoothness and coherence, which is then mapped to a normalized score s_r. The R_Pe for a trajectory is the average of these scores across all image-token segments. This explicitly rewards the generation of perceptually consistent and useful intermediate visual evidence.
- **Policy Optimization:** PeRPO maximizes a PPO-clip objective with a KL regularizer against a fixed reference policy (π_ref). For each input prompt, a set of reasoning trajectory candidates are sampled from the behavioral policy. A group-relative advantage (A^(i)) is computed for each candidate based on its reward (R) relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group. This optimization encourages the policy to generate trajectories that are higher-rewarding in terms of accuracy, format, and perceptual consistency.

#### 3.2. Omni-R1-Zero: Bootstrapping for Zero Interleaved Supervision

Omni-R1-Zero addresses the challenge of limited human-annotated interleaved multimodal rationales by synthesizing training data:

- **Bootstrapping Step-wise Visualization:** This method begins with text-only Chain-of-Thought (CoT) seeds. For each reasoning step in the text-only CoT, the base model is prompted to generate a corresponding image (Text Step k → Image Step k). These generated images are then interleaved with the textual CoT steps, forming synthetic multimodal trajectories. These synthetic traces, while not perfect, provide a scaffold for learning the interleaved reasoning format and exposing intermediate multimodal states.
- **Optimization:** Omni-R1-Zero subsequently uses the *same two-stage PeSFT and PeRPO pipeline* as Omni-R1, but trained on these synthetic interleaved data. PeSFT initializes the model with the bootstrapped format, and PeRPO further refines it using the perception-calibrated reward to improve correctness and stabilize intermediate image generations.

#### 3.3. Omni-Bench and Evaluation Protocol

To rigorously evaluate unified multimodal reasoning, the authors constructed **Omni-Bench**, a custom benchmark comprising various multimodal tasks requiring diverse skills. It includes four **Uni-Tasks**:
- **Natural-Scene Perception:** Requires evidence localization in natural images.
- **Structured-Image:** Involves structured inputs like figures and charts.
- **Diagrammatic Math:** Focuses on diagram-grounded arithmetic and geometric reasoning.
- **Vision-Operational Scenes:** Demands complex visual operations or state prediction (e.g., visual games).
These tasks necessitate four **Uni-Skills**: Grounding (zoom-in), Auxiliary line drawing, Marking, and Visual prediction.
Evaluation on Omni-Bench involves extracting the final answer span and using an LLM judge for binary correctness comparison against a gold reference. Standard general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MME, MM-Vet, V*-Bench, POPE, MMVP, BLINK) are also used for broader evaluation.

The models are instantiated on Unified Multimodal Model (UMM) backbones like Anole and Bagel, with different training durations (PeRPO steps) for variants (e.g., Omni-R1-M/L, Omni-R1-Zero-T/S/M).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero was conducted on both the custom Omni-Bench and a suite of general multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Results on Omni-Bench

- **Consistent Improvements:** Both Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero variants consistently demonstrated performance improvements over their respective base models across the four Uni-Task slices (Natural-Scene Perception, Structured-Image, Diagrammatic Math, Vision-Operational Scenes). For example, Omni-R1-M with the Bagel backbone achieved a +40.7% improvement over the base Bagel model, and Omni-R1-Zero-M with the Anole backbone showed a +95.4% improvement over the base Anole model.
- **Competitiveness:** Omni-R1-M (Bagel backbone) achieved the strongest average performance among the tested models, indicating that the proposed framework is competitive with or surpasses text-only and tool-based reasoning baselines.
- **Effectiveness of Zero-Shot Approach:** Omni-R1-Zero variants (trained with varying PeRPO steps) consistently outperformed their base Anole model. This suggests that the Perception-Calibrated Relative Policy Optimization (PeRPO) stage, even when initialized with bootstrapped synthetic data, can successfully induce generative multimodal reasoning capabilities without extensive human-annotated interleaved traces.

#### 4.2. Results on General Multimodal Benchmarks

- **Strong General Reasoning:** On the Anole-7B backbone, both Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero outperformed baseline models (e.g., Anole (Base), Zebra-CoT) across a range of standard multimodal benchmarks including MME (Perception and Reasoning), MM-Vet, V*-Bench, POPE, MMVP, and BLINK. This indicates that the developed paradigms contribute to stronger general reasoning capabilities beyond the specific tasks of Omni-Bench.
- **Enhanced Visual Perception:** Omni-R1 variants generally showed stronger performance on perception-oriented evaluations (MME-P, MMVP, BLINK). This finding suggests that the integration of multiple generative reasoning skills, particularly through perception alignment loss and reward, leads to improved visual perception in the model.
- **Improved Reasoning and Reduced Hallucination:** Omni-R1-Zero proved more competitive on reasoning-oriented MME-R and performed strongly on the comprehensive MM-Vet. It also exhibited stronger performance on POPE, a benchmark for object hallucination, suggesting better evidence consistency and a reduction in hallucination tendencies.

#### 4.3. In-depth Analysis

- **Ablation Study (Omni-R1-Zero):** An ablation study on Omni-R1-Zero revealed the contributions of PeRPO and the perception-calibrated reward:
 - **Importance of PeRPO:** Removing PeRPO entirely resulted in the most significant performance degradation, particularly in Vision-Operational and Diagrammatic tasks. This highlights PeRPO's critical role in developing and refining multi-step and complex multimodal reasoning abilities.
 - **Impact of Perception Reward:** Disabling the perception-calibrated reward led to a smaller but consistent performance decrease, notably in Natural-Scene Perception tasks. This indicates that the perception-calibrated reward contributes to improved utilization of visual evidence and stabilization of the policy optimization process.
- **Analysis of Generated Multimodal Patterns:** A t-SNE visualization of generated images showed distinct patterns:
 - Omni-R1 generated patterns that concentrated into a few compact modes.
 - Omni-R1-Zero exhibited a more dispersed and multi-modal structure.
 - In both cases, correctly predicted instances clustered more tightly than incorrect ones. This suggests that while trace supervision promotes canonical and stable generations, reward guidance encourages a broader exploration of visual patterns that can still support accurate decisions.
- **Qualitative Case Studies:** Examples of reasoning trajectories demonstrated Omni-R1's ability to produce task-conditioned intermediate visual evidence through various Uni-Skills (e.g., Zoom-In for attribute recognition, BBOX for localization, Marking for graph analysis, Visual Prediction for robotic sequences). Omni-R1-Zero also showcased the capability to generate intermediate visual evidence, even without direct human-annotated multimodal traces, before arriving at the final answer for complex questions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of multimodal reasoning and has potential impacts on the development of more capable MLLMs:

1. **Unified Generative Multimodal Reasoning Paradigm:** The work introduces and instantiates a novel paradigm that unifies diverse multimodal reasoning skills (such as grounding, auxiliary line drawing, marking, and visual prediction) within a single generative MLLM. This moves beyond task-specific or tool-based approaches, proposing that a model can inherently "think visually" by generating intermediate images as part of its reasoning process. This unification suggests a pathway towards more general-purpose MLLMs that can flexibly adapt to a wider array of multimodal problems.

2. **Addressing Functional Image Generation:** A key technical contribution is the introduction of a perception alignment loss and a perception-calibrated reward. These mechanisms specifically address the challenge of generating "functional" and potentially "unnatural" intermediate images (e.g., images with highlights, bounding boxes, or drawn lines) that are crucial for explicit reasoning steps. By stabilizing the generation of these visual aids, the framework enables MLLMs to produce meaningful and coherent visual evidence, enhancing the transparency and interpretability of their reasoning.

3. **Mitigating Interleaved Annotation Scarcity:** The Omni-R1-Zero approach offers a method to bootstrap step-wise visualizations from text-only Chain-of-Thought data. This innovation directly tackles the practical bottleneck of costly and scarce human-annotated interleaved-modal reasoning datasets. By enabling generative multimodal reasoning with limited or no direct multimodal supervision, Omni-R1-Zero significantly lowers the entry barrier for developing sophisticated multimodal reasoning models, potentially accelerating research and development in this area.

4. **Enhanced Reasoning Performance and Generalizability:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate that both Omni-R1 and Omni-R1-Zero achieve performance gains across a custom benchmark designed for diverse reasoning skills (Omni-Bench) and various standard general multimodal benchmarks. These improvements span enhanced visual perception, stronger reasoning capabilities, and a reduction in hallucination, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed methods in advancing MLLM performance.

5. **Future Research Directions:** The work highlights the potential for developing scalable supervision signals that do not rely on extensive manual annotation. This suggests future research into self-supervision, synthetic data generation, or other methods to unlock the full potential of zero-shot generative multimodal reasoning, which could lead to more autonomous and universally applicable AI systems.

In summary, Omni-R1 represents a step towards creating MLLMs that are not only multimodal in perception but also in their internal reasoning processes, capable of actively manipulating and generating visual information to solve complex problems. This could impact various applications, from enhanced scientific diagram understanding to more capable embodied AI agents.
