# SPARROW: Learning Spatial Precision and Temporal Referential Consistency in Pixel-Grounded Video MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Khalifa University developed SPARROW, a framework designed to improve temporal referential consistency and spatial precision in pixel-grounded video MLLMs. It integrates target-specific tracked features for robust temporal supervision and a dual-prompt grounding mechanism, yielding up to +8.9 J&F on MeViS RVOS, a five-point mIoU increase on VidSTG Video Visual Grounding, and consistent gains across other benchmarks when integrated into existing MLLMs.

## Problem

- Existing video MLLMs struggle with temporal referential consistency, leading to spatial drift, identity switches, and unstable segmentation of objects across video frames.
- Unstable first-frame grounding in video MLLMs results in imprecise initial segmentations, which can accumulate and propagate errors throughout the video sequence.
- Current methods often rely on static grounding tokens that lack explicit temporal context, requiring models to infer dynamic object behavior solely from visual cues.

## Method

- Introduced ==Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF)== to inject temporally aligned, object-specific cues during training, fostering identity persistence without explicit inference-time tracking by default.
- **SPARROW** introduces a ==Dual-Prompt Grounding== mechanism combining ==bounding box ([BOX]) tokens== for coarse spatial priors and ==segmentation ([SEG]) tokens== for fine-grained semantic refinement, stabilizing geometric and semantic grounding.
- Curated a large-scale referential video dataset with object-centric, temporally consistent annotations (trajectories, bounding boxes, segmentation masks) to support TSF and dual-prompt training.

## Results

- Achieved consistent J&F score improvements across RVOS benchmarks, including **+8.9** on MeViS (val_u split) and **+7.3** on Ref-DAVIS17 with VideoGLaMM integration.
- Improved mean IoU (mIoU) by approximately **five points** across all integrated backbones on the VidSTG Video Visual Grounding benchmark, representing a **13-18%** relative gain.
- Enhanced mask quality (mIoU gains of **+2 to +3.25 points**) and language metrics (e.g., **+5.4 CLAIR** on VideoGLaMM) in Video Grounded Conversation Generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit temporal supervision through TSF during training, even without inference-time tracking, significantly enhances object identity persistence and reduces spatial drift in video MLLMs.
> - Combining geometric priors from bounding box prompts with semantic refinement from segmentation prompts yields superior spatial precision and referential grounding compared to single-token approaches.
> - A modular, two-stage training strategy allows for seamless integration into existing video MLLMs, providing consistent performance gains across diverse pixel-grounded video tasks with minimal inference overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated progress in combining textual and visual reasoning, from global image question answering to pixel-level grounding in static images. However, extending these capabilities to video presents challenges due to the dynamic nature of motion, object occlusion, and the requirement for maintaining temporal coherence and spatial precision across frames.

Existing video MLLMs frequently rely on static grounding tokens, such as `[SEG]`, to denote objects for segmentation within individual frames. While these tokens provide semantic information, they lack explicit temporal context, which can lead to several issues. These include spatial drift, where the perceived location of an object shifts inaccurately over time; identity switches, where the model confuses different instances or fails to consistently track a single object; and unstable initialization, particularly when objects move, disappear, or reappear. Errors originating from an imprecise first-frame segmentation can propagate throughout a video sequence, degrading overall temporal stability and limiting performance in tasks such as referring video object segmentation (RVOS) and video question answering.

SPARROW addresses these limitations by introducing mechanisms designed to improve temporal stability and spatial precision in pixel-grounded video understanding. The work positions itself as an advancement over current video MLLMs by providing explicit temporal alignment cues and a robust dual-prompt grounding strategy, rather than solely depending on frame-wise semantic tokens. It draws inspiration from prior work in tracking object-specific features for temporal coherence and localized bounding box prompting for fine-grained image grounding, adapting and extending these concepts to the video domain within an MLLM framework. The objective is to enable models to consistently maintain object identity and precise localization over dynamic video sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SPARROW is to enhance referential stability and improve first-frame robustness in pixel-grounded video MLLMs. The motivation for this work stems from two core challenges observed in existing models:

#### 2.1. Temporal Referential Consistency

Current video MLLMs often struggle to maintain the identity and consistent localization of a specific object across a video sequence. This issue arises because most approaches utilize static textual grounding tokens (e.g., `[SEG]`) to specify the target object. While these tokens convey semantic information, they do not inherently provide temporal context regarding an object's position, appearance changes, or motion over time. Consequently, the model must infer these dynamic aspects solely from visual cues, which frequently leads to:
- **Spatial Drift**: The segmented region for a target object gradually moves away from its actual location across frames.
- **Inconsistent Referent Tracking**: The model may lose track of the designated object or confuse it with other similar objects, leading to "identity switches."
- **Unstable Segmentation**: The quality of segmentation masks for the same entity can fluctuate, particularly when the object undergoes significant movement or reappears after occlusion.
The motivation here is to provide explicit temporal information to the model during training, allowing it to learn and maintain object identity more effectively, thereby mitigating drift and identity switches.

#### 2.2. Unstable First-Frame Grounding and Error Propagation

A related challenge is the unreliability of first-frame initialization in many video MLLMs. Since static `[SEG]` tokens offer semantic cues without strong spatial priors, the initial segmentation mask generated for a target object can often be inaccurate or misaligned. These initial errors are then prone to accumulating and propagating through subsequent frames as the video progresses. Such error propagation further degrades temporal stability and overall segmentation performance, especially in long or complex video sequences.
The motivation is to establish a more stable and geometrically informed initial grounding for target objects. This would reduce ambiguity in early frames and provide a more robust foundation for subsequent temporal tracking and segmentation, ultimately limiting the accumulation of errors.

To address these motivations, SPARROW establishes the following key objectives:
- **Introduce Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF)**: To inject temporally aligned, object-specific cues during training, thereby fostering identity persistence without requiring explicit tracking at inference time by default.
- **Develop a Dual-Prompt Grounding Mechanism**: To integrate both bounding box (`[BOX]`) and segmentation (`[SEG]`) tokens. The `[BOX]` token provides a coarse spatial prior, while the `[SEG]` token refines these regions with semantic information, aiming to stabilize geometric and semantic grounding, especially in initial frames.
- **Curate a Specialized Dataset**: To support the training of TSF and dual-prompt grounding, a large-scale referential video dataset with object-centric, temporally consistent annotations (trajectories, bounding boxes, and segmentation masks) is created.
- **Achieve End-to-End Operation**: To enable stable and coherent video understanding without relying on external detectors or trackers during default inference, while maintaining a modular design for integration into existing video MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SPARROW enhances existing pixel-grounded video MLLMs by integrating two core components: Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF) and a Dual-Prompt Grounding mechanism. The framework is designed to be modular and plug-and-play, avoiding modifications to the base LLM or visual backbones.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview and Architecture

The SPARROW architecture consists of:
- **Dual-branch visual encoder**: Comprising a spatial encoder (e.g., CLIP) and a temporal encoder (e.g., InternVideo2) to extract `f_g` and `f_h` features from input video `V`.
- **Visual-to-Language (V→L) adapters**: Project `f_g` and `f_h` into the LLM embedding space (`Z_g`, `Z_h`).
- **Frozen Large Language Model (LLM)**: Adapted using LoRA for instruction following and reasoning.
- **Language-to-Visual (L→V) adapters**: Map grounding-related hidden states back into the visual space, specifically for `[BOX]` (`W_b`) and `[SEG]` (`W_s`) tokens.
- **SAM2-based pixel segmentor**: Used for generating final segmentation masks.
The input to the LLM consists of tokenized query `Q`, spatial features `Z_g`, temporal features `Z_h`, and optional `Z_TSF` tokens during training.

#### 3.2. Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF)

TSF aims to instill object identity persistence during training without requiring explicit tracking at inference.
- **Tracking and Selection (Offline)**: For a given textual query `Q`, GroundingDINO is used to detect the referent in a keyframe. CLDTracker then propagates this detection across the video sequence, generating candidate bounding boxes (`B'`). To manage redundancy and enhance stability, K-means clustering (with K=4) is applied in a joint visual-spatial feature space to select a compact subset of representative boxes (`B`) that capture diverse appearances of the same referent.
- **Feature Encoding and Integration**: The selected regions (`B_j`) are cropped from the video and encoded by the spatial encoder (`F_g`). These features are then projected into the LLM space via `W_g` to form the `Z_TSF` tokens, which are appended to the multimodal input (Eq. 3, 4, 5). This lightweight integration provides temporally consistent referent cues to the LLM during training.
- **TSF Dataset Curation**: To support TSF supervision, a large-scale dataset is curated. This involves harmonizing multiple public datasets (HC-STVG, VID-Sentence, A2D Sentences, LaSOT, MeViS, GOT-10k, Ref-SAV). An offline pipeline processes these sources by regenerating trajectories (where noisy or sparse) using GroundingDINO and CLDTracker, encoding features, and pruning them with K-means clustering. SAM2 is optionally used to generate dense masks. The resulting dataset comprises 30,646 video sequences and 45,231 question-answer pairs with rich annotations (trajectories, bounding boxes, segmentation masks).
- **Inference**: TSF tokens are optional at test time. By default, they are omitted to maintain efficiency (no external detector or tracker required). When enabled for maximal temporal stability, the GroundingDINO → CLDTracker → CLIP → K-means pipeline is executed at inference.

#### 3.3. Dual-Prompt Grounding

This mechanism addresses early segmentation errors and drift by combining `[BOX]` for spatial priors and `[SEG]` for semantic refinement.
- **Bounding-box Prompt (`[BOX]`)**:
 - The LLM generates an `e_BOX` embedding, projected by `W_b`.
 - This embedding conditions a lightweight regression head to predict bounding box coordinates.
 - A class-agnostic proposer is built upon frozen multi-scale Hiera features from SAM2. This proposer uses a Deformable-DETR decoder, replacing its classification branch with a single objectness head, to generate 300 proposals per frame. These are filtered by objectness scores and Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS).
 - Region-of-Interest (ROI) Align pools Hiera features for each proposal, which are then fused with `e_BOX` via cross-attention. A text-conditioned regression head refines these proposals.
 - This process yields a set of confident, spatially localized bounding boxes (`B^*`) that serve as geometric priors.
- **Segmentation Prompt (`[SEG]`)**:
 - The LLM generates an `e_SEG` embedding, projected by `W_s`.
 - For each refined bounding box `ˆb_i` from `B^*`, the pair (`ˆb_i`, `e_SEG`) is fed as a mask query to SAM2’s prompt encoder.
 - SAM2 then generates a precise segmentation mask `ˆM_i`. This language-conditioned refinement on geometrically-informed regions enhances mask precision. The mechanism inherently supports multi-instance queries.
 - The ability to re-emit `[BOX]` and `[SEG]` tokens at any frame allows for drift correction without reliance on external trackers.

#### 3.4. Training Strategy

SPARROW employs a two-stage training strategy:
- **Stage 1: Target-Specific Information Injection**:
 - The multimodal adapters (V→L: `W_g`, `W_h`; L→V: `W_s`) and LoRA parameters within the LLM are optimized. The visual backbone and SAM2 pixel decoders remain frozen.
 - Training utilizes the curated TSF dataset. The objective function combines cross-entropy loss (`L_CE`) for semantic alignment between vision and language tokens and mask losses (`L_mask = L_BCE + L_DICE`) for pixel grounding.
- **Stage 2: Bounding Box Prompt Learning**:
 - **Proposal Generator Pretraining**: A class-agnostic Deformable-DETR head operating on frozen Hiera features from SAM2 is pretrained on a large corpus of object detection datasets (COCO, Objects365, OpenImages, V3Det). Category labels are discarded to enforce class-agnostic localization. The objective (`L_prop`) includes objectness, L1 bounding box regression, and GIoU losses.
 - **Task Adaptation: Filter-Only Fine-Tuning**: A lightweight filtration head (`F_filter`) and the L→V `[BOX]` adapter (`W_b`) are fine-tuned on task-specific grounding datasets. All other components (Hiera encoder, region proposer, other adapters, LLM, SAM2) remain frozen. `F_filter` learns to score and refine region proposals conditioned on textual queries. The optimization (`L_filter`) uses BCE for proposal-language alignment and L1/GIoU for positive box refinement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SPARROW was integrated into three existing open-source video MLLMs: UniPixel, GLUS, and VideoGLaMM. Its performance was evaluated across six benchmarks spanning three pixel-grounded video understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Referring Video Object Segmentation (RVOS)

Evaluated on MeViS (val and val_u splits), Ref-YouTube-VOS (Ref-YTVOS), and Ref-DAVIS17, using Jaccard (J), F-score (F), and their mean (J&F) metrics.
- **MeViS**: SPARROW consistently improved performance across all backbones and splits. Notably, when integrated into VideoGlaMM, it increased the J&F score on the unseen (val_u) split by +8.9, indicating improved compositional generalization for motion-language combinations. UniPixel and GLUS also showed gains ranging from +0.3 to +3.4 J&F.
- **Ref-YTVOS**: SPARROW improved all three backbones. The largest J&F increase was approximately +2.1 on VideoGlaMM, with GLUS and UniPixel showing consistent gains between +0.2 and +1.9. Improvements were observed in both region overlap (J) and boundary quality (F).
- **Ref-DAVIS17**: SPARROW enhanced all three backbones. VideoGlaMM saw a notable J&F increase of +7.3, largely driven by a +14.5 improvement in F-score (boundary accuracy). GLUS and UniPixel also demonstrated consistent increases of +2.6 and +2.2 J&F.
Qualitative examples indicate that SPARROW produces precise first-frame masks and maintains consistent boundaries and object identities even under motion, occlusion, and in scenes with similar distractors.

#### 4.2. Video Visual Grounding (VG)

Evaluated on VidSTG using mean IoU (mIoU).
- SPARROW achieved approximately a five-point mIoU improvement across all integrated backbones, corresponding to a 13–18% relative gain. Specifically, mIoU increased by +5.49 on UniPixel, +5.25 on GLUS, and +5.40 on VideoGLaMM. These uniform gains suggest that SPARROW’s dual-prompt design enhances both spatial precision and referential grounding in language-driven video understanding.

#### 4.3. Video Grounded Conversation Generation (GCG)

Evaluated on VideoGCG, reporting mask quality (mIoU, Recall) and caption quality (METEOR, CIDEr, CLAIR).
- SPARROW consistently improved mask quality across all backbones, with mIoU gains of +2 to +3.25 points and slight increases in Recall. For models generating text, language metrics also improved. CLAIR showed the most significant gain (+5.4 on VideoGLaMM), indicating better alignment between generated phrases and their corresponding visual regions. This suggests that TSF pretraining and the dual-prompt design lead to more accurate masks and more specific textual descriptions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies, primarily conducted on Ref-DAVIS17 (val) with VideoGLaMM, provided insights into SPARROW’s components:
- **TSF Pretraining and Box Prompting**: Training with TSF alone (without TSF at inference) improved J&F by +2.9 over the baseline (69.5 to 72.4). Activating `[BOX]` prompting further increased gains, demonstrating the benefit of geometric priors. The highest score (77.7 J&F) was achieved when TSF was active during both training and inference, though the default setting (TSF at train only, `[BOX]` on) captured most benefits (76.8 J&F) without inference-time tracking overhead.
- **Selection Supervision and Prompt Composition**: Using `[BOX]` features for the Filtration Head supervision was more effective than `[SEG]` features (+1.9 J&F). A detector-side Filtration Head outperformed MLLM-side selection (+2.1 J&F). The dual-prompt combination of `[BOX]`+`[SEG]` achieved 72.5 J&F, outperforming single-token prompts (`[SEG]` only: 69.5; `[BOX]` only: 68.2), confirming the benefit of fusing geometric and semantic cues.
- **TSF Injection Path**: Projecting TSF tokens through the spatial V-L projector (W_g) yielded the best performance (76.8 J&F), indicating its effectiveness in handling object-centric appearance and identity cues.
- **Robustness to Noisy TSF Supervision**: SPARROW demonstrated graceful degradation under corrupted training-time TSF trajectories (box jitter, ID-switch injection, trajectory dropout), consistently outperforming the No-TSF baseline even with moderate noise levels. This suggests robustness to realistic supervision noise.

#### 4.5. Compute and Inference Overhead

- **Inference (default, TSF off)**: SPARROW introduces minor overhead. The parameter increase is +0.017B, and throughput reduction is marginal (e.g., UniPixel from 15.38 to 15.04 FPS). The increase in visual GFLOPs per frame is also small.
- **Inference (TSF on, optional)**: Enabling TSF at inference incurs an additional latency of approximately 20 ms per target per frame on an A100 GPU.
- **Training**: Offline TSF generation is a one-time preprocessing step. The combined SPARROW training (two stages) requires 112 GPU-hours (4.67 A100 GPU-days), which is a modest increase of +32 GPU-hours over baseline fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SPARROW addresses fundamental challenges in extending pixel-level grounding from images to videos, specifically focusing on temporal referential consistency and spatial precision. By introducing Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF) for robust temporal supervision and a dual-prompt grounding mechanism that combines geometric priors (`[BOX]`) with semantic refinement (`[SEG]`), the work offers a method for achieving more stable and accurate object localization and segmentation in dynamic visual content.

The consistent and notable performance gains observed across multiple state-of-the-art video MLLMs and a variety of benchmarks (RVOS, Video Visual Grounding, and Grounded Conversation Generation) suggest that SPARROW provides a universally applicable enhancement. Its modular design allows for integration into existing MLLM architectures without requiring modifications to core backbones, facilitating adoption by the research community. The default inference mode operates without external tracking or detection modules, which helps maintain efficiency in deployment scenarios.

The curated large-scale referential video dataset, comprising over 30,000 videos and 45,000 Q&A pairs with temporally consistent annotations, represents a valuable resource for future research in object-centric video understanding. This dataset, combined with the released code and models, supports reproducibility and further development in the field.

The improvements in referential stability, spatial precision, and temporal coherence achieved by SPARROW have the potential to impact various applications that rely on accurate and consistent object understanding in video. These include advanced video analytics for surveillance or sports, enhanced capabilities for video editing tools, more robust scene understanding for robotics and autonomous systems, and improved human-computer interaction through more precise visual grounding in conversational AI.

While the paper acknowledges limitations related to the reliance on proposal recall and potential accumulation of early `[BOX]` errors in very long sequences, as well as the dependency on pseudo-tracks for TSF, the demonstrated robustness to noisy supervision indicates a practical approach. SPARROW represents a step towards more reliable and precise pixel-grounded video MLLMs, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in complex, dynamic environments.
