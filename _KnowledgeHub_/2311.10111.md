# VideoCon: Robust Video-Language Alignment via Contrast Captions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCLA and Google Research developed VideoCon, a dataset of video-language misalignments and natural language explanations (NLEs), to improve the robustness of video-language alignment models. Finetuning with VideoCon led to a generative model that achieved 84.6 ROC-AUC on entailment, state-of-the-art performance on text-to-video retrieval (e.g., 15.2 mAP on SSv2-Temporal), and accurate NLE generation, demonstrating enhanced fine-grained and temporal understanding.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art video-language models lack robustness to semantically plausible manipulations of video captions (e.g., altering entities, actions, or temporal order), which undermines their trustworthiness.
- Simply scaling up video-language pretraining datasets has shown diminishing returns for improving fine-grained understanding and robustness, indicating a need for higher quality, targeted data.
- Current models struggle with fine-grained details like temporal relationships and object counting, and often cannot provide natural language explanations for misalignments.

## Method

- Developed **VideoCon**, a novel dataset containing diverse, semantically plausible contrast captions and natural language explanations (NLEs) for video-text misalignments.
- Utilized a Large Language Model (LLM, PaLM-2) to scalably generate contrast captions across seven categories (e.g., object, action, event order flip) and corresponding NLEs for filtered, temporally-challenging video-caption pairs.
- Finetuned a generative video-language model (mPLUG-Owl-Video) on the **VideoCon** dataset using a multitask learning approach for both video-language entailment prediction and NLE generation.

## Results

- The finetuned model, Owl-Con, achieved an ROC-AUC of **84.6** on the VideoCon (LLM) entailment task, significantly outperforming Owl-Base (**57.2**) and atemporal baselines (**67.0**).
- Owl-Con established new state-of-the-art performance on text-to-video retrieval tasks, with **15.2** mAP on SSv2-Temporal and **11.4** mAP on SSv2-Events, and 41.1% accuracy on the ATP-Hard video question answering dataset.
- The model demonstrated strong capabilities in generating Natural Language Explanations, achieving a Q2 entailment score of **0.50** and PaLM-2 entailment accuracy of 65.4% for in-domain NLEs, while also generalizing to human-generated explanations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Targeted training with carefully constructed "hard negative" contrast captions is crucial for building robust fine-grained and temporal video-language understanding, outperforming random negative sampling.
> - LLMs can effectively generate high-quality, diverse contrastive data and natural language explanations, providing a scalable strategy to create challenging benchmarks and training examples.
> - Enhancing video-language alignment robustness through contrastive training and NLE generation directly translates to improved zero-shot performance on downstream tasks requiring fine-grained temporal and causal reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has a long-standing goal of semantically aligning data points across diverse modalities, particularly in video-language understanding. Recent advancements have led to the development of powerful models such as VideoCLIP, ImageBind, Flamingo, and mPLUG-Owl-Video. These models typically operate by learning a shared embedding space between video and text or by providing a classification label (e.g., yes/no) when queried about video-language alignment. They are often pretrained on vast amounts of data, aiming for general-purpose understanding.

Despite the scale of their pretraining, prior research has consistently highlighted a significant limitation: these state-of-the-art models often lack robustness to semantically plausible manipulations of video captions. These manipulations, termed "contrast captions" in this paper, involve changes such as altering entities, actions, or flipping the temporal order of events. For instance, a model might fail to distinguish between "dog runs away before it eats food" and "dog runs away after it eats food," even if the video clearly depicts one scenario. This brittleness raises concerns about the trustworthiness and reliability of these alignment models for real-world deployment.

Existing approaches to mitigate these shortcomings, such as simply scaling up video-language pair datasets for pretraining, have shown diminishing returns. This is partly due to the challenges in sourcing new, high-quality, and permissible content, alongside substantial storage requirements. Furthermore, studies have indicated that even applying contrastive objectives to web-scale datasets does not inherently encourage models to grasp the fine-grained details necessary for robust video-language understanding, especially regarding temporal and causal relationships.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a scalable and active strategy for data generation. Instead of merely increasing data volume, the authors focus on creating a high-quality dataset specifically enriched with diverse, semantically plausible misalignments. By deliberately targeting these specific types of "hard negatives," the research aims to instill a more robust understanding of fine-grained details, entity relationships, action understanding, and temporal event order in video-language models. This approach positions itself as a complement to large-scale pretraining, focusing on data quality and targeted challenges to enhance model robustness and trustworthiness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of video-language alignment models against semantically plausible, yet misaligned, contrast captions. The authors aim to improve the models' ability to accurately discern whether a video entails a given description, even when that description contains subtle but critical semantic alterations.

This overarching objective is motivated by several key challenges and shortcomings identified in current video-language understanding systems:

1. **Lack of Robustness:** Despite massive pretraining, current video-language models are not consistently robust to minor, semantically plausible changes in captions. These changes can involve altering objects, actions, attributes, counts, spatial relations, adding hallucinatory information, or flipping the temporal order of events. This lack of robustness undermines the reliability and trustworthiness of these models in practical applications.
2. **Limitations of Data Scaling:** Simply scaling pretraining datasets to include more video-language pairs has shown diminishing returns for improving fine-grained understanding and robustness. The challenge lies not just in data quantity but in data quality and the presence of deliberately challenging examples that force models to learn deeper semantic and temporal reasoning.
3. **Inadequate Fine-Grained Understanding:** Many existing models can align videos and texts at a coarse level but struggle with fine-grained details, such as distinguishing between "before" and "after" in an event sequence, or correctly counting multiple instances of an object. This suggests a deficiency in understanding the entities, their interactions, and the precise temporal ordering of events.
4. **Need for Explanations:** Beyond simply classifying alignment, there is a growing need for models that can explain *why* a video and a caption are misaligned. Generating natural language explanations (NLEs) for discrepancies can provide valuable insights into model reasoning, aid in debugging, and offer a dense form of feedback for further model improvement.
5. **Benchmarking Limitations:** Current benchmarks may not fully capture the nuances of temporal and causal reasoning required for robust video understanding. There is a need for evaluation methodologies that specifically target these challenging aspects to accurately assess model capabilities.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- **Categorize Misalignment Types:** Systematically identify and categorize a broad spectrum of semantically plausible video-language misalignments that models should be robust against. This involves types such as object, action, attribute, counting, relation, hallucination, and event order flip.
- **Construct VideoCon Dataset:** Create a novel, high-quality dataset, VideoCon, by leveraging a large language model (LLM) to generate contrast captions and corresponding natural language explanations (NLEs) for filtered, temporally-challenging video-caption pairs. This dataset is designed to be rich in diverse misalignments.
- **Develop Robust Alignment Model:** Finetune a generative video-language model using VideoCon to enhance its ability to assess video-language entailment and generate accurate NLEs.
- **Evaluate Generalization:** Assess the finetuned model's ability to generalize to unseen videos and human-generated contrast captions and explanations, as well as its performance on zero-shot downstream tasks that require robust temporal and causal reasoning.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a targeted solution to improve the trustworthiness and deep understanding capabilities of video-language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a "scalable, active strategy" for data generation and model finetuning, aimed at improving the robustness of video-language alignment models. This involves the creation of a novel dataset called VideoCon and subsequently finetuning a generative video-language model using this dataset.

#### 3.1. VideoCon Dataset Construction

The VideoCon dataset is constructed in several stages, integrating existing video-language resources with LLM-based generation and human validation:

1. **Source Data Selection:**
 - The process begins with existing datasets comprising natural videos and human-written captions: MSR-VTT, VaTeX, and TEMPO. These datasets collectively offer a wide range of video content and linguistic complexity.

2. **Temporally-Challenging Instance Selection:**
 - A critical step is to filter out "temporally-easy" instances, where video-text alignment could be inferred from a single image frame rather than requiring actual temporal understanding.
 - For MSR-VTT and VaTeX, an End-to-End VNLI model is used to calculate an image-text alignment score, $A_{vle}(V, T) = \max_i(VNLI(I_i, T))$, where $I_i$ are frames from the video.
 - Captions with the *lowest* $A_{vle}(V, T)$ scores (indicating less reliance on single-frame understanding) are retained. TEMPO, by design, already emphasizes temporal challenges. This filtering significantly increased the percentage of temporally-challenging instances in the datasets.

3. **Categories of Contrast Captions:**
 - To ensure broad coverage of plausible misalignments, seven distinct categories are defined for generating contrast captions:
 - **Object:** Replacing a key entity (e.g., "sword" to "shield").
 - **Action:** Changing an action performed (e.g., "giggles" to "cries").
 - **Attribute:** Modifying an attribute of an object (e.g., "brown horse" to "green horse").
 - **Counting:** Altering the numerical count of objects (e.g., "two friends" to "three friends").
 - **Relation:** Changing spatial or other relations between entities (e.g., "rolls under a car" to "rolls on top of a car").
 - **Hallucination:** Introducing plausible but non-existent elements (e.g., "performing on his guitar" to "performing on his guitar with a band").
 - **Event Order Flip:** Reversing the temporal sequence of events (e.g., "passes through a gate then follows the path" to "follows the path then passes through a gate").
 - Misalignment types are assigned to captions based on keywords, numerical presence, and LLM-based event identification (for event order flips on challenging instances).

4. **LLM-assisted Data Generation (VideoCon (LLM)):**
 - A Large Language Model (PaLM-2 API, specifically `chat-bison@001`) is prompted with an original video-caption pair and a specific misalignment type.
 - The LLM generates a semantically plausible contrast caption and a corresponding Natural Language Explanation (NLE) detailing the difference between the original and contrast captions.
 - **Filtering LLM Outputs:**
 - **Contrast Captions:** An NLI model is used to filter out generated contrast captions that do not genuinely contradict the original caption (i.e., if NLI score for `original_caption` entailing `contrast_caption` is > 0.5).
 - **NLEs:** NLEs are filtered if they do not adequately describe the discrepancy between the original and contrast captions (NLI score for `difference_premise` entailing `NLE` < 0.6).
 - This process yields tuples of (Video, Original Caption, Contrast Caption, NLE) for training and evaluation.
 - **Human Verification:** A sample of 500 LLM-generated contrast captions and NLEs undergoes human evaluation, with 91% of contrast captions and 89% of NLEs deemed valid, indicating high quality.

5. **Human-Generated Data (VideoCon (Human)):**
 - To assess generalization capabilities, a separate dataset is collected from human annotators using videos from ActivityNet (a dataset not used in VideoCon (LLM) creation).
 - Human workers are shown video segments and their original captions, then asked to create a semantically plausible contrast caption and an NLE, without explicit instructions on misalignment types.
 - A "hard" subset, VideoCon (Human-Hard), is created by filtering out temporally-easy instances from this human-generated data, similar to the LLM-based filtering.

#### 3.2. Model Finetuning and Evaluation

1. **Generative Video-Language Model:**
 - The mPLUG-Owl-Video model (referred to as Owl-Base) is chosen as the foundation. This model utilizes CLIP as a visual encoder and LLaMA-7B as a text encoder/decoder, having been pretrained on VideoChat.

2. **Finetuning with VideoCon (Owl-Con):**
 - Owl-Base is finetuned on the VideoCon (LLM) training set using a multitask learning approach.
 - **Video-Language Entailment (VLE) Task:** Converted into an instruction-response format: "\[V] Does this video entail the description \[T/C]?" expecting a "Yes" or "No" response.
 - **Natural Language Explanation (NLE) Generation Task:** Converted into an instruction-response format: "\[V] What is the misalignment between this video and the description \[C]?" expecting the generated NLE.
 - The model is trained with a maximum likelihood loss. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is applied to all attention block layers for efficient finetuning.
 - Training utilizes Adam optimizer, a linear-warmup followed by cosine decay learning rate schedule, with 32 video frames sampled from 32 segments.

3. **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Video-Language Entailment:** ROC-AUC score is computed based on the probability of generating "Yes" versus "No" for ground-truth entailing and non-entailing pairs.
 - **NLE Generation:** Q2 score and PaLM-2 API entailment accuracy are used to quantitatively compare generated NLEs against ground-truth NLEs. Human evaluation confirmed the reliability of these automatic metrics.
 - **Zero-Shot Downstream Tasks:**
 - **Text-to-Video Retrieval:** Evaluated on SSv2-Temporal and SSv2-Events datasets, using SSv2-Template captions. Performance is measured by mean Average Precision (mAP).
 - **Video Question Answering:** Evaluated on the ATP-Hard dataset. Questions and multiple-choice answers are recast into imperative statements using PaLM-2. The model then scores the alignment of each statement with the video, and the highest-scoring statement is chosen as the answer. Accuracy is reported.

#### 3.3. Baselines

Performance is compared against several baselines, including:
- End-to-End VNLI (an atemporal image-text entailment model applied to video frames)
- VideoCLIP
- ImageBind (Video-Text)
- Owl-Base (the unfintuned mPLUG-Owl-Video model)
- Owl-Rand (Owl-Base finetuned on VideoCon with randomly selected negative captions, to isolate the effect of *hard* contrastives)
- Additionally, TACT and VFC are included for downstream task comparisons.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that finetuning models with the VideoCon dataset significantly enhances their robustness in video-language alignment and improves performance on various downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on VideoCon Entailment Task

- **Significant Improvement on LLM-Generated Data:** On the VideoCon (LLM) test set, the finetuned model, Owl-Con, achieves an ROC-AUC score of 84.6. This represents a substantial improvement compared to the Owl-Base model (57.2) and the End-to-End VNLI baseline (67.0), which serves as a strong atemporal probe. The low scores of baseline models indicate the inherent difficulty of the VideoCon test set, where negatives are carefully constructed.
- **Value of Hard Negatives:** Owl-Rand, which uses randomly chosen negative captions for finetuning, shows only a marginal improvement over Owl-Base (59.7 AUC). This highlights that the specific, semantically plausible contrast captions in VideoCon are crucial for developing robust alignment capabilities, rather than just any negative examples.
- **Generalization to Human-Generated Data:** On out-of-domain (OOD) data from VideoCon (Human) and VideoCon (Human-Hard), Owl-Con maintains superior performance over all baselines, achieving ROC-AUC scores of 78.3 and 74.4 respectively. This indicates that the robust understanding acquired from LLM-generated contrast captions generalizes to human-generated misalignments, even for unseen video types. The relative gains are smaller than on in-domain data, suggesting potential for further diversification in VideoCon's coverage of OOD cases.

#### 4.2. Performance on NLE Generation Task

- **Accurate Explanation Generation:** Owl-Con demonstrates a significant capability in generating Natural Language Explanations (NLEs). On the in-domain VideoCon (LLM) dataset, Owl-Con achieves a Q2 entailment score of 0.50 and a PaLM-2 entailment accuracy of 65.4%. This is a substantial improvement over Owl-Base (0.19 Q2, 36.8% PaLM-2 accuracy), indicating that Owl-Con can generate explanations that accurately describe video-text discrepancies.
- **OOD Explanation Generalization:** For out-of-domain VideoCon (Human) data, Owl-Con again outperforms Owl-Base (0.32 Q2, 47.1% PaLM-2 accuracy vs. 0.23 Q2, 39.6% PaLM-2 accuracy), albeit with more moderate gains. This suggests an ability to generalize NLE generation, though human linguistic diversity in explanations can be more varied than LLM-generated ones.

#### 4.3. Performance on Video-Text Downstream Tasks

- **Text-to-Video Retrieval (Table 4):**
 - Owl-Con achieves new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on both SSv2-Temporal (15.2 mAP) and SSv2-Events (11.4 mAP).
 - It significantly outperforms the previous best baselines, including End-to-End VNLI (14.6 mAP on SSv2-Temporal, 10.4 mAP on SSv2-Events) and TACT (7.8 mAP on SSv2-Events). This indicates that the robust training with contrast captions, particularly those involving temporal aspects, directly translates to improved zero-shot retrieval capabilities on tasks requiring fine-grained temporal and action understanding.
 - Even Owl-Rand, trained with random negatives, shows an improvement over previous SOTA models, underscoring the general benefit of additional task-specific training data.
- **Video Question Answering (Table 5):**
 - Owl-Con sets a new SOTA on the ATP-Hard dataset, achieving 41.1% accuracy.
 - This represents a 5.1% relative increase over the strong End-to-End VNLI baseline (39.0%). This result reinforces that VideoCon-based training enhances a model's ability to perform causal and temporal reasoning required for complex video question answering.

#### 4.4. Per-Misalignment Entailment Results (Figure 7)

- **Comprehensive Improvement Across Misalignment Types:** Owl-Con outperforms Owl-Base and End-to-End VNLI across all seven categories of misalignments in the VideoCon (LLM) test set.
- **Enhanced Temporal Understanding:** The most significant improvement for Owl-Con is observed on the "Event Order Flip" misalignment type. This directly demonstrates that VideoCon effectively instills temporal reasoning capabilities into the model, addressing a known weakness of prior video-language models.
- **Addressing Atemporal Challenges:** Owl-Con also shows strong improvements in "Hallucination," "Counting," and "Relation" misalignments. This indicates that the dataset is effective not only for temporal reasoning but also for improving detailed comprehension of entities and their properties/relations within a video, even compared to models trained on larger human-curated entailment data (like End-to-End VNLI).

#### 4.5. Qualitative Examples (Figure 6)

- **Success Cases:** The model successfully predicts entailment/contradiction and provides accurate NLEs, including for complex event order flips, demonstrating its fine-grained understanding and explanatory capabilities.
- **Failure Cases:** Examples highlight that the model can still struggle with differentiating highly similar objects (e.g., confusing "buns" with "braids"). This indicates areas where further diversity and contrastive examples in the dataset could improve object-level discrimination.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces VideoCon, a comprehensive dataset and a corresponding finetuning methodology designed to significantly enhance the robustness of video-language alignment models. The findings demonstrate a clear advancement in the field, with several significant implications:

1. **Improved Model Robustness:** The most direct impact is the substantial improvement in the robustness of video-language models against semantically plausible changes in captions. By addressing specific types of misalignments (object, action, attribute, counting, relation, hallucination, and critically, event order flips), the finetuned VideoCon model (Owl-Con) exhibits a far greater ability to correctly identify video-text entailment and contradiction compared to previous state-of-the-art models. This directly addresses a recognized weakness that has hindered the trustworthiness and real-world deployment of such systems.

2. **Enhanced Temporal Understanding:** The marked improvement in handling "event order flip" misalignments signifies a significant step forward in instilling temporal reasoning capabilities in video-language models. This is crucial for truly understanding dynamic events in videos, moving beyond mere static object recognition. This enhanced temporal understanding is reflected in the state-of-the-art performance on temporally-intensive downstream tasks like SSv2-Temporal and ATP-Hard.

3. **Data Efficiency and Quality over Quantity:** The work demonstrates that carefully curated, high-quality contrastive data, specifically targeting known model weaknesses, can be highly effective in improving performance. This suggests a paradigm shift where intelligent data generation strategies, rather than just massive scaling of web data, can lead to more robust and capable models. This has implications for resource-constrained research and for developing more targeted training regimens.

4. **Advancement in Zero-Shot Performance:** The attainment of new state-of-the-art results on several challenging zero-shot downstream tasks (Text-to-Video Retrieval on SSv2-Temporal/SSv2-Events and Video Question Answering on ATP-Hard) indicates that the robustness gained from VideoCon training generalizes well to novel scenarios. This highlights the practical utility of the finetuned models in unseen and demanding applications.

5. **Fine-Grained Explanatory Capabilities:** The model's ability to generate accurate Natural Language Explanations (NLEs) for misalignments provides valuable fine-grained feedback. This capability is significant not only for transparent AI, allowing users to understand *why* an alignment decision was made, but also as a potential mechanism for future model improvement through dense feedback loops in active learning or human-in-the-loop training scenarios.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Foundation for Trustworthy AI:** By improving robustness, VideoCon contributes to building more reliable and trustworthy AI systems for video understanding, which is critical for applications in surveillance, content moderation, autonomous systems, and medical analysis.
- **Advanced Content Generation and Search:** More robust video-language alignment could lead to more accurate text-to-video retrieval systems, enhancing content discovery. It could also provide better evaluation metrics and feedback for next-generation text-to-video generative models, ensuring the fidelity of generated content.
- **Adaptive Learning Systems:** The NLE generation capability opens avenues for developing adaptive learning systems that can receive human feedback in natural language to refine their understanding, potentially leading to faster and more efficient model iteration.
- **Refinement of Diagnostic Tools:** The detailed analysis of misalignment types could inspire new diagnostic tools for identifying specific weaknesses in video-language models, guiding future research and development.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current generation of contrast captions primarily focusing on single misalignments. Future work could explore more complex scenarios with multiple misalignments within a single caption. Scaling VideoCon to web-scale low-quality data, possibly through synthetic data generation, and comprehensively expanding the human-generated dataset to cover a broader range of visual domains (e.g., generated videos) are also identified as important next steps. Addressing linguistic diversity in human explanations would further enhance the model's generalization. Overall, VideoCon represents a significant contribution towards developing more robust, understandable, and ultimately, more useful video-language AI.
