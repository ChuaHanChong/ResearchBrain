# FACT: Failure-Aware Causal Training for World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> FACT is a world-action model that reverses the usual order: it first denoises an action chunk, then predicts future video and a task-progress value conditioned on that executed action. A teacher-forced attention mask lets failure rollouts supervise the world branch while their imitation loss is masked. This raises RoboTwin success to 87.5% and real-world seen-task success to 89%, and cuts success-biased future hallucination.

## Problem

- Video-first world-action models decode actions through a second-stage inverse-dynamics network, requiring the future video to be fully denoised before control can proceed.
- Models conditioning actions on predicted futures train those futures only on expert demonstrations, so a bad test-time action still gets paired with a success-biased future.
- Failed rollouts are discarded because using them naively turns bad actions into imitation targets, corrupting the policy rather than teaching action consequences.

## Method

- ==Causal action-then-future token order== (prefix, noisy action, clean ground-truth action, value, future video) with a ==teacher-forcing attention mask== so world predictions condition on the clean action slot, not the noisy one.
- ==Failure-aware co-training==: successful demos supervise action, value, and video losses; failure rollouts mask the ==action imitation loss== but keep video plus a lowered ==clipped progress target== after failure onset.
- Shared ==video diffusion backbone== initialized from a pretrained video model with a lightweight ==action adapter== on robot tokens after each block, plus ==two-stage inference== with optional ==best-of-N value ranking==.

## Results

- On **50** RoboTwin tasks, video co-training lifts average success from **81.8%** to **85.6%** and failure co-training to **87.5%**, near the strongest baseline at **87.8%** but roughly **3x** faster (**380 ms** vs **1220 ms**).
- Real-world seen tasks: **82%** base, **89%** with failure data, **92%** with **N=4** candidate scoring; unseen variants rise **67%** to **77%** to **82%**. Scoring without failure training gives only **79%**.
- Failure-rollout future prediction improves from **19.51** to **25.92** PSNR while success futures stay flat (**26.12** vs **26.08**); unmasking failed-action imitation loss collapses success to **63%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ordering generation as action-then-consequence turns otherwise unusable failure data into valid supervision, separating what a model should imitate from what it should predict.
> - A value head only becomes a trustworthy ranking signal after seeing failed outcomes; trained on success alone it is optimistically miscalibrated off the expert manifold.
> - Future-prediction co-training acts as a strong regularizer on action generation, and failure-data gains scaled monotonically rather than saturating early.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robot policies is a significant objective within robot learning. Current research efforts largely converge around two primary paradigms: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World-Action Models (WAMs).

VLA models represent a prominent approach, learning to generate robot actions directly from image observations and language instructions. These models often transfer semantic priors from vision-language models to low-level control, exemplified by large-scale robot transformers and open-source policies that leverage heterogeneous robot datasets. Subsequent advancements have incorporated flow- or diffusion-based policies to scale continuous action modeling and enhance open-world generalization.

WAMs extend the VLA paradigm by integrating future visual prediction. These models utilize video prediction capabilities or future-prediction objectives to co-train control policies with an understanding of how the environment evolves under robot interaction. This integration aims to imbue robot policy learning with temporal dynamics and physical priors.

Existing WAMs typically use predicted futures as an intermediate signal for action selection. This can involve two main strategies:
1. **Video-first systems:** These models first imagine future videos and then decode actions using an inverse dynamics model. While benefiting from a strong world prior, this approach often necessitates a second-stage network for action decoding and typically requires fully denoised future videos before control execution.
2. **Future-as-condition systems:** These designs employ predicted future frames or latent representations as conditions for action prediction, serving as an auxiliary signal.

A common limitation across many existing WAMs is their training methodology, which predominantly relies on successful demonstrations. Consequently, these models learn to associate plausible futures with effective actions but receive limited supervision regarding the outcomes of undesirable actions. This can lead to a phenomenon termed "success-biased future hallucination," where even incorrect or "bad" actions at test time might still be erroneously paired with predictions of successful outcomes.

This paper introduces FACT (Failure-Aware Causal Training) to address this specific challenge. FACT distinguishes itself by focusing on the causal role of action-conditioned futures, particularly how failure rollouts can provide structured supervision about undesirable consequences. Instead of treating failed actions as mere low-quality demonstrations for policy imitation, FACT aims to utilize them to teach the world model *what specific wrong behaviors lead to*. This approach represents an advancement in extracting supervision beyond purely successful demonstrations, specifically by converting failure data into a form of action-conditioned future and value supervision to enhance robust policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a recognized limitation in existing World-Action Models (WAMs): their tendency to exhibit "success-biased future hallucination." Current WAMs are predominantly trained on datasets comprising successful task demonstrations. While this enables them to learn robust policies for achieving goals, it provides insufficient experience with the outcomes of suboptimal or failed actions. As a result, when faced with an incorrect action at inference time, these models may still predict a successful future, failing to accurately represent the negative consequences of that action. This undermines the utility of the world model as a reliable predictor of future states and reduces its ability to guide the policy away from failures.

To address this, the authors identify a core problem: how can information from failed rollouts be effectively utilized as supervision for understanding action consequences without inadvertently training the policy to *imitate* those failed actions? Simply including failed trajectories as additional demonstrations could degrade policy performance by instructing the robot on how to fail.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the FACT research are:

1. **Develop a Causal World-Action Model with an Action-Then-Future Sequence:** The first objective is to propose a novel model architecture, FACT, that explicitly structures the prediction process. Unlike some prior WAMs that might predict future states first and then derive actions, FACT is designed to first generate an action and then predict the resulting future video and task progress *conditioned on that executed action*. This causal ordering is foundational to enabling failure-aware training.

2. **Introduce a Teacher-Forced Action-Conditioned Mask:** A specific objective is to implement a mechanism that cleanly separates action generation from future (world-side) prediction. This mechanism, realized through a teacher-forced action-conditioned mask, allows the world model (predicting future video and task value) to be supervised by the *ground-truth outcome* of an action, even if that action was a failure. Concurrently, it ensures that the policy component (action generation) is *not* trained to imitate the failed action. This strategy transforms failure data into valuable consequence supervision rather than corrupting the action decoding process.

3. **Validate Improved Policy Success and Reduced Hallucination:** A critical objective is to empirically demonstrate that incorporating failure data through FACT's proposed methodology leads to enhanced policy performance in robotic manipulation tasks. Furthermore, the research aims to show that this failure-aware training specifically reduces the tendency of the world model to hallucinate successful futures when provided with bad actions, thereby improving the fidelity of future prediction.

4. **Enable Optional Candidate Ranking via a Progress Predictor:** The final objective is to show that by training the world model to be aware of both successful and failed outcomes, the learned task-progress value predictor becomes sensitive enough to action quality to be optionally used for scoring and ranking candidate actions at inference time, offering a configurable trade-off between computation and reliability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FACT (Failure-Aware Causal Training) introduces a causal World-Action Model that generates actions prior to predicting their consequences. The methodology is structured around problem formulation, model architecture, and a specialized training and inference strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as language-conditioned robotic manipulation, a sequential decision-making problem. At each time step $t$, the robot receives a language instruction $\ell$ and an observation $o_t$, which includes multi-view RGB images and proprioceptive robot state. The goal is to output an action chunk $a_{t:t+H}$ that advances the task.
FACT augments a standard language-conditioned policy, $p_\theta(a_{t:t+H} | o_t, \ell)$, with an action-conditioned world branch that predicts future video observations $o'_{t:t+K}$ and a task-progress value $v_t(a_{t:t+H})$. The progress value $v_t \in$ is normalized, with $0$ indicating the start of a task and $1$ indicating completion, derived from cumulative progress rewards.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

The core innovation in FACT's architecture lies in its ability to learn future outcomes from failure rollouts without imitating failed actions. This is achieved through an explicit separation between action imitation and future prediction, facilitated by a teacher-forcing mechanism.

- **Action-Conditioned Future Prediction:** The world branch (predicting $o'_{t:t+K}$ and $v_t$) is conditioned on a *clean ground-truth action* $a_{gt, t:t+H}$, not the potentially noisy action predicted by the policy branch. This is represented as $p_\theta(o'_{t:t+K}, v_t(a_{gt, t:t+H}) | o_t, \ell, a_{gt, t:t+H})$.
- **Token Sequence and Attention Mask:** FACT implements this factorization using a specific token order: $z = [z_{P_{ref}} \| z_{A_{pred}} \| z_{G_{gt}} \| z_{V_{value}} \| z_{I_{future}}]$.
 - $P_{ref}$: Observation prefix (reference images).
 - $A_{pred}$: Noisy predicted action segment (for policy generation).
 - $G_{gt}$: Clean teacher-forced ground-truth action segment (for world prediction).
 - $V_{value}$: Value segment (for task progress prediction).
 - $I_{future}$: Future-video segment (for future visual prediction).
 The attention mask (Figure 3 in the paper) enforces the causal separation. During training, $V$ and $I$ tokens attend to the clean ground-truth action $G$, ensuring that world-side predictions are based on the actual action taken. Critically, $A$ tokens (for action denoising) are prevented from attending to $G$, preventing the policy from directly imitating ground-truth actions, especially failed ones.
- **Shared Video Backbone with Action Adapter:** FACT utilizes a shared video diffusion transformer, initialized from a powerful video generative model (WAN2.2-5B). To tailor it for robot control, a lightweight action adapter is attached to robot tokens after the feed-forward network in each transformer block. This shared backbone allows future-outcome losses to influence action generation implicitly through shared representations, rather than being confined to a separate world expert model.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Strategy

- **Failure-Aware Value Targets:** The task progress target $v_t(a_{gt, t:t+H})$ is defined differently for successful demonstrations ($D_s$) and failure rollouts ($D_f$).
 - For successful trajectories, $v_t(a_{gt, t:t+H})$ is the normalized progress $p_{t+H}$.
 - For failure rollouts, the target is clipped to reflect a lowered score: $clip(p_{t+H} - \lambda_{fail} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{fail}(t+H), 0, 1)$, where $\mathbf{1}_{fail}$ indicates if failure occurred by executing $a_{gt, t:t+H}$. This teaches the model that actions leading to failure result in lower progress.
- **Joint Denoising Losses:** All predicted modalities (action, value, future video) are trained using a flow-matching denoising objective.
 - For successful demonstrations, the model is supervised on action imitation ($L_a$), value prediction ($L_v$), and future-video prediction ($L_I$).
 - For failure rollouts, the action imitation loss ($L_a$) is *masked out*, meaning the model is not trained to reproduce failed actions. However, $L_v$ and $L_I$ losses remain active, supervising the prediction of the observed failed future and its corresponding lower progress value. This is the core mechanism for failure-aware consequence learning without policy corruption.
- **Two-Stage Inference:** Inference proceeds in two stages:
 - **Stage 1:** The model denoises the noisy action segment ($A_{pred}$) conditioned on the observation prefix ($P_{ref}$) to produce a clean action chunk $\hat{a}_{t:t+H}$. This stage is sufficient if only an action is required.
 - **Stage 2:** If consequence prediction or candidate scoring is desired, the clean action $\hat{a}_{t:t+H}$ from Stage 1 is inserted into the *clean action-conditioning slot* ($G_{gt}$). Then, the value and optionally future-video latents are denoised to predict $\hat{v}$ and future frames.
- **Optional Candidate Scoring:** Because the progress predictor is trained on action-conditioned failed outcomes, its value estimates become sensitive to action quality. At deployment, Stage 1 can sample $N$ action candidates. Stage 2 then predicts an action-conditioned progress score $\hat{v}^{(k)}$ for each candidate, and the action with the highest score is selected. This provides a mechanism for value-guided action selection without training a separate critic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated FACT across both simulation (RoboTwin benchmark) and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, comparing it against various robot foundation policies and recent World-Action Models (WAMs). The key findings demonstrate the efficacy of failure-aware causal training.

#### 4.1. Performance on RoboTwin Simulation Tasks

- **Impact of Video Co-training:** Removing video co-training significantly reduced FACT's average success rate from 85.6% to 81.8% (Table 1), indicating that future prediction serves as an important regularizer for action generation.
- **Impact of Failure Co-training:** Incorporating failure data into training further improved FACT's average success from 85.6% to 87.5%.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** FACT with failure-aware training (87.5%) achieved performance comparable to Motus (87.8%), a strong WAM baseline, while operating roughly three times faster at deployment (Appendix E).

#### 4.2. Performance on Real-World Manipulation Tasks

- **Seen Tasks:** On a set of five seen bimanual manipulation tasks (Table 2):
 - FACT (82% average success) outperformed Motus (64%) and other baselines like $\pi_{0.5}$ (88%) and $\pi_0$ (48%).
 - Failure-aware training further boosted FACT's average success from 82% to 89%.
 - Optional candidate scoring, when combined with failure-aware training, led to the highest average success rate of 92%.
 - It was observed that candidate scoring *without* failure-aware training only yielded 79% success, indicating that the value head's utility for ranking is contingent upon its training with negative consequences.
- **Unseen Tasks:** On three held-out unseen task variants (Table 3), which involved changes in object properties and instructions:
 - FACT with failure-aware training improved success from 67% to 77%.
 - The addition of optional scoring further raised performance to 82%. This performance approaches that of $\pi_{0.5}$ (85%), despite FACT not leveraging the large-scale robot pretraining utilized by $\pi_{0.5}$.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Design Choices

- **Video Co-training:** Removing future video co-training consistently degraded performance in both simulation (85.6% to 81.8%) and real-world seen tasks (82% to 58%), reinforcing its role as a regularizer for action generation.
- **Causal Mask:** An ablation without the teacher-forced clean ground-truth action condition (G) for world prediction showed a reduction in real-world seen-task success from 82% to 77%. This suggests the importance of clean action conditioning for robust future prediction.
- **Failed-Action Loss:** When failure rollouts were included but their action imitation loss was *not* masked, policy success dropped significantly to 63%. This finding strongly supports the core hypothesis that failed actions should supervise consequences, not be treated as imitation targets for the policy itself.

#### 4.4. Diagnostics of Future Prediction and Value Function

- **Reduced Future Hallucination:** Qualitative analysis (Figure 5) and quantitative metrics (Table 4) demonstrated that failure-aware co-training mitigates success-biased future hallucination. When presented with a bad action, the success-only model predicted a successful grasp, whereas the failure-aware model accurately predicted the observed failed outcome. Quantitatively, failure-aware training substantially improved future prediction quality (PSNR, SSIM) on failure-rollout futures without compromising prediction quality on successful demonstrations.
- **Failure Data Scaling:** Experiments on RoboTwin showed a monotonic increase in average success as the fraction of incorporated failure rollouts increased, suggesting continued benefits from additional failure data (Figure 6).
- **Value-Guided Candidate Scoring:** Analysis of the number of candidates for optional scoring (Figure 7) showed that increasing candidates from N=1 to N=4 improved task completion, with diminishing returns for larger N, indicating a practical trade-off between performance and latency.
- **Action-Conditioned Value Traces:** Visualizations of task-progress value over rollouts (Figure 8, Figure 12) demonstrated that the predicted value dynamically reflects action outcomes, decreasing upon a missed grasp and recovering after a successful re-grasp. A value heat map (Figure 13) further confirmed that the value head assigns higher scores to successful placements and lower scores to failing ones, validating its utility as an action-conditioned ranking signal.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FACT research makes several contributions to the field of robot learning, particularly in the domain of World-Action Models (WAMs) and general-purpose robot policies.

#### 5.1. Novelty in World-Action Model Design

FACT introduces a novel causal ordering in WAMs, where actions are generated *before* their consequences (future video and task progress value) are predicted. This "action-then-future" sequence, combined with a teacher-forcing mask, represents a significant architectural innovation. It allows for a clean separation between the policy (action generation) and the world model (consequence prediction), which is crucial for the proposed training methodology.

#### 5.2. Effective Utilization of Failure Data

A core contribution is FACT's method for leveraging failure rollouts. Prior work often struggled with how to incorporate failure data without corrupting the policy's ability to execute successful actions. FACT addresses this by using failed actions to supervise the world model's understanding of consequences (what *will* happen) and value (how *bad* it will be), while simultaneously masking the action imitation loss, thus preventing the policy from learning to perform the failed actions (what *not to do*). This transforms previously unusable data into a valuable source of supervision.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Policy Robustness and Performance

The empirical results consistently demonstrate that failure-aware causal training leads to improved policy success rates in both complex simulation environments (RoboTwin) and real-world bimanual manipulation tasks. This enhancement extends to unseen task variations, suggesting improved generalization. The ability of FACT to achieve competitive or superior performance against existing state-of-the-art WAMs and robot foundation policies, sometimes with significantly lower inference latency, underscores its practical utility.

#### 5.4. Mitigation of Success-Biased Future Hallucination

A critical impact of FACT's approach is the reduction of "success-biased future hallucination." By training on the actual outcomes of failed actions, the world model learns to predict realistic negative consequences when given suboptimal inputs. This improves the fidelity and trustworthiness of the future predictions, making the world model a more reliable component for understanding and reasoning about robot interactions.

#### 5.5. Action Candidate Scoring and Compute-Performance Trade-off

The learned action-conditioned task-progress predictor provides a valuable optional interface for ranking candidate actions at inference time. This capability allows for a flexible compute-performance trade-off: a single action can be executed for low latency, or multiple candidates can be scored to select the most promising action, enhancing reliability at the cost of increased computation. This feature is particularly impactful as the value head's sensitivity to action quality is directly cultivated by its exposure to failed outcomes.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The work lays a foundation for future advancements in robot learning. The action-conditioned world modeling framework offers a natural interface for integrating more sophisticated training regimes, such as:
- **Online Rollouts and DAgger-style Corrections:** Continuously refining policies by collecting data during deployment and correcting errors.
- **Reinforcement Learning from Negative Experience:** Leveraging negative outcomes as explicit learning signals within RL frameworks.
- **Scaling and Generalization:** Extending the approach to broader robot and human-interaction datasets could further enhance the physical plausibility of future predictions and the discriminative power of the value head.
- **Alternative Progress Estimators:** Investigating more advanced or learned progress estimators to potentially augment or replace the current value head.

In conclusion, FACT's methodology advances the state of robotic control by enabling world models to learn from failure in a structured and beneficial way, leading to more robust policies and more accurate consequence prediction capabilities.
