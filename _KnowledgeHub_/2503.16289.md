# SceneMI: Motion In-betweening for Modeling Human-Scene Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> SceneMI reformulates human-scene interaction modeling as scene-aware motion in-betweening: a conditional diffusion model with dual global/local scene descriptors (voxel grid + keyframe-centered BPS) and a two-phase denoising schedule that handles noisy keyframes. It generalizes zero-shot from synthetic TRUMANS training to real-world noisy GIMO data and monocular-video HSI reconstruction.

## Problem

- Prior scene-aware HSI generators (SceneDiffuser, HUMANISE) lack controllability, offering no mechanism to condition on user-specified keyframes for practical animation workflows.
- Existing goal-reaching approaches rely on conditional VAEs for scene and pose constraints, giving limited expressivity and poor scalability to diverse scenes.
- Motion in-betweening methods assume clean, precise keyframes and ignore environmental constraints, causing body-scene penetration when naively applied to noisy real-world captures.

## Method

- ==Dual scene descriptors==: a coarse global occupancy voxel grid (==ViT==-encoded) capturing overall layout, plus keyframe-centered local ==Basis Point Set== features encoding fine-grained body-scene proximity.
- ==Keyframe imputation== scheme that substitutes clean values into the noisy diffusion sample at masked frame locations, letting a single ==U-Net== denoiser jointly respect keyframes and scene context.
- ==Noise-aware two-phase sampling== that splits diffusion timesteps at threshold T*, guiding early denoising with raw noisy keyposes then iteratively refining all frames including keyframes for later steps, forming **SceneMI**.

## Results

- On noise-free TRUMANS, SceneMI achieves FID **0.123** versus CondMDI's **0.943** and reduces keyframe MPJE to **0.023 m**, the best across all seven metrics against six baselines.
- On real-world GIMO data, SceneMI cuts foot skating **37.5%** (**0.261** to **0.163**) and jitter **56.5%** (**0.573** to **0.249**) relative to raw noisy captures, despite training only on synthetic TRUMANS.
- Ablations confirm scene-awareness lowers collision frame ratio from **0.237** to **0.162** on close-proximity frames, while noise-awareness (T*=**20** vs T*=**0**) drops FID from **0.157** to **0.118**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reformulating an underconstrained generative task (HSI synthesis) as a constrained in-betweening problem trades some generality for controllability and real-world applicability.
> - Diffusion models' inherent denoising capability can be repurposed to absorb sensor noise directly, avoiding a separate denoising preprocessing stage for imperfect motion capture.
> - Decoupling global layout context from local body-proximity context lets a model generalize across scene acquisition domains (hand-crafted, phone-scanned, video-reconstructed) without retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within computer graphics and artificial intelligence: human motion synthesis, human-scene interaction (HSI) modeling, and motion in-betweening. Its contribution is particularly significant in addressing the real-world challenges inherent in these domains.

**Human Motion Synthesis (HMS)** has seen considerable progress with data-driven approaches, particularly generative models conditioned on various inputs like text, action categories, audio, or interacting objects. A recent trend within HMS is **trajectory-based controllable motion generation**, allowing for finer control over specific body parts or the root joint. SceneMI builds upon these advancements but targets a more complex and practical problem.

**Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) Modeling** is a critical, yet challenging, sub-field. Accurately simulating human behavior in environments requires understanding complex affordances and physical constraints. Traditional approaches often rely on reinforcement learning or hierarchical methods that first plan high-level paths and then synthesize detailed motions. More recently, end-to-end generative models, including diffusion models, have emerged for HSI from text or action labels. However, a persistent limitation across most existing HSI works is a **lack of fine-grained motion controllability and practical flexibility** for real-world applications, such as integrating user-defined keyframes or refining noisy input data.

**Motion In-betweening** is a long-standing problem in animation, focused on generating smooth transitions between sparse keyframes. Historically, this involved spline interpolation or motion planning on graphs, which are often computationally intensive and require extensive manual refinement. Deep learning, especially recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Transformer architectures, has revolutionized this. Generative methods, like diffusion models (e.g., CondMDI), have further enabled diverse and natural transitions. The critical gap that SceneMI identifies and addresses within motion in-betweening is the **neglect of environmental factors** and the **assumption of precise, noise-free keyframes**, which is highly impractical for real-world data acquisition.

SceneMI uniquely synthesizes these areas by formally studying **scene-aware motion in-betweening**. It fills a crucial void by providing a generative, diffusion-based framework that not only generates plausible motions between sparse keyframes but also inherently understands and respects complex 3D scene constraints. Furthermore, its ability to robustly handle noisy keyframes and generalize to diverse scene sources represents a significant step towards bridging the gap between theoretical models and practical, real-world deployment in areas like character animation, AR/VR, and motion capture data refinement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SceneMI stems from the significant limitations of existing approaches in modeling human-scene interactions (HSI) and motion in-betweening when confronted with real-world complexities.

**Primary Motivation:**
1. **Lack of Controllability and Flexibility in HSI Modeling:** Current generative models for HSI often produce impressive motions but lack intuitive user control (e.g., through specific key poses) and flexibility for diverse real-world applications. This makes them less practical for animators or for refining imperfect data.
2. **Absence of Scene-Awareness in Motion In-betweening:** Traditional motion in-betweening methods typically focus solely on human kinematics, often neglecting environmental constraints. This leads to physically implausible results, such as body-scene penetrations, when applied within 3D scenes.
3. **Imperfection of Real-World Data:** Keyframes and scenes acquired from real-world sensors (e.g., noisy IMU data, phone-scanned scenes, monocular video estimates) are inherently noisy and imperfect. Existing in-betweening methods largely assume clean keyframes, limiting their practical utility.

**Key Objectives of SceneMI:**
Based on these motivations, the authors propose to **reformulate the HSI modeling problem as Scene-aware Motion In-betweening**, a task they introduce and formally study for the first time. The specific objectives are:
1. **Synthesize Physically Plausible Motion Transitions:** To generate natural and smooth motion sequences that connect sparse keyframes while respecting environmental affordances and avoiding collisions within a 3D scene.
2. **Robustly Handle Noisy Keyframes:** To develop a framework that can generalize and produce high-quality motions even when input keyframes are imperfect or noisy, drawing inspiration from the denoising nature of diffusion models.
3. **Support Practical Applications:** To enable various real-world use cases, including:
 - **Keyframe-guided character animation:** Allowing animators to directly create 3D character animations in scenes from sparse keyframes.
 - **Enhancing motion quality of imperfect HSI data:** Reducing artifacts like foot skating and jittering in existing, noisy motion capture datasets.
 - **Assisting realistic 3D human-scene interaction reconstruction:** Improving the naturalness and physical plausibility of HSI sequences reconstructed from monocular videos.
4. **Develop a Comprehensive Scene Encoding Strategy:** To effectively capture both global scene layout and local interaction context for guiding motion generation.

By achieving these objectives, SceneMI aims to make human-scene interaction modeling more controllable, flexible, and robust for practical, real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SceneMI tackles scene-aware motion in-betweening by leveraging a conditional diffusion model, designed with novel scene encoding strategies and a specialized denoising procedure for handling noisy keyframes.

#### 3.1. Motion and Shape Representation

Each human pose is represented as a 201-dimensional feature vector, comprising:
- Global joint positions (22x3, including root translation).
- 6D root orientation.
- Local SMPL pose parameters (21x6).
Body shape is represented by a 7-dimensional feature vector, encoding distances between key joint pairs (e.g., root-head, shoulder-shoulder) and body thickness measurements (chest, hip regions). This provides a compact, continuous representation of body volume variations.

#### 3.2. Dual Scene Encoding

To provide comprehensive environmental context, SceneMI employs two distinct scene descriptors:
- **Global Scene Features (`c_g`):** A coarse occupancy voxel grid (48x24x48, 0.1m/voxel) representing the overall scene layout. This grid is processed by a Vision Transformer (ViT) to yield a 512-dimensional feature vector. This provides high-level navigational information.
- **Keyframe-centered Local Scene Features (`c_l`):** These capture fine-grained interaction context around individual keyframes. The Basis Point Set (BPS) approach is used: 64 fixed anchor points are determined on a T-posed SMPL mesh. For each keyframe, the nearest 3D scene point to each anchor is found, and the resulting ordered offset vectors (64x3) form the local scene features. These are embedded via an MLP and concatenated with key pose features, effectively anchoring local scene context to the immediate body pose and generalizing across different scene sources.

#### 3.3. Scene-aware Motion In-betweening (Conditional Diffusion Model)

SceneMI uses a U-Net based architecture with Adaptive Group Normalization (AdaGN) and 1D convolution, known for robust global motion representation.
- **Training Procedure:**
 1. A diffusion timestep `t` is randomly sampled, and `k` keyframes (including start/end) are randomly selected to create a binary mask `m`.
 2. **Keyframe Imputation:** For the noisy sample `x_t`, the feature values at keyframe locations (`m_n = 1`) are replaced with their clean ground-truth values from `x_0`. Similarly, local scene features `c_l` are masked to only retain those visible from the selected keyframes.
 3. The denoising model `D_θ` is conditioned on: the imputed noisy sample (`x'_t`), masked local scene features (`c'_l`), keyframe mask (`m`), body shape (`b`), global scene features (`c_g`), and diffusion timestep (`t`).
 4. **Classifier-Free Guidance:** During training, `c_g` is randomly masked out (10% probability) to enable classifier-free guidance during inference.
 5. **Loss Function:** The model is trained with a combination of reconstruction loss (`L_simple` on `x_0`), and additional losses on global joint positions (`L_joints`) and joint velocities (`L_vel`), computed via forward kinematics (FK) from predicted SMPL parameters, to enhance realism.

- **Inference:**
 1. Global `c_g` and keyframe-centered local `c'_l` features are computed.
 2. At each sampling step, the keyframe features `s` (given input) are imputed into the current noisy sample `x_t`.
 3. Classifier-free guidance is applied to the denoiser output using a guidance scale `w`.

#### 3.4. Handling Noisy Keyframes

Recognizing that real-world keyframes are often imperfect, SceneMI leverages the inherent denoising capability of diffusion models.
- **Training for Noise Robustness:** A noisy motion dataset (`x_noisy_0`) is created by adding scaled Gaussian noise to clean motions. During training, the keyframe imputation (`m ⊙ x_noisy_0`) is restricted to occur only until a specific diffusion timestep `T*`. For subsequent steps (`t < T*`), the model trains to denoise `x_t` without direct keyframe supervision, allowing it to learn to "clean" the motion.
- **Inference with Noisy Keyframes:** The same strategy is applied: noisy input keyframes `s_noisy` guide the denoising process from `T` down to `T*+1`. From `T*` down to `1`, the entire motion sequence, including keyframes, is iteratively denoised, effectively purifying the noisy keyframes. `T* = 20` (out of `T = 1000`) was empirically found to be optimal.

#### 3.5. Datasets and Metrics

- **Training:** Exclusively on **TRUMANS**, a large-scale HSI dataset with motion capture data and hand-crafted indoor scenes. Synthetic noise was added to keyframes during training for robustness.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **TRUMANS (test set):** Both noise-free and synthetic noisy keyframes.
 - **GIMO:** A real-world dataset with noisy IMU-captured motions and smartphone-scanned scenes, for out-of-domain generalization.
 - **PROX:** For demonstrating video-based HSI reconstruction.
- **Metrics:** Frechet Inception Distance (FID), Foot Skating, Jerk (motion naturalness); Mean Joint Position Error (MJPE) at Keyframes and All frames (motion alignment); Collision Frame Ratio, Penetration Max (HSI plausibility).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SceneMI demonstrates superior performance across a range of challenging scenarios, validating its novel approach to scene-aware motion in-betweening and its robustness to real-world data imperfections.

1. **Superior Performance on Noise-Free TRUMANS:**
 - SceneMI significantly outperforms all baseline models (MDM, StableMoFusion, SceneDiffuser, Wang et al., OmniControl, CondMDI) on the TRUMANS dataset, which uses clean keyframes and in-domain scenes.
 - It achieves the best motion quality (FID of 0.123), precise keyframe alignment (MJPE Key of 0.006m, MJPE All of 0.023m), and excellent environmental awareness with the lowest collision frame ratio (0.113) and penetration (0.043m).
 - Baselines, either designed for general motion synthesis or scene-agnostic in-betweening, struggle with effective keyframe alignment or scene interaction, leading to higher errors and collisions.

2. **Ablation Study (Noise-Free TRUMANS):**
 - The ablation study confirms the importance of dual scene descriptors. Removing both global (`c_g`) and local (`c_l`) scene features ("w/o scene awareness") leads to increased collision frames (0.131 vs 0.113) and higher MJPE (0.059m vs 0.023m).
 - Both global and local features contribute to enhanced motion quality and alignment, with global features appearing to play a slightly more prominent role in overall motion guidance. The scene-aware design notably reduces collision ratio in close-proximity interactions from 0.237 to 0.162.

3. **Robust Generalization to Noisy Data (TRUMANS with Synthetic Noise):**
 - When evaluated on TRUMANS with synthetic noise added to keyframes, SceneMI maintains its strong performance (FID 0.118, MJPE All 0.012m), significantly outperforming baselines that degrade due to direct use of noisy keyframes.
 - The specialized noise-aware design (imputation up to `T*`) is critical for this robustness. Experiments with different `T*` values confirm its benefit, with `T* = 20` providing the best balance between motion smoothness (reduced jerk) and keyframe accuracy.

4. **Generalization on Real-World HSI (GIMO Dataset):**
 - Despite being trained exclusively on the synthetic TRUMANS dataset, SceneMI demonstrates remarkable generalizability to the real-world GIMO dataset, characterized by noisy IMU sensor data and phone-scanned scenes.
 - SceneMI's in-betweening process significantly reduces prevalent artifacts in the original GIMO data, decreasing foot skating by 37.5% (from 0.261 to 0.163) and jittering by 56.5% (from 0.573 to 0.249).
 - Ablation on GIMO data reinforces that noise-awareness primarily improves motion quality (less foot skating/jerk), while scene-awareness effectively reduces collision incidents.

5. **Application: Video-Based HSI Reconstruction:**
 - SceneMI is successfully integrated into a novel pipeline for reconstructing 3D human-scene interactions from monocular videos.
 - It effectively refines initial noisy reconstructions, reducing jitters, foot skating, and severe body-scene penetrations.
 - The post-processed motions are significantly smoother, more natural, and maintain plausible interactions with the reconstructed scenes. An autoregressive sampling strategy enables continuous, long-duration sequence generation from arbitrary-length videos.

6. **Efficiency and Robustness to Keyframe Selection:**
 - SceneMI is computationally efficient during inference, obtaining actual SMPL parameters significantly faster (39.6s) than baselines that often require additional optimization steps.
 - The method shows consistent and robust performance across various keyframe selection strategies (uniform or random intervals), highlighting its adaptability to different input densities.

In summary, SceneMI not only excels in classical motion in-betweening but also sets a new standard for robustness against real-world data imperfections and generalization to diverse scene contexts, offering a robust solution for a range of practical HSI applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SceneMI makes several significant contributions to the fields of computer graphics, animation, and artificial intelligence, with far-reaching potential impacts on various industries.

1. **Novel Problem Formulation and Solution:** SceneMI is the first work to formally define and address the problem of **scene-aware motion in-betweening**. This novel framing bridges a critical gap between high-level human-scene interaction modeling and practical, controllable motion synthesis. By reformulating HSI modeling into this more tractable task, SceneMI unlocks new avenues for research and application.

2. **Enhanced Controllability and Practicality:** The ability to generate human motions conditioned on sparse keyframes within a given 3D environment provides a crucial level of **controllability** that many previous generative HSI models lacked. This directly empowers animators, allowing for more intuitive and efficient 3D character animation workflows where environmental constraints are automatically handled. The model's robustness to varying keyframe densities further increases its practical utility.

3. **Robustness to Real-World Imperfections:** A major strength of SceneMI is its innovative approach to handling **noisy keyframes** and generalizing to diverse, real-world scene sources (e.g., phone-scanned environments, IMU-captured motions). This capability is paramount for real-world deployment, where perfect data is rare. By leveraging the inherent denoising properties of diffusion models and implementing dual scene descriptors, SceneMI moves beyond idealized lab conditions, making it applicable to messy, real-world data. This could significantly impact fields reliant on imperfect motion capture data or video-based reconstructions.

4. **Advancement in Human-Scene Interaction Modeling:** The hierarchical dual scene encoding (global voxel grid + local BPS features) is a powerful mechanism for comprehensively understanding environmental context. This strategy allows SceneMI to both navigate overall scene layouts and engage in fine-grained interactions, setting a new benchmark for how scene awareness can be effectively integrated into motion generation pipelines. The significant reduction in collisions and penetrations highlights its effectiveness.

5. **New Pipeline for Video-based HSI Reconstruction:** The demonstration of SceneMI within a complete pipeline for **3D HSI reconstruction from monocular video** is a foundational step. This application has immense potential for creating digital twins of human actions in real-world environments, relevant for AR/VR content creation, digital forensics, or even robotics by generating physically plausible interaction data. The ability to refine noisy, initially reconstructed data into smooth, natural, and physically plausible interactions is a substantial leap.

6. **Impact on Industry (e.g., Snap Inc.):** For its industrial co-author, Snap Inc., this research holds direct and profound implications. Improved HSI modeling is crucial for advanced AR experiences, more realistic virtual characters in Lenses, and sophisticated user-generated content. SceneMI's capabilities could lead to more immersive and believable interactive filters, virtual try-on features, and tools for creators within the Snapchat ecosystem. The efficient runtime also makes it suitable for integration into applications requiring rapid content generation.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on full-pose keyframes, potential for text-conditioned control, and feature-level fusion, these open doors for future research building upon SceneMI's strong foundation. Overall, SceneMI represents a significant step forward in making human motion generation more controllable, realistic, and robust for practical applications across academia and industry.
