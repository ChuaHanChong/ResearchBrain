# Hierarchical Intention-Aware Expressive Motion Generation for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at University College London developed HIAER, a hierarchical framework that enables humanoid robots to generate expressive, socially appropriate motions by interpreting human intent and affective state in real-time. The system demonstrated robust performance across various interaction scenarios, particularly in handling ambiguous social cues, on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Traditional robot motion generation methods often lack the adaptability to dynamic social contexts, leading to movements that may not convey meaning or adapt to unfolding interactions.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) typically focus on explicit, functional goal interpretation rather than implicit, affective intentions, failing to complete the "intention-to-motion" loop with socially expressive responses.
- Existing frameworks for expressive robot behavior frequently rely on demonstration-based methods or predefined templates, limiting their naturalness and adaptability in novel social situations.

## Method

- ==The HIAER== framework hierarchically integrates an Intention-Aware Module ("pi_i") built on a VLM with In-Context Learning to infer human social intent and its underlying affective Valence-Arousal (V-A) context.
- A Real-time Motion Generative Model ("pi_p") translates high-level textual motion commands from "pi_i" into kinematically feasible trajectories for the humanoid robot, conditioned by the V-A estimates to modulate expressive style.
- A Reinforcement Learning-based Whole-Body Controller ("pi_w") ensures robust, stable, and safe execution of the generated expressive motions on a physical humanoid robot, even in dynamic real-world environments.

## Results

- HIAER achieved high inference accuracy (above **80%**) and strong subjective ratings for motion selection and affective appropriateness in scenarios with clear affective cues.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness in ambiguous situations, with expert raters assigning a significantly higher subjective affective appropriateness score (**3.89** vs. **1.76** for baseline) by guiding the robot to produce safe, neutral gestures.
- The system successfully generated a diverse repertoire of socially relevant, expressive gestures on a physical Unitree G1 robot in real-time, operating with an average VLM inference latency of **2.392** seconds per cycle.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Valence-Arousal (V-A) estimates derived from the VLM are crucial for dynamically conditioning motion generation, allowing a single motion primitive to convey different social meanings based on the perceived affective state.
> - The hierarchical design, combined with a confidence-aware fallback mechanism, provides superior robustness in ambiguous social situations by defaulting to safe, neutral gestures, preventing inappropriate robot responses.
> - Multimodal input, combining vision and language, significantly enhances intention inference accuracy (0.87 compared to 0.77 for vision-only) for robust social context reasoning.
