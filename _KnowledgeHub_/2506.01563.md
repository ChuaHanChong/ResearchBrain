# Hierarchical Intention-Aware Expressive Motion Generation for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at University College London developed HIAER, a hierarchical framework that enables humanoid robots to generate expressive, socially appropriate motions by interpreting human intent and affective state in real-time. The system demonstrated robust performance across various interaction scenarios, particularly in handling ambiguous social cues, on a physical Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Traditional robot motion generation methods often lack the adaptability to dynamic social contexts, leading to movements that may not convey meaning or adapt to unfolding interactions.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) typically focus on explicit, functional goal interpretation rather than implicit, affective intentions, failing to complete the "intention-to-motion" loop with socially expressive responses.
- Existing frameworks for expressive robot behavior frequently rely on demonstration-based methods or predefined templates, limiting their naturalness and adaptability in novel social situations.

## Method

- ==The HIAER== framework hierarchically integrates an Intention-Aware Module ("pi_i") built on a VLM with In-Context Learning to infer human social intent and its underlying affective Valence-Arousal (V-A) context.
- A Real-time Motion Generative Model ("pi_p") translates high-level textual motion commands from "pi_i" into kinematically feasible trajectories for the humanoid robot, conditioned by the V-A estimates to modulate expressive style.
- A Reinforcement Learning-based Whole-Body Controller ("pi_w") ensures robust, stable, and safe execution of the generated expressive motions on a physical humanoid robot, even in dynamic real-world environments.

## Results

- HIAER achieved high inference accuracy (above **80%**) and strong subjective ratings for motion selection and affective appropriateness in scenarios with clear affective cues.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness in ambiguous situations, with expert raters assigning a significantly higher subjective affective appropriateness score (**3.89** vs. **1.76** for baseline) by guiding the robot to produce safe, neutral gestures.
- The system successfully generated a diverse repertoire of socially relevant, expressive gestures on a physical Unitree G1 robot in real-time, operating with an average VLM inference latency of **2.392** seconds per cycle.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Valence-Arousal (V-A) estimates derived from the VLM are crucial for dynamically conditioning motion generation, allowing a single motion primitive to convey different social meanings based on the perceived affective state.
> - The hierarchical design, combined with a confidence-aware fallback mechanism, provides superior robustness in ambiguous social situations by defaulting to safe, neutral gestures, preventing inappropriate robot responses.
> - Multimodal input, combining vision and language, significantly enhances intention inference accuracy (0.87 compared to 0.77 for vision-only) for robust social context reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Effective human-robot interaction (HRI) relies significantly on a robot's ability to interpret human intentions and generate appropriate, often non-verbal, responses. Traditional approaches to robot motion generation, such as rule-based or template-based methods, offer predictability but often lack the adaptability required for the dynamic and nuanced nature of real-world social contexts. While these methods have enabled robots to achieve robust locomotion and generate some expressive motions, these capabilities are frequently isolated from immediate social context or confidence-aware selection, leading to movements that may not convey meaning or adapt to unfolding interactions.

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have enabled robots to interpret high-level human intentions with increased sophistication, particularly in decomposing functional goals for tasks like locomotion planning. However, a notable gap exists in the VLM landscape, as their primary focus has typically been on explicit, functional goal interpretation rather than the implicit, affective intentions embedded within dynamic social environments. This limitation often results in VLM outputs that are confined to task decomposition, failing to complete the "intention-to-motion" loop with responses that are both functional and socially expressive.

Concurrently, research in expressive motion generation has explored data-driven methods, including retrieval-based techniques and generative models such as GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models. Diffusion-based models, like MDM and DART, have demonstrated the capacity to generate diverse, high-quality motions, leveraging large-scale motion datasets such as AMASS and HumanML3D. Despite these advances, many existing frameworks for expressive robot behavior still depend on demonstration-based methods or predefined templates, which can constrain their adaptability and naturalness in novel situations.

This research addresses these identified gaps by proposing a hierarchical framework that bridges high-level social inference with the synthesis of physically embodied, expressive robot responses. It integrates the nuanced social reasoning capabilities of VLMs, including affective context assessment, with advanced motion generation and robust whole-body control. By doing so, this work aims to enable humanoid robots to produce real-time, contextually appropriate gestures that enhance communication quality and user engagement, thereby moving beyond purely functional responses to socially intelligent interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the observation that while humanoid robots have achieved considerable progress in robust locomotion and expressive motion generation, these capabilities often lack integration with the immediate social context. Consequently, even physically sophisticated robot movements may fail to convey meaning or adapt appropriately to human interaction, hindering trust, collaboration, and user engagement. The core problem addressed is the deficit in effectively bridging nuanced social inference with the synthesis of physically embodied, socially appropriate, and expressive responses in real-time.

To address this gap, the authors established several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Hierarchical Framework for Intention-Aware Expressive Motion:** The primary objective was to design and implement a hierarchical framework, named HIAER (Hierarchical Intention-Aware Expressive Responses), that enables a robot to interpret human intentions and affective context to generate socially appropriate expressive motions. This framework aimed to integrate high-level reasoning with low-level physical execution.

2. **Integrate Intention-Aware Vision Language Models with Affective Context Inference:** A crucial objective was to leverage a Vision Language Model (VLM) with in-context learning (ICL) to infer not only the primary social intent of a human but also its underlying affective context. This inference was specifically designed to provide Valence-Arousal (V-A) estimates, which would serve as actionable parameters for adaptive decision-making regarding motion style.

3. **Guide Expressive Motion Generation using V-A Estimates:** The research aimed to demonstrate how the inferred V-A estimates could be used to condition a motion generation model to synthesize stylistically appropriate gestures in real-time. This objective focused on modulating the expressiveness of motions based on the perceived affective state of the interaction.

4. **Ensure Robust Whole-Body Execution on a Physical Humanoid:** A practical objective was to implement a reinforcement learning (RL)-based whole-body controller to ensure robust, stable, and safe execution of the generated expressive motions on a physical humanoid robot, even in dynamic real-world environments.

5. **Validate System Performance in Real-World HRI Scenarios:** The work sought to implement and validate the complete HIAER system on a physical humanoid robot through real-world Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) scenarios. This validation aimed to demonstrate the system's capability to produce low-latency, context-appropriate gestures that human evaluators perceive as socially intelligent.

In essence, the motivation was to move beyond robots that simply perform tasks or movements, towards robots that can engage in more natural and effective interactions by understanding and responding to the implicit social and emotional cues of humans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HIAER framework is a hierarchical system designed to couple high-level social reasoning with low-level expressive motion execution. It consists of three main components: an Intention-Aware Module (π_i), a Real-time Motion Generative Model (π_p), and a Whole-Body Controller (π_w).

**A. Intention-Aware and Inference Module (π_i)**
This module is the core perception and reasoning component, built upon the pre-trained Qwen2.5-VL-32B model. It processes multi-modal inputs, `X_I = (V_in, L_in)` (visual and linguistic inputs), along with conversational history `H_I/O`. The VLM's behavior is guided by an In-Context Learning (ICL) strategy, meticulously designed through a pre-prompt (`L_pre`) and a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting strategy.

1. **Output Structure and V-A Model for Expressiveness:** The module generates a structured output `O = [d, i, c, (V, A), <m>]`, which includes a scene description (`d`), the inferred human intent (`i`) with a confidence score (`c`), a Valence-Arousal (V-A) estimate (`(V, A)`) of the affective context, and a corresponding motion primitive (`<m>`). The V-A model, a psychological framework, represents emotional states along two continuous axes: Valence (pleasure-displeasure) and Arousal (activation-deactivation). These V-A estimates are crucial for modulating the expressive style of the selected motion, allowing a single motion primitive (e.g., "wave right arm") to convey different social meanings (e.g., friendly vs. robotic).

2. **Pre-prompt Design:** The pre-prompt is composed of four key elements to structure the VLM's reasoning:
 - **Persona and Task Definition:** Establishes the robot's role as a helpful assistant and defines its multi-step analysis task.
 - **Output Format and CoT Enforcement:** Specifies a rigid output format with six fields and explicitly instructs the model to follow a CoT reasoning path for sequential generation of these fields.
 - **V-A Context Mapping:** Includes a mapping table that semantically grounds each V-A quadrant (e.g., Quadrant I for high Arousal, negative Valence) with specific numerical ranges and characteristic physical gestures (e.g., aggression for Q1, welcoming for Q2). This constrains the VLM's V-A estimates to be consistent and contextually relevant.
 - **Safety Rules:** Incorporates safety guidelines, such as defaulting to a neutral gesture for low inference confidence and prohibiting aggressive motions.

3. **Few-shot Examples:** Complementing the pre-prompt, few-shot examples provide concrete demonstrations. Each example presents a full scenario, mapping multi-modal inputs to a complete, structured output following the CoT logic. The V-A values and motion primitives for these examples were manually annotated to provide expert-labeled baselines for the VLM.

**B. Expressive Motion Generation (π_p)**
This mid-level motion planner translates the high-level textual motion command (`<m>`) from π_i into a kinematically feasible trajectory (`ˆy_t:t+n`) for the humanoid robot through a two-stage process.

1. **Human Motion Generation:** The DART model, a text-to-motion diffusion model, is employed. It was trained on large-scale human motion datasets (HumanML3D, BABEL, from AMASS). The model operates auto-regressively, conditioned on the textual motion primitive (`<m>`) and the preceding motion clip, to synthesize the next motion sequence. It runs online at 12.5 FPS with a moving-window approach (predicting 8 frames) and is initialized with a 4-frame "stand" pose for stability.

2. **Motion Representation and Retargeting:** The generated human motion sequence (`ˆy_t:t+n`) is based on the SMPL model (22-joint skeleton, 135-dimensional vector including root translation and 6D rotation for joints). This sequence is then retargeted to the 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot using a neural retargeting network. This MLP, with two hidden layers, performs a frame-wise mapping from the human pose to the robot's full kinematic state, distilling core motion intent while discarding human-specific articulations.

**C. Whole-Body Controller (π_w)**
This low-level control policy ensures robust motion execution and maintains robot balance while tracking the reference motions generated by π_p.

1. **RL-based Control:** The controller is implemented using Reinforcement Learning (RL). The policy `π_w` maps observations (`o_t` – root state, joint positions/velocities, previous action, reference trajectory) to target joint angles (`a_t`). A PD controller then actuates the motors to track these targets.

2. **Training:** Policy training is conducted in Isaac Lab, leveraging GPU-parallelized simulation with 4096 environments. The training data comprises optimization-based retargeting of AMASS motions into Unitree G1 compatible trajectories. To improve generalization, the training dataset is balanced to achieve uniform wrist workspace coverage, and all trajectories generated by `π_p` are incorporated. Curriculum learning is used, progressively increasing the termination threshold for joint tracking error. Domain randomization and a multi-objective reward function are also employed to enhance robustness (details in Table I of the paper).

The overall framework operates asynchronously, with the VLM (`π_i`) as the primary bottleneck, determining the robot's reaction time. A 3-second timeout mechanism is implemented for the VLM inference cycle to prevent stale responses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate HIAER's ability to infer intent, select appropriate motions, generate socially intelligent responses, and operate robustly in real-time.

**A. Motion Generation Capabilities**
The text-to-motion pipeline, combining `π_p` and `π_w`, demonstrated the capability to generate a diverse repertoire of socially relevant gestures on the physical Unitree G1 robot. This included a range of expressive motions such as a defensive guard stance, a greeting wave, a handshake gesture, a pointing gesture, an excited cheer, a hands-on-hips stance, a cross-armed pose, and a two-armed celebration. This variety showcases the system's capacity to synthesize motions from high-level textual prompts.

**B. Socially-Aware Response Evaluation**
The HIAER framework was quantitatively and qualitatively evaluated against a baseline system (a VLM-based end-to-end motion selector without the structured ICL-based reasoning). Evaluation was performed across 90 trials (15 per scenario) involving six distinct interaction scenarios (Aggression, Celebration, Calm Greeting, Disappointment, Neutral, Ambiguous). Three expert raters assessed:
- **Inference Accuracy (I_acc):** Objective measure of correct intent inference.
- **Motion Selection (S_select):** Subjective rating of the logical coherence of the chosen motion primitive based on inferred intent and V-A state.
- **Affective Estimation (S_affect):** Subjective rating of the overall appropriateness of the robot's motion in the interaction scenario.

**Quantitative Results:**
- **Robustness in Clear Scenarios:** HIAER consistently achieved high `I_acc` (above 80% in all scenarios) and strong `S_select` and `S_affect` scores in scenarios with clear affective cues (e.g., Celebration, Calm Greeting, Disappointment). This indicated accurate upstream inference leading to appropriate downstream responses.
- **Advantage in Ambiguity:** Crucially, HIAER demonstrated superior robustness in ambiguous situations. In the "Ambiguous" scenario (S6), despite a lower `I_acc`, the system's V-A estimation effectively captured the neutral tone. This V-A conditioning guided motion selection towards safe, neutral gestures ("stand still"), which were rated significantly more appropriate (`S_affect` of 3.89) compared to the baseline (`S_affect` of 1.76). This highlighted the effectiveness of the hierarchical design and the V-A safety net.

**Qualitative Case Studies:**
- **High-Arousal, Contrasting Valence:** In "Aggression" (S1), HIAER correctly inferred high arousal (A=0.7) with negative valence (V=-0.6), leading to a defensive "punch" stance. In "Celebration" (S2), high arousal (A=0.8) and positive valence (V=0.6) resulted in an enthusiastic "beat gesture." The robot demonstrated the ability to distinguish affective contexts and convey emotion rather than simply mimicking poses.
- **Low-Arousal Scenarios:** For "Calm Greeting" (S3), low arousal (A=0.3) prompted a calm, reciprocal wave, achieving the highest `S_affect` (4.87). In "Disappointment" (S4), mirroring a "cross arms" gesture was a deliberate choice, signaling alignment within the inferred negative context. In "Neutral" (S5), the system correctly selected "stand still," avoiding unnecessary gestures.
- **Handling Ambiguity:** In "Ambiguous" (S6), when the VLM reported low confidence for an 'Ambiguous' intent, HIAER defaulted to "stand still." This confidence-aware fallback mechanism prevented inappropriate motions and was a significant factor in its improved performance over the baseline in uncertain situations.

**C. System Performance Analysis**
- **System Latency:** HIAER operates as an asynchronous pipeline. The intention-aware module (π_i) was identified as the primary computational bottleneck, with an average latency of 2.392 seconds per inference cycle. A 3-second timeout was implemented to manage this latency. The motion planner (π_p) averaged 0.087 seconds per 8 frames, and the whole-body controller (π_w) operated at 50 Hz.
- **Occlusion Disturbance:** The system demonstrated robustness to partial occlusions (facial, body, limb), maintaining approximately 80% intent recognition accuracy. This was attributed to its use of temporal image history and contextual cues.
- **Multi-person Disturbance:** While the system could detect multiple individuals, it currently lacks the capability to disambiguate between multiple, concurrent intentions, tending to focus on the most visually salient person or action.
- **Multi-modal Input Ablation:** An ablation study showed that combining vision and language inputs achieved significantly higher intention inference accuracy (0.87) compared to vision-only (0.77) or prompt-only (0.20) modalities, highlighting the complementary strengths of multimodal data for robust social context reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HIAER framework presents a notable advancement in the field of human-robot interaction by addressing the critical gap between abstract social inference and the generation of physically embodied, expressive responses. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Social Intelligence and Appropriateness:** By integrating fine-grained reasoning on both social intent and affective context (via Valence-Arousal estimates), the framework enables robots to generate responses that are not merely functional but also socially appropriate and stylistically modulated. This capability is crucial for fostering more natural and intuitive interactions between humans and robots.

2. **Robustness in Ambiguous Situations:** A key contribution is the system's improved robustness, particularly in scenarios where human intent is ambiguous or uncertain. The confidence-aware selection and fallback mechanisms, guided by V-A estimates, allow the robot to default to safe, neutral, yet contextually logical gestures, preventing potentially inappropriate or awkward responses that could undermine trust and engagement.

3. **Hierarchical and Interpretable Design:** The hierarchical architecture, leveraging a VLM with In-Context Learning (ICL) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, provides a structured and potentially more interpretable approach to robot social cognition. This explicit reasoning pathway, which outputs intermediate steps like inferred intent and V-A estimates, allows for better understanding and debugging of the robot's decision-making process.

4. **Real-time Embodied Interaction:** The implementation and validation of the complete system on a physical humanoid robot demonstrate its capability for low-latency, real-time generation and execution of expressive gestures. This is vital for dynamic human-centric environments where timely and fluid interactions are essential. The local deployment of models also underscores its practical viability, reducing reliance on external cloud services.

5. **Foundation for Advanced HRI:** This work establishes a foundation for future research in several directions. Expanding the robot's expressive repertoire beyond upper-body motions to whole-body movements and subtle locomotion will further enrich non-verbal communication. Furthermore, the emphasis on formal user studies to evaluate human perception, trust, and engagement signifies a commitment to understanding the downstream impact of such advanced HRI capabilities.

In summary, HIAER represents a step towards robots that can not only perform tasks but also understand and respond to the emotional and social nuances of human interaction, thereby facilitating more effective collaboration, enhancing user experience, and potentially broadening the societal acceptance and integration of humanoid robots.
