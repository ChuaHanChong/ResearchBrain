# HGeo-TopoMap: Boosting Topological Mapping with Hierarchical Geometric Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> HGeo-TopoMap enhances topological mapping for autonomous driving by integrating hierarchical geometric priors, combining explicit BEV road structure maps with implicit geometric relationships of centerlines. This approach improves the accuracy and robustness of centerline detection and topological reasoning, particularly in complex road scenarios.

## Problem

- Road centerlines often lack explicit visual cues in real-world images, making their accurate detection difficult for autonomous driving systems.
- This absence of distinct visual features leads to performance degradation in topological mapping, especially in complex and unconstrained driving environments.
- Existing methods for topological mapping struggle with reliable centerline detection, which is crucial for downstream tasks like path planning.

## Method

- Introduces **HGeo-TopoMap**, an architecture that integrates hierarchical geometric priors into an online topological mapping framework, building upon the ==TopoLogic== baseline.
- Employs a ==Geometric Adaptive Learning (GAL)== module to leverage explicit priors from potentially noisy Bird's Eye View (BEV) road structure maps derived via ==Inverse Perspective Mapping (IPM)==.
- Utilizes a ==Geometric Consistency Learning (GCL)== module to exploit implicit geometric regularities and spatial relationships among centerlines, such as straightness, curvature, and parallelism, for robust instance modeling.

## Results

- Achieved an overall topological map accuracy (OLS) of **45.5%**, a **+2.0%** improvement over the TopoLogic baseline on the OpenLane-V2 centerline benchmark.
- Demonstrated a mean Average Precision (mAP) of **34.0%**, marking a **+5.7%** improvement over the LaneSegNet baseline on the OpenLane-V2 lane segment benchmark.
- Exhibited significantly better robustness under missing camera view scenarios, with up to a **7.5%** improvement in centerline detection accuracy (DETl) over the baseline when a view was absent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit road structure information, even if noisy due to IPM distortions, can be effectively leveraged through a prior-masked attention mechanism that adaptively focuses on informative regions.
> - Implicit geometric properties and relationships of centerlines (e.g., straightness, parallelism) provide valuable prior knowledge that can guide instance modeling and enforce spatial consistency when visual cues are sparse.
> - Combining both explicit (GAL) and implicit (GCL) geometric priors in a hierarchical manner leads to synergistic improvements in centerline detection accuracy and topological reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems rely on robust perception to generate various map representations for downstream tasks such as path planning and obstacle avoidance. Among these, topological maps are particularly relevant for path planning as they describe road structures, including centerlines and traffic signs, along with their connectivity relationships. The construction of these maps in a Bird’s Eye View (BEV) is a common approach in online mapping, offering cost-effectiveness and adaptability to dynamic environments.

Prior research in topological mapping has explored various methodologies. Approaches like TopoNet and TopoLogic have utilized DETR architectures and graph neural networks to detect instances and infer topological relationships. Some methods, such as TopoLogic, have incorporated geometric distances of centerlines as priors to enhance relationship learning, while LaneSegNet introduced auxiliary lane detection to improve accuracy. However, a persistent challenge in this field is the reliable detection of centerlines. Unlike lane boundaries or other road markings, centerlines often lack explicit visual cues in real-world images, leading to performance degradation in complex driving scenarios.

The broader research landscape for autonomous driving also includes the integration of various forms of "priors" to compensate for sparse 2D visual data. This includes High-Definition (HD) maps, standard definition maps, temporal cues, driving paths, and aerial images. Specifically, several works, such as MGMap and PercMap, have leveraged perspective road semantics and geometry to boost vectorized map decoding. Topo2D and LaneSegNet also utilized 2D road structure information, often through auxiliary learning tasks.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the centerline detection problem through a novel integration of hierarchical geometric priors. It builds upon existing topological mapping architectures, specifically TopoLogic, by proposing methods to explicitly and implicitly leverage geometric information. Instead of relying solely on distance relations or auxiliary tasks requiring extra learning, HGeo-TopoMap aims to harness both the information from easily obtainable perspective road structure maps and the intrinsic geometric properties of centerlines themselves. This approach seeks to provide a more direct and efficient solution to the centerline visibility issue, thereby improving the overall quality and robustness of topological maps generated for autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of topological mapping for autonomous driving by addressing a critical challenge: the lack of explicit visual cues for road centerlines in real-world environments. This absence often makes centerline instances difficult to detect accurately, leading to performance degradation in complex and unconstrained driving scenes.

The motivation for proposing HGeo-TopoMap stems from two main observations regarding geometric information relevant to centerlines:

1. **Explicit Geometric Priors from Road Structure Maps:** Centerlines are not visually distinctive themselves, but they are often situated between clear boundaries or related to other distinct road structures (e.g., lane dividers, stop lines). Semantic segmentation models for perspective road maps (e.g., Mask2Former) have achieved high performance in identifying these structures. While a view discrepancy exists between perspective images and the Bird’s Eye View (BEV) required for topological maps, Inverse Perspective Mapping (IPM) can bridge this gap to provide an initial BEV road structure map. However, this IPM-derived map can be noisy due to boundary ambiguity in segmentation and distortions from the flat-ground assumption. The motivation is to efficiently leverage this "noisy" but available explicit prior by developing a module that can adaptively learn from and select informative regions within this road structure map.

2. **Implicit Geometric Priors from Centerline Properties:** Topological maps inherently contain geometric regularities related to centerlines. Centerlines represent vehicle trajectories, which typically exhibit distinct geometric properties, such as being rectilinear (straight) or curvilinear (turning). Furthermore, centerlines of adjacent lanes often maintain parallel relationships, while centerlines and stop lines are often perpendicular. These intrinsic geometric attributes and relationships among centerlines provide meaningful implicit priors. The motivation is to exploit these inherent geometric properties to guide instance modeling and enforce spatial consistency, thereby complementing the explicit priors and compensating for missing visual features.

By comprehensively exploiting these multi-level geometric priors – explicit information from adaptively processed road structure maps and implicit spatial relations derived from centerline geometry – the research aims to guide instance modeling in topological mapping, specifically for centerline detection. This is intended to improve both the accuracy of detecting individual centerline instances and the overall topological relationships, ultimately contributing to more reliable and robust autonomous driving perception systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HGeo-TopoMap is designed to integrate hierarchical geometric priors into a topological mapping framework. The methodology builds upon an existing baseline architecture, TopoLogic, chosen for its balance of accuracy and computational efficiency.

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The method first defines two key inputs:
1. **Perspective Road Maps:** Generated by a semantic segmentation model (Mask2Former) from multi-view perspective images, yielding a one-hot perspective road map (Mapp).
2. **Inverse Perspective Mapping (IPM):** To bridge the view discrepancy between perspective images and the BEV space of topological maps, IPM is applied. Assuming a flat road surface, IPM transforms the perspective road maps into a BEV road structure map (Map_bev) by fusing information from multiple camera views.

**B. Overall Architecture:**
The HGeo-TopoMap architecture consists of:
- **Image Encoder:** Extracts multi-scale features (Fi) from multi-view images (Ii).
- **View Transformer (BEVFormer):** Projects image features from perspective view to BEV features (F_bev).
- **Traffic Decoder:** A dedicated task head (Deformable DETR) for detecting traffic sign instances.
- **Map Instance Decoder:** Extracts centerline instances from BEV features (F_bev) using a DETR architecture with instance queries (Ql).
- **Topo Head:** A graph convolutional network integrated into the forward feedback network to capture topological connections between centerline queries (Ql) and traffic queries (Qt).
- **Multi-task Heads:** Predict the topology matrix, centerline categories, and centerline points.

Crucially, the proposed method integrates two complementary geometric learning components to exploit multi-level priors: a Geometric Adaptive Learning (GAL) module and a Geometric Consistency Learning (GCL) module.

**C. Geometric Adaptive Learning (GAL) Module:**
This module leverages explicit priors from the BEV road structure map (Map_bev) to compensate for the lack of explicit visual cues for centerlines. It addresses the inherent noise and distortions in IPM-derived maps.
1. **Multi-Category Encoder:** Occupied grids from the BEV road structure map (Map_bev) are sampled to extract occupancy states (Mo) and spatial locations (Mp). A multi-category encoder (consisting of linear layers) generates semantic features (Map_emb) from Mo, and a cosine-based positional encoder (Sinpos) generates positional features (Map_pos) from Mp. This module learns discriminative features for different road structure categories.
2. **Prior-Masked Attention Mechanism:** This mechanism guides the interaction between centerline instance queries (Ql) and the road structure map features (Map_emb + Map_pos). It computes Euclidean distances (Dm) between the 2D vector points of each centerline instance (P’I) and the occupied grid positions (Mp). The distance mask, Dm, is then used to selectively focus attention on informative regions of the road structure map, effectively embedding the prior information into instance decoding. The attention mask is defined as -Dm/k, where k is a scaling factor.

**D. Geometric Consistency Learning (GCL) Module:**
This module exploits the implicit geometric regularities and spatial relationships inherent in topological maps.
1. **Geometry-Aware Decoder:** The standard centerline vector point regression is enhanced. Instead of predicting instance points from a single reference point, a stage-wise regression strategy is employed:
 - **Center Point Regression:** In each decoding layer, the regression first learns from a single reference point (Pr) to the instance center point (Pc) using a task head (CenRegHead).
 - **Instance Point Regression:** An instance point head (InsRegHead) then learns geometric attributes for instance points (PI) based on these center points (Pc).
 - **Geometry-Aware Attention:** Positional encodings of the instance center points (Sinpos(Pc)) are embedded into the map instance queries (Q’il = Qil + Sinpos(Pc)) before they interact with BEV features via cross-attention. This facilitates rapid geometry learning by allowing each centerline instance to regress points from its center and acquire geometric attributes concurrently.

2. **Geometric Contrastive Learning:** This component leverages prior relationships among centerline instances to guide consistent learning.
 - **Geometric Grouping:** Centerline instances are classified into straight and curved groups (Gs, Gc) based on curvature. Straight lines are further clustered into two subsets (Gl1, Gl2) based on slope, forming a set of groups G = {Gl1, Gl2, Gc}.
 - **Sample Selection:** Instance queries (qi) correctly matched to ground truth within each subgroup are designated as anchors ('v'). Positive samples (Spos) are high-scoring instances within the same group, and negative samples (Sneg) are high-scoring instances from other groups (Gother) with distinct geometric features. Low-scoring instances are discarded to prevent misleading guidance.
 - **Contrastive Loss:** A lightweight contrastive learning head extracts spatial orientation features for instance queries. The InfoNCE loss (Lgcl) is then applied concurrently over all subgroups. This loss encourages features of instances with similar geometric properties and spatial orientations to be close, while pushing apart those with distinct properties.

**E. Overall Loss Function:**
The total loss function combines standard topological mapping losses (traffic detection loss Ldet, centerline instance detection loss Ll, and topology reasoning loss Ltopo) with the newly introduced instance center point loss (Lc) and the geometric consistency learning loss (Lgcl).
L = Ldet + Ll + Ltopo + α \* Lc + β \* Lgcl, where α and β are balancing weights.

**F. Implementation Details:**
The method uses a perceptual range of 102.4x51.2 meters, with a BEV feature map size of 200x100. Perspective road maps (388x512) are generated by Mask2Former pretrained on Mapillary Vistas. The effective range of IPM is constrained to 60x30 meters. Specific weight factors (k=0.1 for GAL, α=1, β=0.1 for loss) are used. The model is trained for 24 epochs on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs with a batch size of 4.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HGeo-TopoMap method was evaluated on the OpenLane-V2 dataset across centerline and lane segment benchmarks, as well as robustness tests.

**A. Centerline Benchmark of Topological Mapping:**
- **Performance against TopoLogic (baseline):** HGeo-TopoMap achieved an overall topological map accuracy (OLS) of 45.5%, representing a +2.0% improvement over the TopoLogic baseline (43.5%).
- **Specific Metrics:** Centerline instance detection accuracy (DETl) improved by +1.6% (from 29.7% to 31.3%). Topological reasoning accuracy for lane-lane (TOPll) and lane-traffic sign (TOPlt) also saw gains, with TOPll increasing by +1.2% (from 25.0% to 26.2%) and TOPlt increasing by +0.8% (from 25.5% to 26.3%). Traffic sign detection (DETt) showed an increase from 43.6% to 48.2%.

**B. Lane Segment Benchmark of Topological Mapping:**
- **Performance against LaneSegNet (baseline):** HGeo-TopoMap achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 34.0%, which is a +5.7% improvement over the LaneSegNet baseline (28.3%).
- **Specific Metrics:** Average Precision for lane segments (APls) improved by +4.6% (from 27.7% to 32.3%), and for pedestrian detection (APped) by +6.9% (from 28.8% to 35.7%). Inter-lane topology reasoning (TOPlsls) improved by +2.5% (from 20.1% to 22.6%).

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Core Modules (GAL and GCL):** Both modules contributed positively. GCL alone improved DETl by +0.9% and OLS by +0.8% over the baseline. GAL alone improved DETl by +0.7% and OLS by +1.2% over the baseline. When both GCL and GAL were combined, the improvements were cumulative, leading to the reported overall gains.
- **Weight of Geometric Consistency Learning Loss:** An optimal OLS metric of 44.6% was observed with a weight of 0.01 for the GCL loss, compared to 44.3% with a weight of 0.1. The 0.1 weight achieved a more balanced performance in topological reasoning despite a slight decrease in traffic sign accuracy.
- **Position of Geometric Adaptive Learning:** The optimal insertion layer for the GAL module varied between baselines. For the centerline baseline, early embedding (layer 1) performed best (31.3% DETl, 45.5% OLS). For the lane segment baseline, an intermediate layer (layer 3) yielded optimal results (33.7% mAP). This difference was attributed to their distinct decoding paradigms.
- **Components of GCL (GAD and CL):** Both the geometry-aware decoding (GAD) and contrastive learning (CL) components within GCL were effective. CL alone contributed a 0.6% gain in the OLS metric.
- **Prior Effect on Vectorized Mapping:** When the GAL module was integrated into StreamMapNet, a baseline for vectorized mapping, it resulted in a significant +14.5% improvement in overall map accuracy (mAP), highlighting the broad applicability of the BEV road structure map guidance.

**D. Visual Results:**
Qualitative comparisons demonstrated that HGeo-TopoMap achieved more detailed mapping capabilities. The method more accurately identified centerline structures in complex scenarios like multi-lane merges and showed more precise reconstruction of road elements, such as pedestrians, which was attributed to the prior map guidance.

**E. Robustness Analysis (Missing Views):**
Under various missing-view scenarios (simulating sensor damage), HGeo-TopoMap exhibited significantly better robustness compared to the baseline. While the absence of front-view cameras caused the most substantial performance degradation, the proposed method consistently maintained higher performance. For example, under the rear-left view missing setting, it achieved a 7.5% improvement in centerline detection accuracy (DETl) over the baseline.

In summary, the research demonstrates that the proposed hierarchical geometric priors effectively guide centerline instance detection and strengthen topological relation reasoning, leading to consistent and significant improvements across accuracy benchmarks and under challenging robustness conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HGeo-TopoMap research introduces a hierarchical geometric prior module designed to enhance topological mapping in autonomous driving systems. This work addresses a critical limitation in current online mapping: the difficulty of detecting road centerlines due to their lack of explicit visual markings in real-world environments. By leveraging both explicit road structure maps and implicit geometric relationships of centerlines, the proposed method offers a comprehensive solution to this problem.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

1. **Improved Topological Map Accuracy:** The consistent and substantial improvements demonstrated across multiple OpenLane-V2 benchmarks (e.g., +2.0% OLS on centerline, +5.7% mAP on lane segment) indicate a measurable advance in the quality of online topological maps. Higher accuracy in centerline detection and topological reasoning is fundamental for reliable path planning and decision-making in autonomous vehicles.
2. **Enhanced Robustness:** The robustness analysis under missing-view conditions highlights the method's ability to maintain performance even in challenging scenarios caused by sensor damage. This is a crucial aspect for the safe and practical deployment of autonomous systems, as real-world driving often involves unpredictable environmental factors.
3. **Novel Integration of Priors:** The introduction of a hierarchical geometric prior framework, encompassing both Geometric Adaptive Learning (GAL) for explicit map priors and Geometric Consistency Learning (GCL) for implicit geometric relationships, represents a novel approach to leveraging multi-level contextual information. The modular design allows for flexible integration into existing topological mapping baselines.
4. **Addressing a Core Challenge:** By specifically tackling the issue of indistinct centerline features, the research contributes to overcoming a long-standing obstacle in the field of autonomous driving perception. The ability to accurately identify centerlines, particularly in complex scenarios like multi-lane merges, directly impacts vehicle safety.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The ablation study demonstrating significant improvements in a vectorized mapping task (StreamMapNet) suggests that the principles behind the Geometric Adaptive Learning module, particularly the use of BEV road structure map guidance, could be broadly applicable to other online mapping tasks beyond topological maps.

The potential impact of HGeo-TopoMap includes contributing to the development of more reliable and safer autonomous driving systems by providing high-quality, robust topological maps. The enhanced precision in complex road scenarios (e.g., multi-lane merges) can directly improve the navigational capabilities of self-driving cars, reducing collision risks in high-risk zones. Furthermore, the methodology could inspire future research into integrating diverse, multi-level priors more effectively into various autonomous perception tasks.

The authors also outline future work, which involves exploring temporal cues and optimization algorithms to generate even more optimal road structure maps, aiming to address persistent information deficiencies in single-frame maps under highly complex scenarios. This suggests a continued commitment to enhancing the robustness and completeness of online mapping solutions.
