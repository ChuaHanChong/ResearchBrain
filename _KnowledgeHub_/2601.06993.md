# Can Textual Reasoning Improve the Performance of MLLMs on Fine-grained Visual Classification?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Michigan State University empirically identified the 'Cost of Thinking,' where excessive textual reasoning degrades Multi-modal Large Language Model performance on fine-grained visual classification. They developed ReFine-RFT, a reinforcement fine-tuning framework that achieves state-of-the-art results on FGVC benchmarks by constraining reasoning length and using a multi-perspective reward system.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained visual classification (FGVC), a task requiring discrimination of subtle visual differences.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, while effective for complex linguistic tasks, paradoxically degrades MLLM performance on certain visual perception tasks.
- The root cause of this performance degradation from textual reasoning in visual contexts remained undiagnosed across diverse evaluation and training paradigms.

## Method

- A novel ==Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) framework, ReFine-RFT==, was developed to constrain reasoning length and provide dense, accuracy-oriented feedback for FGVC.
- ReFine-RFT incorporates an ==Ensemble Reward system== with five components: format, classification, a novel thinking length reward, MLLM-based accuracy reward, and embedding similarity reward.
- ==Multi-reward Normalization (MRN)== is introduced to independently normalize each heterogeneous reward component, ensuring stable and balanced optimization.

## Results

- ReFine-RFT achieved an average accuracy of **86.5%** across FGVC benchmarks, outperforming Visual-RFT (**82.9%**) and even larger 8B models like Finedefics-8B (**82.7%**) with a 2B backbone and 4-shot training.
- The diagnostic experiments showed a **3-6%** average accuracy drop in non-reasoning MLLMs when moving from 'Answer-only' to 'Chain-of-Thought' prompts without explicit training.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of Multi-reward Normalization (MRN), which consistently improved performance by **0.**7%** to 2.1%** across datasets, and demonstrated the synergistic benefits of the ensemble reward components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Excessive textual reasoning, termed the 'Cost of Thinking,' consistently degrades MLLM performance on fine-grained visual perception tasks, both in zero-shot settings and with supervised fine-tuning.
> - During RFT for visual accuracy, MLLMs exhibit 'reasoning collapse,' where they learn to suppress verbose reasoning and stabilize at significantly shorter lengths.
> - Explicitly controlling reasoning length is crucial: shorter, more concise reasoning traces lead to higher classification accuracy compared to longer, more elaborate ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated significant advancements in general vision-language understanding, facilitating natural interaction between images and text. These models are increasingly utilized as integrated interfaces for various artificial intelligence tasks, including both perception and reasoning. A prevalent strategy for enhancing the performance of large language models (LLMs) on complex tasks such as mathematics and coding involves eliciting Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, where the model generates intermediate thought steps before providing a final answer.

However, the application of CoT reasoning to visual perception tasks presents a challenge. Prior research has indicated that explicit textual reasoning can paradoxically degrade performance on such tasks compared to direct predictions. These earlier studies, however, often explored the issue from narrow perspectives, either focusing solely on zero-shot evaluations or conducting coarse comparisons between CoT and answer-only predictions. This left an open question regarding the underlying reasons for CoT's performance degradation in perception-heavy scenarios.

Fine-grained Visual Classification (FGVC) is a core perception task that demands the discrimination of subordinate-level categories based on subtle visual differences (e.g., specific car models, plant varieties). This capability is crucial for advanced perception-centric applications, including object-centric visual question answering, and serves as a benchmark for assessing MLLMs' visual grounding and fine-grained feature extraction abilities in demanding domains like ecology or medical imaging. Despite architectural sophistication, current MLLMs often exhibit limitations in FGVC, particularly in few-shot and open-ended contexts where data scarcity is a factor.

This research systematically re-examines the role of CoT in FGVC across various evaluation and training paradigms. It aims to address the observed performance degradation and determine whether textual reasoning is inherently detrimental to fine-grained visual perception or if current methods employ it suboptimally. By investigating this phenomenon, the work contributes to understanding the interplay between linguistic reasoning and visual perception in MLLMs and develops methods to optimize their performance on visually demanding tasks. It builds upon and contrasts with existing reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) methods like Visual-RFT, which integrate CoT, and No-Thinking-RFT, which suggests that visual tasks may not require explicit reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate the impact of textual reasoning, specifically Chain-of-Thought (CoT), on the performance of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC) tasks. This investigation is driven by a primary research question: "Is textual reasoning detrimental to fine-grained visual perception, or do current methods simply employ it in a suboptimal way?"

The motivation for this detailed inquiry arises from several observations and challenges in the field:

1. **MLLM Limitations in FGVC:** Despite exhibiting strong general vision-language understanding capabilities, existing MLLMs demonstrate clear limitations in FGVC. These models frequently fail to capture the subtle visual distinctions required to differentiate fine-grained categories, especially in scenarios with limited training data (e.g., few-shot or open-ended settings). This deficiency hinders their applicability as reliable assistants in visually demanding real-world domains such as medical imaging, industrial inspection, or ecology.
2. **Paradoxical CoT Performance in Visual Tasks:** While Chain-of-Thought reasoning has shown efficacy in enhancing performance on complex reasoning tasks (e.g., math, coding) for Large Language Models (LLMs), several prior studies have reported that explicit textual reasoning can unexpectedly reduce accuracy in visual perception tasks. These findings present a paradox, as one might intuitively expect additional reasoning steps to aid complex visual discrimination.
3. **Incomplete Understanding of CoT Degradation:** Existing studies that observed CoT degradation in visual tasks have often examined the issue from limited perspectives, such as focusing exclusively on zero-shot evaluation or conducting broad comparisons between CoT and answer-only prompts. These approaches did not provide a systematic or in-depth analysis of *why* CoT might degrade performance on perception-heavy tasks. This research aims to fill this knowledge gap by systematically re-examining CoT's role through broader evaluation settings and various training paradigms.
4. **Assessing Visual Grounding and Feature Extraction:** A comprehensive study of CoT in the context of FGVC directly probes the fundamental visual grounding and fine-grained feature extraction abilities of MLLMs. Understanding how reasoning interacts with these core perceptual capabilities is essential for developing trustworthy AI systems that can reliably operate in domains requiring precise visual analysis.
5. **Optimizing MLLM Performance:** The ultimate motivation is to develop methods that enable MLLMs to achieve superior performance on FGVC. If current CoT implementations are suboptimal, identifying the underlying issues can lead to new strategies for effective integration of reasoning that genuinely enhances visual perception. This includes investigating the dynamics of reasoning during training and designing reward mechanisms that align with visual accuracy.

Through this comprehensive investigation, the authors aim to characterize the specific factors contributing to CoT's impact on FGVC, leading to the identification of the "Cost of Thinking" phenomenon, and subsequently propose a novel framework to mitigate this cost and improve MLLM performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic approach to investigate the role of textual reasoning in Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC), encompassing both diagnostic experiments and the development of a novel framework.

**Diagnostic Experiments:**
The study first conducts a comprehensive re-examination of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning's impact on FGVC across different settings:
1. **Zero-shot Evaluation:** Multiple open-source MLLMs (Qwen2/2.5-VL series, InternVL series, R1-OneVision) are evaluated using two prompting strategies: "Answer-only" and "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT). This aims to assess baseline performance degradation without explicit fine-tuning for reasoning.
2. **Reasoning Dynamics in Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):** The research utilizes an RFT framework, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), with a base model (Qwen2-VL-2B). It tracks the evolution of reasoning length (number of tokens in the `<think>` tag) throughout the RFT process when optimizing for a combination of format and classification rewards. This observes emergent behaviors of reasoning generation.
3. **Explicit Manipulation of Thinking Length:** To quantify the direct impact of reasoning length, the RFT framework is modified to include a "thinking length reward" ($R_{len}$). This binary reward encourages the model to generate reasoning content within specified length bounds ($L_{min}, L_{max}$), allowing for controlled experiments to observe the correlation between reasoning length and classification accuracy.
4. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with High-Quality CoT:** To differentiate between the *quality* of CoT and its *length*, the authors perform SFT using high-quality CoT data generated by GPT-4o. This involves comparing SFT with Answer-Only prompts (SFT-AO) against SFT with CoT data (SFT-CoT).

**Identification of "Cost of Thinking":**
Based on these diagnostic experiments, the research identifies a phenomenon termed the "Cost of Thinking," which posits that longer textual reasoning consistently correlates with lower classification accuracy in FGVC.

**Proposed Framework: ReFine-RFT:**
Motivated by the "Cost of Thinking" finding, the paper proposes **ReFine-RFT**, a novel RFT framework designed to constrain reasoning length while providing dense, accuracy-oriented feedback. ReFine-RFT integrates two key technical innovations:

1. **Ensemble Reward:** This reward mechanism combines multiple signals to provide comprehensive and accuracy-centric feedback:
 - **Format Reward ($R_f$):** A binary reward ensuring adherence to the specified output structure (`<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>`).
 - **Classification Reward ($R_{cls}$):** A binary reward for exact string matching between the predicted answer (from `<answer>` tags) and the ground-truth label.
 - **Thinking Length Reward ($R_{len}$):** A binary reward that encourages reasoning content length to fall within predefined bounds ($L_{min}, L_{max}$). In ReFine-RFT, $L_{min}=0, L_{max}=10$ are set to promote concise reasoning.
 - **MLLM-based Accuracy Reward ($R_{mllm}$):** To provide a smoother and semantically aware signal than binary $R_{cls}$, an MLLM (Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct) is used as a teacher model. It grades the semantic alignment between predicted and ground-truth answers on a scale of 0-10, normalized to.
 - **Embedding Similarity Reward ($R_{emb}$):** This reward measures the cosine similarity between text embeddings of the predicted and ground-truth answers, using a pretrained text embedding model (E5). This provides a continuous and differentiable measure of semantic closeness.

2. **Multi-reward Normalization (MRN):** This is a plug-and-play module designed to stabilize training when optimizing across heterogeneous reward signals. Instead of aggregating all rewards into a single scalar value *before* normalization (as in original GRPO, which can lead to dominant rewards), MRN performs group normalization independently for each reward component.
 - For each of the $K$ reward types for $G$ responses, the advantage $A_i^{(k)}$ is computed by normalizing $r_i^{(k)}$ using the mean and standard deviation specific to that reward component across the group of responses.
 - The final advantage $A_i$ for a response $o_i$ is then obtained by summing these independently normalized advantages: $A_i = \sum_{k=1}^K A_i^{(k)}$. This approach ensures that all reward components contribute comparably to the optimization process, preventing any single reward from saturating and diluting the influence of others.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct.
- **Datasets:** Four widely adopted FGVC benchmarks are used: FGVC-Aircraft, Stanford-Cars, Flowers-102, and Oxford-Pets, utilizing a 4-shot training dataset.
- **Training:** LoRA fine-tuning (rank $\gamma=64$, scaling factor $\alpha=128$) is applied to specific modules (q\_proj, k\_proj, v\_proj, o\_proj, gate\_proj, up\_proj, down\_proj) using 4 NVIDIA H100 GPUs.
- **GRPO Parameters:** Number of generations G=8, KL penalty coefficient $\beta=0.04$. Maximum 200 training steps.
- **Reward Models:** Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct for MLLM-based accuracy reward, and E5 for embedding similarity reward.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the interaction of textual reasoning and fine-grained visual classification, culminating in the development and evaluation of the ReFine-RFT framework.

**1. Performance Degradation of CoT in Zero-Shot Settings:**
In zero-shot evaluations, incorporating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting consistently led to a degradation in accuracy across all tested FGVC datasets. Non-reasoning MLLMs experienced an average performance drop of 3–6% when switching from "Answer-only" to CoT prompts. Even the reasoning-oriented model R1-OneVision achieved only moderate accuracy with CoT. This indicates that without specific training for visual reasoning chains, CoT prompts frequently induce superfluous or erroneous explanations rather than improving decision quality.

**2. Reasoning Collapse in Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):**
When MLLMs were fine-tuned using RFT, the average reasoning length of their outputs exhibited a consistent downward trend across all FGVC datasets. In the initial stages of RFT, models produced verbose responses, but as training progressed, the reasoning content length rapidly decreased and stabilized at a more compact range. The final reasoning lengths after RFT were notably shorter than those observed in the zero-shot setting. This phenomenon, termed "reasoning collapse," suggests that RFT implicitly discourages extensive reasoning for visual perception tasks, instead optimizing for concise, confident answer predictions. The model learns to suppress reasoning steps that do not contribute to reward maximization.

**3. Direct Impact of Thinking Length on FGVC Performance:**
Experiments that explicitly manipulated reasoning length during RFT revealed an inverse correlation between reasoning length and classification accuracy. Enforcing longer reasoning sequences resulted in a clear decline in accuracy across all FGVC datasets. Conversely, shorter reasoning traces yielded higher accuracy. This finding indicates that for fine-grained visual perception, excessive textual reasoning can actively degrade performance by potentially introducing noise or distracting the model from critical visual cues.

**4. SFT Results: Answer-only Surpasses CoT Even with High-Quality Data:**
Even when Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) was performed using high-quality CoT data generated by GPT-4o, models trained with "Answer-only" prompts (SFT-AO) consistently outperformed those trained with CoT data (SFT-CoT). This suggests that the observed performance degradation is not merely due to the low quality of self-generated CoT but rather an inherent "Cost of Thinking" associated with excessive textual reasoning in FGVC, affecting both inference and training.

**5. Finding 1: The "Cost of Thinking":**
The cumulative evidence from zero-shot, RFT dynamics, explicit length manipulation, and SFT experiments led to the key finding: for fine-grained visual tasks, thinking length is a critical factor. Excessive reasoning negatively impacts performance, and MLLMs benefit more from concise responses than from elaborate reasoning.

**6. ReFine-RFT Performance:**
The proposed ReFine-RFT framework achieved state-of-the-art performance across multiple FGVC benchmarks, demonstrating consistent gains over baseline methods.
- **LoRA vs. Fully-FT:** LoRA fine-tuning consistently surpassed fully fine-tuning in both SFT and RFT settings, indicating its efficiency and effectiveness with limited fine-grained visual data.
- **RFT Gains:** RFT generally yielded substantial accuracy improvements over SFT, as it directly optimizes accuracy-centric objectives and corrects undesirable generation behaviors.
- **Superiority of ReFine-RFT:** ReFine-RFT, with its ensemble of semantically-aware rewards and Multi-Reward Normalization (MRN), consistently improved performance. ReFine-RFT, using a 2B backbone and 4-shot training, significantly surpassed Finedefics-8B, which was trained on full FGVC datasets.
- **Reasoning Control vs. Prompt Style:** Controlling reasoning length, as implemented in ReFine-RFT, had a greater impact on performance than the specific prompt style (CoT vs. Answer-only). This supports the "Cost of Thinking" analysis, indicating that suppressing excessive reasoning encourages models to prioritize accuracy.

**7. Ablation Studies:**
- **Effects of MRN:** Integrating MRN consistently improved performance (up to +2.1%) across FGVC datasets under both fully fine-tuning and LoRA regimes. This confirms MRN's effectiveness in stabilizing optimization by balancing heterogeneous reward signals. Reward distribution analysis showed MRN achieved higher average reward values and maintained lower standard deviation compared to baseline GRPO, indicating improved stability.
- **Effects of Ensemble Reward:** Each component of the ensemble reward (format, classification, thinking length, MLLM-based accuracy, embedding similarity) contributed to performance gains. The combination of all reward functions yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating that aggregating complementary, accuracy-centric signals provides richer and more stable guidance. Reward correlation analysis indicated these rewards were aligned yet distinct, offering complementary learning signals.
- **Effects of Trainable Parameters:** Increasing LoRA capacity (e.g., r=64, α=128) progressively improved accuracy, eventually surpassing the Fully-FT baseline, highlighting the importance of appropriate LoRA configuration for few-shot scenarios.

**8. Qualitative Results:**
Qualitative comparisons showed ReFine-RFT producing more concise and accurate responses compared to SFT-CoT and Visual-RFT, which often generated longer, incorrect, or less relevant reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in several ways, with implications for both theoretical understanding and practical applications.

**Theoretical Significance:**
The paper empirically characterizes the "Cost of Thinking" phenomenon within Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC). This finding challenges the general assumption that more explicit textual reasoning uniformly improves performance in complex tasks. By demonstrating that excessive reasoning degrades MLLM performance on perception-centric tasks, the research provides a critical insight into the interplay between linguistic processing and visual understanding in these models. It suggests that for tasks heavily reliant on subtle visual cues, concise or task-focused reasoning is more beneficial than elaborate, verbose Chain-of-Thought (CoT). This contributes to a nuanced understanding of when and how textual reasoning should be applied in multimodal contexts.

**Methodological Significance:**
The introduction of Multi-reward Normalization (MRN) is a significant methodological contribution. As a plug-and-play module, MRN addresses the challenge of optimizing across heterogeneous reward signals in multi-objective reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) settings. By independently normalizing each reward component before aggregation, MRN stabilizes training, prevents dominant rewards from diluting the influence of others, and leads to more robust and efficient policy optimization. This technique has broader applicability beyond FGVC, potentially improving other RFT pipelines that utilize multiple, diverse reward functions.

**Practical Impact:**
The proposed ReFine-RFT framework represents a substantial practical advancement in enhancing MLLM capabilities for fine-grained visual understanding. By effectively constraining reasoning length and utilizing an ensemble of accuracy-centric rewards, ReFine-RFT achieves state-of-the-art performance on FGVC benchmarks. This is particularly notable given that it surpasses models with larger backbones trained on more data, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness in few-shot learning scenarios. The practical implications include:
- **Improved Accuracy:** ReFine-RFT's superior performance on FGVC means MLLMs can more reliably discriminate between visually similar categories, which is essential for numerous real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Trustworthiness:** By encouraging concise yet accurate responses and reducing the generation of hallucinated or spurious reasoning, ReFine-RFT promotes transparency and trustworthiness in MLLM outputs. This is crucial for deploying AI systems in high-stakes domains such as medical diagnostics, industrial inspection, biodiversity monitoring, and scientific research, where precise categorization and reliable decision-making are paramount.
- **Efficiency:** Constraining reasoning length can lead to more token-efficient models, reducing computational costs and inference latency, which is valuable for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

**Future Directions:**
The research opens avenues for future work, including deeper investigations into the underlying mechanisms driving the "Cost of Thinking" and extending the ReFine-RFT framework and its analyses to a broader range of multimodal tasks beyond FGVC, such as object detection, visual grounding, or more open-ended vision-language reasoning challenges. This work lays a foundation for developing MLLMs that are not only capable but also efficient, reliable, and better aligned with the specific demands of visual perception tasks.
