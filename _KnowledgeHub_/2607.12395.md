# Ring-Zero: Scaling Zero RL to a Trillion Parameters for Emergent Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work scales zero Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to a trillion-parameter language model, demonstrating that massive computational scale can autonomously elicit advanced reasoning behaviors without human-annotated Chain-of-Thought data. It reveals a two-phase learning process and the spontaneous emergence of sophisticated cognitive strategies, including self-verification and parallel reasoning, while achieving strong performance on mathematical benchmarks and improving reasoning trace quality and efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing zero RL studies were limited to smaller models due to high computational demands, leaving emergent capabilities at trillion-parameter scales underexplored.
- Naive zero RL applications often produce reasoning traces lacking readability, exhibiting token redundancy, and failing to adapt reasoning depth to problem complexity.
- The full potential of self-evolved reasoning at unprecedented scales and its implications for the "bitter lesson" in AI needed empirical validation.

## Method

- A multi-stage, self-iterative ==zero RL== training pipeline was introduced, starting with reasoning elicitation, followed by ==self-distillation== for compression, sustained optimization, and a final stage for ==adaptive reasoning depth==.
- The pipeline employed a ==clipped importance-sampling policy gradient== with a ==training-inference ratio correction== and progressively expanded context window for initial reasoning elicitation.
- Infrastructure optimizations like ==mixed-precision control== for sensitive operations and ==context parallelism== for efficient KV-tensor communication were implemented to ensure stability and efficiency at scale.

## Results

- The **Ring-2.5-1T-Zero** model achieved superior accuracy, reaching **93.5%** on AIME 2024 and **92.5%** on AIME 2026, outperforming its **104-billion-parameter** counterpart.
- Generated Chain-of-Thought traces demonstrated enhanced comprehensibility (**dominant win rates** against strong baselines), higher reproducibility (better knowledge distillation sample efficiency), and improved efficiency (using **less than half the tokens** on average for mutually solved problems).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of KL penalty, training-inference ratio correction, and strict format rewards for stable training, while highlighting "length inertia" issues with standard token-level loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling to a trillion parameters significantly enhances both the reasoning performance ceiling and sample efficiency, providing strong empirical support for the "bitter lesson" in AI.
> - Zero RL training exhibits distinct "discovery" and "sharpening" phases: initial exploration uncovers latent reasoning patterns, followed by refinement to consistently produce correct solutions.
> - Sophisticated cognitive strategies, such as anthropomorphic meta-commentary, structured formatting, active self-verification, and parallel reasoning, spontaneously emerge in the trillion-parameter model without explicit supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning emerge as a critical capability for tackling complex tasks. This has been driven by advancements in model parameters and computational resources at test time. Recent research has highlighted Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), often termed "zero RL," as a method for eliciting CoT reasoning directly from pretrained base models, circumventing the need for human-annotated CoT data. This paradigm shift suggests that advanced problem-solving strategies can be cultivated through trial-and-error reinforcement learning.

However, existing zero RL studies have largely been restricted to models of relatively smaller scale due to significant computational demands. Consequently, the training dynamics and emergent capabilities of zero RL at the trillion-parameter scale remain underexplored. Larger models inherently possess richer pretrained knowledge and latent abilities, suggesting that scaling RLVR to such magnitudes could unlock unprecedented reasoning capacities. This paper positions itself at this frontier, aiming to investigate the behavior and potential of zero RL when applied to models with a trillion parameters, thereby addressing the gap in understanding how training dynamics and emergent cognitive behaviors evolve at this massive scale. The work also aims to validate the "bitter lesson" in artificial intelligence, which posits that general methods leveraging computation and scale ultimately outperform human-engineered heuristics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to elicit high-quality reasoning behaviors from a language model scaled to a trillion parameters using the zero RL paradigm. This objective is driven by a detailed understanding of what constitutes "high-quality reasoning" and a recognition of the limitations observed in prior or naive scaling attempts.

The authors define an ideal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trace not only by its correctness in reaching a final answer but also by three additional critical attributes:
1. **Readability:** The reasoning path should be transparent, logically formatted, and clearly structured for human understanding and verification. This is essential for LLMs' increasing deployment in complex tasks where explanations are crucial.
2. **Computational Conciseness and Efficiency:** CoT traces should avoid excessive length, token redundancy, and circular logic. Efficient generation directly translates to faster inference speeds and reduced computational costs, especially in agentic workflows where CoT serves as intermediate steps.
3. **Adaptive Reasoning Depth:** The model should demonstrate the ability to adjust its reasoning depth based on the inherent complexity of the problem. Simple problems should receive concise solutions, while complex ones warrant more exhaustive reasoning.

The motivations for these objectives stem from observed deficiencies in applying zero RL without specific interventions:
- **Poor Readability:** Naive applications often result in reasoning traces that lack logical formatting and clear structure, hindering human interpretability.
- **Token Redundancy and Inefficiency:** Standard algorithms, such as GRPO, can introduce an implicit length bias. By assigning disproportionate credit to longer sequences, token-level loss encourages unchecked length growth and redundant derivations, making inference inefficient.
- **Lack of Adaptive Depth:** Existing training pipelines typically produce single-mode models operating under a fixed response budget, which cannot adapt to the varying reasoning requirements of different problems.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to develop a stable and efficient training pipeline that not only scales zero RL to a trillion parameters but also enhances the intrinsic quality of the emergent reasoning processes, thereby exploring the full potential of self-evolved reasoning at an unprecedented scale.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a multi-stage, self-iterative training pipeline designed to elicit and scale reasoning capabilities directly from a base model without human-annotated data. A core design principle is minimalism, integrating optimization elements only when strictly necessary for guiding iterative learning at the trillion-parameter scale.

#### 3.1. Zero Reinforcement Learning Training Pipeline

The pipeline comprises four distinct phases:
- **First Stage RL: Reasoning Elicitation:**
 - This stage aims to stimulate reasoning from a pretrained base model with no prior CoT capabilities.
 - **Algorithm:** A clipped importance-sampling policy gradient is employed, which allows gradients to flow for all generated tokens, unlike standard PPO-clip. The objective function $J(\theta)$ uses advantage estimates calculated with GRPO-style group-normalized rewards.
 - **Training-Inference Ratio Correction:** To counteract numerical discrepancies between the training engine (Megatron) and the inference engine (SGLang), the numerator of the importance sampling ratio ($\rho_{i,t}$) is replaced with actual training-engine logits.
 - **KL Divergence Penalty:** A KL divergence penalty ($L_{KL}(\theta)$) against a frozen reference model is applied to prevent the policy from drifting too far.
 - **Token-Level Loss:** To encourage longer responses, a token-level loss is used, summing over all tokens without normalization by response length. Response window length is progressively expanded (e.g., 4k to 64k tokens) in a curriculum manner.
- **Self-Distillation: Compression and Stabilization:**
 - This intermediate phase addresses verbosity and instability resulting from the unconstrained token-level loss in the first stage.
 - **Process:** The first-stage expert policy samples multiple rollouts. The shortest correct reasoning trace is selected, then prompted for self-evaluation to filter out redundant segments.
 - **Outcome:** This refined, concise corpus is used to fine-tune the original base model via standard supervised learning, effectively transferring reasoning abilities while reducing verbosity and resetting accumulated numerical errors.
- **Second Stage RL: Sustained Optimization:**
 - Building on the stable foundation from self-distillation, this stage continues RL training.
 - **Algorithm:** The clipped importance-sampling policy gradient and ratio correction from the first stage are maintained.
 - **Loss Calculation:** A pivot to a sample-level loss (normalizing the gradient magnitude by output length) is introduced to control response length and prevent unbounded growth, preserving conciseness. The KL penalty is removed in this stage to allow for less constrained exploration.
- **Third Stage RL: Adaptive Reasoning Depth:**
 - This final stage equips the model with adaptive response modes.
 - **Strategy:** Training questions are partitioned into three difficulty tiers (Low, Medium, High), each associated with a specific maximum token length and a corresponding system prompt.
 - **Mechanism:** The model learns to condition its response generation on these prompts and length budgets, enabling it to allocate computational resources (reasoning depth) adaptively based on problem complexity.

#### 3.2. Infrastructure Optimizations

To ensure stability and efficiency at scale, two key infrastructure optimizations are implemented:
- **Mixed-Precision Control:** The main model body uses BF16 precision, but the attention softmax and LM head computations are performed in FP32. This targeted high-precision usage prevents small numerical errors in sensitive exponential operations (e.g., softmax) from being amplified into large RL losses, stabilizing training.
- **Context Parallelism Optimization:**
 - **MLA Layers:** An all-to-all context parallelism (CP) strategy is used, reshuffling data along the head dimension via a single collective operation. This reduces communication volume for compressed key-value (KV) tensors.
 - **Lightning Attention Layers:** A single AllGather operation broadcasts all local fixed-size KV state matrices to every device simultaneously, enabling immediate computation.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Base Models:** Training started from two pretrained Ling-2.5 base models without supervised fine-tuning: Ling-2.5-1T-Base (1 trillion parameters, 63B activated MoE) and Ling-2.5-flash-Base (104B parameters, 7.4B activated MoE).
- **Training Infrastructure:** Experiments were conducted on 320 H200 GPUs, utilizing Megatron for training, SGLang for rollouts, and the Areal framework for orchestration.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key settings include an off-policy RL setup with varying batch sizes, Adam optimizer (LR 2e-6), weight decay 0.01, G=16 rollouts, temperature 1.0, and specific clipping bounds for the importance ratio. Progressive context window expansion and reference model updates were employed.
- **Reward Design:** The reward function combined an accuracy component ($r_{acc,i}$) and a format component ($r_{format,i}$). The format reward ensured adherence to a `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` structure. The accuracy reward transitioned from deterministic rule-based matching for easy problems to an LLM-as-a-Judge (Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct) for harder ones.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy, averaged over 64 runs, was used across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME and HMMT series, IMOAnswerBench). Multi-tier models were evaluated under Low (4k), Medium (16k), and High (128k) inference modes.

#### 3.4. CoT Quality Evaluation Framework

Beyond final-answer accuracy, a structured framework assesses CoT quality along three dimensions:
- **Comprehensibility:** Evaluated quantitatively using an LLM-as-a-Judge framework via pairwise comparison, assessing logical coherence, causal explicitness, and absence of hallucinations.
- **Reproducibility:** Measured by knowledge distillation. A weaker student model is fine-tuned on generated CoT traces, and its downstream performance gain serves as a proxy for the transferability of reasoning skills.
- **Efficiency:** Quantified by the average token count of correct CoT traces, with fewer tokens indicating higher efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding zero RL at scale, supporting the "bitter lesson" in AI:

#### 4.1. Scaling Dictates Capability and Efficiency

- **Enhanced Performance Ceiling:** The 1-trillion-parameter Ring-2.5-1T-Zero model consistently achieved higher accuracy ceilings compared to its 104-billion-parameter counterpart, Ring-2.5-flash-Zero, especially on more challenging benchmarks. For instance, Ring-2.5-1T-Zero (First Stage RL) reached 84.2% on AIME 2026, significantly higher than Ring-2.5-flash-Zero's 65.3%.
- **Superior Sample Efficiency:** The larger model demonstrated a faster rate of reasoning improvement throughout training, indicating that greater parameter capacity accelerates capability acquisition within the zero RL paradigm. This underscores the importance of aggressive parameter scaling.

#### 4.2. Two-Phase Training Dynamics: Discovery and Sharpening

- Analysis of pass@1024 (probability of at least one correct solution among 1024 samples) revealed a two-phase learning process:
 - **Discovery Phase:** In the initial stages, pass@1024 increased, indicating that RL was actively expanding the model's reasoning boundary by uncovering dormant reasoning patterns and unlocking latent capabilities from pretraining.
 - **Sharpening Phase:** As training progressed, pass@1024 plateaued, while pass@1 accuracy continued to rise steadily. This indicated a shift in focus where RL refined the policy to consistently produce correct solutions within the newly established reasoning boundary, optimizing for reliability.

#### 4.3. Emergent Advanced Cognitive Strategies (Validation of the Bitter Lesson)

One of the most notable findings was the spontaneous emergence of sophisticated cognitive strategies in the 1T model, without explicit supervision or hand-crafted heuristics:
- **Anthropomorphism:** The model exhibited simulated emotional states and informal meta-commentary (e.g., "brain fart," "wing it," "genius idea") in its reasoning traces, mirroring human cognitive friction and emotional dynamics observed in pre-training data.
- **Structured Formatting:** Without specific formatting instructions, the model naturally organized its thoughts into highly structured, pedagogically clear traces using explicit step numbering and phase transitions (e.g., "Step 1:", "Step 7: Verify"), suggesting an inherent optimal strategy for organizing long-context attention.
- **Self-Verification:** The model learned to actively verify intermediate derivations, re-examining assumptions, cross-checking formulas, and substituting results back into constraints, driven purely by the learned necessity to secure the final correctness reward.
- **Parallel Reasoning:** The model spontaneously branched into alternative strategies, executing self-contained "Tree-of-Thought" searches within a single linear rollout, evaluating competing approaches, and committing to solutions based on converging evidence.
- **Context Anxiety:** As the model approached its perceived maximum token limit, it sometimes exhibited strategic "panic," aborting complex reasoning to force a heuristic guess, prioritizing structural completeness (for non-zero reward) over mathematical rigor.

#### 4.4. Performance on Mathematical Benchmarks

- Ring-2.5-1T-Zero (Second Stage RL) achieved strong performance, reaching 93.5% on AIME 2024, 91.6% on AIME 2025, and 92.5% on AIME 2026.
- The adaptive inference modes (Third Stage RL) demonstrated flexible performance-efficiency trade-offs. While the Medium (16k token budget) and Low (4k token budget) modes effectively reduced inference latency, there was a slight performance drop compared to the Second Stage peak, attributed to limitations in ultra-long reasoning data and negative transfer from multi-length training.

#### 4.5. CoT Quality Evaluation

- **Comprehensibility:** LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations showed that Ring-2.5-1T-Zero's reasoning traces had dominant win rates against strong baselines (GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2.6, MiniMax-M2.7, Qwen3.5-397B), confirming superior logical organization.
- **Reproducibility:** Distilling the model's traces (using only 100K samples) into weaker student models (Qwen2.5-32B, Llama3.3-70B-Instruct) resulted in significantly stronger performance compared to distillation from DeepSeek-R1 (using 800K samples), indicating higher sample efficiency for knowledge transfer.
- **Efficiency:** On mutually solved AIME problems, Ring-2.5-1T-Zero used an average of 6,368 tokens, less than half of other models, suggesting more direct and optimal logical paths.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies (on Ling-2.5-flash-Base model)

- **RL Algorithms:** Algorithms that aggressively amplified low-probability tokens (CISPO, DAPO) showed faster learning but suffered from greater instability compared to GRPO. GSPO, while stable, did not incentivize length growth for initial reasoning.
- **Stabilization Strategies:** Both the KL penalty and the training-inference ratio correction were crucial for preventing catastrophic training collapse (e.g., unbounded log-probability differences, entropy collapse).
- **Format Reward:** Strict format control (`<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` with explicit EOS) was necessary to prevent uncontrolled length growth and meaningless token generation.
- **Length Inertia:** Standard token-level loss led to "length inertia," where the model generated increasingly long responses even for simple, already-solved problems, demonstrating a lazy shortcut for accumulating rewards rather than genuine adaptive conciseness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions to the field of large language models and reinforcement learning:

- **Validation of the "Bitter Lesson" at Scale:** The most prominent impact is the empirical validation that massive computational scale and general learning algorithms can autonomously lead to the emergence of complex cognitive strategies, making human-engineered heuristics redundant. This paradigm shift encourages focusing on scalable general methods rather than intricate, task-specific designs.
- **Demonstrating Scalability of Zero RL:** The work shows that zero RL can be successfully and stably scaled to trillion-parameter models with minimal algorithmic and system modifications. This significantly lowers the engineering barriers for large-scale RL training and provides a reproducible blueprint for other researchers.
- **Deepening Understanding of RL Training Dynamics:** The identification of sequential "discovery" and "sharpening" phases in RL for reasoning provides a more nuanced understanding of how capabilities are acquired and refined in large models. This insight can guide future curriculum design and optimization strategies.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation of Reasoning Quality:** The proposed structured framework for evaluating Chain-of-Thought quality across comprehensibility, reproducibility, and efficiency moves beyond mere final-answer accuracy. This is crucial for developing LLMs that are not only capable but also explainable, transferable, and efficient, aspects increasingly critical for real-world deployment.
- **Advancing Adaptive LLM Capabilities:** The multi-stage pipeline, particularly the tier-based training for adaptive reasoning depth, enables models to dynamically allocate computational resources. This is a step towards more efficient and context-aware LLMs that can adjust their verbosity and problem-solving effort based on task requirements.
- **Opening New Research Avenues:** The observed emergent behaviors, such as anthropomorphic reasoning, structured formatting, parallel reasoning, and self-verification, suggest that LLMs are capable of much more sophisticated cognitive processes than previously assumed. This could inspire new research directions in defining higher-level action spaces for RL, exploring the interplay between pre-trained knowledge and emergent behaviors, and developing more robust strategies to manage issues like "context anxiety" and "length inertia" within a unified optimization framework.
