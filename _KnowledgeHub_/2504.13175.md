# Novel Demonstration Generation with Gaussian Splatting Enables Robust One-Shot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory and partner institutions develop RoboSplat, a framework that generates diverse robotic manipulation demonstrations using 3D Gaussian Splatting from a single expert demonstration, achieving 87.8% success rate across six generalization scenarios while reducing data collection time by 29x compared to manual methods.

## Problem

- Collecting real-world robot demonstrations is time-consuming and expensive
- Existing policies struggle to generalize across different visual domains and scenarios
- Traditional sim-to-real and 2D augmentation techniques have limited accuracy for robot learning

## Method

- Use 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== to reconstruct manipulation scenes from multi-view images
- Generate novel demonstrations through Gaussian manipulation and frame alignment
- Train visuomotor policies on augmented demonstrations using behavioral cloning

## Results

- Achieves **87.8%** success rate in one-shot settings vs **57.2%** with traditional methods, while generating training data **29x** faster than manual collection
- Demonstrates robustness across object poses, types, camera views, and lighting
- Shows improved performance as generated data quantity increases

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D Gaussian Splatting enables high-fidelity, editable scene reconstruction
> - Segmenting Gaussians by scene components allows targeted manipulation
> - Differentiable rendering enables precise alignment with real-world coordinates

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Imitation Learning:** The paper addresses the challenges in imitation learning for robotic manipulation, specifically the difficulty of acquiring diverse and representative training data. This is a well-recognized problem in the field.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The paper acknowledges the challenges associated with traditional Sim-to-Real approaches, which rely on physical simulators and often suffer from inaccuracies in geometric reconstruction and physical simulation.
- **Data Augmentation:** The paper builds on previous work in data augmentation for policy learning, but it criticizes existing 2D image augmentation techniques for lacking spatial accuracy.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS):** The core innovation lies in leveraging 3DGS, a recent advancement in scene reconstruction and rendering, to generate realistic and spatially accurate training data. 3DGS has rapidly gained popularity in computer graphics and vision due to its high fidelity and real-time rendering capabilities.
- **Robotics and Vision:** This work bridges the gap between computer vision and robotics by using a vision-based technique (3DGS) to improve the performance of robot control policies.
- **One-Shot Learning:** The research contributes to the growing field of one-shot imitation learning, aiming to enable robots to learn complex tasks from a single demonstration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a novel method for generating diverse and visually realistic demonstrations for robot manipulation tasks, using only a single expert demonstration and multi-view images of the scene.
- **Motivation:**
 - The high cost and time investment required for collecting real-world robot demonstrations.
 - The limited robustness of policies learned from teleoperated demonstrations due to insufficient coverage of visual domains in the training data.
 - The inaccuracies and limitations of existing Sim-to-Real and 2D data augmentation techniques for robot learning.
 - The potential of 3DGS to provide a high-fidelity, editable 3D representation of real-world scenes, enabling more effective data augmentation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, RoboSplat, consists of the following key steps:

- **Scene Reconstruction:** Use 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to reconstruct the manipulation scene from multi-view RGB images, aided by COLMAP for camera pose estimation and Depth Anything for depth estimation.
- **Frame Alignment:** Develop a novel frame alignment pipeline leveraging differentiable rendering of Gaussian Splatting to align the reconstructed scene with the real-world coordinate frame.
- **Segmentation:** Segment the 3D Gaussians of different scene components (robot, objects, background) using off-the-shelf segmentation models (Grounded-SAM) and the robot's URDF model.
- **Demonstration Augmentation:** Generate novel demonstrations by manipulating the 3D Gaussian representation, using five main techniques:
 - 3D Gaussian replacement for varying object types, scene appearance, and robot embodiments.
 - Equivariant transformations for different object poses.
 - Visual attribute editing for various lighting conditions.
 - Novel view synthesis for new camera perspectives.
 - 3D content generation for diverse object types.
- **Policy Training:** Train a visuomotor policy (using a transformer-based architecture and Behavioral Cloning) on the augmented demonstrations.
- **Real-World Evaluation:** Deploy the trained policy on real robots and evaluate its performance on a set of manipulation tasks, across different generalization scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Efficient Data Generation:** The proposed pipeline can generate demonstrations significantly faster than manually collecting them in the real world (over 29 times faster).
- **Improved Generalization:** Policies trained on demonstrations generated by RoboSplat exhibit significantly improved generalization performance compared to policies trained on real-world data or data augmented with existing 2D techniques.
- **One-Shot Performance:** RoboSplat achieves an average success rate of 87.8% in one-shot settings (starting from a single expert demonstration), across six types of generalization in the real world, which is a substantial improvement over policies trained on hundreds of real-world demonstrations with 2D data augmentation (57.2%).
- **Scalability:** The performance of policies trained on generated demonstrations improves as the amount of generated data increases.
- **Robustness:** RoboSplat demonstrates robustness to various disturbances, including changes in object poses, object types, camera views, lighting conditions, scene appearance, and robot embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Reduced Data Requirements:** The ability to generate high-quality training data from a single demonstration significantly reduces the data collection burden in robot learning.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The proposed method leads to more robust and generalizable visuomotor policies, enabling robots to operate effectively in diverse and unseen environments.
- **Wider Applicability:** By addressing the limitations of traditional Sim-to-Real and 2D data augmentation techniques, RoboSplat opens up new possibilities for applying robot learning to a wider range of real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Potential for Automation:** The autonomous data generation pipeline has the potential to automate the process of training robots for complex manipulation tasks.
- **Future Research:** The research provides a strong foundation for future work in areas such as:
 - Extending the method to handle deformable objects and contact-rich tasks.
 - Incorporating physical constraints into the data generation process.
 - Exploring different policy architectures and learning algorithms.
 - Applying RoboSplat to other robotics domains, such as navigation and perception.

In summary, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning by introducing a novel and effective method for generating diverse and realistic training data using 3D Gaussian Splatting. The results demonstrate the potential of RoboSplat to significantly improve the robustness and generalization capabilities of visuomotor policies, enabling robots to learn complex manipulation tasks from limited data. The work is well-motivated, technically sound, and supported by comprehensive real-world experiments.
