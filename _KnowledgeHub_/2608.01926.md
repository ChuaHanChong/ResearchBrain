# ProWorld: Progress-Aware Hyperbolic World Models for Long-Horizon Visual Goal Reaching

> [!summary] Summary
> ProWorld introduces a model that enhances long-horizon visual goal-reaching by integrating goal-conditioned progress order into hyperbolic world models. This approach structures latent space dynamics to reflect progress towards a goal, resulting in an average absolute success rate gain of 9.67 percentage points over a baseline (LeWM) across four visual goal-reaching tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visual world models struggle with rollout drift, failing to maintain sustained, goal-directed progress over long horizons due to local prediction objectives.
- Latent spaces in current models often cannot distinguish between visually or locally similar future states that represent vastly different levels of progress toward a distant goal.
- Current Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA-style models) are effective for short-horizon tasks but lack mechanisms to ensure explicit goal-aligned progress over many steps.

## Method

- **ProWorld** embeds visual observations into a ==hyperbolic latent space== to naturally represent the coarse-to-fine and asymmetric nature of goal-conditioned progress.
- Introduces ==progress-aware hyperbolic dynamics learning== using novel loss terms: hyperbolic prediction, future discrimination, and a ==progress-induced entailment cone==.
- Employs hyperbolic latent planning within a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) framework, utilizing a multi-component progress-aware planning cost function (terminal, best-intermediate, and mean goal distance).

## Results

- Achieved an average absolute success rate gain of **9.67 percentage points** over the LeWM baseline across four visual goal-reaching tasks (PushT, Cube-S, AntMaze-L, Scene).
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in long-horizon tasks, with a **37.16%** relative gain in success rate on AntMaze-L (from **22.00%** to **32.00%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that hyperbolic geometry, the entailment cone, and future discrimination are crucial for performance, with the future discrimination loss showing the largest individual impact.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hyperbolic geometry serves as an effective inductive bias for modeling hierarchical and asymmetric progress relationships in latent spaces, organizing states radially based on their proximity to the goal.
> - Explicitly enforcing goal-conditioned progress order through specialized losses (entailment cone, future discrimination) substantially mitigates rollout drift and resolves progress ambiguity in long-horizon predictions.
> - A comprehensive planning objective that considers both terminal and intermediate progress signals in the hyperbolic latent space is critical for selecting globally effective action sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of visual reinforcement learning and model-based control, specifically focusing on long-horizon visual goal-reaching tasks. The work builds upon recent advancements in visual world models, particularly those based on Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA-style models).

Existing visual world models, such as PlaNet, Dreamer, MuZero, and TD-MPC, learn predictive environment dynamics from raw pixel inputs to facilitate control and planning. More recently, JEPA-style models (e.g., I-JEPA, V-JEPA, LeJEPA, LeWM) have shifted focus from explicit pixel reconstruction to predicting future latent-space representations. These models have demonstrated efficacy in learning local state transition consistency through next-step representation prediction, yielding strong empirical performance in short-horizon planning benchmarks.

However, the paper identifies limitations of these models in long-horizon settings. While local prediction accuracy is important, it does not inherently guarantee sustained progress toward a distant goal over many steps. Recursive rollouts, common in these models, are susceptible to accumulating model bias, leading to intermediate states that are locally plausible but globally ineffective. Furthermore, existing latent spaces, primarily optimized for local consistency, may struggle to differentiate between visually or locally similar future states that have significantly different implications for long-term goal progress.

The research also situates itself within the broader context of hyperbolic and geometric representation learning. Hyperbolic geometry has gained attention for its ability to compactly embed hierarchical and coarse-to-fine relational patterns with low distortion, a property explored in various applications such as image embeddings, zero-shot recognition, and reinforcement learning. Notably, previous work like GeoWorld has also introduced hyperbolic geometry into JEPA-style world models to mitigate geometric degradation during long rollouts. ProWorld extends this by explicitly modeling *goal-conditioned progress order* within the hyperbolic latent space, aiming to organize state dynamics from broad, lower-progress regions toward more specific, goal-relevant regions.

Therefore, ProWorld attempts to address a critical gap in current visual world modeling: the need for models to not only predict future states accurately but also to explicitly learn and leverage a structured understanding of progress toward a goal, particularly for tasks requiring extended sequences of actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of ProWorld are to overcome two core challenges faced by existing JEPA-style visual world models in long-horizon goal-reaching tasks:

1. **Mitigating Rollout Drift**: Current world models typically learn local transition consistency by aligning predicted next-step latent representations with ground truths. However, this one-step prediction objective does not inherently guarantee that multi-step rollouts, obtained by recursively unrolling the model, will maintain sustained, goal-directed progress. As rollouts extend over long horizons, accumulated model bias can lead to the generation of intermediate states that are dynamically plausible but diverge significantly from goal-relevant trajectories. The objective is to ensure that imagined future trajectories consistently advance toward the specified goal, rather than merely exhibiting local realism.

2. **Resolving Progress Ambiguity**: In complex, long-horizon scenarios, different future states might appear visually or locally similar in the latent space, yet represent substantially different levels of progress towards the ultimate goal. If these states are mapped too closely in a latent space optimized mainly for local consistency, the planning agent may confuse locally viable but globally suboptimal action sequences, thereby selecting ineffective paths. The objective is to enable the latent space to discriminate between such ambiguous states based on their long-term progress semantics.

To address these challenges, the authors introduce the concept of **goal-conditioned progress order**. This concept defines a relative ordering of states based on their advancement toward a given goal. The motivation for this definition is rooted in two observed attributes of long-horizon trajectories:
- **Asymmetry**: Progress is generally unidirectional; moving towards a goal is distinct from moving away.
- **Coarse-to-fine structure**: Early states in a successful trajectory typically retain broader future possibilities, indicating lower, more general progress. As the agent approaches the goal, later states progressively concentrate on more specific, goal-relevant regions, indicating higher, more refined progress.

Motivated by these attributes, the authors propose that hyperbolic geometry, which naturally excels at embedding hierarchical structures with low distortion (where points further from the origin can represent more specific concepts, while those closer represent broader categories), is well-suited to represent this coarse-to-fine, asymmetric goal-conditioned progress order.

Therefore, ProWorld aims to embed visual representations into a hyperbolic latent space and use goal-conditioned progress order as a geometric inductive bias to organize the latent-space dynamics. This involves developing learning mechanisms that explicitly enforce directional progress within trajectories and enhance discriminability between future states based on their progress semantics, ultimately leading to more effective long-horizon visual planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ProWorld operates by learning a latent dynamics model from offline trajectories for goal-conditioned visual planning. The methodology can be divided into three main components: visual encoding and hyperbolic embedding, progress-aware hyperbolic dynamics learning, and hyperbolic latent planning.

**Visual Encoding and Hyperbolic Embedding:**
1. **Visual Encoding**: A visual encoder (`f_theta`) processes pixel observations (`o_t`) to produce Euclidean latent representations. A subsequent Euclidean projection head (`g_psi`) further refines these into `z_t ∈ R^d`.
2. **Hyperbolic Embedding**: A hyperbolic projection head (`q_omega`) maps the Euclidean latent `z_t` into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space. This involves mapping `z_t` to an `m`-dimensional spatial tangent component (`u_t`) at the Lorentz origin. The tangent norm is stabilized, and the vector is lifted to the Lorentz tangent space. Finally, the hyperbolic latent representation `h_t` is obtained by applying the origin exponential map `exp_c^o(0, u_t)` to project it onto the `H_c^m` manifold.

**Action-conditioned Autoregressive Predictor:**
- An action encoder (`e_eta`) converts raw actions (`a_t`) into action embeddings (`alpha_t`).
- An autoregressive predictor (`p_phi`), implemented as a Transformer, forecasts the next Euclidean latent state `z_hat_t+1` based on a history window of previous latent states and action embeddings.
- This predicted Euclidean latent `z_hat_t+1` is then mapped to a predicted hyperbolic point `h_hat_t+1` using the same hyperbolic projection pipeline.

**Progress-Aware Hyperbolic Dynamics Learning (Training Stage):**
ProWorld's training objective integrates four loss terms:

1. **Hyperbolic Prediction Loss (`L_pred`)**: This loss aligns the predicted future hyperbolic state (`h_hat_t+1`) with the encoded true future state (`h_t+1`). It is defined as the squared hyperbolic geodesic distance between these two points: `L_pred = E_t [d_H(h_hat_t+1, h_t+1)^2]`. This term ensures local predictive accuracy within the hyperbolic space.

2. **Hyperbolic Future Discrimination (`L_ctr`)**: To address progress ambiguity, a Lorentz contrastive constraint is introduced. For a batch of predicted and true future points, `L_ctr` uses a symmetric cross-entropy loss to pull matched (`h_hat_i`, `h_i`) pairs closer in hyperbolic distance while pushing unmatched (`h_hat_i`, `h_j` for `i!=j`) pairs apart. This enhances the discriminability of future states with different progress semantics.

3. **Progress-Induced Entailment Constraint (`L_cone`)**: This is the core mechanism for realizing goal-conditioned progress order. Given a hindsight goal observation (`o_g`) encoded as `h_g`, and two temporally separated states from a successful trajectory, an earlier "parent" state (`h_p = h_t`) and a later "child" state (`h_c = h_t+Delta`), the constraint encourages the child to move along the direction from the parent toward the goal.
 - It calculates two tangent directions at the parent point: `v_c` (parent to child) and `v_g` (parent to goal).
 - An angular term `max(0, theta_c,g - A(delta_g_p))` penalizes the child if its direction deviates too much from the parent-to-goal direction. The angular aperture `A(delta_g_p)` is adaptive, widening for states farther from the goal and narrowing for states closer to ensure coarse-to-fine progress.
 - A goal-distance term `max(0, d_H(h_c, h_g) + m_g - d_H(h_p, h_g))` encourages the child state `h_c` to be strictly closer to the goal `h_g` than the parent state `h_p` by a margin `m_g`.
 - This combined loss `L_cone` imposes an asymmetric, goal-dependent progress relation.

4. **Stabilization (`L_sig`)**: A SIGReg regularizer, adopted from prior work like LeWM, is included to stabilize the latent distribution and prevent representation collapse.

The overall training objective is a weighted sum of these four losses: `L = lambda_pred L_pred + lambda_ctr L_ctr + lambda_cone L_cone + lambda_sig L_sig`.

**Hyperbolic Latent Planning (Inference Stage):**
During planning, ProWorld uses the learned dynamics model within a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) framework.
1. The current observation (`o_t`) and goal observation (`o_g`) are encoded into hyperbolic latents (`h_t` and `h_g`, respectively).
2. CEM generates candidate action sequences (`a_1:L`). For each sequence, the model recursively predicts future hyperbolic states (`h_hat_1` to `h_hat_L`).
3. A **progress-aware planning cost (`C`)** evaluates each candidate rollout:
 - `D_g_t`: Squared hyperbolic distance from each predicted state `h_hat_t` to the goal `h_g`.
 - `C_T = D_g_L`: Terminal goal distance.
 - `C_best = min_1<=t<=L D_g_t`: The minimum goal distance achieved along the rollout (best intermediate progress).
 - `C_mean = 1/L sum_L_t=1 D_g_t`: Average goal distance over the entire trajectory.
 - `C = beta_T C_T + beta_b C_best + beta_m C_mean`: The final cost combines these terms with configurable weights (`beta_T`, `beta_b`, `beta_m`).
4. The action sequence that minimizes this progress-aware cost `C` is selected, and its first action is executed in the environment. This process is repeated at each planning step (receding horizon control).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ProWorld was conducted on four visual goal-reaching tasks from the OGBench benchmark suite: PushT, Cube-S, AntMaze-L, and Scene. These tasks represent a range of planning complexities, from 2D planar pushing to 3D object manipulation and long-horizon navigation.

**Quantitative Performance:**
- **Success Rates (SR)**: ProWorld achieved an average absolute success rate gain of 9.67 percentage points over LeWM, a strong JEPA-style world model baseline, across all four tasks.
 - On PushT, ProWorld achieved 94.00% SR, compared to LeWM's 83.33%.
 - On Cube-S, ProWorld achieved 77.33% SR, against LeWM's 71.33%.
 - On AntMaze-L (a long-horizon navigation task), ProWorld showed a substantial improvement with 32.00% SR, compared to LeWM's 22.00% (a relative gain of 37.16%).
 - On Scene, ProWorld achieved 69.33% SR, against LeWM's 57.33%.
- The results indicated that ProWorld's gains were more pronounced in long-horizon tasks like AntMaze-L, suggesting that its explicit modeling of progress relationships is particularly beneficial when goal achievement requires sustained actions over many intermediate steps.
- **Physical State Decodability**: On AntMaze-L, ProWorld demonstrated competitive Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Pearson correlation (r) when decoding physical state information (e.g., Ant position) from its latent space, comparable to or slightly better than baselines. This suggests that the hyperbolic latent space maintains relevant physical semantics necessary for planning.

**Qualitative Observations:**
- **Open-Loop Prediction Visualization**: Qualitative rollouts on PushT and Cube-S illustrated that ProWorld's predictions remained closer to ground-truth trajectories over longer horizons compared to baselines like LeWM. While LeWM's predictions maintained local appearance plausibility, they often diverged from goal-relevant object poses and positions due to accumulated error. ProWorld's ability to sustain goal-directed object movement was attributed to its progress-aware dynamics.
- **Latent Space Organization**: t-SNE projections of ProWorld's latent space on PushT revealed local continuity and a radial expansion trend. This supports the hypothesis that the hyperbolic geometry aids in organizing the latent space in a structured manner, where states are mapped according to their progression towards the goal.

**Ablation Studies (on Cube-S):**
- **Learning Objectives**:
 - The full ProWorld model (78% SR) significantly outperformed a baseline using only the prediction loss (`L_pred`) (46% SR), confirming that local prediction alone is insufficient.
 - Removing the hyperbolic future discrimination loss (`L_ctr`) resulted in the largest performance drop (down to 46% SR), emphasizing its role in state separability.
 - Removing the progress-induced entailment cone loss (`L_cone`) reduced SR to 62%, indicating the importance of structured directional progress.
 - Removing the SIGReg regularizer (`L_sig`) had a minor impact (76% SR), suggesting its primary role is representation stabilization.
- **Geometry and Progress Order**:
 - Replacing the Lorentz hyperbolic space with a Euclidean latent space reduced SR from 78% to 68%, supporting the benefit of negative curvature for progress organization.
 - Reversing the parent-child order or using random state pairs dramatically reduced SR (44% and 52%, respectively), validating that the entailment cone relies on correct goal-conditioned temporal ordering, not just generic regularization.
 - Using a fixed cone aperture (70% SR) performed worse than the adaptive aperture (78% SR), demonstrating the advantage of dynamic aperture scaling based on goal proximity.
- **Entailment Cone Design**: The full entailment cone, combining both angular and goal-distance terms, outperformed variants using only one term, highlighting their complementary contributions.
- **Planning Objective**: Planning with only the terminal goal distance (62% SR) was less effective than the full progress-aware planning cost (78% SR). Removing either the "best-intermediate" or "mean-distance" cost terms also degraded performance, demonstrating that intermediate progress signals are crucial for selecting globally effective action sequences.

**Progress Correlation Analysis:**
- Spearman correlation analysis consistently showed that trajectory progress was positively correlated with goal-direction alignment and negatively correlated with both hyperbolic and physical goal distances across tasks (PushT, Cube-S). This empirically validated that ProWorld successfully learned to align latent space geometry with the desired goal-conditioned progress.
- Radial diagnostics further confirmed an emergent coarse-to-fine organization in the hyperbolic latent space, with later, higher-progress states tending to occupy more specific regions (larger Lorentz radius).

**Computational Overhead:**
- The additional hyperbolic components in ProWorld introduced a minimal computational overhead, increasing total parameters by only 0.83% and rollout FLOPs by 0.03% compared to the LeWM backbone.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ProWorld represents a step forward in the design of visual world models for long-horizon planning. Its significance stems from several key contributions:

1. **Addressing Core Limitations**: ProWorld directly addresses the limitations of existing JEPA-style world models, specifically the issues of rollout drift and progress ambiguity, which are critical challenges for reliable long-horizon planning. By explicitly incorporating goal-conditioned progress order, it moves beyond solely local prediction quality to foster more globally consistent and goal-aligned trajectory generation.

2. **Novel Integration of Geometric Inductive Bias**: The research effectively demonstrates the utility of hyperbolic geometry as an inductive bias for modeling hierarchical and asymmetric progress relationships in latent spaces. This provides a structured way to represent the "coarse-to-fine" nature of goal-reaching, where early states allow for broader possibilities and later states converge to more specific, goal-relevant regions. This approach could inspire further exploration of non-Euclidean geometries in reinforcement learning and predictive modeling.

3. **Comprehensive Progress-Aware Framework**: ProWorld introduces a complete framework that not only learns progress-aware latent dynamics through novel training objectives (hyperbolic entailment and future discrimination) but also leverages this learned structure during planning via a refined, progress-aware cost function. This holistic approach ensures that progress is considered throughout the entire model-based planning pipeline.

4. **Empirical Validation for Long-Horizon Tasks**: The consistent performance gains across diverse visual goal-reaching tasks, particularly the substantial improvements on long-horizon scenarios like AntMaze-L, highlight the practical utility of the proposed methodology. This suggests that incorporating explicit progress signals is crucial for tasks where agents must navigate complex environments and sustain goal-directed behavior over extended periods.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robust Robotic Control**: ProWorld's ability to generate more consistent and goal-aligned long-term plans from visual inputs could enhance the robustness and reliability of robotic systems in complex manipulation and navigation tasks, especially those requiring precise, multi-step operations.
- **Advancement in Model-Based RL**: The findings encourage further research into incorporating richer structural priors and geometric reasoning into world models, potentially leading to more sample-efficient and interpretable model-based reinforcement learning algorithms.
- **Foundation for Hierarchical Planning**: The learned coarse-to-fine progress structure could serve as a foundation for more sophisticated hierarchical planning approaches, allowing agents to abstractly reason about high-level goals while refining actions at lower levels.
- **Generalizable Visual Representation Learning**: The method's focus on learning representations that inherently encode goal-conditioned progress could lead to more semantically meaningful and transferable visual representations for a wide array of embodied AI applications.

While the work shows promise, future research could explore the adaptability of this framework to even more open-ended environments, investigate methods for dynamic or implicit progress estimation beyond hindsight trajectories, and integrate these progress-aware world models with online learning and exploration strategies.
