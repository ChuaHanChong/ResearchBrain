# Efficient Training on Multiple Consumer GPUs with RoundPipe

> [!summary] Summary
> RoundPipe, a distributed training system from Tsinghua University, enables efficient fine-tuning of large language models on consumer-grade GPUs by addressing memory and communication bottlenecks. It achieved up to 2.16x higher throughput and supported 4.7x to 7.3x longer sequence lengths compared to baselines on RTX 4090 servers, successfully fine-tuning a 235B MoE model.

## Problem

- Training large language models (LLMs) on cost-effective consumer-grade GPUs is severely limited by their restricted VRAM (e.g., 24 GB on RTX 4090) and slow PCIe interconnects.
- Traditional pipeline parallelism (PP) schedules suffer from a 'weight binding issue,' where static stage-to-GPU assignments lead to significant pipeline bubbles and GPU underutilization due to imbalanced stage execution times.
- Achieving data consistency and avoiding race conditions during asynchronous optimizer updates, especially when model parameters are offloaded to host CPU memory, presents a challenge for correct and efficient training.

## Method

- **RoundPipe** introduces a ==Computation Dispatch Paradigm== that treats GPUs as stateless execution workers, dynamically dispatching computation stages (with model states residing in host memory) to any available GPU.
- It implements a novel ==round-robin pipeline schedule== that leverages ==asymmetric stage splitting== to balance forward and backward pass execution times across stages and supports ==staleness-1 asynchronous optimizer updates==.
- A ==priority-aware transfer scheduling engine== manages data movement over PCIe, and a fine-grained, distributed ==event-based synchronization protocol== ensures data consistency during concurrent operations on host-resident parameters.

## Results

- RoundPipe achieved **1.48x to 2.16x** higher training throughput and supported **4.7x to 7.3x** longer sequence lengths on **8x RTX 4090** servers compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- It was the only system capable of LoRA fine-tuning the **Qwen3-235B** Mixture-of-Experts model on consumer-grade RTX 4090 GPUs with **24 GB VRAM**.
- The system demonstrated near-linear throughput scaling across multiple RTX 4090 GPUs and achieved at least **76%** of the throughput of existing state-of-the-art frameworks running on datacenter A800s.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling computation stages from specific GPUs, enabled by extensive CPU offloading, effectively minimizes pipeline bubbles by allowing dynamic load balancing and efficient utilization of GPU resources.
> - Fine-grained, layer-level event-based synchronization is critical for enabling high-performance asynchronous optimizer updates when offloading parameters, significantly reducing iteration time compared to blocking approaches.
> - Automated, efficient partitioning of layers considering the asymmetric nature of forward and backward passes is essential for maintaining optimal load balance and overall pipeline throughput in distributed training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has increasingly relied on fine-tuning foundational models for various domain-specific tasks. While pre-training LLMs demands significant computational resources, fine-tuning requires comparatively less, making consumer-grade GPUs an appealing option due to their cost-effectiveness. The NVIDIA RTX 4090, for instance, offers computational capabilities comparable to datacenter A100 GPUs at a substantially lower price point.

However, utilizing consumer-grade GPUs for training large models presents two primary hardware constraints:
1. **Limited Memory Capacity**: Consumer GPUs, such as the RTX 4090 with 24 GB VRAM, have insufficient memory to store the model states alone for large LLMs (e.g., an 8B model requires 128 GB). This issue is compounded by the memory demands of activations, especially with expanding context windows.
2. **Slow Inter-GPU Communication**: Consumer GPUs typically use PCIe interconnects, which offer significantly lower bandwidth (less than 20%) compared to datacenter NVLink, further hampered by PCIe root complex contention.

Existing approaches to mitigate these constraints include:
- **CPU Offloading**: Techniques like ZeRO-Offload and ZeRO-Infinity offload model parameters, optimizer states, and intermediate activations to host CPU memory. This is often combined with activation recomputation (gradient checkpointing) to reduce GPU VRAM usage.
- **Data Parallelism (DP)**: Frameworks like ZeRO-Infinity distribute model states across multiple GPUs in a data-parallel fashion. However, DP requires frequent collective communications (e.g., all-gather) for parameter reconstruction at each layer, leading to substantial communication overhead (up to 70% of training time) over slow PCIe links.
- **Pipeline Parallelism (PP)**: Systems such as Mobius integrate PP with offloading, partitioning model layers into stages assigned to different GPUs. This reduces the need for extensive collective communications, replacing them with more efficient point-to-point transfers of activations and gradients between stages.

The paper identifies a limitation within existing pipeline parallelism schedules, termed the "weight binding issue." In these schedules, the weights and computations of a stage are fixed to a specific GPU. This rigidity causes significant "pipeline bubbles" when stage execution times are imbalanced (e.g., due to a larger Language Model head layer) or due to structural dependencies, leading to underutilization of GPUs. This can result in bubble ratios of up to 30% for models like Llama-3.1-8B.

RoundPipe positions itself in this landscape by addressing the weight binding issue inherent in existing PP schedules. It leverages CPU offloading not just for memory expansion but also as an opportunity to dynamically dispatch computation stages, treating GPUs as stateless execution workers. This aims to create a more flexible and efficient pipeline, specifically optimized for the constraints of consumer-grade GPU servers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enhance the efficiency and scalability of fine-tuning Large Language Models on consumer-grade GPU servers, thereby contributing to the democratization of AI. The cost-effectiveness of consumer GPUs makes them attractive for smaller companies and researchers, but their hardware limitations present significant barriers.

The key objectives and motivations driving the development of RoundPipe are detailed as follows:

- **Mitigate Hardware Bottlenecks on Consumer GPUs**: The work is fundamentally motivated by the need to overcome the limited VRAM (e.g., 24GB on an RTX 4090) and slow PCIe interconnects (e.g., 32 GB/s PCIe 4.0 vs. 200 GB/s NVLink 3.0) prevalent in consumer GPU setups. These limitations restrict the size of models and sequence lengths that can be trained efficiently.
- **Address the "Weight Binding Issue" in Pipeline Parallelism**: Existing pipeline parallelism schedules suffer from inherent inefficiencies when training LLMs with varying layer complexities (e.g., a large LM head). Because model stages are fixed to specific GPUs, the overall pipeline throughput is bottlenecked by the slowest stage, leading to substantial idle times or "pipeline bubbles." RoundPipe aims to resolve this by decoupling stages from specific devices.
- **Leverage CPU Offloading as a New Opportunity**: The widespread adoption of CPU offloading for model parameters and activations is identified not merely as a memory-saving technique but as an opportunity to re-architect pipeline scheduling. By having master weights and activations reside in host memory, the paper posits that computations for the same layer can be dynamically dispatched to any available GPU, transforming GPUs into a pool of stateless execution workers.
- **Achieve Near-Zero-Bubble Pipeline Efficiency**: The core objective is to design a novel pipeline schedule that minimizes or eliminates pipeline bubbles. This involves strategies that allow for dynamic task dispatching and balanced stage execution times, regardless of individual layer complexities.
- **Ensure Training Correctness with Asynchronous Operations**: When combining CPU offloading with asynchronous optimizer updates, potential race conditions arise where the GPU and CPU-side optimizer might simultaneously access or modify shared host-resident data. A key objective is to ensure data consistency and correct execution ordering without introducing performance-degrading synchronization barriers.
- **Automate Load Balancing for Asymmetric Stages**: Manually tuning layer partitions for optimal load balance across pipeline stages is complex and time-consuming, especially with asymmetric forward and backward passes. RoundPipe aims to provide an automated, efficient algorithm for stage splitting that ensures near-optimal load distribution.
- **Enable Training of Extremely Large Models on Limited Hardware**: A practical goal is to enable the fine-tuning of very large LLMs, including Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models (e.g., Qwen3-235B), on consumer-grade GPUs that would otherwise be unable to accommodate such models.

By addressing these objectives, RoundPipe seeks to provide a practical and high-performance solution for democratizing large-scale LLM fine-tuning, making it accessible and efficient for a broader range of users.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoundPipe introduces a novel approach to pipeline parallelism, primarily by decoupling computation stages from specific GPUs, enabled by extensive CPU offloading. Its methodology involves a new scheduling paradigm and specific system designs to manage data transfer, ensure consistency, and automate load balancing.

#### 3.1. Computation Dispatch Paradigm

The fundamental shift introduced by RoundPipe is the "Computation Dispatch Paradigm." Unlike traditional pipeline parallelism where stages are statically bound to GPUs, RoundPipe views GPUs as a pool of stateless execution workers. Model states and activations reside primarily in host memory and are transferred to a GPU only when needed for computation. This allows any GPU to execute any stage, enabling flexible relocation of stage computations to underutilized GPUs without incurring additional communication costs, as weight transfers between CPU and GPU are already necessary in an offloaded setup.

#### 3.2. RoundPipe Schedule

Building on the Computation Dispatch Paradigm, RoundPipe implements a specific pipeline schedule characterized by:
- **Round-Robin Dispatch**: Stages (both forward and backward) are dispatched to GPUs in a continuous round-robin sequence. The total micro-batches ($M$) are divided into multiple rounds ($R$), with $M_R \geq N$ micro-batches processed per round on $N$ GPUs. Within each round, forward stages ($S_f$) are followed by backward stages ($S_b$), forming a linear sequence of $S = S_f + S_b$ stage slots. Stage $i$ is dispatched to $GPU_{(g_0+i) \mod N}$, where $g_0$ is the starting GPU for the round. This ensures a continuous flow of stages and operations.
- **Asymmetric Stage Splitting**: Recognizes that forward and backward passes of a transformer layer have different execution times (backward with recomputation is often slower). Instead of symmetric partitioning, RoundPipe maintains separate layer partitions for forward and backward passes. This allows for balancing the per-stage execution time across the entire forward-to-backward sequence. The final backward stage ($B_1$) layers fuse their forward and backward passes, where the forward pass serves as the recomputation for the backward, thereby saving a dedicated forward pass computation.
- **Asynchronous Optimizer Support**: RoundPipe natively integrates staleness-1 asynchronous optimizer updates. Iterations seamlessly continue their round-robin assignments without pipeline flushes, eliminating warm-up and cool-down bubbles that occur at iteration boundaries in synchronous systems.

#### 3.3. System Designs for Efficiency and Correctness

RoundPipe incorporates several key system designs to realize the potential of its schedule:

- **Priority-Aware Transfer Scheduling Engine**: To manage continuous data movement between host and GPUs over PCIe and avoid head-of-line blocking, RoundPipe uses a multi-stream architecture per device.
 - **Multi-Stream Architecture**: Each GPU maintains four dedicated communication streams (for activation upload/download and parameter/gradient upload/download) alongside the default compute stream.
 - **Overlap Strategy**: Critical-path activation transfers are scheduled one micro-batch early/delayed to overlap with computation. Lower-priority parameter uploads and gradient downloads are chunked and scheduled into idle intervals between activation transfers. A longest-processing-time-first heuristic is used to distribute parameter/gradient chunks across available transfer windows, splitting very large tensors if necessary.

- **Fine-Grained, Distributed Event-Based Synchronization Protocol**: To ensure data consistency during staleness-1 asynchronous optimizer updates, which involve concurrent access to host-resident master and optimizer copies of parameters, RoundPipe implements:
 - **Offloading and Coordination**: Weight and gradient copies are offloaded to an optimizer worker, operating concurrently with GPU workers.
 - **Layer-Level Event Signaling**: Instead of global barriers, RoundPipe uses point-to-point threading events at the individual layer level. These events enforce five specific ordering constraints to protect weight and gradient integrity and optimizer semantics. This fine granularity allows GPU workers to begin processing early layers of the next iteration while deeper layers are still undergoing synchronization, minimizing stalls.

- **Automated Stage Partitioning Algorithm**: To eliminate manual tuning and maintain load balance across asymmetric stages, RoundPipe employs an efficient layer partitioning algorithm:
 - **Problem Formulation**: During initial iterations, it collects per-layer execution time and memory consumption. The goal is to minimize the total pipeline time (modeled as $(M \cdot S + N \cdot (N-1)) \cdot t_{max}$) while respecting GPU memory limits per stage.
 - **O(L^3) Algorithm**: It exploits the contiguous nature of layers within stages. The algorithm iterates through $O(L^2)$ possible maximum stage runtime ($t_{max}$) values. For each candidate $t_{max}$, it greedily packs layers into stages in $O(L)$ time, ensuring no stage exceeds $t_{max}$ or GPU memory limits. The first backward stage is prioritized for maximal filling to capitalize on activation recomputation savings.

Through these integrated methodological components, RoundPipe aims to achieve high throughput and memory efficiency, enabling large-scale LLM training on commodity hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RoundPipe was conducted on two distinct server configurations: an 8× RTX 4090 server (consumer-grade, 24 GB VRAM, PCIe 4.0) and an 8× A800 SXM server (datacenter-grade, 80 GB HBM2e, NVLink 3.0). The study used five open-source LLMs: Qwen3-1.7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B, GPT-OSS-20B, Qwen3-32B (dense transformers), and Qwen3-235B (MoE, fine-tuned with LoRA). Performance was assessed using training throughput (tokens/s) and maximum supported sequence length.

#### 4.1. End-to-End Performance on 8× RTX 4090 Server

- **Training Throughput**: RoundPipe achieved significant speedups over state-of-the-art baselines (ZeRO-2, FSDP, ZeRO-Infinity, Megatron-TP, Megatron-PP, Mobius). For 1.7B to 32B models, RoundPipe demonstrated 1.48× to 2.16× higher throughput. A variant, RoundPipe-sync (with synchronous optimizer updates), still showed 1.15× to 1.63× speedups. This performance was attributed to RoundPipe's pipeline design, which reduced bubbles and improved communication-computation overlap.
- **Large Model Support**: RoundPipe was the sole system capable of performing LoRA fine-tuning on the Qwen3-235B MoE model using 24 GB consumer GPUs, highlighting its memory efficiency.
- **Maximum Sequence Length**: RoundPipe consistently supported longer sequence lengths. It extended the maximum sequence length by 4.7× to 7.3× compared to the next-best baseline. For instance, it could handle 73K tokens for Qwen3-1.7B and 31K tokens for Qwen3-235B-LoRA. The ability to offload model states and stage-boundary activations to CPU memory and recompute layer-internal activations contributed to this capability.

#### 4.2. End-to-End Performance on 8× A800 SXM Server

- **Training Throughput**: On datacenter-grade hardware, RoundPipe delivered competitive throughput, ranging from 0.98× to 1.47× that of other state-of-the-art systems. For smaller models (1.7B and 8B), data parallelism (DP) systems on A800s could match or slightly exceed RoundPipe's performance due to high-bandwidth NVLink. However, for larger models (20B+), RoundPipe demonstrated superior performance by generating less communication than DP/TP and fewer bubbles than existing PP systems.
- **Performance Gap Bridging**: A key finding was that RoundPipe's throughput on 4090s achieved at least 76% of the throughput of existing state-of-the-art frameworks running on A800s across all models. This indicates that RoundPipe effectively narrows the performance gap between consumer and datacenter hardware.
- **Maximum Sequence Length**: RoundPipe increased the maximum sequence length by 1.19× to 5.62× over baselines on A800s, supporting 192K to 288K tokens for smaller models and maintaining its lead for larger ones, again due to its efficient memory management and offloading.

#### 4.3. Scalability

- **Multi-GPU Scaling**: RoundPipe exhibited near-linear throughput scaling from 1 to 8 RTX 4090 GPUs across all model sizes, demonstrating the robustness and efficiency of its partitioning and overlap mechanisms.
- **Sequence Length Independence**: A notable architectural advantage identified was that the maximum sequence length supported by RoundPipe remained independent of the GPU count (from 1 to 8 GPUs). This is a direct consequence of the Computation Dispatch Paradigm, where GPUs only hold data for their actively assigned stage, with other model states residing in host memory.

#### 4.4. Sequence Length Sensitivity

- RoundPipe maintained robust performance for Qwen3-1.7B on 8× RTX 4090s across a wide range of sequence lengths (from 512 to 64K tokens). Throughput decreased smoothly with increasing attention cost, indicating effective support for both short and very long contexts without prohibitive overheads.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Pipeline Schedule (Bubble Ratio)**: Simulations showed that RoundPipe-sync reduced pipeline bubbles by 23% to 55% relative to the best baselines (GPipe, 1F1B, Interleaved-1F1B, Looped-BFS). RoundPipe, with its asynchronous optimizer updates, further decreased the absolute bubble ratio to below 4.5%, effectively eliminating inter-iteration bubbles. The remaining idle time was attributed to minor stage execution imbalances, which the automated partitioning algorithm successfully minimized. The automated stage-splitting algorithm proved very fast, completing partitions in milliseconds for smaller models and 1.47 seconds for Qwen3-235B.
- **Fine-Grained Parameter Consistency Protocol**: Comparing RoundPipe's event-based consistency protocol with a blocking copy baseline, the protocol reduced iteration time by a substantial 2.6 to 14 seconds per iteration, scaling with the size of trainable parameters. This confirmed the critical role of fine-grained, per-layer event signaling in achieving the full benefits of asynchronous optimizer updates by overlapping weight and gradient synchronization with GPU computation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoundPipe presents a significant contribution to the field of large-scale deep learning, particularly in the context of resource-constrained environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Democratization of Large-Scale AI Training**: By enabling efficient fine-tuning of multi-billion parameter LLMs, including highly demanding Mixture-of-Experts models, on cost-effective consumer-grade GPUs, RoundPipe significantly lowers the barrier to entry for advanced AI research and development. This allows smaller organizations, academic researchers, and individual practitioners to train models that previously required access to expensive datacenter infrastructure. The demonstrated performance on RTX 4090s, reaching over 76% of A800 performance, directly bridges the gap between consumer and datacenter hardware.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability**: The system offers substantial speedups (1.48–2.16× on 4090s) and significantly extends the maximum trainable sequence lengths (4.7–7.3× on 4090s) compared to existing solutions. This allows for training larger models and processing more complex, longer contexts, which are critical for advanced LLM applications like reasoning and video generation. The near-linear scaling across multiple GPUs and robust performance across varying sequence lengths further underscore its practical utility.
- **Novel Architectural Paradigm**: The introduction of the "Computation Dispatch Paradigm" represents a fundamental re-thinking of pipeline parallelism for heterogeneous memory systems. By treating GPUs as stateless execution workers and dynamically dispatching computations, RoundPipe overcomes the limitations of static stage-to-GPU binding, which has historically caused significant pipeline inefficiencies. This paradigm could influence future designs of distributed training frameworks.
- **Comprehensive System Design**: RoundPipe integrates several sophisticated mechanisms, including a priority-aware transfer scheduling engine, a fine-grained distributed event-based synchronization protocol, and an automated stage-splitting algorithm. These components collectively address critical challenges related to data movement, consistency, and load balancing, forming a robust and practical solution. The ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of each design choice, particularly the significant reduction in pipeline bubbles and synchronization overhead.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: As an open-source Python library, RoundPipe provides a platform for further research and development in efficient distributed training. Its design principles, especially the dynamic dispatching and fine-grained control over asynchronous operations, could inspire new approaches for optimizing performance on diverse hardware configurations.
- **Practical Applicability**: The ability to fine-tune a 235B MoE model on consumer hardware is a concrete demonstration of its practical impact. This capability was previously largely inaccessible outside of highly resourced environments. The automatic partitioning and consistency protocols also reduce the engineering effort required, making the system user-friendly.

In summary, RoundPipe not only provides a high-performance solution for current LLM training challenges but also lays architectural groundwork that could shape the future of distributed deep learning on commodity hardware.
