# Rethinking Agentic Reinforcement Learning In Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive analysis synthesizes the conceptual foundations, methodological innovations, and effective designs of LLM-based Agentic Reinforcement Learning. The work details advancements in agent components (Action, Planning, Memory, Tools) and adapted RL algorithms, while also identifying critical challenges and outlining future research directions.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning struggles with the open-ended, uncertain nature of real-world tasks, as agents are specialized for narrow, static environments.
- Conventional Large Language Models (LLMs) function as passive autoregressive predictors, lacking inherent mechanisms for dynamic decision-making, long-term planning, or sustained interaction.
- There is a need to bridge the gap between passive language models and truly autonomous, self-improving systems capable of meta-reasoning and multi-step decision-making.

## Method

- Provides a comprehensive ==survey and analytical framework== for LLM-based Agentic RL, reviewing conceptual foundations, methodological innovations, and effective designs.
- Deconstructs LLM-based agent architecture into four fundamental components: ==Action==, ==Planning==, ==Memory==, and ==Tools==, with key innovations and mathematical formulations for each.
- Analyzes RL algorithms (==PPO==, ==DPO==, ==GRPO==, ==GSPO==, ==DAPO==, ==SAPO==) adapted for fine-tuning LLMs.

## Results

- Structured overview of the shift toward LLM-based autonomous agents with **planning, tool use, and memory** capabilities emerging from RL integration.
- Categorized methodological innovations across **four agent components** with focused review of RL algorithms.
- Outlined challenges hindering Agentic RL with LLMs across environments, trustworthy AI, capability boundaries, and system integration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs are undergoing a paradigm shift from passive text generators to proactive, autonomous agents, enabled by the integration of RL principles for dynamic decision-making and long-term goal pursuit.
> - Agentic RL confers critical cognitive-like capabilities to LLMs, including long-horizon planning, strategic tool utilization, and persistent memory, overcoming limitations such as knowledge cut-offs and static context windows.
> - The field faces significant challenges related to dynamic environment optimization, ensuring trustworthy AI (e.g., mitigating hallucinations), overcoming inherent LLM capability boundaries, and developing robust system-level integrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the evolving field of Artificial Intelligence, specifically at the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Language Models (LLMs). Traditionally, RL has focused on training specialized agents in constrained environments with predefined reward functions. The advent of powerful LLMs, however, has enabled the development of autonomous agents capable of handling more complex, open-ended tasks in uncertain, real-world environments. This shift is characterized by the integration of "cognitive-like capabilities" such as meta-reasoning, self-reflection, and multi-step decision-making directly into the learning loop of LLMs, moving beyond their conventional role as passive text generators.

This work addresses a gap in the existing literature by integrating all three domains—LLMs, Agents, and Reinforcement Learning—into a unified analysis. Previous surveys have often focused on these areas individually or in pairwise combinations, such as LLMs and Agents, or RL and LLMs. The paper references existing multi-domain studies like which targets search and recommendation systems, and which provides a library-centric overview. This paper aims to offer a more comprehensive and integrated perspective on LLM-based Agentic RL, providing insights into its conceptual foundations, methodological innovations, and effective designs. It reflects a pivotal shift from static text generation to dynamic decision-making, where LLMs are formulated as active policies operating within Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes, optimizing for cumulative returns over entire trajectories rather than instantaneous rewards.

The work identifies the emergence of a new frontier for complex applications enabled by Agentic RL, including autonomous software development, scientific discovery automation, sophisticated web navigation, mathematical reasoning, financial analysis, and embodied AI. By synthesizing existing knowledge and identifying critical challenges, the paper contributes to a structured understanding of this rapidly evolving paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a detailed and integrated analysis of Agentic Reinforcement Learning in the context of Large Language Models. The authors aim to explore the conceptual foundations, methodological innovations, and effective designs that underpin this emerging trend.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations:
- **Paradigm Shift in LLMs**: The transition of LLMs from passive text generators to proactive agents capable of dynamic decision-making in complex, open-ended tasks. This shift requires integrating RL principles to enable capabilities such as goal-setting, long-term planning, dynamic strategy adaptation, and interactive reasoning.
- **Limitations of Traditional RL and LLM Approaches**: Conventional RL often relies on static objectives and episodic interactions, while standard LLM techniques like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) frequently treat responses as independent instances, focusing on single-step preferences. Agentic RL, in contrast, necessitates a continuous feedback loop and optimization for cumulative returns over entire trajectories.
- **Emergence of Cognitive-like Capabilities**: The integration of RL with LLMs enables the development of agents with advanced cognitive faculties, including long-horizon planning (decomposing queries into sub-goals), strategic tool utilization (interfacing with external APIs to overcome knowledge cut-off and factual hallucinations), and persistent memory mechanisms (retaining context across interactions).
- **Expansion of Application Scenarios**: These enhanced capabilities unlock new frontiers for complex applications beyond simple chatbot interactions. Examples include autonomous software development, scientific discovery automation, advanced web navigation, mathematical reasoning, financial analysis, and embodied AI.
- **Gap in Integrated Surveys**: While prior surveys have covered LLMs, Agents, or RL individually or in pairwise combinations, a comprehensive integration of all three domains is identified as lacking. The paper aims to fill this void by offering a unified perspective on the current state of LLM-based Agentic RL, its underlying mechanisms, and its associated challenges.

In essence, the motivation is to offer a "deep insight" into this synergistic framework, facilitate rapid assimilation of the current industry state, and outline critical challenges and future directions to guide further research and development in building robust LLM-based Agentic RL systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a survey and analytical approach rather than presenting novel experimental research. The methodology involves a structured decomposition and detailed examination of the key components and techniques within LLM-based Agentic RL. The authors aim to provide a "deep looking for formulas, effective designs and innovative skills, adopting a concise format to rapidly assimilate the current state of the industry."

The approach is organized into three main sections:
1. **Designs**: This section categorizes and explains the foundational components that enable autonomous agents and the specific Reinforcement Learning algorithms used to train them.
 - **Paradigm of LLM-Based Agents**: The paper deconstructs an intelligent agent into four core components:
 - **Action**: Defines the means by which an agent interacts with its environment (e.g., predefined vs. dynamically generated actions). It presents formulas for policy ($\pi_\theta(a|s)$) and Q-function ($Q_\pi(s,a)$) and discusses works addressing action space limitations, exploration, and planning hallucinations.
 - **Planning**: Describes the agent's ability to simulate future trajectories without real-world execution. It introduces Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and model-predictive control (MPC) formulas and reviews research on overcoming multi-step reasoning difficulties, dynamic planning, and generalization gaps in long-horizon tasks.
 - **Memory**: Explores mechanisms for storing and retrieving historical information to overcome partial observability. This includes recurrent networks (LSTM), replay buffers (DQN), and external memory banks (vector databases). It highlights works addressing the absence of native long-term memory, learned control over storage/retrieval, and context length explosion.
 - **Tools**: Discusses how agents extend their capabilities by interfacing with external APIs or devices. It frames tool selection as a classification problem and explains the Reasoning + Acting (ReAct) paradigm. It reviews works on interleaved reasoning and action, fine-tuning with diverse trajectories, and integrating computational tools into reasoning.
 - **Paradigm of Reinforcement Learning**: This subsection analyzes and observes key RL algorithms relevant to LLM fine-tuning and agent training. For each, it introduces formulas and principles, followed by elaborations on derivative styles and contributions of key works:
 - **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**: Discusses its clipped objective function ($J_{PPO}(\theta)$), stability, and use in actor-critic architectures.
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)**: Explains its reward-model-free approach for aligning LLMs with human preferences using a binary cross-entropy loss ($J_{DPO}(\theta)$).
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: Describes its critic-model-free approach, calculating relative advantage by normalizing rewards within a group of sampled outputs ($J_{GRPO}(\theta)$).
 - **Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO)**: Introduces sequence-level importance weighting for reduced gradient variance and improved stability ($J_{GSPO}(\theta)$).
 - **Decouple Clip and Dynamic sAmpling Policy Optimization (DAPO)**: Details innovations like asymmetric clipping, dynamic sampling, token-level policy gradient loss, and overlong reward shaping ($J_{DAPO}(\theta)$).
 - **Soft Adaptive Policy Optimization (SAPO)**: Presents a smooth, temperature-controlled gating function to address instability from token-level importance ratios ($J_{SAPO}(\theta)$).
 - **Other RLs**: Briefly mentions SimPO, KTO, VinePPO, VAPO, PSGPO, GMPO, TreePO, and PAPO, highlighting their specific innovations (e.g., reference-free rewards, refined credit assignment, process-based supervision, geometric mean optimization, tree-structured search, multimodal reasoning).

2. **Challenges**: This section identifies critical obstacles in building LLM-based Agentic RL systems, categorizing them into:
 - **Environments**: Focuses on the need for dynamic, optimizable training environments, citing work on automatic environment adaptation.
 - **Trustworthy AI**: Addresses issues like misinformation, hallucinations, and security vulnerabilities, presenting solutions like topology-guided security frameworks and factuality-aware RL.
 - **Capability Boundaries**: Discusses limitations such as inherent LLM capacity, on-policy constraints, vast action spaces, and sparse rewards, introducing hybrid-policy optimization, hierarchical RL, and summarization-based training.
 - **System**: Highlights challenges in unifying different task scenarios and addresses the need for online training environments, rigorous benchmarking, and real-device deployment for GUI agents.

3. **Conclusion**: Summarizes the insights gained and reiterates the ongoing challenges and future directions.

The methodology is thus a comprehensive literature review structured around the foundational components of agents, the relevant RL algorithms, and the practical challenges encountered in the field. It systematically explains concepts with mathematical formulations where applicable and references specific research papers for each innovation discussed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, the "findings and results" are the comprehensive synthesis and structured analysis of the current landscape of Agentic Reinforcement Learning in Large Language Models. The paper does not present new empirical data or experimental results but rather aggregates, categorizes, and elucidates the advancements, methodologies, and challenges reported in existing research.

The main findings and results are presented through the detailed categorization of designs and challenges:

**I. Foundational Components of LLM-Based Agents (Designs - Paradigm of LLM-Based Agents):**
- **Action Mechanisms**: LLM agents are evolving beyond predefined action spaces to dynamic frameworks that allow for on-the-fly program generation and execution (e.g.,). Innovations like SAND improve action deliberation via self-consistency and execution-guided critique, while KnowAgent constrains planning with action knowledge bases to mitigate planning hallucinations. PRAct uses textual gradients from a reflective engine to derive actionable principles.
- **Planning Capabilities**: LLMs exhibit limitations in multi-step reasoning and goal-directed planning despite individual component capabilities. Research focuses on brain-inspired architectures for autonomous coordination, dynamic planning frameworks to flexibly allocate test-time compute, and adaptive global plan-based paradigms like for long-horizon decision-making. MCTS and model-predictive control are foundational planning techniques.
- **Memory Integration**: A critical finding is the persistent absence of native long-term memory in LLMs. Solutions include external memory banks, RL frameworks for active memory management, and constant-memory approaches for multi-turn tasks. Scalable, memory-centric architectures like Mem0 are introduced, with graph-based enhancements for relational context.
- **Tool Utilization**: Agents transcend parametric knowledge limitations by interfacing with external tools. The ReAct paradigm effectively interleaves reasoning and actions. Fine-tuning with diverse trajectories from multiple tasks improves robustness, and frameworks like ReTool integrate tool usage into long-form reasoning with RL feedback for strategic tool invocation.

**II. Advanced Reinforcement Learning Paradigms for LLMs (Designs - Paradigm of Reinforcement Learning):**
- **Policy Gradient Evolution**: The paper details the evolution of RL algorithms from standard PPO, known for stability, to more specialized methods for LLM fine-tuning.
- **Preference-Based Optimization**: DPO emerges as a simplified, stable, and efficient alternative to traditional RLHF by directly optimizing against human preferences without a separate reward model.
- **Group-Based Advantage Estimation**: GRPO is highlighted for eliminating the resource-intensive critic model by calculating relative advantage within groups of sampled outputs, effective for reasoning tasks. This concept is further refined by GSPO, which introduces sequence-level importance weighting for reduced gradient variance.
- **Advanced Policy Optimization Techniques**: DAPO (ByteDance, Tsinghua University) builds on GRPO with innovations like asymmetric clipping (Clip-Higher), dynamic sampling, token-level policy gradient loss, and overlong reward shaping, demonstrating superior performance in mathematical reasoning. SAPO (Qwen team) addresses instability in policy updates with a smooth, temperature-controlled gating function, particularly for MoE models.
- **Diverse RL Objectives**: The survey also identifies other significant RL advancements, including SimPO (reference-free reward using average log probability), KTO (Kahneman-Tversky Optimization for maximizing human utility), VinePPO (unbiased Monte Carlo estimates for reasoning), VAPO (tailored for reasoning models), PSGPO (process-based supervision for code generation), GMPO (geometric mean for reward outlier robustness), TreePO (tree-structured search for exploration), and PAPO (perception-aware optimization for multimodal reasoning).

**III. Critical Challenges Identified:**
- **Dynamic Environments**: A key challenge is moving beyond static training environments to dynamic, optimizable systems where environments themselves can be adapted through RL (e.g., RLAnything).
- **Trustworthy AI**: LLM-based agents face significant issues with misinformation, hallucinations, and security vulnerabilities. Solutions include topology-guided security frameworks like G-Safeguard and factuality-aware RL methods like FSPO to mitigate increased hallucinations from reasoning-oriented RL fine-tuning.
- **Capability Boundaries**: The inherent limitations of base LLMs (on-policy constraints, vast action spaces, sparse rewards) restrict advanced reasoning. RL-PLUS proposes hybrid-policy optimization to transcend these limits, while hierarchical RL (HiPER) addresses long-horizon tasks, and summarization-based training manages context length explosion in tool use. Research emphasizes real end-to-end tool-use trajectories and exploration-friendly techniques.
- **System Integration**: The challenge of unifying diverse task scenarios (e.g., personal conversations, software engineering, GUI tasks) into a single learning loop. OpenClaw-RL proposes a unified policy for universal next-state signals, and ClawGUI offers an integrated lifecycle for training, evaluating, and deploying GUI agents on real devices. MetaClaw introduces continual meta-learning for skill evolution.

In summary, the paper's main findings detail the conceptual frameworks, algorithmic innovations, and practical challenges that characterize the current state of Agentic RL with LLMs, underscoring a field rapidly evolving towards more autonomous and cognitively capable AI systems.
