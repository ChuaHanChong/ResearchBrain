# Few-Shot Task Learning through Inverse Generative Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT, Harvard, and Johns Hopkins developed Few-Shot Task Learning through Inverse Generative Modeling (FTL-IGM), a framework that leverages a pretrained conditional diffusion model to infer latent task concepts from a few demonstrations of novel tasks. This approach enables agents to generalize learned behaviors to new initial states and synthesize new behaviors by composing learned concepts.

## Problem

- Intelligent agents struggle to learn novel task concepts from limited demonstrations and generalize this knowledge to new situations, unlike human learners.
- Traditional imitation learning methods like Behavior Cloning suffer from covariate shift, hindering generalization to new initial states or environments.
- Reinforcement learning and Inverse Reinforcement Learning are typically data-intensive and computationally expensive for acquiring new skills.

## Method

- The **Few-Shot** ==Task Learning== through Inverse Generative Modeling (FTL-IGM) framework involves two phases: pretraining and inverse modeling.
- A conditional diffusion model is pretrained on a large dataset of paired trajectories and explicit task concepts.
- To learn a new task from few demonstrations, the pretrained diffusion model is frozen, and a latent concept representation (or multiple components) is optimized via backpropagation to best explain the observed demonstrations.

## Results

- FTL-IGM consistently outperformed baselines such as Behavior Cloning and VAEs in few-shot concept learning across five diverse domains, including object rearrangement and autonomous driving.
- The method demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, successfully applying learned concepts to novel initial states and composing them with other known concepts.
- In real-world table-top manipulation, FTL-IGM learned a novel "elevated pushing" concept with **55%** accuracy, and achieved competitive closed-loop performance in goal-oriented navigation and autonomous driving.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating few-shot task learning as an inverse generative modeling problem allows leveraging strong priors learned from large datasets to infer task concepts efficiently from sparse demonstration data.
> - By learning an explicit latent representation of task concepts, the system can generalize behaviors to novel initial states and environments, mitigating issues like covariate shift.
> - The inherent compositional properties of diffusion models enable the system to learn novel tasks and then synthesize behaviors by combining them with existing concepts.
