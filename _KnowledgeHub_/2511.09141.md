# RGMP: Recurrent Geometric-prior Multimodal Policy for Generalizable Humanoid Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Wuhan University, Hubei University, and the University of Macau developed the Recurrent Geometric-prior Multimodal Policy (RGMP), an end-to-end framework integrating geometric-semantic reasoning with data-efficient visuomotor control. RGMP achieved an 87% task success rate in generalization tests to unseen objects, demonstrating 5x greater data efficiency compared to Diffusion Policy for learning manipulation skills.

## Problem

- Current data-driven humanoid robot manipulation models often neglect geometric reasoning, leading to ambiguity in skill selection for objects with varying shapes or in unseen environments.
- Learning precise action policies for dexterous manipulation requires extensive training data, resulting in high data collection costs and limiting practical deployment.
- Existing methods struggle with generalization to novel objects or dynamic environments, often overfitting to specific demonstration features.

## Method

- Introduced the ==Recurrent Geometric==-prior Multimodal Policy (**RGMP**), an end-to-end framework combining geometric-semantic skill reasoning with data-efficient visuomotor control.
- Developed the Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS) which integrates VLM output with geometric priors (e.g., object bounding box, common sense rules) to enable fine-grained and context-appropriate skill selection.
- Designed the Adaptive Recursive Gaussian Network (ARGN) with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to adaptively model spatial dependencies, mitigate overfitting with limited data, and refine 6-DoF action predictions.

## Results

- RGMP achieved an average **87%** task success rate in generalization tests to unseen objects (Coke cans, spray bottles, human hands) at random positions, outperforming Diffusion Policy (**70%**) and Dex-VLA (**77%**).
- The Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS) component consistently improved skill selection accuracy (Acc_s) by 15-**25%** across various objects when integrated with Qwen-VL.
- RGMP demonstrated a **5x** greater data efficiency, achieving a **98%** success rate with only 40 training samples for a grasping task, compared to Diffusion Policy which required 200 samples for the same success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating geometric reasoning into multimodal language models significantly improves the robot's ability to select appropriate manipulation skills for diverse and unseen objects.
> - An adaptive recursive network with spatial memory mechanisms can effectively model global spatial relationships and learn robust visuomotor representations from limited demonstrations, drastically reducing data requirements.
> - Factorizing 6-DoF trajectories using a Gaussian Mixture Model refines action predictions by capturing complex, multi-modal action distributions, which is crucial for precise dexterous manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are being developed to perform diverse tasks requiring human-level skills, including adaptive decision-making and complex manipulation. Current research in this field predominantly utilizes data-driven approaches, which often require extensive training datasets to achieve robust multimodal decision-making and generalizable visuomotor control. These methods, such as those employing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like PaLM-E and InstructBLIP, or learning-based action generation techniques like diffusion models and transformer-based architectures, have demonstrated capabilities in task-specific applications and trajectory generation.

However, several limitations persist within the broader research landscape:
1. **Neglect of Geometric Reasoning:** Existing data-driven models frequently overlook geometric reasoning and spatial awareness. This omission restricts robots' ability to perceive contextual information effectively in unseen environments and resolve ambiguities in skill selection (e.g., distinguishing between grasping and pinching based on object shape).
2. **Data Inefficiency:** Learning precise action policies, particularly for dexterous manipulation, often necessitates large-scale training data (e.g., thousands of trajectories). This demand leads to high data collection costs and computational complexity, limiting practical deployment, especially for physical robots where data acquisition is labor-intensive. Imitation learning methods, while partially addressing this, can overfit to specific demonstration features and exhibit limited generalization to unseen objects.
3. **Generalization Challenges:** Many models struggle with cross-domain adaptability and generalization to novel objects or dynamic environments. They may also suffer from suboptimal performance in time-sensitive applications due to slow inference rates.

This work positions itself to address these identified gaps by proposing an end-to-end framework that explicitly integrates geometric-semantic reasoning with data-efficient visuomotor control, aiming to enhance generalization capability and reduce data requirements in humanoid robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome limitations in current humanoid robot manipulation systems, specifically regarding generalization to unseen scenarios and data efficiency in learning control policies. The motivations are articulated through two fundamental questions the authors aim to address:

**I) How can robots leverage spatial-geometric reasoning to enable feasible skill selection?**

- **Motivation:** Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate proficiency in interpreting semantic intent from language and vision inputs and generating task plans. However, these models often struggle to map abstract instructions to contextually appropriate robotic skills when confronted with variations in object shapes or unseen environments. For instance, a VLM might face ambiguity in choosing between a "grasp" or "pinch" action for objects of different geometries. This limitation stems from an insufficient integration of spatial object geometry (such as bounding boxes or shapes) with semantic task specifications. In dynamic environments, where the feasibility of a skill heavily depends on generalized spatial reasoning, this gap is exacerbated. The authors motivate the need for a mechanism that imbues robots with human-like decision-making processes, combining visual geometry and task semantics to select appropriate manipulation skills.

**II) How can the inherent mechanisms of robots learn generalized ability with limited demonstrations?**

- **Motivation:** Acquiring large datasets for training precise action policies in robotics is a significant challenge due to the high cost and labor intensity of data collection. While advanced models like diffusion models and transformer-based architectures can generate trajectories, they typically require extensive training data (e.g., 10,000+ trajectories) and can be computationally expensive at inference. Imitation learning methods, though leveraging human priors, frequently overfit to demonstration-specific features, resulting in limited success rates when encountering unseen objects. The core challenge lies in effectively disentangling task-invariant visual features from task-specific motion patterns and developing methods that can generalize robustly even with sparse demonstration data. The authors seek to develop a framework that enables robots to learn generalized manipulation capabilities from a constrained number of demonstrations, thereby improving data efficiency and practical deployability.

To address these motivations, the authors developed the Recurrent Geometric-prior Multimodal Policy (RGMP), an architecture designed to integrate multimodal spatial-geometric reasoning with data-efficient visuomotor control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RGMP (Recurrent Geometric-prior Multimodal Policy) framework is an end-to-end architecture that combines geometric-semantic skill reasoning with data-efficient visuomotor control. It comprises two main components: the Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS) and the Adaptive Recursive Gaussian Network (ARGN). The policy learns to infer 3D spatial relationships from RGB images by associating visual cues with actions, utilizing an implicit representation rather than explicit 3D reconstruction.

#### 3.1. Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS)

The GSS is designed to address the challenge of fine-grained skill selection for diverse object shapes and unseen scenarios, a limitation observed in traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It operates in two stages:

1. **Object Identification and Localization:** A VLM (Qwen-VL, in this case) interprets human commands (e.g., speech input) and identifies the target object within the observed image. Concurrently, it localizes the object by providing a bounding box.
2. **Skill Selection with Geometric Priors:** Based on the bounding box and the output of the VLM, the system analyzes the target object's common sense information. This includes its relative position and shape information, which is extracted using a model like Yolov8n-seg. The VLM then synthesizes operational directives by integrating its contextual framework (a predefined set of instructions, prompts, and common sense examples) with geometric-based prior reasoning. These geometric priors are described as plug-and-play, modular, and minimal, comprising approximately 20 rule-based constraints (e.g., basic shape/affordance heuristics like "Cylinder, Grasp" or "Squashed, Lift up"). The system subsequently selects a pretrained skill model from a skill library based on the GSS output. The planning function, denoted as `P = plan(I, O | C)`, takes user instruction `I`, visual observation `O`, and predefined context `C` (examples of instruction-observation-plan triplets) to generate an action plan `P`.

The GSS aims to provide a human-like decision-making process, combining visual geometry and task semantics to choose appropriate skills, even for unseen objects and scenes.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Recursive Gaussian Network (ARGN)

The ARGN is developed to model comprehensive spatial dependencies between the robot and target objects adaptively, aiming to mitigate overfitting in scenarios with limited training data and improve visuomotor representation learning. It focuses on identifying end-effector positions relevant to task execution by establishing global connections through recursive operations, forming a spatial memory of observed images.

The ARGN processing pipeline involves several key mechanisms:

1. **Adaptive Decay Mechanism (ADM):** In Stage 1, the initial input feature map (`F0`) is processed by the Spatial Mixing Block. The ADM generates content-adaptive decay factors `W` using a convolutional operation and a Sigmoid activation function. These factors dynamically control the decay rate of historical memory, preventing the loss of critical spatial information and adaptively amplifying weights for task-critical visual patches.
2. **Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE):** RoPE is applied to encode positional information through rotational transformations. This enhances sensitivity to relative spatial offsets within image patches without requiring learnable position parameters.
3. **Recursive Computation in Spatial Mixing Block:** After applying RoPE, the feature maps (`Ks` and `Vs`) are sliced into `16x16` image patches. Recursive computation is then employed within the Spatial Mixing Block to progressively model global spatial relationships across these patches. This involves cumulative memory mechanisms for key and value products, with learnable position compensation (`u`) for local sensitivity and content-adaptive decay factors (`W`) from ADM. The output of this block is modulated by a gating factor to contribute to the current state.
4. **Channel Mixing Block:** The output from the Spatial Mixing Block is residually connected with the initial input. This combined feature map then passes through a Channel Mixing Block, which reallocates channel-wise feature weights to facilitate feature extraction.
5. **Multi-Scale Feature Fusion:** Subsequent stages (Stage 2-3) repeat the processing, and multi-scale features (`F1`, `F2`, `F3`) from these stages are fused using learnable weights. This fusion integrates visual cues from different scales.
6. **Initial Action Prediction:** The fused feature map (`Ff`) is then used to generate an initial predicted action (`ain`) via a linear layer.
7. **Loss Function:** The model is trained to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the initial predicted action (`ain`) and the ground truth action (`aground`) obtained from human demonstrations.

#### 3.3. Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) for Action Refinement

The ARGN framework incorporates a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to refine action predictions, particularly for 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (6-DoF) robotic arms.

- **Motivation for GMM:** A single Gaussian model tends to regress to the mean, which can suppress distinct action modes and lead to suboptimal control accuracy. In contrast, GMM can model separate action clusters, each with its own mean and covariance, allowing for a more accurate representation of the action distribution. This is crucial for capturing the complexities of multi-joint robot motions.
- **GMM Application:** The GMM uses `K=6` components (corresponding to a 6-DoF arm), each defined by a prior (`αk`), mean (`μk`), and covariance (`Σk`). These parameters are estimated using the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm to maximize data likelihood, thereby capturing latent structures in the joint space.
- **Final Action Selection:** The initial action prediction (`ain`) from ARGN is compared to the GMM clusters using Mahalanobis distance (`lk`). The final action (`a*`) is determined as the center of the closest cluster. This mechanism explicitly models goal-conditional density under data scarcity.

The overall training pipeline involves iterating through epochs, processing demonstrations to obtain joint space and observation data, and optimizing the ARGN and GMM parameters. The inference pipeline uses the GSS to select a skill and then the ARGN with GMM for precise action execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The RGMP framework was evaluated on two physical robotic platforms: a humanoid robot (focusing on upper limb tasks) and a desktop dual-arm robot, to assess its effectiveness, generalization, and data efficiency. The evaluation criteria included skill success rate (Acc_s), execution accuracy (Acc_t), and overall success rate (Acc = Acc_s × Acc_t). Training involved 120 trajectories for the skill library, with specific experiments using a low-data setup of 40 interaction samples for particular tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison and Generalization Capability

The RGMP framework was compared against several state-of-the-art methods including ResNet50, Transformer, ManiSkill2-1st, Octo, OpenVLA, RDT-1b, Dex-VLA, and Diffusion Policy (DP).

- **Overall Generalization (Table 2):** In generalization tests, where models were trained on only 40 Fanta can grasping demonstrations and evaluated on unseen objects (Coke cans, spray bottles, human hands) at random positions, RGMP achieved an average success rate of 0.87. This performance exceeded Diffusion Policy (0.70), Dex-VLA (0.77), and other baselines. Specifically, RGMP achieved 0.98 for Fanta, 0.78 for Coke, 0.81 for spray, and 0.90 for human hands, demonstrating its ability to generalize across novel object categories and positions.
- **Diverse Object Manipulation (Table 1):** RGMP consistently showed higher Acc, Acc_s, and Acc_t across regular and irregular objects (Fanta, Sprite, tissue paper, deformed Coke cans, human hands) compared to Qwen-VL combined with various vision backbones (ResNet50, Transformer, ManiSkill2-1st, Diffusion Policy). For instance, with Diffusion Policy as the base, GSS improved overall accuracy for Fanta from 0.49 to 0.65.
- **ManiSkill2 Simulator (Figure 6):** RGMP demonstrated the highest performance across five non-grasp ManiSkill2 tasks (Push Chair, Move Bucket, Plug Charger, Open Cabinet Door, Open Cabinet Drawer), indicating its transferability and generalization capability to more complex manipulation sequences composed of atomic primitives.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to quantify the contribution of key components within RGMP.

- **Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS) (Table 1):** The GSS component significantly improved skill selection accuracy (Acc_s) across various objects and underlying vision models. When integrated with Qwen-VL, GSS consistently yielded a 15-25% accuracy improvement in skill selection compared to Qwen-VL alone. This suggests that infusing geometric inductive biases into the VLM enhances its ability to select appropriate skills.
- **Adaptive Recursive Gaussian Network (ARGN) and Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) (Table 3):** The integration of GMM with ARGN was shown to enhance overall performance. For the task of picking squashed Coke, ARGN with GSS+GMM achieved an Acc of 0.69, while Diffusion Policy with GSS+GMM achieved 0.55, and Diffusion Policy without GMM achieved 0.49. This indicates that GMM contributes to refining action predictions and improving accuracy, particularly for complex object manipulations.
- **Components of ARGN (Table 4):** The contributions of Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), Spatial Mixing Blocks (SMB), and Channel Mixing Blocks (CMB) were individually validated. Their combined use yielded the highest accuracy across all tested objects (Fanta: 0.98, Coke: 0.78, Spray: 0.81, Hand: 0.90), underscoring the importance of each component in capturing spatial dependencies and enhancing visuomotor control.

#### 4.3. Data Efficiency

- **Comparison with Diffusion Policy (Table 5):** RGMP demonstrated a notable improvement in data efficiency. It achieved a success rate of 0.98 with only 40 training samples for grasping a Fanta can, which is a 5× reduction in data compared to Diffusion Policy, which required 200 samples to achieve the same success rate. This indicates that RGMP can learn robust policies with significantly fewer demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RGMP framework introduces an approach to address fundamental challenges in humanoid robot manipulation, primarily concerning generalization to unseen scenarios and data-efficient policy learning.

The **Geometric-prior Skill Selector (GSS)** component significantly enhances the robot's ability to interpret human commands and select appropriate manipulation skills by integrating geometric reasoning with semantic task planning. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured environments where objects vary in shape and context. By requiring minimal spatial common sense tuning (e.g., 20 rule-based constraints), the GSS offers a pathway for robots to make more human-aligned decisions, reducing ambiguity in skill selection (e.g., grasping vs. pinching) and improving robustness in diverse, unseen scenes. This implies a more intuitive and adaptable human-robot interaction paradigm.

The **Adaptive Recursive Gaussian Network (ARGN)**, combined with the **Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)**, provides a mechanism for data-efficient visuomotor control. By dynamically modeling spatial dependencies, employing an Adaptive Decay Mechanism, and leveraging GMM for 6-DoF trajectory factorization, RGMP demonstrates a 5× greater data efficiency compared to state-of-the-art models like Diffusion Policy. This reduction in the requirement for extensive training datasets has substantial implications for the practical deployment of humanoid robots, as it lowers the cost and labor associated with data collection. The ability to learn generalized manipulation skills from sparse demonstrations means robots can be trained and adapted more quickly to new tasks and environments, accelerating their integration into real-world applications.

The overall framework's achievement of an 87% task success rate in generalization tests, alongside its data efficiency, underscores its potential for cross-domain generalization. This suggests that RGMP could facilitate the development of more versatile and data-efficient robotic systems, capable of robustly manipulating a wide range of objects in various contexts without exhaustive retraining. The explicit neuro-symbolic coordination employed by RGMP represents an advancement in creating scalable adaptive manipulation foundations.

Future work outlined by the authors, focusing on functional generalization (inferring trajectories for similar objects based on one primary object's function), further indicates a trajectory towards greater autonomy and efficiency in robotic learning, potentially eliminating the need for extensive teaching for every new object or task variation.
