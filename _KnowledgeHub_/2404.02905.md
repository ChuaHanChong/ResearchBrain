# Visual Autoregressive Modeling: Scalable Image Generation via Next-Scale Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Visual AutoRegressive (VAR) modeling, which redefines image generation by predicting entire token maps at progressively finer scales rather than individual tokens sequentially. This approach enables GPT-style AR models to surpass diffusion transformers in image quality and efficiency, demonstrating power-law scaling laws and zero-shot generalization capabilities akin to large language models.

## Problem

- Traditional visual autoregressive (AR) models flatten 2D image feature maps into 1D sequences, violating mathematical premises of AR and destroying spatial locality.
- Conventional visual AR models suffer from severe computational inefficiency, scaling at O(N^6) for N x N latent tokens, making high-resolution generation prohibitively slow.
- Existing visual AR methods lack the ability to perform certain zero-shot generalizations and have not demonstrated the robust power-law scaling laws observed in Large Language Models.

## Method

- The paper proposes **Visual AutoRegressive (VAR)** modeling, which shifts from "==next-token prediction==" to "==next-scale prediction==" for images, generating image token maps hierarchically from coarse to fine.
- A multi-scale ==VQVAE== is trained to encode images into K discrete token maps, where each scale captures additional detail via a ==residual design==, ensuring dependencies align with the autoregressive structure.
- A ==decoder-only transformer== predicts an entire token map in parallel, conditioned on previously generated coarser maps, reducing computational complexity from O(N^6) to O(N^4) by minimizing autoregressive steps.

## Results

- VAR achieved a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of **1.73** on ImageNet 256x256, surpassing leading Diffusion Transformers (DiT-XL/2 and L-DiT-3B/7B).
- VAR demonstrated significantly faster inference speeds, being **45 times** faster than DiT-XL/2, and showed higher data efficiency, requiring only **350** training epochs compared to DiT-XL/2's **1400** epochs.
- The model exhibited clear power-law scaling relationships for test loss with increasing model parameters and optimal training compute, consistent with observations in Large Language Models, and performed zero-shot image in-painting, out-painting, and class-conditional editing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing visual autoregression as "next-scale prediction" fundamentally resolves efficiency and architectural limitations of traditional visual AR models, enabling state-of-the-art performance.
> - GPT-style autoregressive models can achieve competitive or superior image generation quality and efficiency compared to diffusion models by aligning with the inherent hierarchical structure of images.
> - Visual models, when designed appropriately (like VAR), can exhibit power-law scaling laws and zero-shot generalization capabilities analogous to Large Language Models, suggesting principles for scalable and general AI across modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly for images, has seen rapid advancements through various paradigms, primarily Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Diffusion Models, and Autoregressive (AR) models.

- **GANs** (e.g., BigGAN, StyleGAN) have excelled at generating high-fidelity images but often struggle with mode collapse and training stability.
- **Diffusion Models** (e.g., ADM, LDM, DiT) have recently achieved state-of-the-art performance in image quality and diversity, becoming the foundation for powerful text-to-image systems like Stable Diffusion and video generation models like Sora. Their success stems from an iterative denoising process. The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) further improved these models by leveraging transformer architectures.
- **Autoregressive (AR) Models** (e.g., VQGAN, DALL-E, VQVAE-2, RQ-Transformer) leverage the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) by discretizing images into tokens and generating them sequentially, similar to text. While theoretically powerful due to their probabilistic modeling, traditional AR models for images have significantly lagged behind diffusion models in image quality and suffered from inefficiencies and fundamental limitations when translating the 1D sequential nature of text to 2D images. They typically flatten 2D image token grids into 1D sequences, leading to computational bottlenecks and a loss of inherent spatial locality.

This paper positions Visual AutoRegressive (VAR) modeling as a direct challenge to the dominance of diffusion models and a significant evolution for AR models in computer vision. It aims to bridge the performance gap by redefining autoregressive learning for images. The authors explicitly state that VAR, "for the first time, makes GPT-style AR models surpass diffusion transformers in image generation." This claim directly confronts the prevailing belief that diffusion models are superior for high-quality image synthesis. Furthermore, the work draws a strong parallel to LLMs, aspiring to emulate their "scaling laws" and "zero-shot generalization" capabilities, which have been hallmarks of LLM success and are largely underexplored or unachieved in visual AR models. This pushes the boundaries towards more unified, general-purpose AI models that can scale and generalize across modalities, a key step towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **redefine and significantly improve autoregressive modeling for image generation**, enabling it to surpass the current state-of-the-art, particularly diffusion models, in terms of image quality, diversity, and efficiency. The authors are driven by several key motivations:

1. **Unlocking the Potential of Autoregressive Models in Vision:** While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable scalability and generalization through AR learning (predicting the "next token"), this potential has remained largely "locked" for visual AR models. Existing methods, which flatten 2D image tokens into 1D sequences for raster-scan generation, suffer from several issues.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Vanilla AR Image Generation:** The paper identifies four critical weaknesses of conventional AR models for images:
 - **Mathematical Premise Violation:** The conversion of 2D image feature maps with inherent bidirectional correlations into 1D sequences contradicts the unidirectional dependency assumption of AR models, limiting their expressiveness.
 - **Inability to Perform Certain Zero-Shot Generalization:** The unidirectional nature prevents tasks requiring bidirectional reasoning, such as predicting the top of an image from the bottom.
 - **Structural Degradation:** Flattening destroys the crucial spatial locality and 2D relationships among image tokens, diminishing correlations.
 - **Inefficiency:** Generating an image with $N \times N$ latent tokens requires $O(N^2)$ autoregressive steps and $O(N^6)$ computational cost with self-attention transformers, making it prohibitively slow for high resolutions.
3. **Emulating LLM Success Factors:** The authors are motivated to transfer the appealing properties observed in LLMs—namely, **power-law scaling laws** (predictable performance improvements with increased scale) and **zero-shot generalization**—to the visual domain. Demonstrating these properties in VAR would suggest a path towards more scalable and versatile visual foundation models.
4. **Achieving Faster and More Data-Efficient Generation:** Beyond quality, the objective includes significantly improving inference speed and data efficiency compared to competing generative models, particularly diffusion models, which often require numerous sampling steps and extensive training data.

In essence, VAR aims to provide a more "natural" way for AR models to perceive and generate images, aligning with hierarchical human perception (coarse-to-fine), while simultaneously addressing the technical shortcomings that have held back visual autoregressive models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of VAR modeling lies in its paradigm shift from "next-token prediction" to **"next-scale prediction"** for images. This involves a two-stage training process:

**Stage 1: Training a Multi-scale VQVAE (Visual Quantized Variational Autoencoder)**
- **Tokenization Reformulation:** Instead of flattening a 2D grid of tokens into a 1D sequence, VAR encodes an image into *K* multi-scale discrete token maps ($r_1, r_2..., r_K$). Each map ($r_k$) represents the image at an increasingly higher resolution ($h_k \times w_k$), with $r_K$ matching the original feature map's resolution.
- **Multi-scale Quantization:** The VQVAE architecture used is based on the vanilla VQGAN but with a modified multi-scale quantization layer. A shared codebook of 4096 entries is used across all scales.
- **Residual Design for Feature Maps:** The encoding process (Algorithm 1) progressively quantizes and extracts information from the image feature map ($f$). Crucially, after obtaining a quantized token map ($z_k$) at scale $k$, this information is upscaled and *subtracted* from the original feature map ($f - \phi_k(z_k)$) before processing the next scale. This residual-style design ensures that each $r_k$ primarily captures the *additional detail* not present in coarser scales, ensuring that $r_k$ depends only on its prefix ($r_{\le k}$) and aligning with the coarse-to-fine autoregressive structure.
- **Reconstruction:** The decoding process (Algorithm 2) reconstructs the image by summing up the information from each scale's quantized representation, mirroring the encoding.
- **Training:** The VQVAE is trained on OpenImages dataset with a compound loss, including reconstruction, perceptual, and discriminative losses.

**Stage 2: Training the VAR Transformer via Next-Scale Prediction**
- **Autoregressive Formulation:** The likelihood of the image is factorized as $p(r_1, r_2..., r_K) = \prod_{k=1}^K p(r_k | r_1, r_2..., r_{k-1})$. This means the transformer predicts an *entire token map* ($r_k$) in parallel, conditioned on all previously generated coarser token maps.
- **Architecture:** A standard decoder-only transformer, similar to GPT-2, is adopted. It incorporates Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) for class-conditional synthesis (using class embedding as a start token and AdaLN condition). Query and key normalization to unit vectors are used for training stability. The model avoids more advanced LLM techniques like RoPE or SwiGLU MLP to demonstrate the core effectiveness of the VAR paradigm.
- **Causal Attention:** A block-wise causal attention mask is employed during training to enforce that each token map $r_k$ can only attend to tokens within its own scale and all previous, coarser scales ($r_{\le k}$). During inference, KV-caching is used, and no mask is needed.
- **Scalability Design:** The model width, head counts, and dropout rate are linearly scaled with the transformer's depth, following principles from LLM scaling studies to achieve predictable parameter counts. This design rule (Eq. 7, 8) ensures a systematic way to scale the model.
- **Efficiency:** This "next-scale prediction" approach drastically reduces computational complexity from $O(N^6)$ for traditional AR to $O(N^4)$ for VAR, where $N \times N$ is the resolution of the final token map. This is because tokens within a single scale are generated in parallel, and the number of autoregressive steps is logarithmic with resolution (K scales).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness and scalability of VAR:

- **State-of-the-Art Image Generation:**
 - **ImageNet 256x256:** On the challenging ImageNet 256x256 conditional generation benchmark, the largest VAR model (VAR-d30, 2.0B parameters) achieved a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of **1.73** and an Inception Score (IS) of **350.2** (with rejection sampling). This significantly surpasses previous AR baselines (e.g., VQGAN's 18.65 FID) and, critically, **outperforms leading Diffusion Transformers (DiT-XL/2, L-DiT-3B/7B)**, which achieved FIDs around 2.10-2.28.
 - **ImageNet 512x512:** VAR maintains its strong performance at higher resolutions, achieving an FID of **2.63** and IS of **303.2**, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04).
 - **Inference Speed:** VAR demonstrates remarkable efficiency, achieving a 20x faster inference speed compared to VQGAN and ViT-VQGAN, and being **45 times faster than DiT-XL/2**. It approaches the inference speed of efficient GAN models while offering superior quality.
 - **Data Efficiency:** VAR is shown to be more data-efficient than DiT, requiring only 350 training epochs compared to DiT-XL/2's 1400 epochs.

- **Power-Law Scaling Laws:**
 - **Consistent with LLMs:** VAR models exhibit clear power-law scaling relationships for test loss ($L$) and token error rate ($Err$) as functions of model parameters ($N$) and optimal training compute ($C_{min}$).
 - **Model Parameters (N):** The test loss decreases predictably with increasing model size, following $L \propto N^{-\alpha}$ (e.g., $L_{last} = (2.0 \cdot N)^{-0.23}$, $L_{avg} = (2.5 \cdot N)^{-0.20}$). The Pearson correlation coefficients were extremely high (near -0.998), indicating a strong linear relationship on a log-log plot, mirroring LLM behavior.
 - **Training Compute (C_min):** Similarly, test loss and error rate decrease with optimal training compute, following $L \propto C_{min}^{-\alpha}$ (e.g., $L_{last} = (2.2 \cdot 10^{-5} C_{min})^{-0.13}$). These relationships held across six orders of magnitude in compute, reinforcing that larger VAR models are more compute-efficient.
 - **Visual Fidelity Improvements:** Qualitative results (Figure 7) visually confirm that scaling up model size and training compute leads to noticeable improvements in visual fidelity and soundness of generated images, aligning with the quantitative scaling laws.

- **Zero-Shot Task Generalization:**
 - VAR demonstrates impressive zero-shot capabilities in downstream tasks without any specific architectural modifications or fine-tuning.
 - **Image In-painting and Out-painting:** The model successfully completes or extends images by conditioning on ground truth tokens outside the masked region, producing plausible and contextually coherent content.
 - **Class-conditional Image Editing:** VAR can perform localized image editing within bounding boxes, conditioned on a target class label, showcasing its understanding of object semantics and ability to integrate new content seamlessly.

- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study confirms the critical role of the VAR paradigm itself, showing a massive jump in FID performance (from 18.65 to 5.22) and efficiency (0.013x cost) just by switching from AR to VAR. Further enhancements like AdaLN, Top-k sampling, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), and Attention Normalization cumulatively improve performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Visual Autoregressive Modeling" holds significant implications for the field of generative AI and computer vision, with potential impacts on future research and applications:

- **Paradigm Shift for Visual AR Models:** VAR successfully redefines autoregressive learning for images, moving from inefficient and problematic "next-token prediction" to a highly effective "next-scale prediction." This fundamentally resolves key issues of traditional visual AR models, such as computational inefficiency, destruction of spatial locality, and contradictions with mathematical premises. It proves that GPT-style AR models can indeed be state-of-the-art for image generation, a long-sought goal.
- **Challenging Diffusion Model Dominance:** By empirically demonstrating that VAR can outperform Diffusion Transformers (DiT) in key metrics like FID/IS, inference speed, and data efficiency, this paper challenges the notion that diffusion models are the sole pathway to high-quality image generation. This could spur renewed interest and investment in AR-based approaches, fostering diverse research directions in generative AI.
- **Paving the Way for AGI in Vision:** The most significant contribution is the successful replication of **LLM-like power-law scaling laws and zero-shot generalization abilities** in a visual model. This is a crucial step towards the development of general-purpose AI, suggesting that fundamental principles governing the scalability and adaptability of language models might also apply to other modalities. This could accelerate progress towards unified models that can learn from vast amounts of unsupervised data across different domains.
- **Foundation for Unified Multi-modal Learning:** The inherent architectural similarity to LLMs makes VAR a natural candidate for integration into larger multi-modal systems. The authors explicitly mention text-prompt generation as an ongoing research direction, which could lead to highly capable text-to-image models. The potential for extending VAR to **video generation** via "3D next-scale prediction" is also highlighted, promising advantages in temporal consistency and efficiency compared to traditional AR or even some diffusion-based video models.
- **Practical Implications for High-Resolution Generation:** The improved efficiency ($O(N^4)$ complexity) makes high-resolution image and potentially video generation more feasible and less computationally demanding, lowering the barriers for deployment in real-world applications requiring rapid content creation.
- **Open Science Contribution:** The release of models and code is invaluable for the research community, enabling others to build upon VAR, validate its findings, and further explore the potential of autoregressive models for visual generation and unified learning.

In conclusion, VAR represents a significant leap forward for autoregressive models in computer vision, not only by achieving state-of-the-art performance and efficiency but also by demonstrating fundamental properties of scalability and generalization previously associated primarily with large language models. This work could inspire a new wave of research towards truly general and unified AI systems capable of understanding and generating diverse forms of media.
