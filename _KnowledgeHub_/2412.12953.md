# Efficient Diffusion Transformer Policies with Mixture of Expert Denoisers for Multitask Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An architecture for diffusion policies, MoDE (Mixture-of-Denoising Experts), achieves state-of-the-art performance in multitask robotic imitation learning while operating with 90% fewer FLOPs and significantly faster inference compared to prior diffusion Transformers. Developed by researchers from KIT and MIT, MoDE integrates expert specialization for different noise levels within the denoising process and utilizes an expert caching mechanism to accelerate execution.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies for imitation learning incur high computational costs and slow inference due to large parameter counts and numerous denoising steps, hindering their real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Traditional scaling methods for diffusion models, primarily by increasing model size, lead to unsustainable computational demands and practical limitations (energy consumption, memory footprint).
- Existing diffusion policies lack architectural specialization for the distinct computational requirements across different stages of the denoising process.

## Method

- MoDE employs a Transformer backbone with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture where expert routing is deterministically conditioned *only* on the noise level of the current denoising step, allowing experts to specialize in specific noise regimes.
- A novel router and expert caching mechanism pre-computes and fuses the selected expert MLPs for each noise level before inference, eliminating dynamic routing decisions at runtime to reduce FLOPs overhead by over 80% and double inference speed.
- Noise-conditioned self-attention, incorporating a linear projection of the noise level into input tokens, allows attention patterns to adapt based on the current denoising phase, enhancing performance without increasing parameter count.

## Results

- MoDE achieves state-of-the-art performance across 134 tasks on the CALVIN and LIBERO benchmarks, outperforming larger models like RoboFlamingo and GR-1 and surpassing other diffusion architectures by an average of **57%**.
- It demonstrates exceptional computational efficiency, using **90%** fewer FLOPs and active parameters (e.g., **16x** fewer FLOPs at batch size 512) and achieving nearly **40%** faster inference compared to dense baselines.
- On the SIMPLER generalist policy benchmark, MoDE attains the highest average success rate (**26.30%**) and best average ranking (**1.65**) across tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (7.7B parameters) and Octo despite being trained on a smaller dataset subset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising process in diffusion models is inherently multi-task, benefiting from specialized experts that are dynamically activated based on the noise level, which significantly improves both efficiency and performance.
> - Decoupling expert routing from token content and conditioning it solely on the noise level enables a powerful expert caching strategy that drastically reduces inference-time computations.
> - Both an explicit noise input token and noise-conditioned self-attention are crucial architectural components for optimal performance in diffusion Transformer policies, with an optimal balance found around 4 experts for efficiency and effectiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Imitation Learning (IL), Diffusion Models, Transformer Architectures, and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, all within the context of robotics.

**Imitation Learning (IL)** is a powerful paradigm for training agents from expert demonstrations, enabling them to acquire complex and versatile skills. Recently, **Diffusion Models** have gained widespread adoption as policies for IL, offering appealing properties such as generating diverse multimodal behavior, scaling with more data, and handling discontinuities in the action space. However, a major limitation of current diffusion policies is their **high computational cost**, primarily due to the large parameter counts of modern models (often hundreds of millions) and the many denoising steps required during inference. This computational burden restricts their deployment in real-time robotics applications, especially on resource-constrained platforms like mobile robots.

The paper identifies this computational roadblock as a critical challenge, noting that traditional scaling methods—simply making models larger—will become unsustainable. This observation is in line with recent discussions in the broader AI community about the increasing energy and computational demands of large models, as indicated by "scaling laws."

To address this, the authors turn to **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures**. MoE models allow for scaling model capacity while maintaining lower computational costs (FLOPs) during both training and inference by activating only a subset of parameters for each input. This concept dates back to early neural networks but gained significant traction with the advent of sparse MoE layers in Transformers (e.g., Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022, with Switch Transformers), particularly in Large Language Models (LLMs) like Mixtral of Experts. The core idea is to achieve greater overall model capacity without proportional increases in active parameters or FLOPs.

Within the domain of diffusion models, prior work has explored **multitask learning** by acknowledging that the denoising process itself is inherently multi-task (Hang et al., 2024), where different phases of denoising require distinct computations. Some approaches have explicitly scheduled parameters for different denoising stages or modulated representations based on the denoising stage.

This work specifically distinguishes itself from previous efforts by integrating MoE within a Diffusion Transformer policy for **generalist policies**, focusing on improving computational efficiency and inference time during the denoising process. While other robotic learning models like Octo and BESO use Transformer backbones for diffusion policies, and Sparse-DP (Wang et al., 2024) leverages MoE for different *tasks*, MoDE is novel in applying MoE to specialize experts for different *denoising phases* based on noise level, thereby targeting efficiency at a more fundamental architectural level. By combining the strengths of diffusion models (multimodality, data scaling) with the efficiency of MoE and the representational power of Transformers, MoDE aims to push the boundaries of scalable and deployable robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **alleviate the computational bottleneck** inherent in large-scale diffusion policies, which currently hinders their widespread adoption in real-time and resource-constrained robotic applications. The authors are motivated by the recognition that while diffusion policies offer significant advantages for Imitation Learning, their escalating computational demands, driven by model size and the iterative denoising process, represent a critical limitation.

More specifically, the key objectives of the paper are:

- **Develop a novel, parameter-efficient, and scalable policy architecture:** The goal is to design an architecture that can capture complex capabilities without incurring the prohibitive computational costs of traditional large diffusion models. This leads to the proposal of the Mixture-of-Denoising Experts (MoDE) policy.
- **Surpass current state-of-the-art Transformer-based Diffusion Policies:** MoDE aims not only for efficiency but also for superior performance in complex, multitask robotic manipulation benchmarks.
- **Significantly reduce active parameters and inference costs:** A core objective is to achieve substantial reductions in FLOPs and active parameters, thereby accelerating both training and inference speeds.
- **Enable more effective denoising across different noise levels:** By leveraging the insight that denoising is a multitask process, the architecture seeks to specialize its components to handle various noise levels more effectively. This forms the basis for the noise-conditioned routing mechanism.
- **Achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging multitask benchmarks:** The research aims to demonstrate MoDE's effectiveness across a diverse set of real-world-like robotics tasks, specifically using established goal-conditioned imitation learning benchmarks like CALVIN and LIBERO.
- **Provide insights for designing efficient and scalable Transformer architectures for Diffusion Policies:** Through comprehensive ablation studies, the authors intend to offer guidance on critical design decisions for future research in this domain.

The motivation stems from the practical limitations faced by current diffusion policies: their slow inference speeds, high energy consumption, and large memory footprints make them unsuitable for deployment on robots with limited on-board processing power. By introducing MoDE, the authors seek to enable the development of truly generalist and practical robot policies that are both high-performing and computationally efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoDE (Mixture-of-Denoising Experts Policy) is a novel architecture that integrates a Transformer backbone with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design, specifically tailored for the denoising process in continuous-time diffusion policies.

**Problem Formulation:** The work considers the problem of learning a language-conditioned policy, $\pi_\theta(\bar{a}|\bar{s}, g)$, from a dataset of expert demonstrations. The policy's task is to predict a sequence of future actions $\bar{a}$ given a history of state embeddings $\bar{s}$ and a natural language goal $g$.

**Diffusion Policy Framework:**
MoDE uses the continuous-time diffusion model EDM (Karras et al., 2022) as its policy representation. This framework involves a forward process that iteratively adds Gaussian noise to data samples (action sequences) and a reverse process learned by a neural network to denoise these samples. During training, the network is optimized to approximate the score function using score matching, predicting the denoised actions. For inference, MoDE employs the DDIM-solver (Song et al., 2021) in 10 discrete steps to generate an action sequence from random noise.

**MoDE Architecture (Mixture-of-Denoising Experts):**
The core innovation lies in MoDE's specialized architecture:
- **Input Encoding:** For goal conditioning, a frozen CLIP language encoder generates a latent goal vector. For visual observations, FiLM-conditioned ResNets (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50 depending on the benchmark) are used for static and wrist camera images.
- **Transformer Backbone:** The model processes a sequence of input tokens $X$ (which include state, goal, and noisy action tokens) and a noise level $\sigma_t$. It consists of $L$ transformer blocks, each designed to be specialized for different denoising phases.
- **Noise-Conditioned Self-Attention:** A linear projection $\phi(\sigma_t)$ of the current noise level is added to all input tokens $X$ before the self-attention mechanism, forming $\hat{X} = \phi(\sigma_t) + X$. This allows each token's attention patterns to adapt based on the current denoising phase, enhancing denoising performance without increasing parameter count.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Layer with Noise-Conditioned Routing:** Each transformer block includes an MoE layer. Crucially, the routing function $R(\cdot)$ for selecting experts is **noise-conditioned**. Instead of routing based on the content of the input tokens (as in traditional MoE for LLMs), MoDE's router uses only the encoded noise level $\phi(\sigma_t)$ to select the top-$k$ experts. This design choice is inspired by the multitask nature of the denoising process, allowing different experts to specialize in specific noise regimes.
- **Load Balancing:** To mitigate expert collapse (where only a few experts are utilized) or router collapse, a load balancing loss (LB) (Fedus et al., 2022) is incorporated during training.

**Router and Expert Caching for Efficient Inference:**
A key methodological contribution to efficiency is the **router and expert caching mechanism**. Since the expert routing is deterministically conditioned *only* on the noise level (which follows a predefined schedule during inference), MoDE can pre-compute and fuse the selected expert MLPs for each noise level *before* inference begins. This eliminates the need for dynamic routing decisions and individual expert invocations during runtime, significantly reducing FLOPs (over 80% reduction compared to standard MoE rollouts) and accelerating inference time (two times faster).

**Generalist Policy Pre-training:**
To enhance generalization, MoDE is pre-trained on a diverse mix of multi-robot datasets from the Open-X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset. This pre-training dataset includes 196k trajectories from six diverse sources, featuring various robot platforms and manipulation tasks. During subsequent fine-tuning on specific benchmarks (like CALVIN and LIBERO), the pre-trained routers in each layer are frozen, and only other model components are fine-tuned.

This comprehensive approach combines architectural innovation (noise-conditioned MoE Transformer), algorithmic optimization (expert caching), and data-driven learning (diverse pre-training) to achieve both high performance and computational efficiency in robotic imitation learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that MoDE significantly advances the state-of-the-art in efficient and high-performing diffusion policies for multitask robotic imitation learning. The key findings and results are:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance across Benchmarks:** MoDE achieves superior performance on 134 tasks across four challenging goal-conditioned imitation learning benchmarks: CALVIN (Language-Skills Benchmark) and LIBERO (Lifelong Robot Learning Benchmark).
 - On CALVIN ABC→D (zero-shot generalization), pretrained MoDE achieves an average rollout length of 4.01, and on CALVIN ABCD→D (scaling multitask), it achieves 4.39. These results surpass all tested baselines, including much larger models like RoboFlamingo (1000M parameters) and GR-1 (130M parameters, pre-trained on large-scale video generation).
 - On LIBERO-10 and LIBERO-90, pretrained MoDE achieves average success rates of 0.95 and 0.94 respectively, outperforming all other diffusion architectures and QueST.
 - Overall, MoDE surpasses both CNN-based and Transformer Diffusion Policies by an average of 57% across the four benchmarks.
 - In evaluations on the SIMPLER benchmark for generalist policies, MoDE achieves the highest average success rate of 26.30% and the best average ranking (1.65) across tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (7.7B parameters, 23.70%) and Octo (17.75%) despite being trained on a smaller dataset subset.

- **Exceptional Computational Efficiency:** MoDE drastically reduces computational costs and improves inference speed.
 - It uses 90% fewer FLOPs and fewer *active parameters* compared to default Diffusion Transformer architectures. For instance, at a batch size of 512, MoDE requires 16x fewer FLOPs (361 GFLOPS vs. 5,772 GFLOPS for a dense baseline).
 - Inference is significantly faster: at batch size 512, MoDE achieves nearly 40% faster inference (64ms vs. 104ms for a dense baseline).
 - The novel router caching mechanism reduces FLOPs overhead by over 80% and makes inference two times faster.
 - When comparing against other powerful baselines on CALVIN, MoDE (436M total parameters) achieved 1.53 GFLOPS and 12.2 ms inference time per action, outperforming GR-1 (130M total parameters) which required 27.5 GFLOPS and 12.6 ms per action, demonstrating a superior performance-to-GFLOPS ratio.

- **Ablation Study Insights:** Comprehensive ablations provided critical insights into MoDE's design.
 - **Noise Conditioning:** Both an input noise token and noise-conditioned self-attention are crucial, with their combined use yielding the best performance (0.92 success rate on LIBERO-10).
 - **Optimal Routing Strategy:** Noise-only conditioned routing proved slightly more effective than token-only routing and, critically, enabled the efficient expert caching mechanism.
 - **Expert Utilization:** Visualizations showed that MoDE effectively learns to distribute tokens and utilize different experts for various noise levels, demonstrating specialized experts for different denoising phases, particularly a transition around $\sigma_8$.
 - **Number of Experts:** An optimal number of 4 experts was found to strike the best balance between performance and efficiency. Increasing beyond 4 experts led to performance degradation and underutilization of experts, even with load balancing.
 - **Load Balancing Loss:** A gamma ($\gamma$) value of 0.01 for the load balancing loss provided the best performance, indicating the importance of balanced expert participation.
 - **Latent Dimension:** Increasing the latent dimension from 256 to 1024 (the default) progressively improved performance, showing the importance of representational capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Efficient Diffusion Transformer Policies with Mixture of Expert Denoising for Multitask Learning" carries significant implications for the future of robot learning and the broader application of generative models in real-world systems.

**1. Enabling Real-time Robotic Deployment:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is MoDE's success in mitigating the computational demands of diffusion policies. By achieving state-of-the-art performance with 90% fewer FLOPs and significantly faster inference times (due to sparse experts and expert caching), MoDE makes diffusion-based robotic policies much more practical for real-time applications and deployment on robots with limited on-board computational resources. This is a critical step towards moving advanced robot learning algorithms from research labs to industrial and everyday settings.

**2. Pushing Generalist Robot Policies:** The ability of MoDE, especially its pretrained variant, to outperform much larger existing generalist policies (like OpenVLA and Octo) on challenging benchmarks such as CALVIN and SIMPLER (real2sim tasks), while being more computationally efficient, signifies a major step towards truly capable and adaptable generalist robot agents. This demonstrates that efficiency does not necessarily come at the cost of generalization capabilities, but rather can be a catalyst for them.

**3. Novel Architectural Paradigm for Diffusion Models:** The introduction of noise-conditioned routing within a Mixture-of-Experts Transformer is a novel architectural paradigm for diffusion policies. It creatively leverages the inherent multi-task nature of the denoising process to specialize experts, opening new avenues for designing efficient and effective generative models beyond robotics. This approach could inspire similar expert specialization strategies in other conditional generative tasks.

**4. Insights for Future Model Design:** The comprehensive ablation studies provide invaluable insights for the design of future Transformer and diffusion architectures. Understanding the optimal configuration of experts, the effectiveness of noise-conditioned attention, and the importance of load balancing loss will guide researchers in building more performant and efficient models, avoiding common pitfalls like expert collapse or suboptimal routing.

**5. Scaling Laws Reimagined:** This work challenges the conventional "bigger is always better" scaling law paradigm by demonstrating that intelligent architectural design, specifically sparse activation through MoE, can achieve superior performance and efficiency simultaneously. This suggests a future where computational gains come not just from larger models, but from smarter, more specialized, and sparsely activated architectures.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the paper acknowledges some limitations, such as a slightly higher standard deviation compared to baselines and instances of expert collapse in certain configurations. These point to future research directions focused on stabilizing routing models and exploring alternative routing strategies (e.g., expert-choice routing) to further improve robustness and expert utilization.

In conclusion, MoDE represents a significant advancement by making high-capacity diffusion policies computationally feasible for practical robotic applications. Its innovative architecture and demonstrated performance lay a strong foundation for the development of the next generation of efficient, scalable, and generalist robot learning systems.
