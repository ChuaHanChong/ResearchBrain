# Efficient Diffusion Transformer Policies with Mixture of Expert Denoisers for Multitask Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An architecture for diffusion policies, MoDE (Mixture-of-Denoising Experts), achieves state-of-the-art performance in multitask robotic imitation learning while operating with 90% fewer FLOPs and significantly faster inference compared to prior diffusion Transformers. Developed by researchers from KIT and MIT, MoDE integrates expert specialization for different noise levels within the denoising process and utilizes an expert caching mechanism to accelerate execution.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies for imitation learning incur high computational costs and slow inference due to large parameter counts and numerous denoising steps, hindering their real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
- Traditional scaling methods for diffusion models, primarily by increasing model size, lead to unsustainable computational demands and practical limitations (energy consumption, memory footprint).
- Existing diffusion policies lack architectural specialization for the distinct computational requirements across different stages of the denoising process.

## Method

- MoDE employs a Transformer backbone with a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture where expert routing is deterministically conditioned *only* on the noise level of the current denoising step, allowing experts to specialize in specific noise regimes.
- A novel router and expert caching mechanism pre-computes and fuses the selected expert MLPs for each noise level before inference, eliminating dynamic routing decisions at runtime to reduce FLOPs overhead by over 80% and double inference speed.
- Noise-conditioned self-attention, incorporating a linear projection of the noise level into input tokens, allows attention patterns to adapt based on the current denoising phase, enhancing performance without increasing parameter count.

## Results

- MoDE achieves state-of-the-art performance across 134 tasks on the CALVIN and LIBERO benchmarks, outperforming larger models like RoboFlamingo and GR-1 and surpassing other diffusion architectures by an average of **57%**.
- It demonstrates exceptional computational efficiency, using **90%** fewer FLOPs and active parameters (e.g., **16x** fewer FLOPs at batch size 512) and achieving nearly **40%** faster inference compared to dense baselines.
- On the SIMPLER generalist policy benchmark, MoDE attains the highest average success rate (**26.30%**) and best average ranking (**1.65**) across tasks, outperforming OpenVLA (7.7B parameters) and Octo despite being trained on a smaller dataset subset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The denoising process in diffusion models is inherently multi-task, benefiting from specialized experts that are dynamically activated based on the noise level, which significantly improves both efficiency and performance.
> - Decoupling expert routing from token content and conditioning it solely on the noise level enables a powerful expert caching strategy that drastically reduces inference-time computations.
> - Both an explicit noise input token and noise-conditioned self-attention are crucial architectural components for optimal performance in diffusion Transformer policies, with an optimal balance found around 4 experts for efficiency and effectiveness.
