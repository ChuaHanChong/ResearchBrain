# PLUME: Latent Reasoning Based Universal Multimodal Embedding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed PLUME, a framework for Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) that replaces explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) rationales with a compact, continuous latent rollout within a multimodal large language model. This method achieved an overall score of 61.6 on MMEB-v2, outperforming the explicit CoT baseline UME-R1 (60.1) while providing a 30.3x speedup in inference latency, reducing it from 9023 ms/sample to 298 ms/sample.

## Problem

- Current Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) methods struggle with complex real-world queries requiring deep reasoning, such as compositional spatial understanding or temporal inference.
- Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in UME introduces significant computational overhead (high latency) and a representational bottleneck by discretizing rich multimodal information into text.
- Multimodal reasoning demands adaptive computation allocation across diverse input modalities (images, videos, documents) rather than a uniform, fixed reasoning path.

## Method

- **PLUME** employs a compact, ==autoregressive latent rollout== within an MLLM's hidden space to perform multi-step reasoning without generating explicit textual rationales.
- A ==semantic-anchor-guided transition adapter==, built with a ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)== architecture, enables ==input-adaptive computation==, routing latent steps based on input semantics.
- A ==progressive explicit-to-latent curriculum== is used during training to stably transfer knowledge from verbose CoT supervision to efficient, hidden-state computation, eliminating explicit CoT at inference.

## Results

- **PLUME** achieved an overall MMEB-v2 score of **61.6**, surpassing the explicit CoT baseline **UME-R1** (**60.1**) by **1.5** points and the single-pass **VLM2Vec-V2** (**58.0**) by **3.6** points.
- The framework delivered a **30.3x** inference speedup, reducing latency from 9023 ms/sample (**UME-R1**) to **298 ms/sample** with 8 latent steps, with only 142 ms overhead compared to single-pass methods.
- **PLUME** demonstrated particular strengths in video (**+1.9** points over **UME-R1**) and visual document tasks (**+3.6** points over **UME-R1**), suggesting latent states better preserve dense temporal and spatial semantics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-step reasoning can be effectively internalized into a model's latent space, preserving reasoning benefits while drastically improving inference efficiency.
> - Adaptive allocation of latent computation via a routed MoE adapter is crucial for handling the heterogeneous nature of universal multimodal inputs.
> - A progressive curriculum, gradually replacing explicit reasoning with latent steps, is vital for stable knowledge transfer from explicit verbalized reasoning to implicit hidden-state computation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME), which seeks to map diverse inputs, such as text, images, videos, and visual documents, into a unified embedding space using a single model. The goal of UME is to facilitate cross-modal retrieval by representing heterogeneous data in a semantically aligned manner.

Earlier UME methods, including dual-encoder architectures like CLIP and MLLM-based approaches such as E5-V and VLM2Vec, typically form embeddings through a single forward pass. While efficient, these methods may struggle with complex queries requiring multi-step reasoning, compositional understanding, or integration of dispersed evidence.

To address the limitations of single-pass encoding, recent advancements have explored reasoning-enhanced UME methods, exemplified by approaches like Think-then-Embed (TTE), UME-R1, and TRACE. These methods incorporate explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales, where models generate textual reasoning traces before extracting the final embedding. This approach has shown effectiveness in improving reasoning capabilities for complex queries. However, explicit CoT introduces computational and representational bottlenecks: it incurs substantial inference latency due to autoregressive decoding of hundreds of tokens, and verbalizing multimodal evidence into discrete textual tokens can compress rich information into a narrow bottleneck, potentially discarding fine-grained continuous evidence.

A parallel research direction in Large Language Models (LLMs) involves latent reasoning or internal computation that avoids explicit token generation. Methods like Pause-token, Quiet-STaR, and Coconut explore enhancing internal compute or moving reasoning into continuous hidden spaces. LaSER, for instance, internalized explicit reasoning into latent space specifically for dense text retrieval.

PLUME positions itself at the intersection of reasoning-enhanced UME and latent reasoning in LLMs. It aims to overcome the dual bottleneck of explicit CoT by retaining the benefits of multi-step reasoning while significantly improving efficiency. The work attempts to create a more efficient and adaptive UME paradigm that operates over heterogeneous multimodal inputs, contrasting with prior latent reasoning efforts that often focus solely on text.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for PLUME stems from the observed limitations of existing Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) paradigms, particularly the trade-off between reasoning capability and inference efficiency.

1. **Addressing Complex Retrieval Queries:** Many real-world retrieval queries involve more than surface-level similarity. They frequently necessitate compositional spatial understanding, knowledge-intensive visual inference, or the aggregation of temporally and structurally dispersed evidence across various modalities. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer strong semantic alignment and broad world knowledge, making them attractive backbones for UME to handle such complexity. However, simply using an MLLM as an encoder in a single-pass manner does not fully leverage its reasoning potential for embedding formation.

2. **Overcoming the Dual Bottleneck of Explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Recent reasoning-enhanced UME methods improve performance by generating explicit CoT rationales before embedding extraction. While effective, this strategy introduces two primary bottlenecks:
 - **Computational Overhead:** Generating hundreds of reasoning tokens autoregressively incurs substantial latency and token costs, severely limiting inference throughput. This is prohibitive for practical retrieval systems that demand high efficiency.
 - **Representational Bottleneck:** Routing inherently continuous multimodal reasoning through discrete textual tokens creates a narrow bottleneck. This process can lead to the discarding of fine-grained continuous evidence and may constrain the richness with which multimodal information is carried into the final embedding. The verbalized nature of explicit CoT can be fundamentally mismatched with the efficiency demands of retrieval systems.

3. **Seeking Efficient Intermediate Computation:** The authors hypothesize that what retrieval systems require is intermediate computation to enhance query interpretation and evidence integration, not necessarily verbalized intermediate text. This insight motivates exploring multi-step reasoning that unfolds directly within the continuous hidden space of the backbone model, thereby preserving the sequential dependency structure of reasoning without the overhead of long-form text generation.

4. **Adaptive Reasoning for Heterogeneous Multimodal Inputs:** A significant challenge in transitioning from explicit to latent reasoning for UME is the diversity of multimodal inputs (e.g., videos, images, documents, text). These different modalities demand distinct forms of intermediate computation, involving temporal dynamics, spatial relations, layout structure, and semantic abstraction. A uniform latent reasoning process might not be sufficient. Therefore, a key objective is to enable the compact latent computation to adaptively allocate resources and follow different reasoning trajectories across heterogeneous multimodal queries, rather than forcing every input through the same fixed path.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a UME framework that can leverage the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.
- Eliminate the computational and representational bottlenecks associated with explicit CoT.
- Internalize multi-step reasoning into a compact, continuous latent process within the MLLM.
- Enable this latent reasoning process to adapt dynamically to diverse multimodal input structures.
- Achieve superior retrieval performance compared to explicit CoT methods while significantly improving inference efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PLUME is a latent reasoning framework for universal multimodal embedding, designed to internalize reasoning into a compact hidden-state rollout. It comprises three core components: a latent rollout mechanism, a semantic-anchor-guided transition adapter, and a progressive explicit-to-latent curriculum. The backbone MLLM is fine-tuned, with the adapter and router being the only added lightweight components.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work addresses universal multimodal embedding, where a single model maps diverse inputs (text, images, videos, visual documents) into a shared embedding space for retrieval. The training objective is to maximize the similarity between a query ($q$) and its positive target ($t^+$) against negative targets ($t^-$). This is achieved using the InfoNCE objective, which is applied bidirectionally. A causal language modeling loss ($L_{CE}$) is also applied to the decoded suffix of both query and positive target for token-level supervision.

#### 3.2. Latent Rollout for Universal Multimodal Embedding

PLUME replaces explicit CoT with a short autoregressive rollout in the hidden space. This process involves four stages:

1. **Multimodal Prefix Encoding:** An input `x` (potentially interleaving text tokens with image, video, or document features) is processed by the backbone. A special `<slt>` (start-latent-thinking) token marks the beginning of a latent block, which is followed by `K` `<ct>` (continuous token) positional placeholders and an `<elt>` (end-latent-thinking) token. During this stage, the backbone generates hidden states and a cached key-value (KV) state $C(x)$ for subsequent causal attention. A semantic anchor $c(x)$, providing a fixed summary of the input's semantic intent, is also extracted from a dedicated `<anchor>` token.

2. **Latent State Initialization:** The latent state $z^{(0)}$ is initialized from the hidden state at the `<slt>` position, leveraging the accumulated multimodal context.

3. **Iterative Latent Rollout:** For each of $K$ latent steps, the previous latent state $z^{(k-1)}$ is refined by the routed adapter (described below) to yield an adapted state $\tilde{z}^{(k-1)}$. This adapted state is then fed into the backbone at the corresponding causal position, reusing the accumulated KV cache. This process allows the latent state to attend to the full multimodal prefix and all preceding latent states, maintaining sequential dependency. Each latent step consumes a causal position where an explicit token would otherwise be decoded, replacing discrete token generation with continuous hidden-state transitions.

4. **Suffix Decoding and Embedding Extraction:** After the $K$-step rollout, the latent block concludes with `<elt>`, followed by a `<gen>` token. The final retrieval embedding $e_{gen}(x)$ is extracted from the $\ell_2$-normalized hidden state at the `<gen>` position.

#### 3.3. Semantic-Anchor-Guided Transition Adapter

To enable adaptive latent computation for diverse UME instances, PLUME incorporates a lightweight routed adapter between adjacent latent steps, inspired by mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures.

1. **Routing Mechanism:** At each latent step $k$, a router determines which experts to activate. The router's input concatenates the additively fused previous latent state $z^{(k-1)} + c(x)$ (where $c(x)$ is the fixed semantic anchor from prefix encoding) with a learnable step embedding $e^{(k)}$. This design provides both a global input context ($c(x)$) and a step-specific progression signal ($e^{(k)}$). The router produces routing weights $\pi^{(k)}$ over $M_e$ specialized experts using a Softmax function.

2. **Expert Architecture:** Each expert $E_m$ is a two-layer MLP with a GELU activation and an expansion ratio of 2. Layer normalization is applied to the input before entering any expert, and dropout is used for regularization.

3. **Expert Selection and Combination:** The adapter includes one shared expert $E_0$ (for broadly useful patterns) and $M_e$ specialized experts $\{E_m\}_{m=1}^{M_e}$. The router selects the top $K_r$ specialized experts. The adapted latent state $\tilde{z}^{(k-1)}$ is formed by a residual connection, combining the original input with the output of the shared expert and a weighted mixture of the selected specialized experts. This routing mechanism acts only on the lightweight adapter, not the entire backbone.

4. **Balance Regularizer:** To prevent routing collapse and encourage balanced expert usage, a balance loss $L_{bal}$ is applied. It penalizes deviations from uniform allocation of routing mass across experts and latent steps.

#### 3.4. Embedding Formation and Training Objectives

The final retrieval embedding $e_{gen}(x)$ is derived from the last-layer hidden state at the `<gen>` position, reflecting the full latent-to-suffix computation. An auxiliary anchor embedding $e_{anc}(x)$ is derived from the semantic anchor and receives its own contrastive supervision ($L_{anc\_NCE}$) during training. This auxiliary embedding encourages the anchor to summarize input semantics and stabilizes early-stage training. Importantly, $e_{anc}$ is discarded at inference time, with retrieval solely relying on $e_{gen}$.

The total training objective combines:
- Causal language modeling loss ($L_{CE}$) on the decoded suffix of both query and positive target.
- InfoNCE objective ($L_{gen\_NCE}$) for the generative embedding.
- InfoNCE objective ($L_{anc\_NCE}$) for the auxiliary anchor embedding.
- Balance loss ($L_{bal}$) for routing regularization.

#### 3.5. Progressive Explicit-to-Latent Curriculum

A direct switch from explicit CoT supervision to latent-only execution is prone to instability. PLUME employs a progressive curriculum to gradually transfer reasoning behavior.

1. **Staged Transition:** The explicit rationale for each training instance is split into sentence-level segments. During training, these segments are progressively replaced, from left to right, with a latent block of `<ct>` positions.
2. **Gradual Absorption:** In early stages, most reasoning steps remain explicit and are teacher-forced, ensuring semantic grounding. As training advances, a larger portion of the rationale is absorbed into the latent block, while the remaining unreplaced steps are supervised as suffix tokens after `<elt>`. This teaches the model to continue explicit reasoning from partially latent prefixes.
3. **Final Stage:** In the ultimate stage, both the explicit rationale and the answer span are removed. The latent rollout directly connects to `<gen>`, matching the inference-time execution pattern.

This curriculum provides a structured bridge, stabilizing the transfer of multi-step reasoning from a verbalized format to a compact latent rollout, thus enabling PLUME to retain reasoning benefits without explicit CoT at inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PLUME's evaluation focused on its effectiveness and efficiency in Universal Multimodal Embedding, primarily using the MMEB-v2 benchmark.

#### 4.1. Main Comparison on MMEB-v2

- **Overall Performance:** PLUME achieved an overall score of 61.6 on the 78-task MMEB-v2 benchmark, outperforming UME-R1 (60.1), a strong explicit-CoT UME baseline, by 1.5 points. It also surpassed the strongest single-pass method, VLM2Vec-V2 (58.0), by 3.6 points. All comparisons were conducted with the same Qwen2-VL-2B backbone and training data.
- **Modality-Specific Gains:**
 - **Image:** PLUME achieved 66.3, slightly trailing UME-R1 (66.6). However, it showed the best score on Image Grounding (79.7), suggesting benefits for compositional spatial reasoning.
 - **Video:** PLUME demonstrated clear gains, reaching 44.1 compared to UME-R1's 42.2 (+1.9 points). This advantage was particularly pronounced on Video Multi-modal Retrieval (46.7 vs. 39.7, +7.0 points), supporting the hypothesis that continuous latent states better preserve temporal dynamics and cross-frame relationships than discrete tokens.
 - **Visual Document (VisDoc):** PLUME achieved 67.5, a notable improvement over UME-R1's 63.9 (+3.6 points). It also set the best score on VisDoc OOD (57.4), indicating benefits for structurally complex inputs and out-of-distribution generalization.
- **Per-task Performance:** Visualizations indicated that PLUME consistently outperformed UME-R1 and single-pass baselines across most sub-tasks within MMEB-v2.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Accuracy-Efficiency Tradeoff

- **Inference Speedup:** PLUME compressed reasoning from an average of 403 generated tokens (UME-R1) to 8 latent steps. This resulted in a reduction of per-sample latency from 9023 ms (UME-R1) to 298 ms, representing a 30.3× speedup.
- **Overhead Comparison:** Compared to the single-pass baseline VLM2Vec-V2 (156 ms), PLUME introduced less than 150 ms of overhead while improving overall accuracy by 2.1 points.
- **Pareto Frontier:** The research indicated that PLUME occupies a favorable position on the accuracy-efficiency Pareto frontier. At 6 latent steps, it surpassed UME-R1's accuracy while running over 30x faster. Even at 4 latent steps, it outperformed all single-pass baselines with throughput comparable to VLM2Vec-V2. This suggests that a modest latent computation budget can yield substantial reasoning gains with minimal cost.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Progressive Curriculum:** Removing the progressive curriculum resulted in the largest performance drop (−6.8 overall), particularly affecting Video tasks (−7.6). This confirmed that the gradual transfer from explicit to latent reasoning is crucial for training stability and effective knowledge transfer.
- **Latent Transition:** Eliminating the latent transition (i.e., reading embedding from the last prefix token) reduced overall accuracy by 2.8 points, validating the benefit of iterative hidden-space computation.
- **MoE Adapter:** Replacing the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) adapter with a single shared MLP led to a 2.4-point drop, with VisDoc tasks being most affected (−3.1), indicating the value of specialized expert pathways for document understanding.
- **Semantic Anchor:** Removing the semantic anchor from the router negatively impacted performance, especially on Video tasks (−1.8), suggesting its importance in guiding temporal reasoning with global input context.
- **Latent Steps (K):** Accuracy improved steadily from K=4 to K=8 (+1.7 points overall), with diminishing returns observed after K=6. Latency increased roughly linearly with K. K=8 offered the best absolute accuracy, while K=6 provided a favorable balance.
- **Transition Adapter Design:** Ablations on the adapter confirmed the benefits of a shared expert (complementing specialized experts) and top-2 routing (capturing richer transition patterns than top-1). Both the semantic anchor and step embedding contributed to routing, providing global input context and positional progression, respectively.

#### 4.4. Diagnostic Analysis

- **Task-level Routing:** Analysis of specialized expert activation patterns showed meaningful specialization. For instance, Expert 2 was highly active on video classification and multimodal retrieval, while Expert 1 showed preference for question-answering tasks, and Expert 0 for image grounding and retrieval. These patterns emerged without explicit task labels, indicating that the semantic-anchor-guided router adapts latent computation to structural demands.
- **Latent Trajectory Visualization:** Comparing cosine similarity between intermediate states and the positive target showed that PLUME exhibited smoother trajectories with consistently smaller variance than UME-R1, particularly on video retrieval. This suggested that latent reasoning provides a more consistent intermediate computation path, less susceptible to variability from discrete token generation.
- **Limitations:** PLUME showed a notable gap on some Image QA subsets, particularly text-rich or knowledge-intensive benchmarks like ChartQA and InfographicsVQA. This limitation was hypothesized to stem from the compression of fine-grained textual detail and explicit intermediate semantic organization into a short latent rollout optimized for retrieval-oriented representation. Formal interpretability guarantees for continuous latent trajectories remained an open problem.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PLUME introduces a new paradigm for Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) that addresses a critical challenge in the field: how to effectively integrate multi-step reasoning into embedding formation without incurring substantial computational overhead. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Efficiency in Reasoning-Enhanced Retrieval:** PLUME demonstrates that strong reasoning capabilities can be achieved within UME systems while maintaining, and even significantly improving, inference efficiency. By replacing verbose explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation with a compact latent rollout (reducing reasoning from hundreds of tokens to fewer than ten latent steps), PLUME achieves over 30× faster inference compared to explicit CoT baselines. This efficiency gain is crucial for the practical deployment of UME systems in real-world scenarios that demand low latency and high throughput, such as large-scale multimodal search engines or recommendation systems.

2. **Improved Performance on Complex Modalities:** The observed gains, particularly on video and visual document retrieval, suggest that latent reasoning is well-suited for modalities where evidence is dense, structurally complex, and difficult to linearize into discrete textual rationales. By maintaining continuous hidden states across reasoning steps, PLUME can preserve richer temporal and structural semantics, leading to more robust retrieval performance in these challenging domains. This impact extends to scenarios requiring compositional spatial reasoning or out-of-distribution generalization.

3. **Adaptive and Structured Latent Computation:** The introduction of a semantic-anchor-guided transition adapter allows the latent reasoning process to adapt dynamically to the heterogeneous nature of multimodal inputs. This input-adaptive allocation of a fixed latent computation budget enables different queries to follow distinct reasoning trajectories, optimizing for diverse grounding requirements and reasoning structures without increasing the overall model size or inference cost. This design principle could influence the development of more flexible and general-purpose multimodal models.

4. **Novel Training Methodology:** The progressive explicit-to-latent curriculum provides a stable and effective method for transferring knowledge from verbalized reasoning traces into hidden-state computation. This curriculum serves as a blueprint for training models to internalize complex behaviors, offering a potential solution for overcoming instability during the transition from explicit supervision to implicit execution in other domains of AI research.

5. **Advancing the Theoretical Understanding of Reasoning:** PLUME contributes to the broader understanding of how reasoning can be performed within neural networks. It suggests that structured internal computation can retain the benefits of intermediate reasoning, offering an alternative to explicit symbolic or textual reasoning. The diagnostic analyses, showing smoother and more stable latent trajectories compared to explicit CoT, indicate that continuous latent representations may be less prone to "verbal lock-in" or "bottlenecking" by discrete tokens, thereby offering a more robust reasoning path for certain tasks.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including exploring more sophisticated latent reasoning architectures, enhancing the interpretability of continuous latent trajectories, and addressing remaining weaknesses in areas requiring extremely fine-grained textual or numerical preservation (e.g., specific QA tasks). The framework also sets a precedent for how multimodal foundation models can be fine-tuned to achieve specialized performance while retaining efficiency.

In conclusion, PLUME offers a significant advancement in UME by reconciling the need for complex reasoning with the demand for efficient inference, thereby bringing reasoning-enhanced capabilities closer to practical application in diverse multimodal retrieval systems.
