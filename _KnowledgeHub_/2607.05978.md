# Propose and Attend: Training-free MLLM Grounding Confidence via Multi-Token Localized Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> MTLA (Multi-Token Localized Attention) is a training-free, post-hoc confidence score for localized MLLM predictions: it reads decoder attention from all tokens of a prediction and sums only the attention mass falling inside the region that prediction claims, averaged over heads and middle layers. High scores mark grounded predictions; re-ranking by MTLA lifts zero-shot COCO detection AP from 20.4 to 37.0.

## Problem

- Token log-probabilities conflate grounding quality with input ambiguity, and coordinate tokens turn near-deterministic once the model commits, so they barely separate grounded from hallucinated regions.
- Prior attention-based detectors sum attention over the entire input modality and read a single response token, discarding the spatial and temporal structure that localization outputs carry.
- Concurrent patch-level hallucination detectors need added supervision and training, and MLLMs are seldom evaluated as detectors under standard metrics such as AP, Recall@1, or PSDS1.

## Method

- **Localized Attention** restricts the attention sum to modality tokens intersecting the prediction's own ==proposed region==: image patches inside the predicted box, or frames inside the predicted temporal span.
- **Multi-Token** aggregation averages that localized attention over every token of the prediction (each coordinate digit plus the semantic label), then over all heads and a fixed ==middle layer band L8-21==.
- Optional ==self-consistency voting== draws N stochastic rollouts, merges candidates via ==non-maximum suppression==, and scores each cluster by its maximum **MTLA**, summed instead on COCO to reward recurring regions.

## Results

- Hallucination AUROC beats the SVAR baseline everywhere: **89.0 vs 82.3** on COCO with Qwen3-VL, **75.2 vs 67.1** on Gemma-4, **80.0 vs 41.5** on QVHighlights, **81.3 vs 60.9** on AudioSet.
- Re-ranking raises COCO AP from **20.43** raw output order to **32.12** at one rollout and **37.01** at sixteen, above SVAR's **28.43** and approaching supervised DETR's **42.0**.
- Ablations: localizing the mask adds up to **+43 AUROC** on QVHighlights; Charades R@1@0.5 rises **44.0 to 55.4** and AudioSet PSDS1 **0.20 to 0.26**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Score confidence relative to the region a prediction claims, not globally over the input; global attention aggregation degrades badly as inputs lengthen, as on 150-second video.
> - Grounding evidence is distributed across a prediction's tokens, so any single coordinate or label token gives only a noisy, partial view of where attention went.
> - Internal attention can replace a trained confidence head, but it costs N generative rollouts and needs attention maps that optimized inference stacks often do not expose.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has enabled a unified approach to various tasks, including image captioning, visual question answering, and increasingly, localization tasks across different modalities. MLLMs like Qwen, Gemma, and Audio Flamingo can generate localized predictions, such as spatial bounding boxes for objects in images or temporal windows for events in video and audio, directly through language generation. This contrasts with traditional localization methods that rely on specialized architectures (e.g., DETR, GLIP, OWL-ViT, Grounding DINO) explicitly designed and optimized for these tasks.

A significant challenge confronting MLLMs in localization is the phenomenon of hallucination, where models generate predictions that do not correspond to actual objects or events in the input. While existing research has addressed hallucination in MLLM outputs, particularly in image captioning, these methods often aggregate attention globally across the entire input modality (e.g., Summed Visual Attention Ratio – SVAR) or require additional supervision and training (e.g., methods that modify decoding processes or train patch-level classifiers). The model's own token log-probabilities, a natural candidate for confidence, have been found to be largely uninformative for distinguishing grounded predictions from hallucinations, as they conflate grounding quality with input ambiguity and become near-deterministic once a model commits to generating coordinates.

This paper addresses the gap in reliable, training-free confidence estimation for the localized outputs of MLLMs. It builds upon prior work that uses internal attention or hidden-state representations for hallucination detection but introduces key distinctions: it focuses on the localized nature of grounding predictions by restricting attention to the claimed region, aggregates signals across multiple prediction tokens, and operates as a training-free, post-hoc scoring mechanism rather than requiring decoding-time intervention or additional training. The work positions itself as a method to enhance the reliability of MLLMs as generalist localization systems, enabling them to approach the performance of specialized supervised detectors without task-specific fine-tuning. Furthermore, it advocates for a more rigorous evaluation of MLLMs on standard detection metrics like Average Precision (AP), Recall@1, and PSDS1, which have been less explored in MLLM assessments compared to captioning or chat benchmarks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reliable and training-free method for estimating the grounding confidence of localized predictions generated by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This method aims to identify and suppress hallucinated predictions, thereby improving the overall accuracy and trustworthiness of MLLMs when deployed for localization tasks across various modalities.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations and emerging needs in the field of MLLMs:

1. **High Hallucination Rates in MLLM Localization:** MLLMs, despite their ability to generate spatial bounding boxes and temporal windows, frequently produce "hallucinated" regions that do not correspond to actual entities in the input. The paper highlights that between 58% and 68% of regions emitted by state-of-the-art MLLMs across four localization benchmarks are invalid. This substantial unreliability prevents MLLMs from serving as robust localization systems comparable to specialized detectors.

2. **Inadequate Intrinsic Confidence Signals:** Practical localization systems require accurate confidence estimates for individual predictions to manage the trade-off between precision and recall. However, the most natural confidence signal from MLLMs—their own token log-probabilities—has been shown to be largely uninformative. These probabilities poorly distinguish between grounded and hallucinated predictions because they reflect factors like input ambiguity and the model's commitment during autoregressive generation, rather than the actual visual or temporal grounding. For instance, coordinate tokens might become near-deterministic once the model has committed to a region, regardless of its factual basis.

3. **Limitations of Existing Hallucination Detection Methods:** Prior attention-based approaches for hallucination detection (e.g., SVAR) typically aggregate attention globally over the entire input modality and often focus on a single response token (e.g., the first sub-token of an object word). These methods do not fully exploit the localized nature of grounding predictions, where each output is explicitly tied to a specific spatial or temporal region. They either discard the inherent structural information of localization outputs or require additional supervision and training, making them less suitable for a universally applicable, training-free confidence score.

Based on these observations, the authors identify two key factors essential for effective confidence estimation in MLLM localization:

- **Localized Evidence:** Grounded predictions are expected to draw heavily on modality evidence *within their own proposed region*, whereas hallucinations often rely more on contextual information distributed elsewhere in the input. This suggests that confidence should be measured relative to the prediction's *self-claimed region* rather than globally.
- **Multi-Token Signal:** Localization predictions are inherently multi-token outputs (e.g., multiple coordinate digits, plus a semantic label). Individual tokens may provide noisy or incomplete evidence. Aggregating information across *all* prediction tokens is hypothesized to yield a more robust and complete grounding signal.

The research aims to integrate these insights into a novel confidence scoring framework, termed Multi-Token Localized Attention (MTLA), that is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and applicable uniformly across diverse modalities and MLLM families. The ultimate goal is to enable MLLMs to provide reliable localization outputs, effectively narrowing the performance gap between generalist MLLMs and specialized supervised detectors without requiring task-specific training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Multi-Token Localized Attention (MTLA), is a training-free, post-hoc confidence estimation framework designed for localized predictions generated by MLLMs. It leverages the model's internal attention mechanisms to assess the grounding quality of each prediction.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

The framework addresses grounding tasks where an MLLM autoregressively generates one or more localized predictions. Each prediction `p` consists of a region `R_p` (e.g., a bounding box `[x1, y1, x2, y2]` for images or a temporal interval `[t_start, t_end]` for video/audio) and an associated semantic label. Predictions are typically formatted as structured text (e.g., JSON lists). A prediction is defined as "hallucinated" if its `R_p` does not match any ground-truth region with an Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of at least 0.5. The objective is to assign a scalar score `s(p)` to each prediction, reflecting its likelihood of being grounded.

#### 3.2. Multi-Token Localized Attention (MTLA)

Let `X` be the set of modality tokens for the input (e.g., visual patches, audio frames), and `a^(l,h)_q→k` be the attention weight from a generated response token `q` to an input modality token `k` at layer `l` and head `h`.

1. **Baseline: Global Attention (GA)**: As a point of comparison, Global Attention (similar to SVAR) is defined as the sum of attention from a response token `q` over *all* input modality tokens `k ∈ X`:
 `GA^(l,h)(q) = Σ_{k∈X} a^(l,h)_q→k`

2. **Localized Attention (LA)**: MTLA's first core component is to restrict the attention aggregation. Instead of summing over all modality tokens, LA sums attention only over modality tokens `k` that fall *inside the prediction's own proposed region R_p*. Let `M(R_p)` be the set of modality tokens within `R_p`.
 `LA^(l,h)(q) = Σ_{k∈M(R_p)} a^(l,h)_q→k`
 For images, `M(R_p)` includes visual tokens (patches) overlapping the predicted box. For video and audio, it includes tokens whose timestamps fall within the predicted temporal span. This step requires no modality-specific modification beyond constructing the mask `M(R_p)`.

3. **Multi-Token Aggregation**: The second core component addresses the multi-token nature of localized predictions. Instead of relying on a single response token, MTLA aggregates attention across *all* response tokens associated with a prediction `p`. Let `Q_p` denote the set of tokens encoding `R_p` and its label. MTLA is defined as the average localized attention across all tokens in `Q_p`:
 `MTLA^(l,h)(p) = (1 / |Q_p|) * Σ_{q∈Q_p} LA^(l,h)(q)`
 For models that tokenize numbers digit-by-digit, a single coordinate (e.g., `421`) might span multiple tokens, and all these tokens contribute to `Q_p`.

4. **Layer and Head Reduction**: To obtain a single scalar confidence score `s(p)` for prediction `p`, MTLA is averaged across attention heads and over a fixed band of middle transformer layers (`L`). The paper uses layers 8 to 21 (L8–21) out of 36 layers for Qwen and Gemma, identified from prior work as layers where visual information is integrated, and all layers for Audio Flamingo. This band is fixed and reused across models and modalities without tuning.
 `s(p) = (1 / |L|) * Σ_{l∈L} (1 / H) * Σ_{h=1}^H MTLA^(l,h)(p)`

#### 3.3. Self-Consistency Voting (Optional)

To enhance recall and refine predictions, the method optionally generates `N` stochastic rollouts (multiple generations with temperature sampling) per input. These candidate predictions are then merged using non-maximum suppression (NMS). Each retained prediction is scored by either the maximum MTLA score over its cluster (for single-segment tasks) or the sum of MTLA scores of its cluster (for multi-segment tasks, rewarding recurring regions).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details (from Supplementary Material)

- **Models:** Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, Gemma-4 E4B-it, and Audio Flamingo 3 are used.
- **Decoding:** Greedy decoding for single predictions; temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.95 for self-consistency rollouts.
- **Structured Output Parsing:** A flexible parser adapts to the natural output syntax of each MLLM family (e.g., JSON lists for image boxes, `[start, end]` for temporal events), identifying the token positions for coordinates and labels.
- **Audio Localization Pipeline:** For Audio Flamingo 3, a cascade is used: (i) *Propose* candidate sound events by prompting; (ii) *Normalize* free-text labels to canonical AudioSet classes; (iii) *Localize* by querying the model for temporal windows for each candidate class; (iv) *Score and fuse* predictions with MTLA and NMS. The audio token time mapping accounts for a 25 Hz token rate, and the inside-region mask is dilated by ±2 tokens (±80ms) for PSDS1 results.
- **Attention Extraction:** MTLA requires access to decoder's self-attention weights. A single eager-attention forward pass is run to record attention from response tokens (`Q_p`) onto input modality tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that Multi-Token Localized Attention (MTLA) significantly enhances the reliability of MLLM localization outputs across image, video, and audio modalities, primarily by improving hallucination detection and re-ranking capabilities.

#### 4.1. Image Spatial Grounding on COCO Detection (Qwen3-VL-8B, Gemma-4)

- **Hallucination Rates:** MLLMs exhibit high hallucination rates on COCO val2017, with Qwen3-VL-8B generating 68.1% of predictions that do not match any ground-truth object.
- **Ineffectiveness of Token Probabilities:** Token log-probabilities proved to be a poor signal for distinguishing grounded from hallucinated predictions, achieving only 69.0 AUROC for Qwen3-VL-8B. The distributions of token probabilities for grounded and hallucinated predictions showed high overlap, indicating that these probabilities are driven by token position and refinement level rather than actual grounding.
- **Improved Hallucination Detection:** MTLA consistently outperformed prior training-free baselines in hallucination detection. For Qwen3-VL-8B, MTLA achieved an AUROC of 89.0, a notable improvement over SVAR (82.3) and other baselines like GLSim (70.8) and ContextualLens (68.1). Similar gains were observed for Gemma-4 E4B (MTLA: 75.2 vs. SVAR: 67.1).
- **Enhanced COCO Detection Accuracy:** When used as a confidence score for re-ranking Qwen3-VL-8B predictions, MTLA substantially improved Average Precision (AP). A single generation (N=1) with MTLA increased AP from 20.43 (raw predictions) to 32.12. With self-consistency voting (N=16 rollouts), MTLA further boosted AP to 37.01, nearly doubling the raw-output AP. This performance significantly narrowed the gap to specialized supervised detectors like DETR-R50 (42.0 AP), without any task-specific training.

#### 4.2. Video Temporal Grounding (Qwen3-VL-8B on Charades-STA and QVHighlights)

- **Hallucination Rates:** High hallucination rates were observed (58.5% on Charades-STA, 66.1% on QVHighlights).
- **Improved Hallucination Detection:** MTLA demonstrated substantial improvements in hallucination detection. On Charades-STA, AUROC increased from 51.1 (SVAR) to 66.8. On QVHighlights, the gain was even larger, from 41.5 (SVAR) to 80.0, indicating that global attention is less reliable for longer videos. Token-confidence baselines remained weak (52.7–59.5 AUROC).
- **Enhanced Temporal Grounding Accuracy:** Re-ranking N=16 rollouts with MTLA improved Charades-STA Recall@1@0.5 from 44.0 to 55.4, and QVHighlights AP from 24.4 to 36.6. These scores approached the range of supervised temporal grounding methods like Moment-DETR and QD-DETR, while remaining zero-shot and training-free.

#### 4.3. Audio Temporal Localization (Audio Flamingo 3 on AudioSet-Strong)

- **Improved Hallucination Detection:** MTLA achieved an AUROC of 81.3, significantly outperforming SVAR (60.9) by restricting attention to the predicted time span.
- **Enhanced Localization Accuracy:** Re-ranking and fusing predictions using MTLA improved the PSDS1 metric from 0.20 (raw predictions) to 0.26, nearing the performance of a supervised sound-event detector like BEATs (0.33) without task-specific training.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Aggregated Token Set and Region Mask:** Both components were found to contribute. Replacing global attention with localized attention consistently improved AUROC for every proposal-token set (e.g., label mean: 81.9 to 83.5 AUROC; coord mean: 87.2 to 88.9 on COCO). Aggregating attention across multiple prediction tokens (coordinates and label) provided a more robust signal than relying on a single token, as the grounding evidence is distributed.
- **Layer Band Selection:** Using a fixed band of middle transformer layers (L8–21) yielded consistent improvements on video temporal benchmarks (+5.8 AUROC on Charades, +4.6 on QVH) and was neutral on image/audio. This confirmed that the grounding signal is concentrated in these layers but that MTLA is robust even without specific layer selection.

In summary, MTLA consistently provides a stronger, more discriminative grounding signal than prior training-free baselines across diverse modalities, effectively suppressing hallucinations and substantially improving localization accuracy in a zero-shot, training-free manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Multi-Token Localized Attention (MTLA) holds several significant implications for the field of multimodal AI and the practical deployment of MLLMs:

1. **Bridging the Reliability Gap for MLLMs in Localization:** The primary impact of MTLA is its ability to address a critical limitation of current MLLMs: their propensity to hallucinate localized predictions. By providing a robust, training-free confidence score, MTLA enables MLLMs to become more reliable and trustworthy for tasks requiring precise object or event localization. This increases the practical utility of generalist MLLMs, allowing them to perform tasks traditionally reserved for specialized, supervised models.

2. **Enhancing Zero-Shot Generalization:** MTLA's training-free nature and architecture-agnostic design mean it can be applied directly to a wide range of MLLM families and across different modalities (image, video, audio) without any task-specific fine-tuning or model retraining. This significantly expands the zero-shot generalization capabilities of MLLMs, making them more versatile and cost-effective tools for novel localization problems where labeled training data might be scarce or non-existent. The demonstrated performance gains, such as nearly doubling COCO detection AP for an open-source 8B generalist, underscore its potential to democratize high-quality localization.

3. **Advancing MLLM Evaluation Protocols:** The paper highlights the necessity of evaluating MLLMs on standard localization metrics (e.g., AP, Recall@1, PSDS1), which have traditionally been overlooked in favor of captioning or chat benchmarks. By demonstrating substantial improvements on these metrics, the work reinforces the importance of a comprehensive evaluation framework for MLLMs, pushing the community towards more rigorous assessments of their grounding capabilities. This could lead to better-designed MLLMs that are optimized for both linguistic fluency and factual grounding.

4. **Novel Insights into MLLM Internal Mechanisms:** The findings regarding localized attention and multi-token aggregation offer valuable insights into how MLLMs process and ground information. Identifying that grounded predictions strongly attend to their claimed region and that this signal is distributed across multiple output tokens provides a deeper understanding of MLLM behavior. These insights could inform future MLLM architectures or training methodologies aimed at inherently improving grounding capabilities.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** MTLA establishes a strong baseline for training-free confidence estimation. Future work could explore integrating similar localized, multi-token attention principles directly into the decoding process, investigating alternative aggregation strategies, or extending the approach to other modalities and more complex grounding tasks. While MTLA has computational limitations due to multiple rollouts and attention map access, it paves the way for more efficient, yet equally effective, grounding mechanisms.

In essence, MTLA represents a practical and theoretically grounded step towards making MLLMs truly reliable "generalist" systems for diverse localization tasks, unlocking their potential in applications ranging from autonomous systems to content analysis, where accurate and trustworthy spatial or temporal grounding is paramount.
