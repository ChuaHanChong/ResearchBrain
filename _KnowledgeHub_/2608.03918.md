# When and Where to Look: Adaptive Visual Evidence Scheduling for Efficient Long Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> EcoFrame, a training-free framework developed by Peking University researchers, allows Vision-Language Models to efficiently process long videos by adaptively scheduling visual evidence based on internal VLM signals like output entropy and frame-level attention. This approach achieves substantial speedups (up to 13.5x) and improved accuracy (up to 4.9%) compared to existing methods across various benchmarks and VLM backbones.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face significant computational and memory constraints when processing thousands of frames in long videos, exceeding typical context windows.
- Existing methods for long-video understanding are either inefficient (uniform sampling), lack adaptivity to query demands (static relevance-based), or incur high computational overhead with iterative reasoning (agent-based).
- There is a need for an efficient and query-adaptive framework that can dynamically select relevant visual evidence from long videos without requiring extensive computation or training.

## Method

- **EcoFrame** implements a training-free, iterative framework that leverages a VLM's internal inference signals to adaptively refine visual evidence.
- It uses '==entropy-gated budget scheduling==' to determine when to stop or expand the frame budget based on the VLM's output uncertainty.
- An '==attention-guided candidate proposal==' module employs frame-level attention to efficiently identify and search promising temporal regions for additional evidence, complemented by ==marginal-utility frame reselection== to balance relevance and diversity.

## Results

- EcoFrame achieves up to a **13.5x** speedup over agent-based methods and up to a **1.89x** speedup over static relevance-based methods in inference latency.
- It demonstrates an average accuracy improvement of up to **4.9%** compared to static relevance-based baselines, while efficiently utilizing a lower average number of frames (typically **17-21 frames**).
- The framework maintains strong performance and generalizability across diverse VLM backbones (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3) and long-video question answering benchmarks like Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and MLVU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The output entropy of a VLM correlates with the sufficiency of provided visual evidence, allowing it to signal when enough information has been gathered to answer a query.
> - Frame-level attention scores generated during VLM inference provide a reliable temporal prior, indicating regions of interest to guide the search for additional visual evidence.
> - Balancing both query-frame relevance and temporal diversity is crucial for selecting an optimal set of input frames that comprehensively represent the video content relevant to the query.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in the field of artificial intelligence, specifically in the domain of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) applied to long-video understanding. Modern VLMs, while capable of understanding complex visual and linguistic information, face significant computational and memory constraints when processing long videos. These videos can contain thousands of frames, far exceeding the typical context windows of VLMs, making direct processing prohibitive.

Existing approaches to handle long videos in VLMs primarily fall into two categories:

- **Query-Agnostic Methods**: The most straightforward approach is uniform sampling, which selects frames at fixed temporal intervals. However, this method is inefficient as it fails to account for the varying information density and relevance across a video for a given query.
- **Query-Aware Frame Selection (Static Relevance-based)**: These methods improve upon uniform sampling by creating a candidate pool of frames (e.g., by sampling at a fixed frame rate) and then using a pre-trained vision-language encoder (like CLIP) to compute a relevance score between the query and each candidate frame. A fixed number of top-relevance frames are then selected in a single step. Examples include AKS, BOLT, and FOCUS. While more efficient than processing all frames, these methods are "static" because they typically rely on a fixed frame budget and a pre-defined candidate pool, lacking the adaptivity to adjust based on the specific evidence requirements of each query. A dense candidate pool incurs substantial encoding and relevance-computation costs, while a sparse one may miss crucial localized events.
- **Agent-Based (Agentic) Methods**: These approaches introduce adaptivity by employing an agent that iteratively reasons, verifies candidate frames, and searches for new frames based on prior feedback. Methods like A.I.R. and VideoAgent fall into this category. While highly adaptive, their reliance on multiple rounds of VLM reasoning and iterative search introduces considerable computational overhead and latency, making them less practical for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

The presented work, EcoFrame, positions itself within this landscape by seeking to bridge the gap between the efficiency of static methods and the adaptivity of agentic methods. It aims to achieve query-adaptive visual evidence scheduling without incurring the high computational costs associated with multi-round agent-based reasoning. This is achieved by leveraging internal inference-time signals from the VLM itself, rather than external agents or extensive pre-computation. This approach addresses the fundamental trade-off between efficiency and adaptivity in long-video understanding, aiming to provide a lightweight, training-free, and adaptive solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly efficient and query-adaptive framework for selecting sparse visual evidence from long videos, enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to understand video content accurately without prohibitive computational costs.

The motivation for this work stems from two core observations regarding the limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Inefficiency and Lack of Adaptivity in Current Methods**:
 - **Uniform sampling** is query-agnostic, often providing irrelevant frames or missing critical information, leading to suboptimal accuracy.
 - **Static relevance-based methods** (e.g., BOLT, AKS) select frames in a single, one-shot process from a pre-defined candidate pool with a fixed frame budget. While more query-aware than uniform sampling, they cannot dynamically adjust the number of frames or the search regions based on the complexity or specific demands of a query. A simple query might require only a few frames, while a complex one might need evidence distributed across various temporal segments. Imposing a fixed budget can be either insufficient or redundant.
 - **Agentic methods** (e.g., A.I.R.) offer adaptivity by performing iterative reasoning and evidence verification. However, this adaptivity comes at a significant cost due to multiple rounds of VLM inference and search, leading to high latency and computational overhead.

These limitations highlight a fundamental trade-off: current methods are either efficient but not adaptive enough, or adaptive but computationally expensive. This motivates the central research question: *how can a VLM adapt its visual evidence to each query without expensive iterative reasoning?*

To address this question, the authors identify two coupled challenges that need to be resolved with minimal overhead:

1. **Challenge 1: Evidence Sufficiency Estimation**: The model needs a mechanism to assess whether the currently provided visual evidence is sufficient to answer the query correctly. If not, it must decide when to stop (if enough evidence is gathered) or when to expand the frame budget. A fixed frame budget cannot cater to the variable evidence needs of different queries.
 - **Motivation (Observation 1)**: Through empirical analysis, the authors observe that the VLM's output entropy (characterizing the concentration of its predictive distribution) correlates with evidence sufficiency. Lower entropy generally indicates higher predictive certainty and more sufficient evidence, while higher entropy suggests uncertainty or insufficient evidence. This internal signal can potentially guide budget adjustments.

2. **Challenge 2: Targeted Temporal Search**: When the current evidence is deemed insufficient, the model must efficiently determine *where* to search for additional frames in the long video. Relying on a globally dense candidate pool for initial selection is computationally expensive for encoding and scoring, while a sparse or fixed pool might miss localized but crucial evidence. The challenge is to expand the candidate pool towards promising regions without exhaustive search or costly agent-based techniques.
 - **Motivation (Observation 2)**: The authors analyze the frame-level attention scores generated by the VLM during inference. They find a positive correlation between attention concentration and the relevance of nearby temporal regions. Concentrated attention suggests the VLM has localized a promising area, favoring dense local search. Diffuse attention might indicate widely distributed evidence or the absence of a clear focus, requiring broader temporal coverage. This internal signal could guide the targeted expansion of the candidate pool.

By addressing these challenges using internal VLM inference feedback (output entropy and frame-level attention), the research aims to develop a training-free framework, EcoFrame, that achieves query-adaptive visual evidence scheduling with low computational overhead, thus offering a practical and efficient solution for long-video understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EcoFrame is presented as a training-free framework that performs low-overhead query-adaptive visual evidence scheduling by leveraging internal signals from the Vision-Language Model (VLM) during inference. The methodology involves an iterative process of VLM inference, output analysis, and adaptive evidence refinement.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The process unfolds over a sequence of frame budgets, $B_1 < \dots < B_T \le B_{\text{max}}$. At each round $t$:
1. **Input Preparation**: An input frame set $S_t$ (containing $B_t$ frames) is provided to the target VLM.
2. **VLM Inference**: The VLM processes $S_t$ and the query $Q$, producing an answer $\hat{y}_t$, an output entropy $e_t$, and frame-level attention scores $a_t$.
3. **Scheduling Decision**:
 - **When to Stop/Expand (Entropy-Gated Budget Scheduling)**: If $e_t$ is below a round-dependent threshold $\tau_t$, or if $B_t$ has reached $B_{\text{max}}$, the process terminates, returning $\hat{y}_t$.
 - **Where to Search (Attention-Guided Candidate Proposal)**: Otherwise, the frame budget is increased to $B_{t+1}$. The frame-level attention $a_t$ is used as a temporal prior to expand the candidate frame pool $P_t$ into $P_{t+1}$.
4. **Frame Reselection**: A new input frame set $S_{t+1}$ is constructed from $P_{t+1}$ based on query-frame relevance and temporal coverage, forming the input for the next round.

This closed-loop system reuses signals from the VLM's own answer attempt to refine visual evidence without requiring additional reasoning or verification modules.

#### 3.2. Entropy-Gated Budget Scheduling

This module addresses the "when to stop or expand" challenge.
- **Output Entropy Calculation**: The output entropy $e_t$ is computed over the VLM's generated answer tokens (Eq. 1), averaged across token positions. A lower $e_t$ indicates a more concentrated predictive distribution and higher certainty.
- **Early Exit Condition**: At round $t$, if $e_t < \tau_t$, the current evidence is considered sufficient, and the scheduling terminates. This prevents unnecessary computation for queries that are easily answered. The process also terminates if the maximum budget $B_{\text{max}}$ is reached.
- **Budget Expansion**: If neither termination condition is met, the frame budget is expanded for the next round: $B_{t+1} = \min(\lceil\alpha B_t\rceil, B_{\text{max}})$, where $\alpha > 1$ (typically 2). This geometric schedule allows rapid budget expansion while keeping the number of rounds logarithmic.
- **Relaxing Threshold**: To accommodate query-dependent entropy levels, a round-dependent threshold $\tau_t = \tau_1 + (t-1)\Delta_\tau$ is used, where $\Delta_\tau \ge 0$. This allows for a stricter threshold in early rounds to prevent premature stopping, and a more tolerant threshold in later rounds for difficult queries that might inherently have higher uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Attention-Guided Candidate Proposal

This module addresses the "where to search" challenge when additional evidence is needed.
- **Frame-Level Attention Scores**: During VLM inference, self-attention reflects the model's focus on visual tokens. The token-level attention vector $w_t$ is computed from deep layers (Eq. 2), and then averaged over tokens within each input frame $f_{t,i} \in S_t$ to get a frame-level attention score $a_{t,i}$ (Eq. 3).
- **Temporal Cell Prior**: These sparse frame attention scores are propagated across the entire video timeline. By sorting input frames by timestamp, adjacent midpoints define temporal cells. Each unobserved location $x$ within a cell inherits the attention score of the cell's anchor frame, forming a continuous cell-level prior $A_t(x)$ across the video.
- **Candidate Generation**: When expanding the candidate pool from $P_t$ to $P_{t+1}$, a number of new candidates $M_{t+1} = \lceil m \cdot g(N) \cdot \Delta B_{t+1} \rceil$ are proposed. Here, $m$ is the candidate expansion ratio, and $g(N)$ is a capped length-aware factor (e.g., sublinear growth with video length $N$).
- **Scoring for New Candidates**: An unobserved frame $x$ is scored using a multiplicative score (Eq. 5): $\text{Score}_{\text{cand}}(x; P) = A_t(x)^{\lambda_{\text{attn}}} \cdot D(x; P)$.
 - $A_t(x)^{\lambda_{\text{attn}}}$: Favors regions with high attention (exploitation).
 - $D(x; P)$: Temporal distance to existing frames in $P_t$ (exploration/coverage), ensuring new candidates are not redundant.
 EcoFrame iteratively adds the highest-scoring frame to $P_t$ until $M_{t+1}$ new candidates are selected. Query-frame relevance scores for these new candidates are then computed (using a vision-language encoder like CLIP) and cached, forming $P_{t+1}$. This mechanism adaptively switches between dense local search in high-attention regions and broader global coverage when attention is diffuse.

#### 3.4. Marginal-Utility Frame Reselection

After expanding $P_t$ to $P_{t+1}$, the next input frame set $S_{t+1}$ (of size $B_{t+1}$) is reconstructed from the enlarged pool $P_{t+1}$.
- **Selection Score**: To balance relevance and diversity, each candidate $x \in P_{t+1}$ is scored (Eq. 7): $\text{Score}_{\text{select}}(x; S) = \hat{R}(x)^{\lambda_{\text{rel}}} \cdot D(x; S)$.
 - $\hat{R}(x)$: Normalized cached query-frame relevance (favoring relevant frames).
 - $D(x; S)$: Temporal distance to already selected frames in $S$ (favoring diversity).
- **Iterative Selection**: The process starts by selecting the most relevant candidate to $S$ and then repeatedly adds the highest-scoring remaining frame until $|S| = B_{t+1}$. This ensures that $S_{t+1}$ is both highly relevant and temporally diverse.

#### 3.5. Initialization and Bounded Computation

- **Initialization**: For the first round (before any VLM call provides attention feedback), an initial candidate pool $P_1$ is uniformly sampled, and the initial evidence set $S_1$ is constructed using the marginal-utility reselection process.
- **Bounded Computation**: The maximum VLM input budget is capped at $B_{\text{max}}$. The geometric budget schedule ensures cumulative VLM processing is bounded. Frame encoding and scoring costs for new candidates are bounded by $O(m \cdot g_{\text{max}} \cdot B_{\text{max}})$. This design ensures that computation remains manageable, even for difficult queries requiring multiple rounds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EcoFrame focuses on its accuracy-efficiency trade-off across various long-video question answering benchmarks and VLM backbones.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off (Table 1)

- **Consistent Superiority**: EcoFrame consistently achieves a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off compared to various baselines across Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and MLVU datasets, and generalizes well to LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL-3 VLM backbones.
- **Vs. Static Relevance-Based Methods (AKS, BOLT, FOCUS)**:
 - EcoFrame demonstrates notable accuracy improvements, with an average increase of up to 4.9% (e.g., 64.4% for EcoFrame vs. 63.5% for BOLT on Qwen2.5-VL).
 - It provides significant speedups, achieving up to 1.85x faster inference (on Qwen2.5-VL) compared to AKS and BOLT, and up to 1.89x speedup (on InternVL-3).
 - EcoFrame achieves this while utilizing a lower average number of frames for final answering (typically 17-21 frames), contrasting with static methods that use a fixed 32 frames. This indicates efficient resource allocation.
- **Vs. Agent-Based Methods (A.I.R.)**:
 - EcoFrame maintains comparable accuracy to A.I.R. (e.g., 64.4% for EcoFrame vs. 64.6% for A.I.R. on Qwen2.5-VL).
 - However, EcoFrame achieves a substantial reduction in inference latency, providing up to a 13.5x speedup over A.I.R. (e.g., 2.17s vs. 29.20s on LLaVA-OneVision). This highlights the efficiency benefits of reusing internal VLM signals over explicit multi-step agentic reasoning.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Analysis (Table 2)

- The latency breakdown reveals that EcoFrame introduces a small overhead compared to uniform sampling, but this is offset by a substantial accuracy gain.
- EcoFrame's adaptive nature allows it to allocate computation dynamically: simple queries can exit early, reducing both candidate frame scoring and VLM inference time. More complex queries receive additional, yet bounded, refinement rounds. This adaptive allocation contributes to higher average accuracy with lower average latency compared to static methods.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Entropy-Gated Budget Scheduling**: Comparing EcoFrame to fixed-budget inference demonstrates that the entropy-gated mechanism effectively adapts the frame budget to query demands. It significantly reduces latency (e.g., 2.45s vs. 3.79s for 32f fixed budget) with only a minor accuracy drop (e.g., 61.0% vs. 61.5% on Video-MME), showing a better accuracy-cost trade-off.
- **Attention-Guided Candidate Proposal**: This module is crucial for efficient evidence search. Removing it (using a fixed candidate pool) results in lower accuracy (e.g., 59.0% vs. 61.0% on Video-MME) due to non-adaptability. Removing the attention prior (only using distance for proposal) also leads to a noticeable accuracy drop (e.g., 60.7% vs. 61.0% on Video-MME), confirming the value of attention in guiding the search.
- **Marginal-Utility Frame Reselection**: This component, which balances relevance and temporal diversity, outperforms variants relying solely on relevance (e.g., 60.6% vs. 61.0% on Video-MME) or coverage (e.g., 57.6% vs. 61.0% on Video-MME), indicating its importance for selecting a representative and diverse input frame set.

#### 4.4. Entropy Thresholds (Table 4)

- Analysis of `τ_1` and `Δ_τ` parameters shows a consistent trade-off: stricter thresholds (lower `τ_1`, lower `Δ_τ`) lead to more frames and higher accuracy but also higher latency.
- Crucially, progressively relaxing the threshold (`Δ_τ > 0`) achieves a better trade-off compared to fixed thresholds (`Δ_τ = 0`). This supports the observation that harder queries benefit from more tolerant later-round thresholds, preventing premature exits while still managing budget.

#### 4.5. Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Figure 6)

- EcoFrame's performance is relatively stable across reasonable ranges for the candidate expansion ratio (`m`), attention sharpness (`λ_attn`), and relevance sharpness (`λ_rel`). This suggests that the framework is not overly sensitive to fine-tuning these parameters, allowing for moderate default values.

#### 4.6. Additional Findings from Appendix

- **VLM Encoder Robustness (Table 5)**: EcoFrame's performance is robust across different vision-language encoders for query-frame relevance scoring (CLIP-ViT-B, CLIP-ViT-L, SigLIP-so400m), although CLIP-ViT-L was chosen for its balance of representation strength and latency.
- **Frame Budget Schedules (Table 6)**: The default geometric schedule `B =` achieves a good balance. Reducing the maximum budget or skipping intermediate steps can impact accuracy or efficiency.
- **Length-Aware Candidate Number (Table 7)**: A capped sublinear growth for `g(N)` (Eq. 8) for candidate expansion proves superior. Constant factors do not scale, while linear growth incurs redundant scoring without proportional accuracy gains.
- **Open-Ended QA Generalization (Table 8)**: EcoFrame also shows a better accuracy-efficiency trade-off on ActivityNet-QA, an open-ended video QA benchmark, outperforming baselines and demonstrating that entropy-gated budget scheduling is effective even in free-form response generation contexts.
- **Consistency of Observations (Fig. 7, 8)**: The motivating observations regarding output entropy and frame-level attention correlation with evidence sufficiency and relevance hold consistently across additional benchmarks (LongVideoBench, ActivityNet-QA).
- **Attention Extraction (Table 9)**: Pre-RoPE attention from deep VLM layers (e.g., layers 19-21) provides a more reliable temporal prior, contributing to consistent performance improvements.

In summary, EcoFrame presents an effective and efficient solution for long-video understanding by adaptively managing visual evidence based on internal VLM signals, achieving strong performance metrics across various models and benchmarks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of EcoFrame represents a notable contribution to the field of efficient long-video understanding by Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing a Core Bottleneck**: Long-video understanding is a critical capability for advanced AI applications, yet it is significantly hampered by the computational and memory demands of processing vast numbers of frames. EcoFrame directly addresses this bottleneck by providing a method to efficiently select relevant visual evidence.
- **Innovative Use of Internal VLM Signals**: The core innovation lies in the training-free utilization of a VLM's *internal inference feedback* – specifically, output entropy for evidence sufficiency and frame-level attention for temporal focus. This contrasts with existing methods that either lack adaptivity or rely on costly external agents or complex multi-round verification processes. By "listening" to the VLM's own uncertainty and focus, EcoFrame enables self-adaptive behavior without requiring additional training or separate reasoning modules.
- **Improved Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off**: The results demonstrate that EcoFrame achieves a better balance between accuracy and computational efficiency compared to current state-of-the-art methods. It significantly reduces inference latency (up to 13.5x speedup over agent-based methods and 1.85x over static relevance-based methods) while maintaining or improving accuracy. This makes long-video VLM applications more viable in scenarios with limited computational resources or strict latency requirements.
- **Practical Generalizability and Deployability**: The framework's effectiveness has been demonstrated across diverse VLM backbones (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3) and various long-video question answering benchmarks. Its training-free nature simplifies integration into existing VLM pipelines, making it readily deployable without the need for extensive retraining or model modifications.
- **Adaptive Resource Allocation**: EcoFrame's ability to adapt the frame budget and search regions based on query demand means that computational resources are allocated more intelligently. Simple queries can be answered quickly with minimal evidence, while complex queries receive progressively more targeted evidence, optimizing overall processing. This dynamic resource management is crucial for scalable VLM deployment.
- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The work highlights the potential of leveraging internal model states for intelligent control and adaptation in complex AI systems. This paradigm, where models guide their own data acquisition and processing based on internal uncertainty and attention, could inspire future research in self-regulating and resource-aware AI for other long-context modalities and tasks. It suggests a path towards more autonomous and efficient AI systems.

In conclusion, EcoFrame offers a practical, efficient, and innovative solution for a fundamental challenge in multimodal AI. Its capacity to significantly enhance the performance and applicability of VLMs in long-video understanding tasks, coupled with its generalizability and training-free nature, positions it to have a substantial impact on the development and deployment of next-generation AI applications involving video content.
