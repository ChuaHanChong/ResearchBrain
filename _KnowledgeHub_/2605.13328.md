# What Limits Vision-and-Language Navigation ?

> [!summary] Summary
> StereoNav is a 3B stereo Vision-Language-Action framework for continuous vision-and-language navigation. It renders coarse or precise target-location priors as persistent red visual cues in egocentric stereo views, fuses semantic, structural, and geometric tokens from InternViT, DINOv2, and a stereo cost-volume encoder, and jointly predicts actions and depth, reaching 81.1% SR and 68.3% SPL on R2R-CE.

## Problem

- Prior VLN agents chase gains by scaling VLM backbones and training corpora, yet performance saturates and data scale correlates only weakly with navigation success.
- Existing agents collapse under deployment-grade perceptual instability: one baseline's success falls from 57.0% to 20.1% under viewpoint oscillation, another's SPL to 4.2% under motion blur.
- Natural-language instructions are under-specified, creating directional and docking ambiguity; failed episodes score 51.7 versus 77.3 on directional ambiguity against successful ones.

## Method

- ==Visual Rendering== projects a fuzzy or precise target-location prior into both stereo image planes each step as a red marker, with boundary-placed cues when the goal falls out of view.
- ==Unified Understanding Modeling== fuses InternViT semantic, DINOv2 structural, and FoundationStereo cost-volume geometric tokens with learnable weights 1.0, 0.6, and 0.2 into one representation.
- ==Joint Prediction Modeling== attaches an autoregressive four-step action head and a coarse-to-fine disparity head using ==Soft Argmin==, ==ConvGRU refinement==, and ==convex upsampling== as geometric supervision.

## Results

- On R2R-CE Val-Unseen the **3B** model reaches **2.1** NE, **82.4** OSR, **81.1** SR, **68.3** SPL, beating the best egocentric RGB baseline by **16.4** SR points; RxR-CE SR is **67.5**.
- Real-world Unitree G1 deployment across office, gym, lobby, outdoor scenes averages **60.6%** success versus **24.3%** for StreamVLN and **22.1%** for the DreamNav zero-shot baseline.
- Ablations: rendering priors adds **17.9** SR points over the monocular baseline, stereo 2D modeling **15.1**, depth supervision **7.1**; motion-blur degradation is **8.2%** versus **38.1%** and **89.6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structural priors can beat scale: injecting goal information as persistent in-image visual cues outperforms larger backbones and bigger corpora at a fraction of parameters.
> - Rendering coordinates into the perceptual space, rather than passing them as symbolic tokens, keeps guidance usable even when the prior is off by roughly two meters.
> - Auxiliary geometry helps only in moderation: over-weighting stereo depth tokens injects low-level matching noise and erodes the semantic grounding that action prediction depends on.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a foundational area within embodied artificial intelligence, focusing on enabling autonomous agents to follow natural language instructions within complex, visually rich environments. Traditionally, VLN research has concentrated on enhancing an agent's cross-modal understanding by leveraging increasingly powerful Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones and expanding training datasets. This approach aims to improve the agent's ability to ground language instructions into egocentric visual observations and generate appropriate navigation actions.

Despite significant advancements in simulated environments, a recognized challenge in VLN is the substantial performance degradation observed when agents are deployed in real-world settings. This "Sim-to-Real gap" manifests as brittle behavior and inconsistent execution in the presence of physical realities such as varying lighting conditions, camera motion blur, and unstructured environments. While many existing methods continue to pursue the scaling paradigm—employing larger models and more data—this paper argues that such strategies are approaching diminishing returns.

The authors position their work as addressing two under-explored yet critical bottlenecks that contribute to this Sim-to-Real gap: perceptual instability and instructional under-specification. Previous studies have acknowledged these factors, but often as anecdotal observations rather than subjects of systematic analysis and targeted solutions. By systematically quantifying the impact of these factors and proposing a dedicated framework, this research seeks to move beyond solely enhancing cross-modal understanding. It aims to develop robust structural priors that can effectively bridge the divide between abstract language instructions and the noisy, dynamic nature of physical world perceptions, thereby improving the practical deployability and reliability of VLN agents. This work contributes to the growing body of research emphasizing the importance of embodied perception and robust spatial reasoning for real-world robotic applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify and address the fundamental limitations hindering the reliable real-world deployment of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents, beyond merely scaling up model size and training data. The authors propose that current VLN agents suffer from significant performance degradation during Sim-to-Real transfer due to two specific, often neglected, factors:

1. **Environmental Perception Instability:** In physical environments, egocentric visual observations are frequently corrupted by real-world phenomena such as motion blur, illumination shifts, camera shake, and varying viewpoints (e.g., height fluctuations, viewpoint oscillation). Agents trained on idealized, pristine synthetic data often lack robustness to these common visual disturbances. This instability undermines the agent's ability to accurately perceive its environment and make reliable action predictions, leading to inconsistent and brittle navigation behavior. The motivation here is to develop a perceptual system that can maintain stable understanding and decision-making despite noisy and dynamic visual inputs, crucial for practical robotic deployment.

2. **Instructional Under-specification:** Natural language instructions provided to VLN agents are often inherently sparse, ambiguous, or incomplete, providing insufficient guidance for long-range and precise navigation. Unlike humans, who can leverage a mental map or contextual knowledge to infer implicit spatial goals, current agents struggle to derive a stable spatial understanding solely from vague text. This under-specification creates two types of decision uncertainty: "Directional Ambiguity," which complicates path selection during intermediate navigation, and "Docking Ambiguity," which hinders precise target localization at the termination point. The motivation is to provide agents with stable, persistent spatial guidance that complements ambiguous linguistic cues, ensuring the goal remains anchored even when instructions are underspecified.

To address these identified bottlenecks, the authors propose a novel framework, StereoNav, with the following key objectives:
- To enhance real-world navigation consistency and reliability by integrating robust spatial grounding and cross-domain priors.
- To mitigate perceptual instability by moving beyond conventional monocular perception and incorporating stereo observations, enabling a unified representation of semantics and geometry for precise action prediction through enhanced depth awareness.
- To resolve instructional under-specification by introducing Target-Location Priors, which serve as persistent visual guidance invariant across domains, effectively grounding the agent's target awareness even with vague instructions.
- To achieve state-of-the-art performance on competitive benchmarks while demonstrating superior deployment reliability and execution consistency in complex, unstructured real-world environments, ideally with reduced computational resources compared to scaling-based approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The StereoNav framework is designed as a stereo Vision-Language-Action model to enhance robust embodied navigation by addressing perceptual instability and instructional under-specification.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous 3D Environments. At each time step $t$, the agent receives stereo RGB observations ($o^l_t$, $o^r_t$), a natural language instruction $I$, and a target-location prior $p$ (which can be fuzzy or precise). Based on these inputs and historical stereo observations $H_{t-1}$, the agent jointly predicts a navigation action $a_t$ and a dense depth estimate $\hat{d}_t$. The action space includes "move forward" (0.25 m translation), "turn left" (15° rotation), "turn right" (15° rotation), and "stop here." Action prediction is the primary objective, with depth prediction serving as an auxiliary geometric objective to improve spatial perception and robustness.

#### 3.2. Visual Rendering: Target-Location Priors

To mitigate instructional under-specification, StereoNav explicitly leverages spatial priors. Unlike symbolic coordinate inputs, these target-location priors (ranging from fuzzy to precise) are dynamically updated based on the agent's current pose and rendered directly into its egocentric visual space. Visible targets are marked with a red point, while out-of-view targets are indicated by boundary-aware cues. This design provides a persistent global directional signal, grounding the agent's target awareness and complementing ambiguous linguistic descriptions. The rendering procedure involves transforming the target prior from the world frame to each stereo camera frame, projecting it onto the image plane using camera intrinsics, and clipping the projection to a valid image domain or placing boundary cues if out-of-view. This mechanism aims to alleviate both Directional and Docking Ambiguity.

#### 3.3. Unified Understanding Modeling

To enhance robustness against visual disturbances, StereoNav moves beyond monocular perception by incorporating stereo observations to jointly capture semantic, structural, and geometric cues. This multimodal 2D-3D representation aims to improve perceptual stability through complementarity and binocular redundancy.
- **2D Semantic Features:** An InternViT encoder, pretrained on large-scale data, is used to maintain semantic grounding.
- **2D Structural Patterns:** A DINOv2 encoder captures fine-grained structural patterns such as layouts and contours.
- **3D Geometry Encoder:** Inspired by FoundationStereo, this module processes stereo pairs to construct a 4D cost volume $V_c$, which is then refined through attentive hybrid filtering ($V'_c$) and tokenized.
The resulting unified tokens $T_t$ are formed by a weighted fusion of projected and tokenized semantic, structural, and geometric features, with $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ as learnable fusion weights.

#### 3.4. Joint Prediction Modeling

StereoNav proposes joint action-depth prediction, where depth prediction provides geometric supervision to implicitly enhance 3D perception and support robust action decisions, avoiding the costs of explicit depth sensing or future observation prediction.
- **Action Head:** This head follows prior VLM-based navigation methods, autoregressively generating a textualized four-step action sequence conditioned on a system prompt, historical observations, the current observation, and the navigation instruction. The prompt uses a conversation-style format with segment boundary tokens.
- **Depth Head:** This head estimates dense depth using a coarse-to-fine stereo matching strategy. It takes the filtered stereo cost volume $V'_C$ from the 3D Geometry Encoder and a reconstructed cost volume $V'_{DC}$ from current-view tokens. Depth estimation involves cost-volume fusion, Soft Argmin initialization, recurrent refinement (guided by a geometry-aware contextual feature $V_{co}$ from the 3D Geometry Encoder), and convex upsampling.

#### 3.5. Training Details and Configurations

StereoNav is built upon InternVL-3.5-2B. The 2D semantic encoder is initialized from InternViT, the 2D structure encoder from DINOv2, and the 3D geometry encoder and depth head from FoundationStereo. Training involves a two-stage strategy: Stage I establishes basic target-aware stereo navigation, and Stage II fine-tunes with additional trajectory data. Stereo images are resized to 448x448, and 8 historical frames serve as visual context. The model autoregressively predicts 4 navigation actions. Fusion weights for semantic, structural, and geometric branches are 1.0, 0.6, and 0.2, respectively. Optimization uses AdamW with specific learning rates for language and depth losses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of StereoNav encompassed simulated environments, real-world robotic deployments, and dedicated robustness and reliability assessments, with key findings as follows:

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Performance Evaluation

**Simulated Environments (R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen):**
- **R2R-CE:** StereoNav achieved state-of-the-art overall performance among all observation settings with a 3B egocentric RGB agent. It improved Success Rate (SR) by 13.9% and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) by 3.6% over the previous strongest result, while reducing Navigation Error (NE) by 1.7m. Compared to other egocentric RGB agents, its gains were even more pronounced: 16.4% in SR, 9.8% in SPL, 8.7% in Oracle Success Rate (OSR), and 2.0m reduction in NE. Notably, StereoNav demonstrated competitive performance even without external training data, surpassing other egocentric RGB agents.
- **RxR-CE:** While not outperforming panoramic or RGB-D agents, StereoNav achieved the highest SR of 67.5% within the egocentric RGB setting.
- These results suggest the effectiveness of the visual rendering for target priors, stereo-based unified understanding, and joint action-depth prediction. StereoNav also achieved these results with significantly fewer parameters and less training data than prior scaling-based approaches.

**Real-world Environments (Unitree G1 robot):**
- StereoNav was deployed on a Unitree G1 robot across four scenarios (Office, Gym, Lobby, Outdoor) with both simple and complex instructions.
- It consistently surpassed zero-shot VLN methods (Open-Nav, DreamNav) and supervised egocentric baselines (NaVid, StreamVLN, JanusVLN).
- StereoNav achieved a macro-averaged success rate of 60.6%, which is substantially higher than the strongest supervised baseline (StreamVLN, 24.3%) and zero-shot baseline (DreamNav, 22.1%).
- The performance advantage persisted under complex instructions and challenging scenarios like Lobby and Outdoor, where most baselines exhibited near-zero success rates. This indicated stronger cross-scene generalization and more reliable real-world navigation.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Reliability Evaluation

**Robustness to Visual Disturbances:**
- **Severe Perturbations (Viewpoint Oscillation and Motion Blur):** StereoNav exhibited significantly smaller performance degradation compared to StreamVLN and JanusVLN. Under the strongest viewpoint oscillation, it incurred a 37.0% relative degradation versus 64.7% (StreamVLN) and 65.8% (JanusVLN). Under motion blur, degradation was 8.2% compared to 38.1% (StreamVLN) and 89.6% (JanusVLN).
- **Mild Perturbations (Lighting Perturbations and Camera-Height Fluctuations):** StereoNav showed comparable or smaller SR drops (3.2-5.3%) and consistently achieved the highest absolute SR (67.5-69.6%) across these conditions, outperforming baselines which saw larger drops (up to 10.6% for JanusVLN).
- These results suggest that the stereo-based unified understanding and joint action-depth prediction contribute to perceptual stability under corrupted observations.

**Reliability in Goal Stopping:**
- StereoNav demonstrated more reliable goal stopping behavior. Under a strict 0.5m stopping-error threshold, 93.9% of its successful episodes remained within the target neighborhood, with a 72.7% SPL. This substantially surpassed StreamVLN (36.4% SR / 33.1% SPL) and JanusVLN (15.0% SR / 14.2% SPL).
- This finding supports the hypothesis that the rendered target prior provides persistent visual guidance, aiding in resolving ambiguous routes and making precise stopping decisions.

**Robustness to Fuzzy Target-Location Priors (Simulation):**
- Experiments with controlled target-location deviations on R2R-CE Val-Unseen showed that StereoNav remained effective even when the target prior was imprecise. With an average Euclidean deviation of 0.7m (1.0m preset), SR dropped by only 2.5 points and SPL by 5.7 points. Even with a 2.0m average deviation (3.0m preset), the OSR only decreased by 5.1 points, indicating the agent could still approach the target region despite significant prior noise. This suggests the target-location prior acts as complementary spatial guidance rather than a strict coordinate constraint.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Rendered Target-Point Prior:** The rendered target-point prior improved SR/SPL by 17.9%/17.2% over a monocular semantic baseline, confirming its role in mitigating instruction ambiguity through persistent visual guidance.
- **Unified Understanding Modeling Components:** The full model, incorporating this guidance and unified understanding, achieved 72.8% SR and 56.4% SPL. Stereo-based 2D modeling contributed 15.1%/14.7% gains in SR/SPL, while depth-supervised geometry added another 7.1%/4.6%. This indicated that binocular cues and joint action-depth prediction improved navigation-relevant spatial reasoning.
- **Fusion Weights:** Moderate structural guidance (weight 0.6) and lightweight geometric guidance (weight 0.2) were found to yield optimal performance. Excessive structural emphasis or over-weighting geometric tokens led to performance degradation, suggesting a balance is necessary to preserve semantic grounding while incorporating complementary cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied intelligence by systematically analyzing and addressing critical limitations in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that extend beyond conventional approaches of model and data scaling. By identifying **perceptual instability** and **instructional under-specification** as key bottlenecks for Sim-to-Real transfer, the authors offer a more nuanced understanding of the challenges facing real-world VLN deployment.

The proposed **StereoNav** framework provides a concrete methodological advancement. Its core innovations—the integration of **target-location priors as persistent visual guidance**, **unified understanding modeling leveraging stereo observations for semantic, structural, and geometric cues**, and **joint action-depth prediction for geometry-aware decision-making**—directly tackle the identified problems. The empirical results demonstrate that StereoNav not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in simulated environments but also exhibits significantly enhanced robustness, reliability, and execution consistency in complex, unstructured real-world robotic tasks. This is achieved with fewer parameters and less training data compared to methods relying primarily on larger models and datasets, suggesting a more efficient pathway to robust embodied agents.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. Improved Sim-to-Real transfer for VLN agents is crucial for the practical application of embodied AI. Reliable navigation is a fundamental primitive for advanced robotic tasks such as mobile manipulation, where an agent needs to reach a target with precise spatial alignment, and loco-manipulation, which involves complex interaction with the environment. By providing a more dependable navigation primitive, StereoNav could accelerate progress in these downstream applications, enabling robots to operate more effectively and autonomously in human-centric environments.

A acknowledged limitation of StereoNav is its current reliance on explicit or implicit target-oriented input. Future research, as suggested by the authors, could explore methods for multimodal grounding and spatial estimation to infer target-location priors directly from observations and language, thereby increasing the autonomy and generality of VLN agents. This research sets a precedent for focusing on overlooked physical and instructional challenges, guiding the field towards more practical and deployable embodied intelligence systems.
