# Emergent Neural Automaton Policies: Learning Symbolic Structure from Visuomotor Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed the Emergent Neural Automaton Policy (ENAP) framework, which learns a bi-level neuro-symbolic policy by autonomously reconstructing a probabilistic Mealy machine from raw visuomotor demonstrations. This approach yielded improved control efficacy, enhanced sample efficiency, and human-interpretable task structures for complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- End-to-end robot learning struggles with long-horizon tasks due to lack of structural priors, leading to data-intensive, opaque "black-box" policies.
- Traditional neuro-symbolic methods require manually specified symbolic priors, limiting their adaptability to new tasks and inherent data structures.
- Complex robotic tasks necessitate integrating high-level discrete planning with low-level continuous control effectively.

## Method

- The **Emergent Neural Automaton Policy (ENAP)** framework employs a three-stage pipeline for unsupervised learning of a ==Probabilistic Mealy Machine (PMM)== from visuomotor demonstrations.
- It abstracts continuous observations into discrete symbols via ==HDBSCAN clustering== and uses an RNN for history embedding, then extracts the PMM structure using an extended ==L* algorithm==.
- A bi-level control pipeline uses the learned PMM to provide a coarse action prior, which is refined by a low-level residual network, all optimized through an iterative ==EM-style process==.

## Results

- ENAP achieved comparable success rates to oracle policies on complex manipulation (e.g., **98.8%** on DualStackCube) with significantly fewer parameters (**2.66M** vs. **2.98M** for Oracle, vastly less than VLA models).
- The framework demonstrated high sample efficiency, maintaining performance even with only **25%** of the training data, and automatically extracted semantically meaningful and interpretable state machines for diverse tasks.
- The learned state machines enabled autonomous failure recovery via structural loops and effectively generalized by distilling a generalist policy from heterogeneous demonstrations (e.g., outperforming baselines in MultiGoalPushT).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex symbolic structures for long-horizon robot tasks can emerge autonomously from raw visuomotor demonstrations, obviating the need for manual symbolic engineering.
> - A bi-level neuro-symbolic architecture, combining a discrete state machine with a continuous residual policy, significantly improves sample efficiency and interpretability.
> - The learned Probabilistic Mealy Machine (PMM) inherently supports autonomous failure recovery through structural loops and can generalize by distilling common skills from heterogeneous policy mixtures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of long-horizon execution capabilities for robotic systems represents a significant challenge in achieving generalist robotic agents. Such tasks typically necessitate a combination of high-level discrete planning and low-level continuous control, a problem traditionally addressed within Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) frameworks.

Current approaches to robot learning exhibit distinct characteristics and limitations:
- **End-to-end learning methods**, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, have demonstrated capabilities in short-horizon tasks. However, their scalability to multi-stage and long-horizon problems is often constrained by a lack of inherent structural priors, leading to data-intensive, semantically opaque "black-box" policies. These monolithic networks struggle with the non-convex optimization landscape of complex tasks.
- **Traditional neuro-symbolic TAMP frameworks** leverage bi-level planning to enhance both performance and interpretability. Nevertheless, these methods typically rely on manually specified symbolic priors, such as temporal logic predicates or PDDL operators, which limits their adaptability to novel tasks and intrinsic data structures.

This research posits that complex decision-making in intelligent agents, analogous to human cognitive System 2 (symbolic reasoning, long-term planning) and System 1 (intuitive reactivity, sensorimotor control), benefits from a hierarchical architecture. The paper aims to bridge the gap between these two paradigms by enabling the *emergence* of symbolic structure directly from visuomotor data, rather than prescribing it. This aligns with hybrid system theory, which suggests that complex dynamics can be effectively decomposed into sequences of continuous local behaviors governed by discrete modes.

The work builds upon prior research in neuro-symbolic learning for robotics, which has progressed from manually specified PDDLStream frameworks to learning abstractions from data for action (e.g., deep parameterized skills) and state (e.g., grounding continuous data to discrete symbols). It also draws from symbolic structure discovery, including the learning of PDDL operators, symbolic graphs, and rules, with a particular focus on Finite State Machines (FSMs) for their compact, topological representation and support for formal verification. While classical automata learning algorithms like L\* exist, they typically assume discrete symbolic inputs and an omniscient oracle, requiring adaptation for continuous, stochastic robotic data. This work positions itself as a label-free, self-supervised framework that autonomously discovers interpretable automata from raw visuomotor data, seeking to overcome the limitations of both monolithic neural policies and rigidly pre-defined symbolic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address a pivotal question: "How can the logical structure of System 2 naturally emerge from the perceptual flow of System 1?" This is motivated by the recognized challenges in scaling robot learning to long-horizon tasks, which inherently require sophisticated reasoning and control capabilities.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:
- **Overcoming Limitations of End-to-End Policies:** A key motivation is to mitigate the shortcomings of existing end-to-end learning approaches, such as VLA models. These methods often lack the structural priors necessary for effective long-term reasoning, leading to poor sample efficiency, limited interpretability, and difficulty in generalizing to multi-stage problems. The objective is to introduce a structured approach that can handle the complexity of long-horizon tasks more effectively.
- **Addressing Rigidity of Traditional Neuro-Symbolic Methods:** Conventional neuro-symbolic frameworks typically rely on hand-crafted symbolic priors. This reliance makes them less adaptive to diverse and novel tasks, as explicit manual engineering of symbolic logic is required for each new scenario. The research aims to eliminate this dependency by developing a label-free, self-supervised framework that allows task-specific logical structures to emerge adaptively from visuomotor demonstrations.
- **Developing a Bi-Level Neuro-Symbolic Policy:** Inspired by the hierarchical nature of human cognition (System 1 for reactive control, System 2 for symbolic planning), the objective is to design a bi-level policy. This policy would feature a high-level discrete planner (a state machine) to encapsulate global task phases and a low-level continuous network to model local control within each phase. This structure is intended to unify discrete reasoning, where humans often excel, with continuous control, where robots exhibit strength.
- **Enhancing Sample Efficiency and Interpretability:** By explicitly modeling task structure with discrete transitions and continuous residuals, the framework aims to improve sample efficiency in learning, particularly in low-data regimes. Furthermore, the emergent symbolic representation is intended to provide an interpretable map of the robot's latent decision logic, making its behavior more understandable to human operators.
- **Creating a Kinematically Feasible and Semantically Interpretable Policy Representation:** The proposed "Neural Automaton Policy" representation is designed to be both kinematically feasible for robotic execution and semantically interpretable for human comprehension. This dual benefit seeks to address both practical control requirements and the need for transparent AI systems in robotics.
- **Unsupervised Structure Discovery:** A core objective is the unsupervised reconstruction of a task-level state machine planner directly from raw visuomotor demonstrations, thereby avoiding the need for task-specific labels or expert-defined logical predicates.

In summary, the work aims to develop ENAP (Emergent Neural Automaton Policy) as a framework that adaptively reconstructs and leverages a task-level state machine for hierarchical control, thereby achieving high performance, sample efficiency, and interpretability without relying on manual symbolic engineering.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ENAP (Emergent Neural Automaton Policy) framework is designed to learn a structured policy from visuomotor demonstrations by unsupervisedly reconstructing a task-level state machine and integrating it with a low-level residual network. The methodology proceeds through a three-stage pipeline: Symbol Abstraction, Structure Extraction via an extended L\* algorithm, and a Bi-level Control Pipeline. The problem is formulated within a Learning from Demonstration (LfD) setting, utilizing a dataset of expert trajectories within a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework.

**A. Probabilistic Mealy Machine (PMM) as Structural Backbone:**
The framework adopts a Probabilistic Mealy Machine (PMM) as its core structural representation. A PMM is defined by states (Q), input alphabet (Σ), output alphabet (Γ), a probabilistic transition function (δ), and a probabilistic output function (λ). In ENAP, this maps to:
- **Q:** A finite set of discrete latent task modes (e.g., 'Reach', 'Grasp').
- **Σ:** A finite input alphabet derived from discretizing the continuous observation space (O).
- **Γ:** The continuous action space (A).
- **δ(q'|q, σ):** Models high-level task progression, transitioning between task phases.
- **λ(γ|q, σ):** Acts as a state-conditioned policy, providing a coarse action prior.

**B. Adaptive Symbol Abstraction (Stage 1):**
This stage aims to map continuous observations to discrete symbols and compress symbol sequences into task phases.
1. **Feature Encoding:** Raw visuomotor observations $o_t := (I_t, p_t)$ (visual input and proprioceptive state) are encoded into a semantic feature vector $z_t = \phi_\theta(o_t) \in \mathbb{R}^z$ using an encoder $\phi_\theta$. The choice of encoder depends on the scenario:
 - **Policy Enhancement:** If trajectories come from policy rollouts, the encoder learned within that policy is reused.
 - **Policy Discovery:** If trajectories are from demonstrations, a raw visual encoder (e.g., DINOv2) is fine-tuned using an iterative learning algorithm.
2. **Symbol Discovery:** The feature vectors $z_t$ are then clustered using HDBSCAN, an adaptive clustering algorithm, to unsupervisedly discover the discrete input alphabet $\Sigma$. Each cluster corresponds to a unique symbol $c_t \in \Sigma$, augmenting the trajectory data to $(o_t, a_t, c_t)$.
3. **History Embedding with RNN:** To handle variable-length robotic trajectories and provide a Markovian representation of the current task phase, an RNN is trained. This RNN maps the history $(a_{0:t}, c_{0:t})$ to a fixed-size embedding $h_t \in \mathcal{H}$. The RNN is optimized with a multi-objective loss function $L_{RNN} = L_{pred} + \lambda L_{contrast}$, where $L_{pred}$ (comprising $L_{act}$ for next action and $L_{state}$ for next symbol prediction) drives predictive accuracy, and $L_{contrast}$ enforces phase-aware separation by minimizing cosine distance within constant cluster assignments and maximizing it during phase transitions. This encourages hidden states to cluster around saturated centers, making cosine similarity a metric for state distinction.

**C. Structure Extraction via Extended L\* Algorithm (Stage 2):**
The classical L\* algorithm is adapted to infer a PMM from continuous trajectory data. The adaptation translates L\*'s discrete logical properties into spatial geometric constraints, querying a static demonstration database instead of an active oracle.
1. **Self-Supervised Membership Query (MQ):**
 - A prefix set $U$ is maintained, consisting of representative history embeddings (centroids for latent task nodes $q$). $U$ is initialized with the first trajectory's initial state embedding.
 - The Generalized MQ function, $MQ_D(u)$, queries the demonstration dataset for immediate actions $a_t$ and next-step embeddings $h_{t+1}$ from trajectory segments whose history embeddings are $\tau_{sim}$-cosine similar to $u$.
 - **Closedness:** The algorithm iteratively expands $U$. For every $u \in U$, all reachable $h_{next}$ are identified. If an $h_{next}$ is not $\tau_{sim}$-similar to any existing $u' \in U$, it is added to $U$ as a new centroid, thereby expanding the automaton's nodes until all reachable next-step embeddings fall within known state regions.
 - Once $U$ is closed, each $u \in U$ corresponds to a discrete state $q \in Q$. Transitions are constructed by observing empirical probabilities $\delta(q'|q, c)$ and associating an action prior $\lambda(\cdot|q, c)$ as the empirical mean of actions executed during these transitions.
2. **Non-deterministic Equivalence Query (EQ):**
 - Instead of deterministic verification, a non-deterministic path search is performed. For each test trajectory $\tau$, all possible transition sequences in the hypothesized PMM consistent with its symbol sequence are tracked.
 - A path is considered valid if, at each step, the test action $a_t$ is sufficiently close to the action mean $a_t^{base}$ of the corresponding edge.
 - If no valid path is found, the shortest prefix of $\tau$ causing the failure is returned as a counterexample, added to $U$, and forces state splitting or new transition discovery.
3. **Stable Phase Pruning:** To obtain a compact structure, destination node $q'$ is merged into its source node $q$ if the $q \to q'$ transition is triggered by symbol $c$ and $q$ already has a self-loop on $c$, indicating a stable phase.

**D. PMM as a Reactive Controller (Stage 3):**
The learned PMM guides a bi-level control pipeline during execution.
1. **Coarse Action Prior:** At time $t$, given current state $q_t$ and grounded input $c_t$ (from $\phi_\theta(o_t)$ and HDBSCAN), the PMM provides a coarse action prior $a_t^{base} = E[\lambda(\cdot|q_t, c_t)]$.
2. **Residual Control:** This $a_t^{base}$ is then refined by a low-level reactive residual network $\pi_\psi$. The final action is $\hat{a}_t = a_t^{base} + \pi_\psi(q_t, \phi_\theta(o_t), a_t^{base})$.
3. **State Update:** The controller updates its internal state to $q_{t+1}$ based on the observed next symbol $c_{t+1}$, selecting the most probable successor state whose outgoing edge signature contains $c_{t+1}$.

**E. Iterative Residual Learning and Refinement:**
To jointly optimize the encoder $\phi_\theta$ and the residual network $\pi_\psi$, an iterative Expectation–Maximization (EM)-style training paradigm is employed (Algorithm 2). This handles the non-differentiable nature of clustering and structure extraction.
- **E-Step (Structure Extraction):** The encoder $\phi_\theta^{(k)}$ is frozen, and the structure discovery pipeline is executed to yield updated cluster assignments $\Sigma^{(k+1)}$ and a refined PMM $M^{(k+1)}$.
- **M-Step (Policy Optimization):** The PMM $M^{(k+1)}$ is fixed, and the residual policy $\pi_\psi$ and encoder $\phi_\theta$ are fine-tuned via behavior cloning. The objective function $J(\theta, \psi) = E_{\tau \sim D}[\|a_t - \hat{a}_t\|^2 + \lambda_{reg} L_{center}]$ is used, where $L_{center}$ regularizes features toward their cluster centroids to maintain separability. This iterative process ensures the encoder learns a task-relevant feature manifold that supports both structural consistency for the PMM and effective control for the residual policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate ENAP's performance, interpretability, and unique properties across complex manipulation, long-horizon TAMP, and real-world tasks.

**A. Control Efficacy & Efficiency (Q1):**
- **Performance:** ENAP demonstrates improved control efficacy compared to state-of-the-art end-to-end VLA policies.
 - On complex manipulation tasks (DualStackCube, PegInsert), ENAP (Oracle), which uses an oracle encoder, achieves success rates comparable to a full Oracle policy (e.g., 98.8% vs. 98.3% for DualStackCube) while significantly reducing parameter count (2.66M vs. 2.98M for Oracle, and substantially less than VLA models like OpenVLA at 7652.10M).
 - Even when training DINO from scratch (ENAP\* (DINO)), competitive success rates are achieved (e.g., 76.0% for DualStackCube, 63.2% for PegInsert) with a modest parameter count (22.94M).
 - For long-horizon TAMP tasks (CALVIN), ENAP (FLOWER), distilling from a pre-trained VLA, shows improved success rates (e.g., 97.0% vs. 91.0% for FLOWER on Sequential 3/5, and 28.2% vs. 15.9% on Hierarchical 5/5), indicating its ability to enhance existing black-box policies.
 - In real-world manipulation (Stack Lego, MultiPickPlace, Hanger), ENAP\* (DINO) achieves substantially higher success rates (e.g., 88.24% vs. 58.82% for $\pi_{0.5}$ on StackLego) with significantly fewer parameters (23M vs. 3403M) and faster inference (281ms vs. 6841ms).
- **Sample Efficiency:** ENAP exhibits robustness and high efficacy in low-data regimes. On the DualStackCube task, ENAP maintains high performance even with only 25% of the training data, outperforming other methods that degrade significantly. This suggests that the extracted PMM provides a strong inductive bias that generalizes beyond the immediate training distribution.

**B. Emergent Structure Analysis (Q2):**
- **Interpretability:** ENAP successfully extracts semantically meaningful structures from unlabeled demonstrations. Visualization of PMMs for tasks like StackLego reveals interpretable nodes representing distinct task phases (e.g., 'Grasp', 'Align', 'Insert') and logical transitions.
- **Logical Branching:** The framework autonomously discovers decision points in multi-modal tasks. For example, in StackLego with multiple starting locations, the PMM identifies a branching structure at the "Grasp" phase ($q_2$), where distinct future outcomes exist. The residual network implicitly guides the transition into the correct logical path based on visual input.
- **Structural Metrics:** A quantitative evaluation using six metrics (Success Rate, Success Rate weighted by Node Count, Action Prior Fidelity, Loop-Transition Ratio, Cluster Semantic Separability, Action Separation Distance) on PegInsert policies (Oracle, DINO, GMM) provides insights. ENAP (Oracle) exhibits a balance in LVR and CSS, suggesting an optimal task structure involves resilient abstraction and distinct partitioning of the control space. A higher Action Prior Fidelity (APF) for ENAP (Oracle) implies learning a generalized coarse intent rather than overfitting to specific trajectory noise.

**C. State-Machine-Enabled Property (Q3):**
- **Autonomous Failure Recovery:** A key advantage of the PMM controller is its ability to perform autonomous failure recovery through structural loops. In the StackLego task, when precise alignment is difficult, the agent exploits self-loops on the "Align" node. If visual feedback does not match the "Insert" signature, the controller re-attempts adjustments, enhancing robustness against uncertainties. This behavior emerges naturally from the learned topology.
- **Generalization via Policy Mixtures:** ENAP demonstrates the capability to distill a generalist state machine from mixtures of heterogeneous policies. In the MultiGoalPushT task, where trajectories for pushing a T-block to different targets are combined, ENAP outperforms baselines in both "Either" and "Nearest" goal metrics. This indicates that ENAP effectively separates conflicting data modes into distinct topological paths while retaining shared skills (e.g., pushing mechanics), avoiding collapse to a single dominant behavior.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
Ablations on structure discovery parameters reveal that an intermediate similarity threshold ($\tau_{sim}$) and the inclusion of all RNN training losses (including the phase-aware contrastive loss) lead to peak performance. The phase-aware contrastive loss is critical, as its absence results in fragmented and stochastic automata, confirming its role in shaping interpretable and control-relevant latent geometries. Furthermore, in extended StackLego tasks with six initial positions, the PMM adaptively groups kinetically similar poses into fewer shared latent modes rather than creating separate branches for each, demonstrating a principle of minimal sufficient structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ENAP framework presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic learning:

- **Bridging the Neuro-Symbolic Gap:** ENAP offers a data-driven, label-free approach to discover symbolic structures from continuous visuomotor data. This addresses a long-standing challenge in neuro-symbolic AI by moving away from hand-crafted priors, making symbolic planning accessible to a broader range of robotic tasks without requiring extensive domain expertise.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency in Robot Learning:** By integrating an emergent discrete state machine with a continuous residual policy, ENAP achieves higher sample efficiency and improved performance, particularly in low-data regimes and for complex, long-horizon tasks. This allows robots to learn intricate behaviors with fewer demonstrations and greater robustness.
- **Increased Interpretability of Robot Policies:** The explicit Mealy machine representation provides a human-interpretable map of the robot's high-level decision logic. This interpretability is crucial for understanding, debugging, and trusting autonomous systems, moving beyond opaque "black-box" neural networks.
- **Robustness and Adaptability:** The emergent structural loops facilitate autonomous failure recovery, allowing the robot to retry actions or adjust its strategy in response to unforeseen circumstances, enhancing overall system robustness. The ability to generalize from policy mixtures and adaptively group kinematically similar states also indicates strong generalization capabilities for multi-modal tasks.
- **Foundation for Hierarchical Cognitive Architectures:** ENAP's bi-level architecture, inspired by human cognitive systems, provides a viable path toward developing more sophisticated, generalizable, and interpretable robotic intelligence. It demonstrates how high-level symbolic reasoning can naturally emerge from low-level sensorimotor data.
- **Theoretical Justification:** The framework is grounded in a POMDP perspective, with a theoretical justification that decomposes the Bellman equation to explain how the PMM structure factors the decision process, thereby simplifying the function approximation task for the neural network and contributing to its sample efficiency.

In conclusion, ENAP represents a step towards robots that can not only execute complex tasks effectively but also demonstrate understandable and adaptive decision-making processes. While challenges remain, such as hyperparameter sensitivity and scaling to cross-task/cross-embodiment settings, the work establishes a foundation for emergent, structured, and interpretable robotic learning.
