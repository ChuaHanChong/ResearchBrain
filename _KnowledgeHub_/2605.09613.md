# SABER: A Scalable Action-Based Embodied Dataset for Real-World VLA Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> SABER is a scalable action-based embodied dataset generated from natural human activity in real grocery stores, featuring multi-modal action representations to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for retail robotics. Post-training with SABER-MM improved the mean success rate of NVIDIA's GR00T N1.6 on RoboBenchMart retail tasks by 2.19x, from 13.4% to 29.3%.

## Problem

- General-purpose robot foundation models struggle with low success rates when deployed in complex, unstructured real-world retail environments due to a lack of domain-specific data.
- Existing data collection methods like teleoperation are expensive, logistically challenging, and difficult to scale for the varied manipulation tasks in retail.
- General pretraining distributions for VLA models often lack sufficient representation of intricate object interactions and scene dynamics specific to retail settings.

## Method

- Created **SABER**, a dataset from over **100 hours** of natural human activity in real grocery stores, captured using ==synchronized egocentric and exocentric== camera systems.
- Processed raw footage into ==three complementary action representation streams==: ==LAPA latent actions==, ==Dex-Retargeting== for dexterous hand poses, and ==Body Pose Retargets== for whole-body humanoid poses.
- Applied SABER as a ==domain-specific post-training layer== for VLA models like NVIDIA's **GR00T N1.6**, using a ==conditional flow-matching loss== and data mixture design with ==batch-level weighting==.
- Evaluated the post-trained model on **ten RoboBenchMart tasks**, utilizing both binary success rates and a granular ==Task Progress Score (TPS)== for assessment.

## Results

- **SABER-MM** post-training led to a **2.19x** improvement in the mean success rate on RoboBenchMart retail tasks, increasing it from **13.4%** (baseline) to **29.3%**.
- Specific tasks like `close_fridge` achieved **100%** success, and `open_fridge` improved from **12% to 82%** success after SABER-MM post-training.
- Analysis of the Task Progress Score demonstrated that SABER-MM post-training enabled models to progress further through task milestones, with mean **P_2/3** (substantial progress) for non-fridge tasks increasing from **0.278 to 0.445**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity, multi-modal action supervision derived from natural human video can effectively bridge the data gap for real-world VLA adaptation in specific domains like retail.
> - The integration of latent actions, precise dexterous hand poses, and whole-body poses provides comprehensive kinematic priors that are crucial for robust retail manipulation.
> - Domain-specific post-training with curated human-derived data offers a scalable and practical alternative to robot-in-the-loop data collection for enhancing generalist VLA model performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized challenge in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics: the practical deployment of general-purpose robot foundation models in complex, unstructured real-world environments. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and GR00T N1.6, have enabled robots to learn from large-scale pretraining on heterogeneous datasets and leverage vision-language grounding for improved generalization. These models typically integrate large vision-language models (VLMs) with robot action heads to process diverse inputs and generate control signals.

However, a critical limitation in their real-world application, particularly in domain-specific scenarios like retail, is identified as a "data gap." Existing general robot pretraining distributions often lack sufficient representation of the intricate and varied manipulation tasks, object interactions, and scene dynamics characteristic of retail environments. This deficiency results in suboptimal performance when these models are applied directly to complex unseen tasks such as stocking shelves, retrieving items, or navigating aisles in a grocery store. The paper highlights that obtaining domain-specific data through traditional methods like teleoperated in-store demonstrations is logistically challenging, expensive, and difficult to scale.

SABER positions itself as a solution to this data gap by introducing a scalable, high-fidelity, domain-specific dataset. It integrates with the existing paradigm of VLA models by providing a "domain-specific post-training layer" for these systems. This approach aligns with the understanding that while general pretraining establishes broad capabilities, fine-tuning or post-training with relevant, high-quality data is essential for achieving robust performance in specific deployment domains. The work also contributes to the growing body of research on human-video retargeting as a scalable alternative to robot-in-the-loop data collection, distinguishing itself from simulation-based data generation (e.g., DreamGen) and teleoperation-heavy approaches by leveraging natural human behavior in real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate **SABER**, a high-fidelity, scalable action-based embodied dataset designed to facilitate the adaptation of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems for real-world retail robotics deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in current robotic systems:

- **Performance Gap in Real-World Retail Tasks:** General-purpose robot foundation models, despite their broad pretraining, exhibit limited performance when deployed "out of the box" in complex, unseen tasks within retail environments. Examples include opening refrigerators, retrieving varied products from crowded shelves, or picking items from the floor amidst visual clutter and occlusions. The paper specifically cites NVIDIA’s GR00T N1.6 achieving near-zero success rates on retail tasks from RoboBenchMart when applied directly, indicating a significant discrepancy between laboratory/general capabilities and domain-specific requirements.
- **Data Scarcity for Retail Environments:** The root cause of this performance gap is identified as a "data gap." Retail environments, with their unique object types, interaction patterns (e.g., articulated object manipulation, multi-height shelf reaches, specific grasping types), and dynamic scene variations (e.g., restocking, varied lighting, product deformability), are structurally underrepresented or entirely absent from general robot pretraining distributions.
- **Limitations of Existing Data Collection Methods:** Traditional methods for acquiring domain-specific robot data, such as teleoperation, are deemed prohibitively expensive, logistically constrained, and difficult to scale, especially in active retail settings. Simulation, while scalable and cost-effective, often suffers from sim-to-real transfer issues, particularly in contact-rich tasks.
- **Need for Robust Action Priors:** Achieving reliable robotic deployment in retail necessitates robust action priors that account for real operational variability and the repetition of specific skill families across diverse contexts (e.g., grasping different bottles from various shelf heights).
- **Demand for Multi-Modal and Retargetable Data:** The complexity of human retail behavior encompasses dexterous hand activity, whole-body motion, and spatial context. A comprehensive dataset would ideally capture these different levels of kinematic abstraction and be retargetable to various robot embodiments, supporting diverse downstream use cases like VLA adaptation, dexterous manipulation learning, and humanoid whole-body control.

In response to these motivations, the specific objectives of SABER include:
- To create a dataset from natural, unstaged human activity in fully operational grocery stores, thus capturing realistic retail behaviors.
- To develop a dual-stream capture architecture (egocentric and exocentric) that provides a complete picture of human behavior, from fine-grained hand activity to whole-body motion.
- To process this raw footage into multiple, complementary action representation streams (latent actions, dexterous hand poses, whole-body poses) suitable for robot training, without requiring robot hardware during collection.
- To demonstrate empirically that this high-fidelity, multimodal action supervision can significantly improve the performance of modern VLA systems on domain-specific retail tasks through post-training.
- To establish human video retargeting as a viable and scalable foundation for domain-specific robot adaptation, thereby offering a practical alternative to teleoperation-based data collection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the creation, processing, and application of the SABER dataset.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

The SABER dataset is constructed from approximately 100 hours of natural in-store human activity captured across multiple real grocery store locations. The collection strategy emphasizes skill coverage and repetition of everyday retail tasks performed by human workers, such as stocking shelves, retrieving items, and navigating aisles. This is conducted in fully operational store conditions, avoiding staging or scripting.

Two complementary camera systems are deployed simultaneously during each capture session:
- **Egocentric View:** Primary actors wear head-mounted GoPro cameras recording at 480p, capturing a first-person perspective. This view is primarily used for fine-grained hand-object interactions.
- **Exocentric View:** DreamVu’s ALIA omnidirectional 360° camera provides a fixed, full-scene view, capturing all actors and activities across the entire space. This camera produces six calibrated and synchronized wide-angle views, enabling robust whole-body pose estimation.

#### 3.2. Data Processing and Action Representation Streams

The raw dual-stream footage is processed into three complementary action representation streams, totaling 44.8K training samples:

1. **Stream 1: LAPA Latent Actions (25K episodes):** Derived from egocentric video. The LAPA pipeline processes 10-second egocentric episodes, mapping observed visual transitions between frame pairs (I_t, I_{t+H}) to compact latent action tokens (z_t). This method is self-supervised, encoding visual changes without requiring robot joint labels, and aims to capture whole-arm motion, reach trajectories, and grasping dynamics at a semantic level.
2. **Stream 2: Dex-Retargeting for Hand Retargeting (18.6K episodes):** Derived from egocentric video. A 21-point hand landmark skeleton is initially estimated per frame using a hand pose estimator. Trained human annotators then review and correct these automated estimates frame-by-frame. A rigorous multi-stage Quality Control (QC) process, including standardized training, annotate-QC-re-annotate cycles, and inter-annotator agreement measurements, is implemented to ensure anatomical accuracy and contact consistency. The corrected human hand configurations are then retargeted to the target robot's dexterous hand joint angles using Dex-Retargeting, preserving contact constraints and hand-object interaction geometry. This stream provides explicit, geometrically precise supervision for fine-grained finger control.
3. **Stream 3: Body Pose Retargets to a Humanoid (1.2K episodes):** Derived from exocentric ALIA footage. For a subset of episodes, SMPL parameters (24-joint rotation vectors and 10-dim shape coefficients) are estimated per frame using a specialized human pose estimation framework. Similar to hand pose annotation, trained annotators perform frame-by-frame review and correction, validated through iterative cycles and inter-annotator agreement checks. These human-corrected SMPL parameters are then retargeted to the Unitree G1 humanoid joint configuration. This stream provides whole-body coordination supervision for tasks requiring torso-arm-leg movements.

#### 3.3. Dataset Statistics and Annotation

The raw capture database is characterized by skill-tag distributions (navigation, manipulation, human-robot interaction, perception), episode durations (94% in 5-15s), skills per episode (mean 3.6), and coverage of various manipulation sub-skills (reaching, grasping, holding, different grip types). The dataset is formatted as LeRobot-compatible triples (observation frames, action representations, language instructions), with stream type as metadata.

#### 3.4. Downstream VLA Instantiation and Post-Training

To demonstrate utility, SABER is applied as a post-training dataset for NVIDIA’s GR00T N1.6, a multi-embodiment humanoid VLA.
- **VLM Backbone:** GR00T N1.6 utilizes a pretrained vision-language model to encode camera observations and natural language instructions into a joint embedding.
- **Flow-Matching Action Head:** Instead of discrete action tokens, GR00T N1.6 uses a flow-matching formulation that transforms a noise distribution into continuous robot trajectories in joint-angle space.
- **Post-Training Objective:** The standard conditional flow-matching loss L_fm(θ) is adopted. Each SABER stream is registered as a distinct embodiment with its own state encoder and action decoder, while sharing the VLM backbone and diffusion transformer trunk. A stream-type conditioning embedding is used to route gradients to the appropriate output distribution per stream.
- **Data Mixture Design:** The post-training corpus comprises approximately 52.1K samples:
 - 4.8K NVIDIA Robot Data (singlepanda, unified_gr1) as an anchor.
 - 44.8K SABER data (25K LAPA, 18.6K hand retargets, 1.2K body retargets) as the dominant domain-specific signal.
 - 2.5K RoboBenchMart task-aligned simulation demonstrations.
- **Batch-Level Data Weighting:** To balance the influence of different data sources, explicit weighting factors are applied during batch sampling (LAPA 10.3%, Hand-retargets 10.3%, Body-retargets 10.3%, NVIDIA 34.55%, RoboBenchMart 34.55%). This prevents the larger SABER corpus from overwhelming existing general manipulation priors and ensures balanced exposure to diverse data distributions.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Benchmark

Evaluation is performed on ten tasks from RoboBenchMart, categorized into Fridge (open/close), Move from Board to Board (move items between shelves), Pick from Floor (retrieve items from floor), and Pick to Basket (pick from shelf to basket). Success is binary, based on specific criteria (robot joint velocities, target object position, no disturbance of non-target objects).
Additionally, a **Task Progress Score (TPS)** is used for non-fridge tasks, dividing each task into four ordinal stages {0, 1/3, 2/3, 1} to capture intermediate progress (approach, grasp, full success), providing a more granular assessment beyond binary success rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the impact of SABER-MM post-training on VLA performance in retail tasks.

#### 4.1. Dataset and Annotation Quality

- SABER comprises approximately 44.8K training samples derived from over 100 hours of natural retail activity.
- The dataset is characterized by a high proportion of navigation (47.9%) and manipulation (36.1%) skill tags, indicating coverage of complete shopping workflows.
- Episode durations are concentrated between 5-15 seconds (94%), suitable for capturing coherent action sequences.
- A mean of 3.6 skills per episode suggests that captures include natural action chaining rather than isolated primitives.
- Detailed manipulation sub-skill coverage, including various grasp types (power, precision, pinch), is present, directly informing the Dex-Retargeting stream.
- The rigorous multi-stage QC process for hand and body pose annotations aims to ensure ground-truth accuracy.

#### 4.2. Model Post-Training Results

- **Overall Success Rates (Table 1):** SABER-MM post-training, applied to GR00T N1.6, yields a mean success rate of 29.3% across ten RoboBenchMart tasks, compared to a baseline of 13.4% achieved with RoboBenchMart fine-tuning only. This represents a 2.19x improvement in mean success.
 - **Fridge tasks** show substantial gains, reaching 100% success for `close_fridge` and 82% for `open_fridge` (from 74% and 12% baseline, respectively).
 - **Non-fridge tasks** (board-to-board, floor pick, basket pick) demonstrate consistent improvement, with a mean success of 13.8% for SABER-MM compared to a 6.0% baseline.
- **Task Progress Score (TPS) (Table 2):** For non-fridge tasks, SABER-MM post-training shows improved progression through task milestones:
 - Mean P≥1/3 (task initiated) for SABER-MM is 0.744, compared to 0.838 for baseline. (Note: The baseline sometimes initiates more tasks but fails to progress).
 - Mean P≥2/3 (substantial progress, typically grasping) for SABER-MM is 0.445, significantly higher than the baseline's 0.278.
 - Mean P=1 (full success) for SABER-MM is 0.146, which aligns with the overall success rate improvement (0.066 for baseline).
 - These scores indicate that SABER-MM enables models to advance further in manipulation sequences, suggesting that reaching and grasping are better learned, although precise placement and final positioning remain challenging for some tasks.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Rollout Analysis (Figure 11, Figure 12)

- Qualitative comparison of rollouts shows that SABER post-training produces more purposeful approach trajectories and improved object contact events across board-to-board, floor-pick, and basket-pick categories.
- Improvements are observed on tasks requiring precise hand shaping (e.g., Vanish spray, Fanta can), where Dex-Retargeting supervision contributes to explicit finger-joint priors.
- Successful fridge rollouts exhibit smooth and purposeful trajectories, suggesting transfer of kinematic priors from human fridge-opening clips.
- The qualitative analysis supports the quantitative findings, illustrating the model's enhanced ability to execute retail manipulation behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SABER dataset and the associated methodology have several implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Addressing the Domain-Specific Data Gap:** The primary significance of SABER lies in its direct contribution to filling a crucial data gap for retail robotics. By providing a large-scale, high-fidelity dataset of natural human behavior in real grocery stores, it offers a scalable alternative to costly and logistically complex teleoperation for acquiring domain-specific data. This approach demonstrates that "better data" can be collected today, at scale, without requiring a robot in the loop.
- **Enabling Real-World VLA Adaptation:** The empirical results confirm that domain-specific post-training with SABER substantially improves the performance of generalist VLA models on retail manipulation tasks. This establishes a practical recipe for adapting powerful foundation models to specific deployment environments, bridging the gap between broad pretraining capabilities and the demands of real-world operational variability.
- **Multi-Modal and Retargetable Supervision:** The design of SABER, which integrates three complementary action representation streams (latent actions, dexterous hand poses, and whole-body poses) from the same underlying captures, provides a rich, multi-level kinematic supervision signal. This multi-modal approach supports a broader class of downstream applications, including fine-grained dexterous manipulation learning and whole-body humanoid control, and allows for cross-embodiment retargeting. This flexibility enhances the dataset's utility across different robot platforms and VLA architectures.
- **Scalable Human-in-the-Loop Data Generation:** The methodology of deriving ground-truth annotated action representations from natural human video offers a scalable paradigm for data collection. This reduces reliance on resource-intensive robot teleoperation or often-unrealistic simulation, providing a pathway to generate large volumes of relevant, real-world data more efficiently.
- **Contribution to Foundation Model Ecosystem:** SABER highlights the role of domain-specific datasets within the broader foundation model ecosystem for robotics. It suggests that while generalist models provide a strong base, specialized data layers are essential for achieving robust performance in specific, demanding real-world deployment scenarios.
- **Future Directions for Robot Development:** The success in improving task completion and progression on RoboBenchMart tasks, even in simulation, indicates a potential pathway toward more capable retail robots. The insights gained from the three action streams can inform the design of future VLA models and training strategies, particularly for tasks requiring precise manipulation and complex body coordination.
- **Public Availability:** The release of the dataset and code, including a 10K-sample subset under a CC BY-NC 4.0 license, fosters further research and development in retail robotics by the wider scientific community.

In summary, SABER offers a foundational dataset and a validated approach for overcoming a key bottleneck in the deployment of intelligent robots in complex retail environments. Its emphasis on scalable, high-fidelity, human-derived data is expected to accelerate progress in making generalist robot models more capable and reliable for real-world applications.
