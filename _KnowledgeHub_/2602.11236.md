# ABot-M0: VLA Foundation Model for Robotic Manipulation with Action Manifold Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group's AMAP CV Lab developed ABot-M0, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model that leverages a systematically harmonized dataset of over 6 million trajectories and a novel Action Manifold Learning paradigm to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks, including an average 98.6% success rate on LIBERO.

## Problem

- General-purpose embodied agents face barriers including insufficient data scale, lack of standardization across datasets, and misaligned pre-training paradigms for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Embodied intelligence remains fragmented, with robot policies rarely transferring across different hardware morphologies.
- Existing VLA models often use visual encoders from VLMs that prioritize semantic recognition over the 3D structure and physical dynamics crucial for robot manipulation.

## Method

- Constructed ==UniACT-dataset== by harmonizing and standardizing over 6 million trajectories from six public datasets, unifying ==action representations== and enabling ==dual-arm training==.
- Introduced **Action Manifold Learning (AML)**, a paradigm that directly predicts clean action sequences instead of noise or velocity, based on the hypothesis that effective actions reside on a ==low-dimensional manifold==.
- Developed a ==modular perception mechanism== that integrates VLM semantics with explicit ==geometric priors== from plug-and-play 3D modules (==VGGT==, ==Qwen-Image-Edit==) via ==cross-attention==.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.6%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming strong baselines like Diffusion Policy and OpenVLA.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization with an **80.5%** success rate on LIBERO-Plus, significantly surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (**69.6%**) and UniVLA (**42.9%**).
- Set a new state-of-the-art with a **58.3%** success rate on RoboCasa GR1 tabletop tasks, outperforming previous noise-prediction methods like GR00T-N1.6 (**47.6%**), validating the superiority of Action Manifold Learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective robot actions reside on a low-dimensional, smooth manifold, allowing for more stable and efficient learning through direct action prediction (AML).
> - Systematic data harmonization, including unified action representation (delta actions, EEF frame, rotation vectors) and a pad-to-dual-arm strategy, is critical for achieving cross-embodiment and cross-task generalization.
> - Integrating 3D spatial priors through modular, external components effectively augments VLM semantics, enhancing precision in manipulation tasks without modifying the core VLM backbone.
