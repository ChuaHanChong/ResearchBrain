# ABot-M0: VLA Foundation Model for Robotic Manipulation with Action Manifold Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group's AMAP CV Lab developed ABot-M0, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model that leverages a systematically harmonized dataset of over 6 million trajectories and a novel Action Manifold Learning paradigm to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks, including an average 98.6% success rate on LIBERO.

## Problem

- General-purpose embodied agents face barriers including insufficient data scale, lack of standardization across datasets, and misaligned pre-training paradigms for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- Embodied intelligence remains fragmented, with robot policies rarely transferring across different hardware morphologies.
- Existing VLA models often use visual encoders from VLMs that prioritize semantic recognition over the 3D structure and physical dynamics crucial for robot manipulation.

## Method

- Constructed ==UniACT-dataset== by harmonizing and standardizing over 6 million trajectories from six public datasets, unifying ==action representations== and enabling ==dual-arm training==.
- Introduced **Action Manifold Learning (AML)**, a paradigm that directly predicts clean action sequences instead of noise or velocity, based on the hypothesis that effective actions reside on a ==low-dimensional manifold==.
- Developed a ==modular perception mechanism== that integrates VLM semantics with explicit ==geometric priors== from plug-and-play 3D modules (==VGGT==, ==Qwen-Image-Edit==) via ==cross-attention==.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.6%** on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming strong baselines like Diffusion Policy and OpenVLA.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization with an **80.5%** success rate on LIBERO-Plus, significantly surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (**69.6%**) and UniVLA (**42.9%**).
- Set a new state-of-the-art with a **58.3%** success rate on RoboCasa GR1 tabletop tasks, outperforming previous noise-prediction methods like GR00T-N1.6 (**47.6%**), validating the superiority of Action Manifold Learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective robot actions reside on a low-dimensional, smooth manifold, allowing for more stable and efficient learning through direct action prediction (AML).
> - Systematic data harmonization, including unified action representation (delta actions, EEF frame, rotation vectors) and a pad-to-dual-arm strategy, is critical for achieving cross-embodiment and cross-task generalization.
> - Integrating 3D spatial priors through modular, external components effectively augments VLM semantics, enhancing precision in manipulation tasks without modifying the core VLM backbone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a central challenge in robotics: developing general-purpose embodied agents capable of operating across diverse hardware, often referred to as the "one-brain, many-forms" paradigm. The existing landscape of embodied intelligence is characterized by fragmentation, where policies trained on one robot frequently do not transfer to another, and high-level semantic reasoning struggles to translate into low-level physical precision. This fragmentation is attributed to several fundamental barriers:
- **Data Scale and Availability:** Unlike general vision-language data, embodied data requires precise action labels, which are expensive and slow to collect. Most current approaches rely on pre-training sets limited to a single robot type or platform, restricting the development of general policies.
- **Data Quality and Standardization:** Data quality varies widely across existing datasets, and representations (e.g., action formats, coordinate systems, control frequencies) lack standardization, hindering the extraction of common patterns across embodiments.
- **Mismatched Pre-training Paradigms:** Many Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are built upon Vision-Language Models (VLMs), whose visual encoders are primarily optimized for semantic recognition rather than 3D structure or physical dynamics, which are critical for embodied behavior.

The paper posits that embodied intelligence can advance by aggregating heterogeneous open-source data and building cumulative capabilities through shared infrastructure, rather than relying solely on proprietary, closed systems. It aims to bridge the gap between abstract semantic understanding and precise physical interaction, a crucial step towards a more unified and generalizable approach to robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to construct a unified framework, ABot-M0, that enables the development of general-purpose embodied agents for robotic manipulation across diverse hardware platforms. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives, motivated by the identified barriers in the field:

- **Systematic Data Curation and Standardization:** A key motivation is to overcome the fragmentation of existing robotic datasets. The project aims to integrate disparate open-source datasets into a unified, high-quality foundation for VLA model training. This involves cleaning, preprocessing, and standardizing data formats, action representations, and coordinate systems to enable cross-platform and cross-embodiment alignment of manipulation semantics. This is driven by the belief that data scale, quality, and diversity are foundational for robust generalization.
- **Development of an Efficient and Stable Action Prediction Mechanism:** The paper motivates the need for improved action prediction efficiency and stability. It proposes that effective robot actions exist not in a full high-dimensional space but on a low-dimensional, smooth manifold, governed by physical laws and task constraints. The objective is to develop a learning mechanism, Action Manifold Learning (AML), that directly predicts clean, continuous action sequences, shifting the learning task from denoising (as in traditional diffusion models) to projecting onto feasible action manifolds. This is motivated by the inefficiency and instability observed when models are forced to predict unstructured, high-dimensional noise.
- **Enhancement of Spatial Understanding for Action Generation:** VLMs, while strong in semantic reasoning, are recognized as having limitations in precise 3D spatial understanding, which is critical for fine-grained manipulation. A core objective is to integrate modular perception capabilities that combine VLM semantics with geometric priors and multi-view inputs. This aims to enhance spatial understanding without requiring modifications to the core VLM backbone, mitigating the inherent limitations of 2D visual inputs for 3D physical tasks.
- **Establishment of a Robust Two-Stage Training Paradigm:** The research aims to develop a training strategy that reconciles generality with task-specific precision. This involves a two-stage approach: large-scale pre-training on heterogeneous data for generalizable action priors, followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to incorporate fine-grained spatial priors and enhance execution accuracy on complex tasks, while preserving overall generalization. This addresses the challenge of balancing broad skill acquisition with high-precision task execution.
- **Open-Source Contribution and Reproducibility:** A significant motivation is to foster open, collaborative progress in embodied intelligence. The project intends to release all code and pipelines, aiming to provide a reproducible, scalable, and high-purity foundation for future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ABot-M0 framework integrates data processing, model architecture, and training strategies to address the challenges of general-purpose embodied robotics.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: The UniACT-dataset

To establish a systematic and unified data foundation, the researchers constructed the UniACT-dataset from six public datasets: OXE, OXE-AugE, Agibot-Beta, RoboCoin, RoboMind, and Galaxea. This process involved three main steps:

- **Analysis of Open-Source Datasets:** An initial analysis characterized existing datasets by their scale, quality, and embodiment diversity. It was noted that no single dataset simultaneously satisfies all three criteria, necessitating a multi-source integration.
- **Data Cleaning and Preprocessing:** A multi-stage pipeline was designed to address inconsistencies and quality issues across raw datasets. This included:
 - **Format Conversion:** All trajectories were converted to the LeRobot v2 format for unified processing.
 - **Instruction Cleaning:** Episodes with empty, garbled, or non-English instructions were removed or normalized.
 - **Temporal Alignment:** Mismatches between frame rates and action frequencies were resolved.
 - **Subtask Decomposition:** For long-horizon tasks, high-level task descriptions were decomposed, and frame-aligned subtask instructions were inserted.
 - **Visual and Action Anomaly Filtering:** Trajectories with visually degraded frames (e.g., black images, motion blur), ineffective camera viewpoints, or ambiguous/incomplete action annotations were discarded.
 - After cleaning, approximately 16% of trajectories were discarded, resulting in the UniACT-dataset, comprising over 6 million trajectories and 9,500 hours of data across more than 20 unique robot embodiments.
- **Standardization of Data Formats:** To ensure cross-platform compatibility and efficient learning:
 - **Action Representation:** All actions were standardized into delta actions in the end-effector (EEF) position, with rotations encoded as 3D rotation vectors (axis-angle representation) to enhance stability and avoid singularities. A 7-dimensional action vector `[∆x, ∆y, ∆z, r, gripper]` was used for each arm.
 - **Unified Single- and Dual-Arm Training:** A "pad-to-dual-arm" strategy was implemented, where single-arm trajectories had the unused arm’s action dimensions zero-padded. This allowed a single policy network to handle both single-arm and dual-arm tasks, with the model implicitly learning interaction modes based on task instructions.
 - **Data Distribution Balancing:** A multi-granularity uniform sampling strategy was employed during training to balance embodiment coverage and skill learning efficiency, addressing inherent scale and embodiment imbalances in the raw data (e.g., oversampling rare tasks and balancing robot configurations at the batch level).

#### 3.2. The ABot-M0 Model Architecture

The ABot-M0 model uses a two-component architecture:

- **Visual Language Model (VLM) Backbone:** Qwen3-VL (4B parameters) was chosen as the VLM backbone, responsible for processing stacked multi-view image sequences and natural language instructions. The VLM tokenizes and fuses these modalities into a unified sequence, producing spatially aligned multimodal representations for the action expert.
- **Action Expert:** A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture (0.16B parameters) serves as the action expert, conditioned by the VLM's outputs and robot state.

#### 3.3. Action Manifold Learning (AML)

Central to ABot-M0 is Action Manifold Learning (AML), proposed based on the "Action Manifold Hypothesis."

- **Hypothesis:** Effective robot actions lie on a low-dimensional, smooth manifold, governed by physical laws and task constraints, rather than being randomly scattered in a high-dimensional space.
- **Approach:** Instead of conventional diffusion models that predict noise (ϵ-pred) or velocity (v-pred), AML uses the DiT backbone to directly predict clean, continuous action sequences (a-pred).
 - Given a ground-truth action chunk `A_t`, a diffusion timestep `τ`, and noise `ϵ`, a noisy action `A_τ_t` is constructed.
 - The network `V_θ` takes VLM features (`ϕ_t`), current robot state (`q_t`), and `A_τ_t` to directly predict the denoised action chunk `ˆA_t`.
 - The loss is calculated as mean squared error (MSE) on velocity, derived from `ˆA_t` and `A_t`, with a reweighting function `w(τ)` that adjusts signal strength across noise levels.
- **Inference:** An Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) solving trajectory is used. Starting from pure noise, iterative denoising predicts the estimated pure action, computes instantaneous flow velocity, and updates the action using a numerical integrator. This aims for stable trajectory generation.

#### 3.4. Two-Stage Training Paradigm

- **Stage 1: Large-Scale Pre-training:** The model is pre-trained on the UniACT-dataset to learn general, cross-task, and cross-embodiment manipulation policies. Actions are delta actions in the EEF frame, with a length of 7 per arm (14 for dual-arm). A discretized modeling strategy with an action classification loss and fast token head is used. The pad-to-dual-arm strategy and dual-weighted sampling (task category and robot morphology) are applied to balance distributions and improve zero-shot generalization.
- **Stage 2: Space-Aware Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned to incorporate essential 3D spatial priors, addressing issues like accumulated errors and unstable spatial alignment in high-precision tasks. Both VLM and action expert modules are fine-tuned jointly with a small learning rate, and dropout and action noise perturbation are applied for robustness.

#### 3.5. Perception to Action: VLM Feature Interaction and 3D Information Injection

- **VLM Feature Interaction:** Ablation studies were conducted to identify the most effective VLM features for the action expert, which is crucial given the VLM's robotics pre-training. It was found that feeding the action expert with raw features from the final hidden layer of the VLM outperformed using intermediate layers, multiple layers, or incorporating additional action queries. This indicates that robotics pre-training already aligns the VLM’s deep features with action-space semantics.
- **3D Information Injection:** To address the VLM’s limitations in metric 3D spatial awareness, a plug-and-play 3D information injection module was introduced:
 - **Single-Image 3D Features:** VGGT, a model pre-trained on large-scale 3D data, extracts 3D-aware features from a single RGB image.
 - **Implicit Multi-View Features:** Qwen-Image-Edit is used to synthesize additional viewpoints from the original image, implicitly capturing 3D scene layout.
 - These 3D features are fused with the VLM’s final-layer features just before the action expert. Cross-attention was identified as the optimal fusion strategy among concatenation, cross-attention, and Q-Former. The module is designed to be modular, allowing flexible deployment without retraining the core VLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ABot-M0 involved comprehensive experiments on multiple simulation benchmarks and ablation studies to validate the contributions of its core components.

#### 4.1. Main Results on Benchmarks

- **LIBERO:** ABot-M0 achieved an average success rate of 98.6%, outperforming baselines like π₀.₅ and OpenVLA-OFT. It showed strong performance on spatial (98.8%) and long-horizon (96.6%) tasks, indicating robust spatial understanding and execution capabilities.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** In zero-shot evaluation on this benchmark, which introduces various perturbations, ABot-M0 achieved an 80.5% success rate. This substantially outperformed prior methods such as OpenVLA and OFT by 12.6% to 64.9%, demonstrating robustness against visual and language perturbations.
- **RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop Tasks:** For complex dual-arm tasks with a 29-dimensional action space, ABot-M0 achieved a 58.3% success rate, surpassing noise prediction-based experts like GR00T-N1.6 and action regression methods such as OFT. This suggests the superiority of the Action Manifold Learning (AML) paradigm for high-dimensional action prediction.
- **RoboTwin 2.0:** In multi-task training scenarios (50 tasks), ABot-M0 achieved over 80% success rates in both clean and heavily randomized scenes, outperforming VLA models such as π₀.₅ and X-VLA.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study Findings

- **Action Manifold Learning (AML):** A comparison between AML (direct action prediction) and GR00T (noise prediction) showed that ABot-M0 consistently outperformed GR00T, especially with fewer denoising steps. When increasing the action chunk size to 30 (making the task more difficult), GR00T's performance dropped sharply by 23.6%, while ABot-M0 maintained a 62.8% success rate, validating the "Action Manifold Hypothesis" and AML's efficiency for high-dimensional action prediction.
- **VLM Feature Interaction:** Experiments on LIBERO-Plus indicated that conditioning the action expert on raw features from the VLM's final hidden layer yielded the highest success rate (71%). Using action queries or concatenating features and queries resulted in lower performance, suggesting that robotics pre-training effectively aligns the VLM’s deep features with the action domain, making additional adaptation mechanisms redundant.
- **3D Information Injection:** Integrating VGGT-based single-image 3D features consistently improved performance on both LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus. Single-layer Cross-Attention was identified as the optimal fusion strategy. Incorporating two synthesized views from Qwen-Image-Edit further enhanced generalization, particularly on camera viewpoint perturbation tasks (up to 14% improvement), demonstrating the value of explicit 3D and multi-view information. The benefits of data standardization, architectural changes, and training redesign were found to be orthogonal and additive.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ABot-M0 framework represents a systematic approach to building generalizable embodied agents, offering several significant implications for the field of robotics:

- **Advancement Towards General-Purpose Embodied Intelligence:** By addressing fundamental barriers related to data scale, quality, standardization, and training paradigms, ABot-M0 contributes to the "one-brain, many-forms" vision. The ability to generalize across diverse robot morphologies and task scenarios through unified pre-training is a step towards more versatile robotic systems.
- **Leveraging Open-Source Data:** The creation of UniACT-dataset by systematically curating and standardizing six major open-source datasets demonstrates that high-performance, generalizable embodied intelligence can be achieved without reliance on proprietary data. This approach promotes collaborative progress by enabling cumulative advancements based on shared public resources.
- **Improved Action Prediction Efficiency and Stability:** The introduction of the Action Manifold Hypothesis and Action Manifold Learning (AML) offers a paradigm shift in action prediction. By directly predicting continuous action sequences rather than noise, AML enhances decoding speed and policy stability. This is particularly impactful for high-dimensional action spaces and long-horizon tasks, addressing a core limitation of traditional diffusion-based approaches.
- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning:** The modular, dual-stream architecture, which integrates VLM semantics with geometric priors from plug-and-play 3D modules (like VGGT and Qwen-Image-Edit), improves spatial understanding. This allows the system to bridge the gap between qualitative semantic reasoning and the millimeter-level precision required for fine manipulation tasks, without altering the core VLM backbone.
- **Systematic and Additive Component Benefits:** The comprehensive ablation studies confirm that the various components of ABot-M0—data standardization, architectural innovations, and training redesign—operate independently and offer additive benefits. This indicates that the learned representations are transferable beyond dataset-specific biases, validating the systematic engineering approach.
- **Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to release all code and pipelines promotes reproducibility and facilitates further research. This open-source contribution can accelerate community-driven progress, allowing other researchers to build upon and extend the capabilities demonstrated by ABot-M0.

In summary, ABot-M0 demonstrates that a systematically engineered approach to integrating and optimizing data, architecture, and training can lead to highly performant and generalizable embodied agents, fostering a new paradigm for collaborative advancement in robotics.
