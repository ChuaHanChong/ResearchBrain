# Spark-to-Paper: End-to-End Research Paper Generation as a Composable Skill

> [!summary] Summary
> Spark-to-Paper turns a research idea into a finished paper using thirteen composable skills inside an existing coding assistant, with no separate orchestration platform. It splits model judgment from deterministic gates, pre-commits experiment design before results, bounds self-refutation loops, and reconstructs figures as editable vectors. Across eight topics it reaches 99.5% citation validity, 96.4% figure editability, $8.1 and 3.2 hours per manuscript.

## Problem

- Autonomous research agents ship as standalone applications with their own orchestration servers, graph databases, or schedulers, separate from the coding environments where research actually happens.
- Existing skill-based writing assistants stay infrastructure-free but automate only a narrow slice: literature search, outlining, drafting, without running real experiments end to end.
- Language models fabricate citations and numbers at non-trivial rates, and prior systems release papers with bitmap figures whose elements cannot be edited.

## Method

- Thirteen composable skills orchestrated by **ts-paper** across eight stages (input routing, plan, cite, write, refine, review, figure, assemble), communicating only through persistent project artifacts like blueprint.json and refs.bib.
- ==Integrity stack==: ==deterministic gates== (Template, Blueprint, Citation, Manuscript, Figure, Compilation) plus ==Self-Review== and ==Adversarial Review==, with Proposal/Data-Aware modes and a ==claim-admission protocol== labeling claims supported through contradicted.
- ==Role-aware figure pipeline== plots measured data straight to vector PDF, while method diagrams go image-generation raster then iterative HTML reconstruction; experiment-critique cycles bounded at seven, failures logged as reports.

## Results

- On eight pre-registered topics the full system resolved **99.5%** of 384 references and kept **96.4%** of ~1,900 figure elements editable, versus **81%** citation validity single-pass.
- Ablation on 36 seeded probes: fabrication detection rose **14%** single-pass, **69%** gates only, **81%** plus self-review, **92%** with adversarial review, which held **74%** precision on 57 issues.
- Full runs cost **11.9M** tokens, **$8.1**, and **3.2 hours** per manuscript, against ~$20-25 and up to 15 hours for a prior agent's run; human preprints scored **97.8%** citations, **58%** editability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Split judgment from verification: anything with an explicit correctness rule belongs in deterministic code, leaving the model only genuinely semantic decisions.
> - Pre-committing what evidence a claim needs, before results exist, is a cheap preregistration that stops protocol drift after outcomes are seen.
> - Agents can loop forever refuting themselves; bounding retries and preserving failed trajectories as reports beats forcing every hypothesis into a success narrative.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "Spark-to-Paper" addresses the evolving landscape of autonomous research agents and large language model (LLM) applications in scientific discovery. The broader field is characterized by two main approaches to automating the research process:

1. **Autonomous End-to-End Research Agents:** This category includes systems that aim to automate the entire research cycle, from ideation and experimentation to writing and self-review. Examples such as AI Scientist/v2, AutoResearchClaw, and Kosmos/Robin function as standalone applications, often requiring dedicated codebases, orchestration servers, and sometimes specialized infrastructure like graph databases or cluster schedulers. These systems demonstrate breadth in autonomously proposing and executing experiments.
2. **Lightweight, Skill-Based Writing Assistants:** This approach focuses on integrating automation within existing coding assistants or development environments, leveraging their native capabilities for file inspection, code execution, and tool use. Existing tools like Idea2Story and ARS deconstruct tasks such as literature search, outlining, and drafting into composable steps. However, these systems generally do not encompass the full end-to-end research process, particularly the execution of real experiments and the generation of editable vector figures.

Spark-to-Paper positions itself between these two poles. It seeks to achieve the breadth of end-to-end research automation typically found in standalone agent platforms, but without requiring separate infrastructure. Instead, it operates as a collection of composable skills integrated into an existing coding assistant. This approach leverages the environment where much research work already occurs, aiming to reduce the operational overhead associated with dedicated autonomous research platforms.

Additionally, the paper builds upon general advancements in LLM agents, tool use, and self-critique. Its internal mechanisms draw inspiration from interleaved reasoning-and-action patterns, the use of external tools, iterative refinement with self-feedback, deliberate multi-step reasoning through planning, and persistent, file-backed working states. The system also tackles the challenge of hallucination and fabrication in LLM-generated content, a recognized issue in grounded generation and citation integrity, by implementing retrieval-augmented generation and deterministic verification checks.

The key differentiation of Spark-to-Paper within this landscape is its emphasis on achieving end-to-end research paper generation, including real experiment execution and production of editable vector figures, entirely within an existing coding assistant environment, without the need for additional standing infrastructure.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of Spark-to-Paper is to develop an end-to-end research paper generation system that can transform a research idea into a complete, publication-ready paper. This objective is driven by the recognition that generating a research paper involves a complex, multi-stage process extending beyond mere text generation. The system aims to address several specific challenges inherent in this process:

- **Complexity of Research Workflow:** Traditional research requires diverse tasks such as literature retrieval, experimental design and execution, evidence-based claim revision, figure generation, and maintaining consistency across long-duration projects. Existing LLM-based systems often focus on individual components or require proprietary orchestration, failing to provide a unified, integrated solution that mirrors the full research lifecycle.
- **Integration with Existing Research Environments:** While advanced autonomous research agents exist, they typically operate as standalone applications with their own infrastructure, separating them from the coding environments commonly used by researchers. The motivation is to explore whether end-to-end research paper generation can be implemented as a lightweight, composable workflow directly within an existing coding assistant, thereby leveraging familiar tools and reducing setup overhead.
- **Ensuring Reliability and Groundedness:** Language models are prone to factual inaccuracies and hallucination, especially concerning citations and experimental results. A core motivation is to develop mechanisms that ensure the generated paper's claims are grounded in evidence, citations are valid, and experimental results are verifiable. This involves separating model-based judgment from deterministic operations.
- **Managing Long-Horizon Generation and Self-Correction:** The iterative nature of research means that early claims may be contradicted by later evidence. The system needs robust mechanisms for self-critique and revision over extended generation trajectories, including handling situations where initial hypotheses prove incorrect. A specific motivation is to address and bound "Self-Refutation Loops," where repeated experiments fail to support an objective without convergence.
- **Producing Publication-Ready Artifacts:** Generating high-quality figures, particularly editable vector graphics, is crucial for academic publication. Current systems often produce raster images or lack mechanisms for integrating figure generation directly from experimental data or reconstructing complex diagrams into editable formats.

In essence, Spark-to-Paper seeks to demonstrate that a comprehensive, evidence-grounded, and high-quality research paper generation system can be built as a set of composable skills within a standard coding assistant, thus offering an accessible yet powerful automation tool for the research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Spark-to-Paper is implemented as a collection of thirteen composable skills operating within an existing coding assistant, specifically Claude Code using a Claude family model. This design principle underpins its entire methodology, enabling it to reuse the coding assistant's native capabilities for file interaction, tool use, and code execution without requiring a separate agent platform or orchestration service.

**Overall System Architecture and Skill Design:**
The system's core design involves:
- **Composable Skills:** Each of the thirteen skills is designed to handle a specific research task (e.g., planning, literature retrieval, writing, review, figure generation, experiment execution).
- **Shared Project Directory:** All skills communicate and interact by reading and writing artifacts to a common project directory, allowing the manuscript and associated files to evolve through the pipeline.
- **Separation of Judgment and Execution:** Within each skill, the language model is responsible for decisions requiring interpretation, reasoning, or contextual judgment (e.g., argument organization, literature relevance, evidence-claim alignment). Deterministic programs handle operations with explicit rules that can be mechanically executed and checked (e.g., checking manuscript structure, validating citations, compiling LaTeX, plotting data).

**End-to-End Pipeline (Stages 0-8):**
The execution of skills is organized by a `ts-paper` orchestrator into a defined pipeline:

- **Stage 0: Input Routing:** Inspects user input (short idea or developed proposal) and determines the starting point and result-integrity mode.
 - **Proposal Mode:** No measured results available; unavailable values remain unspecified.
 - **Data-Aware Mode:** Measured data or experimental outputs are available, and quantitative statements must be supported by these results.
- **Stage 1: Planning:** Converts input into a structured paper blueprint (research question, contributions, section structure, experimental design) based on target venue specifications.
- **Stage 2: Citation:** Builds a verified bibliography by searching for relevant literature and validating references using bibliographic metadata (e.g., DOI, arXiv IDs). It emphasizes finding verifiable support over generating references from memory.
- **Stage 3: Writing:** Generates the complete LaTeX manuscript from the blueprint and bibliography, ensuring consistency in terminology and claims. The selected integrity mode controls quantitative writing.
- **Stage 4: Refinement:** Revises the manuscript as a whole to remove repetition, reconcile inconsistencies, improve arguments, and adjust section lengths, followed by deterministic checks.
- **Stage 5: Review:** Challenges the manuscript through multiple isolated review passes (technical soundness, experimental design, evidence strength). Valid issues trigger revisions in the refinement stage.
- **Stage 6: Figure Generation:** Produces figures based on their role (explained below).
- **Stage 7: Assembly:** Combines all manuscript components (sections, bibliography, figures) with the venue template into a compilable LaTeX project, followed by deterministic compilation checks.
- **Stage 8: Experiment Execution (Auxiliary):** Checks if planned experiments can be executed, runs feasible experiments, records outputs, and updates the manuscript with measured results. Claims are revised according to new evidence. If resources are unavailable, results remain unspecified.

**Key Components and Mechanisms:**

1. **Experiment Design and Revision:**
 - **Pre-committed Design:** The planning stage specifies required datasets, baselines, metrics, and result tables *before* experiments are run, fixing the table structure in advance. This acts as a lightweight pre-registration.
 - **Evidence-Grounded Execution:** The experiment stage maps manuscript claims to required evidence and executes only the minimal set of feasible experiments.
 - **Traceable Artifacts:** Execution produces logs, metric files, tables, and figures, with numerical results traceable to their origin (dataset, config, seed, metric).
 - **Evidence-Guided Claim Revision:** Post-execution, claims are classified (SUPPORTED, PARTIALLY-SUPPORTED, UNSUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, NEEDS-CONFIRMATION). The manuscript is then revised (retained, weakened, removed, moved to limitations) based on the evidence, with changes propagated across sections. Null or negative results are preserved.

2. **Integrity and Self-Critique:**
 - **Deterministic Integrity Checks (Gates):** Applied after each stage, these programs enforce verifiable properties such as template structure, citation consistency, notation, figure file existence, successful LaTeX compilation, and result integrity (e.g., no generated values in Proposal Mode, grounded quantitative statements in Data-Aware Mode).
 - **Model-Based Self-Critique:**
 - **Self-Review:** Operates locally to repair terminology drift, redundancy, and local inconsistencies after an edit.
 - **Adversarial-Review:** Conducts manuscript-level passes on technical soundness, experimental design, and systems validity. Issues must be quoted and are checked for presence, prior addressal, or scope before triggering revision.
 - **Self-Refutation Loops and Bounded Recovery:** To prevent unproductive cycles where experiments repeatedly fail to support the original hypothesis, the system bounds experiment–critique–revision cycles to seven. If the objective remains unsupported, the trajectory is terminated, a failure report is generated, and the system starts a new research trajectory from a different idea.

3. **Editable Figure Generation:**
 - **Measured-Result Figures:** Generated programmatically directly from measured experimental data using plotting code, producing native vector PDFs to ensure values are tied to measurements.
 - **Method and Explanatory Figures:** Initially created visually by an image-generation model (e.g., for structure, layout). This raster image serves as a visual target, which the system then reconstructs into editable vector graphics (HTML-based elements like text, shapes, connections) through code. Iterative adjustment refines the reconstruction to match the visual target, ensuring the final PDF is editable. If reliable reconstruction is not possible, the raster image is retained.

The overall approach prioritizes evidence-grounded generation, rigorous integrity checks, iterative self-correction, and the production of verifiable and editable research artifacts, all within the flexible and familiar environment of a coding assistant.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Spark-to-Paper employed controlled experiments, retrospective audits, and qualitative case studies, focusing on artifact quality and generation efficiency.

#### 4.1. Main Comparison Results (Table 3)

Spark-to-Paper was compared against human-written preprints, prior autonomous research systems, and a single-pass LLM baseline.

- **Citation Validity:** Spark-to-Paper achieved a 99.5% (98.4%, 100%) citation validity, meaning nearly all references were successfully resolved. This significantly surpasses the single-pass LLM baseline (81%) and is higher than previously released autonomous research systems (91-96%). Human-written preprints, as a reference, showed 97.8% (94.6%, 99.4%) citation validity.
- **Figure Editability:** The system demonstrated high figure editability at 96.4% (92.7%, 98.6%), indicating that most figure elements could be edited. In contrast, prior autonomous research systems provided little to no figure editability (0-3%).
- **Generation Efficiency:**
 - **Tokens:** The full Spark-to-Paper system used an average of 11.9 million tokens (10.2M, 13.7M) per manuscript.
 - **Monetary Cost:** The average cost per manuscript was $8.1 ($6.9, $9.6).
 - **Wall-Clock Time:** Generation took an average of 3.2 hours (2.6h, 3.9h).
 - The single-pass LLM baseline was faster and cheaper (0.11M tokens, $0.66, 16 min) but with significantly lower citation validity (81%). Prior autonomous systems reported costs ranging from $2.33 to $25 per attempt and runtimes from 19 minutes to 15 hours per run/batch.

These results indicate that Spark-to-Paper produces high-quality research artifacts, particularly in terms of citation integrity and figure editability, which are key limitations in other automated systems, while maintaining competitive generation costs and times compared to other end-to-end autonomous research systems.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study Results (Table 4)

An ablation study demonstrated the incremental contributions of Spark-to-Paper's quality-control stack:

- **Fabrication Detection:**
 - A single-pass draft without integrity gates detected only 14% (5/36) of injected unsupported claims.
 - Adding deterministic and model-based gates increased detection to 69% (25/36).
 - Including self-review further raised detection to 81% (29/36).
 - The complete stack, incorporating adversarial review, achieved 92% (33/36) fabrication detection.
- **Review Precision:** The adversarial-review stage demonstrated a 74% (42/57) precision, meaning most issues raised by the reviewer corresponded to verifiable problems.
- **Incremental Cost:** The gains in quality came with increased inference costs. Adding gates introduced the largest token and monetary overhead (+8.1M tokens, +$5.3), while self-review and adversarial review provided further quality improvements at smaller incremental costs.

These findings highlight the effectiveness of the multi-layered quality-control stack in mitigating fabrication and improving the reliability of generated claims.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Case Study Results (Figure 6)

Two case studies demonstrated the system's ability to autonomously identify research gaps, synthesize novel methods, and maintain academic integrity, even when presented with incorrect user expectations:

- **Clinical Screening Model:** Given a proposal that mistakenly emphasized accuracy as the primary metric, the system implemented multiple evaluation metrics, identified data imbalance as a pitfall, and concluded that Accuracy and F1 scores were "highly deceptive." It proposed a leakage-safe pipeline integrating post-hoc probability recalibration with TreeSHAP audits.
- **Electricity-Load Forecasting:** The system refined a proposal containing an incorrect prior expectation about causal models' performance. Its empirical results verifiably contradicted the user's expectation, showing that causal decomposition models performed "markedly worse" when leakage was removed.

These case studies illustrate the system's commitment to evidence-based scientific truth, prioritizing objective experimental evidence over misleading or potentially biased user prompts, and demonstrating its capabilities in critical analysis and method synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Spark-to-Paper carries several significant implications and potential impacts on scientific research and the broader field of AI agents.

First, the system demonstrates the **feasibility of implementing end-to-end research paper generation as composable skills within existing coding assistants**. This lightweight, infrastructure-free approach contrasts with prior autonomous research agents that typically require dedicated platforms and significant operational overhead. By integrating with environments like Claude Code, Spark-to-Paper offers a more accessible and potentially scalable model for research automation, lowering the barrier to entry for researchers to leverage AI tools.

Second, the emphasis on **evidence-grounded generation and robust integrity checks** significantly addresses the critical challenge of hallucination and factual inaccuracies in LLM-generated content. Mechanisms such as pre-committed experiment design, traceable experimental artifacts, evidence-guided claim revision, and deterministic gates (e.g., for citation validity, result integrity) contribute to the reliability and verifiability of the generated manuscripts. This is particularly important for scientific papers where accuracy and reproducibility are paramount. The reported 99.5% citation validity and 92% fabrication detection rate (with the full stack) represent a notable improvement over single-pass LLM drafts and align with the rigor expected in academic publishing.

Third, the ability to **produce editable vector figures** (96.4% editability) through programmatic plotting for quantitative results and code-based reconstruction for explanatory diagrams addresses a practical need in scientific communication. Unlike raster images, editable vector graphics are essential for publication-quality figures, allowing for post-generation adjustments and adherence to journal specifications. This capability enhances the utility of the generated artifacts.

Fourth, the introduction and mitigation of the **"Self-Refutation Loop"** failure mode offers a novel contribution to agent design for long-horizon tasks. By bounding unproductive experimental-critique-revision cycles and preserving unsuccessful trajectories as "failure reports," Spark-to-Paper acknowledges that not all research ideas will succeed. This approach promotes a more realistic and scientifically sound automation paradigm, preventing the system from forcing a successful narrative when evidence does not support it, and allowing it to move on to more promising research directions.

Finally, the system's demonstrated **capability for critical analysis and method synthesis**, as shown in the case studies, suggests a potential to augment human researchers in identifying research gaps, designing novel experiments, and challenging assumptions, even user-provided ones. While not replacing human researchers, Spark-to-Paper could serve as a powerful assistant for accelerating parts of the research process, from initial ideation and literature review to experimental validation and manuscript drafting, thereby potentially increasing research productivity and the speed of scientific discovery.
