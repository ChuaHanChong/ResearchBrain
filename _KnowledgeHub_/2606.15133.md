# DragMesh-2: Physically Plausible Dexterous Hand-Object Interaction with Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> DragMesh-2 introduces a contact-driven framework for physically plausible dexterous interaction with articulated objects, where the hand's actions alone generate object motion. Its Physically Informed Contact-Aware (PICA) training, which injects observable physical signals into learning without direct force feedback, enabled policies to achieve 0.56 deterministic success under strong out-of-distribution damping (4x nominal), outperforming prior PPO variants.

## Problem

- A significant gap exists between generating object-centric articulated motion and achieving realistic, hand-driven dexterous interaction, which requires sustained physical contact.
- Reinforcement learning policies often overfit to nominal dynamics and contact loads without tactile or force feedback, leading to brittle performance when contact conditions vary.
- Injecting robust physical information into policy learning without relying on direct tactile or force sensing poses a key challenge.

## Method

- The **DragMesh-2** framework employs a contact-driven task formulation where the policy exclusively controls a dexterous hand, making articulated object motion solely dependent on physical hand-handle contact.
- PICA (==Physically Informed Contact-Aware==) training is introduced within a PPO framework, using a Gated Linear Attention temporal encoder and causal-window auxiliary supervision to infer and encode implicit physical states.
- Environment-level reward shaping with terms for contact maintenance, detachment risk, action regularization, and dynamics randomization (varying damping scales) biases policy learning towards contact-conditioned interaction.

## Results

- PICA achieved a **0.56** deterministic success rate under strong out-of-distribution (**4x** nominal) damping, significantly surpassing state-only PPO (**0.27**) and other temporal encoder-based PPO variants (**0.09**-**0.32**).
- An ablation study demonstrated that the full PICA model (combining GLA temporal encoder and physical signals) consistently outperformed its individual components, particularly under high damping conditions.
- Analysis revealed that while nominal success can increase with longer training, strong-damping success may collapse (from **0.55** to **0.10**), correlating with high action-saturation ratios, highlighting the need for OOD evaluation and diagnostics beyond task success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically informed signals, combined with temporal encoding through a GLA network, are complementary and more effective for robust dexterous interaction with articulated objects than using richer temporal encoders alone.
> - Solely optimizing for nominal task success can mask policy brittleness, as prolonged training under nominal conditions may lead to action saturation and a collapse in performance under varied or out-of-distribution dynamics.
> - Robustness to contact-load variation can be significantly improved by injecting observable physical proxies and dynamics randomization into the learning process, even in the absence of explicit force or tactile sensing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous hand interaction with articulated objects is a recognized challenge in robotics, particularly for applications in household robotics, assistive systems, and humanoid manipulation. Traditional robot manipulation often relies on parallel-jaw grippers, which offer less compliant contact patterns compared to multi-finger dexterous hands. Recent advancements in robotics and 3D intelligence have focused on understanding and interacting with articulated objects, encompassing areas such as articulated structure modeling, motion constraint reasoning, and articulated motion generation.

Prior work, including the authors' own DragMesh 1, has explored object-centric articulated interaction, where explicit articulation priors guide user interaction into kinematically constrained motion. However, a significant gap exists between generating articulated motion at an object level and enabling realistic, hand-driven dexterous hand-object interaction (HOI). Unlike static objects, articulated objects cannot be directly actuated; their motion must emerge from sustained physical contact between a dexterous hand and the object's handle. Existing methods, such as geometric trajectory replay, open-loop execution, or direct state control, often fail to adequately model the intricate contact dynamics necessary for this interaction.

Furthermore, reinforcement learning (RL) approaches, while powerful, commonly train policies under fixed dynamics with task completion as the primary objective. In the absence of tactile or force feedback, these policies may overfit to nominal contact loads and dynamics shortcuts, leading to performance degradation when contact loads change, such as under increased damping. A policy that performs well under nominal conditions does not necessarily exhibit stable contact behavior or robustness to dynamic variations.

DragMesh-2 addresses these limitations by shifting the paradigm from object-centric articulated generation to hand-driven, contact-dependent dexterous interaction. It focuses on ensuring that articulated motion is solely a consequence of physical hand-handle contact, thereby tackling the problem of robust and physically plausible manipulation of articulated objects under varying contact conditions, without relying on direct force or tactile sensing. This work contributes to the development of more capable and adaptive robotic systems for complex, real-world manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a contact-driven framework, termed DragMesh-2, for physically plausible dexterous interaction with articulated objects. This framework aims to enable a dexterous hand to manipulate articulated objects where the target part's motion is exclusively a result of sustained physical contact between the hand and the handle, rather than direct control or geometric replay.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges identified in the broader research landscape:

1. **Bridging Object-Centric Generation and Hand-Driven Interaction:** Existing methods for articulated object manipulation often focus on generating object-centric articulated motion, which does not directly translate to realistic hand-driven dexterous interaction. The core problem is that articulated parts cannot be directly controlled; their movement must arise from the complex contact dynamics between a multi-finger hand and the handle. This necessitates a framework that models physical interaction rather than relying on geometric trajectory replay or open-loop execution.

2. **Addressing Overfitting to Nominal Dynamics:** Reinforcement learning policies typically optimize for task completion under fixed dynamics. Without access to tactile or force feedback, such policies are prone to overfitting to specific nominal contact loads. This can lead to reliance on "dynamics shortcuts" where success is achieved through behaviors that are not robust to changes in contact load, such as increased damping. Consequently, a policy that performs well under nominal conditions may fail rapidly when environmental dynamics vary, indicating a lack of stable contact behavior. The objective is to enhance robustness to contact-load variation.

3. **Integrating Physical Signals without Direct Sensing:** A key challenge is to inject physical information into policy learning when explicit tactile or force feedback is unavailable. The goal is to derive observable physical proxies that can guide the policy towards more physically aware and robust contact behaviors.

To achieve these objectives, the paper outlines three main contributions:

- **Introduction of DragMesh-2:** Developing a contact-driven framework that extends articulated interaction from object-centric motion generation to a scenario where the policy controls only the hand, and articulated motion must emerge through sustained physical hand-handle contact.
- **Proposal of PICA (Physically Informed Contact-Aware) Training:** Designing a mechanism that injects observable physical signals into policy learning. These signals include contact maintenance, detachment risk, action-boundary regularization, dynamics randomization, and temporal contact response. PICA aims to bias policy learning towards contact-conditioned interaction, thereby improving robustness to changes in contact loads without relying on tactile or force feedback.
- **Construction of a Systematic Evaluation Protocol and Dataset:** Establishing a protocol to assess robustness under contact-load variation across multiple damping conditions and articulated-object categories. This includes task success alongside contact-aware diagnostics (e.g., action saturation, detachment). Additionally, providing a pure-geometry dexterous interaction dataset to support future research in loco-manipulation and humanoid hand-object interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DragMesh-2 framework integrates a contact-driven task formulation with a physically informed contact-aware learning mechanism, supported by a heuristically generated dataset.

#### 3.1. Contact-Driven Task Formulation

DragMesh-2 defines a "pulling" task involving a 51-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) SMPL-X hand, which includes 6 virtual wrist DoFs and 45 finger joints. A fundamental aspect of the formulation is that the policy *only* controls the hand; the articulated object's joint has no direct action channel. This design mandates that any articulated motion must result solely from physical contact between the hand and the object's handle.

For each object from the GAPartNet dataset, the target joint is identified as the DoF with the largest motion range in a pre-defined geometry-guided reference trajectory. Task success is determined by a relative motion range (`q_done`), and task progress is normalized by an object-specific range, enabling comparison across different articulated object types (e.g., drawers, sliders, doors).

The observation space for the policy is defined by kinematic and geometric states, explicitly excluding visual (RGB, depth, point clouds) or force/tactile signals. It comprises hand joint positions and velocities, handle pose, relative palm-handle geometry, target-joint state, and task-scale features derived from progress and remaining distance to the success threshold. The action space consists of a 51-dimensional increment to the hand's Proportional-Derivative (PD) target, clipped to joint limits. The reference trajectory serves only for initial expert grasp state, target motion scale definition, and as a non-learned tracking baseline; it does not provide expert action labels or object control commands.

#### 3.2. Physically Informed Contact-Aware Learning (PICA)

PICA is a training mechanism designed to enhance policy robustness under varying contact loads by injecting physical signals into policy learning, without relying on additional force sensing. It is implemented within a Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework.

- **Temporal Encoding:** The policy incorporates a history token `h_t`, combining the current PD tracking error and the previous action. A Gated Linear Attention (GLA) encoder maps a block of these recent history tokens to a contact-history feature (`z_hist_t`). This temporal encoding helps the policy infer implicit physical states, such as contact impedance or impending detachment, from observable kinematic responses.
- **Causal-Window Auxiliary Supervision:** A causal-window auxiliary head, taking `z_hist_t` as input, is trained to predict observable contact responses over a recent window. These targets (`y_t`) include recent object joint response, maximum palm-handle distance, detachment risk (binary indicator), and maximum tracking stress (norm of tracking error). This auxiliary loss ensures that the temporal encoder learns representations consistent with contact dynamics, rather than merely relying on task reward.
- **Environment-Level Reward Shaping:** The standard PPO reward is augmented with several terms that encode physically informed constraints:
 - `r_dist`: Encourages contact maintenance, combining a linear penalty on palm-handle distance with an exponential bonus for being near the handle.
 - `r_act`: Penalizes high action magnitudes to regularize control effort.
 - `r_detach`: A one-shot penalty for detachment, triggered only after the hand has initially entered and then left the effective contact range.
 - `r_bound`: Penalizes actions exceeding a soft saturation threshold to suppress brittle pulling driven by saturated commands.
 - `r_contact`: Penalizes excessive palm-handle distance to reinforce contact maintenance.
 - `r_task`, `r_time`, `r_success`: Standard task progress, time cost, and success bonus.
 These terms shift the learning objective from sole task progress to contact-conditioned interaction.
- **Dynamics Randomization:** During training, a damping scale factor for the target object joint is uniformly sampled from a specified interval (e.g., [1.0, 2.0]) and applied to the nominal damping. This exposes the policy to varying resistance during training, reducing overfitting to a single dynamics setting.
- **Policy-Level Loss:** The total policy optimization loss combines the standard PPO clipped policy loss (`L_PPO`), value loss (`L_V`), an actor output-boundary loss (`L_bounds`), and the auxiliary loss (`L_aux`). The `L_bounds` term directly regularizes the actor's output distribution, complementing the `r_bound` reward. The `L_aux` weight is linearly warmed up, allowing basic control to be established before contact-response prediction becomes a stronger constraint.

#### 3.3. Dataset Generation

A pure-geometry reference contact trajectory dataset is generated heuristically from GAPartNet geometry and annotations, without any learning process. For each articulated object, a procedure identifies the target part and handle. It then constructs a phased interaction trajectory (approach, grasp, drag, release) where wrist and finger motions are geometrically consistent with the target joint's kinematics. This involves computing wrist orientation and position relative to the handle, defining open, pre-grasp, and force-closure grasp poses for the fingers, and interpolating motions for each phase. The dataset stores per-frame wrist poses and finger configurations as JSON files, making it physics-backend independent. This dataset, comprising 277 trajectories across 7 GAPartNet categories, serves to initialize expert grasps, define motion scales for RL tasks, and provides a non-learned tracking baseline for evaluation, as well as a resource for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation protocol involved 7 GAPartNet objects (5 revolute doors, 2 prismatic drawers) across three categories (Dishwasher, StorageFurniture, Microwave). Performance was assessed under three damping conditions (×1 nominal, ×2 mild, ×4 out-of-distribution (OOD)) and two execution modes (deterministic, stochastic), with 20 episodes per (method, object, damping, mode) cell. Metrics included task success, action-saturation ratio (`clip099`), and detachment-failure rate (`detach_proxy`).

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance

- **Trajectory-Tracking Reference:** This open-loop baseline achieved 1.00 deterministic success at ×1 damping, confirming the reference trajectories induce physical motion. However, its average success dropped to 0.71 at ×2 and ×4 damping, indicating a lack of robustness to increased contact loads.
- **GT-Part-Pose Parallel-Jaw Primitive:** This geometric primitive showed very limited success (0.14 mean), being unable to substitute for dexterous contact control, even with perfect knowledge of part pose.
- **Learned Policies (Main Comparison):**
 - PICA demonstrated superior mean success rates across all six mode-damping settings compared to state-only PPO, flat-history PPO, GRU-PPO, and Transformer-PPO.
 - Under strong OOD damping (×4), PICA achieved 0.56 deterministic success, significantly higher than state-only PPO (0.27), flat-history PPO (0.32), GRU-PPO (0.30), and Transformer-PPO (0.09).
 - The results indicated that simply using richer temporal encoders (GRU, Transformer, or GLA without physical signals) did not close the performance gap, suggesting the combined contribution of physical signals and temporal contact-response modeling in PICA.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study isolated the effects of PICA's two physical-structure components: the GLA temporal encoder and the physical signals.
- Using only the GLA temporal encoder (w/o physical signals) resulted in 0.65 deterministic success at ×1 and 0.36 at ×4.
- Using only the physical signals (w/o GLA encoder) yielded 0.75 deterministic success at ×1 and 0.43 at ×4.
- The full PICA model achieved 0.89 at ×1 and 0.56 at ×4.
This demonstrated that the full PICA model exceeded either component alone by at least 0.13 at ×4 damping, indicating that the physical signals and the temporal encoder are complementary and contribute along different axes (e.g., physical signals helping more under nominal damping, temporal encoder aiding under stochastic mid-damping).

#### 4.3. Nominal Success Masks Saturation Collapse

A study on training length for a base policy on a single object revealed that optimizing solely for nominal (×1) success can be misleading. While deterministic nominal success increased from 0.90 to 1.00 with longer training (150 to 500 epochs), strong-damping (×4) success collapsed from 0.55 to 0.10, with the action-saturation proxy (`clip099`) climbing towards 1.0. This finding indicates that longer training can drive policies into a saturated, low-robustness regime, emphasizing the necessity of evaluating OOD damping and action saturation alongside task success.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings from Diagnostics

- **Rollout-Level Diagnostics:** Qualitative analysis of rollouts showed that PICA's policies (e.g., "SELECTED") primarily failed under strong damping due to insufficient sustained output in the correct pulling direction, whereas overtrained policies ("OVERTRAINED") often failed to maintain stable contact response and resorted to ineffective perturbations.
- **Limits of Fine-Tuning and Damping Range Expansion:** Extended fine-tuning or broadening the training damping range did not consistently yield stable additional OOD gains. This suggested that for very strong contact loads, the current action interface (position increments) and observation interface (absence of force/tactile feedback) might be limiting factors, requiring more fundamental changes to the control or sensing mechanisms.
- **Importance of Protocol:** The results highlighted that task reward and nominal success alone are not reliable checkpoint selectors for robust policies, reinforcing the importance of OOD evaluation and diagnostics like action saturation and detachment rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DragMesh-2 represents an advancement in the field of dexterous manipulation, specifically addressing the complex challenge of interacting with articulated objects in a physically plausible manner. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Enabling Physically Plausible Interaction:** By formulating articulated object manipulation as a problem solvable solely through physical hand-handle contact, DragMesh-2 bridges a gap between object-centric motion generation and realistic, hand-driven interaction. This approach is fundamental for developing robotic systems that can robustly manipulate objects in unstructured environments, such as households.

2. **Enhanced Robustness to Dynamics Variation:** The introduction of PICA (Physically Informed Contact-Aware) training is a notable contribution to making dexterous manipulation policies more resilient. PICA's ability to inject physical signals—such as contact maintenance, detachment risk, and action regularization—into policy learning, coupled with dynamics randomization, allows policies to achieve higher robustness under varying contact loads, even without explicit tactile or force feedback. This improved robustness is critical for deploying robots in real-world scenarios where contact dynamics are inherently variable.

3. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper provides a systematic evaluation protocol and a pure-geometry dexterous interaction dataset. These resources can serve as benchmarks and starting points for subsequent research in contact-rich manipulation, humanoid robotics, and loco-manipulation. The dataset's independence from specific policies or physics backends makes it broadly applicable, fostering reproducibility and comparative studies.

4. **Implications for Learning from Partial Observability:** The approach demonstrates that rich physical insights can be implicitly learned from kinematic error and control history, even in partially observable environments lacking direct force or tactile sensors. This has broader implications for robot learning where full sensory feedback is often unavailable or expensive.

5. **Guiding Policy Design and Evaluation:** The findings regarding "nominal success masks saturation collapse" are significant. They highlight the limitations of relying solely on task completion metrics and standard training reward for policy optimization. The proposed evaluation protocol, which incorporates contact-aware diagnostics like action saturation and detachment rates across varying damping conditions, offers a more comprehensive assessment of policy robustness and physical plausibility, encouraging the development of more stable and reliable control strategies.

The limitations identified, such as the policy's tendency towards action saturation under strong loads due to the position-increment action interface and lack of direct force feedback, also point to clear and impactful directions for future work. These include enriching the contact interface with wrist force/torque outputs or tactile feedback, exploring different contact modes, and integrating with whole-body control for complex humanoid tasks. Overall, DragMesh-2 pushes the boundaries of dexterous articulated object manipulation, laying groundwork for more capable and adaptive robotic systems.
