# DragMesh-2: Physically Plausible Dexterous Hand-Object Interaction with Articulated Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> DragMesh-2 introduces a contact-driven framework for physically plausible dexterous interaction with articulated objects, where the hand's actions alone generate object motion. Its Physically Informed Contact-Aware (PICA) training, which injects observable physical signals into learning without direct force feedback, enabled policies to achieve 0.56 deterministic success under strong out-of-distribution damping (4x nominal), outperforming prior PPO variants.

## Problem

- A significant gap exists between generating object-centric articulated motion and achieving realistic, hand-driven dexterous interaction, which requires sustained physical contact.
- Reinforcement learning policies often overfit to nominal dynamics and contact loads without tactile or force feedback, leading to brittle performance when contact conditions vary.
- Injecting robust physical information into policy learning without relying on direct tactile or force sensing poses a key challenge.

## Method

- The **DragMesh-2** framework employs a contact-driven task formulation where the policy exclusively controls a dexterous hand, making articulated object motion solely dependent on physical hand-handle contact.
- PICA (==Physically Informed Contact-Aware==) training is introduced within a PPO framework, using a Gated Linear Attention temporal encoder and causal-window auxiliary supervision to infer and encode implicit physical states.
- Environment-level reward shaping with terms for contact maintenance, detachment risk, action regularization, and dynamics randomization (varying damping scales) biases policy learning towards contact-conditioned interaction.

## Results

- PICA achieved a **0.56** deterministic success rate under strong out-of-distribution (**4x** nominal) damping, significantly surpassing state-only PPO (**0.27**) and other temporal encoder-based PPO variants (**0.09**-**0.32**).
- An ablation study demonstrated that the full PICA model (combining GLA temporal encoder and physical signals) consistently outperformed its individual components, particularly under high damping conditions.
- Analysis revealed that while nominal success can increase with longer training, strong-damping success may collapse (from **0.55** to **0.10**), correlating with high action-saturation ratios, highlighting the need for OOD evaluation and diagnostics beyond task success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically informed signals, combined with temporal encoding through a GLA network, are complementary and more effective for robust dexterous interaction with articulated objects than using richer temporal encoders alone.
> - Solely optimizing for nominal task success can mask policy brittleness, as prolonged training under nominal conditions may lead to action saturation and a collapse in performance under varied or out-of-distribution dynamics.
> - Robustness to contact-load variation can be significantly improved by injecting observable physical proxies and dynamics randomization into the learning process, even in the absence of explicit force or tactile sensing.
