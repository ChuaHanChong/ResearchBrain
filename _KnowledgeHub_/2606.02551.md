# AFUN: Towards an Affordance Foundation Model for Functionality Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Michigan and UC San Diego developed AFUN, a framework that integrates a large-scale data pipeline with a unified model to jointly predict task-conditional functional masks and 3D post-contact motion for robotic manipulation. It demonstrated superior performance in affordance segmentation, contact point prediction, and 3D motion estimation across diverse benchmarks, achieving a 90% average success rate on real-world robot tasks without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing affordance understanding methods often focus only on localization or motion, lacking a unified approach for both 'where' and 'how' to interact.
- Many motion prediction methods operate in 2D, leading to ambiguities in 3D robot execution, or rely on heuristics for object localization.
- Current deep learning models for affordance understanding are limited in open-world generalization due to reliance on small-scale datasets with restricted diversity.

## Method

- Developed a large-scale, standardized data pipeline by aggregating and converting 10 diverse public datasets into a unified ==affordance schema== including RGB-D, language, functional masks, and object-centric 3D post-contact motion trajectories.
- Introduced **AFUN**, a unified neural network model that takes an RGB-D observation and a language task phrase to jointly predict a ==task-conditional functional segmentation mask== and a compact ==3D post-contact motion curve (Bézier spline)==.
- Employed a three-stage training strategy that leverages ==frozen Vision-Language Models== (Qwen-VL, SAM3, Sonata) and ==MetaQuery conditioning== for stable and efficient learning of segmentation and motion prediction.

## Results

- Achieved mean gIoU **69.3** / cIoU **67.2** for affordance segmentation (+**23.9**/+**26.3** over the strongest baseline across eight test sets), and substantially more accurate contact point prediction, beating the best baseline by **12.7%** to **61.3%** hit rate.
- Consistently achieved the best 3D motion prediction with lower Average Displacement Error (ADE) (e.g., **0.098m** absolute ADE on AFUN test set) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), alongside a higher Contact-In-Mask (CIM) hit rate (**81.0%** on AFUN test set).
- Deployed on a real Franka Research 3 arm, AFUN achieved an average success rate of **90%** on four real-world manipulation tasks without requiring robot-specific fine-tuning or additional heuristics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified approach that combines large-scale, diverse data with joint mask and 3D motion prediction is effective for open-world affordance understanding in robotic manipulation.
> - Representing post-contact motion as object-centric 3D Bézier spline curves provides a stable, expressive, and directly executable signal for robot planning, handling both linear and orientation-aware manipulation.
> - Leveraging pre-trained foundation models (VLMs, segmenters, 3D encoders) via MetaQuery conditioning enables strong generalization and efficient training for specific robotic tasks without extensive fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Affordance understanding, defined as the perception of an object's possible uses and interactions, serves as an interface between visual perception and physical action for robotic manipulation. This capability is critical for robots operating in unstructured real-world environments. A significant challenge in robotics and embodied AI is the development of an "affordance foundation model" that can generalize across a wide range of environments, objects, and tasks, and comprehensively understand both *where* an interaction should occur and *how* it should be executed.

Existing research in affordance understanding typically addresses only parts of this challenge. Many methods focus on affordance localization, formulating the problem as static segmentation, keypoint detection, or reasoning-based grounding. While these approaches can identify interaction regions, they do not specify the post-contact motion required for manipulation. Conversely, methods that predict motion often do so in 2D, leading to ambiguities in 3D robot execution, or rely on heuristic localization of actionable objects. Furthermore, most current deep learning models for affordance understanding are limited in their ability to generalize to open-world scenarios due to reliance on small-scale datasets with restricted diversity.

To address the limitations of existing methods, the development of an affordance foundation model requires three interconnected components:
1. A large-scale dataset that encompasses the diversity of real-world manipulation tasks to enable generalization.
2. A model capable of accurately generating instruction-conditioned segmentation masks, identifying task-relevant regions and adapting to specific instructions.
3. A model that predicts *how* to perform the interaction, utilizing a 3D motion representation that is both expressive enough for varied behaviors and structured for stable supervision and robot execution.

AFUN aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a unified approach that integrates a large-scale, standardized data pipeline with a model capable of jointly predicting functional masks and 3D post-contact motion, thereby advancing towards a more comprehensive and generalizable affordance foundation model for open-world functionality understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop AFUN as a foundational step towards an open-world affordance foundation model for functionality understanding. This overarching goal is motivated by the limitations of current approaches and the need for more capable robotic systems. The specific objectives and their motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale, Standardized Data Pipeline:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing datasets for affordance understanding are often either too small, lack comprehensive annotations (e.g., only masks, no motion), or are collected from narrow domains. This scarcity of diverse and well-annotated data hinders the development of generalizable models. Robot manipulation tasks in the real world require understanding across heterogeneous objects, environments, and interaction types.
 - **Objective:** To build a robust and scalable data pipeline that can aggregate and convert diverse public datasets (spanning robot demonstrations, human egocentric videos, simulation data, and real-world scans) into a unified affordance schema. This schema includes RGB-D observations, language task descriptions, functional affordance masks (specifying "where to interact"), and object-centric 3D post-contact motion trajectories (specifying "how to interact"). This aims to create one of the largest public affordance datasets to date, supporting open-world generalization.

2. **Design a Unified Model for Joint Affordance Mask and 3D Motion Prediction:**
 - **Motivation:** Most current methods either localize interaction regions (segmentation) or predict motion, but rarely integrate both capabilities within a single, coherent framework. For robotic manipulation, understanding both *where* to act and *how* the object should move post-contact is crucial for actionable intelligence. Additionally, using rigid hand/gripper trajectories as motion signals in prior work can conflate pre-contact hand movements with true object affordance-relevant motion.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a unified affordance foundation model, AFUN, that jointly predicts a task-conditional functional segmentation mask and a compact 3D post-contact motion curve (represented as a Bézier spline curve). This model takes a single RGB-D observation and a language task phrase as input. By predicting object-centric motion, the approach seeks to provide a more direct and unambiguous representation of functionality for robot execution.

3. **Demonstrate Open-World Generalization and Real-Robot Deployability:**
 - **Motivation:** A true "foundation model" for affordance must exhibit strong generalization capabilities, extending its understanding to novel objects, tasks, and environments not explicitly seen during training. Furthermore, for practical utility in robotics, the model's outputs must be directly usable by real robotic systems without extensive fine-tuning or reliance on task-specific heuristics.
 - **Objective:** To evaluate AFUN's ability to generalize across various benchmarks for affordance segmentation, contact point prediction, and 3D motion prediction. Critically, to deploy AFUN on a real robot for manipulation tasks to demonstrate its effectiveness in producing precise masks and actionable 3D motions for successful path planning and execution, without requiring robot-specific fine-tuning. This objective aims to validate the model's robustness and practicality in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AFUN addresses the challenge of open-world affordance understanding by combining a large-scale data pipeline with a neural network architecture designed for joint segmentation and motion prediction.

#### 3.1. Data Pipeline for AFUN (Section 3)

The data pipeline is designed to curate a diverse, large-scale dataset with ground truth for both "what to manipulate" (segmentation) and "how to manipulate" (motion).
1. **Dataset Curation**: The process began by gathering 321,190 raw videos from 10 public sources, including robot demonstrations (AgiBot, DROID, RH20T, RoboMIND, RoboMIND2), human egocentric videos (HOI4D, VITRA), simulation data (Calvin, RLBench), and real-world scans (SceneFun3D). These videos were segmented into 1,242,740 action intervals, providing a broad range of object categories, viewpoints, tasks, and embodiments.
2. **Dataset Preprocessing**: Each dataset was converted into a common interval-based format, which includes an RGB-D observation frame, task language, camera parameters, and corresponding video clips. Dataset-specific adapters handled raw storage formats, normalized cameras, and converted tracked points into the canonical coordinate space of the observation frame. Monocular depth estimators were utilized to enhance depth map quality. For datasets like SceneFun3D, which lack object motion, motion labels were synthesized from existing 3D affordance regions and motion primitives.
3. **Annotating Object Tracks and Masks**:
 - Instead of relying on hand or gripper trajectories (which can include irrelevant pre-contact motion), the pipeline focuses on tracking the object's post-contact motion.
 - A vision-language model (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-FP8) generates a short, manipulable-part query from the task instruction and observation/contact frames. This query precisely identifies the smallest manipulable part (e.g., "drawer handle").
 - SAM3 is then used to track the manipulated object across the action interval, producing an object-centric motion trajectory and a functional affordance mask.
4. **Optimizing 3D Motion Curves**: The 2D tracked masks are back-projected into 3D points, and their mean positions per frame form a discrete 3D trajectory. To mitigate noise from depth estimation and tracking inconsistencies, a smooth parametric Bézier spline curve is fitted to these 3D points. This curve, parameterized by control points, becomes the canonical motion representation for training.
5. **Filtering and Statistics**: Rigorous filtering stages removed low-quality clips (e.g., poor task grounding, occlusion, unreliable segmentation, insufficient motion). This process reduced the initial 1,242,740 intervals to 223,334 with valid motion labels, culminating in 59,867 high-quality training samples after manual annotation and quality control.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture (Section 4.1)

AFUN takes an RGB-D observation and a task phrase as input to jointly predict a functional segmentation mask and a 3D post-contact motion curve.
1. **MetaQuery Conditioning**: The model leverages the MetaQuery mechanism to integrate a frozen Vision-Language Model (VLM) (Qwen3-VL) with downstream components. Two sets of learnable "MetaQuery" tokens (mq_s for segmentation, mq_m for motion) are appended to the VLM's input prompt. The VLM's final hidden states corresponding to these tokens serve as compact conditioning features for the segmentation and motion prediction modules, enabling shared reasoning capabilities.
2. **Segmentation Decoding**: The semantic MetaQuery tokens (mq_s) are passed through a two-layer MLP to map them into SAM3's language-feature space. These features then drive SAM3's mask decoder to predict per-detection boxes, masks, and object query features. This leverages SAM3's pre-trained knowledge for functional segmentation.
3. **Motion Decoding**: A pretrained Sonata network encodes the unprojected depth input into 3D geometric features. The motion decoder, a transformer network, utilizes these 3D features, task-conditioned context, per-object features from SAM3, and the motion MetaQuery tokens (mq_m) to predict the parameters of the 3D motion curves.
4. **Curved Motion Representation**: Post-contact motion is represented as an anchored 3D Bézier spline curve. The centroid of the predicted mask's depth map defines the starting point (P0). The motion decoder predicts K additional control points in relative 3D coordinates. The final trajectory is generated by evaluating the Bernstein polynomial basis, providing uniformly sampled 3D waypoints for robot execution.

#### 3.3. Training Scheme (Section 4.2)

To ensure stable training, AFUN employs a three-stage strategy, keeping the pretrained Qwen-VL, SAM3, and Sonata models frozen:
1. **MetaQuery–SAM3 Alignment**: In the first stage, the MetaQuery tokens and the projection MLP (connecting Qwen to SAM3) are trained. This involves aligning Qwen-derived features with SAM3's native text-conditioning space using the Visual Genome dataset and an MSE loss on decoder hidden states. This step provides a robust initialization.
2. **End-to-End Training for Affordance Segmentation**: The second stage focuses on training the affordance segmentation model end-to-end on a mixture of four affordance datasets (HOVA-500K, RAGNet, InstructPart, ReasonAFF). Only the MetaQuery tokens and projection MLP are unfrozen. The training utilizes SAM3's original objectives, while the motion prediction branch remains disabled.
3. **Joint Motion and Segmentation Training**: The final stage combines the curated AFUN dataset with the Stage 2 training data to jointly train both segmentation and motion prediction. The total objective function is a weighted sum of the SAM3 grounding loss (L_sam3, down-weighted) and a curve loss (L_curve) based on the ℓ1 distance between sampled points of the predicted and ground-truth Bézier curves. Point-sampling supervision is used for motion, found more effective than direct control point regression.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AFUN was evaluated across three primary aspects: affordance mask segmentation, contact point prediction, and 3D motion prediction, culminating in real-robot demonstrations.

#### 4.1. Affordance Segmentation Evaluation

- **Qualitative Results:** AFUN demonstrated accurate segmentation of task-specific affordance regions. This included precise identification of complex interaction areas, such as the handles of scissors with holes, and intent-dependent regions, like a shovel blade for "containing" or a hammer handle for "using," indicating a capacity for task-specific reasoning.
- **Quantitative Results:** The model was evaluated on eight test sets from four benchmarks (HANDAL-Mini, 3DOI, HANDAL Easy, HANDAL Hard, 3DOI Easy, HOVA-500K, ReasonAFF, InstructPart). AFUN outperformed all baselines, including Qwen3-VL-8B+SAM3, Affordance-R1, and AffordanceNet. It achieved a mean gIoU of 69.3 and a mean cIoU of 67.2, representing an improvement of +23.9 and +26.3 points, respectively, over the strongest baseline (Affordance-R1). Even a smaller variant, AFUN-2B, maintained a significant performance advantage.

#### 4.2. Contact Point Evaluation

- AFUN's predicted contact point was derived from the Pole of Inaccessibility of the predicted mask.
- The model was compared against 2D point-based methods (A0, GLOVER++, VRB) using the hit rate (Pr[point ∈ GT mask]) metric across the same eight test sets.
- AFUN achieved substantially more accurate contact points, outperforming the best baseline by a margin of 12.7% to 61.3% (e.g., 55.7% on InstructPart and 61.3% on ReasonAFF).

#### 4.3. 3D Motion Evaluation

- **Evaluation Datasets:** Three distinct test sets were used: the AFUN test set (121 cross-source, quality-controlled examples), the SceneFun3D test set (721 examples from novel scenes), and the RoboMIND2 test set (156 out-of-domain, functionality-related examples).
- **Metrics and Baselines:** 3D motion curves were evaluated using Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) in both absolute and relative scales, and Contact-In-Mask (CIM) hit rate. Baselines included A0, VRB, VidBot, and General Flow (where General Flow was provided with AFUN's predicted masks for its query points).
- **Quantitative Results:** AFUN consistently achieved the best ADE and FDE in both absolute and relative scales across all three test sets. It also significantly outperformed baselines in CIM. For instance, on the AFUN test set, AFUN recorded an absolute ADE of 0.098 meters, compared to 0.110 meters for General Flow and 0.242 meters for VRB, alongside an 81.0% CIM hit rate compared to VRB's 11.0%. This performance was maintained even when baselines were provided with favorable input conditions.
- **Qualitative Results:** AFUN produced task-aligned masks and smooth, physically plausible 3D motion curves. In contrast, baselines frequently exhibited implausible object localization or inconsistent trajectories.

#### 4.4. Ablations

- **LLM Backbone:** An ablation study on the LLM backbone revealed that the default Qwen3-VL-8B model achieved the best segmentation performance (69.3 mean gIoU, 67.2 mean cIoU), outperforming both the smaller Qwen3-VL-2B and the larger Qwen3.5-9B. The researchers hypothesized that the general-purpose Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) design of Qwen3.5-9B might be less suitable for dense vision-language prediction.
- **3D Feature Encoder:** Replacing the default Sonata with DFormerv2 as the 3D feature encoder resulted in a slight decrease in performance on the RoboMIND2 test set (ADEa of 0.273 for DFormerv2 vs. 0.254 for Sonata), suggesting that Sonata's point cloud-derived features offer stronger geometric cues.
- **Motion Curve Parameterization:** The chosen Bézier spline curve representation for motion yielded superior results compared to the parameterization used in OPD (ADEa of 0.282 for OPD vs. 0.254 for AFUN on RoboMIND2), indicating the effectiveness of the chosen representation.

#### 4.5. Real-Robot Demonstration

- AFUN was deployed on a Franka Research 3 arm, utilizing two RealSense D435 RGB-D cameras for input, and AnyGrasp for grasp pose estimation. The deployment required no robot-specific fine-tuning or additional task-specific heuristics.
- Evaluated on four real-world tasks: "Pick Up Screwdriver," "Take Off Pot Lid," "Open Drawer," and "Open Microwave."
- AFUN achieved an average success rate of 90%, demonstrating reliable performance for both contact-centric grasping and orientation-aware manipulation of articulated objects. This indicated the model's ability to predict precise masks and motions directly applicable for robot planning and execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AFUN represents a significant step towards developing an affordance foundation model, addressing key limitations of prior research in robotic manipulation. Its contributions have several implications for the field:

1. **Unified Functionality Understanding**: By jointly predicting task-conditional functional masks ("where to interact") and 3D post-contact motion curves ("how to interact"), AFUN moves beyond fragmented affordance perception. This integrated understanding provides a more complete and actionable interface for robots, enabling them to perform complex, nuanced interactions that require both precise localization and specific motion profiles.

2. **Scalable and Standardized Data for Generalization**: The development of a large-scale, standardized data pipeline is a notable achievement. By converting heterogeneous data sources (robot, human, simulation, real-world scans) into a unified affordance schema, this work addresses a critical bottleneck in training generalizable robotic agents. This curated dataset, one of the largest to date for motion affordances, offers a robust foundation for future research and could accelerate the development of more versatile embodied AI systems.

3. **Open-World Generalization**: The extensive evaluation across diverse benchmarks and out-of-domain test sets, coupled with successful real-robot deployment without fine-tuning, demonstrates AFUN's strong generalization capabilities. This capacity to adapt to novel objects, environments, and tasks without task-specific heuristics is crucial for enabling robots to operate in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings.

4. **Actionable Intelligence for Robotics**: The representation of post-contact motion as a smooth 3D Bézier spline curve provides a structured and executable output for robots. This allows for not only linear movements but also orientation-aware manipulation (e.g., rotational movements for opening a microwave), which is often challenging for simpler trajectory representations. This direct actionability contributes to more autonomous and capable robotic systems.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: AFUN establishes a strong baseline and a methodological framework for future work in open-world affordance models. It highlights the importance of large-scale, high-quality data and a joint perception-action paradigm. The model's architecture, leveraging frozen large language and vision models, also suggests an effective approach for building upon pre-trained knowledge to solve complex robotic tasks.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, particularly that generalization may be constrained by completely novel motions not represented in the training data (e.g., interactions with entirely unfamiliar objects like spray bottle triggers or sun visors). This indicates that the pursuit of truly open-world affordance understanding may require even larger, more diverse datasets. Nevertheless, AFUN represents a substantial step forward in unifying functionality perception with executable action, offering a practical path towards more adaptable and intelligent robotic manipulation.
