# AFUN: Towards an Affordance Foundation Model for Functionality Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Michigan and UC San Diego developed AFUN, a framework that integrates a large-scale data pipeline with a unified model to jointly predict task-conditional functional masks and 3D post-contact motion for robotic manipulation. It demonstrated superior performance in affordance segmentation, contact point prediction, and 3D motion estimation across diverse benchmarks, achieving a 90% average success rate on real-world robot tasks without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Existing affordance understanding methods often focus only on localization or motion, lacking a unified approach for both 'where' and 'how' to interact.
- Many motion prediction methods operate in 2D, leading to ambiguities in 3D robot execution, or rely on heuristics for object localization.
- Current deep learning models for affordance understanding are limited in open-world generalization due to reliance on small-scale datasets with restricted diversity.

## Method

- Developed a large-scale, standardized data pipeline by aggregating and converting 10 diverse public datasets into a unified ==affordance schema== including RGB-D, language, functional masks, and object-centric 3D post-contact motion trajectories.
- Introduced **AFUN**, a unified neural network model that takes an RGB-D observation and a language task phrase to jointly predict a ==task-conditional functional segmentation mask== and a compact ==3D post-contact motion curve (Bézier spline)==.
- Employed a three-stage training strategy that leverages ==frozen Vision-Language Models== (Qwen-VL, SAM3, Sonata) and ==MetaQuery conditioning== for stable and efficient learning of segmentation and motion prediction.

## Results

- Achieved a mean gIoU of **69.3** and cIoU of **67.2** for affordance segmentation, outperforming the strongest baseline by **+23.9** and **+26.3** points respectively across eight diverse test sets.
- Demonstrated substantially more accurate contact point prediction, outperforming the best baseline by **12.7%** to **61.3%** hit rate across various test sets.
- Consistently achieved the best 3D motion prediction with lower Average Displacement Error (ADE) (e.g., **0.098m** absolute ADE on AFUN test set) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), alongside a higher Contact-In-Mask (CIM) hit rate (**81.0%** on AFUN test set).
- Deployed on a real Franka Research 3 arm, AFUN achieved an average success rate of **90%** on four real-world manipulation tasks without requiring robot-specific fine-tuning or additional heuristics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified approach that combines large-scale, diverse data with joint mask and 3D motion prediction is effective for open-world affordance understanding in robotic manipulation.
> - Representing post-contact motion as object-centric 3D Bézier spline curves provides a stable, expressive, and directly executable signal for robot planning, handling both linear and orientation-aware manipulation.
> - Leveraging pre-trained foundation models (VLMs, segmenters, 3D encoders) via MetaQuery conditioning enables strong generalization and efficient training for specific robotic tasks without extensive fine-tuning.
