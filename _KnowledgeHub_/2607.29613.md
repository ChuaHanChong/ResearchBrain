# WCM: A World Critic Model for Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> World Critic Model (WCM) replaces the single-frame critic in VLA reinforcement learning with a lightweight LeJEPA-based model that reads a short observation history, predicts the next latent state, and estimates value jointly. The predictive objective forces the critic to encode dynamics under partial observability. Across 149 tasks on four benchmarks and seven real-robot tasks, WCM beats PPO, Flow-SDE and RECAP critics, especially out of distribution.

## Problem

- Critics in VLA reinforcement learning regress values from single-frame observations or VLM latents, missing motion, contact progress and other dynamics that matter under partial observability.
- Naive history extensions such as frame stacking or temporal ViT critics fail, because high-dimensional visual inputs make history costly to process and hard to exploit.
- Pure scalar return regression gives weak supervision, letting the critic treat history as a static feature vector and overfit, which causes out-of-distribution success to drop late in training.

## Method

- ==LeJEPA==-style **World Critic Model** (WCM): a per-frame encoder (==ViT== or the VLA's VLM backbone), ==CLIP== language cross-attention, and a ==causal Transformer== world predictor feeding separate value and dynamics heads.
- Joint objective combining ==L2 value regression==, action-conditioned ==next-latent prediction== through ==gated FiLM residual blocks==, and ==SIGReg== isotropic Gaussian regularization against latent collapse, all trained end-to-end.
- Drop-in critic for on-policy ==PPO== and ==Flow-SDE== with ==GAE== advantages, and for off-policy ==AWR== and ==RECAP== using mixed teleoperation SFT data plus successful and failed rollouts.

## Results

- ManiSkill with pi0.5: **91.9%** in-distribution and **64.4%** out-of-distribution, beating Flow-SDE (**90.9/49.3**) and pi-StepNFT (**85.4/59.5**); OpenVLA-OFT rises from **0.8%** zero-shot to **98.7%**.
- LIBERO-Plus: starting from one-demo SFT, WCM reaches **72.8-74.0%** total success after about **250** RL steps, matching or exceeding 50-demo full SFT on pi0, pi0.5 and OpenVLA-OFT.
- Real WidowX, seven tasks: pi0.5 with WCM beats RECAP on every task, e.g. stovetop cleaning **33/50** vs **27/50**; a history-only critic without prediction underperforms, and three frames work best.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Giving a critic history is not enough; it needs a predictive objective that forces its representation to model dynamics, consistent with predictive state representation theory.
> - An auxiliary world-prediction loss mainly buys generalization: out-of-distribution success swung 10.6 points across loss weights versus 2.7 in-distribution, with the best weights between 0.3 and 0.5.
> - Critic overfitting can erode out-of-distribution gains during RL; even a zero-value critic beat Flow-SDE out of distribution, so critic design affects robustness, not only sample efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which are pre-trained on large-scale robot manipulation and image-text data. These models often undergo post-training, typically through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using expert demonstrations. However, SFT is inherently limited by the coverage and diversity of available demonstrations, restricting generalization capabilities, especially in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training offers an alternative by improving policies through environment interaction, demonstrating potential for stronger generalization, smoother deployment, and enhanced sim-to-real transfer. Critic-based RL methods, which use a value estimator to provide dense supervision, are central to the sample efficiency and ultimate performance of these systems.

A fundamental challenge in robotic control is its partially observable nature, often modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). Traditional critic models predominantly rely on single-frame observations or single-frame VLM backbone latents, which can miss crucial dynamic information such as motion, contact progression, or future state evolution. While incorporating observation history into the critic has been explored, naive approaches like frame stacking or temporal aggregation face issues like exponential complexity in high-dimensional visual spaces, weak supervision from scalar value regression for learning cross-temporal dynamics, and ineffective representation learning.

This work addresses the limitation of existing critic models in VLA-RL by focusing on the "state approximation problem." It posits that without an explicit world modeling objective, the critic's representation struggles to capture the temporal structure necessary for accurate value estimation. The proposed World Critic Model (WCM) aims to bridge this gap by unifying world modeling and value learning, thus contributing to the ongoing effort to improve the robustness, generalization, and sample efficiency of VLA-RL systems for complex robotic tasks. It builds upon ideas from predictive representation learning and the success of predictive objectives in large models, adapting them for the specific challenges of VLA-RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. Identify and address a representation bottleneck in current critic-based VLA-RL methods, specifically concerning partial observability and the insufficiency of single-frame or weakly supervised history embeddings for learning temporally informative states.
2. Propose and develop the World Critic Model (WCM), a unified critic architecture that integrates future-state prediction with value estimation. The goal is for the critic's representation to be explicitly trained to encode environment dynamics rather than solely regress scalar returns.
3. Demonstrate that WCM can be seamlessly integrated into both on-policy and off-policy VLA-RL pipelines and that it consistently improves performance and generalization across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several identified limitations and observations in the current VLA-RL landscape:
- **Partial Observability Mismatch**: Robotic manipulation is inherently a POMDP. Existing critic-based approaches often operate on single-frame observations, which provide insufficient dynamic information for accurate value estimation. This mismatch leads to suboptimal decision-making.
- **Ineffectiveness of Naive History Incorporation**: Simple methods like frame stacking or temporal aggregation to include observation history have proven ineffective in large observation spaces or with sparse scalar supervision. The critic may treat historical inputs as a larger static feature vector without truly learning cross-temporal dynamics.
- **Lack of Explicit Temporal Dynamics Learning**: Scalar value regression alone offers weak supervision for learning how the environment evolves over time. The authors suggest that an explicit objective is needed to train the critic representation to capture future evolution.
- **Inspiration from Predictive Learning**: The success of predictive objectives in fields like Large Language Models (LLMs) for learning transferable representations, and prior work on representation learning emphasizing the ability to predict one's future as a characteristic of a good state representation, motivates the integration of a world-model objective.
- **Need for a Predictive State Representation**: The authors aim to enable the critic to learn a "predictive state"—a compact and updatable summary of task-relevant future outcomes—by jointly optimizing predictive dynamics and value estimation, rather than treating world modeling as a detached auxiliary task. This is expected to provide richer supervision and improve the critic's ability to approximate the true system state.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The World Critic Model (WCM) unifies world prediction and value estimation within a single, lightweight architecture to address the state approximation problem in VLA-RL.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Robotic manipulation with VLA models is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) (𝒮, 𝒪, 𝒜, 𝒯, ℛ, 𝛾). At each timestep $t$, the agent receives an observation $o_t$ (image, proprioception, language) that partially reveals the hidden latent state $s_t$. The agent takes an action $a_t$ via a VLA policy $\pi_\theta$, leading to a transition to $s_{t+1}$ and a reward $r_t = \mathcal{R}(s_t, a_t)$. The objective is to maximize the expected discounted cumulative reward.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

WCM is built upon a lightweight LeJEPA architecture and comprises four main components:
- **Observation Encoder**: Processes individual observations from a history of $K$ frames ($o_{t-K+1}$ to $o_t$) into per-frame latent embeddings, $\mathbf{z}_{t-k} = \text{enc}_\epsilon(o_{t-k})$. This encoder can be a Vision Transformer (ViT) or the VLM backbone of the VLA policy.
- **Language Conditioning**: The language instruction $\ell$ is encoded by CLIP and mapped into the WCM latent space using a learned adapter $\mathcal{A}_{\text{lang}}$. The encoded visual history then attends to this language token.
- **World Predictor (Causal Transformer History Trunk)**: A causal Transformer processes the language-conditioned sequence of visual history to produce a hidden representation $\mathbf{h}_t$. This component is responsible for integrating temporal information.
- **Decoder Heads**: Two separate heads branch from $\mathbf{h}_t$:
 - **Value Decoder Head ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{value}}$)**: Estimates the value of the current state, $\hat{V}_t = \mathcal{D}_{\text{value}}(\mathbf{h}_t)$.
 - **Action-Conditioned Latent Dynamics Branch ($\mathcal{D}_{\text{world}}$)**: Predicts the next latent state, $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t+1} = \mathcal{D}_{\text{world}}(\mathbf{h}_t, a_t, \mathbf{z}_t)$, using a residual update mechanism implemented with an action encoder and gated FiLM blocks.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The overall training objective combines three loss components:
- **Prediction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}}$)**: An L2 loss between the predicted next latent state $\hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t+1}$ and the ground truth next latent state $\mathbf{z}_{t+1}$ (teacher-forcing), $\mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} = \Vert \hat{\mathbf{z}}_{t+1} - \mathbf{z}_{t+1} \Vert_2^2$. This loss explicitly trains the model to capture future dynamics.
- **Sketched-Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}}$)**: Applied to the latent representations $\mathbf{z}_t$ to prevent feature collapse. It encourages the latent space to match an isotropic Gaussian distribution by enforcing that every one-dimensional projection of $\mathbf{z}$ onto a random unit vector matches the characteristic function of a standard Gaussian.
- **Value Estimation Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}}$)**: An L2 loss between the predicted value $\hat{V}_t$ and the ground truth return $G_t$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{value}} = \Vert \hat{V}_t - G_t \Vert_2^2$. Rewards are defined as 0 for success, $-C_{\text{fail}}$ for failure, and $-1$ otherwise, with returns min-max normalized to $[-1, 1]$.

The complete training objective is $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{value}} + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{pred}} + \eta \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{SIGReg}}$, where $\lambda$ and $\eta$ are hyperparameters weighting the prediction and regularization losses, respectively. All components are trained end-to-end.

#### 3.4. Training Pipeline

WCM integrates into existing VLA-RL training pipelines:
- **On-policy Setting**: For autoregressive (AR) models like OpenVLA-OFT, PPO is used. For flow-matching models like $\pi_0$ and $\pi_{0.5}$, Flow-SDE (a PPO variant) is employed. WCM serves as the critic, utilizing the VLM backbone as its observation encoder. SIGReg is omitted in the on-policy pipeline to reduce computational overhead.
- **Off-policy Setting**: This pipeline leverages a unified data buffer comprising SFT data from teleoperation and data from policy rollouts (including failure cases). For AR models, Advantage-weighted Regression (AWR) is adopted. For flow-matching models, RECAP is used. WCM replaces the original critic model in these algorithms. This setting is particularly relevant for real-world scenarios where data collection is expensive.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive experimental validation across simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of the World Critic Model (WCM).

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

- **ManiSkill Benchmark**: WCM consistently improves performance across 149 tasks. For instance, with OpenVLA-OFT, WCM achieves a 252% improvement from weak SFT baselines. Remarkably, when OpenVLA-OFT is initialized with a very low zero-shot success rate (0.78% on ManiSkill data), WCM boosts performance by 12,551% to 98.7% success.
- **MetaWorld and CALVIN Benchmarks**: WCM outperforms existing methods on MetaWorld, which involves tasks beyond simple pick-and-place, and shows enhanced long-horizon capabilities on CALVIN. These improvements are attributed to WCM's ability to capture higher-order state information, leading to better latent representations and value estimation in POMDP settings.

#### 4.2. Generalization Performance

- **ManiSkill-OOD**: WCM improves both in-distribution (IND) performance and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization on ManiSkill, surpassing traditional Flow-SDE and PPO baselines. It also outperforms the $\pi$-StepNFT baseline, known for its OOD performance, by providing more robust value estimation under distribution shifts.
- **LIBERO-Plus**: Starting from one-shot SFT models (one demonstration per task), WCM-trained policies achieve success rates comparable to or exceeding full-shot SFT models (50 demonstrations) after approximately 250 RL training steps, indicating strong generalization from limited initial data.

#### 4.3. Real-World Performance

- **WidowX-250S Robot**: WCM was evaluated on 7 real-world manipulation tasks, including dynamic grasping, long-horizon tasks, deformable object manipulation, and pick-and-place tasks. Using $\pi_{0.5}$ and OpenVLA-OFT as base policies with off-policy RL (AWR/RECAP), WCM-guided training consistently outperforms standard VLM-critic baselines. For example, it improves performance in tasks like cloth folding (from 15/50 to 38/50 for OpenVLA-OFT) and conveyor belt sushi picking (from 9/50 to 22/50).
- **Efficiency**: The real-world experiments demonstrate WCM's effectiveness and efficiency under limited-data training (hundreds to a few thousand data samples), enabling rapid iterative refinement and accurate critic predictions within less than an hour of training. This leads to smoother operations and higher throughput compared to baselines.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **World Prediction Objective**:
 - Modifying an original MLP critic with more observation history without world prediction leads to suboptimal performance.
 - A history-based ViT (WCM with $\lambda=0$, no world prediction) also proves ineffective.
 - Incorporating the explicit world prediction objective is crucial for leveraging historical information and achieving better performance across all tested models ($\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, OpenVLA-OFT) on ManiSkill and MetaWorld.
- **Observation History Length**: An observation history length of 3 frames performed best on average in experiments. The authors hypothesize this length is sufficient to capture second-order dynamics (acceleration) needed for the tasks, with longer histories providing limited additional benefits.
- **Weight Allocation of Training Objectives ($\lambda$)**:
 - $\lambda$ balances world prediction and value learning. Neither extremely large nor small values yield optimal results; the best performance typically occurs within an intermediate range (e.g., [0.3, 0.5]).
 - A higher weight on the world prediction objective ($\lambda=0.9$) yielded better OOD performance than a higher weight on value regression ($\lambda=0.1$), suggesting world prediction contributes positively to generalization.
 - OOD performance is more sensitive to $\lambda$ variations than IND performance.
- **Critic Model's Impact on Generalization**:
 - Traditional Flow-SDE exhibits a "dropping phenomenon" in OOD performance, indicating critic overfitting in later training stages.
 - A "zero-value" ablation (fixing the value function to 0) achieves better OOD performance than Flow-SDE, supporting the hypothesis that overfitting is linked to the critic model.
 - WCM shows no significant overfitting within the first 500 training steps.
 - Heatmap visualizations of critic-predicted values demonstrate that WCM maintains better value discriminability and is less prone to generating local outliers in OOD settings compared to baselines, indicating stronger generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The World Critic Model (WCM) addresses a critical limitation in Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning (VLA-RL) by tackling the state approximation problem under partial observability. By explicitly training the critic's representation to capture temporal dynamics through joint latent state prediction and value estimation, WCM offers several significant contributions:

- **Improved State Representation**: The core contribution is enabling the critic to learn a more comprehensive and temporally informed state representation, moving beyond single-frame observations or weakly supervised history embeddings. This is crucial for robotic tasks where dynamic information is key.
- **Consistent Performance Gains**: WCM consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of simulation benchmarks (149 tasks across ManiSkill, MetaWorld, CALVIN) with diverse VLA backbones. This indicates its broad applicability and robustness.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities**: A notable impact is WCM's strong generalization to out-of-distribution settings and its ability to learn effectively from limited demonstrations (e.g., outperforming full-shot SFT from one-shot SFT initialization on LIBERO-Plus). This is particularly valuable for deploying robots in varied and unlknown environments.
- **Effective Real-World Deployment**: Validation on seven real-world manipulation tasks with a physical robot (WidowX-250S) confirms WCM's stable and effective deployment, even with limited real-world data and training time. This demonstrates its practical utility for iterative refinement in physical robot systems.
- **Architectural Efficiency**: By building on a lightweight LeJEPA architecture, WCM minimizes model complexity, enabling training from scratch and efficient integration into existing on-policy and off-policy RL pipelines without significant computational overhead.
- **Richer Supervision**: The joint objective of world modeling and value estimation provides a richer supervisory signal than scalar value regression alone, which is essential for learning complex, time-dependent dynamics.

The development of WCM represents a step towards more robust and generalizable VLA-RL agents for robotic manipulation. Its ability to improve both in-distribution performance and out-of-distribution generalization, coupled with demonstrated real-world effectiveness, suggests that explicit temporal dynamics learning in critic models could become a standard component in future VLA-RL systems, potentially accelerating the development of capable and adaptable robot policies.
