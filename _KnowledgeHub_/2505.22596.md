# SAM-R1: Leveraging SAM for Reward Feedback in Multimodal Segmentation via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The SAM-R1 framework from Tsinghua University integrates the Segment Anything Model (SAM) into a reinforcement learning loop to provide fine-grained, pixel-level feedback for training multimodal large language models (MLLMs). This approach enhances multimodal segmentation by improving reasoning capabilities and achieving strong zero-shot performance with high data efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal segmentation methods rely on large, manually annotated datasets, including explicit reasoning, which are costly and time-consuming to produce.
- Previous multimodal reasoning models for segmentation often decouple the reasoning model from the segmentation decoder, leading to a lack of direct pixel-level feedback and a risk of reward hacking.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting and struggle with generalization to out-of-domain scenarios or ambiguous user queries.

## Method

- **SAM-R1** integrates a multimodal large language model (MLLM) with the ==Segment Anything Model== (SAM), using SAM as an external reward provider within a reinforcement learning (RL) training loop to offer direct, IoU-based feedback.
- The framework employs an adapted Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm with modifications: asymmetric clipping thresholds for broader exploration and token-level loss normalization to encourage information-dense responses.
- Three task-specific, rule-based reward functions guide the training: a tiered segmentation-accuracy reward (IoU-based via SAM), a reasoning-format reward, and a segmentation-format reward.

## Results

- Achieved **60.2%** gIoU and **54.3%** cIoU on the ReasonSeg benchmark, outperforming the previous best method, Seg-Zero, by **1.9%** gIoU and **0.9%** cIoU.
- Demonstrated strong generalization with limited training data, achieving **74.7%** cIoU on out-of-distribution RefCOCO+ testA, surpassing Seg-Zero's **73.9%** cIoU.
- Exhibited high data efficiency, with performance saturating at approximately 3,000 training samples, showing negligible gains with more data, and generalized well to the Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) task, scoring **63.8%** on LISA-Grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, when guided by direct segmentation accuracy feedback from a model like SAM, can effectively instill fine-grained perceptual reasoning in MLLMs with limited explicit reasoning annotations.
> - Algorithmic enhancements to GRPO, specifically asymmetric clipping and token-level loss normalization, enable more aggressive exploration and foster higher information density in generated responses while maintaining training stability.
> - Integrating the segmentation model directly into the RL reward loop provides end-to-end alignment between MLLM reasoning and pixel-level segmentation objectives, mitigating issues like reward hacking observed in decoupled systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the evolving field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and their application to visual understanding, particularly in image segmentation tasks. MLLMs have demonstrated capabilities in visual tasks such as image captioning and visual question answering, with a recent expansion into more complex and fine-grained perception like multimodal segmentation. Traditional segmentation methods often rely on simple categorical labels, whereas "reasoning segmentation" requires models to interpret complex textual queries and perform logical reasoning to generate pixel-level masks.

Early approaches, such as LISA, integrated MLLMs with segmentation models using specialized tokens. Subsequent studies adopted similar strategies, primarily relying on large-scale annotated datasets and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to train both the language model and the segmentation decoder. However, these SFT-based methods face challenges including high training costs, potential for catastrophic forgetting of general abilities, and limited generalization to out-of-domain scenarios. They also typically lack explicit, interpretable reasoning processes and struggle with ambiguous user queries.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as an alternative approach to enhance the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) without requiring extensive reasoning-annotated data. Methods like DeepSeek-R1 and Seg-Zero have applied RL, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to visual-language tasks, using rule-based and accuracy-based rewards to foster reasoning. Seg-Zero, for instance, introduced a comprehensive reward system for reasoning segmentation, but its architecture decoupled the reasoning model from the segmentation decoder, limiting pixel-level feedback and increasing the risk of "reward hacking" (where the model satisfies the reward function without achieving the true task objective).

This paper addresses these limitations by integrating a powerful segmentation model, Segment Anything Model (SAM), directly into the RL training loop as a reward provider. This integration is designed to facilitate direct, task-relevant feedback based on segmentation accuracy, thereby aligning model optimization more closely with the final task objective and enabling fine-grained perceptual reasoning in an end-to-end manner, an aspect not fully explored in prior multimodal reasoning models for segmentation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient end-to-end framework, named SAM-R1, that enables multimodal large models to perform fine-grained reasoning in image understanding tasks, specifically multimodal segmentation. The motivation behind SAM-R1 stems from several identified limitations in existing approaches:

1. **Reliance on Costly Manual Annotations**: Current MLLM-based segmentation methods frequently depend on large, manually annotated datasets, often including explicit reasoning processes. The production of such datasets is expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive, limiting scalability and the diversity of training scenarios. SAM-R1 aims to mitigate this by leveraging RL, which can induce reasoning capabilities with less reliance on explicit reasoning annotations.

2. **Lack of Fine-Grained Segmentation Feedback**: Previous multimodal reasoning models for segmentation, even those employing RL like Seg-Zero, often decouple the reasoning model from the segmentation decoder. This prevents the reasoning model from directly receiving pixel-level feedback on the quality of its segmentation outputs, leading to a potential misalignment between the optimization objective and the ultimate task goal. This decoupling can increase the risk of reward hacking, where models optimize for the reward function's proxy signals rather than the actual segmentation quality. SAM-R1 addresses this by incorporating the segmentation model directly into the reward loop.

3. **Limitations in Generalization and Robustness**: SFT-based methods are susceptible to catastrophic forgetting and can struggle with out-of-domain generalization. Reasoning segmentation tasks often involve ambiguous and complex textual queries that demand robust reasoning capabilities from MLLMs to accurately interpret user intent and precisely localize target regions. The authors are motivated to develop a system that not only performs well on in-domain data but also generalizes effectively to out-of-domain and challenging reasoning scenarios.

4. **Inefficiencies in RL Training Algorithms**: While GRPO has shown promise, the authors identify specific areas for improvement in its application to fine-grained segmentation tasks. These include a potential for overly lengthy, low-information responses that waste tokens and increase hallucination risk, and the need for greater flexibility in optimizing task-specific reasoning without sacrificing stability.

To address these motivations, SAM-R1 proposes:
- Integrating fine-grained segmentation settings directly into the training of multimodal reasoning models.
- Designing task-specific, fine-grained reward functions that utilize SAM as a flexible reward provider, offering direct, IoU-based feedback from segmentation outputs.
- Introducing modifications to the GRPO optimization objective (asymmetric clipping, token-level loss normalization) to encourage broader exploration and higher information density in responses, while maintaining training stability.

Ultimately, the objective is to create an end-to-end framework that can interpret complex instructions, localize segmentation targets accurately, and achieve strong performance with high data efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAM-R1 is an end-to-end framework for reasoning segmentation that utilizes reinforcement learning with reward-driven optimization. The framework integrates a multimodal large language model (MLLM) with the Segment Anything Model (SAM) in its training loop.

#### 3.1. SAM-R1 Framework

The SAM-R1 framework takes user-provided questions and images as input. The MLLM processes this multimodal input to perform reasoning and analysis, synthesizing information to identify the target object. It then generates intermediate reasoning outputs, which serve as inputs to the segmentation model (SAM) for mask generation. A key aspect is that the MLLM's outputs are designed to enhance SAM's performance. Critically, SAM is incorporated as a reward provider during the reinforcement learning phase, allowing its outputs to offer detailed, pixel-level feedback that refines the training of the MLLM.

The user prompt for SAM-R1 explicitly guides the model to output a thinking process within `<think>` tags and the final answer within `<answer>` tags. The answer is expected to be in JSON format, containing a bounding box (`bbox`) and points for precise object localization.

#### 3.2. RL Training Algorithm

The core of SAM-R1's training is based on reinforcement learning, specifically an adaptation of the DeepSeek R1-Zero algorithm, which uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

#### 3.2.1. DeepSeek R1-Zero and GRPO

DeepSeek R1-Zero trains models to output a reasoning process and a final answer, with supervision applied only to the answer. GRPO operates by sampling a group of responses for each input question from the current policy. Reward advantages for each response are computed by normalizing the group's rewards relative to their mean and standard deviation. Similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), GRPO employs a clipped objective along with a KL divergence penalty to limit the distributional shift between successive policies. This approach aims to achieve robust reasoning performance with limited explicit reasoning supervision.

#### 3.2.2. Our Training Algorithm

The authors introduce modifications to the GRPO algorithm to enhance its suitability for the multimodal image segmentation task:

1. **Asymmetric Clipping Thresholds**: The standard clipped objective in PPO and GRPO uses a single threshold ($\epsilon$). SAM-R1 decouples this into asymmetric bounds, $\epsilon_{low}$ and $\epsilon_{high}$. While $\epsilon_{low}$ is kept unchanged, $\epsilon_{high}$ is slightly raised (e.g., to 0.3). This modification is intended to encourage broader exploration by allowing greater updates from highly advantageous actions, providing the MLLM more flexibility in optimizing its task-specific reasoning without compromising training stability, which is still maintained by the KL divergence constraint.

2. **Token-Level Loss Normalization**: The authors observed that GRPO can sometimes produce lengthy responses with limited informative content, which can lead to reward hacking. To counteract this, they rescale the loss so that every token within a response group receives the same loss. This discourages redundant and repetitive outputs, encouraging the policy model to generate responses with higher information density. The modified objective function normalizes the sum of losses by the total number of tokens in the sampled response group.

These algorithmic enhancements aim to enable aggressive exploration, foster fine-grained understanding, and ensure stable training without requiring an additional critic model.

#### 3.3. Reward Functions

SAM-R1 employs three task-specific, rule-based reward functions adapted for the multimodal segmentation setting:

1. **Tiered Segmentation-accuracy Reward Function**: This is a central component, treating SAM as an external reward provider. The MLLM's predicted target location (bounding box and points) is passed to SAM, which generates a segmentation mask. The Intersection over Union (IoU) between this generated mask and the ground-truth mask is calculated. A piecewise reward system is then applied:
 - 4 points for IoU > 0.80
 - 3 points for 0.70 < IoU ≤ 0.80
 - 2 points for 0.50 < IoU ≤ 0.70
 - 0 points otherwise
 This tiered structure provides robust positive feedback, guiding the model toward gradual improvement and aligning the reasoning process with segmentation accuracy.

2. **Reasoning-format Reward**: This reward encourages the MLLM to explicitly output its chain-of-thought. A positive reward is given if the thinking process is enclosed within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, and the final answer is placed between `<answer>` and `</answer>` tags. Malformed outputs incur a penalty.

3. **Segmentation-format Reward**: This reward ensures that the MLLM provides fine-grained cues to the downstream segmentation module in a prescribed JSON-like format. The output must include a detected bounding box, reference points, and a descriptive textual flag. Compliance with this schema yields a reward, while deviations incur a penalty.

These reward functions combined with the modified GRPO algorithm form the basis for training SAM-R1, guiding the MLLM to develop both reasoning capabilities and precise segmentation output generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of SAM-R1 across various benchmarks, particularly in zero-shot settings and with limited training data.

#### 4.1. ReasonSeg Benchmark (Zero-shot)

On the challenging ReasonSeg benchmark, SAM-R1 achieved a gIoU of 60.2% and a cIoU of 54.3% on the test set. This performance surpassed Seg-Zero, the previous best, which reported 58.3% gIoU and 53.4% cIoU. The authors attribute this improvement to the fine-grained, SAM-integrated IoU-based reward feedback within the RL loop, which aligns the reasoning process with segmentation. The unified framework and the improved GRPO strategy are also cited as contributors to better generalization and robustness.

#### 4.2. Referring Expression Segmentation

For referring expression segmentation, SAM-R1 was evaluated on in-domain (RefCOCOg) and out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets (RefCOCO testA and RefCOCO+ testA). Trained on only 3,000 samples, SAM-R1 achieved competitive performance:
- On RefCOCOg (in-domain), SAM-R1 achieved 73.1% cIoU, comparable to Seg-Zero (73.3%) despite using fewer training samples.
- On RefCOCO (OOD), SAM-R1 scored 79.2% cIoU, matching Seg-Zero.
- On RefCOCO+ (OOD), SAM-R1 achieved 74.7% cIoU, an improvement over Seg-Zero's 73.9%.
These results indicate SAM-R1's strong generalization capabilities, which the authors link to the fine-grained reward mechanisms and flexible exploration strategy.

#### 4.3. Visualization Analysis

Qualitative results demonstrate SAM-R1's robust zero-shot performance, enhanced by its chain-of-thought approach. Examples include:
- **Multiple Subjects with Fine-Grained Segmentation**: The model successfully identifies and segments "Santa Claus" among distractors by leveraging cues like red clothing and specific actions.
- **Global To Local Reasoning**: It accurately locates specific parts, like "the engines" of an airplane, by reasoning about their spatial relationships to larger structures.
- **Challenging Environment With Distractors**: In cluttered scenes, such as an airport, the model distinguishes a target sign like "Watch Your Step" from similar signs by using contextual references.
- **Complex Boundaries**: For objects with intricate shapes, like a "vaulting table" in gymnastics, the model integrates textual and visual information to infer its function and generate coherent segmentation masks.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Tiered Threshold Strategy

The ablation study confirmed the efficacy of the tiered reward threshold strategy (e.g., different rewards for IoU > 0.8, 0.7, 0.5). It significantly outperformed fixed thresholds (0.5, 0.7, or 0.8) on both ReasonSeg-zero-shot and RefCOCOg. The tiered approach achieved 60.2% gIoU on ReasonSeg test, an improvement of 3.5% over the best fixed threshold. This suggests that the dynamic adjustment of thresholds balances precision and recall more effectively, particularly enhancing OOD generalization.

#### 4.4.2. Algorithmic Components (Token-level and Asymmetric Clipping)

Further analysis of algorithmic components validated the token-level loss normalization and asymmetric clipping.
- **Token-level reward mechanism**: Improved ReasonSeg cIoU by 0.5% (from 51.2% to 51.7%) and RefCOCOg cIoU by 0.6% (from 71.8% to 72.4%), indicating better regulation of output format.
- **Asymmetric clipping (Clip higher)**: Improved ReasonSeg gIoU by 1.3% (from 57.8% to 59.1%) and RefCOCOg gIoU by 0.8% (from 74.1% to 74.9%). This suggests that greater flexibility in updating advantageous actions is more impactful for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Combined effect**: The full method, incorporating both techniques, yielded synergistic improvements, raising ReasonSeg cIoU to 54.3% (a 3.1% improvement over the GRPO baseline) and achieving peak gIoU scores on both ReasonSeg (60.2%) and RefCOCOg (75.4%).

#### 4.5. Generalization to REC task

SAM-R1 also demonstrated strong generalization to the Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) task, despite not being explicitly trained on REC datasets. It achieved 63.8% on the LISA-Grounding benchmark, significantly outperforming prior methods like GroundedSAM (26.2%), OV-Seg (28.4%), X-Decoder (28.5%), and Visual-RFT (43.9%). This performance indicates the effectiveness of the RL-based reasoning framework in complex visual grounding.

#### 4.6. Data Efficiency and Scalability Analysis

An additional experiment showed that SAM-R1 exhibits high data efficiency. Increasing the training data from 3,000 to 10,000 samples resulted in negligible performance gains across ReasonSeg and RefCOCO benchmarks. This suggests that the model's core performance saturates at approximately 3,000 samples, demonstrating its ability to learn effectively from limited data.

#### 4.7. Failure Analysis

Failure cases mainly highlighted two issues: incomplete segmentation and over-segmentation. While SAM-R1's "Thinking" process often correctly comprehended the prompt and localized the target semantically, this understanding did not always translate to a perfectly precise pixel-level mask. For example, a fence might be correctly identified but only partially segmented, or a specific car part requested but the entire car segmented. These indicate limitations in precisely mapping high-level semantic reasoning to fine-grained pixel masks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SAM-R1 represents a notable contribution to the field of multimodal large models, particularly for image segmentation and visual reasoning. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Data Efficiency in Reasoning Cultivation**: The work demonstrates that reinforcement learning, specifically guided by a segmentation model, can effectively instill reasoning capabilities in multimodal models with a limited amount of training data (as few as 3,000 samples). This efficiency addresses a major challenge in MLLM development, where the reliance on large, manually annotated datasets with explicit reasoning chains is a significant bottleneck in terms of cost and time. By using standard segmentation masks as supervisory signals, SAM-R1 bypasses the need for these expensive and potentially biased human annotations.

2. **End-to-End Fine-Grained Perceptual Reasoning**: SAM-R1 is presented as the first approach to integrate fine-grained segmentation settings directly into the training of multimodal reasoning models. By incorporating SAM as an active reward provider within the RL loop, the framework enables direct, pixel-level feedback to the reasoning model. This end-to-end alignment between optimization objectives and the final task goal enhances the model's capacity for fine-grained perceptual reasoning, mitigating issues like reward hacking observed in decoupled systems.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The empirical evidence shows SAM-R1 achieving strong zero-shot performance on complex reasoning segmentation benchmarks (ReasonSeg) and competitive or improved performance on out-of-domain referring expression segmentation tasks (RefCOCO+). The modifications to the GRPO algorithm, including asymmetric clipping and token-level loss normalization, contribute to this robustness by fostering more flexible exploration and promoting information-dense responses.

4. **Advancement in RL for MLLMs**: The study offers new insights into synergizing reinforcement learning with MLLMs, particularly for tasks requiring visual-linguistic reasoning. The success of SAM-R1 reinforces the paradigm where models can learn complex reasoning from task-aligned rewards rather than relying solely on explicit instructions or extensive pre-annotated reasoning chains.

The broader impact of this work includes:
- **Scalability and Accessibility**: By reducing the dependency on costly reasoning annotations, SAM-R1 suggests a more scalable and accessible path for developing advanced MLLMs, making such technologies more attainable for research and application in domains with scarce reasoning data.
- **Applications in Specialized Domains**: The ability to learn complex reasoning from weaker, more accessible supervision is particularly impactful for specialized domains like robotic perception and medical image analysis, where collecting exhaustive reasoning data is challenging.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: SAM-R1's framework and findings lay a foundation for future research into joint optimization of MLLM and segmentation components, and developing methods to address limitations such as generating meaningful negative reference points, which could further enhance discriminative reasoning.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as SAM's frozen parameters creating a one-way information flow, preventing co-adaptation with the reasoning model. Joint optimization of both models is identified as a computationally demanding but compelling next step. The model's difficulty in generating meaningful negative reference points also points to an area for future architectural or algorithmic improvements. Despite these, SAM-R1 contributes to advancing multimodal image segmentation and highlights the potential of reward-guided learning for developing more efficient and adaptable multimodal large models.
