# EvolVE: Evolutionary Search for LLM-based Verilog Generation and Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> EvolVE, an evolutionary search framework developed by researchers at National Taiwan University and Academia Sinica, enhances large language models (LLMs) for Verilog generation and optimization. It achieves a 98.1% pass rate on VerilogEval v2 for functional correctness and improves Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) by a geometric mean of 17% on the new IC-RTL benchmark.

## Problem

- Digital hardware design using Verilog is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and requires extensive domain expertise, creating bottlenecks in chip development.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to accurately capture the formal logic and massive concurrency of hardware systems, often generating functionally incorrect or non-synthesizable Verilog code.
- Existing benchmarks for LLM-aided hardware design lack industrial-scale complexity and do not sufficiently evaluate Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) optimization, hindering meaningful progress.

## Method

- **EvolVE** employs an evolutionary search framework that leverages test-time computation and two distinct strategies: ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== for functional correctness and ==Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR)== for PPA optimization.
- A novel ==Structured Testbench Generation (STG)== mechanism provides high-fidelity, fine-grained functional gradients as feedback, accelerating debugging and guiding the iterative search process.
- The framework formulates Verilog development as a search optimization problem, allowing LLMs to iteratively refine generated code based on functional and PPA metrics.

## Results

- EvolVE achieved a state-of-the-art **98.1%** pass rate on VerilogEval v2 and **92.0%** on RTLLM v2 using DeepSeek-R1-FP4-MCTS, surpassing previous frameworks like REvolution and VerilogCoder.
- The framework demonstrated a geometric mean PPA improvement of **17%** over human-authored baselines on the new IC-RTL benchmark, with up to a **66%** peak PPA improvement for the Huffman Coding problem.
- Structured Testbench Generation (STG) reduced the average node count to resolve problems from 4.78 to 2.49 and decreased total output token consumption by over 60% for DeepSeek-R1-FP4 on Mod-VerilogEval v2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, search-based approach using evolutionary algorithms allows LLMs to overcome their inherent limitations in understanding hardware concurrency and scarcity of domain-specific training data.
> - Providing fine-grained, deterministic feedback via a structured testbench significantly improves the efficiency and convergence speed of LLM-based evolutionary search, reducing computational overhead.
> - Different evolutionary search strategies are optimal for distinct design goals: MCTS excels at resolving functional correctness issues, while IGR proves more effective for global architectural exploration and PPA optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "EvolVE" addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving landscape of digital hardware design: the automation of Verilog generation and optimization using Large Language Models (LLMs). Traditionally, Verilog design is labor-intensive, requiring extensive domain expertise and incurring significant development cycles. While LLMs offer promising automation potential, their core training on sequential text data struggles to accurately capture the strict formal logic and inherent concurrency of hardware systems. This often leads to functionally incorrect or non-synthesizable Verilog code.

The broader research landscape for LLM-aided hardware design can be categorized into two main streams:

1. **RTL Specialization (Fine-tuning):** This stream focuses on adapting LLMs specifically for Hardware Description Languages (HDLs). Works like RTLCoder (Liu et al., 2024a), ScaleRTL (Deng et al., 2025), and CodeV-R1 (Zhu et al., 2025) fall into this category, employing supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning on Verilog-specific datasets (e.g., Pyranet, MGVerilog). EvolVE acknowledges the impressive capabilities of fine-tuned models but highlights their limitations due to constrained model capacity and the scarcity of high-quality, open-source Verilog training data compared to general-purpose software languages.

2. **Agentic and Search-Based Frameworks:** This more recent and active stream guides general-purpose LLMs by structuring the problem-solving workflow. This involves:
 - **Optimization and Debugging:** Early frameworks like RTLRewriter (Yao et al., 2024) and SymRTLO (Wang et al., 2025c) focused on local code rectification and PPA (Power, Performance, Area) constraints.
 - **Collaborative Multi-Agent Systems:** Inspired by human design teams, these frameworks (e.g., MAGE, RTLSquad, VeriOpt, VerilogCoder) decompose complex specifications into sub-tasks, assign roles to different agents, and manage hierarchical pipelines.
 - **Workflow and Evolutionary Search:** This emerging sub-category treats generation as a global search problem. VFlow (Wei et al., 2025) and REvolution (Min et al., 2025) use cooperative evolution and dual-population algorithms to optimize workflows and designs. EvolVE firmly places itself within this stream, emphasizing evolutionary search as a means to iteratively refine LLM outputs and overcome the limitations of fixed-capacity models and data scarcity.

EvolVE distinguishes itself by being the "first framework to analyze multiple evolution strategies on chip design tasks," specifically comparing Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR). It also addresses significant gaps in current benchmarking practices, noting that existing benchmarks (VerilogEval v2, RTLLM v2) suffer from inconsistencies, lack industrial-scale complexity, and often ignore critical PPA optimization metrics. By introducing Mod-VerilogEval v2 and the high-complexity IC-RTL benchmark, EvolVE directly contributes to establishing more rigorous evaluation standards for LLM-aided hardware design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the EvolVE framework are driven by several critical challenges in modern hardware design and the application of LLMs to this domain:

1. **Addressing the Labor-Intensive Nature of Verilog Design:** The digital hardware industry faces an urgent need for faster development cycles without compromising circuit performance. Manual RTL coding in Verilog is inherently labor-intensive, prone to delays, and requires extensive domain expertise, acting as a significant bottleneck in chip design. EvolVE aims to automate and accelerate this process.

2. **Overcoming LLM Limitations in Hardware Design:** While LLMs excel at sequential reasoning, they inherently struggle with the strict formal logic and massive concurrency characteristic of hardware systems described by HDLs like Verilog. Their limited training data in this specialized domain often leads to functionally incorrect or non-synthesizable code. EvolVE seeks to enable LLMs to reason about concurrency, formally explore the design space, and iteratively optimize results, effectively bridging this "performance gap."

3. **Exploring and Optimizing Design Space with Evolutionary Algorithms:** Given the scarcity of domain-specific Verilog training data and the inherent reasoning limitations of fixed-capacity LLMs, EvolVE proposes a flexible framework that leverages evolutionary algorithms. The key motivation is to shift from a "learning-centric" to a "search-based" paradigm, treating HDL generation as state-space exploration rather than a single-pass process. This allows LLMs to iteratively self-correct, mimicking human debugging.

4. **Enhancing Feedback Mechanisms for Iterative Refinement:** Existing evaluation methods often provide sparse, binary feedback, hindering the efficiency of iterative evolutionary frameworks. A core motivation is to provide high-quality, fine-grained feedback that accelerates debugging and guides the evolutionary process toward faster convergence. This led to the development of Structured Testbench Generation (STG).

5. **Establishing Robust and Complex Benchmarks for PPA Optimization:** Current benchmarks (e.g., RTLLM v2, VerilogEval v2) are often criticized for inconsistencies, lack of industrial complexity, and insufficient focus on PPA (Power, Performance, Area) optimization. EvolVE aims to fill this gap by:
 - Rectifying issues in existing benchmarks with **Mod-VerilogEval v2**.
 - Introducing **IC-RTL**, a high-complexity, industry-scale benchmark derived from national design contests, specifically designed to challenge models on complex optimization techniques and PPA metrics.

In summary, EvolVE's overarching objective is to advance LLM-aided hardware design by developing a robust, model-agnostic evolutionary framework that can reliably generate functionally correct Verilog, achieve significant PPA optimizations, and provide a rigorous evaluation platform for future research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EvolVE is an evolutionary search framework designed to empower LLMs for Verilog generation and optimization, overcoming limitations in data scarcity and model scale by leveraging test-time compute. Its methodology is built upon two distinct search strategies and a novel evaluation mechanism.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The Verilog development process is formulated as a search optimization problem where the framework explores a set of nodes $N = \{N_1, N_2, \ldots, N_n\}$. Each node $N_i = (V_i, S_i, F_i)$ contains the candidate Verilog code $V_i$, a quantitative score $S_i$, and diagnostic feedback $F_i$. An evaluator function $E(V_i, T)$ executes code $V_i$ against a testbench $T$ to derive the score and feedback. The goal is to find an optimal design $N^*$ that maximizes $S_i$.

- **Generative Synthesis (Functional Correctness):** The score $S_{gen}(V_i)$ is the pass rate across test cases, with a large negative penalty for compilation or simulation failures.
- **PPA Optimization (Constrained Maximization):** The objective is to minimize the product of Area $A(V_i)$ and Latency $L(V_i)$ while maintaining functional integrity. Valid designs are scored as $- \frac{A(V_i) \cdot L(V_i)}{\eta}$, where $\eta$ is a scaling factor, and invalid designs receive a penalty. The Area-Latency product is used as a fast proxy for PPA, validated to correlate with full commercial PPA metrics.
- **Diagnostic Feedback ($F_i$):** Adapts dynamically, providing error messages for failures or design summaries/optimization guidance for valid designs.

#### 3.2. Frameworks (Evolutionary Strategies)

The EvolVE framework utilizes two distinct algorithmic strategies, Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR) and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), chosen for their complementary strengths in design exploration and debugging (Figure 1). Both share a unified workflow: initial code generation, evaluation, parent node selection, LLM-based refinement, and iterative cycling until objectives are met or budget exhausted.

- **Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR):** (Figure 2a)
 - **Idea Generation:** The LLM first generates multiple high-level architectural concepts (ideas $I = \{I_1, \ldots, I_k\}$), leveraging its intrinsic knowledge, optionally augmented by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) from external papers. Ideas are conditioned on the problem description and previous ideas to ensure diversity.
 - **Sequential Refinement:** Each idea $I_k$ initiates an independent refinement chain ($C_k$). A root node is created by implementing $I_k$. Subsequent steps involve an Aider-style coder generating child code by applying differential edits to the parent code based on feedback. This diff-based approach reduces LLM token consumption and maintains consistent code states, aiding sequential debugging. IGR is employed for global architectural exploration to escape local optima in PPA optimization.

- **Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS):** (Figure 2b)
 - **Tree Structure:** MCTS organizes candidate nodes in a search tree where the root is initialized with LLM-generated Verilog. Each node tracks a visit count $C(N)$ and a cumulative quality value $Q(N)$.
 - **Selection:** The algorithm recursively traverses the tree from the root, selecting child nodes using a variant of Upper Confidence Bound for Trees (UCT). Unexplored children are prioritized to encourage exploration.
 - **Code Generation and Evaluation:** At a selected leaf node, the LLM generates new code, incorporating a design summary and the parent's code into the prompt. This new code is immediately evaluated against the testbench to obtain a quality score.
 - **Expansion and Backpropagation:** A new node is created from the generated code and score, becoming a child of the leaf node. The score is then backpropagated up the tree, updating the visit counts and quality values of all ancestor nodes. MCTS is chosen for precise exploitation to rigorously resolve local functional and timing constraints during Verilog code generation.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Mechanism: Structured Testbench Generation (STG)

To address the limitations of sparse input stimulus and binary feedback in existing benchmarks, EvolVE introduces STG as a deterministic verification engine providing high-fidelity, dense rewards (Figure 2).

- **Phase 1: Automated Signal Classification:** STG parses the Design Under Test (DUT) port interface using regular expressions to categorize signals (Clock/Reset, Control, Datapath) based on industry naming conventions. This ensures fast, deterministic testbench instantiation without LLM inference.
- **Phase 2: Scalable Hybrid Stimulus Generation:** STG employs a width-constrained strategy. For narrow control signals ($\le 8$ bits), it generates exhaustive $2^w$ state-space coverage. For wider control buses or datapath buses, it uses constrained-random sampling with seeded corner cases (e.g., zero, max-value). Testbenches adhere to IEEE 2005 Verilog, compatible with both open-source (Icarus Verilog) and commercial simulators (VCS).
- **Phase 3: Fine-Grained Functional Gradient:** STG enforces strict temporal alignment between DUT and golden reference models. It calculates a continuous fine-grained correctness score $P_{stg} \in$ (pass rate across test vectors). This acts as a functional gradient, allowing the search engine to prioritize partially correct candidates and accelerate convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The EvolVE framework demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across Verilog code generation and PPA optimization tasks, utilizing both open-source and proprietary LLMs.

#### 4.1. Verilog Code Generation

- **Performance Comparison:** EvolVE consistently augments the capabilities of underlying LLMs.
 - The **DeepSeek-R1-FP4-MCTS** configuration achieved the highest pass rates: 98.1% on VerilogEval v2 and 92.0% on RTLLM v2 (at 300 nodes). This significantly surpasses previous SOTA frameworks like REvolution (95.5% with Deepseek-V3) and VerilogCoder (94.2% with GPT-4-Turbo).
 - Even smaller models like **Siliconmind-7B-MCTS** saw substantial boosts, reaching 92.3% on VerilogEval v2 (from a baseline of 82.1%).
 - For proprietary models like **Gemini-2.0-Flash**, EvolVE unlocked latent reasoning capabilities, improving its VerilogEval v2 performance from 80.1% to 87.8% (with MCTS).
 - MCTS generally yielded higher stability and superior peak performance compared to IGR across most architectures for code generation.

- **Scalability and Computational Budget:** (Figure 3)
 - The MCTS approach consistently extracted performance gains as computational nodes increased, unlike standard baselines that plateau quickly.
 - For highly capable models (DeepSeek-R1-FP4), the search was sample-efficient, resolving 84.6% of problems at the first node and requiring an average of only 2.49 nodes per solution.
 - For models with lower baseline pass rates (Siliconmind-7B), MCTS effectively utilized expanded budgets to solve harder problems, showing a monotonic increase from 85.3% (15 nodes) to 92.9% (300 nodes), demonstrating active error correction beyond mere rejection sampling.

- **Efficacy of Structured Testbench Generation (STG):** (Figure 4)
 - While STG-enhanced configurations maintained performance parity at the upper bound (300 nodes), its decisive advantage was in **convergence efficiency**.
 - STG significantly lowered computational overhead: for DeepSeek-R1-FP4, the average node count to resolve Mod-VerilogEval v2 dropped from 4.78 to 2.49.
 - Total output token consumption decreased by over 60% (from 38,818 to 15,440 tokens for DeepSeek-R1-FP4). This confirms STG optimizes the search trajectory, achieving target performance with substantially reduced latency and inference cost.

#### 4.2. PPA Optimization

- **IC-RTL Benchmark:** EvolVE introduces IC-RTL, a novel, high-complexity benchmark suite derived from industry-scale problems and national contests, designed to rigorously evaluate PPA optimization capabilities. The designs are compatible with both open-source and commercial EDA tools.
- **PPA Performance Analysis:** (Figure 5) Using the DeepSeek-R1-FP4 model with IGR, EvolVE demonstrated consistent optimization gains across diverse architectural patterns on IC-RTL, relative to human-authored baselines.
 - **Q2_GEMM (Systolic Array):** Achieved a 12% reduction in Latency with neutral Area and lower Power, by rescheduling control logic to maximize data reuse.
 - **Q4_JAM (Job Assignment Machine):** Significant reductions in both Area (31%) and Power (26%) without sacrificing latency, leading to a 36% composite PPA improvement.
 - **Q5_HC (Huffman Coding):** Delivered the most impressive gain, with a 66% peak PPA improvement, driven by simultaneous reductions in Area (30%) and Power (25%) through restructuring complex decision trees and sorting networks.
 - Across all problems, the framework achieved a **17% reduction in the geometric mean of the PPA product**.
- **Oriented Design Space Exploration:** (Figure 6) EvolVE showcased its ability to steer optimization trajectories toward specific constraints (Area-Centric vs. Latency-Centric) by injecting targeted prompts.
 - For Q5_HC, "Opt Area" achieved a 31% area reduction without degrading performance, while "Opt Cycle" achieved a 26% latency reduction (with a necessary area penalty). This confirms the framework can effectively navigate classic Area-Delay trade-offs and populate the Pareto frontier.
- **Architectural Evolution in GEMM (Ablation Study):** The framework not only performed micro-architectural refinements (e.g., optimizing data buffering from 2n-1 to n rows, eliminating redundant registers) but also demonstrated "Novel Architectural Discovery." It autonomously evolved the design from a pure output-stationary systolic array to a weight-output stationary hybrid, reducing latency from 1280ns to 776ns. This validates EvolVE's capability to discover superior high-level topologies beyond local RTL fixes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvolVE framework represents a significant advancement in the field of LLM-aided hardware design, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

1. **New State-of-the-Art in Verilog Generation and Optimization:** By achieving 98.1% on VerilogEval v2 and 92% on RTLLM v2, EvolVE sets a new benchmark for LLM-based Verilog generation. More importantly, its ability to significantly reduce PPA (up to 66% and a 17% geometric mean improvement on IC-RTL) demonstrates its practical utility for hardware optimization, surpassing even human-authored reference designs.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between LLMs and Hardware Concurrency:** EvolVE successfully addresses the intrinsic challenge of LLMs struggling with the formal logic and concurrency of hardware. By abstracting design as an evolutionary search problem and providing fine-grained feedback, it allows LLMs to iteratively self-correct and explore design spaces that are typically outside their sequential reasoning capabilities. This represents a paradigm shift from LLMs as passive code generators to active, self-improving design agents.

3. **Validation of Evolutionary Search Strategies:** The paper provides the first comprehensive study comparing Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR) for chip design tasks. The finding that MCTS excels at functional correctness while IGR is superior for PPA optimization offers crucial guidance for future research and framework development in this area.

4. **Enhanced Efficiency through Structured Testbench Generation (STG):** STG's ability to provide high-fidelity, dense rewards (fine-grained functional gradients) dramatically accelerates the evolutionary process and reduces computational overhead. This is vital for making iterative LLM-based design practical, as it transfers the heavy computational cost of debugging from expensive LLM reasoning to efficient EDA simulation tools.

5. **Introduction of Industry-Scale Benchmarks:** The creation of Mod-VerilogEval v2 and the high-complexity IC-RTL benchmark suite is a crucial contribution. IC-RTL, derived from national contests and targeting industry-scale problems, directly addresses the scarcity of rigorous, complex benchmarks capable of evaluating true hardware optimization capabilities beyond simple functional pass rates. This will drive more meaningful research and development in the field.

6. **Model-Agnostic Framework:** EvolVE's effectiveness across various LLM scales, from small open-source models (Siliconmind-7B) to larger proprietary ones (DeepSeek-R1-FP4, Gemini-2.0-Flash), highlights its robustness and generalizability. This broad applicability democratizes access to advanced LLM-aided design capabilities.

7. **Potential for Architectural Discovery:** The demonstrated ability of EvolVE to autonomously evolve designs from one micro-architecture to a novel hybrid (e.g., in the GEMM case study) indicates a profound potential for the framework to go beyond incremental RTL fixes and discover genuinely superior high-level topologies. This could revolutionize the initial stages of hardware architecture exploration.

Despite these significant advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations, such as STG's reliance on existing golden reference models and the scarcity of PPA optimization benchmarks. Future work aims to integrate formal knowledge bases of microarchitectural patterns and optimize MCTS for PPA directly.

In conclusion, EvolVE pushes the boundaries of automated hardware design, transforming LLMs from mere code suggestors into powerful, iterative design and optimization agents. Its impact could fundamentally shift the IC industry by making RTL design a more automated, systematic, and efficient discipline, thereby accelerating innovation in digital hardware development.
