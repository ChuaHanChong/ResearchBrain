# EvolVE: Evolutionary Search for LLM-based Verilog Generation and Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> EvolVE, an evolutionary search framework developed by researchers at National Taiwan University and Academia Sinica, enhances large language models (LLMs) for Verilog generation and optimization. It achieves a 98.1% pass rate on VerilogEval v2 for functional correctness and improves Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) by a geometric mean of 17% on the new IC-RTL benchmark.

## Problem

- Digital hardware design using Verilog is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and requires extensive domain expertise, creating bottlenecks in chip development.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to accurately capture the formal logic and massive concurrency of hardware systems, often generating functionally incorrect or non-synthesizable Verilog code.
- Existing benchmarks for LLM-aided hardware design lack industrial-scale complexity and do not sufficiently evaluate Power, Performance, and Area (PPA) optimization, hindering meaningful progress.

## Method

- **EvolVE** employs an evolutionary search framework that leverages test-time computation and two distinct strategies: ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== for functional correctness and ==Idea-Guided Refinement (IGR)== for PPA optimization.
- A novel ==Structured Testbench Generation (STG)== mechanism provides high-fidelity, fine-grained functional gradients as feedback, accelerating debugging and guiding the iterative search process.
- The framework formulates Verilog development as a search optimization problem, allowing LLMs to iteratively refine generated code based on functional and PPA metrics.

## Results

- EvolVE achieved a state-of-the-art **98.1%** pass rate on VerilogEval v2 and **92.0%** on RTLLM v2 using DeepSeek-R1-FP4-MCTS, surpassing previous frameworks like REvolution and VerilogCoder.
- The framework demonstrated a geometric mean PPA improvement of **17%** over human-authored baselines on the new IC-RTL benchmark, with up to a **66%** peak PPA improvement for the Huffman Coding problem.
- Structured Testbench Generation (STG) reduced the average node count to resolve problems from 4.78 to 2.49 and decreased total output token consumption by over 60% for DeepSeek-R1-FP4 on Mod-VerilogEval v2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative, search-based approach using evolutionary algorithms allows LLMs to overcome their inherent limitations in understanding hardware concurrency and scarcity of domain-specific training data.
> - Providing fine-grained, deterministic feedback via a structured testbench significantly improves the efficiency and convergence speed of LLM-based evolutionary search, reducing computational overhead.
> - Different evolutionary search strategies are optimal for distinct design goals: MCTS excels at resolving functional correctness issues, while IGR proves more effective for global architectural exploration and PPA optimization.
