# One Model for All Tasks: Leveraging Efficient World Models in Multi-Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ScaleZero framework, integrating a Mixture-of-Experts backbone and Dynamic Parameter Scaling, addresses representational bottlenecks and inefficient resource allocation in multi-task planning with world models. It achieved competitive performance across diverse Atari, DeepMind Control Suite, and text-based Jericho tasks, while reducing data sampling costs by 28.5% on the DMC18 benchmark.

## Problem

- Unified world models suffer from 'representational bottlenecks and plasticity collapse' in heterogeneous multitask settings, where simpler tasks' gradients interfere with complex tasks' learning.
- Existing multi-task learning approaches use 'static resource allocation,' inefficiently applying uniform computational resources to tasks regardless of their individual learning progress or difficulty.

## Method

- The **ScaleZero** architecture employs a ViT-like encoder and a ==sparse Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) backbone to mitigate gradient conflicts and task interference by routing computations to specialized sub-networks.
- Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS) is an online strategy that adaptively curates active tasks and progressively expands model capacity using LoRA adapters, optimizing resource allocation based on task-specific learning progress.

## Results

- ScaleZero achieved a mean Human-Normalized Score of **0.39** on the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games), outperforming a multi-task UniZero baseline (**0.31**).
- On the DeepMind Control Suite (18 tasks), ScaleZero demonstrated robust generalist performance with a mean score of 769.7 and a median of 887.3, competitive with independently trained single-task agents.
- Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS) reduced environment interactions by **28.5%** on the DMC18 benchmark compared to standard ScaleZero, achieving comparable performance with fewer computational resources.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing dense MLP layers with MoE counterparts effectively reduces inter-task gradient conflicts, fostering expert specialization within the shared model and preserving representational plasticity.
> - Dynamic Parameter Scaling enhances efficiency by preserving foundational knowledge in early network layers while applying targeted plasticity to later layers for unsolved tasks.
> - The theoretical analysis demonstrates that MoE layers maintain a lower upper bound on gradient conflict compared to dense networks, especially as expert routing becomes more specialized.
