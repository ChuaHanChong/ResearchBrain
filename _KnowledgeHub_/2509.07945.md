# One Model for All Tasks: Leveraging Efficient World Models in Multi-Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ScaleZero framework, integrating a Mixture-of-Experts backbone and Dynamic Parameter Scaling, addresses representational bottlenecks and inefficient resource allocation in multi-task planning with world models. It achieved competitive performance across diverse Atari, DeepMind Control Suite, and text-based Jericho tasks, while reducing data sampling costs by 28.5% on the DMC18 benchmark.

## Problem

- Unified world models suffer from 'representational bottlenecks and plasticity collapse' in heterogeneous multitask settings, where simpler tasks' gradients interfere with complex tasks' learning.
- Existing multi-task learning approaches use 'static resource allocation,' inefficiently applying uniform computational resources to tasks regardless of their individual learning progress or difficulty.

## Method

- The **ScaleZero** architecture employs a ViT-like encoder and a ==sparse Mixture-of-Experts== (MoE) backbone to mitigate gradient conflicts and task interference by routing computations to specialized sub-networks.
- Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS) is an online strategy that adaptively curates active tasks and progressively expands model capacity using LoRA adapters, optimizing resource allocation based on task-specific learning progress.

## Results

- ScaleZero achieved a mean Human-Normalized Score of **0.39** on the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games), outperforming a multi-task UniZero baseline (**0.31**).
- On the DeepMind Control Suite (18 tasks), ScaleZero demonstrated robust generalist performance with a mean score of 769.7 and a median of 887.3, competitive with independently trained single-task agents.
- Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS) reduced environment interactions by **28.5%** on the DMC18 benchmark compared to standard ScaleZero, achieving comparable performance with fewer computational resources.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing dense MLP layers with MoE counterparts effectively reduces inter-task gradient conflicts, fostering expert specialization within the shared model and preserving representational plasticity.
> - Dynamic Parameter Scaling enhances efficiency by preserving foundational knowledge in early network layers while applying targeted plasticity to later layers for unsolved tasks.
> - The theoretical analysis demonstrates that MoE layers maintain a lower upper bound on gradient conflict compared to dense networks, especially as expert routing becomes more specialized.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses challenges in developing generalist agents capable of performing effectively across a diverse range of tasks, particularly within the paradigm of multi-task reinforcement learning (MTRL) using unified world models. Existing unified world models, such as UniZero, have demonstrated proficiency in single-task environments by learning a shared latent space representation that facilitates planning with algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS).

However, extending these models to heterogeneous multitask settings—where tasks exhibit diverse observation/action spaces and varying complexities—encounters significant obstacles. Prior work in MTRL has focused on mitigating inter-task interference, primarily in supervised learning or model-free RL contexts, leaving the dynamics of multitask planning within shared world models largely underexplored.

This paper identifies two specific, intertwined problems: "representational bottlenecks and plasticity collapse" and "static resource allocation." Representational bottlenecks refer to the shared model's susceptibility to gradient dominance from simpler tasks, leading to interference, suppressed learning signals for complex tasks, and a loss of network plasticity. Static resource allocation describes the inefficiency of applying uniform learning resources to all tasks regardless of their individual difficulty, wasting computation on converged tasks.

The work builds upon and extends previous research in MCTS with learned latent space models (e.g., MuZero, Transformer-based world models), multi-task reinforcement learning methods, and sparse/parameter-efficient architectures like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). It diverges from existing MoE and LoRA applications by adapting them to non-stationary online data streams and dynamically allocating parameter space in response to learning progress, rather than relying on predefined task boundaries or static fine-tuning. By addressing the unique challenges of plasticity collapse and static resource allocation in online multitask planning, this research contributes to the development of more capable and efficient generalist agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a unified world model capable of effectively handling heterogeneous multitask decision-making while overcoming limitations observed in existing architectures. The motivation stems from two critical, interconnected challenges identified in contemporary unified world models when applied to diverse multitask settings:

1. **Representational Bottlenecks and Plasticity Collapse:**
 - **Problem Description:** In environments with diverse tasks, a shared model can suffer from "gradient dominance," where simpler, faster-converging tasks produce stronger gradients that overshadow and interfere with the learning signals for more complex tasks. This leads to "representation interference," causing the model's ability to adjust its parameters (plasticity) to diminish progressively. The consequence is a "performance collapse" on challenging tasks, as the network becomes unable to adapt to new information.
 - **Empirical Diagnosis:** The authors quantitatively diagnose this issue using UniZero on a suite of eight Atari games. While UniZero rapidly masters simple tasks like Pong, it exhibits initial progress followed by catastrophic performance collapse on complex games such as Seaquest. This external performance degradation is correlated with internal model dynamics: an uncontrolled inflation of the latent state norm and a significant increase in the dormant neuron ratio within the transformer backbone, signaling a loss of model plasticity. A decline in the feature effective rank further corroborates this representational collapse.
 - **Motivation for Solution:** The observed plasticity collapse directly motivates the need for an internal, architectural solution to mitigate interference within a single learning iteration, ensuring the model can maintain diverse features and adapt to varied task demands.

2. **Static Resource Allocation:**
 - **Problem Description:** Traditional architectures employ a uniform, one-size-fits-all approach to learning, applying the same computational resources (e.g., data collection, model updates) to every task. This static strategy is inefficient because resources are "squandered" on tasks that have already converged or are showing diminishing returns, instead of being directed towards tasks that still require substantial learning.
 - **Motivation for Solution:** The inefficiency of static resource allocation motivates the development of an external, procedural strategy that can dynamically allocate model capacity and computational effort throughout the learning process. This aims to optimize resource utilization by coupling model capacity to task-specific learning progress.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a two-pronged approach: the architectural design of **ScaleZero** to mitigate gradient conflicts and plasticity collapse, and the procedural strategy of **Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS)** to optimize resource allocation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured around diagnosing the identified problems and developing two complementary solutions: an architectural modification (ScaleZero) and a dynamic training strategy (Dynamic Parameter Scaling, DPS).

#### 3.1. Diagnosing Plasticity Collapse

The research quantitatively investigates plasticity collapse by tracking two metrics during multitask training of UniZero on eight Atari games:
- **Dormant Ratio:** The proportion of neurons whose activation magnitude falls below a threshold, indicating a loss of active pathways and reduced plasticity.
- **Latent State Norm:** The average L2 norm of the latent state, where uncontrolled inflation is a known indicator of training instability.
The diagnosis reveals that performance collapse on complex tasks correlates with a sharp increase in dormant neuron ratio and inflated latent state norm, alongside a decline in feature effective rank, indicating representational collapse. This failure is attributed to gradient competition and representation interference.

#### 3.2. Architecture Design of ScaleZero via Principled Exploration

To address plasticity collapse, the authors conduct a systematic exploration of the UniZero design space along five axes on the Atari8 multitask benchmark:
- **Explicit Task Conditioning:** Concatenating a learnable task embedding to the latent state. This showed marginal long-term benefits for complex tasks and did not mitigate plasticity collapse, suggesting input-level conditioning is insufficient.
- **Encoder Architecture (ResNet vs. ViT):** Comparing a ResNet-like encoder with a Vision Transformer (ViT). No significant performance difference was found on the Atari8 benchmark, but ViT’s scalability was posited as critical for broader domains. ScaleZero adopts a ViT-like encoder trained from scratch.
- **Latent Normalization (LayerNorm vs. SimNorm):** Evaluating SimNorm against standard LayerNorm to combat latent norm inflation. SimNorm stabilized training but curtailed representational expressiveness, leading to suboptimal final performance in diverse settings. ScaleZero uses LayerNorm.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Backbone:** Replacing dense feed-forward networks in UniZero’s Transformer with sparse MoE layers. This yielded significant performance gains and maintained healthier latent dynamics (lower dormant ratios, stable latent norms). The MoE layer consists of eight specialized expert networks and one shared expert, governed by a sparse top-1 routing gate. This approach directly mitigates representational interference and gradient conflict by routing task-specific computations to distinct sub-networks.
- **Multitask Gradient Correction (MoCo):** Exploring a dynamic gradient re-weighting scheme. This introduced significant computational overhead (∼40% increase per step) and showed inconsistent efficacy across heterogeneous tasks. Its investigation was deferred.

**The ScaleZero Architecture:** Based on this exploration, ScaleZero integrates:
1. A **ViT-like Encoder** for robust feature extraction.
2. An **MoE Backbone** using sparse, conditional computation (one shared expert + eight routed experts with top-1 gating) to increase model capacity and mitigate task interference.
3. **Layer Normalization** for latent states to balance stability and expressiveness.
It also features a modular design with decoupled task-specific encoders (MLP for DMC, text encoder for Jericho) and prediction heads for diverse input/output modalities.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS) for Efficient Multitask Learning

To resolve the static resource allocation issue, DPS is introduced as an online procedural strategy with two synergistic principles:
- **Adaptive Task Curation:** Tasks are deemed "solved" when their performance metric surpasses a predefined threshold. Training (data collection and gradient updates) is then performed only for tasks in the active set of unsolved tasks. Solved tasks are removed, ceasing associated computational overhead.
- **Progressive Capacity Expansion:** Training proceeds in stages. Stage 0 (Warm-up) trains the base model (θ_B) on all tasks. Subsequent stages (s ≥ 1, Expansion) progressively incorporate new, independent LoRA adapters (Δθ_s = B_s A_s). Learnable scaling factors (α_0 for the base model, α_j for each Δθ_j) modulate contributions.
- **Adaptive Stage Transition Triggers:** A stage transition is triggered by either:
 - **Progress-based Trigger:** A quota of newly solved tasks (Q_s) is met.
 - **Budget-based Trigger:** The iteration count in the current stage exceeds a pre-allocated limit (⌈(T_max - T_0)/S⌉).
- **Optimization and Parameter Isolation:** In stage s ≥ 1, the base model (θ_B) and all previous adapters (Δθ_j for j < s) are frozen. Optimization focuses solely on the newly added LoRA module (Δθ_s, with its α_s fixed at 1), the base model scaling factor (α_0), and the scaling factors of previously frozen adapters ({α_j} for j < s). Scaling factors α_j are constrained within a stable range using a re-parameterization trick to prevent instability.

#### 3.4. Training Framework

- **Online Reinforcement Learning:** All experiments are conducted in a purely online RL setting, without expert data.
- **MCTS for Planning:** The agent uses MCTS within its learned latent world model to generate improved policies for action selection and as targets for policy distillation.
- **Composite Loss Function:** The model is optimized using a joint loss function (Equation 1 in paper) combining next-latent prediction, reward prediction, policy prediction, and value prediction losses.
- **Distributed Training:** A multi-GPU, DDP-based framework is used, with static task partitioning, heterogeneous batch construction, gradient accumulation, and synchronized global optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The results validate the effectiveness of ScaleZero in mitigating representational bottlenecks and plasticity collapse, and the efficiency of DPS in optimizing resource allocation.

#### 4.1. Multitask Learning Benchmarks: Atari 100k Benchmark (26 Games)

- **Comparison:** A single multitask (MT) ScaleZero agent was compared against 26 independently trained single-task (ST) UniZero agents.
- **Results:** ScaleZero achieved a higher mean Human-Normalized Score (HNS) (0.39) compared to UniZero (ST) (0.38) and UniZero (MT) (0.31). The median HNS for ScaleZero (0.16) was marginally lower than UniZero (ST) (0.21) but superior to UniZero (MT) (0.16).
- **Interpretation:** ScaleZero effectively mitigates negative interference and facilitates positive knowledge transfer, particularly on difficult exploration tasks like Seaquest. The higher mean score indicates an overall advantage and generalization ability across the diverse suite.

#### 4.1.2. DeepMind Control Suite (18 Tasks)

- **Comparison:** One MT ScaleZero agent was compared against 18 individually trained ST UniZero models.
- **Results:** ScaleZero achieved competitive performance with a mean score of 769.7 and a superior median score of 887.3, compared to UniZero (ST) with a mean of 787.2 and a median of 875.1.
- **Interpretation:** ScaleZero's architectural principles are effective for complex, state-based continuous control problems, demonstrating robust generalist performance.

#### 4.1.3. Jericho: Text-Based Adventure Benchmark (4 Tasks)

- **Comparison:** ScaleZero (MT) was compared against UniZero (ST) and CALM+OC (a language-model-based method) on four tasks (Acorncourt, Omniquest, Detective, Zork1).
- **Results:** ScaleZero achieved performance on par with specialized single-task agents and was competitive with the strong CALM+OC baseline (e.g., 10 on Acorncourt, 10 on Omniquest, 280 on Detective, 44 on Zork1).
- **Interpretation:** The design of ScaleZero is modality-agnostic, extending its efficacy to text-based planning environments requiring language understanding and long-horizon planning.

#### 4.2. Efficiency Evaluation of Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS)

- **Comparison:** Standard ScaleZero was compared against ScaleZero-DPS on the DMC18 benchmark.
- **Results:** ScaleZero-DPS achieved comparable performance while using 71.5% of the environment interactions required by the standard ScaleZero, representing a 28.5% reduction in data sampling and training cost.
- **Interpretation:** DPS effectively resolves the static resource allocation problem by dynamically aligning model capacity with learning progress, leading to substantial sample efficiency gains. A mechanistic analysis revealed that DPS preserves foundational knowledge in early layers while applying targeted plasticity in later layers for unsolved tasks.

#### 4.3. Understanding MoE Efficacy: Empirical and Theoretical Analysis

- **Empirical Findings:**
 - **Gradient Conflict Reduction:** Replacing dense MLP layers with MoE counterparts reduced gradient conflict not only within the MoE layers but also in adjacent networks. The shared expert within MoE layers exhibited significantly higher gradient conflicts than task-specific experts, indicating that the bulk of conflicts are concentrated in shared components, while task-specific experts remain relatively isolated.
 - **Expert Specialization:** Analysis of expert selection entropy showed a gradual decrease as tasks progressed, indicating increasing specialization of experts over time. Wasserstein distance between expert selection distributions also provided evidence of progressive specialization.
- **Theoretical Justification (Informal Theorem 5.1):**
 - An MoE layer maintains a strictly lower upper bound on gradient conflict compared to its dense counterpart. This property is primarily governed by the router's specialization.
 - In the exploration stage, with uniform expert selection, the expected gradient conflict is bounded based on the number of experts and tasks.
 - In the router learning stage, where expert sets stabilize, the gradient conflict is roughly upper bounded by a term related to the intersection size of expert sets for conflicting tasks (E[conflict_i,j] ≈ G * U/(ab) + O(G/d), where U is intersection size, a,b are expert set sizes).
- **Interpretation:** The MoE architecture mitigates gradient conflicts by routing task-specific computations to distinct, specialized expert sub-networks, thereby preserving representational plasticity. The theoretical analysis supports that more orthogonal routing coefficients tend to yield fewer gradient conflicts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing identified limitations in multitask world models, particularly concerning plasticity collapse and resource allocation.

The **ScaleZero** architecture demonstrates that integrating a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) backbone can effectively mitigate gradient conflicts and representational interference in heterogeneous multitask environments. Its robust performance across 48 diverse tasks from Atari, DeepMind Control Suite, and Jericho benchmarks, often matching or surpassing specialized single-task agents, indicates a significant step towards developing generalist agents with enhanced generalization and knowledge transfer capabilities. This suggests that MoE-based designs can provide a more scalable and adaptable architectural foundation for learning complex dynamics across multiple domains.

The **Dynamic Parameter Scaling (DPS)** strategy introduces a novel online mechanism for efficiently allocating model capacity. By adaptively curating active tasks and progressively expanding model capacity through LoRA adapters, DPS achieves substantial reductions in environment interactions (28.5% on DMC18) while maintaining competitive performance. This contributes to the development of more sample-efficient and computationally economical learning paradigms, which is crucial for real-world applications where data collection and training are costly.

The detailed empirical and theoretical analysis of MoE efficacy, explaining how it reduces gradient conflicts and fosters expert specialization, provides insights into the mechanisms underlying its success in multitask settings. This understanding can inform future architectural designs for MTRL.

The work lays a foundation for future research into more capable and efficient generalist agents, particularly in:
- Generalizing DPS to diverse and multi-modal domains (e.g., unified training across Atari, DMC, and Jericho).
- Deepening the synergy between MoE and LoRA for fine-grained, context-aware architectural adaptation.
- Integrating large-scale offline pre-training to improve sample efficiency and "cold start" performance, bridging online RL with foundation models.

Overall, this research offers concrete architectural and procedural solutions for overcoming critical challenges in multitask learning with world models, propelling progress toward robust and scalable generalist AI.
