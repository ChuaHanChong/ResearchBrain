# RayViT: Ray-Conditioned Visual Representations for Viewpoint-Robust Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> RayViT injects camera geometry into pretrained ViT encoders for imitation learning. It patchifies a Plücker ray map into ray features, uses gated cross-attention to build a ray-conditioned class token replacing the ViT CLS token, adds ray features as dense positional embeddings, and applies a cross-view cosine consistency loss. Under camera perturbations it beats RGB baselines by roughly 13 points on RoboCasa and 1.78 completed stages in the real world.

## Problem

- RGB pretrained encoders capture appearance but not camera-to-world geometry, so imitation policies learn viewpoint-specific appearance-to-action mappings that collapse when cameras are moved.
- Prior robustness recipes rely on multi-view data augmentation or synthesized novel views, which is expensive to collect and hard to scale outside simulation.
- Explicit 3D inputs like point clouds or point maps need depth sensors and reconstruction, and remain tied to the depth camera's extrinsics.

## Method

- **RayViT**'s ==Plücker ray map==: per-pixel direction plus moment from intrinsics and extrinsics, average-pooled to patch resolution, projected by a ==two-layer GELU MLP== into patch-aligned ray features.
- ==Gated cross-attention== ray initializer: two blocks where a learnable query attends to ray features under ==sigmoid gating==, producing a ==ray-conditioned class token== that replaces the ViT CLS token.
- Ray positional embedding plus ==cross-view alignment==: ray features added to patch tokens before block one alongside untouched ==RoPE==; ==cosine loss== aligns layer-10 class tokens to the gripper-view anchor.

## Results

- RoboCasa 16-task multi-task: under perturbed cameras RayViT reaches **37.3%** and RayViT-cls **37.0%** versus RGB's **24.0%**, cutting the performance drop from **18.5** to **2.5** points.
- Real Franka, four tasks, 20 rollouts each: RayViT averages **2.69** completed stages under the perturbed camera versus RGB's **0.91**, drop **0.12** against **1.53**.
- Ablations: the cosine loss helps only geometry-aware tokens (RayViT-cls **30.6** to **37.0** variant), and gains persist from scratch (**29.2** vs **10.0**) and on the EUPE backbone (**33.3** vs **18.9**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Camera pose is a nearly free geometric cue: intrinsics and extrinsics alone give dense pixel-to-ray grounding, no depth sensor or reconstruction needed.
> - Auxiliary view-consistency losses pay off only once representations already carry geometry; applied to plain RGB tokens they yielded no stable gain.
> - Inject geometry inside the pretrained token stream rather than late-concatenating it; gating limits distribution disruption, though nominal in-distribution accuracy can dip slightly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Visual imitation learning enables robots to acquire visuomotor skills by observing demonstrations, with RGB images frequently used due to their semantic richness and ease of acquisition. Modern vision foundation models, such as DINOv3, process these images to extract relevant features, forming a basis for robot policies. However, policies derived from pure RGB observations often exhibit fragility when camera viewpoints change or are perturbed. This limitation arises because visual representations learned from RGB images encode scene appearance but typically lack explicit geometric information about the relationship between the camera and the world. Consequently, these policies tend to learn appearance-to-action mappings that are specific to particular viewpoints rather than generalizable, view-invariant task representations.

Addressing this issue requires visual representations that are both camera-aware and consistent across different viewpoints. Existing approaches in the field attempt to tackle this problem through several strategies:

- **Data Diversity:** Some methods collect extensive demonstration datasets from multiple cameras or diverse viewpoints to expose the policy to various visual conditions. While effective, this approach is often resource-intensive and challenging to scale in real-world robotic environments. Prior work, such as that by Jiang et al. (2025), has also conditioned policies on Plücker ray maps but still relied on instantiating numerous cameras as a form of dataset augmentation.
- **Explicit 3D Representations:** Another line of research integrates explicit 3D structures, such as point clouds, point maps, or lifted RGB-D features, into the policy input. These methods provide useful geometric grounding but typically require additional sensors (e.g., depth cameras) or complex reconstruction processes. Challenges associated with these approaches include unifying the density, noise characteristics, and physical scale of 3D points, as well as their often limited compatibility with vision foundation models that are primarily trained on pure image data. Examples include RVT (Goyal et al., 2023) which reconstructs point clouds, and Adapt3R (Wilcox et al., 2025) which localizes 2D features in 3D but needs calibrated depth.
- **View-Invariant Policy Learning:** Some methods specifically design policies to be invariant to viewpoint changes, but these often necessitate point-cloud inputs, limiting their direct integration with image-centric pretrained vision models.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing an alternative strategy that draws inspiration from advances in novel view synthesis and dynamic scene reconstruction. Instead of relying on expensive data augmentation or additional 3D sensors, it leverages Plücker ray embeddings to represent camera geometry. This approach allows for a dense, pixel-aligned encoding of viewing rays directly from intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters. The core innovation lies in a lightweight architectural design, RayViT, which injects these geometric cues into pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones. The paper aims to overcome the limitations of prior work by integrating camera geometry directly into the visual representation learning process, thereby fostering viewpoint-robustness without substantial increases in data collection or sensor complexity, while maintaining compatibility with powerful image-based foundation models.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust visual representation learning framework for imitation learning that specifically addresses the brittleness of robot policies to camera viewpoint perturbations. This robustness is crucial for deploying robotic systems in dynamic or uncontrolled environments where camera positions may change during operation due to various factors, such as mounting on moving parts of a robot or needing to be adjusted to avoid occlusions.

The specific objectives that underpin this research are:

1. **Enhance Viewpoint Robustness:** To enable robot policies to generalize effectively across different camera viewpoints, thereby reducing performance degradation when camera parameters shift between training and test times.
2. **Integrate Camera Geometry into Pretrained Vision Transformers:** To devise a method for injecting explicit camera geometry cues into existing, powerful pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones (e.g., DINOv3). This integration aims to provide geometric grounding to appearance-based features without disrupting the rich semantic knowledge already acquired during pretraining.
3. **Achieve View-Consistent Representations:** To encourage visual representations of the same scene or manipulation task, observed from different camera viewpoints, to be semantically aligned and consistent.
4. **Develop a Lightweight and Sensor-Agnostic Solution:** To achieve these objectives without requiring extensive multi-view data augmentation, which is costly and difficult to scale, or relying on additional 3D sensing modalities like depth cameras, which introduce their own complexities and hardware requirements.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges inherent in current visual imitation learning paradigms:

- **Sensitivity to Camera Shifts:** Policies trained purely on RGB images are highly susceptible to changes in camera pose. Since pretrained visual encoders typically focus on scene appearance, they fail to explicitly model the geometric relationship between the camera and the environment. This leads to policies learning mappings specific to the observation viewpoint, which break down when the viewpoint changes.
- **Limitations of Existing Solutions:** Current methods for improving viewpoint robustness have notable drawbacks. Data augmentation, while effective, is expensive and impractical for real-world scenarios. Approaches using explicit 3D representations often require additional sensors (e.g., depth cameras) or complex 3D reconstruction pipelines, which can introduce noise, density issues, and compatibility problems with advanced vision models.
- **Leveraging Emerging Geometric Cues:** Recent progress in novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction highlights the utility of Plücker ray embeddings as a compact and lightweight representation of camera geometry. These ray maps offer a direct pixel-to-pixel correspondence between appearance and geometry, computable solely from camera intrinsics and extrinsics (which are often readily available or easily calibrated).
- **Maintaining Pretrained Model Strengths:** A key challenge is to integrate geometric cues in a way that complements, rather than degrades, the powerful semantic features learned by large pretrained vision models. Naive concatenation of geometric features with image tokens can disrupt the pretrained representation distribution, necessitating a more integrated approach.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to pave the way for more robust and practical robot learning systems capable of operating reliably in diverse, real-world environments with varying camera setups.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named Ray-conditioned Vision Transformer Encoder (RayViT), focuses on injecting camera geometry into a pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone to create viewpoint-robust visual representations for imitation learning. The approach bypasses the need for multi-view augmentation or additional sensors by leveraging Plücker ray maps.

**Problem Formulation:**
The policy is learned from a dataset of expert demonstrations, where each observation `o_t` at time `t` includes an image `I_t` and a ray map `r_t`. The objective is to learn an encoder `E` that produces a ray-map-conditioned visual representation `v_t = E(I_t | r_t)`. This representation `v_t` then conditions a policy network, typically a score-based diffusion model with a decoder-only xLSTM action head, to predict actions `a_t`.

**Ray Map Modeling:**
Camera geometry is represented using Plücker coordinates, which associate each pixel `p = (u, v, 1)^T` with its corresponding 3D viewing ray in the world frame. This involves:
1. **Camera Parameters:** Utilizing the camera intrinsic matrix `K` and the world-to-camera extrinsic parameters (rotation `R` and translation `t`).
2. **Ray Calculation:** The camera center `o_w = -R^T t` and the normalized viewing direction `d_w(u, v) = R^T K^{-1} p / ||R^T K^{-1} p||_2` are computed for each pixel.
3. **Plücker Representation:** Each ray is then encoded by its direction `d_w(u, v)` and its spatial moment `m_w(u, v) = o_w × d_w(u, v)`. This results in a 6-dimensional vector `r(u, v)` for each pixel.
Stacking these `r(u, v)` vectors across all pixels forms a dense ray map that is spatially aligned with the input RGB image, providing a per-pixel geometric representation of the camera viewpoint.

**RayViT Architecture:**
RayViT introduces two mechanisms to inject camera geometry into a fine-tuned ViT encoder while largely preserving its pretrained structure:

1. **Ray Map Positional Embedding (Patch-aligned Ray Features):**
 - **Downsampling and Alignment:** The high-resolution ray map is downsampled to the ViT's patch resolution using average pooling, with a kernel size equal to the patch size. This non-parametric operation aims to maintain the geometric semantics of Plücker coordinates and ensures a one-to-one spatial alignment between the ray grid and the image patch tokens.
 - **Projection:** The 6-dimensional ray-patch features are projected to the encoder's latent dimension using a two-layer MLP with GELU activation.
 - **Additive Injection:** These projected ray features are added to the image patch tokens at the input of the encoder, *before* the first transformer block. This additive injection complements the existing Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) within the ViT. RoPE primarily provides local spatial reasoning, while the ray-based signal offers absolute 3D grounding for each patch, which is crucial for viewpoint awareness.

2. **Ray Map-conditioned Class Token:**
 - **Replacement of Standard CLS Token:** Instead of using the original learnable `[CLS]` token (which aggregates global visual information from image patches), RayViT introduces a ray-map-conditioned class token.
 - **Gated Cross-attention Module:** This module consists of two lightweight gated cross-attention blocks that match the hidden dimension of the ViT backbone. A learnable query token attends to the patch-aligned ray features.
 - **Geometric Conditioning Regulation:** A learnable element-wise sigmoid gate is applied after the attention mechanism. This gate regulates the extent of geometric conditioning, preventing the ray map information from overpowering or perturbing the pretrained semantic features the ViT expects in its subsequent layers.
 - **Geometry-Aware Summary:** The final output of this module, the conditioned query token, replaces the original `[CLS]` token in the ViT's forward pass, providing a geometry-aware global summary representation.

**Cross-view Alignment Objective:**
To further promote viewpoint-robustness, an auxiliary loss term is introduced to encourage consistency across different camera views.
- **Anchor View Selection:** The gripper camera view is designated as the reference anchor (`g`) for alignment, based on the assumption that it is typically closest to the manipulation target and contains less irrelevant background.
- **Cosine Similarity Loss:** For `N` camera views, a cosine similarity loss `L_cos` is applied to the class tokens extracted from an intermediate layer of the visual backbone. `L_cos = 1 - (1/(N-1)) * sum(cos(c_v, c_g))`, where `c_g` is the reference class token and `c_v` is a class token from another view.
- **Auxiliary Regularization:** This `L_cos` term is added as an auxiliary regularizer to the primary imitation learning loss `L_IL`, forming the total loss `L = L_IL + λ L_cos`, where `λ` is a scalar weight. The research indicates that this objective is effective primarily when applied to representations that already possess geometric information.

The entire system is trained end-to-end. The visual encoder (RayViT integrated with the pretrained ViT backbone) and the policy network are optimized jointly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RayViT was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

**Simulated Experiments (RoboCasa Benchmark):**
Experiments were conducted on 16 diverse manipulation tasks within the RoboCasa benchmark. A single policy was trained and evaluated across all tasks, and results were averaged over 3 random seeds. Robustness was assessed under both default camera configurations and variant configurations where static cameras were randomly rotated, shifted, and had altered azimuth and elevation ranges.

- **Performance under Default Settings:** RayViT-cls (ray-conditioned class token only) and RayViT (class token + patch embeddings) achieved success rates comparable to or slightly exceeding strong baselines, including Adapt3R, PMP-xyz, CamPose, and an RGB-only variant. RayViT-cls, for instance, showed a marginal lead over all baselines. This indicated that the geometry-conditioned visual representation effectively supported policy learning without significantly disrupting pretrained features.
- **Robustness to Camera Perturbations:** Under perturbed camera configurations, baseline methods exhibited substantial performance degradation. In contrast, RayViT methods showed considerably smaller performance drops. RayViT consistently outperformed the 2D RGB baseline by at least 13 percentage points in average success rate. They also surpassed methods relying on explicit 3D representations (e.g., PMP-xyz), suggesting that dense geometry alone is insufficient for robustness without explicit modeling of camera-induced transformations. Compared to Adapt3R, direct ray-map conditioning offered a stronger inductive bias without additional sensing. CamPose, which used late-fused ray-map conditioning, showed a modest performance drop but lower overall performance, suggesting that direct integration of geometry into visual representations is more effective than late fusion.
- **Ablation Study: Effect of Ray-Map Conditioning:** Injecting geometry alone (without the cross-view consistency loss) improved robustness to camera perturbations. However, it led to a minor reduction in performance under default settings, suggesting a potential perturbation of the pretrained feature distribution.
- **Ablation Study: Role of the View-Consistency Objective:** The auxiliary cosine similarity loss consistently improved performance in both default and variant settings. This objective particularly benefited RayViT-cls, while RayViT (with richer geometric information from patch embeddings) showed a smaller, though still positive, impact. Critically, applying this cosine similarity loss to the RGB-vanilla baseline did not yield stable performance gains, indicating that the effectiveness of the cross-view objective is contingent upon the representations already possessing geometric information.
- **Ablation Study: Layer-wise Application of Auxiliary Loss:** Applying the cross-view consistency loss at deeper layers (specifically layers 10-12) of the pretrained ViT backbone resulted in the most significant gains in both default performance and robustness. Applying it at earlier layers yielded only marginal improvements.
- **Ablation Study: Without Pretrained Weights:** When trained from scratch (without pretrained weights), RayViT methods still demonstrated an advantage over pure image representations, indicating that the benefits of geometry-aware representations are not solely reliant on pretrained visual features.
- **Ablation Study: Transferability Across Pretrained ViT Backbones:** Evaluating RayViT with the Efficient Universal Perception Encoder (EUPE) as an alternative backbone confirmed its generalizability. The methods remained effective, improving performance in both default and variant settings. Notably, with EUPE, RayViT (with both class token and patch embeddings) outperformed RayViT-cls, suggesting that richer geometric conditioning may be more beneficial under diverse pretrained feature distributions.

**Real Robot Experiments:**
RayViT was tested on four challenging real-world manipulation tasks: Drawer, Insertion, Pick Place, and Cup Stack. The setup included three cameras (left, right, gripper views) for the default environment, with a substantially perturbed right-side camera replacing the default one in the variant environment. Performance was measured by the average number of completed stages per task.

- **Performance and Robustness:** Both RayViT and RayViT-cls maintained strong performance in real-world settings. Under the default camera configuration, their performance was comparable to the RGB baseline. However, under the variant (perturbed camera) setting, RayViT methods demonstrated clear robustness advantages, improving the accumulated score by at least 0.8 points compared to the RGB baseline and exhibiting significantly greater resilience to camera perturbations.
- **RayViT vs. RayViT-cls in Real World:** RayViT (with both class token and patch embeddings) achieved the strongest performance in both default and variant real-world settings, surpassing RayViT-cls. This observation may be attributed to the higher resolution of real-world images compared to simulation, which results in longer image patch sequences, where richer patch-level geometric information could provide greater benefits.

In summary, the experimental results consistently demonstrate that RayViT significantly improves the robustness of imitation learning policies to camera perturbations in both simulated and real-world environments, without requiring multi-view data augmentation or additional 3D sensors. Both the ray-map conditioning mechanisms and the cross-view consistency objective were shown to contribute to these improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RayViT framework represents a notable advancement in addressing a critical challenge within visual imitation learning: the fragility of learned policies to camera viewpoint changes. This brittleness is a significant impediment to the widespread deployment of robots in real-world, less-controlled environments.

The significance of this work stems from several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Robustness for Real-World Deployment:** By explicitly incorporating camera geometry, RayViT significantly improves policy robustness to camera perturbations. This directly translates to more reliable robot operation in scenarios where camera positions might shift due to robot movement, environmental changes, or recalibration needs, thereby reducing the sim-to-real gap and enhancing the practicality of imitation learning for robotic tasks.
- **Lightweight and Sensor-Agnostic Solution:** A major advantage of RayViT is its ability to achieve this robustness without demanding costly multi-view data augmentation or additional, specialized 3D sensors like depth cameras. This makes the approach more resource-efficient, scalable, and compatible with existing camera setups, lowering the barrier to entry for robust robot learning. The reliance solely on camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which can be obtained through standard calibration, simplifies hardware requirements.
- **Effective Integration with Foundation Models:** The methodology successfully injects geometric cues into powerful, pretrained Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones (like DINOv3), preserving their rich semantic understanding while adding crucial geometric awareness. This demonstrates an effective strategy for combining the benefits of large-scale visual pretraining with explicit geometric grounding for robotics. The careful design using gated cross-attention and additive positional embeddings minimizes disruption to the pretrained feature distribution.
- **Novel Geometric Injection Mechanisms:** The introduction of a ray-map-conditioned class token and patch-aligned ray features as positional embeddings provides a novel and effective way to integrate camera geometry directly into the core components of a ViT. This goes beyond simple concatenation, allowing for a deeper interaction between appearance and geometric information.
- **Insights into Auxiliary Representation Learning:** The finding that the cross-view consistency objective is effective primarily when applied to geometry-aware representations offers a valuable insight. It suggests that establishing a proper geometric grounding might be a prerequisite for effectively aligning representations across different viewpoints, guiding future research in multi-view representation learning.

**Potential Impact:**

The capabilities demonstrated by RayViT could have a substantial impact across several domains:

- **Broader Adoption of Imitation Learning:** By making policies more robust to environmental variations, RayViT could accelerate the adoption of imitation learning for complex manipulation tasks in industrial, domestic, and service robotics.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** Eliminating the need for extensive multi-view data collection or specialized 3D sensors can significantly reduce the data collection and hardware costs associated with developing and deploying robot skills.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** The work contributes to the development of more embodied and intelligent robotic systems that possess a better understanding of their spatial relationship with the environment, moving beyond purely appearance-based reactive policies.
- **New Research Directions:** The limitations noted by the authors, such as extending the approach to convolutional backbones and pretraining geometry-conditioned encoders from scratch, point toward promising avenues for future research that could further solidify the benefits of geometric grounding in visual representations for robotics.

In conclusion, RayViT presents a practical and effective framework for enhancing the robustness of visual imitation learning, offering a significant step towards more reliable and deployable robotic systems in real-world scenarios.
