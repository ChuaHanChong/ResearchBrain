# MMRL: Multi-Modal Representation Learning for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The MMRL framework from Fudan University introduces a multi-modal representation learning and decoupled inference strategy to efficiently adapt Vision-Language Models for downstream tasks. It improved average harmonic mean accuracy by 1.33% over the prior state-of-the-art in base-to-novel generalization and achieved 72.03% average accuracy in cross-dataset evaluation.

## Problem

- Existing VLM adaptation techniques struggle with overfitting in few-shot settings, compromising generalization to unseen data.
- Fine-tuning large VLMs is computationally expensive and can interfere with their foundational, generalized knowledge.
- Current methods often focus solely on optimizing class token features, which limits their ability to balance task-specific adaptation and broader generalization.

## Method

- Establishes a ==shared, modality-agnostic learnable representation space==, with tokens integrated into the ==higher layers== of image and text encoders to learn dataset-specific features while preserving generalized knowledge in lower layers.
- Employs a ==decoupled learning approach== to optimize distinct class features (for generalization) and representation features (for task-specific adaptation), guided by a regularization term.
- Implements a novel ==decoupled inference strategy== that combines both feature types for base classes but uses only generalized class features for novel class predictions.

## Results

- Achieved average gains of **1.33%** in Harmonic Mean over prior SOTA (MMA) for base-to-novel generalization across **11** datasets and few-shot settings (1-16 shots), with margins increasing with more shots.
- Attained the highest average accuracy of **72.03%** in cross-dataset evaluation when trained on ImageNet and evaluated on **10** unseen datasets, surpassing MMA by **1.03%**.
- Ablation studies validated the importance of multimodal interaction, the decoupled learning and inference strategies, and the strategic integration of tokens into higher encoder layers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM lower layers retain generalized knowledge, while higher layers are better for adapting to dataset-specific features.
> - Decoupling task-specific representation learning from generalizable class features, reinforced by cosine similarity regularization to keep task-adapted features aligned with the VLM's original general knowledge, mitigates overfitting and preserves generalization.
> - A shared, modality-agnostic representation space promotes balanced multimodal interaction and prevents bias towards a single modality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP, which have become foundational for transfer learning across diverse tasks due to their ability to learn rich, complementary representations from vast amounts of image-text data. These pre-trained VLMs excel at capturing general visual-textual relationships. However, a significant challenge arises when adapting these large models to specific downstream tasks, especially in **few-shot learning settings** where training data is limited. Fine-tuning the entire VLM is computationally expensive and prone to **overfitting**, which can severely diminish the model's inherent generalization capabilities to new or unseen classes.

The paper positions MMRL as an advancement over existing efficient transfer learning strategies, which broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Prompt Learning:** Methods like CoOp, CoCoOp, and MaPLe learn continuous, trainable vectors (prompts) that are prepended or integrated into the VLM's input or intermediate layers. While more efficient than full fine-tuning, earlier methods often focused solely on text prompts, potentially being sub-optimal. Multi-modal prompt learning (e.g., MaPLe) attempts to address this by coupling visual and text prompts. However, the paper points out limitations, such as applying prompts at shallow layers potentially compromising generalizable knowledge and being overly text-centric.
2. **Adapter-Style Learning:** Methods like CLIP-Adapter and MMA introduce lightweight, trainable modules (adapters) within the frozen VLM architecture to adjust features for downstream tasks. MMA, for instance, introduced multimodal adapters and recognized that different VLM layers encode varying characteristics (higher layers for dataset-specific features, lower for generalizable ones).

MMRL identifies a critical gap in these existing approaches: they often solely optimize **class token features** using task-specific objectives (e.g., cross-entropy loss). This narrow focus makes them vulnerable to overfitting in few-shot settings, leading to a decline in the VLM's crucial generalization and zero-shot learning capabilities. MMRL aims to explicitly address this by introducing a **decoupled representation learning and inference strategy** that differentiates between task-specific and generalizable knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of MMRL is to develop a novel framework that enables **efficient and effective adaptation of large-scale pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to diverse downstream tasks**, particularly under few-shot data constraints, while simultaneously **preserving their inherent generalization capabilities** to new classes and domains.

The motivation stems from the limitations of current VLM adaptation techniques:
- **Overfitting in Few-Shot Settings:** Existing prompt learning and adapter-style methods, by focusing solely on optimizing class token features for specific tasks, tend to overfit to the limited training data. This compromises the VLM's ability to generalize to unseen data or novel classes, which is a core strength of large pre-trained models like CLIP.
- **Compromising Generalizable Knowledge:** Integrating prompts or adapters at shallow layers of the VLM encoders, as some methods do, can interfere with the model's foundational, generalized knowledge learned during large-scale pre-training.
- **Lack of Balanced Multi-Modal Interaction:** Previous multi-modal prompt learning methods might still be biased towards one modality (e.g., text-centric), failing to foster truly balanced and unbiased multi-modal learning.
- **Need for Decoupled Adaptation:** There is a need for a mechanism that can adapt to dataset-specific features without sacrificing the broader, transferable knowledge, and to leverage this distinction appropriately during inference.

In essence, MMRL seeks to achieve an **optimal balance between task-specific adaptation and generalization**, addressing the fundamental trade-off that plagues current VLM fine-tuning strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MMRL framework introduces a sophisticated multi-modal representation learning and decoupled inference strategy built upon pre-trained VLMs, specifically CLIP.

#### 3.1. Learnable Representation Space (R)

MMRL establishes a **shared, modality-agnostic, and learnable representation space R**. This space contains `K` tokens, initialized from a Gaussian distribution. Through distinct linear mapping functions, `F_v(·)` and `F_t(·)`, these shared tokens are projected into modality-specific **visual representation tokens (`R_v`)** and **textual representation tokens (`R_t`)**. This design ensures an unbiased foundation for multimodal interaction, preventing dominance by a single modality.

#### 3.2. Integration into Higher Encoder Layers

A crucial aspect of MMRL is the strategic integration of these representation tokens. Unlike some prior methods that modify lower layers, MMRL concatenates `R_v` and `R_t` into the **higher layers (starting from the J-th layer)** of the image and text encoders, respectively. This decision is motivated by the understanding that lower layers of VLMs retain more generalized knowledge, while higher layers encode more discriminative, dataset-specific information. By inserting representation tokens at higher layers, MMRL aims to learn task-specific features without interfering with the fundamental, generalized knowledge preserved in the lower layers. For the text encoder, `R_t` tokens are inserted before the original text tokens to preserve textual information, and the attention mask is adjusted.

#### 3.3. Decoupled Representation Learning (Training Phase)

During training, MMRL optimizes two distinct sets of features:
- **Class Features (`f_c`):** Derived from the original CLIP class token (`c_L`). Its patch projection layer (`P_c^v`) remains **frozen** to safeguard the pre-trained, generalized knowledge.
- **Representation Features (`f_r`):** Derived from the newly introduced representation tokens (`r_L = Mean(R_v_L)`). Its dedicated patch projection layer (`P_r^v`) is **trainable**, allowing it to learn dataset-specific adaptations.
Both `f_c` and `f_r` are optimized with cross-entropy losses (`L_c_ce` and `L_r_ce`) against the text classifiers (`w`).
To further reinforce the generalization capacity of the class token, a **regularization term** is introduced. This term minimizes the cosine distance between `f_c` and the text classifiers (`w`) with their respective zero-shot features from the *frozen* VLM (`f_0`, `w_0`). This (`L_v_cos`, `L_t_cos`) regularization guides the `f_c` to stay aligned with the original VLM's general knowledge.
The total loss function `L_MMRL = αL_c_ce + (1 − α)L_r_ce + λ(L_v_cos + L_t_cos)` uses `α` to balance the emphasis on class vs. representation features and `λ` as a penalty coefficient for regularization.

#### 3.4. Decoupled Inference Strategy

MMRL employs a novel decoupled inference strategy based on the assumption that class features retain more generalized knowledge, while representation features capture dataset-specific information:
- **Inference on Base Classes (Seen during training):** Both the dataset-specific representation features (`f_r`) and the generalized class features (`f_c`) are combined to make predictions. The final probability is a weighted sum: `p(y = c | x) = α · p(y = c | f_c) + (1 − α) · p(y = c | f_r)`.
- **Inference on Novel Classes or New Tasks (Unseen during training):** Only the class features (`f_c`) are utilized for prediction: `p(y = c | x) = p(y = c | f_c)`. This strategy intelligently leverages the most appropriate feature set for different types of generalization, mitigating overfitting to base classes when evaluating novel ones.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MMRL was extensively evaluated across 15 datasets and four core tasks, consistently demonstrating superior performance over state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Base-to-Novel Generalization

- MMRL achieved a new state-of-the-art, with average gains of 2.48% (Base), 0.36% (Novel), and 1.33% (Harmonic Mean, HM) over the previous best model, MMA, across 11 diverse datasets.
- It showed a strong ability to enhance transfer learning capabilities for base classes (e.g., ImageNet, StanfordCars, SUN397) while effectively preserving generalization to novel classes.
- Even when novel accuracy was slightly lower on some datasets (e.g., UCF101, EuroSAT), MMRL achieved significantly higher base accuracy, indicating a better overall balance and effective task-specific adaptation without major sacrifices in generalization.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset Evaluation

- When trained on ImageNet and evaluated on 10 other unseen datasets, MMRL achieved the highest average accuracy (72.03%), surpassing MMA by 1.03%, demonstrating robust cross-dataset generalization.

#### 4.3. Domain Generalization

- MMRL attained top performance on 2 out of 4 domain-shifted ImageNet variants (ImageNet-A and ImageNet-R), showcasing its resilience to out-of-distribution data.

#### 4.4. Few-Shot Learning

- MMRL exhibited the best average performance across all 11 datasets under all few-shot settings (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 shots). The performance margins increased with the number of shots, confirming its strong transfer learning capability even with limited data.

#### 4.5. Ablation Analysis

- **Multimodal Interaction & Shared Representation Space:** Removing either the text or visual representation tokens (`w/o L`, `w/o V`) or omitting the shared representation space (`w/o RS`) led to significant performance degradation, particularly in generalization. This validated the necessity of balanced multimodal learning and the shared space.
- **Decoupling Strategy:**
 - Using only class features (`w/o DS1`) for all predictions degraded base class performance significantly.
 - Using both features for novel class evaluation (`w/o DS2`) also caused a notable drop in novel accuracy, confirming that representation tokens primarily capture base-class knowledge and should be decoupled for novel tasks.
- **Layer for Integration (J):** Performance was optimal when representation tokens were inserted at higher encoder layers (J=6), confirming the hypothesis that higher layers are better for dataset-specific features, preserving generalizable knowledge in lower layers.
- **Hyperparameters:** Optimal values were found for `d_r` (512 for most, 2048 for EuroSAT), `K` (5 representation tokens), `α` (0.7, balancing class vs. representation features), and `λ` (0.5 for ImageNet, for regularization strength).
- **Regularization Strategy:** Cosine similarity regularization was superior to L1 and MSE for aligning features with frozen CLIP features, as it provided more flexible constraints, helping preserve generalizability while allowing task-specific learning.

#### 4.6. Computational Cost

- MMRL achieved significantly faster training speeds compared to many prompt learning methods (e.g., MaPLe, PromptSRC), with competitive inference speed.
- Even with reduced representation space dimensionality (MMRL*), achieving a parameter count comparable to MMA, MMRL still substantially outperformed the previous SOTA, demonstrating efficiency and robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMRL framework represents a significant advancement in the field of efficient transfer learning for Vision-Language Models, with several key implications:

- **Addressing the Generalization-Adaptation Trade-off:** MMRL provides an elegant and effective solution to the long-standing challenge of balancing task-specific adaptation with the preservation of a VLM's powerful generalization capabilities. This is critical for practical applications where models need to perform well on both seen and unseen tasks or categories with limited data.
- **Robustness in Few-Shot Settings:** By mitigating overfitting and explicitly safeguarding generalized knowledge, MMRL enables VLMs to be more robust and performant in data-scarce scenarios, which are common in real-world deployments and specialized domains.
- **Enhanced Multi-Modal Interaction:** The introduction of a shared, unbiased representation space and its strategic integration into higher encoder layers fosters more effective and balanced multi-modal learning, moving beyond modality-biased prompt learning approaches.
- **Flexible and Intelligent Inference:** The decoupled inference strategy is a novel contribution, demonstrating that different types of knowledge (task-specific vs. generalized) can be selectively leveraged depending on the nature of the task (base vs. novel classes). This offers a blueprint for more intelligent model usage.
- **Efficiency for Large Models:** Despite introducing new learnable components, MMRL maintains competitive computational efficiency in terms of training time and inference speed, making it a viable solution for adapting large foundation models without incurring prohibitive resource costs.

The potential impact of MMRL is broad, extending to areas where VLMs are applied, such as medical image analysis, image/video captioning, and visual question answering. By providing a more reliable and generalizable adaptation mechanism, MMRL can accelerate the deployment of powerful VLMs in various real-world applications, especially those requiring rapid adaptation to new tasks with limited expert-annotated data. This work sets a new benchmark for efficient transfer learning and paves the way for future research into more sophisticated decoupled learning strategies for multimodal AI.
