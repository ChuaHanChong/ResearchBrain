# DexNDM: Closing the Reality Gap for Dexterous In-Hand Rotation via Joint-Wise Neural Dynamics Model

> [!summary] Summary
> DexNDM introduces a sim-to-real framework that leverages a joint-wise neural dynamics model and autonomous data collection to achieve generalized in-hand object rotation. This approach enables robust manipulation of diverse objects, including those with challenging geometries, under various wrist orientations and rotation axes in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- The significant "sim-to-real gap" hinders the transfer of dexterous manipulation policies from simulation to real robots, especially for contact-rich tasks.
- Current in-hand rotation approaches are often constrained by object geometry, manipulation conditions (e.g., wrist orientation), and reliance on specialized hardware.
- Collecting sufficient, diverse, and relevant real-world data for training robust dynamics models for complex dexterous tasks is practically challenging due to safety concerns and data scarcity.

## Method

- Trains category-specific ==oracle policies== in simulation, then aggregates successful trajectories to train a single ==generalist policy== via Behavior Cloning.
- Introduces a ==joint-wise neural dynamics model==, factorizing system dynamics to learn each joint's behavior independently, which theoretically enhances sample efficiency and generalization.
- Employs an autonomous ==Chaos Box data collection== method, using robot interaction with soft balls to generate diverse, scalable, and safe real-world dynamics data.
- Leverages the learned dynamics model to train a ==residual policy== that corrects the generalist policy's actions for real-world deployment.

## Results

- DexNDM demonstrates unprecedented real-world dexterity, successfully rotating complex objects (e.g., animal models, high aspect ratio up to **5.33**, small **2-3 cm** objects) under diverse wrist orientations and rotation axes.
- The framework substantially outperforms existing baselines like AnyRotate, achieving superior rotation performance and handling a significantly broader and more challenging object distribution.
- Ablation studies confirm the joint-wise dynamics model's superior sample efficiency and generalization to out-of-distribution real-world data compared to whole-hand models.
- The autonomous "Chaos Box" data collection is verified as efficient and scalable, providing broad coverage of interaction dynamics and enabling practical teleoperation applications for complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint-wise factorization of neural dynamics models acts as an information bottleneck, improving sample efficiency and generalization by discarding spurious correlations in high-dimensional contact interactions.
> - Autonomous and safe data collection methods, such as the "Chaos Box," are essential for acquiring the vast and diverse real-world interaction data needed to learn complex dexterous dynamics.
> - Training a generalist policy from multiple oracle policies via behavior cloning, then adapting it with a residual policy informed by real-world dynamics, is an effective strategy for robust sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation, particularly in-hand object rotation, is a cornerstone for achieving sophisticated embodied intelligence in robotics. The broader research landscape in this domain has been largely hampered by the formidable "sim-to-real gap." This gap arises from the inherent difficulty in accurately modeling the complex, contact-rich dynamics of dexterous manipulation, leading to policies trained in simulation failing to transfer effectively to the real world.

Prior research in in-hand rotation has made progress but often operates under significant constraints:
- **Limited Object Generality:** Many methods are restricted to simple geometries (e.g., cubes, cylinders), limited object sizes, or specific aspect ratios. Some struggle with small or high-aspect-ratio objects.
- **Constrained Manipulation Conditions:** Policies often assume a consistently up-facing hand or limited wrist orientations, failing to generalize to diverse poses or rotation axes.
- **Hardware Dependencies:** A reliance on expensive, customized hardware with sophisticated tactile sensing is common, limiting broader adoption.
- **Sim-to-Real Strategies:** Existing sim-to-real approaches like Domain Randomization (DR) often rely on heuristic ranges, System Identification (SysID) is bounded by its parameterization, and online adaptation requires extensive dynamics coverage. Neural modeling of real dynamics holds promise but struggles with the volume and distributional relevance of real-world data, especially for complex, contact-rich tasks where failures are catastrophic and state estimation is difficult.

This paper directly confronts these limitations, particularly the "sim-to-real gap" for *generalized* in-hand rotation. It aims to develop a single, general-purpose policy capable of rotating a broad distribution of objects (including challenging geometries and complex shapes) across diverse wrist orientations and rotation axes in the real world, without relying on specialized tactile sensing. By addressing the data-efficiency and generalization issues of learning real dynamics, DexNDM positions itself as a significant advancement towards truly general-purpose dexterous manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **generalized in-hand object rotation in the real world** by effectively bridging the simulation-to-reality gap. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Develop a single, unified policy** capable of rotating a wide variety of objects, including those with challenging geometries (e.g., complex shapes like animals, high aspect ratios up to 5.33, and small sizes).
2. **Achieve robustness across diverse manipulation conditions**, specifically varying wrist orientations (e.g., palm up, palm down, thumb up/down, base up/down) and arbitrary rotation axes.
3. **Overcome the "sim-to-real gap"**: The primary motivation is to address the difficulty in transferring policies from simulation to the real world, which is exacerbated by the complex, contact-rich dynamics of dexterous manipulation. This includes tackling data scarcity, the challenge of obtaining distributionally relevant real-world data, and the limitations of traditional dynamics modeling.

The motivation stems from the recognition that despite advances, existing methods remain constrained, hindering the realization of truly capable embodied intelligence. The authors identify the conflict between the need for massive, diverse data for generality and the practical impossibility of collecting such data (due to catastrophic failures, human intervention, and state estimation difficulties) as a critical bottleneck for the field. DexNDM is explicitly designed to resolve this conflict by rethinking both the model (joint-wise dynamics) and the data collection strategy (autonomous "Chaos Box").

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, DexNDM, tackles the problem with a two-pronged approach: a specialist-to-generalist policy training pipeline and a novel neural sim-to-real strategy.

#### 3.1. Multi-Wrist-Orientation In-Hand Object Rotation Across Multi-Axis (Oracle Policy Training)

The task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **Observations and Actions:** The policy receives a history of proprioception, fingertip and object states, force measurements, contact signals, wrist orientation, and target rotation axis. Actions are relative target joint positions, converted to torques via a PD controller.
- **Reward Function:** Composed of three weighted components: rotation (encourages rotation around the target axis), penalty (discourages off-axis velocity, hand pose deviation, object linear velocity, and excessive joint work/torque), and an intermediate goal-pose reward (guides the object to waypoints on the rotation axis, crucial for difficult cases like long objects).
- **Policy Training:** Category-specific "oracle" policies are trained using PPO in Isaac Gym for different object categories (e.g., cylinders, cuboids, long objects, small objects, complex shapes).

#### 3.2. Generalist Policy Training via Behavior Cloning

To achieve a single, real-world deployable policy that generalizes across object geometries and conditions:
- **Data Aggregation:** Successful trajectories from all trained oracle policies are collected.
- **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A unified "generalist" policy is trained via supervised learning on this aggregated dataset. This approach is chosen over DAgger-style distillation due to the high task difficulty, as BC focusing on high-quality oracle behavior proved more robust on hardware. The generalist policy's observation space is limited to proprioception history, wrist orientation, and rotation axis, making it suitable for real-world deployment without privileged object state information.

#### 3.3. Closing the Reality Gap via Joint-Wise Neural Dynamics

This is the core innovation for sim-to-real transfer, designed to overcome the limitations of data acquisition and modeling complex interaction dynamics.

- **Joint-Wise Neural Dynamics Model:** Instead of modeling the entire high-dimensional hand-object system, DexNDM factorizes the problem by learning the dynamics of each joint independently. Each joint's next state ($q_{i,t+1}$) is predicted using only its own history of states and actions ($h_{i,t} = \{q_{i,j}, a_{i,j}\}_{j=t-W+1}^t$).
 - **Theoretical Rationale (Information Contraction):** The authors provide theoretical claims and proofs (Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 in the paper) demonstrating that this factorization acts as an information bottleneck. By projecting the high-dimensional whole-hand system dynamics into lower-dimensional, joint-specific histories, the model discards spurious correlations and learns only the essential dynamics of each joint. This leads to increased sample efficiency and better generalization across different interaction distributions, without sacrificing expressivity.
 - **Implicit Capture of Net Effects:** The model implicitly captures system-wide influences (self-actuation, inter-joint couplings, object loads) as low-dimensional, task-sufficient "net effects" in each joint's dynamic profile.
- **Autonomous Data Collection ("Chaos Box"):** This strategy addresses the data volume and distributional relevance conflict.
 - **Principles:** Policy-awareness (coarse distributional prior), object-loaded interaction, broad coverage, and scalability.
 - **Implementation:** The robot hand is placed in a container of soft balls ("Chaos Box"). Actions from the simulated base policy are open-loop replayed, with Gaussian noise added to broaden coverage. The interaction with soft balls provides rich, randomized, and continuous loads.
 - **Benefits:** Fully autonomous, hardware-safe, eliminates catastrophic failures and human resets, and collects diverse, large-scale real-world interaction data cheaply and scalably.
- **Bridging Dynamics Gap via Residual Policy:**
 - **Mechanism:** Using the learned joint-wise dynamics model ($f_\psi$), a "residual policy" ($\pi_{res}$) is trained to output corrections to the base generalist policy's actions ($a_t$). This residual action ($a_{res,t}$) aims to make the real-world system's next state match the simulator's predicted next state.
 - **Training:** $\pi_{res}$ is trained in a supervised manner on the same trajectory dataset used to train the base policy. During deployment, the robot executes $a_t + a_{res,t}$. This approach is favored over direct fine-tuning for its stability and ease of implementation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive evaluations in both simulation and the real world, demonstrating the unprecedented generality and robustness of DexNDM.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- The generalist policy, trained via BC, demonstrates strong generalization to novel, complex shapes in simulation.
- It significantly outperforms a re-implemented AnyRotate baseline (by 37%–81% in Rotation Reward) across various rotation axes and randomized wrist orientations.
- Rotating along the gravity direction (±z axis) is consistently the easiest task, aligning with previous observations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- **Unprecedented Dexterity:** DexNDM consistently and significantly improves real-world rotation performance compared to direct transfer of the base policy.
- **Challenging Objects:** It successfully rotates objects with:
 - **Complex Shapes:** E.g., animal models like elephants, bunnies, teapots, cows, trucks.
 - **High Aspect Ratios:** Up to 5.33 (e.g., long cuboids, "Lego" legs, books up to 16 cm).
 - **Small Sizes:** Objects as small as 2-3 cm.
- **Diverse Conditions:** Achieves robust rotation under challenging wrist orientations (including "Palm Down," "Base Up/Down") and various rotation axes. Notably, it is claimed to be the first to rotate long objects (10–16 cm) around their long axis for approximately one full circle in the air in a downward-facing hand configuration.
- **Superiority over Baselines:**
 - **AnyRotate:** DexNDM substantially outperforms AnyRotate on its most difficult replicable objects (e.g., "Tin Cylinder," "Gum Box") in terms of total radians rotated and time-to-fall. DexNDM also handles a much broader and more challenging object distribution (smaller objects, higher aspect ratios) with sophisticated finger gaiting.
 - **Visual Dexterity:** Despite metric differences and VD sometimes using a supporting table, DexNDM achieves comparable or superior "survival rotation angles" on VD's showcased and replicable objects. DexNDM also uniquely manipulates small and high-aspect-ratio objects under diverse wrist orientations.
 - **ASAP and UAN:** These sim-to-real methods, adapted for manipulation by training compensators on object-free transitions, failed entirely in real-world tests (unable to rotate even simple cylinders). This highlights the crucial role of *object dynamics* in dexterous manipulation and the inadequacy of free-hand data for learning these interactions. DexNDM's strategy is more tolerant of real-data imperfections and implicitly accounts for object loads.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Joint-Wise vs. Whole-Hand Dynamics:**
 - **Expressivity:** The joint-wise model is nearly as expressive as the whole-hand model in high-data, in-domain simulation tests.
 - **Sample Efficiency:** In low-data real-world regimes (e.g., 7.5k autonomously collected trajectories), the joint-wise model is significantly more sample-efficient, achieving better in-domain performance.
 - **Generalizability:** The joint-wise model generalizes much better to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) real-world test sets (e.g., task-relevant transitions under "Thumb Up" wrist) in both high and low-data regimes. This validates the theoretical rationale of information contraction.
- **Autonomous Data Collection:**
 - **Efficiency and Coverage:** The "Chaos Box" strategy is fully autonomous, hardware-safe, eliminates human intervention, and provides broad coverage of interaction dynamics by continuously varying loads from soft balls.
 - **Superiority over Task-Aware Data:** Task-aware data collection (with or without object states) is slow, intervention-heavy, noisy, and limited in diversity/coverage (especially for small, occluded, or axis-symmetric objects). An extrapolation suggests matching DexNDM's performance with task-aware data would require millions of trajectories, demonstrating the impracticality of alternative methods.
- **Residual Policy vs. Direct Finetuning:** Direct fine-tuning of the base policy on learned dynamics proved unstable and hyperparameter-sensitive, failing to execute basic rotations, thus validating the choice of a residual policy.

#### 4.4. Applications

- **Teleoperation:** The generalized rotation policy enables a teleoperation system (using a Meta Quest 3 headset) to perform complex, long-horizon dexterous tasks such as tool-using (screwdriver, knife) and furniture assembly (lightbulb). This showcases a practical impact beyond just rotating isolated objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexNDM framework marks a significant leap forward in dexterous manipulation, particularly for in-hand object rotation. Its key contributions and potential impact are:

1. **Breaking the Sim-to-Real Bottleneck for Dexterous Manipulation:** By introducing the joint-wise neural dynamics model and autonomous data collection, DexNDM provides a robust solution to the pervasive sim-to-real gap, which has historically hindered the transfer of complex manipulation skills from simulation to real robots. This methodology is particularly impactful for contact-rich tasks where traditional methods struggle.
2. **Unprecedented Generality and Robustness:** The ability to rotate a wide array of objects (including challenging geometries, high aspect ratios, and small sizes) under diverse wrist orientations and rotation axes sets a new benchmark for in-hand manipulation. This level of generality is crucial for real-world applications where objects and manipulation contexts are highly varied.
3. **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** The autonomous "Chaos Box" data collection strategy is a game-changer for real-world robotics. It dramatically reduces human intervention and data acquisition costs, making it feasible to gather the necessary data for learning complex dynamics, which was previously impractical. This opens avenues for scaling up real-world robotics research.
4. **Theoretical Advancements in Generalization:** The theoretical analysis of the joint-wise dynamics model, demonstrating generalization via information contraction, offers valuable insights into why factorized modeling is effective in learning from distributionally different data. This could influence future designs of dynamics models for other complex robotic tasks.
5. **Enabling Advanced Robotic Applications:** The successful teleoperation application demonstrates that DexNDM's capabilities are not confined to isolated benchmarks. By providing robust in-hand rotation, it acts as a foundational skill that can empower robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks requiring fine-grained dexterity, such as intricate assembly, tool manipulation, and object reorientation in unstructured environments. This brings closer the vision of highly capable embodied intelligence.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the model's ceiling is restricted by partial observations. Future work could explore jointly modeling hand–object transitions from richer signals, such as integrating tactile feedback, to further enhance dexterity and robustness. Despite this, DexNDM's achievements represent a substantial step towards real-world general-purpose dexterous manipulation.
