# Falcon: Fast Visuomotor Policies via Partial Denoising

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Peking University and BIGAI developed Falcon, a training-free method that accelerates diffusion-based visuomotor policies by 2-7x, and up to 34x in specific scenarios, without degrading task success rates or multimodal action expressiveness. It achieves this by reusing partially denoised actions from historical information as starting points for the denoising process, leveraging sequential dependencies in robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Diffusion policies for visuomotor control are computationally intensive during inference, requiring numerous iterative denoising steps, which makes them too slow for real-time robotic applications.
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion policies often involve a trade-off, either introducing approximation errors at very few steps (ODE-based solvers) or sacrificing multimodal expressiveness and requiring task-specific retraining (distillation-based approaches).
- Current solutions lack a flexible, plug-and-play mechanism to accelerate diverse diffusion policies without additional training or extensive modifications.

## Method

- **Falcon** introduces a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration method that reuses partially denoised actions from previous timesteps as the starting point for the current denoising process, instead of starting from pure Gaussian noise.
- It employs a thresholding mechanism to select relevant historical partially denoised actions based on their estimated 'clean' counterparts and their Euclidean distance to the current desired action, ensuring alignment with the current observation.
- A latent buffer (priority queue) stores historical partial denoised actions, and an exploration rate allows occasional starting from Gaussian noise to prevent over-reliance on historical data.

## Results

- Falcon achieved a 2-**7x** speedup in action sampling across 48 simulated tasks (RoboMimic, RoboSuite Kitchen, MetaWorld), and up to **34x** for RDT-1B on ManiSkill2, with negligible to no degradation in success rates.
- It preserved multimodal behavior in tasks like PushT and BlockPush, generating diverse and context-appropriate actions, unlike some existing acceleration methods that lead to mode collapse.
- In real-world dexterous grasping and high-precision insertion tasks, Falcon provided **2.86**-**3.07x** speedups over standalone DDPM while maintaining 90-**100%** success rates and exhibiting improved memory efficiency compared to Streaming Diffusion Policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visuomotor tasks exhibit strong sequential dependencies, where unexecuted portions of previously predicted action sequences closely approximate the desired current action sequence.
> - By initiating the denoising process from a partially denoised state (derived from historical information) rather than pure Gaussian noise, a significant number of denoising steps can be saved.
> - Careful selection of the optimal partially denoised starting point, combined with an exploration rate, is crucial to maintain high performance and multimodal expressiveness while maximizing speedup.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visuomotor control in robotics has seen significant advancements with the advent of diffusion policies. These policies have demonstrated efficacy in addressing complex tasks due to their capacity to model multimodal action distributions and maintain stable training dynamics. Diffusion policies operate by iteratively denoising an initial sample, typically drawn from a standard normal distribution, through a reverse sampling process governed by stochastic differential equations (SDEs) or ordinary differential equations (ODEs).

Despite their capabilities, a primary limitation of diffusion policies is their computational inefficiency during inference. The multiple sampling steps required for action generation lead to slow real-time decision-making, which curtails their applicability in time-sensitive robotic control scenarios.

Existing approaches to accelerate diffusion policy inference generally fall into two categories:
- **ODE-based solvers:** Methods like DDIM and DPMSolver reformulate the sampling process as an ODE and use numerical solvers to reduce the number of denoising steps. However, these solvers can introduce approximation errors when the number of steps is significantly reduced, potentially degrading policy performance.
- **Distillation-based approaches:** Techniques such as Consistency Policy (CP) and OneDP aim for one-step action generation by distilling a pre-trained diffusion policy. While fast, these methods typically necessitate task-specific retraining and may compromise the multimodal expressiveness inherent to diffusion models, limiting their flexibility as general plug-and-play solutions.
- **Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP):** This approach also uses partial denoising for acceleration but requires task-specific retraining and a large noise-time buffer, imposing limitations on flexibility and memory efficiency.

"Falcon: Fast Visuomotor Policies via Partial Denoising" aims to address the identified speed-performance trade-off by introducing a training-free acceleration method that preserves performance and multimodal expressiveness. It seeks to integrate as a plug-in to existing diffusion-based policies and acceleration techniques, offering a distinct contribution by leveraging sequential dependencies in visuomotor tasks without requiring additional training or extensive modifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an acceleration technique for diffusion-based visuomotor policies that mitigates the inherent speed-performance trade-off, thereby enhancing their applicability in real-time robotic decision-making.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Accelerating Inference Speed without Performance Degradation:** Diffusion policies require numerous iterative sampling steps for action generation, rendering them computationally intensive and unsuitable for real-time robotic control. The core motivation is to significantly reduce the number of these sampling steps, thus accelerating inference, while ensuring that the policy's performance, as measured by task success rates, is preserved or minimally affected. The paper reports aiming for a 2-7x speedup with negligible performance degradation.

- **Maintaining Multimodal Expressiveness:** Many existing acceleration methods, particularly distillation-based approaches, can lead to a collapse of multimodal action distributions, meaning the policy may lose its ability to generate diverse and flexible actions in ambiguous situations. A critical objective for Falcon is to maintain the policy's capacity to model and generate multimodal action distributions, which is a strength of diffusion policies for complex tasks.

- **Developing a Training-Free and Plug-and-Play Solution:** Current acceleration techniques often demand task-specific retraining or extensive model modifications, which adds complexity and reduces flexibility. The authors are motivated to create a training-free algorithm that can function as a plug-in module, seamlessly integrating with and enhancing the efficiency of existing diffusion-based policies (such as DDPM, DDIM, and DPMSolver) without requiring additional training data or computational resources for fine-tuning. This enhances its practical utility across diverse robotic tasks.

- **Exploiting Sequential Dependencies in Visuomotor Tasks:** The central insight motivating Falcon is the observation that visuomotor tasks exhibit sequential dependencies between actions. Specifically, the paper notes that an unexecuted action sequence predicted from a previous observation often closely approximates the actual desired action sequence for the current timestep (Figure 2, showing low Euclidean distance). This dependency suggests that starting the denoising process from a partially denoised state derived from historical information, rather than from pure Gaussian noise at each step, could significantly reduce the required sampling steps.

- **Enhancing Applicability in Real-World Robotics:** The overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between the theoretical capabilities of diffusion policies and their practical deployment in real-world robotic systems. By making these policies faster and more efficient, Falcon aims to expand their use in critical applications such as dexterous manipulation and high-precision insertion, where real-time responsiveness is paramount.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Falcon addresses the computational bottleneck of diffusion policies by leveraging sequential dependencies in visuomotor tasks. The method proposes reusing partially denoised actions from historical information, guided by current observations, instead of initiating the denoising process from Gaussian noise at each decision step. This approach is designed to be training-free and compatible as a plug-in module for existing diffusion policies.

The methodology can be broken down into the following components:

#### 3.1. Reference Actions

- **Premise:** The unexecuted portion of an action sequence ($\tilde{a}_{t:t-T_a+T_p}$) predicted at a previous timestep ($t-T_a$) is posited to be a strong approximation of the desired current action sequence ($a_{t:t+T_p}$). This is supported by empirical analysis (Figure 2), which shows a high probability density near zero for the Euclidean distance between these two action sequences.
- **Guidance:** The denoising process for a partially denoised action from historical data is guided by the score vector field ($\nabla_{A_t} \log p(A_t|O_t)$) computed from the current observation ($O_t$). This ensures that the reused historical information is adapted to the current environmental state.

#### 3.2. Thresholding Mechanism

This mechanism determines which specific partially denoised action from the stored historical data should be used as the starting point for the current denoising process.
- **One-step Estimation:** For each partial denoised action $a^k_{\tau:\tau+T_p}$ in a latent buffer, where $\tau < t$ is a historical timestep and $k$ is its noise level, Falcon estimates its "clean" counterpart. This is achieved using Tweedie's formula, which, in the context of diffusion models, allows for the computation of the posterior expectation of the clean action $E[a^0_{\tau:\tau-T_a+T_p} | O_t, a^k_{\tau:\tau-T_a+T_p}]$ (Equation 9). This estimation is conditioned on the current observation $O_t$.
- **Dependency Measurement and Selection:** The dependency between a historical estimated action ($\hat{a}^k_{\tau:\tau-T_a+T_p}$) and the current reference action ($\tilde{a}_{t:t-T_a+T_p}$) is quantified by their Euclidean distance.
- **Candidate Set Formation:** A set $S$ of candidate actions is formed, including all historical partial denoised actions $a^k_{\tau:\tau+T_p}$ for which the Euclidean distance between their one-step estimation and the current reference action falls below a predefined threshold $\epsilon$. This ensures that only relevant and sufficiently aligned historical actions are considered.
- **Starting Point Sampling:** The actual starting point for the current denoising process ($a^{k_s}_{t:t+T_p}$) is sampled from the candidate set $S$. A temperature-scaled softmax distribution (Equation 10) is used, which favors partial denoised actions with lower noise levels (smaller $k_s$), as these are closer to the final clean action.
- **Exploration Rate ($\delta$):** To prevent over-reliance on historical actions and to promote exploration or adaptation in highly dynamic environments, an exploration rate $\delta$ is introduced. With probability $\delta$, the denoising process is initiated from a standard Gaussian distribution, similar to conventional diffusion policies, instead of selecting from the latent buffer.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **Latent Buffer (B):** A data structure, specifically a priority queue, is maintained to store partial denoised actions generated at previous timesteps. This buffer stores $a^k_{\tau:\tau+T_p}$ for various historical timesteps $\tau$ and noise levels $k$. The priority queue mechanism prioritizes the removal of older actions and actions with higher noise levels, which are less likely to be relevant for current decisions, to manage memory usage.
- **Minimum Noise Level ($k_{min}$):** To prevent the selection of actions that are too clean (i.e., already denoised to a great extent) and thus have limited scope for further iterative sampling, a minimum noise level $k_{min}$ is introduced. Partial denoised actions with noise levels below $k_{min}$ are filtered out during the selection process.
- **First Timestep Handling:** At the very first timestep ($t=1$) of a task, where no historical information is available, Falcon defaults to the standard diffusion policy behavior, starting the denoising process from a Gaussian distribution (Algorithm 1, Lines 2-9).
- **Compatibility with Solvers:** Falcon is designed as a flexible module that can be integrated with various SDE/ODE solvers, such as DDIM and DPMSolver. The pseudocode demonstrates how Falcon modifies the standard sampling loops of these solvers (e.g., Equations 11, 13, 15 and Algorithms 2 and 3). The one-step estimation for candidate selection (Algorithm 1, Line 11) is executed in parallel, which is the most computationally intensive part but can be efficiently processed on modern hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of Falcon across simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its ability to accelerate visuomotor policies while preserving performance and multimodal expressiveness.

#### 4.1. Performance in Simulated Environments

The evaluation included 48 tasks spanning five benchmarks: RoboMimic, RoboSuite Kitchen, BlockPush, MetaWorld, and ManiSkill2.

- **Acceleration and Performance Preservation:**
 - **RoboMimic:** Falcon achieved a 2-7x speedup in action sampling (NFE reduction) across RoboMimic tasks, with negligible degradation in success rates (Table 1, 2, 4).
 - In simpler tasks (e.g., Lift, Can), which involve smoother, more predictable movements and strong sequential dependencies, Falcon demonstrated higher speedups (up to 7x for Lift), as it could initiate denoising from very low noise levels (Figure 13, left).
 - For more complex tasks (e.g., Square, Transport, ToolHang), which involve dynamic and precise movements, Falcon still provided approximately a 2x speedup while maintaining performance close to original models (Figure 13, right).
 - **MetaWorld (with 3D Diffusion Policy):** Falcon, integrated with DDIM (3D FalconDDIM), achieved a 3-4x acceleration over the base 3D Diffusion Policy across 39 tasks, with no observable performance drop (Table 7, 8, 9).
 - **ManiSkill2 (with RDT-1B):** When applied to the pre-trained RDT-1B (a vision-language-action foundation model), Falcon demonstrated substantial speedups of 31x (PickCube) and 34x (PushCube) over the 100-step DDPM baseline, maintaining the same success rates (Table 11).

- **Mitigation of Performance Degradation in Reduced-step Solvers:** Falcon-enhanced DPMSolver maintained high success rates while operating at the same reduced number of denoising steps as a baseline DPMSolver* which, without Falcon, exhibited significant performance drops (e.g., 10.6% in Square ph, 35.6% in Square mh) (Table 3). This highlights Falcon's ability to improve performance stability in low-step regimes.

- **Multimodal Behavior Preservation:** In the PushT task, DDPM+Falcon generated diverse reaching trajectories for a symmetric configuration, demonstrating its capability to maintain multimodal behavior, in contrast to the Consistency Policy which exhibited mode collapse (Figure 3). Further analysis in BlockPush confirmed uniform distribution across different action modalities (Figure 12).

- **Long-horizon Tasks (Franka Kitchen):** Falcon preserved the high success rates of the original DDPM model while achieving a 5.25x speedup in the Franka Kitchen environment, indicating its effectiveness for complex, long-horizon tasks (Table 12, 13).

#### 4.2. Real-World Robotic Experiments

Falcon was deployed on a dual-arm robot platform for two manipulation tasks.

- **Dexterous Grasping:** Falcon+DDPM achieved a 3.07x speedup over standalone DDPM. It also outperformed SDP(DDPM) in terms of runtime (0.145s vs. 0.233s) and CPU memory usage (3730MB vs. 3808MB), all while maintaining 100% success rate (Table 5).

- **High-Precision Insertion:** For inserting a square stick into a chip can, Falcon+DDPM matched DDPM's 90% success rate while being 2.86x faster and more memory-efficient (Table 6).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Sensitivity to Threshold $\epsilon$:** Analysis on the Square ph task revealed that an optimal range for $\epsilon$ exists (Figure 4, left). A very small $\epsilon$ results in minimal speedup (frequent sampling from Gaussian noise), while a very large $\epsilon$ leads to significant performance degradation (selecting incorrect historical actions).
- **Sensitivity to Exploration Rate $\delta$:** On the Transport ph task, increasing $\delta$ (probability of starting from Gaussian noise) improved the success rate by mitigating error accumulation, but reduced the overall speedup (Figure 4, right). This indicates a trade-off between exploration and exploitation.
- **Importance of Selection Mechanism:** Disabling Falcon's adaptive selection mechanism by fixing the starting noise level (e.g., K/2 or K/5) resulted in a substantial decrease in task performance, confirming the importance of the adaptive selection for accurate action choices (Figure 7).

#### 4.4. Memory Overhead

Falcon introduced a negligible additional memory overhead of 12 MB, compared to the base 1876 MB of the original diffusion models, demonstrating its efficiency in terms of resource utilization (Table 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Falcon represents an advancement in the practical application of diffusion policies for visuomotor control in robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Real-Time Applicability:** Falcon directly addresses the primary bottleneck of diffusion policies – their computational cost during inference. By achieving significant speedups (2-7x in many tasks, up to 34x in some specialized scenarios) without compromising task success rates or multimodal expressiveness, Falcon makes diffusion policies viable for real-time decision-making in robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, and other interactive AI systems. This could enable more responsive and agile robotic behaviors in dynamic environments.

- **Flexible and Training-Free Integration:** The design of Falcon as a training-free, plug-and-play module is a notable contribution. This allows it to seamlessly integrate with existing pre-trained diffusion policies and other acceleration techniques (like DDIM and DPMSolver) without requiring additional training data, model retraining, or extensive hyperparameter tuning. This flexibility reduces the barrier to adoption and extends the utility of existing diffusion-based models, including large-scale vision-language-action foundation models like RDT-1B.

- **Preservation of Core Diffusion Policy Strengths:** Unlike some distillation-based methods that may sacrifice the policy's ability to model multimodal action distributions, Falcon explicitly aims to preserve this critical characteristic. This ensures that robots can still generate diverse and context-appropriate actions, which is essential for complex tasks with inherent ambiguities or multiple valid solutions.

- **Improved Performance in Low-Step Regimes:** Falcon's ability to mitigate performance degradation in reduced-step solvers (e.g., DPMSolver) suggests it can enable more aggressive acceleration strategies than previously feasible, allowing for faster inference without a substantial drop in task reliability.

- **Broadened Scope for Diffusion Policies:** By making diffusion policies more efficient and adaptable, Falcon expands their potential application to a wider array of complex visuomotor tasks, including long-horizon planning and dexterous manipulation, where both high-quality action generation and real-time responsiveness are critical. The empirical validation across 48 simulated environments and two real-world robot tasks indicates its generalizability.

- **Direction for Future Research:** While Falcon demonstrates substantial advantages, the acknowledgment of its limitation regarding task-specific parameter tuning offers a clear direction for future research, focusing on adaptive parameter selection strategies to further enhance its generalizability. This iterative improvement process could lead to even more robust and autonomous systems.

In summary, Falcon offers a practical and effective solution for deploying sophisticated diffusion policies in real-world robotic scenarios, thereby accelerating the development and application of advanced visuomotor control systems.
