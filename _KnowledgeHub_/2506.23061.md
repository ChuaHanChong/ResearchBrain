# Empowering Small VLMs to Think with Dynamic Memorization and Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) introduced DyME (Dynamic Memorize–Explore), a novel training paradigm that empowers Small-scale Vision-Language Models (SVLMs) with robust thinking capabilities. DyME achieves this by dynamically integrating supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, leading to significant performance improvements and allowing SVLMs to match or even surpass some much larger models in reasoning tasks. For instance, DyME improved SmolVLM's medical VQA score by 6.0% and chart understanding by 6.5%.

## Problem

- Small-scale Vision-Language Models (SVLMs) struggle to perform complex, multi-step reasoning tasks, which are typically exclusive to Large-scale VLMs (LVLMs) due to their limited parameter capacity.
- Existing training paradigms for reasoning, such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), are ineffective for SVLMs, often leading to 'pseudo thinking traces' or 'advantage collapse'.
- Combining SFT and RLVR in a two-stage pipeline is difficult for SVLMs due to their susceptibility to local optima, making it hard to balance memorization and exploration effectively.

## Method

- DyME introduces a ==dynamic training paradigm== that adaptively switches between a memorization mode (SFT) and an exploration mode (==Group Relative Policy Optimization== - GRPO) at each training step based on the model's performance.
- In memorization (SFT) mode, a ==Visual Refiner== enriches ground-truth thinking traces by injecting concrete, image-grounded details to ensure genuine visual grounding and prevent shallow memorization.
- In exploration (GRPO) mode, a ==Visual Checker== assesses the generated thinking trace's alignment with actual visual content and integrates this relevance score into the reward, guiding the model to produce visually grounded reasoning.

## Results

- DyME consistently delivered substantial performance gains across medical VQA, chart understanding, and geometry problem solving for all tested SVLMs, with SmolVLM (0.5B) showing an average improvement of **5.7%**.
- Existing training paradigms (SFT on CoT, standalone GRPO, and two-stage SFT+RL) were found to degrade or offer only marginal gains for SVLMs, validating DyME's core hypothesis.
- DyME-trained SVLMs achieved performance levels comparable to or even surpassed some Large-scale VLMs (LVLMs); for example, SmolVLM (**0.5B**) with DyME outperformed MoVA (**10B**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT and GRPO gradients are mathematically compatible, allowing seamless, dynamic interleaving of these two objectives within a unified training framework for SVLMs.
> - A dynamic selection mechanism that switches between SFT (when struggling) and GRPO (when partially competent) is critical for stabilizing learning, mitigating pseudo thinking, and preventing advantage collapse in SVLMs.
> - Explicit visual supervision, integrated via both a Visual Refiner (for SFT targets) and a Visual Checker (for GRPO rewards), ensures that SVLM reasoning is truly grounded in visual evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of equipping Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities, moving beyond simple recognition to complex problem-solving. Recent advancements in this field, particularly for large-scale VLMs (LVLMs), have leveraged specialized training paradigms to improve performance on tasks such as visual grounding, chart understanding, and geometric problem solving.

Two dominant training paradigms for VLM thinking are Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), often employing algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). SFT involves models memorizing predefined thinking patterns from large-scale CoT annotations. This approach typically requires substantial model capacity to process extensive textual content while maintaining visual grounding. RLVR, conversely, promotes autonomous exploration of thinking patterns, utilizing verifiable rewards derived from structured outputs to reinforce correct generations. This paradigm relies on the VLM's ability to consistently adhere to instructions and produce structured responses.

However, the existing research landscape reveals a significant gap regarding Small-scale VLMs (SVLMs). These models, characterized by limited parameter counts (e.g., under 1 billion parameters), offer high efficiency and are suitable for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Current SFT paradigms often prove ineffective for SVLMs because the large volume of textual information in CoT data can overwhelm their capacity, leading to a compromise in visual grounding and the generation of "pseudo thinking traces." Similarly, RLVR, with its reliance on instruction adherence, frequently results in unverifiable outputs from SVLMs, leading to "advantage collapse" during training. Hybrid methods combining SFT and RLVR, such as two-stage training or annealed SFT losses, have been explored but typically rely on static trade-offs between memorization and exploration. These static approaches are often brittle and unreliable for SVLMs due to their narrow capacity window, making it difficult to achieve a stable balance.

This research aims to bridge this gap by developing a new training paradigm specifically tailored for SVLMs. The objective is to enable SVLMs to develop thinking capabilities without imposing high demands on their base capacity or initial instruction adherence, thereby fulfilling a practical demand for efficient, reliable AI on edge devices for specialized tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to empower Small-scale Vision–Language Models (SVLMs) with thinking capabilities, which is identified as a critical step toward enhancing their performance and reliability in specialized, proprietary tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing training paradigms when applied to SVLMs:
- **Capacity Overload in SFT:** Current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) approaches, which rely on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, often involve verbose textual annotations. For SVLMs with their inherently limited capacity (e.g., under 1 billion parameters), absorbing such extensive textual content can overwhelm the model, leading to compromised visual grounding. This results in the model memorizing superficial patterns rather than generating genuinely grounded thinking, a phenomenon termed "pseudo thinking traces." The paper illustrates this with an example where an LVLM generates accurate, grounded thinking, while an SVLM produces incorrect, ungrounded traces after SFT.
- **Instruction Adherence and Instability in RLVR:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) paradigms, which encourage exploration of thinking patterns, depend on the VLM's ability to generate structured and verifiable outputs (e.g., answers enclosed in specific tags). SVLMs often exhibit limited instruction adherence, making it difficult for them to consistently produce such structured outputs. This lack of verifiable output leads to unstable exploration and a phenomenon referred to as "advantage collapse," where meaningful rewards cannot be reliably computed, hindering effective learning.
- **Inadequacy of Static Hybrid Approaches:** Recognizing the complementary strengths of SFT (for stability and memorization) and RLVR (for exploration and generalization), some researchers have explored hybrid methods. However, these typically involve static trade-offs, such as a fixed sequence of SFT followed by RL (two-stage training) or an empirically determined weighting of SFT and RL losses. The paper argues that for SVLMs, with their minimal capacity, the window for a successful static balance is exceptionally narrow, making these approaches brittle and prone to failure, often resulting in performance degradation below baseline levels.

Therefore, the key motivation is to overcome these fundamental challenges by developing a novel training paradigm that can intelligently manage the trade-off between memorization and exploration. This paradigm must dynamically adapt to the model's current state, ensuring that every update contributes to stable and effective learning without over-relying on the SVLM's constrained capacity or inconsistent instruction adherence. The ultimate goal is to enable SVLMs to achieve performance and reliability comparable to or exceeding LVLMs in specialized domains, making them viable for resource-constrained environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose DyME (Dynamic Memorize–Explore), a novel training paradigm designed to empower SVLMs with thinking capabilities. DyME integrates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a form of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR), through a dynamic switching mechanism, complemented by a synergistic Visual Supervision mechanism.

#### 3.1. Dynamic Memorize–Explore (DyME) Paradigm

DyME's core principle is to adaptively select between memorization (via SFT) and exploration (via GRPO) at each optimization step.

- **Overall Pipeline:** Each training step begins with an input consisting of an image and an instruction. The SVLM policy generates a set of K responses. Each response is parsed into a thinking trace and a final answer, which is then verified for correctness using predefined rules.
- **Dynamic Switching Mechanism (Equation 6):** The training mode is determined by the outcome of the response verification:
 - **GRPO Mode:** If at least one generated response is correct (i.e., `max_k r_a(ỹ_k) = 1`, where `r_a` is the answer reward), the model proceeds with GRPO-based exploration. This indicates that the current policy has discovered a feasible solution, allowing for safe exploitation of relative advantages to drive further exploration.
 - **SFT Mode:** If all generated responses are incorrect (including those that fail to parse), the model falls back to SFT-based memorization. In this scenario, answer rewards are negligible or zero, making GRPO updates unstable for SVLMs. SFT then provides a low-variance, ground-truth-guided gradient to correct the model's behavior.
- **GRPO Mode Refinement (Equation 7):** DyME refines the standard GRPO objective by incorporating an auxiliary reward `r_t` for thinking traces, in addition to the answer reward `r_a`. This `r_t` is computed by evaluating generated traces against expected thinking patterns (e.g., via token-level F1 score comparison with ground-truth). The policy is updated using a modified GRPO objective (`~L_GRPO`) that omits the KL penalty and clipping terms present in standard GRPO. This omission is justified by the dynamic integration with SFT, which inherently stabilizes training, allowing for a cleaner gradient form and smoother alignment.
- **SFT Mode Operation (Equation 1):** When in SFT mode, the model is optimized using the standard supervised loss, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth response `y` under the conditional distribution `p_θ(y | x)`.
- **DyME Objective (Equation 8):** The final DyME loss dynamically combines these two objectives using an indicator function, applying either the modified GRPO loss or the SFT loss based on the switching rule.

#### 3.2. Vision Supervision (Full DyME)

To maximize the SVLM's potential, DyME incorporates a synergistic Visual Supervision mechanism, which refines reward signals during exploration and enhances ground-truth during memorization.

- **Visual Facts (Ic):** These are fine-grained visual components (objects, attributes, states) extracted from each input image. They serve as evidence for evaluating generated responses and as building blocks for constructing ground-truth responses. Acquisition of `Ic` is automated using standard domain tools (e.g., BiomedGPT for medical images, DePlot for charts) or by prompting generalist LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5) to parse images into structured textual descriptions.
- **Visual Refiner:** This module produces visually grounded responses for SFT. High-scoring exploration traces, identified by the visual checker, are stored in a dynamic example pool. An LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B) then draws from this pool, integrating structural templates with visual facts from `Ic` and referencing collected examples, to generate structured, visually grounded ground-truth responses. This process transforms external ground-truth into grounded thinking traces, enriching the SFT targets.
- **Visual Checker:** This module evaluates the quality of self-generated thinking traces during GRPO. It assesses two dimensions: (i) whether the output contains sufficient correct visual elements compared to `Ic`, and (ii) whether it aligns stylistically with provided examples. The scores from the visual checker contribute to the thinking reward `r_t`.

The entire vision supervision process, including the acquisition of Visual Facts, evaluation by the Visual Checker, and synthesis by the Visual Refiner, is implemented through structured prompt engineering using a generalist LLM (Qwen2.5-14B).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate DyME were structured into two parts: Algorithmic Validation (Pure DyME) and System Effectiveness (Full DyME with Visual Supervision).

#### 4.1. Part I: Algorithmic Validation (Pure DyME)

- **Robustness to Data Quality:** Experiments on ChartQA using LLaVA-OV-S (0.5B parameters) demonstrated DyME's robustness. Pure DyME consistently outperformed SFT and two-stage baselines across datasets of varying quality (Low, Medium, High). For instance, Pure DyME trained on Medium quality data (64.9%) achieved higher performance than SFT trained on premium High (GPT-4o generated) data (61.6%). This indicates DyME's ability to maximize data efficiency and learn effectively from diverse data qualities.
- **Optimality of Binary Switching:** The binary switching mechanism of DyME (64.9%) was compared against alternative strategies like Reward Thresholding, SFT Annealing, and SFT Budget. The binary switch proved to be parameter-free, computationally efficient, and empirically optimal, outperforming other heuristics which either showed brittleness (Reward Thresholding), incurred heavy computational costs (SFT Annealing), or yielded inferior results (SFT Budget).
- **Mechanism Generality:** The core dynamic switching mechanism of DyME was shown to be generalizable. It improved the small-scale Qwen2.5-0.5B model on the text-only GSM8K benchmark to 55.3% (+5.8% over GRPO), suggesting its applicability beyond multimodal contexts. Furthermore, it enhanced the performance of a stronger VLM, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, on ChartQA to 89.6% (+2.3%), indicating scalability.

#### 4.2. Part II: System Effectiveness (Full DyME)

- **Consistent Performance Gains Across Diverse Domains:** The Full DyME pipeline, incorporating Visual Supervision, yielded substantial performance improvements across medical VQA, chart understanding, and geometry solving tasks. For instance, SmolVLM (0.5B) improved from 49.9% to 55.6% (+5.7%), LLaVA-OV-S (0.5B) from 50.7% to 55.4% (+4.7%), and InternVL2-S (0.5B) from 56.3% to 58.1% (+1.8%). In contrast, existing paradigms (SFT, GRPO, two-stage training) often led to performance degradation on SVLMs, validating the identified issues of pseudo thinking traces and advantage collapse, which DyME effectively mitigated.
- **Efficacy of Open-Source Supervision:** Full DyME trained with supervision from an accessible open-source model (Qwen2.5-14B) achieved 67.5% with LLaVA-OV-S, a performance level comparable to Pure DyME trained with expensive GPT-4o data (68.5%). This demonstrates that DyME can reduce dependency on costly proprietary data annotation.
- **Competitiveness with LVLMs:** DyME-trained SVLMs (e.g., SmolVLM achieving 55.6%, LLaVA-OV-S 55.4%) demonstrated performance comparable to or surpassing some stronger LVLMs like MoVA (54.2%) on specialized tasks. This suggests DyME enables SVLMs to become viable alternatives to LVLMs in resource-constrained, task-specific applications.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on LLaVA-OV-S quantified the contribution of DyME's components:
- **Dynamic Switching Mechanism:** Removing the memorization mode resulted in a significant performance drop (from 55.4% to 43.9%), indicating a catastrophic failure due to unstable exploration. Removing the exploration mode reduced performance to 50.4%, restricting the model to static, suboptimal imitation. This confirms the symbiotic and essential nature of both modes and their dynamic interplay for stability.
- **Visual Supervision:** Removing the visual checker and refiner led to performance decreases of 4.7% and 6.9% respectively. This highlights the role of visual supervision in bootstrapping from noisy, undesigned data by dynamically filtering and refining raw data into grounded visual facts, thereby enabling robust learning even from weaker supervision.

#### 4.4. Training Efficiency & Discussion

- **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Pure DyME, when using pre-constructed offline CoT data, maintained training throughput comparable to standard GRPO (∼14s/step) while offering superior performance. Full DyME, with its online visual supervision, introduced a computational overhead (∼1.6× training time) but provided data autonomy by enabling high-performance reasoning from open-source models, thereby bypassing expensive proprietary data annotation.
- **Sensitivity to External Model Capacity:** The external helper LLM's size (Qwen2.5-14B vs. 7B) had a negligible impact on Full DyME's performance, indicating that structured prompt engineering effectively decomposes complex reasoning tasks, allowing smaller external models to provide sufficient guidance.
- **Applicability of Visual Supervision:** The effectiveness of Visual Supervision is tied to the explicit extraction of Visual Facts, making it highly suitable for structured or semi-structured domains (e.g., charts, medical images). For domains with abstract semantics or unstructured perception, Pure DyME is suggested as a more robust alternative.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces DyME, a training paradigm that addresses a fundamental challenge in the development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): enabling sophisticated thinking capabilities within Small-scale VLMs (SVLMs). The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Empowering Resource-Constrained AI:** DyME provides a practical solution for instilling reasoning abilities in SVLMs, which are critical for deployment on edge devices and in other resource-constrained environments. This allows for the development of more intelligent and reliable AI systems in settings where large-scale VLMs are not feasible due to their computational and memory demands.
- **Enhanced Performance and Reliability for Specialized Tasks:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across diverse domains (medical VQA, chart understanding, geometry solving) indicate that DyME significantly enhances SVLMs' ability to handle both recognition-intensive and reasoning-intensive vision tasks. This improvement in performance, coupled with the mitigation of issues like pseudo thinking traces and advantage collapse, contributes to the overall reliability of SVLMs in specialized applications.
- **Reduced Dependency on High-Capacity Base Models:** By effectively managing the trade-off between memorization and exploration, DyME substantially reduces the reliance on the base VLM's initial capacity. This broadens the applicability of advanced reasoning techniques to a wider range of models, including those with very limited parameters.
- **Democratization of Advanced Training:** The finding that Full DyME, leveraging open-source supervision models (e.g., Qwen2.5-14B), can achieve performance comparable to methods relying on expensive, proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o for CoT data generation) is significant. This eliminates the need for costly data annotation, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible to researchers and developers without extensive financial resources.
- **Robust and Stable Learning:** DyME's dynamic switching mechanism and synergistic visual supervision effectively address key challenges (e.g., pseudo thinking traces and advantage collapse) that destabilize traditional training paradigms for SVLMs. This results in a more robust and stable learning process, leading to the generation of image-grounded and consistent thinking processes.
- **Generalizability of the Core Mechanism:** The demonstrated effectiveness of DyME's core dynamic switching across different modalities (visual and text-only) and its scalability to stronger base models suggest its potential for broader application in various small-parameter model training contexts beyond multimodal reasoning.

In summary, DyME represents an advancement in training methodology for SVLMs, facilitating the development of efficient, reliable, and intelligent AI systems for a growing array of specialized tasks in resource-limited settings.
