# GIMO: Gaze-Informed Human Motion Prediction in Context

> [!summary] Summary
> GIMO is a real-world dataset and network for gaze-informed human motion prediction. IMU motion capture plus a HoloLens2 headset yield synchronized SMPL-X body poses, egocentric video, 3D eye gaze, and scanned scenes across 19 environments. A bidirectional cross-modal transformer lets gaze and motion features refine each other, cutting destination translation error to 0.245 m and beating gaze-augmented baselines.

## Problem

- Scene-aware motion prediction models condition on geometry alone, leaving human intent unmodeled, so predictions stay ambiguous when many plausible goals fit the same scene.
- Existing datasets lack egocentric views with eye gaze aligned to accurate 3D body poses and scanned scenes, so intent-driven prediction could not be studied.
- Camera-array capture ties recordings to a few studio scenes and virtual-environment data risks being unrealistic, both limiting motion dynamics and scene diversity.

## Method

- **GIMO** dataset: IMU suit at 96 fps plus HoloLens2 gaze and depth, fitted to ==SMPL-X== and ICP-aligned with phone-scanned scenes, giving 217 intentional trajectories over 19 scenes.
- Gaze grounding: ==PointNet++== encodes the scene into global and per-point features; the 3D gaze point queries per-point features by inverse-distance interpolation over its three nearest neighbors.
- Bidirectional fusion: paired gaze-motion and motion-gaze ==cross-modal transformers== (6 layers, 256D) let each modality denoise the other, then a predictive cross-modal transformer decodes future SMPL-X poses.

## Results

- On known scenes destination translation error drops to **0.245 m** versus **0.316** for the best gaze-augmented baseline and **0.442** for MultimodalNet without gaze.
- Adding gaze helps every architecture: RNN destination translation falls **0.538** to **0.389**, MultimodalNet **0.442** to **0.316**; the full model reaches path MPJPE **160.3** on new scenes.
- Ablations: removing gaze raises destination translation from **0.245** to **0.393**; swapping the cross-modal transformer for a vanilla one gives **0.353**, and PointNet-only scene features generalize worse to new scenes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Eye gaze is a cheap surrogate for intent: it disambiguates long-horizon goals that scene geometry alone cannot, and helps regardless of predictor architecture.
> - Fusion design matters more than raw modality count: concatenation or vanilla attention underperforms bidirectional cross-modal attention, where motion denoises noisy gaze rather than only consuming it.
> - Local per-point scene features, not a single global vector, are what transfer to unseen environments; global-only encodings fit training scenes and fail on new geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Human motion prediction is a fundamental area of research with applications in assistive robotics, augmented reality (AR), and virtual reality (VR). Accurate prediction is contingent on understanding both the surrounding scene context and the underlying human intentions. Previous research in human motion modeling has explored various aspects, including high-quality motion capture datasets like CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database and AMASS, and benchmarks such as Human3.6M.

The field has evolved to incorporate scene context, with datasets such as GTA-1M and PROX enabling studies on scene-aware motion prediction and human interaction. Approaches using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), attention mechanisms, and spatio-temporal transformers have been developed to model motion dynamics and exploit spatial-temporal dependencies. Some works have also considered multi-modal inputs, such as music for dance generation.

A specific sub-area of human motion research involves ego-centric data, where information is collected from the perspective of the human subject, often using head-mounted cameras. Datasets like EgoPose, Kinpoly, and HPS provide ego-centric observations for motion estimation and prediction. However, existing ego-centric datasets often lack a combination of diverse 3D scenes, varied human motions with clear intentions, and simultaneously captured eye gaze information.

The critical gap identified by this work is the underexplored integration of eye gaze to infer human intent for motion prediction. While gaze is recognized as correlating with intention and influencing future actions, its effective utilization has been limited by the absence of suitable large-scale, high-quality datasets that provide synchronized 3D body pose, scene context, and ego-centric eye gaze. Current datasets suitable for ego-centric interactions typically offer limited diversity in scenes and motion dynamics, which constrains the generalizability and utility of gaze information for long-term human motion prediction. This research aims to address these limitations by proposing a novel dataset and a dedicated architecture for gaze-informed human motion prediction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance human motion prediction by leveraging eye gaze as a signal for inferring human intentions. The authors contend that incorporating eye gaze can reduce uncertainties in predicting future body movements, particularly in scenarios where initial movements for different tasks appear similar.

The motivation for this work stems from several points:

1. **Criticality for Human-Machine Interaction:** Accurate human motion prediction is essential for safe and comfortable interaction in applications such as assistive robots and AR/VR systems. Predicting a human's next action, such as sitting on a chair versus reaching for an object behind it, is crucial for collision avoidance and proactive system responses.
2. **Ambiguity in Early Motion Phases:** Human actions driven by different intents can exhibit similar initial body movements. For example, the initial steps towards a table might precede sitting, grabbing a cup, or interacting with another object. Eye gaze, however, often reveals distinctive scanning patterns that are correlated with different intentions even when body movements are not yet distinguishable. This suggests that gaze can serve as an early indicator of future actions, helping to disambiguate predictions.
3. **Cognitive Basis of Gaze:** A significant portion of the human brain's visual processing is dedicated to foveal vision, which is modulated by eye gaze. Foveal fixations provide fine details that inform future actions, reinforcing the hypothesis that gaze is a reliable surrogate for inferring human intent.
4. **Limitations of Existing Datasets:** A substantial motivation is the recognized deficiency in current human motion datasets. Most datasets either lack ego-centric views, 3D body pose, and corresponding eye gaze within the same 3D scene, or they offer limited diversity in scene contexts and motion dynamics. This lack of appropriate data hinders comprehensive study and evaluation of gaze's role in human motion prediction.
5. **Need for Robust Architectures:** Beyond data availability, effectively utilizing sparse eye gaze information in a multi-modal setting (gaze, human motion, scene geometry) presents a challenge. Not all gaze points are equally significant for inferring intent, and the system needs to discern relevant gaze cues from distractions. This necessitates a network architecture capable of efficient multi-modal fusion and discerning the significance of gaze in predicting future movements.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper are:
- To propose and release a large-scale, high-quality ego-centric human motion dataset named GIMO, which includes 3D body pose sequences, scene scans, and eye gaze information.
- To conduct an extensive study investigating the benefits of eye gaze for ego-centric human motion prediction using various state-of-the-art architectures.
- To develop a novel network architecture that facilitates bidirectional communication between gaze and motion branches to fully leverage gaze information by mutually disambiguating both modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a new dataset, GIMO, and a novel deep learning architecture to address gaze-informed human motion prediction.

#### 3.1. GIMO Dataset Creation

The GIMO dataset is designed to provide high-quality, synchronized multi-modal data for gaze-informed human motion prediction in diverse real-world environments.

**Hardware Setup:**
- **Motion Capture:** An Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU)-based system, specifically Noitom PERCEPTION NEURON STUDIO, was used. It captures 3D body and hand joint movements at 96 frames per second (fps). This system allows for data collection in varied environments without extensive setup constraints typical of camera-based systems.
- **Gaze Capture:** A Hololens2 augmented reality device was employed, utilizing its Research Mode API. This device records 3D eye gaze points, ego-centric video (30 fps, 760x428 resolution), long-throw depth streams (1-5 fps, 512x512 resolution), and 6D poses of the head-mounted camera.
- **3D Scene Acquisition:** An iPhone 13 Pro Max, equipped with LiDAR sensors, was used to scan environments via a 3D Scanner App. This produced detailed 3D meshes (approx. 500k vertices with photorealistic texture) of the scenes, providing rich geometric context.

**Data Collection with Human in Action:**
- **Subjects and Activities:** 11 university students (4 female, 7 male) were recruited. They performed activities categorized as "Resting," "Interacting with objects," and "Changing the state of objects" (Table 2 in paper). These activities were designed to be long-term with clear semantic intentions, such as walking to open a door.
- **Procedure:**
 1. Subjects donned the Hololens2, IMU motion capture suit, and gloves, followed by system calibration.
 2. Subjects selected an action from a predefined list, considering the scene's affordances.
 3. The 3D scene was scanned.
 4. Subjects executed the planned activities while data from all sensors were collected.
 5. Objects were reset to their original states if the scene geometry was altered.
- **Scale:** The dataset comprises 129,000 ego-centric images, 11 subjects, 217 motion trajectories, and data from 19 diverse indoor scenes.

**Data Preparation:**
- **Synchronization:** A unique hand gesture, observable in the ego-centric view, was used as a starting signal to synchronize the motion capture system and Hololens2 data streams based on their timestamps.
- **Parametric Model Fitting:** The SMPL-X model was fitted to the 3D joints (23 body, 15 left-hand, 15 right-hand) derived from IMU signals, along with the 6D head pose, to obtain full-body pose and hand gestures. This IMU-based approach avoids errors associated with 2D-3D pose estimation from monocular RGB.
- **Alignment:** The Hololens2 coordinate system and the fitted SMPL-X models were aligned with the high-quality 3D scene scans. This involved an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm between the Hololens2's TSDF reconstruction and the 3D scene, followed by transforming SMPL-X sequences using human annotations (start/end body shapes) as anchors.

#### 3.2. Gaze-Informed Human Motion Prediction Architecture

The proposed network aims to predict future human motion (X_t:t+T) given past motion (X_1:t), corresponding 3D gaze (G_1:t), and the 3D scene (S). The motion is represented by SMPL-X parameters (global translation `t`, global orientation `r`, body pose embedding `h`, shape `β`, and hand pose `p`), but the current implementation focuses on {`t`, `r`, `h`}.

**Multi-modal Feature Extraction:**
- **Scene Feature Extraction:** PointNet++ is used to process the input 3D scene point cloud `S`, yielding a per-point feature map (F_P ∈ R^(n x dp)) and a global scene descriptor (F_O ∈ R^do). Features for arbitrary points (like gaze points or SMPL-X vertices) are interpolated from F_P using inverse distance weighting.
- **Gaze Feature Extraction:** The gaze point feature (f_g) is directly queried from the per-point scene feature map F_P at the 3D gaze intersection point `g`, integrating scene context into the gaze representation.
- **Motion Feature Extraction:** A linear layer extracts a base motion embedding (f_m) from the input motion parameter `x`. To contextualize motion with the 3D scene, ambient scene context features (f_mv) are derived by feeding the F_P features of SMPL-X vertices (of the current pose) into a PointNet.

**Attention-Aware Multi-modal Feature Fusion (Bidirectional Scheme):**
The core of the architecture is a bidirectional fusion scheme utilizing cross-modal transformers to integrate motion, gaze, and scene features.
- **Cross-modal Transformer:** This component, based on the attention mechanism, captures correlations between input embedding sequences and facilitates information exchange across modalities.
- **Motion Feature Fusion:**
 1. The base motion feature (f_m) is first updated by the scene context feature (f_mv) using a motion-scene transformer: f_ms = cross_trans(f_mv, f_m). This imbues `f_ms` with 3D scene awareness.
 2. `f_ms` is then further refined by the gaze feature (f_g) using a motion-gaze transformer: f_mg = cross_trans(f_g, f_ms). This step integrates intention clues from gaze into the motion representation.
- **Gaze Feature Fusion:** Recognizing that not all gaze points convey meaningful intent, a gaze-motion transformer updates the gaze feature. The motion embedding (f_m) serves as the query to refine the gaze feature (f_g): f_gm = cross_trans(f_m, f_g). This allows the network to learn which gaze features are more relevant to the predicted motion.

**Motion Prediction:**
The bidirectionally fused gaze feature (f_gm), the intention-aware motion feature (f_mg), and the global scene feature (F_O) are concatenated. This combined representation, along with temporal positional encodings, is fed into a final cross-modal transformer to predict the future motion sequence (X_t:t+T).

**Training:**
The network is trained using an L1 loss function, comprising terms for global translation, global orientation (represented as axis angle), and pose embedding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the GIMO dataset and the efficacy of gaze information and the proposed architecture for human motion prediction.

#### 4.1. GIMO Dataset Contributions

- The GIMO dataset is introduced as a large-scale, real-world ego-centric dataset that uniquely combines high-quality 3D body pose sequences, 3D scene context, and eye gaze information. It addresses the existing gap in datasets by offering diverse scenes and long-term, semantically meaningful human activities captured with IMU-based motion capture and Hololens2. The dataset includes 129k ego-centric images, 11 subjects, 217 motion trajectories, and 19 scenes.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Gaze Information

- **Quantitative Improvement:** The integration of eye gaze consistently leads to improved human motion prediction accuracy across various state-of-the-art baselines. For instance, an RNN-based method showed significant improvements in destination and path errors when gaze input was included, compared to its motion-only variant. Similarly, a MultimodalNet architecture demonstrated enhanced performance with gaze.
- **Qualitative Disambiguation:** Qualitative results (Figure 4) illustrate that gaze information helps disambiguate future actions. For example, in situations where initial body movements are similar, gaze patterns enable the model to predict distinct intentions, such as sitting versus grabbing an object.
- **Intent Indicator:** Analysis of attention maps (Figure 5) reveals that gaze features strongly influence the ending poses of the predicted motion, indicating that eye gaze serves as a robust indicator of the subject's final goal or destination.

#### 4.3. Proposed Architecture Performance

- **Overall Superiority:** The proposed GIMO network achieves the top performance among all evaluated methods in human motion prediction on the GIMO dataset. This is demonstrated by lower destination errors (global translation, orientation, and Mean Per-Joint Position Error - MPJPE) and path errors (mean error over the entire predicted path).
- **Generalization to New Environments:** The GIMO network maintains its performance advantage on both known scenes (from the training set) and new, unseen environments (scanned solely for evaluation), indicating its generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study Results

The ablation studies confirm the contributions of specific components of the GIMO architecture:

- **Role of Gaze:** Re-confirms that gaze input is a critical factor for accuracy, significantly improving both destination and path prediction metrics across baselines and the authors' own method.
- **PointNet++ for Scene Feature Query:** Using PointNet++ for extracting per-point scene features, which in turn informs gaze and motion features, outperforms a simpler PointNet-based approach. This difference is particularly pronounced when generalizing to new environments, suggesting that PointNet++'s ability to capture local geometric structures is beneficial for robust scene understanding.
- **Cross-Modal Transformer:** The use of a cross-modal transformer for multi-modal fusion yields superior performance compared to a vanilla transformer architecture. The vanilla transformer variant showed a loss of accuracy, especially in new scenes, and in some cases, its path error was even higher than models without gaze input. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed bidirectional fusion scheme in capturing complex correlations between multi-modal inputs.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation and Limitations

- **Human Gaze Interpretation:** A human evaluation experiment (Appendix C.1) showed that human subjects could more accurately infer the final pose of a motion trajectory when provided with ego-centric video that included gaze information, underscoring the intuitiveness of gaze as an intent cue.
- **Failure Cases:** The method's performance can degrade when input gazes are noisy or convey little meaningful intent, particularly with sparse inputs. This highlights a dependency on the quality and informativeness of the gaze data for robust prediction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper carries several significant implications and potential impacts across various domains:

1. **Addressing a Critical Data Gap:** The introduction of the GIMO dataset is a substantial contribution. By providing synchronized, high-quality 3D body pose, ego-centric eye gaze, and dense 3D scene context in diverse real-world environments, GIMO directly addresses a long-standing data deficiency in the field. This dataset is poised to facilitate further research into gaze-informed human motion prediction, intention-aware motion synthesis, and ego-centric pose estimation.
2. **Advancement in Human Motion Prediction:** The work empirically demonstrates that integrating eye gaze significantly enhances the accuracy of long-term human motion prediction. This is particularly impactful for resolving ambiguities in early motion phases, where initial body movements may not clearly signal intent. This advancement can lead to more reliable and precise predictions of human actions.
3. **Novel Multi-Modal Fusion Architecture:** The proposed GIMO network, with its bidirectional multi-modal fusion scheme, represents a methodological step forward in integrating disparate data modalities. By allowing gaze and motion features to mutually inform and disambiguate each other, the architecture leverages the full potential of gaze information while also mitigating its potential noise or irrelevance in certain contexts. This fusion strategy can inspire future designs for multi-modal learning.
4. **Enhanced Human-Machine Interaction:** The ability to predict human intent through gaze cues has direct and substantial implications for human-machine interaction systems.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Robots can anticipate user needs more accurately, enabling safer and more collaborative interaction by avoiding collisions and proactively assisting with tasks.
 - **AR/VR Applications:** AR/VR headsets can provide more natural and intuitive interfaces, offer timely warnings, or adapt virtual environments based on predicted user actions, thereby improving comfort and immersion. For instance, an AR system could pre-fetch information about an object a user is gazing at, or a VR environment could dynamically adjust elements based on anticipated movements.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a new benchmark and task definition (gaze-informed human motion prediction), opening new directions for research. Future work could explore incorporating visual information from ego-centric images to further boost accuracy, investigate how to robustly handle noisy or uninformative gaze, or extend the prediction to more detailed elements like hand poses and subtle body shape changes. The dataset's diversity in scenes and activities also allows for studies into environment-specific intentions and motion patterns.

In summary, this research provides a comprehensive resource and a robust methodological framework for integrating eye gaze into human motion prediction, laying a foundation for more intelligent and responsive human-centered AI systems.
