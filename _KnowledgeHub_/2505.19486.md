# VLMLight: Safety-Critical Traffic Signal Control via Vision-Language Meta-Control and Dual-Branch Reasoning Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed VLMLight, a hybrid traffic signal control framework that integrates vision-language models with a dual-branch reasoning architecture. The system reduced emergency vehicle waiting times by over 60% compared to RL-only baselines while maintaining comparable routine traffic efficiency (less than 1% degradation).

## Problem

- Existing traffic signal control methods struggle to balance traffic efficiency with safety-critical situations, often neglecting semantic understanding crucial for emergency vehicle prioritization.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for traffic control often rely on abstract state representations, missing rich visual cues and failing to generalize to complex or 'long-tail' scenarios.
- Large Language Model (LLM) based control offers reasoning capabilities but suffers from high inference latency, making it impractical for real-time, dynamic traffic management as a standalone solution.

## Method

- **VLMLight** employs a ==dual-branch== architecture with a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for scene understanding and an LLM-based meta-controller that dynamically switches between a fast RL policy for routine traffic and a deliberative LLM policy for safety-critical events.
- A custom-built, open-source traffic simulator provides multi-view visual inputs, enabling the VLM to extract rich semantic information like vehicle types and spatial density from images.
- The deliberative LLM branch utilizes a team of specialized agents (Phase Reasoning, Signal Planning, Rule Verification) to process safety-critical scenarios with interpretability and ensure rule compliance.

## Results

- VLMLight achieved a reduction of over **60%** in Average Emergency Travel Time (AETT) and Average Emergency Waiting Time (AEWT) compared to state-of-the-art RL baselines across various real-world intersections.
- Under routine traffic conditions, VLMLight maintained efficiency, showing less than a **1%** degradation in Average Travel Time (ATT) and Average Waiting Time (AWT) compared to top-performing RL methods.
- The total inference latency for the deliberative reasoning process remained within 11.5 seconds, demonstrating practical feasibility for real-time urban deployment where signal phase buffers are typically longer (e.g., 13 seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically switching between a fast, efficiency-focused RL policy and a deliberative, safety-prioritized LLM policy allows for effective real-time control across diverse traffic conditions.
> - Integrating vision-language models enables the system to leverage rich semantic cues from visual data, such as emergency vehicle identification, which is vital for context-aware and safety-critical decision-making.
> - A modular, multi-agent LLM reasoning process provides interpretability and auditable justifications for control actions, enhancing trustworthiness in safety-critical applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Efficient urban traffic management remains a critical global challenge, with congestion leading to significant economic, environmental, and social costs. Traffic Signal Control (TSC) at intersections is a pivotal component in mitigating these issues. Historically, TSC methods have evolved through several paradigms, each with inherent limitations that VLMLight aims to address.

#### 1.1. Traditional Traffic Signal Control Methods

Traditional TSC approaches, such as Webster’s method, MaxPressure control, and Self-Organizing Traffic Lights (SOTL), rely on predefined rules, fixed timing plans, or heuristic-based logic. While these methods are robust and reliable under stable and predictable traffic conditions, their rigidity makes them ill-suited for the dynamic, stochastic, and often unpredictable realities of modern urban networks. They struggle to adapt to unexpected traffic patterns, special events, or safety-critical situations, leading to inefficiencies and potential hazards.

#### 1.2. Reinforcement Learning for TSC

To overcome the static nature of traditional methods, reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for TSC. RL agents learn optimal signal phase adjustments by interacting with the traffic environment and optimizing a reward function (e.g., minimizing delay or maximizing throughput). Studies like IntelliLight, UniTSA, and A-CATs have demonstrated RL's adaptability and effectiveness in routine traffic scenarios. However, most RL-based methods rely on simplified, vectorized state representations (e.g., queue lengths, intersection pressure). These abstractions often miss semantically rich cues, such as vehicle types, specific motion patterns, or the precise spatial density, that are crucial for context-dependent decisions. Consequently, RL agents trained solely on efficiency metrics can fall short in safety-critical situations, where semantic understanding and prioritization of emergency vehicles are paramount. Integrating priority mechanisms into existing RL controllers also presents a significant challenge due to architectural and reward formulation differences.

#### 1.3. Large Language Models for TSC

More recently, the advent of large language models (LLMs) has opened new avenues for incorporating high-level reasoning and generalization into TSC. LLM-based approaches aim to handle complex edge cases and long-tail scenarios that challenge conventional RL. However, current LLM-based systems often depend on templated or manually crafted textual descriptions of traffic scenes, which suffer from information loss compared to real-world visual data. Furthermore, the multi-step reasoning inherent in LLMs typically leads to inference speeds orders of magnitude slower than RL controllers, rendering them impractical as standalone real-time controllers in dynamic traffic environments.

#### 1.4. Limitations of Existing Simulators

A significant bottleneck hindering the advancement of visually grounded TSC is the inadequacy of existing traffic simulators. Widely used simulators like SUMO and CityFlow primarily provide statistical data for traffic intersections and lack the capability to render real-time, multi-view visual inputs. While high-fidelity driving simulators like CARLA offer photorealistic rendering, they are mainly designed for autonomous driving and lack native support for comprehensive signal scheduling or require extensive customization for TSC research. Even newer datasets like SynTraC, which provide image-based data for TSC, are often limited to single-intersection scenarios and lack configurable multi-view camera support, restricting their applicability to broader traffic management research.

#### 1.5. VLMLight's Position

VLMLight positions itself as a pioneering solution that simultaneously addresses these multifaceted limitations. It is the first framework to integrate vision-language meta-control with a dual-branch reasoning architecture, directly leveraging rich visual cues through a novel image-based simulator. By dynamically switching between a fast RL policy for routine efficiency and a deliberative, safety-prioritized LLM reasoning branch for critical events, VLMLight seeks to achieve a harmonious balance between real-time performance, semantic understanding, interpretability, and robust safety in urban traffic signal control. This hybrid approach aims to bridge the gap between low-latency reactive control and high-level proactive reasoning, moving TSC closer to intelligent, human-like decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of VLMLight stem from the recognized shortcomings of existing Traffic Signal Control (TSC) methods in handling the complex, dynamic, and safety-critical nature of urban mobility.

The core motivation is to develop a TSC framework that can **balance efficiency and safety in real-time decision-making**. While traditional and even many reinforcement learning (RL) methods prioritize traffic flow and delay reduction, they often struggle with or explicitly neglect safety-critical scenarios, such as the presence of emergency vehicles.

Specifically, VLMLight aims to:

1. **Overcome the generalization limitations of existing TSC methods:** Rule-based heuristics are too rigid for dynamic environments, and RL-based approaches, despite their adaptability, often struggle to generalize to complex, unexpected, or "long-tail" scenarios due to simplified state representations and static reward formulations.
2. **Incorporate rich, semantically meaningful visual cues into TSC:** Current RL systems rely on abstract, vectorized state representations (e.g., queue lengths, pressure), which omit crucial semantic information like vehicle type (e.g., ambulance, fire truck), exact spatial configuration, and dynamic motion patterns. VLMLight seeks to leverage visual perception to extract these rich cues for more informed decisions.
3. **Enable safety-prioritized decision-making:** A fundamental goal is to ensure that safety-critical events, particularly those involving emergency vehicles, are handled with immediate and appropriate priority, a capability often lacking or inadequately addressed in efficiency-focused RL systems.
4. **Mitigate the latency issues of LLM-based control:** While large language models (LLMs) offer strong reasoning capabilities, their multi-step inference can be too slow for real-time traffic control. VLMLight aims to selectively employ LLMs only when their deliberative reasoning is truly necessary, avoiding their computational overhead during routine operations.
5. **Develop a vision-enabled traffic simulator:** A crucial enabler for vision-language integration is the availability of a simulator that can provide multi-view visual inputs. VLMLight explicitly motivates and contributes the development of such a simulator, which is currently lacking in mainstream TSC research.
6. **Create an interpretable and auditable control system:** By integrating LLM-based reasoning, VLMLight aims to provide not just decisions, but also justifications and rule compliance verification, enhancing the interpretability and trustworthiness of the control actions, especially in high-stakes situations.
7. **Achieve a scalable and robust solution:** The framework seeks to offer a solution that maintains high efficiency in routine traffic while providing robust and safety-aware responses during critical events, making it suitable for practical deployment in next-generation traffic management systems.

In essence, VLMLight is motivated by the need for a more intelligent, context-aware, and human-mimetic TSC system that can effectively manage the dual demands of traffic efficiency and public safety by seamlessly integrating perceptual understanding with advanced reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLMLight proposes a novel, hybrid traffic signal control (TSC) framework that intricately integrates vision-language understanding with a dual-branch reasoning architecture. The methodology centers around balancing the speed of learned policies with the interpretability and robustness of language-based reasoning, all grounded in rich visual input.

#### 3.1. Vision-Based TSC Simulator

A cornerstone of VLMLight's methodology is its **custom-built traffic simulator**, which is highlighted as the first of its kind to enable multi-view visual inputs at urban intersections for TSC. Unlike conventional simulators (e.g., SUMO, CityFlow) that provide only statistical data, this simulator allows for configurable camera placement, replicating real-world monitoring setups. It provides both a bird’s-eye view for overall traffic flow and directional views from each approach. This real-time image rendering capability is critical, as it allows policies to reason over rich visual cues such such as vehicle types, spatial density, and dynamic motion patterns, which are otherwise inaccessible to traditional state representations.

#### 3.2. Scene Understanding via VLM

The first stage of VLMLight involves **Scene Understanding**, executed by a dedicated `Agent_Scene`. This agent utilizes a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen2.5-VL-32B in the experiments, to process the multi-view images captured by the simulator. For each directional image (e.g., North, East, South, West), the VLM generates a **natural language textual summary** describing the traffic conditions. These descriptions include lane-level semantics, congestion levels, and, crucially, the presence of any emergency or special vehicles (e.g., ambulances, fire trucks). This process transforms raw visual data into interpretable, structured semantic descriptions, forming the perceptual basis for all subsequent decision-making.

#### 3.3. Safety-Prioritized Meta-Control

Following scene understanding, a **Safety-Prioritized Meta-Controller** (`Agent_ModeSelector`), implemented as an LLM agent (Qwen2.5-72B in experiments), analyzes the textual scene descriptions. Its primary role is to act as an intelligent router, dynamically deciding whether the current traffic situation requires fast, low-latency control or deliberate, high-level reasoning. If the `Agent_ModeSelector` detects routine traffic conditions (e.g., no special vehicles, normal congestion) consistent with the RL policy's training distribution, it routes control to the **Routine Control Policy (RL branch)**. However, if it identifies safety-critical conditions (e.g., emergency vehicle detection, conflicting traffic goals, abnormal congestion), it activates the **Deliberative Reasoning Policy (LLM branch)**. This meta-control mechanism is key to balancing responsiveness and adaptability.

#### 3.4. Routine Control Policy (RL Branch)

For routine traffic, VLMLight employs a **fast Reinforcement Learning (RL) decision branch**.
- **State Representation:** The current intersection state is constructed using recent statistics (over the past five time steps) across up to 12 traffic movements (e.g., vehicle flow, occupancy, signal status), forming a spatio-temporal input tensor. Each movement vector includes features like average vehicle flow, max/mean occupancy, movement type (straight, left, right), lane count, and signal status.
- **Architecture:** A lightweight Transformer-based module encodes this spatio-temporal input. It first captures spatial dependencies among concurrent movements and then models temporal dynamics across frames.
- **Action Space:** The RL agent selects a traffic signal phase from a predefined discrete action set.
- **Training:** The RL policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), with a reward function designed to minimize intersection-level congestion and delay.
This branch ensures efficient, low-latency control for the majority of non-critical traffic scenarios.

#### 3.5. Deliberative Reasoning Policy (LLM Branch)

When safety-critical or unusual conditions are detected, VLMLight activates its **slow, deliberative reasoning branch**, which involves a team of specialized LLM agents collaborating through structured natural language dialogue. This process unfolds in three sequential stages:

1. **Phase Reasoning (`Agent_Phase`):** This agent takes the directional scene descriptions (from `Agent_Scene`) and reorganizes them into phase-level traffic summaries. Using a predefined mapping from traffic movements to signal phases, it aggregates relevant semantic details (vehicle types, congestion levels) for each candidate phase. This step is crucial for bridging low-level visual perception with the discrete action space of traffic phases.
2. **Signal Planning (`Agent_Plan`):** The `Agent_Plan` evaluates the phase-level descriptions, considering control objectives such as minimizing delay, prioritizing emergency vehicles, or balancing directional load. It then selects the most appropriate traffic phase and generates a textual explanation justifying its decision. This rationale provides interpretability and auditability.
3. **Rule Verification (`Agent_Check`):** The final `Agent_Check` agent verifies whether the chosen action (`a_LLM_t`) complies with the current set of feasible signal phases. If the action is valid, it is executed. If not, the agent selects an alternative from the feasible set that best aligns with the original intent while maintaining safety. The final decision is formatted as a JSON object for execution. A fallback mechanism to the RL policy is in place if multiple verification attempts fail.

This modular, multi-agent LLM approach ensures that complex scenarios requiring nuanced understanding and rule compliance are handled robustly and transparently, mimicking a human-like deliberation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VLMLight across three real-world intersections (Songdo, Yau Ma Tei, and Massy), representing diverse urban settings and traffic patterns, yielded compelling results that validate its hybrid architecture and safety-critical focus. The key findings are summarized below:

#### 4.1. Performance under Routine Traffic Scenarios

VLMLight demonstrates remarkable efficiency in handling routine traffic conditions, maintaining performance comparable to or slightly better than the best-performing RL-only baselines.
- **Marginal Degradation:** Compared to top-tier RL methods like UniTSA and IntelliLight, VLMLight exhibited less than a **1% degradation** in Average Travel Time (ATT) and Average Waiting Time (AWT) across all datasets. For instance, in Songdo, ATT increased marginally from 86.80 s (UniTSA) to 87.14 s (VLMLight), and AWT from 39.53 s to 39.73 s. This confirms the effectiveness of the LLM-based meta-controller in correctly deferring to the fast RL branch for common scenarios, avoiding unnecessary computational overhead from the deliberative LLM.
- **Superior to Vanilla-VLM:** In contrast, the "Vanilla-VLM" baseline, which relies solely on vision-language reasoning without the dual-branch architecture, performed significantly worse in routine traffic. This highlights the inefficiency of using LLMs for all control decisions and underscores the importance of VLMLight's selective activation strategy.

#### 4.2. Emergency Vehicle Prioritization in Safety-Critical Scenarios

The most significant and impactful findings revolve around VLMLight's performance in safety-critical scenarios, specifically its ability to prioritize emergency vehicles.
- **Substantial Reduction in Waiting Times:** VLMLight dramatically reduced both Average Emergency Travel Time (AETT) and Average Emergency Waiting Time (AEWT) by **over 60%** compared to RL-only baselines across all datasets. For example, in the Songdo dataset, AEWT dropped from approximately 22.0 s (RL baselines) to just 7.48 s with VLMLight. In Yau Ma Tei, AEWT reduced from 5.17 s to 2.17 s, and in Massy, from 7.81 s to 2.60 s.
- **Improved over Vanilla-VLM in Criticality:** While Vanilla-VLM also showed benefits in emergency vehicle handling due to explicit scene reasoning, its lack of structured phase mapping and decision modularity often led to action errors, particularly in complex intersections. VLMLight's decomposed reasoning steps (Phase Reasoning, Signal Planning, Rule Verification) ensure correct, auditable, and reliable actions for high-priority traffic.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Case studies (Appendix D) visually illustrate how VLMLight's deliberative reasoning branch successfully detects emergency vehicles (e.g., ambulances, fire trucks) and promptly adjusts signal phases to grant them priority, ensuring unhindered passage.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Inference Latency

Ablation studies provided insights into the contributions of individual components of the deliberative reasoning branch and the system's real-time feasibility.
- **Importance of Phase Reasoning:** Disabling `Agent_Phase` (the phase reasoning module) led to a notable increase in AEWT (e.g., from 48.88 s to 58.93 s in Songdo for AEWT), confirming its crucial role in abstracting low-level visual features into structured, legally grounded traffic phase representations for effective downstream reasoning.
- **Impact of Rule Verification:** Removing `Agent_Check` (rule verification) had a limited effect on overall performance, suggesting that invalid actions are rare when the preceding reasoning steps are robust.
- **Feasible Inference Latency:** Crucially, the total inference latency for the entire deliberative reasoning process (including Scene Understanding, Mode Selection, Phase Reasoning, Planning, and Verification) remained within **11.5 seconds** across all environments (e.g., 11.48 s for Songdo, 9.36 s for Yau Ma Tei, 9.35 s for Massy). This latency is well within the typical signal phase buffer (e.g., 10s green + 3s yellow = 13s), demonstrating the practical feasibility of VLMLight for real-time urban deployment. Scene understanding consumes the majority of this time, while the LLM reasoning steps are comparatively faster.
- **VLM/LLM Model Sensitivity:** Further ablation on different VLM/LLM models (Appendix C.2) revealed that the `Agent_Scene` (scene understanding) is the most sensitive module. The accuracy of the VLM in identifying special vehicles directly impacts the system's ability to prioritize them, while `Agent_ModeSelector` and the deliberative reasoning agents are more resilient to model changes for their text-based tasks.

In summary, VLMLight successfully demonstrates a robust trade-off between traffic efficiency and safety-aware decision-making, setting a new benchmark for intelligent traffic signal control by effectively integrating vision-language understanding and dual-branch reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VLMLight represents a significant advancement in the field of intelligent traffic signal control (TSC), offering several profound implications and potential impacts on urban mobility and AI research.

#### 5.1. Contributions to Safety and Efficiency

The most immediate and critical impact of VLMLight is its ability to **drastically improve safety in urban traffic management**. By reducing emergency vehicle waiting times by up to 65% and travel times by over 60% compared to RL-only systems, VLMLight directly contributes to faster emergency response times, which can be life-saving. This is achieved without compromising routine traffic efficiency, as the system maintains comparable performance to state-of-the-art RL baselines in standard conditions (less than 1% degradation). This dual achievement of enhanced safety and maintained efficiency is a hallmark of truly intelligent and reliable urban infrastructure. The dynamic selection between a fast RL policy and a deliberative LLM policy allows for optimal resource allocation – speed for routine, deliberation for critical.

#### 5.2. Interpretability and Trustworthiness

The integration of LLMs for deliberative reasoning introduces unprecedented levels of **interpretability and trustworthiness** into TSC systems. Unlike black-box RL models, VLMLight's LLM agents can provide textual explanations and justifications for their decisions, as well as verify rule compliance. This human-like deliberation process makes the control actions auditable, crucial for deploying AI in safety-critical public infrastructure where transparency and accountability are paramount. This feature addresses a major challenge in deploying complex AI systems in the real world: the need for understandable decision-making.

#### 5.3. Open-Source Simulator for Future Research

A key contribution of this work, beyond the control framework itself, is the **release of the first vision-based TSC simulator with multi-view image inputs and dynamic scene rendering capabilities**. This open-source simulator is a valuable resource for the research community. It fills a critical gap, enabling future research into perceptually grounded, context-aware, and interpretable traffic control strategies that were previously hindered by the lack of visual simulation environments. This will accelerate the development and evaluation of new vision-language models and AI approaches for various aspects of urban mobility.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

Despite its groundbreaking contributions, VLMLight also acknowledges several limitations that point towards crucial future research directions:
- **Environmental Robustness:** The current simulator lacks diverse weather and lighting conditions. Future work needs to evaluate and enhance the visual robustness of the system under challenging real-world environmental factors (rain, fog, snow, night-time, glare).
- **Realism of Scene Elements:** The current simulation environment lacks pedestrians, bicycles, and other diverse real-world elements. Incorporating these dynamic agents would enhance realism and necessitate more complex safety reasoning.
- **Scalability to Multi-Intersection Networks:** All current experiments are conducted on single intersections. Extending VLMLight to multi-intersection settings requires substantial research into the scalability, coordination, and distributed reasoning of vision-language agents across a broader traffic network, which is a major challenge for any advanced TSC system.
- **Dependency on Large Models:** VLMLight's performance is currently tied to large Vision-Language Models (VLM) and Large Language Models (LLM), which can be computationally intensive. Future work will focus on fine-tuning smaller, more efficient models to improve both traffic scene recognition accuracy and inference speed, making the system more deployable on resource-constrained edge devices.

In conclusion, VLMLight lays a robust foundation for the next generation of intelligent traffic signal control. By integrating rich visual perception, advanced vision-language reasoning, and a dual-branch architecture for dynamic decision-making, it offers a scalable, interpretable, and safety-aware solution that has the potential to significantly enhance urban mobility, reduce congestion, and save lives. Its open-source simulator further empowers the broader research community to push these boundaries, fostering innovation in AI for critical infrastructure.
