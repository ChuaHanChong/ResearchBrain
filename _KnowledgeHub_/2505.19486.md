# VLMLight: Safety-Critical Traffic Signal Control via Vision-Language Meta-Control and Dual-Branch Reasoning Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed VLMLight, a hybrid traffic signal control framework that integrates vision-language models with a dual-branch reasoning architecture. The system reduced emergency vehicle waiting times by over 60% compared to RL-only baselines while maintaining comparable routine traffic efficiency (less than 1% degradation).

## Problem

- Existing traffic signal control methods struggle to balance traffic efficiency with safety-critical situations, often neglecting semantic understanding crucial for emergency vehicle prioritization.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) approaches for traffic control often rely on abstract state representations, missing rich visual cues and failing to generalize to complex or 'long-tail' scenarios.
- Large Language Model (LLM) based control offers reasoning capabilities but suffers from high inference latency, making it impractical for real-time, dynamic traffic management as a standalone solution.

## Method

- **VLMLight** employs a ==dual-branch== architecture with a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for scene understanding and an LLM-based meta-controller that dynamically switches between a fast RL policy for routine traffic and a deliberative LLM policy for safety-critical events.
- A custom-built, open-source traffic simulator provides multi-view visual inputs, enabling the VLM to extract rich semantic information like vehicle types and spatial density from images.
- The deliberative LLM branch utilizes a team of specialized agents (Phase Reasoning, Signal Planning, Rule Verification) to process safety-critical scenarios with interpretability and ensure rule compliance.

## Results

- VLMLight achieved a reduction of over **60%** in Average Emergency Travel Time (AETT) and Average Emergency Waiting Time (AEWT) compared to state-of-the-art RL baselines across various real-world intersections.
- Under routine traffic conditions, VLMLight maintained efficiency, showing less than a **1%** degradation in Average Travel Time (ATT) and Average Waiting Time (AWT) compared to top-performing RL methods.
- The total inference latency for the deliberative reasoning process remained within 11.5 seconds, demonstrating practical feasibility for real-time urban deployment where signal phase buffers are typically longer (e.g., 13 seconds).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically switching between a fast, efficiency-focused RL policy and a deliberative, safety-prioritized LLM policy allows for effective real-time control across diverse traffic conditions.
> - Integrating vision-language models enables the system to leverage rich semantic cues from visual data, such as emergency vehicle identification, which is vital for context-aware and safety-critical decision-making.
> - A modular, multi-agent LLM reasoning process provides interpretability and auditable justifications for control actions, enhancing trustworthiness in safety-critical applications.
