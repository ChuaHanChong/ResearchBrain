# NFT: Bridging Supervised Learning and Reinforcement Learning in Math Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Negative-aware Fine-Tuning (NFT) introduces a supervised learning method that enables large language models to learn from self-generated incorrect answers in math reasoning tasks. This approach achieves competitive performance with reinforcement learning algorithms while offering a more memory-efficient alternative for autonomous model improvement.

## Problem

- Supervised Learning (SL) traditionally struggles with verification-driven training for Large Language Models (LLMs) due to its reliance on positive examples and lack of mechanisms to learn from negative feedback.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been the dominant framework for LLM self-improvement via binary verifiers, perceived as uniquely capable of utilizing mistakes for autonomous growth.
- Existing SL baselines like Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) discard self-generated incorrect answers, preventing LLMs from reflecting on failures and limiting their self-improvement capacity.

## Method

- **NFT** constructs an '==implicit negative policy==' by re-parameterizing it using the target positive policy and the observed correctness rates of the original LLM's generations.
- It optimizes a combined maximum-likelihood objective function that leverages both correct and incorrect self-generated answers to refine the positive policy.
- The method incorporates practical enhancements such as token-level loss calculation, clipping the negative likelihood ratio for stability, and prompt weighting to prioritize more challenging questions during training.

## Results

- NFT achieved an average accuracy of **51.7%** on math benchmarks with **7B** models, matching or slightly outperforming state-of-the-art RL methods like DAPO (**51.2%**).
- On **32B** models, NFT reached **59.2%** accuracy, substantially surpassing the Rejection Fine-Tuning (RFT) baseline's **52.8%** by effectively incorporating negative data.
- Utilizing prompt weighting schemes that prioritize harder questions, such as ω(q)=(1−r^q)/r^q\omega(q) = \sqrt{(1-\hat{r}_q)/\hat{r}_q}ω(q)=(1−r^q​)/r^q​​, consistently improved performance, demonstrating the impact of targeted learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - NFT reveals a theoretical equivalence between supervised learning and Group Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) in strict-on-policy training, suggesting a deeper connection between SL and RL paradigms.
> - Learning from self-generated negative examples is crucial for LLM self-improvement, providing significant performance gains over positive-only learning and fostering exploratory behavior.
> - The ability to leverage negative feedback becomes increasingly important for larger models, where the performance gap between RFT and NFT widens more rapidly.
