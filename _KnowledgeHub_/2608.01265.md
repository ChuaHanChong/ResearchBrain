# Hermite Curves as Trajectory Priors for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Harbin Institute of Technology (Shenzhen), Jiangsu Cytoderm Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd., and National University of Singapore (Show Lab) introduce Hermite trajectory priors to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, addressing issues of intra-chunk incoherence and inter-chunk discontinuity. Their `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` variant, which uses the prior as a training-time inductive bias, achieved an average success rate of 98.7% on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated a 0.48x reduction in replanning seam discontinuity on real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often represent action chunks as flat sequences of controls, leading to intra-chunk incoherence and physically implausible motions.
- During closed-loop replanning, independent action chunks are concatenated without explicit continuity enforcement, resulting in inter-chunk discontinuity and potential instability.
- Current action chunk representations lack explicit trajectory-level constraints, causing pointwise reconstruction losses to overlook jagged or high-frequency movements.

## Method

- The authors introduce a fixed **Hermite trajectory operator** to model the ==smooth continuous subspace== of action chunks, parameterizing motion segments by endpoint positions and velocities.
- Three variants integrate this prior: `HERMITE-VLA_DH` for discrete autoregressive models, `HERMITE-VLA_CH` for continuous generative models with an explicit scaffold, and `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` for ==training-time regularization==.
- The `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` variant employs an ==auxiliary Hermite branch==, supervised against the clean smooth-action trajectory, which is discarded during inference to ensure zero runtime overhead.

## Results

- `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` achieved an average success rate of **98.7%** on the LIBERO benchmark (outperforming `π0.5` at 95.9%) and improved robustness on LIBERO-plus under distribution shifts (**90.9%** vs 85.7%).
- Real-world robot experiments demonstrated `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` achieving a **90.0%** average success rate (vs 63.4% for `π0.5`), substantially reducing replanning seam discontinuity (e.g., **0.48x** reduction on Task 3), and lowering RMS acceleration/jerk.
- Ablation studies confirmed that an optimal segment count (K=2) and regularization strength (λ=10) were crucial for maximizing performance, consistently yielding lower median execution jerk and higher task success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding Hermite trajectory priors into VLA models effectively mitigates intra-chunk incoherence and inter-chunk discontinuities, leading to objectively smoother and more robust robot motions.
> - Utilizing the Hermite prior purely as a training-time inductive bias (`HERMITE-VLA_Reg`) provides significant performance improvements without increasing inference latency, offering a practical solution for real-time robotic control.
> - The piecewise cubic Hermite basis yields superior performance and lower execution jerk compared to other polynomial bases, highlighting the importance of the specific geometric parameterization for motion quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate large pre-trained vision-language backbones with action decoders for robot control. A common architectural choice in these models is "action chunking," where the model predicts a short sequence of future actions rather than a single next-step action. This approach aims to improve temporal consistency and reduce compounding errors.

Existing VLA models, such as OpenVLA, RT-2, and π-series models, typically represent each action chunk as a flat sequence of per-timestep controls. While this representation is straightforward and often effective, it generally treats the chunk as a collection of independent discrete samples without explicitly encoding the underlying continuous trajectory. This can lead to two primary issues:
1. **Intra-chunk incoherence:** Within a chunk, each timestep is optimized independently, often resulting in pointwise reconstruction losses that do not explicitly penalize jagged or physically implausible motions.
2. **Inter-chunk discontinuity:** During closed-loop replanning, independently decoded action chunks are concatenated. Without explicit boundary constraints on position or velocity, abrupt transitions can occur between consecutive chunks, leading to instability or task failure in physical execution.

Previous efforts to structure action chunk representations include token-based policies that quantize actions (e.g., OpenVLA, RT-2), or methods like FAST which apply discrete cosine transforms (DCT) to compress chunks into spectral coefficients. Other research has explored continuous generative decoders using diffusion or flow-matching objectives (e.g., Diffusion Policy, π0.5). More recently, some work has integrated classical motion parameterizations, such as movement primitives or splines, into learning-based policies. However, these methods either offer limited geometric priors or impose runtime constraints.

This work contributes to the landscape by explicitly addressing the lack of continuous trajectory structure in action chunks. It proposes using Hermite trajectory priors to embed smoothness and continuity directly into the action representation, systematically exploring its integration across both discrete and continuous VLA modeling paradigms. The research investigates whether this prior functions best as an explicit policy output or as an implicit learning-stage inductive bias.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and fluency of robotic manipulation executed by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by introducing explicit trajectory priors for action chunk representation. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current VLA models that rely on a "flat" representation of action chunks:

1. **Addressing Weakly Structured Action Interface:** Existing VLA models treat action chunks as sequences of discrete control samples, leading to a weakly structured interface between high-level reasoning and low-level robot control. This flat representation does not inherently capture the continuous and smooth nature of physical motion. The research aims to endow action chunks with a more physically grounded structure.

2. **Mitigating Intra-Chunk Incoherence:** When each timestep within an action chunk is parameterized independently, the training process often optimizes for pointwise accuracy. This can result in predicted motions that are jagged, high-frequency, or otherwise physically incoherent within a single chunk, as there are no explicit trajectory-level constraints promoting smoothness. A key motivation is to structurally guarantee smoothness for intra-chunk motion.

3. **Preventing Inter-Chunk Discontinuity:** In a receding-horizon control loop, VLA models repeatedly predict and execute short action chunks. The concatenation of these independently decoded chunks at replanning seams often lacks explicit continuity enforcement for position and velocity. This absence of boundary constraints can cause abrupt jumps or discontinuities during execution, leading to instability or task failure. The work aims to enforce boundary continuity for inter-chunk replanning.

4. **Developing a Lightweight and Compatible Prior:** The researchers sought to introduce a trajectory prior that is not only effective but also computationally efficient and compatible with existing VLA model architectures. This involves ensuring the operator is compact, differentiable, and inexpensive to integrate, enabling its use across different action modeling paradigms (discrete and continuous).

5. **Exploring Integration Levels for the Trajectory Prior:** A specific objective was to systematically investigate how the Hermite trajectory prior can be integrated into VLA models. This involves addressing two nested design questions:
 - Can a single fixed Hermite operator support both discrete autoregressive and continuous generative action modeling paradigms? This led to the development of `HERMITE-VLA_DH` (discrete) and `HERMITE-VLA_CH` (continuous).
 - Within continuous action modeling, is it necessary for the trajectory prior to explicitly participate in deployed action prediction, or can it function solely as an auxiliary training objective? This question motivated the introduction of `HERMITE-VLA_Reg`, which aimed to retain the baseline inference pipeline while leveraging the prior for learning.

By addressing these motivations, the research seeks to improve the physical plausibility, stability, and overall success rates of VLA models in robotic manipulation tasks across both simulation and real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology involves introducing a Hermite trajectory operator to model the smooth continuous subspace of action chunks. This operator is then integrated into existing VLA paradigms through three distinct variants.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Robot manipulation is framed as imitation learning. The VLA model receives an observation (visual images, language instruction, proprioceptive state) and predicts an action chunk `a ∈ R^(T×D)`, representing `T` future steps of `D`-dimensional controls (e.g., end-effector pose, gripper commands). During deployment, a receding-horizon strategy is used, where predicted chunks are executed, and new chunks are predicted from updated observations. A "replanning seam" occurs every `W` steps, where independently decoded chunks are concatenated without explicit continuity constraints.

#### 3.2. Hermite Trajectory Operator

The central component is a fixed Hermite trajectory operator `D_H(θ) = Hθ`.
- **Smooth Subspace:** The action chunk `a` is partitioned into `a_c ∈ R^(T×D_c)` for smooth continuous channels (e.g., translational/rotational displacement), and non-smooth channels (e.g., binary gripper commands) which are handled separately.
- **Segment Parameterization:** The continuous trajectory within `a_c` is represented as `K` piecewise cubic Hermite segments. Each segment `k` is defined by four boundary variables: `θ^(k) = (p_s^(k), v_s^(k), p_e^(k), v_e^(k))`, representing endpoint positions and velocities. `θ ∈ R^(4K×D_c)` is the concatenation of these variables.
- **Basis Matrix (H):** A precomputed fixed basis matrix `H ∈ R^(T×4K)` maps `θ` back to a dense trajectory of per-timestep controls. This matrix is derived from the cubic Hermite basis functions `h_00(u), h_10(u), h_01(u), h_11(u)` evaluated at the `T` control timestamps. `H` has a block-banded structure, where each block corresponds to a segment and maps its four boundary variables to the timestamps it spans.
- **Target Encoding:** Given a ground-truth chunk `a_gt`, its smooth subspace `a_c_gt` is extracted. `K+1` knots are placed on a rounded uniform grid. For each segment `k`, endpoint positions `p_s^(k)` and `p_e^(k)` are directly read from `a_c_gt`. Endpoint velocities `v_s^(k)` and `v_e^(k)` are computed using one-sided finite differences and rescaled by the segment span. This encoder is deterministic and shared across all variants, ensuring `C0`-exactness at interior knots and biasing towards `C1`-consistency.

#### 3.3. Instantiating the Hermite Trajectory Prior

The Hermite operator is integrated into VLA models via three variants:

1. **Hermite Tokens (HERMITE-VLA_DH):**
 - **Paradigm:** Discrete autoregressive action modeling.
 - **Mechanism:** The `θ_gt` (boundary variables) are uniformly quantized into `B` bins. These quantized tokens `q` (4D_c tokens per segment) are then predicted autoregressively at the segment level (each segment conditioned on preceding ones). A separate head handles the gripper channel.
 - **Loss:** Next-token cross-entropy loss `L_DH`.
 - **Inference:** Segments are decoded sequentially. Predicted tokens are dequantized to `θ`, and the smooth trajectory `a_c_hat` is reconstructed via `D_H(θ_hat)`.
 - **Distinction:** Unlike existing tokenizers, each token has an explicit trajectory-level meaning. The autoregressive loop is confined to the action head, not the backbone, for efficiency.

2. **Hermite Scaffold (HERMITE-VLA_CH):**
 - **Paradigm:** Continuous generative action modeling (flow matching, `x_0`-prediction).
 - **Mechanism:** At each flow time `t`, the action head estimates the clean smooth action `a_c_0_hat` by decomposing it into a Hermite scaffold `Hθ` and a per-timestep residual `r` (i.e., `a_c_0_hat = Hθ + r`). `θ` is predicted from global pooled features of the noised chunk `a_t`, while `r` comes from timestep-wise features.
 - **Loss:** `L_CH = E_t,ϵ ||Hθ + r - a_c_gt||^2`, applying supervision in trajectory space.
 - **Inference:** The Hermite prior actively conditions the generative trajectory during every solver step of the flow matching ODE.
 - **Distinction:** The Hermite scaffold explicitly participates in every action prediction, providing a structural base for the continuous trajectory.

3. **Hermite Regularization (HERMITE-VLA_Reg):**
 - **Paradigm:** Continuous generative action modeling (flow matching).
 - **Mechanism:** An auxiliary Hermite branch predicts `θ_aux` from shared features (similar pooled architecture as `HERMITE-VLA_CH` but without residuals). This `Hθ_aux` is supervised against the clean smooth-action trajectory using `L_herm = E_t,ϵ ||Hθ_aux - a_c_gt||^2`.
 - **Loss:** The total training objective is `L_Reg = L_flow + λL_herm`, where `L_flow` is the original flow-matching loss for the main action head.
 - **Inference:** The auxiliary Hermite head is discarded. The deployed action head and sampling procedure remain unchanged, incurring zero runtime overhead.
 - **Distinction:** The Hermite prior acts purely as a training-time inductive bias, enriching the learning signal without altering the deployed action parameterization.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper includes theoretical propositions:
- **Supervision Geometry:** Shows that trajectory-space losses (`||Hθ - a||^2`) supervise the same least-squares target as `θ`-space loss, but under a metric `H^T H` that weights boundary coordinates by their influence on the executed trajectory, addressing scale disparities.
- **Expressivity and Bandwidth:** Bounds the approximation error of the Hermite scaffold (`O(s_max^2 ||f''||_∞)`) and the maximum jerk for trajectories in its span (`O(K^3)`), explaining the observed optimal segment count `K`.
- **Seam Control:** Demonstrates that position and velocity discontinuities at replanning handovers are bounded by the prediction errors of adjacent chunks and the intrinsic increment of the demonstration, and are thus directly controlled by the trajectory-space objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two simulation benchmarks (LIBERO and LIBERO-plus) and four real-robot tasks across three platforms (single-arm Franka, single/dual-arm Cybopal, dual-arm ARX).

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Standard LIBERO:**
 - `HERMITE-VLA_DH` achieved an average success rate (SR) of 95.4%, surpassing its baseline `π0-FAST` (85.5%).
 - `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` and `HERMITE-VLA_CH` achieved 98.7% and 97.7% average SR, respectively, outperforming the `π0.5` baseline (95.9%).
 - The largest gains were observed on the challenging `LIBERO-10` suite, indicating that explicit boundary parameterization helps prevent error accumulation in long-horizon tasks.
- **LIBERO-plus (Distribution Shift):**
 - `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` achieved the highest overall average SR of 90.9%, improving upon the `π0.5` baseline (85.7%) across most perturbation categories. Significant gains were observed under Camera (+13.4%) and Robot (+6.0%) perturbations.
 - `HERMITE-VLA_DH` also improved over `π0-FAST` (69.4% vs 61.6%).

#### 4.2. Real-world Results

- `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` achieved an average SR of 90.0%, compared to 63.4% for `π0.5` and 81.7% for `HERMITE-VLA_CH`.
- Improvements were most pronounced in tasks requiring precise manipulation (e.g., Task 2 and Task 3, with SR increasing from 26.7% and 46.7% for `π0.5` to 66.7% and 93.3% for `HERMITE-VLA_Reg`). This suggests improved execution of manipulation itself rather than just reaching the manipulation more reliably.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Injection Strength (λ):** For `HERMITE-VLA_Reg`, the average SR followed a bell-shaped curve, peaking at λ=10 (98.7%). The median execution jerk monotonically decreased as λ increased from 0 to 10, confirming enhanced smoothness.
- **Segment Count (K):** For `HERMITE-VLA_Reg`, the average SR peaked at K=2 (98.7%), with K=1 showing under-parameterization and K=3,4 showing performance degradation, consistent with the bias-fidelity trade-off described in the theoretical analysis. K=2 also yielded the lowest median jerk.
- **Continuous-Head Design Choices:** Supervising `HERMITE-VLA_CH` directly in trajectory space (with per-timestep residuals) significantly outperformed `θ`-space supervision or trajectory supervision without residuals.
- **Boundary Basis:** The cubic Hermite representation yielded higher average SR (98.7%) and lower execution jerk compared to other polynomial bases (global cubic polynomial, Bernstein, B-spline). This indicates the specific parameterization contributes to the performance.
- **Inference Cost:** `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` incurred negligible additional latency (48.6ms vs 48.4ms for `π0.5`) and similar peak memory usage. `HERMITE-VLA_DH` was significantly faster (28.9ms) than `π0-FAST` (238.2ms) due to fewer backbone passes.

#### 4.4. Smoothness and Trajectory-Quality Analysis

- **Global Smoothness (SPARC):** `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` consistently achieved the highest spectral smoothness (least negative SPARC values) across all LIBERO suites, indicating suppressed high-frequency spectral content.
- **Temporal Localization of Motion Roughness (Jerk):** `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` suppressed both the noise floor and transient spikes in per-step jerk profiles compared to `π0.5`, leading to smoother execution and often fewer steps to task completion.
- **Smoothness and Task Outcome:** Failed rollouts consistently exhibited higher jerk magnitudes across all variants. `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` showed the lowest jerk in both successful and failed subsets and had the fewest failures, suggesting a correlation between kinematic smoothness and task robustness.
- **Seam Discontinuity on Real-world Platforms:** All policies showed higher acceleration transients at replanning handovers. `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` substantially reduced this seam discontinuity compared to `π0.5` (e.g., 0.48x reduction on Task 3).
- **Kinematic Gains Transfer to Hardware:** Real-robot experiments confirmed that `HERMITE` variants significantly reduced acceleration and jerk RMS. `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` attenuated high-frequency acceleration power spectral density (PSD), leading to smoother physical motion and less path dispersion. Qualitatively, this translated to reduced end-effector jitter, improved gripper-object alignment, and more direct, efficient trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a systematic re-evaluation of action chunk representation in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, proposing Hermite trajectory priors as a mechanism to explicitly integrate continuous motion properties.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key areas:
- **Explicit Trajectory Structuring:** The introduction of piecewise cubic Hermite curves addresses a fundamental limitation of existing VLA models that treat action chunks as weakly structured, flat sequences of controls. By parameterizing chunks through endpoint positions and velocities, the approach explicitly embeds intra-chunk smoothness and inter-chunk boundary continuity, which are critical for physically realistic robot movements.
- **Improved Robustness and Performance:** The consistent empirical improvements across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, LIBERO-plus) and real-world manipulation tasks underscore the practical value of this approach. The significant gains in success rates, particularly in long-horizon and perturbed scenarios, indicate that trajectory priors enhance the robustness of VLA policies.
- **Efficiency in Deployment:** The `HERMITE-VLA_Reg` variant demonstrates that trajectory smoothness can be effectively instilled as a learning inductive bias without introducing any additional inference-time overhead. This "zero-cost deployment" property is crucial for real-time robotic control and makes the integration of such priors highly practical for industrial and research applications.
- **Enhanced Motion Quality:** Beyond task success rates, the detailed kinematic analysis (jerk, SPARC, seam discontinuity, acceleration PSD) consistently shows that Hermite regularization leads to objectively smoother, more stable, and less erratic robot motions. This contributes to reduced mechanical wear on hardware and generally more reliable physical interactions.
- **Unified Framework for Discrete and Continuous Models:** The research successfully integrates the Hermite prior across both discrete autoregressive and continuous generative VLA paradigms, demonstrating its versatility and applicability to a broad range of existing and future VLA architectures.

The **potential impact** of this work includes:
- **Accelerating Real-world VLA Adoption:** By improving the reliability and physical fluency of robot actions, this work could contribute to the broader adoption of VLA models in complex real-world manipulation tasks, where motion quality and continuity are paramount.
- **Guiding Future VLA Design:** The finding that a training-only inductive bias (`HERMITE-VLA_Reg`) yields optimal performance without inference overhead provides a promising paradigm for future VLA model design, encouraging the exploration of other structural priors that can enrich learning signals.
- **Foundational Improvement for Robot Learning:** This research offers a foundational improvement in how VLA models understand and generate robot actions, moving beyond mere statistical reconstruction to incorporate explicit physical constraints and geometric intuition. This could lead to more sample-efficient learning and better generalization.
- **Basis for Advanced Control:** The explicit control over boundary conditions and smoothness could serve as a basis for more advanced control strategies, such as adaptive chunk segmentation or tighter coupling with impedance control, for tasks involving dynamic environments or delicate contact.
