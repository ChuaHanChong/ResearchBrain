# SocialGesture: Delving into Multi-person Gesture Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> SocialGesture introduces the first large-scale multi-person social gesture dataset, sourced from YouTube and Ego4D, with category, bounding-box, subject-target relation, and VQA annotations for four deictic gestures. Benchmarks across temporal localization, recognition, and a novel VQA task show existing video models and VLMs struggle badly on multi-party gesture understanding.

## Problem

- Existing gesture datasets like HaGRID and LD-ConGR are confined to single-person, controlled settings and lack spontaneous multi-person social interactions found in real life.
- No prior dataset provides multi-level annotations linking gesture category, spatial-temporal localization, subject-target interaction dynamics, and language together.
- Gesture recognition research has largely ignored device-independent social deictic gestures, and video captioning/VQA work for human gestures remains extremely limited.

## Method

- **SocialGesture** dataset: 9,889 clips from 372 raw videos across YouTube and Ego4D, annotated with four deictic gesture categories (pointing, showing, giving, reaching) and initiator/target bounding boxes.
- ==Multi-level annotation framework== combining temporal keyframe localization, spatial bounding boxes for subject and target, and natural-language descriptions of interaction context.
- ==Social Gesture VQA benchmark== built via a semi-automated GPT-4o pipeline plus human review, split into Global Perception, Gesture Understanding, and Gesture Localization subtasks.

## Results

- Temporal localization with ActionFormer tops out at **14.73** average mAP using VideoMAEV2 features, far below performance these extractors achieve on THUMOS14 or ActivityNet.
- Best four-class gesture recognition reaches only **56.18%** (VideoSwin-L) on full frames and **64.72%** (UniFormerV2-B/16) when cropped to per-person bounding boxes.
- Zero-shot VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 72B, GPT-4o-mini, Claude 3.7 Sonnet) score at most **29.37%** on target localization, and none exceed **70%** on the simplest human-counting subtask.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature extractors and VLMs pretrained on single-person or generic video corpora transfer poorly to multi-person interaction settings, showing pretraining data composition matters as much as scale.
> - High interdependence between perception, understanding, and localization subtasks means gesture reasoning requires sequential, compositional inference, not isolated classification.
> - Fine-grained social non-verbal cues remain a blind spot for current SOTA VLMs even at 72B+ scale, marking a distinct capability gap from standard VQA benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Human communication is rich with nonverbal cues, and gestures form a crucial component of these interactions, particularly in multi-person social settings. While verbal communication has been extensively studied in AI research, the understanding of social gestures – especially in natural multi-person environments – has remained relatively unexplored. This gap represents a significant limitation in developing truly socially intelligent AI systems that can navigate human interactions effectively.

The research paper "SocialGesture: Delving into Multi-person Gesture Understanding" aims to address this critical gap by introducing a novel dataset and benchmark tasks specifically designed to advance multi-person gesture understanding in natural social contexts.

### 2. The SocialGesture Dataset

The SocialGesture dataset represents a significant advancement in the field of gesture recognition by focusing specifically on multi-person interactions in natural social settings. Unlike previous datasets that primarily addressed single-person gestures or sign language in controlled environments, SocialGesture captures the complexity of real-world social interactions.

Key characteristics of the dataset include:

- **Data Sources**: The videos were collected from YouTube and Ego4D, focusing on natural social interactions featuring multiple people.
- **Diversity**: The dataset includes a wide variety of scenarios, environments, and participants to ensure broad coverage of social gesture contexts.
- **Scale**: The dataset comprises a substantial collection of multi-person interactions, providing sufficient data for training and evaluating machine learning models.
- **Standardization**: All videos were converted to a consistent resolution and frame rate, with longer videos divided into manageable clips.

What sets SocialGesture apart from existing datasets is its comprehensive annotation framework, which includes:

1. **Temporal-spatial annotations**: Precise bounding boxes and timestamps for each gesture instance
2. **Interaction annotations**: Clear identification of subject-target relationships in each gesture
3. **Visual Question Answering (VQA) pairs**: Questions and answers designed to assess understanding of the social context

### 3. Deictic Gesture Categories

The research focuses specifically on deictic gestures – gestures that establish reference through pointing or indicating – due to their fundamental role in social interaction. The SocialGesture dataset categorizes deictic gestures into four distinct types:

1. **Pointing**: Gestures where a person directs attention to a specific location, object, or person by extending a finger or hand
2. **Showing**: Gestures where a person presents or displays an object to others
3. **Giving**: Gestures where a person transfers an object to another person
4. **Reaching**: Gestures where a person extends their hand or body toward an object or person

This categorization is based on David McNeill's established framework for gesture classification, with a specific focus on the subset most relevant to social interaction. Each gesture type involves a different relationship between the subject (person performing the gesture) and the target (person or object receiving the gesture).

### 4. Benchmark Tasks

To facilitate research in multi-person gesture understanding, the paper introduces three benchmark tasks based on the SocialGesture dataset:

#### 4.1. Social Gesture Temporal Localization

This task requires models to identify the precise timeframe within a video clip where a social gesture occurs. As illustrated in Figure 2, the model must process frame features from a video sequence and determine when a gesture begins and ends.

#### 4.2. Social Gesture Recognition

This task is divided into two subtasks:
- **Gesture vs. Non-gesture Classification**: A binary classification problem where models must determine whether a social gesture is present in a given video clip
- **Gesture Type Classification**: A multi-class classification problem where models must identify which of the four deictic gesture types is being performed

#### 4.3. Social Gesture Visual Question Answering (VQA)

This task evaluates a model's ability to answer questions about social gestures in video clips. It includes three levels of complexity:
- **Global Perception**: Basic questions about the scene (e.g., "What are these people doing?")
- **Gesture Understanding**: Questions specifically about the gestures present (e.g., "What type of gesture is being performed?")
- **Gesture Localization**: Questions requiring spatial understanding of gestures (e.g., "Select the bounding box indicating the gesture's target")

### 5. Experimental Setup and Models

The research evaluated a range of existing models on the benchmark tasks to establish baseline performance and identify challenges in multi-person gesture understanding:

#### 5.1. Temporal Action Localization

For the temporal localization task, the researchers evaluated various feature extractors combined with standard temporal localization models, including:
- CNN-based models: TSN, TANet, SlowFast
- Transformer-based models: TimeSformer, MViTv2, VideoSwin, UniFormerV2

#### 5.2. Gesture Recognition

For the gesture recognition tasks, the research tested both binary and multi-class classification performance across the same set of video understanding architectures.

#### 5.3. Visual Question Answering

For the VQA task, the research evaluated several Vision-Language Models (VLMs):
- Qwen2-VL and Qwen2.5-VL
- InternVL-2.5
- LLaVA-NEXT-Video
- GPT-4o-mini
- Claude-3.7-sonnet

All experiments were conducted using NVIDIA L40S and H100 GPUs, with a standardized batch size of 16 and a learning rate of 5e-4 across all models.

### 6. Key Findings

The research yielded several important findings regarding the current state of multi-person gesture understanding:

1. **Poor Performance on Temporal Localization**: Existing feature extractors performed poorly on the temporal localization task, suggesting that features pretrained on single-person action datasets do not generalize well to multi-person scenarios. The best-performing model achieved only modest results, highlighting the difficulty of precisely locating social gestures in time.

2. **Challenges in Gesture Recognition**: Even when provided with cropped bounding boxes of individuals performing gestures, models struggled to effectively classify the four deictic gesture types. This indicates that recognizing social gestures requires more fine-grained movement analysis and an understanding of the relationship between subject and target.

3. **VLM Limitations in Social Contexts**: Vision-Language Models showed varying capabilities in handling the VQA tasks:
 - They performed reasonably well on global perception questions
 - They had moderate success with gesture understanding questions
 - They struggled significantly with gesture localization questions

4. **Benefits of Fine-tuning**: Fine-tuning Qwen2-VL-7B led to improved performance across most VQA metrics, but some performance decreases were observed in binary classification tasks, suggesting potential overfitting issues.

5. **Counting Challenges**: The seemingly simple task of counting people in social interaction scenes proved challenging for VLMs, highlighting fundamental limitations in their understanding of multi-person scenarios.

These findings collectively demonstrate that existing models are not well-equipped to handle the complexities of multi-person social interactions, particularly when it comes to understanding the nuanced relationships expressed through gestures.

### 7. Challenges and Limitations

The research identified several key challenges that current models face when dealing with multi-person gesture understanding:

1. **Temporal-Spatial Complexity**: Social gestures occur in both time and space, requiring models to track multiple people and their interactions simultaneously.

2. **Subject-Target Relationships**: Understanding a gesture often requires identifying both the person performing the gesture (subject) and the person or object receiving it (target), which introduces relational reasoning challenges.

3. **Contextual Understanding**: Gestures derive meaning from their social context, requiring models to integrate information about the environment, participants, and ongoing activities.

4. **Fine-grained Movement Analysis**: Distinguishing between gesture types (e.g., pointing vs. reaching) requires attention to subtle differences in hand movements and body posture.

5. **Data Diversity**: Despite the SocialGesture dataset's improvements, capturing the full range of cultural variations and contextual nuances in gestures remains challenging.

### 8. Implications and Future Directions

The SocialGesture dataset and benchmark tasks open up several promising directions for future research:

1. **Specialized Architectures**: The development of model architectures specifically designed for multi-person gesture understanding, perhaps incorporating relational reasoning mechanisms.

2. **Multimodal Integration**: Combining visual gesture recognition with audio and linguistic cues to achieve more comprehensive social understanding.

3. **Domain Adaptation**: Exploring techniques to help models better generalize across different cultural contexts and gesture conventions.

4. **Applications Development**: Leveraging improved gesture understanding for applications such as:
 - Enhanced human-robot interaction
 - More natural virtual assistants
 - Assistive technologies for communication support
 - Educational tools that can provide feedback on social interactions

5. **Dataset Expansion**: Continuing to enhance the diversity and scale of multi-person gesture datasets to cover more scenarios and cultural contexts.

### 9. Conclusion

The "SocialGesture: Delving into Multi-person Gesture Understanding" paper makes several significant contributions to the field of social AI and gesture recognition:

1. It introduces SocialGesture, a novel dataset focused specifically on multi-person interactions in natural social settings, addressing a critical gap in existing resources.

2. It establishes benchmark tasks that enable systematic evaluation of models on multi-person gesture understanding, providing a foundation for measuring progress in this area.

3. It evaluates a range of existing models on these tasks, identifying their limitations and highlighting the challenges that must be addressed to achieve effective social gesture understanding.

4. It demonstrates that while current Vision-Language Models have made progress in visual understanding, they still struggle with the nuanced spatial, temporal, and relational aspects of social gestures.

This research represents an important step toward creating AI systems that can truly understand and participate in human social interactions, recognizing not just what is said but also the rich nonverbal communication that occurs through gestures. By focusing on the multi-person aspect of gestures, the work addresses a fundamental component of human social intelligence that has been underexplored in AI research to date.
