# FLOWER: Democratizing Generalist Robot Policies with Efficient Vision-Language-Action Flow Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> FLOWER presents an efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy for generalist robotics, achieving competitive or superior performance on 190 tasks across 10 benchmarks. It accomplishes this with a 99% reduction in pretraining compute and significantly lower memory requirements compared to prior large models, by incorporating intermediate modality fusion and a cross-action space Flow Transformer.

## Problem

- Existing generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robot policies are computationally prohibitive, demanding multi-billion parameters, massive pretraining compute (e.g., tens of thousands of GPU hours), and high VRAM.
- The high resource requirements of current VLAs create significant barriers to entry for diverse research communities and restrict practical real-world deployment on commodity hardware.
- Current VLA architectures struggle to effectively balance rich semantic understanding from large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with efficient and accurate action generation.

## Method

- **FLOWER** develops an efficient 950M parameter VLA policy using ==intermediate modality fusion==, which prunes VLM layers and conditions the ==Flow Transformer== on intermediate VLM embeddings for semantic richness with reduced overhead.
- Introduces a ==cross-action space Flow Transformer== leveraging ==Action-Space Global-AdaLN-Zero== to efficiently handle heterogeneous action spaces with 20% fewer parameters, augmented by ==LoRA adapters== for fine-grained modulation.
- Utilizes ==Rectified Flow== for action generation, enabling high-quality action sequences with fewer denoising steps and improved inference efficiency, crucial for low-latency control.

## Results

- FLOWER achieved a new State-of-the-Art (SoTA) performance of **4.53** average sequence length on the CALVIN ABC benchmark, surpassing OpenVLA and prior generative methods.
- The policy demonstrated a **99%** reduction in pretraining compute, requiring only **200 GPU hours** compared to tens of thousands for models like OpenVLA and RDT-1B, while achieving competitive performance across **190** tasks.
- In real-world evaluations, FLOWER doubled the average success rate of the second-best baseline (OpenVLA) across **20** distinct tasks (**61%** vs. **31%**) and showed robust generalization to novel objects and challenging environmental conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate modality fusion provides an optimal balance between VLM semantic richness and computational efficiency for robotic manipulation, outperforming early and late fusion strategies.
> - The novel Action-Space Global-AdaLN-Zero mechanism can significantly reduce the parameter count of the transformer head while maintaining performance, by sharing modulation weights across layers for action-type conditioning.
> - Pre-trained Vision-Language Models emphasizing object detection and visual grounding, such as Florence-2-L, are more effective as backbones for robotic manipulation tasks compared to general reasoning models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "generalist robotic manipulation policies" is a central and ambitious goal in modern robotics. Traditionally, robots have been designed and programmed for highly specific tasks in structured environments. However, the vision of robots capable of executing diverse tasks across various embodiments (i.e., different physical robot platforms) in unstructured, real-world settings necessitates a paradigm shift.

This paper is situated within the rapidly advancing field of **Imitation Learning (IL)**, particularly concerning **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Policies**. VLAs represent a significant leap forward, as they fine-tune large, pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate robot actions directly from free-form natural language commands and visual observations. This approach leverages the vast semantic understanding and visual reasoning capabilities of VLMs, enabling robots to interpret complex instructions and perceive their environment effectively.

Within VLAs, two main approaches for action prediction have emerged: discrete action prediction and diffusion- or flow-based objectives. The latter, which FLOWER adopts, has shown particular promise in modeling complex, multimodal action distributions, crucial for nuanced robotic control.

However, the current state-of-the-art VLAs, such as OpenVLA (7.7 billion parameters), RDT-1B (1.2 billion policy parameters with an 11.4 billion parameter VLM), π0 (3.3 billion parameters), and GR00T-N1 (over 2 billion parameters), suffer from a major limitation: their **prohibitive computational costs and resource requirements**. These models demand massive datasets, extensive pretraining compute (e.g., tens of thousands of GPU hours), and high VRAM for deployment, creating a significant barrier to entry for many researchers and practical applications. This computational bottleneck restricts research diversity and limits the ability to deploy these powerful policies on more common or resource-constrained hardware.

FLOWER directly addresses this critical efficiency challenge. It builds upon previous efforts to incorporate large pre-trained VLMs into policies while simultaneously tackling the scalability issue. The paper implicitly critiques existing methods like Octo (which lacks a pre-trained vision-language encoder, limiting generalization) and RDT-1B (known for its month-long pretraining on 48 A100 GPUs). It also differentiates itself from other efficiency-focused works like TinyVLA (which uses a compact diffusion head with a lightweight VLM via late fusion, without pretraining) and DeerVLA (which explores early-exit strategies for continuous action prediction but is significantly surpassed by FLOWER). FLOWER's contribution lies in introducing novel architectural designs—intermediate modality fusion and action-specific Global-AdaLN conditioning—to create a powerful yet compact VLA, thereby making these generalist policies more accessible and practical. Its explicit focus on open-source availability further reinforces its aim to democratize the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the FLOWER research is to **democratize generalist robot policies** by developing a highly efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that achieves competitive performance with larger, more resource-intensive state-of-the-art models, but at a fraction of their computational cost and resource requirements.

This primary objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Computational Barriers:** Current generalist VLAs are computationally prohibitive, requiring multi-billion parameters, massive datasets, immense pretraining compute, and high VRAM. This limits accessibility to a diverse research community and hinders real-world deployment on commodity hardware. FLOWER aims to overcome this by creating a policy with fewer than 1 billion parameters.
2. **Balancing Semantic Depth and Efficiency:** The authors identify a critical trade-off: deep, off-the-shelf VLM backbones provide rich semantic features but leave the action generation head under-parameterized, leading to poor trajectory modeling. Conversely, shrinking the VLM compromises essential semantic features. FLOWER seeks to resolve this by strategically reallocating model capacity.
3. **Improving Training and Inference Efficiency:** Beyond reducing model size, a key motivation is to achieve faster convergence during training and lower inference latency, making the policies more practical for rapid iteration and real-time robotic control.
4. **Maintaining High Performance and Generalization:** Despite the focus on efficiency, FLOWER aims to match or exceed the performance of existing SoTA VLAs across a broad spectrum of tasks, embodiments, and environments, encompassing both simulation and real-world settings.
5. **Promoting Open Science:** By providing open-source code, pretrained weights, and detailed methodologies, the authors aim to foster broader adoption and further research in efficient generalist robotics. This commitment to openness is crucial for true "democratization."

Quantitatively, the specific objectives include:
- Developing a VLA policy with **fewer than 1 billion parameters** (specifically, 950M parameters).
- Achieving competitive or superior performance across **190 tasks spanning 10 simulation and real-world benchmarks** and demonstrating robustness across diverse robotic embodiments.
- Significantly reducing pretraining costs, aiming for a 99% reduction compared to existing models (e.g., **200 H100 GPU hours**).
- Achieving a new State-of-the-Art (SoTA) performance on benchmarks like **CALVIN ABC (4.53)**.
- Ensuring a small memory footprint (aiming for **~1.85 GB VRAM**) and high inference throughput for practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FLOWER's methodology is built upon two core technical contributions aimed at achieving efficiency without sacrificing performance, integrated into a flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture.

#### 3.1. Intermediate Modality Fusion Vision-Language-Action-Models

The paper proposes **intermediate-level fusion** as a novel strategy to integrate vision-language representations from a pre-trained VLM with the Flow Transformer for action generation. This approach seeks to optimally balance semantic richness from the VLM with computational efficiency.

- **VLM Pruning:** Instead of using the full VLM, FLOWER prunes between 30% and 50% of the pre-trained VLM's layers. For Encoder-Decoder VLMs (like Florence-2), the entire decoder is removed, retaining only the encoder LLM layers. For Decoder-Only VLMs (like SmolFlow2-Video), the final 30% of transformer layers are dropped. This pruning directly reduces the VLM's parameter count and per-step latency.
- **Intermediate Embedding Utilization:** Based on insights from LLM explainability (which suggest penultimate layers capture broad semantics), FLOWER conditions its Flow Transformer on these *intermediate* embeddings. This ensures that the policy benefits from semantically rich contextual features without incurring the overhead of the VLM's final, more specialized layers.
- **Integration Mechanism:** The projected VLM latent tokens (from the intermediate layer) are injected into the Flow Transformer via cross-attention. A linear layer followed by RMSNorm stabilizes this process, allowing each Flow layer to be conditioned on the VLM features, thus preserving spatial and contextual structure and accelerating policy convergence.
- **VLM Backbone Choice:** Through ablations, Florence-2-L (an encoder-decoder VLM pre-trained on a large dataset with emphasis on object detection, segmentation, and visual grounding) was found to be more effective for robotic manipulation tasks compared to SmolFlow-500M (a decoder-only VLM with general reasoning pretraining). FLOWER therefore uses half of the Florence-2-L VLM.

#### 3.2. Cross-Action Space Flow Transformer

To handle heterogeneous action spaces (e.g., varying degrees-of-freedom, control modes) efficiently, FLOWER introduces a novel Flow Transformer design.

- **Action-Space Global-AdaLN-Zero:** This is a unified normalization layer that conditions each transformer block on both temporal signals (e.g., flow time step) and per-action-type embeddings. Unlike standard AdaLN-Zero which uses distinct scale-and-shift parameters per layer (adding up to 30% extra parameters), Global-AdaLN-Zero shares a *single* set of modulation weights across all layers. It generates unique modulation signals for each action category, initialized with zeros for training stability. This mechanism reduces parameter count by over 20%.
- **LoRA Adapters:** To compensate for the potentially lower per-layer expressiveness introduced by sharing modulation weights, lightweight LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) adapters are injected into each layer. These provide fine-grained, layer-specific modulation with minimal additional parameters.
- **Action-Specific Encoders/Decoders:** Each action type is equipped with a small, dedicated encoder and decoder to map actions into and out of the transformer's latent space. This allows for consistent handling of differing action dimensions while maintaining weight sharing within the main Flow Transformer.
- **Architectural Stabilizers:** The Flow Transformer incorporates dual-RMSNorm parameters for each action type to handle different data frequencies and distributions, SwiGlu blocks for feed-forward layers, and QK-value normalization in attention modules, all contributing to training stability.

#### 3.3. Rectified Flow for Action Generation

FLOWER utilizes **Rectified Flow** for action generation.

- **Straight-Line Trajectories:** Rectified Flow models define straight-line velocity fields between a noise distribution and the data distribution (robot actions). This simplifies the diffusion process compared to traditional score-based or stochastic differential equation methods.
- **Efficiency in Inference:** A key advantage is the reduction in inference computation, as it requires fewer denoising steps to generate high-quality action sequences. FLOWER uses N=4 denoising steps for single-arm and N=8 for high-frequency dual-arm settings, which is crucial for low-latency robotic control.
- **Training Objective:** The model optimizes a squared error loss, predicting the velocity field v_θ(z_t, t, s_n, g, e) that transitions from a noisy action z_t to the ground truth action sequence a_n,k, conditioned on state, language goal, and embodiment.

#### 3.4. FLOWER Model Configuration and Cost-Effective Pretraining

Combining these innovations, FLOWER is a 947M parameter VLA policy. It uses an 18-layer Flow Transformer with a 1024 latent dimension. The total VRAM requirement is approximately 1.85 GB.

- **Pretraining Data:** To enable fast and cost-efficient pretraining, FLOWER uses a compact "OXE-soup" of eight public robotic datasets, totaling around 250,000 trajectories. Emphasis is placed on broad scene and embodiment diversity (e.g., Franka Pandas, XARMs) and datasets with varied environments and distractions (75% from Droid, Google Robot, and BridgeV2).
- **Pretraining Strategy:** The model is trained with an action chunk length of 20 and a single static image input. The data mix consists of 74% delta-EEF and 26% single-arm joint-state data.
- **Computational Cost:** Pretraining is completed in just 48 hours (~200 GPU-hours) on a cluster node with 4 H100 GPUs, achieving a 99% reduction in compute cost compared to much larger VLAs. An early stopping criterion, based on zero-shot success on the Bridge SIMPLER benchmark, helps identify the optimal training duration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research effectively addresses its stated objectives through comprehensive evaluations across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Critical Design Decisions (RQ I)

Ablation studies on CALVIN ABC and LIBERO-Long benchmarks provided key insights into FLOWER's design:

- **Intermediate Fusion:** This strategy significantly outperformed early and late fusion. With the Florence-VLM, it achieved 93.4% success on LIBERO-Long, a 61 percentage point improvement over early fusion and 21 points over late fusion. This validates the hypothesis that intermediate-layer features offer an optimal balance of semantic richness and computational efficiency.
- **VLM Backbone:** Florence-2-L (encoder-decoder) was found to be more effective for robotic manipulation than SmolFlow-500M (decoder-only), likely due to its pretraining emphasis on object detection and visual grounding.
- **Global-AdaLN-Zero:** This mechanism successfully reduced transformer head parameters by 20% compared to standard AdaLN without any loss in performance.
- **Diffusion Transformer Capacity:** A high-capacity Flow Transformer proved crucial; a smaller action head (384 latent dimensions, 6 layers) resulted in substantially lower performance (2.60 average sequence length vs. 4.44).
- **Other Insights:** Ablations revealed that freezing the VLM backbone negatively impacts performance, using a smaller Florence VLM also reduces efficacy, and Rectified Flow prediction is essential (L1-prediction or discrete tokenization performed poorly).

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiments (RQ II & III)

FLOWER was evaluated across 190 tasks in 10 diverse simulation benchmarks: CALVIN, LIBERO, SIMPLER (Bridge and Google Robot variants), and Aloha.

- **Overall Performance:** FLOWER consistently matched or surpassed state-of-the-art approaches across most reported benchmarks, demonstrating its versatility across diverse embodiments and task settings.
- **CALVIN Benchmark:** FLOWER achieved a new SoTA of 4.53 (average sequence length) on the CALVIN ABC benchmark, outperforming OpenVLA and π0 by wide margins, despite its smaller size and faster training. For CALVIN D and ABCD, FLOWER also delivered superior results.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** FLOWER significantly outperformed all baselines across every LIBERO variant (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long, and 90-task suite), achieving near-perfect completion rates consistently above 93%. It was the only policy to exceed 90% success on LIBERO-Long (94.9%).
- **SIMPLER Benchmark:** On the Bridge variant, FLOWER outperformed Octo and OpenVLA, showcasing its ability to handle diverse action spaces after heterogeneous pretraining. On the Google Robot benchmark, while RT-1X achieved the highest, FLOWER secured the second-best performance, surpassing other generalist policies.
- **Aloha Benchmark:** FLOWER achieved strong performance on high-frequency bi-manual tasks (Cube Transfer and Insertion), outperforming the specialist ACT policy on the challenging Insertion task. Joint-state droid-only pretraining yielded the best results for this benchmark.
- **Pretraining Efficiency:** FLOWER achieved these competitive results with only 200 GPU hours of pretraining, a 99% reduction compared to models like OpenVLA (21,500 hours) and RDT-1B (35,000 hours).

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation and Generalization (RQ II & III)

FLOWER's capabilities were validated in a real-world kitchen setting with a Franka Panda robot, trained on 417 language-annotated human demonstrations.

- **Single-Task Performance:** FLOWER achieved the highest average success rate of 61% across 20 distinct tasks, effectively doubling the performance of the second-best baseline, OpenVLA (31%). This confirmed its strong performance in real-world settings.
- **Generalization Analysis:** FLOWER consistently outperformed OpenVLA across challenging generalization scenarios: novel objects (33.3% vs 10.0%), flashlight-only lighting (50.0% vs 25.0%), background distractors (69.5% vs 41.7%), and new task compositions (51.1% vs 16.7%), averaging 51.0% success compared to OpenVLA’s 23.4%. This highlights FLOWER's robust adaptation to unstructured real-world variations.
- **Inference Efficiency:** On an RTX 4090 GPU, FLOWER achieved a throughput of 311 Hz, 8% faster than π0 (288 Hz) and over 5000% faster than OpenVLA (6.1 Hz). Its VRAM footprint was the lowest among VLAs at 1.85 GB, making it suitable for commodity hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FLOWER represents a significant advancement in the field of generalist robotics, with profound implications for research and practical deployment.

1. **Democratization of Robotics Research:** The most direct and impactful contribution is the "democratization" of generalist robot policies. By achieving state-of-the-art performance with a mere 950 million parameters and 200 GPU hours of pretraining, FLOWER drastically lowers the computational and resource barriers that currently limit access to powerful VLA models. This enables a wider range of researchers, including those in smaller labs or with limited budgets, to conduct cutting-edge research in generalist robotics.
2. **New Efficiency Paradigm:** FLOWER establishes a new benchmark for efficiency in VLA policies. It demonstrates that highly performant generalist robot control does not necessarily require multi-billion parameter models and exorbitant training costs. This paradigm shift will encourage the development of more resource-aware architectures and training strategies.
3. **Architectural Innovations with Broad Applicability:**
 - **Intermediate Modality Fusion:** This novel fusion strategy offers a principled approach to integrating VLM features with action generators, balancing semantic understanding with computational budget. This insight could be widely applicable to other multimodal learning architectures in robotics and beyond.
 - **Action-Space Global-AdaLN-Zero:** This parameter-efficient conditioning mechanism for heterogeneous action spaces provides a scalable solution for building unified policies that can control diverse robotic embodiments without incurring massive parameter overheads.
4. **Accelerated Research and Development:** With lower training times, reduced VRAM, and higher inference throughput, researchers can iterate faster on new ideas, conduct more extensive experiments, and deploy models more readily on real hardware. This acceleration will likely lead to quicker advancements in areas like domestic robotics, industrial automation, and assistive technologies.
5. **Enhanced Real-World Viability:** FLOWER's strong performance and robust generalization capabilities in real-world kitchen environments, combined with its superior inference efficiency, make it a highly practical solution for real-world robotic deployment. Its ability to handle novel objects, lighting conditions, and background distractors is crucial for robots operating in unstructured human environments.
6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of demos, code, and pretrained weights as open-source resources is critical. It provides a solid foundation for the community to build upon, reproduce results, and further innovate, thereby fostering collaborative progress in generalist robotics.

Despite some limitations (such as reliance on iterative sampling, current focus on manipulation, and remaining performance gaps on certain benchmarks), FLOWER represents a significant step towards making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible, efficient, and impactful in a wider array of applications.
