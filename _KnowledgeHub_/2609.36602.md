# OTRetarget: Joint Robot and Object Motion Retargeting via Optimal Transport

> [!summary] Summary
> OTRetarget jointly retargets human motion and multiple object trajectories to a humanoid by representing surface interactions as signed distances, closest surface points and relative directions, transferring them across human, robot and object geometries via entropic optimal transport, and solving a per-frame constrained inverse kinematics over robot and object poses at native scene scale. On OMOMO it reaches 87% robot-object Jaccard versus 28% for OmniRetarget.

## Problem

- Point-stream retargeters such as GMR and PHC track skeletal joints or keypoints only, modeling neither ground nor manipulated objects, so contacts like hands on floor are lost.
- Interaction-mesh methods like OmniRetarget treat object trajectories as fixed inputs and globally rescale the demonstration, misaligning contacts with unscaled floors and tables and producing unreachable carries.
- Uniform-weight Laplacian interaction meshes couple keypoints by connectivity rather than contact involvement, mixing posture with interaction accuracy and scaling poorly with dense sampling of surface points.

## Method

- ==Surface proximity triples==: dense probe points on human, robot and objects read signed distance, witness point and direction from precomputed object-frame SDF grids, forming geodesic and directional interaction residuals.
- ==Entropic optimal transport correspondence==: ==Sinkhorn== solves a T-pose coupling between each human part and robot link point cloud once, assigning every robot probe its demonstrated interaction target with better extremity coverage.
- ==Joint robot-object constrained IK==: a ==trust-region SQP== optimizes robot configuration and every object pose per frame, balancing interaction residuals against orientation-based style costs under SDF collision constraints; OT maps targets onto substitute objects.

## Results

- On 4,421 OMOMO sequences, robot-object Jaccard reaches **87%** with **8.7 mm** depth error, versus **28%** and **29.3 mm** for OmniRetarget; a scaled fixed-object ablation keeps **82%**.
- On 2,027 AMASS locomotion clips, orientation error drops to **6.3 degrees** versus **25.5** for OmniRetarget with **99%** ground Jaccard, running **12-22x** faster per frame.
- PPO tracking policies on its references reach **28.8 mm** MPJPE versus **33.7 mm**, hitting the baseline's final accuracy at one fifth the budget; a G1 completes **6/8** real pick-and-place trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Skeletal keypoints underdescribe contact; encoding interactions as continuous surface-level distances and witness points transfers contact far better across embodiments with different body proportions.
> - Treating object poses as decision variables instead of fixed references lets retargeting keep native scene scale, resolving reachability conflicts with small object drift rather than rescaling.
> - Retargeting quality propagates downstream: cleaner kinematic references speed RL tracking and lower its error, so investing in reference fidelity may reduce the need for reward engineering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A person lifts a box from the floor to a table. For a shorter robot, that demonstrated box trajectory may be unreachable. Scaling the motion can instead disrupt the box's relation to the unscaled floor or table. The mismatch matters because a tracking policy learns from a motion reference. A reference that misses a floor or object contact can pass that defect downstream.

In *OTRetarget: Joint Robot and Object Motion Retargeting via Optimal Transport*, Guillaume Besset and colleagues let the robot and the box adjust together. Instead of only matching skeleton poses, the method describes how surfaces interact. It then adapts the robot and the manipulated object at the same time. It combines geometric interaction targets with motion-style targets. The authors then test whether the transferred reference keeps the demonstrated contacts.

Author demo of a pick-and-place shown with interaction residuals. Yellow marks the target field transferred by transport, and blue marks the field reevaluated at the solver's returned pose. When the box is fixed to the demonstrated trajectory, it ends up above the robot's head and the robot chases it off the floor. When the robot and object are solved jointly, the box stays within reach and the feet stay down.

The video illustrates the authors' fixed-versus-jointly-solved contrast. It is an example, separate from the benchmark results.

### 2. From surface samples to a shared motion

The method records interactions directly between surfaces. For a sampled point on one surface, it stores three things:

- the signed gap to the other surface, which distinguishes separation from penetration;
- the nearest point on that surface, called the "witness";
- the direction between the two points.

The witness and the direction locate and orient the relationship. Entropic optimal transport then maps these interaction targets from human parts to corresponding robot links.

Next, a constrained inverse-kinematics solver works frame by frame. It balances the interaction targets against the desired motion style and the object trajectory, and it solves for robot and object poses together. Joint limits, velocity limits, and collision avoidance, including self-collision, constrain the solution.

The closest prior method, [[2509.26633|OmniRetarget]], also preserves interactions, using an interaction mesh. It solves only for the robot, treats the object trajectory as fixed, and globally rescales the demonstration. OTRetarget makes the object pose a decision variable alongside the robot pose.

Pipeline: human meshes and motion feed proximity triples; a transport plan, computed once, maps targets; an optional data-augmentation stage changes the scene or object; constrained inverse kinematics jointly solves robot and object poses; RL training and sim-to-real follow.

### 3. The contact test

The comparison uses two metrics. A contact event pairs a body unit, an object channel, and a frame, and it counts as contact within 20 mm. Jaccard agreement is the overlap divided by the union of the demonstrated and retargeted event sets. Depth error is the mean absolute difference from the demonstrated nonnegative gap, measured over demonstrated contact samples.

The paper computes these metrics per sequence and reports medians across the sequences each method returned. On OMOMO, OTRetarget reaches 87% robot–object Jaccard agreement and 8.7 mm depth error, versus 28% and 29.3 mm for OmniRetarget. OmniRetarget reports infeasibility on 424 of the 4,421 sequences, mostly chairs and large tables. Excluding these failures slightly favors that baseline.

The authors also test an ablation of OTRetarget with a scaled scene and a fixed object. On OMOMO it reaches 82% Jaccard and 8.8 mm depth error. The authors read this retained gain as evidence that interaction residuals drive most of the improvement. The ablation is consistent with that reading. It does not show that these residuals alone explain the gap with OmniRetarget, because it does not isolate every solver or design choice.

The result also varies by case. In the single large-box capture, OmniRetarget has lower depth error (5.7 mm versus 10.0 mm), though OTRetarget has higher Jaccard agreement (85% versus 56%).

### 4. A reference that reaches the robot

The retargeted reference is what a downstream tracking policy follows. In the authors' demonstration, a learned policy completed 6 of 8 physical G1 pick-and-place trials without retraining or object perception. This was a single-robot, single-clip demonstration rather than a hardware study.

The established contribution is better measured robot–object contact fidelity on OMOMO, from transferring contact as a surface relationship and solving robot and object together. Its boundary comes from the authors: evaluation is limited to one robot, flat ground, and one two-object capture. The solver is kinematic and frame-by-frame, without force modeling or horizon-wide optimization.
