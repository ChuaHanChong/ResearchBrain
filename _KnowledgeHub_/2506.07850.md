# SAM2Auto: Auto Annotation Using FLASH

> [!summary] Summary
> SAM2Auto introduces a fully automated and training-free pipeline for video annotation, leveraging robust object detection and real-time multi-object video instance segmentation. This system reduces false positives in object detection by 57% and improves Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) by 1393% on the MOT17 dataset, establishing a new baseline for scalable Vision-Language Model dataset creation.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face a 'dataset bottleneck' due to the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale paired visual and textual video annotations.
- Traditional manual video annotation is labor-intensive, expensive, time-consuming, and prone to inconsistencies, especially for maintaining consistent object IDs across frames.
- Existing open-world detection and tracking methods often require human intervention or dataset-specific training, limiting their scalability and generalizability for diverse video datasets.

## Method

- SAM2Auto is an end-to-end automated pipeline consisting of ==SMART-OD for robust open-vocabulary object detection== and ==FLASH for real-time multi-object video instance segmentation==.
- SMART-OD integrates SAM2 for mask generation, YOLO-World for object detection on masked inputs, and a robust verification stage using ==SAHI and dynamic thresholding== to minimize false positives.
- FLASH leverages ==ByteTrack for multi-object tracking== with a memory-based SAM2 (ASH module) to propagate polygon segmentations across frames, ensuring temporal consistency and employing post-processing for quality refinement.

## Results

- SMART-OD reduced false positives by **57%** (from 5,861 to 2,516) and improved precision from **59.4%** to **72.8%** on MOT17 training sets compared to a YOLO-World baseline.
- The full SAM2Auto pipeline increased MOTA by **1393%** (from -0.014 to 0.181) and recall by **52.2%** on the MOT17 training set when applying FLASH to SMART-OD detections.
- FLASH achieved the highest IDF1 score (**72.5%**) and ranked second in HOTA (**62.0%**) on the DanceTrack dataset, and the highest mHOTA (**53.7%**) on BDD100K using curated detections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is feasible to achieve fully automated, training-free video annotation, providing a new paradigm for VLM dataset creation that eliminates human intervention.
> - The primary bottleneck for end-to-end automated tracking performance is the quality of initial object detections, particularly the suppression of false positives, rather than the tracking component itself.
> - Memory-based segmentation trackers like SAM2ASH can achieve competitive performance in specific scenarios, such as DanceTrack, where objects have distinctive visual characteristics and tracking protocols align with its design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Rocky and Wu fits squarely into the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. The authors contextualize their work by highlighting the unprecedented progress of Large Language Models (LLMs) and, in contrast, the comparatively slower pace of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

The core issue identified is the "dataset bottleneck" for VLMs. LLMs have benefited immensely from the vast, readily available, and diverse textual data on the internet, enabling large-scale training with minimal human intervention. VLMs, however, require meticulously paired visual and textual annotations—a process that is inherently labor-intensive, expensive, and time-consuming. This scarcity of high-quality, large-scale annotated datasets severely constrains VLM performance and scalability, despite advancements in web-scale datasets like LAION and self-supervised learning techniques (e.g., CLIP, DINO).

Current approaches to mitigate this data scarcity include:
- **Self-supervised learning:** Aiming to reduce reliance on labeled data (e.g., DINO).
- **Data augmentation and regularization:** Enhancing data efficiency.
- **Annotation-free methods:** Using synthetic data or leveraging pre-trained 2D models for 3D tasks without manual 3D annotations (e.g., EmerNeRF, Stereo4D).
- **Open-vocabulary object detection (OVOD):** Detectors that can identify known and unknown object classes with high confidence, reducing the need for predefined categories.
- **Semi-supervised learning:** Refining labels with minimal human intervention.
- **Human-in-the-loop systems:** Tools like SAM2, which allow human annotators to refine machine-generated annotations, reducing manual effort but not eliminating it.
- **Industry tools:** Commercial platforms integrating foundation models for automated annotation (e.g., Roboflow).

Despite these efforts, the field still lacks a robust, fully automated annotation method for video datasets that can consistently generate high-quality annotations with *zero human intervention or dataset-specific training*. This is the critical gap that SAM2Auto aims to fill, moving beyond semi-supervised or partially automated solutions to a completely self-sufficient pipeline. The paper specifically highlights the challenge of maintaining consistent object IDs across video frames with reliable tracking and minimal ID switches, which is paramount for video annotation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **SAM2Auto**, the first fully automated annotation pipeline for video datasets that requires absolutely no human intervention or dataset-specific training.

The motivations behind this objective are clear and pressing:
1. **Address the VLM Dataset Bottleneck:** The scarcity of high-quality, large-scale paired visual-textual datasets is the most significant impediment to VLM progress, preventing them from matching the rapid advancements seen in LLMs.
2. **Eliminate Labor-Intensive and Expensive Manual Annotation:** Traditional manual annotation is a costly, time-consuming process prone to bias and inconsistencies, especially for complex tasks like dense object tracking or small object annotation in sequential frames. SAM2Auto aims to remove this burden entirely.
3. **Ensure Consistent Object Identification Across Video Frames:** A major challenge in video annotation is maintaining consistent object IDs, even with intermittent detections or occlusions. SAM2Auto is designed to achieve robust, long-term object tracking.
4. **Achieve Generalizability Without Retraining:** Existing open-world detection methods often require frame-specific hyperparameter tuning, making them impractical for diverse, large-scale datasets. A key motivation is to create a system that works across varied datasets without retraining or extensive parameter adjustments.
5. **Accelerate VLM Development:** By providing a practical and scalable solution for automated video annotation, SAM2Auto directly contributes to overcoming the fundamental data bottleneck, thereby accelerating research and development in vision-language understanding.

In essence, the authors are motivated to democratize large-scale video annotation, making it accessible to researchers and practitioners without vast resources, and thus paving the way for more rapid VLM advancement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAM2Auto is presented as a novel auto-annotation pipeline composed of two key, integrated components: **SMART-OD** for robust object detection and **FLASH** for multi-object real-time video instance segmentation (VIS).

#### 3.1. Overview of SAM2Auto Pipeline

The overall SAM2Auto pipeline aims to generate high-quality, instance-level annotations efficiently. It leverages state-of-the-art foundation models and integrates them through a systematic, seven-step deployment approach (Algorithm 1) that includes representative sequence selection, parameter optimization, cross-validation, dataset-wide application, and quality assurance.

#### 3.2. SMART-OD: Robust Open-Vocabulary Detection

SMART-OD (Segmentation, Mask-guided Analysis and Robust Thresholding for Object Detection) is a multistage object detection and verification pipeline designed to generalize open-vocabulary detection from single images to entire video sequences and datasets, minimizing false positives.

- **Stage 1: Segmentation through Auto Mask Generation:** Utilizes **SAM2** (Segment Anything in Images and Videos) to generate instance masks for all potential objects. This step transforms varying illumination conditions into uniform masked representations, providing consistent visual input for subsequent stages. Key parameters include stability score threshold, offset, and box Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS).
- **Stage 2: Mask-guided Analysis in Object Detection:** Employs **YOLO-World** for open-vocabulary object detection on the mask-guided inputs. YOLO-World is chosen for its speed, adaptability, and zero-shot capabilities. Detections are filtered based on area ratio to eliminate unreasonably sized objects.
- **Stage 3: Robust Thresholding in Detection Verification:** This crucial stage minimizes false positives using **SAHI** (Slicing Aided Hyper Inference) for regional processing and dynamic thresholding.
 - **Detection Clustering:** Spatially proximate detections are clustered using DBSCAN to define regions of interest (ROIs).
 - **Dynamic Thresholding:** An adaptive confidence threshold ($\theta_d$) is calculated for each frame based on the confidence score distribution (e.g., Mean-STD, K-means, K-means + Mean-STD, Double K-means), then constrained by a minimum threshold.
 - **ROI Processing and Verification:** Each ROI is processed by SAHI. A detection is verified if its IoU with a SAHI prediction exceeds a verification threshold and its confidence surpasses the dynamic threshold. This dual-criterion approach ensures only high-quality detections are retained.

#### 3.3. FLASH: Real-Time Video Instance Segmentation for Auto-Annotation

FLASH (Frame-Level Annotation and Segmentation Handler) integrates multi-object tracking with high-quality segmentation to propagate polygon segmentations of new objects throughout a sequence, maintaining temporal consistency. It uses a memory-based segmentation model and is designed for real-time multi-object VIS.

- **Initialization Module:** Handles system setup, checkpoint loading, resume capabilities, and memory-efficient processing (adaptive chunk selection for long videos).
- **Online Object Association Module:** Serves as the Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) solution, specifically leveraging **ByteTrack**. It differentiates between first-frame and subsequent-frame processing, matches new detections to existing tracks using IoU, and identifies truly new objects requiring segmentation initialization.
- **Annotation and Segmentation Handler (ASH):** The core of FLASH. It uses **SAM2** as its memory-based segmentation backbone.
 - **Memory-Efficient Processing:** Employs subset frame processing (propagating masks from detection frame to sequence end) and batch object processing to manage computational load.
 - **Transforming VOS to Real-Time VIS:** Assigns unique track IDs from the Online Object Association module to new objects. The memory-based SAM2 propagates binary masks for these objects across subsequent frames, which are then converted into polygon representations for storage efficiency and further analysis.
 - **Post-Processing:** Applies three techniques to enhance segmentation quality:
 - **Empty Mask Removal:** Eliminates "phantom" masks after an object has exited the scene.
 - **Temporal Smoothing:** Applies a weighted average to current and previous frame segmentations to reduce jitter and flickering.
 - **Merging of Redundant Segments:** Combines highly overlapping segments (based on IoU) to address accumulation of errors and avoid multiple instances of the same physical object.

#### 3.4. Robust Chunk-Based Processing

For long video sequences, FLASH employs an adaptive chunking strategy. A Chunk Manager divides sequences into overlapping chunks with configurable sizes. A Chunk Processor handles individual chunks, ensuring continuity across boundaries by initializing the ASH module with object states from previous chunks. An inter-chunk consistency mechanism resolves identity conflicts in overlap regions using precise IoU matching of segmentation masks, creating consistent global IDs. This strategy ensures bounded memory usage for arbitrarily long videos and includes a fallback mechanism between full-sequence and chunk-based processing.

#### 3.5. SAM2Auto: Unified Pipeline and Systematic Deployment

SAM2Auto integrates SMART-OD and FLASH. The deployment follows seven steps:
1. **Representative sequence selection:** Identifies the sequence with the highest object density.
2. **Parameter optimization:** Optimizes SMART-OD parameters on the most crowded frame of the representative sequence to balance recall and precision.
3. **Sequence-level verification:** Applies optimized parameters to the entire representative sequence.
4. **Cross-sequence validation:** Validates generalization on a randomly selected sequence.
5. **Dataset-wide detection:** Applies the validated SMART-OD configuration across the entire dataset.
6. **Full-scale Sequence Manager application:** Processes detected objects using FLASH (with ByteTrack and SAM2ASH) to generate instance segmentations.
7. **Quality assurance:** Verifies annotations on a stratified sample using IoU with manual labels, with refinement for low-scoring sequences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide a comprehensive evaluation of SAM2Auto, its individual components, and comparisons with state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study of SMART-OD (Detection Performance)

- On MOT17 training sets, SMART-OD demonstrated a strategic trade-off, prioritizing precision over recall.
- It significantly reduced false positives (57% decrease from YOLO-World baseline: 5,861 to 2,516), indicating the effectiveness of its robust verification stage.
- While recall decreased (38.0% to 22.4%), precision substantially improved (59.4% to 72.8%).
- The Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) improved from -0.154 to -0.014, suggesting better overall detection performance despite low tracking-oriented metric values at the detection-only stage.
- SAM2 segmentation alone (SAM2-YW) showed minimal improvement, highlighting that its true value emerges only when combined with the SAHI verification stage.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study of FLASH (Tracker Components)

- **Initialization Module:** Critical for robust processing of long sequences, providing checkpoint management and resume capabilities to prevent out-of-memory errors and improve efficiency.
- **Online Object Association Module:** Essential for computational efficiency by filtering redundant objects and crucial for preventing "artifact generation" (inconsistent segments) in occlusion scenarios by maintaining temporal consistency.
- **ASH's Post-Processing (Merging, Smoothing):** Demonstrated to be vital for refining segmentation quality. Varying the `τ_merge` parameter showed that optimal merging (e.g., `τ_merge` = 0.3) significantly reduces redundant segments and enhances overall consistency, especially after an intentionally loose online association.

#### 4.3. Comparison of FLASH with State-of-the-Art Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) Methods

- **Performance on Public Detection Benchmarks (MOT17, MOT20):** FLASH exhibited suboptimal performance (e.g., MOT17 HOTA 43.60%, MOTA 34.3% vs. GHOST 50.7% HOTA, 61.6% MOTA). This was attributed to the lower quality of public bounding boxes, which negatively impacts SAM2's segmentation initialization. Despite this, FLASH achieved competitive identity preservation (e.g., 3rd best IDSW on MOT17) due to SAM2's memory-based tracking.
- **Performance on Private Detection Benchmarks (MOT17, MOT20, DanceTrack, BDD100K):**
 - On MOT17/MOT20 private detections (with higher quality YOLO-X detections), FLASH still trailed leading methods like SPAM and SUSHI, particularly in crowded pedestrian scenarios.
 - **Exceptional performance on DanceTrack:** FLASH achieved the highest IDF1 score (72.5%) and ranked second in HOTA (62.0%). This highlights SAM2's strength in scenarios where objects have distinctive visual characteristics and tracking protocols align with its memory-based design (i.e., less penalty for not predicting fully occluded objects).
 - **Highest mHOTA on BDD100K (53.7%):** This indicates superior tracking quality across diverse object categories (pedestrians, vehicles, cyclists) when weighted equally, suggesting SAM2's temporal consistency is effective here.
- **Understanding FLASH's Variable Performance:** The inconsistent performance is attributed to: 1) False positives from poor initial bounding boxes (especially for occluded objects), which generate artifact segments. 2) Discrepancies in occlusion handling, where MOT datasets provide ground truth for occluded objects, but FLASH's SAM2-based approach doesn't provide bounding boxes for fully occluded objects, leading to metric penalties. 3) SAM2's architectural limitations in distinguishing visually similar objects when interacting closely.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of the Complete SAM2Auto Pipeline

- Applying FLASH to SMART-OD's detections on MOT17 training set showed a dramatic improvement, transforming detection-only results into a robust multi-object tracking system.
- MOTA increased by 1393% (from -0.014 to 0.181), demonstrating the power of memory-based tracking: once detected, objects are persistently tracked, eliminating the need for re-detection in every frame.
- Recall improved by 52.2%, indicating memory-based tracking recovers objects missed in individual frames.
- This validates the strategic design where SMART-OD provides high-precision initial detections, and FLASH ensures persistent tracking regardless of subsequent visual challenges.

#### 4.5. Differentiating SAM2Auto from SPAMming and Its Implications

- **Key Distinction:** SAM2Auto is **fully automated with 0% manual annotation and no dataset-specific training**, while SPAMming uses 3.3% manual ground truth data to train and fine-tune its components (labeling engine and tracker).
- **Performance Trade-off:** The fully automated nature of SAM2Auto generally leads to lower performance on MOT17/MOT20 compared to SPAMming, which benefits from dataset-specific adaptation. For instance, on MOT17 private, SAM2Auto achieved HOTA 40.5 vs. SPAM-ByteTrack 51.6.
- **Detection Quality Bottleneck:** The performance gap between SAM2Auto and FLASH (which uses curated labels) primarily highlights the impact of automatic detection quality rather than tracking capability. Small numbers of false positives from SMART-OD can severely degrade overall tracking performance by propagating erroneous tracklets.
- **DanceTrack Validation:** FLASH (using curated labels) outperformed SPAMming on DanceTrack (HOTA 62.0 vs. 39.5-41.0), indicating SAM2ASH's true potential when tracking protocols align with its design (i.e., focusing on visible objects and not penalizing absence of predictions for fully occluded objects).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SAM2Auto represents a significant step forward in addressing one of the most critical bottlenecks in the advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): the scarcity and high cost of annotated video datasets.

1. **First Fully Automated and Training-Free Pipeline:** The most significant contribution is the introduction of the first truly fully automated annotation pipeline for video datasets that requires absolutely no human intervention or dataset-specific training. This is a paradigm shift from existing semi-supervised or human-in-the-loop approaches, dramatically reducing labor costs and annotation time.
2. **Democratization of Video Annotation:** By eliminating the need for extensive manual labeling or computational training on target datasets, SAM2Auto makes large-scale video annotation accessible to a broader range of researchers and practitioners, particularly those without vast resources. This can accelerate innovation across various fields reliant on video data, from autonomous driving to robotics and surveillance.
3. **New Baseline for Automated Annotation:** The work establishes a novel baseline for fully automated video annotation, demonstrating the feasibility of zero-annotation tracking while achieving competitive performance against methods that leverage some manual supervision or dataset-specific training.
4. **Decoupling Detection and Tracking Challenges:** The comprehensive ablation studies and comparisons between SMART-OD, FLASH, and SAM2Auto provide crucial insights into the relative importance of detection quality versus tracking capability. The finding that *detection quality*, particularly false positive suppression, remains the primary bottleneck for end-to-end performance offers a clear roadmap for future research and development efforts.
5. **Path for Accelerating VLM Development:** By directly tackling the dataset bottleneck, SAM2Auto provides a tangible pathway for accelerating VLM development, enabling the creation of larger, more diverse datasets that are essential for improving model performance and generalization.
6. **Insights into Tracker Design:** FLASH's exceptional performance on DanceTrack, contrasting with its limitations on crowded MOT datasets, offers valuable insights into the architectural strengths and weaknesses of memory-based segmentation trackers like SAM2ASH, especially concerning occlusion handling and distinguishing visually similar objects.

The authors also outline a clear roadmap for future improvements, including bidirectional tracking to handle annotation gaps, architectural enhancements for distinguishing visually similar objects, and adaptive false positive suppression mechanisms. These proposed enhancements, if implemented, hold the promise of closing the performance gap with semi-supervised methods while retaining the crucial advantage of zero manual annotation. Ultimately, SAM2Auto not only provides a practical framework but also charts a clear course toward a future where fully automated, training-free tracking becomes the preferred and most cost-effective approach for many real-world video understanding applications.
