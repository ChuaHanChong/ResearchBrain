# LIBERO-Para: A Diagnostic Benchmark and Metrics for Paraphrase Robustness in VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Soongsil University and Chung-Ang University introduced LIBERO-Para, a diagnostic benchmark, and PRIDE, a novel metric, to systematically evaluate the paraphrase robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. Their analysis showed VLA models exhibit significant performance degradation (22.8-51.9 percentage points drop) when encountering paraphrased instructions, with object grounding identified as the primary bottleneck and failures predominantly occurring at the planning level.

## Problem

- VLA models, fine-tuned on limited environment-specific data, overfit to specific instruction phrasings, leading to poor generalization to semantically equivalent but linguistically varied instructions.
- Existing VLA benchmarks primarily evaluate visual generalization or treat linguistic variation in an unstructured manner, lacking a controlled assessment of meaning-preserving linguistic robustness.
- Current VLA evaluation metrics, often binary success rates, do not differentiate between easy and difficult paraphrased instructions, obscuring the true linguistic understanding of models.

## Method

- Developed **LIBERO-Para**, a ==two-axis diagnostic benchmark== built on ==LIBERO-Goal==, which systematically generates and evaluates ==meaning-preserving paraphrases== of robotic manipulation instructions by varying ==action expressions== and ==object references==.
- Introduced **PRIDE** (==Paraphrase Robustness Index in Robotic Instructional DEviation==), a novel, ==difficulty-aware metric== that quantifies the ==linguistic deviation== between an original instruction and its paraphrase using ==keyword and structural similarity==.
- Evaluated **seven** diverse VLA model configurations, ranging from ==0.6B to 7.5B parameters==, with various architectural families and ==fine-tuning strategies== on the new benchmark.

## Results

- All seven VLA configurations demonstrated a consistent performance degradation, with success rate drops ranging from **22.8** to **51.9** percentage points when evaluated with paraphrased instructions.
- The PRIDE metric revealed that standard success rates overestimated models' actual robustness, with PRIDE scores being **8.4–22.0%** lower, indicating models succeeded mainly on easier paraphrases.
- Object-level lexical variations caused substantial performance drops (**19.8** to **51.0** percentage points), and **79.5–95.5%** of failures were classified as 'Far-GT,' indicating planning-level errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - All VLA models, irrespective of architecture, data scale, or fine-tuning strategy, consistently exhibit significant fragility to paraphrased instructions, suggesting a widespread limitation in linguistic generalization.
> - Object grounding emerges as a primary bottleneck for VLA models, as lexical variations in object references cause substantially greater performance degradation than structural or pragmatic changes in action expressions.
> - Failures under paraphrased instructions are predominantly planning-level errors, where models fundamentally misinterpret the task or target object, rather than minor execution-level motor control failures.
