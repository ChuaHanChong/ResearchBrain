# LIBERO-Para: A Diagnostic Benchmark and Metrics for Paraphrase Robustness in VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Soongsil University and Chung-Ang University introduced LIBERO-Para, a diagnostic benchmark, and PRIDE, a novel metric, to systematically evaluate the paraphrase robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. Their analysis showed VLA models exhibit significant performance degradation (22.8-51.9 percentage points drop) when encountering paraphrased instructions, with object grounding identified as the primary bottleneck and failures predominantly occurring at the planning level.

## Problem

- VLA models, fine-tuned on limited environment-specific data, overfit to specific instruction phrasings, leading to poor generalization to semantically equivalent but linguistically varied instructions.
- Existing VLA benchmarks primarily evaluate visual generalization or treat linguistic variation in an unstructured manner, lacking a controlled assessment of meaning-preserving linguistic robustness.
- Current VLA evaluation metrics, often binary success rates, do not differentiate between easy and difficult paraphrased instructions, obscuring the true linguistic understanding of models.

## Method

- Developed **LIBERO-Para**, a ==two-axis diagnostic benchmark== built on ==LIBERO-Goal==, which systematically generates and evaluates ==meaning-preserving paraphrases== of robotic manipulation instructions by varying ==action expressions== and ==object references==.
- Introduced **PRIDE** (==Paraphrase Robustness Index in Robotic Instructional DEviation==), a novel, ==difficulty-aware metric== that quantifies the ==linguistic deviation== between an original instruction and its paraphrase using ==keyword and structural similarity==.
- Evaluated **seven** diverse VLA model configurations, ranging from ==0.6B to 7.5B parameters==, with various architectural families and ==fine-tuning strategies== on the new benchmark.

## Results

- All seven VLA configurations demonstrated a consistent performance degradation, with success rate drops ranging from **22.8** to **51.9** percentage points when evaluated with paraphrased instructions.
- The PRIDE metric revealed that standard success rates overestimated models' actual robustness, with PRIDE scores being **8.4–22.0%** lower, indicating models succeeded mainly on easier paraphrases.
- Object-level lexical variations caused substantial performance drops (**19.8** to **51.0** percentage points), and **79.5–95.5%** of failures were classified as 'Far-GT,' indicating planning-level errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - All VLA models, irrespective of architecture, data scale, or fine-tuning strategy, consistently exhibit significant fragility to paraphrased instructions, suggesting a widespread limitation in linguistic generalization.
> - Object grounding emerges as a primary bottleneck for VLA models, as lexical variations in object references cause substantially greater performance degradation than structural or pragmatic changes in action expressions.
> - Failures under paraphrased instructions are predominantly planning-level errors, where models fundamentally misinterpret the task or target object, rather than minor execution-level motor control failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm for robotic manipulation, integrating visual, linguistic, and action capabilities, often by leveraging large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs). These models typically undergo fine-tuning using limited, environment-specific demonstration data for deployment in real-world settings such as kitchens or offices. However, this data-scarce fine-tuning can lead to overfitting, where models perform effectively on seen instruction formulations but exhibit reduced generalization to semantically equivalent but linguistically varied instructions, known as paraphrases.

The existing research landscape for VLA models has primarily focused on evaluating visual generalization (e.g., to novel object configurations or scene layouts), with linguistic robustness remaining largely unexamined or insufficiently validated. Benchmarks such as LIBERO evaluate models under identical instructions during training and evaluation, thus not addressing linguistic variations. Other benchmarks like CALVIN and LADEV assess rephrased instructions but treat paraphrasing as unstructured sentence-level variation without linguistic categorization. Multimodal perturbation-based evaluations (LIBERO-PRO, LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-X) include linguistic variation as one axis but treat paraphrasing as a secondary concern within a broader scope. LangGap aims at language conditioning but often introduces perturbations that alter the intended behavior, conflating task-level semantic changes with linguistic variations.

This work addresses the limitations of existing benchmarks by introducing a controlled environment for assessing VLA model robustness to meaning-preserving linguistic variations. It specifically isolates paraphrase robustness and provides a method for fine-grained analysis of how different linguistic factors (action expressions and object references) affect VLA performance, thereby filling a gap in the systematic evaluation of linguistic generalization in VLA models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to paraphrased instructions, a critical aspect for their reliable deployment in real-world scenarios.

The detailed objectives and motivations are as follows:

- **To address the issue of overfitting in VLA models due to data-scarce fine-tuning:** VLA models, when fine-tuned on limited environment-specific data, tend to overfit to the exact phrasing of instructions encountered during training. This creates a robustness gap where models may fail to generalize to instructions that have the same meaning but are expressed differently at deployment. The research aims to quantify this performance degradation.
- **To introduce a controlled benchmark for evaluating paraphrase robustness:** Current benchmarks for VLA models primarily assess visual generalization or treat linguistic variation in an unstructured manner. There is a need for a benchmark that specifically isolates and controls linguistic variations while preserving semantic meaning. LIBERO-Para is developed to fulfill this need, enabling a fine-grained analysis of how specific linguistic changes in action expressions and object references impact model performance.
- **To develop a metric that quantifies paraphrase difficulty:** Existing VLA evaluation metrics typically rely on binary success rates, which do not differentiate between easy and difficult paraphrased instructions. This obscures whether a model genuinely understands diverse linguistic forms or merely succeeds on simpler variations. The PRIDE (Paraphrase Robustness Index in Robotic Instructional DEviation) metric is proposed to quantify the linguistic deviation of paraphrases from original instructions, allowing for a more nuanced assessment of robustness.
- **To provide fine-grained diagnostic insights into VLA model failures:** By systematically varying action expressions and object references and introducing a difficulty-aware metric, the research aims to identify which types of linguistic variations most significantly degrade VLA performance. This includes distinguishing between failures caused by lexical variations, structural changes, or pragmatic shifts in instructions.
- **To understand the nature of failures in VLA models under paraphrasing:** The study seeks to determine whether performance degradation under paraphrased instructions stems from execution-level errors (minor motor control issues while pursuing the correct task) or planning-level errors (fundamental misidentification of the task or target object from the outset). This diagnostic insight is crucial for guiding future research efforts toward the most impactful areas of improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development and application of a new benchmark, LIBERO-Para, and a novel metric, PRIDE, to evaluate VLA model robustness.

**LIBERO-Para Benchmark Construction:**
The benchmark is built upon the LIBERO-Goal setting, where tasks commence from identical initial states, rendering the linguistic instruction the sole determinant of task identification. To create LIBERO-Para, only instructions are paraphrased, while all other environmental and task parameters remain constant. These paraphrased instructions are explicitly held out for evaluation, ensuring assessment of generalization capabilities under data-scarce fine-tuning.

- **Two-Axis Design:** The core of LIBERO-Para is a two-axis design that independently varies two fundamental linguistic components of robotic manipulation instructions:
 - **Action Variation:** This axis captures how actions are expressed linguistically. It is categorized into three types based on established paraphrase taxonomies (Extended Paraphrase Typology by Kovatchev et al., 2018, and Directive Types by Ervin-Tripp, 1976):
 - *Lexical Variation:* Modifies action verbs at the word level, including synonym substitution and adverb insertion (e.g., "turn on" → "switch on", "turn on" → "carefully turn on").
 - *Structural Variation:* Alters the sentence-level realization of the action, such as coordination ("Turn on the stove" → "Go to the stove and turn it on") and subordination ("Turn on the stove" → "Turn on the stove so that it becomes hot").
 - *Pragmatic Variation:* Expresses actions indirectly, encompassing indirect speech acts like need-statements, embedded imperatives, permission directives, question directives, and hints (e.g., "Turn on the stove" → "I need the stove turned on," "Is the stove heating up?").
 - **Object Variation:** This axis focuses on how target objects are referenced. It primarily involves lexical-level changes, derived from the Extended Paraphrase Typology:
 - *Addition:* Adding descriptive adjectives to object nouns (e.g., "the stove" → "the gas stove").
 - *Same-polarity substitution (contextual and habitual):* Replacing object nouns with synonyms or contextually similar terms (e.g., "the stove" → "the cooktop," "the hob").
- **Compositional Variation:** The benchmark also includes compositional paraphrases that combine variations from both action and object axes, enabling the study of interaction effects.
- **Dataset Size and Generation:** LIBERO-Para comprises 4,092 paraphrased instructions, generated from 10 original LIBERO-Goal tasks. Approximately 100 samples are included per variation type to ensure balanced evaluation. The generation process employed large language models (LLMs, specifically Gemini 2.5 Pro) for axis-wise paraphrase generation and subsequent verification to ensure meaning preservation, grammatical naturalness, and adherence to visual/spatial constraints. A human evaluation on a subset of the benchmark confirmed strong inter-annotator agreement (Gwet’s AC1 of 0.854) and a high rate of meaning-preserving paraphrases (94.18%).

**PRIDE (Paraphrase Robustness Index in Robotic Instructional DEviation) Metric:**
PRIDE is designed to provide interpretable robustness evaluations beyond binary success rates by quantifying the linguistic deviation between an original instruction and its paraphrase. It decomposes paraphrase variation along two robot-relevant axes:

- **Keyword Similarity (S_K):** Measures the preservation of core keywords (action verbs, target object nouns) that are task-critical. It uses Sentence-BERT embeddings to represent content words and calculates the average cosine similarity between matched words in the original and paraphrased instructions. This approach aims to be more sensitive to semantic changes in critical task components than traditional n-gram-based NLP metrics.
- **Structural Similarity (S_T):** Quantifies syntactic changes using the Tree Edit Distance (TED) between the dependency trees of the original and paraphrased instructions. Node labels are part-of-speech (POS) tags, and edge labels are dependency relations, making it robust to superficial lexical changes. S_T is normalized by the combined size of the trees to mitigate sentence-length effects.
- **Paraphrase Distance (PD):** Combines S_K and S_T into a single score: PD = 1 - [αS_K + (1-α)S_T], where α (default 0.5) balances the contribution of keyword and structural similarity. Higher PD indicates greater deviation.
- **PRIDE Score:** Defined as (PD, success) if the task is successfully completed, and (0, failure) otherwise. This allows for weighting successful outcomes by the difficulty of the paraphrase.

**Experimental Setup:**
Seven VLA model configurations, ranging from 0.6B to 7.5B parameters, were evaluated. These models represent diverse architectural families:
- Parallel decoding with action chunking (OpenVLA-OFT_goal, OpenVLA-OFT_mixed).
- VLM coupled with a flow-matching action expert (π0.5, Xiaomi-Robotics-0).
- Lightweight bridge-based adaptation (VLA-Adapter).
- Soft-prompted cross-embodiment designs (X-VLA).
The evaluation also included controlled comparisons of fine-tuning strategies (data scope: OFT_goal vs. OFT_mixed; VLM training: π0.5 full vs. expert-only). Each model was evaluated across 5 random seeds per task-paraphrase configuration in the LIBERO simulation environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The analysis of VLA models on LIBERO-Para and with the PRIDE metric yielded three primary findings:

- **Finding 1: Paraphrase Fragility Persists Across Architectures, Data Scales, and Fine-tuning Strategies.**
 - All seven VLA configurations exhibited consistent performance degradation when evaluated with paraphrased instructions on LIBERO-Para compared to their performance on LIBERO-Goal. The success rate drops ranged from 22.8 to 51.9 percentage points.
 - This vulnerability was observed across a spectrum of architectures (0.6B to 7.5B parameters), indicating that it is not an architecture-specific issue but a widespread limitation.
 - Variations in training data scope (OpenVLA-OFT_mixed vs. OpenVLA-OFT_goal) did not substantially mitigate this fragility. Models trained with four times more tasks showed similar drops (32.4 pp vs. 33.2 pp), suggesting that increased task diversity alone does not improve robustness to linguistic variation.
 - The VLM fine-tuning strategy also did not fully resolve the issue. While joint fine-tuning of the VLM and Action Expert (standard π0.5) achieved higher success rates than freezing the VLM and fine-tuning only the Action Expert (π0.5 expert-only), both variants still experienced significant performance drops under paraphrasing.
 - The PRIDE metric revealed that uniform success rates overestimated models' actual robustness. PRIDE scores were 8.4–22.0% lower than standard success rates, indicating that models often succeeded only on easier paraphrases and failed systematically on more difficult linguistic variations.

- **Finding 2: Object Grounding Emerges as a Primary Bottleneck.**
 - Analysis revealed an asymmetry in the sources of degradation: object-level lexical variation proved to be the dominant factor.
 - When object references were paraphrased, even with simple synonym substitutions (e.g., "stove" to "range"), models experienced substantial performance drops, ranging from 19.8 pp to 51.0 pp across different models. This suggests a reliance on surface-level keyword matching for object identification rather than a deeper semantic grounding.
 - In contrast, variations in action expressions, including structural and pragmatic changes that made instructions more indirect, introduced additional degradation but were generally less disruptive than object-level changes. For instance, success rates declined from 82.7% for direct instructions to approximately 48% for highly indirect pragmatic forms (e.g., "Question," "Hint").
 - This asymmetry is attributed to the inherent properties of tabletop manipulation tasks within the benchmark: a limited action space reduces action ambiguity, while a larger, open-ended object space concentrates combinatorial complexity on object references. The observation that LIBERO-Goal training data consistently uses single canonical names for objects may further reinforce models' sensitivity to exact lexical matches.

- **Finding 3: Failures Are Predominantly Planning-Level, Not Execution-Level.**
 - Trajectory analysis using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) distance was employed to classify failures. For most models, 79.5–95.5% of failures were categorized as "Far-GT," meaning the robot generated fundamentally different trajectories from the successful pseudo ground-truth (GT) trajectories. This indicates errors in task identification or planning.
 - Conversely, "Near-GT" failures, where the robot followed a trajectory similar to the GT but failed due to minor execution errors, accounted for less than 5% in most models (with π0.5 expert-only being an exception at 12.5%).
 - This finding suggests that under paraphrased instructions, VLA models primarily struggle with correctly interpreting the instruction and identifying the intended task and target object, rather than failing at the low-level execution of a correctly planned task. The main bottleneck lies in the instruction semantic-to-task mapping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation and vision-language models by providing a structured framework for evaluating and understanding linguistic robustness.

- **Advanced Diagnostic Capabilities:** The introduction of LIBERO-Para and PRIDE provides a diagnostic benchmark and a fine-grained metric that go beyond traditional binary success rates. This enables researchers to precisely identify which types of linguistic variations (lexical vs. structural, action-specific vs. object-specific) pose the greatest challenges to VLA models. This diagnostic capability is critical for guiding targeted improvements.
- **Revealed Fundamental Limitations:** The findings highlight a fundamental limitation of current VLA models: their reliance on surface-level keyword matching, particularly for object references, rather than robust semantic grounding. This suggests that despite impressive performance on seen instruction phrasings, current models may not possess a deep understanding of linguistic nuances relevant to real-world task execution.
- **Guidance for Future Research Directions:**
 - **Prioritizing Object Grounding:** The identified bottleneck in object-level lexical variation indicates a need for research efforts to focus on improving how VLA models semantically ground object references. This could involve developing more robust object recognition and association mechanisms that are less sensitive to synonym substitutions or descriptive additions.
 - **Enhancing Task Identification:** The predominance of planning-level failures suggests that improving paraphrase robustness requires prioritizing the instruction-to-task identification stage over mere low-level motor control refinement. Future work could explore methods to make VLA models more resilient to pragmatic inference and structural reorganizations in instructions.
 - **Informing Data Collection and Augmentation:** The study implicitly suggests the need for training datasets that include richer linguistic variations, particularly in object references, to better prepare models for diverse real-world language use.
- **Advancing Real-World Deployability:** By systematically exposing and quantifying vulnerabilities to linguistic variation, this work paves the way for developing more robust and reliable VLA systems. In practical applications, users naturally employ diverse phrasing, and models capable of understanding these variations are essential for seamless human-robot interaction.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The two-axis paraphrase design and the PRIDE metric offer a template for future evaluations of language understanding in grounded AI systems, promoting more controlled and interpretable assessments of linguistic generalization.

The study acknowledges limitations, including its reliance on simulation and its focus on individual paraphrase types rather than compound variations, indicating avenues for future research to extend these findings to real-world platforms and more complex linguistic phenomena.
