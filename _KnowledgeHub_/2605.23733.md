# Any2Any: Efficient Cross-Embodiment Transfer for Humanoid Whole-Body Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> LimX Dynamics researchers developed ANY2ANY, a framework for efficiently transferring pre-trained humanoid Whole-Body Tracking (WBT) policies across different robot embodiments. The method, which combines kinematic alignment with low-rank adaptation (LoRA), enables adaptation with only approximately 1% of the compute and data required for training from scratch, while achieving comparable or superior tracking performance.

## Problem

- Whole-Body Tracking (WBT) models are highly embodiment-dependent, requiring extensive data, computational resources (e.g., 9,000 GPU hours), and simulation infrastructure for training from scratch on each new humanoid platform.
- Existing cross-embodiment robot learning approaches, such as training universal controllers, typically necessitate expensive pre-training on broad multi-embodiment datasets.
- Direct transfer of pre-trained, embodiment-specific WBT policies to new humanoids is unreliable due to significant differences in morphology, actuator configuration, and sensor interfaces.

## Method

- The ANY2ANY framework proposes a two-stage approach for cross-embodiment transfer: kinematic alignment and dynamic adaptation.
- Kinematic alignment resolves structural incompatibilities by mapping observation and action spaces between source and target robots, utilizing sparse scattering, hip decoupling, and parallel joint coupling matrices.
- Dynamic adaptation employs Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), specifically ==LoRA==, to learn a compact residual adjustment for the target robot's dynamics while freezing the majority of the pre-trained policy's parameters.

## Results

- ANY2ANY adapted pre-trained WBT policies across diverse humanoid platforms (LimX Oli, LimX Luna, Unitree G1, Unitree H1) using only ~**1%** of the compute and data required for training from scratch.
- The framework consistently achieved comparable or superior tracking performance and faster convergence compared to training specialist policies from scratch, demonstrated by lower tracking errors and higher rewards.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of kinematic alignment and showed LoRA outperformed other PEFT methods, particularly when injected into dynamics-aware control pathways like the actor backbone and its input/output projections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-embodiment transfer for WBT policies can be effectively decomposed into distinct kinematic (structural) and dynamic (behavioral) adaptation components.
> - Pre-trained WBT specialist policies contain valuable, generalizable motor priors that can be efficiently reused, significantly reducing the resource cost of deploying WBT on new humanoid platforms.
> - Lightweight adaptation mechanisms like LoRA are sufficient to bridge dynamic mismatches after kinematic alignment, preserving the extensive knowledge encoded in the pre-trained policy.
