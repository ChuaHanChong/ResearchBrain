# Any2Any: Efficient Cross-Embodiment Transfer for Humanoid Whole-Body Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> LimX Dynamics researchers developed ANY2ANY, a framework for efficiently transferring pre-trained humanoid Whole-Body Tracking (WBT) policies across different robot embodiments. The method, which combines kinematic alignment with low-rank adaptation (LoRA), enables adaptation with only approximately 1% of the compute and data required for training from scratch, while achieving comparable or superior tracking performance.

## Problem

- Whole-Body Tracking (WBT) models are highly embodiment-dependent, requiring extensive data, computational resources (e.g., 9,000 GPU hours), and simulation infrastructure for training from scratch on each new humanoid platform.
- Existing cross-embodiment robot learning approaches, such as training universal controllers, typically necessitate expensive pre-training on broad multi-embodiment datasets.
- Direct transfer of pre-trained, embodiment-specific WBT policies to new humanoids is unreliable due to significant differences in morphology, actuator configuration, and sensor interfaces.

## Method

- The ANY2ANY framework proposes a two-stage approach for cross-embodiment transfer: kinematic alignment and dynamic adaptation.
- Kinematic alignment resolves structural incompatibilities by mapping observation and action spaces between source and target robots, utilizing sparse scattering, hip decoupling, and parallel joint coupling matrices.
- Dynamic adaptation employs Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), specifically ==LoRA==, to learn a compact residual adjustment for the target robot's dynamics while freezing the majority of the pre-trained policy's parameters.

## Results

- ANY2ANY adapted pre-trained WBT policies across diverse humanoid platforms (LimX Oli, LimX Luna, Unitree G1, Unitree H1) using only ~**1%** of the compute and data required for training from scratch.
- The framework consistently achieved comparable or superior tracking performance and faster convergence compared to training specialist policies from scratch, demonstrated by lower tracking errors and higher rewards.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of kinematic alignment and showed LoRA outperformed other PEFT methods, particularly when injected into dynamics-aware control pathways like the actor backbone and its input/output projections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-embodiment transfer for WBT policies can be effectively decomposed into distinct kinematic (structural) and dynamic (behavioral) adaptation components.
> - Pre-trained WBT specialist policies contain valuable, generalizable motor priors that can be efficiently reused, significantly reducing the resource cost of deploying WBT on new humanoid platforms.
> - Lightweight adaptation mechanisms like LoRA are sufficient to bridge dynamic mismatches after kinematic alignment, preserving the extensive knowledge encoded in the pre-trained policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots has seen significant advancements, particularly in whole-body tracking (WBT) models, which serve as foundational components for enabling robots to imitate diverse motions with high fidelity. This work situates itself within the evolving field of behavior foundation models (BFMs) in robotics, where policies are pretrained on large-scale data to acquire generalizable motor skills and behavioral priors. Recent WBT systems like TWIST, GMT, SONIC, HoloMotion, and OmniXtreme have demonstrated the capability of end-to-end policies to cover a wide range of whole-body motions through various learning paradigms, scaling up in terms of data, model size, and motion complexity. For instance, SONIC reported training on over 100 million motion frames and requiring approximately 9,000 GPU hours.

However, this progress highlights two primary challenges: the substantial computational resources and large-scale datasets required to train such sophisticated WBT models from scratch, and their inherent embodiment dependence. Existing large-scale WBT policies are typically optimized for a specific robot's morphology, actuator configuration, observation layout, and action space. This tight coupling makes direct deployment of a pretrained policy onto a different humanoid embodiment unreliable, and full fine-tuning of the policy often results in overwriting the previously acquired behavioral priors.

The broader robotics community has explored cross-embodiment methods, often by training universal controllers across multiple embodiments using techniques such as morphology randomization, multi-embodiment datasets, or embodiment-aware architectures. These approaches, while powerful, generally necessitate extensive multi-embodiment data, broad morphological coverage during pretraining, or costly training from scratch on diverse platforms. Such requirements are particularly challenging for high-fidelity WBT, where even specialist policies demand significant resources.

This paper addresses a complementary problem: the post-training adaptation of an *existing, robot-specific* WBT policy to a *new* humanoid embodiment with limited data and computational overhead. This differentiates it from approaches that aim to train generalist models from the outset. The work is also informed by the principle of decoupling kinematics from dynamics in humanoid control, a concept prevalent in both classical and learning-based control pipelines. This decoupling suggests that a pretrained policy might exhibit different degrees of transferability across its components, with kinematic aspects being more universal and dynamic aspects being more embodiment-specific.

The research also draws upon advancements in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, which have been successfully applied in large language models and are beginning to be explored in robotics. PEFT methods, such as Adapter tuning, Prefix-Tuning, and LoRA, aim to adapt pretrained models to new tasks or domains by updating only a small subset of parameters, thereby preserving the original model's capabilities while specializing it. The challenge in closed-loop humanoid WBT, however, is that even minor parameter changes can propagate through complex contact dynamics and balance regulation, potentially destabilizing the control system. This work investigates how and where to apply PEFT effectively in this context.

In summary, this work contributes to the landscape by offering a structured, efficient paradigm for adapting existing, specialized WBT policies to new humanoid platforms, circumventing the high costs and re-learning associated with training from scratch or developing universal multi-robot controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the significant challenges associated with deploying advanced whole-body tracking (WBT) models on diverse humanoid robot platforms.
The key motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **High Cost of Training WBT Models from Scratch:** Training state-of-the-art WBT models requires substantial computational resources (e.g., thousands of GPU hours) and large-scale, high-quality motion data. This makes rapid deployment and iteration on new humanoid hardware economically and practically challenging.
- **Embodiment Dependence of Existing WBT Policies:** Current large-scale WBT policies are tightly coupled to the specific morphology and actuator configurations of the robot they were trained on. Structural and dynamic differences between humanoids, even those that appear similar, prevent direct transfer and reliable deployment.
- **Inefficiency of Full Policy Fine-tuning:** While fine-tuning is a common adaptation strategy, updating all parameters of a complex pretrained WBT policy can be computationally expensive and risks "catastrophic forgetting" – overwriting the valuable, generalizable motor skills and behavioral priors acquired during initial training.
- **Absence of Systematic Cross-Embodiment Transfer for Humanoid WBT:** Prior cross-embodiment methods primarily focus on training generalist controllers from scratch using multi-embodiment data or morphology randomization. There has been a lack of systematic study on efficiently transferring an *already pretrained, robot-specific* WBT policy to a new, distinct humanoid embodiment in a post-training setting.

To address these motivations, the research establishes three key objectives, framed as questions:

1. **Can localized fine-tuning adapt to the target embodiment without destroying the source prior?** This objective aims to investigate if a partial, targeted update to the pretrained policy's parameters can effectively bridge the dynamics gap while preserving the general motor skills already learned by the source policy.
2. **How should the kinematic mismatch be resolved?** This objective seeks to develop a robust mechanism to account for structural differences between source and target robots, such as varying joint counts, link geometries, and observation/action space layouts, to ensure the pretrained policy's inputs and outputs are semantically consistent with the target embodiment.
3. **Which modules should be adapted to maximize cross-embodiment transfer?** This objective focuses on identifying the specific components within a WBT policy that are most sensitive to embodiment-specific dynamics and thus require adaptation, distinguishing them from more generalizable modules that can remain frozen.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate a scalable and cost-effective paradigm for deploying advanced WBT capabilities on new humanoid platforms, leveraging existing pretrained models rather than rebuilding them for each robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named ANY2ANY, decomposes cross-embodiment whole-body tracking (WBT) transfer into two sequential and complementary stages: kinematic alignment and dynamics adaptation. The approach aims to efficiently transfer a pretrained, robot-specific WBT policy from a source robot ($E_S$) to a target robot ($E_T$) with minimal data and computational resources.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The WBT task is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) for a given robot embodiment. The goal is to adapt a pretrained policy $\pi_{\theta_S}: X_S \to A_S$ (where $\theta_S$ are the full parameters) to a target robot $E_T$, whose state space $X_T$ and action space $A_T$ differ from $X_S$ and $A_S$. Instead of retraining $\theta_S$ from scratch on $E_T$, ANY2ANY aims to learn a small, target-specific adaptation $\Delta\theta_T$ such that $|\Delta\theta_T| \ll |\theta_S|$. The target policy is then expressed as $\pi_{\theta_T}(a_t|s_t) = \pi_{\theta_S} \oplus \Delta\theta_T(a_t|s_t)$, where $\oplus$ denotes parameter-efficient injection. This adaptation is trained under a standard reinforcement learning objective to maximize expected return.

#### 3.2. Pretrained Whole Body Tracking Policy Structure

ANY2ANY uses an actor-critic framework, with the actor $\pi_\theta$ outputting target joint position offsets and the critic $V_\phi$ estimating state values. Policies are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). To demonstrate generalizability, three different actor backbones are considered:
- **Unified Observation Formulation:** Policy input consists of proprioceptive feedback ($\phi_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_p}$, including base angular velocity, projected gravity, joint positions, velocities, and previous action) and reference motion information ($g_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_r}$, extracted from the target trajectory).
- **MLP Backbone:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) flattens observations over $H+1$ timesteps and processes them through an $L$-layer MLP to predict actions.
- **Transformer Backbone:** Models temporal dependencies by projecting each modality into a shared embedding space and processing sequences with $N$ causal Transformer Decoder blocks.
- **Sonic Backbone:** Utilizes the Robot Motion Encoder, FSQ bottleneck, and dynamics decoder modules from the Sonic architecture, representing a large-scale WBT policy.

#### 3.3. Cross-Embodiment Adaptation

This is the core of the ANY2ANY method, addressing kinematic and dynamic discrepancies.

##### 3.3.1. Kinematic Alignment

To resolve structural kinematic mismatches (e.g., differing degrees of freedom, joint configurations), a two-level kinematic alignment module is applied to the policy's input and output streams:

- **Level 1: Observation Layout Alignment:** Rearranges the target robot's observation components (base states, reference features, proprioceptive states, action history) to match the semantic ordering and convention expected by the pretrained source policy.
- **Level 2: Joint-Level Kinematic Alignment:** Aligns the internal ordering and geometry of joint-related variables. This involves constructing forward ($\Phi_r: \mathbb{R}^{N_r} \to \mathbb{R}^T$) and inverse ($\Phi_r^+: \mathbb{R}^T \to \mathbb{R}^{N_r}$) mappings between the target robot's $N_r$ degrees of freedom and the source robot's $T$ degrees of freedom (acting as a unified semantic space). This mapping consists of three structured components:
 - **(i) Sparse Scattering Matrix ($S_r \in \{0, 1\}^{T \times N_r}$):** Handles differences in joint counts and ordering by scattering target joint values into the source layout. Joints shared by both robots are mapped, while source positions without a target counterpart are zero-padded, and surplus target joints are dropped.
 - **(ii) Hip Decoupling Matrix ($D_r \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$):** Compensates for structural differences like inclined hip-pitch axes. $D_r$ is an identity matrix with specific $2 \times 2$ decoupling blocks ($H_{L/R}$) applied to the left and right hip coordinates.
 - **(iii) Parallel Joint Coupling ($J_r \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times T}$):** Addresses closed-chain mechanisms (e.g., parallelogram-driven ankles, closed-loop waists) where actuated joint values do not directly correspond to kinematic counterparts in a serial chain. This component applies a Jacobian correction to preserve the effective joint-level relation.

The combined alignment maps are $\Phi_r = J_r D_r^{-1} S_r$ for input observations and $\Phi_r^+ = S_r^\top D_r J_r^{-1}$ for policy actions, ensuring the frozen source policy operates on a consistent kinematic semantic space.

##### 3.3.2. Dynamics Adaptation

After kinematic alignment, the remaining discrepancy is dynamic, relating to differences in mass matrices ($M(q)$), Coriolis/centrifugal terms ($C(q, \dot{q})$), and gravity loading ($G(q)$), as well as unmodeled effects. This "dynamics gap" ($\Delta\eta = \eta_T - \eta_S$) is hypothesized to be low-dimensional.
ANY2ANY addresses this by freezing most pretrained parameters $\theta_S$ and learning a low-rank correction using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation). For each adapted linear projection $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{out} \times d_{in}}$ in the policy, a decomposition $W' = W + BA$ is applied, where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d_{in}}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{out} \times k}$ with $k \ll \min(d_{in}, d_{out})$. Only the low-rank factors $\{A, B\}$ are trained for the target embodiment, while $W$ remains frozen. LoRA modules are strategically injected into the proprioceptive input projection and the actor and critic linear layers, while the reference-motion encoder is kept frozen, based on the hypothesis that the latter extracts more embodiment-agnostic features.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

All policies are trained using PPO within Isaac Lab. To isolate the effect of ANY2ANY, the action space, observations, reward formulation, PPO hyperparameters, reference motion sampling, and domain randomization protocol are kept identical to the source pretraining setup.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the core hypothesis of ANY2ANY regarding the efficient transfer of WBT policies across embodiments.

#### 4.1. Cross-Embodiment Transfer Performance

Experiments were conducted on five source-target transfer pairs across four humanoid platforms (LimX Oli, LimX Luna, Unitree G1, Unitree H1) and two pretrained WBT backbones (self-pretrained Oli-WBT and open-source Sonic).
- **Sonic as Source (SONIC2OLI, SONIC2LUNA, SONIC2H1):** ANY2ANY consistently transferred the Sonic WBT prior to all three target humanoids, which span different kinematic and dynamic gaps (inclined hips, closed-chain mechanisms, varying DoF). The total reward increased faster and converged to higher values compared to training from scratch (Scratch baseline). This gain was consistent in both anchor-position and relative-body-position tracking rewards (Figure 3).
 - On held-out benchmarks (OMOMO for manipulation, LAFAN for dynamic locomotion), ANY2ANY significantly improved performance. For SONIC2OLI, LAFAN success rate increased from 83.1% to 90.6%, MPJPE decreased from 69.7mm to 47.2mm, and base position/orientation errors reduced. Similar improvements in stability, joint-level fidelity, and action smoothness were observed for SONIC2LUNA and SONIC2H1.
- **Oli-WBT as Source (OLIWBT2LUNA, OLIWBT2G1, OLIWBT2H1):** The same transfer paradigm applied to the self-pretrained Oli-WBT policy yielded consistent optimization advantages. ANY2ANY reached high-reward regimes much faster and converged to higher or comparable final rewards than Scratch (Figure 4). Held-out evaluation also showed improvements in joint-level and root tracking for OMOMO and enhanced stability and accuracy for LAFAN.

These results indicate that ANY2ANY efficiently reuses the WBT prior by establishing kinematic alignment and learning a compact dynamics residual, leading to faster convergence and superior generalization on unseen motions.

#### 4.2. Data and Compute Efficiency

The efficiency of ANY2ANY was evaluated on the SONIC2OLI task (Figure 5).
- **Data Efficiency:** With varying amounts of adaptation data (25%, 50%, 100% of AMASS), ANY2ANY consistently achieved higher success rates and better joint-level tracking than Scratch at every data scale. For highly dynamic motions (LAFAN), performance improved with more data. This suggests that the transferred WBT prior significantly reduces the data requirements for learning target-specific whole-body tracking.
- **Compute Efficiency:** When varying the parallel-sampling budget (one, two, or four A100 GPUs), ANY2ANY showed only slight improvements with increased compute, while Scratch fluctuated substantially and remained below ANY2ANY. This demonstrates that ANY2ANY is less dependent on large-scale parallel simulation, achieving strong tracking with limited compute resources.

Overall, ANY2ANY successfully transferred WBT policies using approximately 1% of the compute and data required for training from scratch.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis

Ablation studies on OLIWBT2LUNA clarified the contribution of individual components (Figure 6, Figure 7).
- **Kinematic Alignment:** Training from scratch converged slowly and achieved lower rewards. Full fine-tuning without alignment showed limited improvement, suggesting that the pretrained prior could not be effectively reused without consistent joint semantics. With kinematic alignment, full fine-tuning converged faster and achieved higher rewards, confirming its necessity.
- **Dynamics Adaptation (PEFT Mechanisms):** ANY2ANY using LoRA achieved performance comparable to aligned full fine-tuning but updated only 5.26% of policy parameters, improving FPS from 34.8k to 111.7k and reducing overall adaptation cost. Other PEFT mechanisms like Adapter tuning improved over scratch but converged slower with lower rewards, while Prefix-Tuning was unstable. This supports LoRA as an effective mechanism for closed-loop WBT adaptation.
- **LoRA Injection Scope:** The optimal LoRA injection scope (S7) included the actor backbone, actor proprioception input projection, actor output projection, and critic backbone. The reference-motion encoder was kept frozen. This indicates that the primary dynamics residual after kinematic alignment resides in dynamics-aware control pathways (proprioception, action decoding, critic), while the reference-motion stream is more embodiment-agnostic.

#### 4.4. Real-world Deployment

The adapted SONIC2OLI policy was successfully deployed on the physical LimX Oli robot, demonstrating reliable reproduction of generalized behaviors such as walking, running, turning, and dancing (Figure 8). This confirms the effectiveness of the transferred prior under real-world dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper provides evidence that large-scale, robot-specific whole-body tracking (WBT) policies are not inherently confined to their training embodiment. The ANY2ANY paradigm demonstrates that such policies can be efficiently transferred to new humanoid platforms with a minimal fraction (approximately 1%) of the data and computational resources typically required for training from scratch.

This finding carries several implications:

- **Challenging the Embodiment-Bound Assumption:** Historically, WBT policies have been considered tightly coupled to specific robot morphologies. This work suggests that a significant portion of the motor skills learned, such as balance regulation, inter-limb coordination, and motion timing, are generalizable across humanoids. This reusability is attributed to humanoids often referencing a common, human-centric motor manifold, given that their motion data typically originates from human performances.
- **Scalable Deployment of Humanoid Control:** The ability to efficiently adapt a WBT policy significantly reduces the barrier to deploying advanced whole-body control capabilities on new humanoid hardware. Instead of expensive retraining for each new robot, a lightweight kinematic mapping and a small dynamics adapter can be used to leverage existing expert policies, accelerating research and commercialization efforts.
- **New Design Principles for Humanoid Control:** The research advocates for a new design principle where a compute-intensive WBT backbone serves as a reusable "building block" for motion understanding and whole-body coordination. Each new humanoid would then primarily require a lightweight, embodiment-specific adaptation module. This shifts the focus from building robot-specific controllers to specializing a shared foundation.
- **Advancing Cross-Embodiment Research:** This work represents a systematic study of cross-embodiment transfer for high-fidelity humanoid WBT, which has been less explored compared to general robot locomotion or manipulation. It lays a foundation for future research in this domain.
- **Future Directions and Community Initiatives:** The authors propose the concept of a "Community Reference Humanoid" – a standardized, human-like, and easily transferable platform that could serve as a common hub and benchmark for cross-embodiment research. This would facilitate the development of higher-level skills, including loco-manipulation and dexterous manipulation. Additionally, the findings suggest that pretraining policies across diverse robot sets with structural randomization could lead to even more inherently transferable priors.

In essence, ANY2ANY suggests that the path to widespread and versatile humanoid robot deployment can be significantly streamlined by strategically reusing and adapting existing whole-body intelligence, rather than reinventing it for every new robot. This contributes to the broader vision of scalable and reusable robot learning.
