# Geometry-Aware Implicit Memory for Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University, Kuaishou Technology, and Tsinghua University developed GIM-World, a video world model employing a geometry-aware implicit memory that is directly supervised by a 3D foundation model to encode cross-view scene geometry. This enabled the model to generate long-horizon rollouts with superior geometric consistency, achieving an 81.70 normalized 3D reprojection score in first-person views on the MIND dataset, outperforming strong baselines by up to +7.73 points.

## Problem

- Existing video world models struggle to maintain long-horizon geometric and visual consistency in autoregressive generations, leading to scene deformation and memory drift over time.
- Explicit memory methods (e.g., frame retrieval, online 3D reconstructions) suffer from heuristic errors, appearance redundancy, or high computational overhead and latency.
- Current implicit memory designs compress historical information into a compact state but lack explicit geometric training signals, often encoding redundant appearance rather than fundamental scene structure.

## Method

- Introduced GIM-World, a framework featuring a lightweight, fixed-size implicit memory encoder that aggregates historical frames and camera poses into a compact memory state.
- Developed a camera-queryable geometry supervision mechanism where the memory is directly trained to reconstruct dense per-frame 3D geometric features provided by a frozen 3D foundation model.
- Implemented an information-guided pruning rule based on a Gaussian Process model to efficiently select a maximally informative subset of history frames, managing computational cost for unbounded histories.

## Results

- GIM-World achieved superior memory consistency on the MIND dataset, with an LPIPS of 0.6304 and MSE of 0.0614 in first-person views, outperforming the best explicit memory baseline (FramePack) and implicit memory baseline (VideoSSM's SSM).
- Demonstrated substantial gains in 3D geometric consistency, obtaining an 81.70 normalized 3D reprojection score in first-person settings, significantly higher than FramePack (73.97) and SSM (53.95).
- Qualitatively, the model maintained overall scene geometry, layout, and fine texture details consistently over rollouts extending to a thousand frames, preventing structural collapse and drift observed in baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly constraining the implicit memory to encode cross-view scene geometry via camera-queryable supervision significantly improves long-horizon geometric consistency compared to applying geometry signals to the generator itself.
> - An information-guided pruning strategy that maximizes mutual information between retained and discarded history frames is crucial for efficiently selecting informative historical context, outperforming uniform or camera-based sampling.
> - A compact self-attention mechanism within the memory encoder can drastically reduce computational overhead (less than 0.3% of backbone time) without significant degradation in generation quality, ensuring an efficient memory module.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in the field of video world models: maintaining long-horizon consistency in autoregressively generated visual environments. Video world models aim to learn controllable visual simulators that predict future scene evolution based on past observations and actions or camera motion. As the duration of these generated rollouts increases, ensuring that regions previously visited remain consistent, scene layouts are preserved, and new viewpoints are compatible with established structure becomes crucial for applications such as embodied agents, interactive games, and persistent visual simulators.

Existing approaches to memory in video world models generally fall into two categories:

1. **Explicit Memory:** These methods retain past observations as discrete items, either as historical frames or online 3D reconstructions.
 - **Frame-level retrieval** often relies on heuristic rules (e.g., visual similarity, camera overlap), which can lead to errors and inconsistencies if an incorrect frame is selected. Furthermore, appearance redundancy among retained frames can limit the effective information stored within a bounded memory.
 - **Online 3D reconstructions** mitigate heuristic selection but introduce computational overhead (running a reconstruction pipeline alongside the generator), inference latency, and engineering complexity. They can also be sensitive to dynamic objects, occlusion, or weak textures, potentially accumulating reconstruction errors over long rollouts.

2. **Implicit Memory:** These methods compress historical information into a compact, world-state representation decoupled from the generator. While offering the advantage of fixed-size memory, current implicit memory designs often lack explicit geometric training signals. This can result in the memory predominantly encoding redundant appearance signals across views rather than abstracting and retaining cross-view scene structure.

In parallel, the development of 3D foundation models, which can recover dense per-frame geometric features from video, has led to their increasing integration into video generators. This integration has occurred along several axes: direct conditioning (concatenating geometric tokens), feature supervision (aligning generator backbone features with 3D features), and post-training alignment (deriving rewards or preferences from 3D features). However, these methods primarily focus on injecting geometric information into the *generator* or its *outputs*.

This research positions itself by identifying a gap in both explicit and implicit memory designs, specifically the lack of a robust, geometry-aware mechanism for implicit memory. It proposes that geometry should be a fundamental property encoded within the *remembered world state* itself, rather than solely an external signal for the generator. The work therefore aims to enhance implicit memory by explicitly constraining it to encode cross-view scene geometry, leveraging 3D foundation models as a training-time teacher, but focusing the geometric distillation on the memory component rather than the generator.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a video world model capable of producing long-horizon autoregressive rollouts that exhibit strong geometric coherence and visual consistency with the underlying scene. This is motivated by the observation that existing memory designs, both explicit and implicit, struggle with these properties, leading to visible geometric distortion and memory drift over extended generations.

The specific motivations and objectives are:

1. **Address Limitations of Explicit Memories:** Explicit memories, which store past observations directly (e.g., frames or 3D reconstructions), are prone to heuristic retrieval errors, appearance redundancy, or reconstruction artifacts. The objective is to move beyond these limitations by developing a more robust, compact memory.
2. **Enhance Implicit Memories with Geometric Awareness:** Current implicit memory designs compress history into a compact state but do not explicitly encode cross-view scene geometry. This results in the memory potentially allocating capacity to redundant appearance information rather than fundamental scene structure, leading to consistency issues in long rollouts. The motivation is to design an implicit memory that is specifically constrained to capture and retain 3D scene geometry.
3. **Distill 3D Scene Structure into Memory:** Unlike prior work that injects geometry into the generator, the motivation here is to distill 3D scene structure from a frozen 3D foundation model directly into the *memory state* during training. The objective is to train the memory to answer camera-conditioned geometric queries, thereby embedding geometric knowledge within the world state itself.
4. **Develop a Lightweight and Efficient Memory Framework:** The memory should be compact (fixed-size) and its encoding process should be computationally inexpensive, especially at inference time. This involves developing a lightweight encoder and a pruning mechanism to manage the cost associated with variable-length history. The geometry teacher should also be discardable post-training to maintain efficiency.
5. **Improve Long-Horizon Consistency:** The overarching goal is to achieve superior long-horizon geometric and visual consistency compared to existing explicit and implicit memory baselines, thereby supporting more reliable and realistic simulations for persistent visual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GIM-World (Geometry-Aware Implicit Memory for Video World Models) is designed as a framework that incorporates past observations and camera poses into a fixed-size memory state, which is then supervised to reconstruct geometry features from various viewpoints. The methodology comprises three main components: a memory encoder, a camera-queryable geometry supervision head, and an information-guided pruning rule.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The model predicts future frames `x_{t+1:t+K}` conditioned on an observed history `H_t = {(x_i, c_i)}_{i=1}^t` (frames `x_i` or VAE latents, and camera poses `c_i`) and future actions `a_{t+1:t+K}`. To scale for unbounded histories, a memory operator `M` summarizes `H_t` into a compact memory state `m_t = M(H_t)`, which then conditions the generation process.

#### 3.2. Implicit Memory Encoder

The memory encoder converts a variable-length, camera-indexed history into a fixed number of memory slots.
- **Tokenization:** Historical frames are first transformed into patch tokens `h_{i,p}` using a VAE encoder and a diffusion backbone's patch embedding.
- **Camera Injection:** Camera information is added to the history tokens: `h̃_{i,p} = h_{i,p} + E_c(c_i)`, where `E_c` is a linear camera embedding. The memory queries themselves remain pose-free.
- **Architecture:** A transformer-based encoder uses `N_m` learnable memory queries `Q_0`. These queries are concatenated with the camera-aware history tokens to form the encoder input `Z_0`.
- **Compact Self-Attention:** The encoder uses two blocks. Each block updates `Z` through a compact self-attention branch and a full-resolution feed-forward branch. The `Compact` operator downsamples each `s × s` block of tokens via a shared linear layer, and `Expand` inverts this, allowing self-attention to run on a reduced number of tokens `(N_m + T H_p W_p) / s^2`.
- **Output:** After two blocks, the first `N_m` tokens of `Z` form the memory `m`, which is then concatenated with the target latents along the temporal axis to condition the diffusion backbone.
- **Efficiency:** The encoder is designed to be lightweight, contributing a negligible computational cost (less than 0.3% of the diffusion backbone's time) at inference.

#### 3.3. Camera-Queryable Geometry Supervision

This component addresses the limitation of implicit memories storing redundant appearance data by explicitly forcing the memory to encode geometry.
- **Teacher Model:** A frozen 3D foundation model, specifically VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer), provides dense per-frame geometric features `G_i`.
- **Querying Mechanism:** During training, one historical frame `i` is uniformly sampled. Its camera pose `c_i` is converted into a set of per-patch queries `q_{i,u,v}`. This involves concatenating the camera origin and normalized ray direction for each patch, embedding it with a small MLP, and adding a learnable 2D grid positional embedding.
- **Geometry Head:** A lightweight geometry head takes the memory `m` and the camera-conditioned queries `q_i` as input. It first performs cross-attention between `q_i` and `m` to retrieve relevant information from the memory. This is followed by self-attention over the patch grid and a feed-forward network to project the output into the VGGT feature dimension, yielding `Ĝ_i`.
- **Geometry Loss:** The memory and geometry head are jointly trained using a per-patch cosine similarity loss (`L_geo`) between the predicted `Ĝ_i` and the ground-truth `G_i` from the VGGT teacher. This loss encourages the memory to encode information allowing reconstruction of geometric features from arbitrary viewpoints.
- **Inference:** The geometry head and VGGT teacher are discarded at inference, maintaining a lightweight memory module.

#### 3.4. Information-Guided Pruning

To manage the computational cost that scales with history length, a pruning mechanism is introduced to select a maximally informative subset of history frames.
- **Criterion:** The pruning aims to select a retained subset `S` of size `K` (e.g., `K=200`) that maximizes the mutual information `I(S; H \ S)`, meaning `S` is as informative as possible about the frames it excludes.
- **Gaussian Process (GP) Model:** A Gaussian Process is used to model per-frame observations, with a pose-time kernel `k(c_i, c_j)`. This kernel considers camera position, forward direction, and frame time to quantify similarity between frames. Frames with close cameras, similar viewing directions, and proximity in time are considered highly correlated.
- **Greedy Algorithm:** Since `I(S; H \ S)` is submodular, a greedy algorithm is used to select frames. At each step, it adds the frame that provides the highest marginal gain, measured as a log-ratio of posterior variances. This selects frames that are informative relative to the already retained set, yet well-covered by the rest of the history.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

The entire framework is trained end-to-end in a single stage, without separate pretraining or fine-tuning. The total objective function is `L = L_FM + λ L_geo`, where `L_FM` is the flow-matching objective for the diffusion backbone and `λ L_geo` is the geometry supervision loss. During inference, only the lightweight memory encoder and the diffusion backbone are used; the geometry head and VGGT encoder are discarded.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GIM-World was conducted on the MIND dataset, a closed-loop revisited benchmark for video world models, using 1080p/24fps Unreal Engine 5 videos. The model was compared against several explicit-memory (Matrix-Game 2.0, MIND-World, FramePack, Context-as-Memory) and implicit-memory (VideoSSM's SSM-based module) baselines. All retrained baselines shared the same backbone, memory-token budget, and training data/schedule as GIM-World.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

GIM-World demonstrated improvements across various metrics:

- **Memory Consistency:** GIM-World achieved the best scores on all four reconstruction metrics (MSE, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for both first-person and third-person views. For instance, in the first-person view, GIM-World obtained an MSE of 0.0614 and LPIPS of 0.6304, outperforming the best explicit memory baseline (FramePack: MSE 0.0764, LPIPS 0.7062) and the implicit memory baseline (SSM: MSE 0.0796, LPIPS 0.7439). Similar improvements were observed for the third-person view.
- **3D Geometric Consistency:** A substantial gain was observed in the normalized 3D reprojection score. GIM-World achieved 81.70 in the first-person setting and 87.10 in the third-person setting. This compares to 73.97 and 70.82 for the strongest explicit-memory baseline (FramePack) with comparable metrics, and 53.95 and 61.26 for the SSM baseline. This indicates improved preservation of scene geometry over long rollouts.
- **Action Accuracy:** The model performed comparably to or better than the strongest baselines in terms of relative pose error (RPE) for both translation and rotation, suggesting that enhancing geometric structure in memory does not negatively impact controllable rollout dynamics.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons illustrated the effectiveness of GIM-World in maintaining structural and visual consistency over long horizons:

- **Scene Coherence:** Baselines frequently exhibited structural collapse, with scene layouts drifting, walls and landmark structures deforming or disappearing, and generated content diverging from the input environment within hundreds of frames.
- **Persistent Detail:** GIM-World maintained overall scene geometry, layout, landmark structures, and character poses (in third-person views) consistently with the ground truth across the entire generation horizon.
- **Long-Horizon Memory:** The paper demonstrated a rollout extending to a thousand frames where specific small objects, revisited hundreds of frames apart, retained their spatial positions, relative arrangements, and fine texture details. This suggests that the implicit memory successfully stores a stable 3D representation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Geometry Supervision:**
 - Training without geometry loss (`w/o geometry`) resulted in a reprojection score of 58.80 (first-person).
 - Applying geometry supervision directly to the generator features (`Geometry Forcing`) yielded 63.40.
 - GIM-World's camera-queryable supervision applied to the memory achieved 81.70, indicating that constraining the memory directly is more effective for geometric consistency.
- **Information-Guided Pruning:**
 - Uniform temporal sampling and Camera FPS (farthest-point sampling in pose-time space) performed similarly (e.g., Reproj. scores around 77).
 - The proposed MI greedy pruning strategy (based on mutual information via GP) consistently outperformed both alternatives across all metrics (e.g., Reproj. 81.70), confirming its efficacy in selecting informative history frames.
- **Compact Attention:**
 - "Full attention" provided the quality upper bound but was significantly slower (2.80% of DiT backbone time).
 - "Global Compact" (pooling both attention and FFN) was fast (0.20%) but degraded performance.
 - "Attention Compact" (pooling only the attention branch, `s=2`, GIM-World's default) matched or exceeded "Full attention" quality (LPIPS was best) while being substantially faster (0.26% of DiT time), demonstrating efficient attention without major quality loss. Stronger pooling (`s=4`) was faster but led to quality degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GIM-World framework represents a notable advancement in the development of video world models, particularly concerning their ability to generate long, geometrically consistent rollouts.

- **Addressing Long-Horizon Consistency:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust solution to the pervasive problem of geometric and visual drift in long-horizon video generation. By explicitly embedding 3D scene structure into the implicit memory, the model can maintain coherent environments over extended periods, which is a critical requirement for realistic simulations.
- **Novel Memory Design:** The introduction of a geometry-aware implicit memory, where a fixed-size memory state is directly trained to encode cross-view scene geometry through camera-queryable supervision, departs from prior work that primarily focused on explicit memory schemes or applied geometric signals to the generator. This approach offers the advantages of compactness and efficiency inherent in implicit memory while overcoming its prior limitation of lacking explicit geometric grounding.
- **Efficiency and Practicality:** The lightweight design of the memory encoder, its negligible inference cost, and the single-stage end-to-end training process contribute to the practical applicability of GIM-World. The ability to discard the geometry teacher at inference further streamlines the model. The information-guided pruning mechanism also ensures scalability for processing long histories without incurring unbounded computational costs.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Realism:** By ensuring geometric and visual consistency, GIM-World can facilitate more stable and realistic simulations. This can significantly impact fields such as embodied AI, where agents need to interact with persistent virtual environments, and interactive game development, where consistent world states are fundamental for immersive experiences.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a precedent for how 3D geometric priors can be effectively distilled into and leveraged by compact, implicit world representations. This could inspire further research into integrating other perceptual modalities or abstract knowledge into implicit memory states for more sophisticated world modeling capabilities.
