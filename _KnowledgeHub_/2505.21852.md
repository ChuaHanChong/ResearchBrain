# A Provable Approach for End-to-End Safe Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Provably Lifetime Safe RL (PLS) algorithm offers a method for end-to-end safe reinforcement learning, providing strong safety and near-optimality guarantees throughout the policy's lifetime. It achieves consistent safety across tasks while maintaining high reward performance by adapting a single offline-learned policy to various safety thresholds.

## Problem

- Online Safe RL struggles to ensure safety during early, exploratory learning phases, often only guaranteeing safety after policy convergence.
- Offline Safe RL avoids unsafe exploration during training but faces challenges with distribution mismatch when deploying policies to real environments, potentially leading to unsafe or overly conservative behavior.
- A longstanding goal in Safe RL is to develop a methodology that ensures safety throughout the entire process, from initial policy learning to continuous operation in a real environment, with high probability.

## Method

- **PLS** utilizes offline policy learning via ==Constrained Decision Transformer== (CDT), which conditions actions on target reward and safety cost returns, to avoid unsafe online exploration during initial training.
- It employs Gaussian Processes (GPs) to model the relationship between target returns (specified by CDT) and actual returns in the real environment, providing a theoretically justified way to quantify uncertainty.
- A two-phase Bayesian Optimization strategy is used for safe online policy deployment, prioritizing safe exploration of target returns before maximizing rewards within a pessimistically estimated safe region.

## Results

- PLS provides theoretical guarantees for lifetime safety (cost less than or equal to threshold with high probability) at every iteration of online deployment.
- It is proven to achieve near-optimal target returns with high probability, characterizing the online sample complexity needed for this performance.
- Empirically, PLS consistently satisfied safety constraints across all tested continuous robot locomotion tasks, a feat unmatched by any of the six state-of-the-art baselines, while also achieving the highest reward return in most tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The difference between target returns and actual returns can be effectively modeled by a Gaussian Process, enabling robust prediction and uncertainty quantification for online adaptation.
> - By optimizing only a low-dimensional target return vector online, PLS significantly reduces the number of real-world interactions required, making it computationally efficient and safer than full policy optimization.
> - The method allows a single offline-trained policy to adapt to versatile safety thresholds, enhancing its flexibility and applicability in dynamic real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved remarkable successes in diverse applications, from robotics to data center management. However, its deployment in safety-critical domains necessitates explicit safety guarantees, which has given rise to the field of Safe Reinforcement Learning (Safe RL). The broader landscape of Safe RL grapples with fundamental challenges related to when and how safety constraints are enforced, ranging from ensuring safety in expectation to guaranteeing it with high probability, and whether these guarantees apply only post-convergence or throughout the entire learning and operational lifecycle.

The paper identifies a "longstanding goal" in Safe RL: developing a methodology that ensures safety throughout the entire process, from initial policy learning to its continuous operation in a real environment. Existing Safe RL paradigms inherently struggle with this:

- **Online Safe RL:** Agents learn by interacting with the environment. While highly effective, ensuring safety during the early, exploratory phases of learning is extremely challenging. Techniques like safe exploration, sim-to-real safe RL, or end-to-end safe RL often rely on strong assumptions (e.g., known state transitions, access to emergency reset policies) or lead to overly conservative policies that compromise performance. Many online methods only guarantee safety after convergence, leaving the training phase vulnerable to unsafe actions.
- **Offline Safe RL:** Agents learn exclusively from pre-collected datasets, avoiding unsafe online exploration during training. This is highly appealing for safety-critical systems. However, a major hurdle remains in deploying these learned policies in real environments due to inherent distribution mismatch issues between the offline data and the actual dynamics. This mismatch can lead to policies that are either unsafe or excessively conservative when faced with novel situations.

This work, "Provably Lifetime Safe RL (PLS)," directly addresses this critical gap. It fits into the landscape by proposing a novel integration that leverages the strengths of both offline learning and online adaptation, while mitigating their respective weaknesses. By combining offline policy learning (which ensures no safety risks during training) with a *provably safe* online policy deployment mechanism, PLS aims to achieve the elusive goal of end-to-end safety. Furthermore, its ability to adapt a single policy to "versatile safety thresholds" (a set of candidate constraints rather than a single fixed one) positions it within the emerging area of versatile safe RL, offering greater flexibility and applicability to real-world scenarios where safety requirements might dynamically change.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method that guarantees the safety of a policy "throughout the entire process, from learning to operation," with "high probability." This addresses the "longstanding goal" in safe reinforcement learning that existing paradigms inherently struggle to achieve.

Specifically, the key objectives are:

1. **Lifetime Safety Guarantee:** To ensure that safety constraints are met not just after a policy has converged, but continuously from its initial learning phase through its ongoing deployment and operation in a real environment. This means minimizing unsafe actions throughout the agent's entire "lifetime."
2. **Versatile Safety Adaptation:** To train a *single policy* that can adapt to a *range* of safety thresholds ($b \in B$) rather than being optimized for a single, predefined threshold. This enhances flexibility and applicability in dynamic real-world settings.
3. **High Reward Performance:** To achieve these safety guarantees without unduly compromising the agent's ability to maximize its cumulative reward, aiming for near-optimal performance alongside strict safety.
4. **Provable Guarantees:** To provide theoretical justifications and probabilistic guarantees for both safety and near-optimality.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the critical need for reliable AI systems in sensitive domains like robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare, and finance. Current RL methods, even safe RL variants, often fall short of providing the stringent safety assurances required for such applications. Unsafe exploration during online learning or unpredictable behavior post-deployment due to distribution shift in offline learning can have severe consequences. By addressing these challenges with a provable, end-to-end safe approach, PLS aims to unlock the full potential of RL for real-world impact. The ability to handle versatile safety thresholds further motivates this work, as real-world safety requirements are often not static but dynamic and context-dependent.

### 3. Methodology and Approach: Provably Lifetime Safe RL (PLS)

The Provably Lifetime Safe RL (PLS) algorithm integrates two main components: offline policy learning and safe online policy deployment.

#### 3.1. Offline Policy Learning via Constrained Return-Conditioned Supervised Learning (CDT)

PLS begins by training a policy using an offline safe RL algorithm based on Return-Conditioned Supervised Learning (RCSL). Specifically, the authors employ the **Constrained Decision Transformer (CDT)**.

- **RCSL Principle:** RCSL methods model trajectories as sequences of states, actions, and returns (e.g., reward-to-go). Policies are learned by conditioning action distributions on desired future returns. This allows for direct control over the expected outcome.
- **CDT Extension:** CDT extends the Decision Transformer architecture to constrained RL settings. It conditions the policy not only on target reward return ($R$) but also on target safety cost return ($G$). The policy, denoted $\pi_{\hat{\theta},z}$ (where $z = (R, G)$), is trained offline on a pre-collected dataset. This offline training phase is crucial as it inherently avoids any unsafe interactions with the real environment during the policy learning process itself, thus providing an initial layer of lifetime safety.
- **Initial Challenge:** While CDT allows specification of target returns, there's no guarantee that the *actual* returns ($J_r(\pi_z)$ and $J_g(\pi_z)$) of the deployed policy will perfectly align with the target returns $z$. Empirical evidence (Figure 2 in the paper) shows significant discrepancies, leading to potential constraint violations or overly conservative behavior. This is the gap PLS aims to bridge.

#### 3.2. Theoretical Justification for Gaussian Process (GP) Modeling

A cornerstone of PLS's approach is its theoretical justification for using Gaussian Processes to model the relationship between target returns $z$ and actual returns $J(\pi_z) = (J_r(\pi_z), J_g(\pi_z))$.

- **Theorem 1 (Relation between target and actual returns):** This theorem mathematically characterizes the difference between the actual returns $J(\pi_{\hat{\theta},z})$ and the target returns $z$. It proves that this difference can be decomposed into a small bias term $\epsilon(z)$, a random Gaussian Process term $F(z)$ scaled by $H^2 / \sqrt{n}$ (where $H$ is episode length, $n$ is dataset size), and an asymptotically negligible term. This implies that the mapping from target returns to actual returns can be effectively modeled as a GP, provided certain assumptions (near-deterministic transitions, regularity of return density, soft realizability, and regularity of the learning model) hold. The smoothness of the GP sample path is also linked to the covariance function, implying a continuous relationship.

#### 3.3. Safe Online Policy Deployment and Optimization via Gaussian Processes

Armed with the theoretical justification, PLS uses GPs in a Bayesian Optimization framework to iteratively optimize the target returns $z=(R, G)$ for the pre-trained CDT policy. The goal is to maximize $J_r(\pi_z)$ while ensuring $J_g(\pi_z) \le b$ in the actual environment.

- **GP Setup:** Separate GPs are used to model $J_r(\pi_z)$ and $J_g(\pi_z)$, learning from observations $(z_j, J(\pi_{z_j}))$. The search space for $z$ is discretized.
- **Confidence Intervals:** GPs provide mean $\mu_{\diamond,N}(z)$ and variance $\sigma^2_{\diamond,N}(z)$ for reward ($\diamond=r$) and cost ($\diamond=g$). Confidence intervals $\Omega_{\diamond,N}(z) = [\mu_{\diamond,N}(z) \pm \alpha_{\diamond,N} \cdot \sigma_{\diamond,N}(z)]$ are constructed, where $\alpha_{\diamond,N}$ balances exploration and exploitation (derived from established Bayesian optimization literature).
- **Two-Phase Optimization:**
 1. **Safe Exploration:** The algorithm prioritizes exploring the target return space to confidently identify the set of safe target returns. It iteratively chooses $z$ that maximizes the uncertainty reduction in the safety constraint (i.e., maximizes $u_N(z) - \ell_N(z)$, where $u_N(z)$ and $\ell_N(z)$ are the upper and lower bounds of the safety cost confidence interval, respectively), while ensuring that the pessimistic estimate of the next policy's cost does not exceed the threshold $b$. The set of safe target returns $Y_N$ is expanded iteratively.
 2. **Reward Maximization:** Once the safe set is sufficiently explored (uncertainty below a tolerance $\zeta$), PLS switches to maximizing the reward. It selects the target returns $z$ that offer the highest optimistic reward (i.e., $\mu_{r,N}(z) + \alpha_{r,N} \cdot \sigma_{r,N}(z)$) while operating strictly within the pessimistically estimated safe region $Y_N$.
- **Computational Efficiency:** A key advantage is that PLS only optimizes a two-dimensional vector $(R, G)$ online. This requires significantly fewer online interactions compared to traditional online safe RL algorithms, which is crucial for safety-critical applications where real-world interactions are costly or risky.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical results to demonstrate the effectiveness of PLS.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Guarantees

- **Safety Guarantee (Theorem 2):** PLS provides a strong safety guarantee: at every iteration $j$, if the target returns $z_j$ are chosen within the constructed safe set $Y_j$, then the actual safety cost $J_g(\pi_{z_j})$ will be less than or equal to the safety threshold $b$ with a high probability (at least $1-\Delta$). Crucially, this guarantee applies throughout the *entire process from learning to operation*, addressing the longstanding goal of end-to-end safety. This is achieved by pessimistically ensuring that the GP upper bound on the cost is below the threshold.
- **Near-Optimality (Theorem 3):** PLS is theoretically proven to find near-optimal target returns. After collecting a certain number of GP observations ($N^\sharp$) for reward maximization, PLS can identify a target return vector $z$ such that its actual reward $J_r(\pi_z)$ is within an $\mathcal{E}$ error margin of the optimal feasible reward $J_r(\pi_{z^*})$ with high probability (at least $1-\Delta$). This theorem characterizes the online sample complexity required to achieve near-optimal target returns. The total online interaction is minimal because only a 2D vector is optimized.

#### 4.2. Empirical Performance

The experiments were conducted on multiple continuous robot locomotion tasks from the Bullet-Safety-Gym and Safety-Gymnasium benchmarks, using normalized reward and safety cost metrics. PLS was compared against six baselines: BCQ-Lag, BEAR-Lag, CPQ, COptiDICE, CDT, and CCAC.

- **Unrivaled Safety:** The most significant finding is that **PLS is the only method that consistently satisfies the safety constraint across *all* tested tasks** (for both $b=20$ and $b=40$ thresholds). In contrast, every single baseline algorithm violated the safety constraint in at least one task, highlighting their unreliability in truly safety-critical scenarios. This robust safety performance is a direct realization of the lifetime safety objective.
- **Superior Reward Performance:** Beyond just safety, PLS also demonstrates strong reward performance. It achieves the *highest reward return in most tasks* while maintaining safety, showcasing its ability to balance the safety-reward trade-off effectively. Baselines often either achieve high rewards at the cost of safety violations or are excessively conservative, leading to very low rewards (e.g., CPQ and CCAC in some tasks result in normalized costs of 0.00 but also negligible rewards).
- **Computational Efficiency:** The online optimization using GPs involves only a two-dimensional target return vector, resulting in low computational overhead for this phase. The main computational cost comes from the initial offline policy learning, but a single trained CDT policy can be adapted to multiple safety thresholds by PLS, making it computationally efficient in scenarios requiring versatility.
- **Safe Exploration:** Empirical results (Figure 3 in the Appendix) show that PLS successfully ensures safety during the exploration phase of target returns, aligning with Theorem 2. While minor constraint violations might occur probabilistically, PLS quickly recovers.

In summary, the empirical findings strongly support the theoretical claims, demonstrating that PLS achieves high rewards while ensuring safety throughout the entire process from learning to operation, a feat not consistently matched by current state-of-the-art offline safe RL algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Provably Lifetime Safe RL (PLS) approach marks a significant advancement in the field of safe reinforcement learning, addressing a critical "longstanding goal" that has hindered the widespread deployment of RL in safety-critical applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Realizing End-to-End Safety:** PLS is a pioneering method that genuinely aims for and empirically achieves end-to-end safety guarantees from the policy learning phase to its continuous operation. This provides a crucial bridge between theoretically robust offline learning and practically reliable online deployment, a challenge that has plagued previous approaches. The ability to guarantee safety with high probability throughout a policy's lifetime is transformative for applications where failure can have catastrophic consequences.
- **Enabling Deployment in Safety-Critical Domains:** By offering provable and empirically validated safety, PLS can unlock new opportunities for deploying RL in high-stakes environments such as:
 - **Robotics:** For tasks involving human-robot interaction, complex manipulation, or navigation in unstructured environments, where collisions or unsafe movements must be avoided at all costs.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** In self-driving cars, drones, or industrial control systems, ensuring safe operation under diverse and unforeseen conditions is paramount.
 - **Healthcare:** For personalized treatment plans, drug discovery, or medical robotics, where patient safety is the ultimate priority.
 - **Finance:** In algorithmic trading or risk management, where large financial losses due to unsafe actions must be mitigated.
- **Reduced Online Interaction Costs and Risks:** By performing the bulk of policy learning offline and only requiring minimal, safe online interactions to optimize a low-dimensional target return vector, PLS drastically reduces the financial, ethical, and safety risks associated with online exploration in real environments. This makes RL more accessible and practical for real-world testing and deployment.
- **Versatility with Dynamic Constraints:** The ability of a single PLS-trained policy to adapt to diverse and changing safety thresholds is highly impactful. Real-world safety requirements are rarely static; PLS provides the flexibility to adjust policy behavior without requiring complete retraining, offering significant operational advantages and cost savings.
- **Strong Theoretical Foundation:** The rigorous theoretical analysis, particularly Theorem 1 justifying the use of Gaussian Processes to model the relationship between target and actual returns, provides a robust foundation for the method. This increases confidence in its reliability and predictability, which is often a prerequisite for industrial adoption.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a key limitation: while PLS guarantees near-optimal *target returns*, this does not directly translate into ensuring a *near-optimal policy*. The policy itself (the CDT) is trained offline and its optimality depends on the dataset and the CDT's ability to model optimal behavior. Developing methods that can guarantee both near-optimal target returns and a near-optimal policy, while maintaining end-to-end safety, remains an ambitious and important direction for future research. Nevertheless, PLS represents a substantial step forward by addressing the crucial aspect of safe deployment and continuous operation.
