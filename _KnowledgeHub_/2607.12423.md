# Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control for Multi-Robot Motion Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC), a training-free diffusion planner that analytically estimates diffusion scores using known dynamics, and its multi-robot extension, MDOC-CBS. The system rigorously enforces dynamic feasibility and hard safety constraints through Control Barrier Function (CBF)-constrained projections, achieving superior success rates and 5-10 times faster planning compared to baseline methods in complex multi-robot environments with up to 40 robots.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion planners for motion planning are model-free and require large, expensive expert demonstration datasets to learn score functions.
- Current diffusion methods struggle to rigorously enforce dynamic feasibility and hard safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance), often relying on soft constraints or computationally intensive post-hoc projections.
- Scalability challenges arise in multi-robot settings due to the computational overhead of data-driven learning and inefficient handling of hard constraints, limiting performance in congested environments.

## Method

- **Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC)** is proposed as a training-free, single-robot ==diffusion planner== that analytically estimates ==diffusion scores== using ==Monte Carlo Score-Ascent (MCSA)== with a known dynamics model, eliminating the need for demonstration data.
- **MDOC** integrates ==Control Barrier Function (CBF)==-constrained projections directly into ==model-based diffusion rollouts== to ensure dynamically feasible and collision-free trajectories during the sampling process.
- **MDOC-CBS** extends MDOC to multi-robot scenarios by leveraging a ==Conflict-Based Search (CBS)== framework, resolving inter-robot collisions via the same ==CBF-constrained projection== mechanism within its low-level MDOC planner.

## Results

- MDOC-CBS consistently achieved higher success rates across various robot counts and environments, demonstrating **5-10 times** lower planning times compared to existing model-free diffusion and kinodynamic baselines.
- Trajectories generated by MDOC-CBS were significantly smoother (lower **Laplacian smoothness**) and shorter (reduced **path lengths**) compared to baseline methods, indicating higher quality and efficiency in motion planning.
- The system demonstrated robust scalability, successfully planning for up to **40 robots** in large (**6x6**) maps with long planning horizons, outperforming model-free diffusion methods that experienced sharp performance degradation beyond **30 robots** due to increased inference costs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analytically estimating diffusion scores using known system dynamics allows for training-free trajectory generation, bypassing the data dependency of model-free diffusion models.
> - Directly enforcing safety and dynamic feasibility through CBF-constrained projections within the diffusion rollout process guarantees hard constraints and improves planning robustness.
> - Integrating a model-based, constraint-aware diffusion planner into a CBS framework provides a scalable and efficient solution for complex multi-robot motion planning, effectively coordinating agents while maintaining safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) in continuous environments presents significant challenges due to the combinatorial complexity of the joint trajectory space and the necessity of generating dynamically feasible, collision-free trajectories while adhering to hard safety constraints. Historically, researchers have addressed these complexities by simplifying the problem.

One prominent simplification is Multi-Agent Path-Finding (MAPF), which typically operates in discretized spaces (e.g., grids) and discrete time. Algorithms like Conflict-Based Search (CBS) and its variants have demonstrated scalability and safety guarantees within this abstraction. However, the reliance on discretization assumptions, such as constant velocities or rectilinear motions, limits their applicability to continuous MRMP settings that demand dynamic feasibility and intricate robot interactions.

More recent advancements have shifted towards formulating motion planning as a probabilistic inference problem, where trajectories are sampled from a posterior distribution using diffusion models. These diffusion planners, known for their generative capabilities, learn score functions from demonstration data to represent complex, multimodal trajectory distributions in continuous spaces. Initial work focused on single-robot planning, generating smoother trajectories and overcoming the rigid assumptions of grid-based methods. This paradigm was subsequently extended to MRMP, including integration with CBS frameworks for multi-robot coordination.

Despite their potential, existing diffusion planners exhibit several limitations. They are fundamentally model-free, requiring substantial expert demonstration datasets to learn the underlying score function. This dependency on data can be resource-intensive and often neglects valuable prior knowledge from known system dynamics. Furthermore, these methods frequently struggle to rigorously enforce dynamics during the denoising process, leading to trajectories that may not be truly dynamics-aware. The enforcement of inter-robot collision avoidance often relies on soft constraints, which do not guarantee hard safety, or on computationally expensive post-hoc safety projections applied to the entire trajectory. These issues can impede their performance in large-scale, congested multi-robot environments.

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing a model-based approach that integrates known dynamics and hard safety constraints directly into the diffusion process, aiming to produce dynamically feasible and collision-free trajectories efficiently and without reliance on demonstration data, while also scaling to multi-robot settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from identified shortcomings in current multi-robot motion planning approaches, particularly those utilizing diffusion models:
1. **Data Dependency:** Existing diffusion planners are model-free and require large, often costly, expert demonstration datasets to learn the score functions necessary for trajectory generation. This dependency can hinder their application in scenarios where such data is scarce or expensive to acquire.
2. **Constraint Enforcement:** Current diffusion planners often struggle to rigorously enforce dynamic feasibility and hard safety constraints (e.g., collision avoidance) during the trajectory generation process. They typically rely on soft constraints or computationally intensive post-hoc projections, which do not guarantee strict adherence to safety requirements and can significantly increase planning time in complex environments.
3. **Scalability Challenges:** When extended to multi-robot systems, the computational overhead associated with learning from demonstrations and the inefficient handling of hard constraints can limit the scalability of these planners, especially in highly congested or large-scale multi-robot scenarios.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:
- **Develop Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC):** Introduce a training-free diffusion planner for single robots that analytically estimates diffusion scores using a known dynamics model, thereby circumventing the need for demonstration data.
- **Enforce Rigorous Safety:** Integrate Control Barrier Function (CBF)-constrained projections directly into the model-based diffusion rollouts to ensure dynamically feasible and collision-free trajectories during the sampling process, rather than through post-hoc modifications.
- **Scale to Multi-Robot Settings:** Propose MDOC-CBS, a scalable multi-robot motion planning algorithm that resolves inter-robot collisions by leveraging the same CBF-constrained projection mechanism within the efficient framework of Conflict-Based Search (CBS).
- **Evaluate Performance:** Conduct comprehensive evaluations across diverse environments, start-goal configurations, constraint settings, and planning horizons to demonstrate improvements in success rate, trajectory smoothness, and computational efficiency compared to representative baseline planners.

Specifically, the paper outlines three main contributions:
1. The development of MDOC, a model-based diffusion planner that enforces safety via CBF-constrained projections during model-based rollouts, yielding dynamically feasible and collision-free trajectories.
2. The proposal of MDOC-CBS, a CBS-based coordination method designed to scale to scenarios with up to 20 (and 40 in larger maps) robots, resolving inter-robot collisions through the CBF-constrained projection embedded in its low-level MDOC planner.
3. An evaluation demonstrating that MDOC-CBS improves success rate and trajectory quality, while reducing computation time and maintaining collision-free execution, when compared to representative baselines across various experimental setups.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented revolves around Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC) for single-robot planning and its extension, MDOC-CBS, for multi-robot scenarios.

**A. Problem Statement (Section III-A)**
The Multi-Robot Motion Planning (MRMP) problem is defined as guiding $N$ robots from start to goal states while respecting individual robot dynamics and avoiding collisions with obstacles and other robots. Each robot $i$ has a state $s_i$ and control input $u_i$. A trajectory $\tau_i$ is a sequence of state-control pairs over a finite horizon $H$. The objective function $J(\tau_{1:H})$ is a sum of intermediate and terminal costs for all robots, weighted by $\gamma_i$ and $\beta_i$. The optimization problem is to minimize $J$ subject to discrete-time dynamics ($s^{h+1}_i = f(s^h_i, u^h_i)$) and generic safety constraints ($g^h(s^h, u^h) \le 0$) at each time step.

**B. Model-Based Diffusion for Trajectory Sampling (Section III-B)**
MDOC treats a robot's trajectory as a random variable to be sampled through iterative denoising from Gaussian noise, following Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs). The process involves forward noising, where a clean trajectory $\tau^1_{1:H}$ is progressively corrupted into noisy trajectories $\tau^k_{1:H}$ over $K$ diffusion steps. The reverse process denoises $\tau^k_{1:H}$ to $\tau^{k-1}_{1:H}$ guided by a score function, $S_k(\tau^k_{1:H}) = \nabla_{\tau^k_{1:H}} \log p_k(\tau^k_{1:H})$, which directs sampling towards higher-density regions.

Unlike standard DDPMs that learn this score function from large datasets, MDOC employs a Model-Based Diffusion (MBD) approach. MBD avoids learning an explicit noise predictor by analytically estimating the score using Monte Carlo Score-Ascent (MCSA) with a known dynamics model. At each reverse diffusion step $k$, it samples multiple denoised candidate trajectories, $\tilde{\tau}^m_{1:H}$, from a Gaussian proposal. These candidates are then reweighted by a target density $p_1(\tilde{\tau}^m_{1:H})$ to form an importance-weighted average, $\bar{\tau}_{1:H}$. This average is used to analytically construct the score estimate $\hat{S}_k$, which guides the reverse update of the noisy trajectory.

**C. Planning as Inference (Section III-C)**
The target distribution $p_1(\tau_{1:H})$ is defined to encourage low-cost, dynamically feasible, and collision-free trajectories. Soft optimality is modeled via a Boltzmann distribution, $p_J(\tau_{1:H}) \propto \exp(-J(\tau_{1:H})/\lambda)$, where $\lambda$ is a temperature parameter. Dynamic feasibility and safety are encoded by a feasibility prior $p_{feas}(\tau_{1:H})$. Direct use of hard indicators for feasibility can lead to "dead samples" (zero weight), so MDOC aims to work with candidates that inherently lie within the feasible set $D \times C$, where $D$ ensures dynamic feasibility (by model rollouts) and $C$ ensures safety (by inequality constraints).

**D. Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC) (Section IV-A)**
MDOC, as a single-robot planner, enhances the MBD framework by enforcing safety during sampling. It introduces a feasibility operator $F$ that maps any candidate trajectory into the dynamically feasible and safe set $D \times C$. This is achieved by applying Control Barrier Function (CBF)-constrained projections directly on the control inputs during model-based diffusion rollouts.

For a given state $s^h_i$ and nominal control $u^h_i$, a barrier function $b(s^h_i)$ (e.g., minimum distance from obstacles) is defined. Safety is ensured by satisfying a discrete-time CBF condition: $b(s^{h+1}_i) \ge (1 - \gamma \Delta h) b(s^h_i)$, meaning the safety margin does not decay too quickly. MDOC locally linearizes the dynamics and the barrier function to derive a control-affine inequality, $a^T_h u^h_i \ge d_h$, which defines a polyhedral safe-control set $K_h$.

To enforce safety, each proposed nominal control $\bar{u}^h_i$ is projected onto this safe-control set $K_h$. For a single violated half-space, a closed-form Euclidean projection (Equation 15) is used. This projection minimizes the change in control while satisfying the constraint. The feasibility operator $F$ thus works by iterating over the time horizon: (1) projecting each proposed control onto the safe-control set using CBF, and (2) rolling out the known dynamics to obtain the next state, ensuring both safety and dynamic feasibility.

Algorithm 1 summarizes MDOC:
1. Initialize a noisy trajectory.
2. For each reverse diffusion step $k$:
 a. Sample $M$ candidate trajectories from a Gaussian proposal.
 b. For each candidate, apply the `ROLLOUTWITHCBF` function, which implements the feasibility operator $F$ by projecting controls and rolling out dynamics.
 c. Calculate the Monte Carlo mean $\bar{\tau}_{1:H}$ of these feasible candidates.
 d. Estimate the score $\hat{S}_k$.
 e. Update the noisy trajectory $\tau^k_{1:H}$ to $\tau^{k-1}_{1:H}$ using the score estimate.
3. Return the final denoised trajectory.

**E. MDOC-CBS (Section IV-B)**
MDOC-CBS extends MDOC to multi-robot settings using the Conflict-Based Search (CBS) framework. CBS operates on two levels: a low-level planner for individual robots and a high-level conflict resolver.

Algorithm 2 outlines MDOC-CBS:
1. Initialize a Constraint Tree (CT) root node. For each robot $i$, its constraint set $C_i$ is initialized with obstacle-avoidance constraints.
2. MDOC is used as the low-level planner to compute an initial trajectory $\tau_i$ for each robot.
3. The algorithm iteratively selects the CT node with the fewest conflicts.
4. If the node's joint trajectory is conflict-free, it is returned as a solution.
5. If a conflict (collision) is found between robots $i$ and $j$ at time $h$:
 a. The current node is branched into two new child nodes.
 b. In each child node, a new constraint is added to one of the conflicting robots, forbidding it from entering a small workspace sphere centered at the other robot's state at time $h$.
 c. The pairwise CBF $b_{ij}(s_i, s_j)$ is converted into a single-robot barrier $b^h_{i|j}(s^h_i)$ by fixing the conflicting robot's state. This new constraint is then enforced by the feasibility operator $F$ within MDOC during replanning for the affected robot.
 d. MDOC replans the trajectories for robots $i$ and $j$ under their updated constraint sets.
 e. The new child nodes, with updated trajectories, are added to the CT priority queue.
This process continues until a conflict-free solution is found or no solution exists.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis aimed to assess MDOC's advantages over state-of-the-art single-robot planners and MDOC-CBS's performance, scalability, and constraint adherence against multi-robot planners across various environments and setups.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted on an NVIDIA RTX A4500 GPU with 20 GB memory and 12 CPU cores. Ten trials per method/map were run with different random seeds. A single-integrator model was used.
- **Map Types:** Narrow (2x2), Empty (2x2), Conveyor (2x2), Drop-Region (2x2), Random (2x2, 13.7% obstacle density), and Empty Large (4x4, 6x6). Robot radius was 0.05.
- **Start-Goal Setups:** Circle Setup (symmetric swaps), Weave Setup (exchanges along boundary points), Random Setup (random start-goal pairs).
- **Baselines:**
 - **Single-Robot:** CEM, MPPI, RRT*.
 - **Multi-Robot:** CBS, ECBS (grid-based A* low-level), KCBS (kinodynamic RRT* low-level), MMD-CBS (model-free diffusion low-level trained on RRT*).

**A. Single-Robot MDOC Performance:**
- **Qualitative Observations (Fig. 2):** MDOC's denoising process was visualized, showing that early-stage (90%) distributions explored diverse homotopy classes. As denoising progressed (50%), the distribution converged towards feasible, low-cost paths, safely traversing bottlenecks and avoiding obstacles. This indicates that MDOC systematically refines trajectories through model-informed guidance rather than blind perturbation.
- **Sample Efficiency (Table I):** MDOC achieved a 100.0% "Pass&Free"-Yield (percentage of candidate trajectories that both traverse the bottleneck and remain collision-free) on both Narrow and More-Constrained Narrow maps with a fixed sample size ($M=128$). In contrast, CEM and MPPI had significantly lower yields (0.2-3.2%), and RRT* achieved 41.7-66.1%. This suggests MDOC's superior efficiency in producing useful, feasible candidates within a given sampling budget.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** CEM and MPPI, relying on stochastic resampling, frequently struggled in multimodal or constrained environments. MDOC, by establishing a gradient structure over the trajectory distribution and explicitly incorporating dynamics and constraints, achieved continuous convergence from global exploration to local refinement. RRT*, while probabilistically complete, wasted samples in unproductive homotopy classes. MDOC integrates the global exploration of RRT* with the optimization capabilities of MPPI and CEM, guided by dynamics and hard constraints, leading to higher feasibility and convergence efficiency.

**B. Multi-Robot MDOC-CBS Performance on 2x2 Maps (Empty & Conveyor):**
- **Scalability and Success Rate (Fig. 4):** MDOC-CBS consistently outperformed baseline methods (KCBS, MMD-CBS) in success rate across various robot counts (up to 20 robots). MMD-CBS failed beyond 10-12 robots in Circle and Conveyor setups, and KCBS's success rate degraded significantly after 5 robots.
- **Computation Time (Fig. 4):** MDOC-CBS achieved 5-10 times lower planning time compared to MMD-CBS at similar CT expansion levels. This efficiency is attributed to MDOC's analytical trajectory score computation via MCSA with model-based rollouts, avoiding the repeated invocation of a large diffusion network.
- **Trajectory Quality (Table II, Fig. 3):** MDOC-CBS produced shorter average path lengths (P) and lower geometric (Laplacian) smoothness values (G), indicating smoother trajectories, compared to KCBS and MMD-CBS. This is linked to the model-based diffusion objective promoting rich exploration and dynamic coherence in continuous state space. KCBS and MMD-CBS, derived from RRT* characteristics, tended to generate less smooth paths.
- **Coordinated Motion (Conveyor Map, Fig. 3):** In the Conveyor map, MMD-CBS often led to congestion as all robots attempted to pass through a narrow corridor. MDOC-CBS, however, effectively distributed robots around the outer free space, with only a few robots using the corridor. This behavior resulted in shorter average path lengths, fewer high-level CT expansions, and smoother trajectories, while maintaining safety. This outcome highlights MDOC-CBS's ability to exploit open space and promote dynamically coordinated motion.

**C. Robustness on Drop-Region and Random Maps (Fig. 5):**
- MDOC-CBS and MMD-CBS showed similar success rates, both outperforming KCBS.
- MDOC-CBS consistently maintained lower planning times.
- The study noted higher variability and occasional failures for MDOC-CBS in random maps, particularly when robots collided with obstacles and no valid rollout was returned. This suggests a trade-off between open-space exploration and aggressive safety tightening in compressed free space, indicating areas for future improvement in variance control and constraint projections. Despite this, MDOC-CBS successfully solved instances with up to 15 robots.

**D. Longer-Horizon MRMP on Larger Maps (4x4 & 6x6 Empty Maps):**
- **Scalability with Map Size and Horizon (Fig. 6):** MDOC-CBS demonstrated robustness in larger maps with longer planning horizons ($H=128$ for 4x4, $H=196$ for 6x6), achieving the highest success rate for up to 40 robots.
- **Comparison to MMD-CBS:** MMD-CBS's performance degraded sharply beyond 30 robots. Increasing the horizon directly extended its denoising chain, significantly increasing the inference cost of its model-free diffusion model, causing runs to hit the time limit.
- **MDOC-CBS's Graceful Scaling:** MDOC-CBS scaled more gracefully with horizon length because its model-based diffusion rollouts are performed in parallel under known dynamics, resulting in a sub-linear growth of per-iteration cost compared to model-free denoising. This parallelization and the ability to amortize computation across robots and time steps made MDOC-CBS substantially more robust for long-horizon planning in larger environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in multi-robot motion planning by introducing Model-Based Diffusion Optimal Control (MDOC) and its multi-robot extension, MDOC-CBS. The primary significance lies in its ability to address several fundamental limitations of prior approaches, particularly the data dependency and soft constraint enforcement issues prevalent in existing diffusion-based planners.

By fundamentally shifting from a model-free, data-driven paradigm to a model-based, training-free one, MDOC significantly reduces the reliance on large demonstration datasets. This makes the planning process more autonomous and potentially applicable in contexts where expert data is unavailable or difficult to acquire. The analytical estimation of diffusion scores via Monte Carlo score ascent, directly incorporating known dynamics models, provides a principled way to generate trajectories that are intrinsically dynamically feasible.

The integration of Control Barrier Function (CBF)-constrained projections directly into the model-based diffusion rollouts is a crucial innovation for ensuring hard safety guarantees. Unlike methods that rely on soft constraints or computationally expensive post-hoc corrections, MDOC enforces collision avoidance and other safety criteria during the trajectory generation process itself. This real-time, rigorous enforcement of safety is critical for deployment in real-world robotic systems where failures can have significant consequences.

The successful scaling of MDOC to multi-robot settings through its integration with Conflict-Based Search (CBS) in MDOC-CBS demonstrates its practical applicability. The experimental results, showing superior success rates, reduced computation times, and improved trajectory quality (shorter and smoother paths) compared to state-of-the-art baselines, highlight its effectiveness in complex, multi-robot environments, even with increased robot density and planning horizons. The ability of MDOC-CBS to coordinate robots more efficiently, by intelligently distributing them in congested areas rather than funneling them through bottlenecks, points to a more sophisticated understanding of multi-agent dynamics.

The potential impact of this research is substantial across various domains:
- **Autonomous Systems:** It can lead to more robust and safer motion planning for autonomous vehicles, drones, and mobile robots operating in dynamic, shared environments.
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** The improved scalability and efficiency can enhance the coordination of large fleets of robots in complex industrial settings.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Robots can be deployed more effectively in unknown or hazardous environments where explicit training data is absent, relying on their internal dynamic models and safety constraints.
- **Research in Robotics and AI:** This work opens new avenues for exploring hybrid approaches that combine the generative power of diffusion models with the guarantees of model-based control theory, potentially fostering further innovation in robot learning and control.

Future directions, such as optimizing Monte Carlo variance, enhancing constraint projection efficiency, adapting to online receding-horizon tasks, and exploring richer dynamics models, suggest that this foundational work has strong potential for continued development and broader adoption in advanced robotic applications.
