# HoloAssist: an Egocentric Human Interaction Dataset for Interactive AI Assistants in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft researchers introduce HoloAssist, a large-scale, multimodal egocentric dataset of human-human collaboration for assistive task completion in the physical world. It provides synchronized sensor data and detailed annotations to train AI models for real-time guidance, enabling benchmarks for mistake detection, intervention prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting.

## Problem

- Current AI assistants excel in digital tasks but lack direct experience and interactive capabilities for physical world perception, reasoning, and intervention.
- Existing datasets for embodied AI are often limited to simulated environments or lack the real-time, bidirectional human-human interaction dynamics crucial for collaborative assistance.
- Developing AI that can provide timely, grounded guidance and proactively correct mistakes in complex physical tasks remains a significant challenge due to a lack of suitable training data.

## Method

- The HoloAssist dataset captures 166 hours of multimodal egocentric data from 350 instructor-performer pairs completing 20 physical manipulation tasks.
- It includes seven synchronized sensor modalities (RGB, depth, head pose, 3D hand pose, eye gaze, audio, IMU) along with detailed annotations for fine-grained actions, mistakes, and intervention types.
- New benchmark tasks are defined for mistake detection, intervention type prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting, alongside standard action recognition, to evaluate core competencies for interactive AI assistants.

## Results

- Adding hand poses to RGB improved fine-grained action prediction, and a hands-only model achieved an F-score of 40.19 for mistake detection, outperforming other single modalities.
- The inclusion of hands and eye gaze boosted intervention type prediction, achieving 48.31% precision and 37.59% recall, a ~35 percentage point precision increase over RGB-only.
- A Seq2Seq model forecasted 3D hand poses with mean per joint position errors of 9.80 cm for a 0.5-second horizon and 11.25 cm for a 1.5-second horizon, surpassing a static baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human instructors demonstrate adaptive intervention strategies, correlating timely assistance with the criticality of mistakes and task progression.
> - Instructor guidance frequently utilizes spatial deictic phrases, highlighting the importance of 3D spatial awareness for effective physical assistance.
> - 3D hand poses and eye gaze are particularly valuable modalities, enhancing action understanding and anticipating user intentions for mistake detection and intervention prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of AI assistants capable of perceiving, reasoning, and collaborating with humans in the physical world represents a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. While recent advancements, particularly in large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, have enabled AI systems to assist humans with digital tasks, these systems often lack direct experience and interactive capabilities within the physical environment. This limitation restricts their ability to perceive real-world states and actively intervene in physical task completion.

Existing research has explored various avenues to address this gap. In computer vision, efforts have focused on understanding and guiding users through complex tasks, such as AR Guides using augmented reality (AR) devices. However, many current systems rely on predefined instructions, or frame virtual assistance as question-answering or video understanding problems, often without real-time, bidirectional interaction in physical space. Simulated environments like Habitat, VirtualHome, and AI2-Thor have been developed to train embodied AI agents, but transferring these agents to real-world scenarios remains challenging due to the simplified nature of simulated interactions compared to human-to-human collaboration.

Within egocentric vision, several datasets like EPIC-KITCHENS and Ego4D have emerged, capturing first-person perspectives of human activities. While these datasets provide rich information about user intentions, they generally do not feature multi-person interactive task completion scenarios where explicit guidance is provided and received in real-time. Similarly, research on mistake detection primarily focuses on coarse-grained actions, with less emphasis on the fine-grained actions and proactive interventions observed in human assistance.

HoloAssist positions itself as a complementary resource within this landscape by focusing specifically on real-world, egocentric human interaction for assistive task completion. It bridges the gap between simulated environments and real-world application by providing a large-scale multimodal dataset that captures rich sensor data and detailed human-human interactive dynamics, addressing the need for data that can inform the development of physically-grounded, interactive AI assistants. The dataset’s emphasis on timely, grounded, and mistake-correcting interventions in a collaborative setting provides a unique contribution compared to existing egocentric datasets and embodied AI platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to facilitate the development of interactive AI assistants that can fluidly collaborate with humans in the real world. This goal is motivated by the observation that current AI assistants, despite their advancements in digital domains, often lack the capabilities for physical world perception, reasoning, and interactive guidance that human assistants routinely demonstrate. The authors aim to address the challenges inherent in building intelligent agents that can share a human's perspective and actively guide users through physical tasks.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives include:

- **Bridging the Real-World Interaction Gap:** The authors identify a significant limitation in existing AI research: the lack of comprehensive, real-world egocentric human interaction data for physically-situated AI assistance. Current systems often rely on scripted instructions or simplified interactions, which do not fully capture the complexities of dynamic human-human collaboration, including real-time problem-solving and adaptive guidance.
- **Developing an Interactive AI Assistant Foundation:** As a foundational step, the paper introduces HoloAssist to provide the necessary data for training AI models that can perceive real-world states, understand human intentions, predict actions, and intervene appropriately. The dataset is designed to support the creation of AI agents that can provide proactive, timely, and contextually-grounded assistance, mirroring the capabilities of human instructors.
- **Capturing Rich Multimodal Human Behavior:** The motivation extends to observing and understanding the nuanced behaviors of human instructors and performers during collaborative tasks. This includes how instructors correct mistakes, intervene in task procedures, and ground their instructions using spatial references in the environment. The capture of multiple synchronized sensor modalities (RGB, depth, head pose, 3D hand pose, eye gaze, audio, and IMU) is driven by the need to provide comprehensive input for multimodal AI learning.
- **Identifying and Benchmarking Open Problems:** The work aims to identify core competencies required for effective interactive AI assistance. To this end, the authors construct and present benchmarks for specific tasks:
 - **Mistake Detection:** Motivated by the observation that human assistants often correct errors.
 - **Intervention Type Prediction:** Driven by the need for AI to understand *when* and *how* to intervene.
 - **3D Hand Pose Forecasting:** Aimed at enabling AI to provide spatially grounded guidance and anticipate user actions.
 - Additionally, standard action classification and anticipation tasks are benchmarked to provide a holistic evaluation of multimodal perception.
- **Providing a Publicly Available Resource:** A central objective is to offer HoloAssist as a large-scale, open-source dataset to the research community, along with tooling, to foster further research into designing interactive AI assistants and situated AI assistance applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers around the creation and annotation of the HoloAssist dataset, followed by the establishment of benchmark tasks and baseline experimental evaluations.

#### 3.1. Dataset Design and Data Collection

- **Two-Person Interactive Assistive Task Completion Setting:** HoloAssist is designed around a collaborative scenario where a "task performer" and an "instructor" work together to complete physical manipulation tasks. The performer wears a mixed-reality headset (HoloLens 2) to capture egocentric data, while the instructor watches the performer’s real-time video feed remotely and provides verbal guidance.
- **Scale and Diversity:** The dataset comprises 166 hours of data captured from 350 unique instructor-performer pairs, involving 222 diverse participants. These participants vary in age, occupation, gender, and geography. The tasks involve 20 object-centric manipulation tasks across 16 objects, ranging from common electronics (GoPro, Nintendo Switch) to office appliances (Nespresso machine) and specialized lab equipment (NavVis laser scanner, ATV motor cycle). Tasks include assembly, disassembly, and operational procedures, designed to be challenging for first-time participants, necessitating instructor assistance.
- **Multimodal Data Capture:** Seven synchronized raw sensor modalities are captured:
 - **RGB:** Color images from the front-facing camera (896 × 504 pixels, 29.5 Hz).
 - **Depth:** Depth images from the HoloLens 2 depth camera (504 × 504 pixels, 32.4 Hz).
 - **Head Pose:** Spatial pose of the user's head (4 × 4 matrix, 17.2 Hz).
 - **3D Hand Pose:** Spatial poses for 26 left-hand and 26 right-hand joints (4 × 4 matrix per joint, 43.2 Hz).
 - **Eye Gaze:** Spatial direction of the user's gaze (3 × 1 vector for position and direction, 30.7 Hz).
 - **Audio:** Single-channel audio stream from the microphone array (48 KHz).
 - **IMU:** Accelerometer (3 × 1 vector, 1.1 KHz), Gyroscope (3 × 1 vector, 6.4 KHz), and Magnetometer (3 × 1 vector, 49.5 Hz) data.
- **Data Capture Tool:** A distributed server-client application, developed using the Platform for Situated Intelligence framework, was open-sourced to enable live streaming and multimodal data capture, allowing for longer, uninterrupted sessions.
- **Ethical Considerations:** The data collection process received IRB approval. Participants reviewed and signed consent forms, acknowledging privacy and ethics standards. Data capture primarily occurred in public areas, avoiding human face capture.

#### 3.2. Annotations

The raw sensor data is augmented with several layers of third-person manual annotations:

- **Language Annotations:**
 - **Text Summaries:** Annotators provide a paragraph describing the activities and hand actions throughout each video, offering insights into key interactive moments.
 - **Conversational Transcriptions:** Verbatim transcriptions of all conversations between instructors and performers.
- **Action Annotations:**
 - **Coarse-grained Actions:** These describe high-level steps in a task (e.g., "change battery of a GoPro"). Each coarse action includes a sequence description and an identified verb-noun pair (e.g., "change battery"). The dataset includes 414 coarse actions with 90 nouns and 39 verbs.
 - **Fine-grained Actions:** These represent low-level, atomic actions (e.g., "press button," "grab screw"), typically lasting 1-2 seconds. They are presented as verb-(adj.)-noun pairs. The dataset contains 1887 fine-grained actions with 165 nouns and 49 verbs.
- **Mistake Annotation:** Each fine-grained action is labeled as either "correct" or "mistake." Mistakes are further categorized as "self-corrected by task performers," "verbally corrected by instructors," or "not corrected." Annotators provide explanations for mistakes and link instructor corrections to specific conversation sentences.
- **Intervention Annotation:** Conversational sentences are annotated with two attributes:
 - **Conversation Initiator:** "Task performer" or "instructor."
 - **Sentence Purpose Type:** Categories include instructor "correcting mistakes," "answering questions," "following up with more instructions," "confirming previous actions," "describing high-level task," "opening/closing remarks," or task performer "asking questions." For benchmarks, three intervention types are focused on: correcting mistakes, following up with more instructions, and confirming the previous action.
- **Audit Process:** A rigorous audit process involving self-review, independent review, direct fixes, and targeted consistency checks, along with systematic constraint checks, ensures annotation quality.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Tasks

Based on observations, new benchmark tasks are defined in addition to standard action recognition:

- **Mistake Detection:** Given features from fine-grained action clips, the model predicts if the action is "correct" or "mistake." This is a challenging task due to skewed class distribution (approx. 6% mistakes).
- **Intervention Type Prediction:** The model predicts one of three intervention types (correcting mistakes, following up with more instructions, confirming previous actions) based on a window of 1, 3, or 5 seconds before an intervention.
- **3D Hand Pose Forecasting:** Given 3 seconds of input 3D hand poses, the model forecasts continuous 3D hand poses for the next 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 seconds. The metric is mean per joint position error.
- **Action Recognition:** Standard classification of fine-grained and coarse-grained actions based on video segments.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** TimeSformer (a Vision Transformer-based video model) is used as the backbone for action recognition, mistake detection, and intervention prediction. A Seq2Seq model is used for 3D hand pose forecasting.
- **Multimodal Integration:** TimeSformer is modified to incorporate additional tokens and embedding layers for hand poses, eye gaze, head poses, and depth.
- **Training Details:** Models are trained for 15 epochs using SGD, with learning rate adjustments. Input segments are typically 8 sampled frames. A dataset split of 70% train, 10% validation, 20% test is used.
- **Baselines:** Performance is evaluated against RGB-only models, hand-only models, and various combinations of modalities, both with and without ImageNet pre-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents observations drawn from the HoloAssist dataset regarding human interaction and provides baseline experimental results across the defined benchmark tasks.

#### 4.1. Observations from HoloAssist

- **Correlation between Mistakes and Intervention:** Human instructors demonstrate adaptive intervention strategies. For critical mistakes that impede task progression (e.g., "insert joy con controller," "place tray"), instructors tend to intervene immediately (within 5 seconds). In contrast, less critical mistakes or those in tasks with more flexible progression (e.g., "drop allen wrench" in furniture assembly) are often self-corrected by the performer or addressed later by the instructor. This highlights the nuanced timing and severity assessment in human assistance.
- **Grounded Guidance with Spatial Deixis:** Instructor guidance is frequently grounded in the 3D environment, utilizing spatial deictic phrases to communicate specific and relative locations, directions, and distances. Examples include "It's right below your hand," "The button is on the other side of the Switch," and "Please rotate so the back is facing you." This indicates the importance of spatial awareness for effective assistance.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results

- **Action Recognition (Fine-grained and Coarse-grained):**
 - Models initialized with a pre-trained ImageNet ViT backbone achieve around 35% top-1 accuracy for fine-grained actions and 50% for coarse-grained actions.
 - Pre-training on ImageNet appears to reduce the observed influence of other modalities.
 - When trained from scratch, the inclusion of hand pose data (Hands or R+H) leads to improved prediction of verbs compared to an RGB-only model, demonstrating the value of explicit body pose information for action understanding.
 - Simply concatenating more sensor modalities does not consistently lead to improved performance, suggesting the need for specialized multimodal encoders.

- **Mistake Detection:**
 - The task is challenging due to the highly skewed class distribution (approximately 6% mistakes).
 - Adding hand poses to RGB (R+H) improves the F-score from 35.11 (RGB-only) to 36.18.
 - Notably, a hands-only model achieves an F-score of 40.19, outperforming models with other modalities, suggesting the critical role of hand movements in identifying errors.

- **Intervention Type Prediction:**
 - The inclusion of hands and eye gaze (R+H+E) significantly boosts overall precision and recall to 48.31% and 37.59% respectively, improving by approximately 35 and 4 percentage points over an RGB-only model.
 - This improvement is attributed to eye gaze potentially serving as a forecasting signal, as users often fixate on relevant regions before initiating an action, which can aid anticipation and intervention prediction.

- **3D Hand Pose Forecasting:**
 - Given 3 seconds of input, the Seq2Seq model trained with hand poses (H) can forecast future 3D hand poses with mean per joint position errors of 9.80 cm (0.5 sec), 10.68 cm (1.0 sec), and 11.25 cm (1.5 sec).
 - This performance surpasses a static baseline (Static-H), which uses only the last known hand pose.
 - The task is noted as challenging due to the rapid movement of hands. The accuracy decreases as the forecasting horizon extends.

- **Importance of Hand Poses and Eye Gaze:** Across multiple tasks (action recognition, mistake detection, intervention prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting), 3D hand poses and eye gaze consistently emerge as beneficial modalities. They augment RGB images, contributing to improved model predictions and a deeper understanding of user intentions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HoloAssist dataset and the accompanying research make several significant contributions to the field of AI, particularly in the domain of interactive and embodied intelligence:

- **Pioneering Real-World Interaction Data:** HoloAssist is introduced as a large-scale, multimodal, egocentric human interaction dataset specifically designed for assistive task completion in the physical world. This fills a critical gap in existing datasets by focusing on dynamic, collaborative human-human interaction, offering a more realistic foundation for developing AI assistants compared to simulation-only or non-interactive datasets.
- **Foundation for Interactive AI Assistants:** By providing rich, synchronized sensor data (RGB, depth, hand pose, eye gaze, head pose, IMU, audio) and detailed annotations (fine-grained actions, mistake attributes, intervention types, conversational transcripts), HoloAssist offers the necessary resources to train and evaluate AI models capable of perceiving, reasoning, and collaborating with humans. This could lead to AI assistants that can not only understand tasks but also proactively provide grounded guidance and correct mistakes in real-time.
- **Identification of Key AI Competencies:** The observations from human-human interactions within HoloAssist highlight crucial behaviors like proactive interventions, adaptive mistake correction, and the use of spatial deixis in guidance. This has led to the definition of novel benchmark tasks—mistake detection, intervention type prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting—which are critical components for developing truly interactive and grounded AI assistants.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Learning:** The dataset promotes research into multimodal learning by demonstrating the individual and combined contributions of various sensor streams. The findings emphasize the particular importance of 3D hand poses and eye gaze for understanding human intentions and actions, suggesting that future models for interactive AI assistance will benefit from sophisticated integration of these modalities. The challenges noted in simply concatenating modalities also point towards future research directions in specialized multimodal encoders.
- **Practical Applications:** The dataset and benchmarks have the potential to impact various real-world applications, including:
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) and Mixed Reality (MR) assistance:** AI assistants operating within AR/MR environments, guiding users through complex assembly, maintenance, or operational tasks.
 - **Robotics:** Improving human-robot collaboration by enabling robots to understand human intent, predict actions, and offer intelligent assistance.
 - **Personalized Learning and Training:** Developing intelligent tutoring systems that can adapt to individual user performance, detect errors, and provide tailored, timely feedback in physical skill acquisition.
 - **Accessibility:** Assisting individuals with tasks that require fine motor skills or complex procedures.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The open-sourcing of HoloAssist and its associated data capture tools (Platform for Situated Intelligence framework) lowers the barrier for entry for researchers, fostering collaborative progress in the development of competent AI assistants for everyday tasks in the real world.
