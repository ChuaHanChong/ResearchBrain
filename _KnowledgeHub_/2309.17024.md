# HoloAssist: an Egocentric Human Interaction Dataset for Interactive AI Assistants in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> Microsoft researchers introduce HoloAssist, a large-scale, multimodal egocentric dataset of human-human collaboration for assistive task completion in the physical world. It provides synchronized sensor data and detailed annotations to train AI models for real-time guidance, enabling benchmarks for mistake detection, intervention prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting.

## Problem

- Current AI assistants excel in digital tasks but lack direct experience and interactive capabilities for physical world perception, reasoning, and intervention.
- Existing datasets for embodied AI are often limited to simulated environments or lack the real-time, bidirectional human-human interaction dynamics crucial for collaborative assistance.
- Developing AI that can provide timely, grounded guidance and proactively correct mistakes in complex physical tasks remains a significant challenge due to a lack of suitable training data.

## Method

- The HoloAssist dataset captures 166 hours of multimodal egocentric data from 350 instructor-performer pairs completing 20 physical manipulation tasks.
- It includes seven synchronized sensor modalities (RGB, depth, head pose, 3D hand pose, eye gaze, audio, IMU) along with detailed annotations for fine-grained actions, mistakes, and intervention types.
- New benchmark tasks are defined for mistake detection, intervention type prediction, and 3D hand pose forecasting, alongside standard action recognition, to evaluate core competencies for interactive AI assistants.

## Results

- Adding hand poses to RGB improved fine-grained action prediction, and a hands-only model achieved an F-score of 40.19 for mistake detection, outperforming other single modalities.
- The inclusion of hands and eye gaze boosted intervention type prediction, achieving 48.31% precision and 37.59% recall, a ~35 percentage point precision increase over RGB-only.
- A Seq2Seq model forecasted 3D hand poses with mean per joint position errors of 9.80 cm for a 0.5-second horizon and 11.25 cm for a 1.5-second horizon, surpassing a static baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human instructors demonstrate adaptive intervention strategies, correlating timely assistance with the criticality of mistakes and task progression.
> - Instructor guidance frequently utilizes spatial deictic phrases, highlighting the importance of 3D spatial awareness for effective physical assistance.
> - 3D hand poses and eye gaze are particularly valuable modalities, enhancing action understanding and anticipating user intentions for mistake detection and intervention prediction.
