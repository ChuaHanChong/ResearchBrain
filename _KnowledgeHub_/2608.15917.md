# Pre-training Visual Dexterity in Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SPD pre-trains a causal diffusion-transformer policy on 75 hours of VR teleoperated simulation data for a 56-DoF bimanual dexterous hand, collected robot-free via MuJoCo VR teleoperation. Fine-tuned on 1-2 hours of real demonstrations per task, it outperforms training behavior cloning from scratch across five real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Real teleoperation data for multi-fingered dexterous hands is costly to scale because hardware is fragile and expensive, unlike simple parallel-jaw gripper setups.
- Handheld interfaces like UMI avoid robot dependence but struggle to extend to hands, since devices must give operators control over every degree of freedom without embodiment gap.
- Human hand video is abundant but off-embodiment, requiring lossy pose estimation and retargeting that yields noisy or unfaithfully executable robot supervision.

## Method

- **spd-vr**: a ==MuJoCo-based VR teleoperation== system streaming simulation to a Quest headset, letting operators manipulate virtual objects to collect on-embodiment, robot-free trajectories at scale.
- **spd-teleop**: an aligned real-world teleoperation system sharing camera placement, hands, and arms with spd-vr, used to collect small-scale fine-tuning demonstrations on physical hardware.
- A ==causal diffusion transformer== with ==sliding-window attention==, ==history conditioning==, and short action chunks, trained with ==flow-matching== on interleaved proprioception, action, and vision tokens.

## Results

- Across five bimanual tasks (plate racking, mug hanging, Jenga, cup stacking, bottles in bin), SPD reaches nearly every progress stage more often than from-scratch behavior cloning, with lower flow-matching training loss.
- Ablation over window size w and chunk size c shows the pre-trained w=32, c=8 configuration gains **18** progress points over its from-scratch counterpart, versus **3** points or less for other variants.
- Single-frame context (w=1) with short chunks (c=8) collapses performance to near **0%** on jenga, cups, and bottles, while adding 32-step history recovers strong scores like **85%** jenga and **68.8%** bottles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - History conditioning removes the coherence-versus-reactivity trade-off, letting short action chunks stay both temporally consistent and reactive, unlike single-frame conditioning.
> - Simulation teleoperation on the exact target embodiment sidesteps human-to-robot retargeting entirely, offering action-labeled data without the pose-estimation noise of human video.
> - Pre-training benefits concentrate in the configuration that already generalizes best, suggesting pre-training amplifies good architectural choices rather than compensating for poor ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics presents a challenging problem due to the requirements for precise control, frequent contact interactions, partial observability, and minimal latency. While large-scale pre-training has advanced robot policy fine-tuning by increasing data efficiency, this progress has largely concentrated on simpler robotic embodiments, such as parallel-jaw grippers. Dexterous, multi-fingered robot hands, however, remain comparatively data-starved.

Several existing approaches attempt to address this data scarcity, each with limitations:
- **Real Teleoperation:** While providing ideal on-embodiment data, it is costly to scale for multi-fingered hands due to the fragility and expense of the hardware, which limits data throughput.
- **Handheld Interfaces (e.g., UMI):** These systems enable scalable data collection without direct robot interaction. However, they can introduce an embodiment gap and struggle to provide operators with fine-grained control over every degree of freedom required for dexterous hands.
- **Human Hand Video:** This source offers abundant and inexpensive data. Yet, extracting actionable robot supervision is difficult due to occlusions in contact-rich interactions, leading to noisy hand pose reconstruction. Even with accurate human hand tracking (e.g., via motion-capture gloves), differences in contact points and finger actuation between human and robot hands can result in demonstrations that are not faithfully executable by a robot.

This work, "Simulation Pre-training for Dexterity (SPD)," positions itself within this landscape by investigating whether data collected in physics simulators can serve as a scalable source for pre-training dexterous hands. SPD directly addresses the limitations of real teleoperation by removing the dependence on physical hardware during pre-training, thereby enabling faster data collection and virtual environment resets. It also mitigates the challenges of human hand video and handheld interfaces by collecting action-labeled data directly on the target dexterous-hand embodiment in a virtual environment, thus avoiding the embodiment gap and complex human-to-robot retargeting processes.

Within the context of related work:
- **Pre-training for robot policies:** Existing efforts have leveraged large robot datasets and transformer-based policies for general manipulation, including vision-language-action models and fine-tuning paradigms. SPD extends this pre-training approach to the domain of contact-rich manipulation with dexterous hands, a less explored area in large-scale pre-training.
- **Learning from humans:** Prior work has explored off-robot data sources like handheld interfaces or human video. SPD contributes by using simulation teleoperation to generate action-labeled, on-embodiment data, retaining the scalability benefits of off-robot collection while avoiding the specialized hardware or complex post-processing often required by other human-derived data sources.
- **Simulation for dexterous manipulation:** Simulation has been used extensively for training dexterous skills via reinforcement learning (often with domain randomization) or for collecting task-specific demonstrations via VR teleoperation. SPD differentiates itself by utilizing simulation teleoperation as a *broad pre-training data source* aimed at developing a visuomotor policy for *fine-tuning across multiple downstream real-world tasks*, rather than optimizing for a single simulated skill.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to establish and evaluate a simulation-based pre-training framework for dexterous manipulation, aimed at improving the data efficiency of fine-tuning robot policies for real-world tasks.

The motivation for pursuing simulation pre-training stems from several challenges inherent in training dexterous robotic policies:
- **Data Scarcity for Dexterous Hands:** Unlike simpler grippers, multi-fingered dexterous hands are complex, fragile, and costly. This limits the scalability of real-world teleoperation for data collection, leading to insufficient data for training robust policies.
- **Limitations of Alternative Data Sources:**
 - Human hand videos, though abundant, require complex and often inaccurate pose estimation and retargeting to be useful for robots. They also suffer from inherent embodiment differences.
 - Handheld interfaces designed to circumvent real robot usage can introduce an embodiment gap and may not provide sufficient control over all degrees of freedom needed for dexterous tasks.
- **Demand for Data-Efficient Policy Learning:** To achieve competent dexterous manipulation in the real world, policies must learn broadly useful visuomotor representations that can then be quickly adapted to specific tasks with minimal real-world data. Pre-training on large datasets is a proven method for this, but a scalable source of high-quality data for dexterous hands has been lacking.

Given these motivations, the specific objectives of the SPD framework include:
- **Develop Aligned Teleoperation Systems:** Create `spd-vr`, a virtual reality system for collecting pre-training data in simulation, and `spd-teleop`, a real-world teleoperation system for collecting fine-tuning data, ensuring both systems are closely aligned in terms of robot embodiment and camera setup.
- **Collect a Large-Scale Simulation Dataset:** Use `spd-vr` to collect a substantial multi-task, long-horizon, and diverse dataset of dexterous manipulation demonstrations entirely within simulation.
- **Pre-train a Robust Policy:** Train a causal diffusion transformer policy on this simulation dataset, employing a sequence modeling objective to learn diverse, contact-rich behaviors and low-level dexterous primitives.
- **Evaluate Real-World Transfer:** Assess the benefits of this simulation pre-training by fine-tuning the pre-trained policy with limited real-world demonstrations on a bimanual dexterous robot and comparing its performance against policies trained from scratch.
- **Investigate Policy Architecture Parameters:** Conduct ablation studies to understand the impact of history conditioning and action chunk size on policy performance and the efficacy of pre-training.
- **Open-Source Contributions:** Release the collected pre-training dataset (`spd-75h`), the VR teleoperation software (`spd-vr`), and curated simulation scenes to foster further research in simulation-based dexterous manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Simulation Pre-training for Dexterity (SPD) framework, which leverages simulation for scalable dexterous manipulation data collection and policy pre-training, followed by fine-tuning on real-world robot data.

**Framework Overview:**
SPD consists of two key teleoperation systems: `spd-vr` for collecting pre-training data in a virtual environment and `spd-teleop` for collecting fine-tuning data on a physical robot. A core design principle is the alignment between these two systems, sharing identical bimanual dexterous hands (22-DoF Sharpa Wave) and 6-DoF arms (upgraded YAM Pro), along with consistent top and wrist camera placements, and a similar collection of objects.

**VR Data Collection (`spd-vr`):**
- **Environment and Control:** Human operators manipulate virtual objects and control a simulated bimanual dexterous robot directly within the MuJoCo physics simulator. Hand poses detected from a VR headset (Meta Quest 3) cameras (at 60 Hz) drive the simulated robot arms and hands through inverse kinematics. The simulation itself runs at 480 Hz for physics, with control, streaming, and recording at 60 Hz. The simulated robot arms are rendered translucently to minimize visual occlusion for the operator.
- **Task Design and Diversity:** Data is collected across six broad scenes (spelling blocks, dishes, mugs, bottles, cups, Jenga bricks). Episodes feature long-horizon and open-ended tasks, where only the desired outcome is specified, allowing for diverse strategies and contact-rich behaviors across randomized object instances, physical properties, and initial positions.
- **Scale of Collection:** Five operators accumulated approximately 2,000 episodes, totaling 75 hours of demonstrations, over a single week.
- **Data Processing:** After collection, non-contact periods exceeding ten seconds are filtered out. Trajectories are rendered in parallel using an adapted Madrona renderer at 224x168 resolution. Visual augmentations, applied as GPU transforms, include random tinting of object colors and swapping of background/table textures using segmentation masks. A symmetry augmentation is also applied, swapping the two arms and reflecting corresponding images, proprioception, and actions. The resulting dataset, `spd-75h`, comprises 1,930 episodes.

**Real-World Data Collection (`spd-teleop`):**
- **Hardware Setup:** The physical robot setup includes two upgraded YAM Pro arms, each equipped with a 22-DoF Sharpa Wave dexterous hand. Motor upgrades (40:1 gear ratio on J3 and J4) were implemented to handle the hand weight and reduce overheating. Three RealSense D405 cameras provide visual input: one top-mounted and one on the ulnar side of each hand.
- **Teleoperation Interface:** The real-world teleoperation system mirrors the VR retargeting scheme. For finger tracking, Manus gloves are used, and a Quest controller provides wrist tracking. This combination offers more precise and smoother control, deemed necessary for physical robot operation with occlusions and at a distance.
- **Robot Control:** Arm commands are executed as joint positions, driven by PD gains with gravity compensation computed via MuJoCo inverse dynamics. Hands are position-controlled via their vendor SDK.
- **Fine-tuning Data:** For downstream tasks, 1-2 hours of real-world demonstrations are collected per task.

**Policy Architecture and Training:**
- **Policy Type:** A diffusion transformer is employed, adopting a causal transformer architecture for sequence modeling. This policy denoises action chunks conditioned on visuomotor observations.
- **Objective:** The model is trained using a flow-matching velocity-prediction objective, supervising the denoised action chunks.
- **Input Sequence:** The policy processes an interleaved sequence of proprioception (56-D), previous action (56-D), multi-view image tokens (4 pooled tokens per camera from a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16 vision transformer), and noised future action chunks. Language conditioning is not used.
- **Context and Efficiency:** Each training sequence spans 256 timesteps at 30 Hz. All action chunks within a sequence are denoised in parallel under a causal mask, amortizing the computational cost. A sliding-window attention mechanism with a 32-timestep window is used across all layers to support a fixed-length Key-Value (KV) cache during inference.
- **Vision Pathway:** Images are encoded by a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B/16. Four learnable queries per camera pool patch features into a compact set of tokens via cross-attention, with re-attention to the raw patch bank every two transformer blocks to provide context. Image inputs are subsampled every 8 timesteps.
- **Transformer Trunk:** An 8-block transformer with 222M parameters (including an 86M parameter vision encoder and a 58M parameter action-denoising expert). Modality-specific linear input/output projections are used.
- **Embeddings:** Rotary embeddings encode temporal positions for tokens, while noised action chunks receive additional absolute positional embeddings for the flow-matching timestep and position within the chunk.
- **Training Details:** Models are trained with the Muon optimizer (for matrices) and AdamW (for other parameters) at a fixed learning rate of 10⁻³. An exponential moving average of weights is maintained for inference. Proprioception and action inputs are perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ=0.03) during training to reduce distribution shift.
- **Inference:** During deployment, the transformer operates as an incremental engine using a rolling KV cache. Observations are appended, and on chunk boundaries, the flow ODE is integrated with 10 Euler steps to predict the next 8 actions.

**Evaluation Protocol:**
The benefits of simulation pre-training are evaluated by fine-tuning the policy on 1-2 hours of real-world demonstrations per task. Evaluation is conducted on five bimanual dexterous tasks: plate racking, mug hanging, Jenga playing, cup stacking, and tossing bottles into a bin. For each task, 20 trials are performed per checkpoint, and task progress is scored against a rubric and normalized.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study evaluated the effectiveness of Simulation Pre-training for Dexterity (SPD) by comparing pre-trained policies against those trained from scratch on real-world tasks, and by conducting ablation studies on key policy architecture parameters.

**Benefits of Simulation Pre-training:**
- **Superior Performance Across Tasks:** SPD consistently outperformed behavior cloning (BC) policies trained from scratch across all five real-world bimanual dexterous tasks: plate racking, mug hanging, Jenga playing, cup stacking, and tossing bottles into a bin. The pre-trained policies demonstrated higher average task progress and reached successive manipulation stages more frequently than their from-scratch counterparts.
- **Improved Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training loss curves indicated that SPD checkpoints initiated and converged to lower flow-matching loss values compared to BC policies trained from scratch. This observation correlated with the improved real-world performance, suggesting that pre-training provides a more advantageous initialization and learning trajectory.

**Ablations on History Conditioning and Chunk Size:**
Ablation studies were conducted to investigate the impact of the sliding window size (w, for history conditioning) and the action chunk size (c) on policy performance, both for pre-trained and from-scratch models.
- **Impact of Chunk Size with Limited Context:** When the policy was trained with a single frame of context (w=1) and a short action chunk (c=8), its performance significantly deteriorated, resulting in policies that exhibited shaky and less temporally coherent behavior. This suggested a trade-off where shorter chunks, while offering reactivity, could lead to instability without sufficient historical context.
- **Efficacy of History Conditioning:** Introducing a 32-step sliding window for history conditioning (w=32) mitigated the negative effects of short action chunks. With this history context, the 8-step action chunk (c=8) variant emerged as the most effective configuration, outperforming other combinations in both pre-trained and from-scratch regimes. This indicates that visuomotor history provides the necessary temporal consistency, allowing the policy to leverage the reactivity offered by shorter action chunks.
- **Maximized Pre-training Gains with Specific Configuration:** The `w=32, c=8` configuration (history-conditioned with short, reactive chunks) showed the most substantial benefit from pre-training. Its average task progress improved by 18 points compared to its from-scratch equivalent. In contrast, other architectural variants demonstrated improvements of 3 points or less, highlighting that the combination of comprehensive history and reactive action chunks is particularly conducive to leveraging the advantages of simulation pre-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by demonstrating a viable strategy for scaling dexterous policy learning, with several significant implications:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity for Dexterous Manipulation:** The primary contribution is showing that simulation teleoperation can serve as a scalable and effective source for pre-training general dexterous manipulation policies. This addresses a critical challenge in robotics where real-world data collection for multi-fingered hands is prohibitively expensive, complex, and slow. By enabling robot-free, on-embodiment data collection, SPD offers a pathway to generate large datasets that were previously impractical to obtain.
- **Improving Data Efficiency in Real-World Robotics:** The results indicate that pre-training in simulation significantly enhances data efficiency for real-world fine-tuning. The ability to adapt a pre-trained policy to novel real-world tasks with only 1-2 hours of physical demonstrations represents a substantial improvement over training policies from scratch. This can accelerate the deployment of dexterous robots in new applications by reducing the time and resources required for task-specific learning.
- **Insights into Policy Architecture Design:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the design of visuomotor policies for dexterous control. The findings emphasize that conditioning on visuomotor history is crucial for maintaining temporal coherence when using short, reactive action chunks. This understanding can guide the development of more effective and robust architectures for complex manipulation tasks, particularly those involving continuous contact. The observation that this specific configuration (`w=32, c=8`) benefits most from pre-training suggests an optimal interplay between architectural choices and large-scale data utilization.
- **Resource Contribution to the Research Community:** The public release of the `spd-75h` pre-training dataset, the `spd-vr` VR teleoperation software, and the tuned simulation scenes is a notable contribution. These resources can foster further research, allow for direct comparisons, and lower the barrier to entry for other researchers working on dexterous manipulation, potentially accelerating progress in simulation-to-real transfer and robust policy learning.

**Identified Limitations (as stated by the authors):**
- **Dependence on Simulation Fidelity:** The effectiveness of SPD relies on the physical realism of the simulation scenes. Discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics (e.g., object masses, friction, contact responses) can lead to pre-trained strategies that transfer poorly to the physical domain.
- **Limited Generalization in Current Data:** The current pre-training dataset (spd-75h) has limited diversity in scenes and objects. The real-world evaluation tasks involve objects that are similar to those seen during pre-training. The extent to which the pre-trained policy generalizes to more "out-of-distribution" objects, scenes, and task variations remains to be fully characterized.

**Future Research Directions:**
The authors propose several avenues for future work, including:
- **Multi-Source Pre-training:** Investigating how simulation teleoperation data complements and can be combined with other data sources, such as real-world teleoperation and egocentric human video, within a mixed pre-training corpus.
- **Scaling Pre-training:** Expanding the scale of pre-training to include a greater diversity of scenes, objects, and total training hours to further characterize the generalization capabilities that emerge from large-scale simulation data.
- **Integration with Reinforcement Learning:** Exploring the use of the pre-trained policy as a robust initialization for reinforcement learning algorithms, potentially leveraging the scalability of RL in simulation without operator time constraints.
