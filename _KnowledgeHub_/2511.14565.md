# Masked IRL: LLM-Guided Reward Disambiguation from Demonstrations and Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT CSAIL developed Masked IRL, a framework leveraging large language models to guide inverse reinforcement learning. This method enhances sample efficiency by up to 4.7 times, improves robustness to ambiguous language by 21.4% in win rate, and generalizes effectively in learning diverse robot task preferences from limited demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods are sample-inefficient and often overfit to irrelevant state components due to the ill-posed nature of inferring reward functions from limited demonstrations.
- Existing language-conditioned reward learning approaches do not fully exploit natural language's potential to resolve ambiguity or guide feature learning, often using it only as a simple conditioning signal.
- Real-world human instructions for robots are frequently ambiguous or underspecified, limiting their reliability as standalone signals for precise task intent.

## Method

- **Masked IRL** integrates demonstrations and natural language instructions, utilizing LLMs to ==disambiguate ambiguous commands== by jointly reasoning about language and demonstrations.
- LLMs generate ==binary state-relevance masks== for each instruction-demonstration pair, indicating which state components are pertinent to the task.
- A novel ==implicit masking loss== is introduced, which penalizes changes in the reward function output when irrelevant state components are perturbed, enforcing invariance to irrelevant dimensions during training.

## Results

- **Masked IRL** matched or outperformed baseline language-conditioned IRL, requiring up to **4.7x** fewer demonstrations to achieve comparable generalization performance.
- LLM-based language disambiguation improved state mask prediction F1 scores by **16.9%** and led to a **21.4%** higher average win rate on test preferences when handling ambiguous instructions.
- On a real Franka Panda robot, **Masked IRL** achieved higher average win rates, significantly lower reward variance, and **44.8%** to **59.4%** lower average reward regret in zero-shot generalization compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging LLMs to infer state-relevance masks and disambiguate instructions provides structured supervision that significantly improves reward learning efficiency and robustness beyond simple language conditioning.
> - An implicit masking loss, which penalizes sensitivity to irrelevant state components, is more robust to inaccuracies in LLM-generated masks compared to direct explicit masking.
> - Combining both language disambiguation and the masking loss allows the framework to effectively learn generalizable reward functions from limited, potentially ambiguous human input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in robot learning from human feedback: the inherent ambiguity and data inefficiency of traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods. While robots can learn tasks by inferring reward functions from human demonstrations, such demonstrations often provide limited information, leading to reward models that overfit to spurious correlations and fail to generalize effectively. This is particularly problematic in low-data regimes, where collecting diverse demonstrations is costly.

The paper situates itself within the context of advancements in language-conditioned learning in robotics and the increasing utility of Large Language Models (LLMs). Prior work in IRL often relies on hand-specified feature functions or deep IRL methods that require substantial amounts of data. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), using pairwise trajectory comparisons, reduces human burden but yields low information content per query, necessitating thousands of labels for a single reward function. More recent approaches leverage LLMs to generate reward functions or condition policies and rewards on natural language, facilitating personalization and transfer to novel tasks.

However, existing language-conditioned reward learning methods typically treat language as a simple conditioning signal, not fully exploiting its potential to resolve ambiguity or guide feature learning. These methods also often assume instructions are clear and unambiguous, which is frequently not the case in real-world human-robot interaction.

This work integrates demonstrations and natural language through LLMs to overcome these limitations. It bridges the gap between purely behavioral feedback (demonstrations) and semantic feedback (language), recognizing their complementary strengths. By using LLMs to infer state-relevance masks and disambiguate instructions, the paper aims to enhance sample efficiency, improve generalization, and increase robustness to ambiguous language, thereby advancing the state of the art in learning adaptable and generalizable robot behaviors from limited, human-provided data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and sample-efficient framework for robots to learn diverse human task preferences from a minimal amount of language-labeled demonstrations. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Ambiguity in Reward Learning from Demonstrations:** Reward learning is fundamentally ill-posed; multiple reward functions can explain the same demonstration. For example, a robot observing a trajectory might infer it should avoid a laptop, stay close to a human, or produce curved paths. Traditional IRL methods often overfit to spurious correlations in irrelevant state components due to insufficient data diversity, leading to poor generalization. The motivation is to provide the robot with explicit guidance on *what matters* for a task, beyond just *how* to perform it.

2. **Exploiting Natural Language's Potential Beyond Simple Conditioning:** Natural language can directly specify important task aspects (e.g., "Stay away from my laptop"). While existing language-conditioned reward learning methods use language as a conditioning signal for multitask learning, they often do not fully leverage its capacity to resolve ambiguity or guide the learning process by identifying relevant state features. The motivation is to unlock the full potential of natural language as a rich, structured supervisory signal.

3. **Handling Ambiguous and Underspecified Language:** Real-world human instructions are often ambiguous or underspecified (e.g., "Stay away" without specifying *what* to avoid). This ambiguity limits the reliability of language as a standalone signal for precise human intent. A key motivation is to develop methods that can reason about such ambiguous language in context, specifically using demonstrations, to clarify intent and maintain robustness.

4. **Improving Sample Efficiency:** Collecting diverse, high-quality demonstrations for every possible user preference is time-consuming and costly. Standard deep IRL methods often require a large number of demonstrations to prevent overfitting. The motivation is to drastically reduce the data requirements for learning generalizable reward functions by providing more structured supervision that guides the model to focus on relevant aspects of the state, thus making robot learning more practical for real-world deployment.

5. **Achieving Generalization and Robustness:** The aim is to develop a single reward model that can generalize across various user preferences, rather than training a separate model for each. This generalization should be robust to variations in demonstrations, noise in state observations, and the inherent ambiguities of natural language instructions.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for robots to learn human preferences more efficiently and reliably by intelligently integrating complementary information from both behavioral demonstrations and semantic natural language instructions, particularly in scenarios where both inputs might be individually ambiguous or limited.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Masked Inverse Reinforcement Learning (Masked IRL), is a multitask reward learning framework that integrates demonstrations and natural language instructions. It employs Large Language Models (LLMs) in a structured manner to overcome the limitations of existing language-conditioned reward learning.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The framework builds upon the standard Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) objective, aiming to learn a language-conditioned reward function $r_\theta(s | \ell)$ from a dataset $D$ of language-labeled demonstrations $\{(τ_k, \ell_k)\}$. The objective is to minimize the negative log-likelihood of the demonstrations using the Maximum Entropy IRL objective, where the human is modeled as a noisily rational agent selecting trajectories proportional to their exponentiated reward. The paper highlights that while this Language-Conditioned Reward Learning (LC-RL) provides a framework, it suffers from high sample complexity and susceptibility to spurious correlations in low-data regimes, compounded by the inherent ambiguity of natural language.

#### 3.2. Masked IRL Pipeline Overview

Masked IRL leverages two complementary properties of language and demonstrations:
1. Language specifies what task the human wants and implicitly indicates which aspects of the environment matter.
2. When language commands are ambiguous, examining language in the context of demonstrations can ground instructions and resolve ambiguity.

The overall pipeline (Figure 2) consists of:
- **LLM Disambiguation:** Clarifying ambiguous language instructions using demonstrations and LLM reasoning.
- **State Mask Prediction:** Inferring state-relevance masks from (disambiguated) language instructions using an LLM.
- **Reward Learning:** Training a language-conditioned reward model using a novel masking loss alongside the standard IRL loss to enforce invariance to irrelevant state components.
- **Trajectory Optimization:** Using the learned reward model to select optimal trajectories.

#### 3.3. Generating State Masks from Language

To address overfitting to irrelevant state components, Masked IRL uses LLMs (specifically GPT-4o in experiments) to generate binary state relevance masks ($m \in \{0, 1\}^d$). For each demonstration-language pair $(τ, \ell)$, the LLM is queried with the command $\ell$ and a description of the robot and environment state. The LLM identifies which components of the $d$-dimensional state vector are relevant to satisfying the instruction, producing a mask where $m^{(j)}=1$ for relevant components and $m^{(j)}=0$ for irrelevant ones. This process augments the training data to include these masks.

#### 3.4. State Masking Loss

Instead of directly zeroing out irrelevant state dimensions (explicit masking), Masked IRL employs an *implicit masking* approach by enforcing invariance through a masking loss, $L_{mask}(\theta)$. This loss penalizes changes in the reward function output when irrelevant state components are perturbed with random noise ($\epsilon \sim \text{Uniform}(0, 1)$). Formally, for a perturbed state $s^{(j)}$ (where only element $j$ is modified), the loss is:
$$ \mathbb{E}_{\tau,\ell,m \in D'} \sum_{s \in \tau} \sum_{j=1}^d \left(1 - m^{(j)}\right) \left| r_\theta(s^{(j)} | \ell) - r_\theta(s | \ell) \right| $$
This ensures that the reward model learns to be insensitive to perturbations in irrelevant dimensions. The full training objective combines this masking loss with the standard Maximum Entropy IRL loss:
$$ J(\theta) = L_{IRL}(\theta) + \lambda L_{mask}(\theta) $$
where $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter balancing the two losses. This implicit approach is intended to be more robust to errors in LLM-generated masks compared to hard-masking.

#### 3.5. Clarifying Ambiguous Language Instructions

To handle underspecified language (e.g., "Stay away"), Masked IRL uses LLMs (specifically GPT-5 in experiments) to disambiguate instructions by jointly reasoning about the language and the demonstration. The LLM is provided with:
- A task and environment description.
- The ambiguous language utterance $\ell$.
- A state-based representation of the human demonstration $τ$.
- A state-based representation of a "reference trajectory" (e.g., shortest path between start and end points).

The LLM is prompted to infer clarified commands that explain the difference between the demonstration and the reference trajectory. If multiple clarifications are plausible, the LLM is instructed to return all of them, which serves as a form of data augmentation. These disambiguated instructions are then used for subsequent state mask generation and reward model training.

#### 3.6. Language-Conditioned Reward Model Architecture

The reward model $r_\theta(s | \ell)$ is designed with an inductive bias for sample-efficient conditioning. It uses:
- A pre-trained T5 transformer to encode the input language $\ell$ into an embedding $h_{lang}$.
- Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers to integrate $h_{lang}$ with the input state $s$. The language embedding is mapped through two MLPs to produce scaling ($\gamma$) and shifting ($\beta$) parameters, which modulate the state input: $h_{fused} = \gamma \odot s + \beta$. This allows the instruction to directly influence how reward components are computed, offering a more structured conditioning mechanism than simple concatenation.
- The $h_{fused}$ representation is then passed through a four-layer MLP to output a scalar reward value. The language encoder (T5) is frozen during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Masked IRL was evaluated through simulated and real-robot experiments across three research questions (RQ1-RQ3). The task involved a Franka Emika robot arm performing an object handover, with preferences related to objects (table, laptop, human) and mug orientation.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Efficiency of the Masking Loss

This section investigated the effectiveness of the masking loss in learning human preferences.
- **Experimental Setup:** Simulated environment (PyBullet), 242 distinct preferences based on five semantic features (distance to table, human, laptop, human face, mug orientation). Trajectory datasets generated with varying object configurations and start-goal pairs, each paired with a language instruction describing the ground truth reward.
- **Baselines:**
 1. **Masked IRL (Oracle Mask):** Proposed method with ground-truth masks.
 2. **Masked IRL (LLM Mask):** Proposed method with LLM-generated masks.
 3. **Explicit Mask (Oracle Mask):** Direct zeroing out of irrelevant state dimensions using ground-truth masks.
 4. **Explicit Mask (LLM Mask):** Direct zeroing out using LLM-generated masks.
 5. **LC-RL:** Language-conditioned reward learning baseline (only FiLM conditioning).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average win rate (how often the learned reward correctly predicts preference between two test trajectories) on sparse, medium, and dense reward models.
- **Results:**
 - Masked IRL (both Oracle and LLM Mask variants) consistently matched or outperformed LC-RL across all reward densities and numbers of demonstrations. This indicates that implicitly penalizing sensitivity to irrelevant state elements improves robustness and generalization compared to naive language conditioning.
 - Masked IRL demonstrated improved sample efficiency, achieving strong generalization with as few as five demonstrations per preference. It required up to 4.7 times fewer demonstrations than LC-RL and 33% fewer than Explicit Mask to reach comparable performance.
 - Explicit masking with LLM-generated masks performed poorly, especially with increasing demonstrations, suggesting that hard-masking is sensitive to errors in mask generation. In contrast, Masked IRL remained robust with LLM masks, preventing performance collapse and showing stable gains over LC-RL.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Robustness to Language Ambiguity

This section evaluated the ability to disambiguate underspecified language.
- **Experimental Setup:** Same simulation, but demonstrations were paired with ambiguous instructions (referent-omitted like "Stay close" or expression-omitted like "Table"). Evaluated on sparse rewards where only a single feature was active.
- **Baselines:**
 1. **Masked IRL (DI):** Proposed method with LLM-disambiguated instructions.
 2. **Masked IRL (AI):** Proposed method with original ambiguous instructions.
 3. **Explicit Mask (DI/AI):** Similar to above, but with explicit masking.
 4. **LC-RL (DI/AI):** Similar to above, but with LC-RL.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average win rate, LLM disambiguation instruction accuracy, and mask-based Precision, Recall, and F1 scores.
- **Results:**
 - The LLM disambiguation step achieved an average instruction accuracy of 76.4%.
 - Disambiguated instructions (DI) improved state mask prediction performance significantly, with a 16.9% higher F1 score compared to ambiguous instructions (AI). Clear instructions yielded the highest F1 score.
 - On test preferences, using disambiguated language improved performance for all methods (LC-RL, Explicit Mask, Masked IRL).
 - Masked IRL trained with disambiguated instructions showed the highest generalization performance, achieving a 21.4% higher average win rate than LC-RL trained with ambiguous instructions. This highlights the importance of both language disambiguation and the masking approach for robust learning.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Evaluation in the Real World

This section validated the approach on a physical robot.
- **Experimental Setup:** Real Franka Panda robot performing object handover. 1200 demonstrations collected across 50 preferences by two experts.
- **Baselines:** Same as RQ1: LC-RL, {Explicit Masking, Implicit Masking} × {Oracle Mask, LLM Mask}.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Average win rate, average reward variance (after perturbing irrelevant state dimensions), and average regret of optimized trajectories (difference between ground truth reward of chosen trajectory and actual optimal trajectory).
- **Results:**
 - Masked IRL achieved higher average win rates and significantly lower reward variance compared to all baselines on zero-shot generalization to the real robot. This suggests that language-guided implicit masking makes learned rewards more robust to distributional shifts in the real world.
 - Masked IRL demonstrated lower average reward regret (59.4% lower than LC-RL with Oracle masks, 44.8% lower with LLM masks), indicating that it learns reward functions that lead to better optimized trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Masked IRL, a framework that leverages Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance reward learning from demonstrations and natural language instructions. The work addresses critical limitations in current robot learning paradigms, primarily the ambiguity inherent in demonstrations and language, and the high data requirements for robust reward function acquisition.

The key contributions and their potential impact include:

1. **Improved Sample Efficiency:** By introducing language-guided state relevance masks and a novel masking loss, Masked IRL enables robots to learn generalizable reward functions with significantly less data—up to 4.7 times fewer demonstrations than prior language-conditioned IRL methods. This reduction in data collection burden has a substantial impact on the practicality of deploying robots in real-world settings, where human demonstrations are costly and time-consuming to obtain. It facilitates faster adaptation of robots to new tasks and user preferences.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The masking loss effectively enforces invariance to irrelevant state components, preventing reward models from overfitting to spurious correlations. This leads to more robust and generalizable reward functions that perform better on unseen preferences and novel object configurations, as demonstrated in both simulation and real-robot experiments. The framework's ability to maintain performance even with imperfect, LLM-generated masks further underscores its robustness.

3. **Effective Ambiguity Resolution in Language:** The development of an LLM-based disambiguation mechanism allows the system to clarify underspecified instructions by jointly reasoning about language and demonstrations. This is significant because real-world instructions are frequently vague or context-dependent. By grounding ambiguous commands in behavioral context, Masked IRL makes language a more reliable and actionable interface for human-robot interaction, enabling robots to interpret user intent accurately even when instructions are imprecise.

4. **Structured LLM Integration:** The work demonstrates a structured approach to integrating LLMs beyond simple text conditioning. By using LLMs to infer state relevance masks and resolve ambiguity, the framework leverages the common-sense knowledge and reasoning capabilities of these models to provide structured supervision to the reward learning process. This represents a step towards more intelligent and interpretable language-guided robot learning.

5. **Applicability to Real-World Robotics:** The validation of Masked IRL on a real Franka Panda robot, demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization, lower reward variance, and reduced regret, indicates its potential for direct transfer to physical systems. This is crucial for bridging the simulation-to-real gap and accelerating the deployment of adaptable robotic assistants.

While the framework shows significant advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations such as potential inaccuracies in LLM-generated masks and evaluation on relatively constrained tasks. Future work could focus on refining mask accuracy, extending to more complex environments, and integrating uncertainty estimation. Nevertheless, Masked IRL provides a robust foundation for future research in human-robot interaction, particularly for applications requiring efficient learning of diverse user preferences and adaptable robot behaviors. It positions language as a powerful tool for not only specifying tasks but also for structuring the learning process itself, moving towards more intuitive and capable robotic systems.
