# Masked IRL: LLM-Guided Reward Disambiguation from Demonstrations and Language

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT CSAIL developed Masked IRL, a framework leveraging large language models to guide inverse reinforcement learning. This method enhances sample efficiency by up to 4.7 times, improves robustness to ambiguous language by 21.4% in win rate, and generalizes effectively in learning diverse robot task preferences from limited demonstrations.

## Problem

- Traditional Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods are sample-inefficient and often overfit to irrelevant state components due to the ill-posed nature of inferring reward functions from limited demonstrations.
- Existing language-conditioned reward learning approaches do not fully exploit natural language's potential to resolve ambiguity or guide feature learning, often using it only as a simple conditioning signal.
- Real-world human instructions for robots are frequently ambiguous or underspecified, limiting their reliability as standalone signals for precise task intent.

## Method

- **Masked IRL** integrates demonstrations and natural language instructions, utilizing LLMs to ==disambiguate ambiguous commands== by jointly reasoning about language and demonstrations.
- LLMs generate ==binary state-relevance masks== for each instruction-demonstration pair, indicating which state components are pertinent to the task.
- A novel ==implicit masking loss== is introduced, which penalizes changes in the reward function output when irrelevant state components are perturbed, enforcing invariance to irrelevant dimensions during training.

## Results

- **Masked IRL** matched or outperformed baseline language-conditioned IRL, requiring up to **4.7x** fewer demonstrations to achieve comparable generalization performance.
- LLM-based language disambiguation improved state mask prediction F1 scores by **16.9%** and led to a **21.4%** higher average win rate on test preferences when handling ambiguous instructions.
- On a real Franka Panda robot, **Masked IRL** achieved higher average win rates, significantly lower reward variance, and **44.8%** to **59.4%** lower average reward regret in zero-shot generalization compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging LLMs to infer state-relevance masks and disambiguate instructions provides structured supervision that significantly improves reward learning efficiency and robustness beyond simple language conditioning.
> - An implicit masking loss, which penalizes sensitivity to irrelevant state components, is more robust to inaccuracies in LLM-generated masks compared to direct explicit masking.
> - Combining both language disambiguation and the masking loss allows the framework to effectively learn generalizable reward functions from limited, potentially ambiguous human input.
