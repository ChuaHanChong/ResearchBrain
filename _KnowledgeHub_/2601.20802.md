# Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO), an on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages rich, tokenized environment feedback to improve Large Language Model (LLM) performance. This method enables LLMs to learn from their own explained mistakes, leading to significantly enhanced sample efficiency and higher final accuracy on complex reasoning and coding tasks.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs primarily rely on sparse, scalar outcome rewards, creating a severe credit-assignment bottleneck.
- LLMs struggle to learn effectively from limited information about *why* an attempt failed, leading to slow learning or stalled progress, especially for long and complex generations.
- Rich textual feedback (e.g., runtime errors, judge evaluations) is often available in verifiable environments but is largely underutilized by existing RL methods.

## Method

- ==Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO)==: An on-policy RL algorithm formalizing Reinforcement Learning with Rich Feedback (RLRF) to leverage detailed, tokenized environment feedback.
- ==Self-Teacher Mechanism==: Uses the current LLM policy as a 'self-teacher' that, when conditioned on rich feedback from its own previous attempt, retrospectively re-evaluates the log-probabilities of its original generation.
- ==Logit-Level Distillation Loss==: Minimizes a KL-divergence between the student's next-token distribution and the feedback-informed self-teacher's distribution, providing dense, token-level credit assignment.

## Results

- SDPO consistently outperforms GRPO baselines in scientific reasoning and competitive programming tasks, achieving higher final accuracy (e.g., **68.8%** vs. **64.1%** aggregate on SciKnowEval, **48.8%** vs. **41.2%** on LCBv6) and significantly improved sample efficiency (e.g., **1**0x** speedup** on Chemistry, **4x** fewer generations on LCBv6).
- The method leads to more concise and efficient reasoning in LLM generations, reducing response lengths by over **3x** on average while maintaining or improving accuracy, indicating improved reasoning efficiency.
- SDPO demonstrates effective bootstrapping, with the self-teacher improving over time and the student's performance surpassing initial capabilities, and efficiently accelerates solution discovery during test-time on very hard binary-reward problems, solving some questions uniquely.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rich, tokenized environment feedback provides a dense learning signal that effectively overcomes the credit-assignment bottleneck of sparse scalar rewards in LLM fine-tuning.
> - An LLM can effectively serve as its own 'self-teacher' by leveraging its in-context learning capabilities to infer corrections to past generations when presented with specific error feedback.
> - Distilling these feedback-informed insights directly into the policy weights provides a more permanent and efficient learning mechanism than merely appending feedback to context for iterative self-correction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid progress in Large Language Models (LLMs) has been significantly augmented by post-training with Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly in domains requiring verifiable outcomes like code generation and mathematical reasoning. However, current methods, primarily operating under the paradigm of **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**, face a critical challenge: the "credit-assignment bottleneck." In RLVR, models typically receive only a sparse, scalar reward (e.g., binary pass/fail) at the end of a long generation attempt. This limited feedback makes it difficult for the model to understand *what went wrong* and *where* in its lengthy output the mistake occurred, hindering efficient learning.

The paper argues that this limitation is not inherent to RL but rather to the **information bottleneck** imposed by scalar rewards. Many verifiable environments, especially coding platforms, inherently provide **rich textual feedback** (e.g., runtime errors, specific failing unit tests, LLM judge evaluations) that explain the nature of a failure. This work formalizes a new setting: **Reinforcement Learning with Rich Feedback (RLRF)**, directly addressing this underutilized source of information.

SDPO distinguishes itself from existing approaches in several ways:
- **Against standard RLVR (e.g., GRPO):** SDPO directly tackles the sparse credit assignment problem by converting rich feedback into dense, token-level learning signals, something traditional RLVR methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) struggle with, especially when all rollouts receive zero reward.
- **Against teacher-student distillation:** While distillation from a strong external teacher offers dense supervision, such teachers are often unavailable in online learning scenarios where the goal is to push beyond existing model capabilities. SDPO cleverly sidesteps this by creating a "self-teacher" from the *current* model itself.
- **Leveraging in-context learning:** The work builds on the observation that LLMs already excel at in-context learning, often able to identify errors and suggest corrections when provided with feedback. SDPO operationalizes this inherent capability for self-correction.
- **Online and On-Policy:** Unlike many distillation methods that are off-policy, SDPO is an on-policy algorithm, which is known to be crucial for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in continual learning scenarios.

By formalizing RLRF and introducing SDPO, this research aims to unlock the full potential of RL for LLM post-training by enabling finer-grained, more informative learning signals directly from the environment, without external models or human labeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Formalize Reinforcement Learning with Rich Feedback (RLRF):** Introduce a new paradigm for RL with LLMs that explicitly leverages detailed, tokenized feedback from environments, moving beyond the limitations of scalar rewards in traditional RLVR settings.
2. **Develop Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO):** Propose a novel on-policy algorithm that can effectively utilize this rich feedback. The core idea is to employ the current LLM itself as a "self-teacher," which, when conditioned on feedback, can retrospectively provide dense, logit-level supervision for its own past generations.
3. **Overcome the Credit-Assignment Bottleneck:** Address the fundamental challenge in current RLVR methods where sparse scalar rewards make it difficult to attribute credit or blame to specific parts of a generated sequence. SDPO aims to convert the rich textual feedback into a dense learning signal without requiring any external teacher or explicit reward model.
4. **Improve Sample Efficiency and Final Accuracy:** Demonstrate that by providing dense credit assignment, SDPO can significantly enhance the sample efficiency and ultimate performance of LLMs in verifiable domains like scientific reasoning, tool use, and competitive programming.
5. **Enable Learning on Difficult, Sparse-Reward Tasks:** Show that SDPO can accelerate the discovery of solutions for hard tasks where base models struggle and traditional RLVR methods offer no signal until a successful attempt is found. This includes test-time adaptation for individual problems.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the observation that while RL has improved LLM capabilities, the bottleneck of scalar rewards remains. Many real-world environments already provide verbose, explanatory feedback (e.g., compiler errors, test suite outputs). The hypothesis is that LLMs, with their strong in-context learning abilities, can be trained to interpret and learn from this feedback to self-correct more effectively, thereby enhancing their reasoning and problem-solving skills beyond what is achievable with sparse rewards alone.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology is **Self-Distillation Policy Optimization (SDPO)**, an on-policy algorithm designed to leverage rich feedback from verifiable environments.

#### 3.1. The Self-Teacher Concept

SDPO introduces the concept of a "self-teacher," which is simply the current policy, $\pi_\theta$, re-prompted with the original question ($x$) and the rich environment feedback ($f$). Mathematically, this self-teacher is denoted as $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, f)$. The key insight is that the same model, when given context about its failure (via $f$), can often identify where and why its initial attempt ($y$) was incorrect. Rather than sampling a new response, the self-teacher re-evaluates the log-probabilities of the *original attempt* after seeing the feedback. This mechanism provides dense, logit-level credit assignment without requiring an external, stronger teacher model.

#### 3.2. SDPO Algorithm and Loss Function

The SDPO algorithm proceeds iteratively (Algorithm 1):
1. **Sample Rollouts:** The current student policy $\pi_\theta$ generates responses (rollouts) $y \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | x)$ for a given question $x$.
2. **Obtain Rich Feedback:** The environment provides rich feedback $f$ for each generated response (e.g., runtime errors, failing unit tests, or even a successful solution from another attempt in the batch if available).
3. **Self-Distillation Loss:** SDPO minimizes a logit-level distillation loss that encourages the student policy's next-token distribution to match that of the feedback-conditioned self-teacher. The loss function is a KL-divergence:
 $L_{\text{SDPO}}(\theta) := \sum_t \text{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, y_{<t}) \parallel \text{stopgrad}(\pi_\theta(\cdot | x, f, y_{<t})))$
 Here, $\text{stopgrad}$ prevents gradients from flowing through the teacher, ensuring it acts as a fixed target for the current step. The feedback $f$ can incorporate environment output and/or successful previous rollouts.

#### 3.3. Dense Credit Assignment and Advantages

The gradient of $L_{\text{SDPO}}$ reveals that SDPO is a policy gradient algorithm where the advantages are estimated using the self-teacher. Specifically, the advantages for a token $\hat{y}_t$ at position $t$ are given by $\log \frac{\pi_\theta(\hat{y}_t | x, y_{<t})}{\pi_\theta(\hat{y}_t | x, f, y_{<t})}$. This provides:
- **Logit-level Advantages:** Credit is assigned to *each possible next token* in the vocabulary, not just the token that was actually generated.
- **Dense Signal:** Unlike GRPO, which assigns a constant scalar advantage (often zero) to all tokens in a rollout, SDPO's advantages are specific to each token and its context, allowing for precise identification of mistakes and reinforcement of correct choices.

#### 3.4. Practical Implementations and Stability

- **Computational Efficiency:** SDPO incurs minimal overhead compared to GRPO, primarily from re-computing log-probabilities with the self-teacher's context, which is faster than full generation.
- **Memory Optimization:** To avoid holding full logits in memory, SDPO uses "top-K distillation," approximating the KL-divergence over only the $K$ most likely tokens (e.g., $K=100$), plus a tail term for the remaining probabilities.
- **Teacher Regularization:** To prevent training instability caused by a continuously updating teacher, SDPO employs a regularized self-teacher, either via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student's parameters or an explicit trust-region constraint. This ensures the teacher doesn't diverge too quickly from a more stable state.
- **Loss Formulation:** The symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence is used for the distillation loss to improve training stability, building on prior work in on-policy distillation.
- **Connection to RLVR:** The formulation allows SDPO to be a drop-in replacement for advantage estimation in standard RLVR pipelines, making it easy to integrate into existing frameworks like those using PPO-style clipped importance sampling for off-policy data.

#### 3.5. Handling Scalar-Reward Environments

Even in standard RLVR environments (without explicit rich feedback), SDPO can synthesize feedback. It treats successful attempts within a batch as "implicit feedback" for failed attempts on the same question. By comparing a failed attempt with a correct solution (even one generated by the same model in the same batch), the self-teacher can still identify errors and provide dense credit assignment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical results across three distinct settings, demonstrating SDPO's advantages over strong baselines.

#### 4.1. Learning Without Rich Environment Feedback (Standard RLVR Setting)

- **Tasks:** Scientific Q&A (Chemistry, Physics, Biology, Materials science) from SciKnowEval and Tool Use (ToolAlpaca).
- **Models:** Qwen3-8B and Olmo3-7B-Instruct.
- **Performance:** SDPO consistently outperforms an improved GRPO baseline. For instance, on aggregate, SDPO achieves 68.8% accuracy versus GRPO's 64.1% with Qwen3-8B. On the Chemistry task with Olmo3-7B-Instruct, SDPO achieves GRPO's 5-hour accuracy in just 30 minutes, representing a **10x speedup** in wall-clock training time and significantly higher final accuracy (e.g., 76.8% vs. 54.3% at 5 hours).
- **Concise Reasoning:** A striking finding is that SDPO produces substantially shorter responses (over 3x shorter on average, up to 7x shorter in some cases) while achieving higher accuracy. Qualitatively, GRPO often generates verbose, superficial reasoning with filler phrases and circular logic, whereas SDPO's responses are more concise and efficient. This suggests SDPO improves the *quality* of reasoning rather than just the length.

#### 4.2. Learning With Rich Environment Feedback (Competitive Programming)

- **Task:** Competitive programming problems from LiveCodeBench v6, which provides LeetCode-style rich feedback (runtime errors, failed unit tests).
- **Model:** Qwen3-8B.
- **Performance:** SDPO significantly outperforms GRPO, achieving a higher final accuracy (48.8% vs. 41.2%) and reaching GRPO's final accuracy in **4x fewer generations**. SDPO also surpasses top instruct models like Claude Sonnet 4 (40.5%) and Claude Opus 4 (39.7%) on this benchmark. SDPO shows particular gains on medium and hard questions.
- **Scaling with Model Size:** SDPO's performance gains over GRPO *increase with model scale*. This suggests that the "self-teaching" ability, which relies on in-context learning, is an emergent property that becomes more effective in stronger base models.
- **Dense Credit Assignment Importance:** Ablation studies show that logit-level dense credit assignment is crucial for SDPO's performance. While even sequence-level SDPO (which aggregates advantages to a single scalar per sequence, similar to GRPO but using rich feedback) outperforms GRPO, the full logit-level detail provides the most significant boost. This highlights the value of both rich feedback and fine-grained credit assignment.
- **Self-Teacher Improvement:** The self-teacher's accuracy improves significantly during training, eventually allowing the student model to surpass the initial teacher's performance. This demonstrates a true bootstrapping mechanism where the model continuously learns from an increasingly capable version of itself.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting:** SDPO, as an on-policy method, exhibits better performance-forgetting tradeoffs than GRPO and off-policy supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baselines on holdout tasks (IFEval, ArenaHard-v2, MMLU-Pro), mitigating degradation of prior capabilities.
- **Combining with GRPO:** A hybrid approach (SDPO+GRPO) can be more robust for weaker models (e.g., Qwen3-0.6B), but for stronger models (e.g., Qwen3-8B), the simpler SDPO approach is superior, suggesting GRPO's scalar signal can be "actively harmful" when dense feedback is reliable.
- **Informativeness of Feedback:** Environment output and sample solutions are found to be complementary and highly informative. Including the student's original attempt in the self-teacher's prompt, however, can reduce exploration and diversity.

#### 4.3. Solving Hard Questions via Test-Time Self-Distillation

- **Setting:** Focus on extremely difficult binary-reward LCBv6 questions (pass@64 < 0.5, and even pass@64 < 0.03 for "very hard" tasks) where initial discovery is rare.
- **Challenge:** Standard RLVR offers no learning signal until the first success is found.
- **Performance:** SDPO significantly accelerates discovery compared to best-of-k sampling (i.i.d. attempts) and multi-turn sampling (re-prompting the model with past feedback in-context).
 - On "very hard" tasks, SDPO achieves a 53.2% discovery probability within 2750 attempts, compared to 41.5% for best-of-k and 35.6% for multi-turn. SDPO requires approximately **3x fewer generations** to reach the same discovery probability.
 - SDPO uniquely solves questions that remain unsolveable by baselines within the budget.
- **Mechanism:** SDPO compresses the context (past attempts and feedback) into the model weights, iteratively refining the policy, effectively simulating long-context reasoning while bypassing the memory limits of transformer context windows. Despite the initial self-teacher having near-zero accuracy on these hard problems, SDPO's credit assignment is effective enough to enable iterative refinement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Reinforcement Learning via Self-Distillation" paper introduces a paradigm shift in how LLMs can learn from verifiable environments, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Overcoming a Fundamental Bottleneck:** By formalizing Reinforcement Learning with Rich Feedback (RLRF) and proposing SDPO, the paper directly addresses the long-standing credit-assignment bottleneck in LLM-based RL. This is a critical advancement for enabling more efficient and effective learning, especially in domains like code generation, where detailed error messages are readily available but often underutilized by current RL methods.

2. **No External Teacher or Reward Model Required:** SDPO's most impactful feature is its ability to generate dense learning signals without relying on a stronger external teacher model or a separately trained reward model. This significantly reduces the overhead, cost, and complexity of RL training, making the approach broadly applicable. It democratizes access to dense supervision by leveraging the LLM's intrinsic in-context learning capabilities for self-correction.

3. **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Quality:** The empirical results consistently demonstrate SDPO's superior sample efficiency, faster convergence, and higher final accuracy compared to strong RLVR baselines like GRPO. The qualitative finding that SDPO fosters more concise and less superficial reasoning is particularly important, suggesting that dense credit assignment not only accelerates learning but also improves the *quality* of the model's internal reasoning process.

4. **Scalability and Emergent Abilities:** The observation that SDPO's benefits increase with model size implies that it is a powerful technique for pushing the capabilities of frontier LLMs. As models become better in-context learners, their capacity for self-teaching through rich feedback grows, pointing towards a symbiotic relationship between model scale and SDPO's effectiveness.

5. **Breakthrough for Sparse-Reward Discovery Tasks:** SDPO's ability to accelerate the discovery of solutions for hard, binary-reward questions at test-time is a notable breakthrough. In settings where traditional RLVR methods fail to provide any signal until a solution is found, SDPO can continuously refine the policy based on rich feedback, effectively "learning on the job" and pushing the model beyond its initial capabilities much faster than repeated sampling or multi-turn prompting. This has profound implications for solving challenging problems in areas like scientific discovery or complex software development.

6. **Human-like Learning Mechanisms:** The mechanism of self-distillation via retrospection and rich feedback mirrors how humans often learn—by analyzing specific mistakes and adjusting future behavior based on detailed explanations rather than just a simple pass/fail judgment. This cognitive alignment could lead to more interpretable and robust AI systems.

**Limitations identified by the authors include:**
- Dependence on the base model's in-context learning ability, making it less effective for very weak models.
- Reliance on the quality of environment feedback; uninformative or misleading feedback could hinder learning.
- A small computational overhead for re-computing log-probabilities, though often negligible.

**Future work directions** are exciting and include:
- Applying SDPO to long-horizon and agentic settings.
- Scaling SDPO to large multi-task RL training runs with frontier models.
- Exploring its applicability in scenarios beyond verifiable rewards, such as improving alignment in open-ended text generation or continuous-reward tasks.
- Systematically studying the behavioral differences in reasoning induced by SDPO and optimizing prompting strategies.

In conclusion, SDPO represents a significant step forward in RL for LLMs by leveraging rich environmental feedback and the models' inherent in-context learning abilities for robust, efficient, and scalable self-improvement.
