# Agentic Reasoning for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey details the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model agents, reframing LLMs as autonomous entities capable of planning, acting, and learning through interaction. It introduces a structured taxonomy for agentic reasoning, encompassing foundational capabilities, self-evolving adaptation, and collective intelligence, while also reviewing diverse applications and identifying critical open challenges.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) traditionally operate as passive sequence generators, struggling with dynamic, open-ended environments despite strong performance in static, closed-world reasoning tasks.
- The absence of intrinsic mechanisms for LLMs to continuously act, learn, and improve over time limits their applicability in real-world interactive scenarios.
- The rapidly growing research landscape of LLM agents lacked a systematic, unified framework to categorize and understand diverse approaches to agentic reasoning.

## Method

- A paradigm of ==Agentic Reasoning== is introduced, formally defining LLMs as autonomous agents capable of ==planning==, ==acting==, and ==learning== through continuous interaction with an environment.
- A ==three-dimensional taxonomy== is established, systematically organizing existing research into ==foundational agentic reasoning==, ==self-evolving agentic reasoning==, and ==collective multi-agent reasoning==.
- The framework consistently analyzes mechanisms under two complementary optimization settings: ==in-context reasoning== (inference-time interaction) and ==post-training reasoning== (parameter-updating learning).

## Results

- A coherent conceptual framework for agentic reasoning is established, defining its core components and their interconnections.
- Existing research on single-agent capabilities (planning, tool use, search), self-evolution (feedback, memory), and multi-agent coordination is systematically mapped and categorized.
- A comprehensive overview of real-world applications and benchmarks is provided, illustrating the practical instantiation and evaluation of agentic reasoning frameworks.
- A clear agenda of future research challenges is articulated, spanning personalization, long-horizon interaction, world modeling, and the governance of agentic systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic reasoning fundamentally reframes LLMs from static inference machines to interactive, adaptive entities that bridge internal thought with external action.
> - The acquisition and refinement of agent capabilities can be systematically understood by distinguishing between inference-time workflow orchestration ("in-context reasoning") and parameter-updating learning ("post-training reasoning").
> - Effective LLM agents rely on a set of core mechanisms—planning, tool use, search, feedback, and memory—that can evolve through continuous interaction and learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions "Agentic Reasoning" as a crucial paradigm shift in the field of Large Language Models (LLMs). It moves beyond the traditional view of LLMs as passive sequence generators, reframing them as **autonomous agents capable of planning, acting, and learning through continual interaction with their environment.** This addresses a significant limitation of conventional LLMs: their struggle in open-ended and dynamic environments despite strong reasoning capabilities in closed-world, static settings (e.g., standard math or code benchmarks).

**Evolution from Traditional LLM Reasoning:**
The survey explicitly contrasts traditional LLM reasoning, characterized by:
- **Passive operation**: Generating responses without affecting the environment.
- **Static input**: Relying on fixed prompts and predefined contexts.
- **Single-pass computation**: Reasoning unfolds in one forward pass.
- **Internal compute**: Limited to the model's internal parameters.
- **Context window**: Short-term memory confined to the prompt.
- **Offline pretraining**: Knowledge is fixed after training.
- **Prompt-based goals**: Reacting to immediate instructions.

In contrast, agentic reasoning emphasizes **interactive, multi-step, feedback-driven processes**, utilizing external memory, continual improvement, and explicit goal-oriented planning. This evolution mirrors a fundamental shift from static inference to sequential decision-making under uncertainty, akin to the principles of classical AI agents.

**Positioning Relative to Existing Surveys:**
The authors carefully delineate their contribution from two other categories of existing surveys:
1. **LLM Reasoning Surveys**: These typically focus on eliciting and enhancing reasoning *within* a model's internal computation (e.g., Chain-of-Thought prompting, reinforcement post-training). This paper expands beyond internal reasoning to encompass dynamic interaction and adaptation during deployment.
2. **AI Agent Surveys**: These often explore LLM-based agents from an architectural or system perspective (e.g., reinforcement learning for decision-making, planning, and tool-use modules). This survey complements these by centering on the *reasoning process itself* that these architectures enable, treating reasoning as the "unifying mechanism that links single-agent reinforcement, multi-agent coordination, and self-evolving intelligence."

By focusing on "reasoning as interaction," the survey provides a unique, reasoning-centric lens on intelligent agency, charting how foundational mechanisms, post-training adaptation, and long-term self-evolution together constitute the basis of increasingly interactive, adaptive, and continually improving AI. This work is highly relevant in a research landscape where the ambition for AI is moving rapidly towards autonomous, general-purpose agents capable of complex, real-world problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the observed limitations of current Large Language Models (LLMs) in real-world, dynamic, and open-ended environments. While LLMs excel at reasoning in well-defined, "closed-world" settings—demonstrated through their performance on benchmarks in mathematics and code—they often falter when confronted with evolving information, novel situations, or tasks requiring continuous interaction and adaptation. Traditional LLMs operate as passive sequence predictors, lacking inherent mechanisms to act, learn, or improve over time.

Against this backdrop, the paper identifies the emergence of "agentic reasoning" as a transformative paradigm. The core motivation is to understand and systematize this shift, recognizing that reframing LLMs as autonomous agents capable of planning, acting, and learning through interaction bridges the gap between static "thought" and dynamic "action."

The key objectives of this survey, as explicitly outlined in its contributions, are:

1. **Conceptual Framing**: To formalize and define the paradigm of Agentic Reasoning. This involves articulating a structured understanding that spans foundational capabilities, self-evolving adaptation, and collective multi-agent intelligence.
2. **Systematic Review**: To conduct a comprehensive analysis of existing research on single-agent, adaptive, and multi-agent systems. A critical aspect of this objective is to emphasize reasoning-centered workflow orchestration, distinguishing between "in-context reasoning" (inference-time interaction without parameter updates) and "post-training reasoning" (optimizing behaviors through parameter updates like reinforcement learning or fine-tuning).
3. **Applications and Evaluation Contextualization**: To review and contextualize real-world applications and benchmarks where agentic reasoning frameworks are instantiated and evaluated. This objective aims to illustrate how different reasoning mechanisms are put into practice across diverse domains such as science, robotics, healthcare, autonomous research, and mathematics/code.
4. **Future Agenda Outline**: To identify and discuss pressing open challenges and promising future directions in agentic reasoning. This forward-looking objective aims to guide subsequent research by highlighting areas like personalization, long-horizon interaction, world modeling, scalable multi-agent training, and the governance of real-world agent deployment.

In essence, the overarching goal of the paper is to provide a unified, actionable roadmap for designing and implementing agentic systems, offering both a conceptual framework and practical guidance for researchers and practitioners navigating this rapidly expanding field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology primarily involves **systematic review, synthesis, and conceptual organization** of a vast and rapidly growing body of literature on LLM-based agents. The authors adopt a structured, multi-dimensional framework to categorize and analyze existing work, moving beyond simple chronological or purely architectural reviews.

The core methodological approach is built around a **three-dimensional taxonomy of environmental dynamics**, which serves as the organizing principle for agentic reasoning:

1. **Foundational Agentic Reasoning**: This dimension focuses on the core capabilities required for a single, autonomous agent operating in stable (though potentially complex) environments. It systematically reviews literature concerning:
 - **Planning**: How agents decompose goals, sequence decisions, and formulate strategies. This includes in-context planning (workflow design, tree search, process formalization, decomposition, external aid) and post-training planning (reward design, optimal control).
 - **Tool Use**: How agents augment their intrinsic capabilities by intelligently invoking external modules. This covers in-context tool integration, post-training tool integration (SFT, RL), and orchestration-based tool integration (multi-tool coordination).
 - **Agentic Search**: How agents dynamically acquire and integrate information from external sources. This includes in-context search (interleaving reasoning and search, structure-enhanced search) and post-training search (SFT-based, RL-based).

2. **Self-Evolving Agentic Reasoning**: This dimension examines how agents refine their foundational capabilities through cumulative experience and continuous learning. It categorizes mechanisms for:
 - **Agentic Feedback**: How models iteratively refine reasoning and actions through reflective feedback (self-critique), parametric adaptation (updating model parameters), and validator-driven feedback (outcome-based retry).
 - **Agentic Memory**: How agents store, organize, and synthesize past interactions to accumulate knowledge. This covers flat memory (factual, experience), structured memory (graph-based, multimodal), and post-training memory control (learning-based management).
 - **Evolving Foundational Capabilities**: How planning, tool-use, and search capabilities themselves evolve over time through task generation, strategy refinement, tool creation, and dynamic knowledge synthesis.

3. **Collective Multi-Agent Reasoning**: This dimension extends intelligence to collaborative scenarios where multiple agents coordinate. It explores:
 - **Role Taxonomy**: How agents specialize into generic (leader, worker, critic, memory keeper, communication facilitator) and domain-specific roles (e.g., in software engineering, finance, healthcare).
 - **Collaboration and Division of Labor**: How agents interact, exchange reasoning traces, resolve conflicts, and share information. This includes in-context collaboration (manually crafted vs. LLM-driven pipelines, agent routing, Theory-of-Mind) and post-training collaboration (prompt and topology optimization, policy-based generation).
 - **Multi-Agent Evolution**: How collective intelligence adapts over time, distinguishing intra-test-time (within-episode) and inter-test-time (across-episode) evolution, and focusing on multi-agent memory management.

Across all three dimensions, the survey consistently analyzes two complementary **optimization settings**:
- **In-context Reasoning**: Mechanisms that scale inference-time interaction through structured orchestration and adaptive workflow design *without modifying model parameters*.
- **Post-training Reasoning**: Mechanisms that optimize behaviors and internalize strategies through techniques like reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) *by updating model parameters*.

The survey further contextualizes this roadmap by reviewing **real-world applications** (math, coding, science, robotics, healthcare, web exploration) and **benchmarks** (core mechanism tests, application-specific evaluations), illustrating how different agentic reasoning mechanisms are instantiated and assessed across domains. The paper concludes by outlining **open challenges and future directions**, providing a forward-looking perspective.

In essence, the methodology is a structured decomposition and synthesis, aiming to provide a coherent and comprehensive understanding of agentic reasoning by classifying existing research along meaningful conceptual axes and highlighting their interconnections. The paper also includes a formalization of agentic reasoning using a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) to unify capabilities under a control-theoretic framework, differentiating internal thought from external action.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, its "main findings and results" are not empirical outcomes but rather the **structured synthesis, conceptual framework, and identification of key trends and open problems** within the field of agentic reasoning for LLMs. The paper successfully delivers on its objectives, providing a comprehensive and insightful roadmap.

The core results are presented through its multi-dimensional taxonomy:

1. **Formalized Paradigm of Agentic Reasoning**: The survey successfully formalizes agentic reasoning as the central mechanism for intelligent agents, emphasizing its role in bridging thought and action through continual interaction. This conceptual clarity is a foundational contribution, moving beyond vague notions of "AI agents" to a concrete definition anchored in observable behaviors and underlying mechanisms (planning, acting, learning). The distinction between `π_reason` (internal thought) and `π_exec` (external action) provides a clear theoretical lens.

2. **Systematic Categorization of Agentic Capabilities**:
 - **Foundational Capabilities**: The survey rigorously categorizes core single-agent abilities:
 - **Planning** is shown to manifest in diverse forms, from workflow design and tree search to formal process encoding and decomposition, often augmented by external tools. The distinction between in-context prompting for dynamic plan generation and post-training for internalizing planning policies is a recurring theme.
 - **Tool Use** is identified as a crucial mechanism for extending LLM capabilities, with methods ranging from in-context prompting (e.g., ReAct, ChatCoT) to parameter-updating techniques (e.g., Toolformer, ToolLLM via SFT; SWE-RL, ToolRL via RL) and complex orchestration frameworks (e.g., HuggingGPT, OctoTools).
 - **Agentic Search** is highlighted as more than simple retrieval; it's a dynamic, reasoning-driven process for information acquisition. Techniques include interleaving reasoning with search calls (ReAct, Self-RAG), fine-tuning models to learn search behaviors (Toolformer, Search-R1), and using structured knowledge sources like graphs.
 - **Self-Evolving Adaptation**: The paper reveals the mechanisms through which agents achieve continuous improvement:
 - **Agentic Feedback** is classified into reflective (self-critique, rationale refinement), parametric (trajectory-level SFT, preference alignment), and validator-driven (retry-based on external signals). This spectrum illustrates how models learn from errors and successes.
 - **Agentic Memory** evolves from passive context buffers to dynamic, reasoning-centric architectures. The survey details flat memory for factual/experiential recall, structured memory (graphs, multimodal), and post-training control where agents learn to manage memory actively (MemAgent, Memory-R1).
 - **Evolving Foundational Capabilities** demonstrates that even planning, tool use, and search are not static but can self-improve over time, for instance, through task generation, tool creation, and dynamic knowledge synthesis.
 - **Collective Multi-Agent Intelligence**: The survey elucidates how intelligence scales through collaboration:
 - **Role Taxonomy** highlights that multi-agent systems benefit from specialized roles (e.g., Leader, Worker, Critic, domain-specific roles in software, finance, healthcare), mirroring human organizations.
 - **Collaboration and Division of Labor** are implemented via both in-context (manually crafted or LLM-driven pipelines, explicit routing, Theory-of-Mind augmentation) and post-training (prompt optimization, graph-based or policy-based topology optimization) strategies.
 - **Multi-Agent Evolution** moves from single-agent adaptation to collective co-evolution, with memory management (hierarchical, decentralized) and specific training paradigms (e.g., MARFT, Stronger-MAS) playing crucial roles.

3. **Comprehensive Review of Applications and Benchmarks**: The survey successfully contextualizes these mechanisms by reviewing a broad range of applications (math, coding, science, robotics, healthcare, web exploration) and detailing how agentic reasoning principles are applied in each. It also critically reviews existing benchmarks, categorizing them by the core mechanisms they evaluate (tool use, search, memory/planning, multi-agent systems) and by specific application domains, providing a valuable resource for future evaluation efforts.

4. **Identification of Open Challenges**: The paper provides a clear and actionable agenda for future research by articulating critical open problems:
 - **User-centric Agentic Reasoning and Personalization**: The need for agents to adapt dynamically to individual users over long interactions.
 - **Long-horizon Agentic Reasoning**: Addressing the compounding errors and credit assignment difficulties in extended tasks.
 - **Agentic Reasoning with World Models**: Developing robust internal simulations for better planning and lookahead.
 - **Multi-agent Collaborative Reasoning and Training**: Learning adaptive and robust collaboration policies for large agent populations.
 - **Latent Agentic Reasoning**: Exploring internal, non-explicit reasoning for efficiency while maintaining interpretability and controllability.
 - **Governance of Agentic Reasoning**: Addressing new safety and ethical risks introduced by autonomous, interactive agents in real-world deployments.

In summary, the main "findings" are the **creation of a coherent framework for a burgeoning field, the systematic mapping of current research, and the precise articulation of the challenges ahead**. This synthesis itself is a significant contribution, providing foundational structure and guiding principles for future innovations in agentic AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "Agentic Reasoning for Large Language Models" holds substantial significance and is poised to have a broad impact on the field of artificial intelligence and beyond. Its main contributions lie in providing a much-needed structured understanding of a rapidly evolving and complex domain, offering a clear roadmap for future research and development.

**1. Unifying a Fragmented Field:** The most significant impact of this survey is its success in **unifying a diverse and often fragmented body of research** under the coherent paradigm of "Agentic Reasoning." By systematically categorizing techniques across foundational capabilities, self-evolution, and collective intelligence, and distinguishing between in-context and post-training optimization, the authors provide a common language and framework. This unification helps researchers understand how various seemingly disparate approaches contribute to the larger goal of building truly autonomous and intelligent agents. It facilitates cross-pollination of ideas and prevents redundant efforts by highlighting underlying commonalities.

**2. Guiding Future Research and Development:** The explicit outline of "Open Challenges" serves as an **actionable agenda for the next generation of AI agent research**.
 - **Long-horizon interactions, personalization, and world modeling** are crucial for agents to operate effectively in complex, dynamic real-world environments. Progress in these areas will enable more capable and adaptive AI assistants, intelligent automation systems, and advanced robotics.
 - **Scalable multi-agent training and collaboration** are essential for moving beyond individual agent intelligence to tackle problems requiring distributed cognition, resource allocation, and complex coordination, leading to more robust and resilient AI systems.
 - **Latent agentic reasoning** hints at a future where agents perform complex internal computations efficiently, potentially leading to breakthroughs in computational speed and resource usage, albeit with new challenges for interpretability.

**3. Accelerating Practical Applications and Deployments:** By surveying a wide range of applications (math, coding, science, robotics, healthcare, web exploration), the paper demonstrates the immense practical potential of agentic reasoning. This can inspire:
 - **More reliable and creative AI coding partners** that can autonomously develop, debug, and optimize software.
 - **Accelerated scientific discovery** through AI agents capable of hypothesis generation, experimental design, and autonomous lab execution.
 - **Enhanced robotic autonomy** with agents that can perform complex tasks, adapt to unforeseen circumstances, and collaborate with other robots or humans.
 - **Transformative advancements in healthcare**, enabling intelligent diagnostic assistants, personalized treatment planning, and automated medical research.
 - **More capable and trustworthy web agents** for complex information gathering and task automation.

**4. Emphasizing Trustworthiness and Safety:** The inclusion of "Governance of Agentic Reasoning" as a key open problem underscores the critical importance of safety and ethical considerations. As LLM agents become more autonomous and operate in real-world, high-stakes environments, addressing issues like attribution, auditing, alignment, and preventing unintended consequences becomes paramount. This focus will encourage the development of responsible AI agent systems that are not only powerful but also safe and transparent.

**5. Bridging Academia and Industry:** The diverse affiliations of the authors, combining academic rigor with industry insights, ensure that the survey is highly relevant to both theoretical advancements and practical engineering challenges. This bridging fosters a collaborative ecosystem where theoretical breakthroughs can be rapidly translated into deployable solutions, and real-world problems can inform fundamental research directions.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational document that frames the current state, future directions, and critical challenges of agentic reasoning in LLMs. Its impact will likely be felt in setting research agendas, fostering interdisciplinary collaborations, and accelerating the development of more intelligent, adaptive, and responsible AI agents capable of tackling increasingly complex real-world problems.
