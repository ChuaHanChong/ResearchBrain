# Agentic Reasoning for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey details the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model agents, reframing LLMs as autonomous entities capable of planning, acting, and learning through interaction. It introduces a structured taxonomy for agentic reasoning, encompassing foundational capabilities, self-evolving adaptation, and collective intelligence, while also reviewing diverse applications and identifying critical open challenges.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) traditionally operate as passive sequence generators, struggling with dynamic, open-ended environments despite strong performance in static, closed-world reasoning tasks.
- The absence of intrinsic mechanisms for LLMs to continuously act, learn, and improve over time limits their applicability in real-world interactive scenarios.
- The rapidly growing research landscape of LLM agents lacked a systematic, unified framework to categorize and understand diverse approaches to agentic reasoning.

## Method

- A paradigm of ==Agentic Reasoning== is introduced, formally defining LLMs as autonomous agents capable of ==planning==, ==acting==, and ==learning== through continuous interaction with an environment.
- A ==three-dimensional taxonomy== is established, systematically organizing existing research into ==foundational agentic reasoning==, ==self-evolving agentic reasoning==, and ==collective multi-agent reasoning==.
- The framework consistently analyzes mechanisms under two complementary optimization settings: ==in-context reasoning== (inference-time interaction) and ==post-training reasoning== (parameter-updating learning).

## Results

- A coherent conceptual framework for agentic reasoning is established, defining its core components and their interconnections.
- Existing research on single-agent capabilities (planning, tool use, search), self-evolution (feedback, memory), and multi-agent coordination is systematically mapped and categorized.
- A comprehensive overview of real-world applications and benchmarks is provided, illustrating the practical instantiation and evaluation of agentic reasoning frameworks.
- A clear agenda of future research challenges is articulated, spanning personalization, long-horizon interaction, world modeling, and the governance of agentic systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agentic reasoning fundamentally reframes LLMs from static inference machines to interactive, adaptive entities that bridge internal thought with external action.
> - The acquisition and refinement of agent capabilities can be systematically understood by distinguishing between inference-time workflow orchestration ("in-context reasoning") and parameter-updating learning ("post-training reasoning").
> - Effective LLM agents rely on a set of core mechanisms—planning, tool use, search, feedback, and memory—that can evolve through continuous interaction and learning.
