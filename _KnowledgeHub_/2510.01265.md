# RLP: Reinforcement as a Pretraining Objective

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed RLP (Reinforcement as a Pretraining Objective), which integrates reinforcement learning directly into the pretraining of Large Language Models to encourage Chain-of-Thought generation by rewarding information gain. This method enhances base model reasoning, showing a 19% average performance increase over conventional methods on an 8-benchmark math-science suite for a 1.7B model and achieving a 35% relative improvement for a 12B model with reduced training data.

## Problem

- The dominant next-token prediction objective in LLM pretraining does not explicitly foster long-range reasoning or deep integration of world knowledge.
- Reasoning abilities are primarily induced in costly, late-stage post-training phases like RLHF/RLAIF/RLVR, creating a disconnect from foundational learning.
- Current pretraining methods do not adequately reflect active human cognitive processes where internal deliberation precedes external expression.

## Method

- RLP introduces an ==information-driven reinforcement pretraining objective== into the last phase of pretraining, leveraging exploration.
- ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation== is treated as an explicit exploratory action, sampled by a =='thought policy'== before predicting the next token.
- A ==verifier-free, dense reward signal== is computed as the log-likelihood increase of the next token when conditioned on the sampled CoT compared to a no-think baseline.

## Results

- RLP-trained base models consistently outperformed baselines, with a 1.7B model showing a **19%** average improvement on an 8-benchmark math-science suite.
- A 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer model (Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2) achieved a **35%** relative improvement with RLP using only **250 million tokens**, compared to a baseline trained on 20 trillion tokens.
- RLP demonstrated superior performance (**+4.0%** overall average) compared to Reinforcement Pre-training (RPT) under matched compute, attributing gains to its dense, per-token information-gain reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating reinforcement learning directly into pretraining, using an intrinsic information-gain signal, can effectively teach models 'independent thinking behavior' earlier.
> - The dense, continuous, and verifier-free reward allows RLP to learn powerful reasoning signals from diverse, ordinary text corpora without specialized datasets.
> - The reasoning foundations established by RLP compound with subsequent post-training, leading to more capable and robust aligned models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The prevailing method for developing large reasoning models involves a multi-stage process: initial pre-training using a next-token prediction objective on extensive datasets, followed by post-training phases such as supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning with human or verified feedback (e.g., RLHF, RLAIF, RLVR). While effective in fostering complex reasoning abilities, this sequence relegates reinforcement learning (RL) to the final stages of model development.

The presented work, RLP (Reinforcement Learning Pre-training), challenges this conventional paradigm by integrating core reinforcement learning principles, specifically exploration, directly into the pre-training phase. Traditional next-token prediction pre-training, though widely adopted for its utility, does not inherently incentivize long-range reasoning or the explicit integration of world knowledge, often resulting in models that struggle with complex multi-step reasoning tasks without significant post-training. Human cognitive processes, in contrast, involve a more parallel integration of input with prior knowledge, a mechanism largely absent in current pre-training strategies.

RLP positions itself as a method to bridge the gap between simple next-token prediction and the emergence of sophisticated chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. It differentiates itself from existing RL methods that operate during post-training (which require task-specific verifiers or curated solutions) by introducing a verifier-free, dense reward signal applicable to general-purpose text corpora. Furthermore, it contrasts with other reinforcement pre-training approaches, such as Reinforcement Pre-training (RPT), by offering continuous, position-wise improvement signals across full documents, without relying on auxiliary models or pre-selection of high-entropy tokens. By doing so, RLP aims to instill "independent thinking behavior" into base models earlier in their development cycle.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Reinforcement Learning Pre-training (RLP), an information-driven reinforcement objective, to the pre-training phase of large language models (LLMs). This objective aims to cultivate reasoning capabilities within models before the application of conventional post-training methods.

The motivation for this approach stems from several observations regarding the limitations of the dominant LLM training paradigm:

- **Limitations of Next-Token Prediction:** While effective for acquiring broad linguistic patterns, the next-token prediction loss, used in standard pre-training, does not explicitly foster long-range reasoning or the integration of world knowledge. This limitation necessitates extensive post-training (e.g., SFT, RLHF) to induce complex problem-solving abilities. The authors propose that this sequential approach may not be optimal for developing robust reasoning from the outset.
- **Gap with Human Cognition:** Human comprehension is characterized by a parallel integration of new information with existing knowledge, enabling flexible and robust reasoning. Current LLM pre-training lacks mechanisms that encourage such "independent thinking" or explicit internal reasoning processes during the foundational learning phase.
- **Desire for Earlier Reasoning Induction:** The researchers are motivated to explore whether the "spirit of reinforcement learning—exploration" can be introduced earlier, specifically during the pre-training stage. The goal is to encourage models to "think for itself before predicting what comes next," thereby fostering independent thinking behavior more fundamentally.
- **Need for Scalable and Verifier-Free Rewards:** Existing reinforcement learning methods often rely on sparse, binary rewards from human feedback or external verifiers, which can be expensive to obtain and challenging to scale to vast, diverse pre-training corpora. The authors sought to develop a dense, verifier-free reward signal that could be efficiently computed for full document streams, making RL applicable at the pre-training scale. This reward mechanism would ideally measure the utility of internal "thoughts" in improving the model's predictive accuracy for future tokens.
- **Bridging Pre-training and Reasoning:** A central motivation is to establish a direct link between the pre-training objective and the emergence of useful chain-of-thought reasoning. By explicitly rewarding thoughts based on the information gain they provide for next-token prediction, RLP aims to bridge the gap between a purely probabilistic language modeling objective and the development of explicit reasoning skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLP introduces a pre-training procedure designed to explicitly induce reasoning by augmenting the next-token prediction task with a sampled Chain-of-Thought (CoT).

**Core Mechanism:**
At each position in a text sequence, the model first samples a latent CoT, denoted $c_t$, using a "thought policy" $\pi_\theta(\cdot | x_{<t})$. Subsequently, it predicts the next token $x_t$ using a "reasoned scorer" $p_\theta(\cdot | x_{<t}, c_t)$, which is conditioned on both the preceding context $x_{<t}$ and the sampled CoT $c_t$. To evaluate the utility of this thought, a "no-think baseline" $\bar{p}_\varphi(x_t | x_{<t})$ is employed. This baseline is an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher of the current network, which scores the same token $x_t$ but without conditioning on any CoT.

**Reward Signal - Information Gain:**
The reward for a sampled CoT $c_t$ is defined as the information gain, $r(c_t)$. This is calculated as the log-likelihood ratio between the reasoned scorer and the no-think baseline for the observed next token $x_t$:
$r(c_t) = \log p_\theta(x_t | x_{<t}, c_t) - \log \bar{p}_\varphi(x_t | x_{<t})$.
This reward is verifier-free, as it is derived intrinsically from the model's predictive probabilities, and dense, providing a signal at every token position where thinking improves prediction. The reward is computed under teacher forcing for each position $t$.

**Parameterization and Roles:**
- **Thought Policy / Predictor ($\theta$):** A single neural network parameterized by $\theta$ serves both as the thought policy (generating $c_t$) and the reasoned predictor (scoring $x_t$ given $x_{<t}$ and $c_t$).
- **No-Think Baseline ($\varphi$):** The EMA teacher, parameterized by $\varphi$, provides the counterfactual log-evidence without a CoT. It is initialized to match the current model $\theta$ on the first batch and subsequently updated with $\varphi \leftarrow \tau \varphi + (1 - \tau) \theta$, using a decay rate $\tau = 0.999$. This lagged, smoothed update stabilizes training and mitigates reward hacking.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
1. **Proposition 1 (CE Reduction):** The expected reward $E_{x_t \sim p^*}[r(c_t)]$ (for fixed context and CoT) is equivalent to the reduction in cross-entropy between the no-think baseline and the reasoned scorer with respect to the data distribution $p^*$. This implies that maximizing the expected reward directly translates to maximizing the predictive utility of the thought.
2. **Proposition 2 (Lower Bound):** The expected reasoned log-evidence $E_{c_t \sim \pi_\theta}[S_{pred}(c_t)]$ provides a computable lower bound on the log-probability of the collapsed predictor $\tilde{p}_\theta(x_t | x_{<t})$, which marginalizes over possible thoughts. This ensures interpretability and tractability of the objective.

**Optimization Algorithm (Algorithm 1):**
RLP optimizes the thought policy to produce CoTs that maximize the information gain. The training process does not incorporate a standard next-token prediction loss term. Instead, it minimizes the negative information-gain loss $\mathcal{L}_{IG}(\theta) = -J(\theta) = -E[r(c_t)]$.
- **Gradient Application:** Gradients are applied exclusively to the thought tokens. The reward $r(c_t)$ is treated as a constant during backpropagation through $p_\theta$ and $\bar{p}_\varphi$.
- **Group-Relative Baseline:** To reduce variance in reward estimation, for each context, $G \geq 2$ thoughts are sampled. A corrected inclusive mean is used to calculate advantages $A^{(i)}$ for each thought, with a multiplicative factor of $G/(G-1)$ to ensure unbiased estimation.
- **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** The log-probability of thought tokens is updated using a clipped surrogate loss, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This involves calculating per-token importance ratios $\rho_u^{(i)}$ and applying clipping with bounds $(1 - \epsilon_l, 1 + \epsilon_h)$ to the advantage-weighted importance ratio, ensuring stable policy updates.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology was validated using two model architectures: qwen3-1.7b-base (Transformer) and Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2 (hybrid Mamba-Transformer). RLP was applied during pre-training on diverse datasets, including SFT-style reasoning corpora (e.g., OmniMath, OpenThoughts) and general-purpose pre-training corpora (e.g., academic papers, math textbooks, Common Crawl web pages). Performance was compared against baselines that underwent continuous pre-training (CPT) with next-token prediction, matching input tokens or total FLOPs. Additionally, models were subjected to identical post-training regimens (SFT on OpenThoughts and RL with Verifier Rewards (RLVR) on MATH dataset) to assess the persistence and compounding effects of RLP's pre-training gains. Evaluation metrics focused on math and science reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MMLU, GPQA).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provide evidence for the effectiveness, robustness, and scalability of the RLP pre-training objective across various dimensions.

**Qwen3-1.7B-Base Performance:**
- **Initial Gains:** RLP consistently improved reasoning performance over both the base model ($\mathcal{M}_{base}$) and a continuously pre-trained baseline ($\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$). Specifically, $\mathcal{M}_{RLP}$ showed an overall average improvement of 19% relative to $\mathcal{M}_{base}$ and 17% relative to $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$ across an eight-benchmark math and science suite. Significant gains were observed on reasoning-intensive tasks such as AIME25 and MMLU-Pro.
- **Compounding with Post-Training:** The benefits of RLP pre-training persisted and compounded after subsequent post-training (SFT + RLVR). The final $\mathcal{M}_{RLP}$+Post model achieved the highest scores, surpassing its conventionally trained counterparts by 8% relative to $\mathcal{M}_{base}$+Post and 7% relative to $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$+Post in overall average. This indicates that RLP establishes a robust foundational reasoning ability that is not merely transient but enhances downstream alignment.
- **Cross-Domain Reasoning:** RLP led to particularly strong gains in science domains, with $\mathcal{M}_{RLP}$+Post achieving a 3-point absolute improvement over $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$+Post. This suggests that RLP fosters a broader range of multi-step, explanation-driven reasoning skills, not limited to mathematical problems.

**Scalability and Architecture-Agnosticism:**
- RLP was applied to Nemotron-Nano-12B-v2, a hybrid Mamba-Transformer model, demonstrating its generalizability across different architectures and model sizes. Despite being trained for only 250 million tokens (compared to $\mathcal{M}_{base}$'s 20 trillion tokens), $\mathcal{M}_{RLP}$ achieved a 35% relative improvement in overall average performance over $\mathcal{M}_{base}$. The Science Avg notably increased by an absolute 23%, highlighting RLP's data efficiency and robust cross-domain reasoning capabilities at scale.

**Comparison with Reinforcement Pre-training (RPT):**
- Under matched data and compute budgets, RLP outperformed RPT across all average metrics. RLP achieved higher Math Avg (+4.5% relative), Science Avg (+3.3%), and Overall Avg (+4.0%) compared to RPT. This is attributed to RLP's dense, per-token information-gain reward, which offers richer credit assignment compared to RPT's sparse, binary signal and reliance on auxiliary models for token pre-selection.

**Generalizability Across Diverse Corpora (Ablations):**
- RLP consistently delivered gains across various corpus families, including SFT-style reasoning datasets (OmniMath, OpenThoughts, Nemotron-Crossthink) and general-purpose web corpora (Academic papers, Math-Text, Web-Crawl). Average improvements of 7-9% relative to $\mathcal{M}_{base}$ were observed. This indicates that RLP can extract a valuable reasoning signal even from uncurated, non-reasoning data like Web-Crawl, enabling genuine cross-domain transfer of reasoning abilities.

**Compute-Equivalent Baselines:**
- To ensure fairness, RLP's performance was compared against $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$ baselines under equal input tokens and equal total FLOPs. RLP, trained on 170M tokens, maintained a clear advantage (5.3% average gain) even against a compute-matched $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$ that was exposed to 35 times more data (6B tokens). This confirms that RLP's gains stem from its methodological design rather than simply larger computational budgets.

**CPT with High-Quality Reasoning Data:**
- Even when $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$ was trained on high-quality reasoning-centric data, RLP consistently outperformed it. For instance, $\mathcal{M}_{RLP}$ showed an 8% average gain over $\mathcal{M}_{CPT}$ trained on Nemotron-Crossthink and 5% on a general pre-training data mix. This highlights that RLP's algorithmic design provides a generalizable mechanism for leveraging reasoning data that complements or even surpasses the benefits of high-quality corpus selection alone.

**Ablation Studies on Hyperparameters:**
- **Rollout Count:** Performance improved up to 16 rollouts (Overall 42.17%), with diminishing returns beyond this point.
- **Completion Length:** This was identified as a critical factor, with significant gains observed as the completion length increased up to 2048 tokens (Overall 42.17%).
- **KL Coefficient:** Introducing a KL anchor term did not yield net benefits; a coefficient of 0 performed optimally, indicating that explicit regularization towards a reference policy was not necessary for RLP's effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RLP represents a development in the pre-training paradigm for large language models, offering a principled and general alternative to likelihood-only training. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

- **Early Reasoning Induction:** RLP shifts the integration of reinforcement learning from a post-training phase to the foundational pre-training stage. This approach aims to instill "independent thinking behavior" and explicit reasoning capabilities into base models from an earlier point in their development cycle. This could lead to more inherently reasoning-capable models, reducing the reliance on extensive and costly post-training alignment.
- **Verifier-Free and Scalable Reinforcement Learning:** By employing a verifier-free, dense information-gain reward signal, RLP overcomes a significant limitation of traditional RL methods that depend on external verifiers or human feedback. This intrinsic reward mechanism allows for efficient application to vast, diverse, and uncurated web-scale text corpora, making reinforcement learning scalable during pre-training without additional labeling efforts.
- **Enhanced Foundational Models:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across various benchmarks, particularly in reasoning-heavy math and science tasks, suggest that RLP can produce foundational models with more robust and durable reasoning abilities. These gains persist and compound even after subsequent post-training, indicating a deeper, more stable integration of reasoning skills.
- **Generalizability Across Data and Architectures:** RLP's effectiveness across diverse data domains (SFT-style, academic, web-crawl) and its scalability to different model sizes and architectures (Transformer and hybrid Mamba-Transformer) highlight its broad applicability. This suggests that RLP offers a versatile mechanism for enhancing reasoning that is not confined to specific data types or model designs.
- **Data Efficiency:** The notable performance improvements observed with significantly less data (e.g., a 12B model achieving substantial gains with only 0.125% of the pre-training data compared to a baseline) underscore RLP's data efficiency. This has implications for reducing the computational and data requirements for training highly capable reasoning models.
- **Re-evaluation of Pre-training Objectives:** This work challenges the long-standing dominance of next-token prediction as the sole pre-training objective for LLMs. By demonstrating that a reinforcement-based objective can effectively complement or even surpass likelihood-only training in developing reasoning, RLP encourages further exploration of richer and more complex pre-training objectives that directly target desirable model behaviors like explanation generation and multi-step reasoning.

In summary, RLP offers a new direction for training LLMs by integrating reasoning-focused reinforcement learning into the pre-training phase, potentially leading to a new generation of models with stronger, more intrinsic, and broadly applicable reasoning capabilities.
