# ExpeL: LLM Agents Are Experiential Learners

> [!summary] Summary
> ExpeL is an LLM agent that learns from experience without any parameter updates. It collects success and failure trajectories on training tasks via Reflexion, distills cross-task natural-language insights with ADD/UPVOTE/DOWNVOTE/EDIT operations, and retrieves similar successful trajectories as in-context examples. It beats ReAct on HotpotQA, ALFWorld and WebShop, and transfers insights to FEVER.

## Problem

- Finetuning LLMs for decision-making is compute-heavy, needs parametric weight access, and can hurt generalization, which rules out closed-source API models like GPT-4 and Claude.
- Prompting methods like ReAct rely on a few fixed demonstrations. Context limits leave agents with no memory of past experience, so nothing is learned across tasks.
- Self-improvement methods like Reflexion retry only the same single task and do not retain reusable knowledge across different tasks, so improvement requires repeated attempts at test time.

## Method

- ==Experience gathering==: the agent attempts training tasks with ReAct plus ==Reflexion retries== (up to Z trials), storing successful and failed trajectories in an ==experience pool==.
- ==Insight extraction==: an LLM compares ==success/failure pairs== and lists of successes, then edits a running insight list using ==ADD, UPVOTE, DOWNVOTE and EDIT operations== to capture failure patterns and best practices.
- ==Task-inference stage==: the agent prepends the extracted insights to its prompt and retrieves the most ==task-similar successful trajectories== as in-context examples, then solves each unseen task in a single attempt.

## Results

- ExpeL beats ReAct on HotpotQA (**39%** vs **28%**) and ALFWorld (**59%** vs **40%**) in a single attempt. Insights-only and retrieval-only variants each score lower, showing both parts are needed.
- Transferring HotpotQA insights to FEVER gives **70%** success versus **63%** for ReAct and **58%** for Act. Without task demonstrations the transfer agent reaches **65%**.
- Ablations: hand-crafted insights score **32%**, adding reflections gives **29%**, and random example retrieval gives **42.5%** on ALFWorld versus **59%** for task-similarity retrieval. Combined with Reflexion, ALFWorld reaches **64.2%** at round 3.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frozen LLMs can improve through accumulated natural-language memory, so learning does not require gradient updates. This suits closed-source models and improves as the base model gets stronger.
> - Abstract insights and concrete retrieved trajectories are complementary. Insights help reasoning-heavy tasks like HotpotQA, and retrieved trajectories help action-heavy tasks like ALFWorld.
> - Diverse experience, especially paired failure and success trajectories, matters more than volume. Agent-extracted insights beat hand-written ones, but feeding noisy reflections into extraction can hurt.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "ExpeL" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving areas within artificial intelligence: large language models (LLMs), autonomous agents, reinforcement learning, and retrieval-augmented generation.

**LLM Agents:** The advent of powerful LLMs has opened new avenues for creating autonomous agents capable of complex decision-making, planning, and reasoning. Existing LLM agent research broadly falls into two categories:
 - **Finetuning LLMs:** This involves updating the LLM's parametric weights based on extensive environmental interactions or human-labeled datasets (e.g., Nakano et al. 2021). While effective, this approach is computationally intensive, requires access to the LLM's internal parameters (often proprietary for state-of-the-art models like GPT-4), and can potentially lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where the model loses its generalization capabilities after being specialized for a particular task.
 - **Prompting Methods:** This involves augmenting LLMs with in-context examples or structured prompts (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, ReAct) to enhance their planning and reasoning abilities without altering their parameters. While flexible and compatible with proprietary LLMs, these methods are limited by the LLM's finite context window, meaning they lack a persistent memory or ability to learn from experiences across multiple tasks.

**ExpeL's Position:** ExpeL strategically positions itself as a solution that strikes a balance between these two paradigms. It addresses the growing demand for methods that allow LLM agents to learn from experience *without* requiring parametric updates, thereby making it compatible with powerful closed-source models and avoiding the pitfalls of finetuning. It proposes a novel framework for "experiential learning" that mimics human learning—accumulating diverse experiences, extracting generalizable insights, and recalling specific past successes to inform future decisions, all in natural language.

**Relationship to Existing Methods:**
 - **ReAct (Reasoning and Acting):** ExpeL builds upon the ReAct framework (Yao et al. 2023b) as its base planning algorithm, inheriting its ability to interleave thoughts, actions, and observations.
 - **Reflexion:** ExpeL leverages Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023) during its *experience gathering* phase. While Reflexion focuses on intra-task self-improvement through reattempts and verbal feedback *within a single task*, ExpeL extends this concept to enable *inter-task* learning by accumulating and abstracting knowledge from experiences across *multiple* tasks. This is a crucial distinction, as ExpeL does not require reattempts during its evaluation phase, making it suitable for domains where retries are impractical.
 - **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG):** ExpeL incorporates retrieval mechanisms, similar to RAG approaches that augment LLMs with external knowledge bases (Li et al. 2022). However, instead of retrieving from a generic database or human-curated gold examples, ExpeL retrieves from its *own self-generated experiences*, making the knowledge highly relevant and domain-specific.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** The concept of autonomously gathering experiences and learning from them is reminiscent of reinforcement learning, particularly off-policy learning (Watkins and Dayan 1992) and experience replay (Lin 1992; Schaul et al. 2015). However, ExpeL avoids the complexities of designing reward functions or performing gradient updates, operating purely within the realm of natural language and prompt engineering.

In essence, ExpeL contributes a pragmatic and effective non-parametric learning paradigm for LLM agents, bridging the gap between static prompting and costly finetuning, while enabling robust, interpretable, and cross-task learning from self-generated experiences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the ExpeL framework stem from several limitations and emerging demands in the field of LLM agents:

1. **Overcome Resource Intensity of Finetuning:** Traditional finetuning of LLMs for specific decision-making tasks is computationally expensive, requiring significant data and powerful hardware. ExpeL aims to achieve performance improvements without this high overhead.
2. **Circumvent Proprietary Model Access:** State-of-the-art LLMs like GPT-4 and Claude are often only accessible via API calls, with their internal parameters remaining proprietary. This restricts finetuning capabilities for many researchers and developers. ExpeL's prompt-based approach is designed to work seamlessly with such closed-source models.
3. **Address Context Window Limitations for Long-term Learning:** Current LLMs have a finite context window, which limits their ability to retain and leverage past experiences across different tasks. This means they effectively "forget" what they've learned from previous interactions. ExpeL seeks to enable true long-term, cross-task learning by maintaining a persistent memory of experiences and abstract insights.
4. **Enable Autonomous Learning from Experience:** Inspired by how humans learn—through trial and error, reflection, and generalizing from past successes—the authors aim to design an LLM agent that can autonomously gather diverse experiences, extract actionable knowledge, and improve its performance over time without human supervision. This is akin to a student practicing for an exam and then applying learned insights and recalling similar problems during the actual test.
5. **Enhance Generalization and Adaptability:** By avoiding task-specific finetuning, ExpeL aims to preserve the LLM's inherent world knowledge and generalization capabilities, allowing it to adapt more effectively to new, unseen tasks within similar domains.
6. **Provide Interpretability and Controllability:** Since ExpeL operates by managing natural language insights and trajectories, its learning process is inherently more interpretable than parametric updates. This allows users to inspect, modify, or even inject expert knowledge, fostering greater control and trust in the agent's behavior.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a practical, scalable, and interpretable method for LLM agents to continuously learn and improve their decision-making abilities from their own interactions, without the need for extensive computational resources or access to proprietary model weights.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExpeL operates through a three-stage experiential learning process: Experience Gathering, Learning from Experiences (which includes Insight Extraction and Experience Recall), and Task Inference.

#### 3.1. Experience Gathering

- **Goal:** To autonomously collect a diverse pool of successful and failed task trajectories.
- **Mechanism:** ExpeL leverages the **Reflexion** framework (Shinn et al., 2023). For each training task, the agent attempts it multiple times (up to `Z` retries).
 - **Trial & Error:** The agent uses a base planning algorithm, **ReAct** (Yao et al., 2023b), augmented with manual few-shot examples and past reflections.
 - **Self-Reflection on Failure:** If a trial fails, the agent self-reflects on the failed trajectory to generate a new reflection (`ν`) in natural language. This reflection is then concatenated with previous reflections and used to augment the prompt for the next retry on the *same task*. This process helps to generate successful trajectories and, crucially, valuable success/failure pairs for subsequent insight extraction.
- **Storage:** All generated trajectories (successes and failures) are stored in an "experience pool" (`B`).

#### 3.2. Learning from Experiences

This stage comprises two distinct learning modes that mimic human cognitive processes: storing specific examples and abstracting high-level insights.

- **4.2.1. Similar Experiences as Demonstrations (Experience Recall):**
 - **Goal:** To retrieve highly relevant successful past trajectories as in-context examples for new tasks.
 - **Mechanism:** A **Faiss vectorstore** (Johnson et al., 2019) is used to store embeddings of successful trajectories from the experience pool `B`. The `all-mpnet-base-v2` embedder is used to compute embeddings.
 - **Retrieval:** During evaluation (or task inference), given a new task, the system queries the vectorstore to find the `k` most similar *successful* trajectories (based on task similarity, measured by inner product of embeddings) and uses them as in-context demonstrations (`F_similar_tasks`). This allows the agent to leverage specific, successful patterns from its past.

- **4.2.2. Learning from Successes and Failures (Insight Extraction):**
 - **Goal:** To abstract generalizable knowledge and common best practices or failure patterns from the collected experiences.
 - **Mechanism:** An instruction-following LLM (`LLM_insights`, specifically `gpt-4-0613` empirically found most effective) is prompted to analyze the experience pool `B`.
 - **Input:** The `LLM_insights` is fed either:
 1. **Success/Failure Pairs:** Trajectories for the same task where one attempt succeeded and another failed, allowing direct comparison of correct vs. incorrect actions.
 2. **Lists of L Successes:** Sets of successful trajectories from different tasks, enabling the identification of common successful strategies.
 - **Dynamic Insight Management:** The `LLM_insights` interacts with an existing set of insights (`ˆι`) using specific natural language operations:
 - **ADD:** Introduce a new insight.
 - **EDIT:** Modify an existing insight.
 - **UPVOTE/DOWNVOTE:** Adjust an insight's "importance count." Insights with a count of zero are removed, ensuring the quality and relevance of the insight pool.
 - **Output:** A continually refined and curated set of natural language insights (`ˆι`) that capture cross-task knowledge.

#### 3.3. Task Inference

- **Goal:** To solve new, unseen tasks with a single attempt, leveraging the learned knowledge.
- **Mechanism:** During the evaluation phase, the ExpeL agent's prompt is augmented:
 - The task specifications are concatenated with the full list of extracted insights (`ˆι`).
 - The top-`k` similar successful trajectories retrieved via experience recall (`F_similar_tasks`) are included as in-context examples.
- **Base Execution:** The augmented prompt is then fed to the base LLM policy (`LLM_ExpeL`, which is `gpt-3.5-turbo-0613` in experiments) for sequential decision-making using the ReAct framework.

#### 3.4. Transfer Learning

- **Approach:** ExpeL investigates transferring knowledge between different task distributions (e.g., HotpotQA to FEVER).
- **Mechanism:** Insights (`ˆι`) extracted from a source task distribution are "finetuned" (again, not parametrically, but via prompt engineering) using a small set of few-shot examples from the target task distribution. This process adapts the general insights to the specifics of the new domain, aiming to mitigate hallucinations and improve applicability.

The methodology emphasizes autonomous, non-parametric learning in natural language, making ExpeL a flexible and interpretable framework for enhancing LLM agent capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on three diverse text-based benchmarks (HotpotQA, ALFWorld, and WebShop) demonstrate the robust learning efficacy of the ExpeL agent.

#### 4.1. Experiential Learning Efficacy

- **Consistent Outperformance:** ExpeL consistently outperforms strong baselines, including Imitation Learning (IL), Act, and ReAct, across all tested environments in terms of average task success rates. This highlights the value of autonomously collected experiences and the insights/retrieval mechanisms.
- **Synergy of Insights and Retrieval:** Ablation studies on learning modes (insights-only vs. retrieve-only) show a synergistic effect.
 - **HotpotQA (knowledge-intensive QA):** Benefits more from extracted insights (36% vs. 31% for insights-only vs. retrieve-only). This is attributed to HotpotQA's reliance on analytical reasoning and the need for general guidelines derived from insights.
 - **ALFWorld (embodied multi-step tasks):** Benefits more from retrieved trajectories (50% vs. 55% for insights-only vs. retrieve-only). Its action-set-dependent nature makes specific successful trajectories more valuable.
 - **WebShop (online shopping tasks):** Shows a near-equilibrium benefit from both, indicating its complex nature requires both high-level reasoning (insights) and precise execution patterns (retrieval).
- **Cross-Task Learning Advantage:** ExpeL matches or outperforms Reflexion (a state-of-the-art self-improvement method focused on intra-task reattempts) in HotpotQA (40% vs. 39% at R3) and significantly outperforms it in ALFWorld (59% vs. 54% at R3) *without requiring repeated attempts at test time*. This showcases ExpeL's unique strength in *cross-task* learning and its applicability in single-attempt scenarios.

#### 4.2. Agent Behavioral Analysis and Emergent Capabilities

Qualitative analysis of ExpeL's trajectories revealed several unexpected emergent abilities:
- **Hypothesis Formulation & Constraint Adaptation (HotpotQA):** ExpeL gained the ability to reassess its trajectory and deduce a probable answer even when direct information wasn't immediately available, rather than concluding "Unknown." This stemmed from an insight suggesting to "consider the answer might be in the observations already made."
- **World Model Belief Update (ALFWorld):** ExpeL updated its internal "world model" or priors based on insights. For instance, it learned that pans are typically found on "stoveburners" rather than general "drawers/countertops," leading to more efficient object localization. This was linked to an insight about considering an item's "nature and typical usage."
- **Self-Correction (ALFWorld):** ExpeL demonstrated the ability to identify and rectify its own missteps, such as putting back an incorrectly picked-up object and resuming the task. This was likely encouraged by insights prompting the agent to "reassess the situation and consider alternative actions" if progress halts.

#### 4.3. Transfer Learning

- **Positive Forward Transfer:** Experiments transferring insights from HotpotQA (source) to FEVER (target) demonstrated significant performance gains.
- **Effectiveness of "Finetuning" Insights:** ExpeL Transfer with task demonstrations (few-shot examples during insight adaptation) achieved 70% success, notably higher than ExpeL Transfer *without* demonstrations (65%) and baselines (ReAct 63%, Act 58%). This validates the proposed method of refining insights for target domains.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Diverse Experience:** ExpeL's performance significantly improved with access to diverse experiences (success/failure pairs collected via Reflexion) compared to agents with limited or no diverse experiences. Learning from only existing few-shot examples or only ReAct-gathered experiences was less effective.
- **Superiority of LLM-Generated Insights:** LLM-generated insights (by `gpt-4-0613`) outperformed human-crafted insights, highlighting the LLM's capacity for autonomous knowledge abstraction.
- **Quality of Insight Extraction LLM:** Using a more powerful LLM (`gpt-4-0613`) for insight extraction yielded better performance than `gpt-3.5-turbo-0613`, indicating that ExpeL benefits from advancements in foundational models.
- **Effectiveness of Task Similarity Retrieval:** Retrieving successful trajectories based on task similarity consistently outperformed random sampling or 'reason similarity' based retrieval, confirming the importance of selecting highly relevant in-context examples.

In essence, the findings validate ExpeL's core hypothesis: LLM agents can become more robust, adaptive, and performant through a principled experiential learning process that involves autonomous experience gathering, insight extraction, and targeted experience recall, all without modifying the LLM's parameters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ExpeL presents a significant step forward in the development of autonomous LLM agents, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Technical Significance

- **Non-Parametric Learning Paradigm:** ExpeL offers a powerful alternative to traditional finetuning. By learning solely through prompt engineering and memory management, it bypasses the computational cost and model access limitations associated with parametric updates. This makes advanced LLM agent development more accessible to researchers and practitioners without large computing resources or access to proprietary model weights.
- **Inter-Task Learning:** Unlike many self-improvement methods (like Reflexion) that focus on refining performance within a single task, ExpeL's ability to extract general insights and recall experiences across *multiple* distinct tasks is a crucial innovation. This capability is essential for developing agents that can continuously learn and adapt in diverse, open-ended environments.
- **Interpretable Learning:** All learned knowledge, whether as extracted insights or recalled trajectories, is in natural language. This inherent interpretability allows for easier debugging, human oversight, and the integration of expert knowledge, fostering greater trust and control over agent behavior—a critical advantage in sensitive applications.
- **Synergy with Foundational Models:** ExpeL's performance directly benefits from advancements in the underlying LLMs, particularly those used for insight extraction. This "free performance boost" means ExpeL's capabilities will naturally improve as foundational models become more intelligent.
- **Adaptive Memory Mechanisms:** The dynamic management of insights (ADD, EDIT, UPVOTE, DOWNVOTE) and the use of semantic similarity for experience retrieval provide sophisticated memory mechanisms that go beyond simple context window stuffing, allowing for efficient and relevant knowledge utilization.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Broader Adoption of LLM Agents:** By lowering the barrier to entry (less data, less compute, no proprietary weight access), ExpeL could accelerate the development and deployment of LLM agents across various domains, from customer service and personal assistants to complex scientific discovery and robotics.
- **Foundation for Lifelong Learning Agents:** ExpeL lays groundwork for truly lifelong learning agents that continuously accumulate knowledge from their interactions. While the current context window limits how much raw experience can be active, the insight extraction mechanism offers a scalable way to condense and generalize knowledge. Future work could focus on more sophisticated retrieval of insights for very long-term memory.
- **Enhanced Human-Agent Collaboration:** The interpretable nature of ExpeL's learned knowledge could facilitate more effective human-agent collaboration. Users could understand *why* an agent made a decision, correct its "beliefs" (insights), or provide new expert guidelines in natural language.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to learn from diverse experiences and extract general principles helps agents become more robust to novel situations and generalize better to unseen tasks, moving beyond rote memorization of specific examples.
- **Advancement in AI Research:** ExpeL contributes a novel paradigm that combines strengths from various AI subfields—reinforcement learning (experience gathering), natural language processing (prompting, RAG), and cognitive science (human-like learning processes)—potentially inspiring new lines of research into how complex AI systems can learn efficiently and effectively in dynamic environments.
