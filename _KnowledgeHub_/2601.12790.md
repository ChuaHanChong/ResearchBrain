# FocusNav: Spatial Selective Attention with Waypoint Guidance for Humanoid Local Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> FocusNav presents a spatial selective attention framework for humanoid robot local navigation, leveraging bio-inspired Perception-Prediction-Attention principles to enhance stability and path planning in complex, dynamic environments. The framework achieved an 87.02% success rate in challenging simulation scenarios and demonstrated robust real-world performance on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with robust local navigation in complex, unstructured, and dynamic real-world environments due to the inherent fragility of their mobile navigation systems.
- Existing perceptive locomotion methods often incur computational latency, are susceptible to environmental noise, and lack gait flexibility due to the "velocity-command-tracking" paradigm.
- A challenge exists in balancing the pursuit of long-range navigation goals with ensuring immediate motion stability, particularly on uneven or unpredictable terrain.

## Method

- **FocusNav** introduces a spatial selective attention framework that emulates biological ==Perception-Prediction-Attention (PPA)== circuits for robust navigation.
- It integrates a multi-modal perception encoder, a collision-free waypoint predictor using a backward prediction paradigm, and a dual-layer spatial selective attention module.
- The dual-layer attention comprises Waypoint-Guided Spatial Cross-Attention (WGSCA) to focus on planned paths and Stability-Aware Selective Gating (SASG) to dynamically prioritize immediate foothold safety based on real-time robot stability.

## Results

- FocusNav consistently outperformed baseline methods in simulation, achieving an **87.02%** success rate in dynamic and unstructured scenarios, compared to **50.32%** for Gallant and **63.67%** for PGCA.
- The Stability-Aware Selective Gating (SASG) module was shown to autonomously truncate distal environmental information to prioritize immediate foothold safety when robot stability decreased, preventing falls on challenging terrains.
- Extensive real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in six challenging scenarios (including stairs, slopes, and dynamic obstacles) validated FocusNav's ability to maintain motion stability and execute collision-free navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Emulating biological PPA paradigms with distinct modules for perception, prediction, and attention can yield more effective and robust artificial navigation systems for humanoid robots.
> - Dynamically modulating the robot's perceptual field by explicitly aligning it with planned waypoints and adaptively prioritizing stability based on proprioceptive state is critical for safe navigation in uncertain environments.
> - A goal-conditioned backward prediction approach for waypoint generation enhances global-to-local consistency, leading to more rational and collision-free trajectories.
