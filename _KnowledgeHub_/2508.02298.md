# CAPO: Towards Enhancing LLM Reasoning through Generative Credit Assignment

> [!summary] Summary
> CAPO significantly improves Large Language Model reasoning by providing fine-grained, reliable, and efficient credit assignment through an off-the-shelf LLM used as a generative process reward model, consistently outperforming existing baselines on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) provides only coarse, outcome-based feedback, failing to inform LLMs which specific reasoning steps are correct or incorrect.
- Current fine-grained methods like PPO or Process Reward Models (PRMs) suffer from unreliable value estimations, high costs for manual process supervision, or vulnerability to reward hacking.
- Effectively balancing outcome-oriented and process-oriented learning signals in LLMs to achieve both correct answers and sound reasoning pathways remains a challenge.

## Method

- Introduces ==Credit Assignment Policy Optimization (CAPO)== which utilizes an off-the-shelf LLM as a =='Generative Process Reward Model' (GenPRM)== to efficiently identify erroneous reasoning steps in generated responses.
- Leverages the GenPRM to generate multiple critiques in a single pass, which are then aggregated using a ==voting mechanism== (e.g., intersection, majority) to robustly pinpoint specific incorrect steps.
- Applies an ==asymmetric reward shaping strategy== that prioritizes correct final outcomes while simultaneously encouraging sound reasoning processes at the token level, with `W_whole > W_process`.

## Results

- CAPO consistently outperforms SFT and existing RL baselines, achieving up to **+3.5 points** gain on mathematical benchmarks for Qwen-2.5-7B and **+2.1 points** for Llama-3-3B.
- The method demonstrates enhanced generalization, improving performance on out-of-distribution reasoning tasks such as ARC-c and MMLU-Pro, indicating broader cognitive skill development.
- Ablation studies confirm the necessity of asymmetric reward shaping for optimal performance and show that aggregating more critiques through voting mechanisms consistently improves results across tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful, general-purpose LLMs can effectively serve as deterministic critics for fine-grained process-level error identification without requiring specific fine-tuning for this role.
> - Aggregating multiple critiques from an LLM-as-GenPRM via voting significantly enhances the reliability and accuracy of identifying erroneous steps for credit assignment.
> - A hierarchical, asymmetric reward formulation is crucial for balancing outcome and process rewards, preventing conflicting signals and guiding LLMs towards both accurate and logically sound reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning, Reinforcement Learning (RL), and the critical challenge of credit assignment. In recent years, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a powerful paradigm for enhancing LLMs' reasoning abilities, particularly in domains like mathematics and code. RLVR typically uses rule-based binary feedback, which helps mitigate issues like "reward hacking" often seen with subjective human feedback or complex reward models.

However, the authors identify several fundamental limitations in existing RLVR and fine-grained credit assignment approaches:
- **Coarse-grained Feedback in Standard RLVR:** Methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) treat entire LLM responses as single actions, assigning the same binary reward to every token based solely on the final outcome. This "coarse credit assignment problem" means the model struggles to differentiate between partially correct and entirely flawed reasoning steps, hindering its ability to learn robust reasoning processes. It effectively turns a sequential decision-making problem into a contextual bandit problem, overlooking the temporal dependency of actions.
- **Inaccuracy and Unreliability in Fine-grained Methods:**
 - **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization):** While PPO aims to provide credit assignment through value estimation, these signals can be inaccurate and unverifiable due to limited sampling and the inherent challenges of estimating long-term returns.
 - **Process Reward Models (PRMs):** PRMs offer step-wise rewards but face significant practical hurdles. They require high-quality, often costly, manual annotations for process supervision. Their feedback can be unreliable due to probabilistic reward modeling and the "process-to-outcome" shift, where models might prioritize the final answer over sound reasoning, even if it leads to an incorrect process. Furthermore, their application in online RL is time-consuming due to multiple inference calls for each step.
 - **Densification-based RL (e.g., attention-based credit, Shapley values):** These methods decompose sequence-level rewards to token-level feedback but often rely on auxiliary reward models or other complex external sources. This reliance introduces vulnerability to "reward hacking," where the LLM learns to exploit flaws or biases in the reward model rather than improving true reasoning.

CAPO directly addresses these limitations by proposing a method that is simultaneously fine-grained, reliable, and efficient. It seeks to provide precise credit assignment without the complexities, costs, or vulnerabilities of prior approaches. By leveraging the inherent reasoning capabilities of powerful, off-the-shelf LLMs as generative process reward models, CAPO attempts to bridge the gap between simple but coarse RLVR and complex but unreliable fine-grained methods, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in LLM reasoning enhancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the CAPO research is to **develop a simple, efficient, and reliable method for fine-grained credit assignment in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to significantly enhance the reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs).**

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:
- **Overcoming Coarse-Grained Feedback:** Existing RLVR methods, while robust against reward hacking due to their rule-based nature, provide only coarse, outcome-based feedback. This binary "correct/incorrect" signal for an entire response fails to inform the LLM which specific reasoning steps contributed to success or failure. The authors are motivated to move beyond this limitation to enable more precise learning.
- **Addressing Inaccuracies and Vulnerabilities in Fine-Grained Approaches:** Current methods that attempt fine-grained credit assignment (e.g., PPO, PRMs, attention-based models) suffer from issues such as unreliable value estimations, reliance on costly high-quality process supervision, and susceptibility to reward hacking. The authors aim to devise a mechanism that provides granular feedback without these pitfalls.
- **Improving Efficiency and Generality:** Training auxiliary reward models or requiring multiple inference calls for each step in online RL is computationally expensive and complex. The motivation is to streamline the RL pipeline by utilizing readily available, powerful LLMs to generate step-wise critiques efficiently in a single pass, thus lowering barriers to adoption and improving scalability.
- **Shifting to Process-Oriented Learning:** The authors recognize that true reasoning improvement requires the model to learn sound intermediate steps, not just correct final answers. They are motivated to enable a shift from purely outcome-oriented learning to "process-oriented learning" by providing step-level feedback, while carefully balancing this with the ultimate goal of achieving correct outcomes.
- **Fostering Robust and Generalizable Reasoning:** By identifying and penalizing erroneous reasoning steps, CAPO aims to guide the LLM to learn more robust and logically coherent solution pathways. This is expected to improve not only in-domain performance (e.g., math problems) but also enhance generalization to out-of-distribution reasoning tasks.

In essence, the motivation is to design a credit assignment mechanism that retains the verifiable nature of rule-based rewards while introducing the much-needed granularity for effective policy optimization, leading to more intelligent and reliable LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CAPO (Credit Assignment Policy Optimization) is an elegantly designed method that aims to provide fine-grained, reliable, and efficient credit assignment for LLMs in RLVR settings. Its methodology can be broken down into three main stages: Process Credits Generation, Fine-grained Credit Assignment (with Reward Shaping), and Policy Optimization.

#### 3.1. Process Credits Generation via LLM-as-GenPRM

The core innovation begins with the "Generative Process Reward Model" (GenPRM) – which is an off-the-shelf, general-purpose LLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct or Llama-3-70B-Instruct). This GenPRM is not fine-tuned for the specific verification task but leverages its inherent reasoning capabilities.
1. **Rollout Generation:** During RL training, the policy model (`π_θ_old`) generates `n` rollouts (responses) for a given query.
2. **Single-Pass Critique Generation:** For each rollout, the LLM-as-GenPRM is prompted to generate `k` critiques in a *single pass*. This is crucial for efficiency, avoiding multiple inference calls per step common in other PRM approaches. The prompt instructs the GenPRM to identify erroneous steps based on their *intrinsic correctness*, not just their contribution to the final outcome. Importantly, the GenPRM is provided with the *ground-truth answer* as a reference, which simplifies the task from abstract verification to more grounded error localization.
3. **Error Index Extraction:** From each of the `k` critiques, a set of indices `S_i,j` corresponding to identified wrong steps is extracted.
4. **Voting Mechanism:** To enhance robustness and reliability, these `k` sets of indices are aggregated using a voting mechanism to produce a final, consolidated set of erroneous step indices (`S*_i`) for each rollout.
 - **Intersection Vote:** A conservative approach, where a step is deemed erroneous only if *all* `k` critiques agree. Maximizes precision, robust to false positives.
 - **Majority Vote:** A balanced approach, where a step is erroneous if flagged by at least half of the `k` critiques.
 - **Union Vote:** An aggressive approach, where a step is erroneous if flagged by *any* of the `k` critiques. Maximizes recall, susceptible to false positives.
 - **Greedy:** (k=1) Uses only a single critique, sensitive to noise.
 - **Average:** Assigns a continuous penalty weight based on the frequency of being flagged.

#### 3.2. Fine-Grained Credit Assignment

Once the erroneous steps are identified, CAPO meticulously assigns credit at the token level:
1. **Initial Token Reward:** All tokens in a given response (`y_i`) are initially assigned a score derived from the response-level rule-based reward (`r_v`), scaled by a factor `W_whole`.
2. **Localized Penalty:** For tokens (`o_t`) that belong to any of the identified erroneous steps (`T_i_err`), a penalty scaled by `W_process` is applied.
3. **Reward Formulation:** The token reward (`R_i,t`) is formulated as: `R_i,t = r_v * W_whole - I(t ∈ T_i_err) * W_process`. This equation integrates both outcome and process rewards.
4. **Reward Shaping for Balance:** The authors identify a conflict between outcome-oriented and process-oriented learning signals. To address this, they propose an *asymmetric reward shaping strategy* where `W_whole > W_process` (e.g., `W_whole=2, W_process=1`). This creates a hierarchical learning objective:
 - Correct Answer, Correct Process: +2
 - Correct Answer, Incorrect Process: +1
 - Incorrect Answer, Correct Process: 0
 - Incorrect Answer, Incorrect Process: -1
 This hierarchy ensures that correct outcomes are prioritized, while sound reasoning is secondarily encouraged, preventing the model from overfitting to easily attainable process-level rewards.
5. **Group-Relative Per-Token Normalization:** The per-token rewards (`R_i,t`) are then normalized across a batch of rollouts to compute the advantage estimates (`A*_i,t`). This normalization makes the advantages reflect the fine-grained feedback relative to other samples in the batch.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization

Finally, these fine-grained advantage estimates are used to update the policy model. The objective function (`J_CAPO(θ)`) is a PPO-like formulation, incorporating the advantage term, a clipping mechanism to prevent large policy updates, and a KL divergence regularization term to ensure policy stability relative to the reference policy. This objective guides the model to suppress erroneous segments and reinforce correct ones.

In summary, CAPO's methodology is characterized by its clever use of a powerful, off-the-shelf LLM as a deterministic critic for efficient step-wise error identification, a robust voting mechanism for consolidating critiques, and an asymmetric reward shaping strategy to create a balanced hierarchical learning signal for fine-grained policy optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted by the authors across various LLM backbones and benchmarks yield several significant findings:

- **Consistent Superior Performance:** CAPO consistently outperforms both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and existing RL-based methods (GRPO-Rule, GRPO-GenORM) that lack fine-grained credit assignment.
 - For **Qwen-2.5-7B**, CAPO achieves a notable +3.5 point gain on mathematical benchmarks and an overall average improvement of +2.5 points across all benchmarks.
 - For **Llama-3-3B**, CAPO establishes a solid +2.1 points lead over the best baseline in math reasoning and +1.8 points in all benchmarks.
 - Similar improvements are observed for smaller models like Qwen-2.5-1.5B and Llama-3-1B.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The gains are not limited to in-distribution mathematical reasoning tasks (MATH, Olympiadbench, AIME24, AMC23). CAPO also improves the model's generalization on out-of-distribution (OOD) reasoning tasks such as ARC-c, MMLU-Pro, and GPQA-diamond, indicating that fostering correct reasoning pathways leads to broader cognitive enhancements.
- **Mechanism for Success: Learning Correct Reasoning Pathways:** Analysis indicates that CAPO actively encourages the model to learn and generate correct reasoning pathways. The fine-grained feedback enables the model to effectively explore solution paths, leading to higher accuracy by reinforcing sound intermediate steps and penalizing specific errors.
- **Robustness to LLM-as-GenPRM Selection:** The framework demonstrates low sensitivity to the choice of the generative reward model, performing effectively with both Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct and Llama-3-70B-Instruct as GenPRMs, without requiring any specific fine-tuning. This highlights the versatility and broad applicability of the method, relying on the general reasoning capabilities of powerful, publicly available LLMs.
- **Impact of Critique Scaling (N):** Increasing the number of critiques (N) generated by the LLM-as-GenPRM from 2 to 8 consistently improves performance across both math and general reasoning tasks. This indicates that aggregating more critiques through voting mechanisms leads to more accurate and robust identification of incorrect steps, providing a better signal for credit assignment.
- **Validation of Asymmetric Reward Formulation:** Ablation studies on the reward formulation clearly show that the proposed asymmetric setting (`W_whole > W_process`, e.g., C=2, P=1) is crucial for optimal performance. Configurations with weak process penalties (C=2, P=0.1) perform less well, while symmetric (C=2, P=2) or overly process-focused (C=2, P=5) settings significantly degrade performance. This validates the authors' hypothesis that a hierarchical balance between outcome and process rewards is essential to avoid ambiguous or misleading signals.
- **Positive Correlation with Response Length and Per-Token Reward:** CAPO-trained models consistently generate longer responses compared to baselines. Crucially, this increased length is coupled with a higher average token-level reward, suggesting that the model is learning to produce more elaborate and meaningful exploratory steps while maintaining logical coherence and accuracy.
- **Nuance in Voting Mechanisms:** The optimal voting strategy appears to correlate with the model scale. For smaller backbones (e.g., Llama-3-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B), the stricter "Intersection Vote" provides the highest precision and leads to the best performance, guiding these models to learn robust foundational steps. For larger, more capable models (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B), "Majority Vote" or "Union Vote" performs best, offering a balance between precision and exploratory freedom to tackle more complex problems.
- **Ground Truth Impact:** Interestingly, incorporating ground-truth solutions for GenPRM evaluation does not always yield a significant advantage and can even slightly degrade general reasoning performance, potentially due to distributional discrepancies between GT and policy model outputs introducing noise.
- **Generative Process vs. Outcome Reward Models:** Experiments comparing LLM-as-GenPRM (CAPO) with Generative Outcome Reward Models (GenORM) show that GenORM exhibits less stable and beneficial scaling with an increased number of samples, underscoring the superior value of fine-grained, process-level feedback for model improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CAPO represents a significant advancement in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models through reinforcement learning. Its impact and significance can be outlined as follows:

- **Addressing a Fundamental Challenge in RL for LLMs:** The paper tackles the long-standing "credit assignment problem" in the context of LLM fine-tuning, which has been a major bottleneck for effectively applying RL to complex reasoning tasks. By providing a fine-grained, reliable, and efficient solution, CAPO clears a path for more sophisticated RL-based LLM training.
- **Simplifying and Democratizing LLM Alignment:** CAPO elegantly bypasses the need for costly manual process supervision or the complexities of training specialized auxiliary reward models. By leveraging powerful, off-the-shelf LLMs as critics, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners to conduct RL fine-tuning, making advanced LLM alignment techniques more accessible and reproducible.
- **Fostering Truly Intelligent Reasoning:** The shift from purely outcome-oriented to process-oriented learning is crucial for developing LLMs that not only provide correct answers but also derive them through sound, verifiable reasoning. This promotes a deeper understanding of problem-solving strategies within the model, reducing reliance on spurious correlations or "lucky" generations.
- **Enhancing Trustworthiness and Explainability:** By guiding models to learn correct reasoning pathways and pinpointing erroneous steps, CAPO contributes to building more robust and trustworthy AI systems. The improved process-level transparency can be a stepping stone towards more explainable AI, especially in sensitive domains where reasoning steps are as important as the final answer.
- **Broad Applicability and Generalization:** The method's effectiveness across various LLM backbones, scales, and both mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks demonstrates its broad applicability. This suggests that the principles of fine-grained credit assignment can generalize across different domains and model architectures, making it a versatile tool for diverse LLM applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** CAPO's framework, particularly its use of an LLM-as-GenPRM, opens several exciting avenues for future research, including dynamic step segmentation, advanced prompt engineering for critics, exploration of different voting mechanisms for varied model capabilities, and a deeper theoretical understanding of the interplay between outcome and process rewards.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The single-pass critique generation from the LLM-as-GenPRM, coupled with its flexibility for local or API-based execution, makes CAPO a computationally practical solution for integrating external knowledge-rich verification into online RL loops without prohibitive overhead.

In conclusion, CAPO offers a pragmatic, effective, and broadly applicable solution to a critical challenge in LLM reasoning. By improving the precision and reliability of how LLMs learn from their reasoning processes, this work contributes significantly to the development of more capable, trustworthy, and intelligent artificial general intelligence systems.
