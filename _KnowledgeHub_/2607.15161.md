# On-Policy Delta Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NAVER AI Lab introduce On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD^2), a method for post-training large language models that isolates and distills the specific reasoning capabilities acquired by a teacher model. OPD^2 achieves superior performance over existing on-policy distillation methods across diverse reasoning tasks, model sizes, and generation modes, notably improving math reasoning for a 1.7B parameter model by nearly 20 percentage points.

## Problem

- Conventional On-Policy Distillation (OPD) reward functions directly imitate a teacher model's entire output distribution, potentially transferring stylistic preferences rather than specific reasoning knowledge.
- The direct imitation signal in OPD may not optimally isolate and transfer the nuanced reasoning capabilities acquired by a teacher model during its dedicated post-training (reasoning tuning).
- Existing OPD methods often struggle with stability or can degrade performance when applied to already strong, reasoning-tuned models or when generalizing across different model architectures.

## Method

- The paper introduces a novel ==delta signal== for distillation, defined as the ==log probability difference== between a reasoning-tuned teacher model and its pre-reasoning-tuned base version, specifically isolating post-training knowledge.
- **On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD^2)** is proposed, integrating this delta signal into the ==on-policy distillation== framework.
- Reward design components, including ==centering== to normalize ==advantage functions== and a ==joint conditioning== mechanism (a ==sign-consistent common descent condition==), are implemented to ensure stable training by only applying updates when delta and conventional ==OPD== signals agree on the improvement direction.

## Results

- OPD^2 consistently outperformed standard OPD and ExOPD, achieving the highest average reasoning scores across Math, Code, and Science domains for all Qwen3 model sizes (**1.7B, 4B, 8B**) in both non-thinking and thinking modes.
- For the Qwen3-1.7B model, OPD^2 increased average math reasoning performance from **34.8%** to **54.6%**, exceeding standard OPD and ExOPD by **3.6** and **3.2 percentage points** respectively, with a **Qwen3-4B** model trained with OPD^2 surpassing **8B** models trained with other methods.
- OPD^2 demonstrated robustness by improving the Gemma4-E4B-it model's math performance from **60.6%** to **67.8%** (outperforming ExOPD by **2.5 points**) where other distillation methods often degraded performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "delta signal" effectively isolates and emphasizes the knowledge acquired during a teacher model's reasoning tuning, highlighting reasoning-connective words and providing more consistent negative feedback on incorrect reasoning steps.
> - Analyzing distillation signals at the token level reveals that the delta signal enhances explicit logical connections while suppressing vague expressions or generic problem-solving narration.
> - Combining the delta signal with centering and a sign-consistent common descent condition enables a stable and highly effective distillation process that can enhance even models with strong pre-existing reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses advancements in post-training methods for large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on improving reasoning capabilities. Traditionally, LLMs undergo pre-training through next-token prediction on vast datasets, followed by post-training stages like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to align them with specific downstream tasks and enhance abilities such as reasoning and chat.

On-Policy Distillation (OPD) has emerged as an alternative post-training method, offering benefits over traditional RL. OPD functions similarly to knowledge distillation (KD) but is adapted for generative LLMs by operating "on-policy." Unlike sequence-level KD (which is akin to SFT on teacher-generated data and can suffer from exposure bias), on-policy distillation involves the student model generating its own sequences (rollouts). A high-performing teacher model then scores the sampled tokens, providing dense, token-level supervision. This approach is intended to mitigate concerns associated with reward design in RL and the noise in RL signals, while also potentially reducing computational costs. Previous studies have demonstrated OPD's competitiveness with RL in post-training reasoning.

Despite its growing adoption and benefits, the fundamental design of OPD, particularly its reward function, has been identified as underexplored. Conventional OPD rewards are typically based on the log probability difference between the teacher and student models. This paper posits that such a direct imitation of the teacher's output might not optimally transfer the specific "reasoning capabilities" that the teacher acquired during its own post-training (reasoning tuning). Instead, it argues that a teacher model's output also reflects its "natural preferences and stylistic tendencies" inherited from pre-training, which may not be necessary or desirable to transfer for reasoning performance. This work proposes to refine the distillation signal to more precisely target the knowledge gained during the teacher's reasoning tuning phase.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that existing On-Policy Distillation (OPD) methods, while effective, rely on a distillation signal that may not optimally capture the specific reasoning knowledge acquired by a teacher model during its post-training phase. A teacher model, having undergone pre-training and subsequent reasoning tuning, embodies both foundational linguistic patterns and advanced reasoning capabilities. The conventional OPD reward, which computes the log probability difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher and the student, is hypothesized to distill the teacher's entire output distribution, including aspects unrelated to reasoning enhancement. This broader imitation could lead to the student acquiring natural preferences or stylistic tendencies that do not directly contribute to improved reasoning performance.

Recognizing this potential inefficiency, the key objectives of this paper are:

1. **Introduce a Novel Distillation Signal**: The core objective is to define and utilize a new distillation reward, termed the "delta signal." This signal is conceived as the difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher model and its base model (the same model prior to any post-training or reasoning tuning). The hypothesis is that this delta signal specifically isolates and highlights the changes induced by the reasoning tuning process, thus providing a more direct and targeted learning signal for transferring reasoning capabilities to the student model.

2. **Develop On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD$^2$)**: Based on the delta signal, the paper aims to propose a new on-policy distillation method, OPD$^2$. This involves integrating the delta signal into the on-policy distillation framework as the primary learning objective, moving beyond direct imitation of the teacher's final output.

3. **Design Robust Reward Mechanisms**: To ensure effective and stable utilization of the delta signal, the research aims to introduce two complementary reward design components:
 - **Centering**: This involves subtracting the expected rewards from the policy model's sampling probabilities. This technique is intended to normalize the delta signal, making its direction and magnitude more interpretable and stable during training, especially since the delta signal does not inherently possess the zero-centered property of conventional OPD rewards.
 - **Joint Conditioning**: To address potential convergence stability issues arising from the delta signal (which, unlike traditional OPD rewards, does not intrinsically consider the student's probability and could theoretically lead to unstable one-hot convergence in extreme scenarios), a joint condition is proposed. This condition ensures that learning updates only occur when both the delta signal and the original OPD signal indicate a consistent direction of improvement, thereby stabilizing the training process and aligning the student's learning trajectory with the teacher's intent while leveraging the specific reasoning knowledge from the delta signal.

4. **Empirical Validation**: A significant objective is to empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of OPD$^2$. This involves extensive experimentation across diverse reasoning domains (mathematics, science, and code), various LLM sizes (from 1.7B to 8B parameters), and different operational modes (non-thinking and thinking modes). The performance of OPD$^2$ is to be compared against conventional OPD and other advanced on-policy distillation methods to validate its consistent superiority and the benefits of the delta signal.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on defining and applying a novel "delta signal" for on-policy distillation, coupled with specific reward design strategies to ensure stable and effective training.

**Core Concept: Delta Signal for Distillation**
The paper defines the standard OPD reward for the t-th token y$_t$ as R$_{OPD_t}$ = log π$^*$(y$_t$ | x, y$_{<t}$) − log π$_\theta$(y$_t$ | x, y$_{<t}$), where π$^*$ is the teacher model and π$_\theta$ is the student model. The proposed "delta signal" is defined as R$_\Delta$$_t$ = log π$^*$(y$_t$ | x, y$_{<t}$) − log π$^*_{base}$(y$_t$ | x, y$_{<t}$), where π$^*_{base}$ is the teacher model prior to its post-training (reasoning tuning). This signal is designed to isolate the knowledge acquired by the teacher during its reasoning tuning.

**Analysis of Delta Signal Characteristics**
To provide intuition and validate the properties of the delta signal, three analytical methods are employed:
1. **Word Clouds**: Word clouds are generated for OPD, Base (log π$^*_{base}$ - log π$_\theta$), and Delta signals. This visualization illustrates the types of words that receive strong positive or negative signals. The analysis suggests that the delta signal emphasizes "reasoning-connective words" (e.g., "hence," "however") while suppressing words related to exploration or verification (e.g., "see," "try") that are also present in the base model's preferences.
2. **Token-level Signal Visualizations**: The paper visualizes token-level reward signals for OPD and ∆ on simple reasoning questions with intentionally incorrect reasoning. This demonstrates that ∆ tends to provide more consistently negative signals on incorrect reasoning parts compared to OPD, which might sometimes yield positive signals due to relative probabilities. This indicates ∆'s robustness in correlating with reasoning correctness.
3. **Statistical Analysis**: A statistical analysis is conducted on token-level signal changes when moving from OPD to ∆ across Math, Code, and Science domains. This quantifies that ∆ tends to enhance explicit logical connection words and suppress vague expressions of uncertainty or generic problem-solving narration.

**On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD$^2$) Reward Design**
To integrate the delta signal into a stable training framework, two reward design components are introduced:
1. **Centering**: To make the rewards bias-invariant and easier to handle, both OPD and delta signals are converted into advantage functions by subtracting the expected reward under the on-policy distribution. A$_{OPD_t}$ and A$_\Delta$$_t$ are computed, with expected rewards calculated over the top-k (k=1024) tokens of the student's policy for computational efficiency.
2. **Joint Conditioning**: To address the potential instability of R$_\Delta$ (which does not inherently include the student's probability and could lead to trivial convergence), a condition A$_{D^2_t}$ = A$_\Delta$$_t$ if A$_\Delta$$_t$ * A$_{OPD_t}$ > 0, otherwise 0, is applied. This "sign-consistent common descent" condition ensures that gradients are only computed when both the delta signal and the original OPD signal agree on the direction of update, preventing over-training by the trace signal and maintaining alignment with the teacher's overall signal. This effectively sets a convergence point where no gradients are computed if the student matches the teacher.

**Training Framework**
The student network is trained using an RL-like gradient computation: ∇$_\theta$ J$_{OPD^2}$ (θ) = E$_{x\sim D, y\sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|x)}$ [Σ$_{t=1}^T$ A$_{D^2_t}$ ∇$_\theta$ log π$_\theta$(y$_t$ | x, y$_{<t}$)]. This setup leverages optimized on-policy training codes and settings typically used in RL.

**Experimental Setup**
- **Models**: Qwen3 (1.7B, 4B, 8B) and Gemma4-E4B-it are used as student models. Larger models from the same families serve as teachers, and their base (pre-instruction-tuned) versions are used as teacher-base models. Both non-thinking and thinking modes are evaluated for Qwen3, while only thinking mode is used for Gemma4.
- **Training Data**: A mixed-domain training set of 100,000 questions is constructed by sampling equally from OpenMathReasoning, OpenScienceReasoning-2, and OpenCodeReasoning datasets. Only questions are used, discarding provided reasoning traces and answers.
- **Training Settings**: Implementation is based on TRL's GRPOTrainer. Training involves 100 optimization steps, 1 completion per question, token-level signals, a temperature of 0.7, AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 5 × 10$^{-6}$, cosine LR decay, and gradient clipping at 1.0. Rewards are uniformly scaled by 0.1 to prevent excessive clipping.
- **Evaluation**: Models are evaluated on 14 benchmarks: 7 Math, 3 Science, and 4 Code benchmarks, using the pass@1 metric (single-sample accuracy averaged across repetitions). Performance is compared against the original models, standard OPD, and ExOPD (an advanced on-policy distillation method).

**Ablation Study**
An ablation study investigates the individual contributions of the delta signal, the joint condition, and the centering operation to the overall performance of OPD$^2$.

**Computational Analysis**
The additional computational cost introduced by OPD$^2$ (due to the teacher-base model forward pass) is measured and compared against standard OPD and ExOPD.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD$^2$) consistently outperforms conventional on-policy distillation methods across various models, domains, and generation modes.

#### 4.1. Reasoning Performance

- **Non-thinking Mode (Qwen3)**:
 - All on-policy distillation methods significantly improve the original Qwen3 models across Math, Code, and Science domains, indicating their effectiveness in enhancing relatively weaker non-thinking capabilities.
 - OPD$^2$ achieved the highest average performance across all model sizes (1.7B, 4B, 8B) and domains.
 - In Math, for Qwen3-1.7B, OPD$^2$ increased the average score from 34.8 to 54.6, outperforming OPD and ExOPD by 3.6 and 3.2 points, respectively. Similar gains were observed for 4B (45.8 to 70.3) and 8B (46.9 to 71.6). Notably, the Qwen3-4B model trained with OPD$^2$ surpassed the 8B models trained with OPD and ExOPD, suggesting that OPD$^2$ can provide benefits comparable to or exceeding a model size increase.
 - OPD$^2$ also consistently improved performance in Code (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B: 10.5 to 29.4) and Science (e.g., Qwen3-1.7B: 30.8 to 38.8), achieving the highest average scores for all model sizes.

- **Thinking Mode (Qwen3)**:
 - Improving models that already possess strong reasoning capabilities in thinking mode proved more challenging. Standard OPD often degraded performance, and ExOPD showed only limited or inconsistent improvements, frequently underperforming the original models in average scores for Math.
 - In contrast, OPD$^2$ consistently achieved the highest average performance across all model sizes and domains.
 - In Math, OPD$^2$ improved the original 1.7B, 4B, and 8B models by 3.5, 1.5, and 2.2 points, respectively. Significant gains were noted on challenging benchmarks like AIME25 and HMMT25.
 - In Code, OPD$^2$ improved average scores for 1.7B (29.3 to 40.4), 4B (48.7 to 50.8), and 8B (50.8 to 57.8) models.
 - In Science, OPD$^2$ achieved the best average performance for every model size, whereas other methods often reduced baseline performance. These results indicate OPD$^2$'s ability to enhance already strong reasoning capabilities without compromising overall performance.

- **Gemma4-E4B-it**:
 - On Gemma4, standard OPD substantially degraded performance across all domains, highlighting instability with strong models from different families. ExOPD alleviated this but showed inconsistent effectiveness.
 - OPD$^2$ achieved the best Math performance, improving the average score from 60.6 to 67.8 and outperforming ExOPD by 2.5 points.
 - In Code, while no distillation method surpassed the strong original baseline, OPD$^2$ retained substantially more of the original capability (49.5 average) than OPD (36.9) and ExOPD (45.1).
 - In Science, OPD$^2$ improved the average score from 47.0 to 48.8, achieving the best results on SuperGPQA and SciBench. These findings suggest that OPD$^2$ generalizes across model families and offers a more robust trade-off between improving specific capabilities and preserving existing strengths.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics

Performance trajectories during training for Qwen3-4B in non-thinking mode showed that all methods exhibit rapid initial performance gains. However, OPD and ExOPD often reached peak performance early and then plateaued or degraded. OPD$^2$, conversely, achieved a larger initial improvement and maintained a consistently higher performance throughout the training steps across Math, Code, and Science. This indicates a persistent advantage of OPD$^2$ over the entire training trajectory, not just an isolated peak.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study confirmed the critical role of the delta signal. Replacing the delta signal with the standard OPD signal resulted in the largest performance degradation in both non-thinking and thinking modes, confirming the delta signal as the primary source of OPD$^2$'s gains. Removing the agreement condition or the centering operation had comparatively smaller, though generally positive, effects, indicating their supplementary contributions to overall performance and stability.

#### 4.4. Computational Costs

OPD$^2$ introduces an additional computational overhead due to the required teacher-base model forward pass. This increased wall-clock training time by approximately 24-28% for Qwen3 models and 8% for Gemma4-E4B, relative to standard OPD. However, OPD$^2$'s computational cost remained comparable to ExOPD, and the overhead is considered modest within the typical short-training regimes characteristic of on-policy distillation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of On-Policy Delta Distillation (OPD$^2$) marks a notable development in the field of large language model post-training, particularly for reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Targeted Knowledge Transfer**: The core innovation, the "delta signal," represents a shift from simply imitating a teacher model's final output to explicitly distilling the knowledge gained during its reasoning tuning process. By focusing on the difference between the reasoning-tuned teacher and its base model, OPD$^2$ provides a more precise and relevant signal for transferring reasoning capabilities, potentially reducing the transfer of irrelevant or undesirable stylistic preferences. This targeted approach can lead to more efficient and effective learning for student models.

2. **Consistent Performance Improvement**: OPD$^2$ consistently demonstrates superior performance over conventional on-policy distillation and even advanced methods like ExOPD across diverse reasoning domains (Math, Science, Code), various model sizes (1.7B to 8B), and different generation modes (non-thinking and thinking). This robust improvement, especially in challenging scenarios where other methods might degrade performance (e.g., when trying to enhance already strong reasoning baselines or generalize to new model architectures), highlights its reliability and broad applicability.

3. **Enhanced Model Capabilities**: The ability of OPD$^2$ to significantly boost reasoning performance, even for models already possessing strong capabilities in "thinking mode," indicates its potential to further elevate the performance ceiling of specialized LLMs. The observation that a smaller model (e.g., Qwen3-4B with OPD$^2$) can surpass a larger model (e.g., Qwen3-8B with other methods) suggests that the method offers a pathway to achieve higher performance without necessarily relying solely on increased model scale.

4. **Deeper Understanding of Distillation Signals**: The detailed analyses (word clouds, token-level visualizations, statistical analysis) provide valuable insights into *what* specific linguistic and logical elements are being emphasized or suppressed by the delta signal. This contributes to a better understanding of the underlying mechanisms of knowledge distillation, guiding future research in designing more sophisticated and context-aware distillation objectives.

5. **Practical Efficiency**: While introducing a modest computational overhead compared to standard OPD, OPD$^2$'s training cost remains competitive with other advanced distillation methods, and its rapid convergence (most benefits achieved within a short training period) makes it a practical and efficient post-training solution for improving LLMs.

6. **Broader Research Direction**: This work establishes the delta signal as a viable and effective concept, potentially serving as an "important milestone" for the development of on-policy distillation. It encourages future research to explore other forms of differential signals or "learning traces" from teacher models, opening new avenues for designing more intelligent and context-aware knowledge transfer mechanisms in LLMs. The principles demonstrated by OPD$^2$ could be extended to other forms of skill transfer or alignment beyond reasoning.
