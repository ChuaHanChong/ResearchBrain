# URDF-Anything+: End-to-End Generation for Simulation-Ready Articulated Assets

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, University of Oxford, and University of Washington developed URDF-Anything+, an end-to-end autoregressive diffusion framework that generates simulation-ready URDF models directly from a single RGB image. This system improves geometric reconstruction quality, reduces joint parameter errors, and significantly lowers inference time, enabling robust sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Manually creating simulation-ready articulated object models with accurate geometry and kinematic structure is time-consuming and labor-intensive.
- Recovering these models directly from a single RGB image is complex due to partial and ambiguous visual cues for part geometry and kinematic structures.
- Prior multi-stage pipelines suffer from error accumulation, poor generalization beyond limited asset libraries, and computational inefficiency, often requiring extensive post-processing.

## Method

- **URDF-Anything+** is an end-to-end ==autoregressive diffusion== framework that generates URDF models directly from a single RGB image, unifying ==part geometry== and ==kinematic structure== generation.
- The system uses global visual features and a whole-object ==3D latent representation from TripoSG== as contextual conditioning.
- It iteratively generates each articulated part and its associated joint parameters from a ==shared latent== via a ==Diffusion Transformer==, allowing direct decoding of both 3D geometry and articulation attributes.

## Results

- Achieved improved geometric reconstruction with a Part IoU of **0.879** (vs. **0.784** for PhysX-Anything) and a Whole Object Chamfer Distance of **0.009** (vs. **0.068**).
- Demonstrated lower joint parameter errors, including a Joint Axis Error of **0.129 rad** (vs. **0.191 rad**) and Joint Origin Error of **0.062 m** (vs. **0.103 m**) compared to PhysX-Anything.
- Reduced average inference time to **34.70 seconds** per part and enabled robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving **100%** success for laptop and **90%** for drawer tasks in the "Real-Follow-Sim" paradigm, outperforming conventional sim-to-real.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling part geometry and kinematic structure within a structured latent space improves consistency and accuracy, especially in fine-grained geometry and global structural relations.
> - An autoregressive generation approach with a deterministic, physically meaningful link ordering (immobile base first, then sorted movable parts) facilitates learning dependencies among articulated components.
> - Integrating global 3D guidance alongside image input is crucial for inferring precise geometry and accurate joint placement for complex articulated objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, physics-based simulation, and interactive virtual environments relies on simulation-ready articulated object models. These models, often represented in formats such as URDF or XML, require both accurate geometry and precise kinematic structure to support physical interaction and control. However, the manual construction of such assets is time-consuming. A significant challenge in this area involves recovering these articulated object models directly from visual observations, typically a single RGB image. This task is inherently complex due to the partial and ambiguous nature of visual cues regarding both part geometry and underlying kinematic structures.

Prior research approaches to this problem generally fall into several categories:
1. **Multi-stage pipelines**: These methods often decouple the tasks of segmentation and articulation. For instance, some approaches might first segment an object into parts and then infer articulation parameters in subsequent steps. This decoupling can lead to error accumulation across stages.
2. **Retrieval-based methods**: Some systems rely on retrieving part meshes from pre-existing asset libraries and then fitting articulation on top of these retrieved components. The limitation of this approach is its dependence on the coverage of the asset library, which can restrict generalization to novel object categories or geometries.
3. **Vision-language model (VLM) integration**: Certain methods delegate part segmentation to VLMs before synthesizing geometry and joints. While leveraging powerful language understanding, these pipelines can still be complex and prone to inconsistencies if segmentation and articulation are not tightly integrated.
4. **Point cloud-based methods**: Another line of work involves segmenting part-level point clouds and subsequently generating joint parameters using separate model heads.

The common limitations across these prior methods include the propagation of errors through multi-stage pipelines, poor generalization beyond categories well-represented in retrieval libraries, and computational inefficiency due to complex sequential processing or reliance on large external models.

URDF-Anything+ addresses these limitations by introducing an end-to-end autoregressive diffusion framework. This framework aims to generate simulation-ready URDF models directly from a single RGB image, integrating both part geometry and kinematic structure generation into a unified process. This approach seeks to simplify the overall pipeline, improve accuracy by jointly modeling coupled subproblems, and enhance efficiency, positioning it as an advancement in the direct visual-to-simulation asset generation paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of URDF-Anything+ is to generate complete, simulation-ready URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) models of articulated objects directly from a single RGB image. These generated models explicitly represent part geometries and their kinematic relationships, enabling their direct instantiation in standard physics simulators without manual intervention or further processing.

The key specific objectives and motivations underpinning this work are:

- **End-to-End Generation**: A central objective is to move away from traditional multi-stage pipelines that separate object segmentation, geometry reconstruction, and articulation parameter inference. The motivation is that decoupling these processes can lead to error accumulation and inconsistencies, particularly when dealing with complex or ambiguous visual inputs. URDF-Anything+ aims to unify these steps into a single forward pass, providing a more coherent and robust generation process.
- **Joint Modeling of Geometry and Articulation**: The work is motivated by the understanding that part geometry and kinematic structure are intrinsically linked. Recovering one accurately often requires consideration of the other. The objective is to jointly model both aspects within a structured latent space, allowing the model to predict part geometries and their associated joint parameters concurrently. This approach is intended to ensure global consistency in shape and kinematics across the entire object.
- **Autoregressive Part Generation**: Articulated objects are inherently compositional. The model is designed to sequentially predict each articulated part along with its associated joint parameters. This autoregressive paradigm is motivated by its ability to naturally build complex structures incrementally, leveraging context from previously generated parts to inform subsequent predictions. This sequential generation also enables dynamic determination of the number of parts through a termination token.
- **Direct URDF Executability**: A core motivation is to produce models that are immediately usable in physics engines (e.g., for robotic manipulation or simulation). Existing methods often require external retrieval or post-processing stages to convert generated components into a fully executable URDF. URDF-Anything+ aims to eliminate these steps, directly generating all necessary URDF specifications (link geometries, joint origins, axes, types, and motion limits).
- **Improved Accuracy and Efficiency**: The authors seek to surpass the performance of prior methods in geometric reconstruction quality, joint parameter estimation accuracy, and the rate of physically executable URDFs. Furthermore, a significant motivation is to achieve this with substantially greater computational efficiency, reducing the inference time compared to existing multi-stage approaches which can incur high overhead.
- **Facilitating Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: A practical motivation is to create "faithful digital twins" that can bridge the simulation-to-reality gap for robotic tasks. By generating accurate and simulation-ready URDFs, the work aims to enable manipulation policies trained purely in simulation to transfer directly (zero-shot) to real robots, thereby accelerating the development and deployment of robotic learning systems.

In summary, the work is motivated by the need for scalable, accurate, and efficient methods for creating articulated object models from visual data, addressing the limitations of prior fragmented approaches, and ultimately supporting advanced applications in robotics and simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

URDF-Anything+ employs an end-to-end autoregressive diffusion framework to generate simulation-ready URDF models from a single RGB image. The methodology involves several interconnected stages, starting from image input and culminating in a fully specified URDF file.

**Overall Pipeline:**
Given a single RGB image (I), the system first reconstructs a complete 3D model of the object. It then autoregressively decomposes this object into rigid links, simultaneously inferring their associated articulation parameters.

**Initial Feature Extraction and Global Geometry Reconstruction:**
1. **Visual Features:** A pretrained DINOv3 encoder is used to extract global visual features (f_I) from the input RGB image.
2. **Whole Object Geometry:** An off-the-shelf 3D reconstruction model, TripoSG, is utilized to reconstruct the full object-level 3D geometry (X_whole) from the input image. This mesh is then encoded into a compact global 3D latent representation (z_whole) using TripoSG's encoder. The pair (f_I, z_whole) serves as the global context for subsequent autoregressive generation.

**Autoregressive Articulated Model Generation:**
The core of the method is an iterative process where, at each step *k*, the model generates a single articulated part and its associated joint parameters.
1. **Autoregressive Context:** The generation of the *k*-th part is conditioned on the global context (f_I, z_whole) and the accumulated context from all previously generated parts (z_[1:k-1]_pre).
2. **Latent Diffusion with Shared Latent:**
 - A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model operates in a structured latent 3D space.
 - At each step *k*, the DiT takes a noisy latent (z_t) and the conditioning tokens (derived from f_I, z_whole, and z_[1:k-1]_pre). It then predicts a denoising velocity field.
 - Running the reverse diffusion process from Gaussian noise yields a "shared latent" (z_shared^(k)). This shared latent is designed to aggregate global geometric structure and contextual information relevant to the current part.
3. **Geometry Latent and 3D Decoding:**
 - The geometry latent (z_3D^(k)) for the *k*-th part is obtained by projecting the shared latent (z_shared^(k)) through a dedicated 3D head (h_3D).
 - The actual 3D part geometry (X^(k)) is then reconstructed from an implicit Signed Distance Function (SDF) produced by the TripoSG decoder (D), using marching cubes.
4. **Articulation Parameter Decoding:**
 - Articulation parameters (J^(k)) are predicted directly from the shared latent (z_shared^(k)) without an additional diffusion step.
 - Multiple lightweight MLP heads are employed, each responsible for predicting a specific joint attribute: joint origin (o^(k) via h_orig), axis (a^(k) via h_axis), motion limits (ℓ_low^(k), ℓ_up^(k) via h_low,up), and joint type (τ^(k) via h_type).
 - This direct decoding from the shared latent ensures that joint parameters are geometrically consistent with the generated part.
5. **Re-encoding and Autoregressive Context Update:**
 - After generating the *k*-th part, it is merged with all previously generated geometries to form a combined surface (X_[1:k] = ∪_(j=1)^k X^(j)).
 - A point cloud (P_[1:k]) is uniformly sampled from this merged surface and re-encoded using TripoSG's encoder (E) to update the autoregressive context (z_[1:k]_pre) for the next step.
6. **Termination Condition:** The autoregressive generation continues until a termination condition is met. The model is trained to predict an End-of-Token ([EoT]) latent. If the distance between the predicted latent and the [EoT] latent falls below a predefined threshold, or if a geometric redundancy check (e.g., excessive overlap with a previous part) is triggered, the generation process ceases.

**Training Details:**
- **Two-stage Training:**
 - **Stage 1 (Pretraining):** The model is trained for 500 epochs using only image and part-geometry supervision. This stage excludes the [EoT] signal and URDF-based articulation annotations, focusing on learning robust latent geometry representations and autoregressive part composition.
 - **Stage 2 (Fine-tuning):** Training continues for an additional 100 epochs, enabling the [EoT] signal and URDF-based articulation annotations. This stage allows the model to jointly learn part termination, articulation parameter prediction, and geometry generation.
- **Loss Function:** The total loss includes the latent diffusion objective (L_diff), an End-of-Token prediction loss (L_EOT), and an aggregated joint parameter prediction loss (L_joint). L_joint uses ℓ1 loss for continuous parameters (origin, axis, limits) and categorical cross-entropy for joint type. An axis-aware masking is applied to the origin ℓ1 loss to focus supervision on physically meaningful degrees of freedom.
- **Data Preparation:** The training data is curated from PartNet, PartNet-Mobility, and PhysX-3D, with extensive cleaning and filtering. Assets are normalized to a consistent scale and position. A "mesh thickening" preprocessing step is applied to single-layer surfaces to ensure stable normal estimation and reliable 3D encoding. Rendered images (using Qwen-Image-Edit) are augmented to improve generalization to real-world visual inputs.
- **Link Ordering:** During training, a deterministic ordering of links is used: immobile components first, followed by movable parts sorted lexicographically based on their joint origin coordinates (ZXY). This consistent ordering is critical for autoregressive learning of dependencies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of URDF-Anything+ focused on geometric quality, joint parameter accuracy, inference efficiency, and real-world applicability through a novel deployment scheme.

**Geometric Quality Evaluation:**
URDF-Anything+ was compared against state-of-the-art methods, including URDFormer, Articulate-Anything, SINGAPO, and PhysX-Anything, using standard geometric metrics: Intersection over Union (IoU), F-score, and Chamfer Distance (CD).
- **Quantitative Results:** The method achieved higher IoU and F-score, and a lower CD, for both individual parts and the whole object geometry. For instance, URDF-Anything+ recorded a Part IoU of 0.879 (vs. 0.784 for PhysX-Anything) and a Whole Object CD of 0.009 (vs. 0.068 for PhysX-Anything). This indicates improved surface reconstruction fidelity and geometric completeness. The results suggest that the joint generation of parts and joints from a shared latent contributes to more accurate fine-grained geometry and global structural consistency.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons demonstrated that URDF-Anything+ produced cleaner part boundaries and more consistent kinematic structures across various object categories, including those with complex geometries (e.g., faucets, refrigerators). Baseline methods often exhibited limitations such as failing on shapes outside their retrieval libraries, inconsistent joint relations (e.g., misaligned axes), or mispredicted part assemblies.

**Joint Parameter Prediction Accuracy:**
The accuracy of joint parameter prediction was evaluated for revolute joints, measuring errors in joint axis, origin, and limits.
- **Quantitative Results:** URDF-Anything+ consistently achieved lower errors across all reported joint parameters compared to baselines. It exhibited a Joint Axis Error of 0.129 rad (vs. 0.191 rad for PhysX-Anything), a Joint Origin Error of 0.062 m (vs. 0.103 m for PhysX-Anything), and a Joint Limit Error of 0.225 rad (vs. 0.532 rad for PhysX-Anything). These reductions in error indicate more precise recovery of the kinematic configuration, particularly concerning joint geometry and motion constraints. The end-to-end framework and shared-latent articulation decoding were identified as contributing factors to this improved performance.

**Inference Efficiency:**
The computational cost of generating articulated object models was measured.
- **Results:** URDF-Anything+ demonstrated significantly lower average inference time per generated part (34.70 s) compared to previous multi-stage pipelines. For example, Articulate-Anything required 380.57 s, and PhysX-Anything required 578.38 s. This efficiency gain is attributed to its single-pass, end-to-end design, which avoids the overhead of repeated API calls, external retrieval, and symbolic reasoning inherent in multi-stage approaches.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Input Modalities:** Comparing models trained with "Image Only" versus "Image + 3D Guidance" (using the faucet category), the combination of image and 3D inputs led to superior performance across all geometric and joint accuracy metrics. This suggests that global 3D guidance is beneficial for inferring fine-grained geometry and accurate joint placement, especially for complex structures.
- **Effect of Link Ordering:** An ablation on link ordering (using the refrigerator category) revealed that a spatially consistent ordering (immobile base first, then movable parts sorted by joint XYZ coordinates) resulted in more accurate geometry reconstruction and joint parameter prediction compared to random ordering. This indicates that a stable and physically meaningful ordering facilitates the learning of dependencies among articulated parts in the autoregressive generation process.

**Real-World Experiment (Real-Follow-Sim):**
A novel deployment scheme, Real-Follow-Sim, was introduced and evaluated using a Franka robotic arm for tasks like closing a laptop lid and a drawer.
- **Real-Follow-Sim vs. Sim-to-Real:** The Real-Follow-Sim paradigm, where the simulator is dynamically aligned to the real scene using URDF-Anything+ reconstructions, achieved higher success rates (100% for laptop, 90% for drawer) compared to conventional Sim-to-Real (80% for laptop, 75% for drawer). This indicates that dynamically aligning the simulation can effectively reduce the sim-to-real gap.
- **Comparison with Other URDF Generation Methods:** Within the Real-Follow-Sim framework, URDF-Anything+ demonstrated better real-world robot performance than Articulate-Anything.
- **Comparison with Real Data Training:** Policies trained purely in simulation using URDF-Anything+-generated digital twins achieved comparable or better performance (100% for laptop, 90% for drawer) than policies trained on an equivalent amount of real-world demonstration data (85% for laptop, 75% for drawer). This result suggests that the generated URDFs are faithful digital twins, enabling efficient simulation-only training for robust real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

URDF-Anything+ represents an advancement in the automated generation of simulation-ready articulated object models from visual inputs, with several significant implications:

- **Advancement in End-to-End Generation**: The introduction of an end-to-end autoregressive diffusion framework for joint geometry and articulation generation signifies a departure from multi-stage pipelines. This approach reduces error accumulation and simplifies the overall process, establishing a new baseline for accuracy and consistency in articulated object modeling from single images.
- **Improved Efficiency in Asset Creation**: By operating in a single forward pass and eliminating the need for external retrieval or extensive post-processing, URDF-Anything+ substantially reduces the computational cost and manual effort required to create complex, simulation-ready articulated assets. This efficiency enables faster iteration cycles in simulation development and robotic experimentation.
- **Enhanced Digital Twin Fidelity**: The generated URDFs demonstrate high fidelity in both geometric reconstruction and kinematic parameter estimation. This capability is critical for constructing accurate "digital twins" of real-world objects, which are essential for various applications including industrial design, virtual reality, and augmented reality.
- **Facilitating Robotic Learning and Deployment**: The most notable impact lies in its application to robotics through the "Real-Follow-Sim" paradigm. By dynamically generating accurate URDFs that align the simulator with the real world, URDF-Anything+ enables zero-shot transfer of policies trained purely in simulation to real robots. This addresses a major bottleneck in robotic learning—the sim-to-real gap—by allowing for rapid, data-efficient policy development in simulation without extensive real-world data collection. This could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems capable of manipulating a wider range of articulated objects.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework provides a robust foundation for further research in several areas:
 - **Scalable Digital Twin Construction**: The ability to generate accurate digital twins from single images at high speed points towards scalable creation of simulation environments for large-scale robotic learning.
 - **Free-form Articulated Object Generation**: The model's capacity to handle objects outside its direct training distribution suggests potential for generating novel or highly customized articulated structures.
 - **Integration of Additional Physics Properties**: Future work could extend the model to predict more complex physical properties such as mass distribution, friction coefficients, or material properties, further enhancing the realism and utility of the generated URDFs in physics simulations.
- **Broad Applicability Beyond Robotics**: Beyond robotics, the ability to generate high-quality, executable articulated models could benefit fields such as virtual content creation, animation, interactive gaming, and product design, where efficient and accurate 3D model generation from visual input is valuable.

While the work presents advancements, the identified limitations, such as the current reliance on existing datasets for articulated object diversity and the lack of explicit modeling for all complex physical properties, also highlight areas for future investigation and improvement.
