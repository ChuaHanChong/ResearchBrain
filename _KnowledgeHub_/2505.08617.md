# OpenThinkIMG: Learning to Think with Images via Visual Tool Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Soochow University, Microsoft, and other institutions developed OPENTHINKIMG, an open-source framework, and V-TOOLRL, a reinforcement learning method, enabling Large Vision-Language Models to adaptively use visual tools for complex reasoning tasks. This approach improved accuracy on chart reasoning by 29.83 points over baseline models and outperformed GPT-4.1, teaching agents to efficiently invoke tools with visual feedback.

## Problem

- Current Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) predominantly rely on textual reasoning, even for visual tasks, contrasting with human-like active manipulation of visual information.
- Existing tool-augmented multimodal reasoning approaches suffer from heterogeneous tool definitions, high costs in generating tool-use trajectories, and limited policy generalization from static supervised fine-tuning.
- The absence of a unified framework complicates the integration of diverse visual tools and hinders systematic development and reproducibility.

## Method

- Introduces ==OPENTHINKIMG==, a comprehensive open-source framework with a unified registry for seamlessly integrating diverse vision tools (e.g., GROUNDINGDINO, SAM, OCR) and LVLM backbones.
- Proposes ==V-TOOLRL==, a novel reinforcement learning paradigm that combines a ==supervised fine-tuning cold-start== with ==Group-wise Proximal Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to learn adaptive tool-usage policies using visual feedback.
- Develops a scalable ==three-stage pipeline== for constructing high-quality vision tool-use trajectories, leveraging GPT-4o for planning, batch tool invocation, and multi-stage filtering with rule-based validation.

## Results

- V-TOOLRL achieved **59.39%** accuracy on the CHARTGEMMA test set, outperforming open-source baselines like TACO-8B (**30.50%**) and COGCOM-13B (**15.07%**), and closed-source GPT-4.1 (**50.71%**).
- The full V-TOOLRL framework provided a **+29.83** point accuracy improvement over the Qwen2-VL-2B base model and a **+7.76** point gain over a text-based RL approach, highlighting the impact of visual feedback.
- During V-TOOLRL training, the average number of tool calls per sample decreased from approximately **0.63** to **0.10-0.12**, while the average output length increased from **~66** tokens to **83-86** tokens, indicating greater efficiency and reasoning depth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct integration of visual tool outputs as feedback into the reinforcement learning process (V-TOOLRL) is crucial for maximizing performance on visually grounded tasks, significantly outperforming text-only RL.
> - Reinforcement learning enables LVLMs to learn highly efficient and parsimonious tool invocation strategies, substantially reducing the number of unnecessary tool calls over time while increasing reasoning complexity.
> - The framework and RL approach equip LVLMs with adaptive behaviors for dynamic tool invocation, moving beyond static demonstrations to foster human-like interactive visual cognition.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has witnessed remarkable progress in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), enabling agents to jointly reason over visual and textual inputs. Techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting have significantly boosted performance across various multimodal tasks, including visual question answering, mathematical reasoning, and image captioning. However, a fundamental limitation persists: most current LVLMs predominantly rely on textual intermediate reasoning, even when confronting problems that are inherently visual.

This contrasts sharply with human cognition. Humans actively "think with images," employing visual tools such as sketches, highlights, and spatial cues to externalize, decompose, and manipulate complex visual information. For instance, a person solving a geometry problem might draw auxiliary lines on a diagram to reveal hidden relationships. This biological mechanism motivates the integration of visual tools into AI reasoning, allowing models to iteratively manipulate and interpret visual content, thereby fostering a more grounded and interpretable decision-making process.

Recent research has begun to explore tool-augmented multimodal reasoning, equipping AI agents with the ability to interact with external visual tools and compose intermediate visual representations. These efforts, often relying on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using synthetic supervision and orchestrated tool-use sequences from static datasets, have demonstrated the potential of tool integration. However, the authors identify several critical gaps in these existing SFT-centric approaches:
1. **Heterogeneous tool definitions and interfaces:** A lack of standardization across different tool implementations hinders reproducibility and broad adoption.
2. **High cost of trajectory generation:** Producing high-quality training data for tool-based reasoning is resource-intensive and often relies on brittle heuristics, limiting scalability.
3. **Limited training generalization:** SFT on static demonstrations struggles to generalize to dynamic tool invocation, unseen tools, or novel tasks, lacking mechanisms for exploration and adaptive behavior.

OPENTHINKIMG directly addresses these challenges by offering an end-to-end framework that unifies tool management, scalable data generation, and robust agent training. Furthermore, by introducing V-TOOLRL, a novel reinforcement learning framework, the paper pushes beyond the limitations of SFT, enabling LVLMs to autonomously discover optimal tool-usage strategies through direct optimization for task success, thereby moving towards truly adaptive and interactive visual reasoning. This work positions itself as a critical advancement in moving LVLMs from mere pattern recognition to deeper, human-like visual manipulation and scaffolded thought processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to bridge the gap between current LVLM capabilities and human-like interactive visual cognition for complex problem-solving. While humans effortlessly leverage visual tools to externalize and manipulate information, LVLMs struggle with adaptive tool usage in visual contexts.

The authors are primarily motivated by the inherent limitations of existing LVLM approaches that predominantly rely on textual chain-of-thought reasoning, even for visually-driven tasks. They argue that this paradigm neglects the rich potential of direct visual interaction and manipulation. This leads to the following specific challenges that the paper aims to address:
- **Lack of Standardized Infrastructure:** The absence of a unified framework complicates the integration of diverse visual tools, hindering reproducibility and systematic development.
- **Inefficient Data Generation:** Current methods for generating tool-use interaction data are often resource-intensive and lack scalability, making it difficult to create rich, diverse datasets necessary for training robust agents.
- **Limited Policy Generalization:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on static demonstrations, while useful for initial learning, fails to equip LVLMs with the adaptive behaviors required for dynamic and context-sensitive tool invocation.

To overcome these hurdles, the paper sets forth two key objectives:
1. **Develop a Comprehensive, Open-Source Framework (OPENTHINKIMG):** To provide a standardized and extensible infrastructure that facilitates the integration of various vision tools, scalable generation of tool-use trajectories, and efficient training of multimodal agents. This framework is designed to serve as a foundational platform for the community.
2. **Propose a Novel Reinforcement Learning Paradigm (V-TOOLRL):** To enable LVLMs to learn adaptive policies for invoking external vision tools autonomously. V-TOOLRL aims to move beyond static demonstrations, allowing models to discover optimal tool-usage strategies by directly optimizing for task success using feedback from real-time tool interactions.

Ultimately, the motivation is to empower AI agents to "think with images" – to iteratively manipulate and interpret visual content, leading to more grounded, interpretable, and adaptable decision-making pathways, especially for tasks demanding fine-grained spatial understanding and iterative perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper centers around OPENTHINKIMG, a comprehensive, open-source, end-to-end framework, and V-TOOLRL, a novel reinforcement learning approach tailored for adaptive visual tool usage.

#### 3.1. OPENTHINKIMG Framework

OPENTHINKIMG is designed to streamline the integration of vision tools, scale trajectory synthesis, and support efficient agent training. Its architecture encompasses three main parts:
- **Vision Tools and Models Integration:** The framework provides a unified registry for seamlessly integrating various vision tools and LVLM backbone models. It includes a curated selection of tools crucial for visual reasoning:
 - **GROUNDINGDINO:** For text-driven object detection (e.g., "Where is X?").
 - **SAM (Segment Anything Model):** For precise, object-agnostic segmentation.
 - **OCR (Optical Character Recognition):** To extract and understand text from images.
 - **CROP:** To focus processing on specific image sub-regions.
 - **POINT:** To precisely identify locations or objects by description.
 - **DRAWHORIZONTALLINEBYY / DRAWVERTICALLINEBYX:** To add reference markers for visual aid (e.g., in charts).
 - **ZOOMINSUBPLOT:** To create magnified views of complex visual areas.
 - **SEGMENTREGIONAROUNDPOINT:** To refine local segmentation around a point.
 The framework supports popular LVLM backbones like Gemini, ChatGPT, Qwen-2VL, and Qwen-2.5VL, loaded via the Transformers library or OpenAI repository.
- **Vision Tool Deployment and Inference:** A key design choice is the distributed deployment of vision tools as independent, containerized services. This modularity enhances scalability, fault isolation, and enables independent updates. A central "Tool Controller" orchestrates the entire tool invocation lifecycle. When an LVLM requires tool assistance, it formulates a planned action. The Tool Controller parses, dispatches the request to the corresponding service, aggregates outputs, and augments the LVLM's reasoning context, enabling an iterative problem-solving process.

#### 3.2. V-TOOLRL: Reinforcement Learning with Vision Tools

V-TOOLRL is proposed to enable LVLMs to strategically leverage the toolset. It consists of two modules:
- **Cold-Start for Vision Tool Invocation:** An initial phase involving Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on batch-generated tool-use trajectories. The model learns to generate action-output sequences conditioned on a question and image, optimizing a cross-entropy loss. This provides a basic policy for sequential tool invocation.
- **Reinforcement Learning for Adaptive Tool Usage:** This module employs the Group-wise Proximal Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, extended for vision-tool rollouts. The agent samples candidate action trajectories, executes them via the vision tools to obtain outcomes, and receives a rule-based accuracy reward (+1 for correct, -1 otherwise) based on the final answer. The GRPO objective is then optimized using group-relative advantages and a KL-penalty. Crucially, V-TOOLRL incorporates sampled tool outcomes (visual feedback) into the state and reward computation, allowing the model to learn an adaptive policy for selecting and sequencing vision tools dynamically.

#### 3.3. Vision Trajectory Construction

High-quality tool-use trajectories are essential for both Cold-Start SFT and RL initialization. The authors propose a scalable, three-stage pipeline for this:
1. **Action Trajectory Planning:** GPT-4o, with few-shot task decomposition and five demonstration examples, generates initial symbolic action plans. Plans lacking essential steps or containing unsupported actions are rejected.
2. **Rationale Parsing and Tool Call Completion:** The symbolic plans are executed by batch invoking the vision tools via the tool server. Tool responses are parsed using JSON schema, and outputs are cached and processed in parallel. This yields complete (action, output) reasoning chains.
3. **Filtering and Rule-Based Validation:** A multi-stage filtering process ensures trajectory quality. It discards malformed data and uses QWEN2-VL-72B alongside rule-based checks (e.g., bounding-box consistency, OCR accuracy, logical consistency) to validate intermediate rationales and final answers. Human evaluation provides additional verification.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The methodology is validated on **chart reasoning tasks** using the CHARTGEMMA dataset (14,501 training, 1,000 test samples).
- **Training Data:** 1,471 tool-use trajectories (generated as above) augmented with text-based CoT data formed the Cold-Start dataset (total 2,942 examples). The full 14,501 training samples were used for V-TOOLRL.
- **Model Backbone:** Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct.
- **Training Details:** Utilized NVIDIA Tesla A100 GPUs, DeepSpeed Zero-Stage 3, and FlashAttention-2 for efficiency. Cold-Start involved 2 epochs with a learning rate of 2e-5; V-TOOLRL ran for 500 steps with an initial learning rate of 1e-6.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of OPENTHINKIMG and V-TOOLRL on challenging chart reasoning tasks yielded significant results, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed framework and reinforcement learning approach.

#### 4.1. Model Performance Comparison (Figure 2a)

- **Superiority over Open-Source Baselines:** V-TOOLRL achieved an accuracy of 59.39% on the CHARTGEMMA test set. This significantly surpassed other open-source tool-augmented frameworks, including TACO-8B (30.50%) and COGCOM-13B (15.07%). This outperformance is particularly striking given that V-TOOLRL utilizes a smaller 2B parameter Qwen2-VL base model, whereas TACO and COGCOM employ larger 8B and 13B models, respectively. This highlights the effectiveness of V-TOOLRL's adaptive tool selection and RL paradigm compared to supervised methods.
- **Competitiveness with Closed-Source Models:** V-TOOLRL not only boosted open-source model performance but also notably outperformed prominent closed-source models like GPT-4.1 (50.71%) by 8.68 accuracy points and achieved a competitive result against GEMINI (68.20%) on these complex visual reasoning tasks requiring structured tool interaction.

#### 4.2. Impact of Training Stages & Methods (Figure 2b)

An ablation study on the Qwen2-VL-2B backbone clearly demonstrated the incremental benefits of each V-TOOLRL component:
- **QWEN-BASE:** The foundational model without any fine-tuning scored 29.56%.
- **QWEN-SFT (Cold-Start):** Supervised fine-tuning on generated tool-use trajectories improved accuracy to 45.67%, representing a substantial +16.11 point gain and affirming the value of initial policy learning.
- **TEXT-BASED RL:** An RL agent trained without direct integration of visual tool outputs achieved 51.63%, indicating the general benefit of RL for sequential decision-making.
- **V-TOOLRL (Full Framework):** The full V-TOOLRL framework, incorporating visual feedback from tools into the RL process, achieved the highest accuracy at 59.39%. This constitutes a +29.83 point improvement over the base model, a +13.72 point gain over SFT alone, and a critical +7.76 point advantage over TEXT-BASED RL. This last point specifically underscores the importance of the "V" (visual) component—the direct integration of visual tool outputs—for maximizing performance on visually grounded tasks.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Tool Invocation Efficiency (Figure 3a)

During V-TOOLRL training, the average number of tool calls per sample decreased significantly, from approximately 0.63 calls per sample initially to stabilize at a remarkably low 0.10-0.12 calls per sample. This indicates that the reinforcement learning process effectively teaches the agent to be highly selective and efficient in invoking tools, implicitly penalizing superfluous or redundant actions and fostering a parsimonious tool-use strategy.

#### 4.4. Development of Reasoning Complexity (Figure 3b)

Concurrently, the average completion length of model outputs increased consistently during V-TOOLRL training, from around 66 tokens to 83-86 tokens. This suggests that as the agent becomes more proficient in tool usage, it also develops the capacity to generate more elaborate and detailed reasoning narratives, including comprehensive Chain-of-Thought steps and justifications, crucial for complex chart analysis.

#### 4.5. Learning Dynamics and Impact of Visual Feedback (Figure 3c)

A comparison of reward accuracy during training showed that V-TOOLRL consistently achieved higher accuracy and exhibited a steeper learning curve than the TEXT-BASED RL baseline. This persistent performance gap highlights the critical contribution of direct visual feedback from tool interactions to the reinforcement learning process, confirming its paramount importance for mastering visually grounded tasks.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Case Studies (Figure 4)

Qualitative examples demonstrated V-TOOLRL's superior reasoning accuracy and interpretability. In scenarios like pie chart analysis and line graph trend analysis, V-TOOLRL effectively decomposed complex queries into verifiable, tool-based subtasks (e.g., `ZOOMINSUBFIGURE`, `OCR`, `POINT`, `DRAWVERTICALLINEBYX`), leading to correct answers. In contrast, GPT-4.1, relying on direct visual interpretation without explicit grounding tools, sometimes misinterprets values or fails to discern subtle details, leading to incorrect conclusions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OPENTHINKIMG framework and the V-TOOLRL methodology represent a significant leap forward in equipping Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) with adaptive and interactive visual reasoning capabilities. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Bridging the Human-AI Cognition Gap:** By enabling LVLMs to "think with images" through tool-augmented visual cognition, this work moves AI agents closer to human-like problem-solving, which often involves active manipulation and interpretation of visual information rather than just passive observation. This opens doors for AI to tackle tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, iterative perception, and precise visual interaction.

2. **Standardization and Reproducibility:** OPENTHINKIMG addresses the critical challenge of heterogeneous tool definitions and interfaces by providing a unified, open-source framework. This standardization will foster greater reproducibility in research, lower the barrier to entry for developing tool-augmented LVLMs, and accelerate community-wide collaboration. The commitment to maintaining the code and resources publicly reinforces this potential.

3. **Advancing Adaptive AI Agents:** The V-TOOLRL framework, by leveraging reinforcement learning with direct visual feedback, enables LVLMs to learn adaptive policies for dynamic tool invocation. This goes beyond the limitations of supervised fine-tuning on static demonstrations, allowing agents to autonomously explore and discover optimal tool-usage strategies. This adaptability is crucial for real-world scenarios where predefined tool-use sequences may not suffice.

4. **Enhanced Performance and Interpretability:** The empirical results are compelling, showing V-TOOLRL significantly outperforms existing supervised tool-learning baselines and even state-of-the-art closed-source models like GPT-4.1 on complex chart reasoning tasks, despite using a smaller backbone model. Furthermore, the qualitative studies highlight that explicit, tool-based reasoning leads to more accurate and transparent decision-making, offering improved interpretability compared to opaque end-to-end models. This is vital for building trustworthy AI systems.

5. **Efficiency in Tool Usage:** The observed decrease in the average number of tool calls during V-TOOLRL training indicates that the agent learns to be more parsimonious and efficient in its tool invocation. This optimized resource utilization can lead to more cost-effective and faster reasoning, particularly in environments where tool calls might incur computational or monetary costs.

6. **Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed three-stage pipeline for constructing high-quality vision tool-use trajectories, leveraging models like GPT-4o for planning and rule-based validation, offers a scalable solution to the resource-intensive problem of creating diverse training data. This is crucial for training increasingly capable agents.

In conclusion, OPENTHINKIMG, coupled with V-TOOLRL, provides a foundational and empirically validated solution for advancing tool-augmented visual reasoning in LVLMs. Its open-source nature, coupled with its methodological innovations and strong performance, positions it as a valuable resource that can accelerate the development of next-generation multimodal AI agents truly capable of "thinking with images."
