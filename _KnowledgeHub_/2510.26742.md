# Running VLAs at Real-time Speed

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Dexmal and StepFun developed comprehensive optimization strategies enabling state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to operate in real-time on a single consumer-grade GPU. Their approach reduced inference latency for a two-view 
live
 
live
 model from 106.5 ms to 27.3 ms, achieving a 100% success rate in a falling pen grasping task with sub-200 ms reaction times.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like 
live
 
live
 suffered from high inference latency (hundreds of milliseconds), making them too slow for dynamic, real-time robotic tasks.
- Typical VLA inference speeds necessitated dropping frames from 30 FPS camera streams, impeding a robot's ability to react quickly and precisely to rapidly changing environments.
- The inherent latency created a gap between the strong generalization capabilities of large VLA models and their practical deployability in time-sensitive robotic applications.

## Method

- Implemented a multi-stage optimization strategy, including CUDA graph mechanisms and computational graph simplification (e.g., QKV projection fusion) to drastically reduce CPU and architectural overheads.
- Performed in-depth kernel optimization by manually tuning GEMM tile parameters, fusing gated linear layers, and integrating scalar operations and RMS normalization directly into kernels.
- Addressed system-level latencies through optimized image resizing, pinned memory for data transfer, and static CPU buffers, minimizing non-GPU related overheads.

## Results

- Reduced inference time for a two-view 
live
 
live
 model on an RTX 4090 GPU from 106.5 ms to 27.3 ms, representing a nearly 4x speedup and successfully meeting the sub-33 ms real-time threshold.
- Achieved a 100% success rate over 10 consecutive trials in a real-world falling pen grasping task, with the end-to-end reaction time of the robotic system measured at less than 200 ms.
- Demonstrated that the optimized two-view 
live
 
live
 inference at 27.3 ms is remarkably close to the theoretical lower bound of 20.6 ms, indicating high efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically tackling both high-level software overheads (e.g., Python interpreter, graph complexity) and low-level hardware utilization (e.g., kernel efficiency) is crucial for pushing large VLA models into real-time performance on consumer GPUs.
> - Significant performance gains can be achieved through a combination of CUDA graphs, computational graph transformations, and fine-grained kernel tuning via tools like Triton.
> - The optimized inference enables a
> - Full Streaming Inference
> - paradigm for future hierarchical control systems, allowing concurrent execution of VLM and Action Expert (AE) components at different frequencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial and widely recognized challenge in the rapidly evolving field of learning-based robotic control, specifically concerning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. In recent years, large, billion-parameter VLA models have demonstrated impressive generalization capabilities across diverse robotic manipulation tasks. Examples include models like $\pi^0$, $\pi^{0.5}$, MemoryVLA, and GeoVLA, all cited in this paper, which represent the cutting edge in leveraging multimodal data (vision and language) to drive complex robot actions.

However, a significant impediment to the widespread adoption and real-world utility of these powerful models is their inherent *latency*. A typical forward pass of these large VLA models can take hundreds of milliseconds, making them unsuitable for dynamic, real-time tasks that demand quick reaction times, such as catching a falling object or interacting with rapidly changing environments. This latency creates a substantial gap between the theoretical capabilities of these models (generalization, complex reasoning) and their practical deployability in systems that require sub-33ms (for 30 FPS camera streams) or even faster responses.

The broader research landscape for VLA models has largely focused on architectural innovation, training methodologies, and expanding generalization. This paper shifts focus towards the *efficiency and real-time performance* aspect, which is often considered an engineering challenge rather than a fundamental AI research problem. By demonstrating that $\pi^0$-level VLAs *can* indeed run at real-time speeds on a single consumer-grade GPU (RTX 4090), this work effectively bridges this gap. It pushes the boundaries of what is considered achievable for deploying large AI models in latency-critical robotic applications.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the landscape of GPU acceleration and optimization techniques. It discusses manual kernel design (cuBLAS, CUTLASS, Triton), scheduling-based approaches (TVM), and superoptimization methods (Mirage, Neptune). Unlike these methods, which often focus on individual operators or require extensive search, this work takes a comprehensive, end-to-end engineering approach across the entire VLA inference pipeline, from CPU overheads to specific kernel optimizations. The proposed "Full Streaming Inference" framework also contributes to the conceptual landscape by envisioning a hierarchical, multi-frequency control system driven by VLA components, addressing both low-latency reactive control and higher-level cognitive functions. This positions the work as not just an optimization feat, but also a conceptual advance in how VLAs can be integrated into holistic robot control architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the real-time execution of large, state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically the $\pi^0$-level multi-view VLA, on a single consumer-grade GPU. This objective is quantified by two key performance targets:
1. **30Hz Frame Rate:** Achieving an inference time of less than 33 milliseconds per frame, allowing continuous processing of all frames from a 30 FPS RGB video stream without dropping frames.
2. **Up to 480Hz Trajectory Frequency:** For the action expert component, supporting control signal generation at a significantly higher frequency, potentially enabling very fine-grained and rapid reactive control.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from a critical practical limitation of current large VLA models: their high inference latency. While these models excel in generalization and complex task execution, their typical forward pass times (often hundreds of milliseconds) make them unsuitable for dynamic and time-sensitive real-world robotic tasks. As highlighted in Figure 1 with the "grasping a falling pen" task, many robotic applications demand immediate and precise reactions. The delay inherent in slow VLA inference impedes a robot's ability to respond quickly to environmental changes, effectively rendering these powerful models impractical for a broad category of real-time control scenarios.

The authors note that even a slight delay, like exceeding 33ms, necessitates dropping frames, which can lead to increased latency if critical events occur during missed frames. Their motivation is to eliminate this bottleneck, thereby unlocking the full potential of large VLAs for real-time applications. By achieving human-level reaction times (e.g., <200ms end-to-end for the falling pen task), the research aims to demonstrate the viability of these sophisticated models in challenging, real-world environments. This effort is driven by the vision of intelligent robots that are not only capable of complex reasoning but also possess the agility and responsiveness required for dynamic interaction with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is a comprehensive, multi-stage engineering optimization strategy designed to drastically reduce the inference latency of $\pi^0$-level VLA models. The approach systematically tackles various sources of overhead, from high-level software issues to low-level hardware utilization.

#### 3.1. Eliminating Overheads (High-Level Optimization)

- **Removing CPU Overhead:** The initial step involves eliminating Python execution overhead, which is significant in models with thousands of kernel launches. The authors leverage the **CUDA graph mechanism**, recording the sequence of kernel launches once and replaying them purely on the GPU. This "Ahead-Of-Time (AOT)" compilation technique ensures that the Python interpreter does not introduce latency during repeated inferences, resulting in a roughly two-fold speedup.
- **Simplifying the Computational Graph:** This stage focuses on architectural transformations to reduce computational workload and kernel launches.
 - **RMS Norm Weight Fusion:** Affine parameters from RMS Normalization layers are absorbed into the weights of subsequent linear layers, reducing the number of operations.
 - **Action-Time Embedding Folding:** In the action expert, consecutive linear layers for action values are folded. For time embeddings (only 10 discrete steps), the results of the linear layer are precomputed and fused directly into the bias vector of the next operation, reducing both operators and MACs.
 - **QKV Projection Fusion:** The projection matrices for Query, Key, and Value are combined into a single larger matrix, transforming three smaller matrix multiplications into one larger one, which improves parallelization and reduces kernel launch overheads. RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) operations are also fused and precomputed.
- **Avoiding Other Overheads:** Addressing non-GPU related but crucial system-level latencies.
 - **Image Resizing Optimization:** Instead of general-purpose resizing libraries, the authors suggest using camera-native resolutions close to the target (e.g., 240x320 for 224x224) and carefully optimized, hand-written resize code (e.g., custom CUDA/C++ implementation for x86 CPUs), achieving sub-60us latency.
 - **System-level Best Practices:** Emphasizing the use of pinned memory for CPU-GPU data transfer, static CPU buffers to reduce jitter, and zero-copying for camera frames to minimize latency in the end-to-end system.

#### 3.2. In-Depth Kernel Optimization (Low-Level Optimization)

- **Tuning GEMM Tile Parameters:** Instead of relying on default cuBLAS dispatch, the authors manually tuned tiling strategies for critical General Matrix Multiply (GEMM) operations using a Triton implementation. This ensures optimal configuration for specific matrix dimensions, yielding performance gains.
- **Fusing Gated Linear Layers:** The FFN (Feed-Forward Network) in transformers uses a gated up-projection. The two parallel matrix multiplications (e.g., for `F C_1` and `F C_2` in `F C_1(x, w_1) · GELU(F C_2(x, w_2))`) are fused into a single kernel. This allows concurrent execution, coalesced memory loads, and writing only the combined result, improving tensor utilization and reducing memory operations.
- **Partial Split-K:** For specific problematic GEMM dimensions (e.g., 512x1152x1152), the authors devised a partial split-K strategy. This involves splitting the GEMM into parts that can be more efficiently distributed across GPU Streaming Multiprocessors (SMs) by using appropriate tile sizes and split-K partitions, improving SM utilization.
- **Fusing Scalar Operations:** Common scalar operations like bias addition, residual connections, and activation functions (e.g., GELU, SiLU) are integrated directly into the GEMM kernels. RMS normalization is also fused by computing token-level statistics separately and then applying the division within the subsequent GEMM, reducing kernel launches and memory footprint.

#### 3.3. Full Streaming Inference Framework (Conceptual Extension)

Building on the real-time inference, the paper proposes a "Full Streaming Inference" paradigm for future work. This involves:
- **Overlapping Kernels:** Running IO-bound AE kernels concurrently with compute-bound VLM kernels to maximize GPU utilization.
- **High-Frequency AE Control:** Restructuring the Action Expert (AE) to receive high-frequency inputs (e.g., force sensor data at 480Hz) and generate gradual action outputs, effectively creating a dedicated high-frequency control loop (up to 480Hz). This would involve "auto-regressive decoding" for action chunking.
- **Text Inference Piggybacking:** Fusing autoregressive text inference (e.g., 30 tokens/s) with VLM frame encoding by using shared matrix weights, enabling concurrent reasoning or user interaction.
This conceptual framework envisions a hierarchical VLA control system with multiple feedback loops at different frequencies (480Hz force, 30Hz visual, <1Hz textual).

#### 3.4. Real-World Validation

To verify the effectiveness of their optimization, the authors conducted a real-world experiment:
- **Falling Pen Grasping Task:** A robotic system with two grabbers attempts to catch a marker pen falling from a disturbed position (Figure 1). This task imposes strict time constraints.
- **Setup:** A 30 FPS USB camera observes the pen and the second grabber. Demonstration data (600 episodes) with expected grabber closing times is collected.
- **Training and Inference:** The $\pi^0$ model is trained using the official `openpi` repository with current and previous camera frames. The inference system is carefully implemented with dedicated threads for camera input, network inference, and grabber control, utilizing circular buffers to minimize delay. GPU warm-up is ensured for stable performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling findings that validate the effectiveness of its optimization strategies for real-time VLA inference:

#### 4.1. Significant Inference Speedup

- **Baseline vs. Optimized:** The inference time for a $\pi^0$ model with two input views on a single consumer RTX 4090 GPU was reduced from approximately 106.5 ms (naive PyTorch implementation) to an impressive **27.3 ms** using the full suite of optimizations. This represents a nearly 4x speedup.
- **Comparison to "Official" Implementations:** The optimized solution (27.3 ms) is significantly faster than the "official" OpenPI/JAX inference (53.7 ms for two views).
- **Multi-View Performance:** The optimized approach consistently outperforms baselines across different numbers of views:
 - 1 View: 105.0 ms (naive) -> 43.8 ms (OpenPI/JAX) -> **20.0 ms (ours)**
 - 2 Views: 106.5 ms (naive) -> 53.7 ms (OpenPI/JAX) -> **27.3 ms (ours)**
 - 3 Views: 113.9 ms (naive) -> 67.6 ms (OpenPI/JAX) -> **36.8 ms (ours)**
- **Real-Time Threshold Achieved:** The achieved 27.3 ms for two views (and 20.0 ms for one view) successfully pushes the inference time below the critical 33 ms threshold. This allows for continuous processing of all frames from a 30 FPS RGB video stream, enabling true real-time operation without frame dropping.
- **Breakdown of Optimizations:** The step-by-step analysis (Figure 2, Table 2) clearly shows the contribution of each optimization phase:
 - **+CUDA graph:** Reduced 2-view latency from 106.5 ms to 43.5 ms.
 - **+Simplified graph:** Further reduced to 35.7 ms.
 - **+Optimized kernels:** Final reduction to 27.3 ms.
- **Proximity to Lower Bound:** By establishing a theoretical lower bound (roofline model + synchronization overhead) of 20.6 ms for two views, the authors demonstrate that their implementation (27.3 ms) is remarkably close to optimal, with only about 30% room for further improvement.

#### 4.2. Real-World Validation: Grasping a Falling Pen

- **100% Success Rate:** In a real-world experiment involving grasping a falling marker pen, the $\pi^0$ policy, enabled by the largely optimized inference time, achieved a **100% success rate** over 10 consecutive trials.
- **Human-level Reaction Time:** The end-to-end reaction time of the system was reported to be shorter than 200 ms, which is on par with an average human's reaction time in this type of task. This validates the practical applicability of the optimized VLA for stringent, time-critical robotic operations.

#### 4.3. Conceptual Framework for Full Streaming Inference

- **Overlapped Execution Benefits:** Proof-of-concept measurements showed that concurrently running VLM and AE kernels could reduce overall time (e.g., VLM + 10 AE: 27.3 ms sequential vs. 26.3 ms concurrent).
- **High-Frequency Control Potential:** This concurrency opens the possibility of sustained 30 VLM and 480 AE executions per second, enabling an additional control loop at 480 Hz for the action expert, suitable for high-frequency sensor inputs like force feedback.
- **Multi-Modal Multi-Frequency Control:** The framework proposes the creation of three distinct feedback loops: a 480 Hz force loop, a 30 Hz visual loop, and a <1 Hz textual loop (for reasoning and interaction), all running concurrently.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate a breakthrough in making large VLA models practical for real-time robotic control, proving that speed and sophistication are not mutually exclusive in this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper carries significant implications across the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and high-performance computing, with the potential to profoundly impact the deployment and capabilities of intelligent robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Unlocking Real-time Capabilities for Large VLAs

The most immediate and impactful contribution is demonstrating that billion-parameter VLA models, previously considered too slow for dynamic real-time tasks, can now operate at speeds compatible with continuous 30 FPS camera streams (sub-33ms inference). This overcomes a critical bottleneck that has limited the practical application of these powerful, generalized models in sensitive environments. By achieving human-level reaction times in a challenging task, the work establishes a new benchmark for VLA deployment.

#### 5.2. Enabling a New Class of Robotic Tasks

The ability to run VLAs at real-time speeds opens up previously unattainable dynamic and reactive robotic tasks. Robots can now effectively perceive and act in rapidly changing environments, performing tasks like catching flying objects, precisely manipulating moving parts on an assembly line, or engaging in fluid, interactive tasks with humans where low latency is paramount. This expands the scope of VLA models from high-level planning and slower manipulation to agile, reactive control.

#### 5.3. Democratizing Advanced Robotics with Consumer Hardware

By achieving these performance metrics on a single consumer-grade RTX 4090 GPU, the research democratizes access to cutting-edge robotic capabilities. It suggests that highly intelligent, generalized robots powered by large VLA models might not require prohibitively expensive or specialized computational infrastructure, making them more accessible for research, development, and eventual commercial deployment.

#### 5.4. A Blueprint for Holistic VLA System Design

The comprehensive "bag of strategies" for optimization, ranging from high-level graph transformations to low-level kernel tuning, provides a valuable blueprint for other researchers and engineers working on accelerating large AI models. The systematic approach to identifying and eliminating overheads can be generalized beyond VLA models to other complex neural network architectures.

#### 5.5. Paving the Way for Multi-Frequency Intelligent Control

The proposed "Full Streaming Inference" framework is a forward-looking concept that outlines a novel architecture for integrating VLAs into hierarchical control systems. By envisioning concurrent processing of vision, high-frequency sensor data (like force), and textual reasoning, it suggests a future where robots can seamlessly operate across multiple time scales, exhibiting both rapid reflexes and sophisticated cognitive abilities. This framework challenges traditional robotic control hierarchies by integrating deep learning models into traditionally low-level loops, fostering a more unified and intelligent control paradigm.

#### 5.6. Driving Future Research and Development

The paper clearly outlines future directions, such as increasing visual feedback to 60-120 FPS, scaling to even larger 7B+ parameter models, and exploring more fine-grained feedback loops within the VLA layers. These identified avenues provide fertile ground for subsequent research, including work on quantization for lower precision, adaptive view selection, and innovative hardware utilization strategies.

In conclusion, "Running VLAs at Real-time Speed" is a pivotal work that transforms large VLA models from theoretical marvels into practical, deployable assets for real-world robotics. Its significance lies in bridging the crucial performance gap, providing concrete optimization strategies, and laying a conceptual foundation for the next generation of intelligent and highly responsive robotic systems.
