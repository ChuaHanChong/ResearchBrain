# E-InMeMo: Enhanced Prompting for Visual In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> E-InMeMo enhances visual in-context learning by introducing learnable, pixel-level perturbations to in-context pairs, refining guidance for frozen large-scale vision models. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results on foreground segmentation and single object detection, showing up to 17.04 mIoU improvement over baselines, and extended effectiveness to medical imaging datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) heavily relies on the quality and semantic relevance of retrieved in-context examples, leading to suboptimal performance when examples are not ideal.
- A mismatch often occurs between the content of static in-context prompts and the latent knowledge embedded within large-scale vision models.
- Traditional VICL methods lack a mechanism to actively refine or transform visual prompts to provide more effective guidance to frozen foundation models.

## Method

- Introduces E-InMeMo, a framework that incorporates a ==Prompt Enhancer== to apply ==learnable, pixel-level perturbations== to in-context images and their labels.
- Employs a ==Feature-Map-Level Retrieval (FMLR)== strategy using DINOv2 features to select high-quality in-context pairs.
- Trains only the lightweight Prompt Enhancer module (**27,540 parameters**) using cross-entropy loss, keeping the large-scale vision backbone frozen.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on foreground segmentation (**7.99 mIoU** improvement on Pascal-5i) and single object detection (**17.04 mIoU** improvement on PASCAL VOC 2012) compared to baselines.
- Demonstrated strong robustness to domain shifts and generalizability, achieving state-of-the-art results on medical imaging datasets (Kvasir-SEG and ISIC for segmentation).
- Ablation studies confirmed optimal prompt placement on both in-context image and label, and demonstrated E-InMeMo's data efficiency, outperforming baselines with significantly less retrieval data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learnable, pixel-level perturbations applied directly to in-context image-label pairs can effectively refine prompts and inject task-specific information into the frozen model.
> - By optimizing these small, input-agnostic perturbations, the latent space distributions of enhanced prompts can be aligned with ground truth, leading to improved predictions.
> - Significant performance improvements in Visual In-Context Learning can be achieved through highly parameter-efficient training, without altering the millions of parameters of a large pre-trained vision model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large-scale foundation models, in-context learning (ICL), and computer vision. The field of AI has seen a paradigm shift with the emergence of large-scale models, such as GPT and Gemini, which demonstrate remarkable generalization capabilities across diverse tasks. A key enabler for these models, particularly in Natural Language Processing (NLP), is In-Context Learning (ICL). ICL allows models to perform new tasks by inferring predictions from prompt-based inputs, without the need for costly parameter updates or extensive fine-tuning.

While ICL has revolutionized NLP, its application in computer vision (Visual ICL, or VICL) is still in its nascent stages. Pioneering work like MAE-VQGAN introduced the concept of visual prompting for tasks such as image segmentation, inpainting, and style transfer. This approach typically involves presenting a large vision model with a canvas composed of an input-output image pair (the "in-context pair") alongside a query image, effectively reframing vision tasks as an image inpainting problem. Subsequent efforts, such as Painter and SegGPT, have built upon this foundation, adapting different architectures and generalizing the approach to various visual tasks.

However, a significant challenge in VICL, as identified by existing research, is the critical dependency on the "quality" of these visual prompts. The effectiveness of visual ICL largely hinges on the semantic and visual similarity between the in-context pair and the query image. This dependency often leads to suboptimal performance when ideal in-context examples are unavailable or when there's a mismatch between the prompt content and the inherent knowledge encoded within the large vision model.

This paper addresses this crucial gap by integrating the concept of "learnable prompting" into the visual ICL framework. Learnable prompting, a form of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT), has gained traction in both NLP and computer vision for its ability to adapt frozen models to new tasks by introducing small, trainable perturbations to the input. Previous work in visual prompting has demonstrated its utility in adapting large models like CLIP. E-InMeMo uniquely applies this idea to *enhance the in-context pairs themselves* within the VICL pipeline. By doing so, it aims to "refine or transform the prompt to better guide the model," thereby improving task performance without altering the massive parameters of the underlying foundation model. This approach represents a significant step towards making visual ICL more robust, adaptable, and efficient for real-world deployments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind E-InMeMo stems from a critical limitation of current visual in-context learning (VICL): its strong reliance on the quality and relevance of the retrieved in-context examples. While previous VICL methods have demonstrated promising results, the performance often degrades when the available retrieval datasets or the retrieved in-context pairs are suboptimal, leading to a "mismatch between the prompt content and the latent knowledge embedded within large-scale vision models." This practical challenge raises a fundamental question: can the visual prompts themselves be refined or transformed to provide more effective guidance to the frozen large-scale vision models?

Based on this, the key objectives of the E-InMeMo research are:

1. **Enhance Visual Prompting Quality:** To develop a novel method that actively refines the in-context image pairs used in visual ICL, thereby overcoming the limitations posed by static, retrieved examples.
2. **Improve Task Guidance and Prediction Accuracy:** To ensure that these enhanced prompts lead to more accurate and stable predictions from large vision models across various downstream tasks, particularly in scenarios where initial in-context pairs are not ideal.
3. **Leverage Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT):** To achieve performance improvements while maintaining computational efficiency. This means adapting the model's input without modifying the immense parameters of the pre-trained large-scale vision backbone, making it suitable for practical deployment.
4. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance:** To show empirically that the proposed E-InMeMo approach outperforms existing visual ICL methods on standard vision benchmarks, specifically for tasks like foreground segmentation and single object detection.
5. **Validate Robustness and Generalizability:** To rigorously test the method's ability to perform consistently across different datasets, including natural and medical images, and to exhibit resilience to domain shifts, which are common in real-world applications.
6. **Refine and Optimize Previous Work:** To extend and improve upon their earlier conference paper by introducing more robust strategies, reducing parameter count, and utilizing more advanced feature extractors (DINOv2), showcasing continuous research progress.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between the power of frozen large-scale vision models and the task-specific needs of various visual applications, making ICL a more reliable and efficient paradigm for computer vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

E-InMeMo (Enhanced InstructMeMore) introduces a novel approach to visual In-Context Learning (ICL) by incorporating learnable perturbations into in-context pairs. The framework is built upon a pre-trained large-scale vision model, specifically MAE-VQGAN, which remains frozen throughout the process.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework (E-InMeMo)

The E-InMeMo pipeline begins with a query image `x_q` for which a prediction `y_q` is desired.
1. **In-context Learning Retriever:** A crucial initial step is to select a relevant in-context pair `(x, y)` from a dataset `S`. For this, E-InMeMo employs a Feature-Map-Level Retrieval (FMLR) strategy.
2. **Prompt Enhancer:** The retrieved in-context pair `(x, y)` is then passed through a novel component, the "Prompt Enhancer," denoted as `t_φ`. This enhancer applies learnable, pixel-level perturbations to the in-context image `x` and its corresponding label `y`, producing an "enhanced" pair `(x', y')`.
3. **Canvas Construction:** The enhanced in-context pair `(x', y')`, the query image `x_q`, and an empty cell `r` (designated for the prediction) are arranged into a 2x2 grid, forming an "enhanced canvas" `ĉ_q = [x', y', x_q, r]`.
4. **Prediction by Frozen Model:** This `ĉ_q` is fed into a frozen, pre-trained large-scale vision model (MAE-VQGAN). The model then outputs predicted visual tokens corresponding to the empty cell.
5. **Decoding:** These predicted tokens are subsequently decoded by the VQGAN decoder to generate the final output image `ŷ_q`.

#### 3.2. In-context Learning Retriever (FMLR)

To ensure high-quality in-context pair selection, E-InMeMo utilizes a Feature-Map-Level Retrieval (FMLR) strategy. Unlike methods relying on global features, FMLR extracts dense visual features by employing a pre-trained visual encoder, specifically DINOv2 (an improvement over CLIP used in earlier versions). The full feature map for both the query image and candidate images from the dataset `S` is preserved, flattened into a 1D representation, and `ℓ2`-normalized. The in-context pair `(x, y)` with the highest cosine similarity to the query image's feature representation is selected. This fine-grained feature comparison aims to retrieve semantically and visually similar examples.

#### 3.3. Prompt Enhancer

Inspired by visual prompting techniques, the Prompt Enhancer applies an input-agnostic, learnable perturbation `t_φ` to the in-context pair. This perturbation resides in the image space and consists of trainable pixels concentrated along the image borders. The modified pair is formulated as `(x', y') = (x, y) + δt_φ`, where `δ` is the perturbation strength (set to 1). The design choice of an input-agnostic prompt means a single shared `t_φ` is learned for a given task, effectively acting as a task-specific identifier that helps the frozen model bridge the domain gap between the in-context examples and the query. The total number of trainable parameters for `φ` is significantly lightweight, amounting to only 27,540, a notable reduction from their previous work.

#### 3.4. Training

The training process is highly parameter-efficient. Only the parameters `φ` of the Prompt Enhancer are optimized, while the large-scale MAE-VQGAN model remains entirely frozen.
1. A query `(x_q, y_q)` is randomly selected from the training dataset `S`.
2. An in-context pair `(x, y)` is retrieved from `S` excluding the query itself.
3. The enhanced canvas `ĉ_q` is constructed and processed by the frozen MAE to yield predicted visual tokens.
4. Concurrently, a ground-truth canvas `c_gt = [x, y, x_q, y_q]` (where the empty cell is filled with the true label `y_q`) is encoded by the pre-trained VQGAN encoder to generate ground-truth visual tokens `z`.
5. The loss function is a standard cross-entropy loss computed between the predicted visual tokens (from `ĉ_q`) and the ground-truth visual tokens `z` (from `c_gt`) specifically for the empty cell region.
Optimization is performed using the Adam optimizer with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule, trained for 70 epochs. The efficiency of the approach allows training on a single NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU.

#### 3.5. Interpretation

The learnable prompt `t_φ` is designed to subtly translate the distribution of the in-context prompt, injecting task-specific information that might not be explicitly conveyed by the original in-context pair alone. By optimizing `t_φ` to minimize the cross-entropy loss, the framework aims to align the latent space distributions of the enhanced prompt `ĉ_q` with the ground-truth prompt `c_gt`. This alignment provides the frozen model with more plausible visual tokens for decoding, leading to more accurate predictions `ŷ_q`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

E-InMeMo demonstrates significant advancements in visual In-Context Learning (ICL) across various benchmarks and tasks.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **Superiority on Standard Tasks:** E-InMeMo establishes new state-of-the-art (SOTA) results for both foreground segmentation (on Pascal-5i dataset) and single object detection (on PASCAL VOC 2012).
- **Quantitative Gains:** Compared to the baseline without learnable prompts (FMLR with DINOv2), E-InMeMo achieved a remarkable 7.99 mIoU improvement for foreground segmentation (from 35.92 to 43.91) and an even more substantial 17.04 mIoU increase for single object detection (from 27.18 to 44.22).
- **Outperforming Competitors:** The method consistently surpasses existing visual ICL approaches, including Random, UnsupPR, SupPR, SCS, Partial2Global, and prompt-SelF. Notably, E-InMeMo achieves superior performance with a single inference, unlike prompt-SelF which relies on a bagging mechanism across multiple canvas configurations. It also outperforms meta-learning-based few-shot segmentation methods like OSLSM and co-FCN.

#### 4.2. Robustness and Generalizability

- **Medical Image Analysis:** E-InMeMo's effectiveness extends beyond natural images. It achieved SOTA results on two medical datasets for foreground segmentation (Kvasir-SEG for polyp segmentation and ISIC for skin lesion segmentation), demonstrating its generalizability and potential in specialized domains where precision is critical. Qualitative results visibly show E-InMeMo accurately segmenting regions of interest where baselines fail.
- **Domain Shift Resilience:** The method exhibits strong robustness to domain shifts. When tested with in-context pairs from COCO-5i and query images from Pascal-5i (COCO→Pascal setting), E-InMeMo showed a smaller relative performance drop (5.67%) compared to the FMLR baseline (6.46%), indicating enhanced cross-domain generalization.
- **Qualitative Excellence:** Visual comparisons highlight E-InMeMo's ability to produce highly accurate and fine-grained segmentation and detection results, closely aligning with ground-truth labels. It demonstrates robustness to various visual discrepancies in in-context pairs, such as achromatic images, significant color differences, and foreground size variations, and can even detect objects when the in-context foreground is minimal.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Sensitivity Analysis

- **Optimal Prompt Placement:** Experiments on where to apply the learnable prompt confirmed that applying it to both the in-context image and its label (E-InMeMo's current configuration) yields the best performance. Interestingly, applying the prompt to both the in-context image and the query image (I&Q) led to worse performance, suggesting that the input-agnostic nature of the prompt can be counterproductive when applied too broadly, hindering the model's ability to distinguish task-specific cues.
- **Sensitivity to Retrieval Set Size:** Both E-InMeMo and the FMLR baseline benefit from larger retrieval sets. However, E-InMeMo is significantly more sensitive to data volume, achieving a substantial 21.66 mIoU improvement from 1% to 100% of the retrieval set size. Crucially, E-InMeMo with only 10% of the retrieval set size still outperformed FMLR (DINOv2) using 100% of the data, underscoring its data-efficient fine-tuning capabilities.
- **Sensitivity to Dataset Size (Training):** E-InMeMo generally benefits from more training data. It surpassed the baseline performance with as few as 64 images per class and showed steady improvements up to 256 images, indicating that it can leverage larger datasets while remaining effective in data-limited scenarios.
- **Effect of Padding Size:** An optimal padding size of 15 pixels for the learnable prompt was determined. While smaller padding (e.g., 10 pixels) could achieve high scores in specific cases, 15 pixels offered the best overall balance. Larger padding sizes (e.g., 20 pixels or more) led to performance degradation, possibly due to the learnable prompt overwriting critical visual information from the original in-context pair.
- **Intra- and Inter-Class Generalizability:** While E-InMeMo shows strong intra-class performance (when the prompt is tuned for the specific class), its inter-class generalizability is more nuanced. Prompts trained on one class do not always generalize well to semantically distant classes (e.g., "transportation" to "animal"). This highlights the importance of task-specific prompt tuning, while also revealing interesting patterns of generalization within super-classes.

In summary, E-InMeMo consistently delivers superior performance with a lightweight, efficient, and robust approach, effectively addressing key challenges in visual ICL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The E-InMeMo framework represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision, particularly in making large-scale foundation models more adaptable and efficient for downstream tasks through visual in-context learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Visual In-Context Learning:** E-InMeMo directly tackles the core limitation of existing visual ICL methods: the reliance on static and often suboptimal in-context examples. By introducing learnable, pixel-level perturbations, it allows the model to dynamically refine these prompts, providing more effective guidance. This innovation pushes the boundaries of how effectively large, frozen vision models can be instructed for novel tasks without costly full fine-tuning.

2. **Parameter-Efficient Adaptation:** The method's core strength lies in its remarkable parameter efficiency. With only 27,540 trainable parameters, E-InMeMo offers a lightweight solution for adapting powerful, multi-billion-parameter foundation models. This is crucial for practical deployment scenarios where computational resources are constrained, and it aligns with the broader trend of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) in AI. This efficiency makes it scalable and accessible, reducing the barriers to leveraging large models for specific applications.

3. **Improved Robustness and Generalizability:** E-InMeMo demonstrates enhanced robustness to various real-world challenges, including low-quality in-context pairs, semantic mismatches, and domain shifts. Its ability to maintain performance when transitioning between natural image datasets (e.g., Pascal to COCO) and to specialized domains like medical imaging (Kvasir-SEG, ISIC) is particularly impactful. This improved resilience makes the system more reliable for diverse and often unpredictable real-world data environments.

4. **Enabling Fine-Grained Task Performance:** The significant improvements in mIoU for foreground segmentation and single object detection tasks underscore E-InMeMo's capacity to guide models in capturing fine-grained details and making precise predictions. This is vital for applications requiring high accuracy in object localization and delineation.

5. **Broad Applicability in Specialized Domains:** The successful validation on medical imaging datasets (polyp and skin lesion segmentation) highlights E-InMeMo's immediate potential for applications in healthcare, environmental monitoring, industrial inspection, and other specialized fields. In such domains, acquiring large, labeled datasets for traditional fine-tuning is often difficult and expensive, making data-efficient methods like E-InMeMo invaluable.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By demonstrating the effectiveness of learnable prompting within the visual ICL paradigm, E-InMeMo opens new avenues for research. It encourages further exploration into how task-specific knowledge can be injected into universal foundation models efficiently. The acknowledgment of current limitations, such as the task-specific nature of learned prompts and the minimum data requirements, provides clear directions for subsequent work, including developing more generalizable prompts or exploring more sophisticated data-efficient strategies.

In conclusion, E-InMeMo offers a practical, effective, and efficient solution for enhancing the performance and applicability of large visual foundation models. Its lightweight nature and demonstrated robustness make it a promising candidate for deploying advanced AI capabilities across a wide range of real-world scenarios, particularly in data-scarce or resource-constrained environments.
