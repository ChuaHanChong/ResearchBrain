# E-InMeMo: Enhanced Prompting for Visual In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> E-InMeMo enhances visual in-context learning by introducing learnable, pixel-level perturbations to in-context pairs, refining guidance for frozen large-scale vision models. This approach achieved state-of-the-art results on foreground segmentation and single object detection, showing up to 17.04 mIoU improvement over baselines, and extended effectiveness to medical imaging datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Visual In-Context Learning (VICL) heavily relies on the quality and semantic relevance of retrieved in-context examples, leading to suboptimal performance when examples are not ideal.
- A mismatch often occurs between the content of static in-context prompts and the latent knowledge embedded within large-scale vision models.
- Traditional VICL methods lack a mechanism to actively refine or transform visual prompts to provide more effective guidance to frozen foundation models.

## Method

- Introduces E-InMeMo, a framework that incorporates a ==Prompt Enhancer== to apply ==learnable, pixel-level perturbations== to in-context images and their labels.
- Employs a ==Feature-Map-Level Retrieval (FMLR)== strategy using DINOv2 features to select high-quality in-context pairs.
- Trains only the lightweight Prompt Enhancer module (**27,540 parameters**) using cross-entropy loss, keeping the large-scale vision backbone frozen.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on foreground segmentation (**7.99 mIoU** improvement on Pascal-5i) and single object detection (**17.04 mIoU** improvement on PASCAL VOC 2012) compared to baselines.
- Demonstrated strong robustness to domain shifts and generalizability, achieving state-of-the-art results on medical imaging datasets (Kvasir-SEG and ISIC for segmentation).
- Ablation studies confirmed optimal prompt placement on both in-context image and label, and demonstrated E-InMeMo's data efficiency, outperforming baselines with significantly less retrieval data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learnable, pixel-level perturbations applied directly to in-context image-label pairs can effectively refine prompts and inject task-specific information into the frozen model.
> - By optimizing these small, input-agnostic perturbations, the latent space distributions of enhanced prompts can be aligned with ground truth, leading to improved predictions.
> - Significant performance improvements in Visual In-Context Learning can be achieved through highly parameter-efficient training, without altering the millions of parameters of a large pre-trained vision model.
