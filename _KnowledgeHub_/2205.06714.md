# Learning Keypoints from Synthetic Data for Robotic Cloth Folding

> [!summary] Summary
> A U-Net keypoint detector predicts a spatial heatmap of towel corners from a single RGB image, trained entirely on 30,000 procedurally generated Blender/BlenderProc scenes with randomized geometry, materials, lighting, camera pose, and distractors. Detected corners drive a scripted open-loop grasp and arc fold on a UR3e. Zero-shot sim-to-real gives 77% grasp and 53% fold success.

## Problem

- Cloth deformability makes full state estimation infeasible, and supervised keypoint detectors need large annotated real datasets that are expensive and slow to collect.
- Classical folding pipelines rely on segmentation with known background color distributions, limiting generalization to diverse environments, and perception can take up to 5 seconds.
- Synthetic training leaves a reality gap; prior work finds domain randomization alone insufficient and usually resorts to real-data finetuning or task-specific tuning.

## Method

- ==Procedural dataset generation== in ==Blender==/==BlenderProc==: per-image scene rebuild randomizing towel mesh, HSV plus ==Perlin-noise== procedural material, ==HDRI== environment lighting, camera pose in a spherical cap, and Thingi10k distractors.
- ==U-Net== keypoint detector with ==ResNet-style skip connections== in the spatial bottleneck and bilinear upsampling, outputting one sigmoid heatmap of Gaussian blobs trained with pixel-wise ==binary cross-entropy==.
- Robotic folding pipeline: ==max-filter keypoint extraction==, abort if fewer than four corners, reproject top-four onto the table plane, define a local frame, execute scripted pregrasp, grasp, and arc fold.

## Results

- Zero-shot real-world folding of in-distribution towels reached **51/66** grasp success (**77%**) and **35/66** fold success (**53%**) across natural light, LED light, and distractor settings.
- Out-of-distribution towels with non-uniform textures or different material properties degraded to **48%** grasp and **37%** fold success, exposing the residual reality gap.
- Detector reached **80%** average precision at a **2-pixel** threshold on synthetic validation; data generation cost **1.8 s** per sample, training **55 minutes**, inference under **1 s** versus **20 s** execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-relevant low-dimensional keypoints can be a sufficient state representation for deformable objects, sidestepping full-state estimation that deformability makes intractable.
> - Randomizing lighting and adding distractor objects transferred robustly, but appearance factors left unmodelled, such as non-uniform textures, dominate the residual reality gap.
> - Perception success does not imply task success: open-loop scripted single-arm folding lost 24 points, with 80% of fold failures from corner misalignment during motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The robotic manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth, represents a significant challenge in robotics. This difficulty stems from the high-dimensional state of deformable objects, which complicates perception, control, and modeling. Despite substantial progress, tasks like cloth manipulation remain active areas of research, particularly for applications in general-purpose robotic systems, such as those found in household settings.

Supervised deep learning (DL) has emerged as a promising approach for perception tasks in robotics, offering the potential to generalize across various environments. However, a primary limitation of DL models is their requirement for large quantities of labeled data, which can be prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect manually for complex real-world scenarios.

In response to this data collection challenge, procedural data generation, or the use of synthetic data, has gained attention as an alternative. Synthetic data offers advantages such as low generation cost, perfect annotations, and the ability to generate desired variations. However, a major impediment to the widespread adoption of synthetic data is the "reality gap," which refers to the performance degradation observed when a model trained on synthetic data is deployed in the real world due to inherent differences between simulated and real environments. Existing methods like domain randomization attempt to bridge this gap by increasing the diversity of synthetic data, but research indicates that an excessive degree of randomization can sometimes diminish performance, and a mismatch between synthetic and real-world data distributions often persists, frequently necessitating fine-tuning with real data.

Within this context, the detection of keypoints (or semantic landmarks) has become a crucial aspect for various robotic tasks, including the manipulation of deformable objects. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been successfully employed to detect or even discover these keypoints, typically by predicting spatial heatmaps rather than direct Cartesian coordinates, allowing for variable numbers of detected keypoints. Prior work in robotic cloth folding has often relied on traditional computer vision techniques, such as segmentation combined with polygonal approximations or template matching, which may be limited by assumptions about background color or can incur significant processing delays.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the challenge of data scarcity for keypoint detection in robotic cloth folding. It investigates whether a keypoint detector for this task can be trained *purely* on synthetically generated data and achieve effective performance in real-world scenarios via zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, thereby attempting to mitigate the reality gap without the need for real-world fine-tuning. The work builds upon the understanding that for cloth folding, a low-dimensional state representation derived from a few semantic keypoints can be sufficient, offering a practical approach to a complex problem.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain the feasibility of training a convolutional neural network (CNN) to detect semantic keypoints for robotic cloth folding exclusively using synthetically generated data, and subsequently to evaluate its performance in a real-world robotic setup without any fine-tuning (zero-shot sim-to-real transfer). This objective is driven by several key motivations:

Firstly, the manipulation of deformable objects, such as cloth, presents substantial challenges for robotic systems. To successfully perform tasks like cloth folding, robots require accurate perception of the object's state. For folding tasks, it is often sufficient to identify a limited number of semantic keypoints, such as the corners of an item. Deep learning models, particularly CNNs, possess the capability to perform such perception tasks and generalize across different scenarios. However, these models typically demand large, meticulously annotated datasets for training.

Secondly, the manual collection and annotation of vast datasets for deformable objects are resource-intensive processes. This constraint limits the development and deployment of robust perception systems for cloth manipulation. The researchers are motivated to overcome this data bottleneck by exploring procedural data generation. Synthetic data offers several compelling advantages:
- **Cost-effectiveness:** It is significantly cheaper to generate synthetic data than to collect and label real-world images.
- **Perfect annotations:** Synthetic environments allow for precise and unambiguous labeling of keypoints, avoiding human error or ambiguity inherent in manual annotation.
- **Scalability and Diversity:** Synthetic data generation allows for the creation of a wide range of variations in object appearance, lighting, background, and object configurations, which can enhance the generalization capabilities of the trained models.

The specific task chosen for this investigation is towel folding, which is recognized as a benchmark task in robotic cloth manipulation. The authors assume the towels are unfolded but not necessarily perfectly flattened, a realistic scenario given advancements in cloth unfolding techniques. The motivation is to estimate the 2D positions of the towel's corners from a single RGB image using the trained CNN, which then informs a scripted grasping and folding trajectory.

The stated contributions of this paper directly reflect these objectives and motivations:
- To demonstrate empirically that synthetic data can be effectively utilized to train CNNs for detecting keypoints relevant to cloth folding.
- To conduct a comprehensive evaluation of the performance of both the keypoint detector and the unimanual folding system in real-world conditions.
- To provide qualitative analysis and insights into the types of failures encountered, which can inform future research efforts to address the remaining challenges of the reality gap and robust cloth manipulation.

Ultimately, the core motivation is to advance robotic manipulation of deformable objects by making the perception component more accessible and less reliant on costly real-world data, thereby facilitating the development of more general-purpose robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves a two-stage process: first, training a keypoint detector using a procedurally generated synthetic dataset, and second, evaluating the zero-shot sim-to-real performance of this detector within a robotic cloth folding task.

**A. Synthetic Dataset Generation**
The synthetic dataset was created using Blender and BlenderProc. For each data sample, a unique 3D scene was constructed with randomized elements:
- **Towel Geometry:** Modeled as a rectangular mesh.
- **Towel Material:** A procedural material was designed, combining a random HSV (Hue, Saturation, Value) color with a Perlin noise texture. This approach eliminated the need for complex, realistic fabric textures, which are difficult to acquire.
- **Distractor Objects:** To enhance generalization and reduce false positives on non-towel objects, up to five distractor objects were sampled from the Thingi10k dataset and randomly placed in the scene.
- **Ground Plane:** The ground plane material incorporated a random HSV base color along with a texture from PolyHaven to introduce spatial patterns.
- **Lighting:** Realistic and complex lighting conditions were achieved by using 360-degree images from PolyHaven as environment textures.
- **Camera Pose:** The camera's position was sampled within a spherical cap, and its orientation was set to point towards the center of the scene, ensuring diverse viewing angles.
- **Rendering:** Each scene was rendered into a 256x256 pixel RGB image using Cycles, Blender’s physically-based path tracer.
A total of 30,000 images were generated using this procedure. For ground truth, heatmaps for keypoints were generated by placing 2D Gaussian blobs centered on each corner keypoint and taking a pixel-wise maximum.

**B. Keypoint Detector**
A fully convolutional neural network was employed to predict a single spatial heatmap containing all corners of the cloth.
- **Architecture:** The network utilized a U-net architecture, which effectively combines spatial resolution-preserving paths with downsampling paths to achieve a large receptive field. ResNet-inspired skip connections were incorporated within the spatial bottleneck to facilitate gradient flow and improve learning.
- **Layers and Activations:** Bilinear upsampling was used in the upsampling layers. All hidden layers used the ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) activation function, while the final layer employed a sigmoid activation, with its outputs interpreted as a probability distribution of a keypoint being centered at a given location.
- **Training:** The training objective was pixel-wise Binary Cross Entropy. Keypoints were extracted from the predicted heatmaps using a max-filter from Scikit-Image with a configurable receptive field. The CNN was trained for 15 epochs using Pytorch Lightning, with experiment tracking managed by Weights and Biases.

**C. Robotic Folding**
The robotic folding system operates in a sequential manner based on the output of the keypoint detector:
- **Image Input:** A top-down RGB image of the towel is captured.
- **Keypoint Detection:** This image is fed into the trained keypoint detector network.
- **Keypoint Filtering:** If fewer than four keypoints are detected, the folding attempt is aborted. Otherwise, the four keypoints with the highest probability are selected.
- **Reprojection and Frame Definition:** The detected 2D keypoints are reprojected onto the known table plane in 3D space. A local frame of reference is then defined based on these reprojected keypoints.
- **Trajectory Generation and Execution:** A pre-scripted pre-grasp pose, grasp pose, and fold trajectory, defined within this local frame, are transformed into the robot's coordinate system. The robot then executes this sequence. The grasping strategy involves grasping the towel in the middle of the side designated for folding. The folding trajectory is designed as an arc from this grasp point to the corresponding point on the opposite side of the towel.

**D. Robotic Setup**
The experimental setup included:
- **Robot:** A UR3e collaborative robot.
- **Gripper:** A Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, fitted with 3D-printed fingertips made from flexfill TPU 98A filament. These compliant fingertips were designed to slide under the cloth effectively during grasping.
- **Camera:** A ZED2i camera mounted 1 meter above the table, with its extrinsic calibration performed prior to experiments.
- **Image Processing:** Images from the camera were cropped to the relevant workspace area and then rescaled to 256x256 pixels, matching the input resolution of the CNN.

**E. Evaluation**
The system's performance was evaluated using two distinct sets of towels and three environmental settings:
- **Towel Sets:**
 - **In-distribution Towels:** 11 towels with varied colors and material properties but uniform textures, resembling those modeled in the synthetic data.
 - **Out-of-distribution Towels:** 9 towels with highly non-uniform textures or significantly different material properties, not explicitly represented in the synthetic data, to assess generalization.
- **Environment Settings:** Natural Light, LED Light, and Distractors (presence of 6 randomly chosen objects).
- **Trials:** Each towel was subjected to two folding attempts per setting. Towel poses were manually randomized before each attempt. Larger towels were partially folded to fit within the robot's workspace.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Grasp Success Rate:** Defined as the robot's ability to successfully enclose the cloth at the designated grasp pose.
 - **Fold Success Rate:** Defined as the approximate coincidence of the opposing corners after the manipulation, following established benchmarks.
- **Additional Measurement:** The execution time for the neural network inference and the robotic manipulation sequence was also reported.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded quantitative and qualitative results regarding the keypoint detector's performance and the overall robotic folding system's success rates.

**A. Synthetic Data Generation and Keypoint Detector Training Efficiency:**
The procedural generation of 30,000 synthetic images averaged 1.8 seconds per sample, with 0.6 seconds for scene building and 1.2 seconds for rendering, utilizing a low-range mobile GPU. The keypoint detector was trained for 15 epochs in 55 minutes on an Nvidia V100 GPU, achieving an average precision of 80% (with a 2-pixel threshold) on a synthetic validation set. This indicates efficient data generation and model training.

**B. Real-World Robotic Folding Performance (Zero-Shot Transfer):**

- **In-distribution Towels:**
 - The total grasp success rate for towels resembling those in the synthetic training data was 77% (51 out of 66 trials).
 - The total fold success rate was 53% (35 out of 66 trials).
 - The performance metrics demonstrated that the presence of distractor items or variations in lighting conditions, which represent common real-world domain shifts, did not substantially affect the keypoint detector's performance. This finding suggests that the domain randomization strategies employed during synthetic data generation were effective for these specific environmental variations.

- **Out-of-distribution Towels:**
 - For towels with non-uniform textures or distinct material properties not explicitly modeled in the synthetic data, the grasp success rate decreased to 48% (26 out of 54 trials).
 - The fold success rate for this category was 37% (20 out of 54 trials).
 - This decline in performance indicates a limitation in the model's ability to generalize to visually distinct materials and textures that were not adequately represented in the synthetic training distribution.

**C. Analysis of Failure Cases:**

- **Grasp Failures:**
 - The majority of grasp failures were attributed to inaccuracies or incomplete detection of keypoints by the transferred neural network. This suggests that a reality gap persists, not fully bridged by the current synthetic dataset variations, particularly concerning the visual properties of real-world towels.
 - A secondary cause for grasp failures involved very light towels being pushed away by the gripper fingers instead of being successfully enclosed, due to insufficient friction.

- **Fold Failures:**
 - Approximately 80% of all fold failures occurred due to corner misalignment. This was caused by the cloth bending during the execution of the open-loop fold motion. This limitation is linked to the nature of scripted trajectories and unimanual manipulation, which do not dynamically account for cloth deformation.
 - For towels that were already partially folded, their altered thickness and material properties rendered the pre-defined open-loop trajectory unsuitable, leading to additional fold failures.

**D. System Execution Time:**
The computational time for a forward pass of the neural network was approximately 1 second on a CPU, which was deemed negligible compared to the roughly 20 seconds required for the robot to execute the physical grasp and fold trajectory.

In summary, the results indicate the potential of purely synthetic data for training keypoint detectors for robotic cloth folding, demonstrating reasonable performance for in-distribution items and robustness to some environmental changes. However, challenges related to the reality gap for novel textures and the limitations of open-loop manipulation for deformable objects were identified as key areas for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant implications and potential impacts within the field of robotic manipulation and computer vision:

**A. Validation of Synthetic Data for Deformable Object Perception:**
The most significant finding is the demonstration that procedurally generated synthetic data can serve as a viable and effective resource for training keypoint detectors for robotic cloth manipulation. This directly addresses the long-standing challenge of data scarcity for deep learning models, particularly when dealing with deformable objects whose states are complex and difficult to annotate in real-world settings. By showing that a detector trained purely on synthetic data can transfer to real-world tasks without fine-tuning, the paper supports the argument for synthetic data as a cost-efficient alternative to laborious manual data collection.

**B. Advancement in Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, albeit with limitations, is an important step. It indicates that carefully designed synthetic data and randomization strategies can enable models to generalize to real-world environments to a useful extent without requiring any real-world training samples for adaptation. This capability can significantly accelerate the development and deployment cycles for robotic systems, as it reduces the need for extensive real-world experimentation and data collection.

**C. Insights into the Reality Gap and Domain Randomization:**
The detailed analysis of failure cases provides valuable insights into the remaining challenges of the "reality gap." The robustness to lighting and distractor objects, combined with a performance drop for out-of-distribution textures, suggests that certain aspects of domain randomization (e.g., color, object presence, lighting) were effective, while others (e.g., complex fabric textures, nuanced material properties) require further attention. This informs future research by highlighting specific factors that are critical for bridging the gap more completely and developing more principled guidelines for synthetic data generation.

**D. Informing Future Work in Robotic Manipulation and Control:**
The observed discrepancy between grasp and fold success rates, particularly the issues with cloth bending during the fold, underscores that perception is only one component of robotic manipulation. It emphasizes the need for more robust control strategies that can account for the dynamic properties and deformations of cloth. This suggests a future direction toward closed-loop control, bimanual manipulation, or adaptive trajectories that can respond to real-time changes in the cloth's state, moving beyond purely scripted, open-loop actions. The findings therefore guide future efforts not only in perception but also in the intricate control aspects of deformable object manipulation.

**E. Contribution to Robotic Automation in Domestic and Industrial Settings:**
Robotic cloth manipulation is a foundational task for various applications, from household chores like laundry folding to industrial processes in textile manufacturing. By making the perception component more tractable and scalable, this research contributes to the long-term goal of developing more autonomous and versatile robots capable of operating in unstructured environments. The ability to effectively handle deformable objects would broaden the scope of tasks that robots can perform, enhancing their utility and integration into human environments.

In conclusion, this work significantly contributes to the understanding of synthetic data's role in advancing robotic perception for deformable object manipulation. It validates a promising approach while clearly delineating current limitations, thereby setting a clear agenda for future research to further close the reality gap and enhance the robustness of robotic control for complex tasks like cloth folding.
