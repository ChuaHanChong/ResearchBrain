# DexYCB: A Benchmark for Capturing Hand Grasping of Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA and the University of Washington developed DexYCB, a large-scale RGB-D dataset comprising 582,000 frames of 10 subjects interacting with 20 YCB objects across 8 camera views. This benchmark provides accurate 3D annotations for both hands and objects, facilitating research and evaluation of joint hand and object pose estimation in interactive scenarios.

## Problem

- Accurately tracking both hand and object poses simultaneously during human-object interaction remains a significant challenge for computer vision systems.
- Existing datasets for computer vision focus either on isolated hand pose or object pose estimation, lacking comprehensive coverage of their complex interplay.
- Challenges in hand-object interaction include severe occlusion between hands and objects, complex physical contact dynamics, and the vast diversity of human grasping styles.

## Method

- Researchers from NVIDIA and the University of Washington developed **DexYCB**, a comprehensive ==RGB-D benchmark dataset== for realistic hand-object interactions.
- A sophisticated ==multi-camera setup== with 8 calibrated RGB-D cameras captured data from 10 subjects interacting with 20 YCB objects under natural grasping scenarios.
- Accurate ==3D annotations== for both hands and objects were achieved through a ==multi-view optimization== process, combining 2D keypoint annotations, ==depth consistency==, and hand/object model constraints (==MANO==).

## Results

- DexYCB is a large-scale dataset containing **582,000 RGB-D frames** across **1,000 sequences**, significantly exceeding previous hand-object interaction datasets in size and diversity.
- Benchmark experiments revealed that RGB-D methods generally outperformed RGB-only methods for joint hand and object pose estimation, with **CosyPose** achieving the top baseline performance for object pose.
- Models trained on DexYCB demonstrated superior generalization to other datasets (e.g., HO-3D) compared to models trained on those datasets and then applied to DexYCB, highlighting its quality and comprehensiveness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hand-object interaction scenarios significantly reduce the accuracy of object pose estimation compared to static object scenarios.
> - Generalization remains a challenge for models, with notable performance drops observed when exposed to unseen subjects, camera views, and particularly novel grasping configurations.
> - The DexYCB dataset exhibits a broader diversity of hand poses than prior datasets, which is crucial for developing robust models capable of handling varied natural human grasps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Understanding how humans interact with objects through their hands is fundamental to advancing fields such as robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction. However, accurately tracking both hand and object poses during interaction remains challenging for computer vision systems. To address this gap, researchers from NVIDIA and the University of Washington have developed DexYCB, a comprehensive benchmark dataset that captures realistic hand-object interactions.

DexYCB differs from previous datasets by providing a large-scale collection of RGB-D data capturing realistic grasping scenarios with accurate 3D annotations for both hands and objects. With 582,000 frames across 1,000 sequences featuring 10 subjects interacting with 20 objects from 8 camera views, DexYCB represents a significant advancement in the study of hand-object interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Need for Hand-Object Interaction Datasets

Existing computer vision datasets have traditionally focused on either object pose estimation or hand pose estimation in isolation. While these separate tracks have seen impressive progress, the interaction between hands and objects introduces unique challenges:

1. **Occlusion**: When grasping objects, hands partially occlude the object and vice versa, making it difficult to track both simultaneously.

2. **Contact dynamics**: The physical contact between hands and objects creates complex visual patterns that are hard to model.

3. **Grasp diversity**: Humans can grasp the same object in numerous ways, creating a vast space of possible interactions.

Prior datasets like HO-3D have attempted to address these challenges but have limitations in terms of scale, diversity, and annotation quality. Synthetic datasets offer an alternative, but generating realistic hand-object interactions remains difficult due to the complexity of modeling natural grasp poses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. DexYCB Dataset Creation

The DexYCB dataset combines objects from the established YCB object set (commonly used in robotics research) with realistic human grasping actions. The authors built a sophisticated multi-camera setup to capture this data:

#### 3.2. Hardware Setup

- 8 calibrated RGB-D cameras (Intel RealSense D415)
- Extrinsically calibrated and temporally synchronized
- Arranged to capture a central interaction area from multiple viewpoints

#### 3.3. Data Collection Protocol

Subjects were instructed to:
1. Pick up target objects from a table
2. Hold objects in the air, mimicking a human-to-human handover scenario
3. Perform natural grasping actions without specific instructions on how to grasp

This protocol generated a diverse set of natural grasping poses across:
- 10 different human subjects with varying hand sizes
- 20 YCB objects with different shapes, sizes, and material properties
- 8 camera viewpoints providing comprehensive visual coverage

The resulting dataset contains 1,000 sequences with approximately 582,000 RGB-D frames, making it significantly larger and more diverse than previous hand-object interaction datasets.

#### 3.4. Data Annotation Process

Creating accurate 3D annotations for both hands and objects in interactive scenarios requires a sophisticated approach:

1. **2D Keypoint Annotation**:
 - For hands: 21 pre-defined hand joints were annotated in each view
 - For objects: Distinctive landmark points were selected and tracked
 - Annotation was crowdsourced through Amazon Mechanical Turk

2. **3D Pose Estimation**:
 The authors solved for 3D poses by optimizing an energy function that combined:
 - Depth consistency across views
 - 2D keypoint reprojection constraints
 - Hand model constraints using the MANO hand model
 - Object pose parameterized with standard 6D representation

The optimization process can be formalized as:

$$E(θ_h, θ_o) = E_{depth}(θ_h, θ_o) + E_{keypoint}(θ_h, θ_o) + E_{reg}(θ_h, θ_o)$$

where $θ_h$ represents hand pose parameters and $θ_o$ represents object pose parameters.

This rigorous annotation process resulted in high-quality 3D pose labels for both hands and objects, even in challenging interaction scenarios.

#### 3.5. Benchmark Tasks and Evaluation

The authors established several benchmark tasks to evaluate the dataset's utility:

#### 3.6. 2D Object and Keypoint Detection

Detecting objects and hand keypoints in 2D images, evaluated using standard detection metrics.

#### 3.7. 6D Object Pose Estimation

Estimating the 3D position and orientation of objects, with performance evaluated using the Average Distance of Model Points (ADD) metric.

#### 3.8. 3D Hand Pose Estimation

Predicting 3D hand joint positions, evaluated using mean joint distance error.

#### 3.9. Human-to-Robot Object Handover

A practical robotics application where the system must identify safe robotic grasping points that avoid contact with the human hand.

To evaluate generalization capabilities, the authors created four different train/val/test splits:
- **S0 (default)**: Random split
- **S1**: Unseen subjects
- **S2**: Unseen camera views
- **S3**: Unseen grasping configurations

These splits enable thorough evaluation of how methods generalize to new people, viewpoints, and grasping styles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Applications in Robotics

A key application of the DexYCB dataset is enabling safe human-to-robot object handovers—a common interaction scenario in collaborative robotics.

The handover task requires:
1. Accurate object pose estimation
2. Hand segmentation to identify human contact regions
3. Planning robot grasps that avoid collision with the human hand

The authors demonstrated a baseline approach where safe grasp points are generated by:
```python
def generate_safe_grasps(object_pose, hand_segmentation):
    # Get all potential grasp points for the object
    candidate_grasps = sample_grasp_points(object_pose)

    # Filter out grasps that would collide with the hand
    safe_grasps = []
    for grasp in candidate_grasps:
        if not intersects_with_hand(grasp, hand_segmentation):
            safe_grasps.append(grasp)

    return safe_grasps
```

Through experiments, they showed that while this approach works in principle, its performance is limited by the accuracy of object pose estimation and hand segmentation—precisely the challenging tasks that DexYCB helps to advance.

#### 5.2. Significance and Impact

DexYCB's contributions to the field are multi-faceted:

1. **Dataset Scale**: With 582,000 frames across 1,000 sequences, DexYCB is significantly larger than previous hand-object interaction datasets.

2. **Annotation Quality**: The multi-view optimization approach provides high-quality 3D annotations even in challenging interaction scenarios.

3. **Diversity**: The dataset captures a wide range of subjects, objects, views, and grasping styles, providing broad coverage of the hand-object interaction space.

4. **Benchmark Protocols**: The established evaluation protocols enable fair comparison of different methods and highlight specific generalization challenges.

5. **Robotics Applications**: By focusing on the human-to-robot handover scenario, the dataset directly connects to practical robotics applications.

Cross-dataset experiments showed that models trained on DexYCB generalize better to other datasets (like HO-3D) than vice versa, highlighting the dataset's quality and diversity.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

The DexYCB dataset represents a significant advancement in the study of hand-object interactions. By providing a large-scale, high-quality benchmark that captures realistic grasping scenarios, it enables researchers to develop and evaluate methods for joint hand and object pose estimation in interactive contexts.

The benchmark results demonstrate that while current state-of-the-art methods perform reasonably well on this challenging task, there remains significant room for improvement, especially in generalizing to new subjects, viewpoints, and grasping configurations.

As robots become increasingly integrated into human environments, the ability to understand and interact with human manipulation activities will become ever more important. The DexYCB dataset provides a valuable resource for advancing this capability, potentially accelerating progress in robotics, augmented reality, and human-computer interaction.

By addressing the specific challenges of hand-object interaction, DexYCB helps bridge the gap between perception systems that understand hands, systems that understand objects, and the complex interplay that occurs when the two interact—bringing us closer to machines that can seamlessly collaborate with humans in manipulation tasks.
