# SkyJEPA: Learning Long-Horizon World Models for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Control of Quadrotors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, NYU, and Brown University developed SkyJEPA, a framework that uses a JEPA-style latent dynamics model with a physics-inspired prober for zero-shot sim-to-real control of quadrotors. Trained entirely on automated, domain-randomized simulation data, the method achieved 26-38% lower position RMSE and 33-54% lower attitude RMSE in real-world closed-loop tracking compared to baseline models, demonstrating robust long-horizon prediction on embedded hardware.

## Problem

- Existing classical and neural dynamics models for quadrotors suffer from compounding errors, leading to unstable and physically implausible long-horizon predictions.
- Learning accurate dynamics models for aerial robots traditionally requires extensive, costly, and risky real-world data collection, which can result in biased or incomplete datasets.
- Most learned dynamics models are task-specific and lack zero-shot generalization capabilities across different trajectories, control objectives, or platform configurations.

## Method

- A ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==-style latent dynamics model is introduced to predict future embeddings in a compact latent space, minimizing compounding errors in long-horizon predictions.
- A physics-inspired prober is trained to map the abstract latent space predictions to physically meaningful state variables (e.g., position, velocity, attitude) by learning residual corrections to a differentiable kinematic model.
- An automated, domain-randomized simulation pipeline generates diverse and dynamically feasible training data, reducing reliance on real-world data and improving robustness for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The JEPA-style model with the physics-inspired prober reduced open-loop mean position RMSE by **3.9x** and attitude error by **8.5x** compared to latent models without the prober.
- In real-world closed-loop control, the proposed method achieved 26-**38%** lower mean position RMSE and 33-**54%** lower mean attitude RMSE across diverse trajectories compared to direct predictive baselines.
- The framework maintained robustness under non-nominal conditions, showing **1.2**-**1.4x** lower position and attitude errors than baselines during propeller switching and payload transportation without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning dynamics in a compact latent space, regularized with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), effectively mitigates error accumulation, leading to more stable and temporally consistent long-horizon rollouts.
> - Coupling latent dynamics with a dedicated physics-inspired prober is crucial for translating abstract latent predictions into accurate and interpretable physical state trajectories, significantly improving metric-state recovery.
> - Systematic domain randomization in simulation, combined with diverse trajectory generation, enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and graceful degradation under unmodeled platform variations (e.g., payload changes, propeller damage).
