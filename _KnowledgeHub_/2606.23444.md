# SkyJEPA: Learning Long-Horizon World Models for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Control of Quadrotors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, NYU, and Brown University developed SkyJEPA, a framework that uses a JEPA-style latent dynamics model with a physics-inspired prober for zero-shot sim-to-real control of quadrotors. Trained entirely on automated, domain-randomized simulation data, the method achieved 26-38% lower position RMSE and 33-54% lower attitude RMSE in real-world closed-loop tracking compared to baseline models, demonstrating robust long-horizon prediction on embedded hardware.

## Problem

- Existing classical and neural dynamics models for quadrotors suffer from compounding errors, leading to unstable and physically implausible long-horizon predictions.
- Learning accurate dynamics models for aerial robots traditionally requires extensive, costly, and risky real-world data collection, which can result in biased or incomplete datasets.
- Most learned dynamics models are task-specific and lack zero-shot generalization capabilities across different trajectories, control objectives, or platform configurations.

## Method

- A ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==-style latent dynamics model is introduced to predict future embeddings in a compact latent space, minimizing compounding errors in long-horizon predictions.
- A physics-inspired prober is trained to map the abstract latent space predictions to physically meaningful state variables (e.g., position, velocity, attitude) by learning residual corrections to a differentiable kinematic model.
- An automated, domain-randomized simulation pipeline generates diverse and dynamically feasible training data, reducing reliance on real-world data and improving robustness for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The JEPA-style model with the physics-inspired prober reduced open-loop mean position RMSE by **3.9x** and attitude error by **8.5x** compared to latent models without the prober.
- In real-world closed-loop control, the proposed method achieved 26-**38%** lower mean position RMSE and 33-**54%** lower mean attitude RMSE across diverse trajectories compared to direct predictive baselines.
- The framework maintained robustness under non-nominal conditions, showing **1.2**-**1.4x** lower position and attitude errors than baselines during propeller switching and payload transportation without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning dynamics in a compact latent space, regularized with Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg), effectively mitigates error accumulation, leading to more stable and temporally consistent long-horizon rollouts.
> - Coupling latent dynamics with a dedicated physics-inspired prober is crucial for translating abstract latent predictions into accurate and interpretable physical state trajectories, significantly improving metric-state recovery.
> - Systematic domain randomization in simulation, combined with diverse trajectory generation, enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and graceful degradation under unmodeled platform variations (e.g., payload changes, propeller damage).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate dynamics models are fundamental for informed decision-making in robotic systems, particularly for agile Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating in complex, uncertain, and dynamic environments. Model-based control, which explicitly reasons about future system evolution, task objectives, and dynamical constraints, is an attractive paradigm for aerial robotics.

Classical first-principles models for quadrotors, while computationally efficient and structured, struggle to capture complex, platform-specific effects (e.g., aerodynamic drag, actuator delays, hardware variations) without extensive system identification and manual tuning. This limitation has motivated the development of neural dynamics models, which learn unmodeled nonlinear effects directly from data. Most existing neural dynamics approaches, whether offline or online, rely on an autoregressive encoder-decoder formulation where predicted states or observations are recursively fed back as inputs for future prediction. This autoregressive nature leads to compounding errors, where small one-step inaccuracies accumulate over time, resulting in drift, instability, and physically implausible long-horizon trajectories. Methods like physics-inspired regularization, residual structures, or online adaptation aim to improve local accuracy but do not eliminate the fundamental issue of error accumulation. Furthermore, reconstructing full future states or high-dimensional observations can force the model to preserve nuisance factors, increasing computational cost and sensitivity to noise.

Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) offers another direction by jointly learning a world model and a policy. While capable of strong task performance, MBRL models are often coupled to specific rewards, policies, or visitation distributions, limiting their generalization across different tasks or platform configurations—a crucial requirement for aerial robotics where a single dynamics model should support multiple applications.

Data collection poses a significant challenge for learned dynamics models. Generating informative, diverse, and robust datasets that efficiently cover a broad flight envelope for aerial robots is expensive, risky, and labor-intensive, often relying on hand-designed trajectories or expert pilots, which can lead to incomplete or biased data.

Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) present an alternative by learning to predict future embeddings in a compact latent space rather than reconstructing full future observations. This approach can yield more compact representations and scale to high-dimensional inputs. Prior JEPA-style work in robotics has primarily focused on vision-centric tasks like manipulation and navigation, often studied at the kinematic planning level or in simulation without extensive real-world validation for high-frequency feedback control.

This research addresses the limitations of existing dynamics models by introducing a JEPA-style framework specifically designed for real-time quadrotor control. It aims to mitigate compounding errors, provide physically interpretable rollouts, support real-time inference on embedded hardware, and enable zero-shot generalization by combining latent dynamics modeling with a novel physics-inspired prober and an automated, domain-randomized simulation data generation pipeline. This work extends JEPA-style learning to the domain of high-frequency dynamic control for agile aerial robots, a largely unexplored area for these architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question guiding this work is: Can an efficient dynamic model for aerial robots be learned that provides accurate long-horizon prediction, physically interpretable rollouts, real-time control capability, and zero-shot generalization across tasks, while simultaneously reducing dependence on risky real-world data collection?

This question is motivated by the limitations of existing approaches and the critical requirements for autonomous quadrotor operation. The authors identify four key properties for an effective quadrotor world model:

1. **Accurate Long-Horizon Prediction:** The model's predictions (rollouts) must remain accurate, stable, and physically plausible over the prediction horizons necessary for control. This requires capturing both dominant rigid-body dynamics and complex unmodeled effects such as aerodynamic drag, actuator delays, propeller-airframe interactions, and wind disturbances. Existing autoregressive neural dynamics models often fail in this regard due to compounding errors.
2. **Interpretability:** The model should explicitly represent and expose physically meaningful quantities like position, velocity, attitude, and angular velocity. This is crucial for integrating the model into a controller that enforces constraints, actuator limits, safety bounds, and task costs. Latent space models, by themselves, often lack this direct physical interpretability.
3. **Real-Time Inference:** High-frequency model evaluations are required within resource-constrained onboard optimization loops for real-time aerial control. This necessitates computationally efficient models.
4. **Zero-Shot Task Generalization:** The learned dynamics representation should be reusable across various trajectories, controllers, objectives, and platform configurations without requiring retraining or fine-tuning for each new scenario. Many existing MBRL models are task-coupled, limiting this generalization.

To achieve these objectives, the authors highlight the need for a robust data collection strategy that is diverse, dynamically feasible, and accounts for variations in physical parameters, moving beyond costly and risky real-world data.

The paper's key contributions, reflecting these objectives, are:
- Introduction of a JEPA-style latent dynamics model combined with a physics-inspired prober that maps frozen representations to physically meaningful state variables, enabling physically grounded long-horizon prediction for quadrotor control.
- Demonstration of the integration of this learned latent dynamics model within a sampling-based optimization framework (MPPI) for real-time control and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on embedded hardware.
- Development of a domain-randomized simulation pipeline for automated dataset generation, aiming to reduce the reliance on extensive and potentially unsafe real-world data collection.
- Extensive evaluation against baseline methods through open-loop prediction and outdoor closed-loop control experiments, demonstrating improved long-horizon accuracy, robust control, and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SkyJEPA, combines a JEPA-style latent dynamics model with a physics-inspired prober, trained using an automated domain-randomized simulation data pipeline, and integrated into a sampling-based optimal control solution for real-time quadrotor control.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system dynamics are modeled as a discrete-time transition function $x_{t+1} = f(x_t, a_t)$, where $x_t$ is the state vector (position, velocity, rotation matrix columns, angular velocity) and $a_t$ represents the four motor forces. To address compounding errors from autoregressive state prediction, dynamics learning is performed in a compact latent space following JEPA principles. The model is conditioned on histories of states ($X_t$) and actions ($A_t$) over a window of length $H$. These histories are encoded into latent representations $s_t = \text{Enc}_\theta(X_t)$ and $z_t = \text{Enc}_\phi(A_t)$. A latent dynamics predictor, $\text{Pred}_\varphi$, then estimates the next latent state $\tilde{s}_{t+1} = \text{Pred}_\varphi(s_t, z_t)$, which can be recursively unrolled over a prediction horizon $T$.

**Training Objective:**
The training objective is designed to support accurate multi-step latent dynamics prediction while preventing representation collapse. It consists of two main terms:
1. **Multi-step latent prediction loss ($L_{\text{pred}}$):** This is an L2 loss that enforces consistency between the rolled-out latent predictions ($\tilde{s}_{t+k}$) and the encoded true future states ($s_{t+k}$) over the prediction horizon.
2. **Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) ($L_{\text{SIGReg}}$):** This anti-collapse term encourages the latent embeddings to match an isotropic Gaussian distribution. SIGReg achieves this by comparing random one-dimensional projections of the latent distribution to a standard Gaussian, using the Epps-Pulley test statistic. This approach promotes diversity and isotropy in the latent space with minimal hyperparameters.
The total objective function is $L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{pred}} + \lambda_{\text{sig}} L_{\text{SIGReg}}$, where $\lambda_{\text{sig}}$ controls the regularization strength.

**Physics-Inspired Probing Mechanism:**
To convert abstract latent predictions into physically meaningful quantities required for control, a two-stage training process is employed. After the latent dynamics model is trained and its parameters frozen, a physics-inspired probing network ($\psi$) is trained. Given a predicted latent sequence and the current state $x_t$, the prober predicts residual correction terms: translational acceleration ($\Delta \dot{v}_{t+k}$) and angular acceleration ($K_{t+k}$) for the motor forces. These residuals are then integrated using a compact, differentiable kinematic model:
$\dot{v}_t = \frac{\sum f_{i,t}}{m} R_t e_3 - g + \Delta \dot{v}_t$
$\Delta \tau_t = K_t a_t$
The state is propagated using standard kinematic equations for position, velocity, rotation (via SO(3) exponential map), and angular velocity. The prober is optimized using a supervised L2 rollout loss ($L_{\text{prober}}$) between the integrated predicted states ($\tilde{x}_{t+k}$) and the ground-truth states ($x_{t+k}$). A stop-gradient operation prevents this stage from altering the learned latent dynamics.

**Sampling-Based Control with Learned Dynamics (MPPI):**
The learned dynamics model is integrated into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) framework for real-time trajectory tracking.
1. **Action Sampling:** Candidate action sequences are generated by perturbing a nominal sequence with Gaussian noise, and then clamped to admissible actuator limits.
2. **Latent Rollout and State Prediction:** For each sampled action sequence, the JEPA-style latent dynamics model is unrolled, and the physics-inspired prober maps the predicted latent sequence to physical state trajectories.
3. **Trajectory Cost:** Each trajectory is evaluated using a running cost function that penalizes deviations from reference states and control effort (weighted quadratic tracking errors).
4. **Softmax Weighting and Control Update:** Importance weights are computed for each trajectory based on its cost, using a softmax function. The nominal action sequence is then updated as a weighted average of the sampled actions. Only the first action of the updated sequence is executed, and the process repeats in a receding-horizon manner. The PyTorch latent dynamics model is optimized using NVIDIA TensorRT for accelerated inference on embedded hardware.

**Automated Data Synthesis:**
To facilitate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and avoid costly real-world data collection, a structured pipeline for automated dataset generation in simulation is developed. This pipeline ensures the dataset is diverse, dynamically feasible, and robust to physical parameter variations.
1. **Reference Trajectory Generation:** Randomized, smooth, and dynamically rich reference trajectories are generated using Gaussian processes with sums of periodic kernels for each spatial axis. This ensures broad coverage of the quadrotor's state space (positions, velocities, accelerations, attitudes, angular velocities).
2. **Closed-Loop Trajectory Tracking:** These generated references are tracked in simulation using a combination of Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) and MPPI. This process generates physically feasible state-action trajectories that respect actuator limits and dynamic constraints, and exposes the model to diverse action distributions.
3. **Domain-Randomized Quadrotor Simulation:** Data is collected from a simulator where quadrotor parameters (mass, drag matrix, inertia matrix, motor time constant, thrust/torque coefficients, arm length) are sampled from bounded uniform distributions around nominal values for each rollout. This exposes the learned model to a "family" of systems, improving robustness and sim-to-real transfer by covering potential discrepancies between simulation and reality.

**Implementation Details:**
Experiments are conducted on a 1.3-kg quadrotor with an NVIDIA Orin NX for onboard computation. The training dataset consists of 20,000 10-second reference trajectories generated from 500 distinct domain-randomized quadrotor models, sampled at 20 Hz. The state encoder ($\text{Enc}_\theta$) and action encoder ($\text{Enc}_\phi$) are Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCNs). The latent dynamics predictor is a single-layer GRU, unrolled over 20 steps (1.0 s). The history length is 10 timesteps (0.5 s). MPPI uses a prediction horizon of 15 steps and 512 samples. The overall framework, with its lightweight latent dynamics model (approx. 9K parameters), operates within a 10 ms real-time control budget on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation addresses five key questions: latent dynamics modeling, physics interpretability, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, robustness, and data quality.

**A. Latent Dynamics Modeling**
- **Compounding Error Analysis:** The proposed JEPA-style method demonstrates reduced compounding error compared to a direct autoregressive predictive baseline. While initial compounding ratios are similar or slightly higher for the proposed method at very short horizons (due to sensitivity to small errors), its long-horizon compounding ratio (CR) remains closer to 1 (approx. 1.4 at k=60) compared to the predictive baseline (approx. 2.4 at k=60). The error growth rate (ER) for the proposed method also consistently remains lower across the horizon, indicating slower error accumulation per step. This suggests that latent-space dynamics modeling effectively mitigates recursive error propagation.
- **Temporal Straightening:** Analysis of latent trajectory geometry reveals that both latent models (JEPA-based and reconstruction-based) produce substantially smoother and "straighter" temporal evolution in their representation spaces compared to the direct predictive baseline, which often exhibits negative straightening scores (indicating directional inconsistency). This emergent property in latent space is hypothesized to make recursive rollouts easier to maintain over long horizons, reducing directional drift.
- **Robustness to Noise:** The JEPA-style model consistently outperforms the predictive baseline under increasing levels of i.i.d. Gaussian observation noise applied to the model input. It maintains lower median state RMSE across all noise levels, with more concentrated error distributions, suggesting higher robustness to sensing corruption.

**B. Physics Interpretability**
Comparative evaluation of six dynamics models (Predictive, Predictive + Physics Reg, Reconstruction + Prober, Reconstruction + PI Prober, Ours + Prober, Ours + PI Prober) reveals the impact of latent dynamics modeling and physics-inspired probing on open-loop prediction fidelity:
- **Latent Dynamics vs. Direct Predictive:** Latent dynamics models (both reconstruction-based and JEPA-based) generally outperform direct autoregressive predictive models, demonstrating lower position and attitude RMSE and variance. Adding physics regularization to a direct predictive model only yields modest improvements, suggesting it's insufficient to overcome fundamental instability.
- **Impact of Physics-Inspired Prober:** The most significant improvements in metric-state recovery are observed with the integration of the Physics-Inspired (PI) Prober. For the reconstruction model, the PI Prober reduces mean position RMSE from 6.82m to 1.53m (4.5x improvement) and attitude error from 45.2° to 5.28° (8.6x improvement). For the proposed JEPA model, the PI Prober reduces position RMSE from 5.56m to 1.43m (3.9x improvement) and attitude error from 40.2° to 4.71° (8.5x improvement). Variance is also sharply reduced. This indicates that grounding latent rollouts with a structured kinematic model that learns residual corrections is critical for accurate and stable physical state prediction.

**C. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**
Closed-loop real-world flight experiments, with the model trained solely on domain-randomized simulation data, demonstrate robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:
- The proposed method consistently achieves lower mean position and attitude RMSE across diverse and increasingly aggressive reference trajectories (e.g., circle, oval, figure-8, fish, lemniscate) compared to MPPI controllers using direct predictive models (with or without physics regularization). Position error reductions range from 26% to 38%, and attitude error reductions from 33% to 54%.
- Lower variance in tracking metrics indicates more repeatable closed-loop behavior.
- Qualitative results visually confirm the controller's ability to closely follow diverse real-world paths with low tracking error.

**D. Robustness**
The framework's robustness is evaluated under two non-nominal deployment scenarios—propeller switching (altering actuation) and payload transportation (modifying mass and inertia)—without any retraining:
- **Propeller Switching:** The proposed method maintains approximately 1.2x to 1.3x lower position and attitude RMSE compared to predictive baselines.
- **Payload Transportation (300g payload):** The proposed method exhibits approximately 1.27x to 1.35x lower position RMSE and about 1.4x lower attitude error compared to predictive baselines.
- Consistently lower variance is observed in both scenarios, indicating stable and repeatable performance despite platform changes not explicitly covered in training. These results suggest that the learned JEPA representation captures more transferable dynamics, degrading gracefully under model mismatch.

**E. Data Quality**
- **Trajectory Distribution Quality (TDQ) Score:** A novel TDQ score, combining state-action coverage, transition richness, and parameter robustness (measured via clustering and entropy), is introduced.
- **Correlation with Prediction Error:** An inverse relationship is observed between TDQ and state prediction error (RMSE). As TDQ increases from 0.01 (1k samples) to 0.94 (1.5M samples), the state RMSE decreases from 5.4m to 1.4m. This demonstrates that broader state-action coverage, richer transitions, and greater parameter diversity in the training data lead to improved learned dynamics prediction. The gains tend to saturate at higher TDQ values, suggesting that the dynamics manifold is sufficiently covered, and further improvements may require addressing model capacity or optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, SkyJEPA, that addresses several limitations of existing dynamics modeling approaches for quadrotor control and offers practical advantages for robotic autonomy.

**Meeting Key World Model Properties:** The work demonstrates that the proposed JEPA-style latent dynamics model, coupled with a physics-inspired prober, can satisfy the four critical properties of a useful world model for quadrotor control:
1. **Improved Long-Horizon Prediction:** By operating in a compact latent space and utilizing SIGReg, the model significantly reduces compounding errors compared to traditional autoregressive state prediction, leading to more stable and accurate long-horizon rollouts.
2. **Physically Interpretable Rollouts:** The novel physics-inspired prober effectively translates abstract latent predictions into meaningful metric-state trajectories, making the model suitable for control tasks that require state constraints, cost evaluations, and actuator limits. This structured approach leverages prior kinematic knowledge without requiring precise parameter estimation.
3. **Real-Time Capability:** The lightweight neural architecture and optimized inference (via TensorRT) enable the entire framework to run at high frequencies on embedded hardware, crucial for real-time, closed-loop control of agile aerial vehicles.
4. **Zero-Shot Task Generalization:** The model's ability to generalize across diverse trajectories and non-nominal platform conditions (e.g., payload changes, propeller switching) without retraining is a significant achievement. This task-agnosticism enhances reusability and reduces deployment overhead.

**Advancement in Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A central impact of this work is the demonstration that a meticulously designed domain-randomized simulation data pipeline, combined with an appropriate learning framework, can enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex aerial robot dynamics. This approach systematically generates diverse trajectories and exposes the model to a broad family of plausible quadrotor dynamics, reducing the reliance on costly, risky, and time-consuming real-world data collection and system identification processes. The introduction of the Trajectory Distribution Quality (TDQ) score provides a quantifiable metric for assessing dataset utility, offering guidance for efficient data generation strategies.

**Practicality of Learning Objective:** The use of SIGReg for anti-collapse regularization simplifies the training objective compared to other self-supervised representation learning methods that often require numerous carefully tuned hyperparameters, target networks, or stop-gradient strategies. This simplicity contributes to the framework's practical appeal for general-purpose dynamics representation learning.

**Future Directions and Broader Impact:** The findings suggest that the learned representation captures dynamics more robustly to platform variations than direct predictive models. Future work extending this framework to high-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB, RGB-D images) would be particularly impactful for visual navigation tasks, obstacle avoidance, and goal-directed flight, as JEPA-style learning is well-suited for avoiding the computational and representational challenges of full observation reconstruction. Additionally, incorporating safety considerations more explicitly into the learned representation and planning objectives could enhance the framework's applicability in cluttered and uncertain real-world environments. This research contributes to the development of more autonomous, robust, and generalizable aerial robotic systems.
