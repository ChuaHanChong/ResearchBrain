# Stateful Visual Encoders for Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Stateful Visual Encoders (SVEs) as an architectural extension for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), enabling direct cross-image interaction within the visual encoder itself. This modification enhances the VLM's ability to observe and analyze visual changes across image sequences, leading to a significant reduction in mean absolute error for cross-image spatial aggregation and improved F1 scores in longitudinal radiology tasks.

## Problem

- Existing open-weight Vision-Language Models (VLMs) typically employ "stateless" visual encoders that process each image independently, neglecting prior visual context.
- Subtle but task-critical visual changes can be attenuated or lost during independent per-image encoding, hindering the language model's ability to perform effective cross-image comparisons.
- The prevalent "late fusion" paradigm, where visual comparison occurs primarily within the language model, misses opportunities to emphasize inter-image differences earlier in the VLM pipeline.

## Method

- The paper proposes **Stateful Visual Encoders** (SVEs), a lightweight architectural extension that injects causal cross-image interactions directly into the visual encoding process of existing VLMs.
- Four SVE variants are explored, with the "Cross+FFN" design (inserting a ==cross-attention== layer and a feed-forward block) demonstrating the most robust performance.
- A practical finetuning strategy is developed, incorporating zero-initialization for new module output branches and applying a stop-gradient operation to predecessor features, to stabilize training and leverage pretrained backbones efficiently.

## Results

- The Cross+FFN SVE design consistently outperformed stateless baselines across synthetic tasks, achieving a Dot-Distance Mean Absolute Error of **0.56** compared to 1.17 for the baseline on spatial aggregation and improving multi-object visual differencing change accuracy from **91.1%** to **92.7%**.
- SVEs demonstrated strong generality, showing consistent improvements over stateless baselines across a range of input resolutions (256x256 to 768x768), language model sizes (0.8B to 9B), and diverse VLM architectures (e.g., Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5).
- In real-world applications, SVEs improved performance on longitudinal radiology (CIDEr score of 178.9 vs 145.1 for baseline), fine-grained image comparison, and surpassed prior specialized models for remote sensing change captioning (S*m score of 80.46).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding cross-image interaction directly within the visual encoder prevents the attenuation of subtle visual changes, enabling VLMs to capture temporal relationships more effectively than late-fusion approaches.
> - Careful initialization and optimization choices, such as zero-initializing new module outputs and applying a stop-gradient to prior features, are crucial for successfully integrating statefulness into pretrained VLM backbones.
> - SVEs learn context-dependent visual representations by performing selective cross-image reading, where most spatial positions show near-zero updates, focusing updates on localized features relevant to visual change detection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in various tasks by integrating visual and textual information. A common architectural paradigm for open-weight VLMs involves a visual encoder processing each image independently, a process referred to as "stateless" visual encoding. The resulting visual features are then passed to a language model, which performs cross-image comparisons if multiple images are provided. This late-fusion approach, where visual comparison occurs primarily within the language model, is prevalent in many multi-image VLMs such as MANTIS, LLaVA-NeXT-Interleave, Idefics3, and VILA.

This research addresses a limitation within this prevalent paradigm: the potential for visual encoders to attenuate subtle but task-critical visual changes before they reach the language model. When visual changes do not significantly alter the high-level semantics of a scene, a stateless visual encoder, often pre-trained for static image-language alignment or self-supervised representations, may not preserve the fine-grained differences necessary for comparative tasks.

The work distinguishes itself from several related areas:
- **Specialized Change-Detection Models:** Unlike architectures such as Park et al. (2019) or Chen et al. (2021) that compare images within the visual encoder for specific change detection tasks, this research aims to develop a general-purpose visual encoder extension for VLMs that enables inherent statefulness rather than a task-specific solution.
- **Video Visual Encoders:** Existing video encoders (e.g., I3D, MViT, VideoMAE) are designed to learn spatiotemporal representations from continuous frame sequences. This work, however, targets image-based VLMs that process discrete sequences of images (e.g., sparse observations, before-after pairs, interaction states) and introduces causal cross-image conditioning into an *existing* image encoder, rather than training a new spatiotemporal backbone from scratch. The conditioning is designed to be causal, meaning the current image attends only to past visual features, aligning with interactive settings where future observations are not available.
- **Multi-Image Encoding in VLMs:** While many VLMs can process multiple images, they typically do so via late fusion. This means each image is encoded independently, and the comparison or reasoning across images is performed by the language model based on independently generated visual tokens. This paper identifies and addresses a complementary bottleneck by enabling the current image's visual backbone to retrieve and integrate prior visual features *before* serialization to the Large Language Model (LLM). This earlier integration of visual context aims to prevent the loss of subtle differences.

By introducing a "Stateful Visual Encoder" (SVE), the paper proposes an architectural modification that injects cross-image interaction directly into the visual encoding process. This approach aims to enhance a VLM's ability to observe, track, and analyze visual changes across image sequences, thereby improving performance on tasks that require detailed visual comparisons.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in multi-image, multi-turn agentic settings where decisions rely on visual changes. This is achieved by introducing a "Stateful Visual Encoder" (SVE) that conditions each visual representation on prior visual features directly within the visual encoder.

The motivation for this work stems from a recognized limitation in the current architecture of open-weight VLMs:
1. **Stateless Visual Encoding:** Existing VLMs typically employ visual encoders that process each image independently, without explicit access to previous visual context. This "stateless" nature means that while the VLM as a whole might process a sequence of images, the visual encoder itself does not inherently capture temporal relationships or changes between images.
2. **Attenuation of Subtle Changes:** Visual changes critical for task performance are frequently subtle. Examples provided include the appearance or resolution of a chest X-ray finding, the emergence of a small structure in a satellite image, or localized attribute modifications in an edited image. In a stateless encoding paradigm, these fine-grained differences can be suppressed or attenuated during independent per-image encoding, reducing the likelihood that the subsequent language model can effectively compare them.
3. **Limitations of Late Fusion:** In current multi-image VLMs, cross-image comparisons primarily occur within the language model after visual features have been independently extracted. This "late fusion" approach means that the initial visual encoding step misses the opportunity to actively preserve or emphasize inter-image differences, potentially leading to a loss of information before it reaches the reasoning component of the VLM.

To address these issues, the research sets forth several contributions:
- **Introduction of SVE:** Proposing a simple architectural extension to inject causal cross-image interactions into the visual encoder of open-weight VLMs without requiring a complete visual backbone replacement or retraining from scratch.
- **Development of a Practical Finetuning Strategy:** Establishing initialization and optimization choices designed to stabilize finetuning and improve the learning of state-dependent visual representations in the Supervised Finetuning (SFT) regime. This includes techniques like zero-initialization of output branches and stop-gradient application to prior features.
- **Demonstration of Effectiveness and Generality:** Validating the SVE's performance across controlled synthetic tasks that demand strict visual comparisons (e.g., cross-image spatial aggregation, multi-object visual differencing, visual trajectory behavior cloning). Furthermore, assessing its robustness across varying input resolutions, language model sizes, and diverse VLM backbones.
- **Validation on Real-World Applications:** Applying the SVE to practical, real-world comparison tasks, including longitudinal radiology, fine-grained image comparison, and remote sensing, to demonstrate its utility beyond controlled environments.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond the limitations of stateless visual encoding by enabling earlier and more direct visual interaction within the VLM's visual backbone, thereby equipping VLMs with an enhanced capacity for dynamic visual comparison and reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Stateful Visual Encoder (SVE) as an architectural extension to existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The approach involves several design choices for integrating statefulness, a specific training setup, and evaluation across controlled synthetic tasks and real-world applications.

#### 3.1. SVE Architectural Designs

The study explores four lightweight variants for conditioning current visual representations ($Z_t$) on prior visual features ($Z_{t-1}$) within the visual encoder (Figure 2):
1. **Self-Ext:** This variant extends the key-value set of the pretrained self-attention mechanism by concatenating features from the previous image ($[Z_t; Z_{t-1}]$). This means the current image's queries can attend to both current and previous image features.
2. **AdaLN-Zero:** This design pools features from the previous image to generate adaptive normalization parameters (scale and shift) that modulate the self-attention and feed-forward layers of the current image's processing. For the first image in a sequence, a zero vector is used for conditioning.
3. **Cross:** A full token-level cross-attention layer is inserted before each pretrained self-attention layer. Queries are derived from all visual tokens of the current image ($Z_t$), while keys and values are derived from all visual tokens of the previous image ($Z_{t-1}$).
4. **Cross+FFN:** This variant extends the "Cross" design by adding a feed-forward block immediately after the inserted cross-attention layer.

#### 3.2. Training Setup

The training methodology incorporates specific initialization and optimization strategies to integrate SVE into pretrained VLMs:
- **Initialization:** New parameters within the visual encoder are initialized based on existing pretrained weights. For added cross-attention layers, input projection weights (Q, K, V) are copied from the corresponding pretrained self-attention layer within the same visual encoder block. Output projections ($W_{cross}^O$) are zero-initialized. For the added FFN in Cross+FFN, the first linear layer is copied from the corresponding pretrained FFN, and the second is zero-initialized. This strategy aims to leverage existing feature bases while ensuring that the new modules do not disrupt the pretrained visual encoder's initial behavior.
- **Conditioning:** In cross-attention variants, the current visual features ($Z_t$) serve as queries, and the predecessor visual features ($Z_{t-1}$) provide keys and values. For the first image in a sequence, $Z_1$ itself is used as the key-value source for the predecessor path.
- **Optimization:** During training, a stop-gradient operation is applied to the predecessor branch's keys and values in all cross-attention variants. This prevents gradients from flowing through the previous image's features, treating them as a stable context for retrieval rather than co-adapting them with the current image's update.

#### 3.3. Task Setup (Synthetic)

Three controlled synthetic tasks are used to evaluate SVE designs and finetuning strategies:
1. **Cross-Image Spatial Aggregation:** Models are presented with sequences of images featuring randomly placed red dots on diverse backgrounds. The task requires predicting geometric quantities (Euclidean distance, convex-hull area) across these dots, isolating the VLM's ability to localize small changes and aggregate spatial information. Metrics: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE).
2. **Multi-Object Visual Differencing:** Using a modified CLEVR-Multi-Change engine, models analyze pairs of cluttered scenes (30-40 objects) with four subtle changes (movement, insertion, deletion, replacement). The task is to describe the changed objects and their change types. Metrics: Exact-match accuracy for change prediction, and standard language generation metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, METEOR, SPICE, ROUGE-L).
3. **Visual Trajectory Behavioral Cloning:** Models imitate heuristic-solver demonstrations from VisGym across four tasks (Patch Reassembly, 3D Mental Rotation (Cube/Objaverse), Matchstick Rotation). This tests state tracking and action prediction in interactive settings. Metric: Perplexity.

#### 3.4. Task Setup (Real-World)

The effectiveness of SVEs is validated on three real-world comparison tasks:
1. **Longitudinal Radiology (Medical-Diff-VQA):** Detecting and describing medical changes between paired chest X-ray images from the same patient. Metrics: Standard captioning metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr) and a structured finding-level F1 and change accuracy based on the RATE framework.
2. **Fine-grained Image Comparison (ImgEdit):** Given source and edited image pairs, the model predicts the editing instruction. This evaluates reasoning about observed visual differences matching a requested change. Metrics: MLLM-as-a-judge (Claude-Opus-4.7) for pairwise preference counts against baseline and reference.
3. **Remote Sensing (LEVIR-CC):** Comparing before-after aerial/satellite images to describe scene changes (e.g., new buildings, altered land use). Metrics: Standard captioning metrics and S*m (average of BLEU-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr).

#### 3.5. Generality and Feature Analysis

The research also investigates the generality of the chosen SVE design (Cross+FFN) across different input resolutions (256x256 to 768x768), language model sizes (0.8B to 9B), and diverse VLM backbones (Qwen3-VL, Qwen3.5, GLM-4.6V-Flash, InternVL3.5, Gemma-3). A feature analysis is conducted to understand how SVE affects learned visual features, examining context sensitivity, model win rates on disagreed examples, and the spatial sparsity of cross-image updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness and characteristics of Stateful Visual Encoders (SVEs).

#### 4.1. SVE Design Comparison

Across the controlled synthetic tasks (cross-image spatial aggregation, multi-object visual differencing, and visual trajectory behavioral cloning), the **Cross+FFN** design consistently demonstrated the most robust improvements over the stateless baseline.
- **Spatial Aggregation (Table 1):** Cross+FFN achieved significant reductions in MAE and RMSE across all dot-distance and area estimation tasks, particularly a large reduction in Dot-Distance MAE (0.56 vs 1.17 for baseline). This suggests its utility in precise cross-image localization. Self-Ext. performed worse than the baseline, indicating potential disruption of pretrained features, while AdaLN-Zero showed minimal improvement.
- **Visual Differencing and Behavioral Cloning (Table 2):** On CLEVR-Multi-Change, Cross+FFN improved perplexity, change accuracy (from 91.1 to 92.7), and all language generation metrics (e.g., CIDEr from 529.5 to 543.9). For VisGym behavioral cloning, it improved performance across all four tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablations of the Cross+FFN recipe (Tables 3 and 4) identified key components for its effectiveness:
- **Capacity-controlled baseline (Self+FFN):** This variant, with equivalent parameters and FLOPs but no cross-image attention, showed some improvement over the stateless baseline but generally performed below Cross+FFN. This indicates that the gains are primarily due to explicit statefulness rather than merely increased model capacity.
- **W_Q,K,V,1 cloning and W_O,2 zero-initialization:** Copying input projections from self-attention and zero-initializing output projections of new modules were found to be important. Removing zero-initialization caused the largest degradation, suggesting its critical role in preserving the pretrained feature distribution.
- **Z_1 (K,V) fallback and stop grad(K,V):** Using the first image's features as the key-value source for itself and applying stop-gradient to the predecessor branch were beneficial. Removing stop-gradient led to weaker spatial aggregation and mixed results on visual differencing, supporting the role of prior features as a stable retrieval context.
- **Positional embeddings:** Removing positional embeddings from cross-attention consistently degraded performance, indicating their importance for state-dependent visual understanding.

#### 4.3. Generality

SVEs, specifically the Cross+FFN variant, demonstrated generality across diverse settings:
- **Input Resolutions and Model Sizes (Figure 4):** SVEs consistently outperformed stateless baselines from 256x256 to 768x768 input resolutions and from 0.8B to 9B language model sizes on the multi-object visual differencing task. Smaller SVE models were observed to match or surpass larger stateless baselines.
- **VLM Architectures (Table 5):** SVEs showed consistent improvements over stateless baselines across a range of VLM families, including Qwen3-VL, Qwen3.5, GLM-4.6V-Flash, InternVL3.5, and Gemma-3, despite substantial differences in their visual encoders, connectors, and language backbones.

#### 4.4. Feature Analysis of Stateful Representations

Analysis of learned visual features revealed characteristics of SVE's state-dependent processing (Figure 5):
- **Context Dependence:** SVE produced significantly lower minimum token similarity when comparing representations induced by the true predecessor versus a different predecessor, indicating that the representation of the current image is genuinely dependent on the preceding visual state. Stateless baselines showed invariance.
- **Task Relevance:** In cases where SVE and the capacity-controlled stateless baseline disagreed on CLEVR-Multi-Change, SVE had a higher win rate, suggesting that the context-dependent feature changes are predictive of improved visual change understanding.
- **Spatial Sparsity:** Heatmaps of cross-image update magnitudes showed that most spatial positions had near-zero updates, while a small number of tokens absorbed most of the cross-image change. This indicates that SVE performs selective cross-image reading, updating localized features relevant to state comparison while largely preserving the pretrained representation elsewhere.

#### 4.5. Real-World Task Validation

SVEs demonstrated effectiveness in practical applications:
- **Longitudinal Radiology (Table 6, Figure 6 top):** SVE-equipped VLMs showed improvements in standard captioning metrics (e.g., CIDEr 178.9 vs 145.1) and in finding-level F1 and change accuracy on the Medical-Diff-VQA dataset, indicating better capture of subtle longitudinal medical changes.
- **Fine-grained Image Comparison (Table 7, Figure 6 bottom-left):** On the ImgEdit dataset, an MLLM-as-a-judge evaluation indicated that SVE outputs were preferred over both the stateless baseline and the original reference instructions, suggesting improved instruction-guided image edit descriptions.
- **Remote Sensing (Table 8, Figure 6 bottom-right):** For the LEVIR-CC dataset, SVE improved over the stateless baseline and surpassed all prior specialized models and architectures in change captioning metrics (e.g., S*m 80.46 vs 79.97 for the previous best specialist model).

These results collectively indicate that the Stateful Visual Encoder provides a mechanism for vision-language models to process and compare sequences of images more effectively, leading to improved performance across a range of tasks requiring visual change detection and reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of the Stateful Visual Encoder (SVE) presents a significant development in the architecture and capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in dynamic and comparative visual tasks.

The core significance lies in addressing a fundamental limitation of many existing open-weight VLMs: their reliance on stateless visual encoders that process images independently. By embedding causal cross-image interactions directly within the visual encoder, SVE enables VLMs to perform earlier and more integrated visual comparisons. This early fusion of temporal context mitigates the risk of attenuating subtle but critical visual changes that might otherwise be lost before reaching the language model, thereby improving the VLM's ability to reason over sequential visual inputs.

The practical finetuning strategy developed, including cloned initialization and zero-initialization of new modules, facilitates the integration of SVEs into existing pretrained VLM backbones without requiring extensive retraining from scratch. This makes the SVE approach an accessible and efficient enhancement for the broader VLM community.

The demonstrated effectiveness across a diverse set of tasks highlights the potential impact of SVEs:
- **Enhanced Agentic Capabilities:** In multi-image, multi-turn agentic settings, SVEs can enable VLMs to better observe, track, and analyze visual changes, leading to more grounded decisions and improved performance in tasks such as behavioral cloning and interactive environments.
- **Improved Medical Diagnostics:** In longitudinal radiology, SVEs facilitate the detection of subtle, clinically meaningful changes in sequential scans, potentially aiding in more accurate and comprehensive diagnostic assessments.
- **Finer-Grained Image Understanding:** For tasks like image editing verification and change captioning in remote sensing, SVEs improve the model's ability to identify and describe precise visual differences, which can advance applications in content creation, environmental monitoring, and urban planning.
- **Architectural Flexibility:** The observed generality of SVEs across various input resolutions, language model sizes, and diverse VLM architectures (e.g., Qwen, GLM, InternVL, Gemma) suggests that this approach is not tied to a specific VLM design but offers a broadly applicable enhancement. This robustness indicates that SVEs could be integrated into a wide range of future VLM developments.

Overall, this work proposes a practical architectural path toward equipping generalist VLMs with a more intrinsic understanding of visual dynamics and changes across sequences of images. This could lead to the development of more capable and reliable VLMs in applications requiring sophisticated visual comparison and temporal reasoning, moving beyond the traditional paradigm of late fusion and independent image processing.
