# Exo-Plore: Exploring Exoskeleton Control Space through Human-aligned Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The EXO-PLORE framework offers a simulation-based approach to optimize exoskeleton control, generating human-aligned gait data that replicates observed metabolic energy expenditure and human-exoskeleton interaction patterns. This enables efficient identification of optimal assistive parameters for both able-bodied and various pathological gaits, demonstrating a viable alternative to strenuous human-in-the-loop experiments.

## Problem

- Human-in-the-Loop Optimization (HILO) for exoskeleton control is physically demanding, time-consuming, and often inaccessible for mobility-impaired individuals.
- Existing simulation methods struggle with the "sim-to-real gap," failing to accurately reflect human physiological responses like muscle activations and metabolic energy expenditure.
- Optimizing exoskeleton controllers for personalized assistance, especially for diverse pathological gaits, lacks efficient and scalable methods.

## Method

- A ==neuromechanical== simulation framework (Gait Data Generator) combines Deep Reinforcement Learning (PoseNet), PD control, and a Muscle Coordination Network (MCN) to produce realistic human-exoskeleton interaction.
- A novel reward function in Deep RL incorporates metabolic energy minimization and a Human-Exoskeleton Interaction (HEI) reward based on a resistance-minimization hypothesis, calibrated to match human cost of transport (CoT) curves and adaptive behaviors.
- An Exoskeleton Optimizer utilizes a neural network surrogate model, trained with Latin Hypercube Sampling and a specialized loss function (Huber loss, gradient penalties), for efficient gradient-based optimization of control parameters.

## Results

- The simulation accurately reproduced human-like kinematics, muscle activations, ground reaction forces, and the relationship between walking speed and metabolic cost of transport (CoT) in unassisted gait.
- Under assistance, the framework, particularly with the Human-Exoskeleton Interaction (HEI) reward, replicated human adaptive trends in hip kinematics, assistive power, and metabolic reduction rates, showing high correlation with empirical data.
- Exo-plore identified optimal exoskeleton control parameters for able-bodied individuals and revealed strong linear correlations between optimal gain and pathology severity for most pathological gaits (e.g., crouch, calcaneus, equinus, waddling), providing pathology-specific insights for personalized assistance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding observed human adaptive behaviors, specifically metabolic energy minimization and resistance minimization during interaction, into simulation rewards is crucial for bridging the sim-to-real gap in exoskeleton control.
> - Neural network surrogate models, when trained with appropriate regularization, can effectively learn complex CoT landscapes from noisy simulation data, enabling scalable gradient-based optimization for exoskeleton parameters.
> - The framework successfully generalizes to pathological gaits, revealing pathology-specific optimal assistance strategies where optimal gain can either increase or decrease with severity depending on gait stability and metabolic cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic exoskeletons holds immense promise for enhancing human mobility, particularly for individuals with impairments. However, optimizing exoskeleton controllers for effective assistance remains a significant challenge. The existing "gold standard" approach, Human-in-the-Loop Optimization (HILO), involves extensive human experiments where participants wear exoskeletons and walk for hours while parameters are iteratively tuned to minimize metabolic cost or maximize comfort.

This paper positions **Exo-plore** as a critical advancement addressing several limitations within this landscape:

- **The HILO Paradox:** HILO is physically demanding, making it largely inaccessible to the very population it aims to help—individuals with mobility impairments. Exo-plore directly addresses this "paradox" by replacing demanding human experiments with high-fidelity, human-aligned simulations.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Previous attempts at simulation-based exoskeleton control often struggle with the "sim-to-real gap," where simulated behaviors do not accurately reflect real human responses, especially regarding muscle activations, metabolic energy expenditure, and adaptation to external assistance. Exo-plore specifically tackles this by embedding empirically observed human behaviors (energy minimization, resistance minimization during interaction) directly into its reward function and calibration process.
- **Generalization to Pathological Gaits:** Most simulation frameworks for human locomotion and exoskeleton control have limited validation or application to pathological gaits. Exo-plore explicitly demonstrates its ability to generalize to and optimize for various pathological conditions, which is crucial for personalized assistive device design.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** While previous methods like Bayesian optimization with Gaussian Processes (GP) are data-efficient for HILO, they do not scale well with large datasets generated in simulation. Exo-plore introduces a neural network surrogate model trained on abundant simulation data, enabling fast, gradient-based optimization that is impractical with GP-based approaches.
- **Predictive Neuromechanical Simulation:** The work builds upon recent advances in neuromechanical simulations that couple musculoskeletal dynamics with control policies, moving towards more predictive models of human adaptation. Exo-plore distinguishes itself by developing a unified framework that can both fit controllers to observed human adaptations and predict responses in unobserved assistive conditions, especially for pathological gaits.

In essence, Exo-plore aims to transform exoskeleton controller design from an expensive, time-consuming, and participant-limited experimental process into a robust, scalable, and personalized simulation-driven pipeline, thereby accelerating research and development for a broader range of users.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Exo-plore stems from the inherent difficulties and ethical concerns associated with current state-of-the-art exoskeleton optimization techniques, particularly Human-in-the-Loop Optimization (HILO). The authors highlight a "paradox": individuals who would benefit most from exoskeletons (e.g., those with mobility impairments) are often physically unable to endure the hours-long, strenuous experiments required for HILO. This severely limits the personalization and accessibility of assistive devices for their target users.

Given this challenge, the key objectives of the Exo-plore framework are:

1. **Generate Realistic Gait Data Capturing Human Adaptation:** To create a simulation environment capable of producing human-like gait data that accurately reflects how humans adapt their movement patterns and muscle coordination in response to external assistive forces from an exoskeleton. This involves matching empirically observed trends in metabolic energy expenditure and human-exoskeleton interaction.
2. **Ensure Reliable Optimization Despite Stochasticity:** To develop an optimization pipeline that can consistently identify optimal exoskeleton control parameters, even in the presence of inherent stochasticity from deep reinforcement learning and complex simulations.
3. **Generalize to Pathological Gaits:** To extend the framework's applicability beyond able-bodied individuals to various pathological gait conditions, allowing for the optimization of assistance tailored to specific impairments.
4. **Reproduce Experimental Trends:** To validate the framework by demonstrating that its simulations can reproduce established unassisted and assisted gait trends consistent with human experiments, including metabolic reduction rates and speed-dependent optimal parameters.
5. **Identify Optimal Assistance for Pathologies:** To discover optimal exoskeleton control parameters for different pathological gaits and analyze the relationships between pathology severity and the most effective assistance strategies.

Ultimately, the overarching goal is to provide a viable, simulation-based alternative to demanding human experiments, thereby making exoskeleton development more efficient, accessible, and personalized for a wider population.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Exo-plore is a sophisticated simulation framework that combines neuromechanical simulation with Deep Reinforcement Learning (Deep RL) and a stochasticity-aware surrogate optimizer. Its core functionality is divided into two main components: the **Gait Data Generator** and the **Exoskeleton Optimizer**.

#### 3.1. Gait Data Generator

This component is responsible for producing realistic gait trajectories under hip exoskeleton assistance that align with human experimental trends. It operates on a musculoskeletal character with 164 muscles and a hip exoskeleton model.

- **Human Controller Architecture:** The simulated human controller comprises three modules:
 - **Pose Network (PoseNet):** A Deep RL agent that computes target joint positions. It's trained using a reward function that encourages following a target gait while incorporating metabolic energy minimization and human-exoskeleton interaction.
 - **PD Controller:** A Proportional-Derivative controller that generates joint torques to reduce the error between current and target joint positions. It compensates for the exoskeleton's assistive torque.
 - **Muscle Coordination Network (MCN):** Trained in a supervised manner, the MCN maps the desired joint torques (after PD control and exoskeleton compensation) to muscle activations. An **Intramuscular Regularizer (IMR)** is introduced to enforce coherent activations among line muscles belonging to the same anatomical group, enhancing physiological plausibility.
- **Deep RL Formulation (PoseNet):**
 - **State:** A comprehensive state representation includes skeleton kinematics (positions, orientations, velocities), gait features (target foot position, speed, phase), muscle-related terms (passive forces, torque bounds, muscle parameters, weakness/contracture for pathological gaits), and a history of exoskeleton assistive torques.
 - **Action:** Actions consist of residual pose displacement and phase modulation, allowing for flexible adaptation of gait.
 - **Reward Function:** A composite reward guides the Deep RL agent:
 - `r_gait`: Encourages adherence to target step length, walking speed, and head/body stability.
 - `r_arm`: Imitation reward for natural arm movements.
 - `r_energy`: Regularizes metabolic energy expenditure (`MEE`), computed using a modified Hill-type muscle model with tunable parameters (α, β) to represent effort-like and fatigue-like behaviors.
 - `r_HEI` (Human-Exoskeleton Interaction): A novel reward based on a **resistance-minimization hypothesis**. It penalizes instances where the exoskeleton applies resistive power, reflecting humans' tendency to avoid mechanical power loss and actively adapt to assistance.
- **Sim-to-Real Matching:** This is a crucial aspect for ensuring the simulation's validity:
 - **Metabolic Energy Expenditure (MEE) Calibration:** The `r_energy` parameters (α, β) are calibrated using an iterative algorithm (Algorithm 1 & 2) to ensure the simulated character's preferred walking speed (PWS) and walking speed-CoT curve match human experimental data.
 - **Human-Exoskeleton Interaction (HEI) Reward:** The `r_HEI` is designed to reproduce observed human adaptive behaviors, such as actively modifying kinematics to better utilize assistive forces or avoid resistive forces. An ablation study validates its effectiveness in replicating trends in assistive/resistive power and metabolic reduction rates.

#### 3.2. Exoskeleton Optimizer

This component identifies optimal exoskeleton control parameters (gain κ, time delay ∆t) that minimize the simulated character's metabolic cost of transport (CoT).

- **Surrogate Model:** Instead of sample-efficient but computationally heavy Gaussian Processes (GP) used in HILO, Exo-plore utilizes a **neural network surrogate model (Multilayer Perceptron - MLP)**. This choice is driven by the data-rich nature of simulation, where NNs scale efficiently.
- **Data Sampling:** To train the surrogate network, gait data (control parameters and corresponding CoT values) is generated using the Gait Data Generator. **Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS)** is employed for parameter space exploration, which stratifies each parameter range to ensure uniform coverage and mitigate aliasing artifacts.
- **Surrogate Network Training:** The surrogate network is trained to minimize a loss function combining:
 - **Huber loss:** Reduces the impact of outliers arising from simulation instability and stochasticity.
 - **Gradient penalty:** Encourages smoothness of the CoT landscape by constraining the norm of the output gradient, ensuring stable gradient-based optimization.
 - **L1 and L2 regularization:** Standard techniques to prevent overfitting.
- **Gradient-Based Optimization:** Once trained, the smooth and differentiable CoT landscape provided by the surrogate network enables efficient gradient-based optimization (e.g., SLSQP, trust-region algorithms) to find the optimal (κ, ∆t) for a given gait. Additional regularization terms are added to the optimization objective to discourage extreme parameter magnitudes and promote smooth variations across different walking speeds.
- **Pathological Gait Generalization:** Separate Exo-plore models are trained for five different pathological gait conditions (equinus, waddling, crouch, calcaneus, foot drop) by altering muscle strength and contracture parameters. Simulation parameters are randomized during training to enhance generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents a comprehensive validation of Exo-plore's capabilities across both unassisted and assisted gait scenarios, and its application to optimizing assistance for individuals with pathological gaits.

#### 4.1. Gait Generation – Unassisted

- **Realistic Kinematics and Dynamics:** The gait data generator successfully produced joint kinematics (ankle, knee, hip angles) that qualitatively matched human experimental data (Boo et al., 2025), with the exception of hip internal rotation. Similarly, simulated muscle activations showed patterns consistent with human electromyography (EMG) signals, even without explicit imitation targets. Ground reaction forces (GRF) also exhibited characteristic human-like profiles.
- **Metabolic Energy Expenditure (MEE) and Preferred Walking Speed (PWS) Alignment:** The calibration process for MEE parameters (α, β) proved crucial. The `(α, β) = (1.5, 1.0)` setting yielded a walking speed-CoT curve and PWS that best matched human experimental results (Browning et al., 2006). The predicted step length and frequency trends with walking speed also reasonably aligned with human data (Schwartz et al., 2008).

#### 4.2. Gait Generation – Assisted

- **Replication of Human Adaptation to Exoskeleton Assistance:** Under exoskeleton assistance, the simulated hip flexion angle, hip flexion velocity, assistive moment, and assistive power trends across different walking speeds closely matched human experimental results (Lim et al., 2019b).
- **Critical Role of Human-Exoskeleton Interaction (HEI) Reward:** An ablation study demonstrated the superior performance of the proposed `r_HEI` (resistance minimization) compared to alternative rewards (assistance maximization or no HEI reward). `r_HEI` reproduced the observed human trends for RMS assistive moment, mean assistive power, and mean resistive power with the highest Pearson correlation to empirical data. It also achieved metabolic reduction rates (MRR) that were closest to human experimental results, indicating more effective simulated human adaptation.

#### 4.3. Optimizing Exoskeleton Control Parameters

- **Able-bodied Individuals:** The surrogate neural network successfully learned smooth CoT landscapes from noisy simulation data, enabling stable gradient-based optimization. The optimized time delay (∆t) monotonically decreased as walking speed increased, while optimal gain (κ) showed varied trends, indicating a speed-dependent optimal assistance strategy.
- **Disabled Individuals (Pathological Gaits):** Exo-plore generalized to five pathological gait conditions:
 - **Strong Linear Correlations:** For most pathologies (equinus, waddling, crouch, calcaneus), the optimal exoskeleton gain (κ) exhibited strong linear relationships with pathology severity.
 - **Pathology-Specific Trends:**
 - **Group A (Benefit from Hip Exoskeleton):** Crouch and calcaneus gaits showed *increasing* optimal gains with severity. These pathologies involve stable but metabolically expensive compensation, where assistance effectively reduces energy expenditure. The `r_HEI` reward was crucial for correctly identifying this trend in calcaneus gait.
 - **Group B (Limited Benefit from Hip Exoskeleton):** Equinus and waddling gaits showed *decreasing* optimal gains with severity. These involve inherently unstable compensation strategies where external assistance might disrupt delicate balance, necessitating minimal intervention.
 - **Foot Drop Anomaly:** Foot drop exhibited an irregular trend with no clear correlation to severity. This was attributed to high gait variability caused by frequent toe-ground collisions in severe cases, which hindered stable optimization convergence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Exo-plore represents a significant step forward in the field of exoskeleton control and neuromechanical simulation, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Accelerated Exoskeleton Development:** By providing a robust and human-aligned simulation framework, Exo-plore can drastically reduce the time, cost, and human resources traditionally required for exoskeleton controller optimization. This allows for rapid iteration and testing of new control strategies, speeding up the development cycle of assistive devices.
2. **Personalized Assistance for Mobility-Impaired Individuals:** The ability to generalize to and optimize for pathological gaits directly addresses the "HILO paradox." This opens the door for designing highly personalized exoskeleton assistance strategies for individuals who currently cannot participate in experimental optimization, such as stroke survivors, individuals with cerebral palsy, or those with other neuromuscular conditions. This could lead to more effective and comfortable assistive devices that truly enhance the mobility and quality of life for diverse patient populations.
3. **Enhanced Understanding of Human-Exoskeleton Interaction:** The framework's success in accurately replicating human adaptive behaviors (especially with the `r_HEI` reward) provides a powerful tool for researchers to better understand the complex biomechanical and neural adaptations that occur when humans interact with external robotic assistance. This can inform the design of more intuitive and effective control algorithms.
4. **Foundation for Predictive Biomechanics:** Exo-plore contributes to the broader goal of predictive neuromechanical simulation, demonstrating how Deep RL can be effectively coupled with musculoskeletal models to generate realistic movements and predict human responses to novel interventions. This could extend beyond exoskeletons to other areas like surgical planning or rehabilitation protocols.
5. **Reduced Ethical and Practical Barriers:** Shifting optimization from human trials to simulation reduces ethical considerations related to participant burden and safety, making research more efficient and inclusive.

Despite its promising results, the authors acknowledge the critical need for experimental validation with human subjects, including patient populations, to fully establish the real-world validity and clinical effectiveness of the simulation-based predictions and optimized controllers. However, Exo-plore provides a powerful computational testbed that can pre-screen and refine control strategies, significantly de-risking and focusing subsequent human trials.
