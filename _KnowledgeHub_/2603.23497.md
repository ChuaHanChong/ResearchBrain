# WildWorld: A Large-Scale Dataset for Dynamic World Modeling with Actions and Explicit State toward Generative ARPG

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alaya Studio, Beijing Institute of Technology, and Tsinghua University introduced WildWorld, a large-scale dataset of over 108 million frames from the AAA game *Monster Hunter: Wilds*, offering explicit state and action annotations to enhance the learning of dynamic world models. The accompanying WildBench benchmark, featuring novel Action Following and State Alignment metrics, effectively evaluates models' ability to adhere to actions and maintain state consistency, with state-aware models demonstrating improved interaction fidelity and long-horizon prediction.

## Problem

- Existing datasets for world modeling often contain simple actions with limited semantic meaning, restricting the complexity of learnable interactions.
- Action-observation relationships in current datasets are frequently entangled, directly linking actions to pixel changes without explicit mediation by underlying world states.
- A lack of explicit annotations for internal world states hinders models from disentangling true state transitions from superficial visual variations, leading to inconsistencies in long-horizon predictions.

## Method

- Curation of **WildWorld**, a dataset of over 108 million frames from *Monster Hunter: Wilds*, featuring per-frame ground-truth annotations for diverse actions, ==explicit internal world states== (e.g., health, skeletal poses), and high-fidelity observations (RGB, depth, camera poses).
- An ==automated game recording pipeline== leverages in-game AI and a custom multi-source system to collect large-scale, long-duration trajectories, ensuring diversity and precise synchronization of structured data and visual observations.
- Development of ==WildBench==, a benchmark with novel evaluation metrics, ==Action Following== and ==State Alignment==, designed to quantitatively assess generated videos for their adherence to input actions and the consistency of their underlying state evolution.

## Results

- The WildWorld dataset comprises 108 million frames with 119 annotation columns per frame, covering 29 unique monster species and over 450 distinct character actions, enabling comprehensive long-horizon studies.
- WildBench's Action Following metric achieved an **85%** agreement rate with human judgments, and the State Alignment metric demonstrated **43.23%** coordinate accuracy against ground-truth skeletons, validating their reliability.
- State-aware models like StateCtrl and SkelCtrl, trained on WildWorld, significantly improved interaction fidelity: StateCtrl reduced Camera Control Absolute Trajectory Error to 0.94, and SkelCtrl achieved **92.81%** for Action Following and **22.03%** for State Alignment, outperforming traditional baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit state annotations in dynamic datasets are crucial for enabling models to disentangle underlying world mechanisms from visual observations, leading to more robust and interpretable world models.
> - Interactive world models achieve superior performance in action following and state consistency when trained on datasets offering explicit action and state information, as demonstrated by the improved metrics on WildBench.
> - Evaluation of interactive world models necessitates specialized metrics beyond general video quality, such as Action Following and State Alignment, which effectively assess fidelity to input actions and consistency of underlying state evolution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence: enabling systems to understand and predict how dynamic environments evolve based on observations and actions. This aligns with core concepts from dynamical systems theory and reinforcement learning, which conceptualize the world as a latent-state process where actions drive state transitions and visual observations provide partial information about these states.

Recent advancements in video generation and world models have focused on learning action-conditioned dynamics from large-scale video datasets. However, the authors identify critical limitations in existing datasets that hinder the effective learning of structured and consistent world dynamics:
1. **Limited Action Semantics:** Current datasets often feature simple actions with restricted semantic meaning (e.g., basic movements), rather than complex, meaningful interactions.
2. **Entangled Action-Observation Relationships:** Actions are frequently directly linked to pixel-level changes in observations, without explicit mediation by underlying states. For example, a "move left" action directly translates to a visual shift, but the action "shoot" might affect an unobservable "ammunition count" state before manifesting visually.
3. **Lack of Explicit State Information:** The absence of explicit annotations for underlying world states makes it difficult for models to disentangle true state transitions from superficial visual variations. This entanglement impedes the learning of stable world dynamics and leads to inconsistencies, particularly in long-horizon predictions where minor errors can accumulate.

WildWorld directly addresses these shortcomings by providing a dataset that explicitly links diverse, semantically rich actions to underlying state changes, offering a more comprehensive foundation for training and evaluating interactive world models that aim to infer and manage latent states for robust long-term predictions. The work positions itself as a crucial step towards developing generative models that can reason about and interact with complex environments in a state-aware manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale, Action-Conditioned World Modeling Dataset with Explicit State Annotations:** The central objective is to create "WildWorld," a dataset that overcomes the limitations of existing datasets. This involves gathering data from a complex, photorealistic environment—specifically, the AAA action role-playing game *Monster Hunter: Wilds*—to ensure a rich and diverse set of interactions. The dataset aims to provide explicit, per-frame ground-truth annotations for actions, character skeletons, internal world states (e.g., health, location, animation IDs), camera poses, and depth maps.

2. **Facilitate the Learning of Structured World Dynamics:** The motivation behind providing explicit state annotations is to enable models to learn more structured and interpretable dynamics of the world. By having access to the true underlying states, models can be trained to disentangle implicit state transitions (e.g., changes in health or ammunition) from explicit visual variations, which is challenging when only visual observations are available. This disentanglement is intended to lead to more robust and consistent world models.

3. **Improve Long-Horizon Prediction and Consistency:** Current world models often struggle with maintaining consistency over extended prediction horizons due to the accumulation of errors. By explicitly modeling state transitions, the researchers aim to provide a foundation for models that can maintain coherent world evolution over long action sequences, thereby enhancing their capability for planning, reasoning, and generating consistent interactive experiences.

4. **Curate a Benchmark for Evaluating Interactive World Models:** Beyond the dataset itself, a key objective is to establish "WildBench." This benchmark is designed to provide quantitative metrics that go beyond simple visual plausibility, specifically focusing on "Action Following" (how well generated videos adhere to input actions) and "State Alignment" (how accurately the underlying state of the generated world matches the ground truth). This is motivated by the recognized need for more nuanced evaluation tools for dynamic, interactive generation.

5. **Provide Insights for Future Research in State-Aware Video Generation:** Through extensive experiments using the new dataset and benchmark, the authors aim to identify current challenges and limitations of existing interactive world models. These insights are intended to guide future development towards more effective state transition modeling and long-horizon consistency in generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the WildWorld dataset and the WildBench benchmark. The methodology involves a multi-stage process for data acquisition, processing, and annotation, followed by the design of evaluation metrics and experimental baselines.

#### 3.1. WildWorld Dataset Curation

The WildWorld dataset is automatically collected from the photorealistic AAA action role-playing game *Monster Hunter: Wilds* and comprises over 108 million frames. Its curation pipeline consists of four main parts:

1. **Data Acquisition Platform:**
 - A bespoke toolchain was developed to record per-frame ground-truth data from the game engine.
 - Data is categorized into actions, states, and observations.
 - **Actions:** Player control inputs and character actions (movement, attacks, skill casting). Over 450 distinct actions.
 - **States:** Absolute location, rotation, and velocities of player characters and monsters; current animation IDs; gameplay attributes like health and stamina; and detailed skeletal poses for both characters and monsters.
 - **Observations:** High-fidelity RGB frames, depth maps, and intrinsic/extrinsic camera parameters are recorded by instrumenting the game’s rendering pipeline. The HUD is disabled to produce clean frames reflecting only the game world.

2. **Automated Game Record Pipeline:**
 - **Automated Game Play System:** To enable large-scale and long-duration data collection with minimal human intervention, an automated gameplay system was implemented. This system programmatically navigates in-game menus to randomly sample quests and NPC combinations, ensuring scenario diversity. For combat, the game’s built-in rule-based companion AI is leveraged to control the player’s party autonomously. The in-game camera’s native target-lock system dynamically adjusts to keep engaged monsters in view, maintaining visual stability. The authors argue that the combinatorial action space and stochastic monster behavior, combined with multi-AI interactions, generate sufficiently diverse trajectories despite rule-based AI.
 - **Recording System:** A robust system was developed for simultaneous multi-source recording. Structured data (actions, states) are recorded at each engine tick and serialized as JSON files. For image frames (RGB, depth), a custom system based on OBS Studio and Reshade is used. A Reshade shader partitions the display into sub-windows (e.g., RGB and depth at 720p from a 2K display). A modified OBS Studio records these sub-windows as separate streams with different encoding settings: RGB with lossy HEVC compression (16-20 Mbps) and depth losslessly (around 20 Mbps) to preserve geometric precision. Timestamp information is embedded in all recordings to facilitate cross-source synchronization.

3. **Data Processing and Annotation Pipeline:**
 - **Timestamp Alignment:** Ensures accurate synchronization across all recorded data streams.
 - **Sample Filtering:** A multi-dimensional filtering process is applied to remove low-quality or uninformative samples:
 - *Duration Filtering:* Discards samples shorter than 81 frames.
 - *Temporal Continuity Filtering:* Removes samples with inter-frame gaps exceeding 1.5 times the target frame interval (approximately 50 ms at 30 FPS), indicating stuttering or transitions to non-combat content.
 - *Luminance Filtering:* Filters out samples with over 15 consecutive frames of extreme brightness (either very high or very low).
 - *Camera Occlusion Filtering:* Discards samples where the camera-character distance is abnormally small (indicating foreground occlusion) or which contain abrupt player position changes (e.g., fast travel).
 - *Character Occlusion Filtering:* In the first frame, 3D skeletal keypoints are projected to screen coordinates, and samples with character overlap exceeding 30% of either character's projected area are removed.
 - **Hierarchical Caption Annotations:** Fine-grained textual descriptions are generated. Each sample is segmented into action sequences where the action ID remains constant. For each sequence, RGB frames are sampled at 1 FPS, resized to 480p, and captioned using Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct. The prompts for this model include corresponding action and state ground truth to enhance accuracy for game-specific scenarios. Sample-level captions are then generated by summarizing all action sequence captions using Gemini 3 Flash.

#### 3.2. WildBench Benchmark Design

WildBench is designed to evaluate interactive world models beyond visual plausibility, focusing on action fidelity and state consistency. It comprises four evaluation perspectives:

1. **Video Quality:** Assessed using VBench metrics: Motion Smoothness (MS), Dynamic Degree (DD), Aesthetic Quality (AQ), and Image Quality (IQ).
2. **Camera Control:** Measures the discrepancy between ground-truth camera trajectories and those estimated from generated videos using a structure-from-motion model (ViPE). Metrics include Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) for translation and rotation, with scalar alignment applied to estimated translations.
3. **Action Following:** Evaluates how well the generated video reflects input actions. Each sample is segmented by frame-wise action IDs. Gemini 3 Flash judges consistency between generated and ground-truth clips for each segment, grouped into categories (movement, fast displacement, attack). Scores are averaged across segments.
4. **State Alignment:** Uses character and monster skeletal poses as a proxy for world state. 2D trajectories are extracted from ground-truth skeletons. For generated videos (initialized with the ground truth first frame), keypoints are tracked using TAPNext. State Alignment is defined as the mean coordinate accuracy between predicted and ground-truth trajectories, computed as the average fraction of frames where predicted keypoint locations fall within thresholds (4, 8, 16, 32 pixels) of the ground truth.

**Data Curation for WildBench:** A representative set of 200 samples is manually curated for evaluation, covering diverse difficulty levels, combat scenarios, character/monster types, and in-game events. This set includes 100 samples of player-NPC cooperation against monsters and 100 samples of one-on-one player-monster combat.

#### 3.3. Experimental Baselines

Several interactive world modeling approaches were trained on WildWorld and evaluated on WildBench:
- **CamCtrl:** A camera-conditioned video generation model, fine-tuned from Wan2.2-Fun-5B-Control-Camera using WildWorld’s ground-truth camera trajectories.
- **SkelCtrl:** A skeleton-conditioned video generation model, fine-tuned from Wan2.2-Fun-5B-Control. It uses rendered colored-skeleton videos derived from WildWorld’s 3D skeleton keypoints as a control signal.
- **StateCtrl:** A state-aware model built upon CamCtrl, which injects structured state information into the video generation process. It models discrete states (e.g., monster type) and continuous states (e.g., coordinates, health) hierarchically (entity-level and global-level) using trainable embeddings and MLPs. A Transformer models entity relationships, producing a unified state embedding. This embedding is aligned with video frames and injected into intermediate layers of a DiT model. It incorporates a state decoder and predictor, trained with respective losses, to ensure state preservation and temporal consistency.
- **StateCtrl-AR:** An autoregressive version of StateCtrl, where only the first-frame state is provided as ground truth, and subsequent states are predicted by the model’s state predictor during inference.
All models were trained at 544x960 resolution with 81 frames per sample at 16 FPS.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed statistics for the WildWorld dataset, evaluates the reliability of the WildBench metrics, and compares the performance of various interactive video generation approaches.

#### 4.1. WildWorld Dataset Statistics

- **Scale:** The dataset contains 108 million frames with 119 annotation columns per frame.
- **Entity Diversity:** It covers 29 unique monster species, 4 player characters, and 4 weapon types. Player and weapon types are nearly uniformly distributed, while monster species follow a long-tailed distribution.
- **Scene Complexity:** Gameplay occurs across 5 distinct open-world stages with varied environments, weather, and time-of-day conditions. Approximately 66% of clips are combat-focused, and 34% involve traversal.
- **Temporal and Spatial Dynamics:** Most clips range from 4,000 to 28,000 frames, with some exceeding 40,000 frames (over 30 minutes), enabling long-horizon studies. The median camera-to-character distance is 15.69 units, and character-to-monster distance is 12.63 units, ensuring prominent subjects.
- **Action Richness:** Character states are encoded as (weapon type, bank ID, motion ID) triplets, yielding 5,960 unique triplets across 24 banks and 455 motion IDs. These actions cover a full range of in-game interactions. Monsters exhibit 2,132 unique action pairs. The top-150 character action IDs show a long-tail distribution, indicating rich behavioral variety.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of WildBench Metrics

- **Action Following:** The model-based Action Following metric achieved an 85% agreement rate with human judgments on WildBench, demonstrating its reliability in reflecting human perception of action consistency.
- **State Alignment:** The State Alignment metric achieved 43.23% coordinate accuracy against the ground-truth skeleton, confirming its effectiveness in quantitatively measuring the alignment between generated and ground-truth state evolution.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Interactive Video Generation Models on WildBench

The experiments compared a baseline model (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) against CamCtrl, SkelCtrl, StateCtrl, and StateCtrl-AR.
- **Interaction-Related Metrics Improvement:** All proposed interactive models (CamCtrl, SkelCtrl, StateCtrl, StateCtrl-AR) demonstrated improvements over the baseline on metrics related to interaction fidelity.
 - **Camera Control:** CamCtrl significantly reduced Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) from 4.63 to 2.02 and Relative Pose Error (RPE) from 0.18 to 0.13. StateCtrl further improved ATE to 0.94 and RPE to 0.07.
 - **Action Following and State Alignment:** SkelCtrl showed substantial improvements, with Action Following increasing from 53.77% to 92.81% and State Alignment from 11.29% to 22.03%. StateCtrl also showed improvements, reaching 85.66% for Action Following and 16.06% for State Alignment. These results indicate the usefulness of the WildWorld dataset for training diverse interactive world models.
- **VBench Saturation:** VBench metrics for Video Quality (Motion Smoothness and Dynamic Degree) were consistently high (above 95%) across all methods. However, the models showed significant differences in Action Following and State Alignment. This suggests that traditional video quality metrics may saturate for highly dynamic content, underscoring the necessity of WildBench's fine-grained, interaction-focused metrics.
- **Trade-off in Control Signal Type:** SkelCtrl, which uses visual skeleton videos as direct control signals, achieved larger gains in interaction-related metrics compared to StateCtrl, which learns soft embeddings from state information. However, SkelCtrl exhibited lower Aesthetic Quality (AQ) and Image Quality (IQ) scores. This indicates a potential trade-off where direct visual control signals can improve fidelity to interaction but may compromise general image quality, while state-conditioned models might produce aesthetically clearer images even if they sometimes miss specific visual effects.
- **Autoregressive Model Performance:** StateCtrl-AR, which only uses the first-frame state and autoregressively predicts subsequent states, performed comparably to StateCtrl but showed a noticeable drop in Action Following. This performance degradation is attributed to error accumulation during iterative next-state prediction, a known challenge in autoregressive generation. The authors suggest this paradigm holds promise for integration with autoregressive video generation.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Evaluation

Qualitative comparisons illustrated these findings:
- CamCtrl produced camera motion consistent with ground truth but struggled with accurate monster dynamics.
- StateCtrl generated clearer foreground subjects, sometimes rendering scenes with higher perceptual image quality than the ground truth (e.g., less splashing sand).
- SkelCtrl, conversely, sometimes better reproduced specific environmental effects like splashing sand and gravel, even if this resulted in a lower overall image quality score.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WildWorld dataset and WildBench benchmark represent a significant contribution to the field of generative AI and world modeling, with several potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the State-Action-Observation Gap:** WildWorld directly addresses a critical gap in existing datasets by explicitly providing rich action-conditioned dynamics with synchronized explicit state annotations. This enables researchers to move beyond pixel-level manipulations and train models that learn the underlying mechanisms of world evolution.

2. **Advancing State-Aware World Models:** By offering ground truth for internal world states, the dataset facilitates the development of models capable of inferring and managing latent states. This can lead to more robust, consistent, and interpretable world models that can disentangle the true effects of actions from their visual manifestations, particularly for implicit state changes that are not directly observable.

3. **Improving Long-Horizon Prediction and Planning:** The explicit state information provided in WildWorld is crucial for mitigating the issue of error accumulation in long-horizon video generation and prediction. Models trained on this dataset are expected to achieve greater temporal consistency, enabling more reliable planning and reasoning in complex, dynamic environments.

4. **Establishing a Comprehensive Evaluation Standard:** WildBench introduces novel and essential metrics—Action Following and State Alignment—that go beyond traditional perceptual quality measures. This benchmark provides a standardized and more meaningful way to evaluate the interactive capabilities and state consistency of world models, guiding the development towards truly intelligent and interactive agents.

5. **Accelerating Research in Generative AI and AI-Native Games:** WildWorld and WildBench serve as foundational resources for the research community. They can accelerate advancements in action-conditioned video generation, interactive environment modeling, and ultimately contribute to the creation of more sophisticated AI for applications such as AI-native games, robotics, and interactive simulations where understanding and predicting complex world dynamics are paramount. The findings also highlight ongoing challenges, such as maintaining long-horizon state consistency and the trade-offs between visual fidelity and control signal types, thus guiding future research directions.
