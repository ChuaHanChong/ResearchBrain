# A Stable Whitening Optimizer for Efficient Neural Network Training

> [!summary] Summary
> From UC Berkeley, SPlus introduces a stable whitening optimizer that improves the efficiency of training large Transformer models by addressing common issues in second-order methods. It enables faster loss decrease, matching Adam's validation performance in 44-58% fewer gradient steps and 62-83% less wall-clock time.

## Problem

- Existing whitening-based optimizers like Shampoo are prone to divergence, particularly when computationally expensive matrix inversions are cached.
- Optimal learning rates for these optimizers often lack transferability across different neural network widths, complicating hyperparameter tuning.
- Whitening-based optimizers can suffer from significant parameter noise and oscillations at high learning rates, which can degrade final validation performance.

## Method

- ==Instant-sign normalization== is introduced to bound update magnitudes, preventing divergence and allowing eigendecomposition results to be cached for over 100 steps.
- A ==symmetric shape-aware scaling factor (2 / (m + n))== is applied per-layer to standardize update magnitudes, making learning rates robust and transferable across network widths.
- ==Iterate averaging (Exponential Moving Average)== is incorporated to reduce parameter noise at high learning rates, allowing fast learning without compromising final validation performance.

## Results

- SPlus consistently achieved faster loss decrease across language modeling, image classification, and diffusion modeling tasks, reaching Adam's performance in **44-**58%** fewer gradient steps** and **62-**83%** less wall-clock time**.
- Instant-sign normalization eliminated divergence, allowing SPlus to remain stable with eigendecomposition cached for over **100 gradient steps**, significantly improving wall-clock efficiency.
- The symmetric shape-aware scaling enabled learning rate transfer across different network widths, and iterate averaging consistently improved final validation loss by mitigating parameter noise at higher learning rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Divergence in whitening optimizers can be effectively addressed by bounding update magnitudes through an instant-sign normalization on rotated gradients.
> - Achieving learning rate transferability across network widths is possible with a symmetric shape-aware scaling, which simplifies hyperparameter tuning for adaptive optimizers.
> - Iterate averaging provides an effective annealing mechanism, enabling aggressive learning rates for rapid training while preserving or enhancing final model performance by smoothing parameter updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The efficient training of large-scale neural networks is a cornerstone of modern deep learning, and optimization algorithms are its backbone. Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and its variants, particularly **Adam**, have been the dominant practical choices due to their simplicity and effectiveness. However, as models scale to billions of parameters and datasets grow commensurately, the iterative nature of these first-order methods becomes a significant bottleneck, demanding more efficient gradient updates.

This paper addresses the long-standing challenge of leveraging **second-order optimization methods** in deep learning. These methods, which adapt updates based on the curvature of the loss landscape (e.g., using the Hessian or Fisher Information Matrix), theoretically promise faster convergence by making more informed steps. The core idea is to perform "whitening," which normalizes gradients to have uniform magnitude along each axis of descent, effectively decoupling updates from raw gradient magnitude and allowing faster progress in certain directions while preventing divergence in others.

Historically, practical deployment of full second-order methods has been hampered by computational intractability (calculating and inverting the Hessian for massive networks), numerical instability, and increased hyperparameter tuning complexity. This has led to the rise of various approximations:
- **Diagonal approximations:** Adam and its variants (e.g., Adafactor) use element-wise scaling, approximating the second-order information as diagonal. This is computationally cheap but ignores critical cross-parameter dependencies.
- **Kronecker-factorized approximations:** Methods like **K-FAC** and **Shampoo** approximate the metric tensor (e.g., empirical Fisher or whitening metric) using Kronecker products, significantly reducing computational and memory costs compared to full matrix inversion. Shampoo, in particular, focuses on using the square root of the uncentered gradient covariance (whitening metric) and block-wise, Kronecker-factorized approximations for dense layers.

SPlus directly builds upon the **Shampoo family of algorithms**, positioning itself as an advanced evolution in the whitening-based optimization landscape. While Shampoo offers a more principled approach than diagonal methods like Adam, it has faced its own set of practical challenges, primarily related to stability and the difficulty of hyperparameter tuning, which have limited its widespread adoption despite its theoretical promise. This work aims to bridge this gap, making the power of whitening-based optimizers accessible and robust for real-world, large-scale Transformer training. By addressing Shampoo's specific shortcomings—divergence, lack of learning rate transfer, and parameter noise—SPlus seeks to establish a new state-of-the-art for efficient neural network training, potentially displacing Adam in high-performance settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing scale of modern deep learning models, particularly large neural networks like Transformers. Training these models is inherently time-consuming, making it crucial to maximize the learning progress achieved with each gradient update. While second-order optimization methods offer a pathway to faster learning by adapting updates to the loss landscape's curvature, their practical application has been limited by significant challenges.

The paper identifies three key issues with existing whitening-based optimizers, specifically the Shampoo family, that impede their widespread adoption and performance:

1. **Addressing Instability and Divergence:** Naive Shampoo is prone to divergence, especially when its computationally expensive matrix inversions are cached for extended periods to save wall-clock time. This instability restricts its effective usage, forcing frequent recomputations that diminish its efficiency gains. The objective is to introduce a mechanism that guarantees stability even with infrequent inversions, thereby improving both reliability and computational efficiency.
2. **Enabling Learning Rate Transferability:** A fundamental challenge in hyperparameter tuning is that optimal learning rates often change with network architecture details, such as width. Shampoo, like many optimizers, lacks a natural parametrization that allows the optimal learning rate to transfer across network widths. The goal is to adapt a shape-aware scaling strategy that standardizes update magnitudes, making learning rates more robust and transferable, thus simplifying the tuning process.
3. **Mitigating Parameter Noise at High Learning Rates:** Whitening-based optimizers, by design, tend to apply uniformly-sized updates, which can lead to significant parameter noise and oscillations when high learning rates are used. Unlike first-order methods where update magnitudes naturally decay, this behavior can degrade performance despite fostering faster initial learning. The objective is to decouple fast learning progress from noise, allowing the use of higher learning rates without compromising the final validation performance.

In aggregate, the overarching objective is to develop **SPlus**, an efficient, stable, and easy-to-use whitening optimizer that can significantly reduce both the gradient steps and wall-clock time required to train large Transformer models, ultimately outperforming the widely used Adam optimizer across a broad range of tasks and training stages, without introducing complex new hyperparameters or additional computational passes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SPlus is presented as an efficient optimizer that fundamentally builds upon the Shampoo family of algorithms, which approximate a whitening metric using Kronecker-factorized matrices. The core methodology involves identifying and systematically alleviating three critical shortcomings of naive Shampoo through nuanced improvements. The efficacy of these improvements is then rigorously validated through a comprehensive empirical evaluation suite focused on Transformer models.

#### 3.1. Addressing Divergence: Instant-Sign Normalization

The first major issue identified is Shampoo's propensity to diverge, especially when its matrix inverses are cached for many steps. This instability is hypothesized to arise from stale cached eigenbases interacting with incoming gradients, leading to overly large updates when gradients align with "tail-end" (small historical magnitude) basis directions.

SPlus addresses this with **instant-sign normalization**. Instead of using the full inverse square-root of the historical covariance matrices `Λ_L^(-1/4)` and `Λ_R^(-1/4)` to scale the gradients, SPlus retains the historical eigenbases (`Q_L`, `Q_R`) but replaces the magnitude-dependent scaling with an instantaneous `sign` function on the rotated gradients (Equation 11):
`U = Q_L sign(Q_L^T G Q_R) Q_R^T`.
This modification has a crucial advantage: the update magnitude is strictly bounded (Frobenius norm `||U||_fro <= sqrt(nm)`), inherently preventing divergence. This stability allows for significantly less frequent recomputation of the eigendecomposition (caching for over 100 steps compared to Shampoo's typical 10-25 steps), drastically improving wall-clock efficiency. The instantaneous normalization also provides a more fine-grained, element-wise normalization compared to the Kronecker-approximated inverse factors of Shampoo, without requiring additional parameters in memory like SOAP.

#### 3.2. Standardizing Learning Rate: Symmetric Shape-Aware Scaling

The second challenge is the lack of learning rate transferability across different network widths, which complicates hyperparameter tuning. SPlus adopts a **symmetric shape-aware scaling** factor for its updates.
Building on principles from previous work on SGD and Adam, the goal is to make the expected magnitude of change in intermediate activations invariant to network width. For a dense layer of shape `m x n`, the raw instant-sign update has a Frobenius norm of `sqrt(nm)`. To achieve the desired invariance, SPlus introduces a per-layer scaling factor of `2 / (m + n)` (Equation 15):
`U = Q_L sign(Q_L^T G Q_R) Q_R^T * 2 / (m + n)`.
This symmetric scaling contrasts with "spectral scaling" (1/m) used in some prior works, which the authors empirically found to be detrimental in Transformer MLP blocks. The symmetric approach ensures that learning rates transfer effectively across network widths, leading to more robust default settings and easier tuning.

#### 3.3. Reducing Parameter Noise: Iterate Averaging

Whitening-based optimizers, by taking uniformly-sized normalized updates, can suffer from significant parameter noise at high learning rates, leading to oscillations and suboptimal validation performance. While learning rate decay is a common solution, it can slow down learning progress.

SPlus addresses this by incorporating a **simple iterate averaging scheme (Exponential Moving Average, EMA)**. It maintains two sets of parameters: `θ'` (live parameters) which are updated rapidly with a high learning rate, and `θ` (slow parameters) which are an EMA of `θ'` (Equation 16):
`θ' ← θ' + αU`
`θ ← (1 − β)θ + βθ'`
This technique allows `θ'` to make fast progress while `θ` provides a smoothed, less noisy representation of the model, effectively "annealing" the parameters without explicit learning rate decay. This unblocks the use of higher learning rates, leading to faster learning without degrading the final performance, a strategy commonly used in domains like image generation.

#### 3.4. Empirical Evaluation Approach

To validate SPlus, the authors designed a **wide-scope Transformer training benchmark**:
- **Architecture:** A standard Transformer model based on GPT-2, excluding bias terms and applying LayerNorm pre-attention/pre-MLP.
- **Objectives/Datasets:**
 - Autoregressive Language Model (LLM) on OpenWebText.
 - Latent Diffusion Model (DiT) on Imagenet (for image generation).
 - Image Classification Network (ViT) on Imagenet.
- **Training Stages:** Performance was assessed by starting from three distinct checkpoints (initialization, 10k steps, 50k steps) and training for an additional 10k gradient steps, ensuring robustness across different phases of training.
- **Metrics:** "Steps-to-Adam" and "Wallclock-to-Adam" (fraction of steps/time required to match Adam's validation performance).
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of optimizers including Naive SGD, Adam, Schedule-Free Adam, Sophia, Shampoo, SOAP, PSGD, and Muon.
- **Implementation:** Experiments run on 32 TPUv3 pods using bfloat16, with parameters distributed via fully-sharded data parallel. SPlus updates are applied only to 2D dense layers, with specific handling for non-standard parameters and distributed eigendecomposition for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SPlus against a broad suite of optimizers on a challenging Transformer benchmark reveals significant performance improvements and validates the proposed methodological enhancements.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency Gains

- SPlus consistently achieved the fastest loss decrease across all evaluated objectives (language modeling, image classification, diffusion modeling) and training stages.
- On average, SPlus was able to reach the validation performance of Adam within **44-58% of the gradient steps**. This indicates substantial efficiency in terms of training iterations.
- Crucially, SPlus also demonstrated superior wall-clock efficiency, matching Adam's performance within **62-83% of the wallclock time**. This demonstrates that the per-step computational overhead of SPlus is effectively amortized by the fewer steps required.
- SPlus does not introduce any new critical hyperparameters to tune, nor does it require additional forward/backward passes, making it a "plug-and-play" solution.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Stability through Instant-Sign Normalization

- A key finding was the inherent instability of naive Shampoo, which frequently diverged when matrix-inversion results were cached for more than 25 gradient steps. This significantly limited its practical speed.
- The **instant-sign normalization** in SPlus entirely eliminated this divergence. SPlus remained stable even when eigendecomposition results were cached for over 100 gradient steps, and up to 500 steps in certain conditions. This dramatic improvement in stability directly enabled SPlus to achieve faster wall-clock times by reducing the frequency of computationally expensive inversions.

#### 4.3. Robust Learning Rate Transfer with Symmetric Shape-Aware Scaling

- Initial experiments showed that neither Adam nor Shampoo (or SPlus without this scaling) naturally exhibited learning rate transferability across different network widths. Optimal learning rates varied significantly with width.
- The proposed **symmetric shape-aware scaling** factor of `2 / (m + n)` successfully enabled learning rate transfer for SPlus. This means that an optimal learning rate found for one network width could be effectively applied to models of different widths, simplifying hyperparameter tuning and leading to more robust default settings. The authors empirically demonstrated that this symmetric scaling outperformed a "spectral scaling" (1/m) approach in all tested cases.

#### 4.4. Improved Performance with Iterate Averaging

- Whitening-based optimizers, due to their normalized updates, can suffer from high parameter noise at larger learning rates, leading to performance degradation despite rapid learning progress.
- The simple **iterate averaging (EMA)** scheme successfully mitigated this issue. By maintaining an exponential moving average of the live parameters, SPlus could utilize higher learning rates to accelerate initial learning without the final validation loss being negatively impacted by parameter oscillations. Averaged parameters consistently achieved lower validation losses across all objectives, revealing a stronger optimal learning rate that would otherwise be obscured by noise.

#### 4.5. Comparison with Other Optimizers

- SPlus consistently outperformed other advanced optimizers such as Sophia, Muon, and the original Shampoo (which often diverged).
- It proved to be significantly more robust and efficient than SOAP and PSGD, which are also Kronecker-factorized or second-order-approximating methods.
- While Schedule-Free Adam showed competitive wall-clock performance in some scenarios (likely due to its low per-step computational cost), SPlus generally achieved superior steps-to-Adam, indicating more efficient learning per gradient step.
- The overall evaluation confirms Adam as a strong baseline, but SPlus reliably surpassed it in terms of both gradient steps and wall-clock time required to reach equivalent performance.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Implementation Details

- Ablation studies confirmed that iterate averaging consistently improved performance regardless of the underlying gradient transformation, suggesting its broad utility for reducing noise at constant learning rates.
- The strategy of applying SPlus mainly to 2D dense layers (the Transformer backbone) and handling non-standard layers with simplified updates, combined with distributed eigendecomposition, demonstrated the practical and scalable implementation of the method on TPU hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of SPlus represents a significant step forward in the field of neural network optimization, offering both immediate practical benefits and opening new avenues for research.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Bridging the Gap for Second-Order Methods:** SPlus successfully addresses the long-standing challenges that have hindered the widespread adoption of powerful second-order, whitening-based optimizers. By solving critical issues like numerical instability and hyperparameter sensitivity, it makes these theoretically superior methods practically viable for large-scale deep learning.
- **New State-of-the-Art for Transformers:** The comprehensive evaluation on diverse Transformer tasks demonstrates SPlus's ability to consistently outperform Adam and other leading optimizers. Given the Transformer's dominance across various modalities (language, vision, diffusion), SPlus immediately becomes a strong candidate for a new default optimizer in these critical applications.
- **Increased Efficiency and Resource Savings:** The ability to reach Adam's performance in significantly fewer gradient steps (44-58%) and less wall-clock time (62-83%) translates directly into substantial computational cost savings and faster research iteration cycles. This is particularly crucial as model sizes continue to grow.
- **Simplified User Experience:** By enabling learning rate transfer across network widths and requiring no new critical hyperparameters, SPlus offers a more robust and easier-to-tune alternative to existing adaptive optimizers. The open-source code and clear recommendations further lower the barrier to adoption.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Acceleration of Large Model Development:** Faster training directly facilitates the development and deployment of larger, more complex neural networks across academia and industry. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas currently bottlenecked by training time, such as even larger language models, more capable diffusion models, and advanced robotics policies.
- **Democratization of Advanced Optimization:** By making sophisticated whitening techniques more stable and user-friendly, SPlus could allow a broader range of researchers and practitioners to leverage their benefits without deep expertise in optimization theory or extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The empirically grounded approach and the identified "sweet spots" (e.g., instant-sign normalization, iterate averaging, symmetric scaling) provide clear directions for further research in scalable optimizers. Future work could explore alternate factorizations, low-rank approximations, or hybrid conditioning strategies to further reduce computational costs or apply SPlus to non-Transformer architectures. The proposed rigorous evaluation setup itself serves as a valuable benchmark for comparing new optimization strategies.

#### 5.3. Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors)

- **Increased Memory Footprint:** SPlus requires storing more parameters per dense layer compared to Adam (3nm + 2(m^2 + n^2) vs. 3nm), which can be a concern for memory-constrained environments, especially with extremely large models or batch sizes.
- **Eigendecomposition Overhead:** While amortized, the matrix eigendecomposition step still consumes non-trivial wall-clock time, although the stability improvements allow for infrequent recomputation, mitigating this.
- **Scope of Evaluation:** The evaluation was focused exclusively on Transformer architectures. While Transformers are ubiquitous, the performance on other neural network architectures (e.g., CNNs, RNNs, MLPs in general settings) remains an open question.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** The study primarily used a constant learning rate, common in diffusion models but less standard in language modeling, which might influence performance comparison with optimizers highly tuned for decay schedules.

Despite these limitations, SPlus represents a compelling step towards making advanced, theoretically powerful optimization techniques practical and effective for the most demanding deep learning applications, promising to accelerate the pace of AI research and development.
