# EvoPlan: Evolutionary Neuro-Symbolic Robot Planning with Spatio-Temporal Guarantees

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoPlan from Texas A&M University presents a neuro-symbolic framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with formal methods to enable robust robot planning and execution. The system learns implicit spatio-temporal safety constraints from unannotated one-class demonstration data and uses an LLM-driven evolutionary planner with runtime monitoring, achieving high success rates in complex tasks while significantly reducing safety infractions and collisions in dynamic environments.

## Problem

- Purely generative LLM-based robot planning lacks formal guarantees for plan executability and safety in real-world deployments.
- Classical symbolic (PDDL) planners are rigid, requiring fully formalized problems and struggling to leverage LLMs' contextual understanding or adapt to dynamic environments and implicit operational norms.
- Learning implicit safety norms from one-class robot demonstration data (without explicit negative examples of unsafe actions) is challenging.

## Method

- An offline procedure mines a global ==Signal Temporal Logic (STL)== mobility constraint (Φ_mob) from unannotated, one-class demonstration data by synthesizing ==counterfactual negative examples==.
- An **evolutionary PDDL planner** uses an LLM to propose and repair plans based on formal feedback from a programmatic ==validator cascade==, handling open-world scenarios and vocabulary misalignments.
- A constrained execution loop monitors each planned action's waypoint sequence against the learned Φ_mob at runtime; violations trigger immediate replanning.

## Results

- Mined STL safety shields reduced red-light infractions by **77%** and collisions by **71%** in driving scenarios and removed **82-93%** of avoidable contacts in social navigation tasks, maintaining high success rates.
- The evolutionary PDDL planner achieved a **98.5%** success rate on the ALFWorld Text benchmark, outperforming baselines and demonstrating robustness to vocabulary misalignment and varying plan depths.
- End-to-end execution in a Gazebo factory scene resulted in all planned routes completed with **four out of five** runs collision-free, showing controlled degradation and minimal intimate-zone occupancy (**<2%** of ticks) even with high pedestrian densities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit spatio-temporal norms and codified rules can be effectively learned as STL constraints from one-class demonstration data by generating synthetic negative examples.
> - Combining LLM-driven evolutionary search with a formal validation cascade can produce robust and symbolically valid PDDL plans, even in open-world and semantically misaligned contexts.
> - Enforcing learned STL constraints at runtime through continuous monitoring and adaptive replanning significantly enhances robot safety and reliability during execution in dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "EvoPlan" positions itself at the intersection of large language model (LLM) based planning, classical symbolic planning, and robot safety. Recent advancements have demonstrated that LLMs and vision-language models (VLMs) can translate natural language instructions and visual cues into plausible robot action sequences. However, a significant limitation of these purely generative, neural approaches is their inability to inherently guarantee that the generated plans are executable or safe in real-world robotic systems. Such systems may produce plans that are syntactically correct but semantically flawed or unsafe in dynamic environments.

Conversely, classical Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) planners offer machine-checkable guarantees regarding plan executability and goal reachability. This is achieved through formal problem specification. However, these symbolic planners are rigid; they require a fully formalized problem statement and do not intrinsically leverage the contextual understanding, common-sense reasoning, or repair capabilities of LLMs. They also typically struggle with implicit operational norms or preferences that are not explicitly codified.

This research aims to bridge this gap by integrating the strengths of both paradigms. It proposes a neuro-symbolic framework where LLMs are employed as flexible proposal and repair mechanisms for plans, while symbolic validation and data-derived temporal logic constraints provide formal verification and safety guarantees. This approach builds upon related work in several areas:

- **LLM/VLM planning with formal representations**: Prior work such as VLMFP, NL-PDDL, and PIP-LLM have explored combining foundation models with formal planners. These systems often focus on generating PDDL domains or problems from sensory input or natural language. EvoPlan extends this by introducing learned signal-level execution contracts in the form of Signal Temporal Logic (STL) constraints, which are enforced during continuous execution, rather than solely relying on symbolic plan generation.
- **Safety-aware symbolic planning and verification**: Research like SafeGen-LLM has focused on training LLMs for safety-aware PDDL3 planning, using rewards derived from formal verification. EvoPlan distinguishes itself by learning STL contracts directly from real robot trajectories, enforcing these contracts during continuous execution, and ensuring that symbolic effects are only asserted after the measured trace certifies the actual occurrence of the skill.
- **Temporal logic learning, evolutionary search, and robot safety**: The paper draws inspiration from methods that learn temporal logic predicates from data (e.g., Soroka et al., 2025) and LLM-guided evolutionary search for improving symbolic artifacts (AlphaEvolve by Novikov et al., 2025). EvoPlan applies these concepts to both PDDL plan generation and the learning of STL-based safety contracts. It also aligns with the broader push for grounded and semantically safe robot systems, as highlighted by work on heterogeneous teams and robot constitutions.

By developing a framework that learns implicit spatio-temporal norms from unannotated one-class demonstration data and integrates this learning with an LLM-driven, formally-verified planning and execution loop, EvoPlan contributes to the development of more robust, flexible, and certifiably safe autonomous robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the EvoPlan framework is to develop a robust robot planning and execution system that can interpret natural language tasks, operate in partially known and dynamic environments, and adhere to implicit norms, while providing spatio-temporal guarantees for safety and executability. This is achieved by combining the generative and adaptive capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with the formal verification provided by symbolic logic and planning.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robot planning paradigms:

1. **Lack of Guarantees in Pure LLM-based Planning**: While LLMs excel at understanding natural language and generating plausible action sequences, their outputs are inherently probabilistic and lack formal guarantees regarding plan executability or safety in complex, real-world robotic deployments. A plan generated by an LLM might be syntactically coherent but could lead to unsafe or physically impossible actions when executed by a robot.
2. **Rigidity and Underutilization of Context in Classical PDDL Planners**: Classical PDDL planners offer strong formal guarantees, but only after the problem is fully and formally specified. They are less adaptable to dynamic changes, ambiguous instructions, or the nuances of human interaction. Furthermore, they do not effectively leverage LLMs' capacity for contextual understanding, common-sense reasoning, or iterative plan repair, which are crucial for operating in open-world environments.
3. **Implicit Norms and Underspecification**: Real-world robot operation requires adherence to implicit norms (e.g., social distancing, stopping at red lights, maintaining safe clearances) that are rarely explicitly stated in a natural language task prompt or fully encoded in a PDDL domain. Existing methods often rely on manually codified rules, which can be incomplete or difficult to scale.
4. **Challenge of One-Class Data for Safety Learning**: Learning these implicit norms from real-world demonstrations is complicated by the nature of available data. Robot operation logs typically consist only of "acceptable" behaviors, meaning the data is "one-class" and lacks explicit examples of unsafe or undesirable actions. This absence of negative examples makes it challenging to train conventional supervised learning models for safety constraint extraction.
5. **Deployment Constraints**: For practical on-robot deployment, reliance on cloud-based LLM services can introduce latency, connectivity issues, and privacy concerns. Thus, a system capable of operating with locally-hosted, open-weight LLMs is desired.

To address these motivations, EvoPlan establishes three key objectives:

1. **Offline Learning of Global Spatio-Temporal Constraints**: Develop a procedure to mine a single, global Signal Temporal Logic (STL) mobility constraint (Φ_mob) from unannotated, one-class demonstration data. This constraint should capture both codified rules (e.g., traffic laws) and population preferences (e.g., social navigation behaviors). The process must synthesize negative examples to facilitate learning.
2. **Evolutionary PDDL Plan Synthesis**: Design an evolutionary PDDL planner where an LLM proposes and repairs plans based on formal feedback from a programmatic validator cascade. This aims to generate plans that are syntactically correct, symbolically valid, and goal-reachable, while being robust to linguistic misalignments.
3. **Constrained Execution Loop with Runtime Monitoring**: Implement a constrained execution loop that integrates the learned STL constraint and the evolutionary planner. During execution, each planned action's resulting waypoint sequence must be checked against Φ_mob. In case of a violation, the system should reject the action, commit the verified prefix, update its state, and trigger a replanning process.

These objectives collectively aim to create a robot planning system that is both intelligent and verifiable, capable of learning and enforcing safety guarantees in complex, human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EvoPlan framework integrates neuro-symbolic components across offline learning and online operation, structured into three main parts: evolutionary Signal Temporal Logic (STL) constraint mining, evolutionary PDDL plan synthesis, and a constrained execution loop. All LLM interactions within the framework utilize a locally-hosted, open-weight model (Qwen3-32B in the experiments), designed for on-robot deployment without cloud dependency.

#### 3.1. Evolutionary STL Constraint Mining (Offline Procedure)

The first component is an offline procedure designed to extract a single global mobility constraint, Φ_mob, expressed as an STL formula. This constraint defines acceptable spatio-temporal behavior for the robot, applying uniformly to any mobility action within a plan.

- **Constraint Types**: The same procedure can recover different types of constraints depending on the input demonstration data:
 - **Codified Rules**: For data encoding strict rules (e.g., driving logs from nuPlan), the formula identifies exact thresholds and conditional clauses (e.g., "stop at red lights").
 - **Population Preferences**: For data reflecting implicit social norms (e.g., teleoperation data from SCAND), the procedure learns tighter bounds that align with consistent operator behaviors (e.g., social distancing).
- **Addressing One-Class Data**: Demonstration data is inherently "one-class" (all recorded traces are acceptable). To enable supervised learning of the constraint, counterfactual negative examples are generated:
 - Ten procedural perturbation modes (e.g., `speeding`, `red_light_run`, `crowding_front`) are applied to push robot signals out of typical operating ranges.
 - An LLM-based violation generator synthesizes realistic violations, providing a contrastive set D− (negative examples) to complement the positive set D+ (demonstrations).
- **Evolutionary STL Constraint Fitting**: STL formulas are learned by an LLM-driven evolutionary search, inspired by AlphaEvolve.
 - **Grammar**: The grammar includes atomic predicates over sensor signals (e.g., `d_obs >= c`, `d_human >= c`, `v <= c`, `ttc >= c`) combined with Boolean operators and bounded temporal operators (`always []`, `eventually <>`).
 - **Fitness Function**: Candidate formulas are scored by maximizing robustness on D+ (expert satisfaction) and minimizing satisfaction on D− (rejecting unsafe traces), with penalties for complexity.
- **Downstream Enforcement**: Φ_mob can be enforced in two ways: via a trajectory planner within the constrained execution loop, or as a black-box shield that vetoes discrete-action policies by predicting minimum robustness and using Model Predictive Control (MPC) substitution under a policy-prior weight α.

#### 3.2. Evolutionary PDDL Plan Synthesis

This component addresses the generation of symbolically valid PDDL plans using an LLM within a formally evaluated loop.

- **Evolutionary Search Loop**: PDDL plan search is cast as machine-gradable evolution.
 - **Candidates**: Grounded action sequences (`π`) are evolved against a fixed PDDL problem (`P`) and domain (`D`).
 - **Mutation and Feedback**: In each generation, the LLM proposes edits to the current best candidate, conditioned on descriptive feedback from a programmatic validator cascade. Edits can include inserting missing actions, repairing object bindings, or resampling suffixes.
 - **Validation Cascade**: A four-stage cascade scores each candidate, short-circuiting on failure:
 1. PDDL syntax validity check.
 2. VAL (Howey et al., 2004) simulation for executability.
 3. Goal reachability verification.
 4. (For continuous execution) Predicted robustness against the global mobility constraint Φ_mob.
 - Invalid plans are demoted by the cascade's objective feedback, not by subjective LLM criticism.
- **Verified-Prefix Preservation**: The validated prefix of the plan typically grows monotonically over iterations, ensuring progress.
- **Open-World and Misaligned Deployments**:
 - **Open-world**: If the validator indicates an "object-not-declared" error (due to partial belief states), the system triggers exploration steps, updates the belief state (`B`), rebuilds the PDDL problem, and resumes planning.
 - **Vocabulary Misalignment**: If the goal vocabulary does not match the action-model vocabulary (e.g., "heated" vs. "toast"), a single LLM call generates a predicate-symbol map (`μ`) to rewrite goal predicates in `P`, which is cached and reused for consistency with belief growth.

#### 3.3. Constrained Execution Loop

The final component integrates the learned STL constraint and the evolutionary PDDL planner into a closed-loop execution system.

- **Plan Execution**: The evolutionary PDDL planner generates a plan `π = (a_1, ..., a_n)` for a natural-language task.
- **Action Grounding and Checking**: Each action `a_k` in the plan is:
 1. Dispatched to a navigation layer (e.g., a trajectory planner using Nav2) to produce a waypoint sequence `w_k`.
 2. Checked against the mined global STL constraint Φ_mob *before* execution.
- **Runtime Monitoring and Replanning**:
 - **Satisfaction**: If `w_k` satisfies Φ_mob, `a_k` is scheduled for execution.
 - **Violation**: If `w_k` violates Φ_mob, the action `a_k` is rejected. The verified prefix (`a_1...a_{k-1}`) is committed, the robot's state is refreshed from the resulting world, and the planner is re-invoked with the updated initial state and original goal to generate a new plan suffix.
- **Implementation**: For the Gazebo demonstrations, the navigation layer uses a simulated Jackal robot with Nav2, local distance fields, human tracking, and terrain labels. An online monitor continuously recomputes robustness from the measured trace and emits failure facts (e.g., `route-blocked`, `no-safe-approach`) that inform subsequent replanning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental evaluations across three main areas, demonstrating the effectiveness of EvoPlan's components.

#### 4.1. Mined STL Constraints Transfer to Downstream Policies

The evaluation of the STL constraint mining procedure (Sec. 4.1) focused on its ability to generate effective safety shields, tested across two distinct domains:

- **Rule Showcase (nuPlan → Bench2Drive)**:
 - **Context**: Driving logs from nuPlan were used to mine rules governing driving behavior, such as "stop-on-red-light," clearance floors, and speed caps.
 - **Policy & Benchmark**: A Qwen2.5-VL driving policy was shielded on the Bench2Drive benchmark. The shield operated on discrete decisions which were expanded to continuous actions by a P-controller.
 - **Results**: The shield significantly reduced red-light infractions by 77% and collisions by 71% compared to the unshielded policy. It also improved the overall Driving Score. The constraint transferred zero-shot, without benchmark-specific training.
- **Preference Showcase (SCAND → HA-VLN-CE)**:
 - **Context**: SCAND teleoperation data was used to mine implicit social-navigation preferences.
 - **Policies & Benchmark**: Two discrete-action policies, a pretrained PointNav and a behavior-cloned MLP, were shielded on HA-VLN-CE.
 - **Results**: The shield removed 82% of avoidable contacts for the PointNav policy and 93% for the MLP. Analysis of per-episode safety distributions indicated that the shield primarily collapsed the "unsafe tails" (i.e., extreme unsafe situations), rather than merely shifting average safety metrics. Success rates (SR) were largely maintained, while Collision Termination (CT) rates were significantly reduced (e.g., PointNav CT dropped from 38.1% to 18.5%).

These results demonstrate that the single mining procedure and shield mechanism are adaptable and effective for both codified rules and learned preferences, and can improve safety across different types of policies and environments.

#### 4.2. Evolutionary PDDL Planner

The evolutionary PDDL planner (Sec. 4.2) was evaluated on its ability to generate plans in both open-world and closed-world settings, including scenarios with vocabulary misalignment.

- **Open-World Evaluation (ALFWorld Text)**:
 - **Benchmark**: The planner was tested on the open-world ALFWorld Text benchmark (N=134 unseen validation tasks).
 - **Results**: EvoPlan (using Qwen3-32B) achieved a 98.5% success rate, outperforming several strong baselines including NL-PDDL (94%) and various direct/reflective LLM/VLM-based planners.
 - **Robustness to Misalignment**: The planner maintained a 98.5% success rate even when the goal vocabulary did not match the action-model vocabulary, demonstrating effective handling of semantic mismatches through its predicate-symbol mapping mechanism.
 - **Depth Capabilities**: EvoPlan successfully solved tasks whose optimal plans exceeded the fixed depth limitations of some baseline planners like NL-PDDL.
- **Closed-World Evaluation (Blocksworld and ALFWorld)**:
 - **Blocksworld Variants**: On the NL-PDDL Blocksworld suite, EvoPlan achieved high success rates: 99.7% on Plain, 74.0% on Mystery (renamed predicates/actions), 78.7% on Randomized (gibberish), and 100% on Misalignment (where Fast Downward achieved 0%). This indicates robustness to lexical variations in the domain description.
 - **Closed-World ALFWorld**: On the closed-world ALFWorld domain (N=134 valid_unseen tasks), EvoPlan achieved 90.3% success.
- **Ablation Study**: An ablation study on Blocksworld Plain showed that the evolutionary loop, with its feedback-conditioned mutations, improved the success rate by 13 percentage points (from 86.7% to 99.7%) compared to a single-shot LLM+VAL approach using the same Qwen3-32B backbone, at approximately 1.5 times the token cost. This confirmed the value of iterative refinement.

The findings indicate that EvoPlan's evolutionary approach enhances LLM-based planning by providing formal verification and iterative refinement, leading to high success rates and robustness in challenging open-world and misaligned scenarios.

#### 4.3. Constrained Execution Loop End-to-End

The full EvoPlan pipeline was demonstrated in a Gazebo simulator, focusing on pick-and-drop missions in a factory scene with varying pedestrian densities.

- **Setup**: A simulated Jackal robot executed plans from the evolutionary PDDL planner. Each PDDL action was grounded into a route by Nav2 and checked against the SCAND-mined Φ_mob before execution. Violations triggered replanning.
- **Quantitative Results (5 missions, 9-15 pedestrians)**:
 - All planned routes were completed.
 - Four out of five runs were collision-free. One collision occurred in a dense pedestrian cluster, leading to a replan budget exhaustion and a minimum clearance of 0.05m.
 - Personal-zone exposure (<1.2m to nearest pedestrian) remained below 13% of ticks, while social-zone exposure (<3.6m) was higher (40-68%).
 - The system demonstrated graceful degradation; for example, one mission maintained a high minimum clearance (0.90m) by routing around dense clusters, while another accumulated more personal-space intrusion (P_int) by navigating sustained dense aisles, indicating adaptive behavior.
- **Crowd-Density Ablation**: A sweep of pedestrian densities (0 to 50 pedestrians) revealed that at low densities, the robot maintained high clearances and minimal personal-zone exposure. As density increased, minimum clearance decreased, and personal-space intrusion (P_int) grew, but intimate-zone occupancy (<0.45m) remained very low (<2% of ticks), and collisions appeared only at higher densities. This demonstrates that the system prioritizes safe operation and degrades in a controlled manner under increasing environmental challenges.

These results illustrate the practical utility of the integrated framework, showing that learned spatio-temporal constraints can effectively guide safe robot navigation in dynamic, human-populated environments through continuous monitoring and replanning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EvoPlan framework presents a comprehensive approach to robot planning that integrates neural and symbolic methods, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics:

1. **Enhanced Safety and Reliability**: By introducing data-driven, formally verifiable spatio-temporal (STL) constraints and enforcing them at runtime, EvoPlan directly addresses a critical challenge in robotics: ensuring the safe and reliable operation of autonomous systems, particularly in human-shared environments. The observed reductions in collisions and infractions, along with controlled degradation under stress, highlight this impact.
2. **Learning Implicit Norms from Unannotated Data**: The ability to mine global mobility constraints from one-class, unannotated demonstration data is a notable advancement. This methodology bypasses the need for costly manual annotation of unsafe behaviors or explicit rule codification, making it more scalable and practical for real-world deployment where such explicit negative examples are scarce. It enables robots to implicitly learn human preferences and codified rules.
3. **Robustness and Flexibility for Open-World Planning**: The evolutionary PDDL planner's performance in open-world settings (ALFWorld Text) and its robustness to vocabulary misalignment significantly broaden the applicability of symbolic planning. By iteratively refining plans based on formal feedback and handling belief state growth and semantic mismatches, the system can operate more effectively in partially known and linguistically ambiguous environments, which are characteristic of real-world scenarios.
4. **Practical Neuro-Symbolic Integration**: EvoPlan demonstrates a functional integration of LLMs with classical symbolic reasoning (PDDL) and formal verification (STL). This paradigm utilizes LLMs for their generative and adaptive strengths (plan proposal, repair, violation generation) while leveraging symbolic methods for their guarantees and verifiability (PDDL validation, STL monitoring). This hybrid approach provides a pathway for developing intelligent agents that are both flexible and certifiably correct.
5. **Feasibility for On-Robot Deployment**: The framework's design choice to use locally-hosted, open-weight LLMs (Qwen3-32B) is critical for practical deployment. This eliminates dependencies on cloud services, reducing latency, enhancing privacy, and improving system robustness in environments with limited connectivity, thus enabling real-time operation directly on robotic platforms.
6. **Generalizability of Safety Constraints**: The concept of a single, global STL mobility constraint (Φ_mob) that applies universally to all mobility actions in any plan simplifies safety engineering. This modularity means the constraint can be transferred across different tasks and policies without requiring retraining, which fosters more efficient and reusable safety mechanisms.
7. **Foundation for Future Research**: EvoPlan provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including extending the framework to more complex multi-robot tasks, developing stronger statistical guarantees for learned constraints, and exploring richer, per-skill constraint families beyond global mobility. This work contributes to the ongoing evolution of verifiable AI and safe robot autonomy.

In conclusion, EvoPlan advances the field by offering a principled method to combine the intelligence of LLMs with the rigor of formal methods, paving the way for more capable, trustworthy, and safely deployable autonomous robots.
