# Rethinking Sim2Real: Lower Fidelity Simulation Leads to Higher Sim2Real Transfer in Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Research from Georgia Institute of Technology and Meta AI challenges the conventional view in sim2real, demonstrating that training high-level visual navigation policies for legged robots in lower fidelity kinematic simulations leads to superior real-world transfer. This approach achieved a 100% success rate on a real Boston Dynamics Spot robot, with improved path efficiency and reduced collisions, by enabling substantially larger-scale training compared to high-fidelity dynamic simulations.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption in sim2real is that increasing simulator physics fidelity directly reduces the sim2real gap.
- High-fidelity dynamic simulations are computationally expensive, limiting the volume of training data and wall-clock time available for deep reinforcement learning.
- Policies trained in high-fidelity simulations risk overfitting to specific, imperfect simulated dynamics or struggling with proprietary black-box low-level controllers on real robots.

## Method

- A ==hierarchical control architecture== is proposed, where a ==high-level visual navigation policy== is trained in simulation and a manufacturer-provided ==low-level controller== handles hardware execution.
- A ==lower fidelity kinematic simulation== is developed that abstracts away complex low-level physics by ==teleporting== the robot to desired poses, significantly increasing simulation speed.
- The performance of policies trained using this kinematic simulation is systematically compared against those trained in traditional ==high-fidelity dynamic simulations== for visual navigation tasks.

## Results

- Kinematic-trained policies achieved a **100%** success rate in zero-shot transfer on a real Boston Dynamics Spot robot for point-goal navigation, exhibiting higher path efficiency (SPL of **82-83%**) and significantly fewer collisions.
- Dynamic-trained policies demonstrated reduced real-world success rates (**40-67%**), lower path efficiency, and substantially more collisions due to the sim2real dynamics gap and overfitting.
- Kinematic simulation enabled approximately **10 times** more training steps (**500 million** vs. **50 million**) within equivalent wall-clock time budgets, directly contributing to superior policy performance and generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prioritizing simulation speed over detailed physics fidelity can yield better sim2real transfer for high-level tasks like visual navigation, primarily by enabling greater training scale.
> - Policies trained in simplified kinematic simulations learn more robust and generalizable high-level navigation strategies, preventing overfitting to specific low-level dynamic inaccuracies.
> - Incorporating low-dimensional real-world actuation noise models into fast kinematic simulations further enhances the real-world robustness of policies without sacrificing training speed.
