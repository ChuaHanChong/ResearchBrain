# ZeroDex: Zero-Shot Long-Horizon Dexterous Manipulation via Multi-View 3D-Grounded VLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> ZeroDex presents a zero-shot framework for long-horizon dexterous manipulation, employing multi-view 3D-grounded Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning to execute high-level language instructions in real-world settings. The system achieved substantially higher success rates on various manipulation and tool-use tasks compared to fine-tuned VLA baselines, alongside reducing 3D localization errors for key task points.

## Problem

- Dexterous manipulation tasks demand highly precise 3D grounding, where even small errors in contact points or trajectories can lead to critical failures.
- Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models require extensive data collection and task-specific fine-tuning, hindering their scalability and generalization to novel scenarios.
- Many zero-shot methods rely on 2D or sparse intermediate representations from foundation models, which are often insufficient for capturing the precise 3D geometric information vital for dexterous tasks.

## Method

- **ZeroDex** implements a modular pipeline that decouples VLM semantic reasoning for high-level planning from physical execution via motion primitives and controllers.
- A ==multi-view fusion strategy== lifts 2D VLM predictions into robust 3D quantities (e.g., grasp points, interaction targets) using a combination of RANSAC-style triangulation and a reference-view ray voting scheme.
- The framework incorporates a 'Bag of Atomic Actions' library for tool-use, an affordance-guided dexterous grasp and motion generator with collision-aware refinement, and a closed-loop verification/replanning mechanism for long-horizon tasks.

## Results

- ZeroDex demonstrated 10/10 successes on 'Throw Away Trash' and 8/10 on 'Broom Clean' over 10 trials each, significantly outperforming fine-tuned VLA baselines (GR00T, Being-HO) which achieved 0/5 successes on these tasks.
- The multi-view fusion approach reduced object-centric grasp point localization error (L_grasp) to approximately **4.58**-**4.77** cm, a notable improvement over the **16.43** cm error of a single-view RGB-D baseline.
- The system showed capabilities in long-horizon tasks, achieving 4/6 end-to-end successes for 'Organize Objects,' and the closed-loop retry mechanism effectively enabled recovery from intermediate execution failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling semantic reasoning from physical execution allows for effective leveraging of pre-trained VLM zero-shot capabilities for diverse manipulation tasks.
> - Robustly lifting view-dependent 2D VLM predictions into consistent 3D quantities via multi-view fusion is critical for overcoming depth ambiguity, occlusion, and achieving the precision required for dexterous manipulation.
> - A library of reusable object-centric atomic action primitives, spatially aligned with 3D grounded targets, enables generalizable tool-use and simplifies task execution for complex interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robotic systems capable of performing long-horizon manipulation from high-level language instructions represents a significant goal in robotics. Such systems require not only object recognition but also the ability to ground instructions in task-relevant 3D geometry, which is particularly critical for dexterous hands where small errors can lead to failures.

Historically, the dominant approach has involved training end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. While these models have shown progress in general-purpose manipulation by predicting actions directly from large-scale robot data, they typically necessitate extensive data collection, task-specific adaptation, or environment-specific fine-tuning to achieve reliable performance across diverse manipulation tasks. This requirement limits their scalability and generalization to novel objects, tools, and spatial configurations in open-ended settings. Another avenue, human demonstration retargeting, offers a way to reduce data collection costs. However, it often encounters an embodiment gap between human and robotic hands, which can result in physically infeasible contacts or unstable grasps that require further refinement or reinforcement learning.

More recently, zero-shot manipulation leveraging pre-trained foundation models has emerged as a research direction. These methods typically extract intermediate representations from foundation models. Approaches include large language models generating high-level plans or executable control code, vision-language models (VLMs) providing keypoints, visual marks, or affordances, and generative models predicting future frames or dense point and object flows. A common limitation across many of these zero-shot methods is their reliance on image-space or sparse intermediate representations. These representations can be brittle for capturing the precise 3D geometric information inherently required for dexterous manipulation, such as contact points, placement targets, and tool trajectories.

Research into 3D grounding and dexterous execution has explored lifting 2D observations to 3D, often using multi-view stereo techniques. Recent efforts have aimed to imbue VLMs with spatial reasoning capabilities, aggregate multiple views into 3D groundings, or frame VLM reasoning as 3D constraints, frequently aided by depth or pose foundation models. For execution, prior work has focused on transferring object-centric skills, synthesizing dexterous grasps, and utilizing language or VLM feedback for verification and replanning.

ZeroDex positions itself within this landscape by advocating for a modular design. It decouples semantic reasoning, handled by a VLM, from physical execution, managed by motion primitives. This approach aims to address the limitations of end-to-end VLA models by leveraging the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of VLMs while tackling the critical challenge of robustly lifting 2D VLM predictions into consistent 3D quantities. By integrating multi-view 3D grounding with a library of reusable atomic action primitives, ZeroDex seeks to provide a unified, zero-shot system for dexterous manipulation that generalizes across diverse objects, tools, and environments, thereby bridging a gap in the current state of zero-shot robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a zero-shot framework, named ZeroDex, capable of performing long-horizon dexterous manipulation tasks solely from high-level language instructions. This framework aims to operate in real-world settings by grounding language commands into executable 3D task plans derived from calibrated multi-view RGB images.

The motivation behind ZeroDex stems from several challenges prevalent in current robotic manipulation systems:
1. **Demand for Precise 3D Grounding**: Dexterous manipulation tasks require high precision in 3D grounding. Errors, even minor ones, in specifying where to grasp an object, which part to contact, or how to orient and move a tool, can lead to critical failures such as unstable grasps, collisions, inverse kinematics failures, or incorrect functional contacts. This highlights the insufficiency of purely 2D reasoning for such tasks.
2. **Limitations of End-to-End VLA Models**: While end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown promise, their practical deployment is hindered by the extensive data collection requirements and the need for task-specific adaptation or environment-specific fine-tuning. This makes scaling them across the diversity of objects, tools, and spatial configurations in open-ended environments challenging and resource-intensive.
3. **Challenges with Human Demonstration Retargeting**: Directly retargeting human demonstrations to robotic hands often introduces an "embodiment gap," where human-centric movements translate into physically infeasible contacts or unstable grasps for a robot, necessitating further refinement.
4. **Inefficiency of Training from Scratch**: Modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) already possess considerable zero-shot semantic reasoning capabilities. They can identify what to grasp, where to grasp as a functional affordance, how to move an object, and the sequential order of task steps. The research posits that training a new end-to-end policy is less efficient than leveraging these existing VLM competencies within a modular system.
5. **Addressing 2D to 3D Gap**: VLMs primarily operate on 2D image data. However, the relevant quantities for robotic manipulation, such as grasp locations, end-effector trajectories, and object placement, are inherently three-dimensional. A single-view image rarely provides sufficient geometric information to reliably infer these 3D quantities.

To address these motivations, ZeroDex sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To decouple semantic reasoning (using a VLM) from physical execution (using motion primitives and controllers) to leverage the zero-shot capabilities of VLMs more effectively.
- To develop a multi-view fusion approach that robustly lifts view-dependent 2D VLM predictions into consistent 3D quantities, including 3D contact points, placement targets, and tool-motion anchors, even under occlusion and partial visibility. This involves combining triangulation of VLM groundings with a novel reference-view ray voting scheme.
- To establish a library of reusable object-centric atomic action primitives for tool-use tasks, allowing for their retrieval and spatial alignment to newly grounded task geometry.
- To extend the multi-view 3D grounding to support dexterous-hand execution by estimating functional 3D affordance regions, generating feasible grasp-motion pairs, and filtering them based on inverse kinematics and collision feasibility throughout the entire trajectory.
- To enable long-horizon manipulation through a closed-loop system that incorporates status verification and replanning capabilities, allowing the system to recover from execution failures by re-grounding or replanning subtasks.

By pursuing these objectives, ZeroDex aims to provide a unified, zero-shot framework that demonstrates robust generalization across a variety of manipulation and tool-use tasks involving previously unseen objects and novel environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ZeroDex operates as a modular pipeline that converts calibrated multi-view RGB images and a high-level language instruction into a physically feasible arm-hand execution plan. The methodology is structured into four main stages: Reference-Frame Grounding, Multi-View Fusion-Based 3D Lifting, Object-Centric Atomic Action Alignment for tool-use, and Affordance-Guided Dexterous Grasp and Motion Generation.

#### 3.1. Reference-Frame Grounding

Given multiple calibrated images and a language instruction, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) first selects a reference view, identifies the manipulation mode (pick-and-place or tool-use), and predicts a mode-specific grounding tuple. For pick-and-place, this tuple includes the target object and its 2D destination pixel in the reference view. For tool-use, it additionally specifies the tool object, its skill category (e.g., pouring, sweeping), and the target object. If the target object remains stationary, its destination pixel is marked as null.

Subsequently, the VLM, conditioned on a planning prompt combining the instruction, mode, and grounding tuple, generates a sequence of primitives specific to the reference view. These primitives represent atomic actions such as 'grasp,' 'apply action,' 'waypoint,' 'release,' or 'hold.' Each primitive is associated with one or more 2D keypoints and semantic descriptions. For pick-and-place, a sequence like (grasp, waypoint, release) defines the grasp point on the target object, a transport waypoint, and a placement location. For tool-use, a sequence like (grasp, apply action, release/hold) identifies a grasp point and functional tip on the tool, an interaction point on the target, and a terminal tool location.

#### 3.2. Multi-View Fusion-Based 3D Lifting

To overcome the inherent limitations of 2D VLM predictions, such as single-view depth ambiguity and multi-view occlusion, ZeroDex employs a multi-view fusion strategy to lift the 2D primitive-level keypoints into 3D. For each 2D keypoint identified in the reference view, a grounding prompt is formulated and sent to the VLM to obtain corresponding 2D groundings across all available views.

The fusion process combines two complementary methods:
1. **RANSAC-style Triangulation**: Candidate 3D points are computed by triangulating 2D keypoints from every possible pair of views. Each candidate 3D point is then scored based on how many views, when projected back, have a reprojection error below a predefined pixel threshold. The 3D candidate with the highest consensus support is selected as the triangulation estimate.
2. **Reference-View Ray Voting**: This method addresses situations where triangulation might be less robust. It involves sampling multiple depth candidates along the camera ray of the 2D keypoint in the reference view. These 3D candidates are then projected into all non-reference views, overlaid with numbered visual markers. The VLM is queried again to select which of these numbered markers best matches the semantic description of the keypoint in each non-reference view. The 3D candidate that receives the most votes across these views is chosen as the ray voting estimate.

The final 3D keypoint is dynamically selected. If the maximum score from the triangulation step meets a sufficient inlier threshold, the triangulation estimate is used. Otherwise, the system falls back to the robust reference-view ray voting estimate. All resulting 3D keypoints are then transformed into the world coordinate frame using pre-calibrated camera extrinsics.

#### 3.3. Object-Centric Atomic Action Alignment

For pick-and-place tasks, the system generates a transfer trajectory for the target object from its current pose to the lifted 3D release keypoint using an existing robot motion generation pipeline.

For tool-use tasks, which often require specific interaction patterns, ZeroDex utilizes a "Bag of Atomic Actions" (BoAA) library. This library consists of reusable, object-centric primitives, each encoding a predefined tool-use skill category (e.g., "pour," "sweep") and a corresponding 6D trajectory of the tool object relative to a target. Each atomic action also includes stored start and end anchor points in 3D. This library is constructed offline, potentially from recorded demonstrations or generated trajectories.

At runtime, the system retrieves the atomic action from the library that matches the VLM-inferred skill category. A rigid transformation is then computed to align the stored start and end anchor points of this atomic action with the lifted 3D interaction (apply action) and terminal (release/hold) keypoints in the current scene. This spatial alignment is applied to the stored 6D tool trajectory, resulting in a scene-aligned tool trajectory that guides the robot's motion.

#### 3.4. Dexterous Affordance-Guided Grasp and Motion Generation

The lifted 3D grasp keypoint provides a semantic anchor, but dexterous manipulation further requires identifying a task-conditioned contact region on the object surface. To generate this functional 3D affordance region:
1. The 3D grasp keypoint is projected into each view.
2. An affordance prompt, combining the original instruction and the projected grasp point's description, is sent to the VLM to predict a 2D graspable bounding box in each view.
3. These 2D bounding boxes are then lifted to 3D. This is achieved by projecting the vertices of the manipulated object's mesh into all views and computing a multi-view inclusion score for each vertex, indicating how many views show it within a predicted bounding box. Vertices exceeding a voting threshold define the 3D affordance region.

Dexterous grasp candidates are then generated on this 3D affordance region. For handle-like affordances, a cylindrical template sampler is used. For general geometries, an optimization-based generator, specifically an affordance-augmented version of BODex, is employed, biasing fingertip placements towards the task-conditioned affordance region while maintaining force-closure and collision objectives.

Before generating a full trajectory, collision-aware position refinement is applied to grounding points that define object placement or terminal tool poses. This involves searching a local vertical grid around the initial estimate to find a collision-free position, prioritizing vertical adjustments to maintain the intended horizontal location.

Finally, an off-the-shelf arm-hand motion generator tracks the desired 6D trajectory of the manipulated object (either a simple transfer path or the scene-aligned tool trajectory) for each grasp candidate. This process resolves kinematic and collision constraints. A feasible grasp-motion pair is then selected for physical robot execution.

For long-horizon tasks, the system incorporates a closed-loop verification and retry mechanism, described in the supplementary material. After each execution attempt, the VLM verifies task progress and dynamically updates or replans the remaining subtask queue if failures occur, allowing for recovery and continued task execution within a retry budget.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ZeroDex framework was evaluated through real-world experiments on an xArm equipped with an Inspire dexterous hand in a tabletop environment monitored by multiple calibrated RGB cameras, including a stereo pair. Foundation-Stereo was used for depth estimation and FoundationPose for 6D object pose estimation.

The evaluation compared ZeroDex against a single-view RGB-D grounding baseline and two Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, GR00T and Being-HO. The VLA models were fine-tuned with 30 task-specific teleoperation demonstrations per task, whereas ZeroDex operated entirely zero-shot, without any task-specific training. Metrics included success rate, object-centric localization distance for grasp and apply-action points, collision error, and long-horizon success rates.

#### 4.1. Real-Robot Success

- **Comparison with RGB-D Baseline**: ZeroDex demonstrated improved or matched performance against the single-view RGB-D baseline. On "Throw Away Trash," ZeroDex achieved a 5/5 success rate compared to the RGB-D baseline's 4/5. For "Place Pot on Stove," both achieved 4/5. The advantage of ZeroDex was more evident in scenarios requiring precise placement and robustness in cluttered environments. For instance, in the "Cluttered Precise Pick-and-Place" task, ZeroDex achieved 4/5 success, while the RGB-D baseline managed 2/5.
- **Comparison with VLA Baselines**: ZeroDex significantly outperformed the fine-tuned VLA baselines. On "Throw Away Trash" and "Broom Clean," both GR00T and Being-HO failed in all 5 trials (0/5). In contrast, ZeroDex, operating zero-shot, achieved 10/10 successes for "Throw Away Trash" and 8/10 for "Broom Clean" over 10 trials each.

#### 4.2. 3D Grounding Quality

- **Localization Error**: The multi-view fusion approach substantially reduced object-centric localization errors compared to the single-view RGB-D baseline. The grasp point error (`L_grasp`) was 16.43 cm for Stereo (RGB-D) but reduced to approximately 4.58-4.77 cm for ZeroDex across different view counts. Similarly, the apply-action error (`L_apply`) decreased from 2.72 cm for Stereo to about 1.35-1.94 cm for ZeroDex.
- **Effect of View Count**: Increasing the number of views from 2 to 5 yielded diminishing returns in terms of `L_grasp` and `L_apply` in the evaluated scenes, suggesting that a few wide-baseline views provided strong geometric constraints.
- **Collision Error**: Collision-aware refinement in ZeroDex resulted in the lowest mean maximum penetration depth (`ϕ_m(X_wp)`) at 9.60, marginally improving upon the Stereo baseline's 9.91. This indicates improved collision robustness in placement.
- **Qualitative Assessment**: Qualitative results illustrated that multi-view grounding produced more consistent task-relevant 3D estimates in cluttered environments, where single-view RGB-D grounding often suffered from occlusion and incomplete geometry, leading to misplaced 3D targets.

#### 4.3. Long-Horizon Manipulation

- ZeroDex was evaluated on sequential tasks, demonstrating per-step and end-to-end success rates. For "Organize Objects," the system achieved 4/6 end-to-end successes. For a "Cooking" task, it achieved 1/3 end-to-end successes.
- Failures primarily stemmed from factors such as arm joint limits, environmental collisions, or unstable grasps during execution.
- The closed-loop retry mechanism proved beneficial, allowing the system to recover from intermediate failures by re-grounding or replanning the failed subtask within a predefined retry budget. This capability improved overall reliability for complex, multi-step tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ZeroDex represents a notable advancement in zero-shot long-horizon dexterous manipulation, demonstrating a modular framework that integrates Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning with physical execution through robust multi-view 3D grounding. The significance of this work lies in its specific contributions and the potential impact on general-purpose robotics.

**Key Contributions:**
1. **Zero-Shot Long-Horizon Framework**: ZeroDex introduces a unified framework capable of performing complex, multi-step manipulation tasks from high-level language instructions without task-specific training or demonstrations. This addresses a significant limitation of data-hungry end-to-end learning approaches.
2. **Robust Multi-View 3D Grounding**: The proposed multi-view VLM grounding method, which combines cross-view triangulation with reference-view ray voting, provides more accurate and reliable 3D task-relevant quantities compared to single-view RGB-D methods. This robustness is critical for overcoming challenges like depth ambiguity, occlusion, and view-dependent semantic interpretation, particularly important for precise dexterous manipulation.
3. **Dexterous Hand Execution with Affordance Guidance**: The framework extends 3D grounding to dexterous hands by estimating functional 3D affordance regions. This enables the generation and filtering of physically feasible grasps, considering trajectory-level inverse kinematics and collision constraints, thus facilitating complex interactions like tool use.
4. **Reusable Atomic Action Primitives and Closed-Loop Control**: By representing tool-use behaviors as object-centric atomic actions and incorporating a closed-loop verification and replanning mechanism, ZeroDex can dynamically adapt to unforeseen events and recover from execution failures, enhancing reliability for long-horizon tasks.

**Potential Impact:**
The modular design of ZeroDex allows for effective leveraging of the strong semantic reasoning capabilities of large pre-trained VLMs, decoupling high-level planning from low-level control. This approach has the potential to accelerate the deployment of general-purpose robots by reducing the need for extensive, task-specific data collection and fine-tuning. The demonstrated ability to generalize to unseen objects and novel environments in a zero-shot manner suggests a pathway toward more adaptable and versatile robotic systems. Such systems could find applications in diverse domains, from household assistance to industrial automation, where tasks are often varied and environments unstructured. The enhanced 3D grounding accuracy is particularly impactful for dexterous manipulation, which traditionally struggles with minor geometric inaccuracies.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its capabilities, ZeroDex has identified several limitations. Firstly, its performance remains inherently dependent on the reasoning reliability of the underlying 2D VLM. Errors in high-level task decomposition, affordance selection, or 2D semantic grounding can propagate through the pipeline, even with robust 3D lifting and refinement. Secondly, the reliance on off-the-shelf arm-hand motion planners introduces potential points of failure, such as kinematic singularities, collision-checker timeouts, or unstable grasp executions, which can increase the overall latency from inference to execution. Thirdly, the current framework primarily focuses on object-centric manipulation and tool use, lacking explicit support for fine-grained dexterous in-hand manipulation (e.g., rotating an object within the hand, operating buttons on a held tool).

Future work is proposed to address these limitations. Integrating native 3D vision-language models could potentially enhance reasoning robustness and speed by operating directly in 3D space. Developing more reactive low-level policies could further improve execution speed and robustness, mitigating some of the current planner limitations. Additionally, expanding the framework to explicitly support and incorporate sophisticated in-hand dexterous manipulation capabilities would broaden the range of tasks ZeroDex could effectively address.
