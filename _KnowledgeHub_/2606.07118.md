# QuadVerse: An Integrated Framework Aligning Visual-Physical Reality for Quadruped Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> QuadVerse is a real-to-sim-to-real framework for quadruped learning that treats a reconstructed scene as one calibration substrate. Geometry-constrained 3D Gaussian Splatting gives batched photorealistic ego-view rendering plus collision-ready semantic meshes; those meshes anchor spatially varying friction calibration and a replay-trained residual actuator compensator. Policies transfer zero-shot to outdoor visual navigation at 84% real success versus 92% in simulation.

## Problem

- Prior sim-to-real work fixes visual or dynamics gaps in isolation, ignoring how mismatched perception, contact, and actuation compound into trajectory error.
- Existing 3DGS real-to-sim systems use reconstructed scenes only for rendering or geometry, leaving contact-property and actuator-response calibration underexplored, and lack batched rendering for parallel RL.
- Semantic or appearance-based friction priors are fragile from intra-class variance, while interaction-based identification needs dedicated probing motions, active exploration, or costly online estimation.

## Method

- Geometry-anchored reconstruction: ==GLOMAP== poses plus ==3DGS== with 2D planar regularization, unbiased depth rendering, multi-view photometric consistency and normal priors, then ==TSDF fusion== and ==Marching Cubes== for watertight collision meshes.
- ==Semantic mesh contact calibration==: learnable per-Gaussian semantic features propagated to mesh faces by kNN, LLM-supplied coarse friction priors, then grid-search posterior refinement minimizing replay trajectory discrepancy on slip-prone regions.
- ==Residual dynamics compensation==: ==PPO==-trained action-space residual with asymmetric actor-critic, learned by replaying ten minutes of real trajectories on calibrated terrain, then frozen and clipped during locomotion fine-tuning.

## Results

- Reconstruction leads baselines: **21.04** PSNR, **0.569** SSIM, and **0.932** mesh F1 against LiDAR scans, while rendering ego-views above **2000** FPS at 640x480 for RL.
- Prior-posterior friction calibration cuts mean base-position replay error from **0.70** m uniform-friction and **0.20** m LLM-prior to **0.12** m; residual compensation lowers per-joint tracking error **0.127** to **0.043** rad.
- Zero-shot outdoor navigation reaches **84%** real success versus **92%** in simulation; ablations drop to **36%** without scene reconstruction and **76%** without residual compensation, with **47%** lower locomotion trajectory error.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Calibrate contact before learning actuator residuals: training the residual on flat ground absorbs terrain error, yielding 0.064 rad versus 0.043 rad on calibrated terrain.
> - Semantic priors are enough where friction exceeds a slip threshold; expensive posterior search only pays off in slip-prone regions, so calibration effort should be targeted.
> - Gains come from environment-specific offline calibration, so the recipe assumes static rigid terrain and re-calibration per scene or robot, limiting scalability to changing environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robots, particularly legged robots like quadrupeds, relies significantly on simulation for learning complex locomotion skills. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) is a prevalent paradigm for training these skills. However, a persistent challenge in robotics is the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the discrepancies between simulated and real-world environments that hinder the direct transfer of policies learned in simulation to physical robots.

Existing research has made progress in mitigating the sim-to-real gap by focusing on individual aspects. For instance, techniques like real-to-sim reconstruction using methods such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) aim to improve visual realism for vision-based policy learning. Concurrently, approaches such as domain randomization and system identification are employed to enhance robustness against dynamics mismatches.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by identifying a limitation in these existing, often isolated, strategies. For contact-rich applications like quadruped locomotion, visual observations, terrain geometry, contact parameters, and actuator responses are highly interactive. Errors in any of these components can accumulate, leading to significant trajectory mismatches between simulation and reality. QuadVerse addresses this by proposing an integrated framework that systematically aligns visual perception, physical interaction, and actuator dynamics within a unified, reconstructed scene. This integrated approach aims to provide a more holistic solution to the sim-to-real transfer problem by considering the interconnected nature of these discrepancies, moving beyond individual component improvements to a coordinated alignment strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent and multifaceted sim-to-real gap, particularly its impact on training and deploying policies for contact-rich legged robots like quadrupeds. While deep reinforcement learning has shown promise for generating agile locomotion skills, the costs, risks, and impracticality of extensive real-world training necessitate reliance on physics-based simulators. However, the simulation environment often fails to accurately reflect the real world, leading to performance degradation or failure when policies are transferred.

The paper identifies three critical interfaces where sim-to-real discrepancies predominantly manifest and accumulate:
1. **Visual Observations:** Mismatches between simulated and real visual inputs can alter the policy's perception, leading to incorrect actions.
2. **Physical Interaction:** Inaccuracies in terrain geometry and contact parameters (e.g., friction) result in discrepancies in external forces experienced by the robot's feet.
3. **Actuator Dynamics:** Differences between the simulated actuator model and the real robot's motor responses (e.g., delays, non-linearities) can cause executed motions to deviate from commanded ones.

Existing solutions frequently address these issues in isolation. For instance, visual realism might be enhanced, or dynamics might be randomized, without explicitly considering how these separate mismatches propagate and interact throughout the robot's state evolution. This fragmented approach can lead to a residual sim-to-real gap that hinders robust policy deployment.

Therefore, the key objectives of QuadVerse are:
- **To develop an integrated framework that uses reconstructed real-world scenes as a shared substrate for calibrating and aligning visual perception, physical interaction, and actuator dynamics.** This aims to systematically reduce discrepancies across these interfaces.
- **To achieve high-fidelity visual alignment:** By reconstructing photorealistic ego-view rendering capabilities from captured RGB videos, ensuring the visual input to the robot's policy in simulation closely matches that in the real world.
- **To enable accurate physical interaction modeling:** By extracting collision-ready semantic meshes from the reconstructed scenes, allowing for spatially varying contact calibration, where friction parameters are initialized semantically and refined based on real-world robot trajectories.
- **To compensate for actuator non-idealities:** By training a residual dynamics compensator that models the differences between nominal simulated actuator responses and real-world executed torques, thus improving the accuracy of simulated robot motion during trajectory replay.
- **To demonstrate robust zero-shot visual-navigation policy deployment:** The ultimate objective is to enable policies trained entirely within the QuadVerse simulation environment to be transferred directly to a physical quadruped robot for complex navigation tasks without requiring any task-specific real-world rollouts or fine-tuning.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of piecemeal sim-to-real solutions by offering a comprehensive framework that integrates the alignment of visual, physical, and dynamic aspects within a cohesive, data-driven reconstructed environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QuadVerse is structured as an integrated framework encompassing three main stages: Reconstruction and Calibration, Dynamics Compensation, and Sim-to-Real Deployment. The framework coordinates visual perception, physical interaction, and actuator dynamics within a reconstructed environment.

#### 3.1. Geometry-Anchored Reconstruction and Batched Rendering

To create a digital twin that is both photorealistic and geometrically accurate, QuadVerse processes captured RGB video sequences:

- **Geometry-Consistent Scene Reconstruction:**
 - Camera poses and sparse geometry are initialized using GLOMAP, a global structure-from-motion technique, to prevent cumulative drift in large outdoor scenes.
 - The scene is represented using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). To address the potential for inaccurate surface geometry in vanilla 3DGS, geometric constraints are introduced during optimization:
 - **2D Planar Regularization:** Flattens Gaussian primitives into 2D surfels by minimizing the smallest scale component of the Gaussian, promoting alignment with physical surfaces.
 - **Unbiased Depth Rendering:** Adopts a method that analytically computes ray-Gaussian plane intersections to generate depth maps that conform to the true geometric surface, mitigating positional bias in volumetric rendering.
 - **Multi-View Photometric Consistency:** Uses induced homographies between reference and neighboring views to maximize Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC) of local patches, enhancing global geometric consistency.
 - **Normal Priors:** Incorporates normal maps predicted by StableNormal in texture-less or non-Lambertian regions, regularizing rendered surface normals.
 - The overall optimization objective combines the standard photometric rendering loss with these geometry-oriented regularization terms.

- **High-Throughput Batched Rasterization:**
 - To support visual reinforcement learning (RL) training with multiple parallel environments, QuadVerse implements batched ego-view rendering directly within the simulation pipeline.
 - This involves batching camera matrices across parallel environments while sharing static scene Gaussians. Dynamic Gaussian groups (e.g., task objects) are transformed according to their per-environment poses.
 - A tile-based Gaussian rasterizer is used for rendering this batched scene representation, enabling high-throughput ego-view generation for RL policy training.

#### 3.2. Semantic Mesh-Based Contact Calibration

QuadVerse translates the reconstructed 3DGS scene into a collision-ready semantic mesh and calibrates spatially varying contact parameters.

- **Semantic Collision Mesh Extraction:**
 - Unbiased depth maps rendered from training views are fused into a volumetric Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF).
 - A watertight mesh is extracted using Marching Cubes, and metric scale and coordinate alignment are achieved via similarity transformation using known scene markers.
 - Each Gaussian primitive is augmented with a learnable semantic feature vector, supervised by pseudo-labels from a 2D segmentation model. These 3D semantic features are propagated to mesh faces (e.g., via k-nearest neighbors) to create a semantic collision mesh, which supports region-wise contact property assignment.

- **Prior-Posterior Friction Calibration:**
 - Focuses on friction as the primary calibrated parameter for outdoor, rigid terrains.
 - **LLM-Assisted Coarse Friction Prior:** For each semantic terrain region, a commonsense reasoning model (e.g., GPT-4) provides a coarse nominal friction coefficient. Regions with friction above an empirical slip threshold (e.g., μ = 0.5) retain this prior without further refinement.
 - **Trajectory-Based Posterior Friction Search:** For slip-prone regions, a grid search is performed over the region-level friction coefficient. This search minimizes the trajectory discrepancy between simulated replay of recorded locomotion commands and the real robot's motion on that terrain. This process is conducted region by region, with other regions' friction values held constant.

#### 3.3. Dynamics Compensation via Residual RL

Even with visual and contact alignment, the nominal simulator may not capture actuator-level effects. QuadVerse addresses this with an action-space residual dynamics compensator.

- **Data Collection and Replay:**
 - Real-world locomotion data (high-level commands, joint-space commands, proprioceptive states, LiDAR-tracked global base poses) is collected from the target unstructured terrain (e.g., 10 minutes).
 - During replay, simulation episodes are initialized from sampled timestamps, using corresponding real-world base poses and joint states. Replay occurs on the *contact-calibrated terrain* to prevent terrain-induced contact errors from being erroneously absorbed into the actuator residual.

- **Residual Network Training:**
 - A residual policy is trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to output an additive action correction (a_res). The applied simulation action is `a_sim = a_ref + a_res`, where `a_ref` is the recorded action.
 - An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is used: the actor observes only simulation-available quantities, while the critic leverages privileged real-world trajectory information for value estimation.
 - The reward function balances joint-space tracking, pose/contact consistency, and regularization of the residual actions. Episodes terminate if joint errors exceed a safety threshold to prevent divergence.

- **Locomotion Policy Fine-tuning:**
 - After training, the residual compensator is frozen and integrated into the simulation loop.
 - For downstream locomotion policy fine-tuning, the executed simulation action becomes `a_sim = a_π + clip(a_res, −δ, δ)`, where `a_π` is the nominal policy action, and `δ` bounds the compensation magnitude to prevent aggressive over-compensation outside the replay distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validated QuadVerse's components and its integrated framework on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot across various outdoor scenes.

#### 4.1. Visual Fidelity and Contact Modeling

- **Rendering Quality:** QuadVerse achieved a PSNR of 21.04 and SSIM of 0.569, which were reported as the highest among tested NeRF and 3DGS-based baselines. This indicated superior photometric reconstruction.
- **Geometric Fidelity:** The framework produced coherent watertight surfaces with sharper terrain boundaries, achieving an F1 score of 0.932 for mesh reconstruction. This score was reported to outperform existing geometric reconstruction baselines. The system supports collision-ready interaction and batched ego-view rendering, providing over 2000 FPS rendering throughput at 640x480 resolution for RL training.
- **Contact Calibration:** On mixed-friction terrains, posterior-refined contact calibration resulted in a mean base-position error of 0.12 m during recorded trajectory replay. This was a reduction compared to using LLM coarse priors (0.20 m) and uniform friction (0.70 m), indicating improved accuracy in modeling terrain-robot interaction.

#### 4.2. Actuator Modeling and Tracking Accuracy

- **Open-Loop Joint-Space Replay:** Evaluation of the residual actuator compensator by replaying recorded joint-space commands showed that QuadVerse (0.043 rad mean per-joint tracking error) significantly improved accuracy compared to a nominal simulator (0.127 rad) and a residual model trained on flat terrain (Flat-Replay Residual, 0.064 rad). This suggests that training the compensator on contact-calibrated terrain reduces the entanglement between terrain-induced errors and actuator non-idealities.
- **Policy Fine-Tuning and Real-World Tracking:** Locomotion policies fine-tuned with the compensated dynamics in simulation exhibited a 47% reduction in average real-world trajectory error across various locomotion tasks (e.g., turns, forward movements, figure-eight) compared to policies trained without compensation. This demonstrates that improved replay fidelity translates into more accurate closed-loop deployment in the real world. Cross-platform sim-to-sim transfer experiments on Unitree Go1 and A2 also indicated significant reductions in joint-space replay error (72.5% and 85.4%, respectively), suggesting broader applicability.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Visual Navigation Deployment

The full QuadVerse framework was evaluated on zero-shot real-world visual navigation tasks, where policies trained entirely in simulation were deployed without real-world fine-tuning.

- **Performance:** The full QuadVerse policy achieved an 84% success rate on the real robot, closely approaching its 92% performance in the aligned simulation environment. The average reaching time was 14.24 seconds in real-world deployment.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing scene-specific reconstruction ("QuadVerse w/o Reconstruction") led to a significant drop in real-world success rate to 36% and increased average reaching time to 19.36 seconds. This highlights the importance of the reconstructed visual and geometric scene structure.
 - Removing the residual actuator compensation ("QuadVerse w/o Residual Comp.") resulted in a success rate of 76% and an average reaching time of 18.20 seconds. This degradation was attributed to poorer tracking of high-level velocity commands, leading to accumulated trajectory deviations and less direct navigation paths.

These results collectively indicate that the integrated alignment provided by QuadVerse across visual perception, physical interaction, and actuator dynamics contributes to robust zero-shot policy deployment for complex tasks on physical quadruped robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

QuadVerse presents a significant contribution to robot learning by addressing the sim-to-real gap through an integrated framework that systematically aligns visual perception, physical interaction, and actuator dynamics. Its primary significance lies in moving beyond isolated solutions to provide a comprehensive, coordinated approach to fidelity in simulation.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Robot Learning Efficiency:** By providing a simulation environment that more closely matches physical reality, QuadVerse can reduce the need for extensive and costly real-world data collection and fine-tuning. This can accelerate the development and deployment of advanced robot behaviors, particularly for legged robots operating in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Robust Zero-Shot Transfer:** The demonstrated ability to deploy visual-navigation policies to real robots without task-specific real-world rollouts highlights the potential for significant time and resource savings in robot development cycles. This could enable faster iteration and wider application of robot learning techniques in practical scenarios.
- **Foundational Framework for Future Research:** QuadVerse establishes a structured methodology for integrating various sim-to-real alignment techniques. Its modular components (geometry-anchored reconstruction, semantic contact calibration, residual dynamics compensation) can serve as a foundation for further research into specific aspects of simulation fidelity.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability:** More accurate simulations can lead to the training of more robust and safer robot control policies, as they are developed in an environment that better predicts real-world interactions and dynamics.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By reducing the barriers to effective sim-to-real transfer, QuadVerse could make advanced robot learning more accessible to researchers and developers with limited access to extensive physical robot hardware or testing facilities.

The identified limitations, such as assumptions of static geometry and rigid collision meshes, as well as the reliance on offline trajectory data for calibration, also point to promising avenues for future research. Addressing these could further expand the framework's applicability to highly dynamic, deformable terrains or situations requiring online adaptation to changing environments or robot hardware.
