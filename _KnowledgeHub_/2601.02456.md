# InternVLA-A1: Unifying Understanding, Generation and Action for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The InternVLA-A1 Team developed a unified Mixture-of-Transformers architecture, InternVLA-A1, which integrates semantic understanding, visual foresight, and action execution for robotic manipulation. The model demonstrates enhanced general and dynamic manipulation capabilities, achieving an 86.7% average success rate in real-world dynamic tasks, a substantial gain over prior baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often built on Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), excel at semantic understanding but lack robust physical dynamics reasoning.
- Current policies struggle with generalization to diverse and dynamic environments, partly due to the high cost and inefficiency of collecting sufficiently varied real-world robot data.
- World Models, while offering dynamics prediction, frequently lack strong semantic grounding and can be brittle due to visual prediction errors, while synthetic data introduces a sim-to-real gap.

## Method

- **InternVLA-A1** employs a unified ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture integrating an Understanding Expert, a lightweight Generation Expert for visual foresight, and an Action Expert for control commands.
- A hybrid training strategy leverages large-scale simulated robot trajectories (InternData-A1), real-robot demonstrations (AgiBot-World), and egocentric human videos (EgoDex) to address data diversity and quality challenges.
- The Generation Expert uses ==VAE-based tokenization==, compression, and ==non-autoregressive parallel decoding== to efficiently predict future visual states in real-time (approximately 13 Hz on an RTX 4090 GPU).

## Results

- InternVLA-A1 (3B) achieved an average success rate of **75.1%** across 10 diverse real-world static manipulation tasks, surpassing π0 (**60.6%**) by **14.5%** and π0.5 (**70.7%**) by **4.4%**.
- The model established a clear lead in real-world dynamic manipulation, reaching an **86.7%** average success rate across two tasks, representing a **26.7%** improvement over π0.5 on "Express Sorting" (**80.0%** vs. **53.3%**) and **26.6%** on "In-motion Ingredient Picking" (**93.3%** vs. **66.7%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of pre-training (**51.6%** overall performance drop without it) and the generation expert (**20%** performance drop without it), especially for robust dynamic manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying semantic understanding with dynamic physical world cognition within a single architecture enables foresight-guided decision-making, improving adaptability in complex, dynamic environments.
> - A synergistic training approach combining diverse data sources (simulation, real-robot, human videos) effectively provides scalable diversity and enriches visual representations for robust manipulation, minimizing the sim-to-real gap.
> - Designing a lightweight, efficient visual foresight module is crucial for real-time robotic control, even if it entails a controlled compromise on visual prediction fidelity to capture essential motion trends.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent generalist robots capable of performing diverse tasks in varied environments is a central objective in robotics research. Current approaches predominantly utilize end-to-end learning paradigms and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures, often built upon Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These MLLM-based VLA models, exemplified by systems like $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, and GR00T N1/N1.5, exhibit proficiency in semantic understanding and perform well on daily manipulation tasks after training on extensive real-world robot demonstrations.

However, these models face generalization limitations, particularly in adapting to dynamic or varied environments, due to two primary issues:
1. **Deficient physical world cognition**: MLLMs, mapping visual data to text-based feature spaces, are noted as being less suited for modeling physical laws and dynamics. This leads to policies optimized for reactive perception-to-action mapping, rather than reasoning about future state evolution under motion and contact, a critical limitation in dynamic settings like industrial conveyor belts.
2. **Lack of adaptive manipulation capabilities**: While scaling up real-robot datasets has improved adaptability, such data collection is expensive and inefficient for covering long-tail scene variations and highly dynamic environments.

To address the deficiency in physical world cognition, recent efforts have explored "World Models," often formulated through video prediction (e.g., VPP, Genie Envisioner). These methods aim to generate anticipated observations to guide decision-making, but they frequently suffer from a lack of semantic grounding and can be brittle in the presence of video prediction errors.

Regarding data, while real-world robot data (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, Open X-Embodiment) provides high physical fidelity, its scalability is limited. Simulation data (e.g., InternData-A1, RoboTwin) offers diversity and efficiency in collection but introduces a sim-to-real gap, particularly in contact-rich dynamics. Human videos (e.g., EgoDex, Ego4D) provide rich visual priors and interaction dynamics but lack direct action labels for robot control.

InternVLA-A1 positions itself at the intersection of these challenges by proposing a unified architecture that synergizes semantic understanding with dynamic predictive capabilities. It aims to bridge the gap between MLLM-based semantic reasoning and World Model-style dynamics prediction within a single framework. Concurrently, it addresses data limitations by employing a hybrid training strategy that leverages the diversity of synthetic data, the physical fidelity of real-world data, and the rich visual priors from human videos. This approach seeks to overcome generalization bottlenecks and enhance robustness, especially in dynamic manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing InternVLA-A1 are derived from the identified limitations in the existing robotic manipulation research landscape:

1. **To unify semantic understanding and physical dynamics prediction:** Existing VLA models, often built on MLLMs, excel at semantic understanding but lack robust physical dynamics reasoning. Conversely, World Models, typically based on video prediction, offer dynamics prediction but may lack strong semantic grounding and are sensitive to prediction errors. The motivation is to create a unified architecture that integrates both capabilities, allowing for semantically-grounded, dynamics-aware action generation. This aims to enable foresight-guided decision-making and improve adaptability in complex, dynamic environments.

2. **To enhance adaptability and robustness in diverse and dynamic environments:** Current policies struggle with scene variations and dynamic settings where objects are in motion (e.g., conveyor belts). This limitation is attributed to deficient physical cognition. A core objective is to develop a policy that can reason about how states will evolve under motion and contact, thereby improving performance in tasks requiring dynamic manipulation and cross-scene generalization.

3. **To address the scalability and quality challenges of robot training data:** Relying solely on real-world robot demonstrations is costly and inefficient for collecting large-scale, diverse data, especially for long-tail scenarios and dynamic variations. While synthetic data offers scalability and diversity, it suffers from an inherent sim-to-real gap. Human videos provide rich visual priors but no direct action labels. The motivation is to formulate a synergistic data strategy that combines these heterogeneous sources—large-scale simulated robot trajectories, real-robot demonstrations, and egocentric human videos—to achieve scalable diversity, minimize the sim-to-real gap, and enrich visual representations for complex manipulations, leading to improved robustness and real-world transfer.

4. **To develop an efficient and real-time capable visual foresight module:** Traditional video generation models are often computationally intensive, hindering real-time inference required for high-frequency robotic control. A specific objective is to design a lightweight generation expert that can provide effective visual guidance for action execution without compromising real-time performance, enabling practical deployment on robotic hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InternVLA-A1 employs a unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture to integrate scene understanding, visual foresight generation, and action execution. This design aims to combine semantic reasoning with dynamics prediction to guide action.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The model consists of three specialized "experts" that interact through a unified masked self-attention mechanism:
- **Understanding Expert:** Processes multimodal inputs (images, text) to capture environmental context, generating contextual embeddings.
- **Generation Expert:** Simulates task evolution by predicting future visual states.
- **Action Expert:** Combines semantic context and predictive dynamics to synthesize robot control commands using Flow Matching.

#### 3.2. Core Components

All three experts utilize a decoder-only transformer architecture.

- **Understanding Expert:**
 - Directly adopts the architecture of existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), specifically InternVL3-1B (for InternVLA-A1 2B) or Qwen3-VL-2B (for InternVLA-A1 3B).
 - Encodes multi-view observations ($o_t$) into visual tokens via an integrated vision encoder and language instructions ($l$) into text tokens via a text tokenizer.
 - Processes these tokens through transformer blocks to form contextual embeddings ($h_{und}$), which serve as shared memory for downstream experts.

- **Generation Expert:**
 - Designed as a lightweight module to prioritize inference efficiency.
 - **Input Tokenization:** Takes 6 input images (head view and two wrist views at current ($t$) and historical ($t-15$) timesteps), each resized to 256x256. A VAE-based tokenizer (COSMOS CI8x8) encodes these images into latent grids, preserving fine-grained spatial structure.
 - **Token Compression:** To manage sequence length, a convolution layer downsamples each 32x32 latent grid to 4x4 (16 tokens). This reduces the total input sequence for 6 images to 96 tokens, which are then projected to the transformer's hidden dimension.
 - **Parallel Decoding:** After transformer processing, the 96 output tokens undergo temporal average pooling along the time axis, resulting in 48 tokens (16 per view). These are projected, upsampled via deconvolution to a 32x32 grid, and then reconstructed into predicted future frames ($t+15$) by the COSMOS VAE decoder. This non-autoregressive, single-forward parallel decoding ensures computational efficiency.

- **Action Expert:**
 - Predicts a target action chunk $\hat{a}_{t:t+k}$ conditioned on latent features from the understanding and generation experts, along with the proprioceptive state $q_t$.
 - Trained using a flow matching objective, which models action generation as learning continuous transformation pathways from noise to expert demonstrations, supporting multimodal action distributions.

- **Attention Mechanism:**
 - A blockwise attention mask is applied over concatenated token streams.
 - A cumulative segment mask enforces information flow: understanding $\rightarrow$ generation $\rightarrow$ action.
 - Understanding and generation expert tokens are fully bidirectionally attended within their blocks.
 - Action expert tokens (state and action) attend to earlier blocks and internally, with action tokens also attending to the state token.

#### 3.3. Optimization Objectives

The training process is divided into two stages: Pre-training and Post-training, both sharing a unified optimization framework.

- **Visual Foresight Generation ($L_{gen}$):** The generation expert predicts the latent representation of a future frame $z_{t+m}$ based on current/historical observations and understanding prefix $h_{und}$. The objective minimizes the squared difference between the prediction and the ground truth latent feature ($L_2$ loss).

- **Flow Matching-based Action Prediction ($L_{act}$):** A flow matching framework trains the action expert. It learns a velocity field $v_\theta$ that transports noisy action samples $a_{\tau, t:t+k}$ (interpolated between Gaussian noise $\epsilon$ and ground truth actions $a_{t:t+k}$) towards the target actions. The loss minimizes the squared difference between the learned velocity field and the target velocity field. During inference, an ODE solver (Euler update) iteratively refines Gaussian noise into the predicted action.

- **Total Loss:** $L_{total} = \lambda \cdot L_{gen} + L_{act}$, where $\lambda$ balances the objectives.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Model Scales:** InternVLA-A1 (2B) (1.8B parameters) uses InternVL3-1B as understanding expert and Qwen2.5 for generation/action. InternVLA-A1 (3B) (3.2B parameters) uses Qwen3-VL-2B for understanding and Qwen3 for generation/action. Both achieve approximately 13 Hz inference speed on an RTX 4090 GPU.
- **Training Protocol:** A large-scale pre-training stage (700K steps) on heterogeneous datasets is followed by a task-specific post-training stage (60,000 steps) with different learning rate schedules.
- **Load-balanced Parallel Training (LPT):** A distributed data-loading strategy is used to manage heterogeneous datasets ($>692M$ frames) across workers, reducing memory pressure and mitigating implicit re-weighting effects by ensuring balanced dataset assignment and controlled replication.

#### 3.5. Data Corpus

Pre-training utilizes a mixture of heterogeneous data sources with configurable sampling weights:
- **Simulated Synthetic Data:** InternData-A1 (396M frames) and RoboTwin (17M frames). InternData-A1 provides diversity in embodiments, skills, tasks, and scenes, generated via an autonomous pipeline.
- **Real-world Robot Data:** AgiBot-World (Beta) (206M frames) and RoboMind (5M frames). These datasets offer large-scale trajectory coverage, diverse task distributions, and high-quality teleoperation, crucial for physical dynamics and sim-to-real gap reduction.
- **Egocentric Human Video:** EgoDex (68M frames). This dataset provides rich visual priors of human-hand-object interactions, enhancing the generation expert's ability to capture nuanced dynamics without requiring human action labels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

InternVLA-A1 was evaluated on 12 real-world robotic tasks (10 static, 2 dynamic) and a simulation benchmark, consistently outperforming prior leading models.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on Static Manipulation Tasks

- InternVLA-A1 demonstrated improved general manipulation capabilities across 10 diverse real-world static tasks.
- InternVLA-A1 (2B) achieved an average success rate of 64.7%, surpassing the larger $\pi_0$ (3.3B) baseline (60.6%).
- InternVLA-A1 (3B) achieved an average success rate of 75.1%, representing a +14.5% improvement over $\pi_0$ (60.6%) and a +4.4% improvement over $\pi_{0.5}$ (70.7%).
- Notable gains were observed in long-horizon tasks such as "Make Sandwich" (93.3% vs. 73.3% for $\pi_{0.5}$) and "Operate Oven" (86.7% vs. 80.0%).
- On tasks like "Zip Bag," InternVLA-A1 (3B) achieved 73.3%, compared to 60.0% for $\pi_{0.5}$ and 40.0% for $\pi_0$.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Dynamic Manipulation Tasks

- In challenging real-world dynamic manipulation tasks ("Express Sorting" and "In-motion Ingredient Picking"), InternVLA-A1 established a clear lead.
- InternVLA-A1 (3B) achieved 80.0% success on "Express Sorting" and 93.3% on "In-motion Ingredient Picking," resulting in an average success rate of 86.7% across these two tasks.
- This represents a substantial gain over $\pi_{0.5}$, with +26.7% on "Express Sorting" (80.0% vs. 53.3%) and +26.6% on "In-motion Ingredient Picking" (93.3% vs. 66.7%).
- $\pi_0$ and GR00T N1.5 achieved 20.0% on "In-motion Ingredient Picking," indicating a significant performance gap.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on Simulation Benchmark

- On the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmark (50 bimanual tasks under Easy and Hard settings), InternVLA-A1 (3B) significantly outperformed baselines.
- It surpassed $\pi_0$ by 9.4% (Easy) and 10.1% (Hard) and $\pi_{0.5}$ by 2.6% in both Easy and Hard settings.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Pre-training:** Removing the pre-training stage resulted in a 51.6% overall performance drop (average success rate falling from 77.0% to 25.4%), highlighting pre-training as a crucial inductive prior.
- **Impact of Pre-training Datasets:**
 - Training on simulation data alone yielded strong simulation results (88.3%/88.5% on RoboTwin 2.0 Easy/Hard) but showed lower real-world generalization (53.3% on "Place flower," 33.3% on "Sort parts").
 - Incorporating human videos improved simulation performance slightly (up to 89.4%/89.3%) and provided a minor benefit to real-world tasks (e.g., "Sort parts" to 40.0%).
 - Jointly pre-training on heterogeneous data (simulation + real-world + human videos) achieved the best overall performance, substantially enhancing real-world manipulation (60.0% on "Place flower," 53.3% on "Sort parts"). This validated the effectiveness of the hybrid training strategy.
- **Impact of Generation Expert:** Removing the generation expert significantly reduced the average success rate from 77.0% to 57.6%. The most substantial decline was observed in dynamic manipulation tasks (e.g., "Express Sorting" and "In-motion Ingredient Picking"), confirming the generation expert's contribution to robust foresight-guided decision-making.

#### 4.5. Visualization

- Visualizations of the head-view future predictions showed that while some high-frequency visual details were sacrificed (a deliberate design choice for inference efficiency), the model accurately captured essential motion trends and dynamics, providing sufficient instructive information for action execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InternVLA-A1 represents an advancement in generalist robotic manipulation by integrating semantic understanding, visual foresight generation, and action execution within a unified framework. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Dynamic Environments:** By explicitly coupling semantic reasoning with dynamics prediction, InternVLA-A1 demonstrates marked superiority in dynamic manipulation tasks. This capability to reason about future states under motion and contact, rather than merely reacting to current observations, is critical for real-world applications such as industrial automation, logistics, and assistive robotics, where objects are frequently in motion. This reduces the brittleness observed in prior reactive VLA policies.

2. **Bridging the Semantics-Dynamics Gap:** The unified Mixture-of-Transformers architecture addresses a fundamental limitation in previous VLA models (strong semantics but weak dynamics) and World Models (strong dynamics but weak semantics). InternVLA-A1 shows that a model can simultaneously achieve high-level semantic understanding and robust physical dynamics prediction, leading to more adaptable and intelligent robotic behaviors.

3. **Effective Data Integration Strategy:** The hybrid training approach, leveraging synthetic simulation data, real-world robot demonstrations, and egocentric human videos, provides a scalable and robust pathway for policy learning. This strategy capitalizes on the diversity and cost-efficiency of simulation, the physical fidelity of real-world data, and the rich visual priors from human actions, while minimizing the sim-to-real gap. This approach can inform future large-scale data collection and training pipelines for generalist robots.

4. **Improved Generalization Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains across diverse static manipulation tasks, dynamic scenarios, and simulation benchmarks indicate improved generalization beyond specific tasks or environments. This suggests progress towards the goal of generalist robot policies that can adapt to novel variations in real-world settings.

5. **Practical Inference Efficiency:** The design of a lightweight generation expert with parallel decoding ensures that the visual foresight module can operate in real-time (approximately 13 Hz on an RTX 4090 GPU). This focus on computational efficiency is crucial for the practical deployment of VLA models on robotic hardware, overcoming a common bottleneck in more complex video generation models.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges two primary limitations:
1. The understanding expert is not jointly trained with large-scale multimodal Visual Question Answering (VQA) data, which may restrict its general semantic reasoning and complex instruction following capabilities.
2. The visual foresight generation module's image prediction fidelity was intentionally compromised for inference efficiency, limiting the granularity of generated future frames.

Addressing these limitations in future work could further enhance the model's capabilities and broader applicability. Overall, InternVLA-A1 contributes a model architecture and training methodology that advances the integration of cognitive and predictive abilities in robot control, paving the way for more robust and adaptable autonomous systems.
