# AMBUSH: Collaborative Capture in Complex Environments with Neural Acceleration

> [!summary] Summary
> AMBUSH, a framework for multi-robot collaborative capture, enables slower pursuers to intercept faster, intelligent evaders in complex, cluttered environments by combining a parameterized ambush strategy with a neural-accelerated hybrid Monte Carlo Tree Search. It achieved 100% capture success in diverse simulations and robust performance in hardware experiments, significantly reducing planning time compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing analytical and geometric methods for multi-agent pursuit-evasion often fail in complex, obstacle-dense environments due to reliance on simplified models and inability to handle non-convex obstacles.
- End-to-end reinforcement learning methods for capture struggle with generalization to unseen complex environments, varying speed ratios, or highly intelligent evaders, often requiring extensive environment-specific retraining.
- Designing robust capture strategies for kinematically disadvantaged (slower) pursuers against faster, intelligent, or human-controlled evaders while maintaining real-time computational efficiency for dynamic scenarios is a significant challenge.

## Method

- **AMBUSH** introduces a ==parameterized ambush strategy==, partitioning pursuers into 'hiders' and 'attackers' with assigned roles and goals, leveraging environmental topology for concealment and herding maneuvers.
- A ==Hybrid Monte Carlo Tree Search (H-MCTS)== algorithm optimizes both discrete (role assignments) and continuous (motion coefficients) parameters of the ambush strategy for long-term planning, maximizing capture probability and minimizing capture time.
- A ==Neural Acceleration (NARE)== module, consisting of a ==Graph Neural Network== Ranker (**NAR**) and Evaluator (**NAE**), accelerates H-MCTS by guiding tree expansion and predicting rollout rewards, thereby reducing online computational costs and enabling real-time decision-making.

## Results

- AMBUSH consistently achieved a **100%** capture success rate across diverse and complex simulated environments and maintained robust performance against human-controlled evaders with realistic partial perception.
- The framework demonstrated minimal average capture times (e.g., **9.7s** to **17.0s** in complex scenarios) and significantly reduced online planning time (**18.7-28.6ms** per replan) with a **35x** reduction in replanning frequency compared to baselines.
- Performance metrics showed AMBUSH significantly outperformed analytical, geometric, and RL baselines in terms of success rate, capture time, and generalization, particularly in complex, cluttered environments and against faster evaders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A coordinated ambush strategy, strategically leveraging environmental obstacles, is highly effective for a limited number of slower pursuers to capture faster, intelligent evaders in complex, cluttered settings.
> - Optimizing a hybrid parameter space, combining discrete role assignments with continuous motion strategies through an H-MCTS framework, is crucial for adaptability and robust performance in intricate environmental topologies and dynamic evader behaviors.
> - Neural acceleration significantly improves the real-time applicability of complex search-based planning methods like MCTS by intelligently guiding the search process and efficiently predicting outcomes, enabling robust performance and generalization in unseen environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the problem of dynamic and collaborative capture, where multiple robotic pursuers aim to intercept a dynamic evader. This problem is recognized in both natural phenomena, such as group hunting by animals, and various robotic applications, including security, surveillance, search and rescue, and intruder interception.

Existing approaches to the multi-agent pursuit-evasion problem typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Analytical and Geometric Solutions:** These methods, often rooted in differential game theory (e.g., Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs equations, Voronoi partitioning), aim to find optimal pursuit strategies. While some extensions address slower pursuers or convex obstacles, they are generally constrained to obstacle-free or sparsely-cluttered environments. They may also suffer from convergence issues or suboptimal equilibria when confronted with numerous or non-convex obstacles, thereby limiting their applicability in complex, realistic scenarios.
2. **End-to-End Reinforcement Learning (RL) Methods:** Recent advancements in deep reinforcement learning have enabled robots to learn cooperative pursuit strategies. However, these data-driven approaches often struggle with generalization to complex, unseen environments, particularly those with intricate non-convex obstacles, varying speed ratios between agents, or when facing highly intelligent, even human-controlled, evaders. They typically require extensive environment-specific retraining and may lack theoretical guarantees.

The broader landscape also includes studies on pursuit under limited sensing or visibility constraints. However, these often define "capture" as visual detection rather than physical interception within a capture radius, which is a key distinction for practical robotic applications.

This work positions itself to bridge the gap identified in the existing literature by introducing a "unique perspective" – an "ambush" strategy. Unlike prior approaches that focus on continuous differential games, discrete graph movements, or general encirclement, this paper specifically designs a parameterized ambush strategy to enable a limited number of slower pursuers to capture faster, intelligent evaders in complex, obstacle-dense environments. It combines topological analysis with a hybrid optimization framework and a neural acceleration technique. This approach aims to provide both robust performance in cluttered settings, where traditional methods fail, and computational efficiency, which is a challenge for purely search-based or learning-based methods in dynamic, complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical challenges encountered when deploying robotic teams for dynamic capture missions in real-world, complex environments. Current robotic systems struggle to reliably capture agile evaders, especially when the evader is faster or exhibits complex, intelligent behaviors, including human control, and when the operational environment is rich with obstacles.

Specific motivations include:
- **Addressing limitations of existing methods in complex environments:** Analytical and geometric solutions frequently fail in obstacle-dense settings due to their reliance on simplified environmental models or their inability to handle non-convex obstacles and complex topologies. Learning-based approaches, while flexible, often require extensive environment-specific retraining and demonstrate poor generalization to varied or unseen complex scenarios.
- **Enabling slower pursuers to capture faster evaders:** Many real-world applications involve pursuers that are kinematically disadvantaged compared to the evader. Existing methods often assume faster pursuers or struggle to maintain capture guarantees when pursuers are slower.
- **Coping with intelligent and unpredictable evader behavior:** The evader's strategy is often unknown and dynamic. Designing robust capture strategies against adaptive or even human-controlled adversaries is a significant challenge.
- **Improving computational efficiency for real-time deployment:** Complex planning algorithms, such as Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), can be computationally intensive, hindering real-time adaptation in dynamic environments. Accelerating these planning processes without compromising solution quality is crucial for practical applications.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:
- **To propose and validate the "ambush" strategy:** To demonstrate that a specific, coordinated "ambush" strategy is effective for multiple slower pursuers to capture one faster evader, even with different levels of intelligence, within complex, obstacle-dense environments.
- **To design a parameterized ambush framework:** This framework includes discrete and continuous parameters that account for environmental topological properties, truncated line-of-sight visibility, relative speed ratios, and limited capture ranges.
- **To develop a Hybrid Monte Carlo Tree Search (H-MCTS) algorithm:** This algorithm is designed to optimize the hybrid parameter space (discrete role assignments and continuous motion coefficients) through long-term planning, thereby identifying promising configurations for capture.
- **To introduce and integrate a neural acceleration module (NARE):** This module aims to accelerate the online H-MCTS planning procedure. It involves offline training of a Graph-Neural-Network-based Ranker (NAR) to guide tree expansion by ranking parameter choices and a Neural Network Evaluator (NAE) to predict rollout rewards, replacing computationally expensive simulations.
- **To provide theoretical insights and practical validation:** To establish theoretical conditions for guaranteed capture and validate the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed framework through extensive simulations and hardware experiments against various evader capabilities, including human-controlled behavior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, termed AMBUSH, addresses collaborative capture in complex environments by integrating a parameterized ambush strategy with a hybrid optimization algorithm accelerated by a neural network. The framework operates through three main components: (I) generating pursuer roles and motion parameters, (II) determining motion policies based on these parameters, and (III) overseeing execution and adapting to evader reactions.

**A. Parameterized Strategy of Ambush**
The core of the strategy involves partitioning pursuers into "hiders," who conceal themselves near obstacles, and "attackers," who herd the evader into these hidden positions for a surprise capture. This strategy is defined by a parameter tuple $\xi = (A, \rho)$, where $A$ is the assignment of roles and goals to pursuers, and $\rho$ represents continuous coefficients for the motion strategy.

1. **Goal Generation and Role Assignment (Algorithm 1):**
 - **Topological Map Construction:** The environment's topology is analyzed by extracting obstacle skeleton vertices (using medial-axis transformation) and boundary vertices (projecting skeleton vertices onto workspace boundaries).
 - **Gate Construction:** "Gates" are defined as valid, collision-free line segments connecting specific boundary or obstacle skeleton vertices. These gates represent strategic locations. An integer programming problem is solved to select an optimal subset of gates, minimizing their total length and penalizing unselected gates, while maintaining sufficient topological connections.
 - **Goal Definition:** Attacker goals ($\tilde{g}_a$) are set at the midpoints of the selected gates. Hider goals ($\tilde{g}_h$) are flanking points near obstacle vertices, placed with a small safety clearance.
 - **Assignment ($A$):** A combinatorial assignment procedure assigns each pursuer a unique goal and a corresponding role (attacker if goal is $\tilde{g}_a$, hider if goal is $\tilde{g}_h$).
 - **Capture Graph ($\text{G}_A$):** For each assignment, a "capture graph" is defined as a geometric graph that forms a closed polygonal enclosure around the evader, crucial for subsequent theoretical analysis.

2. **Motion Strategy:**
 - **Stage I (Goal Convergence):** Pursuers first move towards their assigned goals. This is managed using discrete cost maps ($M_i(c_{jk})$) defined over an 8-connected grid of the workspace. The cost at each grid cell combines role-specific tactical objectives, goal attraction, and obstacle avoidance. A parameter $\rho_i \in$ balances tactical objectives against navigational costs. Optimal paths are computed using A\* search.
 - **Stage II (Herding and Capture):** Once pursuers reach their initial goals and the evader is within the capture graph, attackers adopt a sweeping strategy. They partition the angular space around the evader (excluding hider gates) and move to positions on a circumcircle around the evader, herding it towards the hider gates. Hiders remain stationary until the evader is close to their designated gate, then launch a sudden attack. Dynamic collision avoidance (e.g., ORCA) is integrated for local motion refinement.

**B. Hybrid Monte-Carlo Tree Search (H-MCTS) (Algorithm 2)**
H-MCTS is proposed to solve the hybrid optimization problem of selecting the optimal ambush parameter $\xi = (A, \rho)$ that maximizes capture probability and minimizes capture time within a given time window.
- **Problem Formulation (Problem 2):** Seeks to maximize a reward function that balances capture success ($I_{cap}$) and normalized capture time with a penalty for the area of the capture graph ($S(\xi)$) if capture fails.
- **Phases:**
 - **Selection:** Standard Upper Confidence Bound for Trees (UCT) rule guides the search to promising nodes.
 - **Expansion:** When a node is expandable (i.e., has unselected assignments), an assignment $A$ is randomly selected. A candidate set of continuous motion coefficients $\rho$ is generated by discretization and then downsampled based on a cost function ($\Phi(\rho)$) that evaluates visibility risk and cumulative navigation cost. New child nodes are created for these promising $\xi = (A, \rho)$ pairs.
 - **Simulation:** For a newly expanded node, a rollout simulation is performed using a surrogate evader policy ($\hat{\pi}_e$, modeled as a random walk). The simulation runs until capture or a time horizon is reached, and a reward $r$ (based on capture success and capture graph area) is returned.
 - **Backpropagation:** The reward from the simulation is propagated back through the tree, updating visit counts and cumulative rewards of ancestral nodes.
 - **Termination:** After a computational budget is exhausted, the algorithm selects the child node of the root with the highest average reward, and its corresponding $\xi$ is deployed.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Theorem 1 provides sufficient conditions for guaranteed capture (number of exit gates vs. pursuers, evader confinement, capture radius for gate coverage). Theorem 2 states the asymptotic optimality of H-MCTS for finding the optimal parameter over the sampled space.

**C. Neural Acceleration (NARE)**
To mitigate the high computational cost of H-MCTS, particularly for real-time application, a neural accelerator (NARE) is introduced. NARE is trained offline and integrated online.
- **Components:**
 - **Neural Network Ranker (NAR):** Takes a visibility graph representation of attacker goal candidates ($\tilde{G}_r$) and predicts the Q-value ratio ($Q/N$) for candidate assignments. This guides the expansion phase in H-MCTS by prioritizing high-potential assignments.
 - **Neural Network Evaluator (NAE):** Takes a heterogeneous state graph ($\tilde{G}_e$) capturing real-time system dynamics and predicts the immediate simulation reward ($r$) for leaf nodes. This replaces the full rollout simulation, significantly reducing computation.
- **Architecture and Training:** Both NAR and NAE employ Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). NAR uses Graph Isomorphism Networks (GIN) for its homogeneous visibility graph input, while NAE uses Heterogeneous Graph Attention Networks (HGAN) for its heterogeneous state graph. They share a three-stage processing framework: graph encoding, GNN-based message passing, and feature prediction. Training datasets are generated by executing H-MCTS across diverse scenarios, and networks are trained via supervised learning to minimize MSE loss. Validation is performed on unseen environments to ensure generalization.

**D. Online Execution and Adaptation**
The framework employs a dynamic triggering mechanism for replanning and role switching, rather than fixed-interval replanning. Replanning is initiated under three conditions: (I) the evader breaches the current ambush perimeter, (II) all pursuers reach their goals significantly earlier than the estimated capture time, or (III) a maximum replanning interval expires. This approach aims to minimize unnecessary replanning while ensuring responsiveness to dynamic changes.

**E. Generalization**
The framework is designed for generalization across various complexities:
- **Heterogeneous Pursuers:** Supports varying capture radii and velocities by configuring per-pursuer parameters, with motion strategies adapting automatically.
- **Limited View for Pursuers:** Incorporates a phase-switching mechanism: if the evader is not visible, the team switches to a cooperative search-and-exploration strategy; upon detection, it reverts to the ambush mode.
- **Multiple Evaders:** Extends to multi-evader scenarios using task allocation methods, forming dynamic, evader-specific coalitions.
- **Sparse Environments:** Can adapt by bypassing the detailed goal generation (Alg. 1) and using alternative encirclement algorithms during rollouts for minimal obstacle or free-space scenarios.
- **Different Robot Models:** The high-level planner outputs geometric paths, independent of specific robot dynamics. Low-level controllers can be adapted for non-holonomic (e.g., unicycle) or inertial (e.g., double-integrator) models.
- **Non-polygonal Obstacles:** Handles these by using conservative polygonal approximations in the geometric preprocessing step.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive simulations and hardware experiments to validate the AMBUSH framework.

**A. Overall Performance**
- The system achieves real-time policy updates, averaging 5.5 seconds. The pipeline includes offline topological decomposition (approx. 100s) and goal computation (approx. 2s), and an online phase filtering goals and pruning search space (0.1s each). H-MCTS executes gate selection in 5ms/cycle and parameter discretization in 0.5s.
- The ambush strategy is demonstrated in three progressively complex scenarios (Scenario-I: regular obstacles, Scenario-II: polygonal obstacles, Scenario-III: irregular obstacles).
 - In Scenario-I, pursuers effectively transition from initial formation to specialized roles, achieving capture.
 - Scenario-II demonstrates systematic shrinking of the ambush region and emergence-driven capture.
 - Scenario-III highlights adaptation through dynamic gate recomputation and curved path interception.
- Strategy invocation frequency and capture time increase with environmental complexity (e.g., 2.4, 8.8, and 5 replans; 6.7s, 9.7s, 17.0s capture times for Scenario-I, II, III respectively). Role adaptation from attacker-dominated to greater hider involvement is observed, indicating balancing of adaptability and efficiency.

**B. Analysis of Key Parameters**
The method's performance is systematically evaluated under varying pursuer numbers, evader velocities, evader intelligence levels, and capture ranges against five baselines: an Analytic-based Method, a Geometric-based Method (Voronoi), an RL-based Method, a Shooting method (probabilistic gate selection), and a vanilla MCTS (without NARE).

1. **Number of Pursuers (2 to 5):**
 - AMBUSH and its MCTS variant achieve 100% success rates. Capture time decreases with more pursuers (15.7s to 10.08s for 2 to 5 pursuers) but saturates beyond 4 pursuers due to agent redundancy.
 - Baselines show variable performance: Shooting (60-100% success, longer times), Analytic (40-60% success, longer times), Voronoi (0% success in narrow environments), RL (20-100% success, but 52.7% longer capture times at N=5).
2. **Evader Velocity (1.2 to 2.0 times faster than pursuers):**
 - AMBUSH maintains 100% success. Capture times show a convex trend, decreasing from 20.4s at 1.2x speed to 15.9s at 1.8x, then rising to 18.3s at 2.0x. This suggests a trade-off between evader entrapment and escape potential.
 - MCTS variant follows this trend but with 25-40% longer times. Shooting shows random performance. Analytic fails completely. RL baseline, trained at 1.6x speed, exhibits limited generalization, dropping from 70% success below 1.6x to 40% above 1.8x, with much longer capture times.
3. **Evader Intelligence:**
 - Against simulated complex evaders, AMBUSH maintains 100% success, though requiring 108.2% longer capture times than against simple evaders. Baselines demonstrate high sensitivity or poor generalization.
 - **Against human operators:** AMBUSH achieves 60% success with local evader perception, but performance degrades significantly (40% at 1.2x, 20% at 1.6x, 0% at 2.0x) when the human operator has global vision. This indicates that while the ambush strategy is effective against realistic partial-information adversaries, it has inherent limitations against perfect situational awareness.
4. **Capture Range ($d_c$ from 0.4m to 0.8m):**
 - Capture range is identified as a dominant factor. AMBUSH maintains 100% success across all tested speeds (1.2-2.0m/s) and capture ranges.
 - Baselines show strong sensitivity, with MCTS, Shooting, and Analytic methods significantly improving success rates with larger capture ranges. RL shows good generalization here.
 - **Discussion:** The study concludes that capture range optimization is more critical than brute-force scaling of team size or kinematic superiority under resource constraints.

**C. Scalability and Adaptability**
- The method demonstrates adaptability to dynamic changes in team size (e.g., increasing pursuers from 3 to 4 during execution) and environmental layouts (e.g., sequential disappearance of obstacles). In both cases, the system successfully completes the capture task through continuous replanning.

**D. Comparisons against Baselines (Table I, Fig. 15)**
- **Success Rate:** AMBUSH consistently achieves 100% success across all three scenarios, outperforming all baselines. Baselines show significant degradation in success rates, particularly in complex Scenario III (e.g., Analytic 20%, RL 20%, Geometric 0%, Shooting 60%, MCTS 80%). The NARE module is noted for preserving robustness in unseen, cluttered environments, unlike the RL baseline which struggles with generalization.
- **Capture Time:** AMBUSH achieves minimal average capture times (e.g., 11.9s, 9.7s, 17.0s), showing a significant advantage in complex scenarios compared to baselines.
- **Planning Time and Replans:** AMBUSH maintains real-time planning feasibility (18.7-28.6ms) and significantly reduces the number of replans (2.4-8.8 replans per trial), indicating a 35x reduction compared to baselines (128-600 replans).

**E. Neural Acceleration Results**
- NARE effectively guides the H-MCTS expansion. Compared to vanilla MCTS, NARE-guided MCTS prioritizes high-value nodes earlier, achieving similar performance with fewer explored nodes (e.g., 10-node NARE matches 20-node MCTS performance).
- NARE demonstrates good generality in unseen environments, achieving a 90% capture success rate across 50 topologically diverse maps (45 for training, 5 for testing).

**F. Generalization to Varied Conditions**
- **Heterogeneous Pursuers:** Demonstrated compatibility and improved team performance when one pursuer has increased capabilities (capture range, velocity, or both).
- **Limited View for Pursuers:** The integrated search mode and dynamic mode-switching allow successful capture under severely restricted visibility (e.g., 5m LOS range), showcasing robustness.
- **Multiple Evaders:** The modular design successfully handles multi-evader scenarios (5 pursuers, 2 evaders) via dynamic coalition formation and independent ambush strategies, maintaining real-time operation and scalability.
- **Sparse Environments:** Effective captures are achieved in sparse environments (large obstacles, scattered small obstacles, largely free space), with efficiency improving as obstacle density decreases.
- **Different Robot Models:** The framework is validated with both unicycle and double-integrator robot models, confirming that its effectiveness is primarily driven by the high-level pursuit strategy rather than specific low-level dynamics.
- **Non-polygonal Obstacles:** The method successfully constructs effective capture graphs and guides pursuers in environments with circular and curved obstacles through polygonal approximations.

**G. Hardware Experiment**
- **Autonomous Evader:** A 5m x 5m arena with physical obstacles and differential-driven UGVs validated the AMBUSH strategy in the real world. Despite low-level control imperfections, robust capture was achieved through continuous replanning within 14.7s.
- **Human-controlled Evader:** Against human operators with limited first-person view, the strategy achieved successful captures (avg 12.85s). However, when the human operator had a global view, captures failed within 60s, indicating that perfect information allows the evader to exploit fundamental limitations of the ambush strategy. This highlights the strategy's effectiveness against realistic human perception while acknowledging its boundaries against ideal adversaries.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multi-agent robotic control, particularly for dynamic capture problems in complex and cluttered environments.

**A. Novel Approach to Complex Capture:** The introduction of a "parameterized ambush strategy" offers a novel perspective that differs from traditional encirclement or direct pursuit methods. By strategically leveraging environmental topology for concealment and herding, the approach enables slower and fewer pursuers to capture faster and more intelligent evaders, which is a common and challenging scenario in real-world applications.

**B. Hybrid Optimization and Neural Acceleration:** The development of a Hybrid Monte Carlo Tree Search (H-MCTS) addresses the intricate challenge of optimizing both discrete (role assignment) and continuous (motion parameters) decision variables. The integration of Neural Acceleration (NARE) is a key innovation, significantly improving the computational efficiency of MCTS by guiding tree expansion and predicting rollout rewards. This allows for real-time decision-making in dynamic environments without sacrificing solution quality, which is critical for practical deployment.

**C. Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The framework demonstrates high robustness, achieving a 100% success rate across diverse and complex simulated environments and against various evader intelligence levels. Its ability to generalize to unseen environments and adapt to online changes in team composition or environmental layouts is a notable advancement over many learning-based methods that often struggle with out-of-distribution scenarios. The hardware experiments and validation against human-controlled evaders (under realistic perception limits) further underscore its practical viability.

**D. Practical Applicability and Impact:** The findings suggest that the AMBUSH framework can be directly deployed for critical robotic applications such as:
- **Perimeter Security:** Autonomous patrolling and intruder interception in urban or cluttered industrial environments.
- **Search and Rescue:** Locating and containing subjects in complex terrains.
- **Wildlife Protection:** Intercepting poachers in dense natural environments.
- **Unauthorized Drone Interception:** Managing agile targets in cluttered airspace.
The reduced replanning frequency and consistent performance compared to baselines make this approach a compelling candidate for real-world autonomous systems.

**E. Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies clear pathways for future work, including extending the framework to:
- **Distributed Deployment:** Moving from centralized planning to decentralized execution for truly distributed robotic teams, which would enhance scalability and fault tolerance.
- **Partial Observability and Distributed Estimation:** Addressing scenarios where pursuers have limited or uncertain information about the environment and evader positions.
- **Advanced Multi-Evader Pursuit:** Further refining the multi-evader extension to handle more complex coordination and dynamic task allocation challenges.

In summary, this work contributes a robust, efficient, and theoretically grounded framework for collaborative robotic capture, addressing critical limitations of prior methods and demonstrating substantial potential for real-world impact in various security and surveillance applications.
