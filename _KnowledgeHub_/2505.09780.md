# Neural Inertial Odometry from Lie Events

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Lie Events, a novel event-based representation of IMU data on the Special Euclidean Group SE(3), to improve the generalization and robustness of learning-based inertial odometry. The method demonstrates a significant reduction in sensitivity to motion speed and sampling rate variations, achieving up to 21% lower Absolute Translation Error (ATE) and robust performance across diverse datasets and sensor configurations.

## Problem

- Neural Displacement Priors (NDPs) for inertial odometry struggle to generalize across different motion patterns (e.g., varying speeds or gaits) due to the high variability of raw IMU data.
- Existing NDPs are sensitive to changes in IMU sampling rates, requiring retraining or extensive data augmentation for different sensor configurations.
- The inherent drift in IMU integration requires fusion with other sensors (like cameras), but IMU-only methods lack robustness in challenging conditions where visual input is poor.

## Method

- The paper introduces **Lie Events** as a novel input representation for ==Neural Displacement Priors (NDPs)==, generalizing event-based sensing to the Special Euclidean Group SE(3).
- Events are triggered when the geodesic distance of IMU pre-integration on SE(3) exceeds a threshold, with 6D =="Lie polarities"== characterizing the direction of change.
- These Lie Events are converted into fixed-size event stacks and fed into standard ==1D convolutional ResNet architectures==, replacing raw IMU inputs, to predict displacement and covariance for ==EKF fusion==.

## Results

- The method improved Absolute Translation Error (ATE) by up to **21%** on NN-only evaluations and **12%** on EKF-fused evaluations compared to baseline TLIO on various datasets.
- It consistently outperformed standard RoNIN baselines, even when trained on only **50%** of the data, demonstrating improved data efficiency and generalization.
- The approach showed remarkable robustness to varying IMU measurement rates (down to **20Hz**), degrading gracefully unlike baselines that experienced significant performance drops.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Lie Events, generated by tracking geodesic distance on SE(3) and defining 6D Lie polarities, inherently canonicalize IMU data with respect to motion speed and sampling rate variations.
> - This canonicalization reduces data variability, enabling neural networks to learn motion patterns more efficiently and generalize better across diverse trajectories and sensor configurations.
> - Regularizing the learning process by adding noise to Lie polarities during training is crucial for robust performance when the neural network's output is fused with a Kalman filter.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Localization and navigation systems are fundamental components of modern technologies, underpinning applications ranging from Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR) to autonomous driving and mobile robotics. Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) has become a standard approach, combining Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data (accelerometer and gyroscope) with visual observations from a camera to estimate motion and correct for IMU integration drift.

However, VIO's reliability is inherently tied to the quality of visual input. In challenging lighting conditions, feature-poor environments, or during high-speed motion, camera frames can become unreliable, leading to degraded localization performance. This limitation has spurred interest in Inertial Odometry (IO) methods that primarily rely on IMU data.

A promising recent development in IMU-only IO is the use of Neural Displacement Priors (NDPs). NDPs are neural networks that learn to map raw IMU measurements over a time window to displacement and associated uncertainty (covariance) terms. These learned priors can then be seamlessly fused with traditional filtering techniques, such as Extended Kalman Filters (EKFs), to correct for the inherent drift in IMU integration. Surprisingly, NDPs have demonstrated drift-correcting capabilities comparable to visual observations, leading to a resurgence of purely IMU-based odometry.

Despite their potential, a significant challenge for NDPs is their lack of generalization. Raw IMU data exhibits high variability due to factors like IMU mount orientation, motion direction, and especially, diverse motion patterns (e.g., varying gaits or speeds). While methods exist to address orientation variability (e.g., data augmentation, consistency losses, equivariance), effectively handling the variability introduced by different motion patterns has remained an elusive and open challenge. Existing NDPs often struggle to perform robustly when encountering trajectories or speeds different from those seen during training.

This paper directly addresses this crucial generalization problem. It proposes a novel approach to preprocess IMU data, creating "Lie Events" that are inherently robust to changes in IMU sampling rates and trajectory profiles. By transforming raw, variable IMU data into a more canonicalized representation before feeding it to NDPs, this work aims to enhance the generalizability and robustness of existing learning-based inertial odometry systems. It bridges sophisticated geometric theory (Lie groups) with deep learning, drawing a clear parallel to the success of event-based cameras in overcoming limitations of traditional frame-based vision, and extends this philosophy to the domain of inertial sensing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the generalization and robustness of Neural Displacement Priors (NDPs) used in Inertial Odometry (IO). The authors aim to achieve this by introducing a new input representation called "Lie Events," which can effectively canonicalize IMU data with respect to motion variations and sampling rate changes.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations of current NDP-based IO systems:

- **Lack of Generalization to Motion Variability:** Traditional NDPs, which process raw IMU data, struggle to generalize across different motion patterns, such as varying gaits or speeds. This is because raw IMU measurements are highly sensitive to how motion is executed, leading to significant data variability that the neural network must learn to ignore or account for. While orientation variability has been addressed in prior work, variations in motion profiles remain a major bottleneck for robust deployment in diverse real-world scenarios.
- **Sensitivity to Sampling Rate Changes:** IMU data can be sampled at different rates depending on the device or application. Current NDPs are typically trained on fixed-rate inputs, and their performance degrades when encountering IMU data from different sampling frequencies. This limits their applicability and requires extensive data augmentation or retraining for different sensor configurations.
- **Leveraging Event-Based Sensing Principles:** The authors are motivated by the success of event-based cameras in computer vision, which adapt their sampling rate to scene dynamics by triggering "events" only when significant changes occur. They hypothesize that applying a similar dynamic, change-driven sampling paradigm to IMU data, particularly within the framework of Lie Groups, could yield a representation that inherently possesses favorable invariances to motion variability and sampling rate changes.
- **Compatibility with Existing Methods:** A key motivation is to achieve these improvements with "minimal preprocessing," ensuring that the proposed Lie Events can be readily integrated with and enhance the performance of existing, off-the-shelf NDP architectures without requiring major architectural overhauls.
- **Broader Applicability:** The authors envision that the generalization of event-based sampling to Lie Groups could extend beyond IMUs and cameras, opening new avenues for robust and efficient sensing in various domains.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide a geometrically principled, dynamically sampled input to NDPs that inherently reduces data variability related to motion and rate, thereby making the learning process more efficient and the resulting odometry system significantly more robust and generalizable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in replacing the traditional fixed-rate raw IMU data inputs to Neural Displacement Priors (NDPs) with a novel representation called "Lie Events." This methodology is inspired by event-based vision and generalizes its core concepts to the Special Euclidean Group SE(3), which represents 3D rigid body transformations (position and orientation).

#### 3.1. Generalizing Event-Based Sampling to Lie Groups

Traditional event cameras trigger an event when the change in log-intensity at a pixel exceeds a threshold (level-crossing). The authors generalize this to IMU data by:

- **Reference Signal (x(t)):** Instead of pixel intensity, the reference signal `x(t)` is defined as the IMU pre-integration over time. Pre-integration involves forward-Euler integrating bias-corrected and gravity-compensated accelerometer (`$\hat{a}$`) and gyroscope (`$\hat{\omega}$`) measurements. This yields continuous estimates of relative pose `(˜R(t), ˜t(t))` which naturally reside in SE(3).
- **On-Manifold Event Triggering:** An event is triggered at time `$\tau_j$` when the *geodesic distance* between the current pre-integrated pose `x(t)` and the last reference pose `$x_{ref,j-1}$` (the pose at the previous event) exceeds a predefined threshold `$\theta$`. This geodesic distance is measured by mapping the relative pose `$x^{-1}_{ref,j-1}x(t)$` to the Lie algebra `se(3)` via the `Log` map and taking its norm. This is a generalization of level-crossing from 1D signals to a 6D manifold.
- **Lie Polarities (p($\tau_j$)):** Unlike binary polarities in 1D event cameras (`{-1, 1}`), Lie polarities are introduced as normalized elements in the `se(3)` Lie algebra. Specifically, `$p(\tau_j)$` is the normalized vector `Log(x^{-1}_{ref,j-1}x_{ref,j}) / ||Log(x^{-1}_{ref,j-1}x_{ref,j})||$`. These 6D unit vectors characterize the *direction* of significant pre-integration change within the Lie algebra, providing richer information than simple binary signs. The reference pose is then recursively updated as `$x_{ref,j} = x_{ref,j-1}Exp(\theta p(\tau_j))$`.

#### 3.2. Canonicalization Property

A key theoretical contribution is proving that this event generation scheme inherently canonicalizes the IMU data with respect to motion variations. If the IMU trajectory `$T(t)$` can be expressed as a re-parametrization of an underlying canonical path `$T^*(s)$` (i.e., `$T(t) = T^*(\phi(t))$` where `$\phi(t)$` is a time parametrization function representing speed variations), then:
- The generated event timestamps `E = ($\tau_1, ..., \tau_M$)` are simply the inverse mapping of the canonical event timestamps `$E^* = (\sigma^*_1, ..., \sigma^*_M)$` derived from the path (`$\tau_j = \phi^{-1}(\sigma^*_j)$`).
- Crucially, the Lie polarities `$p(\tau_j)$` are identical to the canonical polarities `$p^*(\sigma^*_j)$`, meaning they depend *only* on the underlying path and are *completely independent* of the speed profile `$\phi(t)$`.

This property ensures that the Lie Events capture the intrinsic geometric progression of the motion, rather than being perturbed by how fast or slow that motion is executed. This significantly reduces the data variability that NDPs traditionally struggle with, thereby enhancing their generalizability.

#### 3.3. Network Inputs and Compatibility

- **IMU Preprocessing:** Before event generation, raw IMU measurements are gravity-compensated and bias-corrected using estimates (from an EKF during testing, or ground truth with noise during training). This creates `$\hat{\omega}(t_i)$` and `$\hat{a}(t_i)$`.
- **Event Generation (Continuous Signal):** The discrete pre-integrated samples are interpolated to form a continuous signal `$x(t)$` using geodesic paths on SE(3) (via `Log` and `Exp` maps). Events (`$\tau_j$`, `$p(\tau_j)$`) are then extracted. The instantaneous IMU measurements at these event timestamps (`$\hat{\omega}(\tau_j)$`, `$\hat{a}(\tau_j)$`) are linearly interpolated from the original discrete IMU stream.
- **Event Stacks for Fixed-Size Input:** Since the number of generated Lie Events (`M`) is variable and neural networks typically require fixed-size inputs, the authors adapt the "event stack" concept from event-based vision. The Lie Events (`$\hat{\omega}(E)$, $\hat{a}(E)$, $p(E)$`) are converted into fixed-size tensors (`$E_{a,\omega}$`, `$E_p$`) of shape `B x 6` (where B is typically 200, representing bins). This involves summing/normalizing event data into bins corresponding to linearly scaled event indices. Critically, raw time stamps are *not* fed to the network to maintain the canonicalization property.
- **NDP Architecture:** The Lie Event inputs (`$E_{a,\omega}$` and `$E_p$`, concatenated to `B x 12`) are fed into a standard 1-D convolutional ResNet-18 architecture, similar to existing NDPs like TLIO and RoNIN. The network then predicts displacement (`d`) and its covariance (`$\Sigma$`), which can be fused with an EKF.

In summary, the methodology presents a geometrically rigorous and empirically validated approach to transform highly variable IMU data into a canonicalized, event-based representation. This novel input allows existing neural networks to learn motion patterns more effectively, independent of execution speed or sampling rate, thereby significantly improving the robustness and generalizability of inertial odometry.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments to validate their approach, applying it to two prominent Neural Displacement Prior (NDP) frameworks: TLIO and RoNIN, and evaluating on multiple public datasets.

#### 4.1. Validation of Canonicalization (Toy Example)

- Using synthetic trajectories with varying speed profiles (`$\phi_\alpha(t)$`), the authors demonstrate that event timestamps generated from pre-integrations, when mapped back by the inverse time parametrization, show significantly lower Chamfer distance to canonical event timestamps (errors as low as 1.15%).
- This empirical validation supports the theoretical claim (Theorem 1) that Lie Events are indeed largely invariant to speed variations, especially when pre-integration is accurate (using ground truth further reduced errors to ~0.1%).

#### 4.2. Application to TLIO (Neural Displacement Prior with EKF)

- **NN-only Evaluation (ATE*, MSE*):** When evaluating the neural network output directly (without EKF), Lie Events consistently improved Absolute Translation Error (ATE*). Specifically, ATE* was reduced by 13% on the TLIO Dataset, 11% on Aria Right, and 19% on Aria Left compared to the baseline TLIO. Mean Squared Error (MSE*) also saw reductions (17-21% on Aria, though slightly higher on TLIO).
- **EKF-fused Evaluation (ATE, Drift, RTE, AYE):** When integrated into an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), the proposed method (TLIO + events) achieved 10% higher ATE accuracy on the TLIO Dataset, 3% on Aria Right, and 12% on Aria Left compared to base TLIO. It also outperformed base TLIO in terms of translational drift.
- **Comparison to Rate Augmentation Baselines:** The method was not trained with IMU rate augmentation, unlike some baselines (+interp, +splat), yet it still outperformed them in many metrics, particularly in out-of-distribution performance (Aria datasets).

#### 4.3. Application to RoNIN (2D Pedestrian Tracking)

- The method was applied to RoNIN, a 2D inertial navigation method, using only 50% of the training data (public split).
- Despite less training data, the proposed approach consistently outperformed the standard RoNIN baselines (ResNet, LSTM, TCN) across the RoNIN-U, RIDI-T, and OxIOD datasets in terms of ATE*.
- Notably, it even outperformed RoNIN versions trained with 100% data on RIDI-T and OxIOD.
- The results indicate that Lie Events offer a significant advantage even over methods that incorporate yaw consistency strategies (e.g., RIO's joint training or test-time training) on some datasets (e.g., OxIOD).

#### 4.4. Rate Sensitivity Study

- The method demonstrates remarkable robustness to varying IMU measurement rates (down to 20Hz from 200/800/1000Hz).
- Unlike baselines (TLIO, +interp, +splat) which experienced heavy degradation at lower rates (especially +splat, which failed dramatically), "TLIO + events" degraded gracefully and remained stable, consistently outperforming other methods. This strongly validates the canonicalization property with respect to sampling rate variations.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Sensitivity

- **Reference Signal & Manifold:** Using the full SE(3) pose as the reference signal for event generation yielded the best performance (lowest ATE/ATE*), confirming the importance of modeling the complete 6-DoF motion on the appropriate Lie group. Simpler manifolds like R3 (position only) or SO(3) x R3 performed worse.
- **Polarity Noise:** Adding uniform noise to Lie polarities during training was crucial. Without it, the network overfit to training trajectories, resulting in very low ATE* (NN-only) but drastically high ATE when deployed with the EKF (6.08m vs. 1.282m). This highlights the importance of regularizing the learning process for real-world filter fusion.
- **Hyperparameters:** Optimal contrast threshold `$\theta = 0.01$`, initial velocity noise range `$r_{v0} = 0.5$`, and polarity noise range `$r_p = 0.75$` (chosen as `0.5` for better generalizability across datasets) were identified.

#### 4.6. Runtime, Robustness, and Generalization

- **Runtime:** Event generation (including pre-integration) is highly efficient, taking only 76 μs for 1 second of data, making it suitable for real-time applications (20 Hz measurement updates).
- **Robustness to Pre-integration Accuracy:** The method shows graceful degradation with gravity and initial velocity noise in pre-integration, and low sensitivity to bias noise, outperforming baselines even under such perturbations.
- **Generalization to Limited Data & Out-of-Distribution:** The method maintained relatively stable performance even when trained on as little as 10% of the RoNIN training data, underlining its data efficiency and generalization capabilities. When tested on out-of-distribution Aria datasets, it exhibited lower MSE at higher speeds compared to baselines, demonstrating improved generalization to varying speeds.

In summary, the main findings consistently demonstrate that Lie Events provide a robust, canonicalized input representation for NDPs, leading to significant performance improvements in terms of accuracy, robustness to varying rates, and generalization capabilities across diverse datasets and motion profiles, without requiring extensive data augmentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of inertial odometry, with profound implications for robotic perception, autonomous systems, and AR/VR applications. Its key contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Overcoming a Critical Generalization Barrier:** The most significant impact of this work is its successful mitigation of a long-standing challenge in learning-based inertial odometry: the lack of generalization to diverse motion patterns and IMU sampling rates. By demonstrating that Lie Events canonicalize IMU data with respect to these variations, the authors provide a powerful solution that makes NDPs far more robust and applicable in real-world, unconstrained environments. This means systems can be trained once and perform reliably across different users, speeds, and devices.
- **Novel Event-Based Sensing Paradigm:** The paper introduces a groundbreaking concept by generalizing event-based sensing from 1D signals (like pixel intensities in cameras) to high-dimensional Lie Groups, specifically SE(3). This conceptual leap, including the formalization of "Lie polarities" and on-manifold level-crossing, is a fundamental contribution to sensing theory. It opens up an entirely new avenue for designing robust and efficient sensor processing pipelines for a wide range of modalities beyond vision.
- **Enhanced Performance for Existing Methods:** The research demonstrates that Lie Events can be readily integrated as a minimal preprocessing step for existing, off-the-shelf NDP architectures (like TLIO and RoNIN). This plug-and-play compatibility means that current state-of-the-art IMU odometry systems can immediately benefit from the proposed canonicalization, achieving consistent error reductions (up to 21% in ATE) without requiring complex architectural redesigns.
- **Improved Robustness to Sensor Imperfections:** The demonstrated robustness to varying IMU sampling rates and the graceful degradation under noisy pre-integration estimates are crucial for practical deployment. This makes the system more resilient to differences in sensor hardware or the performance of upstream estimation filters.
- **Efficiency and Real-time Operation:** The event generation process is computationally very efficient, enabling real-time operation. This ensures that the benefits of improved robustness do not come at the cost of increased latency or processing demands, which is vital for real-world robotic and AR/VR applications.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper explicitly conjectures that "many more sensors than IMUs or cameras can benefit from an event-based sampling paradigm." This bold statement highlights the potential for this work to inspire a new wave of research into event-based processing for diverse sensor types (e.g., tactile sensors, LiDAR, radar), leading to more dynamic, robust, and efficient perception systems across various domains. The open-sourcing of the code will facilitate this future exploration.

In conclusion, "Neural Inertial Odometry from Lie Events" offers a conceptually elegant and empirically powerful solution to a critical problem in robust localization. By merging advanced geometric theory with practical deep learning applications, it not only significantly pushes the boundaries of inertial odometry but also lays theoretical groundwork for a broader revolution in event-driven sensing and robust AI systems.
