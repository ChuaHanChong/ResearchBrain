# Competition and Attraction Improve Model Fusion

> [!summary] Summary
> The M2N2 framework from Sakana AI refines model fusion by incorporating dynamic merging boundaries, implicit fitness sharing, and attraction-based parent selection within an evolutionary algorithm. This method improves the combination of pre-trained LLMs and diffusion models, and can even evolve models from an initial state, demonstrating comparable performance to direct weight optimization but with higher computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing model merging techniques often require manual partitioning of model parameters into fixed groups (e.g., layers), which limits the exploration of potential parameter combinations.
- Preserving diversity in evolutionary algorithms for high-dimensional deep learning models is challenging due to the difficulty in defining meaningful distance metrics for complex parameter spaces.
- Inefficient parent selection in evolutionary algorithms can hinder performance, particularly when crossover operations like model merging are computationally expensive.

## Method

- M2N2 introduces ==dynamically evolved split-points== that define where two models' parameters are merged, allowing for flexible exploration of merging boundaries.
- It implements a ==competition-based mechanism== for diversity preservation, where a modified fitness function encourages models to find 'uncontested resources' (data points other models struggle with) via ==implicit fitness sharing==.
- A heuristic-based ==attraction score== guides parent selection, prioritizing the fusion of models that exhibit complementary strengths on different data points.

## Results

- M2N2 successfully evolved MNIST classifiers from scratch, achieving performance comparable to CMA-ES while being significantly more computationally efficient (**1 hour** vs. **15 hours**).
- When merging pre-trained LLMs, M2N2 achieved the highest average score of **63.49%** on combined math and agentic benchmarks, surpassing individual models and other merging baselines.
- For diffusion models, M2N2 produced images with superior quality (NCS, FID) and demonstrated robust preservation of capabilities, including emergent cross-lingual understanding with a CLIP cosine-similarity of **0.787**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically evolving merging boundaries significantly enhances the flexibility and effectiveness of model fusion, moving beyond static architectural divisions.
> - Implicit fitness sharing (inspired by natural resource competition) maintains diversity in high-dimensional model populations, while heuristic-driven parent selection based on complementary performance improves offspring efficiency and quality.
> - Model merging, when enhanced by these evolutionary principles, can serve as a viable gradient-free mechanism for training deep learning models entirely from scratch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of **model merging** and **evolutionary computation**, specifically focusing on advanced **diversity preservation mechanisms** in genetic algorithms.

Model merging has rapidly emerged as a powerful technique in machine learning, particularly with the proliferation of open-source generative models like LLMs and diffusion models. Practitioners frequently fine-tune these base models for specialized tasks, leading to a vast ecosystem of "expert" models. The ability to integrate the specialized knowledge from multiple such models into a single, consolidated model offers significant advantages, reducing memory footprint, improving versatility, and enabling multi-task capabilities without retraining from scratch. Early model merging efforts were often manual, relying on intuitive coefficient adjustments (e.g., linear or spherical linear interpolation for diffusion models like Stable Diffusion). However, the complexity and scale of modern models quickly necessitated automated approaches.

This is where evolutionary algorithms (EAs) entered the scene. Previous work (e.g., Akiba et al., 2024; Kuroki et al., 2024) successfully applied EAs like CMA-ES to automate the search for optimal mixing coefficients to combine pre-trained models. While these methods streamlined the process, a critical limitation remained: the need for **manual partitioning of model parameters into fixed groups** (e.g., layers) before merging. This constraint restricted the exploration of potential parameter combinations, hindering performance and versatility.

"Competition and Attraction Improve Model Fusion" directly addresses this limitation. By proposing **Model Merging of Natural Niches (M2N2)**, the authors aim to introduce dynamic merging boundaries, allowing for a more flexible and comprehensive exploration of the parameter space. This represents a significant advancement over previous model merging techniques that relied on predefined architectural divisions.

Furthermore, the paper revitalizes concepts from classical evolutionary computation, specifically **diversity preservation** and **mate selection**. In genetic algorithms, maintaining diversity within a population is crucial to prevent premature convergence and explore a broader solution space. While methods like crowding and explicit fitness sharing have been studied for decades, their application to high-dimensional deep learning models and tasks remains challenging due to the difficulty of defining meaningful distance metrics. M2N2 reintroduces **implicit fitness sharing**, inspired by natural competition for resources, as a scalable solution for modern AI. Simultaneously, by introducing a **heuristic-based attraction metric** for parent selection, the authors highlight an underexplored aspect of EAs that becomes increasingly critical as crossover operations (like model merging) grow computationally expensive.

Thus, this work pushes the boundaries of model merging by making it more adaptive and efficient, while also demonstrating the continued relevance and potential of classic evolutionary principles when re-contextualized for contemporary AI challenges. It bridges the gap between traditional gradient-based deep learning and biologically inspired optimization, offering a gradient-free alternative with unique advantages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **overcome the limitations of existing model merging techniques**, specifically the requirement for manually partitioning model parameters into fixed groups. The authors aim to develop a more flexible and robust model fusion method that can explore a broader range of parameter combinations and achieve superior performance.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Enhancing Flexibility in Model Merging:** Current methods restrict the search for optimal merging parameters by fixing the boundaries at which models are combined (e.g., at layer boundaries). This significantly limits the potential combinations and the ability to discover emergent synergies between different model components. M2N2 seeks to make these boundaries dynamic and part of the evolutionary optimization process.
2. **Improving Performance and Efficiency:** By allowing dynamic merging boundaries and more effective exploration of the parameter space, the authors anticipate improved performance in merged models. Additionally, the efficiency of the evolutionary search is a critical consideration, especially when dealing with computationally intensive model merging operations.
3. **Leveraging Evolutionary Principles for Modern AI:** The authors are motivated to apply and adapt fundamental evolutionary concepts, such as diversity preservation and mate selection, to the context of complex deep learning models. They argue that traditional diversity metrics are hard to define for high-dimensional AI tasks, thus natural resource competition (implicit fitness sharing) offers a more elegant solution. Similarly, as model merging becomes a costly "crossover" operation, intelligent "mate selection" becomes crucial.
4. **Enabling New Capabilities for Model Merging:** Beyond just merging pre-trained models, the authors aim to demonstrate that their improved model merging technique can be powerful enough to "evolve models entirely from scratch," a novel application of model merging that could open new avenues for model development. They also seek to show its robustness in preserving crucial model capabilities without requiring access to original training data or explicit optimization.

In essence, the motivation is to make model merging a more powerful, flexible, and biologically inspired tool for AI development, capable of addressing current limitations and unlocking new possibilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology is **Model Merging of Natural Niches (M2N2)**, an evolutionary algorithm incorporating three novel features to address the limitations of previous model merging techniques:

1. **Evolving the Merging Boundaries (Dynamic Split Points):**
- **Problem Addressed:** Existing methods merge models by partitioning parameters into fixed groups (e.g., layers) and then optimizing mixing coefficients for these groups. This pre-defined structure constrains the search space.
- **M2N2's Approach:** M2N2 abandons fixed boundaries. Instead, it maintains an archive of models. In each step, two models (A and B) are randomly selected from the archive. Two parameters, a mixing ratio (`w_m`) and a **split-point (`w_s`)**, are sampled. The split-point dynamically divides the models' parameters into two sub-arrays.
- **Merging Mechanism:** The merging function uses Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) (`f_t`) to combine the corresponding sub-arrays of models A and B. Specifically, `f_{w_m}` is applied to the parameters *before* `w_s`, and `f_{1-w_m}` is applied to the parameters *after* `w_s`. The `concat` operation then rejoins these two merged parts. This allows M2N2 to progressively explore a broader set of boundaries and coefficients as evolution proceeds, introducing complexity gradually and efficiently.

2. **Managing Diversity (Competition for Resources via Modified Optimization Goal):**
- **Problem Addressed:** Diversity preservation is crucial in GAs to prevent premature convergence, especially with crossover operations. However, defining effective diversity metrics for complex, high-dimensional AI models is challenging.
- **M2N2's Approach:** Inspired by nature, M2N2 promotes diversity through "competition for limited resources." The "resources" are individual data point scores that contribute to a model's fitness.
- **Modified Fitness Function (Equation 3):** The standard optimization goal (sum of scores `s(x_j | θ)` across examples `j`) is modified. For each data point `x_j`, the total fitness a population can extract is limited by a "capacity" `c_j`. A candidate model's score `s(x_j | θ)` is divided by `z_j`, which is the *sum of scores for that data point across all models in the population*. This `s(x_j | θ) / (z_j + ε)` term effectively limits the reward any single data point can provide, incentivizing models to find "uncontested resources" (i.e., data points that other models struggle with). This is an elegant form of implicit fitness sharing, avoiding the need for explicit, hand-engineered diversity metrics.

3. **Sampling Parents via Attraction (Heuristic-Based Mate Selection):**
- **Problem Addressed:** In EAs with computationally expensive crossover operations (like model merging), random or purely fitness-based parent selection can be inefficient. Identifying complementary parents can significantly improve the quality of offspring.
- **M2N2's Approach:** M2N2 introduces a heuristic for "mate selection." The first parent (`θ_A`) is sampled based on its modified fitness score (Equation 3). The second parent (`θ_B`) is then sampled based on an "attraction score" (`g(θ_A, θ_B)`) tailored for `θ_A`.
- **Attraction Score (Equation 4):** This heuristic prioritizes models `θ_B` that perform well on data points where `θ_A` performs poorly (`max(s(x_j | θ_B) - s(x_j | θ_A), 0)`). It also gives extra preference to data points with high capacity (`c_j`) and low competition (`z_j`). This mechanism encourages the fusion of complementary strengths, aiming for offspring that combine the best aspects of both parents.

**Overall Approach:** M2N2 maintains an archive of models. In each generation, it selects two parents using the attraction heuristic, merges them with dynamically sampled split points and mixing coefficients, and then evaluates the new merged model. If this new model outperforms the worst individual in the archive (according to the modified fitness function), it replaces it, continuously evolving a diverse and high-performing population. The method is gradient-free, relying entirely on evolutionary search and fitness evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and versatility of M2N2 across different tasks and model scales.

1. **Evolving MNIST Classifiers from Scratch:**
- M2N2 successfully evolved two-layer feedforward neural networks for MNIST classification *from scratch*.
- It achieved performance comparable to CMA-ES, a popular evolutionary algorithm that directly optimizes model weights (a cubic computational cost `O(n^3)`), but M2N2 was significantly more computationally efficient (1 hour vs. 15 hours for 10 runs). This is a novel and significant result, demonstrating for the first time that model merging can be used as a primary training mechanism.
- **Ablation Studies (MNIST from Scratch):** The competition mechanism was crucial. Genetic Algorithms (GA) without diversity preservation converged prematurely to inferior solutions, especially with crossover. MAP-Elites, while maintaining diversity, did so with less effective metrics, leading to suboptimal coverage. M2N2, through implicit fitness sharing, maintained high training coverage and a healthy evolution of population diversity (initial rise in entropy followed by a gradual decline of low-performers). Smaller archive sizes or lower competition (smaller `α` in modified fitness) led to faster, but inferior, convergence. The split-point and attraction had minimal impact when evolving from scratch, suggesting their importance scales with model complexity and pre-training.

2. **Merging Pre-trained MNIST Classifiers:**
- When starting from pre-trained models (one on digits 0-4, another on 5-9), M2N2 significantly outperformed baselines.
- **Ablation Studies (MNIST Pre-trained):** In this scenario, the **split-point mechanism became crucial** for performance, highlighting its value when combining specialized knowledge. The **attraction heuristic also significantly improved performance** throughout the training process. GA performed poorly, suffering from low average accuracy and high variance due to its sensitivity to initial merges and lack of diversity.

3. **Combining LLMs with Math and Agentic Skills:**
- M2N2 was applied to merge a math specialist LLM (WizardMath-7B-V1.0) and an agentic skills specialist LLM (AgentEvol-7B) to create a model proficient in both GSM8k (math) and WebShop (web shopping) benchmarks.
- M2N2 achieved the highest average score (63.49%) compared to individual models and other merging baselines, including CMA-ES (49.06%), GA (60.05%), and MAP-Elites (60.14%).
- **Ablation Studies:** Both the attraction mechanism and the split-point were critical for performance in this complex, large-scale scenario, with the split-point showing slightly more importance. The findings on diversity preservation (high training coverage, healthy entropy evolution) from MNIST generalized to LLMs.
- This experiment highlights M2N2's ability to combine vastly different skills and its gradient-free nature, which prevents catastrophic forgetting and avoids requiring access to original training data.

4. **Merging Diffusion-Based Image Generation Models:**
- M2N2 merged multiple SDXL variants (JSDXL, SDXL 1.0, SDXL-DPO, Juggernaut-XL-v9) to create a model that excels in image quality and retains JSDXL's Japanese language understanding.
- The merged model achieved superior Normalized CLIP Similarity (NCS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores on the test set compared to all seed models and the CMA-ES baseline, even though CMA-ES was trained to minimize FID.
- **Qualitative Results:** The M2N2-merged model generated more photorealistic images with enhanced semantic understanding of prompts, effectively combining the strengths of diverse seed models.
- **Language Understanding:** Remarkably, the model retained strong English language understanding capabilities despite being optimized *exclusively* with Japanese captions. This emergent bilingual ability demonstrates M2N2's robustness against catastrophic forgetting and its potential as a transfer learning mechanism. Quantitatively, M2N2 achieved significantly higher cross-lingual consistency (CLIP cosine-similarity of 0.787) compared to seed models (0.567-0.701).

In summary, the main findings confirm that M2N2's key components (dynamic split points, competition for diversity, and attraction heuristic) significantly improve model merging. It demonstrates that model merging can be a viable method for training models from scratch and scales effectively to complex, state-of-the-art LLM and image generation tasks, achieving superior performance and preserving capabilities in a gradient-free manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Competition and Attraction Improve Model Fusion" carries significant implications and potential impact across several domains of artificial intelligence:

1. **Advancement in Model Merging:** M2N2 represents a substantial leap forward in the field of model merging. By introducing dynamic merging boundaries, it removes a critical manual constraint, enabling a far more exhaustive and effective exploration of the parameter space. This flexibility is crucial for combining complex, high-dimensional models like LLMs and diffusion models, leading to merged models with superior capabilities that might not be achievable with fixed merging strategies.
2. **Gradient-Free Model Development from Scratch:** The demonstration that M2N2 can evolve models *entirely from scratch* on tasks like MNIST is a groundbreaking contribution. This challenges the prevailing paradigm of gradient-based optimization for deep learning model training and opens up new avenues for model development where traditional backpropagation might be challenging or inefficient. This could be particularly impactful for hardware-constrained environments or neuromorphic computing.
3. **Robust Transfer Learning and Catastrophic Forgetting Mitigation:** The ability of M2N2 to combine specialized skills (e.g., math and agentic skills in LLMs) and preserve capabilities (e.g., English understanding in a Japanese-optimized diffusion model) without catastrophic forgetting is highly significant. Traditional fine-tuning often leads to forgetting previously learned knowledge. Model merging offers a powerful alternative for integrating diverse functionalities into a single model without extensive retraining or access to original training data, making it invaluable for adapting models to new tasks while retaining broad general capabilities.
4. **Revitalizing Evolutionary Algorithms for Modern AI:** The paper successfully re-contextualizes and demonstrates the power of classic evolutionary concepts like implicit fitness sharing for diversity preservation and heuristic-based mate selection. These mechanisms, often underutilized in the context of large-scale AI, prove to be highly effective for high-dimensional models where explicit distance metrics are hard to define. This work could inspire renewed interest and research in these areas, fostering a more biologically informed approach to AI optimization.
5. **Computational Efficiency for Model Combination:** While model merging itself can be computationally intensive, M2N2 offers benefits such as being gradient-free, which reduces memory footprint by avoiding gradient computations. Its efficiency compared to direct weight optimization (e.g., CMA-ES on MNIST from scratch) highlights its practical applicability for larger models. The smart parent selection via attraction also contributes to overall efficiency by guiding the search towards more promising combinations.
6. **Enabling Multi-modal and Multi-skill AI:** The successful merging of LLMs for distinct tasks (math and web shopping) and diffusion models for diverse linguistic and aesthetic capabilities showcases M2N2's potential to create highly versatile, multi-modal, and multi-skill AI agents. This capability is increasingly important as AI systems are expected to perform a wider range of tasks in complex, real-world environments.
7. **Impact on Open-Source AI Ecosystem:** With the rapid growth of specialized open-source models, M2N2 provides a powerful tool for practitioners to consolidate and enhance these models without needing to rebuild or retrain from scratch. This can democratize access to advanced AI capabilities and foster further innovation within the open-source community, particularly for tasks involving subjective goals where manual fine-tuning is impractical.

In conclusion, "Competition and Attraction Improve Model Fusion" offers a sophisticated, nature-inspired approach to model merging that pushes the boundaries of AI model development. Its novel features, robust performance across diverse tasks, and contributions to evolutionary computation position it as a foundational work with the potential to significantly influence the future of how AI models are designed, combined, and evolved.
