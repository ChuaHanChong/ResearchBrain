# Information Gain-based Policy Optimization: A Simple and Effective Approach for Multi-Turn Search Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO) introduces an intrinsic, turn-level reward mechanism for training multi-turn LLM search agents, which addresses the limitations of sparse outcome-based rewards. The framework achieved an average F1 score of 60.2 across seven QA benchmarks, outperforming leading baselines by 6.3 points while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Sparse outcome-based rewards in multi-turn agent training lead to 'advantage collapse' when rollouts yield identical final answers, providing no gradient signal.
- Lack of fine-grained credit assignment for individual intermediate steps (reasoning, tool calls) in long multi-turn trajectories, hindering effective learning.
- Poor sample efficiency, as an entire multi-turn trajectory yields only a single, sparse learning signal at its conclusion.

## Method

- The Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO) framework defines an intrinsic, ==turn-level reward== as the marginal increase in the policy's log-probability of generating the ground-truth answer at each interaction step.
- An efficient vectorized implementation is used to compute all turn-level information gain rewards in a single forward pass, minimizing computational overhead.
- IGPO integrates these turn-level information gain rewards with traditional outcome-based rewards into a group-relative policy optimization framework, using turn-level discounted returns for dense supervision.

## Results

- IGPO achieved the highest average F1 score of 60.2 across seven diverse QA benchmarks (including NQ, HotpotQA, PopQA), surpassing the best baseline (DeepResearcher) by 6.3 F1 points.
- The framework demonstrated improved learning dynamics and higher F1 scores, with more pronounced improvements for smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B**-Instruct saw a 16.6 F1 point increase over GRPO).
- Information gain reward computation introduced negligible overhead, adding less than **0.02%** to the total end-to-end training step latency, maintaining training speed comparable to standard GRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Turn-level information gain rewards intrinsically guide the policy towards more confident and grounded reasoning by reducing the ground-truth entropy, preventing error accumulation over turns.
> - The combination of dense, intrinsic information gain rewards and sparse, final outcome rewards is complementary, leading to superior performance compared to using either reward type in isolation.
> - The approach effectively addresses the 'advantage collapse' problem by providing varying turn-level signals, even when final outcomes are identical within a training group.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language model (LLM)-based agents capable of interacting with external environments through tool use represents a significant area of research aimed at building autonomous intelligent systems. Agentic search, where LLMs utilize tools like web search engines to acquire knowledge and refine reasoning, is a prominent application.

Current approaches to equipping LLMs with agentic search capabilities have evolved from prompt-based workflows, which often exhibited poor generalization, to supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) methods. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-style methods have become a dominant paradigm in training agentic LLMs. These methods typically rely on *outcome-based rewards*, which are provided only upon the generation of a final answer, usually based on its correctness against a ground truth.

However, the prevailing reliance on sparse, outcome-based rewards in multi-turn agentic settings introduces several challenges:
1. **Advantage Collapse:** When rollouts within a training group yield identical final answers (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), their rewards are identical, leading to zero group-relative advantages and consequently, no useful gradient signal for policy updates.
2. **Lack of Fine-grained Credit Assignment:** In long multi-turn trajectories, discerning the contribution of individual intermediate steps (reasoning, tool calls) to the final outcome is difficult, hindering effective learning from complex sequences of actions.
3. **Poor Sample Efficiency:** The sparsity of the reward signal means that an entire multi-turn trajectory yields only a single learning signal, wasting potentially dense information embedded in intermediate interactions.

Prior attempts to address reward sparsity have included *process-level rewards* (also known as step-wise or intermediate rewards). Some approaches leverage external oracle knowledge or separate reward models to judge intermediate steps, which can be costly and prone to bias. Others employ Monte Carlo simulations to estimate step values, which often suffer from high variance and require extensive sampling. These methods face limitations in scalability, stability, and simplicity.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing an intrinsic, computationally efficient, and stable process reward mechanism. It aims to overcome the limitations of sparse outcome rewards and the drawbacks of existing process-reward designs, thereby enhancing the performance and sample efficiency of multi-turn LLM-based search agents. The work particularly addresses the problem of error accumulation in multi-turn reasoning, providing a theoretical justification for how dense, turn-level signals can lead to more confident and successful reasoning trajectories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient reinforcement learning framework, termed Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO), for training multi-turn LLM-based search agents. This framework aims to provide dense and intrinsic supervision to overcome the inherent limitations of sparse outcome-based rewards prevalent in existing methods.

The motivation for IGPO stems from the detailed analysis of three critical issues exacerbated by reward sparsity in multi-turn agentic settings:

1. **Addressing Advantage Collapse:** In existing GRPO-style methods, if all sampled rollouts for a given query produce the same outcome (e.g., all correct or all incorrect), the group-relative advantage, computed by normalizing rewards within the group, becomes zero. This eliminates any gradient signal, effectively halting learning for that specific training batch. The authors provide empirical evidence (Figure 1) showing a substantial proportion of training iterations suffer from this issue, particularly for smaller models attempting more complex queries. The objective is to provide turn-level signals that vary even when final outcomes are identical, thereby generating non-zero advantages.

2. **Enabling Fine-grained Credit Assignment:** Multi-turn tasks involve sequential decision-making where the correctness of later actions often depends on earlier ones. Outcome-only rewards fail to differentiate between useful and erroneous intermediate steps. A correct intermediate action might be negated by a subsequent error, or an early error might be compensated by later success, blurring the true contribution of each turn. The motivation is to assign credit or blame at the individual turn level, providing specific feedback that guides the agent to identify and reinforce productive reasoning paths.

3. **Improving Sample Efficiency:** With outcome-based rewards, an entire, potentially long, multi-turn trajectory yields only a single scalar reward at its conclusion. This results in inefficient utilization of the rich information generated during intermediate reasoning and tool interactions. The objective is to leverage this intermediate information by providing dense reward signals at each turn, making each sample contribute more effectively to policy learning and reducing the overall data requirement for training.

Furthermore, the authors are motivated to develop a process-level reward mechanism that is intrinsic and reliable, distinguishing it from prior attempts:
- **Avoiding External Reward Models/Oracle Knowledge:** Previous methods often relied on external reward models or human annotations to judge intermediate steps. This introduces costs, potential biases, and scalability challenges. IGPO aims for an intrinsically derived reward.
- **Overcoming Monte Carlo Estimation Limitations:** Approaches using Monte Carlo simulations to estimate step values often suffer from high variance unless a very large number of samples are collected, making them unstable and computationally expensive. IGPO seeks a more stable and efficient alternative.

By addressing these motivations, IGPO aims to facilitate more stable optimization, more effective credit assignment, and significantly improved sample efficiency for LLM-based agents in complex, multi-turn search environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IGPO introduces an intrinsic, dense, and computationally efficient reward mechanism to enhance policy optimization for multi-turn LLM agents. The core of the approach involves defining turn-level rewards based on "information gain" about the ground truth.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation and Agent Interaction

The agent's goal is to answer a question `q` by interacting with an external tool `E` (e.g., web search engine) over `T` turns. Each turn `t < T` consists of a `[think]` step, a `[tool call]`, and a `[tool response]`. The final turn `T` involves a `[think]` step followed by the `[answer]`. The final prediction `â` is compared to the ground-truth `a` using an F1 score.

#### 3.2. Information Gain-based Turn-Level Reward

The central concept of IGPO is to model each agent-environment interaction turn as an incremental process of acquiring information about the ground truth.
- **Definition:** At each turn `t` in a rollout `o_i`, the policy's log-probability of generating the ground-truth answer `a` is calculated, denoted as `log π_θ(a | q, o_i,≤t)`. The immediate information gain reward `r_IG_i,t` for turn `t` is defined as the marginal increase in this log-probability compared to the previous state:
 `r_IG_i,t = log π_θ(a | q, o_i,≤t) - log π_θ(a | q, o_i,≤t-1)` for `1 ≤ t < T`.
 The ground-truth answer `a` is formatted consistently with predicted answers (e.g., wrapped in `<answer>` tags preceded by a `<think>` rationale).
- **Properties:** This reward is designed to be:
 1. **Ground-truth aware:** It increases when a turn's action moves the policy closer to the correct answer's probability distribution and decreases otherwise, intrinsically derived from the model's own belief updates.
 2. **Dense supervision:** It provides a signal at every intermediate turn, addressing advantage collapse and enabling fine-grained credit assignment.
 3. **Computational efficiency:** It does not require external reward models or Monte Carlo estimation.

#### 3.3. Efficient Vectorized Implementation

To minimize the computational overhead of calculating `r_IG_i,t`, which would naively require `T` separate forward passes, a vectorized implementation is proposed.
- **Mechanism:** `T` copies of the ground-truth answer are appended to the end of the trajectory. A custom attention mask is then applied, restricting each copy's visibility to only its corresponding turn's context. This allows for simultaneous computation of `log π_θ(a | q, o_i,≤t)` for all `T` turns in a single forward pass.
- **Benefit:** This approach achieves an asymptotic speedup (approximately `T/3` for `T=10`) and improves GPU utilization, with negligible additional overhead due to the relatively small length of ground-truth answers compared to full trajectories.

#### 3.4. Policy Optimization with Turn-Level Discounted Return

IGPO combines the intrinsic information gain rewards with the traditional outcome-based rewards and integrates them into a GRPO-style policy optimization framework.
- **Reward Combination:** For each rollout `o_i`, the sequence of `T-1` information gain rewards `{r_IG_i,t}` and a final outcome reward `r_O_i` (calculated as an F1 score with a format penalty) are obtained.
- **Group-wise Normalization:** To stabilize training, both information gain rewards and outcome rewards are z-normalized *separately* within each group of rollouts.
 `r̃_i,t = (r_IG_i,t - μ_IG) / σ_IG` for `t < T`
 `r̃_i,T = (r_O_i - μ_O) / σ_O` for `t = T`
 where `μ` and `σ` are the mean and standard deviation of their respective reward types within the group.
- **Turn-Level Discounted Return:** To capture long-horizon dependencies, a discounted cumulative sum of future normalized rewards is computed for each turn `t`:
 `R̃_i,t = Σ_(k=t)^T γ^(k-t) r̃_i,k`
 where `γ ∈ (0, 1]` is the discount factor (set to 1.0 in experiments). This `R̃_i,t` serves as the dense, future-aware supervision signal for tokens generated in turn `t`.
- **Policy Optimization Objective:** The policy `π_θ` is optimized using a clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization, similar to GRPO, but replacing the rollout-level advantages with the turn-level discounted returns `R̃_i,t`. Gradients are applied only to decision tokens (reasoning, tool calls, answers), while tool responses are masked.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on seven QA benchmarks: four in-domain (NQ, TQ, HotpotQA, 2Wiki) and three out-of-domain (MusiQue, Bamboogle, PopQA).
- **Metric:** Word-level F1 score.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of baselines was used, including:
 - Prompt-based methods (CoT, CoT+RAG, Search-o1).
 - Outcome-reward RL-based methods (Search-r1-base/Instruct, R1-searcher, DeepResearcher).
 - Step-reward RL-based methods (StepSearch-base/instruct, ReasoningRAG, GiGPO).
 - General RL algorithms (PPO, Reinforce++, RLOO, GRPO, GSPO) under the same configuration.
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct was the primary backbone model, with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct used for ablation studies.
- **Tools:** Google search API.
- **Hyperparameters:** Consistent settings were maintained across all RL training methods for fair comparison (e.g., batch size 32, 16 rollouts per prompt, max 10 dialogue turns, γ=1.0).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated that IGPO consistently outperformed baselines and provided improvements in various aspects of multi-turn agent training.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Training-based methods generally superior:** All RL-based methods, regardless of reward type, achieved significantly higher F1 scores compared to prompt-based approaches, underscoring the importance of explicit policy optimization.
- **IGPO's superior performance:** IGPO achieved the highest average F1 score of 60.2 across all seven datasets, surpassing the strongest baseline (DeepResearcher at 53.9) by a margin of 6.3 points (Table 1). This indicates robust gains across both in-domain and out-of-domain settings.
- **Outperformance against standard RL algorithms:** When compared against general RL algorithms implemented under the same configuration, IGPO also achieved the highest average F1 score of 60.2, significantly outperforming GRPO (51.9), PPO (51.5), RLOO (49.7), Reinforce++ (47.3), and GSPO (52.0) (Table 2). This demonstrates the effectiveness of IGPO's dense, turn-level supervision compared to solely sparse outcome rewards.
- **Limitations of existing step-reward methods:** While some step-reward baselines showed competitive performance on specific datasets, their overall average performance still lagged behind the leading outcome-reward methods, suggesting issues with signal noise or generalizability.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

- **Complementarity of rewards:** Using only information gain (IG) turn-level rewards or only outcome rewards (F1) yielded inferior results compared to their combination (Table 3). This supports the hypothesis that turn-level intrinsic rewards and final outcome rewards play complementary roles.
- **Robustness of IG rewards:** IGPO using only IG rewards achieved performance comparable to or sometimes exceeding standard GRPO (IGPO w/ F1), suggesting that the information gain reward is robust and less susceptible to "reward hacking" often associated with unstable reward designs.
- **Greater impact on smaller models:** The performance improvements were more pronounced for the smaller Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model. IGPO improved the 3B model by +16.6 F1 points (from 32.3 to 48.9) and the 7B model by +8.3 F1 points (from 51.9 to 60.2) over standard GRPO. This suggests that smaller models, which are more susceptible to advantage collapse, benefit more from dense reward signals.
- **Improved learning dynamics:** Training curves (Figure 4) showed that IGPO consistently converged to higher F1 scores and exhibited faster, more stable learning dynamics throughout training across all datasets, confirming both higher final performance and improved training efficiency.
- **Impact of information gain basis and normalization:** Experiments in Appendix D.1 showed that using log-probability for information gain and performing separate group-wise normalization for IG rewards and outcome rewards resulted in optimal performance, emphasizing numerical stability and appropriate scaling.

#### 4.3. In-Depth Analysis

- **Ground-truth Entropy Reduction:** IGPO consistently achieved a larger reduction in ground-truth answer entropy from the initial query to the final non-answer turn (Figure 5). This indicates that IGPO's turn-level supervision effectively guided the policy towards more confident and grounded reasoning trajectories.
- **Token Efficiency:** IGPO demonstrated superior token efficiency, achieving stronger performance with fewer tokens used for gradient updates, and widening the performance gap over GRPO as training progressed (Figure 6). This validates that its dense rewards provide more informative gradients, addressing poor sample efficiency.
- **Spurious Correlations Analysis:** An analysis using a powerful teacher LLM (gemini-2.5-pro) to infer answers from reasoning traces showed that IGPO-generated traces were more informative and higher quality than GRPO's (Table 8). This suggests that IGPO effectively mitigates spurious correlations by reinforcing ground-truth-guided reasoning. Further evidence includes superior performance on OOD and multi-hop tasks.

#### 4.4. Computational Budget

- Empirical runtime comparisons indicated that IGPO incurred negligible overhead (Figure 7). The information gain reward computation added less than 0.4% to the standard implementation's runtime, and less than 0.02% to the total end-to-end training step latency, maintaining a training speed almost identical to GRPO. Theoretical analysis (Appendix F) confirmed that the additional FLOPs introduced by IGPO are minimal, typically less than `2g/s` where `g` is ground-truth length and `s` is sequence length.

#### 4.5. Failure Analysis

- While IGPO generally outperformed baselines, minor performance degradation (lower F1 scores than GRPO) was observed in approximately 3.6% of test samples (Table 9).
- The primary cause identified was ambiguity in ground-truth labels for inherently open-ended questions (Figure 9). In cases where a question lacks specific context, multiple factually correct answers might exist. If the model uses valid reasoning to arrive at a correct answer different from the designated ground truth, IGPO's turn-level reward mechanism penalizes this "correct but non-ground-truth" reasoning. This leads to misleading negative rewards, suppressing valid behaviors, and exacerbating the impact of data noise. The authors note that this degradation stems from data defects rather than algorithmic flaws, highlighting IGPO's robustness despite these limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO), which offers a notable contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for LLM-based agents.

The primary significance lies in its capacity to address core challenges in training multi-turn agents with sparse outcome-based rewards. By defining an intrinsic, ground-truth-aware, turn-level reward based on information gain, IGPO effectively mitigates:
- **Advantage collapse:** Providing continuous, non-zero gradient signals even when final outcomes are uniform.
- **Lack of fine-grained credit assignment:** Offering specific feedback for each intermediate action, guiding the agent through complex reasoning paths.
- **Poor sample efficiency:** Maximizing the utility of each interaction by extracting dense learning signals from intermediate steps.

The methodology's robust empirical performance across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks, coupled with its significant improvements in sample efficiency (especially for smaller models), suggests that IGPO can enhance the practical development of more capable and generalizable LLM agents. The observed improvements in ground-truth entropy reduction and mitigation of spurious correlations further indicate that IGPO encourages more grounded and faithful reasoning, which is critical for building trustworthy AI systems.

Moreover, the theoretical analysis connecting process rewards to the minimization of snowball errors provides a foundational understanding of why dense, turn-level supervision is beneficial in multi-step reasoning tasks. This theoretical underpinning strengthens the rationale behind IGPO and may inform future research in designing effective intrinsic rewards.

The computational efficiency of IGPO, achieved through a vectorized implementation, ensures that these performance gains are realized with negligible additional training overhead. This characteristic makes IGPO a practical and scalable solution for integrating into existing RL frameworks.

In summary, IGPO presents a simple, effective, and efficient framework for training multi-turn LLM search agents, overcoming key limitations of prior approaches. Its potential impact includes accelerating the development of more advanced, autonomous LLM agents capable of robustly tackling complex, knowledge-intensive tasks in real-world environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The primary limitation identified is IGPO's current reliance on the availability of explicit ground-truth answers. This restricts its applicability to supervised settings and makes it less suitable for open-ended or exploratory agentic scenarios where such ground truth might be unavailable or ill-defined. Future research is planned to extend IGPO to broader agentic settings that do not require explicit supervision, potentially through self-supervised or unsupervised information gain mechanisms.
