# Information Gain-based Policy Optimization: A Simple and Effective Approach for Multi-Turn Search Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO) introduces an intrinsic, turn-level reward mechanism for training multi-turn LLM search agents, which addresses the limitations of sparse outcome-based rewards. The framework achieved an average F1 score of 60.2 across seven QA benchmarks, outperforming leading baselines by 6.3 points while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Sparse outcome-based rewards in multi-turn agent training lead to 'advantage collapse' when rollouts yield identical final answers, providing no gradient signal.
- Lack of fine-grained credit assignment for individual intermediate steps (reasoning, tool calls) in long multi-turn trajectories, hindering effective learning.
- Poor sample efficiency, as an entire multi-turn trajectory yields only a single, sparse learning signal at its conclusion.

## Method

- The Information Gain-based Policy Optimization (IGPO) framework defines an intrinsic, ==turn-level reward== as the marginal increase in the policy's log-probability of generating the ground-truth answer at each interaction step.
- An efficient vectorized implementation is used to compute all turn-level information gain rewards in a single forward pass, minimizing computational overhead.
- IGPO integrates these turn-level information gain rewards with traditional outcome-based rewards into a group-relative policy optimization framework, using turn-level discounted returns for dense supervision.

## Results

- IGPO achieved the highest average F1 score of 60.2 across seven diverse QA benchmarks (including NQ, HotpotQA, PopQA), surpassing the best baseline (DeepResearcher) by 6.3 F1 points.
- The framework demonstrated improved learning dynamics and higher F1 scores, with more pronounced improvements for smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-**3B**-Instruct saw a 16.6 F1 point increase over GRPO).
- Information gain reward computation introduced negligible overhead, adding less than **0.02%** to the total end-to-end training step latency, maintaining training speed comparable to standard GRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Turn-level information gain rewards intrinsically guide the policy towards more confident and grounded reasoning by reducing the ground-truth entropy, preventing error accumulation over turns.
> - The combination of dense, intrinsic information gain rewards and sparse, final outcome rewards is complementary, leading to superior performance compared to using either reward type in isolation.
> - The approach effectively addresses the 'advantage collapse' problem by providing varying turn-level signals, even when final outcomes are identical within a training group.
