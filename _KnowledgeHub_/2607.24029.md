# Moving-Horizon Estimation and Nonlinear Model Predictive Control of Cable-Driven Soft Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> A unified framework combines reduced Cosserat dynamics with Moving-Horizon Estimation (MHE) for state reconstruction and Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for task-space control of cable-driven soft manipulators. This approach achieves real-time end-effector tracking with an RMSE of 0.42 cm on a physical prototype and improves trajectory tracking accuracy compared to static controllers, operating within a 0.1s sampling period.

## Problem

- Soft manipulators pose control challenges due to their high degrees of freedom, nonlinear dynamics, and difficulty in real-time internal state measurement.
- Existing model-based approaches are often computationally intensive for real-time control or lack robust experimental validation for precise and swift positioning.
- Controlling cable-driven soft manipulators requires robust dynamic models that can incorporate cable-length constraints and address unilateral tension-slackness behavior efficiently.

## Method

- A smooth cable-length-driven modeling formulation based on ==reduced Cosserat dynamics== is developed, including analytical Jacobians for dynamics and cable-actuation constraints.
- A **Moving-Horizon Estimation (MHE)** method is introduced for real-time state and shape reconstruction using only end-effector pose and cable-length measurements.
- A **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC)** framework is formulated for task-space control of soft manipulators, incorporating cable-length and cable-rate constraints using ==Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)==.

## Results

- Numerical simulations demonstrated MHE's ability to estimate strain distribution and trunk pose, with estimated poses converging to true poses within approximately **0.11s** and robustness to disturbances.
- Experimental validation on a 4-cable prototype showed the NMPC controller significantly reduced tracking errors, achieving an average volumetric mean error of **0.108 cm** in Y and **0.115 cm** in Z for trajectory tracking.
- The framework achieved real-time end-effector tracking of a dynamic target with an RMSE of **0.42 cm**, with average computation times for MHE and NMPC remaining below **60.4 ms**, allowing online execution within a **0.1s** sampling period.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating the nonlinear complementarity conditions of cable tension and slackness with smooth softplus functions enables their effective integration into differentiable optimization frameworks.
> - Deriving analytical Jacobians for the manipulator's dynamic residual and cable-actuation constraints is crucial for ensuring computational efficiency required for real-time MHE and NMPC.
> - The integrated MHE-NMPC framework, utilizing physics-based Cosserat models, can robustly estimate hidden states and control highly nonlinear soft manipulators under real-time constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of soft robotics has garnered attention due to its potential to overcome limitations of rigid robots through the use of compliant materials, enabling large deformations for applications in areas such as exploration and medical devices. A significant challenge in this domain is the development of accurate yet computationally tractable models for precise control, given the inherently high number of degrees of freedom (DoFs) and nonlinear dynamics of soft systems.

Current methodologies for modeling and control of soft robots broadly fall into two categories: data-driven learning techniques and model-based approaches. Data-driven methods, such such as those employing machine learning, neural networks, or recurrent neural networks, have shown promise in capturing nonlinear dynamics and enabling predictive control. However, these approaches face limitations including the need for extensive and representative data, restricted generalization to novel designs, and potential performance issues with unmodeled disturbances.

Model-based approaches rely on kinematic and dynamic equations. Early methods, like constant curvature (CC) or piecewise constant curvature (PCC), simplify robot geometry but are often too restrictive for complex deformations or external forces. More sophisticated models include Euler-Bernoulli beam theory and Finite Element Method (FEM). While FEM can model complex shapes, its high computational cost often necessitates model reduction techniques for real-time control. Cosserat models, which are geometrically nonlinear generalizations of beam theories, offer a powerful approach for slender soft robots. Full Cosserat models, however, involve nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs), making control design challenging. Finite-dimensional approximations, or reduced Cosserat models, convert PDEs into tractable ordinary differential equations (ODEs), which have been utilized for local controllers and inverse kinematic control. Despite these advancements, many Cosserat-based dynamic controllers have primarily been validated through simulations, with limited experimental validation, particularly for real-world scenarios demanding precise and swift positioning.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has also been applied in soft robotics, often using approximate linear or learned models to address its computational intensity. Integrating comprehensive, physics-based models with MPC, especially for soft robots, remains an area requiring further development. A critical practical challenge for model-based controllers is the need for complete knowledge of the state vector, which is difficult to measure accurately in soft robotic systems due to their infinite dimensionality. State estimation techniques, such as Moving-Horizon Estimation (MHE) and Extended Kalman Filters (EKF), have been applied to aspects like shape and pose estimation for continuum manipulators. However, the integration and validation of state estimation techniques based on full dynamic Cosserat models for controlling cable-driven soft manipulators remain largely unexplored.

This work addresses these gaps by proposing a unified framework that combines reduced Cosserat dynamics with MHE for state estimation and Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) for task-space control, specifically targeting cable-driven soft manipulators. It aims to develop accurate yet computationally efficient models and validate them experimentally, thereby bridging the gap between theoretical modeling and practical, real-time control implementation for these complex systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a comprehensive and generic model-based control framework for soft manipulators. This framework is designed to integrate both state estimation and control, aiming to overcome the inherent challenges in controlling soft robots, particularly cable-driven systems.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges in the control of soft manipulators:
1. **Modeling Accuracy vs. Computational Tractability:** While reduced Cosserat-rod models offer a physics-based, control-oriented description of soft robot dynamics, developing models that are both accurate and computationally feasible for real-time estimation and control remains difficult. The authors aim to refine this balance by developing a smooth cable-length-driven modeling formulation.
2. **Actuation Mechanism Integration:** Cable-driven soft manipulators are common due to their strong actuation force and rapid response. However, direct force control is challenging due to sensor requirements and complex algorithms. Controlling cable length is simpler in practice but necessitates a robust dynamic model that can incorporate cable-length constraints and address the unilateral tension-slackness behavior of cables.
3. **Real-Time State Estimation:** Precise control of soft robots relies on accurate knowledge of their internal state (e.g., strain distribution, trunk pose). These states are often difficult to measure directly in practice due to the infinite-dimensional nature of soft robots. Therefore, a robust state estimation technique capable of reconstructing the manipulator's configuration from partial, measurable outputs (like end-effector pose and cable lengths) is essential.
4. **Model Predictive Control for Nonlinear Dynamics:** Existing NMPC applications in soft robotics often resort to linearized or learned models due to the computational demands of MPC and the high nonlinearity of soft robot dynamics. This limits the integration of complex physical constraints. The authors aim to develop an NMPC framework that can handle the full nonlinearity of reduced Cosserat dynamics, including actuation and rate constraints, while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time operation.
5. **Lack of Experimental Validation:** Despite significant theoretical advancements in dynamic controllers for soft continuum robots, comprehensive experimental validations for many model-based control schemes, particularly those based on reduced Cosserat dynamic models, are scarce. This work seeks to validate the proposed MHE-NMPC framework through both numerical simulations and physical experiments to demonstrate its real-world applicability for precise and swift positioning.

To address these motivations, the research focuses on four specific contributions:
1. Developing a smooth cable-length-driven modeling formulation for cable-driven soft manipulators, including analytical Jacobians for dynamics and cable-actuation constraints.
2. Introducing an MHE method based on reduced Cosserat dynamics for state estimation and shape reconstruction using end-effector pose and cable-length measurements.
3. Formulating an NMPC framework for task-space control of soft manipulators, incorporating cable-length and cable-rate constraints.
4. Validating the entire MHE-NMPC framework through simulations and experiments on a physical prototype, demonstrating pose regulation, trajectory tracking, and real-time end-effector position tracking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research integrates a reduced Cosserat-rod model with Moving-Horizon Estimation (MHE) and Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) within a Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) framework.

**A. Reduced Cosserat-Rod Modeling:**
The soft manipulator's deformation is represented using a finite number of degrees of freedom. The Cosserat framework models the manipulator as a series of rigid cross-sections along its centerline, described by a homogeneous transformation matrix `g(s, t)`. Strain `ξ(s, t)` and velocity `η(s, t)` are defined in the body frame. To reduce complexity, the strain field is parameterized as `ξ(s, t) = ξ₀ + Φ(s)q(t)`, where `q(t)` are generalized coordinates and `Φ(s)` is the shape function matrix. The kinematics are then derived, including the kinematic Jacobian `J(s, t)`.

**B. Control and Estimation Objectives:**
Both control and estimation objectives are formulated as minimizing a pose error. For pose control, the objective `J_g(g)` minimizes differences in orientation (using `tr(I - Rᵀ_r R)`) and position (using `||p - p_r||²₂`) of the end-effector. For state estimation, the objective `J_est(q)` similarly minimizes the error between measured and predicted end-effector pose. Analytical gradients of these objective functions with respect to the generalized coordinates `q` are derived, which is crucial for optimization efficiency.

**C. Dynamics with Actuation Constraints:**
The research focuses on cable-driven soft manipulators.
- **Cable Path and Length:** Each cable follows an internal routing path. The length of this path, `l_c(q)`, is calculated by integrating the linear strain component along the cable path. The gradient `∇_q l_c` is also derived analytically.
- **Generalized Actuation Force:** The general force introduced by cable tension `T` is expressed as `F_act = ∇_q l_c T`. The Jacobian of `F_act` with respect to `q` is also formulated.
- **Cosserat Rod Dynamics:** The dynamics are recalled as a residual `f(¨q, ˙q, q, u) = ∫(JᵀΛ_ine + ΦᵀΛ_int)ds = 0`, where `Λ_ine` includes inertial and external forces, and `Λ_int` includes elastic, viscous, and actuation forces.
- **Smooth Cable-Driven Constraint:** Since cables can only pull (tension) and not push (compression), a nonlinear complementarity condition `0 ≤ T_i ⊥ δ_i ≥ 0` is imposed, where `δ_i = l_i - l_ci(q)` is the cable-length gap. To integrate this into a differentiable optimization framework, the complementarity condition is approximated using a smooth softplus function `E(λ) = (1/c)log(1 + e^(cλ))`. This redefines tension `T = E(λ)` and length difference `l - l_c = E(-λ)`, transforming the complementarity into a smooth equality constraint `l - l_c - E(-λ) = 0`. This enables cable length `l` to be explicitly defined as `l = l_c + E(-λ)`.

**D. Jacobian of the Dynamics:**
To enhance computational efficiency within the optimization framework, analytical Jacobians of the dynamic residual `f` with respect to `q`, `˙q`, and `¨q` are derived. This involves a multi-stage process:
1. Deriving chain rule identities for `η` and `˙η`.
2. Formulating closed-form expressions for the kinematic Jacobian `J` and its time derivative `˙J`.
3. Computing partial derivatives of the adjoint maps (Ad_g, ad).
4. Consolidating these results into a computational procedure for the Jacobian matrix of the dynamic residual, `∂f/∂q`, `∂f/∂˙q`, `∂f/∂¨q`. These are computed by integrating contributions over the manipulator's length.

**E. MHE-NMPC Framework and Solution:**
The continuous-time optimization problem is discretized over finite estimation `[t - t_est, t]` and control `[t, t + t_ctrl]` horizons.
- **Discretization:** The implicit Euler method is used for time discretization of the dynamics, approximating `˙q` and `¨q` in terms of `q` at current and previous time steps. This transforms the continuous dynamics `f(¨q, ˙q, q, u) = 0` into a discrete-time representation `f(x_i, x_{i-1}, u_i) = 0`.
- **MHE Problem Formulation:** The MHE problem (Eq. 39) minimizes `J_est` over the estimation horizon, subject to the discrete dynamics and the smooth cable-driven constraints. A proximal regularization term `μ₁||x_i - x⁻_i||²₂` is included to improve convergence.
- **NMPC Problem Formulation:** The NMPC problem (Eq. 40) minimizes `J_ctrl` over the prediction horizon, subject to discrete dynamics, smooth cable-driven constraints, and additional physical constraints on cable rate `(h˙l_min ≤ l_i - l_{i-1} ≤ h˙l_max)`. A proximal regularization term `μ₂||l_i - l⁻_i||²₂` is also included.
- **Redefinition with Slack Variables:** After substituting `u = E(λ)` and `l = l_c + E(-λ)`, the MHE and NMPC problems are redefined (Eq. 48 and 49) to optimize directly over state `x` and slack variables `λ`, where `l` is implicitly determined.
- **Solving Method - Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP):** Due to the highly nonlinear nature of the problems, SQP is adopted. This method converts the nonlinear program (NLP) into a series of quadratic programming (QP) sub-problems. The Lagrangian function is constructed, and the QPs are formulated by linearizing the constraints and approximating the Hessian of the Lagrangian (using a Gauss-Newton Hessian). An iterative process updates the decision variables (`x`, `λ`) and Lagrange multipliers. The QP formulation for both estimation and control is explicitly derived (Eq. 51 and 52). A line search with a merit function is used to determine step size `r`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed MHE-NMPC framework was validated through numerical simulations on a 16-cable soft manipulator and experimental tests on a four-cable prototype.

**A. Numerical Simulations (16-Cable Manipulator):**
1. **Estimation of Configuration via MHE:**
 - **Scenario:** End-effector position and Euler angles, along with cable lengths, were assumed measurable. Initial configuration estimates were set to the natural state (`q=0`). The manipulator was subjected to cable tensions, and a disturbance was introduced (cable drive stopped) at 3.8s.
 - **Results:** The MHE algorithm effectively estimated the strain distribution and trunk pose. Estimated poses converged to true poses within approximately 0.11s. The strain error `e_q` converged to within ±0.001 within 0.5s. The estimator demonstrated robustness, quickly re-converging within 1s after a significant abrupt change due to the disturbance, with strain error remaining within ±0.01.
2. **Constant Pose Control:**
 - **Objective:** Move the end-effector to a fixed target pose (position `[5 10 8]ᵀ` cm, orientation `ϕ_r =ᵀ` rad) under gravity, with cable tension (0-20 N) and cable speed (-0.4 to 0.4 m/s) constraints. Elastic and gravitational potential energy terms were included as regularization in the objective function.
 - **Results:** The system achieved the target pose rapidly, stabilizing after 0.2s, with a response time of 0.14s. All cable tensions and pulling speeds remained within the specified constraints throughout the control process.
3. **Trajectory Tracking Control:**
 - **Objective:** Track a time-varying trajectory for both orientation and position. The impact of prediction horizon length was investigated by comparing ∆t = 0.01s and ∆t = 0.06s.
 - **Results:** A longer prediction horizon (0.06s) significantly improved tracking accuracy and stability. With ∆t = 0.01s, the manipulator exhibited "short-sighted" behavior, occasionally deviating from the target and showing undesirable curling at the distal end. The longer horizon enabled the controller to anticipate future trajectory changes, resulting in smoother pose adjustments and substantially reduced tracking errors.
4. **Strain-Pose Coupled Control:**
 - **Objective:** Perform a grasping action by simultaneously controlling the pose of a point 4 cm from the end-effector and the bending strain in the distal 4 cm segment.
 - **Results:** The soft manipulator successfully converged to both the target pose and target strain, demonstrating the ability to move to the object and then bend to securely grasp it. Pose convergence occurred within 0.17s, and strain convergence within 0.4s, stabilizing after 0.5s.

**B. Experimental Validation (4-Cable Prototype):**
1. **MHE-Based Observer:**
 - **Scenario:** An operator randomly pulled four cables to induce movement. The MHE estimated the manipulator's shape based on measured end-effector position from a magnetic sensor. The MHE horizon was 0.3s (3 steps of 0.1s).
 - **Results:** The MHE-based estimator successfully tracked the end-effector position, with rapid convergence and bounded estimation error. Qualitative visual comparison confirmed consistency between the estimated shape and the observed physical deformation (Fig. 16, 17).
2. **Trajectory Tracking Control:**
 - **Objective:** Enable the end-effector to follow predefined trajectories in the Y-Z and X-Z planes, within the limited workspace of the 4-cable manipulator. The NMPC (0.3s prediction horizon, 3 steps) was compared against a Static Model-Based Controller (SMBC).
 - **Results:** The NMPC controller significantly outperformed the SMBC, particularly for rapid movements. The SMBC showed larger tracking errors (e.g., AVME 2.274 cm in Y, 2.352 cm in Z for Y-Z plane) due to its omission of velocity and acceleration effects. In contrast, NMPC achieved lower errors (e.g., AVME 0.108 cm in Y, 0.115 cm in Z for Y-Z plane; 0.095 cm in X, 0.086 cm in Z for X-Z plane), demonstrating improved accuracy and suitability for dynamic tasks (Table I, Fig. 18).
3. **Real-Time Marker Tracking:**
 - **Scenario:** The manipulator was controlled to follow a randomly varying target (operator's wand) in real-time. Target and end-effector positions were projected onto an imaginary work surface. Cable speed constraints (based on motor limits) were included. The NMPC prediction horizon was 0.4s (4 steps).
 - **Results:** The proposed controller continuously updated its planned motion, allowing the end-effector to track the dynamically changing target within its reachable workspace (Fig. 20). The tracking error had an RMSE of 0.42 cm, demonstrating the real-time feasibility of the framework for dynamic, unpredictable target following.
4. **Computational Performance:**
 - **Results:** The average total computation time for the MHE and NMPC modules was recorded. For trajectory tracking, it was approximately 51.4 ms. For marker tracking, it was approximately 60.4 ms. Both values are below the 0.1s sampling period, indicating that the framework can be executed online for real-time control on the physical prototype (Table II).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a model-based estimation and control framework for cable-driven soft manipulators that demonstrates both theoretical coherence and practical feasibility. Its significance and potential impact lie in several areas:

1. **Unified and Comprehensive Framework:** The research introduces a unified framework that combines reduced Cosserat dynamics, Moving-Horizon Estimation (MHE), and Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC). This integration addresses the complex interplay between modeling, state estimation, and control, providing a holistic solution for soft robot control.
2. **Addressing Core Challenges in Soft Robotics:** The framework directly tackles persistent challenges in soft robotics, including the difficulty of deriving accurate yet computationally tractable models, the need for robust state estimation from limited sensor data, and the real-time control of highly nonlinear, underactuated systems with physical constraints.
3. **Innovative Cable Actuation Modeling:** The development of a smooth cable-length-driven modeling formulation, which approximates the complementarity relationship between cable tension and slackness using softplus functions, is a notable contribution. This approach enables cable-length control, which is often more practical than direct tension sensing and control in real-world applications, while ensuring differentiability for optimization.
4. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** The analytical derivation of Jacobians for the manipulator dynamics and cable-actuation constraints, coupled with the use of Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) and implicit time differentiation, contributes to the computational efficiency required for real-time control. The experimental results confirm that the framework operates within real-time constraints (e.g., 0.1s sampling period).
5. **Bridging Simulation and Experimentation:** The validation through both extensive numerical simulations on a multi-cable system and real-time experiments on a physical prototype is crucial. This helps bridge the gap between theoretical models and practical implementation, providing confidence in the framework's applicability for real-world scenarios that demand precise and swift positioning.
6. **Enabling Advanced Control Capabilities:** The MHE-NMPC framework enables advanced control objectives such as accurate end-effector pose regulation, dynamic trajectory tracking, and even coupled strain-pose control for complex tasks like grasping. The demonstrated robustness against disturbances and improved tracking performance over simpler controllers highlight its potential.

The potential impact of this research is in advancing the capabilities of soft robots, making them more suitable for practical applications requiring precise and adaptive manipulation in unstructured or human-centric environments. Industries such as medical devices, human-robot interaction, and complex inspection tasks could benefit from more precise and reliable control over soft robotic systems. Future work, as suggested by the authors, includes improving quantitative shape-estimation validation, extending the platform to higher-dimensional pose regulation, and further enhancing computational efficiency and robustness, which could lead to even broader applicability.
