# PhysMani: Physics-principled 3D World Model for Dynamic Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysMani is a framework designed for manipulating fast-moving objects in unstructured 3D environments, integrating a physics-principled 3D Gaussian world model with a future-aware action policy. This approach achieved a 45.9% mean success rate on the new PhysMani-Bench benchmark, outperforming existing methods by 8.1 percentage points, and demonstrated effective real-world dynamic manipulation with a 62.5% mean success rate.

## Problem

- Existing embodied AI and robotic manipulation methods struggle with dynamically moving objects in unstructured 3D environments, largely focusing on static or quasi-static settings.
- Current VLA and world models lack explicit 3D geometric awareness and generate future predictions that often do not adhere to fundamental physical laws, making them unreliable for precise control.
- Inference latency in large visual and language models is prohibitive for real-time, time-sensitive dynamic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- **PhysMani** develops a physics-principled ==3D Gaussian world model== that learns explicit 3D scene geometry and forecasts future dynamics by enforcing a ==divergence-free velocity field== for physical consistency.
- Designed a ==future-aware action policy== model that incorporates predicted 3D scene dynamics from the world model into a ==diffusion-based imitation learning== framework (a ==3DFA== / ==Rectified Flow== backbone with ==cross-attention== dynamics-token fusion) for proactive decision-making.
- Implemented an ==online optimization== algorithm within the world model, coupled with ==CUDA Graph optimization==, to achieve rapid future prediction and low-latency action execution suitable for real-time control.

## Results

- Achieved a mean success rate of **45.9%** on the PhysMani-Bench, outperforming the next best baseline, 3DFA, by **8.1 percentage points** on dynamic manipulation tasks.
- Demonstrated superior future frame prediction quality (PSNR **26.90** for the 1st frame) and low trajectory errors (**0.008m** for the 1st frame), with the world model running **3.0 times** faster than FreeGave.
- Validated effectiveness in real-world scenarios, achieving a **62.5%** mean success rate across four challenging dynamic manipulation tasks, surpassing the strongest baseline's **45.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit, physics-principled 3D dynamics into a world model provides more reliable future predictions for robotic control than purely visual or 2D motion cues.
> - Conditioning an action policy on anticipated future 3D scene dynamics, alongside current observations and language instructions, enables proactive and precise manipulation of fast-moving targets.
> - A lightweight, online optimization framework for 3D Gaussian representations can effectively capture and predict dynamic scene evolution in real-time, critical for time-sensitive robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI, particularly robotic manipulation, has progressed significantly with the development of visual-language-action models (VLAs) and world models. These models enable robots to learn complex behaviors and perform various tasks. However, the majority of this progress has concentrated on static or quasi-static environments. Tasks involving the manipulation of dynamically moving objects in unstructured 3D environments remain a considerable challenge.

Prior work in dynamic object manipulation, such as playing table tennis, grasping objects on conveyor belts, or object handovers, has typically been confined to specific or simplified scenarios. These specialized approaches often lack the generality required for open and unstructured environments where objects exhibit complex, unpredictable motion patterns.

Current powerful VLAs and video-based world models face several limitations when applied to dynamic manipulation:
1. **Lack of 3D Geometric Awareness**: Learned world representations often do not explicitly capture the 3D geometry of the scene.
2. **Physically Meaningful Dynamics**: Generated future frames, while visually plausible, frequently do not adhere to fundamental physical laws, making them unreliable for precise 3D robotic control.
3. **Inference Latency**: The sequential nature of large visual and language models can introduce significant inference delays, which are prohibitive for real-time, time-sensitive dynamic manipulation tasks.

This work addresses these identified gaps by proposing a framework that explicitly integrates physics-principled 3D dynamics prediction with action policy learning. It aims to provide a more general and efficient solution for dynamic object manipulation, extending the capabilities of embodied AI beyond static environments. The development of a new benchmark, PhysMani-Bench, also contributes to the research landscape by providing a standardized evaluation environment for dynamic manipulation tasks, which was previously lacking.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable framework for manipulating fast and dynamically moving targets within unstructured 3D environments. This objective is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing VLA and world models in addressing dynamic manipulation challenges.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

- **Overcome Limitations in 3D Geometry and Physical Dynamics**: Existing models often produce visually realistic future predictions but fail to capture physically consistent 3D dynamics. The researchers aim to develop a world model that learns explicit 3D scene geometry and forecasts future dynamics that adhere to fundamental physical laws, thus providing more reliable predictions for robotic action.
- **Address Inference Latency**: Traditional large visual and language models incur high inference latency, rendering them unsuitable for real-time dynamic manipulation where quick decision-making is critical. The work aims to achieve a lightweight pipeline that enables rapid future prediction and low-latency action execution.
- **Integrate Future Dynamics into Action Policy**: The research aims to design an action policy model that can effectively utilize the predicted future 3D scene dynamics to guide decision-making. This integration is crucial for proactive planning and precise action execution when interacting with moving targets.
- **Establish a Standardized Benchmark**: Recognizing the absence of comprehensive benchmarks for dynamic object manipulation, a significant objective is to introduce "PhysMani-Bench." This benchmark, composed of diverse and challenging tasks, is intended to facilitate rigorous evaluation and comparison of methods in dynamic manipulation scenarios.
- **Demonstrate Real-World Applicability**: Beyond simulation, a key motivation is to validate the proposed framework's effectiveness on physical robot systems in real-world dynamic manipulation tasks, showcasing its practical utility.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose PhysMani, a framework comprising two core designs: a physics-principled 3D Gaussian world model for dynamic forecasting and a future-aware action policy model for decision-making.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhysMani framework integrates two main components: a physics-principled 3D Gaussian world model and a future-aware action policy model. The system is designed for fast prediction and low-latency execution.

#### 3.1. Physics-principled 3D Gaussian World Model

This model is responsible for learning 3D scene geometry, appearance, and physical dynamics. It refines concepts from FreeGave, reformulating its architecture for online optimization.

- **Canonical 3D Gaussian Module**: At time t=0, given RGB-D data from multiple cameras, the module initializes a set of 3D Gaussian kernels. Each Gaussian represents a point in the scene with parameters for 3D position, scaling, orientation, opacity, and color. These kernels are optimized using L1 and SSIM losses against the initial RGB images and depth maps to establish a canonical scene representation.
- **Physics-principled Gaussian Velocity Module**: For each Gaussian at a given time *t*, a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) network, denoted as *f_vel*, learns six basic velocity components: three linear velocities (v_x, v_y, v_z) and three angular velocities (w_z, w_y, w_x). These components form a velocity field *v(g_t, t)* that governs the motion of the Gaussians. A key aspect is that this module enforces a divergence-free property in the velocity field, ensuring adherence to fundamental physical laws.
- **Online Optimization of Future Dynamics**: To enable real-time dynamic manipulation, the world model employs an online optimization algorithm (Algorithm 1). When new RGB-D observations arrive, *f_vel* is updated for a set number of iterations (T=50) to learn precise velocity components. These velocities are then used to predict the future state of the Gaussians. Subsequently, the Gaussian parameters (position, orientation) are refined for a fewer number of iterations (T'=7) to account for minor scene changes, while freezing *f_vel*. This iterative process, performed rapidly (approximately 200ms per round on an RTX 4090 GPU), allows the model to continuously capture ongoing dynamics and forecast future evolution. Static computation graphs (CUDA Graph optimization) are reused during optimization to enhance efficiency and reduce overhead.

#### 3.2. Future-aware Action Policy Model

This model leverages current visual observations and predicted future dynamics to infer robot actions. It is built upon the 3D FlowMatch Actor (3DFA) backbone, which is a diffusion-based imitation learning policy using Rectified Flow.

- **Encoding 3D Visual Geometry, Appearance, and Language**: Current RGB-D observations are processed to generate a sparse 3D scene point cloud, which is then converted into a feature cloud (visual tokens). Language task instructions are encoded into language tokens. These visual and language tokens are fused via cross-attention to produce refined visual tokens.
- **Incorporating 3D Scene Future Dynamics**: The predicted six basic velocity components (*D_t*) from the world model for each 3D Gaussian are integrated into the policy network. This involves several steps:
 1. For each point in the sparse 3D scene point cloud, K nearest neighboring 3D Gaussians are retrieved from the world model.
 2. Relative offsets between the point and its neighboring Gaussians are computed.
 3. The relative offsets and the basic velocity components of the neighboring Gaussians are fed into separate MLPs to generate corresponding token sets.
 4. A learnable vector (*L*) acts as a query token in a cross-attention mechanism, using the offset tokens as keys and velocity tokens as values. This generates "future dynamics tokens."
 5. Finally, these dynamics tokens are added to the visual tokens to create "future-aware tokens" that explicitly condition the robot's predicted actions on anticipated future motion.
- **Denoising End-effector's Keyposes with Rectified Flow**: The policy model predicts the end-effector's keyposes (3D position, 3D orientation, gripper state) for a specified temporal horizon. During training, noisy trajectories are created, and the model learns a velocity field using an L2 loss and gripper openness using Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE). During inference, the future-aware tokens, proprioception, and language instruction are used to denoise the keyposes, which are then converted to joint commands via inverse kinematics.

#### 3.3. PhysMani-Bench

Given the lack of suitable benchmarks, the authors introduce PhysMani-Bench, a dynamic manipulation benchmark comprising 16 tasks. These tasks are inspired by RLBench and categorized into 8 groups, each with normal-speed and high-speed variants. Tasks involve dynamic reaching, grasping, placing, inserting, pushing, and tool-use, with objects exhibiting diverse linear, circular, and rotational motions. The benchmark uses 4 static cameras and sets target speeds comparable to the robot's maximum end-effector speed. For each task, 160 successful trajectories are collected using scripted expert policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both simulated and real-world experimental results to evaluate PhysMani.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on PhysMani-Bench (Simulation)

- **Dynamic Manipulation Success Rate**: PhysMani achieved a mean success rate (SR) of 45.9% across all 16 tasks, outperforming all baselines. It exhibited an 8.1 percentage point lead over the next best method, 3DFA (37.8%). This suggests that PhysMani's integration of 3D scene future dynamics contributes to improved performance.
- **Comparison with Baselines**:
 - **3DFA-OF**: Augmenting 3DFA with 2D optical flow (3DFA-OF) did not yield significant performance gains over vanilla 3DFA, and still fell short of PhysMani. This indicates that 2D motion cues alone provide limited dynamic information compared to PhysMani's explicit 3D dynamics.
 - **pi0.5**: The VLA model pi0.5 performed poorly on high-speed tasks, attributed to its inability to capture 3D spatial representation and complex future dynamics.
 - **ManiGaussian**: This method, designed for future-frame prediction and improving action, showed lower success rates than PhysMani.
- **Future Frame Prediction Quality**: PhysMani demonstrated superior future frame prediction capabilities. For the next 1st frame, it achieved a PSNR of 26.90, SSIM of 0.877, LPIPS of 0.123, logRMSE of 0.060, and a trajectory error of 0.008m. In contrast, ManiGaussian had a PSNR of 14.32 and a trajectory error of 0.388m for the same horizon. PhysMani maintained lower trajectory errors over longer horizons (e.g., 0.039m for the 5th frame, 0.074m for the 10th frame).
- **Prediction Efficiency**: PhysMani ran 3.0 times faster than FreeGave while maintaining higher PSNR scores for future frame prediction under equal compute constraints (205 ms/frame vs. 607 ms/frame for FreeGave). This validates the efficiency of its online optimization.
- **Generalization Ability**: Experiments involving variations in object color, shape, and kinematic parameters for "Drop to Hoop" tasks showed that PhysMani consistently maintained a significant performance advantage over 3DFA, indicating its generalization capabilities.
- **Impact of Inference Speed**: PhysMani demonstrated higher success rates compared to 3DFA across various injected latencies (0-300 ms), suggesting that its 3D future-dynamics prediction helps the policy tolerate slower inference speeds.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Real-world Tasks

- PhysMani was evaluated on four real-world dynamic tasks: "picking up a moving toy," "placing a toy onto a moving belt," "placing a cube onto a rotating rack," and "removing a cup from a rotating rack."
- It achieved a mean SR of 62.5% across these tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline, 3DFA (mean SR 45.3%). This demonstrates the framework's effectiveness in physical robot experiments involving challenging dynamics.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **(1) Removing 3D scene velocity components (D_t)**: This resulted in the most significant drop in success rate, making performance comparable to the 3DFA backbone. This finding underscores the importance of integrating explicit 3D future dynamics for dynamic manipulation tasks.
- **(2) Removing learnable token (L)**: Replacing the learnable token-based cross-attention with pure self-attention also noticeably degraded performance, suggesting the effectiveness of the learnable token in fusing local dynamics information.
- **(3) Optimizing Gaussian velocity field with different iterations T**: PhysMani exhibited robustness to varying numbers of optimization iterations (T from 10 to 50), maintaining stable performance. This indicates that the framework can learn dynamics quickly, which is critical for real-time applications.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Gaussian Velocity

Visualizations of the learned six basic velocity components showed that they naturally concentrate on the moving robot arm and the dynamic target object, largely disregarding the static background. This indicates that the world model provides explicit, physics-grounded signals directly relevant for guiding robot actions.

#### 4.5. Computation Efficiency

PhysMani requires 272.8 ms for a single keyframe action inference. The online optimization of the world model runs in parallel, taking approximately 200 ms. This parallel and optimized design allows for fast and dynamic manipulation, with the policy model inference identified as the current overall system bottleneck.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhysMani framework addresses a critical gap in embodied AI by enabling robots to manipulate dynamically moving objects in unstructured 3D environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Advancement in Dynamic Manipulation**: By coupling a physics-principled 3D Gaussian world model with a future-aware action policy, PhysMani demonstrates a robust approach to dynamic object manipulation. This is a departure from previous methods that often relied on simplified scenarios or reactive control, offering a more generalizable solution for complex, time-sensitive tasks.
- **Physics-Principled World Modeling**: The integration of a divergence-free Gaussian velocity field within an online optimization framework provides a mechanism for forecasting physically meaningful future dynamics. This is crucial for precise interaction, as it overcomes the limitations of purely visual predictive models that may generate physically implausible future states.
- **Efficiency for Real-time Control**: The lightweight pipeline, optimized through techniques like CUDA Graph optimization, achieves low-latency future prediction and action execution. This efficiency makes PhysMani suitable for real-world robotic applications where rapid decision-making is essential.
- **Novel Benchmark for Dynamic Tasks**: The introduction of PhysMani-Bench fills a gap in the evaluation landscape for dynamic manipulation. This benchmark, with its 16 diverse and challenging tasks, provides a standardized platform for future research, enabling consistent comparison and acceleration of progress in this domain.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability**: The success of PhysMani in real-world robotic experiments, beyond simulation, validates its practical utility and potential for deployment in various applications, such as industrial automation, logistics, or assistive robotics, where interaction with moving targets is common.
- **Foundational for Embodied Control**: The research highlights the importance of physics-principled 3D world models for advanced embodied control, offering a practical direction towards developing intelligent agents capable of navigating and manipulating objects in highly dynamic and unpredictable environments.

The findings suggest a pathway toward general dynamic manipulation in open environments, influencing future research in robotic control, world modeling, and the broader field of embodied AI.
