# Biased Dreams: Limitations to Epistemic Uncertainty Quantification in Latent Dynamics Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Model-based RL uses ensemble disagreement as an epistemic-uncertainty signal to guard against exploiting an inaccurate dynamics model, a technique validated on low-dimensional proprioceptive dynamics. This paper tests whether that guard still works once the dynamics model is a latent world model — Dreamer's Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM) — and finds it does not: ensemble disagreement stays locally informative but decouples from the true, compounding rollout error, because RSSM imagination exhibits an attractor bias that pulls predictions toward well-supported latent regions regardless of accuracy, which can make the model overestimate returns.

## Problem

- Ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty is well-established for proprioceptive (low-dimensional state) dynamics models, where it mitigates model exploitation, guides exploration, and promotes caution in offline/model-based RL.
- Whether this same signal is trustworthy for latent dynamics models — dominant for image-based model-based RL via architectures like Dreamer's RSSM — is largely untested; the field implicitly assumes the proprioceptive result transfers unchanged.
- If ensemble disagreement decouples from the true compounding error accumulated over a long imagined rollout, a policy or planner that trusts the uncertainty signal can be silently misled into exploiting the inaccurate part of the latent model, with no warning from the uncertainty estimate itself.

## Method

- An ==external ensemble of one-step latent transition predictors== (M=5) is trained alongside a Dreamer-style **RSSM** (stochastic state size 30, deterministic state size 200) and a **Categorical RSSM** variant (32 categoricals × 32 classes), with epistemic uncertainty measured as the ==Geometric Jensen-Shannon divergence== over the ensemble's predicted next-latent-state distributions.
- Four DMC Suite tasks — **Cartpole Swingup**, **Cheetah Run**, **Hopper Hop**, **Walker Run** — are trained for 5 runs of 1M environment steps each, plus **MuJoCo HalfCheetah** run as a proprioceptive-dynamics baseline for comparison.
- Initial states are partitioned into ==in-distribution (ID) vs out-of-distribution (OOD)== sets by ensemble uncertainty, then N=1000 rollouts of horizon T=50 are unrolled in closed-loop imagination, comparing the ensemble's *predicted* epistemic uncertainty against the *measured* compounding model error (decoded back to proprioceptive state) over the rollout.

## Results

- Proprioceptive ensembles show the expected behavior: unstable, appropriately-elevated uncertainty on OOD rollouts. RSSM latent rollouts instead show an ==attractor behavior== — predictions converge toward dominant, "well-supported" transition flows regardless of whether they track the true environment dynamics.
- OOD ensemble-disagreement uncertainty starts elevated but **rapidly converges to in-distribution (ID) levels** as the rollout proceeds, even as the true proprioceptive-decoded rollout error keeps growing — local uncertainty stays informative step-to-step but decouples entirely from the trajectory-level compounding error it is meant to track.
- The attractor bias causes closed-loop imagined rollouts to **systematically overestimate returns** on dense-reward tasks, because the attractor regions the rollout drifts toward coincide with high-reward behavior regions of the latent space.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Epistemic uncertainty quantification does not automatically transfer from proprioceptive to latent dynamics models — RSSMs have a structural failure mode (attractor bias) that proprioceptive ensembles do not exhibit.
> - A world model's uncertainty estimate can look calibrated locally (single-step ensemble disagreement) while being wrong globally (multi-step compounding rollout error) — local uncertainty is not a license to trust a long imagined rollout.
> - The paper proposes no fix within the current uncertainty-estimation framework; it argues the failure needs to be addressed at the level of the latent dynamics model itself (latent-space restructuring, alternative parametrizations), leaving a formal mechanistic account of the attractor effect as open work.
