# Learning Visual Spatial Planning from Symbolic State via Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation (MGSD) framework improves Vision-language Models' visual spatial planning by bridging the perception–reasoning modality gap. It achieves this by using a symbolic teacher to guide a visual student during training, enhancing both visual state recovery and multi-step reasoning, leading to significant task success improvements on benchmarks like FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehaviour.

## Problem

- Vision-language Models (VLMs) struggle with visual spatial planning due to a "perception–reasoning modality gap," which creates bottlenecks in both accurately inferring state structures from pixels (perception) and generating valid multi-step action plans (reasoning).
- Existing visual planning methods, such as supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, entangle perception and planning failures or provide sparse supervision, failing to fully leverage the benefits of explicit symbolic state representations available in many planning environments.
- Models frequently exhibit a mismatch where improved visual state recovery does not directly translate to enhanced multi-step execution, indicating a deficit in their pure planning capabilities over inferred states.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation== (**MGSD**) framework is introduced, employing a privileged ==symbolic teacher== to guide an image-conditioned student during training without requiring symbolic inputs at inference time.
- The first stage, Perception-Oriented Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), establishes robust perceptual grounding by fine-tuning the visual student on multimodal QA samples to accurately recover task-relevant state information from images.
- The second stage, Symbolic-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), uses a frozen text-only teacher, conditioned on symbolic context and ground-truth plans, to provide dense, token-level log-probability supervision to the visual student as it generates on-policy action sequences, encouraging alignment with symbolic planning behavior.

## Results

- MGSD-4B increased the macro average success rate from **11.2%** to **30.5%**, while MGSD-8B improved it from **17.2%** to **35.6%** across FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehaviour benchmarks.
- MGSD-4B outperformed several larger open-source VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B) and proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-4.5-Haiku) in overall average performance, and MGSD-8B further narrowed the gap to top-tier systems like Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-5.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Cold-Start SFT stage and the Symbolic-Guided OPSD stage are crucial, with the latter boosting overall task success from **17.7%** to **30.5%** and the absence of initial SFT degrading performance to **16.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Establishing a strong perceptual foundation through an initial "cold-start" SFT is critical for reducing early grounding noise and enabling the visual student to reliably recover state structures for effective multi-step guidance.
> - Transferring robust planning behaviors from a privileged symbolic teacher via on-policy distillation is effective in bridging the modality gap, allowing the visual student to learn complex reasoning strategies from explicit symbolic states.
> - The framework achieves balanced improvements in both visual state recovery (perception) and pure planning capability (reasoning), which are both necessary for high end-to-end task success, indicating MGSD addresses the perception-reasoning mismatch.
