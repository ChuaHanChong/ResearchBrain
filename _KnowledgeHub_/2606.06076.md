# Learning Visual Spatial Planning from Symbolic State via Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation (MGSD) framework improves Vision-language Models' visual spatial planning by bridging the perception–reasoning modality gap. It achieves this by using a symbolic teacher to guide a visual student during training, enhancing both visual state recovery and multi-step reasoning, leading to significant task success improvements on benchmarks like FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehaviour.

## Problem

- Vision-language Models (VLMs) struggle with visual spatial planning due to a "perception–reasoning modality gap," which creates bottlenecks in both accurately inferring state structures from pixels (perception) and generating valid multi-step action plans (reasoning).
- Existing visual planning methods, such as supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, entangle perception and planning failures or provide sparse supervision, failing to fully leverage the benefits of explicit symbolic state representations available in many planning environments.
- Models frequently exhibit a mismatch where improved visual state recovery does not directly translate to enhanced multi-step execution, indicating a deficit in their pure planning capabilities over inferred states.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation== (**MGSD**) framework is introduced, employing a privileged ==symbolic teacher== to guide an image-conditioned student during training without requiring symbolic inputs at inference time.
- The first stage, Perception-Oriented Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), establishes robust perceptual grounding by fine-tuning the visual student on multimodal QA samples to accurately recover task-relevant state information from images.
- The second stage, Symbolic-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), uses a frozen text-only teacher, conditioned on symbolic context and ground-truth plans, to provide dense, token-level log-probability supervision to the visual student as it generates on-policy action sequences, encouraging alignment with symbolic planning behavior.

## Results

- MGSD-4B increased the macro average success rate from **11.2%** to **30.5%**, while MGSD-8B improved it from **17.2%** to **35.6%** across FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehaviour benchmarks.
- MGSD-4B outperformed several larger open-source VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B) and proprietary models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-4.5-Haiku) in overall average performance, and MGSD-8B further narrowed the gap to top-tier systems like Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-5.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Cold-Start SFT stage and the Symbolic-Guided OPSD stage are crucial, with the latter boosting overall task success from **17.7%** to **30.5%** and the absence of initial SFT degrading performance to **16.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Establishing a strong perceptual foundation through an initial "cold-start" SFT is critical for reducing early grounding noise and enabling the visual student to reliably recover state structures for effective multi-step guidance.
> - Transferring robust planning behaviors from a privileged symbolic teacher via on-policy distillation is effective in bridging the modality gap, allowing the visual student to learn complex reasoning strategies from explicit symbolic states.
> - The framework achieves balanced improvements in both visual state recovery (perception) and pure planning capability (reasoning), which are both necessary for high end-to-end task success, indicating MGSD addresses the perception-reasoning mismatch.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of multimodal artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the challenges Vision-Language Models (VLMs) encounter in visual spatial planning. While VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in general multimodal understanding, their performance in tasks requiring sequential visual planning remains an area of active research.

The authors identify a "perception–reasoning modality gap" as a core challenge. This gap arises because visual planning necessitates a two-step process: first, inferring task-relevant state structures from raw pixel data (perception), and second, reasoning over these inferred structures to generate a sequence of executable actions (planning). This contrasts with symbolic planning, where objects, constraints, and transitions are explicitly provided, simplifying the reasoning process. This discrepancy creates dual bottlenecks where errors in perception can propagate and corrupt downstream planning, and reasoning failures can occur even with accurate state inference.

Current approaches to visual planning offer partial solutions:
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** for imitating expert action traces often conflates perception and planning failures, making it difficult to pinpoint the source of an incorrect action.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** provides task-level supervision but suffers from sparse and sequence-level rewards, hindering credit assignment to intermediate perception and planning decisions.
- **Visual-centric reasoning methods** (e.g., MVoT, Visual Planning, LatentUM) aim to make reasoning more explicit by introducing intermediate images, visual thoughts, or latent visual states. However, these methods typically operate within the visual or latent modalities and may require additional generation or latent reasoning modules at inference time. They often do not directly utilize the complementary information available in symbolic states, which are more readily solvable in many planning environments.

This paper proposes an alternative by leveraging these symbolic states as privileged supervision during training. This approach differs from standard on-policy distillation, which typically operates within a single modality. Here, a modality-gap-aware variant is introduced, where a symbolic teacher guides an image-conditioned student without exposing symbolic inputs during inference. The work builds upon recent advancements in on-policy distillation, particularly those emphasizing stable local token-level supervision and the importance of cold-start alignment for effective transfer across different information modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the capability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in visual spatial planning by specifically addressing the "perception–reasoning modality gap." The authors identify this gap as comprising two distinct yet interconnected bottlenecks:
1. **Visual State Recovery (Perception):** The difficulty for VLMs to accurately infer task-relevant, actionable state structures (e.g., object locations, environmental constraints, topological information) directly from pixel data.
2. **Multi-Step Planning (Reasoning):** The challenge in generating valid, optimal sequences of actions based on the inferred visual state, even if that state is correctly perceived.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing training paradigms in effectively tackling these dual challenges:
- **Entangled Failures in SFT:** When a VLM makes an incorrect action after SFT, it is often unclear whether the failure originated from a misinterpretation of the visual scene or a flaw in the planning logic. This ambiguity impedes targeted improvement.
- **Sparse Rewards in RLVR:** In complex, multi-step planning tasks, the final reward is often sparse and delayed. This makes it difficult for RL algorithms to assign credit effectively to the specific intermediate decisions in both perception and reasoning that led to the outcome.
- **Modality Confinement of Visual-Centric Methods:** While innovative, many visual-centric reasoning approaches primarily operate within the visual domain or a shared latent space. They do not fully exploit the availability of explicit symbolic states, which inherently simplify the planning problem during training.

Recognizing that symbolic states offer a naturally simpler and more reliable representation for planning problems, the core motivation is to harness this "privileged" information during training. The goal is to transfer the robust planning capabilities derived from explicit symbolic states to a visual student model, without requiring symbolic inputs during inference. This aims to empower the VLM to autonomously perform visual spatial planning by learning to both correctly interpret visual inputs and execute effective planning strategies. By developing a framework that manages this transfer effectively, the authors seek to narrow the performance gap between purely symbolic planning and image-conditioned planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose MGSD (Modality-Gap-Aware Self-Distillation), a two-stage framework designed to bridge the perception–reasoning modality gap in visual spatial planning. The core idea is to leverage readily available symbolic states and ground-truth action plans as privileged supervision during training, while ensuring that the final model operates purely from visual inputs during inference.

Each training instance is represented by a tuple (I, Q, T, A), where I is the visual observation (image), Q is the task-specific planning prompt (question), T is the symbolic state description, and A is the ground-truth executable action plan. The student model receives only (I, Q), while the teacher model is privy to the privileged context (T, Q, A).

**Stage 1: Perception-Oriented Supervised Fine-Tuning (Cold-Start SFT)**
This initial stage aims to equip the visual student with reliable visual state representations, minimizing early perception noise and aligning its visual understanding with the symbolic state space. Instead of directly supervising long-horizon action plans, this stage focuses on training the VLM to recover task-relevant variables from images. These variables include information such as object positions, obstacles, environmental topology, interaction affordances, and legal local actions. This explicit grounding is intended to ensure that the student's initial rollouts are sufficiently accurate to allow for effective multi-step guidance in the subsequent stage.

- **Dataset:** `D_perc` consists of multimodal QA samples, each containing an image `I`, a structured perception question `Q_p`, and a corresponding structured answer `Y_p` derived deterministically from symbolic environment annotations.
- **Objective:** The model is optimized using a standard next-token prediction objective:
 `L_perc(θ) = −E_D_perc [ Σ_{t=1}^{|Y_p|} log π_θ(Y_p,t | X_p, Y_p,<t) ]`, where `X_p = (I, Q_p)`.
- **Outcome:** A perception-adapted model that serves as the initialization for Stage 2.

**Stage 2: Symbolic-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD)**
In this stage, the perception-adapted visual student is further trained to acquire planning capabilities. This is achieved by transferring planning behavior from a privileged symbolic teacher through on-policy self-distillation.

- **Process:**
 1. The visual student, conditioned on an image `I` and a prompt `Q`, generates its own on-policy trajectory (a sequence of actions and intermediate thoughts) `y ∼ π_θ(· | I, Q)`.
 2. A *frozen* text-only teacher model then evaluates the same student-generated prefix `y_<t>` (the sequence of tokens generated up to time `t-1`) under its privileged symbolic context `(T, Q, A)`.
 3. The teacher provides dense, token-level supervision.
- **Loss Function:** The student is optimized using a reverse-KL-style context distillation loss:
 `L_sym(θ) = E_{y∼π_θ} [ Σ_{t=1}^{|y|} w_t (ℓ_s_t − ℓ_T_t) ]`
 where `ℓ_s_t = log π_θ(y_t | I, Q, y_<t>)` is the student's log-probability for token `y_t`, and `ℓ_T_t = log π_T(y_t | T, Q, A, y_<t>)` is the teacher's log-probability. The weights `w_t` are uniformly set to 1 in the experiments.
- **Key Aspects:** This objective provides dense feedback on the student's own rollout distribution, encouraging its image-conditioned predictions to align with the symbolic planning behavior of the teacher. Critically, the teacher and symbolic context are discarded at inference time, ensuring the final model is purely visual.

**Training Procedure:**
The MGSD framework begins by initializing the student VLM. It then undergoes Stage 1 (Cold-Start SFT) using LoRA adapters on structured multimodal QA data. After merging the adapter, the resulting perception-adapted student and a frozen text-only teacher (also initialized from the base VLM) are used for Stage 2 (Symbolic-Guided OPSD). During OPSD, the student samples rollouts from image-conditioned prompts, and the teacher provides token-level distillation signals based on the privileged symbolic context. The student model weights are updated by minimizing the context distillation loss.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Backbones:** Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.
- **Benchmarks:** FrozenLake (safe grid navigation), Maze (topology-aware path finding), and MiniBehaviour (embodied object interaction). These environments cover diverse planning challenges.
- **Training Data:** 18,000 unique environment configurations (6,000 per environment), procedurally generated with strict pairing of visual and symbolic states, ensuring reproducibility, guaranteed solvability, action feasibility, and balanced complexity. The Cold-Start SFT dataset uses structured QA pairs derived from these symbolic annotations.
- **Baselines:** A range of private (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini models) and open-source VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL3-8B, Qwen3-VL variants) were used for comparison. Symbolic-input upper bounds were also established as an oracle reference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MGSD on visual planning benchmarks demonstrated improvements across various metrics and backbones.

**Overall Performance:**
- **Substantial Gains:** MGSD consistently improved task success rates. For the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone, MGSD-4B increased the macro average success rate from 11.2% to 30.5%. Similarly, for the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, MGSD-8B raised the macro average from 17.2% to 35.6%. These gains were observed uniformly across FrozenLake, Maze, and MiniBehaviour, suggesting that the framework enhances the intrinsic visual planning capacity of the base models without altering their inference interface.
- **Competitive Standing:** MGSD-4B outperformed several larger open-source and private VLMs, including Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct, GPT-4o, and Claude-4.5-Haiku in overall average performance. MGSD-8B further reduced the performance gap to high-tier proprietary systems such as Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-5.
- **Narrowing the Symbolic Gap:** Compared to symbolic-input upper bounds (where models receive explicit symbolic state descriptions), MGSD-4B reduced the overall performance gap to 6.4 percentage points, indicating effective transfer of symbolic planning behavior to image-conditioned inference. The residual gap, particularly in MiniBehaviour, was attributed to persistent challenges in visually inferring object affordances and interaction constraints.

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone examined the contribution of MGSD's components:
- **Framework Progression:** Standard baselines like Direct SFT (18.2%) and reinforcement learning via GRPO (16.2%) showed limited improvement over the base model (11.2%). The Cold-Start SFT stage alone improved accuracy to 17.7%, primarily by establishing spatial grounding. However, the subsequent Symbolic-Guided OPSD stage was critical, leading to the full performance of 30.5%, affirming the efficacy of transferring reasoning behavior from a privileged symbolic teacher on student-generated trajectories.
- **Design Choices:**
 - **Necessity of Cold-Start:** Removing the initial perception alignment (termed "w/o Cold Start") reduced accuracy to 16.8%, validating the hypothesis that reliable state recovery is a prerequisite for effective planning behavior absorption.
 - **Importance of Ground Truth:** Withholding reference action plans from the teacher ("w/o Ground Truth") resulted in a performance drop to 21.3%, indicating that a focused distillation signal from the exact target plan is crucial for effective guidance.
 - **Stability of Frozen Teacher:** Replacing the frozen teacher with an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) update ("w/ EMA Teacher") degraded performance to 19.5%, suggesting that a strictly frozen teacher provides a more stable optimization target across the modality gap.

**Diagnostic Analysis: Decoupling Perception and Planning:**
A diagnostic framework was employed to distinguish between failures in visual state recovery (perception) and failures in generating valid action sequences (reasoning). This involved three metrics:
- **State F1:** Measures visual state recovery accuracy (e.g., correctly identifying player, goal, and hazard coordinates).
- **Plan on GT:** Measures pure planning capability by providing the model with ground-truth symbolic states (no images).
- **E2E Acc.:** Standard end-to-end visual-to-action performance.

- **Findings:**
 - Base VLMs exhibited weaknesses in both perception and reasoning.
 - The Cold-Start SFT stage significantly improved visual state recovery (State F1) but led to a degradation in pure reasoning capabilities (Plan on GT), indicating a mismatch between improved perception and subsequent multi-step execution.
 - An upper-bound sanity check ("Base + GT State"), where the base model was given flawless symbolic perception, still capped at a 35.2% success rate, confirming inherent planning bottlenecks.
 - MGSD effectively addressed this perception-reasoning gap. It maintained competitive state recovery while simultaneously achieving the highest pure reasoning capability among all trained models, leading to the highest end-to-end accuracy. This suggested that MGSD learned to both perceive actionable states effectively and reason logically over them by supervising student-generated visual prefixes with a privileged symbolic teacher.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MGSD represents a contribution to the field of visual spatial planning by offering a structured approach to address the perception–reasoning modality gap. The framework's ability to significantly improve visual planning performance across different VLM backbones, coupled with its consistent gains across diverse planning benchmarks (FrozenLake, Maze, MiniBehaviour), highlights its effectiveness.

**Key Contributions and Implications:**
- **Framework for Bridging Modality Gap:** MGSD provides a two-stage method that systematically tackles both visual state recovery and optimal-path reasoning. This approach is demonstrated to strengthen a model's capacity to interpret actionable states from pixels and subsequently plan over the inferred structures.
- **Effective Use of Privileged Supervision:** The research validates the strategy of leveraging symbolic state information during training as a form of privileged supervision. This allows for the distillation of complex planning behaviors into a purely visual model, without requiring symbolic inputs at inference time. This is a practical consideration for real-world deployment where explicit symbolic states may not always be available.
- **Diagnostic Insights:** The causal decomposition diagnostic framework offers a method for understanding the individual contributions of perception and reasoning to overall task success. The results from this analysis provide evidence that MGSD effectively balances these two components, avoiding pitfalls where improved perception does not translate to improved reasoning, or vice-versa. This deeper understanding can guide future research in VLM development for planning tasks.
- **Performance Advancement:** The models trained with MGSD demonstrate competitive performance against significantly larger open-source and private VLMs, and substantially narrow the gap to symbolic-input upper bounds. This indicates progress toward achieving robust visual planning capabilities comparable to those attainable with explicit symbolic representations.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Data Dependency:** MGSD relies on the availability of paired visual and symbolic training data. While feasible in simulator-based environments, acquiring such data for open-world visual planning tasks could be challenging.
- **Scope of Evaluation:** The current evaluation focuses on structured tasks with discrete action spaces and static environments. The framework's applicability to continuous control, dynamic scenes, partial observability, or complex long-horizon real-world manipulation tasks remains to be fully explored.
- **Ambiguity and Distribution Shift:** While MGSD improves visual state recovery and reasoning, it does not guarantee faithful intermediate reasoning under severe visual ambiguity or significant distribution shifts.

Future work could investigate extending MGSD to scenarios involving noisy or automatically extracted symbolic states, and explore methods for stronger intermediate-state verification to enhance robustness in more challenging, less constrained environments.
