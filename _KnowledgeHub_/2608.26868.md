# CGS-SLAM: Collaborative Gaussian Splatting based SLAM for Multi-Agent Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> CGS-SLAM is a hybrid decentralized-centralized multi-agent SLAM system that builds metric 3D Gaussian Splatting maps from only RGB and IMU data. Each agent tracks with IMU pre-integration and back-projects Depth Pro metric depth; agents swap keyframe encodings to densify overlapping regions, and a server aligns submaps with VGGT in under 10 seconds, matching RGB-D rendering quality.

## Problem

- Most 3DGS SLAM systems depend on RGB-D or LiDAR depth priors, which the ruggedized consumer smartphones used by tactical teams simply do not provide.
- Relative monocular depth estimators drift in scale across frames, causing temporal inconsistency and catastrophic map collapse in RGB-only Gaussian Splatting SLAM pipelines.
- Existing collaborative neural SLAM assumes RGB-D input, GNSS availability, or heavy inter-agent consensus traffic, none viable in bandwidth-limited GNSS-denied indoor environments.

## Method

- ==Visual-inertial front-end== chains ==IMU pre-integration== into a pose prior, then refines it frame-to-model with an ==opacity-masked loss== combining L1 photometric error and a Pearson-correlation depth term.
- **CGS-SLAM**'s mapping back-projects ==Depth Pro== metric monocular depth from the very first frame, giving scale-consistent ==3D Gaussians== optimized by photometric plus D-SSIM plus depth losses with ==sliding-window bundle adjustment==.
- ==Dynamic keyframing== broadcasts compact keyframe encodings via the server so agents densify keyframes in overlap regions; the server aligns submaps through ==NetVLAD== matching, ==VGGT== extrinsics, then Adam refinement.

## Results

- Average TUM ATE-RMSE is 7.24 cm, best among 3DGS methods, against 7.97 cm for RGB-D Magic-SLAM and 49.67 cm for RGB-only MM3DGS.
- Rendering beats MM3DGS on every evaluated sequence, for example 24.51 vs 21.58 dB PSNR on Fast-Straight and 19.44 vs 14.73 dB on fr1/desk.
- IMU pre-integration cuts Fast-straight ATE from 12.85 to 6.07 cm; disabling dynamic keyframing degrades global alignment ATE by 43% and 72% on two multi-agent configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Metric monocular depth can replace active depth sensing in dense SLAM: scale consistency across frames, not raw depth precision, is what prevents map collapse.
> - Sharing fixed-size keyframe descriptors instead of Gaussians buys most of the multi-agent alignment gain at under 130 KB/s uplink per agent.
> - Feed-forward view alignment models reduce submap merging to seconds, but heavy depth backbones still require server offloading, so fully onboard deployment remains unsolved.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a fundamental problem in robotics and computer vision, enabling an agent to build a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously determining its own location within it. The broader research landscape for SLAM is characterized by several key trends and challenges that CGS-SLAM addresses:

**Traditional SLAM Limitations:** Many established SLAM systems rely on explicit depth priors, obtained either from dedicated depth sensors (e.g., LiDAR, Time-of-Flight cameras) or through dense Structure-from-Motion (SfM) techniques. These approaches can be computationally intensive and impractical for deployment on resource-constrained platforms, such as consumer-grade smartphones, which typically feature only RGB cameras and Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). Furthermore, traditional output representations like point clouds or meshes can exhibit topological incompleteness and be challenging to interpret in scenarios of sparse exploration.

**Emergence of Neural Representations:** Recent advancements have introduced neural representations, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by Kerbl et al., as a novel primitive for scene reconstruction. 3DGS offers photorealistic rendering at high frame rates and a continuous, opacity-weighted formulation that does not require explicit surface decisions in unobserved regions. While several works have integrated 3DGS into SLAM pipelines, many remain dependent on RGB-D inputs. RGB-only 3DGS SLAM methods exist, but they often struggle with consistent relative monocular depth estimation, leading to scale inconsistencies and map degradation.

**Challenges in Monocular SLAM:** The absence of direct depth measurements in monocular setups necessitates alternative solutions. Early monocular SLAM approaches relied on feature-based visual methods but often lacked robustness in challenging environments. The integration of Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) models has improved geometric coherence, but global scale consistency and temporal consistency across frames due to illumination changes or motion blur remain significant issues.

**Complexity of Collaborative SLAM:** Multi-agent or collaborative SLAM, where multiple agents jointly map an environment, presents additional complexities. These include inter-agent data association, communication constraints (especially in degraded network conditions), and robust map merging. While some collaborative systems use traditional map representations, few leverage neural representations. Existing 3DGS-based multi-agent SLAM approaches often require RGB-D inputs, assume GNSS availability, or involve intensive communication for global consensus and continuous refinement, which limits their applicability in constrained operational scenarios.

CGS-SLAM positions itself within this landscape by specifically targeting the development of a collaborative 3DGS SLAM system that overcomes the limitations of RGB-D dependence, computational overhead, and communication intensity, while providing robust, scale-consistent mapping for resource-constrained monocular-inertial platforms in GNSS-denied environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for CGS-SLAM arises from the operational requirements of high-stakes scenarios such as hostage rescue, disaster response, or reconnaissance missions. In these contexts, law enforcement and military units operate in unfamiliar, GNSS-denied environments under severe time constraints. They require rapid mapping systems that can fuse the local contributions of multiple, concurrently operating teams into a globally consistent, shared environmental representation to facilitate effective decision-making.

The existing technological gaps that CGS-SLAM aims to address include:
- **Sensor Constraints:** Tactical units typically employ ruggedized consumer-grade smartphones, which are limited to RGB cameras and IMUs, lacking dedicated depth sensors like LiDAR or Time-of-Flight cameras. This sensor limitation restricts the applicability of many existing SLAM solutions.
- **Computational Limitations:** Many current SLAM systems, particularly those relying on dense depth priors or SfM, incur significant computational overhead, making them unsuitable for live operation on resource-constrained portable platforms.
- **Map Representation Issues:** Traditional point cloud or mesh-based reconstructions are often incomplete and difficult to interpret when exploration is sparse. While 3DGS offers photorealistic representations and fast rendering, its integration into monocular, collaborative, and resource-constrained SLAM has not been fully explored.

In response to these motivations, the key objectives of the CGS-SLAM project are:
1. **Develop a Monocular-Inertial Local SLAM System:** To produce metrically scaled 3DGS maps from the very first frame using only RGB and inertial data. This involves leveraging a state-of-the-art metric monocular depth estimation (MMDE) model to overcome the inherent scale ambiguity of monocular vision and integrating IMU data as a robust motion prior.
2. **Establish a Lightweight Collaborative Framework:** To enable multi-agent operation in constrained network environments. This framework is designed for low communication cost, facilitating the exchange of keyframe encodings among agents and a central server. It also incorporates dynamic keyframing capabilities to enhance submap alignment by increasing sampling density in overlapping regions.
3. **Implement a Rapid Submap Alignment Process:** To fuse individual local maps into a globally consistent representation quickly. This involves using a view alignment model, VGGT, to estimate initial rigid transformations between submaps, followed by gradient-based optimization for refinement.
4. **Create a Hybrid Decentralized/Centralized Architecture:** To balance local autonomy with global consistency, allowing agents to perform independent local reconstruction while the server handles global map fusion and coordination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CGS-SLAM employs a hybrid decentralized-centralized architecture designed for multi-agent Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) based SLAM using only RGB and inertial data.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The system comprises individual agents and a central server. Each agent operates autonomously, performing local tracking and reconstructing a local 3DGS map. Periodically, agents exchange keyframe encodings via the central server, which facilitates dynamic keyframing and, upon completion of exploration, aligns and merges individual submaps into a globally consistent map.

#### 3.2. Environment Perception (Agent-side)

- **Sensor Fusion (Visual-Inertial SLAM):** To obtain robust camera pose estimates without dedicated depth sensors, CGS-SLAM integrates data from RGB cameras and Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs). IMU data (accelerometers and gyroscopes) are used as a motion prior to compute an initial pose estimate for each new RGB frame. This involves chaining relative transformations through IMU preintegration across all IMU measurements up to the timestamp of the new RGB frame (Eq. 1 in the paper, detailed in Supplementary Material C). This approach aims to provide more accurate pose priors compared to a constant velocity assumption, especially during rapid camera movements or at low frame rates.
- **Monocular Depth Estimation (MMDE):** To circumvent the need for scale recovery common in monocular systems, a metric monocular depth estimation (MMDE) model, Depth Pro, is employed. Depth Pro provides consistent, at-scale depth predictions. At each selected keyframe, depth is inferred by this model and integrated into the SLAM pipeline, providing a metric reference for the 3DGS map initialization and reconstruction. This allows for online operation from the first frame while maintaining geometric consistency.

#### 3.3. Local Reconstruction (Agent-side)

Each agent independently constructs its local 3DGS map using synchronized RGB video and IMU data.
- **Tracking:** The camera pose is optimized in a frame-to-model manner against the current Gaussian map. The tracking loss function (Eq. 3) combines an L1 photometric loss (L_photo) between the ground truth and rendered image with a depth loss (L_depth) based on the Pearson correlation coefficient (Eq. 2). The Pearson correlation coefficient is chosen for its reported benefit over L1 depth terms in handling variations in predicted depth range. A masking function (Eq. 4) ensures that only high-opacity regions (>0.99) contribute to the loss, enhancing optimization stability. For the first frame, no tracking is applied, and an identity matrix is used as the initial camera transformation.
- **Mapping:**
 - **Initialization:** Upon the first frame, pixels are back-projected into 3D space using the metric depth estimate from Depth Pro, providing a scale-consistent initialization of the scene geometry. The 3DGS representation uses parametric Gaussian distributions (Supplementary Material A).
 - **Keyframe Selection:** New keyframes are selected based on a multi-criterion information gain metric balancing geometric coverage, temporal consistency, and computational efficiency. Conditions for keyframe insertion include:
 1. **Spatial Displacement:** A minimum frame interval (s frames) and a minimum number of frames (k frames) since the last keyframe to prevent redundant insertions.
 2. **Sufficient Information Gain:** Evaluated by rendering a depth map from the latest keyframe and checking if the fraction of co-visible Gaussians under the current camera pose is below a predefined threshold (90%).
 3. **Dynamic Keyframing:** Agents receive keyframe encodings from other agents via the server. If an incoming frame's encoding shows high similarity to these, indicating a spatial overlap, the keyframe sampling density is dynamically increased in that region to improve global consistency during map fusion.
 - **Map Optimization:** Keyframes are maintained within a sliding window. Periodic optimization of the Gaussian representation is performed using a composite loss function (Eq. 5), which includes L1 photometric loss (L_photo), structural dissimilarity index (D-SSIM) for perceptual regularization, and depth loss (L_depth). A local bundle adjustment jointly refines camera poses and Gaussian parameters within this sliding window.

#### 3.4. Global Consistency (Server-side)

Once an agent completes local exploration, its local map (RGB keyframes, Gaussian parameters) is transmitted to the server for global reconstruction.
- **Dynamic Keyframing (Server's Role):** The server continuously caches NetVLAD keyframe encodings from all active agents and broadcasts them to all connected clients (Supplementary Material B). This allows agents to locally bias their keyframe distribution towards overlapping regions, enhancing keyframe association and pose refinement during global alignment.
- **Submap Alignment:**
 - **Keyframe Association:** The server identifies pairs of co-visible keyframes across distinct local maps using NetVLAD visual descriptors, assuming visually similar frames indicate overlapping regions.
 - **Pose Estimation with VGGT:** For each candidate keyframe pair and its neighbors (6 frames per submap), VGGT is used to obtain camera extrinsic matrices. These extrinsics, combined with locally tracked poses, provide an initial estimation of a rigid transformation (T_S_1_S_2) to align the second submap into the first's coordinate frame. This involves a two-stage Procrustes analysis (Supplementary Material E).
 - **Refinement:** The initial transformation (R, t) (scale component from VGGT is discarded as agent maps are already metric-scaled) is refined via gradient-based optimization (Adam) over the full set of neighboring keyframes. The optimization minimizes the geometric error between corresponding camera poses (Eq. 6). A final residual rigid correction further refines this pose-based estimate by minimizing depth discrepancy between the two sub-maps rendered from a common keyframe pose using a differentiable rasterizer.
- **Merging Submaps:** The optimal transformation T_S_1_S_2 is applied to every Gaussian in the second client's model, bringing its entire submap into the reference frame of the first client (Eq. 7). Local Gaussian maps are incrementally merged into a global map, without further refinement, using the first completed local map as the reference frame.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments were performed on a consumer-grade laptop (Lenovo ThinkPad T15g Gen 1) for client-side operations (10 FPS, ~1 GB VRAM) and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 for the server. Depth Pro was offloaded to the server due to its computational intensity (~169 ms per keyframe). Communication costs for keyframe encodings were low (<130 KB/s uplink per agent), suitable for degraded network conditions. Final maps ranged from 25 to 75 MB.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental evaluations on multiple datasets to demonstrate the performance of CGS-SLAM across single-agent and multi-agent scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance of Monocular Single Agent Exploration

- **Depth Model Effectiveness:** The integration of the metric monocular depth estimation (MMDE) model, Depth Pro, was shown to provide qualitative improvements over noisy consumer-grade LiDAR data and other MDE methods like MiDaS. CGS-SLAM's depth maps exhibited reduced linear distortion and superior consistency (Fig. 2).
- **Scale Consistency:** CGS-SLAM demonstrated the ability to accurately predict the scale of the trajectory, closely matching the ground truth from the very first frame. This contrasts with methods like MM3DGS, which often exhibit significant scale discrepancies without external scale calibration (Fig. 3).
- **Tracking Accuracy:** On the TUM dataset, CGS-SLAM achieved competitive Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE-RMSE) compared to state-of-the-art RGB-D SLAM methods, such as Magic-SLAM, and outperformed other monocular 3DGS SLAM systems like MM3DGS (Table 1). For example, on the fr1/desk sequence, CGS-SLAM achieved an ATE-RMSE of 4.2 cm, comparable to Magic-SLAM's 4.71 cm (RGB-D).
- **Rendering Quality:** CGS-SLAM consistently outperformed MM3DGS in rendering fidelity across all evaluated sequences (TUM and UT-MM datasets), measured by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics. It also achieved rendering quality comparable to RGB-D baselines, with Magic-SLAM occasionally showing higher fidelity on specific scenes (Table 2, Fig. 4). This indicates that the MMDE model effectively substitutes direct depth sensing while maintaining high visual quality.
- **IMU Pre-integration Impact:** The integration of IMU pre-integration substantially improved tracking performance, particularly in sequences with rapid motions. On the "Fast-straight" sequence, IMU integration reduced ATE by 53% (from 12.85 cm to 6.07 cm) and improved PSNR by 2.73 dB compared to a constant velocity baseline. Notably, CGS-SLAM with a constant velocity assumption still outperformed MM3DGS with IMU integration on some sequences, suggesting independent contributions from the proposed spatial alignment and map initialization (Table 3). The combination of IMU pre-integration and the CGS-SLAM initialization yielded the best overall performance.
- **Alternative MMDE Evaluation (Supplementary):** An alternative MMDE model, Depth Anything v3 (DA3), was evaluated as a potential replacement for both Depth Pro and VGGT. While DA3 showed competitive results on smaller TUM sequences, its performance degraded significantly on larger UT-MM scenes and failed on one sequence (Square-2), justifying the continued use of the dual-model approach (Depth Pro for MMDE, VGGT for alignment) (Table S2, Fig. S2).
- **Replica Dataset (Supplementary):** Testing on the Replica Multiagent dataset without IMU data (constant velocity assumption) showed bimodal performance. While some sequences yielded results comparable to IMU-based runs, others diverged significantly, particularly in scenarios involving near-field obstacles, where the monocular depth estimation struggled with synthetic imagery without the IMU prior (Table S3, Fig. S3, S4).

#### 4.2. Performance on Global Reconstruction

- **Submap Alignment Accuracy:** Using the UT-MM dataset as a multi-agent benchmark, CGS-SLAM successfully aligned trajectories from independently captured runs within a shared coordinate frame. The method achieved sub-meter spatial alignment with the ground truth (translation error < 0.40 m) even with substantial heading differences between agents. The system demonstrated functionality with more than two agents exploring the same scene (Fig. 5).
- **Alignment Speed:** The alignment process was rapid, completing in less than 10 seconds for a global map upon receiving all submaps. For example, aligning the "Square-1" and "Fast-Straight" submaps took approximately 5 seconds per pair, including NetVLAD matching, VGGT inference, and refinement.
- **Dynamic Keyframing Contribution:** The dynamic keyframing mechanism, which biases keyframe distribution in overlapping regions, was shown to improve global map alignment. Disabling this feature resulted in significant degradation of global ATE, with increases of 43% and 72% in two of the three evaluated multi-agent configurations (Table 4). This indicates its effectiveness in enhancing global consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CGS-SLAM system represents an advancement in the field of SLAM, particularly for multi-agent applications in challenging environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling SLAM on Resource-Constrained Devices:** CGS-SLAM addresses a critical limitation by providing a robust SLAM solution that relies solely on RGB cameras and IMUs, standard sensors found in consumer-grade smartphones. This eliminates the dependency on specialized, often unavailable, depth sensors like LiDAR or Time-of-Flight cameras, expanding the accessibility and deployability of high-fidelity 3D mapping technology.
- **Improved Mapping for High-Stakes Operations:** The system is designed to meet the demands of time-critical and GNSS-denied scenarios, such as hostage rescue, disaster response, and reconnaissance. By providing rapid, globally consistent, and photorealistic 3D maps from multiple agents, it can significantly enhance situational awareness and decision-making capabilities for operational units.
- **High-Quality Photorealistic Reconstruction:** The adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting, combined with a metric monocular depth estimation model, results in photorealistic and scale-consistent 3D environmental reconstructions. These representations are generally easier to interpret than traditional point clouds or meshes, especially in situations with sparse coverage, offering a more intuitive understanding of the mapped environment.
- **Efficient and Scalable Collaborative Mapping:** The hybrid decentralized-centralized architecture and lightweight communication framework enable effective collaboration among multiple agents with minimal bandwidth requirements. The dynamic keyframing mechanism further optimizes the process by biasing keyframe selection in overlapping areas, leading to enhanced submap alignment and global consistency without the need for intensive, tightly-coupled bundle adjustments. The rapid submap alignment process ensures that a global map can be formed almost immediately after local explorations are completed.
- **Foundation for Future Autonomy and Integration:** The research establishes a foundation for future developments in autonomous systems. The stated aim to integrate depth estimation and camera pose estimation into a single, computationally lighter model, enabling fully onboard execution on consumer smartphones, suggests a path towards democratizing high-quality 3D mapping and real-time spatial intelligence for a broader range of applications. This could extend its impact beyond specialized operational scenarios to general consumer applications involving AR/VR, robotics, and environmental understanding.
