# Dynamic Modeling and MPC for Locomotion of Tendon-Driven Soft Quadruped

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IIT Gandhinagar and SNIST developed a framework combining Cosserat rod theory for soft leg modeling with a decoupled whole-body dynamic model and real-time convex Model Predictive Control. Their approach accurately predicted soft limb deformation (e.g., tip RMSE 0.89mm) and achieved stable, perturbation-resilient locomotion in a tendon-driven soft quadruped robot during walking and omnidirectional gaits, utilizing only four actuators.

## Problem

- Soft robots exhibit large, nonlinear deformations that are difficult to accurately model and control in real-time.
- Existing soft quadruped robots often lack physics-based leg modeling integrated with real-time, model-predictive whole-body control, especially for tendon-driven systems with minimal actuators.
- High-fidelity modeling methods (e.g., FEM) are computationally expensive for real-time control, while simpler models sacrifice physical accuracy.

## Method

- Developed a physics-informed single-leg model using discrete Cosserat rod theory to accurately capture large deformations and tendon actuation.
- Introduced a modular and decoupled whole-body dynamics framework that integrates the compliant leg models with a rigid torso for computational efficiency.
- Implemented a convex ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) scheme, running on embedded hardware, to optimize ground reaction forces and map them to tendon actuation for stable locomotion.

## Results

- The Cosserat rod model accurately predicted single-leg deformation, showing RMSE values from **0.89** mm (tip) to **5.72** mm, achieving over **95%** accuracy for representative points.
- MPC-driven walking gait maintained desired body height (0.04m) and forward velocity (**0.08** m/s), with an average lateral RMSE of **1.4** cm over an **0.8** m forward displacement in experiments.
- The control system demonstrated asymptotic stability and recursive feasibility, recovering from various deterministic perturbations (e.g., roll, pitch, velocity errors) within **14.553** seconds with zero infeasible optimization steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity continuum mechanics models, specifically Cosserat rod theory, can be made computationally efficient enough for real-time control on embedded hardware for soft robots.
> - A modular, decoupled approach effectively integrates compliant leg dynamics with rigid-body torso dynamics, offering a scalable and reusable framework for soft-rigid hybrid robots.
> - Convex Model Predictive Control enables stable, adaptive, and perturbation-resilient locomotion for complex soft robotic systems using minimal actuation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and long-standing challenge in the field of soft robotics: the accurate modeling and real-time control of compliant systems exhibiting large, nonlinear deformations and dynamic interactions with complex environments. Soft robots offer compelling advantages, such as intrinsic safety for human interaction and adaptability to unstructured terrains, but their inherent flexibility makes them notoriously difficult to model and control effectively.

The paper first surveys the limitations of existing modeling approaches for soft robots. Traditional methods, including rigid-body dynamics, constant-curvature assumptions, and lumped-mass-spring models, are computationally efficient but fail to capture the complex, large-deformation behaviors critical for soft robotic limbs, especially during dynamic tasks like locomotion. While high-fidelity methods like the Finite Element Method (FEM) offer physical accuracy, their high computational cost renders them impractical for real-time control applications in mobile robotics. Similarly, particle-based models lack physical realism, and the Boundary Element Method (BEM) is constrained by assumptions of homogeneous interior properties. Constitutive models, despite accurately capturing material properties, also struggle with the balance between detail and real-time performance. Even advanced machine learning techniques, such as supervised learning and reinforcement learning, often demand extensive datasets or training times and can face generalization challenges.

The authors identify Cosserat rod theory as a promising high-fidelity framework for slender deformable structures, capable of capturing bending, twisting, and stretching modes essential for soft limbs during dynamic gait cycles. However, they highlight a specific gap: while some soft and quasi-soft quadruped robots exist, most focus on demonstrating novel actuation mechanisms or basic locomotion capabilities. These platforms typically do not prioritize physics-based leg modeling integrated with real-time, model-predictive whole-body control, particularly for tendon-driven systems with a minimal actuator count.

This work positions itself as a significant contribution by bridging this gap. It introduces a comprehensive framework that combines Cosserat rod-based modeling of compliant TPU legs with a decoupled whole-body formulation and real-time convex Model Predictive Control (MPC), all implemented on embedded hardware. By focusing on a tendon-driven soft quadruped with only four actuators, the research tackles the underexplored area of achieving stable and accurate locomotion through first-principles dynamics and control, thereby advancing scalable and reusable modeling and control methods for soft quadruped robots. It aims to demonstrate that high-fidelity physics-informed models, when properly formulated, can be efficient enough for real-time control, a crucial step towards robust and autonomous soft robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent challenges in effectively modeling and controlling soft robots, especially quadruped platforms designed for locomotion. While soft robots offer unique advantages in terms of safety and adaptability, their highly nonlinear, large deformation behaviors make them difficult to manage with conventional techniques. The authors specifically point out the lack of physics-based leg modeling integrated with real-time, model-predictive whole-body control in existing soft quadruped platforms, particularly those with a minimal actuator count. This gap necessitates the development of frameworks that can provide both physical fidelity and computational efficiency.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this work are clearly defined:

1. **Develop a Physics-Informed Modeling Framework:** To create a detailed and accurate model for the tendon-driven soft TPU legs of the SLOT quadruped robot. This involves leveraging discrete Cosserat rod theory to capture large deformations, distributed elasticity, tendon actuation, and ground contact interactions without sacrificing physical fidelity.
2. **Introduce a Modular Whole-Body Modeling Framework:** To integrate the compliant leg dynamics with the rigid-body dynamics of the robot's torso. This modular, decoupled approach aims to provide a scalable interface that allows for efficient whole-body simulation and real-time control by representing leg dynamics through physically consistent reaction forces.
3. **Implement a Real-Time Model Predictive Control (MPC) Framework:** To embed the proposed soft-rigid hybrid dynamics into a convex MPC scheme. This controller is designed to optimize ground reaction forces over a prediction horizon and map these forces to tendon actuation using a physics-informed force-angle relationship, thereby achieving stable and accurate locomotion under diverse perturbations.
4. **Experimentally Validate the Framework:** To rigorously test the proposed modeling and control strategy on a physical prototype (SLOT) during various gaits (crawling, walking, omnidirectional). This validation aims to demonstrate high accuracy in predicting soft limb deformation and robot locomotion dynamics, confirming the framework's robustness and generalizability.
5. **Advance Scalable and Reusable Methods:** Ultimately, the work seeks to establish a generalizable approach for integrating continuum soft legs into model-based locomotion control, contributing to the development of more robust, adaptive, and autonomous soft quadruped robots for real-world applications.

These objectives collectively aim to overcome the computational and modeling complexities associated with soft legged robots, paving the way for more practical and high-performing compliant robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-faceted methodology encompassing robot platform design, single-leg modeling, whole-body dynamics, model predictive control, and experimental validation.

**Robot Platform (SLOT):**
The robot under study, SLOT (Soft Legged Omnidirectional Tetrapod), is a tendon-driven soft quadruped with a rigid cuboid torso and four 3D-printed flexible TPU legs. It is designed for compliant, omnidirectional locomotion using only four Dynamixel XL430 servo motors. SLOT has a mass of 2.16 kg, with the torso weighing 2.0 kg and each leg 0.04 kg. The robot operates autonomously with all computation onboard an NVIDIA Jetson Xavier, using an Intel RealSense D435 camera for visual SLAM and orientation estimation. It employs handcrafted gaits for various movements, including crawling, walking, turning, and omnidirectional motion, demonstrating capabilities like climbing inclines and maintaining stability under disturbances.

**Single-Leg Dynamic Model (Cosserat Rod Theory):**
Each compliant leg is modeled using discrete Cosserat rod theory, which captures large, nonlinear deformations with high fidelity.
- **Assumptions:** Each leg is treated as a planar Cosserat rod deforming in the XZ plane to enable real-time simulation. It is discretized into N=31 nodes along a length of L=0.19 m, with a tapered thickness. The TPU material is assumed to be homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic (Young’s modulus E = 1x10^7 Pa, density ρ = 1200 kg/m³). Actuation is achieved via a tendon routed through a fixed virtual pulley. Ground interaction is modeled with a penalty-based spring-damper system for vertical contact forces and Coulomb friction.
- **Formulation:** The continuous Cosserat rod PDEs are spatially discretized into a finite-dimensional system of Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs). Internal elastic forces (axial and bending) and moments are calculated from relative strains and orientations between adjacent nodes. These are then assembled into nodal force and torque balances using Newton-Euler equations.
- **Model Calibration:** Geometric properties are directly from CAD. Material density and initial Young's modulus are from manufacturer specifications and refined through quasi-static single-leg deflection tests. Ground contact parameters (stiffness, damping, friction) are chosen based on standard penalty-based contact modeling. An empirical force-angle relationship, relating servo pulling angle to vertical ground reaction force, is derived from independent single-leg experiments.
- **Force-Moment Balance and Numerical Simulation:** The dynamics of each node are governed by Newton-Euler equations, considering internal forces (axial, bending), tendon tension (time-varying profile), ground contact and friction, restoring forces to the initial configuration, and linear damping. Velocities and positions are updated using explicit Euler integration with a small timestep (Δt = 1x10^-4 s) for numerical stability.

**SLOT Whole-Body Modeling (Modular Coupling):**
The full SLOT system integrates the individual leg dynamics with torso motion.
- **Assumptions:** The torso is modeled as a rigid cuboid with six degrees of freedom. Each of the four legs is independently modeled as a planar Cosserat rod and rigidly attached to the torso at predefined corner locations. Ground contact forces are assumed to be applied only at the leg tips.
- **Dynamics and Modular Coupling:** A decoupled modular approach is adopted for computational efficiency, justified by the large mass disparity between the torso and individual legs, and relatively low angular velocities during gaits. Each leg is simulated independently, and its reaction forces and torques at the attachment points are aggregated to update the torso's translational and rotational dynamics. Torso rotational dynamics are simplified by neglecting precession and nutation terms for small angular velocities.

**Model Predictive Control (MPC):**
An MPC framework ensures stable, responsive, and adaptive locomotion.
- **Control Architecture:** A hierarchical feedback loop. VSLAM-based state estimator provides robot state (position, orientation, velocities). MPC computes optimal ground reaction forces. A leg model converts these forces to joint-level tendon actuation angles. Low-level PID controllers track these angles.
- **Gait Phase Scheduler:** Predefined walking (two-phase) and crawling (four-phase) gaits are used to schedule leg activation, modulating friction coefficients and control constraints.
- **Cost Function and Constraints:** The MPC minimizes a quadratic cost function over a prediction horizon (H=15 steps, 0.495 s). The cost penalizes deviations from a reference trajectory (state weighting matrix Q) and large control inputs (control weighting matrix R). Constraints include maximum normal force per leg, total vertical force balance, and friction cone limits for each leg.
- **Solver and Real-Time Loop:** The MPC problem is formulated as a convex quadratic program and solved using the Splitting Conic Solver (SCS) via the CVXPY framework. The MPC operates at a control frequency of 30 Hz on the onboard NVIDIA Jetson Xavier platform. The entire hardware-in-the-loop pipeline is implemented using ROS2.
- **Leg Model and Low-Level Control:** The empirical force-angle relation, derived from single-leg Cosserat rod simulations, maps desired normal forces to servo pulling angles. A PID controller then tracks these desired joint angles.
- **Stability Analysis:** Recursive feasibility is assessed using a Dynamic Constraint Map (DCM). Stability and robustness are evaluated through perturbation tests (e.g., initial roll, pitch, height, velocity errors, combined perturbations, and continuous noise) over a 25-second simulation horizon, monitoring cost trajectories, settling times, and infeasible optimization steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling results from both model validation and MPC-driven locomotion experiments, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed physics-informed framework.

**A. Model Validation:**

- **Single-Leg Validation:** The Cosserat rod model accurately predicted the vertical deformation patterns of a single TPU leg under tendon actuation. Tracking four representative points along the leg (fixed base, 1/3, 2/3 length, and tip) showed strong agreement with experimental data. RMSE values ranged from 0.89 mm (at the tip) to 5.72 mm (at 1/3 length), with overall accuracy exceeding 95% across all points. The highest NRMSE at the leg tip (19.22%) was attributed to normalization over a very small total displacement rather than significant absolute error.
- **Whole-Body Locomotion Validation:**
 - **Forward Walking Gait:** The simulation of a two-phase forward walking gait closely matched experimental trends for the robot's center of mass (CoM) trajectories. The RMSE for the x-direction CoM was 4.0 mm, and for the z-direction CoM was 4.8 mm, confirming realistic predictions of forward propulsion and vertical body support.
 - **Crawling Gait:** The four-phase crawling gait simulation also accurately reproduced the robot's behavior, showing consistent ground contact and compliant vertical modulation. The RMSE for the x-direction CoM was 5.1 mm, and for the z-direction CoM was 4.6 mm, demonstrating the model's versatility across different gaits.

**B. MPC-Driven Locomotion: Feasibility and Stability:**

- **Walking Gait Performance:** The MPC controller successfully regulated the SLOT robot's locomotion during walking. It achieved a desired body height of 0.04 meters and a forward velocity of 0.08 m/s, while maintaining near-zero mean lateral velocity throughout the gait cycle. Experimental validation showed stable forward locomotion, with a maximum lateral deviation of 3.2 cm over an 0.8 m forward displacement (average lateral RMSE of 1.4 cm). This minor lateral drift was attributed to unmodeled ground friction asymmetries and hardware tolerances, which did not compromise local stability or vertical support.
- **Omnidirectional Gait Performance:** The MPC framework also successfully enabled a 60-degree omnidirectional gait in simulation. The robot's longitudinal velocity (v_x) accurately tracked a commanded 0.052 m/s, and lateral velocity (v_y) tracked a commanded 0.09 m/s. The overall simulated body trajectory aligned with the desired 60-degree diagonal path with an RMSE of 3.5 cm.
- **Stability under Perturbations:** A comprehensive stability analysis under various perturbations (Baseline, Roll, Pitch, Height, Velocity, Combined, and Noise) was conducted.
 - All deterministic perturbations (Baseline, Roll, Pitch, Height, Velocity, Combined) achieved zero final cost and settled within 14.553 seconds, confirming asymptotic stability and recursive feasibility (zero infeasible optimization steps). The controller demonstrated robust recovery from significant initial errors in position, orientation, and velocity.
 - The "Noise" perturbation (continuous Gaussian-distributed disturbances) resulted in persistent oscillations, preventing the system from reaching a zero final cost, as expected for an ongoing disturbance rather than an initial perturbation.
- **Dynamic Constraint Map (DCM):** Visualization of the DCM confirmed consistent feasibility for the walking gait across all time steps and variables. For the omnidirectional gait, brief instances of overall system infeasibility were observed (at 22.374 s and 61.875 s). In such cases, the controller successfully reverted to the most recent feasible control input, preventing divergence and ensuring continued operation.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The control pipeline, running on an NVIDIA Jetson Xavier, demonstrated real-time feasibility. Single Cosserat rod simulation steps took approximately 0.35 ms, torso dynamics updates 0.18 ms, and the MPC optimization (solved at 30 Hz) required 6.2 ms per update. The remaining components consumed less than 0.5 ms per cycle, making the entire framework suitable for embedded hardware.

In summary, the main findings highlight the robustness and accuracy of the Cosserat rod-based model for soft legs, its efficient integration into a decoupled whole-body dynamics framework, and the effectiveness of the convex MPC for achieving stable, agile, and perturbation-resilient locomotion in a tendon-driven soft quadruped robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of soft robotics, particularly for legged locomotion. Its impact can be assessed across several dimensions:

- **Advancing Physics-Informed Design for Soft Robotics:** The core contribution is the successful integration of high-fidelity Cosserat rod theory into a real-time control framework for a soft quadruped. This demonstrates that complex, continuum mechanics models can be made computationally efficient enough for embedded systems, offering a physically realistic alternative to simplified models (e.g., rigid-link, constant-curvature) or computationally prohibitive methods (e.g., FEM). This approach leads to improved accuracy in predicting soft limb deformation and overall robot locomotion dynamics, which is crucial for robust and predictable behavior in soft robots.

- **Enabling Scalable and Reusable Modeling and Control:** The modular, decoupled whole-body modeling framework provides a generalizable approach for interfacing compliant soft limbs with rigid-body locomotion dynamics. This modularity not only simplifies the computational burden but also makes the framework scalable to robots with varying numbers or configurations of soft limbs, and reusable across different gaits and control objectives. This is a critical step towards developing more versatile and adaptable soft robotic platforms.

- **Robust and Adaptive Locomotion for Soft Quadrupeds:** The successful implementation of convex Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the physics-informed model enables stable and adaptive locomotion across diverse gaits (walking, crawling, omnidirectional) and under various perturbations. This is particularly challenging for soft robots, and the demonstrated asymptotic stability, fast transient response, and recursive feasibility of the MPC are key breakthroughs. The ability to control a tendon-driven soft quadruped with only four actuators, while maintaining high accuracy and stability, addresses a previously underexplored area and showcases the potential for minimal actuation soft robots.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a strong foundation for several future research directions. The authors specifically mention developing a coupled torso-leg dynamic model for tighter integration, improving computational efficiency through reduced-order formulations and parallelization, and incorporating model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL). Leveraging the physics-based model as a differentiable prior for MBRL could enable data-efficient policy learning while ensuring physical plausibility, leading to even more adaptive and intelligent soft robotic systems.

- **Practical Applications and Accessibility:** The SLOT robot itself, being an open-source, low-cost platform, serves as an excellent testbed for advancing control and learning in mobile soft robotics. The demonstrated capabilities, such as crawling under obstacles, climbing inclines, and maintaining stability under external disturbances, suggest significant potential for real-world applications. These include exploration in confined or sensitive environments, search-and-rescue operations where human-robot interaction safety is paramount, and various bio-inspired robotics tasks.

In conclusion, this research significantly pushes the boundaries of soft robot control by demonstrating a practical and high-fidelity approach to dynamic modeling and real-time control. Its impact will likely resonate across academic research and potentially inspire the development of a new generation of more capable, robust, and versatile soft robotic systems for a wide array of applications.
