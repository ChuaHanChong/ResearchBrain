# Dynamic Modeling and MPC for Locomotion of Tendon-Driven Soft Quadruped

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from IIT Gandhinagar and SNIST developed a framework combining Cosserat rod theory for soft leg modeling with a decoupled whole-body dynamic model and real-time convex Model Predictive Control. Their approach accurately predicted soft limb deformation (e.g., tip RMSE 0.89mm) and achieved stable, perturbation-resilient locomotion in a tendon-driven soft quadruped robot during walking and omnidirectional gaits, utilizing only four actuators.

## Problem

- Soft robots exhibit large, nonlinear deformations that are difficult to accurately model and control in real-time.
- Existing soft quadruped robots often lack physics-based leg modeling integrated with real-time, model-predictive whole-body control, especially for tendon-driven systems with minimal actuators.
- High-fidelity modeling methods (e.g., FEM) are computationally expensive for real-time control, while simpler models sacrifice physical accuracy.

## Method

- Developed a physics-informed single-leg model using discrete Cosserat rod theory to accurately capture large deformations and tendon actuation.
- Introduced a modular and decoupled whole-body dynamics framework that integrates the compliant leg models with a rigid torso for computational efficiency.
- Implemented a convex ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) scheme, running on embedded hardware, to optimize ground reaction forces and map them to tendon actuation for stable locomotion.

## Results

- The Cosserat rod model accurately predicted single-leg deformation, showing RMSE values from **0.89** mm (tip) to **5.72** mm, achieving over **95%** accuracy for representative points.
- MPC-driven walking gait maintained desired body height (0.04m) and forward velocity (**0.08** m/s), with an average lateral RMSE of **1.4** cm over an **0.8** m forward displacement in experiments.
- The control system demonstrated asymptotic stability and recursive feasibility, recovering from various deterministic perturbations (e.g., roll, pitch, velocity errors) within **14.553** seconds with zero infeasible optimization steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity continuum mechanics models, specifically Cosserat rod theory, can be made computationally efficient enough for real-time control on embedded hardware for soft robots.
> - A modular, decoupled approach effectively integrates compliant leg dynamics with rigid-body torso dynamics, offering a scalable and reusable framework for soft-rigid hybrid robots.
> - Convex Model Predictive Control enables stable, adaptive, and perturbation-resilient locomotion for complex soft robotic systems using minimal actuation.
