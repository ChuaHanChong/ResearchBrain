# ForceMimic: Force-Centric Imitation Learning with Force-Motion Capture System for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ForceMimic is a force-centric robot learning system for contact-rich manipulation. ForceCapture, a handheld robot-free device pairing a six-axis F/T sensor with a SLAM camera, records natural human demonstrations with gravity-compensated interaction wrenches. HybridIL then trains a diffusion policy predicting joint wrench-position trajectories, executed by orthogonal hybrid force-position control primitives, raising zucchini-peeling success 54.5% relatively over pure-vision imitation learning.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning parameterizes policies over vision-guided trajectories alone, ignoring the time-varying interaction forces humans use to correct motion-planning error.
- Teleoperation, even with force feedback, is unnatural and slow for force-centric data, distorting precise force control and taking over 13 minutes per zucchini.
- Handheld in-the-wild collection devices track only hand or gripper pose via SLAM, recording no interaction wrench, so downstream policies stay unaware of contact.

## Method

- **ForceCapture**: 3D-printed handheld rig with six-axis F/T sensor between tool and handle, ==SLAM== camera, encoder-read rack-and-pinion gripper, and a unidirectional self-lock isolating grip force from interaction wrench.
- Data transfer pipeline: least-squares quasi-static ==gravity compensation== of raw wrench, SLAM pose remapped to robot TCP pose, RGB-D backprojected and filtered into 10,000-point voxelized clouds.
- **HybridIL**: ==diffusion policy== over MLP-encoded point cloud plus pose, predicting 20-step wrench-position parameters; a selector switches between IK joint-position and ==orthogonalized hybrid force-position== primitives at a 6N threshold.

## Results

- ForceCapture peels a zucchini in **4.5** minutes versus **13** for teleoperation and **2.9** for bare human hands, with no operational interruptions and near-zero operator training.
- On 20 trials, HybridIL reaches **100%** motion-correct success and **85%** for peels longer than 10cm, versus **80%** and **55%** for the Raw Diffusion Policy baseline.
- Feeding force as input hurts: Force DP scores **10%** and Force+Hybrid DP **20%** on the 10cm criterion; Raw DP averages **20N** contact force versus HybridIL's **9N**, matching the demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting force as an output executed through matched hybrid force-position primitives beats consuming force as a passive sensory input to a position-only policy.
> - Controller mismatch between demonstration collection and deployment shifts the force distribution, so force-conditioned models can degrade counterintuitively below vision-only baselines.
> - Isolating true interaction wrench, via mechanical self-locking plus gravity compensation, is the precondition that makes handheld robot-free demonstrations usable for contact-rich skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, largely driven by progress in imitation learning (IL). Current IL approaches primarily focus on learning trajectory-based policies, often leveraging vision as the primary sensory input. While effective for many tasks, this paradigm often falls short in "contact-rich" manipulation, where precise, time-varying force control is paramount. Humans excel at these tasks (e.g., peeling, grinding, assembly) by intuitively adjusting forces and compensating for visual inaccuracies or environmental uncertainties.

**The existing gaps and challenges addressed by ForceMimic are several-fold:**

- **Limited Force-Awareness in IL:** Most imitation learning algorithms do not explicitly learn or leverage force-related skills. The action space is typically defined by position or velocity commands, with force aspects handled implicitly by low-level impedance controllers or not at all. This limits robot robustness in tasks requiring continuous physical interaction.
- **Challenges in Force-Centric Data Collection:**
 - **Teleoperation:** While widely used for robot demonstration data collection, teleoperation systems (even with force feedback) are often unnatural, unintuitive, and time-consuming for human operators. They introduce a control interface layer that can hinder natural human performance, especially for delicate force maneuvers.
 - **Robot-Free Handheld Devices:** Recent developments in handheld devices (e.g., DexCap, UMI) enable "in-the-wild" human demonstration collection, offering a more natural experience by removing the robot from the loop during data acquisition. However, these systems generally focus on motion capture and lack the capability to record precise interaction force data.
 - **Lack of Integrated Systems:** There's a notable absence of a cohesive system that combines natural, force-aware demonstration collection with an imitation learning framework capable of effectively translating these force-centric human skills to robots.

- **Under-explored Contact-Rich Tasks:** Specific tasks like vegetable peeling, despite being common activities of daily living (IADLs), remain relatively under-explored in robot research. Existing approaches often rely on pre-set assumptions, separate modules, or specific tools (e.g., knives, dexterous hands) rather than generalizable, end-to-end force-aware skill learning with standard tools like peelers.

ForceMimic directly addresses these limitations by providing a holistic solution: a novel hardware system for natural force-motion data collection and a corresponding learning algorithm designed to exploit this data. It aims to bridge the gap between human dexterity in force control and robot capabilities, pushing the boundaries of what robots can learn to achieve in complex, contact-rich environments. The work distinguishes itself from prior research by not just perceiving force, but by enabling the *learning and reproduction* of human-level force-centric manipulation strategies through a unique hybrid control approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ForceMimic stems from the observation that humans naturally and effectively apply time-varying forces during contact-rich manipulation, compensating for vision-guided trajectory inaccuracies. In contrast, current robot learning algorithms primarily focus on trajectory-based policies, with insufficient attention paid to learning and exploiting force-related skills. This disparity leads to a lack of robustness in robots performing tasks that require delicate and adaptive physical interaction.

**The key objectives of the ForceMimic system are:**

1. **Develop a Natural and Efficient Force-Centric Data Collection System:** To overcome the limitations of traditional teleoperation and existing motion-capture systems, a central objective was to design a robot-free, handheld device, named **ForceCapture**, capable of accurately recording high-quality human force-motion demonstrations in a natural and ergonomic manner. This system should be cost-effective and easy to use, facilitating large-scale data acquisition.
2. **Propose a Hybrid Force-Motion Imitation Learning Algorithm:** To effectively utilize the collected force-centric data, another objective was to develop **HybridIL**, an imitation learning algorithm that can learn from combined force and motion demonstrations. This algorithm should be capable of predicting both wrench (force and torque) and position parameters, and critically, translate these predictions into robust robot actions using a hybrid force-position control strategy.
3. **Enable Robust Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that by combining natural force-motion data collection with a force-aware learning algorithm, robots can achieve significantly more robust and successful performance in challenging contact-rich tasks, exemplified by vegetable peeling. This aims to validate the hypothesis that incorporating force-centric learning can lead to policies that are more resilient to real-world uncertainties.

In essence, the authors are motivated to explore the "utility of force in robot learning," drawing inspiration from human motor control where force prediction and integration are fundamental. ForceMimic is their comprehensive attempt to make robots "force-aware" in their learned manipulation skills, moving beyond purely visual or position-based paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ForceMimic is presented as an integrated system with two main components: **ForceCapture** for data collection and **HybridIL** for learning and execution. The overall pipeline involves collecting human demonstrations with ForceCapture, transferring this robot-free data to a pseudo-robot format, and then training a diffusion-based policy (HybridIL) to predict wrench and pose trajectories for hybrid force-position control.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design: ForceCapture

ForceCapture is a novel, low-cost, versatile, and robot-free device designed for natural force-position data capture during human manipulation.
- **Design Objectives:**
 - **Scalability:** Achieved through low cost (approx. $50 for 3D printed parts and encoder), compatibility with various force sensors, and ease of fabrication/maintenance.
 - **On-site Force Realism:** Designed for direct human operation, enabling natural force application without requiring users to adapt to an artificial control environment (unlike teleoperation).
 - **Ergonomic Comfort:** Optimized center of gravity and convenient operation to prevent interference with natural human movements, ensuring realistic force data.
- **Key Components:**
 - **Six-axis Force/Torque (F/T) Sensor:** Placed between the end-effector and the gripping handle to capture interaction wrenches.
 - **SLAM Camera (RealSense T265):** Positioned near the F/T sensor to record motion data (pose trajectory).
 - **RGB-D Camera (RealSense L515):** External camera used for environmental observation (visual data).
 - **Gripper Mechanism (for gripper version):** A rack-and-pinion mechanism with an angle encoder for synchronized gripper movement and width tracking.
- **Unique Feature: Unidirectional Locking Mechanism:** For the gripper version, a self-lock function prevents forces exerted by the hand on the grippers from influencing the force sensor during gripping actions. Fingers can only be opened via a separate lever mechanism, aligning with natural hand logic and ensuring accurate interaction force recording.
- **Physical Characteristics:** Lightweight (0.8kg total, 0.3kg accessories + 0.5kg F/T sensor) and primarily 3D-printed, making it highly accessible.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Transfer

The collected data from ForceCapture needs to be processed and adapted for robot learning:
- **Multi-Sensor Data Collection:** Data is simultaneously collected from the F/T sensor (1000 Hz), T265 SLAM camera (200 Hz), L515 RGB-D camera (30 Hz), and gripper encoder (30 Hz). All data is synchronized to the L515's frequency (30 Hz).
- **Pose Tracking:** Similar to DexCap, the T265's initial position relative to the L515 is calibrated. Then, the T265, mounted on ForceCapture, tracks the device's pose in the environment. This pose is transformed to represent the robot's TCP (Tool Center Point) pose.
- **Wrench Compensation:** To isolate the interaction forces between the end-effector and the environment, the tool's gravitational and inertial forces are subtracted from the raw F/T sensor data. This is done by performing a quasi-static movement, recording pose and wrench, and using a least-squares solution to estimate the tool's center of mass and weight for gravity compensation.
- **Visual Data Processing:** RGB-D images are backprojected into point clouds. To reduce the domain gap between human demonstration and robot deployment, the point clouds are filtered to retain only the end-effector and object points, excluding unrelated background elements. The point clouds are then voxelized to a consistent size (10,000 points).

#### 3.3. Learning Algorithm: HybridIL

HybridIL is an end-to-end imitation learning method designed to leverage the collected force-centric data for robust robot control.
- **Input Representation:** Visual input (point clouds) is encoded into one-dimensional features using an MLP encoder. These features are then concatenated with the robot's TCP pose to form a multimodal input representation.
- **Policy Architecture:** A modified Diffusion Policy is used. Diffusion policies are known for their ability to generate diverse and high-quality action trajectories.
- **Output Prediction:** Crucially, HybridIL predicts *both* position and wrench parameters over a future horizon (next 20 time steps), rather than just position.
- **Hybrid Force-Position Control Primitive:**
 - **Orthogonality:** The paper emphasizes that wrench and position control must be orthogonal. While the model doesn't explicitly enforce this during prediction, the *controller* ensures orthogonality by projecting the predicted force onto the plane orthogonal to the motion direction.
 - **Control Mode Selection:** An empirically determined force threshold of 6N is used to switch between control primitives:
 - **IK-based Joint Position Controller:** Used when the predicted force is below 6N (or if no contact is intended/made).
 - **Hybrid Force-Position Controller:** Engaged when the predicted force consistently exceeds 6N, indicating sustained contact. This primitive tracks both the predicted pose trajectory and the orthogonalized predicted force.
 - **Initial Contact Strategy:** If the robot end-effector has not yet made contact with the object when hybrid control is activated, a pressing control in the opposite direction of force control is applied to ensure stable initial contact.
- **Robot Execution:** The Flexiv Rizon 4 robot, with its precise force sensing and control capabilities, is used for experiments. It utilizes Flexiv's RDK (Robotics Development Kit) for implementing the joint position and hybrid force-position control primitives.
- **Training:** The model is trained using MSE loss for 500 epochs, aiming to minimize the difference between predicted and demonstrated wrench-pose trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of ForceMimic was primarily validated through extensive experiments on the challenging contact-rich task of zucchini peeling.

#### 4.1. Data Collection Efficiency: ForceCapture vs. Teleoperation

A key experiment compared the efficiency of ForceCapture against a traditional force-feedback teleoperation setup for peeling a zucchini:
- **ForceCapture:** Achieved peeling a zucchini in approximately **4.5 minutes**, which is remarkably close to direct human peeling (2.9 minutes). Users became proficient after just one attempt.
- **Teleoperation:** Took significantly longer, averaging over **13 minutes** per zucchini (roughly three times longer). It also required extensive user training and experienced operational errors (three interruptions).
- **Conclusion:** ForceCapture demonstrated superior efficiency, naturalness, and ease of use for human operators in collecting force-centric manipulation data, highlighting its practical advantage over teleoperation.

#### 4.2. Manipulation Performance: Zucchini Peeling (HybridIL vs. Baselines)

The core evaluation involved comparing HybridIL against three baseline imitation learning methods on the zucchini peeling task. A dataset of 438 peeling skill segments (30,199 action sequences) was collected using ForceCapture.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Raw DP:** Standard diffusion policy, taking raw visual perception and robot pose as input, outputting end-effector pose.
 - **Force DP:** Raw visual perception, robot pose, AND robot force sensing as input, outputting end-effector pose.
 - **Force+Hybrid DP:** Raw visual perception, robot pose, AND robot force sensing as input, outputting both pose and wrench sequences, using hybrid force-position control primitives.
- **Metrics:**
 1. **Motion correct:** Any length of zucchini peel successfully removed without damage.
 2. **Continuous peel length > 10cm:** A more stringent criterion for high-quality peeling.

- **Quantitative Results (TABLE I):**
 - **HybridIL (Proposed):** Achieved **100% motion correct success rate** and **85% continuous peel success rate**. This demonstrates its robustness and ability to consistently perform the task. Failures (15%) were attributed to premature ending of predicted force-position parameters leading to early control primitive switches and discontinuities.
 - **Raw DP:** Achieved **80% motion correct** and **55% continuous peel success rates**. Failures included excessive force leading to zucchini damage, no contact, short peels, and peeling breakage due to discontinuous pose outputs.
 - **Force DP & Force+Hybrid DP:** Both models, which incorporated robot force sensing as an *input* to the policy, performed surprisingly poorly. Force DP had 60% motion correct and 10% continuous peel, while Force+Hybrid DP achieved 80% motion correct but only 20% continuous peel.
 - **Counter-intuitive Finding:** Despite having access to force sensing, these models struggled to predict correct actions after contact. The authors attribute this to a mismatch between the high interaction forces experienced by the robot (average 20N, up to 40N for Raw DP) and the lower forces (around 10N) observed in the human demonstration dataset. The inconsistency between the robot's force interaction controller and the human data collection process was also suggested as a contributing factor.

- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 6 & 7):**
 - **Force Curves (Fig. 7):** HybridIL maintained an average interaction force of approximately 9N, closely aligning with the forces predicted by the model and observed in human demonstrations. This resulted in evenly peeled sections with consistent thickness and width (Fig. 6a). In contrast, Raw DP, despite some success, exhibited significantly higher and more erratic interaction forces.
 - **Peeled Skins (Fig. 6):** Visual evidence clearly shows the superior quality and consistency of peels produced by HybridIL compared to the other methods, which often resulted in damaged zucchinis or fragmented peels.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ForceMimic represents a significant advancement in robot learning for contact-rich manipulation tasks, offering both a novel data collection paradigm and an effective learning algorithm.

- **Enabling Force-Centric Robot Learning:** The most profound impact of ForceMimic is its direct addressing of the long-standing challenge of teaching robots human-like force control. By providing a natural, efficient, and robot-free method to capture force-centric human demonstrations, ForceMimic opens up new avenues for collecting the necessary data for a wider range of dexterous manipulation tasks that currently elude robots.
- **Improved Robustness and Performance:** HybridIL's ability to learn and reproduce force-aware policies, combined with its unique hybrid control strategy, demonstrates a substantial improvement in robot robustness and success rates in contact-rich tasks like peeling. This is critical for real-world deployment, where visual uncertainties and contact variations are ubiquitous. The 54.5% relative increase in success rate over state-of-the-art vision-based IL for continuous peeling is a strong indicator of its potential.
- **Reduced Data Collection Burden:** ForceCapture's superior efficiency and naturalness over teleoperation mean that researchers and practitioners can collect higher quality, larger datasets faster and with less effort, accelerating research and development in this domain. Its low cost and 3D-printable design further increase accessibility.
- **Paving the Way for Complex Manipulation:** The success with zucchini peeling, a non-trivial task requiring continuous contact and force modulation, suggests that ForceMimic could be extended to other complex, delicate manipulation tasks in diverse fields such as:
 - **Domestic Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform household chores like cooking, cleaning, and assistance that involve interacting with deformable objects or requiring precise force.
 - **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Automating tasks that require fitting parts with tight tolerances or manipulating flexible components.
 - **Medical Robotics:** Potentially aiding in surgical procedures or rehabilitation exercises where delicate force application is critical.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also clearly identifies important open challenges, particularly regarding how to optimally integrate force *sensing* as an input to policies. The counter-intuitive poor performance of Force DP and Force+Hybrid DP highlights that simply adding force as an input observation is not enough; the policy needs to learn *how* to effectively use this information and the control strategy must align with it. This indicates a rich area for future research into multimodal representation learning, more sophisticated control primitive selection (possibly predicted by the model itself), and generalization to a broader set of force-oriented skills.

In conclusion, ForceMimic makes a significant contribution by bridging the gap between natural human dexterity in force control and robot capabilities, pushing the frontier of contact-rich manipulation. It offers a practical system and a promising learning paradigm that could unlock new levels of autonomy and adaptability for robots operating in complex, interactive environments.
