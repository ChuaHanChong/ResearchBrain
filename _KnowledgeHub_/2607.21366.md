# Hilbert Operator for Progressive Encoding (HOPE): A Mathematical Framework for Deconstructing Learned Representations in Deep Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Introducing the Hilbert Operator for Progressive Encoding (HOPE), a mathematical framework that models deep network neurons as rank-1 Hilbert-Schmidt operators in a continuous Hilbert space, enabling unbiased and data-free (for BN networks) deconstruction of learned representations. This framework facilitated superior structured model compression and a novel transfer learning method, DEFT, which balanced stability and plasticity more effectively than baselines by analytically partitioning core and slack neurons.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks remain opaque, making it challenging to extract and interpret the internal knowledge encoded within their complex representations.
- Existing network compression methods suffer from limitations such as 'scale symmetries,' 'architectural biases,' and reliance on computationally expensive, data-dependent empirical approaches for evaluating neuron importance.
- In continual learning, balancing adaptation to new tasks (plasticity) with the retention of foundational knowledge (stability) poses a significant challenge, often leading to catastrophic forgetting or high computational costs.

## Method

- The **HOPE** framework shifts network analysis from discrete parameter space to a continuous ==Hilbert function space==, modeling each neuron as a rank-1 ==Hilbert-Schmidt operator==.
- HOPE leverages empirical Batch Normalization statistics and the ==Maximum Entropy principle== to analytically evaluate neuron capacity (Hilbert-Schmidt norm) in a data-free manner, mitigating biases from scale symmetries and architectural configurations.
- The framework unifies various compression operations (pruning, merging, block eviction) under a single mathematical metric, guiding an iterative, greedy encoding loop that minimizes a ==distortion-rate objective== to systematically reduce network capacity.
- **DEFT** (Dispersed Elastic Fine-Tuning) applies HOPE's capacity partitioning to split neurons into a frozen "Universal Core" and plastic "Peripheral Slack" for transfer learning.

## Results

- HOPE-compressed ResNet-50 models on ImageNet demonstrated superior accuracy retention across various compression rates compared to conventional structured pruning baselines, validating its effectiveness in preserving model performance.
- DEFT achieved the highest average H-Score (**65.82 ± 3.96**) in cross-domain transfer learning, balancing high target accuracy (**89.79%**) with robust source retention (**52.14%**).
- DEFT provided theoretical guarantees, including a 'Static Initialization Shock Bound' and 'Dynamic Decoupling,' ensuring that the 'Universal Core' of foundational knowledge is protected from interference during fine-tuning for new tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Analyzing neurons in a continuous Hilbert function space provides a robust and unbiased measure of capacity, effectively overcoming limitations of parameter-space analysis like scale symmetries and architectural biases.
> - Model compression, when guided by a principled capacity metric in function space, serves as an objective proxy for deconstructing learned representations, allowing for the progressive separation of a 'universal core' from 'malleable slack'.
> - Utilizing inherent network statistics (e.g., BN parameters) enables data-free and analytical evaluation of neuron importance, significantly reducing computational overhead and improving robustness against distribution shifts compared to empirical, data-dependent methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep neural networks are recognized for their ability to learn complex representations, yet extracting and interpreting this internal knowledge remains a challenge. The research positions itself within the context of network compression as a method to analyze these internal representations. This perspective is motivated by the established link between learning and compression, supported by prior work demonstrating large language models as general-purpose compressors and amortized algorithmic predictors. Early foundational work on network deconstruction through capacity reduction also reinforces this approach.

Existing network compression methods, such as magnitude-based pruning (e.g., L1-norm or BN scaling), often face limitations. These methods can be influenced by "scale symmetries" where arbitrary scaling of weights does not alter the neuron's function, leading to unreliable importance indicators. They may also suffer from "architectural biases" due to their reliance on specific parameter configurations. Furthermore, empirical approaches that track neuron activations or curvature often incur significant computational costs and can be brittle to distribution shifts, as they typically require repeated data passes.

HOPE aims to address these shortcomings by shifting the analysis from discrete parameter spaces to a continuous function space, specifically a Hilbert space. This approach seeks to provide a more robust and unbiased framework for deconstructing learned representations. It also aligns with concepts such as the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, which suggests a shared statistical model underlying diverse networks, and the idea of progressive capacity reduction to separate a "universal core" of invariants from "malleable slack," relevant to areas like interpretability and continual learning. The work also connects to discussions around neuron alignment, model merging, and macro-architectural pruning, offering a unified metric across these different scales of compression.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a mathematical framework, Hilbert Operator for Progressive Encoding (HOPE), designed to progressively deconstruct the representations learned within trained deep network weights. This deconstruction is viewed as a means to objectively study the complex internal knowledge encoded by these networks.

The motivation for developing HOPE stems from several challenges and observations in the field:
- **Deconstructing Opaque Networks:** While deep networks are powerful, their internal workings remain largely opaque. The authors propose model compression as a measurable proxy task to objectively analyze these internal representations.
- **Link Between Learning and Compression:** The fundamental relationship between learning and compression, where learning involves discarding task-irrelevant noise to isolate generalizable patterns, suggests that progressive compression can be an effective tool for achieving this separation. Core invariants are hypothesized to resist pruning longer than malleable slack, allowing for the iterative reduction of capacity to reveal the network's essential feature space.
- **Limitations of Existing Compression Heuristics:** Current network compression methods, particularly parameter-space pruning, often rely on magnitude-based heuristics (e.g., norm of raw weights). These are considered unreliable as magnitudes can be artifacts of the optimization process rather than indicators of functional importance. Such methods are susceptible to "scale symmetries" (e.g., from Batch Normalization) and "architectural biases," which lead to non-objective capacity measurements across heterogeneous layers.
- **Drawbacks of Data-Dependent Approaches:** Empirical methods that rely on passing finite datasets through the network to approximate neuron importance (e.g., activation matching) are computationally expensive and can make the resulting architecture brittle to distribution shifts, potentially degrading performance on long-tail features.
- **Need for Unbiased and Data-Free Evaluation:** To overcome these issues, the authors sought a framework that evaluates and analyzes neurons in function space, rather than parameter space, and can perform such analysis in a data-free manner. This would enable unbiased capacity measurements and architectural decisions across various layers without requiring real or synthetic data samples.

In summary, HOPE aims to provide a rigorous, hyperparameter-free, and data-free (for networks with Batch Normalization) mathematical foundation for understanding and systematically reducing the capacity of deep neural networks, thereby offering a clearer view into their learned representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HOPE framework approaches network compression by re-conceptualizing neurons within a continuous Hilbert space, unifying various compression operations under a single mathematical metric.

**Core Principles:**
1. **Shift to Function Space:** HOPE transitions network analysis from the discrete physical parameter space to a continuous function space. Each neuron is modeled as a rank-1 Hilbert-Schmidt operator within a Hilbert space H. This abstraction evaluates the function a neuron computes, detaching it from its physical parameter shape.
2. **Data-Free Operation with BN Statistics:** For networks incorporating Batch Normalization (BN), HOPE operates entirely data-free. It leverages empirical BN statistics (means and variances) embedded within the trained model checkpoint. By applying the Maximum Entropy principle, these statistics are used to construct a continuous Gaussian surrogate for the local input distribution at each neuron, allowing analytical evaluation of Hilbert space norms.
3. **Mitigating Symmetries:**
 - **Normalization Invariance:** HOPE uses "effective parameters" (𝒘eff_in,𝑖, 𝑏𝑖) that absorb BN operations, ensuring that scaling of raw input weights does not alter the effective signal or the neuron's functional capacity.
 - **Neuron Scale Invariance:** For positively homogeneous of degree 1 (PH-1) activation functions (e.g., ReLU), scaling input weights and biases by λ and output weights by 1/λ cancels out, ensuring the Hilbert norm (capacity) remains invariant to such re-shardings.
 - **Neuron Shape Invariance:** The capacity calculation depends on the input space only through a kernel term (expected squared activation energy), abstracting away the physical dimensionality of the input tensor.

**Formalizing the Neuron and its Capacity:**
- A neuron $f_i$ is defined as a continuous function $f_i(𝒙) = Ψ(y_i)𝒘_{out,i}$, where $y_i = (𝒘_{eff,in,i})^T𝒙 + b_i$.
- The Hilbert space H is defined as $L_2(\mathcal{X}, P_{\mathcal{X}}; \mathbb{R}^c)$, the space of square-integrable functions mapping input space $\mathcal{X}$ to the $c$-dimensional output space.
- Each neuron $f_i$ is represented as a rank-1 Hilbert-Schmidt operator $g_i \otimes 𝒘_{out,i}$, where $g_i(\boldsymbol{x}) = \Psi(y_i)$ is an element of a scalar Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_{in}$, and $𝒘_{out,i}$ is in $\mathcal{H}_{out} = \mathbb{R}^c$.
- The capacity of a neuron is its Hilbert-Schmidt norm, $\|f_i\|_H = \|𝒘_{out,i}\|_2 \cdot \sqrt{K(i,i)}$, where $K(i,j) = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{x}\sim P_{\mathcal{X}}}[\Psi(y_i)\Psi(y_j)]$ is the kernel. Closed-form expressions for these kernels are derived for ReLU activations.

**Layer Transition Costs:**
- A layer's state $\Phi = (f_1..., f_N)$ has total capacity $E(\Phi) = \sum_{k=1}^N \|f_k\|_H$.
- Compression is modeled as a continuous deformation $\Phi(t)$ from an initial state $\Phi_a$ to a reduced state $\Phi_b$.
- A cost functional $J(\Phi_a, \Phi_b)$ quantifies model distortion, satisfying three axioms: Magnitude Neutrality (scale-invariant), Connectivity Preservation (infinite cost for layer extinction), and Infinitesimal Capacity Dependence (driven by capacity reduction).
- The resulting integral cost $J_{capacity} = \int_0^1 -c(\Phi(t)) \frac{\dot{E}(t)}{E(\Phi(t))} dt$ is then refined into a projection cost $J_{proj}$ to capture feature alignment.
- A closed-form upper bound, $J_{bound}(\Phi_a, \Phi_b) \leq c(\Phi_a) \frac{D(\Phi_a, \tilde{\Phi}_b)}{E(\Phi_b)}$, is derived, where $D$ is the Euclidean distance in the configuration space and $E(\Phi_b)$ is the post-deletion capacity. The scale-invariant factor $c(\Phi)$ is set to $N$ (number of active neurons) to normalize by average feature capacity.
- This yields specific costs for:
 - **Pruning:** $J_{prune} = \frac{N \cdot \|f_i\|_H}{E_a - \|f_i\|_H}$. This corresponds to projecting a neuron to the null operator.
 - **Merging:** $J_{merge} = \frac{N \cdot \sqrt{\|f_i - f_p\|^2_H + \|f_j - f_p\|^2_H}}{E_a - \|f_i\|_H - \|f_j\|_H + \|f_p\|_H}$. This involves finding an optimal rank-1 parent neuron $f_p$ that best approximates the 2-dimensional subspace spanned by $f_i$ and $f_j$.

**Generating the Parent Neuron ($f_p$):**
- The optimal parent neuron $f_p$ is found by minimizing $J_{merge}$, which is equivalent to maximizing alignment with $f_i+f_j$.
- The search for $f_p$ is framed as a nested optimization problem for its optimal direction and scale.
- The optimal direction is found by an eigenvalue problem on a rank-2 subspace, which is an approximation to maintain computational tractability. The sign ambiguity of the eigenvector is resolved by evaluating both polarities in the exact non-linearized objective.
- The optimal scale $s^*$ is derived in a closed form by minimizing a 1D problem.
- The derived Hilbert space neuron $f_p$ is then mapped back to physical parameters (raw weights, BN parameters) using an input/output scaling strategy and specific equations for BN parameters that maintain the target effective geometry.

**Block Eviction:**
- HOPE extends the framework to macro-level operations, such as evicting entire residual blocks. This is motivated by the fact that granular pruning cannot fully remove the last layer of a residual block due to dimensional matching with the skip connection.
- Block eviction is defined as forcing the residual pathway $F(X) \to 0$, collapsing the block into a pure identity mapping $Y=X$.
- A macroscopic state $\Omega^{(l)} = (\Phi^{(l)}, \mathcal{I})$ is defined, coupling the internal layer $\Phi^{(l)}$ with the ambient skip connection $\mathcal{I}$.
- The unified macro cost $J_{evict}$ is a sum of linear upper bounds for each internal layer: $J_{evict} = \sum_{l=1}^2 N^{(l)}_{active} \frac{E^{(l)}_{active}}{E_{identity}}$. $E_{identity}$ is the parallel survival capacity provided by the skip connection, evaluated as the sum of RMS energies of individual identity operators. This prevents the cost from diverging to infinity as the layer is emptied.

**Balancing Compression and Distortion (Action Selection):**
- To balance distortion and parameter reduction, HOPE uses a rate-distortion inspired objective: minimizing the Distortion Rate (DR) = $J_k / \Delta P_k$.
- $\Delta P_k$ represents the number of parameters removed by action $k$. To ensure item independence for the greedy selection, a static parameter footprint $\Delta P^{init}_k$ (initial parameter count for that operation) is used, rather than a dynamic one.
- At each step, the algorithm greedily selects the action with the minimum DR.

**Encoding Loop:**
The progressive encoding process is an iterative greedy algorithm:
1. **Initialization:** Precomputes and caches capacities and pairwise geometric cross-capacities for all neurons and layers.
2. **Greedy Scan:** At each iteration, it scans all layers and candidate actions (pruning, merging, block eviction) to find the single optimal action with the lowest DR. Querying costs is O(1) due to cached components and dynamic $E_{rem}$ tracking. Finding optimal action is $O(L \cdot N^2)$.
3. **Localized Update:** After executing the optimal action, only the modified structures and their immediate neighbors are updated in the cache (O(N) operation). This avoids full network recalculations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents proof-of-concept experiments in model compression and fine-tuning to illustrate the practical potential of the HOPE framework. These experiments are not intended as exhaustive benchmarks but rather as validations of the theoretical capabilities.

#### 4.1. Model Compression

- **Methodology:** HOPE, as a structured compression method, was compared against three structured baselines: L1-Norm Input Pruning, L1-Norm Joint Pruning, and BN Scale Pruning. The evaluation was conducted on a ResNet-50 model pre-trained on ImageNet. Performance was assessed by tracking test set accuracy against model density (ratio of active to initial neurons).
- **Results:** The results indicated that models compressed using HOPE generally exhibited superior accuracy compared to the baseline methods across various compression rates. This suggests that HOPE's function-space-based, unbiased capacity measurement approach can lead to more effective structured pruning, preserving more of the model's performance for a given reduction in parameters.

#### 4.2. Cross-Domain Transfer Learning (DEFT)

An application of the HOPE framework, Dispersed Elastic Fine-Tuning (DEFT), was introduced to address the stability-plasticity dilemma in transfer learning.

- **Motivation:** The challenge in continual learning is to adapt to new tasks (plasticity) without forgetting previously acquired foundational knowledge (stability). Traditional methods often suffer from computational expense (e.g., O(N^3) orthogonal matrix projections) or brittleness to large domain shifts (e.g., local approximations like Fisher Information Matrices).
- **DEFT Approach:**
 - **Partitioning:** DEFT leverages HOPE's capacity evaluation to partition the network's neurons into a "Universal Core" (high capacity, frozen, $E_i=0$) and "Peripheral Slack" (low capacity, plastic, $E_i=1$).
 - **Redundancy Resolution:** It utilizes HOPE's merging operation to dynamically resolve redundancy within the core. By consolidating correlated neurons into a single parent, DEFT preserves the feature while releasing the redundant "child" neurons into the plastic slack, thereby generating new capacity for the target task.
 - **Structural Mask:** A structural mask is applied at initialization to sever all connections from upstream plastic (slack) neurons to downstream frozen (core) neurons. This physically isolates the core from target-driven representational drift.
 - **Gradient Scaling:** During fine-tuning, target gradients are element-wise scaled by the elasticity map, ensuring only plastic neurons are updated.
 - **Theoretical Guarantees:** The paper presents theoretical guarantees for DEFT, including a "Static Initialization Shock Bound" (quantifying initial distortion from masking) and "Dynamic Decoupling" (guaranteeing zero interference to the core during fine-tuning), and a "Unified Cumulative Bound" for overall distortion.
- **Experimental Setup:** Models pre-trained on multi-class classification tasks derived from CIFAR-100 (using 4 superclasses/20 fine-grained categories) were fine-tuned on the SVHN (10-class digit classification) dataset. The performance metric used was the H-Score, which is the harmonic mean of source retention and target accuracy, penalizing poor performance in either domain. DEFT was benchmarked against: Standard Full Fine-Tuning (Full FT), Head-Only Fine-Tuning (Head-Only FT), Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT, focusing on BN parameters), and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC).
- **Results:** The evaluations across 20 distinct cross-domain scenarios showed:
 - Full FT achieved the highest target accuracy (94.09%) but severely degraded source retention (7.52%).
 - Head-Only FT preserved source knowledge well (63.13%) but had low target accuracy (36.11%).
 - EWC and PEFT also showed similar trade-offs, with EWC performing similarly to Full FT on target accuracy but with very low source retention (6.74%).
 - DEFT achieved a balance: high target accuracy (89.79%) while significantly retaining source accuracy (52.14%). This balance resulted in the highest average H-Score (65.82 ± 3.96) among all compared methods, indicating its effectiveness in mitigating catastrophic forgetting while enabling substantial adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOPE framework represents a notable contribution to the understanding and manipulation of deep neural network representations. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Rigorous Mathematical Foundation:** HOPE provides a novel and rigorous mathematical framework for deconstructing learned representations. By modeling neurons as rank-1 Hilbert-Schmidt operators and performing analysis in a continuous Hilbert space, it offers a principled approach to quantify neuron importance and functional relationships.
- **Unbiased Capacity Measurement:** The framework addresses critical limitations of existing compression techniques by mitigating scale symmetries and architectural biases inherent in parameter-space methods. Its data-free nature (for BN networks) and reliance on analytical kernels remove dependence on empirical data passes, which can be computationally expensive and prone to distribution shifts. This could lead to more reliable and objective assessments of network capacity and redundancy.
- **Unified Compression Paradigm:** HOPE unifies different scales of network compression—from granular pruning and neuron merging to macro block eviction—under a single, consistent mathematical metric. This unified approach enables unbiased architectural decisions across heterogeneous layers, potentially leading to more optimal and coherent model reductions.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** By progressively peeling away "malleable slack" to expose a "universal core" of invariants, HOPE offers a pathway to better understand the hierarchical and compositional nature of learned features. This deconstruction process can support algorithmic interpretability efforts, helping researchers identify and analyze fundamental abstractions within deep networks.
- **Practical Applications in Model Compression:** The proof-of-concept experiments demonstrate that HOPE can generate highly compressed models with superior accuracy retention compared to conventional structured pruning methods. This suggests its utility for creating more efficient deep learning models suitable for resource-constrained environments.
- **Advancements in Continual Learning and Transfer Learning:** The DEFT application highlights HOPE's potential to address the stability-plasticity dilemma. By analytically identifying and isolating a "Universal Core" from "Peripheral Slack," DEFT offers a parameter-efficient transfer learning strategy that actively generates capacity for new tasks while theoretically guaranteeing the protection of foundational knowledge. This could lead to more robust and adaptable AI systems that can learn new skills without forgetting old ones.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The framework's O(1) decoupled cache and localized update mechanisms for its greedy encoding loop indicate a computationally efficient approach to progressive compression, despite the underlying complex mathematical operations.

In essence, HOPE provides both theoretical tools and practical methodologies for a deeper, more objective understanding and manipulation of deep network representations, with implications for building more efficient, robust, and interpretable AI systems.
