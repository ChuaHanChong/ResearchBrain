# Whole-body Multi-contact Motion Control for Humanoid Robots Based on Distributed Tactile Sensors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from CNRS-AIST JRL and Tokyo University of Science developed a control method for position-controlled humanoid robots, enabling dynamic whole-body multi-contact motions by incorporating distributed tactile sensors on intermediate body parts. This integration allows for online contact region updates, improving the robot's stability against environmental inaccuracies and disturbances in complex environments.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots typically limit multi-contact motion to extremities (hands, feet) due to the lack of adequate sensing on intermediate body parts, hindering robust operation in confined spaces.
- Prior methods for utilizing non-extremity contacts often assume quasi-static motion or employ control paradigms not directly applicable to position-controlled humanoids.
- Challenges exist in maintaining robust balance control when contacts occur at arbitrary body areas without force/torque sensors, leading to over or underestimation of contact regions.

## Method

- An extended multi-contact motion controller integrates distributed tactile sensors into both centroidal and limb motion control modules for position-controlled humanoids.
- Thin, flexible distributed tactile sensors (e-skin) are mounted on intermediate body parts like forearms and thighs, enabling broad-area contact force measurement without altering robot geometry.
- Online contact polygon updates, based on tactile measurements, refine contact region estimation for the ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== of centroidal motion and for impedance-like damping control of limbs.

## Results

- In simulations, tactile feedback expanded the wall height error range for successful walking with elbow contact from [-**0.02** m, **0.04** m] to [-**0.03** m, **0.04** m].
- The robot withstood a wider range of disturbance forces ([-100 N, 70 N] vs. [-90 N, 40 N]) during one-foot, one-knee standing with tactile feedback in simulation.
- A life-sized RHP Kaleido robot successfully performed dynamic stepping forward with forearm support (exerting ~160 N) and maintained stable sitting balance with thigh contacts on a 15 cm narrow seat.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly integrating distributed tactile sensor feedback into the real-time balance control loop of humanoid robots substantially enhances stability and disturbance rejection in multi-contact scenarios.
> - Accurate, online updates of contact polygon vertices using tactile measurements are crucial for effective contact force distribution, preventing instabilities caused by static or inaccurate contact assumptions.
> - Leveraging intermediate body parts for dynamic multi-contact motion through tactile sensing significantly expands a robot's operational robustness and dexterity beyond traditional extremity-only interactions.
