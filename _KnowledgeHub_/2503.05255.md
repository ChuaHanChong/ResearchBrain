# CMMCoT: Enhancing Complex Multi-Image Comprehension via Multi-Modal Chain-of-Thought and Memory Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called CMMCoT, developed by Alibaba Group and Zhejiang University, improves complex multi-image comprehension by integrating interleaved multimodal reasoning and a test-time memory augmentation module. This approach yielded state-of-the-art results on Mantis-Eval, MVBench, and Q-Bench, improving performance on multi-image benchmarks by up to 1.4 points and enhancing single-image understanding by an average of 4.1 points.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with complex multi-image comprehension, relying on a "direct prediction" paradigm that lacks interpretability.
- Current multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods primarily depend on textual reasoning or focus on single-image scenarios, failing to directly supervise visual reasoning across multiple images.
- MLLMs lack human-like capabilities for continuous cross-image visual comparison and dynamic memorization of critical visual concepts during reasoning.

## Method

- The CMMCoT framework, built upon Qwen2-VL, employs ==interleaved visual-textual reasoning chains== that leverage ==coordinate-guided visual tokens== to supervise visual reasoning steps.
- A ==Retrieval-based Image Feature Reasoning Enhancement Module (RIFREM)== is introduced for test-time memory augmentation, facilitating cross-image feature deliberation without increasing model parameters.
- A two-stage training strategy utilizes the newly constructed ==CMMCoT-260k dataset==, comprising 260,000 instances with explicit reasoning chains and spatial annotations for diverse multi-image tasks.

## Results

- The CMMCoT-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL 7B achieved state-of-the-art results on Mantis-Eval (**72.2%**), MVBench (**75.8%**), and Q-Bench (**78.5%**), surpassing the baseline Qwen2.5-VL by an average of **1.4** points on multi-image benchmarks.
- CMMCoT also improved performance on single-image benchmarks, outperforming the baseline Qwen2.5-VL by an average of **4.1** points across datasets like MMMU, MMStar, and SQA.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated CMMCoT's superior ability in accurate object localization, quantitative reasoning, anomaly detection, and providing explicit causal interpretations in complex visual scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly incorporating visual region tokens within multimodal reasoning chains significantly enhances multi-image comprehension and improves model interpretability by supervising visual reasoning steps.
> - Test-time memory augmentation, exemplified by RIFREM, effectively boosts reasoning capabilities by enabling dynamic deliberation over visual contexts from multiple images without increasing model size.
> - The full potential of multi-modal "slow thinking" fine-tuning, like CMMCoT, is realized only when the underlying multimodal model possesses strong visual localization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid advancements, achieving remarkable success in tasks ranging from multimodal recognition to single-image understanding. However, as the paper highlights, existing MLLMs predominantly rely on a "direct prediction" paradigm, which struggles with complex scenarios, especially those involving multiple images. This paradigm often overlooks subtle evidential features across modalities and suffers from a lack of interpretability, crucial for real-world applications.

Inspired by the success of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and "slow thinking" mechanisms in unimodal (text-only) LLMs, researchers have begun exploring CoT for multimodal tasks. Previous multimodal CoT approaches, however, often fall short by primarily relying on external tools for textual reasoning chains or using textual guidance for visual inference. They tend to neglect the direct supervision of visual reasoning processes, or, as observed in works like MVoT and VoCoT, focus mainly on single-image scenarios.

This paper addresses a critical gap: the challenge of **complex multi-image comprehension** requiring "slow thinking" and detailed cross-image visual analysis. Human cognition naturally performs continuous cross-image visual comparison through region-of-interest matching and dynamic memorization of critical visual concepts. Current MLLMs lack these capabilities, struggling with correlating objects across disparate images and integrating textual information for comprehensive reasoning. Furthermore, while test-time scaling has been explored to boost model reasoning without parameter increases, its effectiveness diminishes in complex multi-image contexts, and simply scaling pre-trained model parameters is reaching a performance ceiling. CMMCoT attempts to bridge this gap by introducing a framework that explicitly integrates visual grounding and memory-augmented reasoning into the CoT process for multi-image understanding, mimicking human-like cognitive operations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind CMMCoT stems from the observed limitations of current MLLMs in handling complex multi-image comprehension tasks and the distinct cognitive processes humans employ in such scenarios. Specifically, the authors note that existing "slow-thinking" methods, while successful for single images, are fundamentally constrained when extended to multiple images due to their heavy reliance on text-based intermediate reasoning. Humans, conversely, engage in two complementary cognitive operations: (1) continuous cross-image visual comparison via region-of-interest matching, and (2) dynamic memorization of critical visual concepts throughout the reasoning chain.

Based on these observations, the key objectives of this research are:

- **To propose a novel framework, CMMCoT, that mimics human-like "slow thinking" for multi-image understanding.** This framework aims to significantly enhance reasoning performance in complex multi-image scenarios.
- **To overcome the predominant reliance on text-based reasoning in existing multimodal CoT methods by incorporating interleaved multimodal multi-step reasoning chains.** These chains utilize critical visual region tokens, extracted from intermediate steps, as supervisory signals to facilitate comprehensive cross-modal understanding and enhance model interpretability.
- **To introduce a test-time memory augmentation module (RIFREM) that expands the model’s reasoning capacity during inference.** This module is designed to achieve performance gains without increasing model parameters, addressing the challenge of enhancing inference capabilities in complex multi-image settings.
- **To curate and release a novel multi-image slow-thinking dataset, CMMCoT-260k,** to facilitate further research in this underserved area, providing a structured benchmark with explicit reasoning chains, spatial coordinates, and entity-specific imagery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CMMCoT framework, built upon the Qwen2-VL architecture, addresses complex multi-image reasoning through several innovative components and a structured training approach.

#### 3.1. Baseline Model and Multimodal Sequence Representation

The chosen baseline, Qwen2-VL, is advantageous due to its Vision Transformer (ViT) supporting Naive Dynamic Resolution, allowing it to process images of arbitrary resolutions and convert them into a variable number of visual tokens. Its Multi-modal Rotary Position Embedding (M-RoPE) also effectively models positional information across multimodal inputs. CMMCoT represents complex multi-image and textual data in an interleaved visual-textual format. Special tokens are used for image indexing (e.g., `<IMG>0</IMG>`), and entities are represented by bounding box coordinates (`<|box start|> (x0, y0), (x1, y1) <|box end|>`) normalized to 0-1000, which are tokenized and embedded as textual data. Visual content for entities is denoted by `<|vision start|>` and `<|vision end|>` markers, with entity images encoded by a visual encoder at a minimum resolution of 512 pixels for detailed feature extraction.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

CMMCoT employs a two-stage training strategy to balance performance on complex multi-image tasks with general visual understanding:
- **Stage 1: Multi-Image Training:** The model is initially trained exclusively on the self-constructed CMMCoT-260k dataset, focusing on mastering intricate multi-image tasks.
- **Stage 2: Mixed Training:** The CMMCoT-260k dataset is then mixed with a general dataset at a 1:1 sampling ratio. This phase aims to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and preserve the model's broad visual understanding capabilities.

During training on CMMCoT-260k, the model receives multiple images and questions as input, and outputs an answer encompassing the reasoning process, entity coordinates, and entity images. A specific prompt, "Please answer the question with reasoning and identify key objects," is appended to enhance reasoning. The loss is calculated only on the text, coordinates, and special tokens, not on the entity image part.

#### 3.3. Retrieval-based Image Feature Reasoning Enhancement Module (RIFREM)

RIFREM is a key innovation for test-time memory augmentation, designed to enable comprehensive cross-modal deliberation during inference without increasing model parameters. It mimics human-like cross-image comparison of critical visual regions.
- **Mechanism:** RIFREM treats entity image features as queries (Q) and utilizes multi-image features as keys (K) and values (V). It maintains a memory bank `M` that stores the K/V pairs of multi-image sequences from each decoder layer during initial multi-image input processing.
- **Inference Process:** During CoT prediction, when the `</IMG>` token is encountered (signaling an entity reference), entity image tokens are injected into the decoder layers. Their query vectors are extracted and engage in cross-attention with corresponding K/V pairs retrieved from the memory bank `M`. This process refines the query vectors (`Q'`), which then propagate through subsequent reasoning stages. An ablation study reveals that applying RIFREM across 8 evenly distributed decoder layers provides the best trade-off between accuracy and efficiency.

#### 3.4. CMMCoT-260k Dataset Construction

To address the lack of suitable datasets, the authors constructed CMMCoT-260k, an innovative benchmark with 260,000 meticulously curated data instances. It is built upon existing datasets like GRIT, Flickr30k-Entities, VoCoT, and MANTIS. The dataset incorporates explicit reasoning chains, spatial coordinates, and entity-specific imagery across four distinct task types:
- **Caption:** Generates natural language descriptions for images.
- **Co-reference:** Identifies and links elements referring to the same entity across different images.
- **Comparison:** Performs comparative analyses between multiple images, identifying similarities and differences.
- **Reason:** Addresses complex logical reasoning and decision-making based on inter-image relationships.

The dataset generation pipeline for challenging Comparison and Reason tasks includes:
1. **Constructing QA Rationale Chains:** GPT-4o generates initial reasoning chains and answers. Correct answers retain chains; incorrect ones are refined by re-feeding the question with the correct answer.
2. **Entity Extraction:** Qwen3-235B-A22B extracts textual entities from QA dialogues.
3. **Entity Detection:** Qwen-VL-max generates initial bounding boxes, validated by GPT-4o with an IoU ≥ 0.9 for precision.
4. **Entity Relationship Summarization:** For entities with multiple bounding boxes across images, spatial fusion creates a single unified bounding box to simplify the CoT structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on both multi-image and single-image benchmarks demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the CMMCoT framework.

#### 4.1. Performance Evaluation on Multi-Image Benchmarks

CMMCoT consistently achieved superior performance across both 7B and 3B parameter configurations compared to state-of-the-art models (LLaVA variants, Mantis-Idefics2, InternVL3, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL).
- The **CMMCoT-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL 7B variant** achieved state-of-the-art results on Mantis-Eval (72.2%), MVBench (75.8%), and Q-Bench (78.5%), while showing competitive performance on NLVR2 (89.9%) and BLINK (56.8%). It surpassed the baseline Qwen2.5-VL by an average of 1.4 points.
- The **scaled-down 3B version** also demonstrated robust capabilities, outperforming its parameter-matched counterpart (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) by 2.6 average points, with particular strengths in temporal reasoning (MVBench: 73.1%) and visual-textual verification (NLVR2: 88.9%). This indicates successful balancing of model capacity with cross-modal alignment efficiency.

#### 4.2. Performance Evaluation on Single-Image Benchmarks

The model's performance on single-image datasets (MMMU, MMStar, SQA, RealWorldQA, MME, POPE, HallBench) also showed consistent superiority. The CMMCoT-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL 7B achieved an average score of 75.5%, outperforming the baseline Qwen2.5-VL by 4.1 points. This suggests that the proposed multimodal hybrid sequential chain reasoning strategy generally enhances comprehension capabilities, extending its benefits beyond multi-image scenarios to fundamental visual-semantic alignment tasks.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis and Interpretability

Visualization experiments highlighted CMMCoT's advantages over traditional models like GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-VL.
- **Object Localization and Quantitative Reasoning:** CMMCoT accurately detected, localized, and counted objects (e.g., cutlery) in ambiguous contexts, where other models failed.
- **Anomaly Detection and Logical Reasoning:** The model not only provided correct answers but also identified common-sense violations in visual inputs and offered explicit causal interpretations, demonstrating enhanced reasoning capabilities and interpretability of the intermediate reasoning process.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Modules:** The ablation studies confirmed the incremental value of each component:
 - Grounding-based information alone offered a modest 0.1-point average improvement.
 - Combining grounding with entity images led to a significant gain, improving the average score from 73.2% to 74.4%.
 - Integrating the RIFREM module during inference provided an additional boost, raising the average score to 74.6%, by effectively exploiting cross-image feature relationships.
- **Impact of RIFREM Layer Placement:** Evaluating RIFREM integration at different network depths showed that applying it only at the first and last layers degraded performance. Progressive integration, specifically across 8 evenly distributed layers, achieved the optimal trade-off between accuracy gains and latency overhead.

#### 4.5. CMMCoT Fine-tuning on Other Models

An additional experiment revealed that fine-tuning other models (LLaVA, Mantis-Idefics2, LLaVA-OV) on the CMMCoT-260k dataset often led to a performance decline or only moderate improvement. This indicated that CMMCoT's full potential is realized only when the original multimodal model possesses strong visual localization capabilities, as present in the Qwen2-VL series.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CMMCoT framework represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI, particularly in addressing the complex challenge of multi-image understanding. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging the Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** CMMCoT directly tackles the limitations of existing MLLMs that struggle with intricate multi-image reasoning. By moving beyond "direct prediction" and text-centric CoT, it enables MLLMs to perform sophisticated visual comparisons and integrate information across multiple visual contexts, mirroring human cognitive processes more closely.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Reliability:** The incorporation of coordinate-guided visual token extraction and explicit reasoning chains makes the model's decision-making process more transparent. This increased interpretability is crucial for building trust in AI systems, especially in high-stakes applications where understanding *why* a model reached a conclusion is as important as the conclusion itself.
- **Advancement in Multi-Modal Reasoning Paradigms:** The framework's core innovations—interleaved multimodal reasoning chains with visual tokens and test-time memory augmentation (RIFREM)—introduce novel mechanisms for dynamic deliberation over visual contexts. This paves the way for new architectures that can dynamically adapt their reasoning depth and leverage external memory during inference.
- **Facilitating Research with a Novel Dataset:** The creation of the CMMCoT-260k dataset is a substantial contribution. By providing a meticulously curated benchmark with explicit reasoning chains and rich annotations, it lowers the barrier for researchers to explore and develop advanced multi-image understanding models, accelerating progress in this area.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** Improved multi-image comprehension has vast potential across various domains:
 - **E-commerce:** Better product comparison, anomaly detection in listings, and enhanced visual search.
 - **Medical Imaging:** Analyzing multiple scans for diagnosis, tracking disease progression across images.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Integrating information from multiple cameras and sensors for safer navigation.
 - **Content Moderation:** Detecting nuanced visual patterns or context across a series of images.
 - **Scientific Research:** Analyzing multi-panel figures in scientific papers or comparing experimental results.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work underscores the importance of structured reasoning mechanisms and opens avenues for further exploration into dynamic multi-modal reasoning, more sophisticated memory-efficient architectures, and the development of even more human-like "slow thinking" processes in AI. The observation that only models with strong localization abilities benefit from CMMCoT fine-tuning also provides crucial guidance for future model design.

In conclusion, CMMCoT significantly pushes the boundaries of MLLM capabilities by addressing a critical challenge in complex multi-image comprehension, offering a more interpretable, effective, and human-inspired approach to multimodal reasoning.
