# Core Knowledge Deficits in Multi-Modal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new study by researchers from UCSD, JHU, and CMU, among others, evaluates Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for fundamental cognitive abilities, revealing consistent deficits in core knowledge and a reliance on shortcut learning even in state-of-the-art models.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate strong performance on complex reasoning tasks but struggle with basic, intuitive human cognitive abilities, a phenomenon known as Moravec's Paradox.
- Current MLLMs lack robustness, generalization, and are susceptible to shortcut learning, raising questions about their genuine conceptual understanding.
- Existing benchmarks primarily assess high-level reasoning, failing to evaluate the foundational "core knowledge" that underpins human intelligence from early development.

## Method

- A new large-scale benchmark, ==CoreCognition==, was developed, featuring **1,503** multi-modal samples across **12** core knowledge concepts derived from human cognitive development theory.
- **230** diverse MLLMs were evaluated using robust inference infrastructure and various prompting strategies, including a =="circular evaluation"== method to mitigate bias.
- A novel =="Concept Hacking" methodology== was introduced, comparing model performance on control vs. causally manipulated stimuli to distinguish genuine understanding from shortcut learning.

## Results

- MLLMs consistently underperformed on low-level cognitive abilities, trailing human performance by **15-22%** on average, while showing better but still deficient performance on higher-level abilities.
- Concept Hacking experiments demonstrated that MLLMs, even as they scale, primarily exhibit core deficits or rely on shortcut learning, with few models displaying genuine, robust core knowledge.
- Scaling models, reasoning-augmented approaches, and most prompting techniques did not resolve these deficits, although a specific "cognitive instruction" prompt improved accuracy by over **6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unlike human cognition, MLLMs do not exhibit coherent, hierarchically structured competencies for core knowledge, with weak correlations between foundational and advanced abilities.
> - Core knowledge abilities in MLLMs do not scale with increasing model parameters; performance on low-level tasks shows limited or no improvement, and in some cases, even degradation (e.g., inverse scaling for perspective-taking).
> - Stronger performance on CoreCognition abilities generally predicts better performance on most external high-level MLLM benchmarks, despite the models' internal deficits.
