# Core Knowledge Deficits in Multi-Modal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new study by researchers from UCSD, JHU, and CMU, among others, evaluates Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for fundamental cognitive abilities, revealing consistent deficits in core knowledge and a reliance on shortcut learning even in state-of-the-art models.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) demonstrate strong performance on complex reasoning tasks but struggle with basic, intuitive human cognitive abilities, a phenomenon known as Moravec's Paradox.
- Current MLLMs lack robustness, generalization, and are susceptible to shortcut learning, raising questions about their genuine conceptual understanding.
- Existing benchmarks primarily assess high-level reasoning, failing to evaluate the foundational "core knowledge" that underpins human intelligence from early development.

## Method

- A new large-scale benchmark, ==CoreCognition==, was developed, featuring **1,503** multi-modal samples across **12** core knowledge concepts derived from human cognitive development theory.
- **230** diverse MLLMs were evaluated using robust inference infrastructure and various prompting strategies, including a =="circular evaluation"== method to mitigate bias.
- A novel =="Concept Hacking" methodology== was introduced, comparing model performance on control vs. causally manipulated stimuli to distinguish genuine understanding from shortcut learning.

## Results

- MLLMs consistently underperformed on low-level cognitive abilities, trailing human performance by **15-22%** on average, while showing better but still deficient performance on higher-level abilities.
- Concept Hacking experiments demonstrated that MLLMs, even as they scale, primarily exhibit core deficits or rely on shortcut learning, with few models displaying genuine, robust core knowledge.
- Scaling models, reasoning-augmented approaches, and most prompting techniques did not resolve these deficits, although a specific "cognitive instruction" prompt improved accuracy by over **6%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unlike human cognition, MLLMs do not exhibit coherent, hierarchically structured competencies for core knowledge, with weak correlations between foundational and advanced abilities.
> - Core knowledge abilities in MLLMs do not scale with increasing model parameters; performance on low-level tasks shows limited or no improvement, and in some cases, even degradation (e.g., inverse scaling for perspective-taking).
> - Stronger performance on CoreCognition abilities generally predicts better performance on most external high-level MLLM benchmarks, despite the models' internal deficits.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have led to impressive capabilities in high-level perception and reasoning, often reaching or exceeding human performance on complex tasks like chart understanding, geometry, and action recognition. However, a persistent challenge, often referred to as Moravec's Paradox, is that these advanced AI systems still struggle with tasks that are intuitive and effortless for humans, such as basic counting, perspective-taking, or spatial reasoning. Furthermore, MLLMs exhibit limitations in robustness, generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios, and susceptibility to adversarial attacks or prompt variations.

This paper positions itself directly in this landscape by hypothesizing that these observed deficiencies in MLLMs stem from a fundamental absence of "core knowledge." Core knowledge refers to rudimentary cognitive abilities innate to humans from early childhood, which underpin more advanced reasoning. This concept has deep roots in Western philosophy (Plato, Kant) and modern developmental cognitive science (Piaget, Fodor, Spelke). While existing MLLM benchmarks often focus on high-level reasoning or task coverage (e.g., MathVerse, ScienceQA, M3GIA, Marvel), they do not specifically target these early-emerging, foundational cognitive abilities. DevBench, for instance, focuses on language learning but not multi-modal core knowledge.

The work also connects to the growing concern of "shortcut learning" in deep neural networks. Models are prone to exploiting spurious correlations in training data, leading to brittle performance when conditions change. This paper investigates whether MLLMs truly acquire conceptual understanding or merely rely on such shortcuts for apparent proficiency, especially as models scale in size. By introducing a novel benchmark and evaluation methodology grounded in developmental psychology, this research aims to bridge the gap between advanced AI capabilities and fundamental human cognitive endowments, offering a new lens to understand and address the limitations of current MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to rigorously examine the hypothesis that the observed limitations in Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly their lack of robustness and difficulty with seemingly simple tasks, are due to an absence of core knowledge. The authors aim to explore the existence, representation, and utilization of core knowledge within MLLMs.

The key motivations behind this study are:
- **Addressing Moravec's Paradox:** To provide a plausible explanation for why MLLMs, despite excelling at complex, high-level reasoning and perception tasks, consistently underperform on rudimentary tasks that are intuitive for humans.
- **Evaluating Foundational Cognitive Abilities:** To move beyond current benchmarks that primarily assess high-level reasoning, and instead focus on fundamental cognitive abilities hypothesized to be innate in humans (e.g., object permanence, intuitive physics, spatial reasoning).
- **Investigating Genuine Understanding vs. Shortcut Learning:** To ascertain whether MLLMs genuinely acquire conceptual understanding of the world or if their performance is predominantly driven by shortcut learning and exploitation of spurious correlations in vast training datasets.
- **Understanding Scaling Laws:** To determine if the empirical scaling laws observed in LLMs and MLLMs, which typically lead to predictable performance improvements with increased parameters and data, also apply to the acquisition of core knowledge.
- **Informing Future AI Development:** To identify critical cognitive deficits in current MLLMs, thereby guiding the design of more robust, generalizable, and human-like AI systems.

To achieve these objectives, the authors introduce a new large-scale benchmark and a novel controlled evaluation method.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged methodology: the development of a novel benchmark called **CoreCognition** and a controlled evaluation method termed **Concept Hacking**.

#### 3.1. CoreCognition Benchmark Design

CoreCognition is a large-scale benchmark designed to probe 12 core knowledge concepts, meticulously selected from developmental cognitive science, spanning Jean Piaget's four stages of human cognitive development: Sensorimotor, Preoperational (implicitly covered by Concrete Operational), Concrete Operational, and Formal Operational. These concepts include:
- **Sensorimotor:** Boundary, Continuity, Permanence (Object Permanence), Spatiality, Perceptual Constancy.
- **Concrete Operational:** Intuitive Physics, Perspective-Taking, Conservation, Hierarchy (Hierarchical Relation).
- **Formal Operational:** Intentionality Understanding, Mechanical Reasoning, Tool Using.

The benchmark comprises 1,503 samples, with over 95 examples per concept, presented as multiple-choice questions (MCQs) or true/false questions. The dataset curation process involved:
- **Prototyping:** Operationalizing theoretical constructs into prototype scenarios (e.g., classic infant experiments for object permanence).
- **Instantiation:** Generating diverse visual modalities (single images, videos, multiple images) from various sources (internet, public datasets, synthetic content, original recordings) that abstractly align with the prototypes. Each visual asset is paired with a carefully designed question, options, and ground-truth answers.
- **Quality Control:** Rigorous two-round independent cross-validation by annotators with cognitive science or computer science backgrounds. Criteria for instances included **Discriminativeness** (models lacking target knowledge select incorrect answers), **Minimal Confounding** (questions avoid reliance on unrelated capabilities like object recognition), and **Minimal Text Shortcut** (answers require multimodal comprehension, not just textual cues). An additional round of human validation via Mechanical Turk was performed.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Strategy

The evaluation involved assessing 230 diverse MLLMs, including proprietary (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude, Grok) and open-source models (e.g., Qwen-VL, InternVL, LLaVA, DeepSeek-VL), ranging from 1 billion to 110 billion parameters.
- **Inference:** A scalable evaluation infrastructure was built to handle parallel execution and compartmentalized environments for efficient inference across hundreds of models with varying dependencies. Official codebases were strictly followed.
- **Prompting:** 11 different prompting techniques were used, categorized into "no prompt," "think deep," "explanation," "reward & penalty," "bias mitigation," "role playing," and "cognitive instruction." This allowed for an analysis of prompt influence.
- **Scoring:**
 - **Circular Evaluation:** To mitigate option-position bias, each k-choice question was rotated k times, and the proportion of correct responses over the augmented set was calculated, providing a fairer estimate of true ability.
 - **Two-Stage Scoring for Free-Form Responses:** MLLM outputs were first mapped to one of the predefined options or marked as FAIL if unaligned. This mapping combined rule-based template matching with an LLM-as-a-Judge approach (using an ensemble of four advanced LLMs for robustness). Models with consistently high FAIL rates (≥20%) were excluded.

#### 3.3. Concept Hacking Methodology

Concept Hacking is a novel controlled experiment designed to determine if MLLMs possess genuine core knowledge or merely rely on shortcut learning.
- **Design:** 45 samples from CoreCognition were paired with a "manipulated" version. The manipulated version preserves task-irrelevant conditions but systematically alters causal features to invert the ground-truth label (e.g., a bridge that *appears* to narrow due to perspective in the control task is *actually* narrowed in the manipulated task).
- **Outcome Types:** This method yields four possible outcomes for each model on a control-manipulation pair:
 - **Core Knowledge:** Correct on both control and manipulated tasks, indicating genuine understanding.
 - **Shortcut-taking:** Correct on control but incorrect on manipulated, indicating reliance on spurious correlations.
 - **Core Deficits:** Incorrect on control (regardless of manipulated task performance), indicating absence of core knowledge.
 - **Coincidental Accuracy:** Incorrect on control but correct on manipulated (being right for the wrong reason). This also indicates a lack of genuine understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research uncovered several key findings collectively demonstrating core knowledge deficits in MLLMs:

- **1. Core Knowledge Deficits:** MLLMs consistently underperform on low-level abilities (Sensorimotor and Concrete Operational, excluding Hierarchy) compared to high-level abilities (Formal Operational). This disparity is statistically significant, contrasting sharply with human performance, which is high across all stages. For instance, the best models (GPT-o1, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL) trailed human performance by 15-22% on average for lower-level stages. Proprietary models did not consistently outperform open-source ones, indicating a pervasive deficit. Performance on Perspective Taking was notably poor across all models, likely due to limitations in mental simulation.

- **2. Misaligned Dependency:** Unlike humans, MLLMs do not exhibit coherent, hierarchically structured competencies. Pearson correlations between core abilities showed weak cross-stage correlations, particularly for Sensorimotor abilities (Permanence, Spatiality, Continuity) and some Concrete Operational abilities (Perspective, Conservation, Intuitive Physics). This suggests that foundational competencies do not provide the developmental scaffolding for more advanced stages as observed in human cognition, indicating a lack of structured representational dependencies.

- **3. Core Abilities are Predictive of Higher-Level Abilities:** Despite their internal misalignments, strong performance on core abilities in CoreCognition reliably predicts higher performance on most external high-level MLLM benchmarks (e.g., SEED-Bench) and other abstract tasks. Exceptions like ChartQA (due to textual understanding orthogonality) and Perspective/Intuitive Physics (which require structured internal representations and counterfactual reasoning) reinforce that deficits in core knowledge manifest in broader benchmark evaluations.

- **4. Not Scaling for Low-Level Abilities:** While scaling laws typically predict performance improvements with increased model size, MLLMs exhibit significantly less, or even no, scalability on low-level abilities. For seven out of nine low-level abilities, performance improvement with increasing parameters was limited. Notably, perspective-taking ability showed an inverse scaling trend, declining as models grew larger, suggesting intensified egocentric bias. This indicates that merely increasing model size is insufficient for developing core knowledge.

- **5. Concept Hacking Reveals Shortcut-Taking Behaviors:** The controlled experiments with Concept Hacking demonstrated that MLLMs, even as they scale, predominantly exhibit core deficits or rely on shortcut learning rather than genuine conceptual understanding. A substantial portion of models clustered in quadrants indicating high manipulation accuracy with below-chance control accuracy (core deficits) or high control accuracy with below-chance manipulation accuracy (shortcut-taking). Crucially, almost none of the models achieved the roughly equal accuracy on both tasks characteristic of robust core knowledge and immunity to concept hacking, suggesting that increasing model size amplifies shortcut-taking or the persistence of deficits.

- **Impact of Reasoning and Prompting:**
 - **Reasoning Models:** Reasoning-augmented models and test-time scaling generally did not confer a clear advantage for core cognitive abilities. In 10 of 12 core abilities, no significant differences were observed compared to instruction-tuned counterparts. This suggests current architectures struggle with grounded reasoning and that chain-of-thought prompting or test-time scaling cannot resolve deeper representational deficits.
 - **Prompt Variations:** Most prompt variations failed to improve performance. However, the "cognitive instruction" prompt (a concise description of the conceptual target) significantly outperformed others, increasing accuracy by over 6%. This suggests that explicitly stating the relevant core knowledge acts as a retrieval cue, helping models access diffusely represented foundational concepts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work profoundly impacts the understanding and development of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by revealing a critical gap in their cognitive architecture.

- **Explaining MLLM Limitations:** The findings provide a compelling explanation for Moravec's Paradox and the observed lack of robustness, poor generalization, and vulnerability to perturbations in current MLLMs. It suggests that these issues stem from a foundational absence of core knowledge, which in humans provides a robust grounding for higher-level reasoning and perception.
- **Challenging Scaling Hypothesis:** The demonstration that low-level core abilities do not scale with increasing model parameters (and can even degrade) challenges the prevalent "scaling hypothesis" as a sole pathway to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It implies that simply making models larger or training them on more data is insufficient for instilling genuine conceptual understanding.
- **Guiding Future AI Design:** By identifying specific "failure modes" related to concepts like permanence, spatiality, boundary, and continuity, the research offers concrete directions for developing more robust and interpretable MLLMs. It suggests that future pretraining strategies might need to explicitly target these foundational capacities, perhaps by teaching or distilling core knowledge into models *before* large-scale pretraining to enable more data-efficient generalization, analogous to human learning.
- **Rethinking AGI Evaluation:** The paper argues for the value of evaluating AI progress through a cognitive lens grounded in human developmental trajectories. Even if AGI is multiply realizable, the human path offers an empirically grounded reference point. A benchmark like CoreCognition provides a tool to assess whether AI systems are developing genuine understanding or merely superficial proficiency via shortcut learning.
- **Addressing Shortcut Learning:** The Concept Hacking method offers a powerful diagnostic tool to differentiate between genuine understanding and shortcut-taking. Its findings emphasize that even state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o exhibit shortcut reliance, highlighting a pervasive problem that needs dedicated solutions beyond current scaling paradigms.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
- **VQA Format Constraints:** The reliance on a Visual Question Answering (VQA) format introduces auxiliary demands (linguistic capabilities, counting, object recognition) that are hard to fully mitigate, potentially confounding true core knowledge assessment. It also restricts applicability to models capable of linguistic processing. The authors suggest future work will explore non-linguistic or purely visual formats.
- **Shortcut Exploitation:** Despite meticulous design, MLLMs still exploit shortcuts in CoreCognition, potentially inflating performance. While Concept Hacking addresses this, its resource-intensive nature limits large-scale applicability.
- **Ethical Concerns:** The adversarial nature of Concept Hacking could pose misuse risks if adapted for military purposes.

In conclusion, this research provides a critical perspective on the current state of MLLMs, urging the AI community to consider the foundational cognitive abilities that underpin human-like intelligence. It posits that true progress towards robust and generalizable AI may require more than just scaling, necessitating deliberate efforts to imbue models with core knowledge.
