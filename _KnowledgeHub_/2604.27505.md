# Leveraging Verifier-Based Reinforcement Learning in Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Edit-R1, a framework for image editing, incorporates a verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) within a Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) setup to provide granular, interpretable feedback. The RRM achieves 82.2% accuracy in predicting human preferences, surpassing existing closed-source APIs, and improves image editing models like FLUX.Kontext by increasing its Overall Score from 5.77 to 6.24 and Semantic Consistency from 6.27 to 6.86.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been under-explored in image editing, in contrast to its widespread use in text-to-image generation.
- Current image editing reward models often provide holistic scores, lacking the nuance to evaluate instruction fidelity, preservation of unedited content, and overall visual quality simultaneously.
- The inherent non-differentiable nature of reasoning-based reward models, which produce multi-step traces, makes them incompatible with traditional RLHF algorithms requiring differentiable reward functions.

## Method

- Introduces **Edit-R1**, a framework featuring a verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) that decomposes image editing instructions into verifiable principles (Keep, Follow, Quality) and employs Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- Develops a two-stage training process for the RRM: an initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase utilizing external VLM quality control, followed by Group Contrastive Preference Optimization (GCPO) to align the RRM with human preferences from pairwise data.
- Leverages the trained, non-differentiable RRM to optimize downstream image editing models using an adapted GRPO algorithm, which calculates advantages from the RRM's holistic reward scores.

## Results

- The 7B Edit-RRM, trained with the full pipeline, achieved an **82.2%** accuracy in predicting human preferences on an internal benchmark, surpassing closed-source APIs such as Seed-1.5-VL (**79.3%**) and Seed-1.6-VL (**77.2%**).
- Optimizing FLUX.Kontext with the 7B RL-RRM increased its Overall Score from 5.77 to 6.24 and Semantic Consistency from 6.27 to 6.86 on the GEdit-Bench-EN, demonstrating improved editing model performance.
- Human evaluation using the Good-Same-Bad protocol showed that FLUX.Kontext optimized with the 7B RL-RRM achieved a +23.2 GSB score against the original baseline, indicating a clear preference for the enhanced model's outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from simple scoring to a reasoning-based verifier with explicit instruction decomposition provides more robust, granular, and interpretable feedback for image editing tasks.
> - A two-stage RRM training process, combining large-scale SFT with rigorous VLM-based filtering and subsequent human preference alignment via GCPO, effectively trains complex, CoT-based reward models.
> - Non-differentiable, reasoning-based reward models can be effectively integrated into RLHF frameworks for image editing by using adapted algorithms like GRPO that compute advantages from the RRM's holistic reward signals.
