# Leveraging Verifier-Based Reinforcement Learning in Image Editing

> [!summary] Summary
> Edit-R1, a framework for image editing, incorporates a verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) within a Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) setup to provide granular, interpretable feedback. The RRM achieves 82.2% accuracy in predicting human preferences, surpassing existing closed-source APIs, and improves image editing models like FLUX.Kontext by increasing its Overall Score from 5.77 to 6.24 and Semantic Consistency from 6.27 to 6.86.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has been under-explored in image editing, in contrast to its widespread use in text-to-image generation.
- Current image editing reward models often provide holistic scores, lacking the nuance to evaluate instruction fidelity, preservation of unedited content, and overall visual quality simultaneously.
- The inherent non-differentiable nature of reasoning-based reward models, which produce multi-step traces, makes them incompatible with traditional RLHF algorithms requiring differentiable reward functions.

## Method

- Introduces **Edit-R1**, a framework featuring a verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) that decomposes image editing instructions into verifiable principles (Keep, Follow, Quality) and employs Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
- Develops a two-stage training process for the RRM: an initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase utilizing external VLM quality control, followed by Group Contrastive Preference Optimization (GCPO) to align the RRM with human preferences from pairwise data.
- Leverages the trained, non-differentiable RRM to optimize downstream image editing models using an adapted GRPO algorithm, which calculates advantages from the RRM's holistic reward scores.

## Results

- The 7B Edit-RRM, trained with the full pipeline, achieved an **82.2%** accuracy in predicting human preferences on an internal benchmark, surpassing closed-source APIs such as Seed-1.5-VL (**79.3%**) and Seed-1.6-VL (**77.2%**).
- Optimizing FLUX.Kontext with the 7B RL-RRM increased its Overall Score from 5.77 to 6.24 and Semantic Consistency from 6.27 to 6.86 on the GEdit-Bench-EN, demonstrating improved editing model performance.
- Human evaluation using the Good-Same-Bad protocol showed that FLUX.Kontext optimized with the 7B RL-RRM achieved a +23.2 GSB score against the original baseline, indicating a clear preference for the enhanced model's outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from simple scoring to a reasoning-based verifier with explicit instruction decomposition provides more robust, granular, and interpretable feedback for image editing tasks.
> - A two-stage RRM training process, combining large-scale SFT with rigorous VLM-based filtering and subsequent human preference alignment via GCPO, effectively trains complex, CoT-based reward models.
> - Non-differentiable, reasoning-based reward models can be effectively integrated into RLHF frameworks for image editing by using adapted algorithms like GRPO that compute advantages from the RRM's holistic reward signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image editing has progressed from task-specific systems to sophisticated diffusion and flow-based generative models. Parallel to this, text-to-image (T2I) generation has seen significant advancements, with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) emerging as a crucial post-training paradigm for aligning model outputs with human preferences. This alignment is primarily driven by robust reward models and specialized optimization algorithms.

However, the application of RLHF to image editing has remained comparatively limited. Existing research in image editing often focuses on pretraining and supervised fine-tuning (SFT), rather than incorporating extensive human feedback through RLHF. A primary bottleneck identified is the absence of a sufficiently robust and general reward model for image editing tasks. Current reward models typically operate as holistic scorers, providing a single, undifferentiated score. This approach often fails to adequately address the complex and multifaceted requirements of image editing, which include nuanced aspects like instruction fidelity, preservation of unedited regions, and overall visual quality. The consequence is often biased or inaccurate feedback that may not fully capture human preferences or identify specific deficiencies in edited images.

This paper positions itself as addressing this gap by proposing a fundamental shift in reward modeling for image editing: moving from a simple "scorer" to a "reasoning verifier." This aligns with recent trends in large language models (LLMs) that integrate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for more interpretable and accurate evaluations. The work builds upon advances in visual-language models (VLMs) and RLHF algorithms, aiming to bring the benefits of human preference alignment, previously successful in T2I generation and LLMs, to the domain of image editing. The paper differentiates its approach from existing reward models by integrating generative, pointwise evaluation with verifier-based, principle-decomposition, CoT reasoning, and reinforcement learning, specifically tailored for visual editing tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the limitations of current reward models and RLHF applications in image editing:

- **Address the Lack of Robust Reward Models for Image Editing:** The existing paradigm of treating reward models as holistic scorers often leads to feedback that is biased, inconsistent, or prone to hallucination. Image editing requires a detailed evaluation that considers multiple criteria simultaneously, such as precise adherence to instructions, faithful preservation of elements not intended for modification, and generic visual quality. The motivation is to overcome these deficiencies by introducing a more granular and interpretable evaluation mechanism.
- **Shift from Holistic Scoring to Verifier-Based Reasoning:** The core motivation is to revolutionize how image editing outputs are evaluated. Instead of a single score, the objective is to develop a "reasoning verifier" that can explicitly deconstruct a complex editing instruction into distinct, verifiable principles. This verifier would then systematically evaluate the edited image against each principle, generating a detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) analysis, and subsequently aggregating these evaluations into a fine-grained, interpretable reward. This approach aims to provide clearer, more actionable feedback.
- **Develop a Reliable Verifier-Based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM):** This objective involves two specific challenges:
 - **Cold-Start Training:** Building a verifier that can consistently perform structured reasoning and align with human preferences from noisy initial data. The motivation here is to establish a strong foundational model capable of generating plausible reasoning trajectories.
 - **Aligning Pointwise RRM with Pairwise Human Preference Data:** The pointwise nature of the RRM's detailed reasoning output presents a challenge when aligning it with common pairwise human preference data (e.g., "image A is better than B"). Standard RLHF algorithms are not designed for this type of complex, multi-step output, motivating the need for a novel optimization approach.
- **Develop RLHF Algorithms Compatible with Non-Differentiable RRMs:** The reasoning process within the RRM, which involves generating an explicit multi-step reasoning trace through discrete token sampling, is inherently non-differentiable. This renders many standard RLHF algorithms, which require differentiable reward signals, incompatible. A key motivation is to devise a method that can effectively leverage such a powerful, non-differentiable RRM to achieve stable improvements in downstream image editing models.
- **Enhance State-of-the-Art Image Editing Models:** The ultimate goal is to apply the developed verifier-based RRM within an RLHF framework to improve the instruction-following capabilities, semantic consistency, and perceptual quality of existing advanced image editing models. This aims to demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed reward modeling paradigm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Edit-R1, is structured around building and leveraging a verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) to enhance image editing. The methodology consists of a two-stage training process for the RRM, followed by its application in a reinforcement learning loop for downstream editing models.

#### 3.1. Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) Training

The RRM training proceeds in two stages:

**Stage 1: Verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model with Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**

This stage focuses on constructing a high-quality supervised dataset to initialize the RRM's reasoning capabilities.

1. **Dataset Curation:** A dataset of 200,000 samples is curated from a public image-editing benchmark. This includes a "Random" subset (100,000 samples) for general distribution and a "Hard" subset (100,000 samples) filtered by GPT-4o to include complex, multi-step editing instructions. Each sample comprises a reference image ($x_{ref}$) and an edit instruction ($q$).
2. **Decomposing Instructions into Principles:** For each ($x_{ref}$, $q$) pair, the Seed-1.5-VL API is used to decompose the instruction into a set of verifiable principles ($P = \{p_k\}$). These principles are categorized into "Keep" (elements to preserve), "Follow" (modifications to apply), and "Quality" (overall visual integrity). A few-shot prompting approach with expert-authored exemplars is used to guide the decomposition.
3. **Large-Scale Quadruple Generation:** Various image-editing models (e.g., Flux-Kontext, Bagel, SeedEdit3.0) generate diverse edited candidates ($x_{edit}$). Each candidate forms a quadruple ($x_{edit}$, $x_{ref}$, $q$, $P$), resulting in approximately 2 million quadruples.
4. **VLM Reasoning and Point-wise Scoring:** Vision-Language Models (VLM Pools) process each quadruple. They generate multiple "Think + Score" Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories by assessing the edited image against each principle in $P$, followed by an aggregated scalar score. System prompts are varied, and multiple VLM variants (e.g., Seed-1.5VL-1.5/Seed-1.6-VL) are used to enhance diversity. The reasoning trace adheres to a fixed format.
5. **External Verification and SFT Data Selection:** An external VLM (SeedVLM-1.5) functions as a point-wise verifier to re-evaluate each principle in $P$ for every generated CoT reasoning trace. It calculates a verification accuracy, and the CoT achieving the highest accuracy is selected. This filtered data, comprising the instruction, images, principles, CoT trace, and final score, forms the SFT dataset for the RRM's cold start.

**Stage 2: Reasoning-Reinforced Reward Learning with Group Contrastive Preference Optimization (GCPO)**

This stage refines the RRM using human preference data to mitigate issues like hallucination and misjudgment of edit magnitudes observed after SFT.

1. **Preference Data:** A dataset of approximately 10,000 human-annotated preference pairs ($x_w$, $x_l$) is collected for various contexts $c = (x_{ref}, q)$, where $x_w$ is the preferred (winner) image and $x_l$ is the non-preferred (loser) image. "Same" pairs are excluded.
2. **Win/Loss Ratio Rewards:** For each preference pair, the RRM ($R_\phi$) stochastically generates $N$ distinct reasoning traces and corresponding scalar scores for both $x_w$ and $x_l$. A per-sample win/loss ratio is computed based on exhaustive pairwise comparisons between the scores of the winner and loser groups. A winner candidate's win ratio is the fraction of non-preferred candidates it scores higher than; a loser candidate's loss ratio is the fraction of preferred candidates that score lower than it.
3. **Optimization with GCPO:** GCPO is a novel reinforcement learning algorithm used to optimize the RRM. The RRM ($R_\phi$) itself acts as the policy. Advantages ($A_w$, $A_l$) are computed independently within the preferred and non-preferred rollout groups based on their respective win/loss ratios. The objective function is the sum of the two groups' clipped surrogate losses, without a KL divergence term explicitly mentioned for GCPO, contrasting with some other PPO-like methods.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning for Image Editing

After training, the RRM serves as a reward signal for optimizing downstream image editing models using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

1. **Policy Optimization with GRPO:** The editing model ($\pi_\theta$) acts as the policy. For a given context $c$, it generates a group of $G$ edited images.
2. **RRM-based Reward:** The trained RRM ($R_\phi$) evaluates each generated image $x_i^0$ based on $c$ and $P$, providing a holistic reward score ($\tau_i$).
3. **Advantage Calculation:** The advantage ($A_i$) for each image $x_i^0$ within its group is calculated by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards within that group.
4. **GRPO Objective:** The GRPO training objective maximizes the expected advantage, incorporating a clipped objective function to prevent unstable updates and a KL-divergence penalty term to regularize the policy and keep it close to a reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$). This enables the editing model to optimize for human-perceived quality and instruction fidelity as reflected by the RRM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of the Edit-R1 framework.

#### 4.1. Reward Model Performance

- **Superior Accuracy on Internal Benchmarks:** The 7B Edit-RRM, trained with the full two-stage pipeline (SFT and GCPO), achieved an accuracy of 82.2% on an internal human preference benchmark. This result surpassed strong proprietary VLMs like Seed-1.5-VL (79.3%) and Seed-1.6-VL (77.2%), indicating the efficacy of the training strategy.
- **Outperformance on Public Benchmarks:** On the public EditRewardBench, the SFT-trained RRM already outperformed EditScore-7B (73.3% vs. 65.9%). With inference scaling, EditScore-7B reached 72.7%, still below the SFT-RRM. The application of GCPO further improved the Edit-RRM's accuracy to 78.2%, suggesting that the observed gains are not solely due to internal benchmark biases.
- **Impact of Principled Data Curation:** The "Think" (reasoning generation) and "Verify" (external filtering) components of the cold-start SFT pipeline were shown to be critical. The "Think+Verify" approach consistently yielded better performance (e.g., Qwen-7B accuracy improved from 68.9% with "Think" only to 75.4% with "Think+Verify"). This indicates that detailed, filtered reasoning traces are essential for robust reward model supervision.
- **Effectiveness of GCPO:** GCPO significantly boosted the RRM's performance, improving accuracy from 73.3% to 78.2% on EditRewardBench. This gain is attributed to better alignment with human preferences, despite GCPO utilizing a comparatively small dataset of 10,000 human preference pairs (less than 1% of the SFT data scale).
- **Scalability:** Performance of the RRM consistently improved with increasing model parameters, demonstrating a clear scaling trend from 3B to 7B. Training dynamics showed convergence of SFT loss, steady improvement in SFT evaluation accuracy, and stable training reward during the GCPO phase, with the weighted advantage indicating learning to generate longer reasoning traces for correct judgments.

#### 4.2. Image Editing Performance

- **Enhanced Instruction Following and Quality:** The framework demonstrated strong performance gains when applied to downstream editing models. Optimizing FLUX.Kontext with the 7B RL-RRM increased its Overall Score (O) from 5.77 to 6.24 and its Semantic Consistency (SC) from 6.27 to 6.86 on the GEdit-Bench-EN benchmark. This confirmed the RRM's effectiveness as a reward source for policy optimization.
- **Targeted Improvements for Challenging Edits:** While a state-of-the-art open-source model like Qwen-Edit showed a modest overall improvement (O from 7.45 to 7.50), the framework delivered a substantial 15.2% relative gain in the "Motion Change" category (SC from 4.01 to 4.62). This highlights the RRM's ability to identify and address specific weaknesses, even in highly optimized models.
- **Stricter Evaluation by GCPO-refined RRMs:** Training curves indicated that GCPO-refined RRMs, while potentially providing lower training rewards, consistently yielded higher evaluation rewards. This suggests that GCPO transforms the RRM into a stricter and more reliable evaluator, compelling editing models to achieve higher quality and better adherence to human preferences.
- **Human Evaluation Alignment:** A human study using the Good-Same-Bad (GSB) protocol validated the automatic metrics. FLUX.Kontext optimized with the 7B RL-RRM achieved a GSB score of +23.2 against the original FLUX.Kontext baseline, confirming the human-perceived improvements.
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative analysis demonstrated marked improvements in instruction adherence and visual fidelity. Models optimized with Edit-R1 successfully executed challenging edits (e.g., subject addition/removal, motion changes) where baselines failed. For localized edits (e.g., Color Alter), the approach precisely modified target objects without introducing global shifts or artifacts. An example highlighted the RRM's ability to correct hallucination issues (e.g., incorrectly changing hat color during shirt color change) through fine-grained feedback.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that could significantly advance the capabilities of image editing models and the broader field of visual generative AI.

- **Paradigm Shift in Visual Reward Modeling:** The move from holistic scoring to a reasoning-based verifier offers a more robust, interpretable, and fine-grained approach to evaluating complex visual generation tasks. This paradigm shift could inspire similar developments in other multimodal domains where nuanced human preferences are critical but difficult to capture with simple scalar rewards.
- **Improved Alignment with Human Preferences:** By integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning and a two-stage training process (SFT followed by GCPO), the RRM demonstrates a higher alignment with human judgments compared to existing methods. This is crucial for developing generative models that produce outputs perceived as high-quality and instruction-compliant by users.
- **Novel Algorithm for Pointwise RRM Optimization:** The introduction of Group Contrastive Preference Optimization (GCPO) addresses a key challenge in RLHF: optimizing a pointwise, reasoning-based reward model using pairwise human preference data. This algorithm's ability to leverage sparse human feedback to refine complex reasoning trajectories has implications for training models where direct differentiable rewards are unavailable.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity in Image Editing:** The application of the RRM within a GRPO-based RL loop demonstrably improves the performance of state-of-the-art image editing models. This leads to more precise instruction following, better preservation of unedited content, and higher overall visual quality, particularly in complex and challenging editing scenarios (e.g., motion changes, semantic manipulation).
- **Increased Interpretability and Debuggability:** The verifier's explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning provides clear insights into why an edit is considered good or bad, or where a failure occurred. This interpretability can be invaluable for debugging model errors, refining training data, and iteratively improving the generative models.
- **Foundation for Future Visual Generative AI:** Edit-R1 establishes a strong foundation for integrating verifiable human feedback into the training of increasingly sophisticated visual generative AI systems. This could extend beyond image editing to areas like video generation, 3D content creation, and interactive design tools, where high fidelity and precise instruction adherence are paramount. The work suggests a viable path towards developing more reliable, controllable, and user-aligned AI for creative applications.
