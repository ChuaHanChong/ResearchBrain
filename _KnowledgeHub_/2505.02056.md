# Handling Imbalanced Pseudolabels for Vision-Language Models with Concept Alignment and Confusion-Aware Calibrated Margin

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for improving vision-language model adaptation combines concept alignment through LLM-enhanced text descriptions with confusion-aware calibrated margins to address pseudo-label imbalance, achieving state-of-the-art results across six image classification benchmarks while reducing reliance on manually labeled data.

## Problem

- Vision-language models generate imbalanced pseudo-labels when adapting to downstream tasks
- Concept mismatch and confusion between classes leads to unreliable self-training

## Method

- ==Iterative clustering== identifies concept-mismatched classes and uses LLM to generate enhanced descriptions
- ==Confusion-aware calibrated margin== adjusts loss function based on class similarities
- Separate adapters for reliable and dynamically generated ==pseudo-labels== reduce confirmation bias

## Results

- Outperforms existing methods on **six** benchmark datasets across three learning paradigms
- Achieves higher pseudo-label accuracy compared to standard top-k strategies
- Reduces Expected Calibration Error in concept-confused groups
- Demonstrates effectiveness in both unsupervised and semi-supervised settings

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Addressing underlying causes of pseudo-label imbalance (concept mismatch and confusion) more effective than enforcing artificial balance
> - LLM-enhanced text descriptions can significantly improve pseudo-label accuracy
> - Calibrated margins based on class similarity help distinguish confused concepts

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Context:** This paper directly addresses a practical problem in the burgeoning field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs like CLIP have shown remarkable zero-shot capabilities but often require task-specific fine-tuning to achieve optimal performance on downstream tasks. Acquiring sufficient labeled data for this fine-tuning is expensive and time-consuming. A popular alternative is to use the VLM itself to generate *pseudo-labels* for unlabeled data, and then fine-tune the model on this pseudo-labeled dataset.
- **Problem Addressed:** A major obstacle to this approach is the inherent bias of VLMs, leading to imbalanced pseudo-label distributions. This imbalance can negatively impact fine-tuning, resulting in sub-optimal performance.
- **Related Work:** The paper correctly situates itself within this research landscape by referencing relevant prior work. It acknowledges existing strategies such as enforcing equal numbers of pseudo-labels per class (UPL, FPL) or assigning candidate sets of labels (CPL). The authors argue, however, that these methods don't sufficiently address the *underlying causes* of the imbalance.
- **Novelty:** The key contribution lies in identifying and explicitly addressing "concept mismatch" and "concept confusion" as two primary reasons for pseudo-label imbalance. The proposed "concept alignment" and "confusion-aware calibrated margin" mechanisms are designed to mitigate these specific issues, differentiating this work from more general approaches to imbalanced learning or pseudo-labeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The main objective is to develop a more effective method for adapting VLMs to downstream image classification tasks using pseudo-labels generated from unlabeled data.
- **Motivation:** The primary motivation stems from the high cost and effort associated with acquiring labeled data for fine-tuning VLMs. The authors aim to improve the accuracy and balance of pseudo-labels, thus enabling better and more reliable adaptation of VLMs to downstream tasks without relying on human annotation. They hypothesize that by explicitly tackling the root causes of pseudo-label imbalance (concept mismatch and confusion), they can achieve superior performance compared to existing methods. The motivation is clearly articulated and well-justified in the context of current research challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Overall Approach:** The paper proposes a "concept-adaptive pseudo-labeling (CAP)" framework that addresses imbalanced pseudolabels in VLMs by mitigating concept mismatch and concept confusion.
- **Concept Alignment:** Addresses concept mismatch by 1) iteratively clustering image features to identify concept-mismatched classes; 2) using a large language model (LLM) to generate enhanced textual descriptions for these classes; and 3) using the enhanced descriptions to re-assign more accurate pseudo-labels.
- **Confusion-Aware Calibrated Margin:** Tackles concept confusion by introducing a calibrated margin to the loss function. This margin is derived from the similarity between classes and the model's prediction tendencies, encouraging the model to make more distinguishable predictions between similar classes and more balanced predictions across all classes.
- **Fine-Tuning Framework:** Employs separate adapters on the visual branch to learn from highly reliable pseudo-labels (obtained through concept alignment) and dynamically generated pseudo-labels during training. A main adapter learns only from the high-accuracy pseudolabels, while a pseudo adapter learns from the unlabeled data. This helps to mitigate confirmation bias caused by incorrect pseudo-labels. MaPLe (Multi-modal Prompt Learning) is employed for prompt tuning. The overall methodology is well-structured and explained in detail. The combination of concept alignment and a calibrated margin seems promising.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Experimental Setup:** The authors conducted extensive experiments on six image classification benchmark datasets (RESISC45, DTD, EuroSAT, FGVC-Aircraft, CUB, Flowers102) across three learning paradigms (unsupervised learning, semi-supervised learning, and transductive zero-shot learning).
- **Key Results:** The results demonstrate that the proposed CAP framework consistently outperforms existing methods (FPL, GRIP, CPL), achieving new state-of-the-art results in various settings. The authors show significant improvements, especially in the unsupervised learning setting, and demonstrate competitive performance in semi-supervised learning. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of both the concept alignment and confusion-aware calibrated margin components.
- **Analysis:** The analysis provides insights into the effectiveness of the proposed methods. The accuracy of pseudolabels generated using concept alignment is shown to be significantly higher than that of a top-k strategy. Furthermore, the confusion-aware calibrated margin is shown to reduce local ECE (Expected Calibration Error) in concept-confused groups, indicating improved calibration. The findings are supported by experimental evidence and clearly presented.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Significance:** This paper makes a significant contribution to the field of vision-language learning by providing a deeper understanding of the causes of pseudo-label imbalance in VLMs. The proposed concept alignment and confusion-aware calibrated margin mechanisms offer a novel and effective approach to address these issues.
- **Potential Impact:** The proposed method has the potential to reduce the reliance on labeled data for fine-tuning VLMs, making them more accessible and practical for a wider range of downstream tasks. This could have a significant impact on various applications of computer vision, such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation.
- **Future Directions:** The work could be extended by exploring different types of LLMs for text augmentation, investigating alternative methods for detecting concept-mismatched classes, and applying the proposed approach to other VLM architectures and downstream tasks. Exploring the effectiveness of the approach on more complex or noisy datasets would also be valuable.

In summary, this is a well-written and technically sound research paper that addresses an important problem in the field of vision-language learning. The proposed method is novel, effective, and well-supported by experimental evidence. The paper has the potential to make a significant impact on the development and application of VLMs.
