# Hoi! - A Multimodal Dataset for Force-Grounded, Cross-View Articulated Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the "Hoi!" dataset, a large-scale multimodal collection of human and robot interactions with articulated objects in diverse environments, featuring synchronized visual, pose, force, and tactile data. This dataset revealed that state-of-the-art methods for articulation estimation, tactile force prediction, and visual force estimation show significant performance degradation in unconstrained, real-world scenarios compared to their reported performance on existing benchmarks.

## Problem

- A "data gap" exists between human-centric video datasets (long-horizon activities) and robotics datasets (short-horizon manipulation primitives), hindering cross-domain transfer.
- Existing articulated object datasets often lack paired motion data, force/tactile measurements, multi-view coverage, or cross-embodiment correspondence necessary for grounded physical interaction research.
- Prior video datasets generally lack integrated multimodal sensing (e.g., forces, tactile cues) crucial for robust physical manipulation and for understanding the relationship between visual appearance and physical interaction.

## Method

- Developed the "Hoi!" dataset, comprising 3048 sequences of human interactions with 381 articulated objects, captured from multi-view RGB-D, proprioceptive, and haptic (force/tactile) modalities.
- Designed a custom "Hoi!" gripper with integrated force-torque and GelSight Digit tactile sensors, allowing capture of robot-grade physical sensing during both human and robotic manipulation.
- Implemented a structured data collection protocol across diverse indoor environments, utilizing high-resolution laser scans for precise 3D scene ground truth and rigorous temporal/spatial alignment of all sensor streams.

## Results

- ArtiPoint's recall on "Hoi!" dropped to 26.9% for prismatic and 57.1% for revolute articulation types, while GPT-5 achieved 71.9% prismatic and 89.7% revolute recall for type prediction from egocentric views.
- Sparsh models for tactile force estimation yielded combined force RMSEs of 3.86 N (DINO) and 4.11 N (DINOv2), a substantial increase from millinewton-level accuracy on their original benchmarks.
- ForceSight's visual force estimation showed an RMSE of 2.23 N for projected forces and 2.57 N for raw forces on "Hoi!", a noticeable degradation from its 0.404 N RMSE on its original dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art articulation estimation methods, like ArtiPoint and ArtGS, struggle to generalize in cluttered, unconstrained real-world environments, showing significant performance drops (e.g., ArtiPoint's prismatic recall dropped from 68% to 26.9%).
> - Tactile force estimation models, such as Sparsh, exhibit increased error (RMSE of several Newtons) when exposed to out-of-distribution contact geometries and load regimes characteristic of real-world articulated interactions.
> - Visual force estimation models, like ForceSight, show degraded performance (overall RMSE of 2.23 N for projected forces) on the "Hoi!" dataset, particularly for objects requiring higher operating forces, indicating limited exposure to stiff or force-demanding mechanisms in previous datasets.
