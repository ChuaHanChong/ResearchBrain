# TUR-DPO: Topology- and Uncertainty-Aware Direct Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called TUR-DPO enhances Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) by incorporating explicit reasoning topologies and calibrated uncertainty signals into its objective function. This approach improves large language model alignment, particularly for complex reasoning and factual tasks, achieving consistent performance gains and enhanced robustness while retaining DPO's operational simplicity.

## Problem

- Standard Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) treats human preferences as 'flat' binary comparisons of entire response sequences, overlooking the underlying reasoning structure.
- DPO is susceptible to noisy, inconsistent, or brittle human preference labels, potentially leading to over-optimization on spurious patterns or reduced model calibration.
- Aligning large language models (LLMs) for tasks requiring multi-step reasoning or factual accuracy demands logical soundness and transparency in derivation, which vanilla DPO does not explicitly incentivize.

## Method

- Introduces lightweight reasoning topology elicitation to decompose responses into subclaims and dependencies, then quantifies structural well-formedness using a 'topology score'.
- Integrates a 'semantic score' evaluating factual faithfulness and task utility, and estimates both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty associated with each response's reasoning graph.
- Modifies the DPO objective with a shaped reward that combines semantic, structural, and uncertainty signals, and scales the loss with an uncertainty-aware weight to attenuate noisy updates.

## Results

- TUR-DPO consistently outperformed DPO, achieving notable gains on reasoning tasks like GSM8K (**+4.1%** exact match by LLM judge) and MATH mini (**+2.6%** exact match by LLM judge), and improved performance on factual QA and dialogue tasks.
- The method demonstrated reduced logical errors (cycles, dangling nodes, contradictions) and hallucinations in generated responses, along with improved structural coherence in elicited reasoning graphs.
- Improved model calibration (reduced Expected Calibration Error) and enhanced robustness to synthetic label noise and decoding perturbations, while exhibiting modest computational overhead compared to DPO and lower resource usage than PPO-RLHF.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating reasoning topology and calibrated uncertainty into the DPO objective significantly enhances alignment for tasks demanding logical coherence and factual accuracy.
> - Uncertainty-aware weighting of preference pairs leads to improved model calibration and greater robustness to noisy or inconsistent human feedback.
> - The proposed method maintains the operational simplicity and computational efficiency of DPO while achieving performance comparable to or surpassing more complex PPO-based RLHF on reasoning-intensive benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences, a critical step in developing useful and reliable AI systems. The prevailing methods for this alignment fall into two main categories: Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), typically implemented with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).

RLHF with PPO is an established method that has demonstrated strong results in various tasks, including summarization and dialogue. However, it is characterized by its operational complexity, requiring the training of a separate reward model, online rollouts, a value head, and careful management of KL divergence regularization. These factors can contribute to instability and fragility in the training process.

DPO emerged as a simplification of RLHF, offering an RL-free objective that directly optimizes a policy based on pairwise preferences without the need for an explicit reward model or on-policy sampling. DPO has shown comparable or superior performance to PPO-based RLHF on several benchmarks while reducing complexity. However, standard DPO treats preferences as flat comparisons between entire response sequences, lacking mechanisms to explicitly reward the reasoning structure or to account for uncertainty and noise in the preference labels. This limitation is particularly relevant in tasks requiring multi-step reasoning or factual accuracy, where the *how* an answer is derived is as important as *what* it says.

Previous research has explored incorporating uncertainty and structural information into LLM explanations, for example, by eliciting lightweight reasoning topologies or estimating semantic uncertainty. However, these concepts had not been directly integrated into preference-based optimization objectives. TUR-DPO positions itself within this landscape by extending DPO to explicitly incorporate these structural and uncertainty-aware signals, aiming to improve alignment fidelity, particularly for reasoning-intensive and fact-sensitive tasks, while retaining the operational simplicity of DPO. It also relates to other recent RL-free objectives like ORPO, KTO, and RRHF, distinguishing itself by its specific integration of structured reasoning and calibrated uncertainty.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing TUR-DPO are:

- **Addressing the "flatness" of DPO preference signals:** Standard DPO treats human preferences as binary (winner/loser) signals over complete response sequences, without distinguishing *how* an answer is derived. This approach can be insufficient for complex tasks that require multi-step reasoning, where the logical coherence, soundness of intermediate steps, and overall structural integrity of the explanation are crucial. TUR-DPO aims to overcome this by rewarding the underlying reasoning process, not just the final output.
- **Mitigating sensitivity to noisy or brittle preferences:** Human preference data, whether from human annotators or LLM judges, can be noisy, inconsistent, or brittle, especially when underlying reasoning chains are fragile or ambiguous. DPO is susceptible to these noisy signals, which can lead to over-optimization on spurious patterns or reduce the model's calibration. TUR-DPO seeks to enhance robustness by introducing an uncertainty-aware mechanism to modulate the learning pressure from such unreliable preference pairs.
- **Improving alignment for reasoning- and factuality-sensitive tasks:** For domains like mathematical reasoning, factual question answering, and detailed summarization, LLMs must generate responses that are not only fluent but also logically sound, factually accurate, and transparent in their derivation. The paper argues that current DPO, by not explicitly valuing reasoning structure, may struggle to enforce these qualities consistently. TUR-DPO is motivated by the need to explicitly steer LLMs towards more coherent and faithful reasoning outputs.
- **Preserving operational simplicity and avoiding online rollouts:** Despite the desire for more sophisticated alignment signals, a key motivation is to maintain the practical advantages of DPO, namely its RL-free nature, stability, and avoidance of complex components like online rollouts, separate value heads, or elaborate KL scheduling. TUR-DPO aims to integrate structural and uncertainty signals as augmentations to the DPO objective, rather than introducing an entirely new, complex training pipeline.
- **Enhancing model calibration and faithfulness:** The paper aims to improve the calibration of LLMs (i.e., the alignment between predicted probabilities and actual correctness) and their factual faithfulness. By down-weighting uncertain preferences and explicitly rewarding robust reasoning, TUR-DPO intends to foster models that are not only more accurate but also more aware of their own certainty and less prone to hallucination or logical errors.
- **Extending applicability to multimodal and long-context settings:** The challenges of reasoning and factual grounding intensify in multimodal and long-context scenarios due to increased complexity and potential for logical drift. The objectives include demonstrating that the topology- and uncertainty-aware approach can generalize and provide consistent gains in these more demanding environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TUR-DPO extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) by integrating lightweight reasoning topologies and calibrated uncertainty signals into the DPO objective. The methodology maintains the core DPO training loop, which involves a supervised policy ($\pi_\theta$) and a reference policy ($\pi_\text{ref}$), typically fixed or updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA). The training data consists of pairwise preferences: $(x_i, y^+_i, y^-_i)$, where $x$ is a prompt, and $y^+$ and $y^-$ are preferred and dispreferred responses, respectively.

The key methodological components are:

#### 3.1. Topology Elicitation and Scoring

For each candidate response $y$ to a prompt $x$, a small directed graph $G = (V, E)$ is elicited.
- **Graph Extraction:** The response text is decomposed into atomic subclaims or steps (nodes $V$), and support/dependency relations are identified as edges $E$. This process uses a deterministic prompt template and minor perturbations for uncertainty estimation. Sanitization steps remove self-loops, merge paraphrases, and break cycles by minimal edge cuts.
- **Topology Score ($s_\text{topo}(G)$):** This score quantifies the structural well-formedness of the reasoning graph by aggregating several indicators:
 - **Minimal valid path coverage ($q_\text{path}$):** Measures how many nodes and edges are part of an acyclic path from premises to the final claim.
 - **Cycles ($c_\text{cycle}$):** Detects circular justifications.
 - **Dangling nodes ($d_\text{dangling}$):** Identifies unsupported claims.
 - **Contradiction signal ($q_\text{contradict}$):** Summarizes local logical conflicts along edges, often using a Natural Language Inference (NLI) verifier.
 - These indicators are combined into a linear score: $s_\text{topo}(G) = \alpha_1 q_\text{path} - \alpha_2 c_\text{cycle} - \alpha_3 d_\text{dangling} - \alpha_4 q_\text{contradict}$. The weights $\alpha_j$ are tuned on a validation set to achieve a target structural coherence distribution.

#### 3.2. Semantic Score ($s_\text{sem}(x, y)$)

This score evaluates task utility and factual faithfulness, penalizing hallucinations. It is computed from node-level verification and task-specific metrics:
- $s_\text{sem}(x, y) = \beta_1 q_\text{fact}(x, y) + \beta_2 q_\text{task}(x, y) - \beta_3 q_\text{hall}(x, y)$.
 - **$q_\text{fact}$:** Aggregates correctness of atomic claims, verified against external knowledge (e.g., Wikipedia snapshots).
 - **$q_\text{task}$:** A standard task-specific metric (e.g., exact match for mathematics, ROUGE for summarization). If automatic metrics are unreliable, LLM judges can provide scores.
 - **$q_\text{hall}$:** Penalizes unsupported or contradicted entities.
- Coefficients $\beta_j$ are tuned on a held-out set for calibration and faithfulness. Scores are standardized per domain.

#### 3.3. Uncertainty Estimation and Pair Weighting

Uncertainty is used to modulate the learning pressure from potentially noisy preference pairs.
- **Total Uncertainty ($u(G)$):** A weighted combination of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty: $u(G) = \lambda_\text{epi} u_\text{epi}(G) + \lambda_\text{ale} u_\text{ale}(G)$.
 - **Epistemic Uncertainty ($u_\text{epi}(G)$):** Measures dispersion across multiple re-elicited graphs. It is calculated as the variance of topology scores from $K$ perturbed extractions and the Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) between their normalized path or edge distributions. $K=3$ re-elicited graphs are typically used.
 - **Aleatoric Uncertainty ($u_\text{ale}(G)$):** Averages a coverage-corrected binary entropy across the nodes, using correctness probabilities $p_v$ from a calibrated verifier for each node $v$.
- **Pair Weight ($w$):** The average uncertainty of the preferred and dispreferred responses in a pair ($\bar{u} = \frac{u(G^+) + u(G^-)}{2}$) is mapped to a weight that attenuates noisy updates: $w = \text{clip}(\frac{\tau_w}{1 + \bar{u}}, w_\text{min}, 1)$. This acts as a per-example learning rate multiplier, reducing the impact of highly uncertain pairs while keeping a minimum contribution.

#### 3.4. Shaped Reward Formulation

A compact shaped reward $r_\phi(x, y, G)$ is formulated by combining semantic, structural, and uncertainty signals. This reward shapes the preference margin without introducing a separate parametric reward model.
- $r_\phi(x, y, G) = a f^\text{sem}_\phi(s_\text{sem}(x, y)) + (1-a) f^\text{topo}_\phi(s_\text{topo}(G)) - \lambda u(G)$.
 - $f^\text{sem}_\phi$ and $f^\text{topo}_\phi$ are linear calibrators to ensure stability.
 - The mixing parameter $a$ balances semantic and structural quality.
 - $\lambda$ penalizes uncertainty to discourage reliance on fragile explanations.

#### 3.5. Objective Function

TUR-DPO modifies the standard DPO logistic loss by incorporating the shaped reward difference and scaling the loss with the per-pair uncertainty weight.
- **Pairwise Objective:** $L_\text{TUR-DPO} = -w \cdot \log \sigma(\beta [\Delta \log \pi_\theta - \Delta \log \pi_\text{ref}] + \gamma \Delta r_\phi)$.
 - $\Delta \log \pi_\theta = \log \pi_\theta(y^+|x) - \log \pi_\theta(y^-|x)$ and $\Delta r_\phi = r_\phi(x, y^+, G^+) - r_\phi(x, y^-, G^-)$.
 - $\beta$ controls the sharpness of the policy, and $\gamma$ controls the reward strength.
- **Listwise Objective (Optional):** When multiple candidates are available, a listwise objective using Plackett-Luce utilities can be used for fuller data utilization and variance reduction.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Foundations

The paper establishes theoretical connections:
- **Weighted Bradley-Terry Model:** Minimizing the TUR-DPO loss is equivalent to weighted logistic regression in the margin under a Bradley-Terry likelihood. If weights are independent of the label given the pair, the estimator is Fisher consistent for the conditional preference probability.
- **KL-Regularized Policy Optimization:** TUR-DPO optimizes a KL-regularized policy under a shaped reward, clarifying its relation to vanilla DPO and providing intuition for stability with an EMA-updated reference policy as a soft trust region. A lemma provides a bias bound for the weighted estimator under label noise and weight-label dependence, showing that clipping weights reduces worst-case bias.

#### 3.7. Training Protocol

The training loop involves: graph elicitation, computation of semantic/topology scores and uncertainty, mapping to a pair weight, updating optional calibrator parameters, and updating policy parameters. The reference policy can be fixed or updated via EMA. This protocol is designed to preserve DPO throughput with modest overhead for graph and verifier computations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of TUR-DPO was conducted across a range of 7-8B open models and benchmarks, including mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH mini), factual question answering (Open QA), summarization (Summ TLDR), and helpful/harmless dialogue (HH single-turn). The evaluation included both LLM-judge and human evaluations, as well as analyses in multimodal and long-context settings.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Gains:** TUR-DPO consistently improved performance over DPO across all evaluated tasks, as measured by LLM-judge win-rates, faithfulness, and calibration.
- **Reasoning-centric Tasks:** The most significant gains were observed on GSM8K, MATH, BBH, and Open QA, tasks that are hypothesized to benefit most from structural and uncertainty-aware signals.
 - GSM8K EM: DPO 58.7% -> TUR-DPO 62.8% (LLM judge); 59.2% -> 63.1% (Human eval).
 - MATH mini EM: DPO 33.4% -> TUR-DPO 36.0% (LLM judge); 34.1% -> 36.4% (Human eval).
 - BBH Acc.: DPO 43.9% -> TUR-DPO 46.7% (LLM judge); 44.5% -> 47.2% (Human eval).
- **Comparison with PPO-based RLHF:** TUR-DPO demonstrated comparable or superior win-rates to PPO-based RLHF on reasoning-centric tasks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, BBH, Open QA), while maintaining a simpler training pipeline. PPO-RLHF retained a slight advantage on stylistic dialogue tasks like HH single-turn, where nuanced reward shaping is critical, but TUR-DPO significantly narrowed this gap compared to vanilla DPO.
- **Longer Output Lengths:** Relative improvements of TUR-DPO over DPO increased with output length across tasks like GSM8K, Open QA, and BBH, suggesting its effectiveness for more complex, multi-step responses.

#### 4.2. Structural Signals and Error Reduction

- **Improved Structural Coherence:** TUR-DPO led to a reduction in cycles and dangling nodes and an increase in minimal path coverage in elicited reasoning graphs for tasks like GSM8K and QA. This supports the hypothesis that explicit structural signals improve reasoning quality.
- **Reduced Error Rates:** Manual error categorization showed that TUR-DPO substantially reduced logical leaps and contradictions, aligning with its structural incentives. It also decreased hallucinated entity errors. A side effect was an increase in formatting errors, which could be mitigated with post-processing.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Other RL-Free Objectives

- TUR-DPO outperformed other recent RL-free preference optimization methods (ORPO, SimPO, KTO, IPO) on structure-sensitive tasks, with gains of 2.7-4.1 EM points over these baselines. This indicates that the shaped reward and uncertainty weighting contribute benefits beyond the architectural differences of these methods. TUR-DPO also exhibited reduced Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and higher structural scores compared to these methods.

#### 4.4. Human Evaluation and Judge Correlation

- Human evaluations confirmed the trends observed with LLM judges. TUR-DPO improved both inter-rater agreement and Kendall's $\tau$ correlation between LLM judge and human preferences, suggesting better alignment with human judgment. Judge-human disagreements often involved fluent but weakly supported claims, whose influence was reduced by uncertainty-weighted training.
- Statistical significance tests showed small to medium effect sizes with significant improvements on TLDR, HH, GSM8K, and Open QA win-rates/EM scores (p-values < 0.05).

#### 4.5. Calibration and Robustness

- **Improved Calibration:** TUR-DPO consistently reduced both Expected Calibration Error (ECE) and Brier score across tasks, indicating that uncertainty-weighted updates prevent overconfident learning from brittle pairs. The largest gains were observed in high-confidence bins.
- **Robustness to Noise:** Under synthetic label noise (randomly flipping preference labels), TUR-DPO degraded more gracefully than DPO, retaining a higher percentage of its clean-data win-rate and exhibiting smaller increases in ECE.
- **Decoding Robustness:** TUR-DPO maintained stable performance across a wider range of decoding temperatures and exhibited smaller accuracy degradation under prompt perturbations (paraphrases, filler tokens) compared to DPO.

#### 4.6. Sample Efficiency and Compute

- **Increased Sample Efficiency:** TUR-DPO was found to be more token-efficient than DPO for achieving target HH win-rates, approaching PPO's efficiency at higher targets without its rollout overhead. This was attributed to uncertainty weighting suppressing noisy updates and shaped rewards providing additional gradient signals.
- **Modest Computational Overhead:** TUR-DPO operates with a modest computational overhead relative to DPO, requiring substantially less memory and time than PPO-RLHF. Training to a 65% HH win-rate required 42 GPU-hours for TUR-DPO, compared to 48 for DPO and 67 for PPO-RLHF, translating to lower carbon emissions.

#### 4.7. Multimodal and Long-Context Evaluations

- **Multimodal Reasoning (LVLMs):** On visual question answering tasks (ChartQA, ScienceQA-IMG), TUR-DPO achieved higher accuracy (e.g., +4.2% on ChartQA) and improved structural coherence, suggesting that explicit reasoning topology transfers to multimodal settings.
- **Long-Context Multi-hop Reasoning:** For long-context multi-hop QA (HotpotQA-Long, MuSiQue-Long), TUR-DPO improved EM/F1 scores (e.g., +3.8% on HotpotQA-Long) and structural metrics (path coverage, fewer cycles), indicating effectiveness as context length increases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TUR-DPO represents a significant step in the field of preference-based alignment for large language models. Its primary significance lies in moving beyond "flat" sequence-level preference signals to incorporate explicit structural reasoning and calibrated uncertainty directly into the DPO objective, while maintaining the practical advantages of an RL-free approach.

The potential impacts of this work include:

- **Improved Reliability and Trustworthiness of LLMs:** By rewarding how answers are derived, not just what they state, TUR-DPO has the potential to produce LLMs that are more logically sound, factually consistent, and robust to reasoning errors. This could lead to more reliable and trustworthy models, particularly for critical applications requiring intensive reasoning such as advanced problem-solving, complex question answering, and scientific summarization.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Transparency:** The emphasis on reasoning topologies encourages models to generate more structured and coherent explanations. This could improve the interpretability of LLM outputs, making it easier for users to understand the model's logic and verify its conclusions, fostering greater transparency in AI systems.
- **Robustness to Noisy and Brittle Data:** The uncertainty-aware weighting mechanism directly addresses a common challenge in preference-based alignment: the presence of noisy, inconsistent, or fragile human feedback. By attenuating the influence of such unreliable data, TUR-DPO can lead to more stable and calibrated models that are less susceptible to over-optimization on spurious patterns.
- **Operational Simplicity and Accessibility:** TUR-DPO preserves the operational simplicity of DPO, avoiding the complexities of online RL pipelines (e.g., reward models, value heads, online rollouts, complex KL scheduling). This makes advanced alignment techniques more accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners who may not have specialized RL infrastructure or expertise, facilitating reproduction and further research.
- **Advancement in Multimodal and Long-Context Reasoning:** The demonstration of TUR-DPO's effectiveness in multimodal and long-context settings indicates its potential to address complex reasoning challenges across diverse data types and lengthy inputs, pushing the boundaries of what aligned LLMs can achieve in these demanding scenarios.

However, the paper also acknowledges potential limitations and ethical considerations:
- **Dependency on Graph Extraction Fidelity:** The method's effectiveness relies on the accuracy and completeness of the reasoning topology extraction. Errors or omissions in this process can directly impact the reward signal and, consequently, model alignment.
- **Uncertainty Estimator Coverage:** While the uncertainty estimator helps reduce label noise, it works with a limited number of re-elicited graphs and may not capture all edge-case failures.
- **Potential for Bias Amplification:** If the underlying parsers, preference data, or structural rewards are inherently biased (e.g., based on flawed or culturally biased logic), TUR-DPO could inadvertently reinforce these biases, potentially leading to "confidently wrong" models that deliver incorrect information through seemingly logical, but ultimately unsound, reasoning structures.
- **Caution in Safety-Critical Applications:** The authors advise against deploying models trained with TUR-DPO in highly safety-critical situations (e.g., medical diagnosis, legal advice) where logical mistakes could have severe real-world consequences, recognizing that while the method improves logical soundness, it does not eliminate all risks.

Overall, TUR-DPO contributes a pragmatic framework for creating more reliable and logically coherent LLMs, providing valuable tools for advancing the general goal of aligning machine learning systems with human intentions, while also highlighting the ongoing need for careful consideration of data quality and ethical implications.
