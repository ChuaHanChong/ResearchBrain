# CompCPZ: Preserving Multi-Modal Intent in Language-Guided Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> CompCPZ is a sound algebraic compiler that recursively composes per-primitive constrained polynomial zonotopes along a language parse tree, using Boolean intersection/union/complement with conformal coverage, to preserve all disjunctive modes of a manipulation instruction instead of collapsing them into one region; it wins 1,900/1,918 paired trials against convex, multi-peak, and VLA baselines on ManiSkill3 and transfers zero-shot to a Go2 quadruped.

## Problem

- Language-conditioned pipelines encode disjunctive instructions as a single connected region (convex hulls, value maps) or commit to one action, silently violating the true multi-modal feasible set.
- No prior work proves these failures are structural: existing systems lack a topological lower bound showing any sound single-component encoder must miss modes by at least half the inter-mode gap.
- Multi-peak decoders and single-action VLA/diffusion policies provide no distribution-free uncertainty guarantee and degrade under run-time mode occlusion with no principled switching mechanism.

## Method

- ==Compositional Instruction Grammar== closes a primitive predicate set (goal, obstacle, property, relational) under AND/OR/NOT, parsed from free-form language via ==chain-of-thought== VLM prompting.
- ==Two-Sided CPZ Extraction== builds per-primitive ==constrained polynomial zonotope== enclosure pairs with ==split-conformal calibrated margins==, guaranteeing high-probability containment of the true feasible set.
- **CompCPZ**'s ==CPZ Composition Algebra== recursively realizes intersection, union, and axis-aligned exact complement bottom-up along the parse tree, yielding a k-component enclosure with run-time containment-checked mode switching.

## Results

- Pooled closed-loop Franka benchmark: CompCPZ wins **1,900/1,918** paired sign-test trials over convex AABB baselines (p far below 10^-30), reaching **1.00** vs 0.13 in-GT-mode on multi-modal families.
- Zero-shot OpenVLA-7B on the same 13 multi-modal families achieves only **30.8%** in-GT-mode and **0%** task success, and adversarial mode-blocking drops hand-rolled union baselines to the 1-1/k floor while CompCPZ retains **0.90**.
- On 36 real-robot Unitree Go2 trials, CompCPZ reaches the correct ground-truth mode in **12/12** cases while the convex baseline achieves **0/12** and walks onto a forbidden region in **3/3** negation trials.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compositional language grounding should be evaluated by whether the represented feasibility set preserves connected-component structure, not just by decoded-target success rate.
> - A representation's soundness class (single-component vs k-component) is a topological property no amount of additional training can fix, independent of the underlying learned perception or parser.
> - The algebraic composition layer is orthogonal to and decouples cleanly from swappable learned components (parser, detector, calibration), letting perception improvements and set-soundness guarantees be engineered separately.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of language-conditioned robot manipulation aims to enable robots to understand and execute natural language instructions. A significant challenge in this area involves grounding abstract linguistic concepts into concrete geometric actions and feasibility regions in the robot's workspace. Current approaches predominantly fall into a few categories, each with limitations addressed by this work:

- **Sound Convex Enclosures:** Many systems represent feasible regions as convex sets (e.g., axis-aligned bounding boxes, minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoids). While geometrically sound, these representations are fundamentally limited when instructions are disjunctive (e.g., "near X *or* near Y"). A single convex set cannot accurately represent disconnected feasible regions, often collapsing them into an inter-mode centroid, leading to semantic failures.
- **Dense Scalar Fields / Pointwise Affordances:** Approaches like value maps, reachability heatmaps, or affordance points can represent multi-peak distributions, but they typically do not provide *sound* set enclosures. Their outputs often lack formal guarantees of containing the true feasible set, making them susceptible to errors or requiring arbitrary thresholds.
- **Single Committed Actions (Vision-Language-Action Models - VLAs):** Some prominent VLA models directly output a single 7-DoF action or a fixed action chunk. While effective for simple, unambiguous instructions, these models inherently cannot represent a disjunctive feasible *set*. When faced with multiple possible valid modes, committing to a single action before runtime observation of mode availability can lead to failure if the chosen mode becomes obstructed.

This paper identifies a "silent semantic failure" in these existing paradigms, particularly when dealing with compositional (Boolean `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`) and multi-modal instructions. It argues that the failure is structural, not merely a matter of tuning or representation capacity, proving that sound encodings with fewer components than the instruction's modes will inevitably miss parts of the true feasibility set. This work positions itself as a principled algebraic layer designed to overcome these structural limitations by preserving the connected-component structure of user intent, thereby enhancing the robustness and semantic fidelity of language-guided robot policies. It builds upon and extends the mathematical framework of Constrained Polynomial Zonotopes (CPZs) for set representation and formal composition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the observed "silent semantic failure" in language-conditioned robot manipulation, where robots appear geometrically successful but semantically fail to satisfy the full intent of an instruction.

The key motivations are:

1. **Addressing Disjunctive Instruction Failure:** Instructions like "place the cup near the red plate *or* the blue plate" often cause current systems to collapse the two distinct feasible regions into a single centroid, leading the robot's end-effector to a location that satisfies neither disjunct. This work aims to enable robots to correctly interpret and act upon disjunctive multi-modal instructions by explicitly representing all feasible modes.
2. **Overcoming Structural Limitations of Representations:** The authors argue that many existing geometric output representations (e.g., convex sets) are fundamentally incapable of preserving all feasible modes of a disjunctive instruction. They prove that any sound encoding whose component count is less than the number of instruction modes will miss the true feasibility set by a quantifiable margin. The objective is to develop a representation that can inherently preserve the multi-modal, disconnected structure of compositional language.
3. **Improving Planner Robustness under Runtime Uncertainty:** When a robot commits to a single action in a multi-modal scenario, it becomes vulnerable to runtime uncertainties such as occlusions or environmental changes blocking the chosen mode. If other valid modes exist but are not represented or considered, the robot fails. This work aims to provide a representation that allows for dynamic mode switching, improving robustness in uncertain environments.
4. **Enabling Compositional Language Grounding with Soundness Guarantees:** Beyond simple single-target instructions, real-world tasks often involve complex instructions combining multiple primitives with Boolean logic (e.g., "near X *and* not on Y *or* near Z"). The objective is to develop a compositional framework that can translate these instructions into precise, sound geometric representations with distribution-free coverage guarantees.
5. **Extending Beyond Axis-Aligned Primitives:** The desire to handle more complex geometric constraints, such as curved surfaces (e.g., ellipsoid avoidance) or non-linear relationships, motivates the use of a versatile set representation that goes beyond simple bounding boxes.
6. **Providing an Algebraic Foundation for Robot Learning:** The authors posit that the problem is not merely a learning algorithm deficiency but a structural issue in how language is *grounded* into geometric sets. Therefore, a core objective is to offer a principled algebraic layer that learning systems can wrap, ensuring that semantic intent is preserved, rather than trying to *learn* this preservation implicitly.

In essence, the central objective is to develop a robust and theoretically sound system, CompCPZ, that can explicitly represent and compose multi-modal, non-convex, and potentially disconnected feasible regions from natural language instructions, thereby preventing silent semantic failures in robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CompCPZ addresses the limitations of existing approaches by introducing a sound algebraic layer that precisely represents and composes multi-modal disjunctive feasible regions based on natural language instructions.

#### 3.1. Core Representation: Constrained Polynomial Zonotopes (CPZs)

The foundation of CompCPZ is the **Constrained Polynomial Zonotope (CPZ)**. A CPZ is a compact set defined as the image of a polynomial map in factor variables, subject to polynomial equality constraints. This representation offers several advantages:
- **Generality:** CPZs generalize simpler set representations like zonotopes, constrained zonotopes, and polynomial zonotopes.
- **Closure Properties:** The class of CPZs is closed under exact intersection, union (via a binary indicator factor), and complement (exact for axis-aligned cases, approximate otherwise). These properties are crucial for Boolean composition.
- **Non-convexity and Polynomial Primitives:** CPZs can represent non-convex sets and support polynomial constraints, enabling handling of primitives like ellipsoid avoidance or quadratic "between" regions, which simpler representations (like Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes - AABBs) cannot represent soundly and tightly.

#### 3.2. Compositional Instruction Grammar

CompCPZ employs a formal grammar `L ::= ϕ | L1 ∧ L2 | L1 ∨ L2 | ¬L1`, which closes a fixed set of primitive predicates (Φ) under Boolean logic (`AND`, `OR`, `NOT`). The primitives are templated natural-language predicates with fixed geometric semantics, categorized into:
- **Goal:** e.g., "near ⟨object⟩"
- **Obstacle:** e.g., "avoid ⟨object⟩"
- **Property:** e.g., "keep ⟨property⟩ level"
- **Relational:** e.g., "between ⟨object1⟩ and ⟨object2⟩"
A Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting, is used to parse free-form natural language instructions into this structured grammar.

#### 3.3. Two-Sided CPZ Extraction

For each primitive ϕ identified in the instruction's parse tree, CompCPZ extracts a **two-sided CPZ enclosure** ( ˆC−ϕ , ˆC+ϕ ). This pair provides an inner approximation ( ˆC−ϕ ) and an outer approximation ( ˆC+ϕ ) of the true feasible set Cϕ, such that P[ ˆC−ϕ ⊆ Cϕ ⊆ ˆC+ϕ ] ≥ 1 − δϕ with high probability.
- **Per-Primitive Templates:** Standard CPZ constructors are used as templates for geometric primitives (e.g., "near" as a centroid box, "avoid" as an ellipsoid complement).
- **Conformal Margin Calibration:** Distribution-free split-conformal prediction is applied to calibrate the margin ϵcal, ensuring that the extracted enclosures maintain the specified coverage probability (1 − δϕ) without making strong distributional assumptions about the perception noise.
- **Learning Components Integration:** This extraction relies on a visual detector (fine-tuned YOLOv8n) for object detection and pose estimation, feeding the geometric primitives. The choice of learning component can be swapped, as the algebra is designed to be invariant to it.

#### 3.4. CPZ Composition Algebra

The extracted per-primitive CPZ pairs are composed recursively, bottom-up along the language parse tree, using the following rules for logical operations:
- **Conjunction (∧):** `(L1 ∧ L2)` maps to `( ˆC−L1 ∩ ˆC−L2 , ˆC+L1 ∩ ˆC+L2 )`. Intersection is exactly handled by concatenating constraint generators within the CPZ framework.
- **Disjunction (∨):** `(L1 ∨ L2)` maps to `( ˆC−L1 ∪ ˆC−L2 , ˆC+L1 ∪ ˆC+L2 )`. Union is represented as a CPZ by introducing a binary indicator factor or, more practically, as a lazy list of CPZs to preserve multi-modality.
- **Negation (¬):** `(¬L1)` maps to `(( ˆC+L1 )c, ( ˆC−L1 )c)`. The inner and outer enclosures are swapped and complemented to preserve containment. For axis-aligned grammars, complement is exact; for non-axis-aligned CPZs, an interval hull over-approximation is used for the inner bound.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Guarantees

CompCPZ provides several theoretical guarantees:
- **Compositional Coverage:** Under per-primitive conformal coverage, the composed enclosure satisfies P[ ˆC−L ⊆ F(L) ⊆ ˆC+L ] ≥ 1 − Pδi, offering a distribution-free coverage guarantee for compositional language.
- **Mode Resolution:** If the per-primitive thickness is below a certain threshold related to the inter-mode separation (ϵ < η/(2κdepth(L))), CompCPZ's upper enclosure ( ˆC+L ) recovers all k semantic modes.
- **Topological Lower Bound:** For any sound encoding S that contains the true feasible set F(L) but has fewer connected components than F(L) (i.e., m < k), its Hausdorff distance to F(L) is at least η/2, where η is the inter-mode separation. This formalizes the structural failure of single-component (e.g., convex) representations.
- **Single-Action Lower Bound:** Any planner committing to a single action `a` before observing runtime mode availability has a worst-case success rate of at most 1/k under symmetric uncertainty and 0 under adversarial uncertainty.
- **Lipschitz Stability and Runtime:** The composition process is Lipschitz stable with respect to extractor errors, and for axis-aligned grammars, the Lipschitz constant κ=1, making stability depth-independent. Composition runs in linear time with respect to the parse tree size (O(|L|)).

#### 3.6. Runtime Operation

In operation, CompCPZ's output is a k-component feasibility set. For execution, the robot steers to the centroid of the nearest valid mode. If that mode becomes blocked at runtime, CompCPZ facilitates switching to the next valid and available mode due to its explicit representation of all modes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents empirical results across simulated and real-world experiments to validate CompCPZ's performance, particularly focusing on its ability to handle multi-modal and compositional instructions.

#### 4.1. ManiSkill3 Simulation Benchmark

A custom ManiSkill3 tabletop manipulation benchmark was developed with 49 (extended to 191) instructions stratified by compositional depth and primitive family. This benchmark explicitly targets multi-modal scenarios (where Ctop(L) > 1), unlike existing benchmarks.
- **Metrics:** Geometric success (end-effector within 5 mm of target) and in-GT-mode (end-effector inside any analytic ground-truth feasible region).
- **Against Convex Baselines (AABB, MVEE):** On multi-modal tasks, CompCPZ achieved an in-GT-mode rate of 1.00, while convex baselines (AABB and the tighter Minimum Volume Enclosing Ellipsoid, MVEE) collapsed to 0.13. This 1.00 vs. 0.13 gap is attributed to the structural limitation predicted by Theorem 11, where convex sets fail to preserve multiple modes, landing in the inter-mode gap. Pooled paired sign tests showed CompCPZ winning 1,900/1,918 decisive trials against convex baselines (p ≪ 10−30).
- **Against Multi-peak Decoders (Heatmap Argmax, Sampling Decoder):** Non-sound multi-peak baselines, even with access to all k predicted modes, typically fell to the 1 − 1/k success rate floor predicted by Theorem 13 when mode blocking was introduced. CompCPZ maintained a pooled mean of 0.90 in-GT-mode in these adversarial scenarios, demonstrating its ability to switch modes.
- **Against Zero-shot Vision-Language-Action Models (OpenVLA-7B):** OpenVLA-7B achieved only 30.8% in-GT-mode and 0% task success on multi-modal tasks, consistent with its single-action commitment failing to handle multiple modes effectively.
- **Polynomial Primitives:** For instructions involving ellipsoid avoidance (a polynomial primitive), CompCPZ achieved 1.00 in-GT-mode. AABB-union variants, which approximate ellipsoids with boxes, performed significantly worse (0.928 for bounding, 0.715 for inscribed), demonstrating the necessity of CPZs for sound and tight representation of curved geometries.
- **Explicit Negation:** On instructions with negation (e.g., "NOT on the rug"), an AABB-union baseline without a complement primitive resulted in a 14.75% violation rate (robot landing in the negated region), whereas CompCPZ maintained 1.00 in-GT-mode due to its exact axis-aligned complement operation.
- **Oracle GMM Baseline:** An oracle GMM, given ground-truth mode counts and centroids, achieved 90.2% in-GT-mode on the multi-modal subset, still outperformed by CompCPZ's 100%, highlighting the inherent limitations of a sampling policy that must commit to a single action.

#### 4.2. Cross-Embodiment Hardware Validation (Unitree Go2)

The identical CompCPZ compiler was deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot with planar configurations, controlled via motion capture, across four scenarios (2-mode disjunction, 3-mode disjunction, negation, adversarial mode blocking).
- **Performance:** CompCPZ achieved 12/12 correct-mode rate across all scenarios.
- **Baseline Failures:** The convex-hull baseline landed inside no true mode in 0/12 trials, and on the negation scenario, landed inside the forbidden rug region in 3/3 runs. A single-clause baseline (committing to the first disjunct) failed on 3/3 trials in the adversarial mode-block scenario, directly confirming Theorem 13 on physical hardware.
- **Transferability:** These results suggest the compiler's ability to transfer across different robot embodiments without retuning, separating the set-grounding behavior from embodiment-specific biases.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Runtime

- **Composition Runtime:** Composition operations were sub-millisecond at depth 4 and < 1.1 ms at worst-case depth 7, scaling consistently with the O(2^d) theoretical bound.
- **Empirical κ:** The empirical Hausdorff Lipschitz constant (ˆκ) was consistently 1.00 across 6,828 internal nodes, validating the depth-independent stability for axis-aligned grammars.
- **Conformal Coverage:** Conformal prediction successfully tracked the 1 − α coverage rate within ±1% across various noise distributions, supporting the distribution-free claim.
- **Perception Envelope:** CompCPZ maintained 1.00 in-GT-mode up to 10 mm (4x cube half-extent) of centroid noise. Off-the-shelf open-vocabulary detectors on ManiSkill3 RGB had median centroid errors (e.g., Grounding-DINO+SAM2 at 15.8 cm) often exceeding this envelope. A data-efficient fine-tune of YOLOv8n (200 synthetic frames, 3 min training) reduced median error to 0.73 cm, bringing perception within CompCPZ's working envelope.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces CompCPZ, a system that fundamentally addresses a critical limitation in language-guided robot manipulation: the "silent semantic failure" arising from the inability of current methods to preserve multi-modal intent in compositional instructions. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Semantic Fidelity:** By explicitly representing multi-modal feasible regions as connected components, CompCPZ allows robots to understand and act upon complex instructions (e.g., "near X *or* Y, and *not* on Z") with high semantic fidelity. This moves beyond merely achieving a geometric target to ensuring that the robot's actions genuinely satisfy the user's full, potentially disjunctive, intent. This leads to more reliable and trustable robot behavior.
2. **Theoretical Foundation for Compositional Grounding:** The work provides rigorous theoretical guarantees, including a topological lower bound for single-component representations and a single-action planner lower bound. These bounds demonstrate that the observed failures are structural rather than empirical, establishing a new benchmark for evaluating compositional language grounding—not just by decoded-set volume or single-action success, but by the preservation of the connected-component structure of the user's intent. This shifts the paradigm for designing and assessing future systems.
3. **Principled Uncertainty Propagation:** Through distribution-free conformal coverage propagation, CompCPZ offers a method to quantify and propagate uncertainty through complex compositional instructions. This allows for reliable risk assessment and safer decision-making, which is crucial for real-world robotic deployments where safety and predictability are paramount.
4. **Generality and Extensibility:** The use of Constrained Polynomial Zonotopes (CPZs) provides a powerful and general representation capable of handling non-convex sets and polynomial primitives (e.g., curved obstacle avoidance), which are beyond the capabilities of simpler, convex representations. The algebraic framework is designed to be a "wrapper layer" for various learning components (VLM parsers, visual detectors), allowing for modular development and easy integration of new perception or language models.
5. **Practical Demonstrations and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** Validation on a complex simulated manipulation benchmark (ManiSkill3) with diverse instructions, combined with real-robot trials on a Unitree Go2 quadruped, demonstrates the practical applicability and transferability of CompCPZ across different embodiments. This indicates that the core principles of compositional set grounding are robust to changes in robot platform and control stack.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** By identifying and formalizing structural limitations, this work provides clear directions for future research in robot learning and language grounding. It suggests that efforts should focus on designing representations that inherently capture the topological complexity of intent, rather than solely optimizing single-action policies or learning implicit solutions that may lack formal guarantees.

While the current work has limitations (e.g., depth-independent stability primarily for axis-aligned grammars, focus on single-shot grounding, planar real-robot validation), its contributions lay a strong foundation for developing more intelligent, robust, and semantically aligned language-guided robotic systems.
