# VLBiMan: Vision-Language Anchored One-Shot Demonstration Enables Generalizable Bimanual Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen, developed VLBiMan, a framework for generalizable bimanual robotic manipulation that learns from a single human demonstration. The system adeptly adapts to novel object placements, instances, and dynamic scene changes, achieving success rates up to 85.3% on basic manipulation tasks and demonstrating robust cross-embodiment transfer to humanoid robots without requiring policy retraining.

## Problem

- End-to-end visuomotor imitation learning methods demand extensive teleoperated demonstrations and retraining for adaptation to new objects or tasks, limiting scalability in open-world environments.
- Modular Vision-Language-Action pipelines, while offering some generalization, often lack flexibility in dynamic scenes and can be brittle with zero-shot prompt engineering.
- A fundamental dilemma exists between the high data requirements of powerful imitation learning and the robustness limitations of flexible modular approaches in unstructured settings.

## Method

- **VLBiMan** employs a ==three-stage pipeline== that decomposes a single human demonstration into ==task-invariant and adaptable atomic skill modules==.
- It uses ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== for semantic scene understanding and a ==Vision-Feature Matching (VFM)== approach to geometrically adapt variable modules based on 2D mask properties without 6-DoF pose estimation.
- Adapted and invariant modules are composed into new executable trajectories using ==progressive Inverse Kinematics (IK) refinement== and ==dynamic collision compensation==, preserving the original temporal structure.

## Results

- VLBiMan achieved superior performance on basic bimanual tasks, demonstrating average success rates of **85.3%** for new placements with same objects and **78.0%** for novel instances without interference, significantly outperforming baseline methods.
- The framework successfully performed complex, long-horizon multi-stage and tool-use tasks, reaching up to **52.0%** success rate on new placements with same objects, showcasing its capability for skill composition and handling multi-object, contact-intensive actions.
- Skills learned from human demonstrations were effectively transferred to a humanoid dual-arm robot, achieving comparable success rates (e.g., **83.8%** average for same objects) and demonstrating strong cross-embodiment generalization without retraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizable bimanual manipulation can be achieved from minimal demonstrations by segmenting tasks into reusable, invariant skills and vision-language anchored adaptable components.
> - Leveraging robust VLMs and simple 2D mask-based geometric reasoning (e.g., centroid, principal axis) allows for flexible and robust adaptation to unseen objects and poses without requiring explicit 6-DoF pose estimation or CAD models.
> - Integrating iterative IK refinement and collision avoidance within trajectory composition is crucial for ensuring kinematic feasibility and robustness in real-world environments, particularly for initial grasping and dynamic object interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied robotic manipulation has seen substantial advancements, particularly through visuomotor imitation learning with large-scale teleoperated demonstrations. Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as those used in the ALOHA series, RDT-1B, π0, and FAST, can map raw sensory inputs to motor commands, achieving performance in high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) bimanual tasks. This end-to-end approach bypasses explicit modeling of task- or object-specific priors by embedding complexities into latent representations.

However, a primary limitation of these data-intensive methods is their reliance on extensive data collection and retraining for adaptation to new objects, tasks, or robot embodiments. This constraint hinders scalability in open-world environments characterized by an unbounded number of task-object combinations and diverse robotic platforms.

In response to these challenges, a parallel trend in robotics integrates modularized VLA pipelines, leveraging the generalization capabilities of pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). These foundation models handle perception and semantic grounding, while motion execution is managed by optimization-based controllers or pre-trained visuomotor modules (e.g., atomic skills, diffusion policies). This modular design allows for partial inheritance of generalization from foundation models, offering increased flexibility and interpretability. Keypoint, affordance, and correspondence representations serve as anchors between perception and control in these modular systems. Examples include ReKep, which plans motion via predicted relation points, MOKA, which extracts functional regions via multi-modal visual question answering, and RobotPoint, which identifies object-centric task-relevant point clusters. These approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of abstract representations for transferring behavior across varying objects, viewpoints, and instances.

This work, VLBiMan, builds upon the modular approach by focusing on one-shot bimanual manipulation without requiring model retraining. It distinguishes itself from prior zero-shot methods, which often rely on fragile prompt engineering and may suffer from unreliable trajectory execution, by being demonstration-conditioned. It structures action plans based on a single, finely labeled demonstration, then adapts these plans using language-grounded object anchors and motion optimization. The paper aims to bridge the gap between extensive data requirements of end-to-end imitation learning and the flexibility limitations of modular methods, offering a solution for generalizable bimanual manipulation in unstructured settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, VLBiMan, that enables generalizable bimanual robotic manipulation. This framework aims to achieve efficient learning from minimal human input, specifically a single human demonstration, while maintaining adaptability to real-world uncertainties and diverse robotic embodiments.

The motivation for this work arises from a recognized dilemma in existing robotic manipulation approaches:
1. **High Data Requirements of Imitation Learning:** End-to-end imitation policy learning methods, while capable of impressive performance on complex bimanual tasks, demand extensive teleoperated demonstrations to cover task variations. Adapting to new objects or tasks typically necessitates a complete data collection and model retraining cycle, which is impractical for scaling in dynamic, open-world environments with numerous task-object combinations and various robot types.
2. **Lack of Flexibility in Modular Methods:** While modular VLA pipelines, leveraging pre-trained LLMs and VLMs, offer some generalization capabilities by defining generalizable representations (e.g., keypoints, affordances), they often struggle with flexibility in dynamic scenes. Some zero-shot methods, relying on prompt engineering, can be brittle and lead to unreliable trajectory execution.

To address these limitations, VLBiMan proposes a novel approach centered on deriving reusable skills from a single human example. The core motivations include:
- **Drastic Reduction in Demonstration Requirements:** Minimize the need for large-scale data collection and repeated retraining.
- **Enhanced Generalizability:** Enable adaptation to novel but semantically similar objects, dynamic scene ambiguities (background changes, object repositioning, visual clutter) without policy retraining.
- **Compositional Generalization:** Facilitate the construction of long-horizon tasks by splicing atomic skills.
- **Robustness to Disturbances:** Maintain performance under external interference.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** Allow skills learned from human demonstrations to be instantiated on different robotic platforms.
- **Preservation of Invariant Primitives:** Decompose tasks into reusable, invariant skills that serve as anchors.
- **Dynamic Adaptation through Vision-Language Grounding:** Adjust flexible task components using vision-language models and geometric feasibility constraints.
- **Human-like Hybrid Control:** Support mixed synchronous and asynchronous use of both arms for efficient manipulation.

The research is guided by the principle that "what to achieve matters more than how to execute it," focusing on capturing relative spatial relationships and coordination rather than exact poses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLBiMan operates through a three-stage pipeline to synthesize executable dual-arm trajectories from a single demonstration and a task description, adapting to new scenes with relocated or varied objects.

**Preliminaries:**
The system is given a textual task description (T) and a one-shot demonstration (D) consisting of multimodal observations and bimanual actions. The goal is to synthesize an adapted bimanual trajectory for a new scene (S_new). This involves three core challenges:
1. **Task-object semantic grounding:** Mapping the task description to relevant objects in the scene using visual-language grounding.
2. **Executable module decomposition:** Partitioning the demonstration into temporally ordered motion primitives (M_i), categorized as either task-invariant or requiring adaptation.
3. **Trajectory composition with kinematic feasibility:** Synthesizing new trajectories by composing adapted primitives under geometric and kinematic constraints.

#### 3.1. Task-Aware Bimanual Decomposition

This stage parses the one-shot demonstration into semantically meaningful and structurally reusable modules.
- **Spatiotemporal Segmentation:** The one-shot demonstration is recorded using a third-person stereo RGB camera (10 FPS) capturing dual-arm end-effector 6-DoF poses and gripper states. A keypose-driven segmentation scheme is employed. Initial segmentation is automated by detecting waypoints based on motion dynamics (velocity discontinuities, acceleration spikes) or state changes (gripper open/close). Inverse kinematics (IK) is used to validate the feasibility of trajectory segments. A human-in-the-loop refinement process manually adjusts waypoints for spatial continuity and execution robustness, resulting in a segmentation policy.
- **Atomic Skill Extraction:** Semantic labels are assigned to segments (M_i) based on object-robot couplings. A segment is classified as "pre-contact adaptation dependent" if no object is rigidly grasped. Once an object is grasped and rigidly coupled (verified by gripper state and object pose consistency), subsequent motion is considered task-invariant if the object's geometry is approximately equivalent to that in the demonstration. Otherwise, the segment requires adaptation. This process yields a decomposition into invariant (M_inv) and variable (M_var) atomic skill modules for reuse.

#### 3.2. Vision-Language Anchored Adaptation

Variable modules (M_var) are adapted using semantic perception and geometric reasoning.
- **VLM-Based Scene Understanding:** Task-relevant prompts (p_k) are extracted from the text description and mapped to object categories. These prompts are fed to VLMs (e.g., Florence-2, SAM2) to obtain high-quality 2D semantic masks (M_2D) from the new scene observation. VLMs' robustness to lighting and distractors is leveraged for grounding object identity without explicit detection or 3D models.
- **VFM-Based Geometric Feasibility:** To adapt grasping or alignment poses, a three-step process is used:
 1. **Relative Position Transformation:** Calculate ∆T between new and demonstrated object placements using task-specific representative points (e.g., 2D mask centroid or a table-contact point). 3D positions are back-projected from 2D points using stereo and camera intrinsics.
 2. **Orientation-Sensitive Object Adaptation:** For objects requiring orientation (e.g., pen, spoon), the principal axis is computed from second-order image moments of the 2D mask to derive relative rotation (∆θ). The adapted grasp pose is obtained by applying (∆x, ∆θ) to the original grasp pose in robot coordinates.
 3. **Category-Level Variation:** Shape-induced feasibility changes (e.g., height/width differences from object point clouds) are measured to adjust vertical placement or inter-arm distances.
The methodology explicitly avoids 6-DoF pose estimation or generic grasp pose detection to prevent reliance on pre-defined CAD models, ambiguous proposals, and fragility.

#### 3.3. Autonomous Trajectory Composition

Adapted and invariant modules are composed into a new executable trajectory, following the original temporal structure.
- **Progressive IK Refinement:** For initial grasping motions, Inverse Kinematics (IK) is iteratively solved with interpolated splines approaching the target pose. This refinement provides closed-loop correction for object displacement. The interpolation density (n) is a configurable parameter (set to 6 in experiments).
- **Dynamic Collision Compensation:** Proximal and vertical compensation terms are added to the position item during grasp approach to mitigate early contact risks. After trajectory synthesis, a physical replay is performed to detect unintended collisions, and the plan is adjusted.
This modularity also supports cross-task module assembly and long-horizon tool-based task compositions by reusing invariant modules, enabling generalization beyond single tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validated VLBiMan across ten diverse bimanual tasks, including six basic skills, two long-horizon skill combinations, and two multi-stage tool-use tasks. Evaluations were performed on a stationary dual-arm platform and a humanoid dual-arm robot, under conditions of novel placements, novel instances, and dynamic external interference, as well as uneven illumination. VLBiMan was compared against Robot-ABC, ReKep, ReKep+ (enhanced ReKep), Mechanisms, and MAGIC.

#### 4.1. Effective and Robust Bimanual Manipulation

- **Basic Tasks (Table 1):** VLBiMan demonstrated superior capabilities compared to baselines. For "new placements + same objects," VLBiMan achieved an average success rate of 85.3% without interference and 69.3% with interference, significantly outperforming ReKep+ (63.3% and 38.0% respectively) and other baselines (e.g., Robot-ABC at 36.0% and 18.0%). For "new placements + novel instances," VLBiMan maintained high success rates (78.0% without interference, 59.3% with interference), demonstrating its ability to handle unseen objects and orientations.
- This performance is attributed to VLBiMan's ability to timely adjust end-effector 6-DoF poses for unseen object configurations and its decoupling and reuse of invariant modules for fine-grained dual-arm coordination. Baselines that re-plan motions for each new placement without fully leveraging demonstration insights performed less effectively.

#### 4.2. Generalization on Novel Scenarios and Skill Combinations

- **Long-Horizon Multi-Stage Tasks (Table 2):** VLBiMan again showed significant advantages on more complex tasks like "reorient+unscrew" and "tool-use scoop." For "new placements + same objects," VLBiMan achieved an average success rate of 52.0% without interference and 38.0% with interference. For "new placements + novel instances," these rates were 41.0% and 24.0% respectively.
- These results indicate VLBiMan's capability for skill composition, handling intermediate grasping stages, and managing multi-object, contact-intensive actions in tool-use tasks. The system's powerful vision perception (VLMs) and skill reuse design alleviated challenges that severely hampered baseline performance.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer (Table 6, Fig. 6, 12):** VLBiMan successfully transferred skills learned from human demonstrations to a humanoid dual-arm robot. On tasks like "inserting," "unscrew," "pouring," and "reorient," VLBiMan achieved comparable success rates on the new platform (e.g., 83.8% average without interference for same objects, 76.3% for novel instances) to those on the original platform, demonstrating strong cross-embodiment generalization without retraining.

#### 4.3. System Performance Ablation and Analysis

- **Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
 - Using advanced VLMs (Florence-2, SAM2) was critical for performance (59.2% Avg. SR vs. 35.8% with SAM+DINOv2).
 - The proposed initial grasp alignment scheme was more robust than using a generic grasp detector like AnyGrasp (59.2% vs. 31.7%).
 - IK refinement and collision avoidance mechanisms were crucial for feasibility and performance (59.2% vs. 29.2% without IK refinement, and 59.2% vs. 34.2% without collision avoidance).
- **Error Breakdown (Fig. 7):** The most prominent errors (45%) originated from initial grasp execution (e.g., singularity points, early collisions), highlighting the difficulty of real-world grasping. Dual-arm coordination accounted for the second-largest share (21%). VLM perception and anchoring (12%) and trajectory optimization (6%) contributed less, suggesting their reliability.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings from Appendix

- **Robustness to Lighting Changes (Table 5, Fig. 9):** VLBiMan maintained high success rates under uneven illumination with dynamic interference (e.g., 67.5% for same objects, 55.8% for novel instances on basic tasks), while baselines experienced substantial performance degradation. This is attributed to the robustness of VLMs and the invariance of mask-based position/orientation estimation to lighting.
- **Efficient Synchronous Dual-Arm Movement (Fig. 10):** The modular design allowed for synchronized execution of certain motion segments, leading to an average 22% improvement in execution efficiency across tasks.
- **Interference Frequency (Fig. 11):** While increased interference frequency reduced success rates, the decline diminished with more perturbations, possibly due to reduced spatial freedom for effective disturbance as the end-effector approaches the object.
- **Robustness of Object Representing Points (Fig. 14, 15):** The simple 2D mask centroid or contact point proved robust for cross-instance transfer, supporting accurate alignment for objects significantly different from the demonstration, without needing 6D pose estimation or complex shape descriptors.
- **Cluttered Scenarios (Fig. 16):** VLBiMan successfully identified and manipulated target objects in cluttered environments with distractor objects, demonstrating robustness to semantic ambiguity and spatial interference, even with dynamic perturbation or partial occlusion.
- **Pre-grasp Interpolation Density (Table 7):** An optimal interpolation density (e.g., n=6 or n=7) for pre-grasping improved stability, safety, and robustness against perturbations, by allowing continuous re-evaluation and correction of object-relative anchors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces VLBiMan, a framework that advances the field of bimanual robotic manipulation by enabling generalizable skill acquisition from minimal human input. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By leveraging one-shot demonstrations and avoiding extensive retraining, VLBiMan significantly reduces the data collection and computational overhead typically associated with modern imitation learning methods. This addresses a major bottleneck for scaling robotic manipulation in diverse, real-world environments.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability:** The framework demonstrates robustness to novel object instances, arbitrary object placements, and dynamic scene changes (e.g., external interference, uneven lighting, cluttered scenes) without requiring policy retraining. This level of adaptability is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured settings where precise object models or fixed environments cannot be guaranteed.
- **Compositional Skill Learning:** VLBiMan's task-aware decomposition enables the reuse and recomposition of atomic skills, facilitating the creation and execution of complex, long-horizon tasks and tool-use scenarios. This modularity allows for scalable skill extension and offers a pathway towards more versatile robot agents.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The demonstrated ability to transfer skills learned from human demonstrations to different robotic platforms, including humanoid dual-arm robots, broadens the applicability of the learned policies across various hardware configurations. This contributes to making robot learning more platform-agnostic.
- **Efficient and Interpretable Control:** The integration of vision-language grounding, geometric reasoning, and kinematic-aware trajectory composition provides a structured and interpretable approach to bimanual control. This contrasts with opaque end-to-end models and offers insights into how the robot adapts its behavior. The support for human-like hybrid (synchronous/asynchronous) arm control also contributes to operational efficiency.
- **Practical Feasibility:** The system's robustness to real-world uncertainties, including dynamic interference and perceptual challenges, makes it a step towards more practical and deployable dual-arm manipulation solutions in industrial, service, or domestic robotics.

Despite its advancements, VLBiMan has limitations, including its current restriction to rigid objects, lack of runtime anomaly detection and recovery, and dependence on fixed-base hardware without force/tactile sensing. Addressing these areas in future work could further enhance the system's capabilities and expand its impact to more delicate and complex manipulation tasks. Overall, VLBiMan contributes to bridging human priors with adaptive robotic execution, moving towards more versatile and intelligent robotic systems.
