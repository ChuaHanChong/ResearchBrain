# cVLA: Towards Efficient Camera-Space VLAs

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Freiburg's cVLA presents an efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system that predicts end-effector keyposes directly in camera-space coordinates, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for tabletop manipulation tasks with a real robot arm. This system leverages fine-tuned pre-trained VLMs and a multi-level decoding strategy, achieving superior multi-trajectory prediction and successful execution on real-world objects.

## Problem

- Large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face immense computational costs, making research and development difficult for many groups.
- High-quality, multimodal real-world datasets for VLA training are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming to collect.
- Standardized evaluation of VLA models relies on hard-to-reproduce real-world robot rollouts and lacks robust metrics for diverse predictions.

## Method

- A lightweight VLA system built on a fine-tuned PaliGemma2 VLM that predicts two key trajectory waypoints directly in 2D image-frame coordinates for a single forward pass.
- Training uses a meticulously curated simulated dataset from ManiSkill, incorporating diverse 3D assets (CLEVR, Objaverse) and strong randomization strategies for sim-to-real transfer.
- Novel inference-time strategies are introduced, including input image cropping and `beam-search-NMS` for generating and evaluating diverse, spatially distinct trajectory predictions.

## Results

- The cVLA system demonstrates strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, successfully performing 15 distinct tabletop manipulation tasks on a real Franka Panda robot without real-world fine-tuning.
- The proposed `beam-search-NMS` decoding strategy significantly improves multi-trajectory prediction, reducing Top-3 error from **33.94%** to **25.00%** and achieving a substantially higher mAP (**0.45** with crop vs. **0.16** for standard beam search).
- cVLA successfully extends to one-shot imitation learning, achieving **70%** success on CLEVR-easy and **28%** on CLEVR-hard simulated tasks, showing generalization to real data and unseen objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting end-effector keyposes in camera-frame coordinates, rather than robot-base coordinates, leverages VLM strengths and improves VLA performance, offering robot embodiment agnosticism.
> - Integrating depth information via colorized depth images significantly enhances VLA performance and reduces failure rates by providing crucial 3D spatial context.
> - Carefully randomized and diverse simulated datasets, especially with varied 3D assets, are crucial for achieving robust sim-to-real transfer and generalization to novel objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a frontier in artificial intelligence and robotics, aiming to create generalist robots capable of understanding complex instructions, perceiving their environment visually, and executing diverse manipulation tasks. Recent years have seen a surge in large-scale VLA models, often termed "foundation models" (e.g., PaLM-E, Octo, OpenVLA, GR00T N1), which are trained on vast datasets and exhibit impressive generalization capabilities across various robots and environments.

However, the authors identify several critical constraints hindering the widespread adoption and further advancement of these powerful VLA systems:
a) **Computational Costs**: Training large-scale VLAs demands immense computational resources, making experimentation and iterative development challenging for many research groups.
b) **Data Limitations**: Gathering high-quality, multimodal real-world datasets encompassing vision, language, and interaction data is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
c) **Evaluation and Benchmarking**: Standardized evaluation often relies on real-world robot rollouts, which are difficult to reproduce consistently and compare across different setups.

"cVLA" directly addresses these constraints by proposing a **lightweight and efficient VLA system** that focuses on solving manipulation tasks by leveraging existing Vision Language Models (VLMs) and simulated data. Unlike the trend towards general-purpose foundation models, this work consciously limits its scope to a "narrow data distribution with a limited set of tabletop tasks" and "quasi-static manipulation" with "low temporal resolution actions." This strategic narrowing allows the researchers to conduct focused experiments and gain insights into critical factors affecting VLA performance, with the ultimate goal of informing future scaling efforts.

The paper connects with several specific areas of ongoing research:
- **Trajectory Prediction and Waypoint Representations**: This work contributes by predicting trajectory *waypoints* (keyposes) in image-frame coordinates, rather than low-level controls. This aligns with approaches like Perceiver-Actor and Hierarchical Diffusion Policy (HDP), which use inferred keyposes, and PPI which combines 6-DoF keyposes with pointflows. By predicting camera-space coordinates, cVLA also draws inspiration from methods like Molmo and RoboPoint that use pointing directly in image space.
- **Training from Simulation**: The heavy reliance on simulated data for training positions cVLA within the active research area of sim-to-real transfer. While simulation offers scalability (DexGraspVLA, CALVIN, RLBench), bridging the reality gap remains a challenge. cVLA's focus on curated datasets with strong randomization strategies directly contributes to this area.
- **Auxiliary Visual Tasks**: The incorporation of depth images as input echoes trends in LLARVA, Gemini Robotics-ER, and 3D-VLA, which utilize auxiliary visual information (like 2D traces, keypoint detection, or depth) to improve spatial grounding and generalization.
- **Evaluation of VLAs**: The paper's investigation into inference-time strategies and novel metrics like mAP for diverse predictions contributes to the ongoing discourse on robust VLA evaluation, complementing recent efforts in real-to-sim evaluations and metrics for prediction diversity.
- **Few-Shot Imitation Learning**: The extension to one-shot imitation learning aligns with recent advancements in data-efficient robot learning, building upon meta-learning approaches, metric learning, and in-context learning with transformers.

In summary, cVLA positions itself as a practical and insightful approach within the VLA landscape, offering a lightweight alternative to large foundation models, while rigorously exploring crucial aspects like action representation, data efficiency, and inference strategies that are vital for the field's progression. It seeks to demystify some of the complexities of VLA development by providing an efficient, controllable experimental setup.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind cVLA stems from the aforementioned challenges in developing and deploying generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: high computational costs, scarcity of real-world multimodal datasets, and difficulties in standardized evaluation. The authors argue that these constraints impede rapid experimentation and iterative development, which are essential for advancing the field.

The key objectives of this research are multi-faceted:

1. **Develop an Efficient and Lightweight VLA System**: The primary objective is to create a VLA model that is significantly less resource-intensive to train and deploy compared to existing large-scale foundation models. This aims to lower the barrier to entry for VLA research and enable faster iteration cycles.

2. **Leverage 2D Vision-Language Model (VLM) Strengths for Robot Control**: Instead of directly predicting low-level joint controls or complex 6D end-effector poses in robot-base coordinates, the model aims to directly infer robot end-effector poses within the 2D image frame. This design choice capitalizes on the robust performance of pre-trained VLMs on 2D image understanding tasks, simplifying the action space.

3. **Propose Trajectory Waypoint Prediction for Efficiency and Embodiment Agnosticism**: The model is designed to predict only a few key trajectory waypoints (keyposes) rather than a full continuous trajectory or low-level actions. This not only makes training more efficient but also renders the model more "robot embodiment agnostic," as a low-level planner can convert these high-level waypoints into specific robot movements for various kinematic structures.

4. **Explore the Potential of Depth Information and Inference-Time Techniques**: The authors explicitly aim to investigate how incorporating depth images, which provide crucial 3D spatial context, can enhance VLA performance. Furthermore, they seek to adapt and explore inference-time strategies from language models (e.g., decoding strategies, image cropping) to improve the robustness and accuracy of VLA predictions.

5. **Demonstrate Strong Sim-to-Real Transfer Capabilities**: A significant objective is to train the VLA model on a synthetic, curated dataset and then demonstrate its effectiveness and generalization capabilities on real robotic systems without any real-world fine-tuning. This addresses the data limitation challenge by validating the efficacy of simulation-based training.

6. **Provide Insights for Future VLA Scaling**: While not aiming to create another general-purpose foundation model, the project seeks to "provide helpful insights into the factors affecting VLA performance" through small-scale experiments, thereby contributing foundational knowledge that can inform and guide future efforts to scale VLA systems effectively.

In essence, the motivation is to offer a practical, efficient, and well-analyzed VLA framework that can serve as a robust experimental platform, ultimately guiding the community towards more scalable and deployable robotic AI solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The cVLA approach focuses on building an efficient VLA system by judiciously leveraging pre-trained VLMs, a novel action representation, carefully curated simulated data, and refined inference strategies.

#### 3.1. Base Model Architecture and Action Representation

The core of cVLA is the **fine-tuning of a pre-trained Vision Language Model (VLM)**, specifically PaliGemma2. This choice is motivated by the desire for an efficient and robust VLA system, benefiting from the VLM's powerful visual and linguistic understanding capabilities without starting from scratch.

The model's input prompt follows a standard VLA convention: `<live img(s)> + <robot state> + <task description> → <estimated trajectory>`. Crucially, cVLA introduces several design decisions for `<estimated trajectory>`:
- **Keypose Prediction**: Instead of predicting a full, dense trajectory, the model predicts only *two* keyposes (e.g., grasp and release positions). These keyposes are then converted into a complete robot trajectory by a separate low-level planner.
- **One-Step Prediction**: The model predicts the entire trajectory (via its keyposes) for a given scene in a single forward pass. This choice, while restricting flexibility for multi-step reasoning, significantly enhances training efficiency.
- **Lightweight Fine-tuning**: The VLM is fine-tuned only on its self-attention layer parameters, a lightweight modification that enables strong trajectory prediction while keeping the model size manageable.

A novel aspect lies in its **Action Representation**:
- Unlike typical approaches that predict 6-DoF gripper poses or deltas in robot-base coordinates, cVLA encodes actions as **absolute positions in either robot-base coordinates or, more prominently, *image-frame coordinates***. Image-frame coordinates represent the normalized width, height, and distance from the camera. This capitalizes on the VLM's inherent 2D processing capabilities.
- PaliGemma2's existing special tokens for image detection and segmentation are repurposed for pose prediction. Specifically, localization tokens (n=1024) are used for position (and depth, when predicted separately), while segmentation tokens (n=128) encode orientations. The authors also experiment with varying token resolutions (e.g., n=512, 256, 128) for position to free up tokens for separate depth prediction.

#### 3.2. Depth Integration

To incorporate depth information, which is critical for 3D understanding, cVLA converts raw depth maps into **RGB images using Matplotlib's viridis colormap**. These colorized depth images are then fed into the *same pre-trained image encoder* as the natural RGB images, allowing the model to leverage its existing visual processing pipeline for depth perception.

#### 3.3. Robotic Imitation (Few-Shot)

The base approach is extended to **few-shot imitation learning** by modifying the prompt structure. Instead of a natural language task description, the model is conditioned on a demonstration image-trajectory pair: `<demo img> + <demo trajectory> + <live img> → <estimated trajectory>`. The model must infer the task from the demonstration and apply it to the new live scene. This effectively trains the model to perform in-context imitation. To enable this, a task-demonstration sampler is built to create numerous demonstration-query pairs from available scenes for training.

#### 3.4. Dataset Generation

The training of cVLA relies heavily on **simulated data** generated using the ManiSkill simulator. This allows for scalable and controlled data collection.
- **Generation Procedure**: Objects and instructions are spawned, target object poses are calculated, and analytical grasp and release poses are generated. The simulation can also execute and verify task success.
- **3D Model Assets**: The dataset uses two sets of objects: simple geometric shapes inspired by **CLEVR** (cubes, blocks, spheres in different sizes and colors) and diverse real-world objects scraped from **Objaverse**. Objaverse assets undergo a meticulous curation process involving GPT-4V for descriptions and scaling, and SigLIP for description alignment.
- **Augmentations and Randomization**: To enhance sim-to-real transfer, standard image augmentations, background image randomization (using indoor scenes from), and text randomization are applied. Crucially, the dataset includes **"easy" and "hard" variants** with varying levels of scene and camera field-of-view randomization. The hard variant includes visibility tests to ensure physical plausibility.

For **real-world evaluation**, subsets of the DROID dataset are used. This data is manually filtered for consistent extrinsic calibration and tasks involving moving cubes ("move-A-to-B" actions). Two subsets, **DROID-hard** (with confounding objects) and **DROID-easy** (with blurred confounding objects), are created to test different aspects of model robustness. Offline evaluation uses an L1 error metric for combined position and rotation.

#### 3.5. Inference-Time Strategies

The paper explores how techniques from VLMs can be adapted to VLA:
- **Input Image Cropping**: To address sensitivity to object scale and low input resolution, various cropping strategies are investigated: centering the crop on the image, the starting object, or the midpoint between start and end objects, with and without zero-padding.
- **Multiple Prediction Generation and Evaluation**:
 - Standard decoding strategies like **Beam Search** and **Sampling** are evaluated.
 - A novel decoding strategy, **`beam-search-NMS` (Non-Maximum Suppression)**, is proposed. This method performs beam search and then applies NMS over spatially contiguous tokens in the predicted pose distribution to identify multiple distinct peaks, addressing the issue where standard beam search might yield near-identical top-k predictions for dense pose tokens.
 - To evaluate the diversity and accuracy of multiple predictions, the **mean Average Precision (mAP)** metric, adapted from object detection (COCO), is proposed. It uses L1 error between predicted and ground-truth trajectories at various thresholds instead of IoU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in cVLA highlight the effectiveness of its lightweight design, the importance of various architectural choices, data generation strategies, and inference-time techniques for VLA performance.

- **Efficiency of cVLA Architecture**: The core finding is that the lightweight, fine-tuned PaliGemma2 architecture, designed for one-step prediction of two end-effector keyposes in image-frame coordinates, effectively learns meaningful and executable robot trajectories. This demonstrates that an efficient, specialized VLA system can achieve strong performance without the immense scale of general-purpose foundation models.

- **Impact of Depth Input**: Incorporating depth information into the model significantly boosts performance, leading to improved simulation success rates and a reduction in drastic failure cases (Table 1). This underscores the value of 3D spatial context beyond purely RGB images for robust manipulation.

- **Action Representation**: Comparing robot-frame versus camera-frame coordinate predictions, the authors observe that **camera-frame coordinates generally yield better performance** (Figure 2). While this might be partially due to easier overfitting in simple simulated environments, it suggests that predicting directly in the image space, aligning with the VLM's natural output, is a promising direction.

- **Dataset Diversity and Randomization**: The ablation studies (Table 1) reveal that training solely on CLEVR assets leads to good performance on CLEVR-based simulations but **fails to generalize to Objaverse-based simulations**, emphasizing the critical need for diverse 3D assets in training data. Furthermore, using "harder" versions of the dataset (i.e., those with more camera and scene randomization) proves **essential for achieving robustness** and better generalization to real data and novel objects, even if it slightly reduces performance on the "easy" simulated test sets (Table 2).

- **One-Shot Imitation Learning**: The cVLA system can be effectively extended for one-shot trajectory-conditioned imitation from demonstrations. By inferring tasks from demonstration image-trajectory pairs, the model achieves respectable success rates in simulation (70% on CLEVR-easy, 28% on CLEVR-hard) and shows good generalization to both real data (DROID-easy) and completely unseen objects in the Objaverse dataset (Table 2, Figure 8). This highlights the model's ability to learn abstract task representations.

- **Inference-Time Strategies**:
 - **Input Image Cropping**: Cropping strategies, particularly those centered on the task-relevant objects, **consistently improve performance** by enhancing object localization (Figure 3). This is especially beneficial for lower-resolution inputs and single-step prediction models susceptible to object scale.
 - **Multiple Prediction Generation (`beam-search-NMS`)**: The proposed `beam-search-NMS` decoding strategy demonstrates superior performance over standard beam search. While the Top-1 error is similar, `beam-search-NMS` significantly improves the "Top-3" error (from 33.94 to 25.00, Table 3), indicating its ability to find more diverse and accurate alternative trajectories. Qualitatively, it effectively detects correct target object locations even when the most probable beam is incorrect (Figure 4). The mAP evaluation confirms this, with `beam-search-NMS` achieving a significantly higher mAP (0.31 without crop, 0.45 with crop) compared to standard beam search (0.11 without crop, 0.16 with crop), demonstrating better distributional accuracy and ability to capture multiple plausible solutions (Figure 10).

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: A crucial finding is the model's **strong sim-to-real transfer capabilities**. The system performs zero-shot inference on a real Franka Panda robotic arm for 15 distinct tabletop manipulation tasks involving everyday household items, without any real-world fine-tuning (Figure 5). This validates the effectiveness of their simulated dataset and randomization strategies for practical robotic deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "cVLA: Towards Efficient Camera-Space VLAs" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the field of robot learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) research.

1. **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for VLA Research**: By proposing an efficient and lightweight VLA system, cVLA makes advanced robot learning research more accessible. The high computational costs and data demands of large foundation models often limit VLA experimentation to well-resourced institutions. cVLA demonstrates that a smaller-scale, fine-tuned VLM can effectively tackle complex manipulation tasks, enabling more researchers to engage with and contribute to the VLA domain. This democratization of VLA research could accelerate innovation and diversify research perspectives.

2. **Validation of Camera-Space Action Representation**: The shift from predicting 6D poses in robot-base coordinates to directly inferring end-effector keyposes in camera-frame coordinates is a notable contribution. This approach simplifies the action space, leverages the strengths of 2D Vision Language Models, and makes the system more robot embodiment-agnostic, as a low-level planner can adapt these image-space waypoints to different robot kinematics. This design choice opens new avenues for creating flexible and transferable robot policies.

3. **Emphasis on Effective Simulation and Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The paper strongly validates the power of carefully designed simulated data, robust randomization, and diverse asset collections (like Objaverse) for achieving strong sim-to-real transfer. Demonstrating zero-shot inference on a real robot without fine-tuning on real data is a critical step towards scalable robot learning. This work provides concrete strategies for constructing effective synthetic datasets, reducing reliance on expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection.

4. **Novel Contributions to Inference-Time Strategies**: The introduction of `beam-search-NMS` is a significant methodological advancement. By adapting non-maximum suppression to the dense, multi-modal output distributions of VLA pose tokens, this decoding strategy allows the model to identify and leverage multiple plausible trajectories. This not only improves overall performance but also enhances the model's ability to handle ambiguous situations, which is crucial for robust real-world robotics. The proposed mAP metric for evaluating multi-trajectory predictions is also valuable for future VLA assessment.

5. **Foundation for Future VLA Scaling**: While cVLA is not a foundation model itself, it is explicitly designed to provide "helpful insights into the factors affecting VLA performance." Its controlled experiments on various design choices (depth input, action encoding, dataset randomization) and inference strategies offer a clearer understanding of what contributes to VLA success. This systematic investigation lays a solid foundation for guiding the development of more robust, scalable, and generalist VLA systems in the future.

6. **Open Science Contribution**: The stated commitment to publicly releasing code, datasets, and models upon acceptance is a vital contribution to the scientific community. This fosters reproducibility, allows other researchers to build upon their work, and further accelerates progress in VLA and robot learning.

In conclusion, cVLA provides a practical, efficient, and rigorously evaluated framework for developing VLA systems. Its focus on accessibility, novel action representations, effective sim-to-real transfer, and advanced inference strategies makes it a significant contribution that could influence the direction of future research in robot manipulation and embodied AI.
