# Teleoperator-Aware and Safety-Critical Adaptive Nonlinear MPC for Shared Autonomy in Obstacle Avoidance of Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Virginia Tech researchers developed a teleoperator-aware, safety-critical adaptive nonlinear Model Predictive Control (ANMPC) framework for shared autonomy in quadrupedal robots performing obstacle avoidance. This system integrates online human intent adaptation using a Boltzmann model and formal Control Barrier Function (CBF) safety guarantees, achieving 100% task success rates and robust obstacle avoidance on a Unitree Go2 robot in human-in-the-loop experiments.

## Problem

- Integrating human teleoperator input with autonomous control for legged robots in cluttered, safety-critical environments while ensuring safety and managing unpredictable human commands.
- Addressing the computational complexity of incorporating nonlinear human intent models and formal safety guarantees within real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) for high-dimensional, unstable legged systems.
- Developing a unified control framework that accounts for the inherent instability, underactuation, and hybrid dynamics of quadrupedal robots while enabling online adaptation to evolving human behavior.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture comprising a high-level Adaptive Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (AN**MPC**) for shared autonomy, a mid-level NMPC for dynamics, and a low-level Whole-Body Controller.
- Incorporation of a noisily rational Boltzmann model for human intent, with its parameters adapted online using a Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm based on observed human commands.
- Embedding Control Barrier Function (CBF) constraints directly into the NMPC formulation to provide formal safety guarantees for forward invariance of safe sets, specifically for obstacle avoidance.

## Results

- Achieved a **100%** success rate in safely reaching designated goals during human-in-the-loop obstacle avoidance tasks on a Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot.
- Maintained consistently larger safety margins from obstacles compared to baseline fully human control, with the high-level ANMPC demonstrating an average solve time of 27 ms for real-time operation.
- The framework successfully approximated and adapted to human operator behavior for linear velocity commands, enabling the robot to initiate navigation with minimal human intervention for significant durations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The NMPC can efficiently incorporate the nonlinear human action model as an equality constraint, implicitly solving for human actions along the prediction horizon without needing a non-existent closed-form solution.
> - Online adaptation of human intent parameters allows the robot to dynamically learn and respond to the teleoperator's evolving priorities and behaviors in real-time, moving beyond static blending strategies.
> - Integrating CBFs into NMPC successfully extends formal safety guarantees over the prediction horizon for legged robots, enabling robust collision avoidance even with uncertain human inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of humans and autonomous robots, particularly legged robots, in shared autonomy paradigms presents significant challenges and opportunities for deployment in complex, safety-critical environments such as disaster response and search-and-rescue. Legged robots are characterized by high degrees of freedom, inherent instability, underactuation, and hybrid dynamics, making their control intricate. Human inputs are often unpredictable and noisy, necessitating robust autonomous systems that ensure safety.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has emerged as a method for trajectory optimization and control of legged robots, often utilizing reduced-order models like the Single Rigid Body (SRB) model for computational efficiency. While quadratic programming (QP)-based MPC is efficient for linearized models, it frequently struggles with nonlinear effects and collision avoidance. Nonlinear MPC (NMPC) addresses these limitations by directly incorporating nonlinear robot dynamics and safety constraints, having been applied to gait planning and safety-critical locomotion. Concurrently, Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) offer formal guarantees for system safety, and recent work has integrated CBFs with MPC to extend these guarantees over prediction horizons for legged robots.

Despite these advancements, a notable gap in the existing literature is the explicit incorporation of human-in-the-loop shared autonomy and human intent modeling within MPC/NMPC frameworks for legged robots. While shared autonomy has been explored for robot arms, quadrotors, and smart vehicles, these methods often do not directly translate to the dynamic and stability challenges unique to legged locomotion. Previous work combining intent blending with safety guarantees, such as stochastic optimal control frameworks with Boltzmann human behavior models, typically focused on autonomous driving or robotic manipulation, without addressing the specific high-dimensionality and underactuation characteristics of legged systems. Some prior research has explored human input for specific control aspects of quadruped robots (e.g., front leg control for static stability), but a comprehensive teleoperator-aware, safety-critical NMPC for obstacle avoidance in legged robots has remained an area for further development. This paper positions itself by aiming to fill these gaps, providing a unified framework that combines online human intent adaptation, formal safety guarantees, and a hierarchical control architecture suitable for the complex dynamics of quadrupedal robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a computationally efficient, teleoperator-aware, safety-critical, and adaptive nonlinear model predictive control (ANMPC) framework specifically for shared autonomy in quadrupedal robots performing obstacle-avoidance tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several fundamental challenges inherent in deploying legged robots in collaborative and safety-critical roles:

1. **Ensuring Safe and Effective Human-Robot Collaboration:** In unstructured and cluttered environments, effective collaboration between human teleoperators and autonomous legged robots is crucial. Conventional shared-control approaches often employ fixed blending strategies that may not adequately account for the complex, dynamic nature of legged locomotion or provide sufficient safety assurances. The paper aims to address this by developing a system that actively considers human input while prioritizing robot safety.
2. **Addressing Human Input Uncertainty and Robot Instability:** Human inputs are frequently noisy, unpredictable, and potentially conflicting with optimal robot behavior. Simultaneously, legged robots are inherently unstable systems with numerous degrees of freedom, underactuation, and hybrid dynamics from contact switching. The framework seeks to manage this interplay, allowing the robot to interpret and adapt to human commands while maintaining stability and safety.
3. **Integrating Formal Safety Guarantees with Online Adaptation:** Existing MPC/NMPC solutions for legged robots often assume full autonomy and lack explicit mechanisms for shared control or modeling human intent. When human intent modeling is considered, it usually involves complex stochastic approaches or is not directly applied to legged systems. The authors aim to integrate formal safety guarantees, provided by Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), with an online adaptation mechanism for human behavior parameters, ensuring forward invariance of safe sets despite uncertainties in human actions.
4. **Overcoming Computational Challenges of Nonlinear Human Models:** Modeling human actions within an NMPC framework, especially using nonlinear and parameterized models like the noisily rational Boltzmann model, introduces significant computational complexity. The optimal human action often lacks a closed-form solution, making its direct inclusion in predictive control problematic. A key motivation is to devise an efficient method to incorporate these nonlinear human action constraints without resorting to computationally expensive approximations.
5. **Developing a Hierarchical Control Architecture for Legged Robots:** The full-order dynamics of legged robots are high-dimensional and complex. To enable real-time control, a multi-layered approach is often necessary. The paper aims to develop a hierarchical architecture that effectively bridges high-level goal planning (incorporating human intent and safety) with mid-level dynamic stability control and low-level whole-body control, ensuring feasibility and real-time performance on physical hardware.

By addressing these motivations, the authors seek to enable more robust, safe, and intuitive shared autonomy systems for legged robots in demanding applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework is a teleoperator-aware, safety-critical, adaptive nonlinear Model Predictive Control (ANMPC) system designed for shared autonomy in quadrupedal robots. It operates under a hierarchical control architecture comprising high, mid, and low levels, each with distinct functions and operating frequencies.

**A. Shared Control and Human Action Model:**
The core shared autonomy mechanism involves a weighted combination of human ($u_H(t)$) and robot ($u_R(t)$) control inputs: $u(t) = \lambda u_R(t) + (1-\lambda) u_H(t)$, where $\lambda \in$ is a fixed arbitration weight. Instead of just blending, the framework explicitly models human input using a noisily rational Boltzmann model, which describes human action selection based on minimizing a state-action value function, $Q(x, u_H, \theta)$.

The human’s state-action value function, $Q(x, u_H, \theta)$, is parameterized as a convex function consisting of:
- A quadratic term encouraging goal-reaching (minimizing distance between robot’s predicted output and target, weighted by $\theta_1$).
- A quadratic term penalizing human effort (magnitude of $u_H$, weighted by $\theta_2$).
- Logarithmic barrier functions promoting obstacle avoidance (penalizing proximity to obstacles, weighted by $\theta_3$).
The parameter vector $\theta = \text{col}(\theta_1, \theta_2, \theta_3)$ captures the human's intent and is assumed to be unknown and time-varying. The optimal human action, $u_H^\star(x, \theta)$, is found by minimizing $Q(x, u_H, \theta)$, which results in a nonlinear algebraic equation $\phi(x, u_H, \theta) = 0$ (Equation 6) for which no general closed-form solution exists.

**B. Hierarchical Control Architecture:**

1. **High-Level CBF-based ANMPC (10 Hz):**
 - **Purpose:** Generates blended human-robot velocity references ($u(t)$) for the robot’s kinematic model (e.g., a kinematic car model).
 - **NMPC Formulation:** The central element is a CBF-based NMPC (Equation 13) that optimizes robot actions, predicts human actions, and ensures safety.
 - **Decision Variables:** Predicted state trajectories $x(\cdot)$, robot control inputs $u_R(\cdot)$, human control inputs $u_H(\cdot)$, and CBF slack variables $\delta(\cdot)$ over a prediction horizon $N$.
 - **Cost Function:** Minimizes a sum of terminal and stage costs for robot state tracking and input effort, human input effort, and a penalty on CBF slack variables.
 - **Constraints:**
 - Robot dynamics: $x_{t+k+1|t} = f(x_{t+k|t}, \lambda u_{R,t+k|t} + (1-\lambda) u_{H,t+k|t})$.
 - Human Action Model: The non-linear algebraic constraint $\phi(x_{t+k|t}, u_{H,t+k|t}, \hat{\theta}(t)) = 0$ is included as an *equality constraint* within the NMPC. This is a key methodological choice, as it allows the NMPC to implicitly solve for the human action along the prediction horizon without requiring an explicit, non-existent closed-form solution, thereby preserving computational efficiency.
 - Safety: Control Barrier Function (CBF) constraints $\psi(\cdot) \ge \delta_{t+k|t} \mathbf{1}$ (Equation 11) are enforced to guarantee the forward invariance of a safe set $S = \{x \mid \|C_{xy}x - o_\ell\| \ge d_{th}\}$, ensuring a minimum distance from obstacles $d_{th}$. Slack variables $\delta$ are used to ensure the NMPC's feasibility.
 - Input admissibility: $u_R \in U_R$.
 - **Output:** The first optimal robot action, $\pi_R(x, \hat{\theta}(t))$, is applied to the robot. The estimated human action $u_{H, t|t}$ is used by the adaptive layer.

2. **Outer Loop: PGD-based Adaptive Law (Online Adaptation):**
 - **Purpose:** Updates the estimated parameters $\hat{\theta}(t)$ of the human’s value function in real-time.
 - **Mechanism:** A Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm is used. The update rule aims to minimize a cost function $J(\hat{\theta})$ defined as the squared difference between the estimated human action from the NMPC ($u_{H, t|t}$) and the actually observed human joystick command ($u_H(t)$).
 - **Gradient Calculation:** The gradient $\nabla_{\hat{\theta}} J(\hat{\theta})$ is derived by differentiating $J(\hat{\theta})$ with respect to $\hat{\theta}$. Critically, since $u_{H, t|t}$ is implicitly defined by the algebraic constraint $\phi(x, u_H, \hat{\theta}) = 0$, the chain rule involves calculating $\partial u_{H, t|t} / \partial \hat{\theta}$ (Equation 17), which requires inverting $\partial \phi / \partial u_H$. This inversion is stated to be consistently practicable.
 - **Projection:** The updated parameter estimate $\eta(t+1)$ is projected onto the feasible parameter set $\Theta$ to ensure valid parameter values, yielding $\hat{\theta}(t+1)$.

3. **Mid-Level Dynamics-Aware NMPC (60 Hz):**
 - **Purpose:** Enforces the reduced-order Single Rigid Body (SRB) dynamics of the quadruped to track the linear and angular velocity references provided by the high-level ANMPC.
 - **Functionality:** Optimally computes Ground Reaction Forces (GRFs) to ensure dynamic stability and satisfy friction cone constraints.

4. **Low-Level Nonlinear Whole-Body Controller (WBC) (500 Hz):**
 - **Purpose:** Imposes the full-order dynamics of the quadrupedal robot.
 - **Mechanism:** Implemented as a real-time Quadratic Program (QP) that tracks the SRB states and GRFs prescribed by the mid-level NMPC.

This multi-layered approach allows for real-time operation by decoupling the complex control problem across different time scales and levels of abstraction, while integrating human intent modeling and safety guarantees at the highest planning layer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed teleoperator-aware, safety-critical ANMPC framework was validated through extensive numerical simulations and human-in-the-loop hardware experiments conducted on a Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot.

**A. Experimental Setup:**
- **Hardware:** Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot (15 kg, 18 DoFs, 12 actuated joints) equipped with a Unitree L1 4D LiDAR for obstacle detection.
- **Human Interface:** Sony DualShock 4 controller for human teleoperation commands (linear and angular velocities).
- **Computational Platform:** Offboard desktop workstation (Intel Core i9-12900F, 64 GB RAM) for ANMPC execution in a multithreaded configuration, communicating via LAN. RaiSim physics engine used for numerical simulations.
- **Environment:** 8.1 × 5.4 m² laboratory space containing 10 cylindrical obstacles (radius 0.125 m) and two designated target goals (gA, gB) with desired xy coordinates and yaw angles.
- **Participants:** 12 subjects, each completing four trials: two under fully human (baseline, $\lambda=0$) control and two under shared autonomy (proposed ANMPC, $\lambda=0.35$). Total of 48 experiments. Participants were unaware of the active control mode.
- **Hyperparameters:** High-level ANMPC used a sampling time $T_s=0.1$ s, prediction horizon $N=100$, and specific gain matrices. CBF gain $\gamma=0.1$ and keep-out radius $d_{th}=0.5$ m. Initial estimates for human parameters $\hat{\theta}$ were chosen, with PGD adaptation parameters $\Gamma = \text{diag}\{0.01, \dots, 0.01\}$ and $\mu=1$. Control commands were saturated to physical limits.
- **Real-time Computation:** The high-level NLP (Equation 13) involved 803 decision variables. It was implemented in CasADi and solved using IPOPT, with an average solve time of 27 ms per iteration, restricted to 10 solver iterations with warm-starting. No infeasibility issues were encountered during experiments. The mid-level NMPC operated at 60 Hz.

**B. Quantitative Analysis:**
- **Success Rate:** The shared autonomy controller achieved a 100% success rate in safely reaching the designated goals across all trials.
- **Safety Performance (Fig. 4):** The proposed CBF-based ANMPC consistently maintained larger safety margins from obstacles compared to the baseline (fully human control). While the guaranteed safe distance of $d_{th}=0.5$ m was occasionally slightly compromised, this was attributed to the intentional introduction of slack variables for optimization feasibility.
- **Human Input Approximation (Fig. 3):** The framework's Boltzmann model demonstrated the ability to approximate and adapt to the human operator's behavior, particularly for linear velocity commands. The ANMPC enabled the robot to initiate navigation with minimal human intervention for a significant duration (e.g., first 30 seconds), in contrast to the baseline controller which required continuous human input to prevent the robot from remaining static.
- **Online Parameter Adaptation (Fig. 5):** The estimated human intent parameters ($\hat{\theta}$) updated online via the PGD-based adaptation law. Parameter updates primarily occurred when the human provided joystick inputs, pausing when no significant commands were issued. The parameters generally showed stable convergence, with relatively small changes during human-subject experiments due to effective initialization.

**C. Qualitative Analysis (User Study & Ablation Study):**
- **User Perception (Fig. 2):** A post-demo survey using a 7-point Likert scale (Table I) revealed that participants generally felt more comfortable with the baseline (fully human) controller. This was interpreted as a consequence of participants sometimes attempting to override or correct the robot's assistive actions, stemming from a lack of awareness of the shared autonomy mode. However, participants reported that the assistive movements provided by the shared autonomy system were perceptible ($p < 0.001$) and that shared autonomy was beneficial for task completion.
- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Arbitration Weight ($\lambda$):** A value of $\lambda=0.35$ was found to provide an effective balance between human influence and autonomous assistance. Values like $\lambda=0.5$ could lead to actions canceling out, while $\lambda=0.1$ granted excessive human authority, compromising obstacle avoidance reliability.
 - **CBF Slack Variables Penalty ($w$):** Slack variables were crucial for NMPC feasibility when $\lambda < 0.5$. A penalty weight of $w=10^3$ offered a good balance between ensuring strong safety and allowing for minor constraint violations to accommodate operator commands.
 - **CBF Threshold ($d_{th}$):** The chosen $d_{th}=0.5$ m minimized human opposition in narrow passages. Minor collisions occurred in some cases over longer distances, attributed to IMU drift.
 - **Human Behavior:** Some participants, particularly novices, exhibited behaviors (e.g., treating the task like car driving, favoring straight paths) that occasionally conflicted with ANMPC predictions, indicating a mismatch between user intuition and the autonomy's strategy.

In summary, the experiments demonstrated the framework's capability for real-time obstacle avoidance, online adaptation to human intent, and safe human-robot collaboration on a physical quadrupedal robot. While user studies highlighted areas for improved human-robot interaction and transparency, the system's robust safety guarantees and performance were validated.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic control and human-robot interaction by introducing a comprehensive framework that integrates adaptive human intent modeling and formal safety guarantees into a hierarchical model predictive control scheme for legged robots.

The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to address several critical challenges in shared autonomy for highly dynamic systems:
- **Explicit Human Intent Modeling and Adaptation:** By integrating a noisily rational Boltzmann model of human behavior and adapting its parameters online using a Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) law, the robot can learn and respond to the teleoperator's evolving intent. This moves beyond static blending strategies and enables more adaptive and intuitive human-robot collaboration.
- **Formal Safety Guarantees:** The embedding of Control Barrier Function (CBF) constraints directly into the Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) formulation provides formal guarantees of safety, ensuring the forward invariance of safe sets despite uncertainties in human behavior. This is crucial for deploying legged robots in safety-critical applications where collisions must be rigorously avoided.
- **Computational Efficiency for Complex Models:** The methodology addresses the computational challenge of including a non-linear human action model (whose optimal action lacks a closed-form solution) within the NMPC by treating it as an equality constraint. This approach avoids computationally expensive approximations or stochastic methods, enabling real-time operation with a competitive average solve time for the NMPC.
- **Hierarchical Control for Legged Robots:** The three-layered control architecture (high-level ANMPC for kinematic planning, mid-level NMPC for SRB dynamics, and low-level Whole-Body Controller for full-order dynamics) effectively manages the complexity of legged locomotion. This structure is a practical approach for achieving dynamic stability and trajectory tracking while integrating higher-level shared autonomy objectives.
- **Hardware Validation:** The extensive validation on a Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot, including human-in-the-loop experiments and a user study, demonstrates the framework's practical applicability and robustness in a real-world setting. This moves the research beyond theoretical and simulation-based studies.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Reliability and Trust:** By providing explicit safety guarantees and adapting to human behavior, the framework can foster greater trust in autonomous legged robots, making them more acceptable for deployment in collaborative and hazardous environments like search-and-rescue or industrial inspection.
- **Improved Operability for Complex Systems:** For highly dynamic and complex systems like quadrupedal robots, shared autonomy can reduce the cognitive load on human operators, allowing them to focus on high-level goals rather than intricate low-level control. The online adaptation further refines this assistance over time.
- **Foundational Research for Human-Robot Teaming:** The insights gained from the user studies, particularly regarding user perception and potential conflicts between human intuition and autonomous assistance, highlight the importance of designing more transparent and intuitive shared autonomy interfaces. This provides a basis for future research aimed at improving human-robot teaming by addressing communication and training aspects.
- **Advancement in Robotics Control:** The methodology for efficiently integrating complex non-linear models (like human intent) and formal safety constraints (CBFs) within an NMPC framework is a generalizable contribution that could be adapted to other robotic platforms and control problems.

Overall, this research represents a step towards creating more capable, safe, and collaborative legged robotic systems for real-world applications by systematically addressing the interplay between human intention, robot dynamics, and safety constraints.
