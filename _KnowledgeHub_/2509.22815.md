# Teleoperator-Aware and Safety-Critical Adaptive Nonlinear MPC for Shared Autonomy in Obstacle Avoidance of Legged Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Virginia Tech researchers developed a teleoperator-aware, safety-critical adaptive nonlinear Model Predictive Control (ANMPC) framework for shared autonomy in quadrupedal robots performing obstacle avoidance. This system integrates online human intent adaptation using a Boltzmann model and formal Control Barrier Function (CBF) safety guarantees, achieving 100% task success rates and robust obstacle avoidance on a Unitree Go2 robot in human-in-the-loop experiments.

## Problem

- Integrating human teleoperator input with autonomous control for legged robots in cluttered, safety-critical environments while ensuring safety and managing unpredictable human commands.
- Addressing the computational complexity of incorporating nonlinear human intent models and formal safety guarantees within real-time Model Predictive Control (MPC) for high-dimensional, unstable legged systems.
- Developing a unified control framework that accounts for the inherent instability, underactuation, and hybrid dynamics of quadrupedal robots while enabling online adaptation to evolving human behavior.

## Method

- A hierarchical control architecture comprising a high-level Adaptive Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (AN**MPC**) for shared autonomy, a mid-level NMPC for dynamics, and a low-level Whole-Body Controller.
- Incorporation of a noisily rational Boltzmann model for human intent, with its parameters adapted online using a Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) algorithm based on observed human commands.
- Embedding Control Barrier Function (CBF) constraints directly into the NMPC formulation to provide formal safety guarantees for forward invariance of safe sets, specifically for obstacle avoidance.

## Results

- Achieved a **100%** success rate in safely reaching designated goals during human-in-the-loop obstacle avoidance tasks on a Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robot.
- Maintained consistently larger safety margins from obstacles compared to baseline fully human control, with the high-level ANMPC demonstrating an average solve time of 27 ms for real-time operation.
- The framework successfully approximated and adapted to human operator behavior for linear velocity commands, enabling the robot to initiate navigation with minimal human intervention for significant durations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The NMPC can efficiently incorporate the nonlinear human action model as an equality constraint, implicitly solving for human actions along the prediction horizon without needing a non-existent closed-form solution.
> - Online adaptation of human intent parameters allows the robot to dynamically learn and respond to the teleoperator's evolving priorities and behaviors in real-time, moving beyond static blending strategies.
> - Integrating CBFs into NMPC successfully extends formal safety guarantees over the prediction horizon for legged robots, enabling robust collision avoidance even with uncertain human inputs.
