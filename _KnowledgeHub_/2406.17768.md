# EXTRACT: Efficient Policy Learning by Extracting Transferable Robot Skills from Offline Data

> [!summary] Summary
> The EXTRACT framework introduces an unsupervised method to learn a discrete set of continuously parameterized robot skills directly from offline image-action data by leveraging pre-trained vision-language models and K-means clustering. This approach enables robots to learn new manipulation tasks up to 10 times more sample efficiently than prior skill-based reinforcement learning methods in simulation and achieves superior performance in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning policies operating on low-level actions struggle with transfer learning, becoming overly task-specific and hindering generalization to new objectives.
- Existing skill-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods often require resource-intensive expert supervision for skill definition or rely on heuristic-based approaches that limit skill adaptability and transferability.
- Robots face challenges in quickly acquiring novel tasks, especially in complex, sparse-reward, and image-based manipulation environments.

## Method

- **Offline Skill Extraction**: Utilizes pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) to compute trajectory-level embedding differences, which are then clustered via K-means to assign discrete, high-level skill labels to action sequences, followed by a median filter for smoothing.
- **Offline Skill Learning**: Trains an autoregressive Variational Autoencoder (==VAE==) to encode variable-length action sequences into continuous latent skill arguments and decode them back into low-level actions, alongside prior networks for discrete skill selection and continuous argument generation.
- **Online Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning**: Employs a hierarchical RL policy that learns to select discrete skills and their continuous arguments, guided by the pre-trained prior networks, to efficiently solve new tasks using an entropy-regularized algorithm like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC).

## Results

- EXTRACT achieved a **10x** improvement in sample efficiency compared to the SPiRL baseline in the Franka Kitchen simulation, reaching comparable performance levels with significantly fewer environment steps.
- Consistently outperformed various baselines (e.g., SPiRL, SAC, Behavior Cloning) across all four LIBERO task suites and a real-world FurnitureBench assembly task, demonstrating higher task success rates and faster learning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that VLM-derived features are critical for extracting semantically meaningful skills, with R3M and CLIP performing well, and that the method is robust to the specific number of clusters (K) chosen for skill extraction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging VLM embedding differences (change in VLM embedding over time) effectively captures high-level behavioral changes and allows for the unsupervised discovery of semantically meaningful robot skills, robust to variations in initial states.
> - Representing skills as discrete entities modulated by continuous arguments simplifies the high-level reinforcement learning problem, allowing the policy to focus on *when* to select a skill and *how* to adapt its execution, rather than learning raw actions.
> - A hierarchical architecture, combining a frozen, pre-trained skill decoder with prior-guided high-level policies, provides a stable and efficient framework for learning complex, temporally extended tasks with sparse rewards.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The majority of contemporary reinforcement learning (RL) methodologies concentrate on developing optimal policies within low-level action spaces. While these methods can achieve performance in their specific training environments, they often encounter challenges when tasked with learning new objectives. This limitation arises because policies trained on low-level actions tend to become highly specific to their initial tasks, hindering generalization and transferability.

Skill-based reinforcement learning (RL) emerges as an alternative paradigm designed to address this challenge. By enabling RL agents to operate over "skills"—defined as useful, temporally extended sequences of actions—rather than atomic low-level actions, the aim is to facilitate more efficient learning of novel tasks. Skills can be transferred across tasks and contribute to more informed exploration, thus enhancing learning effectiveness.

However, existing skill-based RL approaches face their own set of limitations. Some methods necessitate expert supervision to define meaningful skills, a requirement that is resource-intensive and difficult to scale to a wide range of tasks or environments. Other approaches attempt to learn skill spaces from offline data but often rely on heuristics, such as defining skills as randomly sampled action sequences. These heuristic-based definitions can restrict the adaptability and transferability of the learned skills, making them less effective for diverse downstream tasks.

This work positions itself within this landscape by introducing a method, EXTRACT, that seeks to overcome these limitations. It aims to develop a framework for automatically discovering a discrete set of semantically meaningful and continuously parameterized skills directly from offline data, without requiring human supervision. By leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), EXTRACT attempts to extract adaptable skills that can guide efficient transfer learning for new robotic tasks. This contrasts with prior work by offering an unsupervised, data-driven approach to skill definition that is intended to enhance both expressiveness and adaptability compared to purely heuristic or human-defined methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is inspired by human learning, where individuals can rapidly acquire new skills by adapting existing competencies to novel situations. For example, a squash player can learn racquetball more easily than a novice because they already possess foundational skills like serving or returning, requiring only adjustments for the new sport's specific parameters. The paper aims to translate this intuitive process to robotic learning.

The key objectives of the EXTRACT framework are outlined as follows:

- **Address Transfer Learning Challenges in Robotics**: The core problem is the difficulty robots face in quickly learning new tasks, particularly when traditional RL policies are tied to low-level actions and become overly task-specific. This research seeks to accelerate transfer learning by providing robots with a transferable skill repertoire.
- **Unsupervised Skill Extraction**: A central objective is to extract a discrete set of high-level, semantically meaningful skills from an offline dataset of robot trajectories without any human supervision or manual definition. This addresses the scalability and cost issues associated with expert-defined skills in prior work.
- **Parameterized Skill Representation**: The method aims to represent these extracted skills as discrete entities, each modulated by continuous arguments. This parameterization is conceptualized like a programmatic API (e.g., `def forehand(x, y)`), where the robot learns *when* to select a specific skill (the discrete ID) and *how* to modify its execution (the continuous arguments) for a new task.
- **Facilitate Efficient Online Learning**: Once skills are extracted and parameterized, the objective is to leverage this skill-space to guide an RL agent in efficiently learning new, unseen tasks in an online setting. This involves simplifying the action space for the RL policy to act over skill IDs and their arguments, rather than raw low-level actions.
- **Demonstrate Empirical Superiority**: The research aims to empirically demonstrate that EXTRACT can enable faster learning of new tasks compared to existing skill-based RL methods, particularly in challenging, sparse-reward, and image-based robot manipulation environments, in both simulation and real-world settings. This includes achieving significant gains in sample efficiency and overall performance.
- **Leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: A specific technical objective is to utilize the semantic alignment capabilities of pre-trained VLMs to identify and cluster distinct high-level behaviors from raw image-action data, despite VLMs not inherently accounting for variations in object or robot arm starting positions.

By pursuing these objectives, the research intends to offer a more scalable and adaptable approach to skill acquisition and transfer in robotics, moving towards agents that can learn new tasks with greater efficiency given prior experience.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EXTRACT framework is structured into three sequential phases: offline skill extraction, offline skill learning, and online skill-based reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Offline Skill Extraction

This phase focuses on identifying and labeling discrete, high-level skills from an existing offline dataset of image-action trajectories.
- **Feature Extraction**: The method utilizes pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), such as R3M, which are trained to align images with natural language descriptions, resulting in semantically aligned image embeddings. Acknowledging that raw VLM embeddings may not inherently capture high-level robotic behaviors due to variations in object placement or robot arm initial positions, EXTRACT computes *trajectory-level embedding differences*. Specifically, for each timestep `t` in a trajectory, an embedding difference `e_t = VLM(s_t) - VLM(s_1)` is calculated, where `s_1` is the initial observation of that trajectory. This differential embedding is designed to represent the *change* in high-level behavior, such as picking up an object, irrespective of its starting location.
- **Skill Label Assignment**: After computing embedding differences for all timesteps, an unsupervised clustering algorithm, K-means, is applied to these features across the entire dataset. This process assigns a discrete skill label (cluster ID) to each timestep. To mitigate noise and reduce the frequency of short, transient label assignments, a median filter (e.g., `kernel_size=7`) is then applied along the sequence of labels within each trajectory. This smoothing operation groups consecutive actions representing similar behaviors into coherent, temporally extended skill segments. The output of this phase is a skill-labeled dataset (`D_d`), where each segment (`τ_d`) consists of sequential image-action tuples belonging to a single skill `d`.

#### 3.2. Offline Skill Learning

In this phase, a generative model is trained to encode and decode these extracted skills, parameterizing them for downstream use.
- **Generative Skill Model**: EXTRACT builds upon the SPiRL framework but extends it to handle discrete skill choices and variable-length action sequences. It trains an autoregressive Variational Autoencoder (VAE) consisting of:
 - **Skill Argument Encoder `q(z | ¯a, d)`**: A recurrent neural network that maps a variable-length action sequence `¯a` and its assigned discrete skill ID `d` to a continuous latent argument `z`.
 - **Autoregressive Skill Decoder `p_a(¯a | z, d)`**: A recurrent neural network that generates a variable-length sequence of low-level actions `¯a`, conditioned on the continuous latent argument `z` and the discrete skill ID `d`. This decoder also learns to output a continuous value `l` at each timestep, representing the proportion of the skill completed (`t/N`). This `l` value is used during online execution to determine when a skill sequence has concluded.
- **Skill Prior Networks**: To guide online learning, two prior networks are trained:
 - **Discrete Skill Selection Prior `p_d(d | s)`**: Predicts the likelihood of selecting a particular discrete skill `d` given an initial observation `s`.
 - **Continuous Skill Argument Prior `p_z(z | s, d)`**: Predicts appropriate continuous arguments `z` for a given state `s` and a selected discrete skill `d`. These priors are trained using the initial observation `s_1` of sampled skill trajectories.
- **Training Objective**: The VAE and prior networks are trained jointly by maximizing an objective function. This objective comprises the β-VAE loss (action reconstruction and KL regularization for the encoder) and log-likelihood terms for the discrete skill and continuous argument priors. A stop-gradient operator is applied to `z` when computing the prior losses to prevent them from influencing the encoder.
- **Optional Fine-tuning**: For highly challenging transfer scenarios, EXTRACT allows for an optional fine-tuning step. The pre-trained skill learning model can be fine-tuned on a smaller, task-specific demonstration dataset, with skill labels assigned using the same clustering model from the first phase.

#### 3.3. Online Skill-Based Reinforcement Learning

The final phase integrates the learned skill components into an RL framework for new task acquisition.
- **Policy Parameterization**: The pre-trained skill decoder `p_a(¯a | z, d)` is treated as a frozen, lower-level policy. A higher-level skill-based policy `π(d, z | s)` is trained to select a discrete skill `d` and its continuous argument `z` based on the current state `s`. This policy is factorized into two components: `π_d(d | s)` (for discrete skill selection) and `π_z(z | s, d)` (for continuous argument selection). The complete policy that outputs low-level actions is `π(a | s) = p_a(¯a | z, d) · π_d(d | s) · π_z(z | s, d)`.
- **Policy Learning**: An entropy-regularized RL algorithm, such as Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) or RLPD, is employed for online training. The objective for the skill-based policy `π_d, π_z` is to maximize the expected Q-value while being regularized by KL divergence terms against the pre-trained skill priors `p_d(d | s)` and `p_z(z | s, d)`. The critic network `Q(s, z, d)` is also modified to account for these regularization terms. The high-level policies `π_d` and `π_z` are initialized from their respective prior networks `p_d` and `p_z`. This hierarchical architecture, with a frozen lower-level decoder and prior-guided high-level policies, is designed for training stability.
- **Skill Execution**: During online rollouts, the selected skill `(d, z)` is decoded into an action sequence by `p_a(¯a | z, d)`. The execution of this skill continues until the learned progress variable `l` reaches 1, or until a predefined maximum skill length is reached, at which point a new skill is sampled.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EXTRACT investigated its capacity to discover meaningful skills and subsequently aid robots in learning new tasks across various challenging manipulation environments.

#### 4.1. Offline Skill Extraction

- **Qualitative Skill Alignment**: Visualizations using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on K-means clustered VLM embedding differences in the Franka Kitchen environment (with K=8 clusters) demonstrated that EXTRACT produces distinctly separable clusters. Examples included identifiable clusters for "cabinet opening" or "twisting/grabbing knob" behaviors. Similar clarity was observed in LIBERO skill cluster visualizations.
- **Quantitative Skill Statistics**: Analysis of skill length distributions in Franka Kitchen, LIBERO, and FurnitureBench revealed that EXTRACT extracts skills of variable lengths, with average lengths around 30 timesteps across environments. This indicated clear differentiation among skills based on their temporal extent and behavioral characteristics.

#### 4.2. Online Reinforcement Learning of New Tasks

- **Simulation Environments (Franka Kitchen & LIBERO)**:
 - In the Franka Kitchen environment, EXTRACT achieved performance comparable to an oracle baseline (RAPS), which uses human-designed ground truth skills. Critically, EXTRACT demonstrated a 10x improvement in sample efficiency compared to SPiRL, an existing skill-based RL method, reaching similar performance levels with significantly fewer environment steps.
 - Across all four LIBERO task suites (Object, Spatial, Goal, 10), EXTRACT consistently exhibited superior performance in terms of either sample efficiency or final task success rate. Its advantages were particularly pronounced in LIBERO-10, which features longer-horizon tasks.
 - Baselines such as standard Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Behavior Cloning (BC) performed poorly across these tasks, underscoring the necessity of skill-based RL and pre-training for these complex, sparse-reward scenarios.
 - The EXTRACT-UVD baseline, which used Universal Visual Decomposer (UVD)'s skill segmentation mechanism, generally performed worse and showed instability in Franka Kitchen. This was attributed to less distinguishable skill embeddings compared to EXTRACT's clustering approach.
- **Real-World Environment (FurnitureBench)**:
 - On the real-world FurnitureBench one-leg assembly task, EXTRACT demonstrated effective skill transfer. It outperformed SPiRL both before and after 100 episodes of real-world RL fine-tuning, achieving a higher average number of completed subtasks.
- **Performance Rationale**: The superior performance of EXTRACT was attributed to its ability to create a semantically aligned and structured skill-space, separating discrete skill choices from their continuous arguments. This structure simplifies the RL problem by allowing the policy to learn *when* to select a high-level skill (e.g., a "drawer-opening" skill) rather than memorizing distinct continuous actions for every specific instance of that behavior. Additionally, EXTRACT's extraction of longer average skills (approximately 25 timesteps, compared to SPiRL's 10) effectively shortens the task horizon for the high-level policy, which can improve the efficiency of temporal-difference learning.

#### 4.3. EXTRACT RL Ablation Studies

- **VLM Feature Selection**: Ablations in Franka Kitchen compared different feature sources for clustering. EXTRACT using R3M VLM-extracted features performed optimally. Using raw robot action differences ("Action") or ground truth state differences ("State") resulted in lower performance. Furthermore, EXTRACT with CLIP VLM embeddings performed on par with R3M, indicating robustness to the specific choice of VLM, while proprioceptive state differences were less effective. These results suggest that VLM-derived features are more effective for extracting semantically meaningful high-level skills.
- **Number of Clusters (K)**: Experiments varying the number of K-means clusters (K=3, 5, 8, 15) in Franka Kitchen showed that EXTRACT's performance remained relatively stable across a range of K values, with a notable drop only at K=15. This indicates the method's robustness to the specific number of discovered discrete skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EXTRACT framework contributes to the field of robotic reinforcement learning by addressing the challenge of efficiently acquiring new tasks through skill transfer. The primary significance of this work lies in its ability to unsupervisedly extract a discrete set of semantically meaningful and continuously parameterized robot skills from offline data. This approach moves beyond prior skill-based RL methods that often require costly human supervision or rely on restrictive, heuristic-based skill definitions, thereby enhancing scalability and adaptability.

The empirical results demonstrate that EXTRACT enables robots to learn new, complex manipulation tasks more quickly and with greater sample efficiency compared to established baselines, including other skill-based RL methods, behavior cloning, and purely online RL. This performance advantage was observed across multiple sparse-reward, image-based robotic manipulation domains in both simulation (Franka Kitchen, LIBERO) and real-world settings (FurnitureBench). The structured representation of skills—as discrete choices with continuous arguments—simplifies the downstream reinforcement learning problem, allowing agents to focus on high-level task planning rather than low-level motor control.

The potential impact of EXTRACT is multi-faceted:
- **Accelerated Robot Learning**: By providing robots with a transferable library of skills, EXTRACT could significantly reduce the time and data required for robots to master new tasks, making them more versatile and adaptable in dynamic environments.
- **Reduced Human Reliance**: The unsupervised nature of skill extraction lessens the reliance on human experts for defining and annotating skills, which is a major bottleneck in current robot learning pipelines.
- **Foundation for Generalization**: The semantic alignment and adaptability of the extracted skills contribute to the development of more generalizable robotic agents that can leverage prior experience effectively across a broader range of tasks and environments.
- **Advancement in Hierarchical RL**: This work offers a practical framework for hierarchical reinforcement learning, demonstrating how high-level policies can effectively coordinate a library of lower-level, temporally extended skills.

However, the research also acknowledges certain limitations. EXTRACT currently requires an initial offline dataset from environments that are sufficiently similar to the target task environment to extract relevant skills. Future work could explore methods to extend skill learning to data from significantly different robots or environments, potentially leveraging techniques like sim-to-real transfer. Additionally, while EXTRACT improves sample efficiency, it still necessitates a notable amount of online interaction for new task learning. Integrating EXTRACT with offline reinforcement learning methods could further reduce online sample requirements. Finally, the reliance on Vision-Language Models implies a dependency on image observations; exploring skill learning from diverse input modalities represents another avenue for future research.
