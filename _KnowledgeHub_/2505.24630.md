# Reasoning Models Hallucinate More: Factuality-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at National University of Singapore developed Factuality-aware Step-wise Policy Optimization (FSPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that significantly reduces hallucinations in large reasoning models by incorporating step-wise factuality verification. FSPO achieved up to 83.0% accuracy on HaluEval-QA and 89.5% on GSM8K, outperforming baseline and other reasoning-focused models.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate factually incorrect information, or "hallucinations," despite their advanced reasoning capabilities.
- Outcome-driven Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning, while improving reasoning abilities, paradoxically exacerbates factual hallucinations, particularly in intermediate reasoning steps.
- Current RL training paradigms for reasoning tasks primarily focus on final answer correctness, neglecting the factual reliability of the step-by-step reasoning process.

## Method

- Proposed ==Factuality-aware Step-wise== Policy Optimization (**FSPO**), an RL fine-tuning algorithm designed to explicitly incorporate factuality verification at each reasoning step.
- Designed a composite reward function that combines a standard binary reward for final answer correctness with a dense, step-wise factuality reward generated by an automated verifier.
- Introduced a novel factuality-aware advantage adjustment mechanism that dynamically modifies token-level advantages based on the factual correctness of individual reasoning sentences, guiding the model towards factually sound reasoning paths.

## Results

- FSPO significantly reduced hallucinations, achieving **83.0%** accuracy on HaluEval-QA for Qwen-Base, surpassing its base model (**48.0%**) and strong API-based models like GPT-4o (**62.6%**).
- The method simultaneously enhanced mathematical reasoning, boosting Qwen-Base's GSM8K accuracy from **65.2%** to **89.5%** and MATH-500 from **35.7%** to **75.5%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the token-level factuality-aware advantage adjustment is essential for FSPO's superior performance in both hallucination reduction and reasoning enhancement, outperforming variants using only average factuality rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-driven binary rewards in RL for reasoning tasks lead to high-variance gradients, unstable training, and create spurious local optima where models can become confidently incorrect without corrective feedback.
> - The necessity for models to maintain high entropy for exploration in early RL training stages inherently increases the risk of generating hallucinated outputs.
> - Providing dense, fine-grained factuality feedback at each intermediate reasoning step, rather than solely relying on sparse final answer rewards, is critical for mitigating hallucinations and improving overall reasoning reliability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress, particularly in complex reasoning tasks, driven by advancements in training paradigms such as fine-tuning with long chain-of-thought (CoT) solutions and Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based optimization. State-of-the-art models now demonstrate impressive capabilities in mathematics, multi-hop question answering, and coding, often by decomposing problems into iterative reasoning steps.

However, alongside these advancements, a critical and pervasive challenge has emerged: the problem of "hallucination," where LLMs generate factually incorrect or fabricated information. This issue has been extensively studied, with prior research focusing on detection methods, mitigation strategies during pre-training (e.g., data curation), and fine-tuning approaches like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF).

This paper identifies a crucial, yet underexplored, aspect of the hallucination problem: the exacerbation of hallucinations in reasoning models *specifically due to outcome-driven RL fine-tuning*. While RL has been celebrated for enhancing reasoning abilities, this work empirically observes that such optimization paradoxically increases the prevalence of factually incorrect intermediate reasoning steps, even if the final answer might occasionally be correct. This finding highlights a significant gap in the current research landscape: most RL-based efforts for reasoning prioritize final answer correctness, overlooking the factuality of the step-by-step rationale. The paper posits itself as an "early effort focusing on analyzing and mitigating hallucinations in large reasoning models" within this specific context, thereby bridging the divide between improving reasoning capabilities and ensuring factual reliability. It shifts the focus from merely achieving correct answers to ensuring the correctness and trustworthiness of the *entire reasoning process*.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from an empirical observation: while Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved impressive reasoning capabilities through Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization, this very process seems to critically increase the prevalence of hallucinations. The authors find that these hallucinations often appear as "incorrect intermediate reasoning" – unsupported or false claims within the chain-of-thought – undermining the reliability and interpretability of RL-trained reasoning models.

Given this critical drawback, the key objectives of this work are threefold:

1. **To theoretically analyze the RL training dynamics and identify the root causes of increased hallucinations:** The authors aim to understand *why* outcome-driven RL fine-tuning exacerbates hallucinations. This involves a rigorous theoretical investigation into the mechanisms that lead to unstable training, forced exploration, and convergence to factually incorrect states.
2. **To propose an innovative RL fine-tuning algorithm that explicitly incorporates factuality verification at each reasoning step:** Building upon their theoretical insights, the authors seek to design a novel method that provides denser and more accurate feedback during the training process, specifically targeting the factual correctness of intermediate reasoning steps, not just the final answer.
3. **To empirically demonstrate that the proposed method effectively reduces hallucinations while simultaneously enhancing reasoning accuracy:** The ultimate goal is to validate the effectiveness of their new algorithm across various benchmarks, showcasing its ability to improve both the reliability (reduced hallucinations) and performance (enhanced reasoning) of LLMs.

In essence, the paper aims to diagnose a critical problem in advanced LLM training and offer a practical, theoretically grounded solution to build more trustworthy and reliable reasoning models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a comprehensive approach that combines empirical observations, theoretical analysis, and a novel algorithmic solution, followed by extensive experimental validation.

**Preliminary Experiments:**
The authors first conduct preliminary experiments to empirically validate their hypothesis that RL training increases hallucinations. They compare several groups of LLMs, including DeepSeek-V3 vs. DeepSeek-R1, Qwen2.5 vs. QwQ, and various R1-Distill models against their base versions. These models are evaluated on hallucination benchmarks such as TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and HalluQA. The results consistently show that models undergoing large-scale RL or long CoT training exhibit dramatically higher hallucination rates. A manual analysis of 200 samples from HaluEval-QA further reveals that most errors (71.5%) originate from "incorrect reasoning steps," reinforcing the focus on intermediate factuality.

**Theoretical Analysis (Section 4):**
To understand the mechanisms behind RL-induced hallucinations, the paper presents a theoretical analysis of binary reward RL dynamics for reasoning tasks. It identifies three key factors:
1. **High-Variance Gradient (Theorem 4.1):** When rewards are binary (1/0) and correct answers are rare, the policy gradient estimator has extremely high variance. This leads to unstable training updates, as the model primarily receives zero feedback, with occasional large, noisy signals.
2. **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off and Hallucination Risk (Theorem 4.2):** To reliably discover rewarding outputs, especially in early stages, the policy must maintain a sufficiently high entropy (i.e., explore broadly). This necessity for exploration, however, inherently increases the risk of generating hallucinated or incorrect outputs.
3. **Spurious Local Optima and Incorrect Convergence (Theorem 4.3):** Binary reward functions create "spurious local optima." A policy that confidently outputs a specific wrong answer receives zero reward and, critically, zero gradient feedback. This means the model can get stuck, being confidently wrong without any incentive to correct itself.

**Proposed Approach: Factuality-aware Step-wise Policy Optimization (FSPO):**
To address these theoretical and empirical issues, the authors propose FSPO, a novel RL fine-tuning algorithm.

- **Reward Function Design:**
 - **Step-wise Factuality Reward (R_factuality):** For knowledge-intensive tasks, an automated LLM-based verifier (HHEM-2.1) checks each reasoning sentence against given evidence (e.g., Wikipedia snippets). Sentences are rewarded +1 (entailed), 0 (neutral), or -1 (contradicts). This provides dense, fine-grained feedback.
 - **Answer Correctness Reward (R_answer):** A standard binary reward (1 for correct final answer, 0 otherwise).
 - **Final Reward (R_final):** A composite reward combining the `R_answer` and the average `R_factuality` across all reasoning steps. This mitigates the sparse signal problem identified in Theorem 4.1.

- **Factuality-Aware Policy Optimization:**
 - FSPO builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which uses a group of sampled outputs to estimate advantages without a separate value model.
 - **Factuality-Aware Advantage Adjustment ($\hat{A}_{i,t}$):** This is the core innovation. Instead of assigning the same advantage (derived from the overall response) to all tokens, FSPO adjusts the advantage of each individual token $o_{i,t}$ within a sentence $z_j$ based on its step-wise factuality reward $R_{factuality}(z_j)$.
 - If the overall advantage ($A_i$) is positive, factually correct tokens are further reinforced, while factually incorrect tokens are penalized (their advantage is inverted).
 - If the overall advantage ($A_i$) is negative, factually incorrect tokens are further penalized, and surprisingly, factually correct tokens are inversely penalized (their advantage is also inverted to push away from this undesirable (negative advantage) overall trajectory, even if they were locally good).
 - This dynamic adjustment ensures that even if the final answer is incorrect, the model receives non-zero gradient signals for factually correct or incorrect intermediate steps, enabling it to escape spurious local optima (Theorem 4.3).

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted using Qwen2.5-7B-Base/Instruct and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct as backbone models. The training dataset is a mixture of a challenging subset of HotpotQA (2K examples for knowledge-intensive QA) and SimpleRL (8K examples for mathematical problems). Evaluation is performed on three hallucination benchmarks (TruthfulQA, HaluEval-QA, HalluQA) and four mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, AIME 2024, AIME 2025). The HHEM-2.1 model is used for step-wise factuality verification. The models are trained on 4xH100 80G GPU cards for approximately 24 hours.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the significant effectiveness of Factuality-aware Step-wise Policy Optimization (FSPO) in simultaneously reducing hallucinations and enhancing reasoning capabilities in LLMs.

#### 4.1. Impact of RL on Hallucination (Preliminary Experiments)

The initial experiments (Figure 1) confirm the central problem: models fine-tuned with large-scale RL (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, QwQ, R1-Distill models) exhibit dramatically higher hallucination rates across TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and HalluQA benchmarks compared to their non-RL-trained counterparts (e.g., DeepSeek-V3, Qwen2.5-Instruct). A manual analysis of error sources (Figure 2) reveals that 71.5% of hallucinations in RL-trained models stem from "incorrect reasoning steps," directly motivating the step-wise factuality approach.

#### 4.2. Overall Performance of FSPO (Table 1)

- **Hallucination Reduction:** FSPO consistently achieves superior performance in mitigating hallucinations. For instance, FSPO (Qwen-Base) significantly boosts HaluEval-QA accuracy to 83.0%, far surpassing its base model (48.0%) and all other open-source and reasoning models, and even outperforming strong API-based models like DeepSeek-V3 (70.6%) and GPT-4o (62.6%). Similar improvements are observed on TruthfulQA and HalluQA.
- **Reasoning Enhancement:** FSPO also substantially improves mathematical reasoning. FSPO (Qwen-Base) increases GSM8K accuracy from 65.2% to 89.5% and MATH-500 from 35.7% to 75.5%. These scores are competitive with, and often surpass, other reasoning-focused open-source models (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-7B: GSM8K 84.3%). While still trailing larger API-based models on highly advanced AIME tasks, FSPO demonstrates robust mathematical reasoning for its model size. This indicates that FSPO successfully enhances reasoning without compromising factuality.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Ablation Analysis (Figure 4):** This study validates the core components of FSPO.
 - `GRPO` (using only answer reward) shows limited improvement.
 - `GRPO w/ factuality` (incorporating both answer and average factuality reward) performs better than vanilla GRPO.
 - `FSPO` (with the proposed factuality-aware advantage adjustment) consistently outperforms both variants, demonstrating the critical role of token-level factuality-aware advantage adjustment in achieving superior reasoning and hallucination reduction.
- **RL Algorithm Analysis (Figure 5):** FSPO's design proves adaptable beyond GRPO. When integrated with Reinforce++, `FSPO (Reinforce++)` significantly outperforms vanilla `Reinforce++`, closely matching the performance of `FSPO (GRPO)`. This highlights the generalizability and robustness of the factuality-aware approach.
- **Number of Training Samples (Figure 6):** FSPO demonstrates efficiency, achieving significant accuracy improvements on HaluEval-QA with as few as 2K training samples. For MATH-500, performance remains stable between 1K and 4K samples, suggesting that the method can be effective even with relatively small, high-quality factual QA data.
- **Factuality Improvement Analysis (Figure 7):** This analysis directly confirms that FSPO effectively enhances the factual correctness of reasoning steps. While both FSPO and GRPO generate responses of similar lengths, FSPO achieves significantly higher step-wise factuality scores. This indicates that FSPO encourages models to generate more factual intermediate content without degrading the overall quality or length of the generated explanations.

In summary, the results conclusively show that FSPO successfully addresses the core problem of increased hallucinations in RL-trained reasoning models, simultaneously boosting reasoning performance by providing denser, more granular, and factuality-aware feedback during optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) by addressing a critical and previously underexplored challenge: the increased propensity of reasoning-oriented Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning to induce hallucinations.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novel Problem Identification and Diagnosis:** The paper empirically identifies and theoretically explains a fundamental flaw in current RL paradigms for LLMs. By proving that outcome-based binary rewards lead to high-variance gradients, enforce high entropy for exploration (increasing hallucination risk), and create spurious local optima (where models get confidently stuck on wrong answers), it provides a crucial diagnostic framework for understanding LLM unreliability. This theoretical grounding is a key differentiator, moving beyond mere empirical observation.
- **Innovative Solution (FSPO):** The proposed Factuality-aware Step-wise Policy Optimization (FSPO) offers a highly effective and generalizable solution. By integrating explicit step-wise factual verification into the RL process and dynamically adjusting token-level advantages, FSPO provides a dense and informative reward signal. This innovative approach directly tackles the root causes of hallucination identified in the theoretical analysis, steering the model towards not just correct answers but also factually sound reasoning paths.
- **Dual Improvement:** A key significance is FSPO's ability to achieve dual benefits: substantially reducing hallucinations while simultaneously enhancing reasoning accuracy across various complex benchmarks. This demonstrates that improving factuality does not come at the expense of reasoning capabilities but rather complements them, leading to more robust and reliable AI systems.
- **Efficiency and Adaptability:** The method's effectiveness with relatively small training datasets and its adaptability to different RL algorithms (e.g., Reinforce++) underscore its practical viability and broad applicability.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Reliability of LLMs:** The most profound impact of FSPO is its potential to significantly improve the trustworthiness and reliability of LLMs, especially in critical applications where factual accuracy and transparent reasoning are paramount. This includes domains such as:
 - **Education:** Generating accurate explanations and problem-solving steps.
 - **Healthcare:** Providing factually grounded medical information or diagnostic support.
 - **Scientific Research:** Assisting with literature review, hypothesis generation, or data interpretation, where factual integrity is non-negotiable.
 - **Legal and Financial Services:** Ensuring policy models provide verifiable and truthful rationales.
- **Improved Interpretability:** By incentivizing factual correctness at each reasoning step, FSPO inherently leads to more interpretable models. Users can have greater confidence in the intermediate steps of a model's chain-of-thought, making it easier to audit, debug, and understand how an answer was derived.
- **Guidance for Future RL Research:** This work provides clear directions for future research in RL for LLMs, emphasizing the need for more nuanced reward functions that consider process-level quality (like factuality) in addition to outcome-level performance. It encourages the development of more sophisticated feedback mechanisms that move beyond sparse, binary rewards.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge a few limitations. Their theoretical analysis primarily focuses on binary reward modeling, although they suggest it can extend to arbitrary bounded real rewards. Furthermore, computational cost constrained their experiments to 7B/8B models. Future work could involve extending the theoretical analysis to more complex reward schemes and applying FSPO to much larger models to assess its scalability and effectiveness in broader contexts.

#### 5.4. Broader Societal Impacts (from Appendix B)

- **Positive Impacts:** FSPO's ability to reduce factual hallucinations in complex reasoning can bolster the utility of LLMs in critical domains, making them more dependable tools for human users.
- **Negative Impacts/Risks:** While enhancing factuality, the technology could be misused. For example, it might be applied to train highly persuasive but selectively factual systems in areas like political propaganda or misinformation campaigns. In such scenarios, the step-wise coherence fostered by FSPO could mask underlying biases or strategic omissions, making the generated content appear more credible than it is. Addressing these potential misuses will require ongoing ethical considerations and the development of safeguards.
