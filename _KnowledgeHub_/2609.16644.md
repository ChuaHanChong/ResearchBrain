# WholeBodyWAM: Generalizing Pre-trained World-Action Priors to Humanoid Loco-Manipulation via WBC-Grounded Coordination

> [!summary] Summary
> WholeBodyWAM extends a pre-trained 14B video-action diffusion transformer to humanoid loco-manipulation. It adds a separate whole-body control token stream alongside the inherited future-video and manipulation streams. A unified controller interface gives heterogeneous controller commands shared physical meanings, and manipulability-gated attention routes manipulation intent into body commands. It reaches 91.9% simulation success and cuts cross-controller variance by 70%.

## Problem

- World action models are studied almost only on tabletop or arm-centric manipulation, leaving bipedal whole-body loco-manipulation, where body motion must coordinate with hands, largely unexplored.
- Expanding a pre-trained WAM's action head to humanoid joints leaves heterogeneous controller semantics and coordination to be learned implicitly from scarce fine-tuning data, yielding poorly grounded commands.
- Monolithic humanoid-native whole-body models relearn behavior from scratch, entangle policies with one controller's conventions such as SONIC motion latents, and need costly large-scale whole-body demonstrations.

## Method

- ==Structured action factorization==: a shared Diffusion Transformer, initialized from DreamZero's 14B checkpoint and LoRA post-trained on 15K simulated demonstrations, jointly flow-matches future video, inherited manipulation actions, and a distinct whole-body control stream.
- **Unified Whole-Body Controller Interface (UWBC)**: a 56-D command vector with 46 shared slots of fixed physical meaning (base velocities, pelvis pose, lower-body joints) plus 10 controller-specific residual slots, with unsupported fields masked per controller.
- **Coordination-Aware Self-Attention (CASA)**: a logit bias on whole-body-query to manipulation-key attention within each prediction block, gated by the drop in each arm's task-directional manipulability relative to an offline voxelized map.

## Results

- SIMPLE simulation, six tasks: **91.9%** overall success versus Cosmos-3 **86.4%**, Ψ0 **82.2%**, DreamZero-PT **73.6%** (same post-training data), DreamZero **65.0%**, GR00T N1.6 **47.5%**.
- Real Unitree G1, eight tasks versus DreamZero: **81.3%** vs **57.5%** in-distribution, **68.8%** vs **40.0%** out-of-distribution success, with OOD task progress **0.82** vs **0.59**; DoorEntry OOD gap reaches **45** points.
- Across SONIC, AMO and GEAR controllers: **89.2%** mean success and **10.5 pp²** variance versus Cosmos-3's **80.2%** and **35.0**; ablations drop success to **86.9%** without CASA, **84.7%** without UWBC, **80.8%** with a monolithic stream.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained tabletop world-action priors can transfer to humanoids if body commands get their own addressable stream; flattening them into one action stream hurt most in ablation.
> - Structure beats volume: giving the same post-training data to an unstructured baseline recovers only about a third of the gain, so interface design, not extra humanoid data, drives transfer.
> - Canonical physically-meaningful command spaces with residual slots decouple policy intent from controller conventions, stabilizing adaptation to new whole-body controllers; classical kinematic manipulability makes a cheap, interpretable coordination gate.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position Within the Broader Research Landscape

WholeBodyWAM addresses the intersection of three active research areas: world–action models (WAMs), humanoid whole-body control, and general-purpose visuomotor or vision-language-action policies.

World–action models jointly predict future visual observations and robot actions. Instead of treating control as a purely reactive mapping from observations to commands, these models learn relationships between actions and their expected effects on the environment. Recent WAMs such as DreamZero, Fast-WAM, and Cosmos Policy have mainly focused on tabletop, bimanual, or arm-centric manipulation. Their learned priors can support object interaction, but they generally do not model the locomotion, torso adjustment, balance, and lower-body coordination required by humanoid robots.

Humanoid loco-manipulation introduces additional complexity. A robot may need to walk toward an object, reorient its torso, adjust its pelvis, maintain balance, and manipulate an object simultaneously. These behaviors are usually mediated by whole-body controllers (WBCs). However, WBCs expose different command formats and physical meanings. One controller may use joint targets, another may use task-space velocities, and another may require discrete modes or specialized motion latents. Consequently, a policy trained for one controller may not transfer directly to another.

Existing approaches address this problem in two main ways. The first expands a pre-trained manipulation model’s action vector and fine-tunes it on humanoid data. This retains some manipulation knowledge but requires the model to infer WBC semantics and whole-body coordination from limited demonstrations. The second trains a humanoid-specific world–action model from the ground up using large-scale whole-body data. Such models can be controller-compatible, but they require substantial supervision and may become tied to a particular embodiment or controller representation.

WholeBodyWAM proposes an intermediate strategy. It retains the manipulation pathway and world–action backbone of a pre-trained WAM while adding explicit grounding for whole-body control. The paper’s central premise is that existing WAMs already encode useful manipulation priors; the missing capability is a structured mechanism that translates manipulation intent into appropriate whole-body adjustments across different WBCs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The main objective is to generalize pre-trained world–action priors to humanoid loco-manipulation without relearning the entire world–action mapping from scratch.

The authors identify three related technical problems. First, humanoid manipulation requires coordination between arm-level actions and whole-body movement. A hand trajectory that is feasible in isolation may become unreachable because of limited arm manipulability, object placement, or the robot’s current posture. The robot may need to step, rotate, or adjust its torso before completing the manipulation.

Second, heterogeneous WBCs use incompatible command ontologies. Treating all commands as entries in a flat vector obscures whether a value represents a velocity, a joint target, an orientation, or a controller-specific mode. This can make learning less transferable and can produce commands that have different effects under different controllers.

Third, humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations are relatively scarce compared with the large datasets used to pre-train general manipulation WAMs. The proposed method therefore seeks to make more effective use of limited humanoid supervision by preserving the pre-trained manipulation representation and learning only the additional grounding and coordination mechanisms.

The paper evaluates whether this design improves overall task success, performance under out-of-distribution conditions, fine-tuning efficiency, and robustness across heterogeneous WBCs. It also examines whether the individual components—structured action factorization, the Unified Whole-Body Controller interface, and Coordination-Aware Self-Attention—each contribute to performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

WholeBodyWAM models a conditional distribution over three predicted streams:

1. Future visual dynamics, denoted \(v_{t:t+H}\).
2. Manipulation actions, denoted \(m_{t:t+H}\).
3. Whole-body controller commands, denoted \(u_{t:t+H}\).

The conditioning context includes visual history, the current robot state or proprioception, and a language instruction. The model is formulated as a joint world–action predictor rather than as a strictly sequential system in which video is first generated and actions are then produced. Its factorization describes the dependency between future scene dynamics and action generation while the streams are jointly processed.

#### 3.2. Shared Diffusion Transformer

The model is initialized from a pre-trained 14-billion-parameter WAM and retains its video Diffusion Transformer backbone. Visual observations are compressed into latent tokens using a pre-trained Wan video VAE. Language instructions are encoded using a pre-trained T5 text encoder, and a lightweight state encoder converts proprioceptive information into conditioning tokens.

The model extends the original visual and manipulation token sequence with a separate UWBC token stream. At each future time step, visual tokens, manipulation tokens, and whole-body command tokens are jointly processed by the DiT. The manipulation stream preserves the pathway inherited from the pre-trained WAM, while the UWBC stream provides an explicit representation for controller-facing behavior.

Training uses a modality-weighted flow-matching objective. Separate losses are calculated for the visual, manipulation, and UWBC streams, with coefficients used to balance their contributions. This allows the model to learn future visual evolution and both types of action jointly.

#### 3.3. Unified Whole-Body Controller Interface

The Unified Whole-Body Controller (UWBC) interface is designed to provide shared physical semantics across different downstream WBCs. It defines a 56-dimensional command vector.

The first 46 dimensions contain shared fields:

- Desired base linear velocity.
- Desired base angular velocity.
- Desired heading represented by sine and cosine.
- Desired pelvis planar position and height.
- Desired pelvis tilt.
- Desired torso-to-pelvis orientation.
- Lower-body and waist joint positions.
- Lower-body and waist joint velocities.

The remaining ten dimensions are residual slots for controller-specific commands, including discrete control modes.

Each downstream WBC activates the fields that correspond to its capabilities and physical command conventions. Unsupported fields are masked. This design separates the model’s internal whole-body intent from the exact command interface required by a particular controller. During training and evaluation, the semantic fields are mapped to the available commands of SONIC, AMO, or GEAR.

#### 3.4. Coordination-Aware Self-Attention

The paper introduces Coordination-Aware Self-Attention (CASA) to make manipulation-to-whole-body coordination state dependent.

The mechanism estimates whether an arm is losing manipulability in the direction of the task. The task direction is derived from consecutive observed end-effector positions. For each arm, the method calculates directional manipulability using a damped manipulability matrix that incorporates joint speed limits. An offline voxelized map provides a reference capability for the current end-effector position. The coordination gate is based on the relative reduction between the current and reference manipulability.

If either arm exhibits a substantial reduction, the gate increases. This gate adds a bias to self-attention logits only when a UWBC query attends to a manipulation key within the same prediction block. Thus, manipulation information is selectively amplified when the arm’s current configuration suggests that body repositioning or other whole-body assistance may be needed.

#### 3.5. Data, Training, and Evaluation

The authors collect 15,000 whole-body demonstrations in the SIMPLE simulation environment using a PICO 4 Ultra headset and wrist trackers. Demonstrations are converted into SMPL motion sequences and retargeted to the Unitree G1 embodiment using GMR. UWBC supervision is computed from the retargeted robot references according to the predefined physical semantics of each interface field.

The model is post-trained with LoRA using rank 4 and scaling parameter 4. Training runs for 50,000 steps with a learning rate of \(10^{-5}\) and a batch size of one per GPU. Inference uses 16 denoising steps with a second-order UniPC flow-matching sampler.

Simulation experiments use six tasks and three SIMPLE perturbation levels. Real-world experiments use a Unitree G1 humanoid with BrainCo Revo 2 dexterous hands, one head-mounted RealSense D435i camera, and two wrist-mounted cameras. The real-world suite contains eight tasks, including towel placement, basket carrying, teapot pouring, box transfer, cart serving, door entry, table cleanup, and plant watering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Simulation Performance

WholeBodyWAM obtains an overall simulation task success rate of 91.9% with the SONIC controller. This exceeds DreamZero at 65.0%, DreamZero-PT at 73.6%, Cosmos-3 at 86.4%, Ψ0 at 82.2%, and GR00T N1.6 at 47.5%.

The comparison with DreamZero-PT is particularly relevant because both methods receive post-training on the same whole-body dataset. WholeBodyWAM’s higher result indicates that the improvement is not explained solely by adding humanoid demonstrations. The structured action representation, UWBC grounding, and coordination mechanism provide additional benefits.

The improvements are larger on tasks requiring body repositioning. Relative to Cosmos-3, WholeBodyWAM improves success by 10 percentage points on PickBetweenTables and 11.7 points on MoveBendPick. Performance is more similar on Handover and TabletopGrasp, which involve less demanding whole-body adjustment.

#### 4.2. Cross-Controller Robustness

Across SONIC, AMO, and GEAR, WholeBodyWAM achieves a mean task success rate of 89.2% and a cross-controller variance of 10.5 percentage points squared. Cosmos-3 obtains a mean of 80.2% and a variance of 35.0 percentage points squared.

The lower variance indicates that WholeBodyWAM is less sensitive to the choice of WBC. The paper attributes this to UWBC, which establishes a shared semantic representation before controller-specific command mapping. In contrast, baselines initially trained around one controller must learn new command conventions during adaptation, making their performance more variable.

#### 4.3. Fine-Tuning Efficiency

Experiments using 50, 100, 200, and 300 task-specific demonstrations show that WholeBodyWAM has its largest relative advantage when only a small number of demonstrations are available. This result is consistent with the claimed reuse of pre-trained world–action and manipulation priors. The model does not need to learn all humanoid manipulation behavior anew, but instead learns how to ground existing manipulation capabilities into whole-body control.

#### 4.4. Real-World Results

On eight in-distribution tasks, WholeBodyWAM achieves an average success rate of 81.3%, compared with 57.5% for DreamZero. The difference is modest on manipulation-dominant TableCleanup, where the scores are 90% and 85%, respectively. The gap is larger on tasks requiring locomotion and body reconfiguration: WholeBodyWAM improves over DreamZero by 35 percentage points on BasketCarry and DoorEntry, and by 40 points on BoxTransfer.

Under out-of-distribution conditions involving changed object configurations and language instructions, WholeBodyWAM achieves 68.8%, while DreamZero obtains 40.0%. The corresponding in-distribution-to-OOD drop is 12.5 points for WholeBodyWAM and 17.5 points for DreamZero. Mean task progress is 0.82 versus 0.59 under OOD conditions.

One qualitative example involves moving the towel support 10 centimeters farther from the robot. The first grasp attempt fails because the object is outside the initial workspace. The robot then takes a short forward step and rotates or adjusts its torso, enabling a second successful grasp. This recovery behavior was not present in the demonstrations, according to the paper.

#### 4.5. Ablation Results

Removing CASA reduces overall simulation success from 91.9% to 86.9%, with larger losses on coordination-intensive tasks. Replacing UWBC with native SONIC commands lowers performance to 84.7%, indicating that semantic command organization contributes independently of the existence of a whole-body stream. Removing structured action factorization and using a monolithic action stream reduces performance further to 80.8%. This is the largest ablation decrease and supports maintaining a separate manipulation pathway while adding explicit UWBC tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WholeBodyWAM provides evidence that humanoid whole-body control can benefit from adapting pre-trained manipulation WAMs rather than training a complete humanoid world–action model from scratch. Its main conceptual contribution is to treat WBC grounding as a distinct representation and coordination problem.

The UWBC interface may support more portable policies by separating controller-independent physical intent from controller-specific execution details. CASA provides a mechanism for linking manipulation demands to body-level adjustments using a measurable kinematic signal rather than relying entirely on implicit learning.

The reported results suggest potential benefits for data efficiency, out-of-distribution adaptation, and deployment across different WBC architectures. However, the evaluation remains limited to one humanoid embodiment, a defined set of controllers, and a relatively small number of real-world tasks. The method also depends on retargeted demonstrations, controller capability profiles, manipulability maps, and manually specified semantic mappings. These requirements may introduce engineering effort when extending the system to other robots.

Overall, the paper supports a structured transfer strategy in which general manipulation priors are preserved while humanoid-specific coordination and controller grounding are learned explicitly. This approach could contribute to scalable loco-manipulation systems, particularly in settings where whole-body demonstrations and task-specific data are limited.
