# Wan: Open and Advanced Large-Scale Video Generative Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Alibaba Group's Wan team developed a suite of open-source, large-scale video generative models that close the performance gap with commercial solutions and address key limitations of existing open-source models. The models consistently outperform competitors on benchmarks, achieve visual text generation in both Chinese and English, and offer a 1.3B variant that operates efficiently on consumer-grade GPUs with 8.19 GB VRAM.

## Problem

- A persistent performance, capability, and efficiency gap exists between state-of-the-art closed-source and publicly available open-source video generative models.
- Existing open-source solutions often exhibit suboptimal performance, limited applications beyond basic text-to-video, and high computational demands that hinder broader accessibility.
- The inability to accurately generate visual text within videos and to support diverse downstream video creation tasks presented significant limitations for content creators.

## Method

- **Wan** developed a series of open-source, large-scale video generative models built on the ==Diffusion Transformer== paradigm, incorporating ==Flow Matching== for stable training.
- They implemented a multi-stage training pipeline leveraging a novel ==3D causal Spatio-temporal VAE== and a comprehensively curated dataset, including a unique visual text data generation approach.
- The solution integrates sophisticated optimizations for training and inference efficiency, such as ==2D Context Parallelism==, memory optimizations, and quantization, alongside a ==umT5 text encoder== for multilingual support.

## Results

- Wan 14B consistently outperforms leading open-source models and achieves competitive or superior performance against commercial solutions like Sora on internal (Wan-Bench **0.724**) and external (VBench **86.22%**) benchmarks.
- The models are the first to generate visual text accurately in both Chinese and English, supporting **eight** downstream applications including image-to-video, editing, personalization, and real-time generation.
- The 1.3B model demonstrates consumer-grade efficiency, requiring only **8.19 GB** VRAM, making high-quality video generation accessible on consumer GPUs while still achieving a VBench score of **83.96%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, large-scale data curation, including novel visual text data, is paramount for training robust video generative models.
> - A custom 3D causal VAE and specific text encoder choices (umT5) contribute to efficient and high-fidelity spatio-temporal compression and text-video alignment.
> - Advanced training and inference optimizations, such as 2D Context Parallelism, activation offloading, and diffusion caching, are critical for scaling video diffusion models and enhancing accessibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large-scale video generative models has seen explosive growth, particularly since the introduction of OpenAI's Sora in early 2024. This work positions "Wan" directly at the forefront of this rapidly evolving landscape, aiming to narrow the persistent gap between state-of-the-art closed-source models and publicly available open-source alternatives.

**Diffusion-based Frameworks:** The paper explicitly states that Wan is "Built upon the mainstream diffusion transformer paradigm," combining the successes of Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and Flow Matching. This places it firmly within the dominant architectural trend for high-quality image and video generation, a paradigm proven effective by models like Sora, Stable Diffusion, and PixArt.

**Comparison with Closed-Source Models:** The paper acknowledges the "significant leap" made by models like Sora (OpenAI, 2024), Kling (Kuaishou, 2024.06), Luma 1.0 (LumaLabs, 2024.06), Runway Gen-3 (Runway, 2024.06), Vidu (Bao et al., 2024), Hailuo Video (MiniMax, 2024.09), and Veo 2 (DeepMind, 2024.12). Wan directly claims "competitive or even superior performance over these commercial models across both internal and external benchmarks," aiming to meet or exceed the capabilities previously exclusive to these proprietary systems.

**Comparison with Open-Source Models:** The authors recognize the valuable contributions of the open-source community, citing projects such as HunyuanVideo (Kong et al., 2024), Mochi (GenmoTeam, 2024), and CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2025b). Wan aims to surpass these existing open-source models, which, despite their advancements, still face "Suboptimal Performance," "Limited Capabilities" (often confined to general text-to-video), and "Insufficient Efficiency." By open-sourcing a powerful and efficient model, Wan seeks to "significantly expand the creative possibilities of video production" and provide academia with "high-quality video foundation models," thereby accelerating community growth.

**Addressing Key Gaps:** The paper identifies three critical gaps Wan seeks to address:
1. **Performance Gap:** Commercial models often outperform open-source counterparts. Wan strives to close this by demonstrating "clear and significant performance superiority."
2. **Capability Gap:** Most foundational models are limited to basic text-to-video. Wan extends to "multiple downstream applications, including image-to-video, instruction-guided video editing, and personal video generation, encompassing up to eight tasks." It also pioneers "visual text in both Chinese and English."
3. **Efficiency Gap:** Large models are computationally intensive, hindering accessibility. Wan introduces a 1.3B model designed for "consumer-grade GPUs," balancing performance and resource efficiency.

In essence, Wan positions itself as a comprehensive, high-performance, and accessible open-source alternative that challenges the dominance of closed-source solutions while significantly advancing the capabilities and efficiency of video generation for both research and industry.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of Wan are clearly articulated in the paper's introduction and abstract, driven by the identified limitations of existing video generative models:

1. **Advance State-of-the-Art Performance:** The primary objective is to "push the boundaries of video generation" by achieving "leading performance" that consistently "outperforms the existing open-source models as well as state-of-the-art commercial solutions." This reflects an ambitious goal to set a new benchmark for quality, fidelity, motion dynamics, and prompt adherence.

2. **Enhance Model Comprehensiveness and Versatility:** Moving beyond general text-to-video (T2V) tasks, Wan aims to offer a broad spectrum of capabilities. This includes supporting "multiple downstream applications" such as image-to-video (I2V), instruction-guided video editing (V2V), personalized video generation, camera motion control, real-time generation, and even synchronized audio generation. A specific motivation is to be "the first model that can generate visual text in both Chinese and English," addressing a critical practical need.

3. **Improve Efficiency and Accessibility:** A significant motivation is to make high-quality video generation more accessible. This is tackled by developing models that demonstrate "consumer-grade efficiency," notably the 1.3B model requiring "only 8.19 GB VRAM," making it compatible with a wider range of consumer GPUs. This addresses the "impracticality for creative teams with limited computational resources."

4. **Foster Open-Source Community Growth:** A central objective is to contribute significantly to the open-source community. By "open-sourc[ing] the entire series of Wan, including source code and all models," the authors aim to "foster the growth of the video generation community," "significantly expand the creative possibilities of video production in the industry," and "provide academia with high-quality video foundation models." This commitment includes sharing details on data construction, training strategies, and evaluation algorithms.

5. **Share Insights into Large Model Training:** The paper also aims to provide "detailed results and insights" from the "resource-intensive training of large generative models," believing these findings will "significantly advance video generation technology." This includes offering comprehensive design details and experimental analyses.

In summary, Wan is motivated by the desire to build a superior, more versatile, efficient, and openly accessible suite of video foundation models, thereby democratizing advanced video content creation and accelerating the overall progress of the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Wan's development leverages a multi-faceted approach, combining architectural innovations, large-scale data engineering, and sophisticated training and inference optimizations.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture

At its core, Wan adopts the **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** paradigm, known for its scalability and performance in visual generation. This is combined with **Flow Matching**, a theoretically grounded framework for learning continuous-time generative processes, enabling stable training. The architecture consistently employs cross-attention for text conditioning and meticulously optimizes for computational efficiency and controllability. A **full spatio-temporal attention mechanism** is integrated to capture complex dynamics.

#### 3.2. Data Processing Pipeline

High-quality, diverse, and large-scale data is paramount. The pipeline involves:
- **Pre-training Data Curation:** A four-step cleaning process applied to billions of videos and images from internal and public sources:
 - **Fundamental Dimensions:** Filters based on text detection, aesthetic evaluation (using LAION-5B classifier), NSFW scores, watermark/logo detection, black border detection, overexposure detection, synthetic image detection, blur detection, and duration/resolution constraints. This stage eliminated ~50% of initial data.
 - **Visual Quality:** Selects high-quality data through clustering (100 clusters) and manual/expert model scoring to ensure diverse distribution.
 - **Motion Quality:** Classifies videos into six tiers (optimal, medium, static, camera-driven, low-quality, shaky) to prioritize natural and significant motion.
 - **Visual Text Data:** Novel approach combining synthesized text-containing images (rendering Chinese characters) and real-world images/videos (OCR-extracted Chinese/English text fed to Qwen2-VL for natural descriptions). This enhances visual text generation accuracy and realism.
- **Post-training Data Refinement:** Focuses on improving visual fidelity and motion dynamics using high-quality image and video subsets, emphasizing category balance and diversity.
- **Dense Video Captioning:** An internal LLaVA-style caption model (ViT encoder + Qwen LLM) is developed to generate highly descriptive captions. It's trained on open-source vision-language datasets and extensive in-house datasets covering specific aspects like celebrities, object counting, OCR, camera angle/motion, fine-grained categories, relational understanding, re-captioning, editing instructions, and human-annotated captions. This aims to improve prompt following.

#### 3.3. Model Design and Training

- **Spatio-temporal Variational Autoencoder (Wan-VAE):** A novel 3D causal VAE that compresses spatio-temporal dimensions (4x8x8) efficiently.
 - **Design:** Replaces GroupNorm with RMSNorm, halves input feature channels in spatial upsampling for memory reduction, and achieves a compact 127M parameters.
 - **Training:** Three stages: 2D image VAE training, inflation to 3D causal Wan-VAE with low-res/small frame videos, and fine-tuning on high-quality videos with GAN loss.
 - **Efficient Inference:** Implements a feature cache mechanism within causal convolution to support arbitrarily long videos chunk-wise, optimizing memory and ensuring temporal continuity.
- **Video Diffusion Transformer:** Comprises a patchifying module, transformer blocks, and an unpatchifying module.
 - **Transformer Blocks:** Utilize cross-attention for text conditions and a shared MLP for time embeddings (reducing parameters by ~25% and improving performance).
- **Text Encoder:** **umT5** (Chung et al., 2023) is chosen for its strong multilingual capabilities (Chinese/English), superior composition, and faster convergence compared to other LLM-based encoders.
- **Training Objective:** Leverages the **flow matching framework** (Rectified Flows) with MSE loss to predict velocity (x1 - x0).
- **Multi-stage Training:**
 - **Image Pre-training:** Initializes 14B model with low-resolution (256px) text-to-image pre-training to establish cross-modal alignment and geometric fidelity.
 - **Image-Video Joint Training:** Progressive resolution scaling through three stages (256px images/192px videos, 480px, 720px) to balance throughput and memory.
 - **Post-training:** Fine-tunes on higher-quality, post-training datasets at 480px and 720px resolutions.

#### 3.4. Model Scaling and Training/Inference Efficiency

- **Workload Analysis:** Identifies attention mechanism as the primary computational bottleneck due to quadratic scaling with sequence length.
- **Parallelism Strategy:** For DiT, uses **Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP)** for parameter sharding and a novel **2D Context Parallelism** (combining Ulysses and Ring Attention) for sequence length sharding, minimizing communication overhead. Distributed strategy switching optimizes resource usage for VAE/Text Encoder.
- **Memory Optimization:** Prioritizes **activation offloading** combined with **gradient checkpointing** to reduce GPU memory footprint in long sequence scenarios.
- **Inference Optimization:**
 - **Parallel Strategy:** Employs FSDP and 2D Context Parallelism for near-linear speedup.
 - **Diffusion Cache:** Leverages attention and CFG similarities across sampling steps to reduce computational workload (1.62x speedup for 14B T2V).
 - **Quantization:** Applies FP8 GEMM for linear layers (1.13x speedup) and optimized 8-Bit FlashAttention (hybrid Int8/FP8, FP32 accumulation, block size tuning) for attention layers (1.27x speedup) to enhance efficiency without significant quality loss.
- **Prompt Alignment:** Enhances model inference by:
 - Augmenting each video with multiple captions (diverse styles/lengths) during training.
 - Rewriting concise user prompts into detailed, training-aligned descriptions using an LLM (Qwen2.5-Plus) to improve generation quality.

#### 3.5. Extended Applications

Wan extends its capabilities to numerous downstream tasks, often by adapting the core DiT architecture with specific conditioning mechanisms:
- **Image-to-Video (Wan-I2V):** Concatenates condition image latent (first frame) and a binary mask with noise latent. Uses CLIP image encoder and decoupled cross-attention for global context. Unified framework for I2V, video continuation, and first-last frame transformation.
- **Unified Video Editing (VACE):** A framework unifying various editing and controllable generation tasks. Introduces a **Video Condition Unit (VCU)** to integrate images, videos, and masks as inputs. Employs a **concept decoupling strategy** (reactive vs. inactive frames) and offers two training modes: full fine-tuning or pluggable **Context Adapter Tuning**.
- **Text-to-Image Generation:** Benefits from joint training on large image and video datasets, allowing cross-modal knowledge transfer.
- **Video Personalization:** Directly conditions on input face images in the latent space of Wan-VAE. Extends input video with segmented face images and uses an inpainting fashion. Data synthesized using Instant-ID to improve diversity.
- **Camera Motion Controllability:** Injects camera trajectory conditions using a camera pose encoder (Plücker coordinates, convolutional modules) and a camera pose adapter (adaptive normalization layers).
- **Real-time Video Generation:** Transforms the fixed-length DiT into a streaming model using **Streamer** (sliding temporal window, denoising queue) for infinite-length video generation. Further accelerated by **Consistency Model Distillation** (integrating LCM/VideoLCM for few-step inference) and **quantization** (int8 for attention/linear, TensorRT for overall model) for consumer-grade GPU deployment.
- **Audio Generation (V2A):** Produces synchronized ambient sound and background music for video clips. Uses a **1D-VAE** for raw waveform compression, CLIP for visual embeddings, and umT5 for textual prompts. Employs a random masking strategy for audio captions during training to enhance visual cue learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for Wan's capabilities across multiple dimensions:

1. **Leading Performance:**
 - **Benchmarks:** The 14B model of Wan "consistently outperforms the existing open-source models as well as state-of-the-art commercial solutions" across internal (Wan-Bench) and external (VBench) benchmarks.
 - **Wan-Bench:** Wan 14B achieves a weighted score of 0.724, surpassing competitors like Sora (0.700), Hunyuan (0.673), and Mochi (0.639) (Table 2). It particularly excels in "Physical Plausibility" and "Smoothness."
 - **VBench Leaderboard:** Wan 14B achieves a benchmark-leading aggregate score of 86.22% (86.67% visual quality, 84.44% semantic consistency), outperforming Sora (84.28%) and MiniMax-Video-01 (83.41%) (Table 4).
 - **Human Evaluation (T2V):** Wan 14B demonstrates superior performance across "visual quality," "motion quality," "matching," and "overall ranking" in pairwise comparisons against commercial models like CN-TopA/B/C and Runway (Table 3).
 - **Visual Text Generation:** Wan is the "first model that can generate visual text in both Chinese and English" with high realism (Figure 2, Figure 15).

2. **Comprehensiveness:**
 - Wan offers two capable models (1.3B and 14B parameters) for efficiency and effectiveness respectively.
 - It covers up to eight downstream applications, including Image-to-Video, Unified Video Editing, Text-to-Image, Video Personalization, Camera Motion Controllability, Real-time Video Generation, and Audio Generation, with qualitative results demonstrating strong performance across these tasks (Figures 19-24, 27, 29, 30, 31, 33).

3. **Consumer-Grade Efficiency:**
 - The 1.3B model requires "only 8.19 GB VRAM," making it highly compatible with consumer-grade GPUs.
 - Despite its smaller size, it "exhibits superior performance compared to larger open-source models" (VBench score of 83.96%, Table 4).
 - Real-time generation on a single RTX 4090 GPU (consumer-grade) at 20 FPS is demonstrated through quantization (Figure 31).

4. **Openness:**
 - The paper commits to open-sourcing the entire series of Wan, including source code and all models, demonstrating a strong commitment to fostering community growth.

5. **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Adaptive Normalization Layers:** Shared AdaLN design (Full-shared-adaLN-1.5B extended) is more effective than non-shared AdaLN or half-shared designs, suggesting that increasing model depth is more beneficial than increasing parameters in AdaLN layers (Figure 16).
 - **Text Encoder:** umT5 demonstrates superior text embedding performance and convergence compared to other powerful LLM-based encoders like Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and GLM-4-9B, even with adapter layers (Figure 17, Table 6).
 - **Autoencoder:** The proposed Wan-VAE consistently achieves lower FID scores (better image quality) compared to a VAE-D variant using diffusion loss for reconstruction (Table 5).

6. **Quantitative Evaluation of Sub-Modules/Applications:**
 - **Wan-VAE:** Exhibits competitive PSNR and 2.5x faster reconstruction speed than HunYuan Video, showcasing superior video quality and processing efficiency (Figure 7).
 - **I2V Model:** Performs favorably across visual quality, motion quality, and matching in human evaluation against other I2V models (Table 7).
 - **Video Personalization:** Achieves competitive Arcface Similarity scores compared to other leading commercial competitors (Table 8).
 - **Audio Generation:** Qualitative results show superiority in long-term consistency, cleaner output, and rhythmic sound patterns compared to MMAudio (Figure 33).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and open-sourcing of Wan represent a significant advancement in the field of video generation, with potential impacts across research, industry, and creative domains.

**1. Advancing the State-of-the-Art:** By consistently outperforming existing open-source and competitive commercial models, Wan sets a new benchmark for video generation quality, motion fidelity, instruction following, and visual text integration. This pushes the technical frontier, demonstrating that high-quality video generation is no longer exclusive to closed-source, heavily resourced environments.

**2. Democratizing Access to Advanced Video Generation:** The introduction of the 1.3B model, requiring only 8.19 GB VRAM, is a crucial step towards making advanced video generation accessible to a much broader audience, including individual creators, small studios, and researchers with limited computational resources. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for experimentation and content creation, fostering a more diverse ecosystem of innovation.

**3. Catalyzing Open-Source Community Growth:** The commitment to open-sourcing the entire Wan suite (code and models) is perhaps its most profound impact. This provides researchers and developers with a robust, high-performance foundation model to build upon, accelerating academic research, enabling specialized domain adaptations, and fostering collaborative development. It can lead to a virtuous cycle of improvement within the open-source community, pushing the entire field forward.

**4. Expanding Creative Possibilities and Practical Utility:** Wan's comprehensiveness across multiple downstream tasks (I2V, editing, personalization, camera control, real-time, audio) unlocks vast creative possibilities. Content creators can leverage a single, powerful model for diverse video production needs, from animating static images and personalizing characters to complex video editing and generating synchronized soundtracks. The ability to generate Chinese and English text within videos directly addresses a critical need for localized content.

**5. Insights into Large Model Training:** The paper's detailed exposition of its data processing pipeline, training strategies, and optimization techniques (e.g., spatio-temporal VAE, 2D Context Parallelism, diffusion caching, quantization) offers invaluable insights for the broader AI community. This transparency can guide future research into efficient training and inference of large-scale generative models, contributing to the development of more stable and performant systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its significant achievements, the paper openly acknowledges several limitations:
- **Fine-grained Details with Large Motion:** Preserving intricate details in videos with substantial motion remains a challenge, highlighting an area for continued research.
- **Computational Cost of Large Models:** Even with optimizations, the inference cost of the 14B model (30 minutes on a single GPU without further optimization) is still high, indicating a need for further research into efficiency and scalability for widespread, instant application.
- **Lack of Domain-Specific Expertise:** As a foundational model, Wan aims for general capabilities, but performance in highly specialized domains (e.g., education, medicine) may be insufficient. The authors hope open-sourcing will encourage community-driven adaptation for these specific use cases.
- **Audio Generation Limitations:** The current audio generation model deliberately excludes human vocal sounds (speech, laughter, crying) due to data preparation choices, which is a significant limitation for realistic multimodal content.

In conclusion, Wan represents a pivotal contribution to the generative AI landscape. By delivering a high-performing, versatile, and efficient open-source video foundation model, Alibaba Group aims to democratize access to cutting-edge video generation technology, inspire further research, and profoundly impact the future of digital content creation.
