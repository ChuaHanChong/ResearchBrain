# Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space: An Action Space for Reinforcement Learning in Contact-Rich Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford AI Lab performed a systematic study of action spaces for deep reinforcement learning in robotic manipulation, introducing Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space (VICES). This method demonstrated improved sample efficiency, safety, and zero-shot transferability across diverse contact-rich tasks in simulation and on a real robot.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning research in robotics has often overlooked the systematic evaluation and design of action spaces, focusing more on observation spaces and reward models.
- Existing action spaces (e.g., joint-space control, fixed end-effector impedance) are often ill-suited for contact-rich and constrained manipulation tasks, leading to inefficient learning, instability, or inability to perform dynamic force modulation.
- Policies learned in joint space lack transferability across different robot embodiments, and manually specifying time-varying compliance for complex tasks is not scalable.

## Method

- A comprehensive empirical study was conducted comparing seven common action spaces, including joint torques, velocities, positions, fixed end-effector impedance, and variable impedance in joint space, within a PPO-based deep reinforcement learning framework.
- The paper introduces Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space (VICES), an action space where the policy learns to output desired 6-D end-effector poses and dynamically adapts the 6-D impedance gains (stiffness and damping) along each axis.
- A hierarchical control structure was used, where the RL policy generates high-level references in the chosen action space, which are then translated into low-level joint torque commands by an analytical controller.

## Results

- VICES policies achieved high task success rates across free-space, constrained (e.g., door opening, near 100% success), and contact-rich (e.g., surface wiping, highest wiped unit ratio) tasks with improved sample efficiency.
- Superior zero-shot transferability was demonstrated for VICES, both between simulated robots (Panda to Sawyer) and from simulation to a real Panda robot for a wiping task (successfully wiped over 75% of the line in 8 out of 10 trials) while maintaining controlled forces (mean 28 N).
- VICES enabled learning of more energy-efficient behaviors and safer interactions by dynamically adjusting stiffness and maintaining applied forces within operational limits, outperforming fixed-impedance and joint-space methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The choice of action space is a critical design decision in deep RL for robotics, significantly influencing sample efficiency, safety, physical effort, and generalization capabilities.
> - Defining the action space in end-effector (operational) space, as in VICES, inherently abstracts away robot-specific kinematics and dynamics, which is crucial for transferability across different robot platforms.
> - Dynamic adaptation of impedance parameters is essential for safely and efficiently performing contact-rich tasks, enabling robots to dynamically modulate forces and compliance to interact with uncertain environments.
