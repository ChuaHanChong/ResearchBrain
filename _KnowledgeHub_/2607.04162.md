# ACE: Agentic Control for Embodied Manipulation via Zero-shot Workflow Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called ACE (Agentic Control for Embodied Manipulation) enables robots to perform complex tabletop pick-and-place tasks with zero-shot generalization, achieving 50-70% success rates on tasks like formula assembly and constraint-based retrieval. It achieves this by decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control through a mask-mediated vision-action interface and a multi-timescale memory, outperforming baselines that received task-specific supervision.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with open-ended tabletop manipulation due to underspecified natural language instructions and dynamic environments.
- Traditional task-specific policies exhibit limited generalization to novel or logically complex, long-horizon tasks, requiring extensive task-specific training data.
- Current methods often lack robust mechanisms for post-execution verification and recovery from physical or semantic failures, hindering real-world applicability.

## Method

- An embodied reasoning agent employs a zero-shot, ==closed-loop workflow== to decompose complex tasks into explicit semantic sub-goals, enabling dynamic replanning based on observed states or user feedback.
- A **mask-mediated vision-action interface** translates high-level semantic intent into verifiable 8-bit grayscale masks for pick and place targets, allowing human verification and persistent tracking.
- A task-agnostic ==Diffusion Policy==, trained only on generic mask-conditioned primitives, executes the physical actions guided by these tracked masks, thereby decoupling from task-specific semantics.

## Results

- **ACE** achieved **50.0%** and **70.0%** success rates on "Semantic Formula Assembly" and "Constraint-based Retrieval" tasks, respectively, while task-supervised baselines achieved **0.0%**.
- The mask-mediated vision-action interface (using "Mask Only" input) resulted in a **90.0%** grasp success rate, demonstrating improved visual generalization compared to using raw RGB.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of the agentic planner and highlighted that human verification improved success rates on complex tasks (e.g., from **30%** to **50%** for formula assembly).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level cognitive reasoning from low-level physical control is effective for achieving zero-shot generalization in complex, open-ended manipulation tasks.
> - Intermediate, human-verifiable visual representations (e.g., masks) are crucial for safety, interpretability, and allowing in-the-loop human corrections during execution.
> - Robustness in dynamic environments relies on a multi-timescale memory architecture that enables continuous reasoning, perception, and automatic recovery from execution failures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence aims to enable robots to understand and execute complex tasks in dynamic, real-world environments. A significant challenge within this domain is open-ended robotic control, particularly for tabletop manipulation, where natural language instructions are often underspecified, and task constraints can evolve during execution.

Traditional approaches to robotic manipulation frequently rely on task-specific policies or predefined rule sets, which exhibit limitations in generalizing to novel or significantly varied goals. Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have offered new avenues for interpreting open-ended instructions and decomposing complex tasks through iterative reasoning and tool use. However, many existing methods either generate high-level textual plans without direct physical grounding or depend on action mapping that lacks explicit memory structures and robust visual decoupling. This can restrict interpretability, dynamic adaptability, and recovery mechanisms in the face of unexpected outcomes or failures.

Visual grounding, the process of linking language to visual observations, is critical for bridging high-level reasoning with physical execution. However, in dynamic scenes, static grounding is insufficient, requiring persistent tracking of targets as environments change. Furthermore, incorporating human supervision and feedback is often necessary for ensuring safety and refining intent in complex robotic workflows. Existing memory architectures for embodied systems aim to maintain coherence between semantic intent and perceptual object identities, but their effectiveness in robustly coordinating asynchronous planning, tracking, and interaction processes can vary.

The ACE framework positions itself by addressing the limitations of direct language-to-action mapping and rigid task planning. It proposes to enhance generalization and robustness by explicitly decoupling high-level cognitive reasoning from low-level physical control. By introducing a closed-loop workflow reasoning mechanism, a mask-mediated vision-action interface, and a multi-timescale memory system, ACE seeks to improve the robot's ability to adapt to dynamic conditions, user corrections, and execution failures without requiring task-specific retraining for every new task. This approach distinguishes itself by making explicit workflow replanning and a verifiable intermediate representation central to achieving zero-shot task generalization across diverse manipulation settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework for open-ended tabletop pick-and-place tasks that enables zero-shot generalization from natural language instructions. The authors aim to achieve this by moving beyond direct low-level action mapping and instead focusing on workflow reasoning.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in open-ended robotic manipulation:
1. **Underspecified Instructions and Dynamic Environments:** Natural language instructions for robotic tasks are often abstract or incomplete, and the environment can change unpredictably. Robots need to adapt to these dynamic conditions and emergent constraints rather than executing a fixed plan.
2. **Generalization Limitations of Traditional Systems:** Many existing manipulation systems employ task-specific policies or rigid rule libraries. These systems struggle to generalize when confronted with goals that differ significantly from their training data, limiting their applicability in diverse, open-ended scenarios. The authors specifically highlight the inability of these systems to handle "logically complex, long-horizon tasks" such as forming mathematical equations or retrieving objects based on abstract constraints (Section 1).
3. **Need for Embodied Reasoning:** To achieve true task-level generalization, robots require capabilities beyond mere physical control. This includes inferring latent structure from language, decomposing complex tasks into manageable sub-goals, and adapting their strategy when execution outcomes deviate from expectations.
4. **Robustness to Failures:** Robotic manipulation is susceptible to physical and semantic failures (e.g., missed grasps, object shifts, user changes of mind). Current systems often lack robust mechanisms for post-execution verification and recovery, which are crucial for maintaining task progress in dynamic settings.
5. **Data Efficiency:** Training end-to-end policies for every new complex task demands extensive and expensive task-specific demonstrations. The authors motivate the need for a framework that can leverage existing generic manipulation primitives and compose them for novel tasks, thus improving data efficiency (Section 1).

In response to these motivations, ACE aims to:
- Formulate open-ended tabletop pick-and-place as a zero-shot, closed-loop workflow reasoning problem.
- Decouple high-level semantic reasoning (planning and adaptation) from low-level physical control (execution of primitives).
- Introduce a mask-mediated vision-action interface to bridge this gap, translating high-level intent into verifiable, stable visual targets for a task-agnostic robot policy.
- Implement a multi-timescale memory architecture to support continuous reasoning, perception, and recovery, enabling the system to automatically verify execution outcomes and trigger retries, repairs, or replanning.
- Demonstrate task-level zero-shot generalization on logically complex, long-horizon tasks, where the downstream physical controller has no prior knowledge of the specific semantic task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ACE (Agentic Control for Embodied manipulation) is designed as a zero-shot, closed-loop workflow reasoning framework. The core principle is the decoupling of high-level semantic reasoning from low-level physical robot control. The system achieves this by employing an embodied reasoning agent that orchestrates two robot-facing executable skills: a visual grounding interface and a reusable pick-and-place primitive.

#### 3.1. Zero-Shot, Closed-Loop Workflow Reasoning

The central intelligence of ACE is an agentic planner, implemented using the Qwen-Agent framework, powered by Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Given a natural language instruction and scene context, this planner generates a workflow (W), which is a sequence of explicit semantic sub-goals (w_k), rather than raw motor commands. This shifts the complexity of adaptation from low-level control to the agent's reasoning.

A crucial aspect is online replanning. The workflow is not rigid; it can be dynamically adapted (W') based on newly observed states, user feedback, or post-execution outcomes (Δ). Replanning can be local (modifying the grounding of an active sub-goal) or global (regenerating the remaining workflow). This closed-loop adaptability ensures the agent can revise its strategy in real-time.

The agent only exposes two external robot-facing skills: `MaskInterface` and `PickPlace`. Other operations like workflow decomposition, memory querying, sub-goal revision, and replanning are handled internally by the agent framework, keeping the executable skill library minimal.

#### 3.2. Mask-Mediated Vision-Action Interface

The `MaskInterface` is the first external skill, designed to bridge semantic reasoning with low-level robot control. For each active sub-goal (w_k), the system predicts an executable visual target in the form of a unified 8-bit grayscale mask (M_k). This mask, generated by SAM3 and tracked by Cutie, strictly encodes the operational role of each pixel:
- `127`: Pick target (object to be grasped).
- `255`: Place target (destination for the object).
- `0`: Otherwise (background).

This interface serves three critical execution phases:
1. **Human Verification:** Before any action, the mask M_k is rendered to the user. This human-in-the-loop step allows users to verify or correct the intended spatial target, enhancing safety and creating a clear division of labor between agent reasoning and human confirmation.
2. **Persistent Tracking:** Once approved, the mask is initialized into a tracker (T_k) and continuously updated across subsequent frames (T_update) to maintain tracking of the target as the environment evolves.
3. **Task-Agnostic Execution:** The tracked mask is then fed directly into the downstream vision-action (VA) policy. By abstracting the semantic goal into a purely spatial representation, the system enables the reuse of a single VA model across diverse logical constraints.

#### 3.3. Reusable Pick-and-Place Skill and Action Chunk Generation

The `PickPlace` skill is the second external skill, responsible for physical execution. It first invokes `MaskInterface` to ground the symbolic intent of the sub-goal (w_k) into a unified pick-and-place mask (M_k). After potential human verification, the continuously tracked mask (M_k,t) is passed to a task-agnostic VA policy (π_VA), which is a Diffusion Policy (DP) trained with Lerobot for approximately one hour on generic mask-conditioned pick-and-place demonstrations. This policy is explicitly not exposed to any complete semantic task trajectories (e.g., formula assembly or constraint retrieval).

The VA policy then predicts an action chunk (ˆa_t:t+K-1) in a receding-horizon manner, with continuous updates of visual observation and the tracked mask. This skill is reusable because it contains no task-specific semantics (e.g., arithmetic, numerical constraints, object roles). Its role is purely to execute the physical pick and place actions defined by the mask. After execution, the skill returns an execution report to the workflow manager, enabling ACE to advance to the next step, retry the current step, repair the mask, or trigger replanning.

#### 3.4. Multi-Timescale Memory for Robust Recovery

ACE employs a shared scene-state manager backed by a multi-timescale memory architecture to coordinate asynchronous modules and support post-execution verification. This hierarchy includes:
- **Live execution memory:** Manages the current workflow position, active masks, tracker state, and fast-timescale execution progress.
- **Task-scoped semantic-visual memory:** Maintains object names, aliases, roles, and visual associations, crucial for identity consistency and user corrections.
- **Appearance reference memory:** Stores image features or crops for identity-preserving reacquisition if objects move out of view.
- **Conversational context memory:** Preserves a bounded dialogue history to interpret user feedback within the ongoing task.

This memory system centralizes state management, ensuring semantic and perceptual consistency. After each step, the agent verifies successful execution by comparing target masks with updated visual observations. If an error is detected, the agent uses the memory to replan the workflow.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated ACE on logically complex, long-horizon tabletop tasks, specifically "Semantic Formula Assembly" and "Constraint-based Retrieval," demonstrating its task-level zero-shot generalization without task-specific retraining of the low-level controller.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Baselines

The experiments compared ACE against two baselines: ACT (Imitation) and π_0.5 (End-to-End VLA). Notably, these baselines received task-specific supervision, being trained or fine-tuned with approximately one hour of full-task demonstrations (including formula assembly and constraint-based retrieval). In contrast, ACE's downstream Vision-Action (VA) policy was trained for a similar duration but only on generic mask-conditioned pick-and-place primitives, without exposure to complete semantic task trajectories.

The results (Table 1) indicate a substantial performance difference:
- **Baselines (ACT and π_0.5):** Both models achieved a 0.0% Success Rate (SR) across both tasks. Their Fine-Grained Score (FGS, max 20) was marginal (3.2 for ACT, 3.0 for π_0.5). The authors attribute these low scores to the baselines' struggle with explicit compositional reasoning, semantic selection, and robust grounding required by these tasks, particularly in a low-data task-supervised setting.
- **ACE:** Demonstrated significantly higher performance:
 - **Semantic Formula Assembly:** Achieved a 50.0% SR and a high FGS of 17.8.
 - **Constraint-based Retrieval:** Achieved a 70.0% SR.
 - **Grounding Accuracy (GA):** Maintained a robust 90.0% GA across both tasks, indicating the vision-language model's strong ability to localize and mask the intended targets.

These results suggest that ACE's approach of decoupling cognitive reasoning from physical control, along with its explicit sub-goals and human-verifiable masks, provided a more robust and effective strategy for zero-shot generalization on logically demanding tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies (Table 2) were conducted to assess the contribution of individual components:

- **Policy-Level Ablation (Visual Input):**
 - **DP (Mask + Original Image):** When the Diffusion Policy (DP) received both the mask and the raw RGB image, grasp success rate plummeted to 30.0% in the presence of unseen object colors or novel backgrounds. This indicated overfitting to raw RGB input and vulnerability to domain shifts.
 - **DP (Mask Only) [Ours]:** Relying solely on the mask-centric representation achieved a 90.0% grasp success rate. This finding supports the effectiveness of the mask-mediated interface in filtering irrelevant environmental noise and spurious visual features, enhancing visual generalization.

- **System-Level Ablation (Reasoning & Verification):**
 - **ACE (w/o. Agentic Planner):** Removing the high-level reasoning agent reduced the system to a standalone Diffusion Policy. This configuration resulted in a 0.0% SR and an average FGS of 3.4 across both tasks. This outcome emphasizes the necessity of explicit high-level planning for logically complex, long-horizon tasks, demonstrating that the downstream policy alone cannot implicitly learn such semantics.
 - **ACE (w/o. Human Verification):** Operating ACE in a fully autonomous mode (bypassing human approval) led to a measurable drop in task success:
 - Formula Assembly: SR decreased from 50.0% to 30.0%. GA decreased from 90.0% to 70.0%.
 - Constraint Retrieval: SR decreased from 70.0% to 20.0%. GA decreased from 90.0% to 60.0%.
 This decline was primarily attributed to occasional inaccuracies in the agent’s spatial placement predictions. This result reinforces the practical value of the human-in-the-loop verification, which enables users to correct minor grounding errors before physical execution, thereby improving overall system reliability.

#### 4.3. Failure Analysis (from Limitations Section)

The majority of ACE's failures were identified to stem from perceptual grounding or physical execution rather than high-level cognitive planning. Specifically, errors most frequently occurred due to imprecise visual masks generated by the agent (grounding failures) or the downstream Diffusion Policy failing to execute a stable grasp (low-level control failures). While a slight increase in reasoning errors was observed in the more complex Semantic Formula Assembly task, logical reasoning errors remained infrequent across trials. This distribution of errors validates the robust zero-shot cognitive capabilities of the agentic engine and highlights the advantages of the decoupled, interpretable architecture in localizing failure points.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ACE framework demonstrates a significant advancement in the field of embodied manipulation, particularly for open-ended tasks guided by natural language. Its key contributions and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Adaptability:** By formulating open-ended tabletop pick-and-place as a zero-shot, closed-loop workflow reasoning problem, ACE enables task-level generalization to novel semantic constraints and randomized tabletop scenes without task-specific retraining. This addresses a core limitation of traditional robotic systems that often struggle outside their training distributions. The ability to adapt online to user corrections, scene changes, and physical failures through verification and recovery mechanisms makes the system more robust and practical for real-world deployment.

- **Data Efficiency:** The approach decouples high-level reasoning from low-level control, allowing the downstream vision-action policy to be trained only on generic mask-conditioned pick-and-place primitives. This contrasts with end-to-end baselines requiring task-specific demonstrations for every new complex task, thus offering a more data-efficient route to intelligent robot behavior, especially when primitive-level robot data is readily available.

- **Interpretability and Safety through Verifiable Representations:** The introduction of a mask-mediated vision-action interface provides an interpretable intermediate representation. By grounding semantic sub-goals into human-verifiable pick-and-place masks, the system's intent becomes explicit to users. This "human-in-the-loop" verification improves safety, allows for intuitive error correction before physical execution, and fosters trust in autonomous systems. The ablation study confirming the value of human verification underscores its practical importance.

- **Robustness through Closed-Loop Recovery:** The multi-timescale memory architecture facilitates continuous reasoning, perception, and recovery. The system's ability to automatically verify execution outcomes and trigger retries, mask repairs, or replanning contributes to its robustness in dynamic and failure-prone environments. This closed-loop mechanism is critical for sustained performance on long-horizon tasks where errors can accumulate.

- **Framework for Future Research:** The paper identifies that the majority of ACE's failures stem from perceptual grounding or low-level physical execution, rather than high-level cognitive planning. This diagnostic capability, enabled by the decoupled and interpretable architecture, provides clear directions for future work. Efforts can focus on improving visual grounding fidelity and low-level control policies, rather than solely on scaling cognitive planners. Future research could explore reducing human approval burden (e.g., via calibrated confidence estimation), extending workflow reasoning to a broader range of manipulation skills beyond pick-and-place, and developing stronger memory mechanisms for even longer-horizon embodied interaction.

In summary, ACE offers a practical and data-efficient paradigm for adaptable robotic manipulation, demonstrating that explicit workflow reasoning, coupled with verifiable representations and robust recovery mechanisms, can enable significant task-level zero-shot generalization in complex, open-ended scenarios.
