# Mitigating Cross-Modal Distraction and Ensuring Geometric Feasibility via Affordance-Guided and Self-Consistent MLLMs for Task Planning in Instruction-Following Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University and National Taiwan University developed an MLLM-based planning framework to enhance robotic task planning by addressing cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility. Integrating Chain-of-Thought, Self-Consistency, and affordance-guided replanning, the approach achieved a 76.7% overall success rate on a new benchmark for instruction-following manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with interpreting complex natural language instructions and executing long-horizon tasks in real-world environments due to ambiguous commands and the need for continuous physical feasibility assessment.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), while integrating visual input, often suffer from 'cross-modal distraction' where irrelevant visual information degrades reasoning, and frequently generate geometrically infeasible plans (e.g., beyond reach, collision-prone).
- Existing benchmarks for embodied AI lack comprehensive evaluation for critical requirements like quantity estimation, reachability, relative positioning, and collision avoidance simultaneously in closed-loop, instruction-following scenarios.

## Method

- A planning pipeline was introduced that combines Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for structured reasoning, ==Self-Consistency== verification for iterative prediction stability, and an affordance-guided replanning module.
- The Self-Consistency module mitigates cross-modal distraction by comparing current skill selections with a majority vote from buffered historical predictions, allowing for self-correction.
- A skill affordance module ensures geometric feasibility by checking action preconditions (predicates) and provides structured feedback for replanning when infeasible actions are proposed, thereby preventing kinematically impossible or collision-causing movements.
- A new benchmark, QuARC (Quantity, Analysis, Relative positioning, Collision), was developed in IsaacGym to provide a comprehensive evaluation platform for these critical requirements in robotic manipulation.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an overall success rate of **76.7%** across all QuARC benchmark categories, which is more than double the **36.7%** success rate of the ViLa (CoT only) baseline.
- The Self-Consistency mechanism, alongside CoT, improved success rates in Semantic Reasoning tasks from **53.3%** (ViLa) to **66.7%** and enhanced the MLLM's ability to determine task stopping conditions.
- The Skill Affordance module dramatically increased success rates for geometrically constrained tasks: in Reachability Analysis, it improved from **40.0%** (ViLa+SC) to **80.0%**, and in Collision Avoidance, from **30.0%** (ViLa+SC) to **76.7%**.
- Cross-modal distraction was quantified, showing that a Naive MLLM (with images) achieved only **26.7%** success in semantic reasoning, a significant drop from the **86.7%** success of a Naive LLM (text-only) in the same category.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Irrelevant visual input can significantly degrade MLLM reasoning performance in robotic task planning, a phenomenon termed 'cross-modal distraction,' leading to erroneous actions even when textual cues are sufficient.
> - Structured reasoning via Chain-of-Thought, when combined with iterative Self-Consistency checks on predicted action sequences, significantly enhances MLLM robustness against cross-modal distraction and improves its ability to reason about long-term task objectives.
> - Explicitly incorporating geometric feasibility checks through an affordance predictor and providing precise, structured feedback for replanning is crucial for MLLMs to generate physically executable and safe manipulation plans.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence (AI) and robotic manipulation is increasingly focused on enabling robots to understand and execute complex, long-horizon tasks based on natural language instructions. This requires robots to interpret ambiguous human commands, perceive their environment, ground referred objects across modalities, execute manipulation skills, and continuously assess the physical feasibility of their actions.

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated their capacity to generate structured plans from natural language. Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate visual input, represent a step towards enabling these models to perceive and reason about the physical world. MLLMs are being explored for various robotic applications, including generating action sequences, acting as success detectors, precondition checkers, or scene descriptors, often in the form of visual question answering. However, a significant challenge remains in allowing MLLMs to autonomously extract and implicitly utilize information for precise action planning.

Existing work has highlighted several limitations of applying LLMs and MLLMs in robotics. These include the difficulty in bridging the information gap between textual input and environmental observations, the potential for visual inputs to introduce hallucinations or misleading outputs (cross-modal distraction), and the persistent challenge of ensuring geometric feasibility (reachability and collision avoidance) for planned actions. While methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Self-Consistency have been developed to enhance the reasoning abilities of large models, their application in closed-loop, multimodal, and geometrically constrained robotic tasks presents specific challenges. Current robotic benchmarks often focus on isolated aspects of manipulation, such as mobile navigation (ALFRED, BEHAVIOR-1K), object repositioning (CALVIN), or specific collision scenarios (TableEnv), but lack comprehensive evaluation across crucial requirements like quantity estimation, reachability, relative positioning, and collision avoidance simultaneously.

This research contributes to this landscape by systematically investigating the application of MLLMs with in-context learning for closed-loop task planning. It identifies and addresses key limitations of current MLLMs in this context, specifically cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility, and introduces a novel benchmark to facilitate holistic evaluation. The work adapts and integrates reasoning techniques (CoT, Self-Consistency) and adds a geometric feasibility check through skill affordances, aiming to improve the robustness and reliability of MLLM-based robotic task planning without requiring extensive fine-tuning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and improve the performance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for closed-loop task planning in instruction-following manipulation, utilizing an in-context learning paradigm.

The motivation for this work stems from several identified challenges and gaps:

1. **Complexity of Instruction-Following Manipulation:** Real-world robotic tasks, such as food preparation, require agents to interpret natural language instructions, ground referents in the environment, execute manipulation skills, evaluate action feasibility, and adapt their behavior dynamically. This demands a holistic understanding and reasoning capability that current systems often lack.
2. **Four Essential Requirements for Task Planning:** The authors identify four critical requirements for successful task execution in such scenarios:
 - **Quantity Estimation:** Interpreting vague instructions related to amounts (e.g., "scoop from the fuller bowl").
 - **Reachability Analysis:** Ensuring the robot's end-effector can kinematically reach target objects for interaction.
 - **Relative Positioning:** Understanding spatial relationships from instructions (e.g., "place food into the bowl next to the tool holder") and grounding objects accordingly.
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Guaranteeing that planned motions do not result in unintended contact with other objects in the environment.
3. **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Current benchmarks for embodied AI and manipulation often focus on isolated aspects of these requirements, failing to provide a comprehensive platform for evaluating instruction-following task planning in a closed-loop setting across all four dimensions. This makes it difficult to assess the holistic capabilities of MLLMs.
4. **Challenges with MLLMs in Closed-Loop Planning:**
 - **Cross-Modal Distraction:** While MLLMs are designed to process visual inputs, the research identifies that incorporating images can sometimes lead to degraded reasoning performance, hallucinations, or misleading outputs, particularly when visual input is unnecessary for a specific decision. This instability is problematic for closed-loop execution.
 - **Geometric Infeasibility:** MLLMs frequently generate plans that violate spatial constraints, misidentify targets, or lead to collisions, indicating a lack of inherent understanding of physical executability. This includes failing to recognize obstacles or planning movements beyond kinematic limits.
5. **Desire for General-Purpose Models:** The authors aim to leverage general-purpose MLLMs with in-context learning to reduce the cost, time, and effort typically required to adapt to new tasks and environments, thus making the approach more scalable.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:
- To introduce a new benchmark, QuARC (Quantity, Analysis, Relative positioning, Collision), that integrates all four essential requirements for comprehensive evaluation.
- To reveal and characterize the specific limitations of current MLLMs in instruction-following manipulation, namely cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility.
- To develop and evaluate a new MLLM-based planning model that mitigates cross-modal distraction through Chain-of-Thought with Self-Consistency and ensures geometric feasibility by incorporating an affordance predictor.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach through extensive evaluation and ablation studies in a closed-loop task planning setting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a planning pipeline designed to enhance MLLM performance in closed-loop instruction-following manipulation by mitigating cross-modal distraction and ensuring geometric feasibility. The approach involves three main stages: MLLM Planning, Self-Consistency Verification, and Skill Affordance and Replanning.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined as selecting a sequence of skills (π) from a predefined skill set (Π) to satisfy an instruction (I), given current visual observations (O_t) and previously selected skills. This process is iterative, looping until a special `DONE` skill is chosen.

**Pipeline Overview:**
1. **MLLM Planning:** Utilizes Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting to guide the MLLM in generating an explanation of the sub-goal, reasoning through the current iteration, and then determining a sequence of skills.
2. **Self-Consistency Verification:** Addresses cross-modal distraction by evaluating the consistency of MLLM predictions across iterative reasoning steps.
3. **Skill Affordance and Replanning:** Focuses on geometric feasibility by checking action preconditions via an affordance predictor and triggering replanning with structured feedback if an infeasible skill is selected.

**Zero-Shot Chain of Thought (CoT):**
The MLLM is prompted to first generate a natural language description of the current sub-goal, followed by a step-by-step reasoning process, and finally the selection of a skill sequence for the current and subsequent iterations. This explicit reasoning aims to improve decision-making, particularly in the presence of distracting visual inputs. The MLLM is guided to describe the current iteration's goal rather than using a generic "Let's think step by step" prompt.

**Self-Consistency Verification:**
Instead of re-sampling multiple reasoning paths at each iteration, the proposed method leverages the MLLM's ability to produce a *full sequence* of skills. The skill designated for the current iteration is considered for execution, while the subsequent skills in the generated sequence are stored in a buffer. At each new iteration, the currently proposed skill is checked against a majority vote of *previous* decisions for that specific step from the buffered sequences. If a discrepancy exists (i.e., the current choice differs from the historical majority for that step), the MLLM is prompted to reconsider and resolve the conflict, allowing it to account for both model instability and genuine environmental changes. If the current choice aligns, the skill is confirmed, and the rest of the predicted sequence is used to update the buffer for future consistency checks.

**Skill Affordance and Replanning:**
This module ensures geometric feasibility by utilizing predicates – binary values that answer specific questions about the environment state. These predicates form preconditions for each skill, allowing the system to evaluate the "affordance" (likelihood of successful execution) of a chosen skill.
Example predicates include:
- `spoon on hand`: Is the robot grasping the spoon?
- `food on hand`: Does the spoon contain food?
- `dumbwaiter opened`: Is the dumbwaiter door open?
- `close to target`: Is the end-effector near a target bowl?
- `obstacle blocked holder`: Is the spoon holder obstructed?
- `obstacle blocked dumbwaiter`: Is the dumbwaiter door obstructed?
- `reachable`: Is the target within the robot's kinematic reach for the specific skill?

If the MLLM selects an infeasible skill (one whose preconditions are not met), a replanning process is initiated. The affordance module provides structured feedback (e.g., "the bowl is too far," "spoon is on hand, please put it back first") to guide the MLLM in identifying and resolving the issue. During replanning, the previous skill sequence in the buffer is cleared to prevent past errors from influencing future decisions.

**Benchmark (QuARC):**
To systematically evaluate the approach, a new benchmark called QuARC (Quantity, Analysis, Relative positioning, Collision) was developed.
- **Platform:** Built in IsaacGym, a GPU-based physics simulation environment.
- **Scenario:** A food preparation task involving a Franka Emika Panda 7-DoF robot arm, a worktable, a tool holder, a dumbwaiter, and various bowls containing tofu pudding or colored toppings.
- **Categories:** QuARC comprises five categories, each with 30 unique configurations (150 total):
 - **Semantic Reasoning:** Evaluates general planning abilities in the semantic domain (e.g., color, food categories, number of scoops) without visual input necessity.
 - **Quantity Estimation (Qu):** Assesses the ability to perceive extrinsic features like food quantity from images.
 - **Reachability Analysis (A):** Evaluates considering kinematic reach for skills (e.g., repositioning a bowl if too far).
 - **Relative Positioning (R):** Tests the ability to identify spatial relationships from images and ground objects.
 - **Collision Avoidance (C):** Assesses the ability to recognize and account for potential obstacles (e.g., a bowl blocking a dumbwaiter door or spoon path).
- **Environment Diversity:** Varying spatial arrangements, bowl colors, and topping types introduce perceptual and manipulation complexity.

**Baselines:**
The proposed method is compared against:
- **Naive LLM:** MLLM used without image input, expected to perform well only on semantic tasks.
- **Naive MLLM:** MLLM with all available information (text and image) as direct input, generating a single skill.
- **ViLa (CoT):** Naive MLLM with Chain-of-Thought prompting, specifically asking the MLLM to describe its sub-goal before skill selection, based on the ViLa approach.
- **ViLa+SC:** ViLa baseline augmented with the proposed Self-Consistency mechanism.

**Setup and Implementation:**
- **Environmental Information:** MLLM receives image observations (1080x1920 resolution from a high-angle camera) and a container list with toppings (assuming a reliable object detector).
- **Skills:** A predefined set of atomic actions (e.g., `grasp spoon`, `scoop`, `open dumbwaiter`). Low-level control policies are simplified as manually defined trajectories.
- **Affordance:** Predicate values are determined by information such as object distances and executed skill sequences.
- **Base Model:** OpenAI GPT-4o is used as the MLLM.
- **Prompting:** A structured system prompt includes skill descriptions, scenario format, I/O format, and two few-shot in-context learning examples.
- **Success Criteria:** Task completion within three additional steps of a reference answer, without transferring unmentioned toppings, opening/closing dumbwaiter unnecessarily, or spilling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation results on the QuARC benchmark indicate the effectiveness of the proposed approach in addressing cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility in MLLM-based task planning.

- **Overall Performance:** The proposed method, combining CoT, Self-Consistency, and Skill Affordance, achieved an overall success rate of 76.7% across all QuARC categories. This significantly outperformed the ViLa baseline (CoT only), which achieved a 36.7% success rate. The Naive LLM obtained 26.7%, and Naive MLLM achieved 40% (these are for individual categories, not overall baseline).

- **Impact of CoT with Self-Consistency:**
 - The combination of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Self-Consistency mechanisms enhanced the MLLM's reasoning ability. This improvement was particularly evident in tasks within the **Semantic Reasoning**, **Quantity Estimation**, and **Relative Positioning** categories.
 - In Semantic Reasoning, for example, ViLa (CoT) achieved 53.3% success, and ViLa+SC (CoT+Self-Consistency) further improved it to 66.7%. The full proposed method also reached 66.7% in this category.

- **Impact of Skill Affordance:**
 - The integration of skill affordance enabled the robot to understand geometric feasibility. This allowed for effective assessment of action preconditions, leading to successful task completion in the **Reachability Analysis** and **Collision Avoidance** categories.
 - In Reachability Analysis, ViLa+SC had a 40.0% success rate, which increased to 80.0% with the full proposed method (including Skill Affordance).
 - Similarly, for Collision Avoidance, ViLa+SC achieved 30.0%, while the full method reached 76.7%.

- **Cross-Modal Distraction (Impact of Visual Input):**
 - A notable finding was the decrease in performance in the "Semantic Reasoning" category when comparing the Naive LLM (without image input) to the Naive MLLM (with image input). The Naive LLM achieved a success rate of 86.7% in semantic reasoning, where solutions could be derived purely from text. In contrast, the Naive MLLM, despite having access to images, struggled, achieving only 26.7%. This difference highlights the phenomenon of "cross-modal distraction," where irrelevant visual input can degrade the MLLM's reasoning ability, leading to erroneous actions or misinterpretations of the image.

- **Role of CoT in Addressing Distraction:**
 - Analysis of Naive MLLM failures revealed that the MLLM sometimes recognized its mistakes and provided correct answers during its explanations, even when generating an incorrect skill. This suggested that MLLMs are not always capable of directly generating accurate skills under visual distraction. CoT prompting was effective in structuring the MLLM's thought process, helping it to mitigate the influence of such distractions and produce more robust plans.

- **Role of Self-Correction in Improving Stopping Conditions:**
 - When using CoT alone, a common failure mode was the robot's inability to recognize when to stop a task. The Self-Consistency mechanism, which compares current skill selections with a majority of previous decisions, demonstrated an improvement in the MLLM's reasoning about long-term objectives and stopping conditions. While Self-Consistency helped, it correctly resolved 50% of the 18 identified inconsistent skill selections.

Overall, the results demonstrate that combining structured reasoning (CoT), iterative consistency checks (Self-Consistency), and explicit geometric feasibility verification (Skill Affordance) significantly improves the robustness and success rate of MLLM-based agents in complex instruction-following manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI and robotics by addressing several critical challenges in deploying MLLMs for real-world instruction-following manipulation tasks.

1. **Mitigation of Key MLLM Limitations:** The work directly tackles two significant limitations of MLLMs in robotic planning: cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility. By showing that irrelevant visual input can degrade MLLM reasoning, it highlights a crucial area for future model development and robust system design. The proposed solutions—Chain-of-Thought with Self-Consistency and affordance-guided planning—offer practical strategies for overcoming these issues, leading to more reliable task execution.
2. **Introduction of a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The creation of QuARC is a significant contribution. By integrating the four essential requirements (quantity estimation, reachability analysis, relative positioning, and collision avoidance) into a single benchmark, it provides a much-needed tool for holistic evaluation of instruction-following capabilities. This benchmark can facilitate standardized comparisons across different methods and drive future research toward more capable and generalizable robotic systems.
3. **Enhanced Task Planning Robustness:** The proposed pipeline, which iteratively plans with feedback and consistency checks, moves beyond simple single-shot MLLM predictions. This closed-loop approach, coupled with explicit precondition checks through skill affordances, improves the robot's ability to recover from errors and make geometrically feasible decisions, thereby increasing the overall robustness of task planning.
4. **Leveraging In-Context Learning:** By utilizing MLLMs with in-context learning, the approach reduces the need for extensive fine-tuning for new tasks or environments. This lowers the entry barrier for applying large models in robotics, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of intelligent robotic agents in diverse scenarios.
5. **Path Towards More Capable Embodied Agents:** The findings underscore the potential for MLLMs to serve as general-purpose planners in complex physical environments, provided their inherent limitations in multimodal reasoning and physical understanding are systematically addressed. The work lays a foundation for future research to develop more sophisticated MLLM architectures or integrate specialized modules that can inherently handle nuances like precise quantity estimation, detailed spatial reasoning, and real-time collision prediction.

The demonstrated success rate of 76.7% on a challenging benchmark, without requiring additional fine-tuning of the MLLM, indicates a notable step forward in enabling robots to reliably follow human instructions in complex, interactive settings. This research can inform the design of future robotic systems for various applications, including service robotics, manufacturing, and personal assistance, where robust instruction-following and safe physical interaction are paramount.
