# Mitigating Cross-Modal Distraction and Ensuring Geometric Feasibility via Affordance-Guided and Self-Consistent MLLMs for Task Planning in Instruction-Following Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University and National Taiwan University developed an MLLM-based planning framework to enhance robotic task planning by addressing cross-modal distraction and geometric infeasibility. Integrating Chain-of-Thought, Self-Consistency, and affordance-guided replanning, the approach achieved a 76.7% overall success rate on a new benchmark for instruction-following manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robots struggle with interpreting complex natural language instructions and executing long-horizon tasks in real-world environments due to ambiguous commands and the need for continuous physical feasibility assessment.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), while integrating visual input, often suffer from 'cross-modal distraction' where irrelevant visual information degrades reasoning, and frequently generate geometrically infeasible plans (e.g., beyond reach, collision-prone).
- Existing benchmarks for embodied AI lack comprehensive evaluation for critical requirements like quantity estimation, reachability, relative positioning, and collision avoidance simultaneously in closed-loop, instruction-following scenarios.

## Method

- A planning pipeline was introduced that combines Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for structured reasoning, ==Self-Consistency== verification for iterative prediction stability, and an affordance-guided replanning module.
- The Self-Consistency module mitigates cross-modal distraction by comparing current skill selections with a majority vote from buffered historical predictions, allowing for self-correction.
- A skill affordance module ensures geometric feasibility by checking action preconditions (predicates) and provides structured feedback for replanning when infeasible actions are proposed, thereby preventing kinematically impossible or collision-causing movements.
- A new benchmark, QuARC (Quantity, Analysis, Relative positioning, Collision), was developed in IsaacGym to provide a comprehensive evaluation platform for these critical requirements in robotic manipulation.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved an overall success rate of **76.7%** across all QuARC benchmark categories, which is more than double the **36.7%** success rate of the ViLa (CoT only) baseline.
- The Self-Consistency mechanism, alongside CoT, improved success rates in Semantic Reasoning tasks from **53.3%** (ViLa) to **66.7%** and enhanced the MLLM's ability to determine task stopping conditions.
- The Skill Affordance module dramatically increased success rates for geometrically constrained tasks: in Reachability Analysis, it improved from **40.0%** (ViLa+SC) to **80.0%**, and in Collision Avoidance, from **30.0%** (ViLa+SC) to **76.7%**.
- Cross-modal distraction was quantified, showing that a Naive MLLM (with images) achieved only **26.7%** success in semantic reasoning, a significant drop from the **86.7%** success of a Naive LLM (text-only) in the same category.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Irrelevant visual input can significantly degrade MLLM reasoning performance in robotic task planning, a phenomenon termed 'cross-modal distraction,' leading to erroneous actions even when textual cues are sufficient.
> - Structured reasoning via Chain-of-Thought, when combined with iterative Self-Consistency checks on predicted action sequences, significantly enhances MLLM robustness against cross-modal distraction and improves its ability to reason about long-term task objectives.
> - Explicitly incorporating geometric feasibility checks through an affordance predictor and providing precise, structured feedback for replanning is crucial for MLLMs to generate physically executable and safe manipulation plans.
