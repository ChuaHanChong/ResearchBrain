# PianoMime: Learning a Generalist, Dexterous Piano Player from Internet Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> PianoMime enables a robotic agent to learn dexterous piano playing from internet videos by converting 2D human demonstrations into 3D robot-actionable data. The system combines imitation and reinforcement learning to train song-specific policies, then distills this knowledge into a generalist policy, demonstrating the ability to play hundreds of different songs in a MuJoCo simulation environment.

## Problem

- Robotic dexterous manipulation, such as piano playing, requires precise finger coordination and timing, which is challenging for robots due to embodiment differences.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, labeled demonstration data for complex robotic skills, making it difficult to train generalist policies.
- Leveraging readily available internet videos for robot learning is difficult as they lack explicit 3D information and action labels.

## Method

- A framework called **PianoMime** extracts 3D fingertip trajectories from 2D YouTube piano performance videos by pairing them with MIDI files and using homography for coordinate transformation.
- Song-specific policies are trained using a hybrid approach combining imitation learning to mimic human movements and ==reinforcement learning== to optimize for correct note playing, incorporating residual policy learning.
- A generalist policy is created via policy distillation from song-specific policies, employing a hierarchical policy architecture and representation learning with a pre-trained observation encoder using Signed Distance Fields (SDFs).

## Results

- Song-specific policies achieved F1 scores above **0.95** across most songs, outperforming baseline methods by integrating human demonstrations with reinforcement learning.
- The distilled generalist policy, utilizing a hierarchical architecture with SDF embedding, demonstrated the best generalization performance, maintaining high F1 scores on unseen songs.
- The final model successfully plays hundreds of different songs in simulation, showing performance improvements with increased training data and producing more natural, human-like movements compared to inverse kinematics solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internet videos, even without specialized motion capture, can be effectively transformed into usable 3D robot demonstration data for complex dexterous tasks by combining computer vision with external contextual information like MIDI files.
> - A hybrid learning approach combining imitation learning (mimicking human trajectories) with reinforcement learning (optimizing for task success) and residual policy learning leads to improved dexterous performance.
> - A hierarchical policy architecture, coupled with learned representations from Signed Distance Fields (SDFs), enables a generalist robotic policy to effectively generalize complex skills to new, unseen scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation remains one of the most challenging frontiers in robotics. Among these skills, piano playing stands out as particularly demanding, requiring precise finger coordination, timing, and adaptability. The research paper "PianoMime: Learning a Generalist, Dexterous Piano Player from Internet Demonstrations" introduces a novel approach to teaching robots this complex skill by leveraging the vast repository of human piano performances available on the internet.

The core innovation of PianoMime lies in its ability to transform regular YouTube videos of pianists into actionable data for robotic learning without requiring specialized motion capture equipment. This approach addresses a fundamental limitation in robot learning: the scarcity of high-quality demonstration data. By tapping into internet-scale data, PianoMime creates a pathway for robots to learn complex dexterous manipulation skills that were previously difficult to acquire.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

Robotic piano playing presents several unique challenges:

1. **Dexterity Requirements**: Playing the piano demands coordinated movement of multiple fingers and both hands simultaneously.

2. **Embodiment Differences**: Human hands differ significantly from robotic end-effectors in their structure, degrees of freedom, and physical capabilities.

3. **Internet Data Limitations**: Videos found online lack explicit action labels and 3D information, making them difficult to use directly for robot learning.

4. **Generalization**: Moving beyond single-song performance to a generalist policy that can play any musical piece requires sophisticated learning architectures.

The complexity of piano playing makes it an ideal benchmark for dexterous manipulation tasks. Success in this domain could transfer to other precision-oriented applications like surgical robotics, assembly tasks, and general object manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PianoMime framework consists of three main components:

1. **Data Collection and Processing**: Extracting useful information from YouTube videos of piano performances.

2. **Song-Specific Policy Learning**: Training individual policies for each song using a combination of imitation learning and reinforcement learning.

3. **Policy Distillation**: Creating a generalist policy that can play any song by distilling knowledge from the specialized policies.

This hierarchical approach enables the system to first master individual songs before developing the ability to generalize to new, previously unseen pieces of music.

#### 3.1. Data Collection and Processing

The data collection process begins with scraping YouTube videos of piano performances alongside their corresponding MIDI files. This pairing is crucial as it provides both visual information (human hand movements) and ground truth data (which notes should be played when).

To extract usable information from videos, the researchers employ MediaPipe to detect 2D fingertip positions. However, since videos only provide 2D information, an additional step is needed to infer 3D positions:

```python
# Pseudocode for 3D position inference
def infer_3d_position(fingertip_2d, piano_state):
    # Compute homography matrix using Eight-point Algorithm
    H = compute_homography(reference_points_2d, reference_points_3d)

    # Transform 2D pixel coordinates to x-y coordinates in simulation
    fingertip_xy = apply_homography(H, fingertip_2d)

    # Infer z-coordinate based on whether key is pressed
    fingertip_z = infer_z_from_piano_state(fingertip_xy, piano_state)

    return concatenate(fingertip_xy, fingertip_z)
```

The homography matrix $H_{3×3}$ enables the transformation from video pixel coordinates to simulation coordinates, creating a bridge between human demonstrations and robot learning.

#### 3.2. Song-Specific Policy Learning

With processed demonstration data in hand, the next step involves learning song-specific policies. Each policy is trained to play a particular song using a combination of:

1. **Imitation Learning**: Learning to mimic human fingertip trajectories
2. **Reinforcement Learning**: Optimizing for playing the correct notes at the right time

The reward function combines two components:

$$R = \alpha R_{task} + (1-\alpha) R_{style}$$

Where:
- $R_{task}$ rewards playing the correct notes at the right time
- $R_{style}$ encourages mimicking human fingertip motions
- $\alpha$ is a weighting parameter that balances these objectives

A key innovation is the use of residual policy learning, where the policy learns to adjust a nominal behavior derived from inverse kinematics:

$$a_t = \pi_\theta(s_t) + \text{IK}(g_t)$$

Where:
- $a_t$ is the final action
- $\pi_\theta(s_t)$ is the learned policy output
- $\text{IK}(g_t)$ is the inverse kinematics solution for target fingertip positions

Experimental results demonstrate that integrating human demonstrations with reinforcement learning significantly improves performance compared to baseline methods. The inclusion of residual learning proves crucial for exploiting human demonstrations effectively.

#### 3.3. Policy Distillation

The final challenge is creating a generalist policy capable of playing any song. PianoMime addresses this through policy distillation, which involves training a single behavioral cloning policy to mimic the collection of song-specific policies.

The researchers explore several architectural innovations:

1. **Hierarchical Policy Architecture**: Decoupling the policy into a high-level component that predicts fingertip trajectories and a low-level component that generates joint-space actions.

2. **Representation Learning**: Pre-training an observation encoder to learn spatially informative features using a Signed Distance Field (SDF).

3. **Expressive Generative Models**: Experimenting with advanced models like Diffusion Policies and Behavior Transformers to capture the multimodality of piano playing.

The hierarchical approach proves particularly effective, with the two-stage architecture outperforming single-stage alternatives. Additionally, incorporating an observation encoder pre-trained with SDFs leads to better generalization on unseen songs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The researchers conducted extensive experiments to evaluate PianoMime's performance:

1. **Song-Specific Policies**: The proposed approach achieves F1 scores above 0.95 across most songs, significantly outperforming baseline methods.

2. **Policy Distillation**: The hierarchical policy with SDF embedding achieves the best generalization performance, maintaining high F1 scores even on unseen songs.

3. **Data Scaling**: Performance consistently improves with more training data, suggesting the model has not yet reached its performance ceiling.

4. **Generalization**: The final model can successfully play hundreds of different songs, with performance gradually declining as songs become more complex or dissimilar from the training data.

The comparison between human demonstrations, inverse kinematics solutions, and PianoMime's approach reveals that while inverse kinematics provides technically correct solutions, PianoMime achieves more natural, human-like movements that better capture the style and subtleties of piano playing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PianoMime demonstrates that robots can learn complex dexterous manipulation skills from internet videos without requiring specialized data collection. By combining computer vision techniques, hierarchical policy architectures, and representation learning, the approach achieves impressive results in robotic piano playing.

The key contributions of this work include:

1. A framework for extracting 3D fingertip trajectories from 2D YouTube videos
2. A hybrid reinforcement learning approach that combines task success with stylistic imitation
3. A hierarchical policy architecture that effectively generalizes across different songs
4. Empirical validation showing successful piano playing across hundreds of songs

As robots continue to move from controlled factory settings into our homes and workplaces, the ability to learn from everyday demonstrations will become increasingly important. PianoMime provides a roadmap for teaching robots complex skills using the vast reservoir of human expertise already available on the internet.

#### 5.1. Impact on Robotic Learning

PianoMime represents an important step forward for several reasons:

1. **Internet-Scale Learning**: It demonstrates a successful approach for learning from internet videos without requiring specialized data collection setups.

2. **Dexterous Manipulation**: The piano-playing task pushes the boundaries of what robots can achieve in terms of coordinated, precise movements.

3. **Policy Architecture**: The hierarchical approach with representation learning offers insights applicable to other complex robotic tasks.

4. **Benchmark Creation**: The dataset and framework provide a valuable benchmark for future research in dexterous manipulation.

The techniques developed in PianoMime could be applied to other domains where human demonstrations are plentiful but action labels are scarce, such as surgical procedures, assembly tasks, and daily household activities.
