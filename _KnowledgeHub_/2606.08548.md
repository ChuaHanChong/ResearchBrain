# OASIS: From Simulation Data Collection to Real-World Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The OASIS framework, developed by researchers from TeleAI and Fudan University, introduces a simulation-data-driven approach for training humanoid loco-manipulation policies that enables zero-shot transfer to real robots. This system achieves significantly higher data collection efficiency, up to 1.84 times faster than real-robot teleoperation, and generates policies whose real-robot success rates often match or exceed those trained with real-world data, primarily through automated asset generation and extensive visual domain randomization.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots require large volumes of high-quality demonstration data for complex loco-manipulation tasks, but such data is scarce and challenging to acquire.
- Real-robot teleoperation, while providing embodiment-aligned trajectories, is resource-intensive, time-consuming, prone to errors requiring manual resets, and carries a risk of hardware damage.
- Existing simulation-based methods often target simpler tasks, fixed-base manipulation, or rely on computationally expensive reinforcement learning for data acquisition, limiting their applicability to complex humanoid behaviors.

## Method

- **OASIS** uses an automated ==simulation scene construction== pipeline that generates high-resolution 3D meshes from reference images, using VLMs (e.g., **Qwen3-VL**) to estimate physical dimensions and assign material properties.
- A two-stage data collection process involves ==VR-based teleoperation== in simulation for capturing kinematic trajectories, followed by offline, scalable rendering with extensive ==visual domain randomization== (texture, lighting, camera extrinsics) to generate diverse image observations.
- A ==hierarchical visuomotor policy== combines a ==Transformer==-based high-level planner, trained with ==Flow Matching== and conditioned on reference motion commands, with a low-level ==whole-body controller==, employing a ==curriculum-based rollout== for stability.

## Results

- OASIS demonstrated up to **1.84x** higher data collection efficiency compared to real-robot teleoperation across various loco-manipulation tasks, with greater gains for more difficult tasks.
- Policies trained exclusively on OASIS simulation data achieved real-robot success rates comparable to, and in some tasks even surpassing, those trained on real-robot data (e.g., **1.00** success rate for "Kneel and Wipe Under Table").
- Full visual domain randomization yielded the highest average success rate (**0.83**), with lighting randomization identified as the most impactful individual factor, and mixing simulation and real-robot data further enhanced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling real-time VR teleoperation for trajectory collection from computationally intensive, high-fidelity rendering allows for efficient generation of core kinematic data which can then be massively augmented offline.
> - Extensive visual domain randomization in simulation, particularly lighting randomization, is critical for bridging the sim-to-real gap and enabling effective zero-shot transfer to real humanoid robots.
> - Training the high-level planner with reference motion commands as proprioception and incorporating a curriculum-based rollout mechanism improves policy stability and generalization by mitigating compounding errors over long-horizon autoregressive deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, particularly humanoid robot loco-manipulation, faces a persistent challenge in acquiring sufficient high-quality demonstration data. Humanoid robots are envisioned for tasks requiring coordinated locomotion and manipulation within human environments. Robust and generalizable policies for these robots necessitate extensive, high-quality data, which is currently limited for humanoid platforms.

Existing approaches to data collection for robot manipulation include human videos, egocentric recordings, and real-robot teleoperation. Real-robot teleoperation has been a prevalent method due to its ability to generate trajectories precisely aligned with the robot's embodiment and provide direct action supervision. However, this approach is resource-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, demanding substantial hardware, physical space, and manual intervention for scene resets and failure recovery. Physical interaction also carries risks of damaging equipment.

In response to these limitations, the research community has increasingly explored the use of simulation as a scalable training source. Prior work in simulation-based data generation has focused on automating task and asset construction, or augmenting trajectories by replaying human demonstrations under varied initial conditions. However, many such efforts are limited to fixed-base or upper-body manipulation, or require computationally expensive reinforcement learning processes for data acquisition. Some recent humanoid-specific efforts like GR00T N1 incorporate synthetic data, but their simulated components often focus on simpler tasks. Others, such as VIRAL, rely on reinforcement learning in simulation, which can be resource-intensive itself.

OASIS positions itself within this landscape by offering a simulation-data-driven framework that aims to overcome the scalability and efficiency bottlenecks of real-world data collection for humanoid loco-manipulation. It specifically addresses the trade-off between trajectory quality and data scalability by leveraging high-fidelity simulation and automated asset generation. The proposed framework distinguishes itself by decoupling trajectory collection (via VR teleoperation in simulation) from photorealistic rendering, allowing a single teleoperated trajectory to be expanded into a diverse and large dataset through extensive visual randomization. This approach seeks to enable zero-shot transfer of policies trained purely on simulation data to real-world humanoid robots, contributing to the development of more robust and generalizable humanoid control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the OASIS project is to develop a framework for learning robust and generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation policies entirely from data collected in simulation, thereby enabling zero-shot deployment on real-world humanoid robots.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality:** Humanoid robots require large volumes of high-quality demonstration data for complex loco-manipulation tasks. Existing data sources often force an unsatisfactory trade-off between the quality of trajectories and the scalability of data collection. Real-world teleoperation provides high-quality, embodiment-aligned trajectories but is inherently limited in scale due to practical constraints.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Real-World Data Collection:**
 - **High Cost and Resource Intensity:** Collecting teleoperation data on real robots demands expensive hardware, significant physical space, and considerable financial investment.
 - **Time Consumption and Inefficiency:** Real-world data collection is inherently fragile and slow. Long-horizon tasks are particularly problematic, as any failure or need for repositioning requires manual reconfiguration of the robot and environment, which is time-consuming. Figure 2 illustrates this contrast, highlighting how simulation enables instantaneous scene resets compared to tedious manual recovery processes in the real world.
 - **Risk of Damage:** Physical interaction poses a risk of damaging robot hardware or objects in the scene, which further impedes the collection process and adds to operational costs.
3. **Leveraging Simulation for Scalability and Efficiency:** Simulation offers an alternative by providing a controlled environment capable of generating clean, embodiment-aligned data at scale without the constraints of physical hardware. The ability to instantly reset scenes, avoid physical damage, and parallelize data generation presents a significant advantage over real-world methods.
4. **Enhancing Visual Diversity and Robustness:** Real-world data collection often occurs in relatively fixed environments, limiting the visual diversity of training samples. This can lead to policies that struggle with domain shift during deployment in varied real-world conditions. The motivation is to use simulation's capacity for extensive domain randomization (e.g., lighting, textures, camera poses) to generate a training dataset that covers a much richer set of visual conditions, thereby fostering greater robustness and generalization for the learned policies.
5. **Decoupling Trajectory Collection from Rendering:** The framework aims to separate the real-time constraints of operator teleoperation from the computational demands of high-fidelity rendering. This allows for efficient collection of core kinematic trajectories and subsequent offline augmentation into a large, visually diverse training set, effectively multiplying the value of operator effort.

In summary, the motivation for OASIS is to establish a scalable, efficient, and robust data collection and policy learning paradigm for humanoid loco-manipulation by fully embracing simulation, thereby mitigating the current bottlenecks associated with real-world data generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OASIS is presented as a simulation-data-driven framework for humanoid loco-manipulation, structured into four main stages: automated simulation scene construction, a two-stage data collection pipeline (teleoperation and rendering), hierarchical whole-body policy training, and zero-shot real-robot deployment.

#### 3.1. Data Collection

The data collection process within OASIS is designed to be scalable and efficient, relying heavily on simulation.

##### 3.1.1. Simulation Scene Construction

To overcome the labor-intensive nature of manual scene creation, OASIS implements an automated asset generation pipeline:
- **Real-to-Sim Asset Generation:** Given reference images of real-world objects, the system first utilizes Hunyuan3D, a large-scale 3D synthesis system, to generate high-resolution textured 3D meshes. These outputs, however, lack physical scale and material properties. To address this, Qwen3-VL, a vision-language model (VLM) with visual reasoning capabilities, is prompted with the reference image and a category description to estimate the object's physical dimensions and material category (e.g., "length cm": X, "width cm": Y, "height cm": Z, "material": "").
- **Physics Parameter Assignment:** The predicted dimensions are used to rescale the normalized mesh to its real-world size. The estimated material category references a predefined table (Appendix A) to retrieve effective density, friction, and restitution coefficients. Mass and inertia are calculated assuming uniform density, and friction/restitution are applied to the collision body. To account for potential estimation errors, all physical properties are randomized around their predicted values during data collection.

##### 3.1.2. Teleop Trajectory Collection

With the simulation scene constructed, humanoid manipulation trajectories are collected through VR-based teleoperation:
- Human operators control a simulated humanoid using VR devices, such as PICO 4U. A first-person view from the robot's head camera is transmitted to the VR headset in real-time.
- Operator motions are retargeted to the humanoid by GMR (General Motion Retargeting) to generate whole-body reference motions. These motions are then fed into Teleopit, an open-source reinforcement learning-based whole-body controller, to drive the simulated humanoid.
- To ensure low-latency teleoperation, IsaacSim's Real-Time rendering mode is employed.
- During this stage, two types of data are recorded: the whole-body kinematic state of the robot and all interactive rigid bodies in the scene (for trajectory replay), and the GMR-retargeted reference motions (for high-level policy training).

##### 3.1.3. Scalable Trajectory Rendering

Following teleoperation, a scalable offline rendering process generates diverse image observations for training:
- Each recorded trajectory is replayed offline, allowing for high-fidelity Path-Tracing rendering in IsaacSim without real-time constraints.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** To expand a single demonstration into a large, visually diverse dataset and enhance sim-to-real transfer, the replayed trajectories are rendered under randomized visual conditions. This includes randomization of background textures (walls, floor, table, roughness, metallic constant, rotation, translation, UVW projection), environmental lighting (dome light and indoor light intensity, color temperature, and color), and camera extrinsics (small position and rotation offsets). (Details in Appendix B)

#### 3.2. Whole-Body Policy Learning

OASIS employs a hierarchical visuomotor policy for humanoid loco-manipulation.

##### 3.2.1. Model Architecture

- **Reference Motion Command:** The per-frame reference motion command `m_t` is represented as a 67-dimensional vector, encoding trigonometric roll/pitch, yaw difference, local root translation, root height, joint positions, and joint increments.
- **Hierarchical Design:** The system comprises a high-level planner and a low-level controller.
 - **High-level Planner:** This is a Transformer-based, action-chunking policy that generates future motion sequences using Flow Matching. Its denoiser takes three inputs:
 - Text instruction, encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder.
 - Three-view images, encoded by a frozen DINOv2 visual encoder.
 - Robot proprioception (reference motion commands) over the most recent H=2 frames, encoded by an MLP.
 These features are concatenated into a condition token sequence `c`. The denoiser predicts a whole-body reference motion `m_t:t+F` over the next F=32 frames.
 - **Flow Matching Training:** The denoiser `v_θ` is trained using the Flow Matching objective, which regresses the constant-velocity field along a linear path between a Gaussian prior and the target action chunk. During inference, actions are generated by integrating the learned velocity field with an Euler solver over 10 denoising steps.
 - **Low-level Controller:** The low-level controller, Teleopit, converts the predicted reference motion into 29-DoF body joint angles. Combined with 14-DoF hand joints, the system outputs 43-DoF whole-body joint angles.

##### 3.2.2. Training Recipe

Two specific strategies are implemented to enhance policy training:
- **Reference Motion Commands as Proprioception:** The high-level planner is conditioned on reference motion commands as proprioception input, rather than the actual robot state. This is to provide a consistent, noise-free history, preventing the accumulation of tracking errors and noise that would arise from conditioning on executed robot states.
- **Curriculum-based Rollout Training:** To improve stability over long horizons and enable the planner to cope with its own prediction errors, a curriculum-based rollout mechanism is used. During training, P=4 consecutive segments are sampled. The first segment uses ground-truth history, while subsequent segments reuse the predecessor’s last H predicted frames with a probability `p_rollout`. This `p_rollout` starts at 0 for the initial 20% of training and then linearly increases to 0.8, gradually exposing the model to its own errors.

#### 3.3. Deployment

The trained system is deployed on a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid robot, equipped with 7-DoF three-fingered dexterous hands. It features a Realsense D435i camera on the head and Realsense D405 cameras on each wrist. The high-level planner operates at 25 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, while the low-level controller executes the 32-step action chunk at 50 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot aimed to evaluate OASIS's efficiency, the impact of its data augmentation, and the effectiveness of its simulation data compared to real-world data.

#### 4.1. Data Collection Efficiency (Q1)

- OASIS demonstrated significantly higher data collection efficiency compared to real-robot teleoperation across all tested loco-manipulation tasks (Place Cup in Box, Wipe Monitor, Lift Basket and Place Cup, Kneel and Wipe Under Table).
- Speedup ranged from 1.15× for "Place Cup in Box" to 1.84× for "Kneel and Wipe Under Table." The efficiency gain increased with task difficulty.
- This efficiency difference is attributed to the instantaneous and automatic scene resets in simulation, which eliminate the considerable manual overhead (repositioning robot, rearranging objects) inherent in real-robot collection after each attempt.
- Furthermore, simulation mitigates the fragility of physical interaction, allowing operators to perform tasks without concern for damaging hardware or objects, which could otherwise slow down collection due to caution.

#### 4.2. Ablations on Data Augmentation (Q2)

- **Necessity of Randomization:** Disabling all domain randomization during offline rendering (w/o All) resulted in policies that almost completely failed to transfer to the real robot, achieving an average success rate of 0.05 (e.g., 0/10 for "Place Cup in Box" and "Lift Basket and Place Cup"). This confirms that visual randomization is critical for effective sim-to-real transfer.
- **Contribution of Individual Factors:**
 - **Lighting Randomization** was identified as the most significant contributor among individual factors. Removing it (w/o Light) led to an average success rate of 0.30, lower than removing texture (0.43) or camera extrinsics (0.63). This suggests that illumination differences constitute a major sim-to-real visual gap.
 - The full configuration (Ours), which applies all randomizations (texture, lighting, camera extrinsics), achieved the highest average success rate of 0.83, outperforming any ablated variant. This indicates that these randomizations address complementary aspects of the sim-to-real gap and are most effective when applied in combination.
- **Number of Renderings per Trajectory:** The success rate increased steadily with more renderings per trajectory, approaching saturation between 15 and 20 renderings. The final configuration used 20 randomized environments per trajectory, balancing performance and computational overhead.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Simulation Data (Q3)

- **Comparison with Real-Robot Data:** Policies trained exclusively on simulation data from OASIS achieved real-robot success rates comparable to, and in some tasks (e.g., "Kneel and Wipe Under Table" with 1.00 success rate), even higher than those trained on real-robot data, given the same total number of trajectories (50 per setting).
- This outcome suggests that OASIS-generated simulation data can serve as an effective substitute for real-robot data, offering supervision quality without the associated time and hardware costs. The superior performance in certain cases is attributed to the extensive visual diversity provided by simulation's randomized re-rendering, which enables greater robustness to varying deployment conditions compared to the visually less diverse real-robot data collected in fixed environments.
- **Complementarity of Data Sources:** Mixing simulation and real-robot data (under the same trajectory budget) generally outperformed policies trained on either source alone. For instance, "Place Cup in Box" achieved 0.90 with mixed data compared to 0.80 for sim-only and 0.70 for real-only. This indicates that the two data sources are complementary: simulation provides broad visual generalization, while real-robot data contributes insights into real interaction and perception characteristics that simulation may not fully capture.
- **Curriculum-based Rollout (Appendix D):** An ablation study confirmed the necessity of the curriculum-based rollout mechanism. Training without rollout (w/o Rollout) resulted in significantly lower success rates across all tasks (e.g., 0/10 for "Lift Basket and Place Cup" and "Kneel and Wipe Under Table") compared to training with rollout, highlighting its role in mitigating compounding errors over long-horizon autoregressive deployment.

In summary, the results indicate that OASIS provides an efficient method for generating high-quality, visually diverse data in simulation. Policies trained on this data demonstrate effective zero-shot transfer to real humanoid robots, often matching or surpassing the performance of policies trained on real-world data, and showing further improvement when combined with real data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OASIS framework presents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in addressing the critical bottleneck of data acquisition for loco-manipulation tasks.

Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that **high-fidelity simulation, when combined with realistic asset generation and extensive visual domain randomization, can serve as a practical, scalable, and effective alternative to real-robot teleoperation.** This finding challenges the conventional reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection for complex humanoid behaviors.

The potential impacts of this work include:

1. **Accelerated Robot Learning:** By dramatically increasing the efficiency of data collection (up to 1.84× faster than real-robot teleoperation), OASIS can accelerate the development and iteration cycles for humanoid robot policies. This speedup is crucial for progressing beyond simple tasks to more complex, long-horizon, and contact-rich scenarios.
2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The systematic application of large-scale visual randomization in simulation yields policies that exhibit improved robustness to real-world visual perturbations, such as varying lighting conditions, background clutter, and camera motion blur. This contributes to more generalizable humanoid behaviors that can operate reliably in diverse, unstructured environments.
3. **Reduced Costs and Accessibility:** Shifting data collection from physical hardware to simulation significantly lowers the financial and spatial overhead associated with developing humanoid robots. This could make advanced humanoid research and development more accessible to a broader range of institutions and researchers, fostering innovation in the field.
4. **Enabling Complex Loco-Manipulation:** By providing a scalable source of high-quality, embodiment-aligned data, OASIS can facilitate the training of increasingly sophisticated policies for coordinated locomotion and manipulation, which are essential for humanoids to operate effectively in human-centric environments. The demonstrated zero-shot transfer on various tasks, including kneeling and under-table wiping, showcases the potential for tackling diverse real-world challenges.
5. **Complementarity with Real Data:** The finding that mixing simulation data with a limited amount of real-robot data can further enhance performance highlights the synergistic relationship between these data sources. Simulation can provide broad coverage and visual diversity, while real data can ground the policies in precise physical interactions and perception nuances. This suggests a hybrid approach could be optimal for future development.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the current focus on visual augmentation over motion diversity and potential inaccuracies in automatically generated asset geometry or physical parameters for complex, contact-rich tasks. These areas represent avenues for future research to further refine the sim-to-real transfer capabilities of such frameworks. Overall, OASIS contributes a significant step towards enabling the practical deployment of capable humanoid robots in real-world settings by addressing a fundamental challenge in their development.
