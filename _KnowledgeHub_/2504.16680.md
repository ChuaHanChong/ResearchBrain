# Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model Makes Offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning Work on Real Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich developed a pipeline for offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning that effectively trains and deploys policies on physical quadruped and humanoid robots, utilizing an Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model and uncertainty-penalized policy optimization. This approach yields policies from fixed datasets that achieve robust performance on real hardware, even surpassing online model-free baselines when combining simulated and real-world offline data.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces challenges like distribution shift and compounding errors when learning from fixed datasets, limiting its ability to discover novel, high-performing behaviors.
- Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) in offline settings struggles with dynamics model inaccuracies accumulating over long horizons, leading to unreliable synthetic data and suboptimal policy learning.
- Existing offline MBRL methods are predominantly evaluated in simulation or with simpler control tasks, lacking robust transferability to complex, long-horizon real-world robot locomotion.

## Method

- An ==Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model== (**RWM-U**) extends previous work by incorporating an ==ensemble-based method== to quantify both aleatoric and critical ==epistemic uncertainty== in its autoregressive dynamics predictions.
- **MOPO-PPO** (Model-Based Offline Policy Optimization) adapts the ==MOPO== framework to ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==, a stable algorithm for robot control, by penalizing rewards with RWM-U's epistemic uncertainty.
- The integrated pipeline trains RWM-U entirely offline, then iteratively uses it to generate uncertainty-penalized imagined trajectories for PPO policy optimization, steering the policy away from unreliable model predictions.

## Results

- RWM-U reliably estimates epistemic uncertainty, which closely correlates with the model's actual long-horizon prediction errors, confirming its use as a trust metric.
- MOPO-PPO consistently outperforms model-free (**CQL**) and uncertainty-unaware model-based (MBPO, MBPO-PPO) baselines across various simulated robotic tasks, particularly in complex quadruped and humanoid locomotion.
- Policies trained entirely offline using a combination of simulated and real-world data achieved the highest performance (e.g., 0.91 ".03 normalized reward on ANYmal D), surpassing state-of-the-art online model-free baselines and demonstrating successful deployment on physical ANYmal D and Unitree G1 robots.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The epistemic uncertainty estimated by RWM-U, calculated as ensemble disagreement, reliably correlates with the true long-horizon prediction errors of the dynamics model.
> - The uncertainty penalty coefficient in MOPO-PPO is a critical hyperparameter, where a balanced value (e.g., ".0") enables robust policies by preventing overfitting to model inaccuracies while still allowing for effective exploration.
> - Combining a moderate amount of real-world offline data with simulated data yields the most robust policies, effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap while leveraging diverse experience.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several critical and challenging fields: Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), Uncertainty Quantification, and Real-World Robotics.

**Offline RL** is a paradigm that aims to learn optimal policies exclusively from fixed, pre-collected datasets, without any further interaction with the environment. This is highly appealing for robotics, where data collection is often expensive, time-consuming, and potentially dangerous. However, offline RL faces significant hurdles, primarily **distribution shift** and **compounding errors**. Policies trained on static data might encounter states or take actions not represented in the original dataset, leading to unreliable predictions and potentially catastrophic failures when deployed. Many existing offline RL methods, particularly model-free ones (e.g., CQL, IQL), address this by enforcing strict conservatism, keeping the learned policy close to the behavior policy that generated the data. While safe, this often limits the ability to discover novel, higher-performing behaviors.

**Model-Based RL (MBRL)** offers an alternative by learning a predictive model of the environment's dynamics. This model can then be used to generate synthetic "imagined" rollouts, effectively augmenting the static offline dataset and allowing for more efficient policy optimization and generalization beyond observed transitions. However, in an offline setting, inaccuracies in the learned dynamics model can accumulate rapidly over long-horizon rollouts, leading to biased synthetic data and suboptimal policy learning. While methods like PlaNet and Dreamer have shown great success with world models in online MBRL, their direct application to fully offline scenarios, especially in real robotics, remains underexplored.

The crucial missing piece for robust offline MBRL, particularly in the noisy and partially observed real world, is **Uncertainty Quantification**. Without the ability to interact with the environment and correct model errors online, the learning system must internally gauge the reliability of its predictions. Existing works like MOPO (Model-Based Offline Policy Optimization) have introduced uncertainty-penalized policy optimization, using bootstrap ensembles to estimate uncertainty in simulated benchmarks. However, these methods often rely on short-horizon, one-step rollouts and are predominantly evaluated in simulated environments or with simpler control tasks, making their direct transfer to complex, long-horizon real-world robot locomotion challenging.

This paper directly addresses the significant gap between successful offline RL demonstrations in simulation and their robust deployment on **physical robots**. As highlighted in Appendix A.5, state-of-the-art real-world locomotion often relies on **on-policy policy-gradient methods like PPO** due to their stability and scalability in massively parallel simulations. Off-policy, value-function-based methods (like those used in many offline RL benchmarks) tend to struggle with stability, distributional shift, and tuning when scaled to complex, high-dimensional robotic control. This work specifically adapts uncertainty-penalized policy optimization to the PPO framework, aligning with the practical realities of modern robot control.

Furthermore, this work builds upon and extends the authors' prior "Robotic World Model (RWM)" (Li et al., 2025), which demonstrated a self-supervised, autoregressive neural simulator capable of capturing long-horizon robot dynamics. The original RWM, however, lacked uncertainty estimation and required online policy training. By integrating uncertainty awareness into RWM and pairing it with a stable, uncertainty-penalized policy optimization, this paper pushes offline MBRL into a practical domain for real-world robotics, effectively bridging the gap between theoretical advancements and tangible hardware deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a principled pipeline that makes offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) effective and practical for real-world robotic systems.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address the following critical challenges in the current landscape of robot learning:

1. **Enabling Data Reuse and Reducing Development Costs:** Current high-performing robotic RL pipelines are highly task-specific and typically require extensive, often costly, online data collection for each new task. The authors are motivated by the desire to leverage existing, fixed datasets (e.g., simulation logs, suboptimal real-world experience) more effectively, allowing for greater data efficiency and reducing the need for continuous, expensive real-world interaction. Offline RL offers the promise of reusing "past robotic experience" rather than treating each development in isolation.
2. **Mitigating Compounding Errors and Distribution Shift in Offline MBRL:** While MBRL is data-efficient, learning from fixed datasets in offline settings exacerbates problems like distribution shift (where the policy explores states outside the training data distribution) and compounding model errors (where small inaccuracies in dynamics predictions grow over long horizons). These issues lead to unreliable synthetic rollouts and prevent the learning of robust policies. The goal is to develop mechanisms to manage and counteract these errors.
3. **Achieving Robustness on Physical Robots:** Existing offline MBRL methods often demonstrate success in controlled simulation environments but struggle with the inherent complexities of real-world robotics, including noise, biases, partial observability, and safety constraints. The research aims to develop a pipeline that is robust enough to handle these real-world challenges and enable stable policy deployment on physical hardware.
4. **Integrating Uncertainty Quantification for Trustworthy Predictions:** The authors recognize that without online environment interaction, the learning system must understand when its model predictions are unreliable. A key motivation is to explicitly incorporate epistemic uncertainty estimation into the dynamics model to identify regions where data is sparse or biased. This uncertainty signal is then used to guide policy optimization, preventing the policy from exploiting erroneous model predictions.
5. **Adapting to Practical Robotics Frameworks:** Given the empirical evidence that on-policy policy-gradient methods like PPO are more robust and stable for high-dimensional real-world locomotion (as detailed in Appendix A.5), the work is motivated to adapt uncertainty-penalized policy optimization to this stable framework, rather than relying on off-policy, value-based methods common in other offline RL benchmarks.
6. **Extending Prior Work for Offline Applicability:** Building upon their "Robotic World Model (RWM)," which provided robust long-horizon dynamics prediction but lacked uncertainty awareness and required online policy training, the motivation is to evolve RWM into an "Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model (RWM-U)" to specifically address the needs of fully offline learning.

In essence, the paper seeks to demonstrate that with a principled integration of accurate, uncertainty-aware dynamics modeling and robust policy optimization, fully offline MBRL can transition from a simulation-centric concept to a powerful tool for practical, real-world robotic control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology presented in this paper involves a principled pipeline for offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL), consisting of two main components: an **Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model (RWM-U)** and **Model-Based Offline Policy Optimization with Proximal Policy Optimization (MOPO-PPO)**.

#### 3.1. Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model (RWM-U)

RWM-U extends the previously developed Robotic World Model (RWM) by incorporating explicit uncertainty quantification, making it suitable for offline settings where dynamics model errors cannot be corrected through online interaction.

- **Autoregressive Dynamics Modeling:** RWM-U retains RWM's fundamental autoregressive structure. It is a neural network-based world model trained to predict future observations (and privileged information like contacts) over long horizons. It does this by feeding its own predictions back into the model, simulating multi-step rollouts without domain-specific inductive biases. The training objective minimizes a multi-step prediction loss (Eq. 1) over a forecast horizon N, using a history horizon M of past observations and actions.
- **Ensemble-Based Uncertainty Estimation:** To quantify uncertainty, RWM-U employs a bootstrap ensemble of multiple neural networks.
 - **Architecture:** After a shared recurrent feature extractor (e.g., a GRU), the model branches into several independent "heads," each corresponding to an ensemble member.
 - **Aleatoric Uncertainty:** Each ensemble member `b` independently predicts the mean ($\mu^b_{o_{t+k}}$) and variance ($\sigma^{2,b}_{o_{t+k}}$) of a Gaussian distribution over the next observation (Eq. 3). The predicted variance component captures the inherent stochasticity or noise in the environment (aleatoric uncertainty).
 - **Epistemic Uncertainty:** The critical addition for offline learning is the estimation of epistemic uncertainty, which reflects the model's confidence due to limited or biased training data. This is quantified as the variance of the predicted means across the ensemble members: $u_{t+1} = \text{Var}_b[\mu^b_{o_{t+1}}]$ (Eq. 4). This ensemble disagreement increases in regions of the state-action space that are poorly represented in the offline dataset.
- **Temporally Consistent Uncertainty Propagation:** By predicting both observations and their associated uncertainties autoregressively, RWM-U can effectively propagate epistemic uncertainty over long imagined horizons (up to 100 steps in experiments). This allows the system to gauge the trustworthiness of its predictions as rollouts extend into less familiar regions.

#### 3.2. MOPO-PPO (Model-Based Offline Policy Optimization with PPO)

MOPO-PPO is an adaptation of the Model-Based Offline Policy Optimization (MOPO) framework to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely adopted and stable on-policy algorithm in real-world robotic control. It leverages the uncertainty signal from RWM-U to guide policy learning.

- **Uncertainty-Penalized Reward Function:** The core of MOPO-PPO is a modified reward function for policy training in imagination. The original environment reward ($r_t$) is penalized based on the epistemic uncertainty ($u_{p_{\phi}}$) predicted by RWM-U (Eq. 5):
 $\tilde{r}(o_t, a_t) = r_t(o_t, a_t) - \lambda u_{p_{\phi}}(o_{t-M+1:t}, a_{t-M+k:t})$
 Here, $\lambda$ is a hyperparameter that controls the strength of the penalty. This encourages the policy to favor regions of the state-action space where the world model is more confident and to avoid exploiting unreliable model predictions.
- **Policy Optimization with PPO:** The policy is then optimized using the PPO algorithm on these uncertainty-penalized imagined trajectories. PPO is chosen for its empirical stability and effectiveness in complex locomotion tasks, contrasting with off-policy, value-based methods that often struggle with distributional shift and scaling in robotics (Sec. A.5).
- **Offline Training Process (Algorithm 1):**
 1. The RWM-U world model is first trained entirely on the fixed offline dataset.
 2. In an iterative loop:
 - Initial observations are sampled from the offline dataset to seed the imagined rollouts.
 - The current policy interacts with the RWM-U model for T steps, generating imagined trajectories (e.g., 100 steps). During these rollouts, RWM-U predicts future observations and their epistemic uncertainties.
 - For each imagined transition, the uncertainty-penalized reward is computed.
 - The policy network (and value function) is updated using PPO based on these imagined trajectories and penalized rewards.

This integrated approach ensures that the policy learns from plausible, synthesized experience while being cautiously guided away from regions where the learned dynamics model is unreliable. The use of PPO provides stability, and the long-horizon, autoregressive nature of RWM-U, combined with uncertainty propagation, is crucial for complex, continuous control tasks like robot locomotion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate RWM-U and MOPO-PPO across diverse simulated and real robotic platforms, demonstrating their effectiveness and robustness.

1. **Reliable Autoregressive Uncertainty Estimation:**
 - RWM-U's epistemic uncertainty estimates (variance across ensemble predictions) align closely with the actual long-horizon prediction errors of the model (Figure 3). As the autoregressive rollout progresses and prediction errors accumulate due to compounding inaccuracies, the epistemic uncertainty also consistently increases.
 - This strong correlation validates epistemic uncertainty as a reliable "trust metric" for the dynamics model, justifying its use in penalizing policy exploration in uncertain regions. Aleatoric uncertainty, reflecting environmental stochasticity, remains low, indicating that the model effectively distinguishes between inherent noise and model uncertainty.

2. **Effectiveness of Uncertainty-Penalized Imagination:**
 - The uncertainty penalty coefficient ($\lambda$) in MOPO-PPO is critical for balancing exploration and exploitation.
 - **Small $\lambda$ (e.g., $\le$ 0.5):** Leads to policies that achieve high imagined rewards but overfit to model inaccuracies. When deployed on real robots, these policies are overconfident, unstable, and often lead to catastrophic failures (e.g., falling, collisions).
 - **Large $\lambda$ (e.g., $\ge$ 2.0):** Results in overly conservative policies that restrict exploration to low-uncertainty regions. These policies make minimal progress on real robots, often standing still or performing ineffective movements, leading to low task performance.
 - **Appropriate $\lambda$ (e.g., 1.0):** Strikes a balance, allowing the policy to explore novel behaviors in imagination while staying within trustworthy regions of the learned model. This leads to steadily increasing imagination rewards and controlled epistemic uncertainty, resulting in robust and effective policies on real hardware (Figure 4, Figure 7).

3. **Generality and Robustness Across Robotic Environments:**
 - MOPO-PPO consistently outperforms model-free baselines (CQL) and uncertainty-unaware model-based baselines (MBPO, MBPO-PPO) across a range of tasks: Reach-Franka (manipulation), Velocity-ANYmal-D (quadruped locomotion), and Velocity-Unitree-G1 (humanoid locomotion).
 - The performance advantage is particularly pronounced in complex locomotion tasks (quadruped and humanoid) where baselines, especially uncertainty-unaware MBRL methods, often suffer from catastrophic failures due to overfitting to model inaccuracies (Figure 5).
 - Policies trained on "Mixed" datasets (combining replay buffer data from various PPO training stages) generally yield the highest rewards, as the broader state-action coverage helps mitigate distribution shift.

4. **Offline Learning with Real-World Data and Outperformance of Online Baselines:**
 - A significant finding is that MOPO-PPO policies trained *entirely offline* on simulated data alone (1M sim, 0 real) can already surpass the performance of the policy that was used to collect that data (Table 1). This demonstrates offline MBRL's ability to distill stronger policies than those observed in the raw dataset.
 - Crucially, incorporating a *moderate amount* of real-world data alongside simulated data (e.g., 200K real + 800K sim) further boosts performance, achieving the best results (0.91 $\pm$ 0.03 normalized reward on ANYmal D). This *surpasses state-of-the-art online model-free baselines trained purely in simulation* (0.88 $\pm$ 0.01). This highlights the power of fusing real-world experience, even limited, into an offline MBRL framework.
 - Excessive reliance on real-world data alone (e.g., 600K real + 400K sim) can reduce performance, suggesting that real data, while mitigating domain shift, may lack the diverse, risky, or failure-mode transitions that are valuable for robust world model and policy learning, which simulation can provide.

5. **First-time Physical Robot Deployment:**
 - The paper claims the **first demonstration of uncertainty-penalized offline MBRL operating full-scale tasks on physical quadruped (ANYmal D) and humanoid (Unitree G1) robots**, with policies trained entirely from offline datasets. This is a substantial step towards practical deployment of offline RL in complex robotics.
 - Supplementary videos on their project webpage visually confirm the robust locomotion behaviors achieved on hardware (Figure 6, Figure 7).

In summary, the main findings confirm that RWM-U provides reliable uncertainty estimates, MOPO-PPO effectively leverages this uncertainty for robust policy learning, and the combined pipeline generalizes well across diverse robotic tasks and data sources, achieving unprecedented offline MBRL performance and deployment on real robots.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning for robotics, with broad implications and substantial potential impact:

1. **Enabling Practical Offline RL for Real Robots:** The most immediate and profound impact is the demonstration that fully offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) can effectively train policies that are robustly deployable on physical, high-dimensional robots (quadrupeds and humanoids). This addresses a long-standing challenge in the field, moving offline RL beyond simulation benchmarks and into tangible hardware applications. This is explicitly stated as the first demonstration of uncertainty-penalized offline MBRL on physical hardware for full-scale tasks.
2. **Revolutionizing Data Reuse in Robotics:** The framework provides a principled method for reusing diverse, pre-existing robotic data, including simulation logs and real-world trajectories, across different tasks. This can drastically reduce the exorbitant costs and time associated with data collection in robotics, which typically requires extensive online interaction for each new task. It shifts the paradigm from isolated task development to a more integrated approach that leverages accumulated robotic experience.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Safety in Robotic Control:** By explicitly quantifying and propagating epistemic uncertainty over long imagined rollouts, the method ensures that policies learn cautiously, avoiding regions of the state-action space where the dynamics model is unreliable. This "uncertainty-aware conservatism" is crucial for developing safer and more reliable robot controllers, a paramount concern for real-world deployment in sensitive applications.
4. **A Hybrid Paradigm Outperforming Pure Sim-to-Real:** The finding that policies trained on a *fusion* of simulated and moderate real-world offline data can outperform state-of-the-art *online model-free policies trained purely in simulation* is highly significant. It suggests a powerful hybrid learning paradigm where offline training with diverse (simulated for coverage, real for domain adaptation) datasets can be more effective than traditional online sim-to-real pipelines. This opens new avenues for optimized data acquisition and training strategies.
5. **Addressing Key Challenges in MBRL:** The work tackles fundamental limitations of MBRL in offline settings, namely compounding model errors and distribution shift, through its innovative combination of autoregressive world modeling, ensemble-based uncertainty estimation, and uncertainty-penalized policy optimization adapted to the stable PPO framework.
6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work lays a robust foundation for future research in several directions, including:
 - Exploring more sophisticated uncertainty estimation techniques within world models.
 - Developing methods for intelligent data curation and augmentation for offline datasets, especially for real-world data that might lack risky but informative transitions.
 - Investigating how this framework can be extended to handle multi-task learning or rapid adaptation with limited new data.
 - Understanding the fundamental trade-offs between realism (real data) and diversity (sim data, or varied behaviors) in offline robotic datasets.

**Potential Societal and Ethical Impacts (as acknowledged by authors):**
The authors responsibly outline the dual-use potential of generalizable control methods. Positively, the framework can benefit domains such as **healthcare, disaster response, and logistics** by enabling more robust and cost-effective robot deployment. It also has the potential to **reduce environmental and hardware costs** associated with physical experimentation. However, the authors acknowledge potential risks, including the misuse of such advanced autonomy in **surveillance or autonomous enforcement systems**, and the acceleration of **automation in labor-sensitive sectors**. Mitigation strategies, such as policy training entirely in imagination and validation under domain shifts, along with explicit modeling of failure events, are highlighted to ensure safety.

Overall, this paper represents a significant step forward for offline RL in robotics, demonstrating a practical and robust solution that could transform how robotic policies are developed and deployed in the real world.
