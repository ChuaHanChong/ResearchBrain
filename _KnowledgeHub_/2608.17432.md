# UniReflex: Plug-and-Play Force Control for Pretrained Generative Policies via Fast-Slow Reflex

> [!summary] Summary
> UniReflex is a plug-and-play framework that adds closed-loop variable impedance force control to frozen generative manipulation policies (DP, VLA, WAM) by hooking pre-projection latent features from the action head and driving a lightweight fast reflex GRU, without any backbone fine-tuning. It gates between position-dominant and force-dominant execution, improving contact-rich task success 20-60 points while cutting training backward latency 25-66x.

## Problem

- Generative imitation policies predict kinematic motion with fixed-gain tracking, so small geometry or disturbance variations destabilize contact-rich skills like wiping, insertion, and peeling.
- Existing force-aware methods (observation fusion, hybrid force-position control, fast-slow tactile hierarchies) require end-to-end training or architectural refits, incompatible with reusing frozen foundation policies.
- Discarding or heavily fine-tuning large pretrained vision-only policies to add force modalities is inefficient and risks destroying their generalized learned distributions.

## Method

- ==Non-invasive latent interception==: a forward hook on the action head's pre-projection decoder extracts a unified slow-intent representation shared across diffusion, flow-matching, and VLA/WAM backbones.
- ==Force-stiffness decoupling==: a causal ==GRU== reflex jointly predicts normalized anisotropic stiffness directions and reference contact forces, supervised via teleoperation-derived Cartesian stiffness labels.
- ==Adaptive contact-triggered phase gating== switches control emphasis from frozen-policy pose tracking (Phase I) to reflex-driven variable impedance force regulation (Phase II) upon detected contact.

## Results

- Across five contact-rich benchmarks with DP, pi0, and DreamZero backbones, UniReflex improves Phase II (contact) success by **20-60 percentage points** while changing Phase I success by at most **10 points**.
- UniReflex+DP and UniReflex+pi0 meet or beat monolithic force-aware baselines RDP, ForceVLA, and TA-VLA on most contact-heavy cells while preserving Phase I alignment quality that those baselines degrade.
- Ablations show removing learned stiffness modulation drops Phase II success from **70-90%** to **20-50%** and raises force error from **5.8-8.9%** to **28.3-38.7%**; reflex training is **25-66x** faster than joint fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Late pre-projection latents in generative action heads carry recoverable multimodal intent, letting a small add-on module tap high-level planning without touching backbone weights.
> - Decoupling stiffness direction from reference force magnitude gives more physically consistent contact regulation than fixed or wrench-only impedance schemes.
> - Contact-triggered gating, not continuous fast-network authority, is essential: an ungated reflex network degrades pre-contact position accuracy that gating otherwise fully preserves.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative imitation learning policies, such as Diffusion Policy (DP), vision-language-action (VLA) models, and world action models (WAMs), have achieved proficiency in visuomotor manipulation, particularly in tasks involving trajectory planning for tabletop and pick-and-place scenarios. These models typically predict kinematic motion targets, which are then tracked by low-level controllers with fixed gains.

However, a limitation of these policies is their performance in contact-rich, force-sensitive tasks, including wiping, insertion, peeling, and sustained pressing. In such scenarios, minor variations in geometry, compliance, or disturbances can destabilize contact, an issue not effectively resolved solely through data scaling. This highlights a gap between purely geometric motion planning and the physical demands of robotic interaction.

To address contact-rich manipulation, a distinct line of research incorporates force information into learning and control. These approaches generally fall into three categories:
1. **Force-conditioned imitation learning**: Force/torque or tactile sensor data is used as additional input observations, while the policy continues to predict trajectory-like actions.
2. **Hybrid force-position/variable impedance control**: Visuomotor policies are coupled with controllers that explicitly regulate certain degrees of freedom in force and others in pose.
3. **Fast-slow hierarchies**: These decouple slower visual reasoning from high-rate contact feedback, often using reactive modules or cascaded decoders.

A recurring challenge across these existing methods is the requirement for end-to-end training, architectural modifications, or joint optimization of the entire system. This presents a practical dilemma in the context of foundation models, where highly capable generative policies are pre-trained on extensive, often vision-only, datasets. Modifying or extensively fine-tuning these large pre-trained models to accommodate new force modalities is computationally expensive, risks diminishing their generalized distributions, and can lead to catastrophic forgetting.

UniReflex positions itself by addressing this specific challenge: providing a lightweight, plug-and-play module for closed-loop contact regulation that can be integrated with frozen, pre-trained generative policies without requiring their fine-tuning or architectural redesign. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level visual intent from foundation models and robust physical interaction in contact-rich tasks through a decoupled approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a universal plug-and-play framework, named UniReflex, that can equip frozen generative imitation learning policies with variable impedance control (VIC) for closed-loop force regulation during contact-rich manipulation.

The motivation for this objective stems from several factors:
- **Limitations of existing generative policies**: While powerful for trajectory planning, current generative policies lack explicit closed-loop force regulation, rendering them unreliable for tasks requiring sustained contact or force interaction.
- **Inefficiency of current force-aware integration**: Existing methods for incorporating force control into robotic policies often necessitate end-to-end training or architectural modifications of the entire network. This approach is computationally intensive and impractical for large, pre-trained foundation models (e.g., VLAs), which are trained on vast datasets and are expensive to fine-tune or re-train.
- **Preservation of pre-trained models**: There is a motivation to preserve the well-generalized distributions and learned capabilities of pre-trained generative policies. Subjecting them to extensive fine-tuning for force modalities risks degrading their performance on tasks they were initially designed for.
- **Specialized nature of force feedback**: Force feedback is often a specialized and relatively sparse observation. The motivation is to integrate it as an independent module rather than conflating it with the generalized visual-kinematic planning.
- **Scalability**: The goal is to offer a scalable alternative to monolithic end-to-end training pipelines, enabling the efficient adaptation of powerful generative policies for robust physical interaction.

Specifically, UniReflex aims to achieve the following:
- Non-invasively intercept deep latent representations from the action head of frozen generative policies to derive "slow intent."
- Drive a fast reflex network that decouples active force exertion from external interaction response.
- Predict normalized anisotropic stiffness directions for directional compliance allocation based on force-direction intent collected during demonstration.
- Integrate an adaptive gating mechanism to facilitate seamless transitions between position-dominant planning and force-dominant execution phases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniReflex is structured as a hierarchical control framework comprising a slow vision-driven planner (the frozen generative policy), a fast proprioceptive reflex network, and a real-time execution layer. The core methodology involves non-invasive integration of force control without modifying the pre-trained generative backbone.

#### 3.1. Active Effort Recovery and Variable-Stiffness Labeling

Supervision signals for the reflex network are derived from bilateral teleoperation demonstrations. The interaction-force intent ($F_{intent}$) is approximated from the mismatch between the master and slave end-effector poses ($\Delta x = x_m - x_s$) during teleoperation. This mismatch, scaled by an effective Cartesian stiffness map ($K_{virtual}$), provides a loggable proxy for the human operator's intended interaction force.

To generate stiffness targets, the principal effort axis, $n = \Delta x / \parallel \Delta x \parallel$, is extracted from the teleoperation mismatch. This axis indicates the direction of intended force exertion. This intent is then projected into a decoupled diagonal stiffness template, $K_{label} = diag(|n_x|, |n_y|, |n_z|) + \epsilon I$, where $\epsilon > 0$ ensures positive-definiteness. This labeling strategy encodes anisotropic stiffness magnitudes, implying that stronger effort along a particular direction in the demonstration should result in a stiffer axis in the robot's impedance control, enhancing contact stability using only wrist force/torque sensing.

#### 3.2. Non-Invasive Internal Representation Interception

UniReflex leverages a common pattern in the action heads of generative policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy, $\pi^0$, DreamZero). Regardless of whether they predict noise (diffusion) or flow velocities (flow matching), these policies form a temporally structured hidden representation before the final action projection. This internal state, particularly near the end of the reverse synthesis process (where the sampled trajectory geometry is largely committed), encapsulates a comprehensive understanding of the trajectory.

UniReflex intercepts this pre-projection state by registering a forward hook on the decoder's aggregation function ($\Phi_{dec}$). This extracts a unified latent representation, $z \in R^{B \times T_{slow} \times H}$, where $B$ is batch size, $T_{slow}$ is the action horizon, and $H$ is the hidden dimension. This hook is read-only, ensuring that the frozen backbone weights of the generative policy remain unaltered. This approach enables UniReflex to function as a backend-agnostic module across diverse generative policy architectures, providing a "slow intent" interface without requiring architectural modifications or fine-tuning of the generative backbone.

#### 3.3. Intent-Conditioned State Observation and Reflex Decoding

To address the latency gap between the slow generative policy's chunked action predictions and the high-frequency requirements of contact dynamics, a fast reflex network ($\phi_{reflex}$) is employed. This network emits a single-step update during each control cycle.

At each fast control step $t$, the reflex network receives an input $u_t$ constructed by concatenating three encoded features:
- **Step-wise slow intent condition ($s_t$)**: Derived from the temporally aligned latent representation $z'_t$, which is extracted from the generative policy.
- **Real-time feedback ($r_t$)**: Encoded proprioceptive observations ($e_t$).
- **Learnable step positional embedding ($p_t$)**: An embedding indicating the current phase within the slow action chunk ($Emb(t)$).

A causal temporal model (implemented as a GRU) processes $u_t$ along with its previous internal state $h_{t-1}$ to update its current internal state $h_t$. Finally, a decoder function maps the current internal state ($h_t$) and input ($u_t$) to three execution primitives:
- **$x_{reflex}$**: A single-step pose correction that can override the chunk after contact to mitigate geometric drift.
- **$F_{ref}$**: The desired contact wrench for sustained normal-pressure tracking.
- **$\Phi_K$**: Encodes end-effector stiffness, supporting the decoupling of the direction and magnitude of applied effort.

#### 3.4. Contact-Triggered Phase Gating

At deployment, UniReflex employs an adaptive gating mechanism to switch between two execution regimes:
- **Phase I (Position-dominant planning)**: Before sustained contact is detected, the low-level controller prioritizes tracking the pose targets generated by the frozen generative policy. This ensures accurate approach and alignment.
- **Phase II (Force-dominant execution)**: Once proprioceptive sensing detects contact, the system transitions emphasis to regulating the predicted reference force ($F_{ref}$) and the impedance parameters specified by $\Phi_K$ via the VIC interface. During this phase, $x_{reflex}$ corrections are still streamed to account for any geometric drift. This gating mechanism allows the robot to leverage the generative policy's planning quality while acquiring robust regulation capabilities during interaction.

The training process for UniReflex involves only the lightweight reflex network, keeping the generative backbone frozen throughout.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate UniReflex across five contact-rich benchmarks (Wipe (Curve), Wipe (Incline), Plug Charger, Peel Note, Flip Chip Box) using three mainstream generative backbones: Diffusion Policy (DP), $\pi^0$, and DreamZero.

#### 4.1. Task Performance Across Execution Phases (Q1)

- **Preservation of non-contact performance**: UniReflex-augmented policies generally maintained the generative backbones' performance during Phase I (non-contact visuomotor behavior). The change in Phase I success rate was observed to be within 10 percentage points relative to the vanilla backbones.
- **Improvement in contact-stage performance**: In Phase II (sustained contact), UniReflex demonstrated significant improvements in success rates, ranging from 20 to 60 percentage points across all tasks and backbones.
- **Task-specific observations**: The "Plug Charger" task showed less pronounced improvement, which was attributed to its reliance on brief contact transients and tight insertion tolerances rather than sustained force regulation.

#### 4.2. Force-Dominated Execution vs. Monolithic Baselines (Q2)

- UniReflex+DP and UniReflex+$\pi^0$ either matched or improved Phase I alignment quality compared to their vanilla counterparts.
- Conversely, end-to-end force-aware monolithic baselines (RDP, ForceVLA, TA-VLA) often exhibited degraded performance in Phase I, with success rates trailing by tens of points or being consistently lower.
- In Phase II, UniReflex+DP and UniReflex+$\pi^0$ achieved or surpassed the success rates of the best monolithic baselines, indicating superior performance in force-dominated execution.

#### 4.3. Component Contributions (Ablation Studies) (Q3)

Ablation studies conducted with a frozen DP backbone on Peel Note, Wipe (Curve), and Wipe (Incline) revealed the following:
- **Importance of learned force/stiffness outputs**:
 - Disabling learned stiffness modulation, using fixed isotropic stiffness, or only predicting wrench (without stiffness direction) led to substantial drops in Phase II success (from 70-90% to 20-50%) and increased contact-stage force tracking error (from 5.8-8.9% to 28.3-38.7%).
 - A manually designed impedance controller performed better than these reduced learned variants but still below the full UniReflex formulation. These results indicate that learned anisotropic stiffness modulation is crucial for robust contact performance.
- **Role of residual/gating controls**:
 - Using a gated $\Delta x$ GRU (retaining the gate but replacing learned stiffness with $\Delta x$-based force regulation) preserved Phase I success but limited Phase II success to 40-60%. This suggests that $\Delta x$-based regulation accumulates pose error under contact constraints.
 - An ungated GRU (fast network active throughout both phases) drastically reduced both Phase I and Phase II success rates (0-30% and 0-10% respectively), confirming the necessity of the adaptive gating mechanism to protect the frozen planner before contact is established.

#### 4.4. High-Frequency Recovery under Disturbances (Q4)

- Under dynamic disturbances (e.g., continuous adjustment of support inclination, pressing the board, perturbing the chip box), UniReflex demonstrated high recovery rates (80-100%) and re-established target contact force within 0.87-1.28 seconds.
- In contrast, frozen DP policies recovered only 10-20% of executions under similar dynamic disturbances.
- UniReflex also exhibited compensatory capabilities for bounded approach errors, recovering Phase II execution in 70-90% of trials with a 1-2 cm pre-contact TCP bias.

#### 4.5. Interaction-Time Force Tracking (Q5)

- UniReflex-augmented policies tracked demonstration force trajectories more accurately and closely during contact-rich tasks like peeling compared to frozen baselines.
- This improved force tracking stability was identified as a contributor to higher Phase II success rates. Specifically, UniReflex consistently aligned measured 3D forces with predicted references, maintaining contact, whereas frozen DP failed to keep forces within a stable contact band.

#### 4.6. Training Throughput and Memory Efficiency (Q6)

- Training only the fast GRU network in UniReflex (Stage II) resulted in significant computational efficiency gains. It reduced backward-pass latency by 25-66 times compared to the estimated cost of joint fine-tuning the entire generative stack.
- The reflex network's parameter count was remarkably small, representing only 0.188-0.375% of the backbone parameters. This led to substantial reductions in optimizer-state storage and activation traffic, making the approach memory efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

UniReflex addresses a fundamental limitation in applying powerful generative imitation learning policies to real-world robotic tasks requiring physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the planning-execution gap**: The framework effectively bridges the gap between the high-level trajectory planning capabilities of large-scale generative models and the low-level, high-frequency demands of robust physical contact. This extends the applicability of these models to a wider range of manipulation tasks that were previously unreliable.
- **Leveraging pre-trained foundation models**: By enabling plug-and-play integration with frozen generative policies, UniReflex maximizes the utility of existing pre-trained foundation models (VLAs, WAMs) without requiring expensive re-training or fine-tuning. This preserves the valuable generalized distributions learned by these models while adding a crucial physical interaction capability.
- **Efficiency and scalability**: The fast-only training approach offers substantial computational and memory efficiencies. This makes integrating force control a practical and scalable endeavor, especially for increasingly large generative backbones, by avoiding the prohibitive costs associated with end-to-end retraining.
- **Enhanced robustness and reliability**: The demonstrated improvements in contact stability, success rates, force tracking, and recovery from disturbances directly translate to more reliable and robust robotic systems in contact-rich environments. This can lead to increased task autonomy and reduced failure rates in applications such as assembly, inspection, and service robotics.
- **Generalizable methodology**: The non-invasive interception of deep latent representations and the decoupled fast-slow architecture represent a generalizable paradigm. This approach could potentially be extended to integrate other specialized modalities (e.g., haptic feedback, proprioception beyond force) into complex, pre-trained models without disrupting their core functionality.
- **Future robot capabilities**: The capability to seamlessly transition between position-dominant planning and force-dominant execution enables robots to perform more dexterous and human-like manipulation, adapting to dynamic and uncertain contact conditions.

**Limitations**: The paper acknowledges limitations, including smaller gains for long-horizon world-action backbones (due to slow action chunk refresh rates) and reduced efficacy for brief, high-impulse rigid insertion tasks. Furthermore, overall responsiveness remains constrained by the quality of force sensing, the low-level controller, and hardware bandwidth. Despite these, UniReflex represents a notable advancement in making advanced generative policies more capable in physical interaction.
