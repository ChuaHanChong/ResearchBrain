# Backpropagation-Free Test-Time Adaptation via Probabilistic Gaussian Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> A method named ADAPT, developed by researchers at Sungkyunkwan University, improves Vision-Language Model robustness to distribution shifts through a backpropagation-free, probabilistic Gaussian alignment approach. It achieves an average accuracy of 66.53% in online settings on natural distribution shifts, outperforming prior methods while reducing inference time on ImageNet to 1h 11m, compared to over 9 hours for optimization-based techniques.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP experience significant performance degradation when encountering real-world data outside their pre-training distribution.
- Current Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods often rely on computationally intensive backpropagation or iterative optimization, limiting real-time deployment and resource efficiency.
- Gradient-free TTA approaches frequently lack explicit modeling of class-conditional feature distributions, leading to unstable decision boundaries and less calibrated predictions.

## Method

- **ADAPT** reframes Test-Time Adaptation as a probabilistic inference task, utilizing ==Gaussian Discriminant Analysis== (GDA) to model class-conditional feature distributions.
- It employs ==knowledge banks== — dynamically updated buffers storing high-confidence samples — to guide source-free and unsupervised adaptation.
- The method derives a ==closed-form label estimator== and parameter updates, enabling a one-pass, backpropagation-free solution for efficient inference without iterative optimization.

## Results

- ADAPT achieved **66.53%** average accuracy in online settings and **67.09%** in transductive settings on natural distribution shift benchmarks, outperforming all prior SotA methods.
- Improved corruption robustness to **28.56%** accuracy on ImageNet-C, and reduced inference time to **1h 11m** on ImageNet (vs. 9+ hours for optimization-based alternatives).
- Consistent gains across VLM backbones (CLIP-RN50, BLIP, ALBEF) and fine-grained categorization and few-shot adaptation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained Vision-Language Model features, when conditioned on their respective classes, can be effectively approximated by Gaussian distributions, facilitating efficient probabilistic inference.
> - Utilizing dynamically maintained "knowledge banks" of high-confidence pseudo-labeled samples provides stable, source-free, and supervision-free statistics for robust adaptation, mitigating noise from early predictions.
> - A closed-form solution for parameter estimation and label prediction can achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, delivering significant computational efficiency over iterative, backpropagation-based Test-Time Adaptation methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the challenge of enhancing the robustness of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP, when they encounter data from test-time distributions that differ from their pre-training data. This phenomenon, known as distribution shift, can significantly degrade the zero-shot performance of VLMs. Test-time adaptation (TTA) has emerged as a promising approach to mitigate this by leveraging unlabeled test data during inference.

The broader research landscape of TTA can be categorized by several characteristics:

- **Optimization-based vs. Training-free approaches:**
 - **Optimization-based methods** (e.g., prompt tuning, adapter tuning) refine additional parameters or layers through iterative optimization and backpropagation. While effective, these methods incur high computational and memory costs, limiting their applicability in real-time or streaming scenarios.
 - **Training-free approaches** aim for efficiency by avoiding backpropagation. Examples include methods that leverage memory banks for sample similarity-based adaptation or propagate labels via graph construction. However, these often require careful hyperparameter tuning, access to external datasets, or still involve iterative updates.
- **Inductive (Online) vs. Transductive learning:**
 - **Online TTA** processes samples sequentially, adapting the model with access to current and past data but not future data. This is crucial for streaming applications.
 - **Transductive learning** assumes full or partial access to the entire target test dataset during inference, allowing for global structure capture. While effective in offline batch processing, these methods are often unsuitable for strict online environments.
- **Explicit distribution modeling vs. implicit similarity matching:** Many existing TTA methods rely on text-based prototypes or adjust similarity scores without explicitly modeling class-conditional feature distributions. This can result in unstable decision boundaries and reduced robustness, as they may not effectively capture intra-class variation or inter-class confusion.

This work, ADAPT, positions itself as a novel solution within this landscape by addressing several unfulfilled aspects simultaneously. It aims to provide a **backpropagation-free and distribution-aware** TTA method that supports both **online and transductive settings** without requiring source data, external supervision, or iterative optimization. By explicitly modeling class-conditional feature distributions under a Gaussian assumption, ADAPT seeks to offer a more robust and efficient alternative to existing methods, which often fall short in either computational efficiency or explicit statistical modeling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an advanced test-time adaptation method, named ADAPT, that enhances the zero-shot robustness of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) under various distribution shifts. This objective is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations observed in the existing TTA landscape:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency:**
 - **Motivation:** A significant drawback of many current TTA methods is their reliance on backpropagation or iterative optimization procedures during inference. This computational overhead limits their scalability and makes them unsuitable for real-time deployment, edge devices, or scenarios with streaming data where low latency is critical.
 - **Objective:** To design a TTA framework that is entirely backpropagation-free and offers a closed-form, one-pass solution for adaptation, thereby ensuring high efficiency and scalability.

2. **Incorporating Explicit Class-Conditional Distribution Modeling:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing TTA approaches primarily depend on text-based prototypes or similarity scores to define decision boundaries. They often lack explicit modeling of class-conditional feature distributions, which is crucial for understanding intra-class variation and inter-class confusion in the feature space. This absence can lead to less reliable decision boundaries and uncalibrated predictions, especially under significant distribution shifts where feature clusters might move or deform.
 - **Objective:** To explicitly model class-conditional feature distributions using a probabilistic framework. Specifically, the authors propose reframing TTA as a Gaussian probabilistic inference task, assuming that class-conditional features can be approximated by Gaussian distributions with a shared covariance matrix. This aims to produce more stable and accurate decision boundaries.

3. **Enabling Adaptation without Source Data or Full Target Data Access:**
 - **Motivation:** Current transductive TTA methods often assume access to the entire target test dataset, which is not feasible in online or streaming scenarios where samples arrive sequentially. Moreover, some methods require access to the source training data or external datasets, further limiting their general applicability in real-world settings where such data might be unavailable due to privacy, storage, or computational constraints.
 - **Objective:** To develop a TTA method that requires no source data, no full access to target data (for the online setting), and no external supervision. This adaptability allows the method to support both stringent online (sample-by-sample) and batch-based transductive settings, expanding its practical deployment flexibility.

4. **Mitigating Likelihood Bias and Ensuring Stability:**
 - **Motivation:** In online settings with limited samples, early predictions can be overconfident or unreliable, leading to biased likelihood estimates if directly used for adaptation.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a lightweight regularization mechanism that corrects potential likelihood bias. This involves leveraging CLIP priors for semantic consistency and maintaining a historical knowledge bank of high-confidence samples to provide stable, context-aware regularization and prevent error accumulation.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between efficient, deployable TTA and robust, statistically grounded adaptation by developing a method that is both computationally lightweight and distributionally aware, capable of operating effectively across diverse data access scenarios without relying on extensive external resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ADAPT reframes Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) as a Gaussian probabilistic inference task, enabling a backpropagation-free and training-free solution that supports both online and transductive settings. The methodology involves a generative model, Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA), for classification, combined with mechanisms for robust parameter estimation and bias correction using a knowledge bank and CLIP priors.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Zero-Shot Classification with VLMs:** The method utilizes pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, which provide image embeddings ($x_i = \theta_v(x_i)$) and text embeddings for class names ($t_k = \theta_t(t_k)$). Initial zero-shot predictions ($\hat{y}_{i,k}$) are made by computing cosine similarity between image and class prototypes, scaled by a temperature parameter $\tau$.
- **Constructed Knowledge Banks:** To enable source-free test-time adaptation, ADAPT maintains a set of fixed-size, class-wise knowledge banks ($B = \{B_k\}_{k=1}^K$). Each $B_k$ stores a small set of high-confidence features from previously seen test samples associated with pseudo-class $k$. Confidence is measured using the negative entropy of the CLIP-derived prediction. A new sample $x_i$ with pseudo-class $k$ is added to $B_k$ if $B_k$ is not full or if its confidence exceeds the lowest-confidence entry in $B_k$. This priority-based mechanism ensures the banks remain fresh and informative.

#### 3.2. ADAPT: Backpropagation-free and Distribution-aware TTA

- **Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA):** ADAPT adopts GDA as its core probabilistic classifier. It assumes that features conditioned on class $k$ follow a Gaussian distribution with a class mean $\mu_k$ and a shared covariance matrix $\Sigma$: $P(x_i|y_k) = \mathcal{N}(x_i; \mu_k, \Sigma)$. This assumption allows for closed-form inference.
 - Under a uniform class prior, the Bayes optimal prediction label for $x_i$ is derived from the argmax of a linear discriminant function: $\tilde{y}_{i,k} = w_k^\top x_i + b_k$, where $w_k = \Sigma^{-1}\mu_k$ and $b_k = -\frac{1}{2}\mu_k^\top \Sigma^{-1}\mu_k$. The parameters $\mu_k$ and $\Sigma$ are estimated in an optimization-free manner, initially.

- **Correcting Online Likelihood Bias via Constructed Knowledge Banks:** To address biases from limited samples and overconfident early predictions in online settings, ADAPT minimizes a regularized objective, $L_{\text{online}}(z_i, \mu, \Sigma)$, for each incoming test sample $x_i$. This objective combines three components:
 1. **Online Negative Log-Likelihood:** Encourages the predicted class distribution $z_i$ to align with the class-conditional Gaussian likelihoods $P_i$.
 2. **CLIP Prior-based Regularization:** Introduces a prior over $z_i$ using the zero-shot CLIP prediction $\hat{y}_i$ (KL divergence $KL(z_i \| \hat{y}_i)$). It also softly constrains the learned GDA parameters ($\mu_k, \Sigma$) to stay close to CLIP-derived estimates ($\hat{\mu}_k, \hat{\Sigma}_k$), stabilizing adaptation.
 3. **Knowledge Bank-guided Consistency Regularization:** Utilizes the historical knowledge banks $B$ to provide stable regularization. It enforces consistency between GDA likelihoods $P_j$ and their pseudo-labels $\hat{y}_j$ within $B$, and aligns the current prediction $z_i$ with these pseudo-labels, weighted by cosine similarity $w_{ij}$. This term helps mitigate single-sample update bias.

- **Closed-form Solution without Sub-iterations:** The regularized online objective allows for a closed-form label estimator for $z_i$:
 $z^*_{i,k} = \frac{\hat{y}_{i,k} \cdot \exp(\tilde{y}_{i,k} + \sum_{j \in B_k} w_{ij} \hat{y}_{j,k})}{\sum_{K=1}^K \hat{y}_{i,k} \cdot \exp(\tilde{y}_{i,k} + \sum_{j \in B_k} w_{ij} \hat{y}_{j,k})}$.
 This formula fuses CLIP zero-shot logits, GDA predictions, and knowledge bank consistency.

- **Estimation of Class Means ($\mu_k$) and Shared Covariance ($\Sigma$):**
 - **Class Means:** To ensure stability and prevent early overfitting, the current instance $x_i$ is excluded from the online class mean estimation. Instead, $\mu_k$ is estimated as a weighted combination of the empirical mean of high-confidence features in $B_k$ (denoted $\mu'_k$) and the CLIP prior mean $\hat{\mu}_k$. The weighting factor $\alpha$ balances these two sources. $\hat{\mu}_k$ is typically initialized from CLIP prototypes.
 - **Shared Covariance:** A Bayesian ridge estimator with shrinkage regularization is employed to approximate the shared covariance matrix $\Sigma$. This addresses the challenge of directly inverting potentially ill-conditioned empirical covariance matrices in high-dimensional feature spaces, ensuring numerical stability without source data. $\hat{\Sigma}_k$ is initialized from prompt variance or a fixed diagonal matrix.

#### 3.3. Extension to Transductive Optimization

In the transductive setting, where the entire test set $D_u$ is available, the objective $L_{\text{trans}}(z, \mu, \Sigma)$ extends the online objective by summing over all samples.
- **Label Estimator:** The closed-form solution for $z_i^*$ remains identical to the online case (Equation above).
- **Class Means:** For transductive mean estimation, ADAPT uses a one-pass estimate by substituting the latent soft labels $z_i$ with CLIP's zero-shot predictions $\hat{y}_i$, combined with high-confidence samples from the knowledge bank and the CLIP prior. This avoids iterative updates while leveraging global information.
- **Shared Covariance:** The same covariance matrix estimation strategy as in the online setting is applied.

#### 3.4. Algorithms Summary

- **Algorithm 1 (Online ADAPT):** Iterates through test samples $x_i$ sequentially. For each $x_i$, it computes confidence, conditionally updates class-wise knowledge banks $B_k$, updates $\mu_k^*$ and $\Sigma$, and then computes $z_i^*$.
- **Algorithm 2 (Transductive ADAPT):** Computes confidence for all test samples, builds knowledge banks $B_k$ from the top-L confident samples per class from the full test set, updates $\Sigma$ and $\mu_k^*$ once using all test data and the banks, and then computes $z_i^*$ for all samples.

Both algorithms are designed to be fully optimization-free, relying on one-pass closed-form updates for efficient adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results demonstrating the performance, efficiency, and robustness of ADAPT across various benchmarks and settings.

#### 4.1. Performance across Diverse Tasks

- **Natural Distribution Shift (Table 2):** ADAPT achieved an average accuracy of 66.53% in the online setting, outperforming all compared online methods, including those reliant on backpropagation (e.g., TPT, DPE) and other backpropagation-free approaches (e.g., TDA, BCA). In the transductive setting, ADAPT further improved to 67.09%, surpassing several strong transductive methods (e.g., TransCLIP, Frolic) on the OOD (Out-of-Distribution) average.
- **Corruption Robustness (Table 3):** Under a variety of synthetic corruptions (ImageNet-C), ADAPT demonstrated superior robustness. It achieved 28.56% accuracy in the online setting and 30.29% in the transductive setting, consistently outperforming all baselines across different corruption types.
- **Fine-Grained Categorization (Table 4):** For fine-grained recognition datasets, ADAPT showed strong generalization. In the online setting, it achieved 70.76% average accuracy, surpassing the CLIP zero-shot baseline by 7.31% and ranking highest among backpropagation-free methods. In the transductive setting, it reached 72.43%, a 8.98% improvement over CLIP, and remained competitive with methods using full target data, narrowing the gap to Oracle performance (77.09%) to within 5%.

#### 4.2. Efficiency and Scalability

- **Cost Comparison (Table 6):** ADAPT exhibited superior computational efficiency. For ImageNet, in the online setting, it achieved high accuracy with 1 hour 11 minutes of computation and 0.93 GB memory, significantly lower than backpropagation-based methods like TPT (9h 45m, 4.29GB) and DiffTPT (>20h, 4.60GB). In the transductive setting, it required only 0.73 minutes and 3.37 GB, outperforming other transductive methods in speed.
- **Closed-form vs. Iterative Optimization (Table 8):** The proposed closed-form solution matched the accuracy of iterative optimization methods while providing a 4x speedup in the online scenario and also demonstrating efficiency gains in the transductive setting.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Component Ablation (Table 5 & 17):** The knowledge bank (B) was identified as a crucial component, significantly improving performance and especially stabilizing the covariance update process. Jointly updating both class means ($\mu$) and shared covariance ($\Sigma$) yielded the best results, highlighting their complementary roles. Using the knowledge bank for regularization (R(z_i; B)) was shown to be highly effective.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (Figure 2):** Online performance was sensitive to small knowledge bank sizes (L) but stabilized for L ≥ 8. The transductive setting was robust to L. Performance generally improved with a larger $\alpha$ (weight for historical samples), indicating the benefit of reliable historical data over static CLIP priors.
- **Mean Initialization (Table 7):** While diverse and descriptive CLIP text prototypes improved performance, ADAPT demonstrated robustness across different initialization choices for class means.
- **Online Input Order (Table 9):** Processing samples from easy-to-hard confidence generally performed best, but the default random order still yielded competitive results, indicating the method's robustness.
- **Visualization (Figure 3):** Visualization of decision boundaries demonstrated that ADAPT, by explicitly modeling Gaussian distributions, created more compact class clusters and smoother decision boundaries compared to methods relying purely on feature similarity.
- **Robustness under Low-confidence (Table 21):** The online method was robust with few samples (4-6) per class, while transductive ADAPT maintained high performance even with just two high-confidence samples per class.
- **Robustness under Significant Shifts (Table 20):** ADAPT consistently outperformed baselines across increasing severity levels of synthetic corruptions.
- **Long-tailed Adaptation (Table 23):** ADAPT showed stronger self-correction capabilities and achieved higher accuracy than CLIP under long-tailed adaptation scenarios with early noisy predictions.

#### 4.4. Generalizability across Backbones and VLMs

- **CLIP-RN50 Backbone (Tables 14-16):** ADAPT consistently outperformed baselines when using the CLIP-RN50 backbone across natural distribution shift, corruption robustness, and fine-grained categorization tasks, reaffirming its effectiveness irrespective of backbone architecture.
- **Different VLMs (Figure 6):** ADAPT demonstrated consistent performance improvements across various VLMs, including CLIP (ViT-B/16 and RN50), BLIP, and ALBEF, indicating its broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on ADAPT presents several significant contributions and has considerable potential impact on the practical deployment of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in real-world scenarios:

1. **Bridging Efficiency and Robustness in TTA:** ADAPT offers a method that is both computationally efficient (backpropagation-free and closed-form) and robust (distribution-aware and memory-guided). This addresses a critical trade-off in Test-Time Adaptation, where many methods either achieve high accuracy at the cost of significant computational overhead or are efficient but lack robustness under severe shifts. This balance is crucial for applications requiring real-time inference or operating on resource-constrained platforms.

2. **Enhanced Reliability of Decision Boundaries:** By explicitly modeling class-conditional feature distributions using a Gaussian assumption, ADAPT provides a statistically grounded approach to defining decision boundaries. This is a notable improvement over methods that rely solely on text-based prototypes or similarity scores, which can lead to unstable boundaries and less calibrated predictions. The resulting smoother and more compact class clusters contribute to improved classification accuracy and reliability under distribution shifts.

3. **Broadened Applicability and Deployment Flexibility:** ADAPT's design, requiring no source data, no gradient updates, and supporting both online (sequential) and transductive (batch) settings, significantly expands its applicability. This flexibility makes it suitable for diverse operational environments, from continuous data streams (e.g., surveillance, robotics) to offline batch processing (e.g., medical imaging, document analysis). The absence of a need for source data also mitigates privacy concerns and reduces data management overhead.

4. **Practical Solution for Real-world Challenges:** The demonstrated superior performance across challenging benchmarks, including natural distribution shifts, synthetic corruptions, and fine-grained categorization, highlights ADAPT's effectiveness. Its ability to maintain robustness under low-confidence scenarios, significant shifts, and even with early noisy predictions positions it as a practical solution for real-world applications where data quality and distribution can be unpredictable.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper's re-framing of TTA as a Gaussian probabilistic inference task, along with the proposed bias correction and robust parameter estimation mechanisms, provides a new theoretical and practical foundation. This could inspire future research into exploring more flexible generative models (e.g., Gaussian mixtures or non-parametric estimators) for TTA, while maintaining the critical efficiency gains achieved by the backpropagation-free approach. The trade-off between model complexity and computational efficiency remains an area for further investigation.

In summary, ADAPT represents a step towards making TTA a more viable and widespread technique for enhancing the generalization of VLMs. Its emphasis on efficiency and distributional awareness has the potential to impact fields requiring robust and real-time vision-language understanding, from autonomous systems to advanced content analysis.
