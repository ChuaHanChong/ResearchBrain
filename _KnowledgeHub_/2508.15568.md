# Backpropagation-Free Test-Time Adaptation via Probabilistic Gaussian Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> A method named ADAPT, developed by researchers at Sungkyunkwan University, improves Vision-Language Model robustness to distribution shifts through a backpropagation-free, probabilistic Gaussian alignment approach. It achieves an average accuracy of 66.53% in online settings on natural distribution shifts, outperforming prior methods while reducing inference time on ImageNet to 1h 11m, compared to over 9 hours for optimization-based techniques.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP experience significant performance degradation when encountering real-world data outside their pre-training distribution.
- Current Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods often rely on computationally intensive backpropagation or iterative optimization, limiting real-time deployment and resource efficiency.
- Gradient-free TTA approaches frequently lack explicit modeling of class-conditional feature distributions, leading to unstable decision boundaries and less calibrated predictions.

## Method

- **ADAPT** reframes Test-Time Adaptation as a probabilistic inference task, utilizing ==Gaussian Discriminant Analysis== (GDA) to model class-conditional feature distributions.
- It employs ==knowledge banks== — dynamically updated buffers storing high-confidence samples — to guide source-free and unsupervised adaptation.
- The method derives a ==closed-form label estimator== and parameter updates, enabling a one-pass, backpropagation-free solution for efficient inference without iterative optimization.

## Results

- ADAPT achieved **66.53%** average accuracy in online settings and **67.09%** in transductive settings on natural distribution shift benchmarks, outperforming all prior SotA methods.
- Improved corruption robustness to **28.56%** accuracy on ImageNet-C, and reduced inference time to **1h 11m** on ImageNet (vs. 9+ hours for optimization-based alternatives).
- Consistent gains across VLM backbones (CLIP-RN50, BLIP, ALBEF) and fine-grained categorization and few-shot adaptation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained Vision-Language Model features, when conditioned on their respective classes, can be effectively approximated by Gaussian distributions, facilitating efficient probabilistic inference.
> - Utilizing dynamically maintained "knowledge banks" of high-confidence pseudo-labeled samples provides stable, source-free, and supervision-free statistics for robust adaptation, mitigating noise from early predictions.
> - A closed-form solution for parameter estimation and label prediction can achieve state-of-the-art accuracy, delivering significant computational efficiency over iterative, backpropagation-based Test-Time Adaptation methods.
