# SenseNova-MARS: Empowering Multimodal Agentic Reasoning and Search via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Developed by SenseTime Research, Tsinghua University, and USTC, SenseNova-MARS is a multimodal agentic reasoning and search framework enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to dynamically integrate image search, text search, and image crop tools via a novel Reinforcement Learning algorithm, BN-GSPO. This framework achieves state-of-the-art performance, including a score of 41.64 on the new HR-MMSearch, matching Gemini-3-Flash, and excels across various search-oriented and fine-grained visual understanding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision-Language Models (VLMs) primarily rely on text-based reasoning, struggling to seamlessly integrate visual analysis directly into the reasoning process.
- Existing VLM agents often invoke external tools in an isolated or sequential manner, hindering their ability to handle complex, multi-step problems requiring adaptive tool use.
- Human-like proficiency in real-world tasks demands coordinating external tools with iterative reasoning for both extensive knowledge retrieval and precise visual perception, particularly with high-resolution images.

## Method

- Introduces SenseNova-MARS, a multimodal agentic framework that dynamically integrates image search, text search, and image crop tools into a ==continuous, multi-turn reasoning process==.
- Employs a two-stage training pipeline, beginning with supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for basic tool usage, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) to refine multi-tool collaboration and adaptive reasoning.
- Develops ==BN-GSPO (Batch-Normalized Group Sequence Policy Optimization)==, a novel RL algorithm that uses a ==two-stage normalization method== to stabilize training and ensure consistent learning signals across diverse multi-tool trajectories.

## Results

- SenseNova-MARS-8B achieved leading performance on search-oriented benchmarks, scoring **67.84** on MMSearch and **41.64** on the new HR-MMSearch, matching Gemini-3-Flash.
- The model demonstrated superior fine-grained visual understanding with state-of-the-art scores of **92.2** on V* Bench, **83.1** on HR-Bench 4K, and **78.4** on HR-Bench 8K.
- The proposed BN-GSPO algorithm significantly improved the stability and effectiveness of multi-tool RL training, outperforming GRPO and GSPO in ablation studies and leading to more streamlined, efficient tool usage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically interleaving tool invocation (search and image manipulation) with continuous reasoning enables VLMs to address complex visual and knowledge-intensive tasks more effectively than isolated tool use.
> - Reinforcement Learning, specifically with robust algorithms like BN-GSPO, is crucial for teaching VLM agents adaptive and efficient multi-tool collaboration strategies in dynamic environments.
> - A hybrid training data distribution, combining both search-oriented and fine-grained perception tasks, is essential for developing a unified multi-tool policy that balances external knowledge retrieval with precise visual analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the forefront of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), agentic AI, and multimodal reasoning. The broader research landscape has seen significant advancements in VLMs, enabling them to tackle complex tasks by combining visual and linguistic understanding. However, these models traditionally suffer from several limitations that SenseNova-MARS aims to address:

- **Text-oriented Chain-of-Thought:** Many VLMs primarily rely on text-based reasoning chains, struggling to seamlessly integrate visual analysis directly into the reasoning process. This limits their effectiveness in scenarios demanding deep visual understanding.
- **Isolated Tool Invocation:** Existing VLM agents often invoke external tools in a sequential or isolated manner, rather than dynamically interleaving tool use with continuous, adaptive reasoning. This "fire-and-forget" approach hinders their ability to handle complex, multi-step problems where tool outputs inform subsequent reasoning and further tool choices.
- **Knowledge-Intensive and Visually Complex Scenarios:** Human-like proficiency in real-world tasks requires coordinating external tools (e.g., search engines for knowledge, image cropping for fine-grained detail) with iterative reasoning. Prior models frequently fall short in scenarios demanding both extensive external knowledge and precise visual perception, especially with high-resolution images.

SenseNova-MARS directly tackles these challenges by proposing a framework that integrates image search, text search, and image crop tools into a dynamic, multi-turn reasoning process. This work unifies several emerging paradigms in VLM research:

- **Search-Based Agentic VLMs:** Building upon works like MMSearch-R1, which integrated image and text search, SenseNova-MARS extends this by adding image manipulation capabilities to address fine-grained visual details. It moves beyond static Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods towards truly agentic systems that dynamically decide *when* and *how* to use search tools.
- **"Thinking with Images" Paradigm:** Inspired by OpenAI-o3 and further explored by DeepEyes and Pixel Reasoner, this paradigm emphasizes interleaving image and text reasoning through image manipulation tools (like cropping). SenseNova-MARS incorporates this by enabling the VLM to use image crop for fine-grained analysis, a capability often missing in search-focused agents.
- **Multi-Tool Agentic VLMs via Reinforcement Learning:** The paper explicitly acknowledges the growing role of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in developing adaptive multi-tool utilization strategies for LLM/VLM agents. SenseNova-MARS leverages RL to learn optimal tool-calling policies and reasoning trajectories, integrating previously disparate tool functionalities (search and image manipulation) into a cohesive agent.

By combining these elements, SenseNova-MARS attempts to bridge the gap between broad knowledge retrieval and precise visual understanding, making a significant step towards more human-like, adaptive, and versatile multimodal agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a novel multimodal agentic reasoning and search framework, SenseNova-MARS, that empowers Vision-Language Models with advanced, interleaved visual reasoning and tool-use capabilities. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

- **Overcome Limitations of Existing VLMs:** The primary motivation stems from the observation that current VLMs, despite their reasoning capabilities, are constrained by text-oriented reasoning and isolated tool invocations. They lack the "human-like proficiency" to seamlessly interleave dynamic tool manipulation with continuous reasoning, especially in complex, real-world visual and knowledge-intensive tasks.
- **Enable Dynamic Multi-Tool Integration:** The paper aims to dynamically integrate a suite of tools – image search, text search, and image crop – within a VLM's reasoning process. This integration is crucial for addressing challenges that require both external knowledge retrieval (via search) and fine-grained visual analysis (via cropping).
- **Advance Agentic VLM Capabilities through Reinforcement Learning:** A key objective is to leverage Reinforcement Learning (RL) to teach VLMs *when* and *how* to invoke these diverse tools adaptively. This includes developing a robust RL algorithm, BN-GSPO, to improve training stability and enhance multi-tool collaboration.
- **Develop a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** Recognizing the limitations of existing benchmarks for evaluating VLM agents on complex visual search tasks, the authors aim to introduce **HR-MMSearch**. This new benchmark is specifically designed with high-resolution images and knowledge-intensive, search-driven questions to rigorously assess fine-grained visual analysis and multi-tool invocation.
- **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate superior performance across diverse open-source search and fine-grained image understanding benchmarks, surpassing both existing open-source agentic models and leading proprietary models.
- **Facilitate Future Research:** By releasing all code, models, and datasets, the authors aim to contribute to the open-source community and accelerate further research in agentic VLMs and multimodal reasoning.

In essence, SenseNova-MARS seeks to create a more intelligent, adaptive, and human-like VLM agent capable of tackling real-world visual understanding challenges by strategically combining multiple external tools through learned reasoning processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SenseNova-MARS employs a sophisticated multi-faceted approach, combining a novel framework, a two-stage training pipeline, and a custom RL algorithm.

#### 3.1. Framework Design: Multimodal Agentic Reasoning and Search

SenseNova-MARS is designed as a multimodal agent that dynamically integrates reasoning with tool use. At each turn, given a query and an initial image, the agent generates a reasoning step and then chooses one of four actions:
1. **Text Search:** Query the web for textual information using a text query (powered by Serper Search API, summarized by Qwen3-32B).
2. **Image Search:** Perform a reverse image search for visually similar or contextually related images (powered by Serper Image Search API).
3. **Image Crop:** Zoom into a specific image region using bounding box coordinates for fine-grained analysis.
4. **Final Answer:** Produce the answer when sufficient information is gathered.

This iterative process allows for a dynamic "think-act" loop where tool outputs are appended to the interaction history, informing subsequent reasoning steps.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

The model undergoes a two-stage training process to bootstrap foundational capabilities and then refine agentic behaviors:

- **Cold-start Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** This initial stage involves fine-tuning the base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Qwen3-VL-8B) on a small, meticulously curated dataset of approximately 3,000 multi-turn interaction trajectories. The purpose of this stage is to teach the model basic tool-usage patterns and adherence to the interaction protocol. Only the language model is fine-tuned, while the vision encoder and multi-modal projector remain frozen.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following SFT, the model's multi-tool collaboration and reasoning capabilities are further refined using RL. This stage is crucial for enabling the agent to learn more effective and efficient tool invocation strategies in dynamic environments.

#### 3.3. BN-GSPO (Batch-Normalized Group Sequence Policy Optimization) Algorithm

To address the challenges of stable training in multi-tool, multimodal RL settings, SenseNova-MARS introduces BN-GSPO, an extension of the standard Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) algorithm.
- **Problem:** Standard GSPO can be sensitive to the diverse trajectory structures, varying reward magnitudes, and heterogeneous prompts that arise in multi-tool tasks, potentially destabilizing training.
- **Solution:** BN-GSPO applies a two-stage normalization to the advantage estimates:
 1. **Group-level Standardization:** Normalizes rewards within each group of responses for a given question.
 2. **Minibatch-level Normalization:** Further normalizes these group-normalized values across the entire training minibatch.
- **Objective:** This two-step normalization helps mitigate reward scale variance, ensures consistent learning signals, and stabilizes optimization across heterogeneous prompts. The optimization uses a clipped sequence-level objective to prevent excessively large policy updates and a KL divergence term to prevent overfitting to the reference policy.

#### 3.4. Reward Modeling

The RL process is guided by a sequence-level reward function `R(τ) = R_acc(τ) + R_format(τ)`:
- **Accuracy Reward (R_acc):** Measures the semantic agreement between the predicted answer and the ground truth, evaluated by an LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, scoring 1.0 for correct, 0.0 otherwise).
- **Format Reward (R_format):** Ensures strict compliance with the interaction protocol (e.g., proper use of `<think>`, `<tool_call>`, `<answer>` tags, JSON schema for tool calls; scoring 0.5 for compliant, 0.0 otherwise).

#### 3.5. Training Dataset Construction

- **Cold-start SFT Data:** A structured three-phase pipeline is used:
 1. **Data Mining:** Merges FVQA train set, Pixel-Reasoner warm-start corpus, and expert-annotated multimodal QA pairs.
 2. **Trajectory Synthesis:** For "hard" QA samples (where Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct fails to answer in 8 rollouts), Gemini-2.5-Flash synthesizes complete solution trajectories with tool invocations.
 3. **Quality Verification:** GPT-4o verifies format compliance, logical coherence, and answer plausibility, yielding ~3,000 high-quality samples.
- **RL Data:** A larger, more diverse dataset combining FVQA-train, VisualProbe-train, and DeepEyes-4K-train is used to generalize reasoning and tool-use policies across factual QA and high-resolution visual analysis.

#### 3.6. HR-MMSearch Benchmark Construction

To evaluate agents on complex visual tasks, the paper introduces HR-MMSearch, a novel benchmark:
- **Design:** Composed of 305 4K-resolution images from recent 2025 events (to prevent data leakage), spanning 8 diverse, high-impact domains.
- **Questions:** Manually crafted knowledge-intensive, search-oriented questions focusing on key visual subjects (small objects or text occupying less than 5% of the image), requiring at least one multimodal tool.
- **Difficulty:** Questions are categorized as "Hard" (model fails all 8 rollouts) or "Easy" based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct's performance, ensuring challenging scenarios requiring complex reasoning and multi-tool coordination.

This comprehensive methodology, from framework design and algorithm development to data curation and benchmark creation, highlights a systematic effort to build and rigorously evaluate advanced multimodal agentic systems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SenseNova-MARS demonstrates significant advancements across various benchmarks, establishing new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance for multimodal agentic reasoning.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on Search-Oriented Benchmarks

- SenseNova-MARS-8B achieves the leading performance among open-source agentic models and outperforms several proprietary models.
- On **MMSearch**, SenseNova-MARS-8B scores **67.84**, surpassing proprietary models like Gemini-3-Flash (62.57), GPT-5 (52.63), and existing open-source agentic models like MMSearch-R1 (53.80) and DeepEyesV2 (63.70).
- On the newly introduced **HR-MMSearch**, SenseNova-MARS-8B scores **41.64**, matching Gemini-3-Flash (41.64) and significantly outperforming GPT-5 (38.36), GPT-4o (30.16), and MMSearch-R1 (20.33). This indicates its strong capability in handling high-resolution images combined with knowledge-intensive search.
- It also achieves SOTA performance on **FVQA (67.11)**, **InfoSeek (70.19)**, **SimpleVQA (61.70)**, **LiveVQA (56.22)**, and **MAT-Search (84.67)**, demonstrating robust performance across a diverse range of knowledge-intensive and information-seeking tasks.
- Overall, SenseNova-MARS-8B attains an average performance gain of 12.68 points compared to its base model, Qwen3-VL-8B, and 2.94 points over Gemini-3-Flash on search-oriented benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Superior Fine-grained Visual Understanding

- SenseNova-MARS-8B also exhibits superior fine-grained perception capabilities on high-resolution benchmarks, validating its effectiveness in detailed pixel-space analysis.
- It achieves leading scores of **92.2 on V\* Bench**, **83.1 on HR-Bench 4K**, and **78.4 on HR-Bench 8K**, outperforming specialized visual reasoning models like Mini o3 (88.2 on V\* Bench, 77.5 on HR-Bench 4K, 73.3 on HR-Bench 8K), DeepEyesV2, and Pixel Reasoner.
- On **MME-RealWorld**, it scores **67.9**, outperforming other models.
- Compared to the base Qwen3-VL-8B, SenseNova-MARS-8B shows an average performance gain of 4.9 points on visual understanding benchmarks.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Proposed BN-GSPO Algorithm (Ablation Study)

- The ablation study demonstrated the crucial role of the BN-GSPO algorithm for stable and effective multi-tool RL training.
- In a pure RL setup (without cold-start SFT), BN-GSPO achieved significantly better overall performance across MMSearch (56.72), V\* Bench (79.05), and HR-Bench 4K (69.12) compared to GRPO (50.88, 67.54, 61.38) and GSPO (53.80, 53.93, 44.50).
- This confirms that the batch normalization component effectively mitigates reward scale variance, leading to more consistent and robust learning.

#### 4.4. Impact of Data Distribution on RL Performance (Ablation Study)

- The study showed that a hybrid dataset, including both search and perception tasks, is essential for optimal performance.
- Training only on specialized fine-grained perception data led to overspecialization (e.g., V\* Bench performance improved, but search-oriented tasks dropped significantly compared to the SFT baseline).
- Using the full hybrid dataset allowed SenseNova-MARS-7B to achieve the best results on search-oriented metrics, demonstrating its ability to learn a unified, multi-tool policy that balances external knowledge retrieval with fine-grained visual analysis.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Tool Use Behavior

- SenseNova-MARS-8B exhibits adaptive tool-use behavior. On knowledge-intensive MMSearch, it primarily relies on image and text search. For fine-grained perception tasks like V\* Bench, it heavily uses the image crop tool.
- On the challenging HR-MMSearch, which demands both, SenseNova-MARS demonstrates a balanced integration of localized visual cues and external knowledge, using a combination of all three tools.
- RL training also streamlines tool usage, reducing redundant actions and improving both the efficacy and efficiency of the agent's interaction trajectory (average tool calls reduced from ~4 to ~2 for SenseNova-MARS-7B).

In summary, the results unequivocally establish SenseNova-MARS as a leading multimodal agentic model, capable of robust reasoning and adaptive multi-tool invocation across complex, knowledge-intensive, and high-resolution visual tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SenseNova-MARS represents a significant step forward in the development of agentic Vision-Language Models, with broad implications and potential impacts across research and application domains.

- **Advancing Agentic VLMs:** The framework pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities by enabling seamless interleaving of dynamic tool manipulation with continuous reasoning. This moves beyond simplistic "chain-of-thought" or isolated tool calls, fostering more human-like, adaptive, and intelligent agent behaviors. It lays a crucial foundation for future research in general-purpose AI agents capable of operating in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Unified Multi-Tool Reasoning:** By effectively integrating image search, text search, and image crop tools, SenseNova-MARS demonstrates a unified approach to multimodal reasoning. This allows agents to address a wider spectrum of real-world problems that require both external knowledge acquisition and fine-grained visual analysis, a capability previously fragmented across specialized models.
- **Robust Reinforcement Learning for Tool Use:** The introduction of BN-GSPO is a notable algorithmic contribution. By improving the stability and consistency of RL training for multi-tool agentic VLMs, it addresses a critical challenge in learning complex interaction policies. This can benefit future research in developing more reliable and efficient RL algorithms for agentic systems.
- **New Benchmark for Complex Visual Tasks:** The creation of **HR-MMSearch** addresses a significant gap in VLM evaluation. Its focus on high-resolution images, knowledge-intensive questions, and the necessity for multi-tool interaction provides a rigorous standard for assessing fine-grained perception and agentic reasoning. This benchmark will be instrumental in driving progress and enabling fair comparisons in this challenging subfield.
- **Catalyst for Open-Source Research:** The commitment to release all code, models, and datasets is highly significant. This open-source contribution will lower the barrier to entry for researchers, accelerate innovation, and foster a collaborative environment, allowing the broader AI community to build upon SenseNova-MARS's advancements.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The enhanced capabilities of SenseNova-MARS, particularly in handling high-resolution visual details and integrating external search, have direct implications for various applications:
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** More capable virtual assistants that can answer complex questions requiring visual context and web knowledge.
 - **Visual Search and Information Retrieval:** Improved systems for visual question answering, product search, or identifying details in large image datasets.
 - **Content Understanding:** Advanced AI for analyzing and extracting information from complex documents, news images, or scientific figures.
 - **Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality:** Agents that can interpret real-world scenes with greater fidelity and provide contextual information.
 - **Scientific and Medical Imaging:** Potentially aiding in tasks requiring detailed analysis of high-resolution images combined with domain-specific knowledge.

In conclusion, SenseNova-MARS not only achieves impressive empirical results but also provides a robust methodological framework and valuable resources that will likely inspire and facilitate subsequent research in the burgeoning field of multimodal agentic AI.
