# Transformer-Based Warm-Starting for Feasible and Optimal Terminal Approach to Tumbling Objects with Space Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University developed a transformer-based warm-starting method to enhance the efficiency and robustness of Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) for generating trajectories for space manipulators approaching tumbling objects. The approach reduced SCP iterations by up to 28% and overall runtime by 23% in the computationally intensive second stage, while maintaining solution quality for complex 3D orbital missions.

## Problem

- On-orbit robotic servicing missions to non-cooperative, tumbling targets pose challenges due to complex, nonlinear coupled spacecraft bus-manipulator dynamics, rotating keep-out zones, and visibility constraints.
- Trajectory generation using optimal control is high-dimensional, computationally intensive, and highly sensitive to initial guesses, hindering real-time onboard implementation.
- Existing learning-based warm-starting methods for space manipulators have not comprehensively addressed the full complexity of 3D orbital mechanics, tumbling targets, and dynamic coupling simultaneously.

## Method

- A two-stage ==Sequential Convex Programming (SCP)== pipeline is utilized: Stage 1 optimizes the system center of mass (COM) translational motion, and Stage 2 handles internal torque reconfiguration (bus attitude, manipulator joints).
- A Causal Transformer (CT) architecture is employed for conditional behavior cloning to generate high-quality warm-start trajectories specifically for the computationally dominant Stage 2.
- The approach explores different action chunking strategies (single-step vs. H-step prediction) and decoder architectures (linear vs. flow matching) to map the transformer's latent outputs to control inputs.

## Results

- The CT warm-start reduced the second-stage SCP iteration count by up to **28%** and decreased total Stage 2 runtime by approximately **23%** (from **4.69** s to **3.61** s mean runtime) compared to heuristic initialization.
- All CT warm-start methods achieved SCP success rates of at least **99.3%** and preserved the final control cost distribution, demonstrating no degradation in solution quality.
- When SCP was used for feasibility projection, the CT warm-start maintained a **99.7%** feasibility rate and bounded control costs (99th percentile increased from **0.66** to **0.79**), while the heuristic method's 99th percentile control cost dramatically increased to **10.94**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer-based warm-starts consistently improve the computational efficiency of SCP solvers for complex coupled bus-manipulator dynamics, reducing iterations and runtime without sacrificing solution quality.
> - Flow matching decoders exhibit stronger sample efficiency compared to linear decoders, maintaining performance with significantly smaller training datasets, suggesting a beneficial lightweight nonlinear mapping from latent representations to actions.
> - For feasibility-oriented trajectory generation, learned warm-starts enable rapid convergence to low-cost, dynamically consistent solutions, outperforming heuristic methods that often yield prohibitively expensive feasible trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of on-orbit robotic servicing, including tasks such as satellite repair, refueling, and debris removal, relies on the precise guidance and control of space manipulators. These operations often involve approaching non-cooperative, tumbling target objects, which presents significant challenges due to the complex, nonlinear coupling between the spacecraft bus motion and the manipulator dynamics. Additional complexities arise from maintaining safety within a rotating keep-out zone and ensuring visibility for perception-aware control.

Traditional approaches to trajectory generation for such missions frequently frame the problem as a Nonlinear Programming (NLP)-based optimal control problem. While effective, these problems are often high-dimensional, computationally intensive, and highly sensitive to the initial guess, or "warm-start," provided to the solver. A poor warm-start can lead to convergence failures or excessive computation times, making real-time onboard implementation difficult.

Prior research in space manipulator motion planning has often addressed these complexities by making simplifying assumptions. Some approaches focus solely on manipulator torque while assuming a fixed or station-keeping bus. Others adopt a two-stage planning scheme, separating the center-of-mass (COM) translational motion from the internal attitude and manipulator torque allocation. While effective in reducing problem dimensionality, the second stage, involving coupled attitude–manipulator dynamics, typically remains computationally demanding.

In parallel, learning-based methods, particularly sequence modeling and transformer architectures, have emerged as a promising avenue for generating warm-start trajectories in various robotic and aerospace applications. These models can autoregressively predict state–action sequences conditioned on task context and history. However, existing applications of learning-based warm-starting for space manipulators have generally been limited to simplified scenarios, such as planar motion, low-dimensional systems, or static obstacles, without simultaneously addressing the full complexity of 3D orbital mechanics, tumbling targets, dynamic coupling, rotating safety zones, and visibility constraints.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the gap in applying advanced learning-based warm-starting to the full, complex 3D orbital terminal approach problem for space manipulators. It aims to leverage the capabilities of transformer models to provide high-quality initial guesses for Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) solvers in real-time, thereby improving both computational efficiency and solution robustness for highly constrained, safety-critical space missions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a learning-based warm-starting framework, specifically utilizing a causal transformer (CT), to enhance the efficiency and robustness of Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) for generating terminal approach trajectories for space manipulators engaging with tumbling targets.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in on-orbit robotic servicing missions:
1. **Computational Burden of Optimal Control:** The terminal approach phase of space manipulator operations requires precise trajectory generation, which is naturally formulated as a Nonlinear Programming (NLP)-based optimal control problem. However, the coupled bus–manipulator dynamics, relative orbital motion, and various safety and operational constraints (e.g., visibility, rotating keep-out zones) result in high-dimensional, nonlinear, and nonconvex optimization problems. Solving these problems directly and reliably in real-time, particularly on resource-constrained onboard systems, is computationally prohibitive.
2. **Sensitivity to Initial Guesses:** NLP and SCP solvers are highly sensitive to their initial trajectory guesses, often referred to as warm-starts. A poor warm-start can lead to slow convergence, local optima, or even solver failure. Generating a high-quality initial guess that is dynamically consistent, constraint-satisfying, and close to an optimal solution is critical for practical application.
3. **Limitations of Heuristic Warm-Starts:** While heuristic methods can provide initial trajectories, they often lack the fidelity or robustness required for complex, dynamically coupled systems. They may not adequately account for all constraints or dynamic couplings, potentially leading to increased SCP iterations, longer runtimes, or convergence to undesirable solutions.
4. **Complexity of the Target Scenario:** The specific scenario of a 3D orbital terminal approach to an *uncontrolled tumbling target* introduces significant challenges. These include synchronizing with the target's attitude, managing rotating safety constraints, and ensuring the manipulator's end-effector (EE) remains within a visibility cone while maintaining coupled bus–arm dynamics. Existing learning-based warm-starting methods have not been thoroughly demonstrated in such a comprehensive and high-fidelity setting.
5. **Addressing the Bottleneck:** Within a two-stage trajectory planning architecture (separating system COM translation from internal attitude-manipulator motion), the second stage, which involves the nonlinear coupled bus–manipulator dynamics and recursive dynamics evaluation, typically constitutes the dominant computational bottleneck. Targeting this specific stage for warm-starting offers the most significant potential for overall performance improvement.

To address these motivations, the research focuses on the following specific objectives:
- **Development of a CT Warm-Start Framework:** To create a causal transformer architecture capable of generating high-quality warm-start trajectories for the coupled attitude–manipulator dynamics in the terminal approach phase.
- **Integration with a Two-Stage SCP Pipeline:** To seamlessly integrate the learned warm-start into the second, computationally intensive stage of an existing two-stage SCP framework.
- **Performance Quantification:** To quantitatively evaluate the benefits of the learned warm-start in terms of reduced SCP iteration count, decreased runtime, and preservation of solution quality (control cost distribution) compared to heuristic warm-starting.
- **Decoder Architecture and Action Chunking Comparison:** To assess the impact of different decoder designs (linear vs. flow matching) and action chunking strategies on the warm-start's effectiveness and sample efficiency.
- **Feasibility Projection Evaluation:** To investigate the utility of the learned warm-start when SCP is employed for fast feasibility projection (finding any constraint-satisfying trajectory) rather than strict cost optimization, and to compare its robustness against heuristic initialization in this mode.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper integrates a learning-based warm-starting technique with a two-stage Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) pipeline for trajectory generation in space manipulator terminal approach scenarios.

**A. Nonconvex Trajectory Optimization Formulation**
The terminal approach is formulated as a nonconvex trajectory optimization problem. The system state includes the bus position and velocity (in a target-centered Radial–Tangential–Normal (RTN) frame), bus attitude quaternion and angular velocity, and manipulator joint angles and rates. The control inputs comprise bus translational forces, bus torques, and joint torques. The coupled bus–arm dynamics incorporate relative orbital dynamics (Hill–Clohessy–Wiltshire equations) and are discretized using forward Euler integration.

To mitigate the computational complexity of a full-state optimization, a two-stage decomposition, inspired by prior work, is adopted:

1. **Stage 1: System COM Translational Trajectory Optimization:**
 - **Decision Variables:** System COM position and velocity, and net translational force.
 - **Objective:** Minimize a weighted sum of control effort and control smoothness.
 - **Constraints:** Discrete-time translational dynamics (HCW equation), initial and terminal boundary conditions, maximum translational force, and a rotating safety constraint (minimum clearance between the servicer’s COM and the tumbling target’s keep-out geometry).
 - This stage generates an optimal translational trajectory for the system COM.

2. **Stage 2: Internal Torque Reconfiguration:**
 - **Decision Variables:** Bus attitude and angular velocity, manipulator joint angles and rates, and bus/joint torques.
 - **Objective:** Minimize a weighted quadratic control cost for internal torques.
 - **Constraints:** Reduced-form coupled bus–arm dynamics (conditioned on the optimal COM trajectory and forces from Stage 1), control input magnitude bounds, joint angle limits, a pointing constraint (docking port within the servicer's field of view), terminal joint profile, terminal bus angular velocity, and a terminal attitude condition for the end-effector approach direction.
 - This stage resolves the internal motion given the prescribed COM trajectory, which is identified as the dominant computational bottleneck.

**B. Sequential Convex Programming (SCP) and Heuristic Initialization**
Both Stage 1 and Stage 2 optimization problems are solved using SCP. SCP iteratively solves convexified subproblems under trust region constraints. An augmented Lagrangian function penalizes nonconvex constraint violations, with automatically updated trust region radii and penalty weights. All variables are scaled to the range [−1, 1] to improve numerical conditioning.

- **Heuristic Warm-Start for Stage 1:** A warm-start is obtained by solving a convex relaxation of Stage 1, specifically by omitting the nonconvex keep-out-zone safety constraint.
- **Heuristic Warm-Start for Stage 2:** The initial guess for Stage 2 involves a reference trajectory where the bus attitude tracks the target's line of sight, arm joints linearly interpolate to their terminal configuration, and bus angular velocity/joint rates are obtained via finite-differencing.
- **SCP Robustness:** To handle potential infeasibility of the first convex subproblem due to strict trust region constraints, an initial trust-region expansion heuristic is employed until a feasible subproblem is found. Quaternion re-normalization is applied at each nonlinear constraint evaluation within SCP iterations for Stage 2.

**C. Conditional Behavior Cloning with Sequence Model (Proposed Warm-Start)**
The learning-based warm-starting is applied exclusively to Stage 2, which is confirmed to be the computational bottleneck. The approach utilizes conditional behavior cloning with a Causal Transformer (CT) architecture.

- **Data Generation:** A dataset of 90,000 feasible and locally optimal trajectories is generated using the two-stage SCP pipeline initialized with the heuristic warm-starts. To promote generalization, initial conditions (positional offset, initial velocity, target attitude) are sampled from uniform distributions for each scenario.
- **Trajectory Tokenization:** The model predicts expert state-control trajectories conditioned on task specifications. The tokenized trajectory sequence includes: time-to-go (ttg), tumbling target's attitude state, the optimal Stage 1 COM trajectory and control profile, and the current Stage 2 state. The model then predicts the Stage 2 action (internal torques).
- **Action Chunking:** Two strategies for action prediction are explored:
 - **Single-step prediction (H=1):** Predicts only the immediate control input.
 - **H-step action sequence (H=5):** Predicts a sequence of H future actions. For linear decoders, a predict-H/execute-1 receding horizon strategy is used; for flow matching, a predict-H/execute-H open-loop strategy is employed.
- **Decoder Architectures:** Two variants are compared for mapping the transformer's latent output to physical control inputs:
 - **Linear Decoder:** A simple linear layer (affine transformation) recovers the action. Trained with a batched mean-square error loss on predicted states and actions.
 - **Flow Matching Decoder:** Learns a conditional velocity field that transports samples from a simple prior distribution (e.g., Gaussian) to the more complex, potentially multimodal action distribution in the dataset. Inference involves numerically integrating a learned Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE). This is expected to offer more expressive action mapping. The number of forward Euler integration steps (`n_flow`) for the flow matching decoder is also investigated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative evaluations of the proposed transformer-based warm-starting framework across various configurations, comparing its performance against a heuristic baseline. Experiments were conducted on 300 held-out scenarios using a servicer with a 3-linked arm and a tumbling 80x80x40 cm target object in low Earth orbit.

**A. Computational Bottleneck Verification**
Initial measurements confirmed that Stage 2 (internal torque reconfiguration) is the dominant computational bottleneck. The mean runtime for Stage 1 was 0.68 ± 0.21 seconds, while Stage 2 required 4.69 ± 2.12 seconds, accounting for approximately 87% of the total solve time with heuristic warm-starts. This validated the focus on warm-starting Stage 2.

**B. Flow Matching ODE Steps Sensitivity**
A sensitivity analysis on the number of forward-Euler integration steps (`n_flow`) for the flow matching decoder (Table II) showed:
- Inference time scaled approximately linearly with `n_flow`.
- Increasing `n_flow` beyond 1 or 2 did not improve warm-start quality (terminal end-effector position error or subsequent SCP iterations).
- `n_flow = 1` and `n_flow = 2` achieved the lowest terminal errors and SCP iterations, maintaining high SCP success rates (99.7% and 99.3%, respectively). This suggests that excessive flow integration can accumulate errors from the learned velocity field and that a simple unimodal distribution (derived from deterministic optimization data) does not significantly benefit from complex flow integration. `n_flow = 1` was selected for subsequent experiments.

**C. Comparison of Decoder Options and Action Chunking**
Comparative results across different decoder architectures and action chunk lengths (Table III), using 90k training trajectories, indicated:
- **Solution Quality:** All methods, including the heuristic baseline and CT warm-starts (linear and flow matching decoders with H=1 and H=5), achieved nearly identical SCP success rates (all ≥ 99.3%) and final control cost distributions. This demonstrates that the learned warm-starts preserved the quality of the optimized trajectories.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - The CT warm-start reduced the second-stage SCP iteration count by up to 28% (from 18.5 ± 8.4 for heuristic to 13.4 ± 6.0 for CT FM H=1).
 - This reduction translated into an approximately 23% decrease in total Stage 2 runtime (from 4.69 ± 2.12 s for heuristic to 3.61 ± 1.51 s for CT FM H=1), even after accounting for the network’s inference time.
- **Action Chunking Impact:** Increasing the action chunk length to H=5 did not improve the quality of the warm-start or subsequent SCP convergence for either decoder type. This suggests that for initializing SCP, which performs trajectory-level correction, single-step prediction leveraging the most recent history is sufficient and potentially more effective than longer open-loop action sequences.

**D. Sample Efficiency**
An additional experiment (Fig. 4) compared the sample efficiency of linear and flow matching decoders when trained with different dataset sizes (5k, 10k, 90k trajectories):
- **Flow Matching Decoder:** Maintained approximately 13 SCP iterations across all dataset sizes (5k, 10k, 90k), indicating stronger sample efficiency. Its runtime remained consistently lower than the heuristic baseline.
- **Linear Decoder:** Showed sensitivity to dataset size. As the training dataset size decreased, its runtime and SCP iterations increased, approaching the performance of the heuristic SCP baseline.
- This suggests that the flow matching decoder provides a more expressive, lightweight nonlinear mapping from the transformer's latent representation to the action sequence, which is beneficial when the training data is limited, even if it does not fully exploit multi-modality due to the deterministic nature of the training data.

**E. Feasibility Problem**
The research also explored the performance of learned warm-starts when SCP is used for feasibility projection (objective set to zero), rather than strict cost optimality (Table IV):
- **Reduced Iterations for Feasibility:** For both heuristic and CT warm-starts, solving the feasibility problem roughly halved the SCP iterations compared to cost-optimal SCP.
- **Heuristic Initialization for Feasibility:** While achieving faster convergence, the heuristic warm-start exhibited significantly degraded solution quality. The 99th percentile of the realized control cost dramatically increased from 0.68 to 10.94, and the feasibility rate dropped to 97.3%. This indicates that the heuristic can converge quickly to feasible trajectories that are prohibitively expensive and undesirable for operation.
- **CT Initialization for Feasibility:** The CT warm-start, when used for feasibility projection, maintained bounded costs (99th percentile increased only from 0.66 to 0.79) and a high feasibility rate (99.7%). This demonstrates that the CT warm-start initializes SCP in a favorable region, allowing the feasibility projection to find low-cost, dynamically consistent solutions with minimal local correction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for enhancing the efficiency and robustness of trajectory generation for space manipulators approaching tumbling targets, addressing a critical challenge in on-orbit servicing (OOS) operations. The findings demonstrate several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Improved Computational Efficiency for Onboard Systems:** The implementation of a transformer-based warm-start for the computationally dominant second stage of the SCP pipeline significantly reduces the number of SCP iterations (by up to 28%) and overall runtime (by 23%). This acceleration makes complex NLP-based optimal control more viable for real-time onboard execution on space vehicles, which typically have limited computational resources. This is crucial for autonomous operations where rapid decision-making is necessary.

2. **Enhanced Trajectory Robustness and Reliability:** The learned warm-starts consistently guide the SCP solver toward high-quality, locally optimal solutions, maintaining the same final control cost distribution and SCP success rates as the heuristic baseline, but with faster convergence. This robustness is particularly valuable for safety-critical missions where consistent and reliable trajectory generation is paramount.

3. **Advanced Application of Learning-Based Methods in Space Robotics:** This work extends the application of sequence models, specifically causal transformers, to a high-fidelity, complex 3D orbital space-manipulator scenario. It effectively integrates challenging aspects like relative orbital motion, tumbling target synchronization, coupled bus–arm dynamics, rotating safety constraints, and visibility cones, which were often simplified in previous studies. This demonstrates the potential of machine learning to address previously intractable problems in space robotics.

4. **Guidance for Decoder Architecture Selection:** The comparison of linear and flow matching decoders provides insights into model design for behavior cloning in this domain. The flow matching decoder exhibits improved sample efficiency, maintaining performance with smaller training datasets. This suggests that while the dataset's deterministic nature limits the exploitation of multimodal sampling, the lightweight nonlinear mapping of flow matching still offers benefits, particularly for scenarios where data generation is expensive.

5. **Robustness in Feasibility-Oriented Planning:** The research highlights a critical advantage of learned warm-starts when the optimization objective shifts from strict cost optimality to rapid feasibility projection. While heuristic warm-starts can quickly find feasible solutions, they often converge to trajectories with unacceptably high operational costs. In contrast, the transformer-based warm-start enables fast feasibility projection while preserving bounded, low-cost trajectories, making it a valuable tool for time-critical situations where a safe, executable path is needed promptly, without incurring excessive control effort.

In conclusion, this work contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable space manipulators by providing a method for generating robust and efficient trajectories in complex operational environments. The findings suggest that learned sequence models serve not only as fast trajectory predictors but also as effective trajectory priors, guiding optimization algorithms toward favorable solution basins that are both feasible and operationally efficient. Future extensions, such as incorporating uncertainty in the tumbling target, could further enhance the framework's applicability to real-world scenarios.
