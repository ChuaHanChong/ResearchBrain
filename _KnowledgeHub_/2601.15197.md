# LangForce: Bayesian Decomposition of Vision Language Action Models via Latent Action Queries

> [!summary] Summary
> LangForce introduces a framework that uses Bayesian decomposition of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to counteract the "vision shortcut," where models ignore language instructions in favor of visual cues. It achieves this by employing latent action queries and a Log-Likelihood Ratio objective to maximize the conditional mutual information between actions and instructions, leading to improved generalization in out-of-distribution environments and preserving the underlying Vision-Language Model's capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit a "vision shortcut" or "Information Collapse," learning to predict actions predominantly from visual observations rather than robustly grounding them in language instructions.
- This issue stems from deterministic mappings in common goal-driven robotic datasets, where language instructions become highly predictable from visual scenes (sharp p(ℓ | v)), causing models to effectively degenerate into vision-only policies.
- Such behavior limits VLA models' generalization to novel instructions, ambiguous scenarios, and out-of-distribution environments, and can lead to catastrophic forgetting of general VLM capabilities during fine-tuning.

## Method

- **LangForce** employs a Bayesian decomposition approach to explicitly maximize the conditional ==Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI)== between actions (a) and language instructions (ℓ), given visual observations (v), using a Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) objective.
- It introduces Latent Action Queries (LAQs), K=64 learnable tokens, which act as an information bottleneck and leverage causal masking within a unified VLM to precisely control information flow for dual-branch processing.
- A dual-branch training framework (Priori for vision-only context and Posteriori for vision+language context) shares VLM weights and uses flow-matching losses, with the LLR objective actively regularizing language grounding.

## Results

- LangForce achieved an average success rate of **66.5%** on the SimplerEnv benchmark, representing an **11.3%** absolute improvement over the QwenGR00T baseline (**55.2%**).
- It demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa with a **52.6%** average success rate (outperforming QwenGR00T by **+4.8%**) and **99.4%** on LIBERO Goal, effectively resolving visual ambiguity.
- Real-world experiments showed superior OOD generalization for colored-block pick-and-place (9/30 successes vs. 2/30 for QwenGR00T) and an overall **80.8%** success rate for vegetable pick-and-place, while qualitative analysis confirmed preservation of the VLM's general conversational and reasoning abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "vision shortcut," where VLA models ignore language when visual cues suffice, is a critical pathology limiting generalization and robustness, evidenced by empirical validation.
> - Explicitly maximizing the conditional mutual information between action and instruction (via the LLR objective) is crucial for compelling genuine language grounding and preventing information collapse.
> - Latent Action Queries provide an architecturally efficient mechanism to control the VLM's attention, enabling the distinct information processing required for Bayesian decomposition without adding inference overhead, and also improving computational efficiency for the downstream action head.
