# LangForce: Bayesian Decomposition of Vision Language Action Models via Latent Action Queries

> [!summary] Summary
> LangForce introduces a framework that uses Bayesian decomposition of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to counteract the "vision shortcut," where models ignore language instructions in favor of visual cues. It achieves this by employing latent action queries and a Log-Likelihood Ratio objective to maximize the conditional mutual information between actions and instructions, leading to improved generalization in out-of-distribution environments and preserving the underlying Vision-Language Model's capabilities.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit a "vision shortcut" or "Information Collapse," learning to predict actions predominantly from visual observations rather than robustly grounding them in language instructions.
- This issue stems from deterministic mappings in common goal-driven robotic datasets, where language instructions become highly predictable from visual scenes (sharp p(ℓ | v)), causing models to effectively degenerate into vision-only policies.
- Such behavior limits VLA models' generalization to novel instructions, ambiguous scenarios, and out-of-distribution environments, and can lead to catastrophic forgetting of general VLM capabilities during fine-tuning.

## Method

- **LangForce** employs a Bayesian decomposition approach to explicitly maximize the conditional ==Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI)== between actions (a) and language instructions (ℓ), given visual observations (v), using a Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) objective.
- It introduces Latent Action Queries (LAQs), K=64 learnable tokens, which act as an information bottleneck and leverage causal masking within a unified VLM to precisely control information flow for dual-branch processing.
- A dual-branch training framework (Priori for vision-only context and Posteriori for vision+language context) shares VLM weights and uses flow-matching losses, with the LLR objective actively regularizing language grounding.

## Results

- LangForce achieved an average success rate of **66.5%** on the SimplerEnv benchmark, representing an **11.3%** absolute improvement over the QwenGR00T baseline (**55.2%**).
- It demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa with a **52.6%** average success rate (outperforming QwenGR00T by **+4.8%**) and **99.4%** on LIBERO Goal, effectively resolving visual ambiguity.
- Real-world experiments showed superior OOD generalization for colored-block pick-and-place (9/30 successes vs. 2/30 for QwenGR00T) and an overall **80.8%** success rate for vegetable pick-and-place, while qualitative analysis confirmed preservation of the VLM's general conversational and reasoning abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "vision shortcut," where VLA models ignore language when visual cues suffice, is a critical pathology limiting generalization and robustness, evidenced by empirical validation.
> - Explicitly maximizing the conditional mutual information between action and instruction (via the LLR objective) is crucial for compelling genuine language grounding and preventing information collapse.
> - Latent Action Queries provide an architecturally efficient mechanism to control the VLM's attention, enabling the distinct information processing required for Bayesian decomposition without adding inference overhead, and also improving computational efficiency for the downstream action head.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotics aimed at enabling general-purpose robot manipulation by grounding natural language instructions into physical actions. These models typically leverage the representational power of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and are trained on large-scale datasets of human demonstrations to learn policies, denoted as π(a | v, ℓ), that execute tasks specified by language ℓ given visual observations v.

While VLA models have demonstrated performance in in-distribution settings, a notable challenge remains in their ability to generalize to novel instructions or complex multi-task scenarios, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments. Existing literature has highlighted issues such as catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning on narrow, task-specific datasets, which can impair the VLM’s broader capabilities.

This paper identifies and addresses a specific pathology termed "Information Collapse" or "vision shortcut," prevalent in current VLA training paradigms. The authors observe that in many goal-driven robotic datasets, the mapping from visual scenes to language instructions is often deterministic. For instance, seeing a specific object in a fixed scene might almost invariably imply a particular action instruction. This deterministic coupling leads to a sharp conditional distribution p(ℓ | v), where the language instruction becomes highly predictable from visual observations alone. Consequently, VLA models may learn to bypass the language instruction, effectively degenerating into vision-only policies p(a | v) and ignoring linguistic constraints. This behavior limits the models' robustness and generalizability, particularly in ambiguous situations or when deployed in environments that differ from the training data.

The work builds upon the foundation of large VLA architectures such as the GR00T series and π0 models, which integrate VLMs with action generation mechanisms, often diffusion transformers. While other approaches like BayesVLA (Xu et al., 2025) have also explored Bayesian decomposition to improve instruction following, they often require multi-stage training processes with frozen components. LangForce distinguishes itself by proposing a single-stage, end-to-end training framework that explicitly intervenes to maximize the information gain provided by the action relative to the language instruction, thereby directly counteracting the identified information collapse. This objective-driven approach aims to improve language grounding without requiring new data collection or complex multi-stage procedures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind LangForce stem from the identified limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly concerning their generalization capabilities and reliance on language instructions.

The central problem identified is the "vision shortcut" or "Information Collapse." This phenomenon occurs when VLA models, trained on typical goal-driven robotic datasets, learn to predict actions predominantly from visual observations (v) rather than truly grounding them in language instructions (ℓ). The authors hypothesize that this issue is rooted in a dataset bias where goal-driven data collection results in a near-injective mapping from visual scenes to language instructions. For example, if a specific object (e.g., a cabinet) consistently appears in a scene with a particular instruction (e.g., "open the cabinet"), the model learns a strong statistical correlation (p(ℓ | v) is sharp).

From a Bayesian perspective, the optimal policy can be decomposed as π(a | v, ℓ) = [p(ℓ | a, v) p(a | v)] / p(ℓ | v). When p(ℓ | v) is sharp (i.e., the instruction is highly predictable from vision alone), the conditional mutual information I(ℓ; a | v) tends to vanish. This implies that the action (a) provides little additional information about the instruction (ℓ) beyond what is already available from the visual observation (v). Consequently, the likelihood term p(ℓ | a, v) collapses to p(ℓ | v), leading the posterior policy π(a | v, ℓ) to approximate the vision-only prior p(a | v). In essence, the model "degenerates" into a vision-only policy that ignores the language input, becoming fragile in ambiguous scenarios or when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) visual contexts.

The key motivations for developing LangForce are:
1. **To counteract the vision shortcut:** To prevent VLA models from defaulting to vision-only policies and instead compel them to genuinely utilize and ground language instructions.
2. **To improve generalization:** By enforcing true language conditioning, the aim is to enhance the model's ability to perform tasks specified by novel instructions or in visually distinct OOD environments where superficial visual correlations are unreliable.
3. **To maximize instruction-action mutual information:** The theoretical basis for LangForce is to maximize the conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) between actions and instructions, given the visual observation. This is equivalent to maximizing the Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR): log p(ℓ | a, v) − log p(ℓ | v). This objective specifically penalizes reliance on the vision shortcut by rewarding actions that explicitly explain the language command, providing information about ℓ that cannot be inferred from v alone.
4. **To preserve general VLM capabilities:** Fine-tuning VLMs for robotic control often leads to catastrophic forgetting, degrading their general reasoning and multimodal understanding abilities. LangForce aims to mitigate this by ensuring that language tokens remain functionally useful, thereby regularizing the VLM backbone and preserving its broader competencies.
5. **To provide an efficient solution:** The method aims to achieve these benefits without requiring additional data collection or introducing significant computational overhead during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LangForce addresses the vision shortcut pathology through a Bayesian decomposition approach, implemented via a dual-branch architecture and optimized with a Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) objective. The framework builds upon existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, specifically using the QwenGR00T architecture from the StarVLA framework.

#### 3.1. Objective Formulation: Maximizing Conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI)

The core idea is to move beyond standard imitation learning, which can be susceptible to the vision shortcut, by explicitly maximizing the conditional PMI between the action (a) and the language instruction (ℓ), given the visual observation (v). This objective is formulated as maximizing the LLR:
$L_{LLR} = \log \frac{\pi(a | v, ℓ)}{p(a | v)} = \log p(ℓ | a, v) - \log p(ℓ | v)$.
This objective effectively rewards actions that make the instruction significantly more probable than what could be inferred from visual context alone, thereby forcing the model to extract and utilize instruction-specific information.

#### 3.2. Latent Action Queries (LAQs)

To instantiate this Bayesian decomposition within a unified VLM architecture, LangForce introduces Latent Action Queries (Q). These are K=64 learnable tokens, inserted into the VLM’s input sequence.
- **Purpose:** LAQs serve as a bottleneck, aggregating task-relevant information from vision and language tokens into a compact latent representation. This representation is then forwarded to a downstream Diffusion Transformer (DiT) which acts as the continuous action head.
- **Information Control:** The key innovation is using the causal masking inherent in decoder-only VLMs. By controlling the position of these queries within the input sequence, the information they can attend to (and thus encode) is precisely regulated, enabling the strict separation of vision-only and vision-language contexts required for the dual-branch strategy. Unlike architectures that feed all input tokens to the action head, LAQs decouple the DiT's complexity from the VLM input context length, improving computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. Dual-Branch Training Framework

The framework utilizes two parallel branches that share the same VLM weights but process different input configurations:

1. **Priori Branch (Vision-Only):**
 - **Input Sequence:** `[v, Q, ℓ]` (vision tokens, Latent Action Queries, language tokens).
 - **Information Flow:** Due to causal attention masking, the LAQ tokens (Q) can attend to the visual observations (v) but are causally masked from attending to the language instructions (ℓ), which appear later in the sequence.
 - **Function:** The hidden states `H_prior_Q` from this branch encode purely visual information, allowing the model to estimate the vision-only prior p(a | v).
 - **Training:** A flow-matching loss `L_FM(ψ; H_prior_Q)` is used to learn the action distribution based on this visual-only context. Importantly, `H_prior_Q` is detached from the computation graph during this loss optimization to ensure that VLM backbone updates are not influenced by visual shortcuts related to action prediction for the prior, confining gradient updates primarily to the DiT action head.

2. **Posteriori Branch (Vision + Language):**
 - **Input Sequence:** `[v, ℓ, Q]` (vision tokens, language tokens, Latent Action Queries).
 - **Information Flow:** Here, the LAQ tokens (Q) appear after ℓ, allowing them to attend to both visual observations (v) and language instructions (ℓ).
 - **Function:** The hidden states `H_post_Q` encode the full vision-language context, enabling the model to estimate the language-conditioned posterior policy π(a | v, ℓ).
 - **Training:** A main flow-matching loss `L_FM(ψ; H_post_Q)` is optimized to learn the expert action from this full context.

#### 3.4. Maximizing the Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR)

In addition to action prediction, the LLR objective is explicitly optimized. The VLM’s language modeling loss serves as a proxy for log p(ℓ | ...).
- **LLR Loss:** `L_LLR = log p(ℓ | v, H_prior_Q) - sg (log p(ℓ | v))`. This term is minimized (equivalent to maximizing the LLR).
- **Interpretation:** The first term approximates the log-likelihood of instruction ℓ given vision and the action information encoded by the *prior* (i.e., action explained by vision). The second term, with a stop-gradient (sg), represents the log-likelihood of instruction ℓ given vision alone. Maximizing the difference forces the action representation to contribute uniquely to explaining the language, beyond what vision already provides. The stop-gradient prevents trivial maximization by degrading the baseline p(ℓ | v).

#### 3.5. Total Training Objective

The overall training loss combines the flow-matching losses from both branches and the LLR term:
$L_{total} = (1 - λ)L_{FM}(ψ; H_{post\_Q}) + λL_{FM}(ψ; H_{prior\_Q}) - βL_{LLR}$
where λ balances the contribution of the prior and posterior action losses (set to 0.3), and β controls the strength of LLR regularization (set to 0.1).
During inference, only the Posteriori Branch is utilized, ensuring no additional computational overhead compared to standard VLA baselines. The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) uses the Rectified Flow Matching objective for continuous action generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LangForce was conducted across multiple benchmarks, including simulation environments (SimplerEnv, RoboCasa, LIBERO) and real-world robotic setups, providing both empirical validation of the vision shortcut and evidence of LangForce's efficacy.

#### 4.1. Empirical Validation of the Vision Shortcut

The paper first substantiated the "vision shortcut" hypothesis through three pilot experiments using a Qwen3VL-4B-GR00T model:
- **ID Testing (RoboCasa):** A vision-only model achieved a 44.6% success rate, close to the language-conditioned baseline's 47.8%, suggesting that models can often succeed without explicit language reliance in in-distribution settings.
- **Ambiguous Scenarios (LIBERO Goal):** While vision-only models performed comparably to full VLA models on unambiguous LIBERO subsets, their success rate plummeted to 9.8% (vs. 97.4% for baseline) on the LIBERO Goal subset, where multiple tasks correspond to the same visual scene. This confirmed models rely on the prior p(a | v) in ambiguous contexts.
- **OOD Generalization (SimplerEnv):** A vision-only model trained on diverse datasets (BridgeDataV2, Fractal) achieved action loss comparable to a full VLA model. However, it exhibited near 0% success when evaluated on the visually distinct SimplerEnv OOD benchmark, indicating that optimizing for low training loss does not guarantee true language grounding or OOD generalization.
- **Information Collapse Quantification (LIBERO Goal):** Quantitative analysis showed LangForce achieved a significantly higher Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) and Perplexity (PPL) for p(ℓ | v) (9.47 nats/token, 12964.9 PPL) compared to the QwenGR00T baseline (8.51 nats/token, 4964.1 PPL). This indicates that LangForce successfully preserves the inherent uncertainty of language given only vision, preventing information collapse and compelling the model to use language for disambiguation.

#### 4.2. Performance on SimplerEnv Benchmark

LangForce achieved an average success rate of 66.5%, outperforming comparison baselines:
- An 11.3% absolute improvement over the QwenGR00T baseline (55.2%).
- Specific task improvements: "Put Carrot on Plate" (+13.6%) and "Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket" (+15.0%).
- Surpassed other strong competitors like π0.5 (57.1%) and Isaac-GR00T-N1.6 (57.1%).
These results validate LangForce's ability to mitigate the vision shortcut and enhance generalization to OOD simulation environments.

#### 4.3. Performance on RoboCasa Benchmark

LangForce achieved a state-of-the-art average success rate of 52.6% on the 24 diverse manipulation tasks in RoboCasa:
- It surpassed baselines such as QwenOFT (48.8%), Isaac-GR00T N1.5 (48.2%), and QwenGR00T (47.8%).
- Notably, it substantially outperformed the VisionOnly baseline (44.7%), demonstrating the impact of language grounding. For example, in "PnP Novel From Placemat To Bowl," LangForce reached 62.0%, significantly higher than VisionOnly (32.0%) and QwenGR00T (44.0%).

#### 4.4. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark

On the LIBERO benchmark, where many baselines already achieve high scores, LangForce still showed improvement on the challenging Goal suite:
- It achieved a 99.4% success rate, outperforming the Qwen3-VL-GR00T baseline (97.4%) by +2.0%, further confirming its effectiveness in resolving visual ambiguity.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments (Franka Research 3 arm)

- **Colored-Block Pick-and-Place:**
 - In-domain (single block): LangForce achieved 25/30 successes, comparable to π0.5 and slightly better than QwenGR00T (21/30).
 - Out-of-Distribution (OOD) with an unseen Red block: LangForce achieved 9/30 successes, significantly surpassing QwenGR00T (2/30). This highlights enhanced instruction following and generalization to novel semantic concepts.
- **Vegetable Pick-and-Place:** LangForce achieved an overall success rate of 80.8% (97/120), outperforming QwenGR00T's 59.2% (71/120). This indicated the benefit transferred to real objects with varied geometry and appearance.

#### 4.6. Preservation of General Capabilities

A qualitative comparison demonstrated that LangForce preserved the VLM’s original text-only conversational and reasoning abilities, successfully solving mathematical problems and answering open-domain questions. In contrast, the standard QwenGR00T baseline exhibited catastrophic forgetting, producing repetitive or meaningless output when prompted with pure text. This is attributed to the LLR objective enforcing a strong dependency on language for action generation, preventing linguistic representations from collapsing. An ablation study showed that merely adding an auxiliary p(ℓ | v) objective was insufficient, as it could lead to overfitting to robot-training instructions without genuinely tying language to action-relevant distinctions.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Bayesian Decomposition:** The full LangForce model (66.5% average success) significantly outperformed an ablation with only Latent Action Queries ("+ Action Query," 57.5%), demonstrating that the dual-branch Bayesian learning objective and LLR maximization are central to the performance gains.
- **Potential of Latent Action Queries:** Even without the LLR objective, introducing Latent Action Queries improved performance over the QwenGR00T baseline (55.2% to 57.5%). This suggests LAQs serve as an effective architectural inductive bias, compressing task-relevant information efficiently and reducing computational complexity in the Diffusion Transformer.
- **Hyperparameter Analysis (λ and β):** The study found optimal performance at λ=0.3 and β=0.1. Even with λ=0 (prior branch only for LLR), performance was 63.3%, indicating LLR's regularization power. Setting β=0 (no LLR) yielded 61.3%, showing the dual-branch architecture itself offers benefits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, LangForce, that contributes to the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by systematically addressing a fundamental pathology identified as the "vision shortcut" or "Information Collapse." This issue, where models learn to bypass language instructions by exploiting spurious visual correlations in goal-driven datasets, significantly limits the robustness and generalizability of VLA policies.

The primary significance of LangForce lies in its methodological approach and demonstrated efficacy:

1. **Addressing a Core Limitation:** The work identifies and empirically validates a critical failure mode in VLA training, providing a theoretical foundation rooted in Bayesian decomposition and conditional mutual information. By proposing to maximize the conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) between actions and instructions via a Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) objective, LangForce offers a principled method to counteract this shortcut learning.
2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent improvements demonstrated across diverse benchmarks (SimplerEnv, RoboCasa, LIBERO) and real-world tasks (colored-block and vegetable pick-and-place) highlight LangForce's ability to foster genuine language grounding. This leads to more robust performance in ambiguous scenarios and, notably, in out-of-distribution (OOD) environments, which is a key challenge for real-world robotic deployment. The significant gains in OOD tasks are particularly impactful, as they indicate a move towards more transferable robot skills.
3. **Preservation of Foundational Capabilities:** A notable impact is the preservation of the underlying Vision-Language Model's (VLM) general reasoning and conversational abilities. Unlike standard fine-tuning approaches that often lead to catastrophic forgetting, LangForce's LLR objective acts as a regularizer, maintaining the functional utility of language tokens. This ensures that the VLM backbone remains a capable multimodal agent rather than devolving into a specialized, narrowly-trained controller, thereby retaining its potential for higher-level reasoning and adaptability.
4. **Architectural and Computational Efficiency:** The introduction of Latent Action Queries provides an efficient interface between the VLM and the action decoder. By acting as a bottleneck and leveraging causal masking, these queries allow for the distinct processing required by the dual-branch Bayesian framework without incurring additional computational overhead during inference. This makes the proposed method practical for integration into existing VLA architectures.
5. **Implications for Data Collection and Utilization:** The findings suggest that future data collection strategies could benefit from prioritizing ambiguous scenarios to naturally compel models to rely on language. Simultaneously, LangForce offers a method to extract more meaningful language grounding from the large volume of existing, potentially biased, goal-deterministic datasets, making current data resources more valuable.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper opens avenues for further exploration, such as leveraging human video data (which may inherently have less sharp p(ℓ | v) distributions) and considering alternative Bayesian formulations like those found in World Models, which could offer another robust path to solving the vision shortcut.

In summary, LangForce provides a systematic solution to a critical problem in VLA training, contributing to the development of more generalizable, robust, and language-conditioned robotic policies without sacrificing the broad capabilities of their underlying foundation models.
