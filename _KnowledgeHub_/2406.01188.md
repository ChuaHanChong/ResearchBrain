# UniAnimate: Taming Unified Video Diffusion Models for Consistent Human Image Animation

> [!summary] Summary
> UniAnimate introduces a framework for consistent human image animation, generating long-duration videos from a single reference image and pose sequence. It achieves this by unifying the appearance and motion modeling into a single 3D-UNet and leveraging a temporal Mamba architecture for efficient, long-range temporal coherence, producing one-minute videos with superior fidelity and consistency compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion models for human image animation rely on an extra reference network, increasing architectural complexity and optimization difficulty.
- Temporal Transformers, commonly used, have quadratic computational complexity, severely limiting generated video length (typically 24 frames) and causing inconsistencies when stitching segments.
- Achieving consistent identity and smooth transitions across extended video sequences remains a challenge, with current methods often suffering from artifacts and appearance drifts.

## Method

- A Unified Video ==Diffusion Model== (Unified VDM) that integrates reference image features and noised video latents into a single 3D-UNet, reducing parameters and streamlining optimization.
- A 'unified noised input' strategy with first-frame conditioning enables iterative generation of long videos by using the last generated frame as the starting point for the next segment, ensuring seamless transitions.
- Replaced computationally intensive temporal Transformers with temporal Mamba modules, providing linear-time complexity for efficient and scalable long-range temporal modeling.

## Results

- UniAnimate consistently outperformed state-of-the-art methods on the TikTok dataset, achieving the lowest Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 148.06 and best metrics across L1, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Unified VDM and explicit reference pose are crucial, with their removal significantly degrading performance (e.g., FVD increased to 205.28 without unified VDM).
- Temporal Mamba showed comparable performance to Transformers but with significantly lower memory consumption for long sequences, processing 360 frames (78.8GB) where Transformer-based models ran out of memory (OOM) on an 80G A100 GPU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating appearance and motion processing within a single unified network, alongside explicit reference pose guidance, leads to more robust identity preservation and simplified model optimization.
> - Iteratively conditioning subsequent video segments on the previous segment's final frame effectively overcomes temporal discontinuities and enables the generation of much longer, coherent videos.
> - State Space Models (SSMs), specifically temporal Mamba, can efficiently model long-range temporal dependencies with linear complexity, offering a scalable alternative to Transformers for video generation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human image animation, which aims to generate realistic and high-quality videos of humans performing desired movements based on a single reference image and a pose sequence, has witnessed rapid advancements. This task is crucial for various applications, including virtual try-on, content creation, gaming, and digital avatars.

Historically, the landscape was dominated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Early methods (e.g., FOMM, MRAA, TPS) leveraged intermediate pose-guided representations to warp reference appearances, then used GANs for plausible frame prediction. While these approaches showed promise, they often struggled with training instability, poor generalization, and the generation of non-negligible artifacts and inter-frame jitters, particularly when dealing with complex motions or diverse identities.

More recently, the advent of diffusion models has revolutionized generative AI, including video generation. Diffusion-based methods (e.g., Disco, MagicAnimate, Animate Anyone, Champ) have demonstrated superior performance in terms of training stability, high-fidelity synthesis, and strong transferable capabilities compared to their GAN-based predecessors. These models typically employ a 3D-UNet architecture for video denoising and often utilize a ControlNet-like mechanism to incorporate conditional guidance, such as pose information and reference images. Many state-of-the-art diffusion models for human image animation, such as Animate Anyone and MagicAnimate, use an additional "reference network" (often a mirrored branch of the main 3D-UNet) to extract appearance features from the reference image, which are then aligned with the main video generation pipeline.

Despite these significant advancements, two critical limitations persist in the current diffusion-based human image animation landscape, which UniAnimate directly addresses:

1. **Architectural Complexity and Optimization Burden:** The common practice of employing an *extra reference network* to align the identity image with the main video branch substantially increases model parameters and optimization difficulty. This architectural redundancy can hinder efficiency and make training more resource-intensive.
2. **Limited Video Length and Temporal Coherence for Long Sequences:** Most existing methods rely on temporal Transformers for modeling spatio-temporal dependencies. While effective, Transformers have a *quadratic computational complexity* with respect to the video's temporal dimension. This fundamental limitation restricts the generated video length, typically to short segments (e.g., 24 frames), which is insufficient for many practical applications. Although some methods employ "slide window" strategies (e.g., MagicAnimate) to synthesize longer videos by stitching short segments, these often suffer from non-smooth transitions and appearance inconsistencies at segment connections.

UniAnimate strategically positions itself as a solution that builds upon the strengths of diffusion models while mitigating these two core weaknesses. It aims to simplify the architecture, improve appearance alignment, and most notably, enable consistent and efficient generation of *long-term videos*. By introducing a unified model architecture and a novel temporal modeling component, UniAnimate seeks to push the boundaries of practical human image animation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the UniAnimate framework are to address the aforementioned limitations in diffusion-based human image animation, specifically aiming to enable **efficient** and **long-term** human video generation while maintaining **consistent** identity and smooth motion.

The motivations driving UniAnimate can be broken down as follows:

- **Reducing Optimization Difficulty and Model Complexity:** Existing diffusion-based methods typically employ an additional, separate reference model to extract and align identity features from the input image with the main video generation branch. This redundant architecture leads to a significant increase in model parameters and makes the overall optimization process more challenging. The authors are motivated to streamline this process by integrating identity and motion modeling more cohesively.
- **Enhancing Temporal Coherence and Appearance Alignment:** Despite progress, achieving perfect, enduring temporal coherence and precise appearance preservation from the reference image across entire generated videos remains a challenge. Artifacts, inter-frame jitters, or slight deviations from the reference identity can still occur, especially in methods that rely on loosely coupled networks for appearance and motion. The motivation here is to ensure that the generated video faithfully represents the reference character's appearance throughout its duration.
- **Overcoming Video Length Limitations for Practical Applications:** The reliance on temporal Transformers, with their quadratic complexity, severely limits the maximum length of videos that can be generated in a single pass (often to just 24 frames). This short duration significantly restricts the practical utility of these models. Current strategies for longer videos, like slide windows, often introduce visual discontinuities and appearance inconsistencies at segment boundaries. A key motivation is to enable the generation of substantially longer videos (e.g., one minute or more) with seamless transitions, which is critical for real-world deployment in areas like film, advertising, and interactive media.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency for Long Sequences:** Directly linked to the video length limitation, the high computational cost of temporal Transformers becomes prohibitive for longer sequences. There is a clear motivation to find a more efficient temporal modeling mechanism that scales linearly with video length, thus making long-duration video generation feasible within reasonable memory and time constraints.

In essence, UniAnimate is motivated by the desire to push human image animation towards greater practical applicability by making the generation process more efficient, more consistent in appearance and motion, and capable of producing significantly longer, high-quality video outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniAnimate builds upon the foundation of latent diffusion models (LDMs), which perform denoising in a compact latent space rather than the high-dimensional pixel space, leveraging a pre-trained Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for encoding and decoding. The core denoising model is a 3D-UNet. UniAnimate introduces three key architectural and strategic innovations to address its objectives:

1. **Unified Video Diffusion Model (Unified VDM):**
 - **Departure from Prior Work:** Unlike previous methods (e.g., Animate Anyone, MagicAnimate) that use an extra, separate network (often a mirrored 3D-UNet branch without temporal modules) to process the reference image for appearance alignment, UniAnimate proposes a single, unified 3D-UNet.
 - **Joint Encoding:** This unified VDM is designed to simultaneously process the reference image features and the noised video latent. This means the appearance information from the reference image and the motion information for the generated video are learned and processed within the same neural network, specifically within shared layers of the 3D-UNet.
 - **Reference Guidance:** The reference image is first encoded into a latent feature `C1 x h x w` using the VAE encoder. Crucially, to provide explicit structural guidance, the skeletal pose (extracted by DWpose) of the *reference image* is also encoded via a pose encoder and fused with the reference image latent to create `f_ref`.
 - **Motion Guidance:** The target driving pose sequence is encoded by a separate pose encoder. This driving pose feature is then concatenated along the channel dimension with the noised input video latent (`f_v`).
 - **Unified Input to 3D-UNet:** The `f_ref` (reference appearance and pose) and `f_v` (noised video and driving pose) are then stacked along the temporal dimension. This combined feature `f_merge` (with `t+1` temporal length, where `t` is video length, `+1` for reference image) is fed into the unified 3D-UNet.
 - **Advantages:** This unified structure ensures that the feature representations of the reference image and the generated video exist in a common feature space, facilitating more effective and robust appearance alignment. It also significantly reduces the total model parameters and simplifies the optimization process. The explicit incorporation of the reference pose helps the model better understand the human body structure and layout, further aiding in appearance preservation.

2. **Unified Noised Input for Long Video Generation:**
 - **Addressing Discontinuities:** To overcome the limitations of short video generation and the inconsistencies arising from slide window strategies, UniAnimate introduces a "unified noised input" strategy.
 - **First-Frame Conditioning:** This strategy supports two modes: standard random noised video input or "first frame conditioned" input. The latter is critical for long-term generation. When generating a new video segment, the model can take the *last frame of the previously generated segment* (after VAE encoding and noise addition) as its explicit "first frame condition." This ensures a seamless continuation of the video.
 - **Iterative Generation:** By iteratively applying this first-frame conditioning, UniAnimate can sequentially generate multiple short video segments, where each subsequent segment begins precisely where the previous one ended. This "chaining" mechanism allows for the synthesis of much longer videos (demonstrated up to one minute) with smooth and visually coherent transitions.
 - **Flexibility:** This also allows users to input any custom image as the starting frame for a video sequence.

3. **Temporal Modeling with Temporal Mamba:**
 - **Overcoming Quadratic Complexity:** To replace the computationally intensive temporal Transformers, UniAnimate explores temporal Mamba for modeling temporal coherence. Mamba, based on State Space Models (SSMs), offers a linear-time complexity with respect to the sequence length, making it highly efficient for long sequences.
 - **Mechanism:** Mamba conceptually merges the benefits of parallelism and non-locality. The paper utilizes a discretized version of Mamba that incorporates a bidirectional scanning mechanism to effectively capture temporal dependencies across frames.
 - **Integration:** Temporal Mamba modules are integrated into the temporal blocks of the 3D-UNet, replacing the traditional temporal Transformer layers.
 - **Advantages:** This architectural swap significantly improves the efficiency of long video generation by drastically reducing memory consumption and computation time, without sacrificing performance quality.

**Training and Inference:**
The model is trained using a standard denoising objective (L2 loss) to estimate the noise added to the latent video. To enhance robustness, random dropout is applied to input conditions (first frame and reference image). During inference, the DDIM sampler with 50 steps is used. For long videos, the first segment is generated using the reference image and driving pose, and subsequent segments use the reference image along with the last frame of the preceding segment as a first-frame condition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

UniAnimate's effectiveness is rigorously evaluated through extensive quantitative, qualitative, and human evaluation, along with detailed ablation studies. The main findings and results are:

- **Superior Quantitative Performance:**
 - **TikTok Dataset:** UniAnimate consistently outperforms all existing state-of-the-art counterparts (including Disco, MagicAnimate, Animate Anyone, and Champ) across all measured metrics. It achieved the best L1 (2.66E-04), PSNR (30.77), PSNR\* (20.58), SSIM (0.811), LPIPS (0.231), and most notably, the lowest Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 148.06, indicating superior video fidelity and realism.
 - **Fashion Dataset:** Similarly, UniAnimate demonstrated impressive performance on the Fashion dataset, achieving the best SSIM (0.940) for structural preservation and the lowest FVD of 68.1, outperforming methods like Animate Anyone and DBMM. These results collectively affirm UniAnimate's strong capabilities in synthesizing visually faithful animations across different domains.

- **Outstanding Qualitative Performance:**
 - Visual comparisons (Figure 3) against MagicAnimate and Animate Anyone on the TikTok dataset reveal that UniAnimate generates high-quality, coherent pose transfer results that closely adhere to input conditions. Competitors often exhibit issues like limb generation artifacts, appearance misalignment, or undesirable visual glitches. UniAnimate, in contrast, consistently produces realistic and well-controlled animations.

- **Positive Human Evaluation:**
 - A user study (Table 3) confirmed the superior performance of UniAnimate. Users rated UniAnimate highest in visual quality (85%), identity preservation (89%), and temporal consistency (91%) compared to MagicAnimate, Animate Anyone, and Champ, reinforcing the objective metrics with subjective perception.

- **Validation of Key Components (Ablation Study):**
 - **Unified Video Diffusion Model (VDM) and Reference Pose:** The ablation study (Table 4, Figure 4) demonstrates the critical importance of both the unified VDM and the explicit incorporation of the reference pose.
 - Removing the reference pose led to a significant drop in performance across all metrics (e.g., FVD increased from 148.06 to 182.41) and resulted in visual artifacts like "disconnected limbs."
 - Replacing the unified VDM with a ControlNet-like architecture (i.e., separate networks for appearance and temporal coherence) also degraded performance (FVD increased to 205.28) and caused appearance inconsistencies and mismatched backgrounds, validating the effectiveness of the unified approach in facilitating feature alignment and simplifying optimization.

- **Effectiveness and Efficiency of Temporal Mamba:**
 - **Performance Comparison:** When comparing temporal Mamba with the default temporal Transformer (Table 5), Mamba achieved comparable, and in some metrics, slightly better performance (e.g., lower L1, LPIPS, and higher PSNR\*). This indicates that temporal Mamba can effectively model temporal dependencies without compromising generation quality.
 - **Memory Efficiency for Long Sequences:** Crucially, temporal Mamba exhibited significantly lower memory consumption (Table 6) as video length increased, confirming its linear complexity advantage. While the Transformer-based model ran out of memory (OOM) at 360 frames on an 80G A100 GPU, temporal Mamba successfully processed 360 frames (at 78.8GB) and showed a much more graceful increase in memory usage for longer sequences (e.g., 62.4G for 256 frames vs. Transformer's 73.6G). This confirms Mamba's suitability for long-range temporal modeling.

- **Successful Long Video Generation with Smooth Transitions:**
 - The unified noised input with the first-frame conditioning strategy effectively enabled the generation of long videos (e.g., highly consistent one-minute videos, as shown in Figure 1).
 - Qualitative comparison (Figure 6) with the conventional slide window strategy highlighted UniAnimate's superior ability to create smooth and seamless transitions between video segments, avoiding the discontinuous appearance and inconsistent backgrounds that plague slide window approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniAnimate framework represents a significant advancement in the field of human image animation, addressing several critical limitations of existing state-of-the-art diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Redefining Efficiency and Consistency in Animation:** By introducing a **unified video diffusion model**, UniAnimate streamlines the architecture, reducing the parameter count and simplifying the optimization process. This architectural simplification directly contributes to more efficient training and inference while simultaneously enhancing the consistency of appearance alignment between the reference image and the generated video. This is a crucial step towards more practical and deployable animation systems.
- **Breakthrough in Long-Term Video Generation:** The most impactful contribution of UniAnimate is its robust solution for **long-term video generation** with seamless transitions. The **unified noised input** strategy, particularly the **first-frame conditioning mechanism**, effectively solves the challenge of stitching together short video segments without introducing visual discontinuities or appearance shifts. The demonstrated capability to generate highly consistent one-minute videos (and potentially longer) represents a major leap forward, overcoming the severe length limitations of previous methods.
- **Pioneering Temporal Modeling with Mamba:** UniAnimate is among the first to successfully integrate **temporal Mamba** into the human image animation task as an effective replacement for the computationally expensive temporal Transformers. This demonstrates the viability and efficiency of State Space Models (SSMs) for complex spatio-temporal modeling in generative AI. The linear complexity of Mamba offers a scalable solution for processing extended video sequences, paving the way for future research into even longer and higher-resolution video generation tasks across various domains.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity:** The comprehensive quantitative and qualitative results, corroborated by human evaluation, firmly establish UniAnimate's superiority in generating high-fidelity, visually appealing, and precisely controllable human animations. The model faithfully preserves identity while accurately translating desired pose sequences into dynamic, lifelike movements.
- **Broadening Practical Applications:** The ability to generate consistent and long-duration animated human videos unlocks numerous practical applications. This includes:
 - **Creative Industries:** Film, television, and advertising can benefit from faster and more efficient character animation workflows.
 - **Gaming and Virtual Reality:** Creation of realistic digital avatars and NPC animations.
 - **Personalized Content Creation:** Empowering users to easily animate their photos for social media or other platforms.
 - **Virtual Try-on and E-commerce:** Generating dynamic garment previews on diverse body types.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** Development of more expressive and lifelike virtual assistants.
- **Fostering Open Research and Future Directions:** The authors' commitment to publicly releasing their code and models will undoubtedly foster further research and innovation in the field. Furthermore, the explicit discussion of limitations, such as fine-grained details in facial/hand regions and potential inconsistencies in invisible parts during long video generation, provides clear and valuable directions for future improvements, encouraging the community to build upon UniAnimate's foundational work.

In conclusion, UniAnimate significantly advances the state-of-the-art in human image animation by offering a more efficient, consistent, and scalable framework. Its novel architectural design and adoption of linear-complexity temporal modeling open up exciting possibilities for creating truly practical and high-quality long-form human video content.
