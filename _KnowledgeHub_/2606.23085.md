# Foresight: Failure Detection for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation with Action-Conditioned World Model Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> A new three-stage framework named Foresight detects failures in long-horizon robotic manipulation by leveraging action-conditioned world model latents and Functional Conformal Prediction. The system achieved calibrated balanced accuracy of up to 0.94 on LIBERO-Long and improved ROC-AUC by 0.04 on BEHAVIOR-1K compared to baselines, while offering policy-agnostic monitoring with statistical guarantees.

## Problem

- Detecting failures in long-horizon robotic tasks is challenging due to ambiguous failure onset, accumulated small deviations, and the impracticality of dense temporal annotations.
- Interpreting robot states requires integrating temporal and action-conditioned context, as visual observations' implications vary with task stage and intended actions.
- Existing failure detection methods are often policy-specific, relying on internal representations, which limits their generalizability across diverse robot policies.

## Method

![[2606.23085-method.png]]
*he Foresight framework operates in three stages: extracting features from an action-conditioned world model, training a causal sequence model to predict failure scores, and calibrating those scores for reliable deployment.*

- The **Foresight** framework extracts ==action-conditioned predicted latents== from a frozen V-JEPA 2-AC world model, which, combined with positional encodings, forms timestep tokens.
- A causal sequence model (e.g., Transformer) is trained on these tokens using only sparse, trajectory-level success/failure labels to predict per-timestep failure scores.
- Functional Conformal Prediction (FCP) is integrated to generate adaptively calibrated, time-varying thresholds, providing statistically guaranteed false positive rates for binary failure alarms.

## Results

- Foresight-Transformer achieved the highest calibrated balanced accuracy (**0.94**) and ROC-AUC (**0.89**) on LIBERO-Long, and improved balanced accuracy to **0.78** on BEHAVIOR-1K, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines in simulation.
- The framework demonstrated strong real-world applicability with ROC-AUCs up to **0.93** on ReactorX and **0.89** on Franka arms, showing robust performance across different robot platforms and policies.
- Ablation studies confirmed the superiority of action-conditioned predicted latents over hidden latents for failure detection, with a **0.04** ROC-AUC improvement on LIBERO-Long, and validated the efficacy of causal sequence models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-conditioned predicted latents from world models are crucial for detecting long-horizon failures, as they effectively identify inconsistencies between intended actions and observed state transitions.
> - Causal sequence models, particularly Transformers, excel at processing temporal dependencies in these latents, leading to more robust failure prediction than feed-forward networks.
> - A policy-agnostic framework can be developed using general world model embeddings, reducing reliance on policy-specific internal signals and enhancing scalability across different robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The detection of failures in robotic operations is a critical area within robotics research, aimed at enhancing the reliability and autonomy of robotic systems. Prior work in failure detection has explored various signals and approaches. Some methods estimate failure scores using policy uncertainty, policy-internal representations, or vision-language models for detecting visible mistakes and providing explanations. Other approaches have utilized embedding distributions or world-model latents. While these methods have demonstrated the possibility of early failure detection, many have focused on short-horizon tasks, isolated visual anomalies, or policy-specific confidence signals.

The challenge intensifies for "long-horizon" robotic tasks, which involve multiple subgoals, complex action sequences, and potentially hundreds or thousands of steps. In such scenarios, small deviations can accumulate over time, leading to irreversible failures that are difficult to predict solely from local observations or policy-internal signals. The meaning of a visual state in long-horizon tasks is highly dependent on the action history and task stage, requiring detectors to assess consistency with implied progress rather than just visual anomaly.

A growing line of research has investigated the use of world models for learning general-purpose representations or simulating physical dynamics from large-scale video data. Specifically, action-conditioned world models, such as V-JEPA 2-AC, learn to predict future latent states conditioned on robot actions. These models can encode task-relevant state cues, including spatial relationships, motion patterns, and interaction dynamics, which are relevant for monitoring consistency between observed execution and intended behavior. Foresight positions itself within this emerging trend by leveraging action-conditioned world model latents to address the specific challenges of failure detection in long-horizon robotic manipulation, aiming for a unified and policy-agnostic framework. This approach differentiates it from methods that rely on policy-specific signals or simpler visual features, particularly in its focus on the temporal and action-conditioned nature of long-horizon failures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable framework for failure detection in long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The motivation stems from several specific challenges inherent to these tasks:

- **Ambiguity of Failure Onset:** In long-horizon tasks, the exact moment a failure occurs or becomes irreversible is often unclear. Small deviations can accumulate over many steps before leading to a definitive task failure, making pinpointing the failure onset difficult for manual annotation or simple thresholding.
- **Lack of Dense Temporal Annotations:** Training failure detection systems typically requires data indicating when failures happen. However, acquiring dense, step-by-step failure annotations for long-horizon robot trajectories is labor-intensive and impractical. The authors aim to overcome this by training the system using only sparse, trajectory-level success or failure labels.
- **Context-Dependent State Interpretation:** The visual appearance of a robot's state can have different implications depending on the ongoing actions and the task stage. For example, an object on a table could be expected before a grasp, a sign of failure after a lift command, or correct after a placement action. An effective detector must integrate this temporal and action-conditioned context to interpret observations correctly.
- **Policy-Agnostic Detection:** Many existing failure detection methods are tailored to specific robot policies, relying on policy-internal representations (e.g., logits, hidden states, uncertainty estimates). This limits their generalizability across different policies or new policy deployments. The authors aim to create a framework that is policy-interface-agnostic, meaning it can be applied to different policies (e.g., vision-language-action (VLA) and visuomotor policies) without requiring access to their internal mechanisms.
- **Adaptive Calibration for Reliability:** To provide actionable failure alarms, the detection system needs to output a binary decision with controlled false positive rates. Fixed thresholds are often inadequate due to variations in task difficulty and execution conditions. The motivation is to integrate adaptive thresholding mechanisms to ensure statistically reliable detection.

By addressing these challenges, the research seeks to enable robots to recognize not only when a task has failed, but also when an ongoing execution is drifting towards failure, thereby allowing for timely intervention or recovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Foresight is presented as a three-stage failure detection framework that leverages latent representations from an action-conditioned world model.

#### 3.1. System Overview

The framework processes visual observations ($I_t$) and policy-predicted action chunks ($A_t$) at each timestep $t$ to produce a per-timestep failure score ($s_t$). This score is then compared against a calibrated, time-varying threshold ($\delta_t$) to trigger a binary failure alarm. Foresight is designed to be policy-interface-agnostic, relying on features from an action-conditioned video world model rather than policy-internal signals, which promotes cross-policy generalization.

#### 3.2. World Model Feature Extraction

The core of Foresight’s feature extraction relies on V-JEPA 2-AC, an action-conditioned world model.
1. **Frozen Visual Encoder:** The visual encoder ($f_\phi$) of the pretrained V-JEPA 2-AC model is kept frozen. This encoder takes an observation context ($c_t$, typically a sliding window of recent image frames) and produces an observed latent representation ($z^h_t$) by pooling spatial patch embeddings. This latent $z^h_t$ captures what is currently perceived in the environment.
2. **Trained Action Predictor:** An action predictor ($g_\psi$) is attached to the frozen visual encoder and trained from scratch on robot rollouts. This predictor takes the observed latent $z^h_t$ and the policy-predicted action chunk ($A_t$) as input. It then generates an action-conditioned predicted latent ($z^p_t$), which represents the world model's expectation of how the scene should evolve given the proposed actions.
3. **Timestep Token Formulation:** The predicted latent $z^p_t$ is used to form the timestep token ($u_t$), which is the input to the downstream failure detector. This token is created by a linear projection of $z^p_t$ combined with a sinusoidal positional encoding ($p_t$), integrating both action-conditioned state prediction and temporal context. The choice of $z^p_t$ over $z^h_t$ is based on the hypothesis that action-conditioned predictions better capture execution-level failure cues, such as mismatches between intended actions and observed state transitions.

#### 3.3. Failure Scoring with Causal Sequence Models

A causal sequence model is employed to predict the per-timestep failure score ($s_t$) based on the sequence of timestep tokens ($U_{\le t}$) available up to the current time $t$.
1. **Causal Architecture:** The model uses a causal mask to ensure that the prediction at any timestep $t$ only depends on information observed up to and including $t$, preventing "peeking" into future events.
2. **Detector Implementations:** Three architectures were evaluated for the detector ($D_\theta$):
 - **MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron):** Processes each timestep independently.
 - **LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory):** A recurrent neural network that can capture temporal dependencies across the sequence.
 - **Causal Transformer:** Utilizes masked self-attention and positional encodings to process the sequence, designed to capture long-range dependencies efficiently.
3. **Training with Trajectory-Level Labels:** The detector is trained using only binary, trajectory-level success or failure labels. To encourage early detection, each timestep in a failed rollout inherits the rollout-level failure label, and an early-detection weighting is applied during training.

#### 3.4. Conformal Prediction Thresholding

To convert continuous failure scores into reliable binary alarms with statistical guarantees, Foresight incorporates Functional Conformal Prediction (FCP).
1. **Time-Varying Threshold:** FCP constructs a one-sided, time-varying upper band $\delta_t = \mu_t + h_t$.
2. **Mean Score Trajectory ($\mu_t$):** This is estimated from the mean of failure scores generated by successful rollouts in a dedicated calibration set.
3. **Calibrated Bandwidth Term ($h_t$):** A time-varying modulation term $\sigma_t$ (capturing score deviation) is used to compute nonconformity scores ($R_i$) for calibration rollouts. The $(1-\alpha)$-quantile ($\hat{q}$) of these nonconformity scores determines the bandwidth $h_t = \hat{q}\sigma_t$. The parameter $\alpha$ controls the desired false positive rate.
4. **Binary Alarm:** A failure is declared at the first timestep ($t^*$) where the predicted failure score $s_t$ exceeds the calibrated threshold $\delta_t$. This approach offers a statistical guarantee that the false positive rate on truly successful rollouts is controlled at level $\alpha$, assuming mild exchangeability conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Foresight was conducted across diverse long-horizon simulation benchmarks and real-robot rollouts, comparing it against several state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Benchmarks

- **Simulation:** LIBERO-Long (tabletop, OpenVLA/π0-FAST, ~250 steps), ManiSkill-Long (compositional, π0-FAST, ~1,500 steps), and BEHAVIOR-1K (mobile manipulation, revised π0.5, ~8,500 steps). These covered horizons from hundreds to thousands of steps.
- **Real-World:** ReactorX-200 arm with ACT, π0.5, and SmolVLA policies on three tabletop arrangement tasks, and a Franka arm with GR00T N1.5 policy on a toy pick-up task (~1,200 steps).

#### 4.2. Baselines

Foresight was compared against FAIL-Detect, SAFE, RND, and Gauge. These baselines include uncertainty-based, policy-internal representation-based, embedding distribution-based, and world-model-based (video latents) approaches.

#### 4.3. Main Results

- **Simulation Performance:** Foresight-Transformer consistently achieved the highest calibrated balanced accuracy and ROC-AUC across all three simulation benchmarks.
 - LIBERO-Long: 0.94 ± 0.06 balanced accuracy, 0.89 ± 0.02 ROC-AUC.
 - ManiSkill-Long: 0.80 ± 0.10 balanced accuracy, 0.84 ± 0.03 ROC-AUC.
 - BEHAVIOR-1K: 0.78 ± 0.02 balanced accuracy, 0.76 ± 0.02 ROC-AUC.
 - Notably, on BEHAVIOR-1K, the longest and most challenging benchmark, Foresight-Transformer showed a substantial improvement (0.14 balanced accuracy and 0.04 ROC-AUC) over the best non-Foresight baseline (SAFE-LSTM, 0.64 ± 0.05 balanced accuracy, 0.72 ± 0.02 ROC-AUC), suggesting the efficacy of action-conditioned world-model features for extended trajectories.

- **Real-World Performance:** Foresight also demonstrated strong performance in real-robot settings, achieving the best ROC-AUC in three out of four scenarios.
 - ReactorX / ACT: 0.93 ± 0.01 ROC-AUC.
 - ReactorX / π0.5: 0.87 ± 0.03 ROC-AUC.
 - Franka / GR00T N1.5: 0.89 ± 0.10 ROC-AUC.
 - Foresight-LSTM also showed competitive results, while Foresight-MLP generally performed near chance levels, indicating the importance of sequence-level detectors for robust real-world monitoring.

- **Cross-Policy Generalization:** Foresight exhibited the capability for cross-policy generalization.
 - A detector trained on π0.5 rollouts transferred effectively to ACT rollouts in the real world (0.94 ± 0.02 ROC-AUC, 0.82 ± 0.08 balanced accuracy).
 - However, transfer was policy-dependent and asymmetric; training on ACT rollouts transferred poorly to π0.5 rollouts (0.56 ± 0.07 ROC-AUC). This asymmetry is attributed to differences in policy behavior and failure modes, where some policies might include recovery trajectories not present in others.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **World-Model Backbone Comparison:** V-JEPA 2-AC as the world-model backbone provided stronger failure detection features (e.g., Foresight-Transformer ROC-AUC 0.89 ± 0.02 on LIBERO-Long) compared to Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B robot-AC (ROC-AUC 0.84 ± 0.02). This suggests that V-JEPA-style latent prediction, which focuses on predictable aspects of a scene rather than pixel-level details, is more effective for capturing relevant cues for failure detection.
- **Hidden Latents vs. Action-Conditioned Predicted Latents:** Using action-conditioned predicted latents ($z^p_t$) consistently outperformed hidden latents ($z^h_t$) across all detector architectures and benchmarks (e.g., on LIBERO-Long, Foresight-Transformer with $z^p_t$ achieved 0.89 ± 0.02 ROC-AUC vs. 0.85 ± 0.02 with $z^h_t$). This validates the hypothesis that action conditioning is crucial for capturing mismatches between intended actions and observed state transitions, which are critical for detecting long-horizon failures that are not merely visual anomalies.

#### 4.5. Runtime and Deployment

Inference latency measurements on an NVIDIA H200 GPU showed that the V-JEPA 2-AC world-model backbone dominated Foresight's total inference time (approximately 183 ms), while the detector heads (MLP, LSTM, Transformer) added less than 1 ms. Although this is higher than baselines that leverage existing policy-internal features, the latency is still within the budget for typical robot replan intervals (e.g., 16 control steps for π0-FAST), indicating practical deployability for runtime monitoring.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Foresight makes several contributions to the field of robotic failure detection, particularly for long-horizon manipulation tasks:

- **Novel Framework for Long-Horizon Failure Detection:** The research introduces a framework that specifically addresses the challenges of failure detection in long-horizon robotic tasks, where failure onset is often ambiguous and dense temporal annotations are unavailable. By leveraging action-conditioned world-model representations, Foresight provides a method that looks beyond instantaneous visual anomalies to assess consistency with implied task progress.
- **Scalable and Policy-Agnostic Approach:** Foresight demonstrates that failure detection can be achieved effectively using only trajectory-level success/failure labels, significantly reducing the annotation burden. Its reliance on general action-conditioned world model embeddings, rather than policy-specific internals, renders it policy-interface-agnostic. This capability enables the same framework to be applied across different vision-language-action and visuomotor policies, promoting scalability and adaptability in real-world robot deployments.
- **Enhanced Performance on Complex Tasks:** The consistent outperformance of Foresight, particularly on challenging long-horizon benchmarks like BEHAVIOR-1K and in real-world experiments, suggests that action-grounded world-model embeddings provide effective signals for identifying execution failures. This is especially impactful for tasks requiring extended executions and complex interactions.
- **Reliable Detection with Conformal Prediction:** The integration of functional conformal prediction provides adaptively calibrated detection thresholds with statistical guarantees on false positive rates. This enhances the reliability of the failure alarms, which is crucial for building trust in autonomous systems and enabling timely intervention.

The findings suggest that action-conditioned world-model embeddings represent a promising interface for scalable and policy-adaptable runtime monitoring in robotic manipulation. This could lead to more robust and autonomous robotic systems capable of identifying and potentially recovering from failures in complex, multi-stage real-world scenarios.

**Limitations:** The authors acknowledge that the computational cost and latency of pretrained world models remain a key limitation. The current inference time, while acceptable for certain replan intervals, might restrict applicability to highly reactive or agile tasks demanding very fast closed-loop control. Additionally, while conformal calibration offers statistical guarantees, its effectiveness depends on the calibration distribution accurately matching deployment conditions, which can be a challenge in dynamic real-world environments.
