# SkillPlug: Unsupervised Skill Mining for Few-Shot Adaptation in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SkillPlug is an unsupervised skill-mining framework developed by researchers at Nanyang Technological University that learns transferable skill abstractions from raw multi-task demonstrations. This framework significantly enhances multi-task generalization and data-efficient few-shot adaptation for robotic manipulation policies across simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- End-to-end visuomotor policies often struggle with data-efficient generalization and rapid few-shot adaptation to novel tasks due to implicitly learned behavioral structures.
- Existing skill learning methods either demand laborious manual annotation or produce redundant and non-transferable skills without explicit objectives for reusability and distinctness.
- Achieving multi-task generalization typically requires extensive retraining or fine-tuning for new tasks, leading to high data inefficiency.

## Method

- **SkillPlug** augments existing visuomotor policies with a plug-in ==skill-conditioning module== and a shared library of learnable, continuous latent skill embeddings.
- The framework employs multiple self-supervised objectives, including ==reconstruction loss==, ==KL regularization==, ==behavioral skill alignment==, and ==skill disentanglement==, to extract explicit, shared, and transferable skills.
- For few-shot adaptation to novel tasks, only lightweight components (router and action head) are fine-tuned, while the learned skill library and policy backbone remain frozen, enabling data-efficient specialization.

## Results

- SkillPlug substantially improved multi-task success rates on DISCOVERSE (e.g., jujube pick from **6.7%** to **94.0%**) and achieved competitive results on LIBERO when augmenting ACT and OpenVLA-OFT base policies.
- It demonstrated significant gains in few-shot adaptation, increasing success rates on unseen DISCOVERSE tasks (e.g., cuplid cover from **0.7%** to **18.7%** with **10** demonstrations) and LIBERO cross-suite tasks (e.g., LIBERO-Object from **47.4%** to **79.6%** with **5** demonstrations).
- Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each self-supervised loss term, with skill disentanglement yielding the largest performance boost, and real-world validation showed improved average success counts on a physical robot for both seen and unseen tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly mining and utilizing shared, scene-agnostic behavioral primitives acts as a robust prior, substantially enhancing both multi-task performance and data-efficient few-shot adaptation in robotic manipulation.
> - A combination of self-supervised objectives, particularly skill disentanglement and behavioral skill alignment, is crucial for learning a compact, non-redundant, and reusable skill library directly from raw demonstrations.
> - Integrating a transferable skill abstraction layer as a plug-in module allows existing end-to-end visuomotor policies to leverage their core strengths while gaining structured, compositional, and adaptable control capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen substantial progress with visuomotor imitation policies, particularly those trained on extensive demonstration datasets and scaled with transformer architectures. These policies often operate as end-to-end mappings from observations to low-level actions. While effective for multi-task performance, this end-to-end paradigm frequently leaves underlying behavioral structure implicit, hindering the efficient transfer of learned behaviors to novel tasks, especially when only a limited number of demonstrations are available. The primary challenge lies in achieving data-efficient generalization and rapid adaptation to unseen tasks without extensive retraining.

Current approaches to skill learning in imitation-based robotic manipulation generally fall into two categories:
1. **Human-annotated or VLM-based skill definition:** This approach involves manually segmenting trajectories and labeling phases (e.g., approach, grasp, place) or using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for segmentation. Hierarchical controllers are then trained conditioned on these explicit labels. While offering interpretable structures, these methods demand significant manual effort in design, segmentation, and boundary specification, and the predefined skill granularity may not align with the policy's actual control requirements.
2. **Data-driven skill learning:** This category includes methods that learn skills directly from data through techniques such as trajectory clustering, learnable skill embeddings, or mixture-of-experts models where experts implicitly represent skills. However, without specific objectives that encourage reusability and distinctness, these learned skills may converge to similar behaviors or become entangled with visual context, leading to redundant or non-transferable skill libraries.

SkillPlug addresses these limitations by proposing an unsupervised skill-mining framework. It aims to extract an explicit, shared, and transferable skill abstraction directly from raw multi-task demonstrations. By augmenting existing visuomotor policies with a skill-conditioning module and employing self-supervised objectives, SkillPlug seeks to create a compact, reusable, and non-redundant skill library. This approach differentiates itself by providing explicit behavioral structure that is shared across tasks, thereby facilitating data-efficient generalization and few-shot adaptation, without requiring manual annotation or relying solely on low-level action supervision for skill emergence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing SkillPlug are:

1. **Overcoming limitations of end-to-end policies for transfer and few-shot adaptation:** Existing visuomotor policies, despite their success in multi-task settings, often struggle with data-efficient generalization to novel tasks from limited demonstrations. This is primarily because they learn implicit action-observation mappings, failing to explicitly uncover and reuse shared behavioral patterns across tasks. SkillPlug aims to provide an explicit skill abstraction layer to enhance transferability.
2. **Addressing the data inefficiency of retraining:** Traditional end-to-end policies often necessitate full end-to-end retraining or extensive fine-tuning for new tasks, which is data-intensive. SkillPlug is motivated by the need for a more efficient adaptation mechanism where only lightweight components are fine-tuned, leveraging a fixed, transferable skill library as a prior.
3. **Eliminating the need for manual skill annotation:** Many hierarchical control approaches rely on human-designed or VLM-based skill definitions, which are labor-intensive and may not yield optimal skill granularity. SkillPlug seeks to learn skills in a fully unsupervised manner directly from raw multi-task demonstrations, thus removing the need for explicit manual design or annotation.
4. **Promoting reusable and non-redundant skill learning:** Data-driven skill learning methods, without explicit structural objectives, can result in skill libraries where skills are redundant (collapse to similar behaviors) or heavily tied to specific visual contexts, limiting their reusability across tasks and scenes. SkillPlug's design includes self-supervised objectives specifically formulated to encourage compactness, behavioral alignment, and disentanglement, leading to distinct, reusable, and scene-agnostic behavioral primitives.
5. **Enhancing multi-task generalization:** Beyond few-shot adaptation, the framework is motivated by the hypothesis that an explicit, shared skill library can also improve the overall multi-task generalization capabilities of a base policy by providing a structured prior for compositional control.
6. **Maintaining architectural compatibility:** SkillPlug is designed as a plug-in, architecture-agnostic framework. This motivation stems from the desire to augment existing successful visuomotor policies without requiring substantial modifications to their core backbones, thereby leveraging their established strengths while adding skill-level capabilities.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between powerful end-to-end policies and the need for robust, data-efficient, and generalizable robotic manipulation by introducing an unsupervised, explicit, and transferable skill abstraction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SkillPlug is presented as a plug-in framework designed to augment existing visuomotor policies, enabling unsupervised skill mining for few-shot adaptation in robotic manipulation.

**Problem Setup and Notation:**
The work focuses on offline multi-task visuomotor imitation learning. At each timestep `t`, the robot receives an observation `o_t` and predicts an action chunk `τ_t = (a_{t,1}, ..., a_{t,T})`. Each training sample is associated with a task label `l`. Training occurs on a dataset `D = {(o_t, τ_t, l)}` of multi-task demonstrations.
- **Multi-task training:** The initial stage involves jointly training a policy on demonstrations from various tasks to self-supervisely mine a transferable skill library and learn a skill-conditioned policy.
- **Few-shot adaptation:** For novel tasks not seen during training, the learned skills are reused as a transferable prior. Adaptation involves fine-tuning only lightweight components (router and action head), with the skill library and backbone frozen, to achieve data-efficient specialization.

**Skill-Conditioned Policy Architecture:**
SkillPlug conceptually decomposes any base policy into a scene-context encoder `f_enc` and an action head `f_act`. The original policy typically feeds the encoder's hidden feature `h_t = f_enc(o_t)` directly into the action head. SkillPlug modifies this by applying a skill-conditioned transformation to `h_t`, leaving `f_enc` and `f_act` untouched.

1. **Skill Library:** A shared library of `K` learnable continuous latent skill embeddings `{s_k ∈ R^{d_s}}` is maintained. Each `s_k` conditions the policy to execute a specific primitive behavior and is shared across all tasks and stages (training and adaptation). This serves as a transferable prior.
2. **Trajectory–Skill Posterior Encoder:** A VAE-style posterior encoder `q_φ` is introduced during multi-task training. It takes an action segment `τ` and a skill embedding `s_k` as input, mapping the action chunk `τ_t` to a chunk-level feature `f_τ` using an MLP `f_φ`. This encoder forms a variational posterior `z_k ~ q_φ(z_k | f_τ, s_k)`. Its purpose is to act as an information bottleneck, encouraging skills to capture stable, reusable, trajectory-aligned behaviors rather than scene-specific details. This component is discarded after multi-task training, and only the deterministic skill embeddings are used for inference.
3. **Skill Interactor and Router:**
 - **Interactor:** Given the scene feature `h_t` from `f_enc` and the skill library `{s_k}`, the interactor computes a per-skill feature `u_{t,k} = Interactor(h_t, s_k)`. This is implemented as a lightweight cross-attention module that modulates the scene representation with each skill.
 - **Router:** A router predicts chunk-level routing weights `r_τ ∈ R^K` over the `K` skills. This distribution is constant within an action chunk. SkillPlug then aggregates the per-skill features `{u_{t,k}}` into a single skill-conditioned representation `~h_t` via a weighted sum, which is then fed to the original action head `f_act`.

**Learning Skill Representations (Self-Supervised Objectives):**
To learn a transferable skill library, SkillPlug optimizes a weighted sum of four self-supervised objectives:

1. **Reconstruction Loss (L_rec):** An `ℓ_1` behavior cloning loss is applied to encourage faithful imitation of demonstrated actions. It supervises the predicted `n`-step action chunk `^a_{t:t+n-1}` from observation `o_t` against the ground-truth `a_{t:t+n-1}` from the dataset `D`.
2. **KL Regularization (L_KL):** A KL divergence regularizer is applied to the trajectory–skill posterior encoder `q_φ(z_k | f_τ, s_k)` against a prior `p(z)`. By conditioning only on `f_τ` and `s_k` (without direct observation access), this loss promotes compactness and biases skills towards trajectory-level behavior, reducing scene-specific encoding.
3. **Behavioral Skill Alignment (L_BSA):** This objective encourages mutual dependence between an action segment `τ` and the selected skill embedding `s`. It is implemented as a soft-label classification problem over the skill library, where the router assignment `r_{τ,k}` serves as a soft target distribution. A scoring function `T_η(τ, s_k) = sim(g_τ(f_τ), g_s(s_k))` (inner product between projected trajectory and skill embeddings) is used. Gradients are stopped through `r_{τ,k}` to avoid circular co-adaptation, ensuring `L_BSA` primarily shapes the skill library.
4. **Skill Disentanglement (L_SD):** To prevent redundant skills, this loss penalizes similarity between skill-conditioned features produced by different skills under the same observation. It computes a `K × K` similarity matrix `S_τ` from temporally averaged and normalized interactor outputs `u_{τ,k}` and minimizes the off-diagonal elements.

The total loss `L = L_rec + λ_KL L_KL + λ_BSA L_BSA + λ_SD L_SD` is minimized, where `λ` are hyperparameters for weighting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated SkillPlug on two simulation benchmarks (DISCOVERSE and LIBERO) and a real robot, using two base policies (ACT and OpenVLA-OFT). The evaluation protocol involved multi-task training followed by few-shot adaptation.

**Overall Performance:**

- **Multi-task performance:**
 - **DISCOVERSE (with ACT):** SkillPlug significantly improved performance on harder tasks. For example, jujube pick (J.P.) success rate increased from 6.7% to 94.0%, stack block (S.B.) from 15.3% to 80.7%, block bridge place (B.B.P.) from 0.7% to 36.7%, and drawer open (D.O.) from 0.0% to 100.0%. Performance on easier tasks (e.g., kiwi place, coffeecup place) remained high.
 - **LIBERO (with OpenVLA-OFT):** OpenVLA-OFT already demonstrated high performance (around 93-98%). SkillPlug showed competitive results, slightly improving on LIBERO-Long (92.9% to 93.5%), Object (97.6% to 97.8%), and Goal (96.5% to 96.7%), while being comparable on Spatial.
- **Few-shot adaptation:**
 - **DISCOVERSE (with ACT, 10 demonstrations):** SkillPlug substantially improved success rates on unseen tasks. For cuplid cover (C.C.), it increased from 0.7% to 18.7%; block place (B.P.) from 36.0% to 56.0%; jujube place (J.P.) from 0.0% to 29.3%; and mouse push (M.P.) from 82.0% to 87.3%.
 - **LIBERO (with OpenVLA-OFT, 5 demonstrations):** SkillPlug showed substantial gains in cross-suite transfer. Success rates improved from 47.4% to 79.6% on LIBERO-Object, 45.4% to 83.2% on LIBERO-Goal, and 42.0% to 86.8% on LIBERO-Spatial.

**Loss-term Ablation:**
A progressive ablation study on DISCOVERSE demonstrated the contribution of each self-supervised loss term:
- Adding KL regularization (L_KL) to the reconstruction loss (L_rec) provided modest gains, increasing average multi-task success from 63.7% to 68.6% and few-shot from 27.5% to 36.0%.
- Adding Skill Disentanglement (L_SD) yielded the largest improvement, boosting multi-task average to 83.7% and few-shot to 41.0%, indicating the importance of distinct skills.
- Adding Behavioral Skill Alignment (L_BSA) further improved the full objective, reaching 86.1% average multi-task and 47.8% average few-shot success.

**Analysis of Skill Properties:**

- **Scene-agnostic behavior:** Experiments showed that individual skills, when hard-coded, consistently induced specific motions (e.g., open-gripper) across different scene configurations, suggesting that SkillPlug learns transferable, behavior-level primitives independent of visual appearance.
- **Compositionality and interpretable routing:** The learned skills exhibited compositionality; for instance, combining an "open gripper" skill and a "move forward" skill via equal routing weights resulted in a combined "move forward while opening gripper" behavior. Temporal routing visualizations for tasks like "kiwi place" showed phase-structured activations, with different skills dominating during approach, grasping, and transport phases.
- **Redundancy vs. number of skills:** Performance on DISCOVERSE generally improved with an increasing number of skills (K), from 76% at K=2 to 88% at K=4, with diminishing returns for K=6 (90%). Analysis of pairwise cosine similarities between skill embeddings indicated that while skills were well-separated at K=2, mild redundancy emerged at K=4, and increased at K=6. This suggested that learned skills are reusable primitives rather than one-to-one task surrogates, and that overly large K introduces redundant variants.
- **Inference efficiency:** SkillPlug reduced the average number of inference steps per successful episode on DISCOVERSE. For example, on stack block, steps decreased from 126 to 86, and on block bridge place, from 181 to 119, indicating more efficient control.

**Real-World Performance:**
On a real-world tabletop manipulation platform:
- **Multi-task training (6 seen tasks):** SkillPlug improved the average success count from 9.7/20 to 15.7/20 trials per task.
- **Few-shot adaptation (3 unseen tasks, 5 demonstrations):** The average success count increased from 6/20 to 11.7/20, with notable gains on specific tasks like "stand cup" (3 to 12 successes) and "banana & bread to plate" (4 to 9 successes).
- Routing visualizations on the real robot for tasks like "pick banana and bread" showed two similar pick-and-place phases, with motion-dominant segments using one skill, gripper state changes using another, and a third skill briefly activating for wrist rotation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SkillPlug contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing critical challenges in generalization and data efficiency, particularly for few-shot adaptation to novel tasks.

1. **Improved Generalization and Data Efficiency:** The core significance lies in demonstrating that unsupervisedly mined, transferable skills can act as a robust, shared behavioral prior. This prior consistently enhances both multi-task performance and, critically, data-efficient few-shot adaptation. By freezing the learned skills and only fine-tuning lightweight components (router and action head), SkillPlug enables rapid specialization to new tasks with minimal demonstrations, which is a key requirement for deploying robots in varied, unstructured environments.
2. **Explicit Behavioral Structure:** Unlike many end-to-end policies that implicitly learn behaviors, SkillPlug introduces an explicit skill abstraction layer. This allows for the reuse and recombination of learned behavioral primitives, making the policy's operation more structured and potentially interpretable. The analysis of skill properties, such as scene-agnostic behavior and compositional routing, supports that SkillPlug learns meaningful and transferable units of action.
3. **Architecture Agnosticism:** The plug-in nature of SkillPlug, allowing it to augment existing visuomotor policies (demonstrated with ACT and OpenVLA-OFT), increases its practical applicability. It enables the benefits of skill learning to be integrated into established and potentially highly performant backbones without requiring significant redesign or retraining of the entire foundational model.
4. **Unsupervised Skill Mining:** The framework eliminates the need for labor-intensive manual skill annotation or reliance on pre-defined taxonomies. By learning skills directly from raw multi-task demonstrations through self-supervised objectives (reconstruction, KL regularization, behavioral skill alignment, and skill disentanglement), SkillPlug offers a scalable and automated approach to skill acquisition.
5. **Enhanced Control Efficiency:** Beyond improving success rates, the observed reduction in inference steps per successful episode suggests that the learned skills provide a stronger temporal prior, leading to more efficient and direct control sequences. This has practical implications for real-time robotic operation where computational and temporal resources may be limited.
6. **Real-World Applicability:** Validation on a real robot demonstrates the framework's robustness and transferability from simulation to physical systems, indicating its potential for practical deployment in real-world robotic tasks.

One identified limitation is that the skill library remains fixed after multi-task training, which might constrain adaptation if novel tasks require primitives beyond the learned set. Addressing this, by making the skill library expandable in continual learning and cross-embodiment settings, is highlighted as an important future research direction.

In summary, SkillPlug represents a step towards creating more adaptive, generalizable, and data-efficient robotic manipulation policies by providing a principled, unsupervised method for mining and utilizing transferable skill abstractions.
