# Process Reward Models That Think

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces THINKPRM, a generative process reward model that enables large language models to provide verbalized, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) verification for their reasoning steps. The model, developed by a collaboration including University of Michigan and LG AI Research, achieves high performance with orders of magnitude less step-level supervision than traditional discriminative models, while significantly improving reliability and generalizability.

## Problem

- Existing discriminative process reward models (PRMs) require immense volumes of costly step-level human annotations, making them difficult to scale and adapt.
- Generative verifiers (like LLM-as-a-Judge) are often unreliable, prone to errors such as overthinking, infinite looping, or generating unparseable outputs, and have limited ability to leverage long Chain-of-Thought for verification.
- The inability to efficiently scale verification compute and lack of interpretable reasoning pose challenges for robust LLM reasoning.

## Method

- The authors fine-tuned open-weight reasoning models (LRMs) into ==generative PRMs== by framing verification as a language generation task that produces comprehensive =='verification CoTs'==.
- They created a small (1K verification CoTs, 8K step labels) yet high-quality synthetic dataset by prompting a powerful LRM and rigorously filtering outputs to ensure format adherence, agreement with gold step labels, and appropriate CoT length.
- The trained ==THINKPRM== models utilize this lightweight fine-tuning to provide interpretable, step-by-step verification, capable of effective ==test-time scaling== through both parallel and sequential (deeper thinking) compute utilization.

## Results

- THINKPRM-14B, fine-tuned on **8K** process labels, outperformed discriminative PRMs (DiscPRM-14B) trained on **~700K** labels on the ProcessBench benchmark.
- The model substantially improved verification F1-score (e.g., **>70** F1 points for R1-Qwen-1.5B) and significantly reduced invalid judgment rates compared to LLM-as-a-Judge baselines.
- THINKPRM demonstrated superior reasoning accuracy in Best-of-N selection and verifier-guided search across MATH-500 and AIME '24 benchmarks, and showed strong out-of-domain generalization on GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality generative process reward models can be trained with orders of magnitude less step-level supervision than discriminative PRMs, achieving comparable or superior performance.
> - Rigorous, process-based filtering of synthetically generated verification Chain-of-Thoughts is crucial for mitigating issues like invalid outputs and overthinking in generative verifiers.
> - Fine-tuning large reasoning models on even a small, high-quality synthetic dataset dramatically enhances their reliability, reduces undesirable inference behaviors, and improves F1-score for process-level verification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of large language model (LLM) reasoning, test-time compute scaling, and the development of robust reward models. In recent years, LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in various tasks, but their reasoning abilities, particularly on complex multi-step problems, often benefit from external mechanisms for verification and search.

The broader research landscape recognizes that simply increasing model size or pre-training data might not be sufficient for reliable, expert-level reasoning. Instead, techniques that enable LLMs to "think" or "deliberate" more effectively at test-time are gaining prominence. This usually involves generating multiple solutions or partial solutions and then employing a verifier to score their correctness, guiding a search process or selecting the best output (e.g., Best-of-N selection, tree-based search).

**Process Reward Models (PRMs)** are central to these test-time scaling strategies. They evaluate not just the final answer but also the intermediate steps of a solution. Historically, PRMs have taken two main forms:

1. **Discriminative PRMs:** These are trained as classifiers to predict the correctness of each step in a solution. While effective (e.g., Cobbe et al., Uesato et al.), their major drawback is the immense cost of acquiring **step-level supervision**. Training high-performing discriminative PRMs often requires hundreds of thousands of meticulously annotated step-level labels, which are expensive and time-consuming to obtain (e.g., Lightman et al., Wang et al. 2023b). They also use fixed compute, limiting dynamic scalability.

2. **Generative Verification (LLM-as-a-Judge, GenRM):** More recently, researchers have explored treating verification as a generation problem, where an LLM generates a rationale (a Chain-of-Thought, CoT) followed by a decision.
 - **LLM-as-a-Judge** (Wang et al. 2023a, Liu et al. 2023b, Zheng et al. 2023) uses general-purpose LLMs directly as verifiers. While interpretable, it is known to be prone to errors, sensitive to prompting, and can exhibit undesirable behaviors like overthinking, infinite looping, or generating invalid outputs (Huang et al. 2023, Bavaresco et al. 2024). These models are not specifically trained for verification.
 - **GenRM** (Zhang et al. 2024a) represents a step towards specialized generative verifiers but has been largely limited to outcome verification via *short* CoTs, thereby limiting its ability for extensive test-time scaling.

This paper identifies a critical gap: the need for a **data-efficient, reliable, and scalable generative PRM** that can leverage *long Chain-of-Thought (CoT)* for verification. "Process Reward Models That Think" (THINKPRM) directly addresses this by proposing a method to fine-tune open-weight reasoning models using *minimal* step-level supervision to create powerful, verbalized PRMs. By capitalizing on the inherent reasoning abilities of these pre-trained models and providing targeted, high-quality synthetic data, THINKPRM aims to overcome the data inefficiency of discriminative PRMs and the unreliability/limited CoT of prior generative verifiers. This work thus represents a significant step towards unlocking more advanced and interpretable reasoning capabilities in LLMs through smarter, cheaper, and more scalable verification mechanisms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind THINKPRM stems from the limitations of existing approaches to process-level verification for large language models (LLMs). The authors identify two primary challenges:

1. **High Supervision Cost for Discriminative PRMs:** Traditional discriminative Process Reward Models (PRMs), which classify each step of a solution as correct or incorrect, demand an enormous volume of expensive step-level human annotations (process labels). Datasets like PRM800K, requiring hundreds of thousands of such labels, exemplify this cost, making it a significant barrier to widespread adoption and scalability. This cost prevents rapid iteration and adaptation to new domains.
2. **Suboptimal Performance and Unreliability of Generative Verifiers (LLM-as-a-Judge):** While generative approaches, such as LLM-as-a-Judge, offer interpretability through verbalized rationales, they frequently suffer from poor accuracy, sensitivity to prompt phrasing, and undesirable inference behaviors like "overthinking," infinite looping, or generating unparseable outputs. Existing generative reward models (like GenRM) are also limited by their reliance on short CoTs, restricting their ability to fully leverage sophisticated reasoning.

Against this backdrop, the **key objectives** of this research are:

- **To develop a data-efficient PRM:** The primary goal is to build a high-quality process reward model that requires orders of magnitude *fewer* step-level annotations for training compared to discriminative PRMs. This directly addresses the cost barrier.
- **To create a verbalized, long CoT verifier:** The aim is for the PRM to verify solutions by generating an extended "verification chain-of-thought" (CoT), providing interpretable reasoning for its judgments. This goes beyond simple binary labels and offers deeper insights into the correctness of reasoning.
- **To enhance the reliability and effectiveness of generative verification:** By fine-tuning, the authors seek to mitigate the issues of unreliability, invalid outputs, and overthinking observed in off-the-shelf LLM-as-a-Judge models.
- **To enable effective test-time scaling of verification compute:** The proposed PRM should be capable of leveraging additional compute at inference time, both through parallel sampling of multiple verification CoTs and sequentially by encouraging deeper, multi-round "thinking" during verification.
- **To demonstrate superior performance across diverse reasoning tasks:** The objective is to show that this new generative PRM (THINKPRM) can outperform both discriminative PRMs (trained on much more data) and LLM-as-a-Judge baselines on challenging math, scientific, and code reasoning benchmarks, including out-of-domain generalization.

In essence, the motivation is to create a "THINKing PRM" that can not only provide step-by-step verification but also do so efficiently, reliably, and interpretably, thereby making advanced LLM reasoning more accessible and robust.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "Process Reward Models That Think" revolves around transforming existing open-weight large reasoning models (LRMs) into highly effective and data-efficient generative Process Reward Models (PRMs) through a process of lightweight fine-tuning on carefully curated synthetic data.

#### 3.1. The Core Idea: Generative Verification with Long CoT

The central premise is to frame process-level verification as a language generation task, where a verifier not only outputs a decision (correct/incorrect) for each step but also generates a comprehensive "verification chain-of-thought" (CoT) to justify its reasoning. This contrasts with discriminative PRMs that merely output a score or label. The authors emphasize that this generative nature allows for scaling verification compute by enabling the model to "think through" a longer CoT.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Data Collection and Filtering (See Fig. 3)

The most crucial part of the methodology is the creation of a high-quality, yet small, synthetic dataset for fine-tuning.
- **Base Model for Generation:** The authors leverage a powerful, general-purpose reasoning model, QwQ-32B-Preview, to generate verification CoTs. This model possesses strong inherent reasoning abilities that are then guided.
- **Input Data:** The model is prompted with problem-solution pairs from the PRM800K dataset, which is notable for providing human-verified *step-level labels* (gold process labels), not just outcome labels.
- **Prompting Strategy:** The QwQ-32B-Preview model is instructed to verify *each step* in a given solution prefix, generating a detailed critique for each step and concluding with a boxed judgment (e.g., \boxed{correct} or \boxed{incorrect}).
- **Rejection Sampling / Filtering:** To ensure the quality of the synthetic training data, a rigorous filtering process is applied to the sampled CoTs. This is critical as directly generated CoTs might suffer from issues like incorrect reasoning or malformed outputs. The filtering criteria are:
 1. **Format Adherence:** The generated CoT must strictly follow the expected format, including an extractable decision label for each step.
 2. **Process Label Matching:** The generated step-level judgments (e.g., \boxed{correct}) must precisely match the *gold step labels* from PRM800K. This process-based filtering ensures the generated rationales align with ground truth at a granular level, rather than just the final outcome.
 3. **Length Constraint:** The CoT length must be within a maximum token budget (e.g., 4096 tokens) to prevent the "overthinking" and infinite looping issues observed with LLM-as-a-Judge.
- **Data Scale:** This rigorous filtering yields a remarkably small dataset of only 1K high-quality verification CoTs, corresponding to approximately 8K step labels. This is orders of magnitude less than the data required by discriminative PRMs.

#### 3.3. Lightweight Fine-tuning (THINKPRM Models)

The filtered synthetic data is then used to fine-tune several open-weight reasoning models, creating THINKPRM.
- **Base Models:** The authors experiment with R1-Distill-Qwen{1.5B, 7B, 14B} and QwQ-32B-Preview. These are large reasoning models, pre-trained for strong reasoning capabilities.
- **Fine-tuning Technique:** Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used for larger models (Qwen-14B, QwQ-32B) to make training efficient, while smaller models (Qwen-1.5B, 7B) undergo full training.
- **Efficiency:** The training process is notably lightweight, with QwQ-32B-Preview fine-tuning taking only 4.5 hours on a single A100 GPU.

#### 3.4. Test-Time Scaling and Evaluation

The trained THINKPRM models are extensively evaluated across various challenging benchmarks and test-time scaling scenarios:
- **Evaluation Scenarios:**
 - **Best-of-N Selection:** Generating N solutions and selecting the best one based on the verifier's score (using weighted majority voting or direct score ranking).
 - **Verifier-guided Beam Search:** Using the PRM to score and prune candidate next steps during the generation process, guiding the LLM towards correct reasoning paths.
- **Scaling Verifier Compute:**
 - **Parallel Scaling (@K):** Sampling K independent verification CoTs for a single solution and averaging their scores to get a more robust judgment.
 - **Sequential Scaling:** Forcing the PRM to "double-check" or "self-correct" its initial verification by generating additional thinking rounds using trigger phrases.
- **Baselines:** THINKPRM is rigorously compared against:
 - **DiscPRM:** A discriminative PRM based on the *same base model* but trained on the *full PRM800K dataset* (712K step labels). This is the primary comparison for data efficiency.
 - **LLM-as-a-Judge:** The same base model as THINKPRM, used in a zero-shot prompting setup for verification. This highlights the value of fine-tuning.
 - **Unweighted Majority Voting (Self-Consistency):** A common baseline for test-time scaling.
 - **Off-the-shelf PRMs:** Other state-of-the-art PRMs like RLHFFlow-Deepseek-PRM and MATH-Shepherd-PRM.
- **Benchmarks:**
 - **In-domain (Math):** ProcessBench (for direct verification F1), MATH-500, and AIME '24.
 - **Out-of-domain:** GPQA-Diamond (scientific reasoning) and LiveCodeBench (code generation).

By systematically comparing THINKPRM against strong baselines under various compute budgets and across diverse tasks, the authors aim to demonstrate its superior data efficiency, reliability, and scaling capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling evidence for the effectiveness and efficiency of THINKPRM, a generative, long CoT process reward model. The main findings are summarized as follows:

- **Exceptional Data Efficiency (Fig. 1 Left, §5.1):** THINKPRM-14B, fine-tuned on a mere **8K process labels** (derived from 1K synthetic verification chains), significantly outperforms discriminative PRMs (DiscPRM-14B) trained on **~100 times more data** (700K process labels from the full PRM800K dataset) on the ProcessBench benchmark. This is a monumental finding, demonstrating that high-quality generative verifiers do not require the extensive, costly supervision previously thought necessary.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Reduced Undesirable Behaviors (Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, §3.1, §3.2):**
 - LLM-as-a-Judge models, when used off-the-shelf, frequently generate invalid judgments, engage in overthinking, or get stuck in infinite loops. This leads to low F1-scores and unparseable outputs.
 - THINKPRM's lightweight fine-tuning on filtered synthetic data drastically mitigates these issues. It achieves a substantial boost in verification F1-score across all base models (e.g., R1-Qwen-1.5B gains over 70 F1 points), significantly reduces the percentage of invalid judgments, and produces more accurate and reasonably-lengthy CoTs, preventing excessive overthinking (Fig. 4 Right shows CoT lengths remaining within 1K-5K tokens with high accuracy).
- **Superior Performance in Test-Time Scaling Scenarios (Fig. 1 Right, Fig. 7, Fig. 8, §4.2):**
 - **Best-of-N Selection:** THINKPRM consistently leads to higher or comparable reasoning accuracy compared to DiscPRM and LLM-as-a-Judge on MATH-500 and the more challenging AIME '24 benchmarks, especially as the number of sampled solutions (N) increases.
 - **Verifier-Guided Search:** On MATH-500, THINKPRM-1.5B outperforms DiscPRM and LLM-as-a-Judge.
 - **Outperforming Off-the-Shelf PRMs:** THINKPRM-1.5B (trained on only 8K labels) surprisingly surpasses larger and more extensively trained off-the-shelf PRMs like RLHFFlow-8B-Deepseek and MathShepherd-7B by over 7% across various beam sizes on MATH-500.
- **Effective Scaling of Verifier Compute (Fig. 2, Fig. 8, Fig. 19, §4.2, §E.6):**
 - **Sequential Scaling (Longer CoTs):** THINKPRM can effectively utilize more verification compute by being prompted to "think longer" (e.g., re-checking its verification). It exhibits better scaling behavior than LLM-as-a-Judge (which degrades after 16K tokens) and outperforms DiscPRM-14B by 15 F1 points.
 - **Parallel Scaling (@K):** Sampling multiple verification CoTs and averaging their scores (e.g., THINKPRM-1.5B@4) provides an additional performance boost, improving accuracy by more than 5% on MATH-500 under verifier-guided search.
 - While both parallel and sequential scaling boost performance, parallel scaling shows a slight advantage under certain compute budgets.
- **Strong Out-of-Domain (OOD) Generalization (Fig. 10, §4.2):** Despite being trained exclusively on math data, THINKPRM-14B demonstrates superior OOD performance. It outperforms DiscPRM-14B by 8% on GPQA-physics (scientific reasoning) and 4.5% on LiveCodeBench (code generation). This highlights the robustness of generative PRMs and the fragility of discriminative PRMs under domain shifts.
- **Generalization to Long Reasoning Traces without Clear Delimiters (Fig. 11 Left, §5.3):** THINKPRM effectively verifies long, unstructured CoTs from different generators, demonstrating its ability to extract and judge individual reasoning steps embedded within complex textual flows, even without explicit step boundaries it was trained on.
- **Outperforms Self-Consistency in Compute-Matched Analysis (Fig. 11 Mid/Right, §5.4):** When compared under the same compute budget, THINKPRM (via best-of-N) shows a clear advantage over simply sampling more solutions and using majority voting (self-consistency), especially at higher compute regimes.
- **Crucial Role of Process-Based Filtering (Fig. 9, §5.7):** The study shows that filtering synthetic data based on agreement with *gold step-level labels* (process-based filtering) is significantly more effective for training high-quality PRMs than filtering based solely on final answer correctness (outcome-based filtering, as in GenRM), even with fewer training examples.
- **Impact on Difficult Problems (§5.6):** THINKPRM's reasoning capabilities particularly shine on more challenging problems (higher difficulty levels in MATH-500 and GPQA-Physics), where it provides substantially greater improvements over DiscPRM.
- **Agnostic to Labeling Strategy (§5.5):** THINKPRM performs comparably well when trained on either manual PRM800K labels or automatically generated Math-shepherd labels, indicating flexibility in data sourcing.

In summary, THINKPRM successfully addresses the data efficiency and reliability challenges of process reward models, establishing generative, long CoT verification as a powerful paradigm for scaling LLM reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Process Reward Models That Think" has profound significance and substantial potential impact across several dimensions of LLM research and application:

1. **Paradigm Shift in PRM Training:** This work fundamentally challenges the long-held assumption that high-quality process reward models necessitate massive, expensive human step-level annotations. By demonstrating that effective generative PRMs can be trained with *minimal synthetic supervision* (orders of magnitude less data), THINKPRM opens the door to a more scalable and accessible approach to developing verifiers. This is a game-changer for academic research and smaller organizations with limited annotation budgets.

2. **Unlocking Advanced LLM Reasoning:** THINKPRM provides a powerful, data-efficient, and interpretable mechanism for enhancing LLM reasoning. By enabling LLMs to "think through" verification via long CoTs, it pushes towards more robust "system 2" capabilities in LLMs, allowing them to deliberate, self-correct, and learn from their mistakes more effectively. This is crucial for tackling increasingly complex, multi-step reasoning problems in domains like mathematics, science, and engineering.

3. **Cost Reduction and Accessibility:** The significant reduction in supervision requirements means that developing high-quality verifiers becomes much less resource-intensive. This lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, allowing for faster iteration, experimentation, and deployment of verifier-guided LLM systems across a wider array of specialized tasks and languages where human annotation is scarce or expensive.

4. **Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** As a generative PRM, THINKPRM produces verbalized verification CoTs, offering transparent insights into *why* a particular step is judged correct or incorrect. This interpretability is invaluable for debugging LLM reasoning failures, understanding model behavior, and building trust in AI systems, moving beyond opaque binary judgments.

5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated strong out-of-domain generalization of THINKPRM (e.g., from math to science and code) suggests a more robust and versatile verification mechanism compared to discriminative PRMs, which often struggle with distribution shifts. This implies that a single, lightly trained THINKPRM could be adapted to various tasks, further amplifying its utility and efficiency.

6. **Effective Test-Time Compute Scaling:** THINKPRM's ability to effectively scale verification compute, both in parallel (sampling multiple CoTs) and sequentially (forcing deeper thinking), provides powerful knobs for optimizing LLM performance at inference time. This allows practitioners to trade off compute for accuracy, tailoring systems to specific latency and performance requirements.

7. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future exploration, such as developing more calibrated scoring mechanisms for generative PRMs, addressing subtle limitations like "step label interference," and exploring more sophisticated prompting strategies for sequential scaling. It also encourages further research into how to best leverage synthetic data and pre-trained reasoning models for other forms of reward modeling.

In conclusion, "Process Reward Models That Think" is a landmark paper that redefines the landscape of process reward model development. It offers a practical, data-efficient, and highly effective approach to building intelligent verifiers, thereby paving the way for more reliable, interpretable, and scalable reasoning capabilities in large language models.
