# References Improve LLM Alignment in Non-Verifiable Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces "RefEval", a method leveraging high-quality reference outputs to guide large language model (LLM) evaluators, effectively acting as soft verifiers in non-verifiable domains. This approach enhances LLM evaluation accuracy by up to 17.4% for smaller models and enables self-improvement through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) that matches or exceeds models trained with external reward models, without costly human or AI preference data.

## Problem

- LLM alignment in "non-verifiable domains" (e.g., creative writing, summarization) is challenging due to the lack of objective ground-truth verifiers, making direct application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) difficult.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning from Human/AI Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF) methods are resource-intensive, requiring extensive human annotation or expensive frontier models for preference labeling.
- LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations without references often suffer from subjectivity, biases, and lower reliability, particularly for less capable models.

## Method

- Develop "**RefEval**," a prompting strategy that guides LLM-judges to explicitly use a provided "==Reference== Output" as a benchmark for pairwise comparison of candidate LLM responses, serving as a "soft verifier."
- Integrate these reference-guided LLM-judges into a two-stage self-improvement pipeline: initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality reference outputs, followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using preferences generated "on-policy" by the LLM-judge itself.
- Leverage readily available high-quality references (e.g., from powerful frontier models) to reduce dependency on costly human preference data or the need to train separate, discriminative reward models.

## Results

- The `RefEval` method achieved the highest average evaluation accuracy (**79.1%**) across five diverse datasets and 11 open-source LLM judges, demonstrating an absolute improvement of approximately **17.4%** for Llama-3-8b over reference-free baselines.
- Reference-guided self-improvement (`RefEval`) using DPO achieved average absolute gains of +20.2 points on AlpacaEval and +17.1 points on Arena-Hard over the SFT distillation baseline.
- Models aligned with `RefEval` reached performance (e.g., **73.1%** on AlpacaEval, **58.7%** on Arena-Hard for Llama-3-**8B**-Instruct) comparable to or better than DPO using a powerful, separately finetuned reward model (`ArmoRM`).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reference-guided prompting significantly improves LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation accuracy and inter-judge agreement, providing greater benefits for less capable LLM judges by offering objective grounding.
> - High-quality references can effectively serve as "soft verifiers" for LLM alignment in domains traditionally considered non-verifiable, bridging a methodological gap between RLVR and RLHF/RLAIF paradigms.
> - LLMs can effectively self-supervise their alignment training using reference-guided self-judges, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding methods that rely on external, separately finetuned reward models without their associated data costs.
