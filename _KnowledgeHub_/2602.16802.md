# References Improve LLM Alignment in Non-Verifiable Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces "RefEval", a method leveraging high-quality reference outputs to guide large language model (LLM) evaluators, effectively acting as soft verifiers in non-verifiable domains. This approach enhances LLM evaluation accuracy by up to 17.4% for smaller models and enables self-improvement through Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) that matches or exceeds models trained with external reward models, without costly human or AI preference data.

## Problem

- LLM alignment in "non-verifiable domains" (e.g., creative writing, summarization) is challenging due to the lack of objective ground-truth verifiers, making direct application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) difficult.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning from Human/AI Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF) methods are resource-intensive, requiring extensive human annotation or expensive frontier models for preference labeling.
- LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations without references often suffer from subjectivity, biases, and lower reliability, particularly for less capable models.

## Method

- Develop "**RefEval**," a prompting strategy that guides LLM-judges to explicitly use a provided "==Reference== Output" as a benchmark for pairwise comparison of candidate LLM responses, serving as a "soft verifier."
- Integrate these reference-guided LLM-judges into a two-stage self-improvement pipeline: initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality reference outputs, followed by Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) using preferences generated "on-policy" by the LLM-judge itself.
- Leverage readily available high-quality references (e.g., from powerful frontier models) to reduce dependency on costly human preference data or the need to train separate, discriminative reward models.

## Results

- The `RefEval` method achieved the highest average evaluation accuracy (**79.1%**) across five diverse datasets and 11 open-source LLM judges, demonstrating an absolute improvement of approximately **17.4%** for Llama-3-8b over reference-free baselines.
- Reference-guided self-improvement (`RefEval`) using DPO achieved average absolute gains of +20.2 points on AlpacaEval and +17.1 points on Arena-Hard over the SFT distillation baseline.
- Models aligned with `RefEval` reached performance (e.g., **73.1%** on AlpacaEval, **58.7%** on Arena-Hard for Llama-3-**8B**-Instruct) comparable to or better than DPO using a powerful, separately finetuned reward model (`ArmoRM`).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reference-guided prompting significantly improves LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation accuracy and inter-judge agreement, providing greater benefits for less capable LLM judges by offering objective grounding.
> - High-quality references can effectively serve as "soft verifiers" for LLM alignment in domains traditionally considered non-verifiable, bridging a methodological gap between RLVR and RLHF/RLAIF paradigms.
> - LLMs can effectively self-supervise their alignment training using reference-guided self-judges, achieving performance comparable to or exceeding methods that rely on external, separately finetuned reward models without their associated data costs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language model (LLM) alignment, reinforcement learning, and automated evaluation. The paper directly addresses a critical challenge in the current landscape of LLM development: effectively aligning LLMs with human preferences and instructions, particularly in "non-verifiable domains."

Traditionally, LLM post-training often relies on **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)** or **Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF)**. These methods involve training a separate reward model or using a powerful LLM-as-a-Judge to generate supervision signals (preference labels) that guide the LLM's improvement. However, this process is resource-intensive, requiring extensive human annotation or expensive frontier models.

A parallel paradigm, **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)**, has shown strong effectiveness in "verifiable domains" (e.g., mathematical reasoning, code generation) where ground-truth verifiers (e.g., unit tests, theorem provers) can objectively determine the correctness of an output. The key distinction is that RLVR provides *reference-grounded supervision* – a direct comparison to a correct answer – which is often more robust than subjective preference signals.

This paper identifies a critical "methodology gap" between RLVR and RLHF/RLAIF. Non-verifiable domains (e.g., creative writing, summarization, general instruction following) lack explicit ground-truth verifiers, making direct application of RLVR challenging. The prevailing RLHF/RLAIF approaches in these domains typically operate in a "reference-free" manner, where reward models or LLM-judges evaluate outputs without direct comparison to an ideal reference.

The authors propose to bridge this gap by investigating whether **reference-guided LLM-evaluators can act as "soft verifiers"** in non-verifiable domains. This approach aims to extend the advantages of reference-grounded supervision from RLVR to the more subjective alignment tasks of RLHF/RLAIF, without needing actual verifiable rewards or costly human preference data. This is particularly relevant given that high-quality reference outputs (e.g., human-written examples, or strong frontier model outputs) might be available even when pairwise preference labels are not.

The work builds upon and differentiates itself from several related lines of research:
- **LLM-as-a-Judge:** The paper acknowledges existing work on LLMs as evaluators (Zheng et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023; 2024), which face challenges like positional bias. This research enhances these judges by incorporating references.
- **Role of References in LLM Evaluation:** While traditional NLG metrics like BLEU and ROUGE rely on references, their direct utility in LLM-as-a-Judge for alignment has been underexplored. Recent works like LLMBar (Zeng et al., 2024) and HREF (Lyu et al., 2024) have begun to incorporate references, but the current paper provides a more systematic and comprehensive investigation across diverse tasks and models. It also contrasts with approaches that use canonical metrics (Chang et al., 2025) by leveraging the nuanced judgment capabilities of LLMs themselves.
- **Self-Improving LLMs and Generative Reward Models:** The concept of LLMs supervising their own training (Yuan et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024) and acting as generative reward models (Zhang et al., 2024) is a rapidly evolving area. This paper integrates reference guidance into this self-improvement paradigm, offering a practical pathway to robust alignment without external human or AI feedback loops typically required for training separate discriminative reward models.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of LLM alignment by demonstrating a novel and efficient method for leveraging readily available high-quality references to improve both evaluation and training, thereby making advanced alignment techniques more accessible and robust in diverse application contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the limitations of existing LLM alignment paradigms, particularly the inability of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to be directly applied to "non-verifiable domains" where objective ground-truth verifiers are absent. Concurrently, dominant methods like RLHF/RLAIF often rely on reward models or LLMs-as-Judges that evaluate outputs in a "reference-free" manner, which can be less reliable and more prone to biases compared to reference-grounded evaluation.

The **key objective** of this work is to investigate whether **reference-guided LLM-evaluators can bridge this methodological gap by serving as "soft verifiers"** to support effective reinforcement learning for LLM alignment, *without requiring external human or AI supervision* for preference labeling.

This overarching objective is driven by two primary motivations:

1. **Extending Reference-Grounded Supervision to Non-Verifiable Domains:** RLVR's success in verifiable tasks highlights the advantage of having a clear, objective reference point for evaluation. The authors are motivated to transfer this benefit to complex, subjective tasks (like instruction following, creative writing, summarization) where definitive "correct" answers are scarce, but high-quality examples or "references" might exist. By making LLM-judges reference-aware, the aim is to instill a more grounded and consistent evaluation capability akin to a soft verifier.
2. **Leveraging Available High-Quality References for Practical Value:** The practical reality of LLM development often involves scenarios where generating exhaustive human preference labels is prohibitively costly or time-consuming. However, high-quality reference outputs (e.g., expert-written examples, outputs from powerful frontier models, or even well-curated SFT data) may be more readily available. The motivation is to explore if these existing references can be directly leveraged for alignment, reducing the dependency on expensive preference data and making the alignment process more efficient and scalable.

To achieve this core objective, the study sets out with two specific goals:

- **Develop Effective Reference-Guided LLM-Judges:** The first goal is to design and comprehensively evaluate prompting strategies that enable LLM-based evaluators to effectively utilize reference outputs. This involves demonstrating that incorporating references, especially with targeted guidance, significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of LLM-judges, particularly for less capable models, and can even enhance frontier models when provided with human-quality references.
- **Demonstrate Utility in Self-Improvement Settings:** The second goal is to show that these improved, reference-guided LLM-judges can be successfully integrated into self-improvement training pipelines for LLM alignment. Specifically, the aim is to prove that LLMs, using themselves as reference-guided judges, can effectively fine-tune their own responses via preference optimization (DPO), achieving performance comparable to or exceeding methods that rely on external reward models or traditional reference-based metrics. This self-improvement aspect is crucial for reducing external supervision.

Ultimately, the paper aims to present a practical and effective framework for LLM post-training that capitalizes on the often-underutilized resource of high-quality reference outputs, thereby pushing the boundaries of autonomous and cost-efficient LLM alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper is structured into two main phases: (1) developing and evaluating reference-guided LLM-judges, and (2) applying these judges in a self-improvement setting for LLM alignment tuning.

#### 3.1. Developing Reference-Guided LLM-Judges (Phase I: Evaluation)

The first phase focuses on enhancing the accuracy and robustness of LLM-as-a-Judge paradigms by incorporating reference outputs.

- **Evaluation Setting:** The primary focus is on **pairwise comparison**, where an LLM-judge compares two candidate outputs (Output A and Output B) for a given instruction and selects the superior one. This aligns with standard preference optimization algorithms like DPO. Pointwise scoring experiments are also conducted to confirm generalizability (Appendix B). Evaluation accuracy is measured against human annotations, averaged across swapped candidate orders to mitigate positional bias.
- **Reference-Guided Prompting Strategies:**
 - **Ref-Free (Ours):** A strong baseline reference-free prompt designed for direct pairwise comparison, instructing the model to assess instruction-following, factuality, and verbosity.
 - **RefEval:** The core proposed method. It explicitly guides the LLM-judge to use a provided "Reference Output" as a benchmark, comparing candidate outputs against it for factual correctness, overall quality, and adherence to instruction. It emphasizes understanding how the reference "properly delivers a helpful, accurate, and natural response."
 - **RefMatch:** An additional method that instructs the LLM-judge to act primarily as a semantic and stylistic matcher, determining which candidate output more closely resembles the reference based on specific matching rules.
 - **Variants:** Several other variants (RefEval-Rules, RefMatch-Rules-CoT, Multi-Ref Avg, Multi-Ref MAX) are explored in the appendices for comprehensiveness.
- **Datasets:** Five diverse human-annotated datasets are used for robust evaluation:
 - **LLMBar-Natural (Nat) and LLMBar-Adversarial (Adv):** Instruction-following datasets, with the latter designed to challenge judges.
 - **MTBench (MT):** A benchmark for multi-turn conversational abilities.
 - **Instrusum (Ins):** Focused on instruction-controllable summarization, featuring longer and more complex instructions.
 - **HREF (Human Response-Guided Evaluation of Instruction Following):** Contains human-written reference responses.
- **Obtaining Reference Outputs:** For most experiments, a strong frontier LLM, **GPT-4o**, is used as an "oracle" to generate high-quality reference outputs. A focused study also uses human-edited "Oracle" references for stronger judges.
- **LLMs Evaluated as Judges:** 11 diverse open-source LLMs (ranging from 7B to 72B parameters, including Llama-3.1, Qwen-2.5, Gemma-2, Mistral-Nemo, GLM-4) are evaluated, along with frontier models like GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 for specific analyses.
- **Baseline Evaluation Methods:** Performance is compared against established LLM-as-a-Judge prompting strategies, including LLMBar-Base, CoT, Self-Ref, Self-Metric-Ref, LLMBar-Ref, PrePAIR, HREF-Base, and HREF-Ref.

#### 3.2. Reference-Guided Self-Improvement (Phase II: Training)

The second phase leverages the insights from Phase I to apply reference-guided LLM-judges in a self-improvement training pipeline.

- **Two-Stage Training Process:**
 1. **Training Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on Reference Outputs (DSV3-Distill):** The base LLM is initially fine-tuned directly on high-quality reference outputs. This serves as a strong starting point and a baseline for subsequent preference optimization.
 2. **Training Stage 2: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with Reference-Guided Judges:** The SFT-tuned model is further improved using DPO. Crucially, the preference annotations for DPO are constructed "on-policy." This means candidate outputs are sampled from the model being fine-tuned, and then an LLM-judge is used to evaluate these outputs and generate preference pairs (best vs. worst). The novel aspect here is using the *reference-guided LLM-judge* (RefEval, with the model itself acting as the judge) to provide these supervision signals.
- **Preference Data Generation:** For each instruction, 5 candidate outputs are sampled from the model (with temperature 0.8). These 5 outputs generate $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairwise comparisons. The LLM-judge assigns a quality score, and the best and worst candidates are selected to form the (preferred, dispreferred) pair for DPO.
- **Base Models for Training:**
 - **Llama-3-8B-Instruct:** A moderately post-trained model.
 - **Qwen2.5-7B-SFT:** Fine-tuned from a pretrained Qwen2.5-7B on the Tulu3 SFT data mixture, representing a model with less initial post-training.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks (for Trained Models):** Two widely-used instruction-following benchmarks are used:
 - **AlpacaEval (AE):** Uses gpt-4-1106-preview as the judge.
 - **Arena-Hard (AH):** Uses gpt-4o-2024-08-06 as the judge.
- **Data Sources:**
 - **Instruction Set:** 60K instructions from the UltraFeedback dataset (Cui et al., 2024).
 - **High-Quality References:** Generated by **DeepSeek-V3** (Liu et al., 2024a) over the entire instruction set, chosen for its strong capabilities and moderate API cost.
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard settings for SFT (2 epochs, batch size 128, linear LR scheduler) and DPO (1 epoch, batch size 64, cosine LR scheduler, grid search for $\beta$) are used, following established practices.
- **Training Baselines:** Comparisons are made against:
 - **Base:** Untrained base models.
 - **ArmoRM-Base:** DPO on the base model using a strong finetuned reward model, ArmoRM-Llama3-8B.
 - **DSV3-Distill:** The SFT-only baseline (Stage 1).
 - **ROUGE and BERTScore:** DPO fine-tuned from DSV3-Distill using traditional semantic similarity metrics as rewards.
 - **ArmoRM:** DPO fine-tuned from DSV3-Distill using the ArmoRM reward model.
 - **RefFree:** DPO fine-tuned from DSV3-Distill using a reference-free self-LLM-judge.
 - **RefEval (Our Method):** DPO fine-tuned from DSV3-Distill using the reference-guided self-LLM-judge.
- **Ablation Studies:** Investigate the impact of reference quality on training by using references from a weaker model (GPT-4o-mini).

This comprehensive methodology allows for a thorough investigation of reference-guided evaluation and its subsequent application to a robust self-improvement training framework for LLM alignment in challenging non-verifiable domains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded significant findings across both the evaluation and training phases, strongly supporting the hypothesis that references can substantially improve LLM alignment in non-verifiable domains.

#### 4.1. Findings on Reference-Guided LLM-Judges

- **Superior Evaluation Accuracy:** The proposed `RefEval` method consistently achieved the highest average evaluation accuracy (79.1%) across five diverse datasets and 11 open-source LLM judges. It significantly outperformed both reference-free baselines (e.g., LLMBar-Base: 72.3%, CoT: 71.2%) and other reference-based methods (e.g., HREF-Ref: 74.8%, LLMBar-Ref: 74.0%). The `RefMatch` method also demonstrated strong performance (77.7%), ranking second.
- **Greater Benefit for Less Capable Models:** Smaller, less capable LLM judges showed more pronounced gains from reference guidance. For instance, Llama-3-8b saw an absolute improvement of approximately 17.4% with `RefEval` over `LLMBar-Base`. This indicates that high-quality references effectively compensate for the inherent limitations of smaller models, enabling them to achieve evaluation quality closer to that of much larger models.
- **Improved Inter-Judge Agreement:** `RefEval` substantially increased the average pairwise agreement among different LLM judges (81.4% for RefEval vs. 76.6% for Ref-Free). This suggests that references provide a shared objective grounding, reducing the subjectivity and variance in decision-making across different models.
- **Enhancement for Frontier Judges with Human References:** A focused case study revealed that even highly capable frontier LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, GPT-4.1) can be further enhanced as judges when provided with high-quality, human-edited "Oracle" references, particularly on challenging adversarial tasks.
- **Robustness to Reference Source:** The benefits of reference-guided evaluation were found to be robust even when references were generated by different frontier models (Claude-3.5, Claude-3.7, Gemini-2.0, DeepSeek-V3). Multi-reference voting strategies further enhanced accuracy. Even references from weaker models provided a clear signal over reference-free baselines.
- **Pointwise Scoring Improvement:** The advantages of reference guidance extended to pointwise scoring settings, where `RefEval-point` consistently outperformed `Base-point`.

#### 4.2. Findings on Reference-Guided Self-Improvement

- **SFT with References as a Strong Starting Point:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality DeepSeek-V3 references (`DSV3-Distill`) proved to be a highly effective initial step. It generally outperformed direct DPO training from the base model using a strong finetuned reward model (`ArmoRM-Base`). This highlights the inherent value of strong reference outputs for foundational model training.
- **Effective LLM Self-Improvement:** LLMs demonstrated clear self-improvement capabilities. `RefFree` (reference-free self-improvement) substantially improved upon the `DSV3-Distill` baseline, showing gains of +13.6 on AlpacaEval and +11.6 on Arena-Hard for Llama-3-8B-Instruct.
- **Reference-Guided Judges Elevate Self-Improvement:** The core finding is that reference-guided self-improvement (`RefEval`) consistently outperformed its reference-free counterpart (`RefFree`). It achieved average absolute gains of **+20.2 points on AlpacaEval and +17.1 points on Arena-Hard over the SFT distillation baseline**, and **+5.3 points on AlpacaEval and +3.6 points on Arena-Hard over reference-free self-improvement**.
- **Competitive with Finetuned Reward Models:** DPO with a reference-guided self-LLM-judge (`RefEval`) achieved performance comparable to or better than DPO using a powerful, separately finetuned reward model of the same parameter size (`ArmoRM`), without requiring the costly human or AI feedback data typically needed to train such reward models. For example, `RefEval-Llama3-8B-Instruct` achieved 73.1% on AlpacaEval and 58.7% on Arena-Hard, compared to `ArmoRM`'s 73.9% and 58.6%.
- **Outperformance of Traditional Metrics:** `RefEval` significantly outperformed methods relying on traditional reference-based metrics like ROUGE and BERTScore, demonstrating the superior nuanced judgment of LLM-judges.
- **Overall Performance against Strong Baselines:** The final reference-guided self-improved models (e.g., `RefEval-Llama3-8B-Inst`: 73.1% AE, 58.7% AH; `RefEval-Qwen2.5-7B`: 70.0% AE, 74.1% AH) showed clear advantages over competitive methods like SimPO-Llama3-8B-Inst and the official Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct checkpoint.
- **Robustness to Reference Quality in Training:** Ablation studies confirmed that while higher-quality references yielded better results, the reference-guided supervision mechanism itself provided structural benefits, with `RefEval` still outperforming `RefFree` even when using weaker references (e.g., from GPT-4o-mini).
- **Task-Specific Benefits:** The benefits of reference-based self-improvement were most pronounced in "Coding&Math" tasks for both models. For "Creative Tasks," the benefit was substantial for Llama-3-8B-Instruct but less significant for Qwen2.5-7B-SFT, suggesting that open-ended tasks might require more extensive post-training to fully leverage references.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate that integrating high-quality references with LLM-as-a-Judge paradigms leads to more accurate evaluation and significantly more effective and resource-efficient self-improvement for LLM alignment, particularly in domains previously considered non-verifiable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of large language model (LLM) alignment and post-training, with substantial potential impact across both research and practical applications.

1. **Bridging the RLVR-RLHF/RLAIF Gap:** The most profound significance is the successful demonstration of using reference-guided LLM-evaluators as "soft verifiers" in non-verifiable domains. This effectively bridges the methodological gap between Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and Reinforcement Learning from Human/AI Feedback (RLHF/RLAIF). By bringing reference-grounded supervision to domains lacking explicit ground truths, the work expands the applicability of robust RL techniques, moving beyond the binary "verifiable" vs. "non-verifiable" distinction.

2. **Enabling Cost-Effective LLM Alignment:** The findings offer a practical and cost-efficient pathway for LLM post-training. By showing that high-quality reference outputs can be effectively leveraged for both evaluation and self-improvement, the dependency on expensive human preference labeling or the constant use of powerful frontier models for AI feedback is significantly reduced. This democratizes access to advanced alignment techniques, allowing smaller research groups or companies to achieve strong alignment outcomes with more readily available data (references).

3. **Enhancing LLM-as-a-Judge Reliability:** The proposed `RefEval` and `RefMatch` prompting strategies set a new standard for LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation. The demonstrated improvements in accuracy, particularly for less capable models, and increased inter-judge agreement, make LLM-judges a more reliable and trustworthy tool for both research and development. This will improve the quality of automated evaluation benchmarks and the robustness of data collected for preference optimization.

4. **Advancing Self-Improvement Paradigms:** The integration of reference-guided judges into self-improvement pipelines provides a powerful mechanism for LLMs to iteratively refine themselves. Achieving performance comparable to or exceeding models trained with finetuned reward models, but without the external feedback required for reward model training, represents a significant step towards truly autonomous and continuous LLM alignment. This reduces the need for constant external intervention, making the development cycle faster and more scalable.

5. **Practical Utility of Existing Data:** The work highlights the underexplored value of high-quality reference datasets, which are often collected for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or readily generated by strong models. Instead of solely serving for distillation, these references can now play a central role in preference-based alignment, maximizing the utility of existing data assets.

6. **Insights into Model Capabilities:** The categorical analysis of task performance (e.g., strong gains in Coding&Math) provides valuable insights into where reference guidance is most impactful and where further research (e.g., for complex creative tasks) is needed. This informs future model architectures and training strategies.

**Potential Impact Areas:**
- **Accessibility of Advanced LLMs:** Lowering the barrier to entry for effective alignment will lead to a wider range of high-performing specialized LLMs tailored for specific tasks.
- **Automated Content Generation and Curation:** Improved alignment in non-verifiable domains, such as creative writing, summarization, and content generation, can lead to more contextually appropriate and high-quality outputs, requiring less human post-editing.
- **Educational AI Tools:** Reference-guided feedback could be instrumental in tutoring systems, providing more nuanced and helpful corrections to student responses.
- **Custom LLM Development:** Organizations can more easily fine-tune LLMs to their specific brand voice, style guides, or factual requirements using their own domain-specific reference outputs.

The paper concludes by pointing towards future work, particularly exploring the effectiveness of references in more specialized domains requiring domain expertise and developing dedicated reward models that intrinsically utilize references. This reinforces the idea that the presented framework is a foundational step towards a new generation of LLM alignment techniques that are both powerful and practical.
