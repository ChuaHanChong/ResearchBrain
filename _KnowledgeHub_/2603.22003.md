# VP-VLA: Visual Prompting as an Interface for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VP-VLA introduces a dual-system architecture that uses structured visual prompts as an explicit interface to improve the spatial precision and robustness of Vision-Language-Action models. It achieved higher success rates on manipulation benchmarks and demonstrated better generalization in real-world out-of-distribution scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on a monolithic architecture, struggling with simultaneous instruction interpretation, spatial grounding, and low-level control.
- Poor spatial precision and limited robustness in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where models tend to overfit to specific training scene distributions.
- Ineffective integration of high-level reasoning with low-level execution, leading to brittle performance on complex multi-stage tasks.

## Method

- A decoupled dual-system architecture separates high-level ==System 2 Planner== reasoning from high-frequency ==System 1 Controller== execution.
- Structured ==visual prompts== (e.g., crosshairs for interaction, bounding boxes for spatial constraints) are generated by the planner and overlaid on visual observations as an explicit interface for the controller.
- An auxiliary ==visual grounding objective== is introduced during training, applied selectively to key frames, to ensure the controller explicitly internalizes these visual prompts.

## Results

- VP-VLA achieved an average success rate of **53.8%** on the Robocasa-GR1-Tabletop benchmark, surpassing the QwenOFT baseline by **5.0%**, with consistent gains in challenging novel generalization splits.
- In real-world waste-sorting, it demonstrated an **85%** out-of-distribution success rate, exhibiting a minimal generalization gap (**2.5%**) compared to baseline models (**16.7%** drop).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the auxiliary visual grounding objective and the structured design of visual prompts are crucial for the framework's improved performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control through an explicit visual interface enhances spatial precision and robustness in VLA models.
> - Selective application of a visual grounding objective at critical decision points is more effective than dense, continuous supervision for integrating visual prompts.
> - Structured visual prompts, such as crosshairs, provide more effective spatial guidance than simpler cues, improving the policy's ability to ground instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have contributed to the field of robotic manipulation, leading to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to translate semantic understanding derived from linguistic instructions and visual observations directly into low-level robot control signals. By fine-tuning pre-trained VLMs on large-scale robotic datasets, VLA models endeavor to acquire embodied skills and generalize across various tasks and objects, moving towards the development of generalist manipulation policies.

However, existing VLA frameworks exhibit several limitations. A primary challenge is their tendency to overfit to specific training scene distributions, which can hinder their ability to truly ground instructions within dynamic environments. This limitation is evidenced by observations that VLA performance may not significantly degrade even when meaningful language instructions are replaced with arbitrary input. Consequently, these monolithic models often demonstrate reduced robustness when encountering novel object categories or previously unobserved spatial configurations, struggling with spatial precision. The "black-box" mapping approach, where a single forward pass simultaneously handles instruction interpretation, spatial grounding, and low-level control, contributes to these issues.

To mitigate these problems, prior research has explored the use of intermediate interfaces, such as goal images or dense geometric supervision, to provide more detailed guidance to robotic policies. Nevertheless, these methods often focus on static, single-task scenarios and rely on rigid interface representations, which may not adequately account for the dynamic and multi-stage nature of complex manipulation tasks. Furthermore, the generation of dense geometric data can be resource-intensive, and the quality of predicted affordances can be inconsistent. A fundamental difficulty remains in effectively integrating high-level reasoning capabilities with low-level execution within a unified end-to-end model. Some approaches leverage VLMs for subtask reasoning and target localization, but training-free pipelines may suffer from low precision, while methods incorporating dense segmentation masks (e.g., from expert models) can introduce modality mismatches against native RGB inputs, necessitating complex architectural modifications or increased computational overhead. Other methods, such as those relying on static first-frame coordinates, may disconnect visual prompts from the dynamic manipulation process.

VP-VLA addresses these challenges by proposing a decoupled dual-system architecture. It aims to bridge the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level control by introducing a structured, modality-consistent visual prompting interface. This approach seeks to enhance spatial precision and robustness, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, without introducing the complexities and limitations observed in alternative intermediate representations or monolithic VLA architectures. The work distinguishes itself by providing explicit spatial anchors directly within the native RGB observation space, aiming to create a computationally efficient and flexible grounded plan for manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. Current VLA models typically employ a "black-box" mapping that directly translates visual observations and linguistic instructions into control signals. This monolithic approach necessitates a single forward pass to manage instruction interpretation, spatial grounding, and low-level control concurrently. Such a design often results in several performance deficiencies:

- **Poor Spatial Precision:** The implicit nature of spatial grounding within a single, end-to-end model can lead to inaccurate object localization and imprecise manipulation actions.
- **Limited Robustness in Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Scenarios:** Existing VLA policies often overfit to the specific distributions of their training data, leading to a decline in performance when encountering novel objects, unseen spatial configurations, or varied environmental conditions. This indicates a lack of true instruction grounding beyond memorization.
- **Challenges in Decoupling Reasoning and Control:** End-to-end VLA models frequently struggle to effectively integrate high-level symbolic reasoning with the fine-grained demands of low-level motor execution. The simultaneous handling of these distinct cognitive processes can compromise overall task performance and generalization.
- **Modality Mismatch with Intermediate Representations:** Attempts to introduce intermediate interfaces, such as dense masks, affordance maps, or additional control-specific representations, often suffer from modality mismatch with the native RGB inputs. This can necessitate complex architectural modifications, additional training for vision encoders, specialized fusion layers, or reliance on expensive data curation, increasing computational overhead and implementation complexity.
- **Inconsistent Affordance Predictions:** The quality and consistency of predicted affordances, intended to guide actions, can be unreliable in existing systems, further hindering robust manipulation.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the VP-VLA framework are:

- **Decouple High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Execution:** To improve the robustness and precision of VLA models, the framework aims to separate complex instruction interpretation and spatial understanding from the direct generation of control signals. This is achieved through a dual-system architecture.
- **Establish a Structured Visual Prompting Interface:** The research seeks to introduce an explicit, structured interface that translates high-level planning into modality-consistent visual cues. These prompts, such as crosshairs for target objects and bounding boxes for goal locations, are rendered directly within the native RGB observation space, thereby avoiding modality mismatch and providing clear spatial anchors.
- **Enhance Spatial Precision and Robustness:** By providing explicit spatial guidance through visual prompts, the objective is to reduce the policy's search space and enable more reliable and precise low-level execution, particularly for tasks requiring accurate localization and placement. This also aims to improve generalization in OOD settings.
- **Integrate an Auxiliary Visual Grounding Objective:** To ensure the low-level controller effectively utilizes the visual prompts, a novel auxiliary training objective is proposed. This objective encourages explicit spatial awareness within the VLA controller, aligning its internal representations with the provided visual cues during training.
- **Maintain Competitive Performance:** The framework aims to demonstrate superior or competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art end-to-end VLA baselines across diverse simulation and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, validating the efficacy of the decoupled approach and visual prompting mechanism.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VP-VLA operates as a decoupled dual-system framework designed for robotic manipulation, comprising a "System 2 Planner" (P_S2) for deliberative reasoning and a "System 1 Controller" (π_θ) for high-frequency visuomotor tracking.

#### 3.1. Preliminary

Standard VLA policies typically map a language instruction and visual observations to action sequences. This framework conceptualizes an action `a_t` as a chunk of actions `{a_t^1..., a_t^n}` predicted from `a_t = π_θ(l, o_t)`. VP-VLA addresses the monolithic bottleneck of such policies by explicitly decoupling reasoning and control.

#### 3.2. System 2 Planner (P_S2)

The System 2 Planner is responsible for high-level reasoning and generating the visual interface `I_t^vp`. This module employs an event-driven execution loop and a visual prompt generation mechanism.

- **Event-Driven Task Decomposition:** To avoid continuous, computationally intensive reasoning, P_S2 is invoked only when a "transition event" `E_t` occurs. This event signifies a change in the robot's physical interaction state `S_t`. In the context of tabletop manipulation, a change in gripper status (open to closed or vice-versa) serves as a proxy for a semantic phase shift (e.g., completion of grasping, initiation of placing). This selective invocation triggers a re-evaluation of the visual prompt to reflect the next sub-goal.
- **Visual Prompt Generation:** Once an event is triggered, the planner performs two stages:
 - **Semantic Reasoning:** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM planner) processes the language instruction `l`, current observation `o_t`, and robot state `S_t`. It then identifies the current subtask `s_k` and extracts relevant entities, specifically the target object `e_obj` and target location `e_loc`, from the scene.
 - **Spatial Grounding:** A pre-trained segmentation model (e.g., SAM3) maps these identified entities to precise visual prompts `ψ_t`. These prompts consist of an interaction anchor `C` (a 2D crosshair at the object's centroid for manipulation primitives like "pick") and a spatial constraint `B` (a bounding box defining the placement region for primitives like "place"). These visual prompts are then overlaid onto the overhead camera observation, creating the visual interface image `I_t^vp`. This process translates abstract linguistic instructions into explicit geometric priors, effectively reframing the VLA's task from interpreting intent to visuomotor tracking of the provided prompts.

#### 3.3. System 1 Controller (π_θ)

The System 1 Controller is an extended VLA policy that integrates the generated visual prompt.

- **Policy Definition:** The policy `π_θ` now takes the language instruction `l`, original observation `o_t`, and the visual prompt image `I_t^vp` as input to generate actions `a_t = π_θ(l, o_t, I_t^vp)`. The controller comprises a VLM backbone `f_ω` for multimodal input processing and an action decoder `h_ψ` (e.g., MLP or diffusion model) for generating continuous control signals.
- **Training Objective:** A crucial aspect of VP-VLA is ensuring the policy treats visual overlays as semantic anchors. This is achieved through an auxiliary visual grounding objective, applied only during key frames (the first frame and frames where a transition event `E_t=1` occurs).
 - **Grounding Task:** Grounding is formulated as a classification task over `N=1000` discretized spatial bins for image dimensions. For a crosshair, the VLM within `π_θ` predicts its 2D location `(x, y)`. For a bounding box, it predicts `[x1, y1, x2, y2]`.
 - **Loss Function:** A Cross-Entropy (CE) loss is used for grounding, providing a structured training signal, while an L1 loss is used for action prediction. The grounding loss is exclusively backpropagated through the VLM parameters `ω` of the controller, not the entire policy.
 - **Total Loss:** `L_total = L_action(θ) + λ * 1_event * L_grounding(ω)`, where `λ` balances the two objectives. This selective grounding ensures internal representations are aligned with prompts, promoting precise and robust manipulation.

#### 3.4. Data Preparation

The process involves rule-based decomposition of original tasks into subtasks. At key frames, the VLM predicts the current subtask, target object, and target location names. A text-conditioned segmentation model (SAM) then generates masks and bounding boxes for all frames until the next key frame. These annotations are converted into visual prompts (crosshairs and bounding boxes) and stored with per-frame masks, boxes, and VLM subtask records. For experiments on the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset, episodes with empty prompts are discarded.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

SAM3 is used for visual prompt generation. The codebase is based on the starVLA framework, trained on 8 GPUs. QwenOFT architecture, with Prismatic VLM replaced by Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, is adopted for the System 1 Controller, and Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct is also used for the System 2 Planner. The AdamW optimizer is used with learning rates of 1e-5 for the VLM and 1e-4 for the action model. The coefficient `λ` for the grounding loss is set to 0.1. Baselines are compared fairly, using the same training datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental evaluations in both simulated and real-world environments to validate the VP-VLA framework.

#### 4.1. Experiments on Robocasa Benchmark

VP-VLA was evaluated on the RoboCasa-GR1-Tabletop benchmark, a simulation environment with 24 diverse multi-step pick-and-place tasks.
- **Overall Performance:** VP-VLA achieved an average success rate of 53.8%, surpassing the QwenOFT baseline (48.8%) by 5.0%. It also outperformed other baselines, including Isaac-GR00T N1.5 (48.2%), Isaac-GR00T N1.6 (47.6%), QwenGR00T (47.8%), and QwenPI (43.9%).
- **Specific Task Gains:** Notable improvement was observed in the "PnP * to * Close" setting, where VP-VLA reached 54.3% compared to QwenOFT's 43.7%.
- **Generalization:** Consistent gains were reported in challenging novel generalization splits (e.g., "PnP Novel From Placemat To Plate": 70.0% vs. 52.0% for QwenOFT; "PnP Novel From Tray To Plate": 66.0% vs. 56.0%). The VLM reasoner successfully decomposed complex instructions and accurately detected subtask transitions, shifting target objects and affordances as needed. This indicates improved generalization across varying backgrounds, object attributes, and positions.

#### 4.2. Experiments on SimplerEnv Benchmark

Evaluations were conducted on two large-scale subsets of the Open X-Embodiment (OXE) dataset: BridgeDataV2 and Fractal, encompassing four manipulation tasks.
- **Overall Performance:** VP-VLA achieved an average success rate of 58.3%, demonstrating an 8.3% absolute improvement over the QwenOFT baseline (50.0%). It also surpassed other strong competitors such as π0.5 (57.1%), Isaac-GR00T-N1.6-Bridge (57.1%), CogACT (51.3%), and VideoVLA (53.1%).
- **Task-Specific Improvements:** Significant improvements were noted in tasks requiring precise object identification and target location grounding, such as "Put Spoon on Towel" (66.7% vs. 58.3%) and "Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket" (95.8% vs. 70.8%). This suggests that VP-VLA more effectively leverages language-conditioned signals for action selection.

#### 4.3. Experiments on Real-world Scenarios

VP-VLA was tested on a Franka Research 3 7-DoF robot arm across various real-world manipulation tasks.
- **Robotic Waste-Sorting Categorization:**
 - **In-Domain (ID):** 87.5% success rate for VP-VLA compared to 80% for QwenOFT.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) (novel objects):** 85% success rate for VP-VLA versus 63.3% for QwenOFT.
 - VP-VLA exhibited a minimal generalization gap (2.5%) between ID and OOD performance, whereas QwenOFT showed a 16.7% drop, indicating that VP-VLA maintained robust object-level grounding in cluttered scenes.
- **Object Reference by Attribute (color-based egg picking):**
 - **ID:** 77.1% accuracy for VP-VLA, outperforming QwenOFT by 18.8%.
 - **OOD Color (novel purple and green eggs):** VP-VLA maintained 75.0% success, while QwenOFT dropped to 29.2%. The minimal gap (2.1%) for VP-VLA between ID and OOD color suggested learned representation captured attribute semantics.
 - **OOD Position (unseen grid locations):** VP-VLA achieved 75.0% success compared to 54.2% for QwenOFT, demonstrating spatial generalization.
- **Location Reference with Spatial Grounding (egg carton placement):**
 - **ID (language-specified coordinate with user-provided visual prompt for VP-VLA):** 91.25% accuracy for VP-VLA compared to 70.63% for QwenOFT.
 - **OOD (novel row-column combinations):** VP-VLA achieved 68.75% compared to 55% for QwenOFT, indicating superior geometric grounding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments on the RoboCasa Tabletop simulation investigated the contribution of core design components.
- **Effect of Grounding Objective:** Removing the grounding loss reduced the average success rate from 53.8% to 49.4%. Applying grounding to every frame ("w/ all frame grounding") yielded 49.5%. This indicates that explicit, selective grounding at key decision frames is crucial for prompt utilization and stable training.
- **Effect of Visual Prompt Design:** Changing the target object prompt from a crosshair to a single point ("w/ point") degraded performance to 47.3%. Directly overlaying prompts on the primary RGB image ("w/ direct overlay") resulted in 50.8%. These results suggest that the structured crosshair provides more effective spatial information and that separating the visual prompt from the raw image helps prevent interference.
- **Impact of Reasoning Decomposition:** An additional ablation on SimplerEnv showed that models without temporal decomposition (rendering all prompts simultaneously) experienced performance degradation, especially in multi-step tasks like "Put Eggplant in Yellow Basket," supporting the need for sequential objective handling.

#### 4.5. Tool-Use Demonstration

A "scoop bean" task was used to demonstrate applicability beyond pick-and-place. VP-VLA achieved a 50% success rate compared to 30% for QwenOFT, indicating potential for long-horizon, stage-dependent tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VP-VLA framework introduces a novel approach to robotic manipulation by effectively decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control through a structured visual prompting interface. This design addresses several critical limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, potentially influencing the development of more robust and generalizable robotic systems.

The significance of this work lies in several key contributions:

- **Improved Spatial Precision and Robustness:** By translating abstract linguistic instructions into explicit, modality-consistent visual prompts (crosshairs and bounding boxes), VP-VLA enables the low-level controller to achieve more precise spatial grounding and execution. This is particularly evident in its superior performance across in-distribution and challenging out-of-distribution scenarios in both simulation and real-world tasks, where it consistently outperforms end-to-end baselines.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The framework demonstrates a reduced generalization gap between in-domain and out-of-distribution settings, indicating that the learned policies are less prone to overfitting to specific training distributions. This improvement in handling novel objects, unseen spatial configurations, and fine-grained attribute-based references suggests a move towards more adaptable and versatile robotic agents.
- **Effective Integration of Reasoning and Control:** The dual-system architecture, with its event-driven System 2 Planner and prompt-guided System 1 Controller, provides a modular and interpretable approach to integrate complex reasoning with precise execution. This decoupling avoids the "black-box" limitations of monolithic architectures, potentially simplifying debugging and improving system reliability.
- **Novel Auxiliary Grounding Objective:** The introduction of an auxiliary visual grounding objective during training helps the VLA controller internalize the semantic meaning of the visual prompts, ensuring that these cues are actively utilized for action generation rather than being treated as extraneous noise. This explicit alignment contributes to the policy's precision and robustness.
- **Computational Efficiency and Flexibility:** The use of lightweight visual overlays directly within the native RGB image space, coupled with the selective invocation of the high-level planner, provides a computationally efficient interface. This approach avoids the need for complex architectural modifications or extensive modality fusion layers often associated with other intermediate representations.

The potential impact of VP-VLA extends to several areas within robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Advancement of Generalist Manipulation Policies:** The ability of VP-VLA to generalize across diverse tasks, object attributes, and spatial variations in cluttered environments contributes to the long-term goal of developing general-purpose robots capable of performing a wide range of tasks with minimal human intervention.
- **Practical Robot Deployment:** The demonstrated robustness in real-world scenarios, particularly in OOD settings, suggests that VP-VLA could facilitate the deployment of more reliable robotic systems in unstructured and dynamic human environments, such as waste sorting or home assistance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's modularity and the insights gained from the ablation studies (e.g., the importance of selective grounding and prompt design) can serve as a foundation for further research into visual prompting strategies, more sophisticated reasoning architectures, and richer visual prompt representations (e.g., 3D splats for complex manipulations).

While the current interface primarily uses 2D bounding boxes and crosshairs, the demonstrated benefits of this approach suggest a promising direction for developing more capable and adaptable robotic systems.
