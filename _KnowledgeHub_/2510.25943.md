# InputDSA: Demixing then Comparing Recurrent and Externally Driven Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> InputDSA introduces a method for comparing input-driven dynamical systems by disentangling their intrinsic recurrent dynamics from the effects of external inputs. The method, based on Koopman operator theory, utilizes a novel SubspaceDMDc algorithm to robustly estimate dynamic operators from partially observed and noisy data, demonstrating superior performance on synthetic data. Applications to reinforcement learning agents showed that successful learning correlates with consistent input processing, and analysis of biological neural recordings revealed how circuits shift between input-driven and intrinsically-driven states during cognitive tasks.

## Problem

- Existing dynamical similarity metrics primarily focus on autonomous systems, failing to account for the pervasive influence of external inputs on real-world systems like neural circuits or AI agents.
- Traditional methods often conflate intrinsic system dynamics with input-driven effects, leading to inaccurate comparisons when systems are non-autonomous.
- Analyzing real-world systems is challenging due to partial observability (only a subset of states can be measured) and the use of noisy or surrogate inputs.

## Method

- **InputDSA** extends ==Dynamical Similarity Analysis== (DSA) by defining a metric based on the Frobenius norm difference of system controllability matrices, allowing for separate, quantitative comparison of intrinsic (recurrent) and input-driven dynamics.
- The method introduces SubspaceDMDc, a novel operator estimation technique that combines Subspace DMD and DMDc, to robustly identify system operators from partially observed and noisy data.
- InputDSA leverages Koopman operator theory to embed nonlinear dynamics into a higher-dimensional observable space, where system evolution can be described by linear operators, enabling efficient comparison via orthogonal Procrustes analysis.

## Results

- InputDSA, when combined with SubspaceDMDc, accurately discriminated intrinsic from input-driven dynamics in partially observed, noisy synthetic systems, yielding substantially higher silhouette scores (**0.94** for state, **0.83** for input) than standard DMDc (**0.68** for state, **0.19** for input).
- The method demonstrated robustness to various forms of input noise and effective use of surrogate inputs, with high correlations (r > **0.75**) between ground truth and noisy InputDSA distances even at low signal-to-error ratios.
- Applications to reinforcement learning RNNs showed that high-performing agents in a plume tracking task developed more similar input-driven dynamics, activating a broader range of state dimensions, while intrinsic dynamics were not significantly different between performance groups.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately disentangling intrinsic and input-driven dynamics is crucial for a comprehensive and robust comparison of real-world non-autonomous systems, especially when data is partially observed and noisy.
> - In reinforcement learning, superior agent performance can be linked to more similar and effective input-driven dynamics, rather than solely differences in intrinsic recurrent dynamics.
> - Biological neural circuits exhibit dynamic reorganization across cognitive task epochs, transitioning between input-dominated states during evidence accumulation and intrinsically-dominated states during decision commitment.
