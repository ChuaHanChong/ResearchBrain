# InputDSA: Demixing then Comparing Recurrent and Externally Driven Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> InputDSA introduces a method for comparing input-driven dynamical systems by disentangling their intrinsic recurrent dynamics from the effects of external inputs. The method, based on Koopman operator theory, utilizes a novel SubspaceDMDc algorithm to robustly estimate dynamic operators from partially observed and noisy data, demonstrating superior performance on synthetic data. Applications to reinforcement learning agents showed that successful learning correlates with consistent input processing, and analysis of biological neural recordings revealed how circuits shift between input-driven and intrinsically-driven states during cognitive tasks.

## Problem

- Existing dynamical similarity metrics primarily focus on autonomous systems, failing to account for the pervasive influence of external inputs on real-world systems like neural circuits or AI agents.
- Traditional methods often conflate intrinsic system dynamics with input-driven effects, leading to inaccurate comparisons when systems are non-autonomous.
- Analyzing real-world systems is challenging due to partial observability (only a subset of states can be measured) and the use of noisy or surrogate inputs.

## Method

- **InputDSA** extends ==Dynamical Similarity Analysis== (DSA) by defining a metric based on the Frobenius norm difference of system controllability matrices, allowing for separate, quantitative comparison of intrinsic (recurrent) and input-driven dynamics.
- The method introduces SubspaceDMDc, a novel operator estimation technique that combines Subspace DMD and DMDc, to robustly identify system operators from partially observed and noisy data.
- InputDSA leverages Koopman operator theory to embed nonlinear dynamics into a higher-dimensional observable space, where system evolution can be described by linear operators, enabling efficient comparison via orthogonal Procrustes analysis.

## Results

- InputDSA, when combined with SubspaceDMDc, accurately discriminated intrinsic from input-driven dynamics in partially observed, noisy synthetic systems, yielding substantially higher silhouette scores (**0.94** for state, **0.83** for input) than standard DMDc (**0.68** for state, **0.19** for input).
- The method demonstrated robustness to various forms of input noise and effective use of surrogate inputs, with high correlations (r > **0.75**) between ground truth and noisy InputDSA distances even at low signal-to-error ratios.
- Applications to reinforcement learning RNNs showed that high-performing agents in a plume tracking task developed more similar input-driven dynamics, activating a broader range of state dimensions, while intrinsic dynamics were not significantly different between performance groups.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately disentangling intrinsic and input-driven dynamics is crucial for a comprehensive and robust comparison of real-world non-autonomous systems, especially when data is partially observed and noisy.
> - In reinforcement learning, superior agent performance can be linked to more similar and effective input-driven dynamics, rather than solely differences in intrinsic recurrent dynamics.
> - Biological neural circuits exhibit dynamic reorganization across cognitive task epochs, transitioning between input-dominated states during evidence accumulation and intrinsically-dominated states during decision commitment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The comparison of complex systems is a fundamental objective across diverse scientific disciplines, from deep learning and neuroscience to physics. Traditional approaches for characterizing system similarity, such as Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA), Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA), Procrustes Analysis, Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA), and Pearson Correlation, primarily focus on the *geometry* or *topology* of system states or activations. While valuable, these methods inherently fall short in capturing how systems evolve over time – their *temporal dynamics*.

Recognizing this limitation, recent research has begun to explore metrics centered on dynamical similarity. The foundational work in this vein is Dynamical Similarity Analysis (DSA), introduced by Ostrow et al. (2023). DSA marked a significant advancement by measuring the similarity of two systems based on their *recurrent dynamics* rather than static structural properties. It achieves this by embedding nonlinear dynamics into a high-dimensional (Koopman) space where they can be approximated by linear state-transition operators, which are then compared. DSA has since been successfully applied to various contexts, including recurrent network dynamics, training dynamics, and biological neural data. Other contemporary methods for dynamics comparison also exist, often employing techniques like neural networks or shape metrics.

However, a critical gap persisted in the landscape of dynamical similarity: nearly all existing methods, including the original DSA, implicitly assume *autonomous* dynamics. This means they do not explicitly consider the influence of external inputs on a system's temporal evolution. This assumption is a significant departure from reality for many systems of interest. In neuroscience, for instance, neural circuits continuously receive sensory inputs, motor commands, or signals from other brain regions. Similarly, deep neural networks, especially those in reinforcement learning, are constantly driven by observations from their environment. When inputs are ignored, two systems that possess fundamentally similar intrinsic dynamics but are driven by different external signals might be erroneously classified as dissimilar. Conversely, two dynamically distinct systems could appear similar if their inputs coerce them into similar trajectories.

**InputDSA directly addresses this crucial limitation.** It extends the robust framework of DSA to explicitly account for *non-autonomous* or *input-driven* dynamics. By enabling the disentanglement and comparison of both intrinsic (recurrent) and input-driven components, InputDSA provides a more realistic, comprehensive, and accurate lens through which to understand and compare complex dynamical systems. This work represents a vital next step in the evolution of dynamical systems analysis, moving from idealized autonomous models to real-world driven systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a novel metric, **InputDSA (iDSA)**, capable of quantitatively comparing the similarity of two dynamical systems that are explicitly driven by external inputs. This central goal is underpinned by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Gap with Real-World Systems:** The vast majority of complex systems studied in fields like neuroscience and machine learning are inherently non-autonomous. Biological brains process continuous sensory information, and artificial intelligence agents in reinforcement learning environments constantly interact with and receive feedback from their surroundings. Existing dynamical similarity metrics, including the authors' prior work on Dynamical Similarity Analysis (DSA), primarily focus on *autonomous* dynamics. This limitation means that two systems with fundamentally similar underlying recurrent dynamics might be erroneously judged as different if they are driven by diverse external inputs, or conversely, dissimilar systems might appear alike if inputs force their trajectories to converge. InputDSA aims to overcome this artificial constraint, enabling more realistic and ecologically valid comparisons.

2. **Disentangling Intrinsic vs. Input-Driven Dynamics:** A core motivation is to provide a method that can cleanly separate the contributions of a system's internal, recurrent dynamics from the effects of external input drive. This disentanglement is crucial for understanding the "nature of emergent computations" in both biological and artificial neural networks. For example, in a neural circuit, is a specific activity pattern primarily generated by internal feedback loops, or is it a direct consequence of ongoing sensory input? InputDSA seeks to quantify the relative similarity of these two components, offering deeper insights into system function.

3. **Robustness to Real-World Data Challenges:** The application of dynamical system analysis to real-world data, particularly neural recordings, presents significant challenges:
 - **Partial Observation:** It is almost always impossible to observe the complete state of a complex system (e.g., recording only a subset of neurons in the brain). A robust method must account for inputs affecting unobserved components that then indirectly influence observed dynamics.
 - **Noisy and Surrogate Inputs:** The "true" inputs driving a biological system are rarely perfectly known or measurable. Researchers often rely on noisy measurements or proxy variables (e.g., behavioral parameters, task events). The method needs to demonstrate resilience to these practical limitations.

4. **Improving Operator Estimation:** Beyond merely *comparing* input-driven systems, a related motivation is that explicitly modeling the input can actually *improve* the estimation of the intrinsic dynamic operator (A matrix). By accounting for external driving forces, the model can more accurately isolate the system's self-generated evolution.

In essence, InputDSA is motivated by the need for a more comprehensive and ecologically valid framework for comparing complex dynamical systems, one that acknowledges and quantifies the pervasive influence of external inputs while simultaneously addressing the practical challenges of data acquisition in scientific research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

InputDSA extends the foundational Dynamical Similarity Analysis (DSA) framework by introducing a novel metric and estimation approach tailored for non-autonomous systems. The methodology can be broken down into three core components: (1) the theoretical basis in Koopman Operator Theory, (2) the novel InputDSA metric for comparison, and (3) a robust method for estimating the underlying linear operators, particularly for partially observed systems.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: Koopman Operator and Linearization

Like its predecessor DSA, InputDSA leverages the Koopman Operator theory. This theory posits that any nonlinear dynamical system can be linearized by embedding its state into a sufficiently high-dimensional "observable" space. In this lifted space, the system's evolution can be described by a linear operator (the Koopman Operator). This linearization simplifies the comparison of dynamics, as conjugacy maps (which define equivalence between systems) become linear in this high-dimensional space. The practical implementation involves using nonlinear embeddings (e.g., delay embeddings, kernel functions, or neural networks) to project observed data into this higher-dimensional space where linear models are fit.

#### 3.2. The InputDSA Metric

The core innovation of this work is the InputDSA metric, designed to quantify similarity between systems described by state-space models: $x_{t+1} = A x_t + B u_t$.
- **Controllability as the Key:** The metric is inspired by the concept of *controllability* in linear systems, which describes the ability of inputs to drive a system to any desired state. Controllability is encoded in the T-step controllability matrix, $K = [B \quad AB \quad A^2B \quad \dots \quad A^{T-1}B]$. This matrix captures how inputs propagate through the system's intrinsic dynamics to influence future states.
- **Procrustes Alignment on Controllability Matrices:** The InputDSA metric defines dissimilarity as the minimum Frobenius norm difference between the controllability matrices of two systems, $K_1$ and $K_2$, under an orthogonal transformation $C$:
 $InputDSA(A_1, A_2, B_1, B_2, T) = \min_{C \in O(n)} ||CK_1 - K_2||_F^2$.
 This seeks an optimal rotation $C$ that best aligns the *entire input-driven dynamical behavior* of system 1 with system 2.
- **Efficient Optimization:** A significant advantage of this formulation is that finding the optimal $C$ is a *closed-form solution* via orthogonal Procrustes analysis, offering an exponential acceleration over the iterative optimization required by the original DSA for state-only comparisons. The authors further enhance robustness by also including powers of the transpose of A in the controllability matrix, $K = [B \quad AB \quad A^TB \quad \dots \quad A^nB \quad A^nB]$, which also retains a closed-form solution for $C$.
- **Disentangled Similarity Scores:** After determining the optimal alignment $C^*$, InputDSA allows for separate quantification of:
 - `InputDSA_state(A_1, A_2, C^*) = ||C^*A_1 C^{*T} - A_2||_F^2` (similarity of intrinsic dynamics).
 - `InputDSA_input(B_1, B_2, C^*) = ||C^*B_1 - B_2||_F^2` (similarity of how inputs map to states).
- **Handling Input Space Misalignment:** In scenarios where the external inputs themselves might be differently represented or transformed across systems, the framework can be extended to include an additional orthogonal transformation $C_u$ on the input space, leading to a jointly optimized alignment.

#### 3.3. Operator Estimation: Subspace Dynamic Mode Decomposition with Control (SubspaceDMDc)

Estimating the $A$ and $B$ operators from data is crucial. While Dynamic Mode Decomposition with Control (DMDc) is a standard method, it presents a critical flaw for *partially observed* systems, which are ubiquitous in real-world data (e.g., neuroscience).
- **The Partial Observation Problem:** When only a subset of a system's states is observed, inputs can influence observed states both instantaneously and indirectly via unobserved states that evolve according to the system's intrinsic dynamics. Applying standard DMDc to such data biases the estimation of the $B$ matrix, incorrectly mixing intrinsic and input-driven effects.
- **SubspaceDMDc Solution:** To overcome this, the authors introduce **SubspaceDMDc**, a novel extension combining Subspace DMD (Takeishi et al., 2017b) and DMDc. This method draws heavily from classical control theory's *subspace identification algorithms* (e.g., N4SID or PO-MOESP). These algorithms are designed to identify state-space models ($x_{t+1} = Ax_t + Bu_t$, $y_t = Cx_t$) directly from observed outputs ($y_t$) and inputs ($u_t$), even when the full state $x_t$ is not measured (i.e., partial observability, where $C$ is a selection matrix).
- **Robustness to Noise:** Subspace identification methods are inherently designed to handle both observation and process noise, lending InputDSA greater robustness to imperfect data.
- **Nonlinear Lifting:** Like other advanced DMD variants, SubspaceDMDc can incorporate nonlinear feature mappings (e.g., delay embeddings, kernels) to effectively linearize the underlying nonlinear dynamics in a higher-dimensional space.

#### 3.4. Hyperparameter Tuning

The methodology includes a suggested two-step hyperparameter tuning pipeline for delay embedding length and rank of the latent state space. This involves evaluating prediction accuracy using Mean Absolute Standardized Error (MASE) and model complexity using Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) on test data, aiming for a balance between predictive power and model parsimony.

In summary, InputDSA develops a theoretically sound, computationally efficient, and robust framework for comparing input-driven dynamical systems by extending Koopman theory and DSA, critically addressing partial observability and noisy inputs through the novel SubspaceDMDc algorithm and a fast Procrustes-based similarity metric.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a series of compelling findings, starting with validation on synthetic data and progressing to applications in recurrent neural networks and biological neural recordings. These results collectively demonstrate the efficacy and utility of InputDSA.

#### 4.1. InputDSA Discriminates Intrinsic from Input-Driven Dynamics in Partially Observed Systems

- **Synthetic Data Validation:** The authors constructed simulated nonlinear, discrete-time systems with known ground truth for intrinsic (A) and input (B) dynamics, varying these operators across different systems. Crucially, these systems were *partially observed* (e.g., 2 observed dimensions from a 20-dimensional system) and corrupted by noise.
- **Superiority of SubspaceDMDc:** When comparing similarity metrics (InputDSA scores) derived from standard DMD, DMDc, and the novel SubspaceDMDc, the latter demonstrated significantly superior performance. SubspaceDMDc yielded noticeably "sharper" similarity matrices, indicated by substantially higher silhouette scores (0.94 for state similarity, 0.83 for input similarity) compared to DMDc (0.68 for state, 0.19 for input) or DMD (0.6 for state).
- **Robustness to Partial Observation:** The core advantage of SubspaceDMDc was highlighted by its ability to accurately estimate input similarity, which DMDc failed to do robustly due to biases from partial observation. This robustness was maintained even as the total system size increased dramatically (from 2 to 1000 dimensions) while keeping the observed dimensions constant (2D). This finding confirms that SubspaceDMDc successfully disentangles intrinsic and input-driven effects under challenging real-world conditions.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Input Noise and Transformation

- **Generalized Noise Robustness:** InputDSA distances proved remarkably robust to various forms of noise perturbations (e.g., Gaussian, pink, multiplicative, impulse, drift, partial observation, temporal smoothing, rotation) applied to the input signal. The correlations between ground-truth InputDSA distances and those derived from noisy inputs remained high (r > 0.75) even when the signal-to-error ratio (SER) approached 1. This resilience is attributed to the delay embedding and reduced-rank regression used in SubspaceDMDc.
- **Surrogate Input Efficacy:** The method was tested using task-relevant *surrogate inputs* (e.g., RNN actions, positions, velocity, goals) in a "Random Target Reach" task performed by RNNs. Even with surrogates that were only moderately correlated with the true input, the estimated *intrinsic (state) similarities* remained strongly correlated with ground truth. For *input-driven similarities*, accuracy improved with higher correlation between surrogate and true inputs, demonstrating practical utility even when true inputs are unknown.

#### 4.3. Applications

- **Application 1: Evolution of Individual Differences in Reinforcement Learning RNNs (Plume Tracking Task)**
 - **Performance Differences Correlate with Input Dynamics:** In a simulated "Plume Tracking" task, InputDSA was applied to RNNs trained with deep reinforcement learning. It revealed that high-performing ("Top") agents had significantly *more similar input-driven dynamics* to each other compared to low-performing ("Bottom") agents. Conversely, intrinsic dynamics were not significantly different between these groups. This suggests successful plume tracking relies heavily on consistent and effective processing of sensory input (wind, odor).
 - **Mechanism of Difference:** Top agents showed a slower decay in the singular values of their input-mapping (B) operator, indicating that inputs activate a broader range of dimensions in their RNN states. This implies better integration of recent sensory information.
 - **"Anna Karenina" Principle:** Over the course of training, top agents converged to a more consistent set of input-driven dynamics, while bottom agents diverged into heterogeneous, idiosyncratic dynamics. This illustrates the "Anna Karenina principle" in RL: successful solutions are dynamically similar, while failures are diverse.

- **Application 2: Neural Population Dynamics Across Cognitive Task Epochs (Rat Auditory Evidence Accumulation)**
 - **Identifying Dynamical Regime Shifts:** InputDSA was applied to Neuropixels neural data from rats performing an auditory evidence accumulation task, focusing on dynamics before and after the "neural time of commitment" (nTc) – the point where the animal commits to a decision.
 - **Transition from Input-Driven to Intrinsically-Driven:** Significant shifts in both intrinsic and input-driven dynamics were identified across the nTc.
 - **Pre-nTc (Evidence Accumulation):** Dynamics were more input-driven, characterized by smaller power-law exponents of the A matrix eigenvalues, suggesting greater input controllability and faster decay of intrinsic dynamics.
 - **Post-nTc (Decision Commitment):** Dynamics became more intrinsically dominated and less input-sensitive. The average magnitude of intrinsic dynamics strengthened, while input-driven dynamics weakened.
 - **Reorganization of Subspaces:** Analysis of subspace angles between the A and B operators indicated a reorganization of neural dynamics across the nTc, with activity occupying different dynamical subspaces before and after commitment.
 - These findings align with previous studies suggesting a transition from sensory-driven evidence accumulation to an internally-generated decision phase.

In summary, the main findings establish InputDSA as a robust, accurate, and insightful tool for comparing complex, input-driven systems. It successfully addresses theoretical and practical challenges and provides concrete, biologically plausible insights into the dynamics of both artificial and biological intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

InputDSA represents a significant methodological advancement with far-reaching implications across computational neuroscience, machine learning, and broader scientific modeling of complex systems.

#### 5.1. Methodological Breakthroughs

- **First Robust Comparison of Input-Driven Dynamics:** InputDSA provides the first theoretically motivated and robust method to quantitatively compare *both* the intrinsic recurrent dynamics *and* the effects of external input on two dynamical systems directly from data. This moves beyond the restrictive assumption of autonomous systems, making the analysis relevant to a vast array of real-world phenomena.
- **Addressing Real-World Data Challenges:** The development of **SubspaceDMDc** is critical. By leveraging subspace identification algorithms, InputDSA successfully handles partial observation and noisy data – ubiquitous issues in experimental neuroscience and other fields. This enhances the reliability and applicability of the method to imperfect, real-world measurements.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The novel optimization algorithm for the InputDSA similarity metric, which transforms a complex iterative problem into a fast, closed-form solution via Procrustes alignment on controllability matrices, significantly accelerates analysis, making it practical for larger datasets and systems.

#### 5.2. Impact on Neuroscience

- **Deeper Understanding of Neural Circuits:** InputDSA offers a powerful new lens for dissecting how brain circuits operate. It enables researchers to precisely quantify the interplay between internally generated dynamics and externally driven responses. This is crucial for understanding how sensory information is integrated, how decisions are formed, and how motor commands are executed.
- **Validating Computational Models:** The ability to compare intrinsic and input-driven dynamics provides more stringent tests for validating computational models of neural activity against biological data. Researchers can perturb both a model and a neural circuit (e.g., optogenetically) and use InputDSA to compare their internal dynamics and impulse responses, moving beyond simple input-output matching.
- **Elucidating Cross-Region Communication:** InputDSA could be used to infer the information content of signals exchanged between different brain regions. By testing various potential "surrogate inputs" derived from upstream regions, researchers could identify which signals yield the lowest InputDSA input distance, thereby pinpointing the most relevant communication pathways.
- **Principled Use of Proxy Inputs:** The demonstrated robustness of InputDSA to noisy and surrogate inputs provides methodological support and a framework for evaluating the common practice in computational neuroscience of using proxy variables (e.g., behavioral parameters) when true neural inputs are unobservable.

#### 5.3. Impact on Machine Learning and AI

- **Understanding Learning in RNNs:** The application to reinforcement learning agents in the Plume Tracking task demonstrated InputDSA's ability to identify mechanisms underlying performance differences. It can reveal how successful agents converge to similar dynamic solutions while less successful ones diverge, offering insights into generalization and failure modes in deep learning.
- **Characterizing Model Evolution:** InputDSA can track how dynamic properties of RNNs evolve during training, helping to understand how different learning rules or architectures shape the internal computations of these models.
- **Benchmarking and Comparison:** It provides a novel metric for comparing different RNN architectures, training paradigms, or even models trained on different tasks, based on their inherent dynamic properties rather than just their input-output behavior.

#### 5.4. Broader Scientific Applications

- While primarily demonstrated in neuroscience and RNNs, InputDSA is a general method applicable to any time series data from complex dynamical systems across diverse fields (e.g., physics, robotics, climate modeling) where external drivers play a significant role. Its value lies in the ability to systematically compare dynamics under the realistic assumption of non-autonomy.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the assumption of additive input (which may not capture multiplicative effects) and potential challenges with closed-loop systems where input is a linear function of state. However, these are common challenges in systems identification. Despite these, the efficiency and robustness of InputDSA, coupled with its ability to disentangle intrinsic and input-driven dynamics in partially observed, noisy environments, position it as a powerful and indispensable tool for future research into complex dynamical systems.
