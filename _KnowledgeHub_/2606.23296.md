# IOI: Decoupling Kinematics and Physics for Interactive World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University developed the IOI framework, which decouples deterministic robot kinematics from stochastic environmental dynamics within interactive world models. This method significantly improved kinematic fidelity and physical compliance, reducing Frechet Video Distance by 36.47% compared to Ctrl-World and achieving a 0.9992 Pearson correlation with ground-truth simulators for policy evaluation.

## Problem

- Physics-based simulators suffer from a 'sim-to-real gap' due to simplified physical rules and require intensive manual asset creation.
- Real-world teleoperation datasets provide authentic interactions but offer limited opportunities for agent exploration of counterfactual actions or active learning.
- Existing purely data-driven interactive world models exhibit issues like action deviation and physically implausible state transitions due to the absence of explicit physical grounding.

## Method

- **IOI** employs a hybrid modeling paradigm that explicitly decouples deterministic robot kinematics from learned stochastic scene dynamics.
- It extracts ==explicit kinematic priors== using analytical forward/inverse kinematics from URDF models and re-parameterizes 3D robot geometry into view-invariant orthographic projections (front, side, top views).
- A Multi-view Kinematic Aggregation and Injection (MKAI) module fuses these orthographic renderings and injects them as kinematic tokens into a kinematics-guided video diffusion model.

## Results

- IOI achieved an FVD of **41.23**, representing a **36.47%** reduction compared to Ctrl-World, and a **67.30%** reduction compared to IRASim on RoboTwin **2.0**, demonstrating superior kinematic fidelity and perceptual realism.
- The framework maintained strong generation quality on zero-shot out-of-distribution tasks, achieving the best FVD (**69.16**) and outperforming Ctrl-World by **12.23%**.
- Policy evaluation success rates using IOI closely aligned with ground-truth physics simulators, showing an average difference of only +**1.6** percentage points and a Pearson correlation coefficient of **0.9992**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating analytical kinematic priors provides precise, geometry-consistent guidance for robot motion, resolving the trade-off between control alignment and stochastic visual feedback.
> - Decoupling deterministic robot motion allows the generative model to dedicate its representational capacity primarily to modeling complex, stochastic environmental interactions.
> - Using view-invariant orthographic projections for kinematic guidance enhances robustness and practical deployability by eliminating the need for precise extrinsic camera calibration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist embodied agents capable of operating in diverse real-world scenarios necessitates scalable, visually realistic, and interactive environments for robust policy learning and evaluation. Traditionally, this domain has relied on two primary approaches: physics-based simulators and real-world teleoperation datasets.

Physics-based simulators, while providing a controlled environment, often require intensive manual asset creation and rely on simplified physical rules. This results in a "sim-to-real gap," where visual appearance and contact dynamics in simulations do not accurately reflect the real world, limiting the direct transfer of learned policies. Real-world teleoperation, exemplified by systems like ALOHA 2, captures authentic physical interactions and visual fidelity. However, it typically provides only offline trajectories, offering limited avenues for agent exploration of counterfactual actions or active learning within an interactive sandbox.

To address the limitations of both traditional simulators and static real-world datasets, interactive world models have emerged as a promising paradigm. These models frame environmental dynamics as an action-conditioned video generation task, learning visual transitions directly from real-world observations. This approach bypasses the need for explicitly programmed physical rules or extensive physical asset construction, theoretically enabling agents to explore hypothetical action sequences in photorealistic and interactive environments.

Despite their potential, existing interactive world models largely operate under a purely data-driven paradigm. By exclusively mapping visual observations and actions to future video frames, these models rely on pixel-level statistical correlations. This fundamental absence of explicit physical grounding makes them susceptible to two critical failure modes:
1. **Action Deviation:** Accumulated prediction errors cause synthesized motion to diverge progressively from intended control instructions over time.
2. **State Implausibility:** Models fail to maintain physically consistent object states, leading to unrealistic deformations, geometric penetrations, and violations of object permanence.

These persistent visual artifacts and dynamic violations render the generated trajectories unreliable for robust policy learning and evaluation. The work presented in this paper, IOI, directly addresses these limitations by introducing explicit structural constraints and analytical priors into the world model framework, aiming to bridge the gap between purely data-driven methods and the requirements for reliable, physically grounded interactive simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to resolve the dilemma between enforcing precise geometric control and generating stochastic visual feedback within interactive world models. The authors propose to achieve this by explicitly decoupling deterministic robot kinematics from complex environmental dynamics.

The motivation for this approach stems from several observations regarding the limitations of prior work:
- **Lack of Explicit Structural Constraints:** Existing, purely data-driven interactive world models struggle with simultaneously ensuring precise control alignment and physically plausible visual feedback. This is attributed to their reliance solely on pixel-level statistical correlations without incorporating explicit physical or geometric structure.
- **Spatiotemporal Drift and Implausibility:** Without explicit physical grounding, purely data-driven generative approaches are prone to issues such as spatiotemporal drift, where synthesized motions deviate from intended actions, and state implausibility, leading to unrealistic object deformations, geometric penetrations, and violations of object permanence. These issues render generated trajectories unreliable for tasks like policy learning and evaluation.
- **Inefficient Representational Capacity:** In traditional world models, the generative network is forced to implicitly learn both the deterministic embodiment geometry of the robot and the stochastic dynamics of environmental interactions from low-dimensional action embeddings. This entanglement overburdens the model's representational capacity and can lead to inconsistencies.

To address these challenges, IOI aims to:
- **Integrate Analytical Kinematic Priors:** By leveraging analytical knowledge of robot kinematics (e.g., from URDF models), IOI seeks to provide explicit, deterministic guidance for robot motion.
- **Decouple Dynamics:** The core idea is to separate the deterministic robot motion (kinematics) from the stochastic physical interactions and environmental dynamics. This allows the generative model to dedicate its representational capacity primarily to modeling the complex, unpredictable aspects of the scene.
- **Ensure Geometry-Consistent and View-Invariant Guidance:** By employing orthographic projections of the robot's kinematic trajectory, IOI aims to provide consistent geometric cues that are independent of the observation camera's extrinsic parameters, thereby improving robustness and practical deployability.
- **Enhance Reliability for Policy Learning:** Ultimately, the goal is to create a world model that generates trajectories accurate enough in both visual realism and dynamic consistency to serve as a reliable platform for policy learning and evaluation, closely aligning with ground-truth physics simulators and real-world performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

IOI employs a hybrid modeling paradigm that combines analytical resolution of deterministic robot kinematics with learned stochastic scene dynamics through conditional video generation. The overall pipeline consists of three main stages: Kinematic Prior Extraction, Multi-view Kinematic Aggregation and Injection (MKAI), and Kinematics-Guided Video Diffusion.

#### 3.1. Kinematic Prior Extraction

The process begins by analytically deriving an explicit kinematic trajectory from the input action sequence and the robot's Universal Robot Description Format (URDF) model (M).
- **Action-to-Joint-Space Conversion:** Given an action sequence $A_{t+1:t+T}$, it is first converted into a unified joint-space trajectory state $\{q_{t+1}, \dots, q_{t+T}\}$.
 - For joint-space actions, $q_k = q_{k-1} + \Delta q_k$.
 - For Cartesian end-effector actions, inverse kinematics (IK) is solved, $q_k = \text{IK}(a_k, q_{k-1}; M)$, using the previous joint state for temporal coherence.
- **Forward Kinematics and 3D Geometry Reconstruction:** Forward kinematics (FK) is applied to the joint-space trajectory to obtain the 3D pose of each robot link at every timestep, $\{T_{l,k}\}_{l=1}^{|L|} = \text{FK}(q_k; M)$. The complete robot geometry $G_k$ at timestep $k$ is then reconstructed by applying these transformations to the canonical meshes of each link.
- **Orthographic Projection for View-Invariant Representation:** To overcome the limitations of perspective projection (e.g., noise, inaccuracies from extrinsic camera parameters), IOI re-parameterizes the 3D robot geometry $G_k$ into synchronized orthographic renderings. Three canonical views (front, side, top) are used.
 - For each timestep $k$, $G_k$ is rendered under these three projections: $R_v^k = R(G_k; \Pi_v)$, where $v \in \{\text{front, side, top}\}$. $R(\cdot)$ denotes a rasterization-based renderer.
 - These stacked 2D renderings, $R_{v,t+1:t+T}$, provide explicit, yet compact, geometric control signals that are view-invariant. Orthographic projection preserves true geometric proportions and the three views complement each other to cover 3D robot motion effectively.

#### 3.2. Multi-view Kinematic Aggregation and Injection (MKAI)

The MKAI module is designed to encode the tri-view orthographic renderings, aggregate them into a unified geometric representation, and inject this kinematic prior into the diffusion backbone.
- **Kinematic Fusion Embedder:** The orthographic renderings $R_{v,t+1:t+T}$ are first mapped into latent representations $z_v = \text{Enc}(R_{v,t+1:t+T})$ using a frozen VAE. Learnable temporal embeddings $E_{time}$ are added to preserve temporal order. These temporally augmented latents $\{\tilde{z}_f, \tilde{z}_s, \tilde{z}_t\}$ are then fused into a unified kinematic representation $C_k$ via a shared two-layer MLP fusion encoder $E_{fusion}$.
- **Alignment Embedder:** The aggregated kinematic representation $C_k$ is tokenized into Kinematic Tokens $K$ that reside in the hidden space of the diffusion backbone. This is achieved using a 3D convolutional patchification design, similar to the VACE Context Embedder, and 3D sinusoidal positional encodings. This step ensures that the kinematic tokens share the same spatial resolution, temporal structure, and feature dimensionality as the video tokens in the main diffusion model.
- **Kinematic Blocks and Injection:** The Kinematic Tokens $K$ are processed by $M$ Kinematic Blocks. Each block includes layer normalization, self-attention over kinematic tokens, cross-attention with the hidden states $H_i$ of the corresponding diffusion transformer (DiT) block, a feed-forward network, and an output projection. The output of each Kinematic Block, $K_{i+1}$, is additively injected into the corresponding frozen DiT Block $H_{main,i+1}$ via a learned gating projection $W_g$. $W_g$ is initialized to zero to allow gradual learning of geometric guidance without immediately disrupting the pretrained generative capacity. Only the Kinematic Fusion Embedder, Alignment Embedder, and Kinematic Blocks are trainable; all DiT parameters remain frozen.

#### 3.3. Kinematics-Guided Video Diffusion

IOI utilizes a pretrained latent-space video diffusion model (specifically, WAN 2.1) based on flow matching as its generative backbone.
- **Latent Space Mapping:** Historical observation frames $V_{t-h:t}$ and future target frames $\hat{V}_{t+1:t+T}$ are mapped into latent representations $z_h = \text{Enc}(V_{t-h:t})$ and $z_f = \text{Enc}(\hat{V}_{t+1:t+T})$ using a pretrained video VAE.
- **Flow Matching Objective:** The flow matching objective is applied to the future latents $z_f$, conditioned on the historical observation latents $z_h$ and the aggregated kinematic tokens $C_{agg}$ from MKAI. A linear interpolation is defined between a Gaussian noise prior $\epsilon$ and the target future latent $z_f$, resulting in a noised latent $z_\tau$. The neural network approximates the target velocity field $u_\tau = z_f - \epsilon$.
- **Training:** The model is trained by minimizing the flow matching loss $L_{FM} = E_{z_f,\epsilon,\tau} \| (z_f - \epsilon) - v_\theta(z_\tau, \tau; z_h, C_{agg}) \|_2^2$. This objective guides the network to reconstruct future video trajectories while explicitly conditioning on the robot’s deterministic kinematic prior. This allows the generative model to focus its capacity on stochastic scene dynamics (e.g., contact-induced object interactions), as embodiment motion is handled by the kinematic guidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IOI was conducted across three simulation settings, two real-world scenarios, and two ablation studies, primarily utilizing the RoboTwin 2.0 simulator and a physical Franka Emika manipulator.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment Results

- **Experiment 1: Kinematic Fidelity and Physical Compliance (Table 1 & Figure 4)**
 - **Quantitative:** IOI achieved state-of-the-art performance on RoboTwin 2.0 across metrics evaluating temporal coherence, structural preservation, and perceptual realism. It recorded the best Frechet Video Distance (FVD) of 41.23, Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of 0.8637, and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) of 0.0695. The FVD score represented a 36.47% reduction compared to Ctrl-World (64.90) and a 67.30% reduction compared to IRASim (126.20).
 - **Qualitative:** Visual comparisons (Figure 4) demonstrated that IOI consistently maintained rigid robot morphology and precise hand-object interaction dynamics throughout long-horizon manipulation sequences. In contrast, baselines like IRASim exhibited catastrophic structural collapse (e.g., vanishing robot arms, morphological distortion), and Ctrl-World showed issues with fine-grained physical interactions, such as object hallucination and incorrect spatial boundaries.
- **Experiment 2: Zero-Shot OOD Generalization (Table 2 & Table 3)**
 - **Setup:** The model was trained on 40 distinct manipulation tasks and tested on 10 unseen, out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks that required the recombination of learned manipulation primitives in novel contexts (Table 2).
 - **Quantitative:** IOI maintained strong generation quality on OOD tasks, achieving the best performance across all four metrics: FVD (69.16), PSNR (24.08), SSIM (0.8127), and LPIPS (0.1309). It outperformed Ctrl-World by 12.23% in FVD and IRASim by 58.39%. This indicated that the explicit kinematic prior effectively generalized to novel manipulation scenarios without task-specific fine-tuning.
- **Experiment 3: Policy Evaluation Utility (Table 4)**
 - **Setup:** IOI was used to autoregressively simulate a $\pi^0$ vision-language-action (VLA) policy across five manipulation tasks, with success rates evaluated by independent human evaluators.
 - **Quantitative:** Success rates evaluated by IOI closely aligned with those from a ground-truth physics simulator. The average difference was only +1.6 percentage points. IOI exhibited a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.9992 with ground-truth simulator evaluations, indicating that it accurately modeled physical constraints and fine-grained contact dynamics without hallucination.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiment Results

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a physical Franka Emika manipulator for a sandwich pick-and-place task, using successful teleoperation demonstrations for training.
- **Qualitative (Figure 5):** IOI successfully maintained the rigid structural integrity of the robotic arm and synthesized precise hand-object contact dynamics under various real-world conditions (unstructured lighting, varying object textures). The generated trajectories showed no severe visual distortions or temporal drift. The model also demonstrated emergent generalization to complex and unseen action trajectories, suggesting it captured underlying physical interaction patterns rather than just memorizing demonstrations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Analysis of Action Conditioning Mechanisms (Table 5):** This study compared IOI's explicit geometric conditioning (URDF + MKAI) against four mainstream implicit action injection baselines (Add, Concat, Cross-attention, AdaLN).
 - **Quantitative:** URDF + MKAI achieved the best performance across all metrics (PSNR 25.73, SSIM 0.8637, LPIPS 0.0695, FVD 41.23). This represented a 26.97% FVD reduction over the best implicit method, AdaLN (FVD 56.47). The results suggested that explicit geometric conditioning significantly improved performance by allowing the model to focus on stochastic scene dynamics rather than implicitly inferring robot kinematics.
- **Upper-Bound Analysis of MKAI Module (Table 6):** The MKAI module's effectiveness in learning spatial mappings without explicit camera parameters was validated against an oracle baseline (IOI-Explicit) that used ground-truth camera intrinsics and extrinsics.
 - **Quantitative:** IOI-MKAI, without requiring target camera extrinsics, achieved competitive performance compared to the IOI-Explicit oracle (IOI-MKAI FVD 41.23 vs. IOI-Explicit FVD 37.15). This indicated that the MKAI module successfully learned complex implicit spatial mappings from orthographic views with only a marginal precision trade-off, thereby enhancing robustness and generalization by eliminating reliance on strict camera calibration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The IOI framework introduces a paradigm shift in interactive world modeling by systematically integrating analytical kinematic priors with learned physical dynamics. This decoupling addresses fundamental limitations prevalent in purely data-driven generative models, offering several significant implications:

- **Mitigation of Spatiotemporal Drift and State Implausibility:** By providing explicit, geometry-consistent guidance for robot motion, IOI directly tackles the issues of action deviation and physically implausible visual artifacts that compromise the reliability of generated trajectories in prior models. This makes the simulated environment more trustworthy for complex robot learning tasks.
- **Enhanced Reliability and Generalization:** The research demonstrates that IOI achieves superior kinematic fidelity, structural preservation, and perceptual realism compared to existing methods. Its robust zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution tasks indicates that the model learns fundamental interaction logic rather than merely memorizing specific task semantics. This makes IOI a more versatile and adaptable tool for training generalist embodied agents.
- **Reliable Policy Evaluation and Training:** IOI's strong correlation with ground-truth physics simulators in policy evaluation metrics suggests its utility as a reliable surrogate for real-world testing. Furthermore, policies trained exclusively on IOI-synthesized data perform comparably to those trained on real-world teleoperation demonstrations, validating its practical value as a scalable data generation engine for embodied policy learning.
- **Reduced Deployment Friction:** The use of view-invariant orthographic projections eliminates the need for precise extrinsic camera calibration, a practical challenge in real-world robotic deployments due to occlusions, dynamic lighting, and calibration drift. This significantly simplifies the setup and enhances the framework's robustness in unstructured environments.
- **Foundation for Physics-Informed World Models:** This work encourages future exploration into hybrid approaches that leverage explicit physical knowledge to augment deep generative models. By freeing the generative model's representational capacity from modeling deterministic kinematics, it can focus on the more complex, stochastic aspects of environmental dynamics, potentially leading to more accurate and generalizable simulations.

While IOI represents a notable advancement, the authors identify areas for future work, such as extending applicability to higher-level action abstractions and incorporating appearance-level details for more granular contact-rich interaction modeling. Nevertheless, this research contributes a principled methodology for building interactive world models that are both photorealistic and physically grounded, with substantial implications for the development and evaluation of embodied AI.
