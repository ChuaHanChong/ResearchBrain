# Exploration vs Exploitation: Rethinking RLVR through Clipping, Entropy, and Spurious Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper investigates the dynamics of clipping, policy entropy, and spurious rewards within Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models. It establishes that clipping acts as an implicit entropy minimizer and regularizer for stability, while spurious rewards can benefit stronger models beyond data contamination, challenging prior interpretations of exploration-exploitation in this framework.

## Problem

- A puzzling paradox in RLVR where both spurious rewards (hindering exploitation) and entropy minimization (reducing exploration) have been observed to improve LLM reasoning performance.
- Conflicting explanations for the benefits of spurious rewards, with prior work attributing gains to PPO-style clipping bias or model contamination, and inconsistent reports on their generalizability.
- The direct causal relationship between policy entropy changes and LLM reasoning performance in RLVR was not definitively established.

## Method

- Conducted theoretical analyses on ==upper-clipping bias== within GRPO and introduced a novel formulation for the one-step policy-entropy shift to understand entropy dynamics under clipped and unclipped training.
- Proposed a probabilistic reward-misalignment model to explain the potential benefits of spurious rewards, quantifying 'correct-response advantage loss' and its dependence on correct rollouts.
- Performed extensive empirical evaluations across multiple LLM families (Qwen2.5-Math, R1-Distill-Llama, QwQ-32B) and datasets (DeepScaleR, MATH500, AIME Past) to validate theoretical findings.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis and empirical tests demonstrated that the raw surrogate sum's magnitude significantly overshadows the clipping correction term, indicating that clipping bias does not provide a meaningful learning signal.
- Clipped training consistently led to a monotonic decrease in policy entropy and prevented gradient explosions, confirming its role as an implicit entropy minimizer and crucial regularizer for training stability.
- Performance improvements under random rewards were observed in stronger models (e.g., Llama, QwQ families) even on challenging datasets, supporting the reward-misalignment model and refuting contamination as the sole explanation for their benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Clipping in RLVR primarily functions as an implicit entropy minimization mechanism and a regularizer, enhancing training stability rather than providing a direct learning signal from spurious rewards.
> - The effectiveness of policy entropy minimization for performance is not universal; its benefits are context-dependent and influenced by model strength and task difficulty.
> - Spurious rewards can enhance the performance of stronger LLMs in RLVR by modulating entropy, and their benefits extend beyond effects solely attributable to data contamination or clipping bias.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a set of dynamics within Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a framework increasingly utilized for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in domains like mathematics and STEM. RLVR's success is notably evidenced by models achieving human-level performance on challenging benchmarks such as the International Mathematical Olympiad. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is a prominent method within RLVR due to its computational and memory efficiency.

In classical reinforcement learning, the exploration-exploitation trade-off is typically managed by encouraging exploration via stochastic policies or bonuses and reinforcing exploitation through accurate value estimation. RLVR for LLMs departs from this paradigm due to several characteristics: rewards are outcome-level and sparse, exploration occurs in sequence space influenced by decoding temperature, and policy updates rely on ratio clipping with group-normalized advantages.

Previous research has presented seemingly contradictory findings within this RLVR context:
- **Spurious Rewards:** Rewards misaligned with the true outcome (e.g., random noise) have been observed to improve performance in some RLVR settings, particularly with Qwen-Math models (Shao et al., 2025). This phenomenon has been partly attributed to upper-clipping bias and potential data contamination (Wu et al., 2025). This contradicts classical RL intuition, where spurious rewards would typically hinder exploitation.
- **Entropy Minimization:** Reducing policy entropy, which leads to more deterministic and confident outputs, has also been widely adopted and empirically linked to performance gains in RLVR (e.g., Zhang et al., 2025b; Zhao et al., 2025b; Cui et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025). This strategy discourages exploration.

The juxtaposition of these two observations—that both discouraging exploitation (spurious rewards) and discouraging exploration (entropy minimization) can enhance reasoning performance—highlights a poorly understood set of learning dynamics unique to RLVR. This paper positions itself to investigate and reconcile these effects, challenging existing interpretations (such as contamination being the sole driver for spurious reward benefits) and offering a more nuanced understanding of exploration-exploitation in RLVR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate how clipping, policy entropy, and spurious (random) rewards collectively influence model performance within the RLVR framework. By clarifying these mechanisms, the authors aim to provide foundational principles for more effective RLVR training strategies.

The motivation for this investigation arises from several points:

- **Puzzling Paradox in RLVR:** The observation that two seemingly contradictory mechanisms—the use of spurious rewards (which, in classical RL, would hinder exploitation) and entropy minimization (which reduces exploration)—can both lead to improved LLM reasoning performance within RLVR. The underlying principles connecting these effects and their utility remain unclear.
- **Conflicting Explanations for Spurious Reward Benefits:** Prior work (Shao et al., 2025) attributed performance gains from spurious rewards to PPO-style upper-clipping bias, which could preferentially amplify high-prior responses. Other research (Wu et al., 2025) linked these gains to model contamination, suggesting improved performance might stem from reinforcing memorized trajectories rather than enhanced reasoning. Conversely, some studies (Oertell et al., 2025) reported that random-reward fine-tuning did not consistently improve reasoning and could even degrade it, casting doubt on the generalizability of these benefits. This paper seeks to address these conflicting findings and provide a more robust understanding.
- **Unclear Relationship between Policy Entropy and Performance:** While some studies suggest avoiding "entropy collapse" for effective exploration (Yu et al., 2025), others propose that reducing entropy can be beneficial, with some even positing a monotonic relationship between lower entropy and better performance (Cui et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025). The direct causal relationship between policy entropy and performance is not definitively established in RLVR, motivating its further examination.
- **Distinctive RLVR Dynamics:** RLVR's departure from classical RL in terms of reward sparsity, exploration mechanisms, and policy update methods suggests that intuitions from classical RL may not directly apply. A deeper theoretical and empirical understanding of these unique dynamics is necessary to advance the field.

To address these motivations, the paper focuses on two fundamental questions:
1. How policy entropy relates to performance in RLVR.
2. Whether spurious rewards yield performance gains, and if so, through what mechanisms, particularly considering the roles of clipping bias and potential model contamination.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical analysis and extensive empirical experimentation to investigate the interplay of clipping, policy entropy, and spurious rewards in RLVR.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis

- **Analysis of Clipping Bias:** The authors rigorously analyze the upper-clipping bias within the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.
 - **Decomposition of Surrogate:** The clipped surrogate objective is decomposed into a raw (unclipped) term and a clipping-correction term (`C_tot+`).
 - **Bounds on Clipping Correction:** Theorem 3.2 derives explicit upper bounds on the expected magnitude of the total clipping-correction term. This bound is expressed in terms of observable training parameters, including the clipping activation rate, which quantifies the proportion of tokens exceeding the clipping threshold.
 - **Comparison to Raw Surrogate:** Theorem 3.4 establishes a lower bound for the expected magnitude of the raw surrogate sum (`N_raw`) and compares it to the clipping-correction term, demonstrating that the raw surrogate term is significantly larger under practical hyperparameter settings.
- **One-Step Policy Entropy Change:** To understand the dynamics of policy entropy, the paper introduces a novel formulation for the one-step policy-entropy shift, moving beyond prior approximations that did not account for clipping or random rewards.
 - **Unclipped Training:** Theorem 4.1 analyzes entropy dynamics under unclipped training, showing its dependence on the initial policy distribution's skewness (measured by Φ(π_old)). This allows for conditions where entropy can increase.
 - **Clipped Training:** Theorem 4.3 extends this analysis to clipped training, demonstrating how clipping can lead to a decrease in policy entropy.
- **Reward-Misalignment Model:** To explain the potential benefits of spurious rewards, the authors propose a probabilistic reward-misalignment model.
 - **Advantage Loss:** Definition 5.1 formalizes "correct-response advantage loss" (`Δ`), quantifying the misallocation of reward signal due to random mislabeling of correct and incorrect rollouts.
 - **Expected Damage and Variance:** Proposition 5.2 calculates the expected value and variance of this advantage loss, showing its dependence on the number of correct rollouts (`n_c`) within a group.
 - **Conditional Analysis:** Theorem 5.3 refines this by decomposing the damage into conditional means based on whether false positives (FP) or false negatives (FN) dominate, providing insights into why stronger models might benefit more.

#### 3.2. Empirical Evaluation

- **Model Families and Sizes:** Experiments were conducted across multiple LLM families and sizes to assess generalizability: Qwen2.5-Math (1.5B, 7B), R1-Distill-Llama-8B, and QwQ-32B. This includes both base and distilled variants.
- **Datasets:** The DeepScaleR dataset (Luo et al., 2025) was used for training, providing a clean training environment by avoiding overlap with potential contamination sources. The MATH500 validation set was used for performance evaluation, and the AIME Past series was introduced as a more challenging training environment for certain experiments.
- **Training Setup:** The experiments utilized the `verl` framework (Sheng et al., 2025) for RLVR training. Key hyperparameters included a batch size of 128, group size of 16, decoding temperature of 1.0, a clipping ratio of 0.2 (or varied for ablations), a learning rate of 5 × 10^-7, and a KL coefficient of 0. Random rewards were generated from a Bernoulli(0.5) distribution. The default `verl` Qwen prompt, which explicitly instructs the model to box its final answer, was used to ensure consistent verification.
- **Key Experiments:**
 - **Clipping's Direct Effect on Performance:** Comparisons of Qwen2.5-Math-7B training with and without clipping (Figure 1), along with ablation studies on clipping ratio (ε = 0.1, 0.15 vs. 0.2 and no clipping in Figure 5) and group size (G = 8 vs. 16 in Figure 6).
 - **Clipping's Effect on Entropy:** Tracking policy entropy evolution under unclipped versus clipped random-reward training for Qwen2.5-Math-7B (Figure 2, Left & Middle). Observation of gradient explosion in unclipped R1-Distill-Llama-8B training (Figure 2, Right).
 - **Entropy-Performance Relationship:** Training Qwen2.5-Math-7B, QwQ-32B, and R1-Distill-Llama-8B on the harder AIME dataset with clipped and unclipped training, correlating entropy changes with validation performance (Figure 3, Figure 7).
 - **Model Strength and Random Reward Benefit:** Evaluating Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and R1-Distill-Llama-8B performance under clipped random-reward training (Figure 4) to test the reward-misalignment hypothesis across models of varying capabilities.
 - **Policy Skewness Measurement:** Empirical evaluation of the Φ(π) measure for actual Qwen-Math-7B policies to contextualize theoretical predictions about entropy growth (Figure 8).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several findings regarding the dynamics of clipping, policy entropy, and spurious rewards in RLVR:

- **Clipping Bias Does Not Constitute a Meaningful Learning Signal:**
 - **Theoretical Proof:** The theoretical analysis in Section 3.1 and Corollary 3.6 demonstrates that the magnitude of the raw surrogate sum (`N_raw`) significantly overshadows the upper-clipping correction term (`C_tot+`) under typical training hyperparameters. This suggests that the clipping bias itself does not provide a substantial directional signal for learning.
 - **Empirical Evidence:** Experiments with Qwen2.5-Math-7B showed that disabling clipping often led to performance improvements or comparable performance compared to clipped training (Figure 1). Ablation studies with stricter clipping thresholds also did not consistently degrade performance (Figure 5). This evidence challenges the prior hypothesis that upper-clipping bias directly drives performance gains from spurious rewards.

- **Clipping Functions as an Implicit Entropy Minimization Mechanism and Regularizer:**
 - **Theoretical Link:** The paper's novel one-step policy-entropy shift formulation (Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 4.3) establishes a direct, deterministic link: clipping systematically reduces policy entropy, pushing the model towards more confident and deterministic outputs.
 - **Empirical Confirmation:** Clipped training consistently resulted in a monotonic decrease in policy entropy over time, whereas unclipped training often led to an increase in entropy (Figure 2, Left & Middle).
 - **Role in Stability:** Clipping acts as a regularization mechanism by limiting per-token likelihood ratios, which constrains the update step size and prevents the policy from drifting too far from its previous distribution. This also functions to prevent gradient explosion, a critical safeguard for larger models or longer rollouts, as demonstrated by the catastrophic training collapse observed in an R1-Distill-Llama-8B model when clipping was disabled (Figure 2, Right).

- **Policy Entropy and Performance Exhibit No Direct Causal Relationship:**
 - **Entropy Reduction Not Always Beneficial:** While clipping implicitly reduces entropy, this reduction does not uniformly translate to performance gains. On a more challenging dataset (AIME), a weaker model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) showed that entropy minimization through clipping could lead to performance stagnation or degradation, even as entropy decreased (Figure 3, Right). This suggests that lower entropy does not guarantee improved accuracy.
 - **Entropy Increase Can Coincide with Gains:** Conversely, in some unclipped training scenarios, performance improvements were observed alongside increases in policy entropy (Figure 7, Right). This reflects a stronger exploration capacity that can be beneficial.
 - **Regime-Dependent Effectiveness:** The effectiveness of entropy minimization appears to be dependent on the model's strength and the dataset's difficulty. For strong models on easier data, concentrating probability mass on correct trajectories can be advantageous. However, for weaker models or harder data, it might reinforce incorrect reasoning patterns, hindering progress.

- **Spurious Rewards Benefit Stronger Models Beyond Contamination Effects:**
 - **Reward-Misalignment Model Validation:** The proposed reward-misalignment model explains that models with a higher baseline accuracy (i.e., more correct rollouts `n_c`) experience less "advantage loss" and more stable training dynamics under random rewards (Proposition 5.2, Theorem 5.3). This suggests that stronger models are inherently more likely to benefit.
 - **Cross-Model Generalization:** Empirical results demonstrated that performance improvements under random rewards are not exclusive to potentially contaminated Qwen-Math models. Stronger models from the Llama and QwQ families (R1-Distill-Llama-8B, QwQ-32B) exhibited similar or greater gains, even on harder datasets like AIME (Figure 3, Left & Middle-L; Figure 4, Middle). In contrast, a weaker Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B model showed less improvement (Figure 4, Left). This finding refutes the prevailing view that contamination is the primary or sole explanation for spurious reward benefits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a more nuanced understanding of the complex interplay between exploration, exploitation, clipping, and entropy within the RLVR framework for LLMs, challenging several established interpretations and offering new insights.

- **Clarification of RLVR Mechanics:** The paper disentangles previously ambiguous relationships, particularly regarding the role of clipping and entropy in RLVR training. It establishes that clipping primarily serves as a regularization and stability mechanism rather than a source of direct learning signal from spurious rewards.
- **Reconciliation of Conflicting Observations:** By theoretically and empirically demonstrating that spurious rewards can modulate entropy and that their benefits depend on model strength and dataset difficulty, the work helps reconcile conflicting findings in the literature about the effectiveness of spurious rewards and entropy minimization.
- **Overturning Previous Hypotheses:** The findings directly challenge the prevailing view that gains from spurious rewards are mainly attributable to upper-clipping bias or are confined to models affected by data contamination. This broadens the understanding of when and why random rewards can be effective.
- **Informed RLVR Training Strategies:** The insights offer guiding principles for future RLVR training:
 - Clipping should be viewed as a stability-enhancing regularizer rather than a mechanism for extracting learning signals from noisy rewards.
 - Entropy minimization, while implicitly occurring with clipping, is not a universal guarantor of performance improvement; its utility is context-dependent.
 - Spurious rewards can be leveraged to modulate policy entropy and foster exploration or concentration, particularly for stronger models, suggesting a more active role for these signals in balancing exploration-exploitation.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens avenues for further research into combining true and spurious rewards more effectively to achieve a better balance between exploration and exploitation in RLVR, and exploring adaptive entropy modulation techniques in settings where policies become highly skewed.
