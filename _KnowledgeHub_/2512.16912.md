# Exploration vs Exploitation: Rethinking RLVR through Clipping, Entropy, and Spurious Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper investigates the dynamics of clipping, policy entropy, and spurious rewards within Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models. It establishes that clipping acts as an implicit entropy minimizer and regularizer for stability, while spurious rewards can benefit stronger models beyond data contamination, challenging prior interpretations of exploration-exploitation in this framework.

## Problem

- A puzzling paradox in RLVR where both spurious rewards (hindering exploitation) and entropy minimization (reducing exploration) have been observed to improve LLM reasoning performance.
- Conflicting explanations for the benefits of spurious rewards, with prior work attributing gains to PPO-style clipping bias or model contamination, and inconsistent reports on their generalizability.
- The direct causal relationship between policy entropy changes and LLM reasoning performance in RLVR was not definitively established.

## Method

- Conducted theoretical analyses on ==upper-clipping bias== within GRPO and introduced a novel formulation for the one-step policy-entropy shift to understand entropy dynamics under clipped and unclipped training.
- Proposed a probabilistic reward-misalignment model to explain the potential benefits of spurious rewards, quantifying 'correct-response advantage loss' and its dependence on correct rollouts.
- Performed extensive empirical evaluations across multiple LLM families (Qwen2.5-Math, R1-Distill-Llama, QwQ-32B) and datasets (DeepScaleR, MATH500, AIME Past) to validate theoretical findings.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis and empirical tests demonstrated that the raw surrogate sum's magnitude significantly overshadows the clipping correction term, indicating that clipping bias does not provide a meaningful learning signal.
- Clipped training consistently led to a monotonic decrease in policy entropy and prevented gradient explosions, confirming its role as an implicit entropy minimizer and crucial regularizer for training stability.
- Performance improvements under random rewards were observed in stronger models (e.g., Llama, QwQ families) even on challenging datasets, supporting the reward-misalignment model and refuting contamination as the sole explanation for their benefits.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Clipping in RLVR primarily functions as an implicit entropy minimization mechanism and a regularizer, enhancing training stability rather than providing a direct learning signal from spurious rewards.
> - The effectiveness of policy entropy minimization for performance is not universal; its benefits are context-dependent and influenced by model strength and task difficulty.
> - Spurious rewards can enhance the performance of stronger LLMs in RLVR by modulating entropy, and their benefits extend beyond effects solely attributable to data contamination or clipping bias.
