# Spatial-Interactor: Learning Spatial Reasoning through Interaction with the Observable Physical World

> [!summary] Summary
> Spatial-Interactor post-trains vision-language models to model physical-world state transitions from interaction trajectories. A three-level curriculum (passive world-state, active self-state, long-horizon) backs LSI-108K, 108K verifiable QA pairs; supervised fine-tuning learns local transitions and on-policy distillation from a privileged-trace teacher learns long-horizon integration, lifting overall spatial-benchmark scores 16.4-25.0 points over base models.

## Problem

- Existing spatial training is dominated by static QA about object attributes and relations, giving little direct supervision for how spatial states change over time.
- Frame shuffling barely hurts base VLM accuracy, only 0.8-0.9 points, showing reliance on order-invariant visual cues instead of modeled local state transitions.
- Even when local interaction is perceived, integration over long trajectories collapses: camera-displacement scores fall far below local-task scores for open and proprietary models alike.

## Method

- ==Three-level curriculum==: L1 passive world-state changes, L2 active ego-motion self-state changes, L3 long-horizon trajectory integration, instantiated as **LSI-108K**, 108K template-rendered verifiable QA pairs.
- The **LSI-108K** pipeline mines ==observation-action-observation triplets== from simulator interactions plus real camera, scan and robot trajectories, filtered by a frozen visual-language verifier.
- Two-stage training: SFT on L1-L2, then ==On-Policy Distillation== inside GRPO, where a privileged teacher conditioned on segment transition traces supervises student reasoning prefixes via ==masked forward KL==.

## Results

- Overall gains of **16.4-25.0** points across four Qwen backbones; the 8B variant reaches the best overall score of **65.9** and top VSI-Bench, MindCube and VSTI-Bench averages.
- OPD beats matched GRPO, **60.1** versus **59.2** overall on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with **+5.1** Route Planning and **+3.1** Camera Displacement; cross-benchmark transfer gains **4.3-10.6** points.
- Closed-loop success rises from **7.0** to **14.0** on WalkerBench and **32.9** to **41.2** on ESI-Bench, while mean executed actions drop from **17.71** to **6.52**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interaction triplets of observation, action, next observation supply supervision static spatial QA cannot: they bind a visual difference to its physical cause.
> - Temporal order matters more than evidence volume: a shuffled four-segment trace loses most of its benefit, while eight ordered segments barely beat four.
> - Frame-order sensitivity is a cheap diagnostic; a model unhurt by shuffling is exploiting order-invariant cues rather than reasoning about temporal change.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Dynamic spatial reasoning means tracking what changed in a scene, whether because an object moved or the observer's camera moved, and carrying those updates forward while navigating or searching. A VLM that recognizes each view but barely uses the order of views may lose track of where it is. In the authors' VSTI-Bench diagnostic, shuffling the same 32 video frames reduced Base Qwen2.5-VL-3B and -7B accuracy by only 0.9 and 0.8 points. The authors interpret this as weak sensitivity to temporal order, not proof that these models ignore time in every setting.

The proposed fix works in two steps. First, the model learns from an observation, the action or change between views, and the next observation. Then it learns to connect those small changes across a route. The resulting model is called Spatial-Interactor. For Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the paper's Overall score rises from 35.1 for Base to 60.1 for Spatial-Interactor, a 25.0-point gain.

Right: a local SAT-Real interaction task contrasted with long-horizon VSTI-Bench Camera Displacement, including GPT-5.5 with and without added self-generated segment descriptions. ### 2. Teach the change, then the chain

Training starts from a local record: an observation, an intervening action or physical event, and the following observation. The curriculum has three levels:

- **L1** attributes changes to the external world under an approximately stable viewpoint. These changes include object movement, state changes, visibility, and operations. Multi-step object operations belong to L1.
- **L2** attributes visual changes to camera motion and its consequences for viewpoint.
- **L3** combines ordered local transitions across a complete trajectory.

The dataset, LSI-108K, contains 107,518 verified QA pairs sourced from simulated and real interactions. Targets are derived from action, pose, or trajectory metadata. Filtering removes negligible, discontinuous, ambiguous, or visually unanswerable examples.

The closest prior work, [[2412.07755|SAT: Dynamic Spatial Aptitude Training for Multimodal Language Models]], already used simulated scenes to train on static relations and dynamic ego- and object-motion questions. Spatial-Interactor differs in three ways. It organizes before/action/after transitions into local and trajectory-level supervision. It incorporates measured real camera and robot trajectories. It also uses privileged process supervision for long-horizon reasoning.

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) trains the local L1/L2 transitions. The next stage, On-Policy Distillation (OPD), uses a frozen annotator's question-independent descriptions of four ordered video intervals as a training-only trace. A teacher branch sees the trace and scores the same student-generated reasoning prefixes as the student. Answer rewards supervise the final answer, while distillation supervises reasoning tokens. The released model does not receive the trace at inference.

Overview of OPD. A 32-frame video is divided into intervals, and a frozen annotator's ordered descriptions form the privileged trace. The student and the trace-conditioned teacher evaluate the same student-generated prefixes. Answer reward and reasoning-token process loss feed a joint objective.

### 3. Does the chain help?

Overall is the average of four benchmark averages: VSI-Bench, MindCube, VSTI-Bench, and SPBench-MV. Their official protocols use accuracy for categorical questions and MRA for numerical questions. Gains over the corresponding Base models range from 16.4 to 25.0 points across the four evaluated Qwen backbones.

The ablation on Qwen2.5-VL-7B tests each stage. Adding interaction-derived L1/L2 examples to the external-data SFT raises Overall from 56.0 to 58.4. From that Full-SFT checkpoint, matched GRPO reaches 59.2 and OPD reaches 60.1. Both runs share the same initialization, L3 questions, rollout groups, answer rewards, and optimization schedule. Under those conditions, OPD exceeds matched GRPO by 5.1 points on VSI-Bench Route Planning and 3.1 on VSTI-Bench Camera Displacement.

This result has two limits. OPD adds privileged conditioning and process distillation together, so the comparison does not identify either as the sole cause. It is also a comparison of benchmark scores, not elapsed time, token use, or number of attempts.

Ablation results on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, official scores (%). Overall for Base, External-data SFT, Full SFT, Full SFT + GRPO, and Full SFT + OPD is 35.1, 56.0, 58.4, 59.2, and 60.1. Route Planning is 28.9 for GRPO and 34.0 for OPD; Camera Displacement is 33.5 and 36.6.

### 4. Spatial state, carried forward

Recognizing a view is not the same as keeping an updated account of the world while moving. Spatial-Interactor's contribution is to make the intervening change, and its place in a sequence, the thing training teaches. The controlled comparison and measured gains are consistent with that design helping spatial reasoning. They are not evidence of improved real-world deployment in general.
