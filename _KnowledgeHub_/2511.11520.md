# Scalable Policy Evaluation with Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework for robot policy evaluation that utilizes action-conditional video generation models as world models. The approach demonstrates strong correlations between predicted and actual policy performance in both synthetic and real-world settings, offering a scalable alternative to costly physical testing and traditional simulations.

## Problem

- Robot policy evaluation faces high costs, time consumption, and safety risks with real-world testing, alongside limited reproducibility for complex generalist policies.
- Traditional simulation methods demand extensive manual engineering, struggle with the "sim-to-real gap" in physical and visual fidelity, and lack scalability for diverse tasks.
- A need exists for a scalable and reproducible evaluation system that can efficiently assess increasingly complex and diverse robot manipulation policies.

## Method

- Leveraging ==action-conditional video generation models==, adapted from large-scale pre-trained ==diffusion models==, to function as interactive world models for predicting environment dynamics.
- Employing an automated pipeline where policies are rolled out ==autoregressively== within the video world model, and off-the-shelf ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== assess task success from generated videos.
- Augmenting training datasets with diverse policy rollouts and incorporating ==Fourier feature mappings== and ==MLPs== to enable action conditioning in pre-trained video diffusion architectures.

## Results

- Achieved **Pearson correlation 0.833–0.879** and **MMRV 0.015–0.217** for policy ranking in various synthetic ==RoboMimic== tasks.
- Demonstrated improved video prediction quality (e.g., **PSNR 18.712 → 20.622** for in-distribution data with rollouts) and evaluation accuracy through data augmentation and pre-trained weights.
- Exhibited a **Pearson r=0.687** and **MMRV 0.171** with actual real-world policy performance when evaluating generalist policies on the **Bridge dataset**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained internet-scale video models, combined with augmented policy rollout data, are crucial for building effective video world models that generalize well and provide accurate policy evaluations.
> - The proposed video-based world model framework reliably correlates with actual policy performance (e.g., Pearson up to 0.879 in synthetic, 0.687 in real-world), offering a viable scalable evaluation tool.
> - Systematic analysis identifies common failure modes like hallucinations, multi-view inconsistency, and VLM annotation errors, highlighting critical areas for future improvements in video-based simulators.
