# Scalable Policy Evaluation with Video World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a framework for robot policy evaluation that utilizes action-conditional video generation models as world models. The approach demonstrates strong correlations between predicted and actual policy performance in both synthetic and real-world settings, offering a scalable alternative to costly physical testing and traditional simulations.

## Problem

- Robot policy evaluation faces high costs, time consumption, and safety risks with real-world testing, alongside limited reproducibility for complex generalist policies.
- Traditional simulation methods demand extensive manual engineering, struggle with the "sim-to-real gap" in physical and visual fidelity, and lack scalability for diverse tasks.
- A need exists for a scalable and reproducible evaluation system that can efficiently assess increasingly complex and diverse robot manipulation policies.

## Method

- Leveraging ==action-conditional video generation models==, adapted from large-scale pre-trained ==diffusion models==, to function as interactive world models for predicting environment dynamics.
- Employing an automated pipeline where policies are rolled out ==autoregressively== within the video world model, and off-the-shelf ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== assess task success from generated videos.
- Augmenting training datasets with diverse policy rollouts and incorporating ==Fourier feature mappings== and ==MLPs== to enable action conditioning in pre-trained video diffusion architectures.

## Results

- Achieved **Pearson correlation 0.833–0.879** and **MMRV 0.015–0.217** for policy ranking in various synthetic ==RoboMimic== tasks.
- Demonstrated improved video prediction quality (e.g., **PSNR 18.712 → 20.622** for in-distribution data with rollouts) and evaluation accuracy through data augmentation and pre-trained weights.
- Exhibited a **Pearson r=0.687** and **MMRV 0.171** with actual real-world policy performance when evaluating generalist policies on the **Bridge dataset**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained internet-scale video models, combined with augmented policy rollout data, are crucial for building effective video world models that generalize well and provide accurate policy evaluations.
> - The proposed video-based world model framework reliably correlates with actual policy performance (e.g., Pearson up to 0.879 in synthetic, 0.687 in real-world), offering a viable scalable evaluation tool.
> - Systematic analysis identifies common failure modes like hallucinations, multi-view inconsistency, and VLM annotation errors, highlighting critical areas for future improvements in video-based simulators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning has made progress in developing generalist robot manipulation policies capable of understanding natural language instructions and executing diverse tasks across various environments and robot embodiments. However, a significant challenge remains in systematically and efficiently evaluating these increasingly complex policies at scale.

Current evaluation methodologies for robot policies largely fall into two categories: real-world testing and simulation-based approaches. Real-world testing, while providing ground-truth performance, is characterized by high costs, extensive infrastructure requirements, time consumption, labor intensiveness, safety risks, and limited reproducibility due to the need for frequent environment resets. Simulation-based methods aim to address these issues but often require substantial manual engineering effort to create realistic 3D models and environments, perform reward engineering, and apply domain randomization. Despite these efforts, a substantial "sim-to-real gap" persists, both in terms of physical accuracy and visual fidelity, which limits the transferability of simulation-based evaluations to real-world performance.

Recent advancements in action-conditional video generation models have presented a potential avenue for addressing these limitations by enabling the creation of "world models." These models learn to predict future frames given current observations and actions, offering a data-driven simulation framework. Previous work has explored their use in entertainment, planning, and reinforcement learning. Specifically, models like SIMPLER attempted to construct physical simulators from real-world data but still relied on extensive domain randomization and manually curated 3D data. AutoEval focused on autonomous real-world evaluation, which still inherits the challenges of physical interaction. WorldEval proposed using video models for evaluation but required specific feature conditioning from action decoders, limiting its general applicability to new policies.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing to leverage action-conditional video generation models as scalable world models for general robot policy evaluation. It aims to overcome the limitations of both real-world testing and traditional simulations by learning directly from real-world data and generating interactive, visually rich environments without manual engineering. A key aspect of this work is adapting pre-trained, internet-scale video generation models for robotic tasks by incorporating action conditioning, thereby reducing the need for extensive, expensive paired video-action datasets. The work systematically investigates the factors influencing the reliability of such video models for policy evaluation, including dataset diversity, pre-trained weights, and common failure modes, thus contributing to the understanding and development of data-driven simulators for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and evaluate a scalable, fully automated framework for robot policy evaluation that utilizes action-conditional video generation models as world models. The motivation stems from several critical limitations in current robot policy evaluation paradigms:

1. **High Cost and Inefficiency of Real-World Testing:** As generalist robot manipulation policies become more sophisticated and support a wider array of tasks, evaluating them through real-world deployment becomes prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. Real-world rollouts necessitate substantial data collection and annotation, significant robotic infrastructure, and frequent environment resets. Furthermore, deploying unproven policies on physical robots carries inherent safety risks and limits reproducibility. The cumulative effect of these factors hinders rapid iteration and development cycles for new policies.

2. **Limitations of Traditional Simulation:** While simulations offer an alternative to real-world testing, they introduce their own set of challenges. Manually creating and populating realistic simulation environments with 3D assets is an intensive engineering effort. More significantly, bridging the "sim-to-real gap" in terms of physics accuracy and visual fidelity remains a persistent hurdle. Efforts like careful 3D modeling, reward engineering, and domain randomization often fall short, resulting in simulations that do not accurately reflect real-world dynamics, thereby compromising the reliability of policy evaluations performed solely in simulation.

3. **Need for Scalability and Reproducibility:** The increasing diversity and complexity of robot policies and tasks demand an evaluation system that is scalable—meaning it can accommodate a vast range of scenarios without a linear increase in manual effort—and reproducible, ensuring consistent results for comparative analysis. Current methods often struggle with these aspects.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the paper include:

- **Incorporating Action Conditioning into Pre-trained Video Models:** To adapt large, internet-scale pre-trained video generation models, which are not inherently designed for action conditioning, into interactive world models suitable for robotics. This involves methods like adding action embeddings to time embeddings using Fourier feature mappings and MLPs.
- **Leveraging Internet-Scale Data:** To utilize the knowledge encoded in pre-trained models from internet-scale, "in-the-wild" online video data, thereby alleviating the need for a large, expensive-to-collect dataset of paired video-action data specifically for robotic manipulation.
- **Automated Task Success Assessment:** To employ off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to automatically assess task completion from generated rollout videos, eliminating the need for human intervention and manual annotation during evaluation.
- **Systematic Investigation of Design Factors:** To systematically analyze the impact of key factors on the performance of action-conditional video prediction models for policy evaluation. This includes examining the effect of dataset diversity (e.g., through policy rollout augmentation), the benefits of pre-trained weights, and the influence of different video model backbones.
- **Identification of Failure Modes:** To identify and characterize common failure cases (e.g., hallucinations, multi-view inconsistency, replicated predictions, VLM annotation errors) in video models that may affect the reliability and accuracy of policy evaluation, providing insights for future research.
- **Correlation with Real-World Performance:** To demonstrate a strong correlation between policy performance estimated by the video world model and actual performance observed in both synthetic and real-world environments, validating the proposed framework's reliability.

In essence, the paper seeks to establish action-conditional video generation as a viable, scalable, and data-driven paradigm for robot policy evaluation, bridging the gap between expensive real-world testing and inaccurate traditional simulations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on leveraging action-conditional video generation models as world models for scalable robot policy evaluation. The approach involves three main components: defining policy evaluation metrics, learning action-conditional video prediction models, and augmenting training data.

**A. Evaluating Robot Policies**
The goal is to construct a world simulator (S) such that the relative performance of policies in the simulator ($R_{S,i}$, $R_{S,j}$) strongly correlates with their real-world cumulative performances ($R_i$, $R_j$).

- **Metrics:** The primary metrics used are the Pearson correlation coefficient and Mean Maximum Rank Violation (MMRV), adapted from existing work.
 - **MMRV:** Quantifies the extent to which the world model misranks policies relative to their true performance margins. It calculates the absolute difference in real-world performance between two policies, weighted by an indicator function that is 1 if their ranking in simulation contradicts their real-world ranking. MMRV aggregates these violations by computing the average worst-case rank violation for each policy. A lower MMRV indicates better ranking alignment.
 - **Pearson Correlation Coefficient:** Measures the linear correlation between the predicted policy success rates and the actual policy success rates. A higher coefficient indicates better alignment.
- **Policy Rollout and Assessment:** Once an action-conditional video model is trained, a policy is "rolled out" autoregressively by repeatedly interacting with the model. The policy receives an initial frame, executes an action, and the model predicts the next frame. This predicted frame then becomes the new observation for the policy to determine its next action, simulating an interaction trajectory. After a simulated rollout, an off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM) is used to automatically assess task completion by analyzing subsampled frames from the generated video with a specific prompt. This eliminates the need for human annotation of success or failure.

**B. Learning Action-conditional Video Prediction Models**
The core of the simulator is an action-conditional video prediction model that learns the environment dynamics $p(o_{t+1} | o_{1:t}, a_{1:t})$, predicting the next observation ($o_{t+1}$) given past observations ($o_{1:t}$) and executed actions ($a_{1:t}$).

- **Task-Agnostic Approach:** This approach is designed to be task-agnostic, meaning the model learns general dynamics from video frames, enabling knowledge transfer across multiple tasks sharing the same observation space.
- **Model Architecture:** The chosen backbone is the open-sourced pre-trained video diffusion model, Cosmos-Predict2-Video2World-2B (Cosmos), which is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). This model has 2 billion trainable parameters and is pre-trained on large-scale text-to-video and image-to-video datasets.
- **Action Conditioning:** Cosmos models are not intrinsically designed for action conditioning. To introduce this capability, raw action signals (robot end-effector poses) are first processed using Fourier feature mappings to enable the network to learn high-frequency functions from low-dimensional inputs. The mapped features are then processed through a small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to generate action embeddings. These action embeddings are subsequently added to the time embeddings within the Diffusion Transformer architecture, allowing the video diffusion model to become action-conditional. This modification is designed to have minimal impact on inference and training efficiency.
- **Training:** The entire framework, including the modified Cosmos architecture, is trained end-to-end using the EDM (Elucidating the Design Space of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) objective, following the setup used for the original Cosmos models.

**C. Policy Rollout Data Augmentation**
To enhance the generalization capability of the action-conditional video prediction model, the training dataset of human demonstration trajectories is augmented.

- **Problem with Demonstration Data:** Human demonstration trajectories often represent successful task completions and may have a limited distribution of actions or failure modes. This can hinder the video model's ability to accurately predict outcomes for policies that exhibit diverse behaviors or make mistakes.
- **Augmentation Strategy:** The demonstration dataset is augmented with rollouts from various policies with different manipulation performances. These policy rollouts introduce a broader spectrum of behaviors, including both successful and unsuccessful trajectories, which is critical for training a world model that can reliably evaluate a range of policies. The policies used for generating these augmentation rollouts are distinct from those evaluated in later experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both synthetic and real-world settings, investigating various aspects of the proposed policy evaluation pipeline.

**A. Synthetic Setting**
The approach was first tested in a controllable synthetic environment using four tasks from RoboMimic: Lift (simplest), Can, Square, and Tool Hang (most complex). The video model was trained on a dataset combining human demonstrations and policy rollouts. Policy evaluation utilized policies of varying quality (CNN- and transformer-based, trained for different steps).

- **Overall Policy Evaluation Performance:** The proposed approach, using Cosmos-Predict2-2B, demonstrated sufficient accuracy in providing reliable policy ranking signals. Pearson correlation coefficients ranged from 0.833 (Tool Hang) to 0.879 (Lift), with MMRV values between 0.015 and 0.217.
- **Task Complexity Impact:** Correlation generally appeared weaker for long-horizon tasks (e.g., Tool Hang and Square) compared to shorter ones (e.g., Lift and Can), suggesting accumulated errors in longer rollouts might affect evaluation accuracy.

**B. Ablation Study**
A systematic study examined the effects of data augmentation, pre-trained weights, and different model backbones.

- **1) Effectiveness of Data Augmentation (Policy Rollouts):**
 - **Video Prediction Quality:** Adding policy rollouts significantly improved video prediction performance, especially for out-of-distribution (OOD) data. For instance, PSNR increased from 18.712 to 20.622 for in-distribution data and from 14.931 to 15.996 for OOD data when rollouts were included. Models without rollouts exhibited more frequent visual artifacts, such as disappearing grippers.
 - **Policy Evaluation Accuracy:** Excluding policy rollouts led to a sharp decrease in the correlation between actual and predicted policy performance, indicating that diverse rollouts are crucial for the video model to accurately evaluate policies.
 - **Quantity of Rollouts:** Increasing the number of policy rollouts in the training set consistently enhanced policy evaluation accuracy, with a more pronounced effect on harder tasks like Square compared to simpler ones like Lift.
- **2) Effectiveness of Pre-trained Weights:**
 - **Video Prediction Quality:** Removing pre-trained weights from Cosmos-Predict2-Video2World-2B resulted in a substantial drop in video prediction quality for both in-distribution and OOD data (e.g., PSNR 17.731 vs. 20.622 in-distribution). Models trained from scratch showed more frequent distortions, hallucinations, and a lack of physical plausibility (e.g., object disappearance during interaction).
 - **Policy Evaluation Accuracy:** Pre-trained priors significantly improved policy evaluation accuracy, suggesting that large-scale pre-training provides crucial knowledge about physical dynamics that is difficult to learn from limited demonstration data alone.
- **3) Different Pre-trained Models:**
 - **Performance Comparison:** Cosmos-Predict2-2B, pre-trained on physical-AI data, consistently achieved the best policy evaluation performance. Other models like Wan-2.1-14B-LoRA (pre-trained on general video data) performed less effectively, and IRASim (trained entirely from scratch) performed the worst. This highlights the importance of both pre-training and domain-specific alignment of the pre-training dataset for effective policy evaluation.

**C. Policy Evaluation in Real-World Setup**
The approach was tested for generalization to real-world environments and generalist policies using tasks from the Bridge dataset (e.g., lifting a carrot, pot, eggplant, cup). OCTO-Small, OCTO-Base, and OpenVLA policies were evaluated.

- **Correlation with Real-World Performance:** The world model demonstrated a positive correlation between predicted policy performance and actual real-world performance (Pearson correlation of 0.687 for Cosmos vs. 0.613 for IRASim, with MMRV of 0.171 vs. 0.611). This indicates the video model's capability to serve as an effective evaluation environment for real-world policies.

**D. Failure Case Analysis**
Despite promising results, several limitations and failure modes were identified:

- **Hallucinations:** Inconsistent object behavior and lack of object permanence (e.g., disappearing object handles, hallucinating objects into grippers) were observed. These artifacts can bias policy evaluation, and errors accumulate autoregressively, particularly in long-horizon tasks (e.g., Tool Hang with 700 transitions).
- **Multi-View Inconsistency:** The model, which takes two concatenated views (gripper and agent) as input, sometimes generated inconsistent predictions across these viewpoints, where the same object might appear differently or be missing in one view. This is attributed to the absence of an explicit view-consistency loss during training.
- **Replicated Prediction:** For very small gripper displacements, the diffusion model occasionally replicated the conditional image as the predicted image, leading to a static environment. This prevents policy progress and is more common in tasks requiring precise, fine-grained motions, such as Tool Hang.
- **VLM Annotation Errors:** The automated task completion judgments by VLMs were not perfect, achieving only 65–80% accuracy. These errors can propagate and affect the overall reliability of the evaluation pipeline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant step towards developing scalable and efficient evaluation pipelines for robot policies, particularly for generalist manipulation. The key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Addressing Core Evaluation Challenges:** The proposed framework directly tackles the long-standing problems of expensive real-world testing and the persistent sim-to-real gap in traditional simulation. By using action-conditional video models, it offers a data-driven alternative that learns directly from real-world data, thereby capturing complex visual and dynamic properties more effectively than handcrafted simulations. This could significantly reduce the reliance on costly physical robotic infrastructure and extensive manual engineering efforts.

2. **Enabling Scalable and Reproducible Evaluation:** The automated nature of the video-model-based evaluation, combined with VLM-based task success assessment, allows for highly scalable evaluation across diverse policies and tasks. Unlike real-world testing, it ensures reproducibility, which is crucial for benchmarking algorithmic progress and conducting consistent comparative studies. This scalability is vital as robot policies become more general and complex.

3. **Leveraging Large-Scale Pre-training:** The work demonstrates the substantial benefits of leveraging pre-trained, internet-scale video generation models. By adapting these models with action conditioning, the framework capitalizes on vast amounts of "in-the-wild" video data, mitigating the need for prohibitively expensive, large-scale, paired video-action datasets specifically for robotics. This indicates a promising path for transferring knowledge from general video understanding to embodied AI.

4. **Insights into World Model Design:** The systematic ablation studies provide valuable insights into the factors critical for effective video world models in robotics. The findings underscore the importance of diverse training data (including policy rollouts) and, particularly, the role of pre-trained weights in instilling physical priors and improving generalization. The comparative analysis of different pre-trained backbones further guides future model selection and development by highlighting the benefit of domain-aligned pre-training.

5. **Accelerating Generalist Robotics Research:** By offering a more accessible, faster, and cheaper evaluation method, this research has the potential to accelerate the development and iteration cycles of generalist robot policies. Researchers can quickly test new policy variations and algorithms without constantly deploying them on physical robots, fostering innovation and progress in the field.

6. **Highlighting Future Research Directions:** The detailed failure case analysis, including hallucinations, multi-view inconsistency, replicated predictions, and VLM annotation errors, not only acknowledges the current limitations but also clearly delineates specific areas for future research and improvement. Addressing these issues will be critical for enhancing the robustness and reliability of video-based world models, pushing them closer to becoming fully reliable simulators for real-world robotic applications.

In conclusion, this research establishes action-conditional video prediction models as a viable and promising foundation for data-driven evaluation pipelines in robotics, contributing to the broader goal of developing more capable and generalist robotic systems.
