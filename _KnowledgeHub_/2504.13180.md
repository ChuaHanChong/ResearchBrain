# PerceptionLM: Open-Access Data and Models for Detailed Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> The PerceptionLM project introduces open-access Vision-Language Models, comprehensive datasets, and a benchmark for detailed image and video understanding, developed collaboratively by Meta FAIR and academic partners. This initiative provides a fully reproducible framework, achieving competitive performance without relying on proprietary models, and notably excels in fine-grained spatio-temporal video reasoning.

## Problem

- The Vision-Language Model (VLM) research landscape is dominated by high-performing, closed-source models from proprietary entities, hindering scientific understanding, reproducibility, and open innovation.
- Existing synthetic data and benchmarks fall short in fostering detailed video understanding, especially for fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning, often relying on 'image-centric' approaches.
- The reliance on 'distillation from black-box models' further entrenches dependence on opaque systems, impeding fundamental knowledge about VLM training from scratch.

## Method

- Developed ==PerceptionLM==, an open-access VLM framework leveraging Llama 3 as the language decoder and a pre-trained Perception Encoder, trained through a three-stage process including projector warm-up, large-scale synthetic data training, and supervised fine-tuning.
- Created an open-source data engine to generate a vast synthetic dataset (**66.1M** samples) for general VLM capabilities across images and videos, enabling scaling law analysis.
- Introduced two novel, large-scale, human-annotated datasets: ==PLM–FGQA== (**2.4M** fine-grained QA pairs) and ==PLM–STC== (**476.2K** spatio-temporal captions), specifically designed to enhance detailed video understanding, alongside the new PLM–VideoBench for evaluation.

## Results

- PerceptionLM-8B achieved strong and competitive performance across a wide range of **40** image and video benchmarks, often matching or exceeding other open-access models and, in specific cases, proprietary models.
- PLM-8B demonstrated substantial outperformance on the newly introduced PLM–VideoBench, notably excelling in spatio-temporal tasks like RDCap (**52.8** SODA† compared to GPT-4o's **20.9**), largely closing the gap with human performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the custom human-annotated data (PLM-STC and PLM-FGQA) provided significant performance boosts specifically for spatio-temporal tasks (**+27.4 points**) and fine-grained activity understanding (**+13.1 points**), addressing limitations of synthetic data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fully open and reproducible VLM development paradigm can achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art open-weight models without relying on proprietary model distillation.
> - While synthetic data scales effectively for established VLM tasks like OCR and general QA, its efficacy significantly diminishes for 'challenging' video tasks requiring fine-grained spatio-temporal understanding.
> - Targeted, high-quality human-annotated datasets are crucial for bridging the performance gap in complex video reasoning, enabling models to grasp nuanced spatio-temporal dynamics beyond what synthetic data provides.
