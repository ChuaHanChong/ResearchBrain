# PerceptionLM: Open-Access Data and Models for Detailed Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> The PerceptionLM project introduces open-access Vision-Language Models, comprehensive datasets, and a benchmark for detailed image and video understanding, developed collaboratively by Meta FAIR and academic partners. This initiative provides a fully reproducible framework, achieving competitive performance without relying on proprietary models, and notably excels in fine-grained spatio-temporal video reasoning.

## Problem

- The Vision-Language Model (VLM) research landscape is dominated by high-performing, closed-source models from proprietary entities, hindering scientific understanding, reproducibility, and open innovation.
- Existing synthetic data and benchmarks fall short in fostering detailed video understanding, especially for fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning, often relying on 'image-centric' approaches.
- The reliance on 'distillation from black-box models' further entrenches dependence on opaque systems, impeding fundamental knowledge about VLM training from scratch.

## Method

- Developed ==PerceptionLM==, an open-access VLM framework leveraging Llama 3 as the language decoder and a pre-trained Perception Encoder, trained through a three-stage process including projector warm-up, large-scale synthetic data training, and supervised fine-tuning.
- Created an open-source data engine to generate a vast synthetic dataset (**66.1M** samples) for general VLM capabilities across images and videos, enabling scaling law analysis.
- Introduced two novel, large-scale, human-annotated datasets: ==PLM–FGQA== (**2.4M** fine-grained QA pairs) and ==PLM–STC== (**476.2K** spatio-temporal captions), specifically designed to enhance detailed video understanding, alongside the new PLM–VideoBench for evaluation.

## Results

- PerceptionLM-8B achieved strong and competitive performance across a wide range of **40** image and video benchmarks, often matching or exceeding other open-access models and, in specific cases, proprietary models.
- PLM-8B demonstrated substantial outperformance on the newly introduced PLM–VideoBench, notably excelling in spatio-temporal tasks like RDCap (**52.8** SODA† compared to GPT-4o's **20.9**), largely closing the gap with human performance.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the custom human-annotated data (PLM-STC and PLM-FGQA) provided significant performance boosts specifically for spatio-temporal tasks (**+27.4 points**) and fine-grained activity understanding (**+13.1 points**), addressing limitations of synthetic data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fully open and reproducible VLM development paradigm can achieve competitive performance against state-of-the-art open-weight models without relying on proprietary model distillation.
> - While synthetic data scales effectively for established VLM tasks like OCR and general QA, its efficacy significantly diminishes for 'challenging' video tasks requiring fine-grained spatio-temporal understanding.
> - Targeted, high-quality human-annotated datasets are crucial for bridging the performance gap in complex video reasoning, enabling models to grasp nuanced spatio-temporal dynamics beyond what synthetic data provides.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have become central to computer vision, yet the landscape is increasingly dominated by high-performing, closed-source models from proprietary entities (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini). These models, while powerful, withhold crucial details about their data, design, and training methodologies. This opacity creates significant challenges for scientific progress, as researchers struggle to understand the true drivers of performance improvements—whether they stem from genuine algorithmic advancements, novel architectural designs, or simply proprietary training data that may overlap with evaluation benchmarks.

In response, the academic community has often resorted to "distillation from black-box models," where proprietary models are used to label training data, effectively mimicking their performance. While this approach can yield strong benchmark results, it further entrenches the reliance on opaque systems and hinders the fundamental understanding of VLM training from scratch. Key questions regarding training stages, synthetic data effectiveness, and critical data gaps remain unanswered due to this paradigm.

"PerceptionLM" directly addresses this critical issue by advocating for and providing a fully open and reproducible framework for VLM research. This work positions itself as a counter-narrative to the closed-source trend, emphasizing transparency in data, models, and training recipes. Beyond the general call for openness, the paper identifies a specific and pressing gap: the limitations of existing synthetic data and benchmarks in fostering detailed video understanding, particularly for fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning. Most current video benchmarks remain "image-centric," answerable by a few frames, neglecting the complex temporal and spatial dynamics inherent in genuine video understanding. By focusing on detailed video understanding and releasing new human-annotated data, PerceptionLM aims to push the boundaries of VLM capabilities in a scientifically verifiable manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the PerceptionLM project are multifaceted:

1. **Promote Open and Reproducible Research:** The overarching goal is to foster a transparent research environment for image and video understanding. By releasing open-access data, training recipes, code, and models, the authors aim to enable the scientific community to scrutinize, reproduce, and build upon their work without relying on opaque, proprietary systems.
2. **Analyze Standard VLM Training Pipelines:** The authors explicitly set out to study the efficacy of standard training pipelines *without* distillation from proprietary models. This objective seeks to deconstruct the "black-box" effect and understand the true impact of different training components and data sources on VLM performance.
3. **Identify Critical Data Gaps:** A core motivation is to pinpoint deficiencies in existing datasets, especially concerning detailed video understanding. The research investigates the scaling laws of synthetic data to understand its limitations, particularly for fine-grained spatio-temporal reasoning in videos, where existing data falls short.
4. **Bridge Identified Data Gaps with High-Quality Human Annotations:** Recognizing the shortcomings of synthetic data, the project aims to create and release novel, large-scale, human-labeled datasets. These datasets are specifically designed to provide the rich, fine-grained supervision necessary for advanced video understanding tasks.
5. **Develop a Comprehensive Benchmark for Detailed Video Understanding:** To ensure that progress in fine-grained and spatio-temporal video understanding can be accurately measured, the authors introduce a new benchmark suite, PLM–VideoBench. This benchmark is tailored to evaluate capabilities often neglected by existing, more image-centric video evaluation sets.
6. **Release Competitive Open-Access VLM Models:** The project aims to develop and release VLM models (PerceptionLM) that achieve strong performance comparable to state-of-the-art open-weight models, but crucially, without relying on distillation from proprietary sources. This demonstrates that high-performance can be achieved within an open framework.

In essence, the motivation is to bring scientific rigor and transparency back to the development of powerful VLMs, particularly for the challenging domain of detailed video comprehension, which is critical for many real-world AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of PerceptionLM is characterized by a three-stage training pipeline, a sophisticated synthetic data generation engine, and the creation of two novel human-annotated video datasets, complemented by a new evaluation benchmark.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

PerceptionLM (PLM) comprises a **vision encoder** and a **language decoder**.
- **Vision Encoder:** Utilizes a pre-trained Perception Encoder (PE), specifically PE L/14 for 1B and 3B Llama3 models, and PE G/14 for the 8B Llama3 model.
- **Language Decoder:** Leverages the open-source Llama 3 large language model, in 1B, 3B, or 8B parameter variants.
- **Projector:** A 2-layer MLP connects the vision encoder to the language decoder, facilitating multimodal understanding.
- **Input Handling:** For images, PLM incorporates dynamic tiling, supporting high-resolution inputs (up to 36 tiles of 448x448 pixels). For video, it processes up to 32 frames at 448x448 resolution. In both cases, a 2x2 average pooling step compresses visual tokens before feeding them to the LLM.

#### 3.2. Training Stages

PLM undergoes a three-stage training process:
1. **Projector Warm-up (Stage 1):** The vision encoder and LLM are frozen, and only the MLP projector is trained on 1M synthetic image captions (from SA-1B), initializing its parameters for stability.
2. **Large-scale Midtraining with Synthetic Data (Stage 2):** PLM is trained on a vast and diverse mix of 72.5M samples (66M synthetic + 6.5M existing open-source human data). This stage focuses on general VLM capabilities across image and video domains, using up to 16 tiles for images and 16 frames for videos.
3. **Supervised Fine-tuning with Human-Annotated Data (Stage 3):** This final stage uses 19.1M high-quality samples, including the newly created human-annotated datasets. It employs higher resolutions (up to 36 tiles for images, 32 frames for videos) to tackle more challenging video tasks, such as fine-grained QA and spatio-temporally grounded reasoning.

#### 3.3. Data Generation Strategies

- **Synthetic Data Engine (∼66.1M samples):** The authors developed an entirely open-source data engine, leveraging Llama 3 models, to generate a large volume of annotations.
 - **Image Data Engine:** Produces short/long captions and QA pairs for natural images, documents, charts, and OCR-rich content. Llama 3 is prompted to generate factual, detailed captions, and text-only LLMs use OCR/captions/metadata for QA generation.
 - **Video Data Engine:** Extracts 30-second video clips using a scene detector. Llama 3 generates frame-level captions from keyframes, and an initial PLM generates video captions. An LLM refines these captions with video metadata (action labels, time tags) to create cohesive, detailed video-level captions and corresponding QA pairs.
 - **Scaling Law Analysis:** This extensive synthetic data generation allowed the authors to study scaling laws, revealing that while synthetic data effectively improves performance on established VLM tasks (e.g., OCR, general QA), its efficacy diminishes for "challenging" video tasks requiring fine-grained spatio-temporal understanding.

- **Human-Annotated High-Quality Data (2.8M instances):** To address the limitations of synthetic data, two novel datasets were created:
 - **PLM–FGQA (Fine-Grained Question Answering - 2.4M QA pairs):** This dataset focuses on "what" activities humans perform and "how" they perform them in videos. A multi-stage data engine extracts salient video segments, generates initial questions and answers (using text-only LLMs or early PLM versions), and then human annotators verify and refine these answers. It covers diverse question types like fine-grained recognition, temporal perception, and spatial understanding.
 - **PLM–STC (Spatio-Temporal Captions - 476.2K training samples):** This dataset provides detailed activity descriptions for video regions, including timestamps ("when") and specific subjects identified by masklets ("where"). A two-stage annotation process involves annotators selecting moving objects and refining SAM 2-generated mask tublets, followed by a separate set of annotators writing temporally localized descriptions focused on action changes. This data is converted into three training tasks: RCap (region captioning), RTLoc (region temporal localization), and RDCap (region dense captioning).

#### 3.4. PLM–VideoBench

To evaluate the novel capabilities enabled by the human-annotated data, the authors introduced PLM–VideoBench, a benchmark suite for core, video-centric understanding:
- **FGQA (Fine-Grained QA):** Multiple-choice questions probing nuanced activity understanding (e.g., "vertically" vs. "horizontally").
- **SGQA (Smart Glasses QA):** Open-ended questions about egocentric videos from smart glasses, simulating real-world AI assistance.
- **RCap (Video Region Captioning):** Generating detailed descriptions of events involving a masked subject within a specific time interval.
- **RTLoc (Region Temporal Localization):** Identifying the precise time interval for a described event involving a masked subject.
- **RDCap (Region Dense Video Captioning):** Generating a sequence of (start, end, caption) tuples covering all events for a masked subject across the video duration.

This comprehensive methodology, from open-source LLM-driven synthetic data to meticulously human-annotated video datasets and a tailored benchmark, represents a robust and transparent approach to advancing VLM research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and compelling results that validate the PerceptionLM approach:

#### 4.1. Performance on Image and Video Benchmarks

- **Competitive Open-Access Performance:** PLM demonstrates strong performance across a wide spectrum of 40 image and video benchmarks. Crucially, it achieves this *without distilling from proprietary models* and by relying solely on open-access data and its white-box data engine. PLM-8B shows competitive performance against existing state-of-the-art open-weight models (e.g., InternVL2.5) and significantly outperforms fully open models (e.g., Molmo).
- **Image Benchmarks:** PLM exhibits robust performance across various image tasks, including charts, diagrams, documents (DocVQA, ChartQA, TextVQA, OCRBench), general perception and reasoning (VQAv2, OK-VQA, VizWiz, CV-Bench), and hallucination detection (POPE). For instance, PLM-8B scores 94.6 on DocVQA, 85.5 on ChartQA, and 86.5 on TextVQA, often matching or exceeding other open-access models.
- **Video Benchmarks:** PLM also delivers strong results on widely adopted video benchmarks, covering video captioning (DREAM-1K), general video QA (MVBench, NExT-QA, STAR), fine-grained video QA (EgoSchema, TemporalBench, TOMATO, MotionBench, TempCompass), and hallucination detection (VideoHallucer, EventHallusion). PLM consistently outperforms or is highly competitive with both open-access and proprietary models across different scales. For example, PLM-8B achieves 77.1 on MVBench and 84.1 on NExT-QA. It also shows superior performance in video captioning (35.9 F1† on DREAM-1K) and hallucination detection tasks.
- **Image Grounding:** PLM-3B and PLM-8B outperform specialist grounding models and other VLM baselines on RefCOCO/+/g datasets, indicating strong visual grounding capabilities.

#### 4.2. Scaling Laws with Synthetic Data

- **Clear Power-Law Relationships:** The analysis of synthetic data scaling reveals clear power-law relationships between training compute (FLOPs) and average benchmark errors for Video QA, OCR QA, and Natural Image QA.
- **Diminished Returns for Challenging Tasks:** A critical finding is that while synthetic data scales effectively for established, base VLM tasks (e.g., OCR QA with α=-0.20), its effectiveness significantly wanes for "challenging" video tasks. These tasks, demanding thorough spatio-temporal and fine-grained semantic understanding (e.g., HardQA), show a poor scaling trend (α=-0.03). This indicates that simply scaling synthetic data is insufficient to bridge the gap for complex video reasoning.

#### 4.3. Performance on PLM–VideoBench

- **Significant Outperformance on Novel Tasks:** PLM-8B demonstrates a substantial performance gap over proprietary and open-source baselines on the newly introduced PLM–VideoBench.
 - **FGQA:** PLM-8B achieves 67.7 MBAcc, significantly outperforming GPT-4o (61.2) and other open-access models.
 - **Spatio-Temporal Tasks (RDCap, RCap, RTLoc):** This is where PLM truly shines, largely closing the gap with human performance. For RDCap, PLM-8B scores 52.8 SODA†, vastly superior to GPT-4o (20.9) and InternVL2.5-8B (5.7). Similar trends are observed for RCap (46.6 score† vs. GPT-4o's 35.7) and RTLoc (59.1 meanR vs. GPT-4o's 33.1). This highlights the direct impact of the newly created human-annotated spatio-temporal data.
 - **SGQA:** While PLM performs reasonably well (46.2 acc†), it still lags behind the best proprietary model (GPT-4o at 63.7), suggesting areas for improvement in broader knowledge and chatbot-focused reasoning in egocentric contexts.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- Ablation studies confirm the individual importance of each data component.
 - Stage 2 synthetic data boosts general performance across the board.
 - Adding PLM-STC data significantly improves spatio-temporal tasks (+27.4 points for PLM-STC, +6.8 points for spatial & temporal category) and video captioning.
 - Adding PLM-FGQA data distinctly enhances fine-grained activity understanding (+13.1 points for PLM-FGQA, +7.3 points for PLM-SGQA) and video hallucination tasks.
- Crucially, the human-annotated data effectively addresses the limitations of synthetic data on challenging video QA tasks (HardQA), showing significant improvements.

In summary, the main findings underscore the viability and effectiveness of a fully open and reproducible VLM development paradigm. They highlight the limitations of synthetic data for complex video understanding and demonstrate that targeted, high-quality human annotations are crucial for advancing capabilities in fine-grained and spatio-temporal reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PerceptionLM project holds significant implications for the future of AI research and the development of Vision-Language Models:

1. **Advancing Open and Reproducible AI:** This work directly challenges the prevailing trend of closed-source foundational models by offering a comprehensive, open-access alternative. By releasing models, training recipes, code, and especially the large-scale human-annotated datasets, PerceptionLM sets a new standard for transparency and reproducibility in VLM research. This fosters a more inclusive and verifiable scientific environment, where innovations can be properly scrutinized, replicated, and built upon by the wider academic and industrial community.
2. **Redefining Detailed Video Understanding:** The paper makes a crucial contribution by identifying and addressing a critical data gap in detailed video understanding. The new PLM–FGQA and PLM–STC datasets, being orders of magnitude larger and richer than previous efforts, provide an unprecedented resource for training models on fine-grained activities and spatio-temporally grounded reasoning. This will drive future research into truly "video-centric" VLMs that move beyond superficial frame-level analysis.
3. **Novel Benchmark for Advanced Capabilities:** The introduction of PLM–VideoBench directly facilitates progress in these challenging areas. By offering tasks like dense region captioning and fine-grained QA, the benchmark pushes models to reason about "what," "where," "when," and "how" within video content, which are essential for applications like robotics, activity recognition, and smart assistance. The significant gap between PLM and other models on this benchmark highlights its utility in distinguishing advanced capabilities.
4. **Challenging the Reliance on Distillation:** PerceptionLM demonstrates that highly competitive VLM performance can be achieved without resorting to distillation from proprietary black-box models. This provides a strong argument for prioritizing fundamental research into model architectures, training strategies, and data generation from scratch, rather than relying on "mimicry" approaches. This scientific independence is vital for long-term progress.
5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** The released datasets and models will serve as a foundational resource, enabling researchers to explore new architectures, optimization techniques, and downstream applications for detailed visual understanding. The identified limitations (e.g., long video modeling, broader world knowledge in egocentric settings) also provide clear directions for future work, stimulating further innovation.
6. **Ethical Considerations and Broader Impact:** The authors' commitment to open science naturally supports safer and more accountable AI development. By being transparent about data sources and training, it becomes easier to identify and mitigate biases. The efforts to remove sensitive content (human faces, CSAM, NSFW) and instruct models to refuse harmful questions demonstrate a proactive approach to responsible AI. The datasets focused on everyday activities and instructional videos have positive potential applications in education, assistance for people with visual impairments, and skill learning.

In conclusion, "PerceptionLM" is a landmark contribution that not only delivers state-of-the-art open-access models and data for multimodal understanding but, more importantly, champions the principles of transparency and reproducibility, laying a robust foundation for the next generation of scientifically rigorous VLM research.
