# ViewSpatial-Bench: Evaluating Multi-perspective Spatial Localization in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University introduced ViewSpatial-Bench, the first comprehensive benchmark to evaluate multi-perspective spatial localization in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), revealing existing VLMs struggle significantly with viewpoint transformations. Their Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM), fine-tuned on a new dataset, achieved an 82.09% accuracy, representing a 46.24% absolute improvement over its backbone.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit significant limitations in sophisticated spatial reasoning, particularly when understanding objects and environments from perspectives other than the camera's fixed viewpoint (allocentric reasoning).
- Existing VLM benchmarks primarily focus on 2D spatial relationships or object localization from a static, egocentric perspective, lacking comprehensive multi-task assessment of perspective transformation.
- The inability to perform robust multi-perspective spatial reasoning hinders the practical deployment of VLMs in embodied AI scenarios like robotics, where agents must interpret human-centric spatial cues.

## Method

- Developed ==ViewSpatial-Bench==, the first comprehensive benchmark for multi-viewpoint spatial localization, comprising over **5,700** question-answer pairs across **1,338** 3D scenes and five distinct task types (both camera and human perspectives).
- Created an ==automated 3D annotation pipeline==, leveraging ScanNet and MS-CoCo, to generate precise directional labels and large-scale, diverse image datasets with rich multi-perspective spatial relationship data.
- Trained a ==Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM)== by fine-tuning the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone on approximately **43,000** diverse, perspective-aware spatial relationship samples, using a ==multi-perspective fine-tuning strategy==.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLMs, including GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-Flash, achieved average accuracies of **34.98%** and **32.56%** respectively on ViewSpatial-Bench, performing only slightly above random chance (**26.33%**).
- The Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM) achieved an overall accuracy of **82.09%** on ViewSpatial-Bench, demonstrating a **46.24%** absolute performance improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone.
- MVSM showed substantial gains across all task categories, with notable improvements in orientation tasks (e.g., **+54.32%** for camera-perspective and **+51.00%** for human-perspective Object View Orientation), and demonstrated strong transfer learning capabilities on related benchmarks and real-world application datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art VLMs, including powerful proprietary models, demonstrate a fundamental and systemic deficiency in perspective-dependent spatial reasoning, performing only slightly better than random chance.
> - VLMs show inconsistent and asymmetric performance across different spatial task categories and perspectives (egocentric vs. allocentric), indicating a lack of a coherent, unified 3D spatial representation.
> - Explicit training on diverse, perspective-aware spatial annotations can effectively enable VLMs to develop unified 3D spatial representations that function robustly across both camera and human viewpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved remarkable progress in various multimodal tasks, including visual question answering and image captioning, by leveraging large-scale image-text datasets. However, a significant and well-acknowledged limitation in the current VLM landscape is their struggle with sophisticated spatial reasoning, particularly when it involves understanding objects and environments from perspectives other than the camera's fixed viewpoint. This paper directly targets this critical research gap.

Existing VLM benchmarks and research in spatial reasoning primarily focus on egocentric (camera-centric) spatial understanding. Works like EmbSpatial-Bench, What’sUP, and VSI-Bench primarily evaluate 2D spatial relationships or object localization from a static, camera-bound perspective. While some benchmarks, such as 3DSRBench and SPHERE, have started to explore cross-viewpoint spatial understanding, they often lack comprehensive multi-task assessment or deep evaluation of perspective transformation. This limitation hinders the development of VLMs for real-world embodied AI applications, where agents (like robots) must interpret spatial cues from a human's perspective, navigate complex 3D environments, or collaborate with others by adopting their frames of reference.

This work addresses this deficiency by introducing ViewSpatial-Bench, the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed to evaluate multi-perspective spatial localization across five distinct task types. By rigorously assessing both camera-centric and human-centric spatial reasoning, ViewSpatial-Bench pushes the boundaries of VLM evaluation beyond shallow 2D understanding or limited egocentric views. It explicitly tackles the cognitive science concept of "perspective-taking," which is fundamental for human-level spatial intelligence and crucial for robust human-machine interaction, spatial navigation, and multi-agent collaboration. The paper also contributes by developing an automated 3D annotation pipeline, which is a significant advancement for generating the kind of rich, perspective-aware spatial data currently lacking in most VLM training datasets. By providing both a rigorous evaluation tool and a methodology for data generation and model improvement, this research positions itself as a foundational step towards endowing VLMs with truly generalizable and embodied spatial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: despite the impressive advancements of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in visual content understanding, they exhibit a profound and systematic deficiency in tasks requiring sophisticated spatial reasoning, especially cross-viewpoint comprehension. Humans effortlessly adopt various perspectives to interpret spatial relationships—a skill termed "perspective-taking"—which is vital for communication and interaction in physical spaces. In stark contrast, current VLMs largely operate within an egocentric framework, anchoring their spatial understanding solely to the camera's viewpoint.

The authors identify this limitation as a major impediment to the practical deployment of VLMs in embodied AI scenarios, such as robotics. For instance, a robot needs to understand "the mug on *my* right" from the human's perspective, not its own. This challenge is further exacerbated in 3D environments, where viewpoint transformations involve complex considerations of depth, occlusion, and camera pose, moving beyond simple 2D plane changes.

The core objectives of this work are thus multifaceted:

1. **To identify and rigorously benchmark the limitations of current VLMs:** Specifically, to highlight their failure to generalize spatial reasoning from egocentric (camera) to allocentric (another entity's) viewpoints.
2. **To develop ViewSpatial-Bench:** The first comprehensive benchmark designed specifically for multi-viewpoint spatial localization recognition. This benchmark aims to systematically assess VLMs across five distinct task types, evaluating spatial reasoning from both camera and human perspectives.
3. **To create an automated 3D annotation pipeline:** This pipeline is crucial for generating precise directional labels and large-scale, diverse image datasets with rich spatial relationship data, addressing the sparsity of 3D spatial annotations in existing VLM training data.
4. **To train and evaluate a Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM):** Using the newly generated multi-perspective spatial dataset, the authors aim to demonstrate that fine-tuning VLMs with explicit perspective-aware training can significantly enhance their spatial comprehension capabilities across multiple viewpoints, partially validating their hypothesis regarding training data limitations.
By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a crucial foundation for developing spatial intelligence in embodied AI systems that can better align with human cognitive patterns.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research revolves around the construction of a novel benchmark, ViewSpatial-Bench, and the development of a specialized fine-tuning strategy for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to address multi-perspective spatial reasoning.

#### 3.1. ViewSpatial-Bench Construction

ViewSpatial-Bench is a comprehensive benchmark comprising over 5,700 question-answer pairs across 1,338 unique 3D scenes. Its construction involved a systematic pipeline:

- **Data Sources:**
 - **ScanNet:** Utilized for its rich 3D scene reconstructions and accurate spatial coordinates, primarily for tasks involving object-to-object spatial relationships and scene simulation. Maximum Coverage Sampling (Algorithm 1) was applied to select diverse frames, ensuring comprehensive spatial representations.
 - **MS-CoCo:** Employed for its diverse images featuring human subjects and annotated keypoints, crucial for tasks requiring person-centric understanding and gaze direction recognition.
- **Five Task Categories:** ViewSpatial-Bench evaluates spatial localization across five distinct tasks, balanced between camera and human perspectives:
 - **Camera Perspective:**
 - **Object Relative Direction (Cam-Rel. Dir.):** Determines spatial relationships between objects from the camera's viewpoint (e.g., "Where is object1 relative to object2?").
 - **Object View Orientation (Cam-Obj. Ori.):** Identifies a person's gaze direction relative to the camera (e.g., "Which direction is the person facing from the camera's viewpoint?").
 - **Human Perspective:**
 - **Object Relative Direction (Per-Rel. Dir.):** Requires adopting a character's viewpoint to determine object relationships from their perspective. Due to complexity, 864 instances were manually annotated.
 - **Object View Orientation (Per-Obj. Ori.):** Involves assuming a character's position to determine their gaze direction (e.g., "As the man in green, which direction are you facing?").
 - **Scene Simulation Relative Direction (Per-Sce. Sim.):** Demands modeling oneself within a scene, potentially across frames, to determine relative object positions (e.g., "Imagine standing at object1 facing object2, where is object3?").
- **Automated 3D Annotation Pipeline:**
 - For ScanNet-based tasks, 3D spatial coordinates and camera parameters were used to derive relative positions. For Per-Sce. Sim., object triads and simulated viewpoints were employed.
 - For MS-CoCo-based orientation tasks, an "Orient-Anything-Large" model (Algorithm 2) was used to calculate head and body rotation angles, inferring gaze direction from angular offsets.
- **QA Dataset Creation:** Raw spatial data (3D coordinates, orientation angles) were converted into standardized directional relationships (e.g., "front-right") using an angle-based mapping system. Question templates were then populated, and distractors were generated using specific rules to avoid ambiguity.
- **Filtering and Human Verification:** A multi-stage filtering process was implemented, including automated checks for invalid data and manual verification to ensure clarity of objects, correctness of localization, and unambiguous answers.

#### 3.2. Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM) Development

To address the identified limitations, the authors developed MVSM using a systematic enhancement approach:

- **High-Quality Training Data:** Leveraging the automated spatial annotation framework, approximately 43,000 diverse spatial relationship samples were generated across all five task categories, using the training sets of ScanNet and MS-COCO. This dataset was supplemented with Spatial-MM data for the challenging Person-perspective Relative Direction task.
- **Multi-Perspective Fine-Tuning Strategy:** All training data were structured as image-text pairs with consistent natural language templates, articulating spatial relationships and directional classifications. This explicit training strategy aims to enable the MVSM to develop a unified representation of 3D spatial relationships, fostering robust reasoning across both camera and human perspectives.
- **Backbone Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B was selected as the backbone for fine-tuning. The vision encoder and multi-modal projector were frozen, while the language model was kept trainable for efficiency.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

Evaluations were conducted using accuracy as the primary metric, involving a diverse set of open-source (InternVL2.5/VL3, LLaVA-NeXT-Video/OneVision, Llama-3.2-Vision, Kimi-VL-Instruct, Qwen2.5-VL) and proprietary (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash) VLMs under zero-shot settings on ViewSpatial-Bench. MVSM was also evaluated on VSI-Bench and a custom "ViewSpatial Interaction Application Dataset" (VSI-App) for transfer learning performance in practical applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation on ViewSpatial-Bench and subsequent experiments with the Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM) yielded several key findings:

1. **Fundamental Limitations in Perspective-Based Spatial Reasoning:**
 - Current state-of-the-art VLMs, including powerful proprietary models like GPT-4o (34.98%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (32.56%), demonstrated surprisingly weak spatial localization capabilities, performing only slightly better than random chance (26.33%).
 - This pervasive poor performance across diverse architectures confirms the initial hypothesis that VLMs fundamentally struggle with perspective-dependent spatial reasoning, indicating a systemic deficiency rather than mere implementation issues.

2. **Egocentric vs. Allocentric Reasoning Gap:**
 - Counter-intuitively, most VLMs showed slightly *lower* spatial localization accuracy from camera perspectives (averaging 33.2%) compared to human viewpoints (averaging 35.7%).
 - This finding suggests that VLMs lack a coherent, flexible understanding of spatial relationships that can be easily transformed across viewpoints. Instead, their performance might be influenced by implicit spatial biases encoded from web-harvested training data, potentially favoring certain "third-person" compositions.

3. **Task-Specific Performance Asymmetries:**
 - VLMs generally performed worse on "Object View Orientation" tasks from camera perspectives than on "Relative Direction" tasks. However, the opposite pattern was observed for human perspective tasks, where "Object View Orientation" (42.6% average) slightly outperformed "Relative Direction" (36.9% average).
 - This striking asymmetry highlights the models' inability to construct a unified 3D representation that is consistently navigable from different perspectives, suggesting they treat different perspective-task combinations as separate problems.

4. **Effectiveness of Perspective-Aware Training (MVSM):**
 - The Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM), fine-tuned on the multi-perspective spatial dataset, achieved a dramatic **46.24% absolute performance improvement** over its Qwen2.5-VL (3B) backbone, reaching an overall accuracy of 82.09%.
 - MVSM showed consistent and substantial gains across all task categories. The most significant improvements were observed in orientation tasks: a 54.32% gain for camera-perspective and a 51.00% gain for human-perspective "Object View Orientation" tasks.
 - This symmetrical improvement demonstrates that explicit training on diverse, perspective-aware spatial annotations enables the development of unified 3D spatial representations that function effectively across different viewpoints.

5. **Empowering Spatial Interaction Applications (Transfer Learning):**
 - **VSI-Bench Evaluation:** MVSM outperformed its backbone on "Object Relative Direction" and "Route Planning" tasks from VSI-Bench, with a notable +9.54% gain in "Route Planning." This indicates MVSM's enhanced ability to model dynamic trajectories and not just static spatial relationships.
 - **VSI-App Evaluation:** On the custom VSI-App dataset designed for real-world, human-centric spatial reasoning in embodied interaction, MVSM showed substantial improvement, particularly in indoor environments (+23.00%) and modest gains in outdoor scenarios (+9.00%), confirming its generalization capabilities in practical settings.
 - **Perspective Confusion Analysis:** Visual examples from VSI-App illustrated that while GPT-4o sometimes attempted human-perspective reasoning, it frequently defaulted to camera-centric judgments, leading to "perspective confusion." MVSM, in contrast, consistently adhered to the specified perspective frame, showcasing a more coherent internal model of 3D space.

In summary, the research conclusively demonstrates a significant gap in current VLM spatial reasoning capabilities, particularly concerning multi-perspective understanding, and successfully provides a benchmark and a training methodology to address this critical challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and embodied AI, with profound implications for the development of more intelligent and intuitive human-machine interaction systems.

1. **Establishing a Critical Benchmark:** ViewSpatial-Bench is the first comprehensive benchmark specifically designed for multi-perspective spatial localization. Its introduction fills a crucial void in VLM evaluation, moving beyond rudimentary 2D or egocentric spatial understanding. By providing 5,700 meticulously curated samples across five distinct task types and two complementary perspective frameworks, ViewSpatial-Bench offers a robust tool for researchers to systematically assess and compare the spatial intelligence of future VLMs. This standardized evaluation framework will accelerate progress in a previously underexplored but critical area.

2. **Highlighting Fundamental VLM Limitations:** The comprehensive evaluation of various advanced VLMs, including leading proprietary models, definitively exposes their significant limitations in perspective-based spatial reasoning. The observed performance disparity between camera and human viewpoints, coupled with task-specific asymmetries, provides empirical evidence that current models lack a coherent, generalizable 3D spatial representation that can be flexibly navigated from different frames of reference. This finding underscores the need for architectural innovations and more sophisticated training paradigms beyond simply scaling up existing models.

3. **Pioneering Automated Spatial Annotation:** The automated 3D annotation pipeline developed in this work is a substantial contribution. Generating large-scale, precisely annotated multi-view datasets with accurate directional labels is a non-trivial challenge due to the inherent complexity of 3D environments and human pose variations. This pipeline provides an efficient and scalable method for creating the rich spatial relationship data that is currently sparse in VLM training corpora, laying important groundwork for future data generation efforts.

4. **Advancing Spatially Intelligent VLMs:** The Multi-View Spatial Model (MVSM) demonstrates the efficacy of perspective-aware training. The dramatic performance improvement (46.24% absolute gain) validates the hypothesis that explicit training on diverse multi-perspective spatial data can significantly enhance VLMs' corresponding spatial comprehension capabilities. This success provides a clear path forward for developing VLMs that can construct more unified 3D spatial representations and reason robustly across various viewpoints.

5. **Enabling More Intuitive Embodied AI:** The MVSM's generalization capabilities, demonstrated on VSI-Bench and the custom VSI-App dataset, highlight its potential for real-world embodied interaction scenarios. Robots, autonomous vehicles, and intelligent agents require the ability to understand commands and environments from a human's perspective, plan routes, and interact collaboratively. By fostering better alignment with human cognitive patterns in spatial communication, this work represents a crucial step towards creating more intuitive, effective, and human-like human-machine interactions. This could lead to advancements in areas such as robotics for assistance, smart environments, and virtual reality applications.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to fertile ground for future research:
- **Annotation Challenges:** The reliance on manual annotation for some human-perspective tasks suggests a need for more robust semi-supervised approaches to scale data generation.
- **Domain Constraints:** The benchmark's primary focus on indoor environments from ScanNet limits generalizability to outdoor settings, which present different spatial scales and visual characteristics. Expanding to outdoor scenes is a clear next step.
- **Static vs. Dynamic Reasoning:** ViewSpatial-Bench currently evaluates static spatial comprehension. Future work could extend the benchmark to incorporate dynamic spatial reasoning, where objects or observers move through environments, which is crucial for truly embodied AI systems.

Despite these limitations, this research provides both a critical diagnostic tool and a promising solution pathway for one of the most significant hurdles in developing truly spatially intelligent Vision-Language Models. Its impact will be felt across VLM research, embodied AI, and the broader field of human-computer interaction.
