# Proxy Target: Bridging the Gap Between Discrete Spiking Neural Networks and Continuous Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The Proxy Target framework stabilizes Spiking Neural Network (SNN) training for continuous control by addressing the incompatibility of discrete SNN outputs with continuous target network updates. This approach enables SNNs, including those with simple LIF neurons, to achieve higher performance than traditional ANNs in MuJoCo environments (e.g., 5.84% average performance gain) while maintaining significantly lower energy consumption (e.g., 11.09 nJ per inference for PT-LIF vs. 281.78 nJ for ANN).

## Problem

- Off-policy Reinforcement Learning algorithms for continuous control rely on stable target network soft updates, which assume continuous output changes.
- Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) produce discrete, non-differentiable outputs, leading to instability and degraded performance when directly used in these algorithms.
- Conventional SNN-RL methods struggle to match or exceed ANN performance in continuous control while retaining SNN energy efficiency, especially with simpler neuron models.

## Method

- Introduces a "==proxy target network==" using continuous activation functions to provide smooth target updates during SNN-RL training.
- Employs an implicit gradient-based alignment mechanism to minimize the output gap between the continuous proxy target network and the discrete online spiking actor network.
- The proxy network and ANN critic are used only during training and are discarded for inference, preserving SNN energy efficiency.

## Results

- The framework consistently improved SNN performance and stability across various neuron models, achieving faster convergence and higher average returns in MuJoCo tasks.
- SNNs using the proxy target, even with simple LIF neurons, achieved an average performance gain of **5.84%** over ANN (TD3) baselines in continuous control environments.
- PT-LIF models demonstrated significantly lower energy consumption (11.09 nJ per inference) compared to ANN-based TD3 (281.78 nJ per inference).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The discrete nature of SNN outputs is a fundamental source of instability in off-policy RL's target network soft updates.
> - Algorithmic adaptation tailored to SNN dynamics, rather than just more complex neuron models, is crucial for achieving high-performance SNN-based continuous control.
> - Simple LIF neurons, when stabilized by the proxy target framework, can outperform complex neuron models and even ANNs in continuous control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly when combined with Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), has achieved considerable success across diverse domains such as game playing, autonomous driving, and large language model training. Continuous control problems, which are directly applicable to robotics and embodied AI, represent a significant area of focus within this landscape. However, the computational expense and high power requirements of ANN-based RL algorithms limit their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, such as drones, wearables, and Internet of Things (IoT) sensors.

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs), inspired by biological neural systems, offer an alternative computational paradigm characterized by sparse, event-driven processing, low latency, and energy efficiency, particularly when implemented on neuromorphic hardware. These attributes make SNNs appealing for RL applications in environments with limited resources.

Previous research efforts to integrate SNNs into RL for continuous control can be categorized into several approaches:
- **Synaptic Plasticity:** Approaches like reward-modulated spike-timing-dependent plasticity (R-STDP) are biologically plausible and energy-efficient but have demonstrated limited performance on complex tasks.
- **ANN-SNN Conversion:** Methods that convert pre-trained Deep Q-Networks (DQNs) into SNNs offer lower inference energy consumption but necessitate extensive ANN pre-training.
- **Gradient-Based Direct Training:** Works utilizing Spatio-Temporal Backpropagation (STBP) or eligibility traces for direct SNN training have achieved competitive results in discrete action spaces but generally do not extend effectively to continuous control.
- **Hybrid Frameworks with Spiking Actor Networks (SANs):** For continuous control, a prevalent strategy involves hybrid frameworks where a spiking actor network (SAN) is co-trained with an ANN critic. These frameworks often employ STBP for training the SAN. While some such approaches have reported performance comparable to or exceeding ANNs in specific tasks, they commonly rely on complex neuron models (e.g., current-based LIF or second-order dynamic neurons), which can increase computational cost and training complexity. Furthermore, a central limitation identified in these hybrid frameworks is their tendency to retrofit SNNs into existing ANN-centric RL algorithms without adequately adapting the algorithms to the distinct dynamics of SNNs.

This paper addresses a specific challenge arising from the direct application of ANN-centric RL algorithms to SNNs: the incompatibility between the discrete, non-differentiable nature of spiking neuron outputs and the continuous, smooth updates required by mechanisms like target network soft updates, which are crucial for stabilizing off-policy RL training. The present work seeks to bridge this gap by introducing an SNN-tailored algorithmic modification, proposing a framework that allows SNNs to leverage stable target updates while retaining their inherent efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the fundamental gap between the discrete dynamics of Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) and the requirements of continuous-control Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms. This overarching goal is motivated by the desire to harness the low-latency and energy-efficient decision-making capabilities of SNNs on neuromorphic hardware, thereby enabling their deployment in resource-constrained edge devices for RL applications.

The key motivations and more specific objectives are as follows:

1. **Identify and Address the Mismatch in Target Network Updates:** The authors pinpoint a critical conflict arising from the target network soft update mechanism, a widely adopted stabilization technique in off-policy RL algorithms. This mechanism, which gradually updates target networks to provide stable learning signals, relies on continuous and smooth changes in network outputs. SNNs, however, produce discrete, binary spikes, making their outputs inherently non-differentiable and prone to abrupt shifts.
 - **Motivation:** The conventional approach of retrofitting SNNs into ANN-centric RL algorithms without addressing this mismatch leads to training instability and degraded performance. Specifically, the discrete nature of SNN outputs can cause erratic transitions in target values, resulting in unstable optimization objectives and oscillatory updates. This instability can manifest as high sensitivity to random seed initialization, hindered convergence, and reduced reliability in real-world deployment.

2. **Propose a Proxy Target Framework:** To counteract the instability caused by discrete SNN target outputs, the research aims to develop a novel framework.
 - **Motivation:** A solution is needed that allows for smooth target updates without sacrificing the energy efficiency of SNNs during inference. The proposed framework should introduce continuous and differentiable dynamics into the target update process.

3. **Ensure Alignment Between Proxy and Online SNN:** While a continuous proxy network can provide smooth target updates, a direct substitution of spiking neurons with continuous activations (e.g., ReLU) in the target network could introduce a discrepancy, or "output gap," between the proxy's behavior and the actual online SNN's behavior.
 - **Motivation:** Such a discrepancy would distort the critic's learning targets and negatively impact policy performance. Therefore, a mechanism is required to align the proxy network with the online SNN's behavior accurately.

4. **Demonstrate Performance and Stability Improvements:** The framework should demonstrate consistent improvements in training stability and achieve higher performance across a range of continuous control benchmarks and various spiking neuron models.
 - **Motivation:** To validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach and highlight its general applicability. A specific ambition is to enable SNNs with simple neuron models, such as Leaky Integrate and Fire (LIF) neurons, to match or exceed the performance of their ANN counterparts in continuous control, which has been a significant challenge for SNN-based RL.

5. **Maintain Energy Efficiency During Inference:** The proposed solution should ensure that any added components for stabilization during training do not introduce additional computational or energy overhead during the actual deployment phase.
 - **Motivation:** Preserving the inherent energy efficiency of SNNs is crucial for realizing their potential on neuromorphic hardware and for edge device applications.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the algorithmic incompatibility that currently hinders SNNs from fully realizing their potential in high-performance, energy-efficient continuous control tasks, thereby paving the way for more robust neuromorphic intelligent agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a "proxy target framework" designed to address the instability in Spiking Neural Network (SNN) based Reinforcement Learning (RL) for continuous control, specifically stemming from the target network soft update mechanism.

#### 3.1. Problem Analysis: Discrete Targets and Performance Degradation

The authors first analyze the incompatibility between traditional target network soft updates and SNNs. In off-policy Actor-Critic RL, target networks are updated slowly using the Polyak function (e.g., $\phi' \leftarrow \tau\phi + (1-\tau)\phi'$). This mechanism assumes that small parameter changes lead to continuous, smooth changes in network outputs, which holds for ANNs with continuous activation functions. However, SNNs, with their binary, non-differentiable firing functions, produce discrete output shifts. Empirical observations (Figure 2a-b) illustrate that while ANN targets transition smoothly, SNN targets exhibit frequent, discontinuous jumps. These erratic shifts in SNN target outputs propagate instability to the critic's optimization objectives, leading to oscillatory and unreliable learning dynamics.

#### 3.2. Proxy Target Framework Design

To mitigate this issue, the core of the proposed methodology is the introduction of a **proxy target network**.

- **Proxy Network for Smooth Updates:** Instead of using an SNN as the target actor, the framework employs a differentiable **proxy actor network** that utilizes continuous activation functions, similar to ANNs (e.g., ReLU). This design ensures that the proxy network produces gradual and continuous output transitions during updates (Figure 2c), thereby stabilizing the soft update process and preventing abrupt shifts in target outputs. This component is crucial for stabilizing the overall Actor-Critic learning process.

- **Implicit Gradient-Based Alignment (Addressing Target Output Gaps):** A direct replacement of spiking neurons with continuous activations in the proxy network could introduce a significant "output gap" between the proxy's behavior and that of the online spiking actor network. To address this discrepancy, the authors propose an **implicit proxy update method** rather than explicitly copying parameters.
 - The proxy actor $\pi_{\phi'}^{\text{Proxy}}(s)$ is implicitly updated to imitate the behavior of the online spiking actor $\pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}(s)$. This imitation is formulated as minimizing a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function:
 $L_{\text{proxy}} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \| \pi_{\phi'}^{\text{Proxy}}(s_i) - \pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}(s_i) \|_2^2$
 where $s_i$ are states sampled from the replay buffer.
 - This gradient-based optimization gradually reduces the output difference between the online SNN and the proxy target.
 - Theorem 1 theoretically supports that minimizing $L_{\text{proxy}}$ with a small proxy learning rate (lr_proxy $\rightarrow 0$) ensures sufficiently small and smooth policy updates, thus promoting stable optimization.
 - Empirical results (Figure 2d) demonstrate that while an SNN target can diverge from the online SNN, the proxy network remains closely aligned throughout training, effectively mitigating target output gaps and providing precise and stable optimization goals for the RL process.
 - The proxy network is a multi-layer feedforward model, enabling it to asymptotically approximate the SNN actor's output.

#### 3.3. Overall Training Procedure (Algorithm 1)

The complete training framework integrates the proxy target mechanism into an Actor-Critic algorithm (specifically TD3 in experiments):

1. **Initialization:** Initialize the SNN actor network ($\pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}$) and ANN critic network(s) ($Q_{\theta}^{\text{ANN}}$). Initialize the proxy actor ($\pi_{\phi'}^{\text{Proxy}}$) and ANN target critic network(s) ($Q_{\theta'}^{\text{ANN}}$). A replay buffer $D$ is also initialized.
2. **Interaction:** The SNN actor $\pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}$ interacts with the environment, executing actions and storing transitions ($s, a, r, s'$) in the replay buffer.
3. **Proxy Actor Update (Training):** In each update episode, the proxy actor $\pi_{\phi'}^{\text{Proxy}}$ is optimized for $K$ iterations by minimizing $L_{\text{proxy}}$ (Eq. 8). This aims to reliably approximate the online SNN actor's outputs.
4. **Target Critic Update (Training):** The ANN target critic $Q_{\theta'}^{\text{ANN}}$ is updated using the standard Polyak soft update: $\theta' \leftarrow \tau\theta + (1-\tau)\theta'$.
5. **Critic Update (Training):** Target values for the critic are computed using the proxy actor $\pi_{\phi'}^{\text{Proxy}}$ (for action $a'$) and the target critic $Q_{\theta'}^{\text{ANN}}$. The ANN critic $Q_{\theta}^{\text{ANN}}$ is then updated by minimizing the TD error.
6. **SNN Actor Update (Training):** The online SNN actor $\pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}$ is updated by maximizing the critic's output (e.g., $Q_{\theta}^{\text{ANN}}(s_i, \pi_{\phi}^{\text{SNN}}(s_i))$).

#### 3.4. Inference Phase

Crucially, the proxy network and the ANN critic are used *exclusively during training*. They are discarded during deployment, ensuring that the deployed SNN retains its low-latency and energy-efficient characteristics without incurring additional computational overhead.

#### 3.5. Spiking Actor Network (SAN) Details (Appendix A.2)

The SAN consists of:
- **Population Encoder:** Gaussian receptive fields with soft reset Integrate-and-Fire (IF) neurons encode the input state into spikes.
- **Multi-layer SNN:** Standard SNN layers process the encoded spikes.
- **Decoder:** The membrane potentials of non-firing neurons in the last SNN layer are used as continuous outputs for actions.
The SAN is trained using STBP with a rectangular surrogate gradient function.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** MuJoCo continuous control tasks from OpenAI Gymnasium (InvertedDoublePendulum-v4, Ant-v4, HalfCheetah-v4, Hopper-v4, Walker2d-v4).
- **Spiking Neuron Models:** Leaky Integrate and Fire (LIF), Current-based LIF (CLIF), and Dynamic Neuron (DN).
- **RL Algorithm:** TD3 (Twin Delayed DDPG) algorithm.
- **Evaluation:** Results are averaged over five random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate the proposed proxy target framework yielded several key findings regarding performance, stability, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Improved Performance and Stability Across Spiking Neurons

The proxy target framework consistently improved the performance of SNNs, irrespective of the underlying spiking neuron model (LIF, CLIF, or Dynamic Neuron). Learning curves (Figure 5) demonstrate that the framework facilitates both faster convergence and higher final average returns across diverse neuron types and environments compared to vanilla Actor-Critic SNNs. This indicates the general applicability of the proposed approach. Furthermore, the framework enhanced training stability, evidenced by a reduction in performance variance across different random seeds after training (Figure 6a). This stability is critical for reliable real-world deployment, where retraining costs and consistent behavior are significant considerations.

#### 4.2. Exceeding State-of-the-Art and ANN Performance

A notable finding is that the proxy target framework enabled SNNs, even those utilizing simple Leaky Integrate and Fire (LIF) neurons, to surpass the average performance of their ANN counterparts in continuous control tasks. Table 1 presents a comparison where the PT-LIF model achieved an average performance gain (APG) of 5.84% relative to the ANN (TD3) baseline, while other SNN baselines often showed negative APG or slightly positive but lower gains. Specifically, PT-LIF achieved up to 32% higher performance compared to vanilla LIF SNNs (Table 8). This outcome is highlighted as the first instance (to the authors' knowledge) where SNNs with basic LIF neurons outperform ANNs in continuous control, surpassing even state-of-the-art SNN-based RL algorithms that employ more complex neuron models or connection structures.

#### 4.3. Simple Neurons Demonstrate Superior Performance

An unexpected result was that the simplest LIF neuron achieved the highest overall performance when integrated with the proxy target framework (Table 1, PT-LIF APG of 5.84% compared to PT-CLIF at 5.46% and PT-DN at 5.06%). This observation contrasts with prior research where more complex neuron models were typically associated with better performance in SNN-based RL. The authors suggest that once the SNN's performance matches or exceeds its ANN counterpart through algorithmic improvements, the primary performance bottleneck shifts away from the neuron model itself. Consequently, introducing additional complexity in spiking dynamics may lead to increased training difficulty without necessarily yielding further performance benefits.

#### 4.4. SNN-Friendly Design Validation

To confirm that the benefits of the proxy target framework were specific to SNNs, it was also tested with ANN-based RL. The results (Figure 6b, Table 11) indicate that the proxy target framework did not improve the performance of ANNs. This suggests that the framework's advantages arise from effectively addressing the unique challenges and dynamics of SNNs rather than providing a generally stronger RL algorithm applicable to all neural network types. This specificity validates the "SNN-friendly" nature of the design.

#### 4.5. Energy Efficiency

The energy consumption analysis (Table 2) demonstrates a significant advantage for SNN-based models over traditional ANN-based TD3. The ANN (TD3) model consumed substantially more energy (average 281.78 nJ per inference), whereas both the vanilla LIF and PT-LIF spiking models operated at dramatically lower energy levels (average 12.21 nJ and 11.09 nJ, respectively). Notably, the proposed PT-LIF model achieved the lowest average energy consumption among the tested spiking models. This improved energy efficiency in PT-LIF is attributed, in part, to a slightly lower average firing rate (32%) compared to the vanilla LIF model (33%), further enhancing its suitability for energy-constrained applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a fundamental incompatibility between Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) and standard continuous control Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, offering several implications for the development of energy-efficient artificial intelligence.

**Enabling High-Performance, Energy-Efficient SNN-RL:** The primary significance of this work lies in demonstrating a method that allows SNNs to achieve performance levels competitive with, and in some cases surpassing, Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs) in continuous control tasks. Crucially, this is achieved while preserving the inherent energy efficiency of SNNs, making them more viable for deployment on resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware and edge devices. This capability could accelerate the development of autonomous agents and robotic systems that operate with lower power consumption.

**Highlighting the Importance of SNN-Tailored Algorithms:** The findings underscore that simply retrofitting SNNs into ANN-centric RL frameworks is suboptimal. By identifying and addressing a specific mismatch—the discrete nature of SNN outputs conflicting with continuous target network soft updates—the research demonstrates that SNN-specific algorithmic adaptations can yield substantial improvements. This work opens avenues for further investigation into designing RL algorithms that intrinsically account for SNN dynamics, potentially leading to more robust and efficient SNN-based learning systems.

**Simplification of SNN Models:** The observation that simple Leaky Integrate and Fire (LIF) neurons perform optimally within the proposed framework suggests a potential shift in SNN research focus. It indicates that elaborate neuron models, previously considered necessary for competitive performance, might be less critical than the algorithmic mechanisms that stabilize learning. This could simplify SNN architecture design and reduce the associated computational complexity during both training and inference.

**Advancement of Neuromorphic AI Agents:** By facilitating stable and high-performance SNN-based continuous control, this research contributes to the broader goal of developing sophisticated neuromorphic AI agents. Such agents could integrate low-power, event-driven computation with advanced decision-making capabilities, finding applications in areas like intelligent wearables, IoT sensing, and portable robotics.

**Future Research Directions:** While this work operates at the simulation level, its findings lay foundational groundwork for future efforts involving real-world implementation on physical neuromorphic hardware and edge devices. The challenge of deploying and validating these systems in practical, real-world scenarios is highlighted as a logical next step, which would further validate the potential for low-power, high-performance intelligent decision-making.

In summary, this research provides an SNN-friendly algorithmic solution that not only stabilizes training and improves performance but also paves the way for a new generation of energy-efficient, high-performance neuromorphic agents capable of addressing complex continuous control problems.
