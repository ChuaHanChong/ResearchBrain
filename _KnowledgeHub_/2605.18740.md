# Vision-OPD: Learning to See Fine Details for Multimodal LLMs via On-Policy Self-Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Vision-OPD is a regional-to-global self-distillation framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models to perceive fine-grained details from full images in a single forward pass. A 9B Vision-OPD model achieved an average score of 79.68% on fine-grained visual understanding benchmarks, surpassing larger open-source models and competing with closed-source MLLMs, all while preserving general multimodal abilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models often fail to discern small, crucial details in complex full images, leading to inaccurate responses despite general understanding.
- A "regional-to-global perception gap" shows MLLMs are more accurate when fine-grained evidence is pre-cropped, indicating a difficulty in focusing within a global context rather than a lack of local recognition.
- Current approaches for fine-grained understanding, such as agentic models, suffer from high inference overhead or require extensive external supervision (teacher models, ground-truth labels, reward verifiers).

## Method

- A regional-to-global on-policy self-distillation framework, **Vision-OPD**, is proposed to transfer fine-grained perception from cropped views to full-image processing.
- The method uses the same base MLLM to create a "teacher" (conditioned on an evidence-centered crop) and a "student" (conditioned on the full image).
- The student learns by minimizing the token-level Jensen-Shannon divergence between its own and the EMA-regularized teacher's next-token distributions on student-generated rollouts, using an efficient top-K logits distillation.

## Results

- The 9B Vision-OPD model achieved an average score of **79.68%** across fine-grained visual understanding benchmarks, outperforming larger open-source MLLMs and competing with closed-source models like Gemini-3.1-Pro (**79.25%**).
- Vision-OPD consistently surpassed other training strategies (SFT, RLVR, OPSD) on fine-grained tasks while concurrently preserving or enhancing general multimodal capabilities, avoiding performance degradation.
- Training with Vision-OPD substantially reduced the "regional-to-global perception gap," indicating that models learned to effectively attend to fine-grained details directly from full images in a single forward pass.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a "regional-to-global perception gap," performing significantly better on cropped, evidence-centered images than on the corresponding full images.
> - An MLLM's own enhanced understanding of cropped regions can be leveraged as an internal, self-supervised signal for distillation, eliminating the need for external teachers or ground-truth labels.
> - On-policy self-distillation, particularly with Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher regularization and top-K logits distillation, effectively internalizes fine-grained perception into the MLLM's single-pass processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in general visual understanding and reasoning. However, a limitation of current MLLMs lies in their performance on fine-grained visual understanding tasks. These tasks often require identifying and interpreting small, decisive details within an image that can be easily overlooked when the model processes the entire visual context.

The existing research landscape for addressing this limitation includes several approaches:
- **Agentic "Thinking-with-Images" Methods:** These methods equip MLLMs with visual tools, such as cropping and zooming, which allow the model to inspect specific regions of interest during inference. Examples include DeepEyes, Thyme, DeepEyesV2, and SenseNova-MARS. While these methods can improve fine-grained understanding by making local evidence more salient, they typically incur substantial inference overhead due to repeated image encoding and sequential model calls.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A straightforward approach involves supervised fine-tuning on responses conditioned on cropped image regions. However, this off-policy training can lead to issues such as distribution mismatch and exposure bias, where the model's behavior during training (on teacher-generated prefixes) differs from its behavior during inference (on its own generated prefixes).
- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** Methods like GRPO and DAPO optimize on-policy rollouts using reinforcement learning. These approaches typically provide sparse, sequence-level feedback (e.g., binary rewards based on correctness) and necessitate ground-truth labels and external verifiers for reward assignment.
- **On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** OPD frameworks combine on-policy sampling with dense, token-level supervision. While effective for autoregressive generation, existing OPD methods often depend on stronger external teacher models or the availability of ground-truth labels to generate supervision signals.

The work presented in "Vision-OPD" positions itself within this context by addressing the aforementioned challenges. It aims to develop a method that can internalize the benefits of visual zooming directly into the MLLM's training process, enabling fine-grained visual understanding in a single forward pass without the inference overhead of agentic tools, and without relying on external teachers, ground-truth labels, or reward verifiers. Vision-OPD specifically tackles the "regional-to-global perception gap" observed in MLLMs, where the same model performs better on cropped evidence than on full images, by leveraging this gap as a self-supervision signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the fine-grained visual understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This objective is motivated by a critical observation: current MLLMs frequently struggle to accurately answer questions that depend on small, decisive details within an image, particularly when these details are embedded within a broader visual context. In such scenarios, MLLMs may produce answers that are plausible given the overall scene but incorrect due to a failure to focus on the specific local evidence required.

The core motivation for Vision-OPD stems from an identified "regional-to-global perception gap." The authors observed that an MLLM often achieves higher accuracy on fine-grained questions when presented with an evidence-centered crop of an image compared to when it processes the full image. For example, a qualitative case with Qwen3.5-9B showed that the model incorrectly identified the color of ear protectors from a full image but correctly answered when given only a cropped region containing the ear protectors. This phenomenon was quantitatively supported by evaluations on ZoomBench, where regional-input accuracy consistently surpassed global-input accuracy by 18 to 22 percentage points across various MLLMs, including large open-source and closed-source models such as GLM-4.6V, GPT-5.4, Gemini-3.5-Flash, and Gemini-3.1-Pro. This consistent gap indicated that the MLLM's bottleneck was not primarily a lack of local recognition ability, but rather an inability to effectively attend to and utilize relevant fine-grained evidence when it is situated within a more complex global visual context.

Given this observation, the key objective became to devise a training methodology that could allow an MLLM to "internalize" the benefits of visual zooming. This would enable the model to exploit fine-grained evidence directly from a full image in a single inference pass, thereby avoiding the substantial inference overhead associated with agentic "Thinking-with-Images" methods that perform iterative cropping and zooming. The research sought to achieve this internalization without requiring external, stronger teacher models, explicit ground-truth labels for every token, or reward verifiers, thereby enhancing scalability and reducing dependency on annotated data. The ultimate goal was to allow the model's own privileged regional perception to serve as an internal supervision signal for improving its full-image perceptual capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Vision-OPD framework is a regional-to-global self-distillation approach designed to train Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for improved fine-grained visual understanding. It leverages the model's inherent ability to perceive details in cropped regions to enhance its performance on full images.

**Data Synthesis:**
The method begins with the construction of a synthetic dataset comprising triplets of the form \({(x_i, x'_i, q_i)}\), where \(x\) is the full image, \(x'\) is an evidence-centered crop, and \(q\) is a fine-grained question.
1. **Region Proposal:** Object identification and segmentation techniques are applied to raw images (\(I\)) to propose bounding boxes. Only small regions (area ratio \(<\tau\)) likely to contain fine-grained evidence are retained.
2. **Question Generation:** For each retained region (\(R\)), a powerful MLLM (Qwen3.5-397B) acts as a question generator to produce a question (\(q\)) that is answerable solely from \(R\).
3. **Grounding and Cropping:** To ground the question to the full image and mitigate referential ambiguity, the bounding box of \(R\) is overlaid onto \(I\) to create \(x\). A spatial constraint (e.g., "Only focus on the objects inside the red bounding box") is appended to \(q\). The image \(I\) is then cropped to the bounding box of \(R\) and resized (by 2x) to produce \(x'\), serving as the privileged regional input.
4. **Answer Generation and Filtering:** Qwen3.5-397B also generates answers based on the region \(R\). To ensure quality, multiple responses are sampled, and a question is retained only if a strict consensus (\(>0.75\)) is reached among the majority answers.
This process yields a dataset of 6.2K synthetic samples used for training.

**Vision-OPD Framework:**
The core of Vision-OPD involves instantiating two conditional policies from the *same* MLLM, differentiated by their visual input:
1. **Teacher Policy (\(p_T\)):** This policy is conditioned on the evidence-centered crop (\(x'\)) and the question (\(q\)), formally \(p_T(\cdot | x', q) := p_\theta(\cdot | x', q)\). It represents the privileged view where fine-grained details are isolated and unambiguous.
2. **Student Policy (\(p_S\)):** This policy observes the full image (\(x\)) and the question (\(q\)), formally \(p_S(\cdot | x, q) := p_\theta(\cdot | x, q)\). This mimics the standard inference setting where the model must extract details from the global context.

The training process follows an on-policy self-distillation paradigm:
1. **On-Policy Rollout Generation:** For a given training sample \((x, x', q)\), the student policy (\(p_S\)) first generates an output sequence \(y = (y_1, \ldots, y_{|y|})\) by sampling from its own distribution \(y \sim p_S(\cdot | x, q)\).
2. **Token-Level Divergence Minimization:** Both the teacher and student policies evaluate this student-generated trajectory. At each token position \(n\) in the sequence, they induce next-token distributions \(p_S(y_n | x, q, y_{<n})\) and \(p_T(y_n | x', q, y_{<n})\). The training objective minimizes the expected per-token divergence between these two distributions over the student's rollouts. The generalized Jensen–Shannon divergence JSD\(_\beta\) with \(\beta = 0.5\) is utilized for this purpose. The total loss is defined as:
 \(L_{\text{Vision-OPD}}(\theta) = E_{(x,x',q)\sim D} [E_{y\sim p_S(\cdot|x,q)} [\frac{1}{|y|}\sum_{n=1}^{|y|} D(p_T(\cdot | x', q, y_{<n}) || p_S(\cdot | x, q, y_{<n}))]]\)
3. **Gradient Flow:** Crucially, gradients are backpropagated *only* through the student policy (\(p_S\)). The teacher policy (\(p_T\)) acts as a fixed target for the student, conditioned on the privileged crop. This design prevents co-adaptation between the teacher and student, ensuring that the teacher provides a stable supervisory signal.

**Key Methodological Details:**
- **Teacher Regularization:** To prevent training collapse due to co-adaptation, the teacher policy is regularized using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) with an update coefficient \(\alpha = 0.05\). This ensures the teacher remains a robust guide while the student updates.
- **Divergence Computation:** For efficiency and to capture most of the distributional information, a "top-K logits distillation" strategy is employed (with \(K=100\)). This computes the divergence over the top-K logits of both the student and teacher distributions, complemented by a tail-probability term for the remaining vocabulary.
- **Generation Length:** The maximum on-policy generation length for student rollouts is set to 1024 tokens to provide a rich supervision signal.
- **Base Models:** The Vision-OPD framework is applied to existing MLLMs, specifically Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B, as base models for experimentation.

This approach ensures that the student MLLM learns to effectively focus on and interpret fine-grained visual evidence within a full image by aligning its internal reasoning processes with the "privileged" perception offered by the crop-conditioned teacher, all within a single forward pass and without external labeling or verification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Vision-OPD demonstrate its effectiveness in enhancing fine-grained visual understanding in MLLMs.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art MLLMs

- **Performance Gains:** Vision-OPD models (4B and 9B parameters) consistently exhibited improved performance compared to their initial Qwen3.5 baselines across all fine-grained visual understanding benchmarks. This indicates that Vision-OPD effectively instills fine-grained perception capabilities into existing MLLMs.
- **Superiority over Larger Open-Source Models:** Vision-OPD models achieved competitive or superior performance against significantly larger open-source models. For instance, Vision-OPD-9B attained an average score of 79.68%, outperforming Qwen3.5-397B (77.44%), GLM-4.6V (106B, 71.53%), and Kimi-K2.6 (1T, 72.81%).
- **Competitive with Closed-Source Models:** Vision-OPD-9B also demonstrated strong performance against closed-source models. It surpassed GPT-5.4 (72.77%) and was competitive with Gemini-3.1-Pro (79.25%) and Gemini-3.5-Flash (79.24%), achieving a slightly higher average score.
- **Outperforming Agentic Models:** Despite operating in a single forward pass, Vision-OPD models outperformed "Thinking-with-Images" agentic models like SenseNova-MARS 8B (73.05%), which rely on explicit cropping and zooming during inference. Furthermore, Vision-OPD-9B demonstrated faster inference speeds compared to these agentic baselines (Appendix A, Figure 6).

#### 4.2. Comparison with Alternative Training Strategies

- **Improved Fine-Grained Performance:** Vision-OPD consistently surpassed alternative training strategies, including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on self-teacher, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods (GRPO and DAPO), and On-Policy Self-Distillation with ground-truth labels (OPSD), on the fine-grained visual benchmarks for both 4B and 9B model scales.
- **Robust Generalization and Reduced Forgetting:** A notable advantage of Vision-OPD was its ability to maintain or improve performance on holdout benchmarks (MMVP, CV-Bench, MMStar, POPE) which were outside the training distribution. In contrast, SFT on self-teacher exhibited significant forgetting of previously acquired general capabilities, and RLVR methods also showed degradation on holdout tasks. OPSD, while employing on-policy self-distillation, showed slightly lower holdout performance compared to Vision-OPD, suggesting that the self-generated token-level supervision in Vision-OPD contributes to better generalization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study & Analysis

- **Effect of Teacher Regularization:** An ablation study confirmed that proper teacher regularization is critical for the stability and success of Vision-OPD. Training without regularization (using the current policy as teacher) led to complete model collapse. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) regularization with an update coefficient of \(\alpha = 0.05\) yielded the highest average performance (79.68%), outperforming using a frozen initial policy or trust-region regularization.
- **Effect of Divergence Objective:** Comparing different divergence measures, JSD (\(\beta = 0.5\)) proved to be the most effective for per-token distribution matching, resulting in the best average performance (79.68%) compared to forward KL (78.70%) and reverse KL (78.53) divergences.
- **Effect of Generation Length:** Increasing the on-policy generation length from 512 to 1024 tokens resulted in performance improvements (79.68% vs 78.62% average), indicating that longer rollouts provide a richer and more effective supervision signal for the student model.
- **Learning Objective Comparison (Top-K Logits vs. Sampled-Token):** The use of "top-K logits distillation" (with \(K=100\)) for divergence computation was found to be more effective (79.68% average) than a sampled-token policy-gradient objective (78.62% average). This supports the premise that dense logit-level credit assignment provides more robust learning than scalar per-token shaping.
- **Reduction of Regional-to-Global Gap:** Analysis demonstrated that Vision-OPD training significantly and steadily reduced the regional-to-global perception gap. Both Vision-OPD-4B and Vision-OPD-9B models ended with a smaller gap than many larger or closed-source models. This result supports the core hypothesis that Vision-OPD enables MLLMs to internalize the ability to focus on fine-grained evidence directly from full images without requiring inference-time tools.

In summary, the main findings indicate that Vision-OPD is a robust and efficient framework that substantially improves MLLMs' fine-grained visual understanding, achieves competitive performance against larger and more complex models, and maintains general capabilities without incurring significant inference overhead or relying on extensive external supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vision-OPD framework represents an advancement in the capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the domain of fine-grained visual understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Fine-Grained Visual Understanding:** The work demonstrates that MLLMs can be effectively trained to perceive and utilize small, decisive visual details within complex scenes. This directly addresses a known limitation of many existing MLLMs, which often perform well on general visual tasks but struggle with minute particulars.
- **Efficiency in Inference:** A key impact of Vision-OPD is its ability to internalize the benefits of "visual zooming" into a single forward pass. This design eliminates the substantial inference overhead associated with agentic "Thinking-with-Images" methods that require multiple sequential image encodings and model calls for cropping and re-inspection. Consequently, Vision-OPD models offer a more practical solution for real-time applications requiring detailed visual analysis.
- **Novel Self-Distillation Paradigm:** Vision-OPD introduces a novel regional-to-global self-distillation formulation. By leveraging an MLLM's own privileged crop-conditioned behavior as a teacher for its full-image policy, the framework provides dense, token-level supervision without requiring external stronger teachers, ground-truth labels for every token, or reward verifiers. This self-supervised approach increases the scalability and applicability of the training method, as it can operate on unlabeled image corpora.
- **Robust Generalization:** The research highlights Vision-OPD's ability to avoid the performance-forgetting tradeoff. Unlike some alternative training methods (e.g., SFT or certain RL approaches) that may degrade general multimodal capabilities when specializing, Vision-OPD maintains or improves performance on holdout tasks, indicating a more robust and holistic improvement in model learning.
- **Bridging the Perception Gap:** The successful reduction of the "regional-to-global perception gap" is a direct measure of the framework's effectiveness. By enabling MLLMs to better recover evidence visible in crops from full images, Vision-OPD contributes to a deeper understanding of how these models can learn more human-like visual attention mechanisms.
- **Practical Applications:** The improved fine-grained understanding capabilities could have a broad impact across various domains. These include:
 - **Medical Imaging:** More accurate detection of subtle anomalies in scans.
 - **Industrial Quality Control:** Precise identification of defects in products.
 - **Environmental Monitoring:** Detailed analysis of remote sensing data.
 - **Content Moderation:** Finer detection of prohibited content.
 - **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Enhanced perception of intricate details in complex environments.
- **Direction for Future Research:** Vision-OPD's success with synthetic data and a self-supervised approach suggests avenues for further exploration in data generation strategies and internal self-supervision signals for MLLMs and other large foundation models, potentially extending to other modalities or reasoning tasks.

In conclusion, Vision-OPD offers a significant methodological contribution by enabling MLLMs to "see" fine details more effectively and efficiently, fostering both higher performance on challenging visual tasks and broader practical applicability.
