# Pre-training Auto-regressive Robotic Models with 4D Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> ARM4R introduces a pre-training method for robotic models that utilizes low-level 4D representations (3D point tracks over time) extracted from human video data. This approach enables robotic models to achieve higher success rates in both simulated and real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines like PerAct in simulation (59.47% vs 55.33%) and OpenVLA on real robots (83.1% vs 37.2%).

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, diverse robotic data limits the development of generalizable foundation models for robotics.
- Many existing robot learning methods, especially those relying on high-level visual features or 2D representations, struggle to acquire a deep understanding of the physical world.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often use high-level pre-training objectives that may not adequately address the nuances of low-level action prediction required for precise robotic control.

## Method

- ARM4R employs a three-stage auto-regressive pre-training approach that leverages 4D representations (3D point tracks over time) derived from monocular human video data.
- The 4D representations are generated by lifting 2D video points to 3D using monocular depth estimation and then tracking these 3D points across frames.
- The training pipeline involves pre-training on human videos, an intermediate fine-tuning stage on robotic videos for 4D tracking, and a final fine-tuning for direct robotic control.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **59.47%** on the RLBench simulation benchmark, surpassing PerAct (**55.33%**) and demonstrating best performance on 4 out of 12 tasks.
- Outperformed all baselines on 13 real-world tasks with a Kinova Gen3 robot, reaching an average success rate of **83.1%**, significantly higher than OpenVLA (**37.2%**) and ATM (**6.4%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both human video pre-training (Stage 1) and robotic video fine-tuning for 4D tracking (Stage 2) are essential for optimal performance and generalization, with Stage 1+2+3 performing best.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-level 4D representations from abundant human video data can effectively capture physically grounded dynamics that are transferable to robotic control tasks.
> - The shared geometric structure between 3D point tracks and robot end-effector states allows for efficient transfer learning from human demonstrations to various robotic manipulation tasks.
> - An intermediate fine-tuning step on robotic video data, focusing on 4D tracking, is crucial for bridging the embodiment and data distribution gaps between human and robotic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper situates itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of **foundation models (FMs)**, which have revolutionized domains like natural language processing and computer vision through pre-training on massive, unlabeled datasets. While FMs demonstrate remarkable generalization, their success has been difficult to replicate in robotics due to inherent challenges such as the scarcity of large-scale, diverse robotic data and the need for representations that effectively model the physical world.

The broader research landscape in robotics is actively seeking solutions to these data limitations and generalization issues. Current approaches broadly fall into a few categories:
- **Robotic-data-centric pre-training:** Efforts like RT-1-X, RT-2-X, Octo, and OpenVLA utilize large-scale robotic datasets (e.g., OpenX) for pre-training. While valuable, these datasets are still orders of magnitude smaller than those used for language and vision FMs, limiting their generalization.
- **Representation learning from human data:** Some methods (e.g., Track2Act, ATM) leverage the abundance of human video data, recognizing similarities between human and robot manipulation. However, the paper argues these often lack a deep understanding of the physical world or are limited to 2D representations, hindering effective generalization.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models:** These models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and LLARVA, implicitly use human data by incorporating pre-trained components from Vision-and-Language Models (VLMs). They typically fine-tune language decoders (e.g., for visual question answering or image captioning) to predict robot actions. The paper criticizes these for a "discrepancy between these models’ high-level pre-training objective and the goal of enabling robotic models to handle low-level action prediction," suggesting they might not adequately capture the nuances of precise manipulation and spatial reasoning.
- **3D Motion Fields:** Research in 3D motion estimation (scene flow, 3D point tracking) aims to enrich geometric understanding. While 2D motion fields have been used for fine-grained control, 3D-based methods (e.g., ToolFlowNet, RVT) are seen as offering more spatially grounded representations, though many still rely on explicit 3D structures or ground-truth RGB-D data which are not always available at scale.

ARM4R distinguishes itself by proposing a novel pre-training approach that leverages **low-level 4D representations (3D point tracks over time)** derived from **human video data**, a departure from relying primarily on high-level language understanding or expensive robotic datasets. By lifting 2D video data to 3D via monocular depth estimation and then tracking points across time, ARM4R aims to learn a robust understanding of the physical world directly from readily available human actions. The core differentiator is its focus on a *low-level pre-training objective* (next-token prediction of 4D representations) that is designed to have a "shared geometric structure" with robot state representations, facilitating more efficient transfer learning to low-level robotic control. This positions ARM4R as a promising alternative for achieving generalizable robotic models without requiring vast amounts of costly robotic data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **develop a pre-training approach for robotic models that effectively leverages the vast amount of unlabeled human video data to overcome the scarcity of robotic data**, ultimately leading to better generalization and performance in diverse robotic tasks.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics:** Unlike natural language and computer vision, robotics lacks large-scale, diverse datasets. This bottleneck hinders the development of generalizable foundation models for robotics. Human video data, available in abundance, offers a scalable alternative if appropriately utilized.
2. **Enhancing Physical World Understanding:** Many existing approaches for robot learning, especially those relying on high-level visual features or 2D representations, struggle to grasp the inherent properties of the physical world. This limits their effectiveness in tasks requiring precise manipulation and spatial reasoning. The authors aim to learn representations that intrinsically encode physical understanding.
3. **Bridging the Gap Between Human and Robot Data:** While human videos contain rich information about manipulation, effectively transferring this knowledge to robotic systems is challenging due to differences in embodiment, camera dynamics, and action spaces. The research seeks a representation that maintains a "shared geometric structure" between human actions and robot states to enable efficient transfer.
4. **Improving Low-Level Robotic Control:** Existing VLA models, often pre-trained on high-level vision-language tasks, may not be optimally suited for the precise, low-level action prediction required for robot control. ARM4R aims for a pre-training objective that directly contributes to better low-level control capabilities.
5. **Achieving Generalization and Transferability:** The ultimate goal is to create a robotic model that can transfer efficiently across various robot environments and configurations, performing well on downstream tasks with minimal fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper aims to unlock the potential of readily available human video data by introducing a novel pre-training paradigm that focuses on learning foundational, physically grounded 4D representations, thereby building more robust and generalizable robotic models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARM4R (Auto-regressive Robotic Model with 4D Representations) employs a novel three-stage training methodology to leverage low-level 4D representations for robotic control.

#### 3.1. 4D Representations

The core of ARM4R is its **4D representations**, which are defined as **3D point tracks across time (x, y, z, t)**. These are derived from monocular human videos by:
1. **Lifting 2D representations to 3D:** Using monocular depth estimation, 2D pixel coordinates from video frames are converted into 3D spatial coordinates.
2. **Tracking 3D points:** An off-the-shelf 3D point tracker (SpatialTracker) is used to track a grid of initialized 3D points across successive frames, maintaining their identities.
A key insight is that these 3D point tracks maintain a **shared geometric structure**—up to a linear transformation—with robot state representations (e.g., end-effector Cartesian positions and orientations). This geometric correspondence is crucial for efficient transfer learning.

#### 3.2. Architecture

ARM4R is an **auto-regressive model** designed for next-token prediction. Its architecture consists of:
- **Language Encoder:** A frozen CLIP text encoder with a learnable linear projection layer, processing language instructions (l) into a language token `z_l`.
- **Image Encoder:** A frozen Vision Transformer (ViT-Base), pre-trained with CrossMAE on ImageNet and OpenX, to encode visual observations (`i_t`) into an image token `z_imt`. This pre-training helps encode both non-robotic and robotic data.
- **4D Representations Encoder:** A 2-layer MLP to encode the 3D point coordinates (`p_t`) into a feature `z_ptst`. During robotic control fine-tuning, this is replaced by an MLP encoding robot states (`s_t`).
- **Attention Pooling:** The image token `z_imt` and point/state token `z_ptst` are combined via an attention pooling layer to form the current observation token `z_obst`.
- **Causal Transformer:** A randomly initialized ViT-Base causal Transformer takes a sequence of tokens (`z_l`, `z_obst`, `ˆz_t`, `z_l`, `z_ob(t+1)`, `ˆz_(t+1)`...) and is trained for standard next-token prediction.
- **Decoder and Loss Function:** The predicted token `ˆz_t` (which corresponds to future point tracks or robot states) is decoded by a two-layer MLP into the predicted point tracks `ˆp_(t+1)` or robot states `ˆs_(t+1)`. The loss is calculated as the L1 distance between the predicted and ground truth values. The model is trained to predict multiple future actions/states (e.g., 16 actions), but only the first is executed during inference.

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Training Procedure

1. **Stage 1: Human Videos Pre-training (3D Point Tracking):**
 - **Objective:** Learn generalized low-level 4D representations from large-scale human video data.
 - **Data:** 75,041 videos from the Epic-Kitchens100 dataset (egocentric human activity videos).
 - **Task:** The model is trained to predict 3D point tracks (`p_(t+1)`) given past information (`l, i_t, p_t`). Pseudo-annotations for 3D point tracks are generated using an off-the-shelf tracker (SpatialTracker), which lifts 2D points to 3D using monocular depth and tracks them. These tracks are in the camera coordinate frame, capturing both object and camera motion.

2. **Stage 2: Fine-tuning on 3D Point Tracking for Robotic Settings:**
 - **Objective:** Adapt the learned 3D point tracking capabilities from human videos to specific robotic camera dynamics and embodiments.
 - **Data:** A modest amount of robotic video data (e.g., 5-10% of Stage 1 data) specific to the target robot setup.
 - **Task:** The model continues to predict 3D point tracks, but now on videos of the robot performing tasks, reconciling the discrepancies (camera dynamics, embodiment gaps) between human and robotic scenes. This stage only needs to be performed once per robot setup.

3. **Stage 3: Fine-tuning for Robotic Control:**
 - **Objective:** Transfer the learned 4D representations to directly control the robot for downstream tasks.
 - **Data:** A small number of robotic demonstrations for specific tasks.
 - **Task:** The input and output point track MLPs are replaced with MLPs processing current and predicted robot proprioceptive states (`s_t` and `s_(t+1)`). The model then learns to predict future robot states (end-effector Cartesian position, rotation, and gripper state) based on visual observations, language instructions, and past robot states. The "shared geometric structure" hypothesis is critical here for efficient transfer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ARM4R demonstrates superior performance across both simulated and real-world robotic tasks, validating its approach of pre-training with low-level 4D representations from human video data.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation (RLBench)

- ARM4R was evaluated on 12 diverse tasks in the RLBench benchmark against baselines like Image-BC (ViT), C2FARM-BC, ManiGaussian, LLARVA, and PerAct.
- **Key Result:** ARM4R achieved the **highest average success rate of 59.47%**, outperforming the next best baseline, PerAct (55.33%). It also achieved the best success rate on 4 out of 12 tasks.
- **Notable Comparisons:** ARM4R surpassed PerAct, which uses ground-truth voxel information (an expensive 3D input), highlighting the effectiveness of learning 3D understanding through 4D point tracking. Its superior performance over LLARVA, a VLA that uses a pre-trained language decoder, underscores the benefits of low-level 4D representations over high-level vision-language objectives for robotic control.
- **Failure Cases:** The model struggled with "unnatural rotations" (e.g., a simulated whole-arm rotation for knife grasping) and tasks requiring extreme precision (e.g., "screw bulb"), suggesting areas for future refinement in trajectory learning and fine-grained control.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Evaluation (Kinova Gen3)

- Evaluated on 13 real-world tasks grouped into pick, destack, stack, pick & place, and push categories, using a 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 robot. Compared against ATM, LLARVA, π0-FAST, and OpenVLA.
- **Key Result:** ARM4R significantly **outperformed all baselines across all tasks, achieving an average success rate of 83.1%**, far surpassing OpenVLA (37.2%) and ATM (6.4%).
- **Notable Comparisons:** The large performance gap with ATM, which uses 2D point trajectories, emphasizes the advantage of 3D coordinates for robotic tasks. The superior performance against state-of-the-art VLAs like OpenVLA and π0-FAST, despite ARM4R's pre-training relying *exclusively on human video data* (while VLAs often use robotic datasets like OpenX), suggests the crucial role of low-level 4D representations and 3D scene understanding.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Human Video Pre-training (Stage 1):** Models including Stage 1 (human video pre-training) showed significantly better performance than those without it (e.g., Stages 1+2+3 vs. Stages 2+3, and Stages 1+3 vs. Stage 3 only). This indicates a substantial benefit from learning 4D representations from human videos, especially when robotic pre-training data is scarce.
- **Importance of Robotic Video Fine-tuning for 4D Tracking (Stage 2):** Adding Stage 2 (robotic video fine-tuning for 3D point tracking) to the training pipeline further improved performance (e.g., Stages 1+2+3 performed best, outperforming Stages 1+3). This validates Stage 2's role in bridging the distribution and embodiment gap between human and robotic data.

#### 4.4. Additional Experiments

- **Effectiveness of Pre-training:** Compared to MVP, RPT, Octo, ATM, OpenVLA, and LLARVA on cube tasks, ARM4R consistently achieved higher success rates (e.g., 96.0% for pick cube vs. 93.75% for LLARVA), confirming the strength of its 4D point-tracking based pre-training.
- **Cross-Robot Generalization:** A model pre-trained on Epic-Kitchens and fine-tuned on Kinova videos, then applied to a Franka Emika Panda robot (with different configuration), showed a 19.6% average performance improvement compared to training only on Franka data. This demonstrates the generalizability of the 4D representations across different robotic setups.
- **Robustness Analysis:** ARM4R exhibited robustness to dynamic environments (object movement during rollout) and various real-world noise factors (dim lighting, background distractors, tabletop distractors). Its continuous step-by-step prediction contributes to this robustness, though performance dropped with tabletop distractors, suggesting improvements needed in focusing on target objects amid clutter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Pre-training Auto-regressive Robotic Models with 4D Representations" holds significant implications for the field of robotic learning, addressing critical challenges related to data scarcity and generalization.

#### 5.1. Key Significance

- **Paradigm Shift in Robotic Pre-training:** ARM4R demonstrates that effective pre-training for robotic control can be achieved by leveraging vast, freely available *human video data*, rather than relying solely on expensive and scarce *robotic datasets*. This offers a scalable and data-efficient alternative for developing generalizable robotic policies.
- **Importance of Low-Level, Physically Grounded Representations:** The paper highlights the power of 4D representations (3D point tracks across time) in capturing the physical world's dynamics. Unlike high-level vision-language objectives, these low-level geometric cues are shown to be more directly transferable and beneficial for precise, low-level robotic action prediction.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Transferability:** By learning a shared geometric structure between human actions and robot states, ARM4R achieves remarkable transfer efficiency. This is evident in its strong performance across diverse tasks in both simulation and real-world settings, as well as its ability to generalize across different robotic platforms (Kinova to Franka).
- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The model's robustness to dynamic object movements and various environmental disturbances (lighting, background noise) indicates its potential for deployment in less-controlled, real-world scenarios.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerated Robotics Development:** By significantly reducing the reliance on costly robot demonstrations for pre-training, ARM4R could accelerate the development and deployment of intelligent robotic systems in various industries, from manufacturing and logistics to healthcare and home assistance.
- **Broader Accessibility of Robotics Research:** Lowering the data barrier for effective pre-training could make advanced robotics research more accessible to a wider range of researchers and institutions, fostering innovation.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** ARM4R lays a robust foundation for future work in several directions:
 - **World-Coordinate Tracking:** Future research could focus on pre-training with 3D tracks in world coordinates (rather than camera coordinates) to disentangle object and camera motion, potentially improving invariance and precision.
 - **Adaptive Point Tracking:** Moving beyond a fixed grid to selectively track relevant or moving points could enhance resolution in critical areas and improve the model's ability to focus on task-critical objects.
 - **Scaling Pre-training Data:** Further scaling the human video pre-training data could lead to even greater generalization capabilities across diverse camera viewpoints and complex scenarios.
 - **Multi-Modal Fusion:** Incorporating multi-view fusion could improve robustness to occlusions and provide richer 3D understanding.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Camera Coordinate Dependency:** Tracking 3D points in camera coordinates couples object and camera motion, making disentanglement difficult and potentially leading to inaccuracies due to a lack of invariance to camera intrinsics and motion.
- **Fixed Uniform Grid:** The use of a fixed uniform grid of points might not be optimal, potentially lacking resolution for small objects or focusing on irrelevant background points, impacting the model's ability to prioritize task-critical information.

Despite these limitations, ARM4R represents a significant step towards unlocking the vast potential of human video data for robotic learning, paving the way for more scalable, data-efficient, and generalizable autonomous systems.
