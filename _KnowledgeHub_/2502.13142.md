# Pre-training Auto-regressive Robotic Models with 4D Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> ARM4R introduces a pre-training method for robotic models that utilizes low-level 4D representations (3D point tracks over time) extracted from human video data. This approach enables robotic models to achieve higher success rates in both simulated and real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines like PerAct in simulation (59.47% vs 55.33%) and OpenVLA on real robots (83.1% vs 37.2%).

## Problem

- The scarcity of large-scale, diverse robotic data limits the development of generalizable foundation models for robotics.
- Many existing robot learning methods, especially those relying on high-level visual features or 2D representations, struggle to acquire a deep understanding of the physical world.
- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often use high-level pre-training objectives that may not adequately address the nuances of low-level action prediction required for precise robotic control.

## Method

- ARM4R employs a three-stage auto-regressive pre-training approach that leverages 4D representations (3D point tracks over time) derived from monocular human video data.
- The 4D representations are generated by lifting 2D video points to 3D using monocular depth estimation and then tracking these 3D points across frames.
- The training pipeline involves pre-training on human videos, an intermediate fine-tuning stage on robotic videos for 4D tracking, and a final fine-tuning for direct robotic control.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **59.47%** on the RLBench simulation benchmark, surpassing PerAct (**55.33%**) and demonstrating best performance on 4 out of 12 tasks.
- Outperformed all baselines on 13 real-world tasks with a Kinova Gen3 robot, reaching an average success rate of **83.1%**, significantly higher than OpenVLA (**37.2%**) and ATM (**6.4%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both human video pre-training (Stage 1) and robotic video fine-tuning for 4D tracking (Stage 2) are essential for optimal performance and generalization, with Stage 1+2+3 performing best.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-level 4D representations from abundant human video data can effectively capture physically grounded dynamics that are transferable to robotic control tasks.
> - The shared geometric structure between 3D point tracks and robot end-effector states allows for efficient transfer learning from human demonstrations to various robotic manipulation tasks.
> - An intermediate fine-tuning step on robotic video data, focusing on 4D tracking, is crucial for bridging the embodiment and data distribution gaps between human and robotic environments.
