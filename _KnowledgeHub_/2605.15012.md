# Boosting Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards via Randomly Selected Few-Shot Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign developed FEST, an algorithm that boosts Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by integrating demonstration guidance from as few as 128 expert examples. The method significantly improves performance on mathematical reasoning tasks while preserving exploration capabilities and robust generalization.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) struggles with low sample efficiency on complex reasoning tasks due to sparse binary rewards, hindering effective policy optimization.
- Existing demonstration-guided RLVR methods require large, expensive, and labor-intensive Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) datasets for expert demonstrations, making them impractical for many researchers.
- Alternative data sourcing like bootstrapping or distillation from existing models carries risks related to proprietary costs, legal issues, and potential model collapse.

## Method

- **FEST** is a ==few-shot demonstration-guided RLVR== algorithm that optimizes a Large Language Model using a minimal, randomly selected expert SFT dataset and a large, answer-only RL dataset.
- The method integrates three core components: ==on-policy learning== to expand the learning basis, ==adaptive weight scheduling== to prevent overfitting, and a ==supervised learning signal for expert guidance==.
- It implements two variants, **FEST-DPO** and **FEST-GRPO**, which jointly optimize DPO loss on the SFT data and GRPO loss on the RL data, with FEST-GRPO specifically designed to mitigate gradient magnitude mismatch.

## Results

- Both FEST-DPO and FEST-GRPO consistently outperformed all established baselines across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks using only **128 SFT demonstrations**.
- FEST variants maintained superior Pass@8 scores, indicating robust exploration capabilities and a higher potential for further optimization compared to baselines that exhibited reduced reasoning diversity.
- The approach demonstrated strong out-of-distribution generalization, achieving superior Pass@1 performance on the MMLU-Pro benchmark in a zero-shot setting compared to all evaluated baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal performance in few-shot demonstration-guided RLVR stems from the synergistic combination of supervised learning, on-policy learning, and an adaptive decaying weight strategy.
> - The proposed decaying weight strategy in FEST effectively addresses the instability and rapid distribution shifts observed when naively integrating SFT and RL on small, potentially overfitted datasets.
> - For long Chain-of-Thought and few-shot settings, lower β values (0.001-0.1) in DPO are more effective than standard practice (0.1-0.2), yielding stronger and more stable learning signals due to larger log-ratio differences in extended sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Model (LLM) development has seen significant advancements driven by reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms. Initially, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) gained prominence with models like GPT-3.5, where human preferences guide reward assignment. However, RLHF often relies on subjective and potentially vague human judgments. A more recent paradigm, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), addresses this by employing objective, verifiable rewards. These rewards are typically derived from deterministic rules such such as unit tests for code generation or ground-truth comparisons for mathematical problems. RLVR is particularly effective for reasoning-heavy tasks, where it has enabled state-of-the-art LLMs to achieve high performance in specialized competitions and frontier research problems.

Despite its capabilities, RLVR faces a long-standing challenge common in reinforcement learning: low sample efficiency, especially on complex tasks. When correct rollouts are difficult to generate (e.g., in mathematical problems with binary rewards), a batch yielding no correct answers provides a zero learning signal, hindering progress. Existing approaches, such as DAPO and CISPO, attempt to mitigate this by repeated sampling until success, which can significantly increase computational costs.

Recent research has introduced "demonstration-guided RL" or "unified post-training" frameworks, which integrate Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with RL. These methods aim to provide expert guidance, particularly when RL exploration struggles. However, a significant limitation of these approaches is their reliance on large volumes of SFT data. Acquiring such data is often expensive, requiring extensive human curation (e.g., thousands of graduate-degree holders for a few thousand questions). Additionally, alternative methods like bootstrapping or distilling from existing models present legal, proprietary cost, and model collapse risks. While answer-only RL data is more readily available, high-quality SFT demonstrations typically require substantial filtering or rewriting of raw community answers.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the high data requirement of demonstration-guided RLVR. It proposes a few-shot approach to demonstration-guided RLVR, aiming to achieve competitive performance with significantly less SFT data, thereby reducing costs and mitigating the challenges associated with large-scale data acquisition. The work seeks to leverage limited, uncurated expert demonstrations to enhance RLVR, pushing beyond the capabilities of pure RL-trained models without incurring the substantial data-related overheads of prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by integrating demonstration guidance using a minimal amount of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data. The proposed algorithm, FEST (FEw-ShoT demonstration-guided RLVR), aims to achieve compelling results with as few as 128 randomly selected demonstrations from an SFT dataset.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted, stemming from several challenges identified in current RLVR and demonstration-guided RL paradigms:

1. **Low Sample Efficiency in RLVR:** RLVR, particularly on difficult reasoning tasks such as math and coding, struggles when correct rollouts are infrequent. In scenarios with binary rewards, a batch of failed attempts yields no learning signal, making effective policy optimization challenging and slow. This necessitates approaches that can efficiently guide the learning process even in sparse reward environments.
2. **High Data Cost of Prior Demonstration-Guided RL:** While demonstration-guided RLVR (or unified post-training) has shown promise in mitigating RLVR's exploration limitations, existing methods typically demand a large volume of SFT data. Acquiring and curating such high-quality data, especially involving long reasoning traces, is prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive. For instance, creating just 2,500 questions for "Humanity’s Last Exam" required 1,000 graduate-degree holders.
3. **Ethical and Practical Concerns of Data Sourcing:** Bootstrapping or distilling SFT data from existing models, while an alternative, raises significant concerns regarding legality, proprietary API costs, and the potential for "model collapse" where models degrade over generations when trained on synthetic data. The need for a more sustainable and compliant data sourcing method is evident.
4. **Challenges of Few-Shot Learning in RLVR:**
 - **No On-Demand Data:** With limited expert access, demonstrations cannot be generated for arbitrary questions where the model fails, a flexibility assumed by many prior works. The method needs to operate effectively with a static, small dataset.
 - **Limited Semantic Coverage:** A small SFT dataset may not adequately cover the diverse reasoning paradigms required for complex tasks. The algorithm must extract substantial performance gains even from such limited and potentially uncurated data.
 - **Overfitting Risk:** Training repeatedly on a minuscule SFT dataset poses a severe risk of overfitting, which can degrade the model's generalization capabilities and overall reasoning performance. The approach must integrate mechanisms to prevent this.

To address these motivations, the paper seeks to demonstrate that robust performance improvements can be achieved by carefully integrating specific learning signals and strategies, even when provided with a very small, randomly selected, and uncurated SFT dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, FEST, is a FEw-ShoT demonstration-guided RLVR algorithm. It operates by optimizing a Large Language Model (LLM) using two distinct datasets: a few-shot SFT dataset ($D_E$) containing expert-curated reasoning traces, and a large-scale, answer-only (and thus imperfect) RL dataset ($D_I$). The primary goal is to leverage the minimal reasoning traces in $D_E$ to enhance model performance beyond what is achievable with standard RLVR on $D_I$ alone.

**The Three Unique Challenges and Three Vital Components:**
The authors identify three specific challenges inherent in few-shot demonstration-guided RLVR and propose three vital components to address them:
1. **No on-demand data & Limited Semantic Coverage:** Addressed by **on-policy learning**. This allows the model to evaluate its own rollouts against SFT traces, expanding the learning basis for limited questions in $D_E$ and mitigating exposure bias.
2. **Overfitting Risk:** Addressed by **adaptive weight scheduling**, specifically a decaying weight strategy. This ensures the model prioritizes learning from $D_E$ in early stages but prevents overfitting as the RLVR signal on $D_I$ becomes more dominant.
3. **Lack of external knowledge beyond binary rewards:** Addressed by a **supervised learning signal**. This provides expert guidance, which is crucial for tasks where RLVR's binary rewards are sparse.

**FEST: FEw-ShoT Demonstration-Guided RLVR (FEST-DPO):**
FEST jointly optimizes the LLM policy ($\pi_\theta$) using a semi-online Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) loss ($L_E$) on the few-shot dataset ($D_E$) and a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) loss ($L_I$) on the answer-only dataset ($D_I$). The overall training objective is $L = c \cdot L_E + L_I$, where $c$ is a constant coefficient.

- **GRPO Loss ($L_I$):** This standard RLVR loss is applied to the answer-only dataset $D_I$. It minimizes a clipped objective based on token-level log probabilities and advantages calculated relative to the group mean reward.
- **Semi-Online DPO Loss ($L_E$):** For $D_E$, SFT data are treated as preferred rollouts ($y^+$), and agent-generated rollouts as non-preferred rollouts ($y^-$). The loss is defined as $-E \log \sigma(\beta r^+ - \beta r^-)$, where $r^+$ and $r^-$ are log-ratios of the current policy to a reference policy for preferred and non-preferred responses, respectively. The gradient of this DPO loss implicitly incorporates the three vital components:
 - **Supervised Learning:** Through the $\nabla \log \pi_\theta(y^+|x)$ term.
 - **On-policy Training:** Through the $\nabla \log \pi_\theta(y^-|x)$ term, where $y^-$ are agent-generated.
 - **Decaying Weights:** Through the $\sigma(\beta(r^- - r^+))$ term. This term naturally decays as the agent's policy improves.
- **Adaptive $\beta$ Strategy:** To account for varying task difficulty and prevent uniform learning strength, the hyperparameter $\beta$ in the DPO loss is made adaptive. It varies based on whether the agent's rollouts for a given question yield any correct answers:
 - $\beta_1$: If all agent rollouts for a question are incorrect (unsolvable question).
 - $\beta_2$: If the specific agent rollout is incorrect, but at least one rollout in the group is correct (RLVR-solvable question, current rollout incorrect).
 - $\beta_3$: If the specific agent rollout is correct.
 This allows for more fine-grained control over learning from different types of agent experiences.

**FEST-GRPO: Mitigation of Gradient Mismatch:**
A limitation of FEST-DPO is the gradient mismatch between the sequence-level objective of DPO ($L_E$) and the token-level objective of GRPO ($L_I$). To address this, FEST-GRPO re-interprets the semi-online DPO loss as a combination of a weighted SFT loss and a REINFORCE-like loss with a negative reward. FEST-GRPO then substitutes the REINFORCE component with a GRPO loss featuring negative advantages. This modification aims to unify both objectives within a consistent token-level framework, thus balancing gradient magnitudes and simplifying hyperparameter tuning for $c$.

**Training Recipe:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B.
- **Training Steps:** 600 steps.
- **Hardware:** Two NVIDIA GH200 (96GB) GPUs.
- **Datasets:** OpenR1-Math-46K-8192 dataset. 128 problems with expert reasoning traces are randomly sampled for $D_E$, and the remaining data form $D_I$.
- **Rollouts:** $n=8$ rollouts per prompt, temperature 1.0, max sequence length 8192.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with a cosine learning rate decay (from $1 \times 10^{-5}$ to $5 \times 10^{-6}$).
- **Batch Size:** Global batch size of 128 questions from each of $D_E$ and $D_I$, mini-batch size of 512 rollouts.

**Baselines:**
The paper compares FEST against several strong baselines:
- **Vanilla RLVR:** Pure GRPO.
- **Multi-objective approaches:** SRFT, LUFFY, CHORD-ϕ.
- **RL-SFT switching strategies:** MIFO, HPT, ReLIFT.
Most baselines were re-implemented, while SRFT used an official checkpoint and MIFO's results were taken from its paper. Standalone SFT and SPIN were excluded due to poor performance in the few-shot regime.

**Evaluation and Metrics:**
Performance is assessed on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks: AIME25, AMC23, AIME24, MATH-500, OlympiadBench, and Minerva. Metrics include mean and standard deviation of Avg@8 (average accuracy over 8 rollouts) and Pass@8 (percentage of questions with at least one correct response in 8 trials), measured at 600 training steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy and characteristics of the FEST algorithm in few-shot demonstration-guided RLVR.

**Overall Performance:**
- **Superiority of FEST:** Both FEST-DPO and FEST-GRPO consistently outperformed all established baselines across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks when utilizing only 128 SFT demonstrations (Table 2). They were the only methods to yield significant performance gains over a vanilla RL baseline in this sparse-data setting.
- **Matching Full Dataset Performance:** FEST-DPO demonstrated strong scaling ability with more data, eventually achieving performance comparable to HPT models trained on a full 46K SFT dataset (Figure 2).

**Stability and Generalization:**
- **Instability in Naive SFT and RL Integration:** Baselines that directly applied RL to the few-shot gold dataset ($D_E$), such as HPT-G and ReLIFT-G, exhibited significant performance degradation mid-training (Appendix D.2, Figure 5). This instability was hypothesized to arise from rapid distribution shifts caused by SFT on an already overfitted dataset. FEST’s decaying weight strategy addressed this issue, maintaining training stability.
- **Preserved Exploration Potential:** While some baselines like RL-G could achieve high nominal accuracy, they suffered from significantly lower Pass@8 scores (Table 3), indicating reduced reasoning diversity and potential overfitting on the limited $D_E$ dataset. FEST variants consistently maintained superior Pass@8, demonstrating robust exploration capabilities and higher potential for further optimization.
- **Consistency Across Different Few-Shot Datasets:** FEST consistently achieved performance gains when evaluated on various few-shot datasets, including additional random 128-shot splits from OpenR1 and a subset of LIMOv2-8192 (Table 4), indicating robustness to data source.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** FEST demonstrated robust generalization to Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) tasks, achieving superior Pass@1 performance on the MMLU-Pro benchmark compared to all evaluated baselines in a zero-shot setting (Table 6).

**Component Contributions and Methodological Insights:**
- **Importance of Three Vital Components:** An ablation study confirmed that the optimal performance of FEST is achieved through the synergy of all three identified vital components: supervised learning, on-policy learning, and a decaying weight (Table 5). Removal of any component led to degraded performance, with naive integration of components (e.g., "RL-G with our weight") leading to model divergence.
- **Efficacy of Pure RL Baseline (Optimized):** When the learning rate was optimized (increased from $1 \times 10^{-6}$ to $5 \times 10^{-6}$), the pure RL baseline exhibited strong performance, sometimes matching ReLIFT performance on the full dataset, contrary to findings in some prior works.
- **Gradient Mismatch Mitigation (FEST-GRPO):** FEST-GRPO was introduced to address the significant gradient magnitude mismatch between sequence-level DPO and token-level GRPO (Appendix D.1, Figure 4). By unifying both objectives within a token-level framework, it eliminated the need for exhaustive tuning of the loss coefficient.
- **Sensitivity to $\beta$ Parameter:** Analysis of the $\beta$ hyperparameter in FEST-DPO revealed that optimal values for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and few-shot settings are typically lower (0.001–0.1) than standard DPO practice (0.1–0.2). This is attributed to significantly larger log-ratio differences in extended sequences, where smaller $\beta$ values can paradoxically yield stronger and more stable learning signals within a certain range (Appendix D.3, Figure 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces FEST, a novel post-training paradigm for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) that offers several significant contributions and potential impacts.

**Resource Efficiency and Scalability:** The primary significance of FEST lies in its ability to significantly boost RLVR performance with minimal Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data, using as few as 128 randomly selected demonstrations. This addresses a critical limitation of previous demonstration-guided RLVR methods that require substantial, expensive SFT datasets. By reducing data dependency, FEST offers a more resource-efficient and scalable solution for training high-performing LLMs, particularly in domains requiring complex reasoning.

**Ethical Data Sourcing and Labor Practices:** The reduced reliance on massive human-labeled datasets has implications for ethical data sourcing. Current LLM development often involves extensive crowdsourcing and "ghost work" for data annotation, which can raise ethical concerns regarding labor exploitation. FEST's few-shot approach mitigates the systemic dependence on such large-scale human annotation, promoting more sustainable and ethical AI development practices.

**Democratization of AI Development:** The high cost and effort associated with acquiring extensive SFT data act as a barrier to entry for smaller research groups, academic institutions, and independent researchers. FEST provides a cost-effective and compliant alternative to data-intensive practices like model distillation or reliance on proprietary APIs. This contributes to the democratization of state-of-the-art RLVR techniques, enabling a broader range of entities to participate in and contribute to advanced LLM research.

**Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated effectiveness of FEST with randomly selected, uncurated few-shot data suggests that the method does not require labor-intensive dataset selection or specialized curation to be effective. This enhances its practical applicability. Furthermore, its strong performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks highlights robust generalization capabilities, indicating that the learned reasoning patterns are broadly applicable.

**Theoretical Advancements:** The research contributes to the theoretical understanding of unified post-training frameworks by extending the HPT framework to formally incorporate Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The decomposition of the DPO objective into weighted SFT and negative REINFORCE-like signals, and the subsequent proposal of FEST-GRPO to address gradient mismatch, provides novel insights into the relationships between different RL and SFT objectives.

**Future Directions:** While the work demonstrates strong results on mathematical reasoning tasks with a 1.5B parameter model, scaling FEST to larger model architectures and broader domains such as code generation or general instruction following remains a promising direction for future research. The authors also acknowledge the inherent risks of enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities, such as potential misuse or reward-hacking, emphasizing the need for continued safety alignment and transparent reporting.
