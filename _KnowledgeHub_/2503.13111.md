# MM-Spatial: Exploring 3D Spatial Understanding in Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Apple researchers developed MM-Spatial, a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) architecture, to significantly enhance 3D spatial understanding. The work introduces the Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA) dataset, enabling MM-Spatial to achieve state-of-the-art performance on 3D spatial tasks while retaining generalist MLLM capabilities, and demonstrating surprising intrinsic metric depth estimation abilities.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) largely struggled with complex 3D spatial reasoning, limiting their application despite proficiency in 2D visual tasks.
- The absence of robust 3D understanding impeded MLLM deployment in critical domains like robotics and augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR) that require precise 3D object perception.
- Existing datasets for 3D spatial understanding in MLLMs often lacked comprehensive modalities like metric depth, suffered from limited scope, and were prone to language priors, hindering true visual reasoning evaluation.

## Method

- Developed ==Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA)==, a high-quality, large-scale supervised fine-tuning dataset and benchmark for 3D object perception, leveraging ground-truth 3D scene data and incorporating multi-view images with diverse metric depth maps.
- Trained **MM-Spatial**, a generalist MLLM based on the ==MM1.5 3B== architecture, by integrating CA-VQA data through ==supervised fine-tuning==, aimed at acquiring advanced 3D spatial understanding while preserving other general MLLM capabilities.
- Implemented a data generation pipeline with automated QA pair creation and a ==blind filtering== strategy for the benchmark, effectively mitigating language priors to ensure visual reasoning capabilities were genuinely tested.

## Results

- **MM-Spatial-3B** demonstrated a substantial improvement on aggregated 'Spatial' category benchmarks (**70.1%** vs. **39.9%** baseline) while maintaining competitive performance across general, knowledge, text-rich, and 2D referring/grounding tasks.
- The model achieved **state-of-the-art** performance across all CA-VQA spatial tasks, including metric regression, outperforming much larger open-source and commercial models like **GPT-4o**.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for depth prediction and utilizing ground-truth depth via tool-use consistently improved accuracy, with MM-Spatial's CoT approach for depth estimation rivaling dedicated monocular depth estimators.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A 'generalist' MLLM can be trained to achieve state-of-the-art performance on 3D spatial understanding tasks without compromising its performance on other established MLLM benchmarks.
> - The incorporation of multi-view images and precise metric depth information (particularly via tool-use) substantially enhances an MLLM's 3D perception and metric estimation capabilities.
> - MLLMs, through supervised fine-tuning on curated data, can acquire intrinsic monocular metric depth estimation abilities that are comparable to or even exceed dedicated specialist depth estimation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current research landscape in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) is rapidly evolving, with significant progress in 2D visual understanding tasks such as referring, grounding, and spatial relation prediction (e.g., "left" vs. "right"). However, a critical limitation has been their ability to effectively reason about 3D space, which requires capabilities like estimating relative depth, metric sizes and distances, and precise 3D object localization. This gap is particularly salient given the foundational role of 3D understanding in practical applications such as robotics (for navigation and manipulation) and AR/VR (for realistic scene interaction and content placement).

Previous efforts to integrate 3D understanding into MLLMs have largely fallen into two categories: scene-level understanding (e.g., 3D scene captioning and VQA using multiple views or explicit 3D representations) and object-level 3D spatial perception (e.g., Spatial-VLM, Cube-LLM, SpatialRGPT). While these works have made strides, they often suffer from several limitations:
- **Limited scope of tasks:** Many only address a subset of 3D spatial reasoning challenges.
- **Insufficient input modalities:** Comprehensive assessment of diverse inputs like metric depth maps (both sensor-based and estimated) and multi-view images has been lacking.
- **Data quality and bias:** Existing datasets for 3D spatial understanding with MLLMs often rely on pseudo-annotations or axis-aligned bounding boxes, leading to potential inaccuracies and language priors that can reduce reliance on true visual reasoning.

"MM-Spatial" directly addresses these shortcomings. By introducing the Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA) dataset and benchmark, which leverage high-quality, ground-truth 3D scene data and incorporates multi-view images and various types of metric depth maps, the paper positions itself as a significant advancement in providing a more robust foundation for 3D spatial understanding. It aims to train a "generalist" MLLM that not only excels at these challenging 3D tasks but also retains its performance across other general MLLM capabilities, moving beyond specialized models. This work contributes to bridging the gap between sophisticated 2D vision-language processing and the demands of real-world 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to significantly enhance the 3D spatial understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models. The authors identify a crucial gap where current MLLMs, despite their prowess in 2D visual tasks, struggle with complex 3D reasoning. This limitation impedes their application in critical domains like robotics and AR/VR, where precise 3D object perception is indispensable for navigation, manipulation, and realistic interaction.

To address this, the paper outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Novel Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) Dataset:** The primary goal is to create `Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA)`, a high-quality, large-scale dataset for 3D object perception. This dataset is designed to be comprehensive, covering diverse indoor scenes and including unique input signals such as multi-view images and various types of metric depth maps (ground truth, ARKit, and monocularly estimated depth). The motivation here is to provide rich, accurate data necessary for MLLMs to learn robust 3D reasoning.
2. **Introduce a New Evaluation Benchmark for 3D Spatial Understanding:** Complementing the dataset, the work aims to release a benchmark derived from CA-VQA. This benchmark is specifically designed to assess diverse spatial tasks, including relative and metric distance/size estimation, and 3D grounding. A critical motivation for this objective is to create a benchmark that is less susceptible to language priors, thus truly testing an MLLM's visual reasoning capabilities rather than its ability to exploit linguistic biases.
3. **Train a Strong Generalist MLLM for 3D Spatial Understanding:** The authors aim to train `MM-Spatial`, an MLLM capable of achieving state-of-the-art performance on 3D spatial understanding benchmarks. A key motivation is to demonstrate that specialized 3D capabilities can be integrated into a generalist MLLM without degrading its performance on other established MLLM tasks (general VQA, knowledge, text-rich, 2D referring/grounding).
4. **Investigate the Impact of Multi-view and Metric Depth Inputs:** A specific objective is to explore how incorporating multi-view images and different types of metric depth information (sensor-based vs. estimated) can further improve an MLLM's 3D understanding. This involves studying different modalities and methods for leveraging depth, such as full encoding versus tool-use or Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.
5. **Assess MLLM Capabilities in Monocular Depth Estimation:** The research aims to demonstrate that MLLMs, through supervised fine-tuning on curated data, can acquire depth perception abilities comparable to, or even exceeding, dedicated monocular depth estimation models.

The overarching motivation is to push MLLMs beyond 2D interpretation towards a more holistic comprehension of visual scenes, which is essential for developing truly intelligent agents that can interact effectively with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in "MM-Spatial" is multi-faceted, encompassing novel data generation, a robust model architecture, and comprehensive training and evaluation strategies.

#### 3.1. Data Generation Pipeline (CA-VQA Dataset and Benchmark)

The core of the methodology lies in the creation of the Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA) dataset and an associated benchmark. This process leverages the high-quality 3D scene data from Cubify Anything 1M (CA-1M) and ARKitScenes.
- **Source Data:** CA-1M provides exhaustive 3D bounding boxes (gravity-aligned 7-DOF boxes with yaw orientation) and human-labeled open-set annotations (object class, material, color, shape) for objects within ARKitScenes.
- **Frame Sub-sampling:** Videos are sub-sampled at 1 FPS for training and 0.1 FPS for evaluation to manage data volume and redundancy.
- **3D Ground Truth Processing:** For each frame, 3D boxes are transformed to camera space, including all visible amodal boxes. Point clouds are constructed from ground truth depth maps and camera intrinsics.
- **QA Pair Generation:** A fully automated, template-based approach generates diverse QA pairs (open-ended and multi-choice) covering various spatial tasks, ensuring questions are unambiguous.
- **Blind Filtering:** To mitigate language priors and ensure vision-reliant evaluation, benchmark examples are filtered if they can be correctly answered by a majority of leading MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4, LLaVA-OneVision) based on text input alone.
- **Spatial Task Categories:** CA-VQA covers:
 - **Counting:** Number of objects of a given class.
 - **Viewpoint-dependent Binary:** "Left/Right," "In front/Behind" based on camera pose.
 - **Metric Regression:** Egocentric distance (camera to object), object-to-object distance (minimum or center), object size (width, length, height) in metric units.
 - **2D/3D Referring/Grounding:** Localizing objects via 2D or 3D bounding boxes.
 - **Multi-choice:** Covers other categories with randomized options and distractors.
 - **External Benchmark Templates:** Includes QA templates from CV-Bench and SpatialRGPT-Bench for consistent evaluation.
- **Multi-view and Metric Depth Data:**
 - **Multi-view:** For each reference frame, up to four preceding support frames are sampled based on camera movement thresholds (≥15° angular or ≥30cm linear). Camera intrinsics and relative poses are provided as JSON strings.
 - **Metric Depth:** Three types of depth maps are provided per frame: Ground Truth (FARO laser scanner), ARKit Depth (LiDAR-fused), and Monocular (DepthPro, a SOTA estimated depth).
- **Depth Modality Integration:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) / Tool-Use:** Metric depth can be leveraged by training the model to predict depth values as an intermediate step (CoT) or by providing depth values at specific 2D coordinates via an external tool-call. This contrasts with full visual encoding of depth maps.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture (MM-Spatial)

The MM-Spatial model is built upon the MM1.5 architecture, specifically its mobile-friendly 3B variant.
- **Base Architecture:** Comprises a DFN-CLIP image encoder, a decoder-only LLM backbone, and a C-Abstractor for bridging vision and language modalities.
- **High-Resolution Processing:** Uses an image resolution of 672x672 during fine-tuning, enhanced by static image splitting into four sub-images and an overview image.
- **Multi-view Extension:** Supports multi-image input, concatenating reference and support views. Image splitting is only applied to the reference image. Camera intrinsics and relative poses are interleaved as JSON with image tokens.
- **Depth Encoding (Alternative Approach):** Explores encoding normalized and colorized depth maps using the image encoder and a separate depth connector, following SpatialRGPT. This method is primarily suited for relative depth and is contrasted with the CoT/Tool-use approach for metric depth.

#### 3.3. Data and Training

The training regimen follows the established stages for MLLMs:
- **Pre-training and Continual Pre-training:** Utilizes the MM1.5 stages.
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Starts from the MM1.5 single-image SFT mixture, which covers a broad range of tasks (General VQA, Knowledge, Text-rich, 2D Referring & Grounding).
- **CA-VQA Integration:** The CA-VQA data is added as a new "Spatial" category to this mixture. Ablations (in supplementary material) are used to determine an optimal mixture ratio, with 2:1 (spatial:general) found to provide a good balance between improving spatial understanding and maintaining other capabilities.
- **Training Parameters:** Uses the same hyperparameters as MM1.5, with both the image encoder and LLM unfrozen during SFT. Training is conducted using AXLearn.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings across multiple evaluation benchmarks:

#### 4.1. Generalist MLLM Capabilities

- **Strong Spatial Improvement without Compromise:** MM-Spatial-3B demonstrates a substantial improvement (70.1% vs. 39.9%) on the aggregated "Spatial" category benchmarks while maintaining competitive performance across "General," "Knowledge," "Text-rich," and "2D Referring & Grounding" categories compared to the MM1.5-3B baseline. This confirms the success in developing a "generalist" MLLM capable of integrating advanced 3D spatial understanding without sacrificing broad capabilities.

#### 4.2. CA-VQA Benchmark Performance (Table 3)

- **State-of-the-Art on Spatial Tasks:** MM-Spatial-3B significantly outperforms much larger top open-source and commercial models, including GPT-4o, across all CA-VQA spatial tasks (e.g., Binary, Counting, 2D/3D Grounding, Multi-choice, and Metric Regression). This highlights the effectiveness of the CA-VQA dataset for SFT and the robust architecture.
- **Effectiveness of Blind Filtering:** The "Blind eval" results (MM-Spatial-3B without image input) show significantly worse performance than vision-enabled models, and the improvement from using vision increases substantially after blind filtering (Table 11). This validates the filtering strategy in creating a benchmark that truly probes visual reasoning and is less susceptible to language priors.
- **Benefits of Multi-view Input:** MM-Spatial + Multi-view consistently shows improved performance over single-view models, demonstrating that additional views enhance 3D perception.
- **Metric Depth Superiority (Tool-Use):** Using ground-truth depth via tool-use (MM-Spatial + Depth (Tool; GT)) provides the strongest performance, especially for metric estimation tasks like Ego-Distance. This indicates the unmatched accuracy of direct depth input for precise measurements. Multi-view input can partially compensate for the absence of depth sensors.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Efficacy:** CoT prediction consistently improves over direct prediction, suggesting that explicit multi-step reasoning, including 2D grounding and depth prediction, enhances model accuracy.
- **Limitations of Full Depth Encoding:** Full depth encoding (MM-Spatial + Depth (Encoded; GT)) performs much worse than tool-use and only marginally better than RGB-only baselines. This suggests that existing methods for fully encoding depth maps into MLLMs may be insufficient for effectively leveraging metric depth.
- **MLLM's Intrinsic Depth Estimation:** MM-Spatial's CoT approach (where the model predicts depth values) outperforms tool-use with monocularly *estimated* depth (DepthPro). Furthermore, quantitative analysis (Table 5) reveals that MM-Spatial + CoT achieves metric depth estimation accuracy comparable to, or even exceeding, dedicated SOTA monocular depth estimators like DepthPro, particularly on indoor scenes. This is a surprising finding, indicating MLLMs can acquire strong depth perception capabilities through data curation alone.

#### 4.3. CV-Bench Results (Table 4)

- **SOTA Performance and Generalization:** MM-Spatial-3B substantially outperforms much larger SOTA models like Cambrian-1-34B. It achieves near-perfect accuracy on indoor 3D tasks and demonstrates strong out-of-domain generalization to outdoor 3D tasks.
- **Language Bias in CV-Bench:** The "Blind eval" on CV-Bench's 2D Object Count task shows remarkably high accuracy, revealing a significant language bias in this specific benchmark, unlike CA-VQA.

#### 4.4. SpatialRGPT-Bench Results (Table 6)

- **Addressing Data Definition Mismatches:** Evaluating MM-Spatial on SpatialRGPT-Bench, which uses axis-aligned 3D boxes (AABBs) versus CA-VQA's oriented bounding boxes (OBBs), initially showed discrepancies. To address this, a CA-VQA* variant was created using AABB definitions.
- **SOTA with Mixed Training Data:** MM-Spatial (trained on a mixture of CA-VQA* and OSD) achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming SpatialRGPT-VILA-1.5-8B (which encodes depth fully).
- **Improved Generalization via Scale Augmentation:** Initial models struggled with generalizing metric tasks to outdoor scenes due to scale differences. A simple scale augmentation strategy applied to training data significantly improved outdoor performance.
- **Complementary Datasets:** Training on a mixture of CA-VQA* and OSD yields the best results, demonstrating the complementary benefits of high-quality ground-truth (CA-VQA) and diverse pseudo-annotated data (OSD).
- **Tool-Use vs. Full Encoding of Depth:** Tool-use for monocular depth significantly improves MM-Spatial's performance, whereas SpatialRGPT's full encoding of relative depth provides lesser benefits.
- **Significant Language Bias:** SpatialRGPT-Bench also exhibits significant language biases, with blind models performing well on several tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "MM-Spatial" research represents a significant leap forward in empowering Multimodal Large Language Models with robust 3D spatial understanding capabilities. Its potential impact spans several domains:

1. **Advancing MLLM Frontier:** The work successfully addresses a critical limitation of MLLMs by enabling them to move beyond primarily 2D visual reasoning to encompass complex 3D perception. Demonstrating that an MLLM can achieve SOTA on spatial tasks while retaining generalist capabilities is a key contribution to the field of general-purpose AI.
2. **High-Quality Data and Benchmarking:** The introduction of the `Cubify Anything VQA (CA-VQA)` dataset and benchmark is a major contribution. By leveraging high-quality 3D ground truth, incorporating multi-view images and diverse depth maps (ground truth, sensor, and estimated), and employing a rigorous blind filtering strategy, CA-VQA provides an unprecedented resource for training and evaluating MLLMs on genuine 3D spatial reasoning tasks. This sets a new standard for data quality and benchmark design, crucial for objective progress.
3. **Real-world Applications in Robotics and AR/VR:** The ability of MM-Spatial to perform accurate metric estimation, 3D grounding, and spatial relationship prediction is directly applicable to domains requiring precise interaction with the physical world.
 - **Robotics:** This work is foundational for developing more capable robots that can understand their environment, navigate complex spaces, manipulate objects with precision, and execute natural language commands involving spatial relationships (e.g., "put the red block behind the blue one, 20 cm away").
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** For AR/VR applications, accurate 3D scene understanding is paramount for realistic digital content placement, environment mapping, object interaction, and creating immersive user experiences. MM-Spatial's capabilities could lead to more intelligent AR systems that better understand user intentions and scene context.
4. **Novel Insights into Depth Perception:** The surprising finding that MLLMs, through SFT on curated data, can develop monocular metric depth estimation abilities comparable to dedicated specialist models is profound. This suggests that explicit depth supervision can be effectively integrated into the multimodal learning paradigm, potentially simplifying system architectures by reducing reliance on separate depth estimation modules or sensors in certain scenarios. It also opens new avenues for research into how MLLMs internally represent and reason about depth.
5. **Future Research Directions:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research, including:
 - Exploring more advanced ways to encode and integrate full depth maps into MLLMs, addressing the current limitations observed with the "full encoding" approach.
 - Extending the scope to outdoor scenes, building on the success with indoor environments and addressing challenges related to scale and diversity.
 - Investigating more complex and nuanced 3D reasoning tasks, potentially involving temporal reasoning or understanding object affordances in 3D.
 - Further studying the emergence of physical common sense and geometric understanding in MLLMs.

In conclusion, "MM-Spatial" not only pushes the technical capabilities of MLLMs in 3D understanding but also provides critical tools (dataset, benchmark) and insights that will accelerate research and development in multimodal AI and its real-world applications.
