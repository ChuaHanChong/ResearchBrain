# Seeing is Believing? Mitigating OCR Hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces KIE-HVQA, the first benchmark for evaluating OCR hallucination in degraded documents, and a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based framework with a specialized reward mechanism. The framework, applied to MLLMs, achieved a ~28% absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy over GPT-4o on the KIE-HVQA benchmark while preserving general OCR performance.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate 'hallucinatory content' when processing visually degraded documents due to overreliance on linguistic priors or misaligned visual-textual reasoning.
- There is a lack of key information extraction (KIE) training data and instruction fine-tuning strategies specifically for MLLMs to handle degraded visual scenarios.
- Existing benchmarks do not specifically evaluate and quantify OCR hallucination in document understanding tasks, hindering progress in this critical area.

## Method

- Developed **KIE-HVQA**, a novel benchmark featuring diverse real-world ==degradation simulations== and pixel-level annotations to evaluate OCR hallucination in degraded document understanding.
- Introduced a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based reinforcement learning framework, initialized with supervised fine-tuning on a multimodal CoT-OCR dataset.
- Designed a rule-based reward function for GRPO that enforces visual-textual fidelity by guiding the model to accurately recognize legible characters, identify partially obscured ones, and refuse to output unrecognizable characters.

## Results

- The proposed model achieved a **58.05%** average distance score on the KIE-HVQA benchmark, demonstrating a ~**28%** absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy compared to GPT-4o's **30.21%**.
- It showed enhanced robustness in scenarios with partially occluded text, achieving **61.34%** accuracy in 'not clear character-level OCR distance' evaluations, significantly outperforming GPT-4o's **36.13%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the framework successfully maintained comparable performance on standard OCR tasks, indicating that the enhanced degradation handling does not negatively impact general OCR capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a critical 'incompleteness' when confronted with visually degraded documents, often failing to recognize uncertainty and generating hallucinatory content.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline combining supervised fine-tuning with a GRPO-based reinforcement learning approach can effectively foster 'self-awareness of visual uncertainty' in MLLMs.
> - A fine-grained, multi-objective reward function tailored to character clarity and degradation levels is crucial for successfully mitigating OCR hallucination without compromising general OCR performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), document understanding, Optical Character Recognition (OCR), and the increasingly critical challenge of AI hallucination. In recent years, MLLMs have revolutionized document understanding by seamlessly integrating visual and textual information, demonstrating near-human performance on various tasks like automated processing of identity cards and invoices. These advancements are driven by improved language models, higher-resolution visual encoders, and refined layout-based instructions.

However, despite their remarkable progress, existing MLLMs exhibit a significant "incompleteness" when confronted with real-world complexities, particularly visual degradation such as blur, occlusion, or low contrast. Under these challenging conditions, models frequently fail to adequately perceive visual degradation and ambiguity, leading to an overreliance on linguistic priors or misaligned visual-textual reasoning. This often results in "hallucinatory content" – the generation of information not present or accurately derivable from the input image. This phenomenon is a critical concern, especially in sensitive applications where factual accuracy is paramount (e.g., medical documents, financial records).

The authors meticulously identify three root causes for this OCR hallucination issue in MLLMs:
1. **Pre-training Data Deficiency:** A lack of key information extraction (KIE) data and annotations specifically for degraded visual scenarios.
2. **Instruction Fine-tuning Oversight:** The common assumption during fine-tuning that OCR tasks involve non-degraded inputs, leading to models that lack necessary reasoning for real-world degraded documents.
3. **Evaluation Gap:** The absence of dedicated benchmarks to quantify OCR hallucination in document understanding tasks, hindering progress and comprehensive assessment.

In the broader context of reasoning in MLLMs, previous work has explored Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques to enhance reasoning capabilities. However, the authors note a "paucity of research focused on applying reasoning to OCR tasks, particularly in addressing hallucination issues," stating their approach is the first to utilize RL for this specific problem in OCR. Similarly, while numerous OCR benchmarks exist for various text recognition challenges, including those for MLLMs like OCRBench, DocLocal4K, and FOX, they predominantly focus on overall document understanding and information retrieval, neglecting the specific issues of hallucinations and misrecognitions caused by image degradation.

Thus, this paper addresses a critical, under-researched gap: the reliable performance of MLLMs for OCR in degraded real-world documents and the robust evaluation of hallucination in these scenarios. By introducing a novel benchmark and a targeted mitigation framework, the authors aim to push MLLMs toward more trustworthy and vision-faithful reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly mitigate OCR hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models when processing visually degraded documents. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims:

1. **Establish a Standardized Benchmark:** To propose KIE-HVQA, the first benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate OCR hallucination in the context of degraded document understanding. This benchmark aims to simulate diverse real-world degradation scenarios (e.g., blur, occlusion, low contrast) and provide pixel-level annotations for OCR reliability, thereby enabling a comprehensive and quantitative assessment of models' capacities to distinguish reliable visual information and refuse to answer when uncertainty is high.
2. **Develop a Vision-Faithful Reasoning Framework:** To introduce a novel Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based framework equipped with a specialized reward mechanism. This framework is intended to foster "self-awareness of visual uncertainty" within the model, encouraging it to prioritize visual evidence over potentially misleading linguistic priors and to initiate refusal to answer when a precise, visually verifiable response is not feasible.
3. **Enhance Model Robustness and Accuracy:** To train MLLMs (specifically based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) using this framework to achieve substantial improvements in hallucination-free accuracy on degraded document understanding tasks, without compromising their performance on standard, non-degraded OCR tasks.
4. **Demonstrate Practical Viability:** To provide empirical evidence that the proposed methodology leads to more reliable and trustworthy MLLM outputs, thereby advancing their applicability in real-world critical domains such as identity verification, financial processing, and medical information extraction.

The motivation for this work is rooted in the significant real-world implications of MLLM hallucinations. Current models, despite their advanced capabilities, often fail when presented with the visual ambiguities inherent in real-world documents. This failure to recognize uncertainty and the subsequent generation of hallucinatory content can lead to "catastrophic misinterpretations" in critical applications. For example, misreading a dosage on a blurred prescription or an amount on an occluded invoice can have severe consequences. The absence of proper training data, fine-tuning strategies that account for degradation, and dedicated evaluation metrics has created a gap that this research seeks to fill, paving the way for truly reliable and context-aware MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces a two-pronged methodology: first, a novel benchmark dataset, KIE-HVQA, for evaluating OCR hallucination, and second, a GRPO-based reinforcement learning framework with a specialized reward mechanism to mitigate it.

#### 3.1. The KIE-HVQA Benchmark

KIE-HVQA is presented as the first benchmark specifically for evaluating OCR hallucination in degraded document understanding.
- **Task Description:** It defines a visually-grounded question answering task where models must precisely align textual semantics with degraded visual evidence. Given a degraded document image and a question, models need to identify relevant text elements, assess character-level recognition confidence (based on edge sharpness, contrast ratios, contextual coherence), and generate answers based *only* on visually verifiable content, explicitly indicating regions of uncertainty.
- **Annotation Curation:** The dataset was meticulously assembled in three stages:
 - **Dataset Collection:** Diverse sources including OCRBench, WildReceipt, and GPT-4o-generated synthetic images (for IDs and documents with fictional information to ensure privacy).
 - **Instruction Formulation:** Key information queries from OCRBench were preserved. For WildReceipt, MLLMs generated questions from entity-type answers. For GPT-4o images, corresponding Q&A pairs were designed.
 - **Manual Verification:** Character localization models extracted coordinates, and GPT-4o matched character order. Critically, random degradation processes (e.g., motion blur, low contrast, obfuscation) were applied to these coordinates. Multiple MLLMs (GPT, Qwen2.5-VL-72B) were then used to evaluate the degraded results by extracting degraded characters and determining their visibility, ensuring correct annotations for different levels of degradation (legible, partially obscured, unrecognizable).
- **Dataset Size and Scope:** It includes 2,000 annotated training samples and 400 rigorously curated test instances, spanning document types like identity cards, receipts, and invoices.
- **Evaluation Criteria:** Three metrics assess performance:
 - **Legible Character Accuracy:** Character-level accuracy in high-visibility regions, measuring basic OCR capabilities.
 - **Degraded Character Accuracy:** Recognition accuracy in predefined degraded regions. For ambiguous but recognizable characters, the model should output them; for high-hallucination-risk areas, it should refuse.
 - **Global OCR Performance:** Task-specific text extraction quality measured by Accuracy of OCR results for question-critical text and Normalized Levenshtein distance between OCR-extracted text and ground truth.

#### 3.2. GRPO-based Vision-Faithful Reasoning Framework

Inspired by advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) for LLM reasoning (e.g., DeepSeek-R1), the authors employ a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework. GRPO simplifies RL by comparing groups of generated responses and their rewards, eliminating the need for a critic model.
- **Cold-start Initialization:** To bridge the gap between language-based reasoning models and multimodal data, a multimodal CoT-OCR dataset is created:
 - Existing MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) convert multimodal input (image-question-answer pairs) into purely textual information, generating "pseudo-CoT" including image descriptions and reasoning processes.
 - This textual data, combined with prompts, is fed into a pure language reasoning model (DeepSeek-R1) to generate high-quality CoT processes that mimic human cognitive behavior.
 - The resulting pure textual CoT data is then paired with corresponding images to create an integrated multimodal CoT dataset for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a cold-start for the MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
- **RL with OCR Reward:** After SFT, the model undergoes further training using GRPO with a novel, rule-based reward function specifically designed for degraded OCR scenarios. This reward function enforces visual-textual fidelity by categorizing character clarity and guiding the model's output:
 - **Legible Character:** Accurate recognition and retention.
 - **Partially Obscured but Human-Recognizable Characters:** Identified as "anomalous" (e.g., `<part_occluded>[e]`), included in output.
 - **Unrecognizable Characters:** Not included in output, but represented by a space or specific tags (e.g., `<occluded>`) to prevent hallucination.
 - **Reward Calculation:** The reward function (F) combines OCR edit distance metrics (f) for different categories (clear, unclear, final answer) and numerical calculation evaluation metrics (g) for specific elements. This multi-stage analytical process ensures precise alignment between visual faithfulness and textual accuracy, with error type differentiation driving targeted improvement.
- **Implementation Details:** The cold-start dataset was prepared using GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1. Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct served as the base model for SFT (5 epochs, 1e-6 learning rate) and subsequent GRPO training.

This comprehensive approach, combining a targeted benchmark with a sophisticated RL framework and a degradation-aware reward system, aims to make MLLMs more reliable and resistant to hallucinations in challenging real-world document understanding tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the proposed KIE-HVQA benchmark and the GRPO-based framework in mitigating OCR hallucinations.

#### 4.1. KIE-HVQA Benchmark Performance

- **Superior Hallucination Mitigation:** The authors' model, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieved a remarkable average distance score of 58.05% on the KIE-HVQA benchmark. This represents a **∼28% absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy** compared to the state-of-the-art closed-source model GPT-4o, which scored 30.21%.
- **Robustness to Degradation:** Specifically, in scenarios with partially occluded text, the proposed model achieved 61.34% accuracy in "not clear character-level OCR distance" evaluations, substantially outperforming GPT-4o's 36.13%. This highlights the efficacy of the uncertainty-aware grounding mechanism in reducing hallucination under visual ambiguity.
- **Balanced Performance:** The model consistently demonstrated balanced superior performance across various document subsets (OCRbench-KIE, WildReceipt, Card subsets) and all evaluation dimensions (clear characters, not clear characters, final OCR, and average results). This indicates its capability to adapt to different levels of text degradation and complexity.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

The paper includes crucial ablation studies to validate the contribution of different components of the proposed methodology:
- **Analysis of Training Strategy:**
 - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on the custom cold-start dataset significantly improved the model's performance on KIE-HVQA.
 - Subsequent optimization with Reinforcement Learning (RL) through GRPO further enhanced these gains, enabling the model to tackle more complex problems and improve overall reasoning and thinking capabilities. This confirms the complementary roles of SFT and RL in the training pipeline.
- **Analysis of General OCR Capability Preservation:**
 - Evaluations on standard OCR domains from OCRbench (Scene Text-centric VQA, Doc-oriented VQA, Key Information Extraction) showed that the proposed model maintained comparable performance to specialized baseline models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MiniCPM-o-2.6). This is a critical finding, demonstrating that the enhanced handling of degraded regions and uncertainty-aware grounding mechanism does not negatively impact the model's general OCR capabilities.
- **Analysis of Reward Setting:**
 - Ablation studies on the GRPO reward function highlighted the necessity of integrating composite rewards. When only clear character rewards or only final character rewards were used, the model's performance, especially on "not clear characters," significantly declined. The framework's superior performance with "all rewards" (combining clear, not clear, and final character matching aspects) validates that multi-objective reward synthesis is crucial for effectively handling diverse real-world document degradation patterns.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate that the KIE-HVQA benchmark effectively quantifies OCR hallucination, and the GRPO-based framework with its novel reward mechanism substantially mitigates this issue, leading to more accurate and reliable MLLM performance on degraded documents without sacrificing general OCR capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the development of robust and trustworthy Multimodal Large Language Models for real-world document understanding. Its contributions have both immediate practical implications and substantial impact on future research directions.

1. **Novel and Critical Benchmark:** The introduction of KIE-HVQA is a pioneering effort. As the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate OCR hallucination in degraded documents, it addresses a crucial gap in MLLM evaluation. This benchmark provides a standardized, challenging, and realistic testbed for future models, enabling systematic assessment of their ability to handle visual uncertainty and prevent hallucinations. This will undoubtedly accelerate research and development in this vital area.

2. **Innovative Methodological Framework:** The GRPO-based framework, coupled with its novel multi-objective, degradation-aware reward mechanism, represents a significant methodological contribution. By explicitly modeling OCR hallucination and enforcing "vision-faithful reasoning" through reinforcement learning, the authors provide a powerful paradigm for training MLLMs that prioritize visual evidence. The incorporation of "uncertainty-driven rejection behaviors" is particularly impactful, moving MLLMs towards a more human-like understanding of when to refuse an answer rather than hallucinating.

3. **Enhanced Reliability for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated ∼28% absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy over state-of-the-art models like GPT-4o has profound practical implications. This advancement makes MLLMs significantly more reliable for critical applications in various industries:
 - **Healthcare:** More accurate extraction of information from prescriptions (e.g., dosage, frequency) despite fading or poor handwriting, reducing the risk of medical errors.
 - **Finance/Legal:** Trustworthy processing of invoices, contracts, and other financial documents, even if scanned imperfectly, minimizing fraud or misinterpretations.
 - **Identity Verification:** Robust recognition of information from ID cards with glare, blur, or partial occlusion, enhancing security and efficiency.
 - **General Document Management:** Improving automation for any business process involving scanned or photographed documents where quality cannot be guaranteed.

4. **Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving state-of-the-art performance with a 7B-parameter model (compared to much larger, typically proprietary, models) highlights the computational efficiency and potential accessibility of this solution. This could enable wider adoption and deployment on less resource-intensive hardware.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. It encourages deeper exploration into:
 - **Uncertainty Quantification:** Developing more sophisticated mechanisms for MLLMs to quantify and express their uncertainty at fine-grained levels.
 - **Explainable AI (XAI):** Understanding how and why MLLMs make decisions under degradation, and providing more transparent justifications for their outputs or refusals.
 - **Generalization to Diverse Degradations:** Further research into handling an even wider array of real-world visual degradations and document types.
 - **Human-AI Collaboration:** Designing interfaces where humans can easily review and correct MLLM outputs, especially in uncertain regions, leveraging the model's "refusal" signals.

In conclusion, this paper successfully tackles a pressing challenge in multimodal AI, moving beyond ideal conditions to address the complexities of real-world data. By providing both a benchmark and an effective mitigation strategy, the authors have made a substantial contribution towards building more robust, reliable, and ultimately, more trustworthy MLLMs.
