# Seeing is Believing? Mitigating OCR Hallucinations in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces KIE-HVQA, the first benchmark for evaluating OCR hallucination in degraded documents, and a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based framework with a specialized reward mechanism. The framework, applied to MLLMs, achieved a ~28% absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy over GPT-4o on the KIE-HVQA benchmark while preserving general OCR performance.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently generate 'hallucinatory content' when processing visually degraded documents due to overreliance on linguistic priors or misaligned visual-textual reasoning.
- There is a lack of key information extraction (KIE) training data and instruction fine-tuning strategies specifically for MLLMs to handle degraded visual scenarios.
- Existing benchmarks do not specifically evaluate and quantify OCR hallucination in document understanding tasks, hindering progress in this critical area.

## Method

- Developed **KIE-HVQA**, a novel benchmark featuring diverse real-world ==degradation simulations== and pixel-level annotations to evaluate OCR hallucination in degraded document understanding.
- Introduced a Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)-based reinforcement learning framework, initialized with supervised fine-tuning on a multimodal CoT-OCR dataset.
- Designed a rule-based reward function for GRPO that enforces visual-textual fidelity by guiding the model to accurately recognize legible characters, identify partially obscured ones, and refuse to output unrecognizable characters.

## Results

- The proposed model achieved a **58.05%** average distance score on the KIE-HVQA benchmark, demonstrating a ~**28%** absolute improvement in hallucination-free accuracy compared to GPT-4o's **30.21%**.
- It showed enhanced robustness in scenarios with partially occluded text, achieving **61.34%** accuracy in 'not clear character-level OCR distance' evaluations, significantly outperforming GPT-4o's **36.13%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the framework successfully maintained comparable performance on standard OCR tasks, indicating that the enhanced degradation handling does not negatively impact general OCR capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs exhibit a critical 'incompleteness' when confronted with visually degraded documents, often failing to recognize uncertainty and generating hallucinatory content.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline combining supervised fine-tuning with a GRPO-based reinforcement learning approach can effectively foster 'self-awareness of visual uncertainty' in MLLMs.
> - A fine-grained, multi-objective reward function tailored to character clarity and degradation levels is crucial for successfully mitigating OCR hallucination without compromising general OCR performance.
