# Tree Search for LLM Agent Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiamen University, Alibaba Group, and Southern University of Science and Technology developed Tree-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (Tree-GRPO), an RL method that enhances the training efficiency and stability of LLM agents in multi-turn tasks. This approach uses an agent step-level tree search and constructs fine-grained, step-wise supervision from sparse outcome rewards, leading to up to 112% relative performance improvement in multi-hop QA under budget constraints.

## Problem

- Multi-turn LLM agent rollouts are computationally expensive, involving long trajectories with redundant computations in chain-based RL.
- Long-horizon, multi-turn trajectories suffer from sparse supervision, typically receiving only a single outcome reward, making credit assignment difficult for intermediate steps.
- Existing token/sentence-level tree search methods are not well-suited for online RL training of agents with distinct step structures.

## Method

- Introduces Tree-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (**Tree-GRPO**), integrating an agent ==step-level tree== search for efficient online rollouts by sharing common prefixes among interleaved trajectories.
- Develops a mechanism for estimating grouped relative advantages (both intra-tree and inter-tree) to generate implicit step-level preference learning objectives from sparse outcome rewards.
- The policy model is updated by maximizing a Tree-GRPO objective function, incorporating these grouped relative advantages and a KL divergence regularization term.

## Results

- Tree-GRPO consistently outperformed chain-based GRPO across 11 datasets for Multi-Hop QA, Single-Hop QA, and Web-Agent QA, achieving up to **112%** relative improvement in Multi-Hop QA under highly constrained budgets.
- The method demonstrated superior budget efficiency, achieving better performance while utilizing only a quarter of the rollout budget required by chain-based methods, and showed improved training stability across different learning rates and KL term coefficients.
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining both intra-tree and inter-tree group relative advantages yielded the most stable and improved performance, and agent step-level tree search was superior to token/sentence-level for structured agent tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An agent step-level tree search strategy significantly improves rollout budget efficiency by acquiring a larger number of agent rollouts within a fixed token/tool-call budget compared to chain-based methods.
> - Leveraging the tree structure enables the generation of fine-grained, step-wise process supervision signals purely from sparse outcome rewards, effectively addressing the credit assignment problem.
> - The intra-tree Group Relative Policy Optimization objective is theoretically equivalent to a step-level Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective under a binary preference assumption, providing a principled understanding of process-level learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a significant post-training paradigm for Large Language Models (LLMs), contributing to the development of advanced models capable of complex reasoning. Initial successes have been observed in single-turn tasks such as mathematical proof and code generation, indicating that LLMs can learn through active interaction with dynamic environments, beyond static imitation. This success has motivated the extension of RL to more intricate agent settings that involve dynamic, multi-turn interactions. This domain, known as agentic RL, is considered important for the development of next-generation foundation models that can exhibit long-horizon intelligence in open-ended environments.

However, the application of RL to multi-turn LLM agents presents two notable challenges within the current research landscape. First, LLM rollouts in agent settings are resource-intensive. Multi-turn interactions generate trajectories containing thousands of tokens and multiple tool calls, leading to substantial computational costs and time. Existing group-based RL methods typically sample independent trajectories in a chain-based manner, which can involve considerable redundancy in the sampling process. Second, these long-horizon, multi-turn trajectories suffer from sparse supervision. Most agentic RL approaches rely on a single, scalar outcome reward delivered at the end of a trajectory. This sparse signal makes it difficult to ascertain which specific steps or actions within a complex, interdependent sequence contributed to success or failure, potentially leading to an imbalanced learning process or training instability.

Previous research on tree search in LLMs has primarily focused on generating offline DPO datasets or enhancing test-time inference by exploring multiple reasoning paths at the token or sentence level. However, directly applying these token/sentence-level tree search methods to online RL training for agents with distinct step structures remains an area with limited exploration, largely due to challenges in parallelized inference and the semantic granularity of the tree nodes. This paper positions itself within this context by proposing a tree-based RL approach that specifically addresses the budget efficiency and sparse supervision challenges in online multi-turn LLM agent training, while introducing an agent step-level node definition for tree search.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from two identified challenges in the field of multi-turn LLM agent reinforcement learning:
1. **Heavy Rollout Budget**: Multi-turn agent tasks require LLMs to interact with environments over extended sequences, generating trajectories with numerous tokens and multiple tool calls. Current group-based RL methods employ chain-based rollouts, sampling independent trajectories that often involve redundant computations. This leads to high costs, particularly for tool calls (e.g., search APIs), and makes the rollout phase a dominant factor in overall training time. The objective is to increase the number of achievable rollouts within a fixed budget of tokens or tool calls.
2. **Sparse Supervision**: Despite the increasing length of agent trajectories, existing agent RL methods primarily rely on a single outcome reward for the entire trajectory. This sparse, trajectory-level signal complicates credit assignment, making it difficult for the model to identify effective intermediate steps or actions. This can lead to inefficient learning, instability, and even training collapse. The objective is to construct more fine-grained, step-wise process supervision signals, even when only outcome rewards are available.

These challenges collectively pose a fundamental question that motivates the paper: "Can we construct more fine-grained supervision signals for agent RL under a limited rollout budget while still solely based on outcome rewards?"

To address this question, the key objectives of the proposed Tree-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (Tree-GRPO) are:
- To introduce a tree-based rollout strategy with nodes defined at the complete agent step level (Thought-Action-Observation) to enhance rollout efficiency.
- To develop a mechanism for estimating grouped relative advantages at both intra-tree and inter-tree levels to generate implicit step-level preference learning objectives from sparse outcome rewards.
- To demonstrate, through theoretical analysis and empirical evaluation, that this tree-based RL approach can achieve higher performance and greater training stability under reduced rollout budgets compared to chain-based methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Tree-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (Tree-GRPO), integrates a tree-search-based sampling strategy with a novel advantage estimation technique to address the efficiency and sparse supervision challenges in LLM agent RL.

#### 3.1. Agent Step-Level Tree Search for Rollouts

Unlike conventional RL approaches that sample independent, chain-based trajectories, Tree-GRPO utilizes an online rollout strategy based on tree search. A key aspect of this design is that each node in the search tree represents a complete agent interaction step, encompassing a Thought-Action-Observation (τ, α, o) tuple. This contrasts with previous tree-based RL methods that typically operate at the token or sentence level, which the authors argue is less suitable for agent tasks due to a lack of clear contextual segmentation and potential for wasted exploration.

The tree-search sampling process follows an "initialize-then-expand" approach:
1. **Initialization**: For each given prompt, `M` independent chain-based trajectories are generated by the current policy model, forming the initial `M` trees.
2. **Sampling**: Subsequently, `N` non-leaf nodes are randomly sampled from each of these `M` trees for expansion.
3. **Expansion**: For each selected node, the LLM continues generating the remainder of the response, starting from the context of that node up to the root. This new segment forms a new branch appended to the source tree.

This iterative process, repeated `L` times, generates a total of `M × (L × N + 1)` rollouts. By sharing common prefixes among interleaved trajectories, this tree-search method is designed to acquire a larger number of agent rollouts for a given token/tool-call budget compared to chain-based methods. The expected budget for tree-search sampling is formulated as `E[B_tree] = M ⋅ B + L ⋅ N ⋅ B/2`, where `B` is the expected budget of a single complete trajectory.

#### 3.2. Tree-Based Group Relative Advantages

To address sparse supervision, Tree-GRPO leverages the inherent structure of the generated trees to construct fine-grained process supervision signals from outcome rewards.
- **Intra-tree Group Relative Advantage**: At every branching point in a tree, outcome rewards from the respective subtree leaves are back-propagated. The differences in these rewards across sibling branches are used to form a preference-learning objective for the subtrees. This process provides step-level supervision signals, with the granularity determined by the subtree depth. The advantage for a trajectory `H_i` within an intra-tree group `G_Intra-tree(T_i)` is estimated as `A_Intra-tree(H_i) = (R(H_i) - mean({R(H_j)}_{G_Intra-tree(T_i)})) / std({R(H_j)}_{G_Intra-tree(T_i)})`.
- **Inter-tree Group Relative Advantage**: To enhance the stability of RL training, especially when the number of rollouts within a single tree is limited, rollouts are also grouped across all `M` trees. The inter-tree advantage is calculated similarly: `A_Inter-tree(H_i) = (R(H_i) - mean({R(H_j)}_{G_Inter-tree(T_i)})) / std({R(H_j)}_{G_Inter-tree(T_i)})`.
- **Combined Advantage**: The final advantage estimate, `A_tree(H_i)`, is obtained by summing the intra-tree and inter-tree advantages.
- **Policy Optimization Objective**: The policy model `π_θ` is then updated by maximizing a Tree-GRPO objective function (Eq. 8), which is a variant of policy optimization incorporating these grouped relative advantages and a KL divergence regularization term against a reference policy `π_ref`.

#### 3.3. Implicit Step-Level Preference Learning

The paper provides a theoretical analysis demonstrating that, under a binary preference assumption, the intra-tree Group Relative Policy Optimization objective is structurally equivalent to a step-level Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) objective. Both objectives share the same "preference advantage gradient" term, differing only in their weight terms. This theoretical link suggests that Tree-GRPO inherently performs fine-grained, step-level preference learning in an online setting, leveraging the tree structure to derive process signals without requiring explicit human-annotated step-level preference data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Tree-GRPO was evaluated across 11 datasets spanning three types of QA tasks: Multi-Hop QA, Single-Hop QA, and Web-Agent QA, using Qwen2.5 and Llama3.2 models of varying scales (1.5b to 14b).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Multi-Hop QA**: In tasks requiring multi-turn interactions, Tree-GRPO consistently demonstrated substantial performance improvements over chain-based GRPO. For smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5b and Llama3.2-3b), relative improvements ranged from 16% to 69%. Notably, Tree-GRPO enabled base models (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5b) to engage in multi-turn tool-use, a behavior that chain-based methods struggled to elicit without supervised fine-tuning. For larger models (Qwen2.5-14b), where RL benefits were generally less pronounced, Tree-GRPO still achieved an average relative improvement of 8.4%. These results suggest the value of process-level signals.
- **Single-Hop QA**: In tasks with fewer interaction turns, Tree-GRPO showed stable improvements, particularly for smaller models. However, the gains were less significant compared to multi-hop scenarios. This is attributed to the typically shallow tree depth (around 2 turns) in single-hop tasks, which limits the potential benefits of fine-grained process signals over trajectory-level rewards.
- **Web-Agent QA**: On web-agent tasks, Tree-GRPO consistently outperformed chain-based GRPO. The most significant improvement was observed on GAIA, with a 28% average F1 score increase. However, for highly challenging benchmarks like BrowseComp, overall RL gains were marginal, likely due to limitations in the available training data.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Analysis

- **Budget Efficiency**: Tree-GRPO demonstrated superior performance under various rollout budget constraints. Notably, when operating with a highly constrained budget (approximately two complete agent trajectories per prompt), chain-based RL struggled significantly, whereas Tree-GRPO achieved a 112% relative improvement in Multi-Hop QA. In several configurations, Tree-GRPO achieved superior performance while utilizing only a quarter of the rollout budget required by chain-based methods. This highlights the efficiency gained from prefix sharing in tree search.
- **Beyond Performance (Action Number and Stability)**: Beyond performance metrics, Tree-GRPO encouraged LLM agents to engage in longer interaction sequences (more tool calls) in multi-hop QA, increasing the average number of actions from 2.4 to 3.0. This indicates a learning tendency towards more thorough exploration. The method also exhibited greater stability across different learning rate warmup ratios and KL term coefficients, critical hyperparameters in LLM RL training, particularly for smaller models.
- **Ablation Study on Tree-based Advantages**: The study on advantage estimation methods indicated that combining both intra-tree and inter-tree group relative advantages yielded the most stable and improved training performance. Pure intra-tree advantage estimation was prone to collapse with limited branching (small N) due to high variance in baseline estimation, while inter-tree advantages acted as a stabilizing fallback. Even with only inter-tree advantage, the tree-based sampling still outperformed chain-based GRPO due to its efficient rollout generation.
- **Ablation Study on Tree Search Level**: Experiments comparing agent step-level tree search with token/sentence-level tree search demonstrated that the agent step-level approach was superior. Token/sentence-level tree search performed worse than agent step-level and even below chain-based GRPO, suggesting that fine-grained exploration at the token level is less effective for structured agent tasks and can hinder learning performance.
- **Ablation Study on Tree Structures**: The study explored the impact of tree parameters (M: number of initial trees, N: number of nodes sampled for expansion, L: number of expansion iterations). It suggested that M influences sampling diversity (exploration), while N and L affect the granularity of the process signal (exploitation). An appropriate balance between these parameters is crucial for optimal performance, with diminishing returns for N, L values significantly exceeding the typical task interaction length.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Tree-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (Tree-GRPO) contributes to the field of LLM agent reinforcement learning by addressing two critical limitations: computational budget and sparse supervision.

- **Enhanced Efficiency**: By employing an agent step-level tree search, the method significantly reduces the rollout budget (in terms of tokens and tool calls) required for training, enabling more samples to be obtained within the same resources. This is particularly impactful for multi-turn agent tasks where interactions are costly. The ability to achieve superior performance with a fraction of the budget compared to chain-based methods has practical implications for scaling LLM agent training and making it more accessible.
- **Improved Supervision and Learning Stability**: Tree-GRPO successfully generates fine-grained, step-wise process supervision signals purely from outcome rewards by leveraging the tree structure. This addresses the sparse supervision problem inherent in long-horizon tasks, leading to more effective credit assignment. The theoretical equivalence of intra-tree GRPO to step-level DPO provides a principled understanding of how process-level learning occurs. This improved supervision translates to more stable and robust RL training, especially for smaller LLM models that traditionally struggle with complex agent behaviors.
- **Advancement in Agentic Capabilities**: The method's ability to encourage longer, more complex interactions and enable base models to adopt multi-turn agent paradigms without explicit supervised fine-tuning suggests a step towards more capable and autonomous LLM agents. This could accelerate research and development in areas requiring sophisticated sequential decision-making, such as complex reasoning, knowledge-intensive QA, and web-based interaction.
- **Flexible and Robust Training**: The demonstrated stability across various hyperparameter settings and the flexibility in configuring tree structures (M, N, L) indicate a more robust training framework for LLM agents. This reduces the sensitivity of the training process and could simplify the deployment of RL for diverse agent applications.

Overall, Tree-GRPO offers a practical and theoretically grounded approach to advance the training of LLM agents, making online reinforcement learning more efficient, stable, and capable for complex, multi-turn tasks. It paves the way for future research into integrating richer exploration strategies and reflective reasoning within tree-based online RL frameworks for highly autonomous agents.
