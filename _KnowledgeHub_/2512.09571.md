# Mastering Diverse, Unknown, and Cluttered Tracks for Robust Vision-Based Drone Racing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed a vision-based autonomous drone racing framework that navigates diverse, unknown, and cluttered tracks. The approach enables agile flight exceeding 5 m/s in real-world cluttered environments and demonstrates robustness to gate position errors over 1 meter through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) solutions for drone racing struggle with generalization to novel and cluttered environments, often lacking robustness to gate position command noise.
- The exploration-exploitation conflict in high-speed, collision-prone environments hinders the learning of aggressive racing behaviors and creates an imbalance between speed optimization and obstacle avoidance.
- Perceptual ambiguity between gates and obstacles in depth maps makes it difficult for vision systems to acquire discriminative representations without explicit semantic supervision.

## Method

- A two-phase ==reinforcement learning== framework (soft-collision followed by hard-collision) was introduced to facilitate learning aggressive flight behaviors before refining precise obstacle avoidance.
- An adaptive noise-augmented curriculum, combined with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture, was employed to enhance policy robustness to noisy gate commands and improve perception-aware gate recognition.
- A track primitive generator was used to create diverse training tracks, and Local Lipschitz Continuity Constraints (L2C2) were applied to improve action smoothness and temporal stability for better sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The two-phase training method achieved higher average speeds across terrain difficulty levels compared to one-phase training, demonstrating improved aggressive racing without sacrificing safety.
- The adaptive noise-augmented curriculum maintained nearly an **80%** success rate under **2.1** m gate position noise, significantly outperforming policies trained with fixed or no noise.
- The trained policy achieved agile flight exceeding 5 m/s in diverse, cluttered real-world environments with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and demonstrated robustness to gate position errors over 1 meter in physical tests.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A phased training approach, starting with 'soft' collisions, allows the agent to explore aggressive racing trajectories without early termination, effectively balancing speed and safety learning.
> - Dynamically increasing noise in gate position commands during training forces the policy to prioritize robust visual perception over potentially inaccurate external guidance, leading to better generalization.
> - Decoupling actor and critic observations (asymmetric actor-critic) enables the actor to learn from noisy, realistic inputs while the critic maintains a stable value estimate from ground truth, enhancing policy learning and robustness.
